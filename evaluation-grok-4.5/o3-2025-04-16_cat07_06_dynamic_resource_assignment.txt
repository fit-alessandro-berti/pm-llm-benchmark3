## Evaluation

The submission hits the required seven-section structure and shows real grasp of multi-objective, socio-technical assignment. Hard vs soft constraints are mostly separated, the worker explanation is usable, and the audit/PM4Py sections are directionally right. Under strict grading, though, the core artifacts—the scoring logic and the C001–C016 table—contain material constraint breaches, inconsistent training claims, and a ranking function that does not do what the text claims. That caps the score well below “strong.”

### What works

- **§1** lists the main safety rules (Security/Approval skill floors, batch cap 4, OT avoidance, continuity for high-complexity/high-handover, QC batching) and soft goals (SLA, rework, fairness, training ≥20%, resilience).
- **§2** names the right score families and includes eligibility, supervision pairs, post-checks, and escalation.
- **§5** is clear, non-punitive, and operationally credible.
- **§6** covers the required indicator set with sensible definitions.
- **§7** wires in many of the named PM4Py entry points (format, SNA, roles/similarity, batches, waiting/service, variants, filters, performance DFG).
- Several continuity-heavy choices (C002, C004, C005, C007, C008, C011) match the scenario evidence on reassignment and handover cost.

### Major flaws (heavy deductions)

**1. Hard-constraint violations in the recommended table**

- **C010 → R2_Bruno (normal, 20h SLA):** Bruno is **overtime risk = high**. Constraint 4 forbids loading high-OT resources unless the case is urgent and no safer option exists. Many safer Approval-capable options exist (e.g. Farid skill 3 already owning other work but with low OT; Eva skill 2; Jonas skill 1 only prepare—not finalize). This is a direct rule break.
- **C014 → R4_David (normal, 25h):** David is high OT, 14 open cases, **3.0h left**, already given **C003**. Normal priority does not justify another high-OT assignment.
- **C001 → R3_Celine skill Security=1 + Gita supervise (urgent, 3h, complexity 0.88, “needs specialist”):** Rule 6 allows skill 1–2 under Gita/Hugo, so it is not illegal—but with **3h SLA** and historical evidence that weak skill + keep-owner adds ~3.3h service time, this is a poor urgency/safety trade. A specialist primary (Gita/Hugo) with continuity as secondary would be more defensible. Treating this as the flagship “continuity” win understates SLA risk.

**2. Training labels are often wrong (undermines the 20% claim)**

Documented training priorities: Amal=diagnosis, Bruno=quality, Celine=negotiation, David=approval, Eva=security, Farid=diagnosis, Hugo=approval, Iris=negotiation, Jonas=security.

| C010 Bruno+Farid | Yes | **Invalid** — Bruno’s priority is **quality**, not approval; Farid’s is **diagnosis**, not “approval coaching” |
| C013 Farid+Bruno | Yes | **Invalid** — Eligibility ≠ Farid’s diagnosis priority; “indirectly” is hand-waving |

Solid training-support assignments are roughly **C009 and C015** (maybe borderline others). The summary’s “4 of 11 normal/low = 36%” does not match the table (five Yes flags; wrong denominator; inconsistent arithmetic). The post-check that “ensures 20%” is not evidenced by correct case-level logic.

**3. Scoring algorithm is logically broken**

- `total_score` adds **`0.35 * sla_score(c)`**, which is **constant across candidates for a given case**, so it **never changes who wins**—only an unused offset. SLA urgency does not drive resource choice inside the score.
- Eligibility line is incomplete: `skill_level(...) required_min(c)` (missing `>=` / threshold logic).
- `c.prioritynormal` is not valid logic; training gate is underspecified vs urgent/high exclusion.
- **No case processing order** (urgent-first / constrained matching). With a global cap of 4 new assignments and shared supervisors, greedy per-case scoring without order or joint assignment will violate caps and double-book reviewers; the write-up never solves that.
- `resilience_boost` for bottleneck skills when `skill < 3` is ambiguous and can **encourage** non-specialists onto bottlenecks—the opposite of “reserve specialist capacity” in §1.
- Handover/continuity terms are double-counted in spirit (`continuity` plus `handover_cost`) without clear normalization; weights are asserted, not justified or sensitivity-checked.
- Supervisor load “does not count toward the 4-limit” weakens the fairness constraint while Celine/Farid are still used as standing reviewers.

**4. Workload / bottleneck picture is internally inconsistent**

Informal tally: Farid main roles on **C002, C011, C013** (and review on C010); Celine on **C001, C007** plus reviews on **C009, C016**; David on **C003, C014** despite OT/hours. The “all ≤4” claim may hold for *main* assignees only if reviews are free—socio-technically they are not, and resilience suffers. C006 “batch later” still assigns Iris now without a batching mechanism in the algorithm.

**5. Baseline comparison overclaims precision**

“+8% rework”, “<2% throughput gap”, “lowest overall SLA risk” are presented as results without a model, simulation, or even a worked first-available / specialist-only assignment of the same 16 cases. A fair comparison needed at least a side-by-side assignment table or explicit counting (handovers, OT hits, skill&lt;2, training share). Qualitative dimensions are fine; fake precision is not.

**6. PM4Py sketch is plausible but sloppy**

- `df['service_time'] = pm4py.get_service_time(df)` is not a faithful use of the usual API (typically parameterized; returns aggregations, not a clean column assign).
- `save_vis_dfg` / `discover_batches(..., activity_key='Quality Check')` are shaky relative to real PM4Py surfaces.
- `filter_case_performance` is in the prompt’s relevant list and barely used.
- Violation filter assumes a `skill_level` column never joined from the resource matrix.
Still acceptable as a *sketch*, but not “audit-ready.”

**7. Smaller issues**

- QC hard rule omits **low** priority (prompt allows normal/low).
- C012 “could have been training” then denied because “already in quota” while quota math is wrong.
- C013 new case with owner `none` should be a clean eligibility routing to a real eligibility strength (Amal/Bruno), not a forced Farid training fiction.
- Exception path (manual escalation) is one line; no conflict resolution when supervisor capacity is the scarce resource (Gita 4.5h, urgent security).
- Socio-technical note is strong in §5 but thin in §2–3 (no mention of worker agency beyond override, gaming of `assignment_reason`, or trust calibration).
