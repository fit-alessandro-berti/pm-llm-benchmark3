## Deductions (hypercritical)

**1. Recommended portfolio  any defined portfolio (major structural fault)**  
Section 3 defines:
- Quick-win: A1, A5, A9, A10  
- Bottleneck: A4, A5, A6, A7  
- Compliance-first: A3, A4, A9, A12  

Section 7 recommends **A4, A5, A6, A9** and labels it a “slightly modified bottleneck.” The required flow is: define portfolios  analyze them  pick one. Inventing a hybrid only at the end breaks that contract and forces the reader to assemble the real answer.

**2. No unified process-level effects for the actual recommendation**  
Section 4 only scores the three named portfolios. Effects of **A4+A5+A6+A9 together** (interaction of cleaner KYC path + doc quality + analyst inflow surge + contract hard-gates, without A7’s valuation relief) are never stated as one package. That is a material gap for “expected process-level effects.”

**3. Bottleneck portfolio likely violates the compliance mandate**  
A4/A5/A6/A7 are primarily flow/labor/sync tools. None clearly “strengthen controls” against the stated findings (KYC-before-signing, decision–contract match, four-eyes, auditable remediation) the way A9/A3/A12 do. Section 3 does not flag this; the fix is postponed to section 7. Portfolio design should have internalized the hard constraint.

**4. Cost of dropping A7 under-argued**  
58h external wait and Equipment-finance concentration are among the largest single delays in the log. Swapping A7 for A9 is defensible for compliance, but section 7 understates residual cycle-time damage for collateral-heavy variants relative to the emphasis A7 received in the matrix and bottleneck write-up.

**5. Rollout vs four-module cap**  
Phase 3 still rehearses A7 inside the first-year narrative (“if budget permits… beyond the first four”). Under a hard cap of four, year-1 scope should stay cleanly at A4/A5/A6/A9; A7 belongs in an explicit year-2 backlog without ambiguity.

**6. Lesser nits**  
- Quick-win includes A10 while KYC gate is still weak—risk is noted but the portfolio still packages it as a “win.”  
- A5 as rank-1 is defensible as prerequisite to A6, but process-level “highest” impact vs A4/A6/A9 is contestable; matrix could have been tiered rather than forced 1–12.  
- Deep-integration choice (A6+A9) is reasonable; light-touch claim for A5 portal nudging is optimistic if core case state must stay consistent.  
- Four-eyes is handled mainly via A9/workflow commentary; A8 is correctly deferred, but High-exposure month-end 5.8% could use a sharper interim control in phases 1–2.
