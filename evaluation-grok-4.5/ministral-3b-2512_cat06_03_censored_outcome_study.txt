The submitted design fails the core quality bar on multiple fundamental dimensions (selective observation via activation, as-of semantics, honest target/estimand, cohort arithmetic, competing-risk logic, and a working PM4Py skeleton). It is not a near-miss; it contains logical contradictions, factual errors against the supplied snapshot, incorrect use of process-mining primitives, and non-executable code. Below is a section-by-section critique under the required strictness.

**1. Target definition**  
Primary target is framed as “probability of default \ldots conditioned on the case being activated” with unit of analysis “activated accounts only.” This directly contradicts the business question (“every credit application”) and never confronts that default is undefined for non-activated cases under the observed policy. Prediction time is left vague (“first event \ldots that meets the prefix criteria”). The alternative immediately labels rejected/withdrawn/transferred as “competing outcomes (non-default)”—precisely the convenience labeling the prompt forbids—and then claims a CIF “among activated cases,” which is incoherent. No clear estimand, no population statement that survives contact with the 48 k applications, and no discussion of the stronger assumptions a counterfactual/policy target would need. Fatal.

**2. Cohort construction**  
Inclusion rule “start event before 1 Jan 2024” is simply wrong given the stated observation window. The cohort-flow table mis-states the data: it lists “Activated cases 21 600” (the matured labeled export) and then a separate “Activated (unlabeled) 3 600,” so the arithmetic only appears to sum to 48 k by double-counting categories; true activated volume is 25 200. Transferred cases are simultaneously “excluded” and “treated as censored.” Still-open and not-yet-mature cases are not given distinct as-of / maturity rules. No proper accounting of which of the 48 k are labeled, right-censored, competing, selectively unobserved, or immature. Violates the explicit requirement to account for every application.

**3. Censoring and missing-label strategy**  
The primary analysis proposes a Fine-Gray model “for activated cases, with \ldots Rejection/transfer as competing risks.” Once a case is activated, rejection is no longer possible; the competing-risk construction is therefore logically impossible. Ordinary right-censoring, delayed labels, selection-through-activation, transfer, and permanently missing partner feeds are named but then collapsed into an incoherent model. Sensitivity analyses are listed without stating the distinct assumptions each requires. Testability claims are superficial. The section does not separate “undefined under the observed policy” from ordinary censoring—the central methodological point.

**4. Prefix extraction**  
Fixed-length-3 + ad-hoc 24 h rule is underspecified for document-loop rework. Short-case handling is simply “drop,” discarding information without justification. Activity renaming is delegated to `pm4py.discover_dfg`, which does not perform canonical name mapping; that is the wrong primitive. No explicit “events \le prediction timestamp” filter is stated, nor any handling of the 15 Oct 2025 rename/duplicate problem. The supplied code fragment already uses a non-recommended importer and assumes a DataFrame schema that `get_prefixes_from_log` does not guarantee.

**5. Process-aware features**
