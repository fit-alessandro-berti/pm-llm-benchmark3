## Major defects

**Fatal DECLARE encoding of R5 (and consequent matrix falsehoods).**  
The model places a global  
`precedence: ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")`.  
Standard DECLARE semantics: if Activate occurs then Opinion must have occurred earlier. This is *unconditional*. R5 is conditional (“after Sanctions Escalation \ldots”). Consequently every normal happy-path trace (T1, T2, T4) that activates without a legal opinion is a DECLARE violation. The matrix nevertheless marks them `pass`. The accompanying text even calls the rule “Strict Legal Gating (R5)” while simultaneously claiming the happy paths succeed. This single inconsistency collapses the DECLARE layer, the test-case section, and the claim that Resolution A was cleanly implemented.

**Procedural model versus claimed conformance.**  
The ProcessTree deliberately omits Request Clarification / Receive Clarification, Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion, Change Bank Details and Suspend Vendor. Yet the matrix awards `pass` to T2 (contains clarification pair), T6 and T7 (contain escalation + opinion). In any standard PM4Py process-tree conformance those extra activities produce log moves / deviations. The exclusion is disclosed in §3 but then ignored when verdicts are assigned. The procedural column is therefore not trustworthy.

**Incoherent handling of the R5/R6 conflict.**  
§1 correctly lists two resolutions and selects A for the control-flow artefacts. For T6 under Resolution B the matrix suddenly claims Procedural = pass, DECLARE = pass. No alternative `declare_model` or ProcessTree is supplied; the only artefacts given already embed the R5 precedence. The “policy-dependent” label is used, but the layer-by-layer cells are falsified. Silent switching of models is exactly what the prompt forbids.
