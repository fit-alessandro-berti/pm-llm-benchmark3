## Grading rationale (strict)

### Strengths that prevent a low score
- Exact required headings are present and populated.
- Both briefs acknowledge the other side’s strongest points (prosecution concedes 707/709/711/706; defense concedes 703/708/705/714/712).
- Burden-of-proof framing is coherent and usable.
- Most case calls are defensible with stated dominant reasons; MIXED is largely avoided as a dodge.
- M1 design correctly separates what POWL can and cannot enforce; limitations on the RMI loop and route guards are explicit.
- Overlay table + data schema address the real failure modes (expiry, eligibility snapshots, immutable notification links, alias SoD).

### Material defects (each material enough to block a high score)

**1. Classification inconsistency / soft-pedaling (material)**  
- **SUP-713 as COMPLIANT** is weakly justified. Mapping was approved *after* the case; treating retroactive ontology ratification as pure “evidence interpretation” while hammering 705/714 for lacking contemporaneous authority is a double standard the brief does not fully resolve. MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT (or COMPLIANT with low confidence and a governance defect flag) would be tighter.  
- **SUP-711 as COMPLIANT** papers over that the observed log *as recorded* does not fit M0 without a preprocessing rule that is not part of M0. DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE or an explicit “compliant after mandatory dedup” subclass would be cleaner; calling it plain COMPLIANT inflates the compliant count.  
- **SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT** overreaches relative to the materials: no rule prohibits the relationship; only disclosure is missing. Without a cited binding disclosure rule tied to onboarding controls, this is at best MIXED or a soft organizational finding. Severity “Low” does not fix the category stretch.  
- **SUP-702 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at Moderate-High confidence** while MDV (a hard PD-3 precondition) is unproved is too confident. Conditional / lower confidence was required by the brief’s own burden rule.

**2. Authority handling has a quiet slide**  
The judgment correctly rejects constructive extension and “imminent amendment,” but then treats PD-3 as clearly authorizing 702 while the Compliance observer dispute is only half-digested. The prosecution brief raises the ratification issue; the final judgment mostly waves it away with “committee may run a monitored pilot.” That is plausible but under-argued given CS-9’s controlled status. A stricter opinion would mark 702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only *if* pilot authority is accepted, with an explicit residual legal risk.

**3. Model-semantics slip**  
M0 analysis is mostly right on concurrency, but the defense/prosecution discussion sometimes implies M0 “requires both checks before reject” as if that were a CS-9 reading rather than a pure M0 structural fact. The matrix handles 709 well; the narrative occasionally blurs model defect vs norm defect.

**4. Evidence matrix quality is uneven**  
- Several “What would reverse” cells are thin or non-operational (705’s “standing emergency policy,” 709’s nested condition).  
- Material risk for 712 as “High” is assertive without evidence of concealment or loss; Moderate-High would fit the record better.  
- Aggregate evidence (14 ineligible, 9 unknown, 6 August) is cited in briefs but barely integrated into the system-level quantitative judgment beyond a gloss.

**5. PM4Py / architecture issues (real, not nitpicks)**  
- **Operator.LOOP + SilentTransition construction** is a reasonable approximation, but the code never validates that PM4Py’s POWL layer accepts this composition; presenting it as drop-in executable overclaims.  
- **Three routes each embedding their own Sanctions/Quality nodes** with duplicate labels is acknowledged, but the proposal does not specify how conformance checking would avoid double-counting or mis-alignment in practice.  
- **Emergency route** omits any representation of daily monitoring (even as a documented non-POWL obligation tied to case attributes)—mentioned in text, weak in the operationalization.  
- **Reject branch** with no preceding checks, justified by case typing, creates a hole: nothing in M1 prevents a FULL_ONBOARDING case from rejecting without sanctions. That needs an explicit engine-level guard; “scope rules” alone is hand-waving.  
- Auto route forces Quality after Activate always; there is no path for auto-approve with quality already done (edge case, but the architecture claims generality).  
- No DECLARE/log-skeleton fragments are actually specified—only named. For a section that promises layered formalism mapping, that is incomplete.  
- Code demo does not exercise quality-within-2-business-days, spend cap, or open-RMI checks—the obligations most needed to discipline 702/704/705.

**6. Dialectical fairness shortfalls**  
- Prosecution brief is strong on 703/708/expiry/normalization, weaker on why *eligible in-window* pilot use is still attackable if CS-9 is supreme (it raises the observer point then underuses it).  
- Defense brief underplays Internal Audit’s severity flags and the 14 ineligible cases as a system control failure (engine should have blocked), not only as “14 bad apples.”  
- Final judgment’s “qualified compliance” is close to a soft split: the task forbade mere difference-splitting. The case table is decisive, but the system paragraph re-centers toward balance without a sharp primary finding (e.g., “noncompliant exception governance with an obsolete model” as the lead sentence).

**7. Traceability gaps**  
- Master-data validation absence for 702 is noted then not carried into the classification severity/confidence with enough force.  
- SUP-704 “daily monitoring exists” is accepted at face value with no document standard.  
- N2’s “automated master-data validation” never appears as a first-class matrix column factor for 702/703/714.

**8. Writing / precision**  
- Occasional overclaim (“strict-liability in many regimes”) without grounding.  
- System-level math “3 compliant + 2 JE + 6 NC + 3 defect + 1 indeterminate = 15” depends on the contested 711/713/715 calls; brittle.  
- Section 6 is conceptually sound but denser on taxonomy than on enforceable acceptance criteria.

### Score calibration
- A 9–10 would need airtight case categories, sharper primary system finding, fully specified non-POWL constraints, and code/guards that close the reject-without-sanctions and MDV holes.  
- A 7–8 is blocked by the 713/711/715/702 confidence problems, incomplete DECLARE operationalization, and residual authority hand-waving.  
- Below 6 would be unfair: the piece is substantially complete, mostly faithful to M0 semantics, and stronger than a generic split-the-difference essay.

**Final grade: 6.4** — solid structured dialectic and a credible M1 direction, undermined by several classification stretches, incomplete enforcement specification outside POWL, and uneven application of the brief’s own contemporaneous-authority standard.
