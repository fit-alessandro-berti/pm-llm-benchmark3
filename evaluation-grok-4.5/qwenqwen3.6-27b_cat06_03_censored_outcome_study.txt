The design sections (1–8) correctly identify selective observation via activation, refuse to treat rejected/withdrawn/open cases as non-default, separate ordinary right-censoring from undefined outcomes, use an as-of/maturity cutoff, prefer temporal validation over random splits, and supply a usable leakage table plus deployment-feedback limitations. These elements satisfy the core statistical and process-aware requirements and avoid the classic failure modes listed in the quality criteria.

However, multiple concrete defects prevent a higher score:

- Section 4 contains a logical error: “the final prediction prefix is the union of both constraints” would admit more (later) events; the correct construction is the prefix truncated at the *earlier* of the two cut-offs.
- Section 9 code is only ~45 lines (well below the mandated ~80–160), does not build an actual leakage-free feature table, applies `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` to the full log (potential look-ahead), and contains a broken assert (`assert not df[leak_cols].notna().any()`) that would fire on any realistic extract because those columns are present by design. The skeleton therefore fails the “concrete enough to implement” and “prevent post-prefix columns \ldots from entering the feature table” requirements.
- Case-level metadata construction is incomplete (terminal status, `label_available_at`-driven maturity, transfer linkage, and competing-event flags are only partially realized).
- Minor wording and consistency slips appear elsewhere (prediction-time phrasing, population vs. analysis-set mismatch, incomplete repair logic for `documents_total`/`risk_score` in code).

These are not peripheral; they directly violate stated output constraints and introduce implementable errors. Under the mandated hypercritical standard the answer is therefore only moderately successful.
