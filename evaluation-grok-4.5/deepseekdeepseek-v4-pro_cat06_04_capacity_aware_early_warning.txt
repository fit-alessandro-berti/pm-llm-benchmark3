**7.4**

The final answer (post-reasoning) is structurally complete, hits every required section/header, correctly deems I-03 ineligible, correctly suppresses I-05 (unchanged repeat) while re-evaluating I-07 (new log), internalizes spillovers, applies segment calibration adjustments, runs Optimistic/Base/Adverse scenarios, and supplies concrete (not merely principle-level) fairness/feedback and monitoring safeguards. These are the core strength criteria. However, under the mandated hypercritical standard the answer falls well short of near-flawless.

Major and minor defects that force a substantial deduction:
- Numerical incoherence across sections: Section 1 and the priority scores in Section 2 use SP  €2000 (and the derived ENBs such as I-04 = +5030); Section 3 suddenly switches to a €120 k value-at-risk base and SP = €2400 / €4800 / €7200. The same portfolio therefore receives inconsistent net-impact numbers. This is not “approximate range” language; it is an internal contradiction that makes the tables non-auditable against one another.
- Invented point estimates presented with false precision. The €100 k / €120 k total diagnosis-stage value, the exact 0.156 / 0.25 adjusted reductions, the €500 ENB threshold, the 90-minute / 4-hour cooldowns, and the 0.80 / 2 h emergency overrides are all stipulated without sensitivity bounds or derivation from the supplied data. The problem explicitly warns that effect ranges are not known causal effects; the answer nevertheless rescales them proportionally and then treats the resulting mid-points as operationally decisive.
- Column mismatch and missing ranges. Section 3 header promises “Direct economic benefit range” yet supplies only point estimates; the same is true for several other cells. Adverse-scenario net impact is shown as negative yet the policy still selects the identical two-case portfolio with no robustification (maximin, chance constraint, or explicit “only if adverse also non-negative” rule).
- Residual logical gaps. Spillover accounting continues to charge the full six-case €120 k even after I-09 is removed from the untreated set (over-stating harm). Non-monetary / regulatory obligations beyond a generic “emergency override” receive almost no weight. The decision rule never clarifies how the two specialists’ ordinary diagnosis work is re-prioritized or how the 8–20 min aggregate delay is mapped into the 0.01–0.03 probability increments under congestion. Typos and missing hyphens (“90minute”, “basescenario”, “valueatrisk”, etc.) further reduce operational polish.
- Calibration and feedback treatment, while present, remains mechanical. The proportional rescaling of risk reduction is never stress-tested against the possibility that the supplied ranges already embed observational bias; the hold-out / counterfactual labelling proposal is mentioned but not sized or costed.

Collectively these issues—especially the cross-section numerical inconsistency and the elevation of arbitrary point estimates to decision thresholds—prevent any score in the 9+ band. The answer is still clearly superior to naïve probability-threshold or single-scenario baselines, hence the mid-7 range rather than a collapse into the 5s.
