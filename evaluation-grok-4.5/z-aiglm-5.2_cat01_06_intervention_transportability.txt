### Deductions (hypercritical)

**1. Quantitative scenarios — material inconsistency (major)**  
“Expected median-cycle change” is not on a single population basis:
- Optimistic: 4 to 7 h at 38% eligible (reasoning later says eligible subset).
- Digital-only: 2 to 4 h **overall** at the same 38%.  
Same eligibility, different column meaning, weak differentiation of assumptions (assessment slack, IT, etc.). For a section scored on quantitative scenario analysis, this is a real flaw.  
Bottleneck-shift reasoning is also loose: in steady state, upstream parallelization does not raise assessment arrival *rate* unless system throughput rises; the answer asserts saturation from “faster verification” without that distinction. Tail-worsening is possible (transient WIP, throughput lift, legal queue) but overstated as a simple rate effect.

**2. PM4Py / model fidelity — likely non-runnable API (major for §8)**  
- `StrictPartialOrder(..., order=[...])` does not match common PM4Py POWL APIs (`nodes` + `add_edge` / binary relation). A comment is not a fix.
- `Operator` from `pm4py.objects.powl.obj` is version-fragile; usually process-tree `Operator`.
- `pm4py.check_soundness` / `play_out(..., num_traces=)` are doubtful as used.
- Nested POWL + LOOP only approximates supplement  Document Check; limitations text is good, but the runnable model is weaker than claimed.
Conceptual control-flow (Assessment after both checks; legal XOR; loop) is right; execution fidelity is not.

Required nodes appear, but effect modification is mostly drawn as direct arrows into `true_overlap` rather than explicit modifier structure on the treatmentoverlap edge. `S_legal_requirement  legal_capacity` is an odd selection encoding.

**4. Smaller issues**  
- Optimistic vs digital-only scenarios blur (both ~38% eligible).
