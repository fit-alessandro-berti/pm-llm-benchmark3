### Critical defects

**1. Formalization is not actually formal.**  
The “normalized” table and Datalog-like block systematically drop operators and predicates (`jurisdictionDE`, `amount100k`, `risk=HIGH  cust=PEP`, `Sanctions_List_Update <L  Release_Funds`). Classification logic never properly encodes three-valued/missing triggers (unknown amount should block `needs_R1`, not silently falsify it). This is a major miss against §2.

**2. Required code is not a real deliverable.**  
- CSV is a placeholder (`<PASTE THE WHOLE CSV…>`), so the block is not executable as given.  
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` is not used for the main analysis path.

- No exemption-register join or evidence-time fields → R5/TX-302/TX-310 not machine-checked.

- DECLARE snippet looks API-invented (`parse_declare_specification`, template string shape, `res['fulfillments']`) and would not reliably run.  
Against §5’s explicit checklist, this is largely aspirational pseudocode.

**3. Presentation errors that affect trust.**  
TX-305’s findings row is column-shifted (`n/a | n/a | INDETERMINATE` lands classification off-grid). Section 1 has garbled text (`>20 % (R4)orrisk_level`). Several rule-table cells are unreadable. For a graded artifact, that is not cosmetic.

**4. Semantic imprecision even where answers are “right.”**  
- “Material data change” is broadened to any risk/jurisdiction/customer_type change, then applied unevenly; R4 only defines amount increases for review invalidation.  
- TX-305 deviation “ER present but may be stale” is unmotivated (no known post-review amount change).

- Ambiguities note on TX-306 confuses the “20% rule” with a case whose increase is ~4.5%.  
- Confidence/severity are thin and sometimes mis-slotted.

**5. §§6–7 are adequate but not excellent.**  
The six-plus failure modes and logging recommendations cover the required themes (final-state leakage, post-hoc exemptions, material change, conditional guards, missing data, same-timestamp order). They are the second-best part of the answer, yet remain generic and do not compensate for broken formalization/code.

### What “strict” means here
Nearly all case labels being correct would support a high score **only if** temporal semantics, rule formalization, and PM4Py/pandas checking were correspondingly rigorous. They are not. The submission reads like a competent manual review wrapped in incomplete formal and engineering scaffolding—with several places where symbols/columns are wrong enough to invert or obscure meaning.
