## Evaluation

The submission is a strong, well-structured hybrid modeling answer that hits most quality criteria: it detects the R5/R6 conflict, separates formalisms appropriately, supplies coherent artifacts, and produces a layered test matrix. Under hypercritical review it is clearly not near-flawless. Several inconsistencies, under-disclosed limitations, and one incomplete dual-policy analysis keep the score well below the top band.

### Section 1 — Policy Interpretation (strong, minor nits)

Strengths: explicit assumptions; two real R5/R6 resolutions; chooses strict legal for activity-only layers; states false-positive risk on T6.

Nits:
- Escalation is described as an “optional overlay” on the high-risk route, but Section 3 implements a fourth exclusive XOR branch. That is not an overlay.
- Assumes classification irrevocably selects reject vs low vs high, with no post-escalation/post-EDD rejection. Defendable from the backbone text, but never justified against the possibility that a adverse legal opinion should enable `Reject Vendor`.

### Section 2 — Mapping Table (strong)

R1–R12 are all present; authoritative layers are usually identified; R6/R9/R11 correctly leave activity-only formalisms.

Deductions:
- R5’s DECLARE encoding is called an “overlay” while also being the mechanism that makes T6 fail; the interaction with an exception-aware mode is not mapped.
- R11’s optional DECLARE approximation (`response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)`) is mentioned but never added to `declare_model`.
- R2’s DECLARE coverage is only negative (noncoexistence). The positive obligation “accepted  exactly one approval” is left entirely to the net, which is fine only if stated more sharply as a DECLARE blind spot.

### Section 3 — Procedural Backbone (good net, inconsistent description)

The accepting Petri net is the right formalism choice. Silent AND-split after receive, clarification lock via `p_clar_ready`/`p_wait_clar`, XOR after classify, and AND-join of compliance + opinion before escalated activate are mostly correct. Excluding post-activation bank-change from the backbone is a sound choice. Code shape is self-contained and PM4Py-plausible.

Real problems:
1. **Compact description  net.**  
   `Sanctions Escalation  (EDD  Opinion)  Compliance  Activate` forces both EDD and opinion before compliance. The net only sequences compliance after EDD; opinion may finish after compliance as long as both precede activate (T7-compatible, and more permissive). The prose sentence under the formula is right; the formula is wrong. Internal incoherence.
2. **No reject after escalation/EDD.** Once the escalated (or non-escalated high-risk) branch is taken, the case is committed to activatepayclose. If legal opinion is negative, the model cannot reject. Not discussed.
3. **Clarification is only pre-classify.** R3’s “before any final outcome” is satisfied indirectly because finals are after classify, but post-classify request/response cycles are structurally impossible. Limitation is under-disclosed.
4. “Overlay” language from §1 is not what the XOR implements.

These are not fatal, but they are more than nits.

Problems:
- `nonsuccession(Activate, Opinion)` is global, not conditioned on escalation (acknowledged, still crude).
- Under exception-aware R6, this rule must be removed or weakened; §8 never says so when claiming T6 can “pass” under the alternative policy.

- Sub-1.0 confidence values are arbitrary and risk being misread as normative (ironically warned about in §9).
- R11 approximation absent from the dict.

**Material flaw on T6:**  
Matrix shows procedural/DECLARE fail under strict policy (correct). Explanation says under exception-aware the trace can pass if external auth exists. That is only true if:
- the Petri net is replaced/relaxed, and  
- DECLARE `nonsuccession(Activate, Opinion)` is removed, and
