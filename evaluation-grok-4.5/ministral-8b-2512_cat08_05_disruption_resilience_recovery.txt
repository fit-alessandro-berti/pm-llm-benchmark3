**Major factual/data-interpretation errors (severe):** The adaptation table repeatedly misreads the supplied metrics and invents “net improvements.” Baseline controlled-substance dual-control missing = 0.2 %; T2 = 2.9 %; T3 = 1.4 %. That is clear deterioration, yet the answer claims for B “Reduced \ldots from 0.2 % to 2.9 % 1.7 % (net improvement)” and for F the same mangled arithmetic plus an erroneous cold-chain claim. These are not typos; they invert the central evidence the judge is told to use. Parallel garbling appears in the cold-chain column (“4.8 % 2.3 %”). Because the entire evaluation hinges on correct reading of the before/during/after numbers, this alone collapses credibility.

**Timeline and labeling flaws:** T1 is written “T1 (T0 morning)”—internally inconsistent. The summary under-uses the concrete cycle-time, fill-rate, and DFG frequency numbers supplied; risk-posture statements stay high-level and occasionally recycle the same erroneous improvement language.

**Internal contradictions:** Patterns marked “Retire” (D, G) reappear unchanged inside the proposed recovery process model. Tier definitions overlap (Tier 4 high-risk products vs. customer tiers), and the BPMN-style flow still routes Tier-1 orders through the very snapshot and dispatch-without-scan steps the table rejected. This violates the “concrete recovery redesign” criterion.

**Weaker grounding:** DFG arc frequencies, move-on-log/move-on-model deviations, and conformance fitness scores are mentioned only in passing; abductive links between those quantities and the keep/modify/retire decisions are thin. Fairness and hidden-workload points are asserted rather than quantified from the given backlog and audit-remediation counts.

**Minor but cumulative defects:** Mangled numeric strings, occasional over-claim (“no new errors”), and a recovery model that still permits phone PoD for controlled substances without the stronger safeguards later recommended. These would be tolerable in isolation; stacked on the data errors they confirm carelessness.

**What remains acceptable:** All seven required sections exist and follow the mandated order. The resilience-vs-concealed-noncompliance distinction is conceptually sound, the indicator table covers the requested categories, recommendations are prioritized with effort/benefit/risk, and the final trade-off paragraph is decision-oriented. Structure and coverage therefore prevent a still-lower score, but the factual inversions and inconsistencies make anything above the mid-range impossible under strict grading.
