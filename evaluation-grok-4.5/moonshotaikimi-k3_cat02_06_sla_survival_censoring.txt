The submission is conceptually and structurally excellent, but it is undermined by a systematic arithmetic error in the headline survival numbers that the narrative treats as verified fact.

**What is strong**

- **Estimands before estimators.** E1–E4 are defined cleanly, mapped to decisions, and distinguished from the cause-removed KM hypothetical. Right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches are separated with the right anchor cases (S03, S09, S06, S08).
- **Encoding and bias sections.** Transfer under three assumptions, open-below-72 vs open-above-72, survivor bias, informative transfer censoring (3/3 escalated), and cohort/priority confounding are all handled with concrete IDs and the right qualitative conclusions.
- **Methods plan.** AJ as primary, KM for E3, RMST for workload, landmark/multi-state deferred for missing timestamps — justified by n=32 and only 9 at risk at 72h, not a generic laundry list.
- **Process-aware design.** Baseline vs time-varying vs leakage vs immortal time is careful; `escalated`/`rework_count` as baseline covariates are correctly rejected; prefix truncation and length-2 init patterns are the right leakage-safe moves.
- **Code.** The single Python block meets all seven requirements: embedded data, three transfer scenarios, two+ estimands, open cases retained, cohort/priority strata, stalled-case sensitivity, PM4Py `parse_event_log_string` + `get_variants` (with fallback), no fabricated timestamps. The KM/AJ implementations themselves are algorithmically correct (`n -= len(g)` on every time group, including pure censorings).
- **Format.** Exact nine headings, one fenced code block, assumptions labeled, non-causal operational language, fairness/visibility gap on P1 transfers.

**What fails under strict reading**

The hand-computed KM/AJ figures that dominate §§1, 3, 4, 6 and the “expected outputs” comment are systematically wrong. After the first censoring (S31 at 23h), the prose keeps the pre-censor risk-set size for the next event (e.g., t=26 uses n=26 instead of n=25). That error compounds through every later step.

Consequences (recomputed from the same algorithm the code implements):
- Pooled E3 is ~**38%**, not 42.9%.
- Pooled E1 is ~**69.6%**, not 71.5%; F_R(72) ~0.304, not 0.285.
- Composition-at-72, RMST (~50.4h), cohort E1/E3 (73.3/70.6, 40/47), P2 stratum, and the entire §6 sensitivity table (E2 “doubling” to 27.1%, E3 range 34.8–47.8%, etc.) inherit the bug.
- The separate claim that naive resolution-KM gives 30% vs AJ 28.5% is also wrong: with competing events censored, 1S_res(72) is ~**36%**, so the competing-risk bias is understated (true gap ~7–8pp, not 1.5pp).
- Several quantities that do not depend on intermediate censorings are correct (risk set at 72 = 9; P1 E1=75%; P3 E1=80%; resolved-only medians/means; 27 variants; 3/3 transfers escalated). That makes the failure harder to notice, not smaller.

The code would print correct numbers; the narrative says they were “hand-verified” and then reports the incorrect ones as primary results. That is an internal-consistency failure on the main quantitative claims, not a rounding or tie-breaking quibble. Qualitative conclusions (E1 relatively robust, E2/E3 assumption-sensitive, cohort gap small and confounded, transfers informative) mostly survive, which is why this is not a lower score — but a hypercritical grade cannot treat wrong headline statistics as a minor issue when the rubric explicitly values coherent calculations and internal consistency.

**Net:** near-top-tier process-mining and survival design, correct executable analysis, and a clean document structure, offset by pervasive, avoidable error in the reported numerical results. **7.0**.
