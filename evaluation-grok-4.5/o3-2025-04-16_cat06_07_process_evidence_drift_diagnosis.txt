### Summary
The answer gets the core audit story right: April is a pipeline/schema incident, June is routing/mix (process + covariate) without needing concept drift, August–September is multi-causal (supplier process deterioration, V3 risk change, Partner Z), and October is dominated by label immaturity/censoring. That triangulation is the main thing the rubric rewards. Under a strict standard, though, the write-up is not nearly flawless: it treats some conjectures as evidence, blurs concept drift with missing covariates, under-delivers falsifiers in the diagnosis table, and the monitoring code misses several explicit requirements.

### What works
- **Primary month-level diagnoses match the evidence.** Steady breach with spiked mean risk, UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY, missing queue features, and unmapped variants in April is correctly read as pipeline failure, not process change. June’s V2 jump with lower overall breach and stable remote-fix latency is correctly not forced into concept drift. October’s 55% maturity, 52 d delay, and drop in observed breach while predicted risk stays high is correctly treated as non-comparable.
- **Section 2 is coherent and non-homogeneous.** It separates intended policy change from exogenous supplier failure from population onboarding from censoring—proportionate and usable for ops.
- **Remediation staging is mostly sensible.** Freeze/recalibrate before full retrain, exclude immature Sep–Oct, repair April features before trusting them, prefer variant-aware calibration over premature Partner-Z models.
- **Risks section is concrete** and tied to failure modes the scenario actually tempts (retrain on censored long paths; calibrate on April; ignore within-V3 deterioration; “fairness” patch for Partner Z).

### Major issues (score drivers)
1. **Invented evidence presented as support.** In the concept-drift row: “Even when controlling for ‘Order Part→Part Received’ duration buckets, late-Aug cases breach more often…” No such stratified analysis is in the materials. That is a *proposed test*, not supporting evidence. In an audit answer this is a serious evidence-hygiene failure and inflates concept-drift confidence beyond what is observed.
2. **Concept vs missing-covariate drift is under-disciplined.** V3 matured breach rising with part-wait exploding from ~42 h → 130 h+ is fully compatible with **unmeasured/poorly measured lead-time covariates** (and a model never trained on that regime) without a true change in \(P(Y \mid X)\) for a complete \(X\). The table’s low–medium confidence helps, but the timeline still asserts “covariate and concept drift” and “inside-V3 breach probability higher than predicted” without showing residual risk after lead time. A stricter answer would keep “possible residual concept drift after conditioning on wait” clearly hypothetical.
3. **Required “what would falsify each explanation” is thin in §1.** Falsifiers mostly appear later as tests. The diagnosis table’s “contradicting/insufficient” column is uneven (good for some rows; weak/circular for others) and does not systematically separate observation vs hypothesis per drift type.
4. **Python sketch fails the stated checklist in material ways.**
   - Does not use the requested APIs (`pm4py.filter_time_range`, `pm4py.get_variants`, `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`).
   - Imports dead/legacy pieces (`conversion.log.factory`) and uses older filter/variant helpers.
   - **Case-start cohorts are not implemented**—only gestured at in prose—despite an explicit requirement to build both event-calendar and case-start cohorts and contrast interpretations.
   - Performance-DFG handling is hand-wavy/`dict.get` on an object whose real pm4py shape is typically a richer structure/tuple; median part-wait extraction is not clearly correct.
   - Temporal-feature extraction for audit lineage is absent.
   Normalization + dedup intent is fine; executability and requirement coverage are not.
