## Simulation-Design and Process-Mining Weaknesses

- **Warm-up (Section 5):** 4 weeks is inadequate given stated median cycle times ~45 d (and much longer tails). Discarding only ~28 d leaves substantial initial-bias in queues and resource state; the “~50 % of initial cases complete” justification is statistically weak. No Welch graphical method, MSER, or longer multiple of mean cycle time is used.
- **Service/waiting collapse:** Repeatedly acknowledged, yet the concrete extraction (time between successive *end* timestamps) is still treated as usable “waiting” input in places, and the fitting still leans heavily on the problem’s summary table rather than a disciplined start/end imputation or interval-censoring approach. The limitation is noted but not operationally solved.
- **Parallelism and joins:** The process tree’s `+` (Planning  Fire  Environmental) and the explicit requirement that Decision drafting waits for all applicable predecessors are described in scope but never given precise state-machine or barrier semantics in the model or skeleton. This is a core control-flow gap.
- **Cross-case dependencies:** Architect clustering and batching are conceptually present, but the mechanics (max wait before forced unbundling, how objection flags propagate across overlapping public-notice windows, interaction with non-stationary arrivals) remain high-level and lightly formalized. The simulation therefore still risks drifting toward loosely coupled independent cases under load.
- **Parameter estimation:** Heavy reliance on the supplied empirical summary table (median/p90  lognormal) is pragmatic but under-uses the log itself for activity-duration regression on case_type/district/architect. Rework detection by simple activity re-occurrence is crude (no distinction of true rework vs. multi-instance). Arrival clustering intensity is estimated but the generative model (Poisson cluster vs. mixture) is left vague.
- **PM4Py grounding:** Plan mentions correct high-level intents (format_dataframe, DFG, variants) but then invents or approximates APIs and never closes the loop in code. Performance DFG is required yet missing.

## Experiment Planning, Uncertainty, and Realism Flaws

- **Effect sizes in the experiment matrix are unrealistically precise and optimistic.** D1 (pre-submission checker reducing missing-doc loops 40 % + 1 FTE) is claimed to drop median cycle time to 8 d; D10 to 25 d / p90 50 d. These numbers are presented without supporting sensitivity or partial-effect calculations and contradict the dominance of planning/inspection/legal queues and public-notice minima. They read as marketing rather than grounded simulation expectations.
- **Stress scenarios** are present (>3) and useful, yet the factorial claim (“16 configurations”) and the 10-replicate plan are under-powered for the heavy tails repeatedly acknowledged (p90, 260 d complex cases). Normal-based 95 % CIs are the default; bootstrap/percentile methods are only mentioned in passing.
- **Assumptions** are well-categorized, but several operational choices are arbitrary without sensitivity hooks (council meetings = first week of every month, 15 % objection rate, exact travel uniforms, rework caps = 3, bundling delay = +1 d, environmental capacity exactly 60 %). The text correctly flags missing data yet still injects point values that later appear in expected outcomes.
- **Decision criteria** contain reasonable metrics and a CI-overlap heuristic, but the numeric SLA thresholds and “high/medium/low confidence” bands are asserted without linkage to validation acceptance criteria or loss functions. Fairness metrics (district max–min, architect correlation) are good but lack operational definitions of how they would be estimated under batching policies that intentionally couple cases.
- **Validation strategy** lists the right categories (face, replay, distributional, utilization, bottleneck, out-of-sample) and gives numeric gates, yet the gates themselves (MAE < 10 %, coverage > 80 %, utilization ±10 pp) are not justified against the known data gaps (missing starts, missing objections, flattened log). Replay on 500 cases is proposed without addressing that the log lacks start timestamps and true objection events, so “observed” service times are themselves inferred.

## What Prevents a Higher Score

A 8–10 would require:
- a skeleton that actually exercises `pm4py.format_dataframe`, real variant/DFG/performance-DFG calls, and a documented parse attempt on the tree string;
- precise synchronization semantics for parallel reviews and decision barriers;
