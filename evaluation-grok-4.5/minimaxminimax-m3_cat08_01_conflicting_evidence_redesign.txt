### Deductions (hypercritical)

1. **Real quantitative reasoning error (material):** “14% × 22% = ~150 cases into the wrong fast path” multiplies segment share by *complaint rate* and treats the product as misrouting volume. Those are different quantities. This is not a harmless slip; it is process-mining arithmetic used as if it were diagnostic evidence.

2. **Overclaim on mechanism scope:** Redesign A is said to remove “the 410 EUR wrong-replacement exposure on the ~324 cases.” The 324 figure is *all* fast-path complaint/reopens (14.1% × ~2,300), not high-value wrong-replacement incidents. Conflating them inflates A’s quality case and blurs the DFG vs. rework-stat distinction the prompt requires the answer to master.

3. **Redesign B is not fully standalone:** it routes completed evidence into “the segment-aware auto-screen of Redesign A’s disposition logic.” Material difference of *mechanism* remains, but B is written as dependent on A rather than as an independent alternative the comparison table pretends to evaluate.

4. **Unsupported operational inventions stated as design fact:** “model-assisted scoring,” “5-minute human confirm,” and “60% of cases scoring below threshold” in A, and repurposing Quality-check inspectors for *risk-signal* work in B, are not grounded in the prompt. QCpart verification (C) is defensible; QCfraud/risk triage (B) mixes skill sets the resource table keeps separate (EUR 52/h specialists vs. EUR 38/h inspectors). These should have been labeled assumptions.

5. **Language overreach on conformance:** A will “eliminat[e]” the move-on-log deviation “Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices.” At best it reduces it; residual leakage, model error, and capacity overflow are acknowledged elsewhere but contradicted by “eliminating.”

6. **Minor presentation defects:** “Fast  efficient” is a broken character/typo in the diagnosis synthesis; a few cumulative case-hour figures (e.g., “~11,200 h”) are presented with more authority than the underlying “30% of 34 h on ~1,100 cases” sketch deserves.

7. **Table note soft-merges the three redesigns into a composite (“A+C for high-value, B for unclear”).** Intellectually reasonable, but it slightly undercuts the brief to compare three alternative redesigns and makes the “dominated?” column harder to interpret cleanly.
