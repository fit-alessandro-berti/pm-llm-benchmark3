## Evaluation

### Overall
The answer has the right shape: both sides are argued, cases are classified with mostly defensible labels, burden of proof is stated, and a layered architecture is sketched. Under strict review it falls well short of a high score because Section 7’s revised POWL is semantically invalid and fails the task’s minimum behavioral requirements, and several authority/evidence claims are sloppy or invented.

---

### Section 1 — Stipulated / disputed / burden
**Strengths:** Clear split between stipulated facts and interpretive disputes; burden rules (preponderance + applicable written rule; exception needs authorized overlay + satisfaction of its conditions; indeterminate when scope/data fail) are appropriate and operational.

**Defects:**
- Treating “SUP-703/708 violated written rules” as stipulated is slightly over-strong for a facts section (conclusionary), though low-cost.
- Disputed list omits important items the materials actually put in play: label equivalence timing (mapping after the fact), whether same-person approval via aliases is proved identity, and whether COI without disclosure is control-flow noncompliance vs HR policy.
- Does not explicitly assign who bears the risk of missing eligibility attributes (the 9 aggregate cases)—material for pilot claims.

### Section 2 — Prosecution

**Defects (material):**

### Section 7 — PM4Py model (largest failure)
This section is where a strict grade collapses.

1. **Invalid POWL:** `StrictPartialOrder` with edges  
   `loop_entry → request_info → loop_back → loop_entry`  
   is a **cycle**. Partial orders are acyclic; this is not a well-formed POWL. The text admits “no native LOOP” while PM4Py exposes `Operator.LOOP`—the defensible approximation was not used.

2. **Fails required behaviors:**
   - Main path forces `checks_parallel = sanctions AND quality` **before** any approval/activation choice. That **blocks** PD-3 and FD-7 paths where quality (and under FD-7, manual approval) follow provisional activation.
   - `provisional_path` is only `Provisional_Activate` with no sanctioned pre-order (sanctions before activate) and no post-order (quality, manual approval, monitoring) in the model.
   - Auto path still sits behind full pre-activation quality—so it does **not** represent the pilot route the brief defends for 702.

3. **API / style issues:** `SilentTransition(label=...)` is nonstandard (silent transitions are unlabeled); `Operator.AND` may work as a process-tree parallel but diverges from the given M0 idiom (`StrictPartialOrder` without edges) without justification; approval XOR does not structure standard vs pilot vs emergency as data-guarded overlays so much as unconstrained free choice (eligibility not approximable in POWL—should be stated as XOR + external guards, with emergency *reordering* explicit).

4. **Policy table + data proposal:** Overlay table is directionally right but sparse (no spend, SoD, monitoring, rejection scope). Event fields are good (stable `notification_id`, `rule_set_id`, eligibility snapshot, immutable timestamps). Not enough to offset the broken control-flow artifact.

5. **Limitations paragraph** partly confesses the damage but then leaves the unsafe approximation in place instead of implementing `Operator.LOOP` and a real provisional branch (e.g., sanctions → provisional activate → quality ∥ retrospective manual approval → notify → close) XOR standard/pilot branches.
