## Justification

### Strengths
The answer is structurally complete, hits all ten required headings, and makes genuine hybrid-modeling choices rather than dumping everything into one formalism. The R5/R6 conflict is identified, given multiple resolutions, and handled without silent merging—Resolution B with quarantine + 24h + out-of-band evidence is coherent and the compliance risk is stated clearly. ### Deductions (hypercritical)

**Log skeleton semantics & design (0.6).**  
The whitelist / `trace  model` reading of PM4Py log-skeleton conformance is defensible for some implementations, and the answer stakes that claim explicitly. Still, building the entire `always_before`/`equivalence` design on that reading is fragile: under the more common “model constraints must hold on the trace” reading, simultaneous `(OLO, AV)` and `(AV, OLO)` is unsatisfiable. Even under the stated semantics, `equivalence` excludes only two pairs and otherwise admits almost everything (including masses of 0=0 pairs), so it is nearly non-normative noise with one clever T8 catch. That is a weak use of the formalism sold as a full layer.

**§7 code quality (0.4).**  
`g.attrs.get("joint_authorization_ref")` on a pandas `groupby` group is not reliable as written. The R3 backstop condition (`all(tx2 >= rc for ...)`) is opaque and easy to get wrong for “RC before first outcome.” These are presented as “executable-style” checks; they need cleanup before they would run correctly. Minor, but the task asked for executable-style precision.

**Procedural over-permissiveness (0.3).**  
`Sanctions Escalation` sits before the outcome XOR, so SEMA and SERV are admitted. That is never justified by the scenario or tests and weakens the claim that the tree is the structural backbone. OLO without prior SE is also freely admitted via multiple slots (only DECLARE/activ_freq constrain it). Acceptable as a permissive hybrid, but under-discussed relative to the care given elsewhere.

**Interpretive hardness (0.2).**  
`succession(AV, RFP)` as a hard obligation (A5) is a choice, not forced by the text; it piles secondary DECLARE failures onto T8 and would reject “activate then never pay” cases. Disclosed, but still a sharp edge. Same for CBD/SV at-most-once and OLO at-most-once assumptions baked into `activ_freq`.

**Minor residual issues (0.1).**  
A few adjacency/`always_before` margin cases are hard to audit exhaustively; temporal lower-tail handling is noted but not modeled; the answer is very long with some repeated argumentation.

### What kept it from the 9+ band
A nearly flawless answer would have (1) a log-skeleton layer whose constraints remain meaningful under both plausible PM4Py semantics, (2) cleanly executable §7 code, and (3) tighter SE/OLO placement aligned with the backbone story. None of the failures are conceptual collapses—the hybrid architecture, conflict handling, and test analysis are clearly above average—but under the instructed strictness those defects are enough to pull a “near-excellent” piece down from the top tier.

### Summary
Strong, coherent, and largely correct hybrid design with excellent conflict and test-case analysis; deducted for a brittle/noisy log-skeleton construction, non-runnable spots in the resource/data layer, and a few over-permissive or hard interpretive choices in the procedural/DECLARE core.
