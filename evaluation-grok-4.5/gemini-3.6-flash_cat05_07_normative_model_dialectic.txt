**7.0**

The submission is thorough, well-structured, and largely faithful to the required headings and dialectical mandate, but it contains multiple material defects in model semantics, completeness, construction validity, and strict adherence to the prosecution/defense acknowledgment rules. These prevent a higher score under hypercritical review.

**Section 1** is clear and correctly separates stipulated facts from disputed propositions (authority of PD-3/FD-7, scope of M0 for rejections/reactivations, post-expiry practice, logging vs. process failure). The burden-of-proof framing is usable. Minor deduction for not more sharply flagging that CS-9’s resource rule and the ambiguous scope language are already outside M0’s expressiveness.

**Section 2 (Prosecution)** is forceful on formal hierarchy, expiry, SUP-703/SUP-708/SUP-712/SUP-715, normalization (SUP-705/SUP-714), and the non-retroactivity of “desirable” outcomes. It properly cites aggregates. However, it fails the explicit instruction that “the prosecution must acknowledge genuine model/data defects.” The brief treats missing loops, CRM missingness, label changes, and scope gaps almost entirely as excuses to be rejected rather than conceding that M0 is incomplete/obsolete on those points while still arguing residual noncompliance. This tilts the dialectic.

**Section 3 (Defense)** is stronger on the required acknowledgment of risk (explicitly flags SUP-703 and distinguishes good-faith post-expiry from high-risk abuse). It correctly attacks concurrency gaps, missing loops, pilot/emergency overlays, CRM migration, reactivation, rejected-lead scope, and label equivalence. The distinction between trace deviation and misconduct is well-drawn. Slight overclaim that PD-3/FD-7 are fully “valid executive overlays” without grappling with the Compliance observer’s later disclaimer and lack of CS-9 incorporation.

**Section 4 (Evidence Matrix)** is complete (all 15 cases), uses the demanded columns, and generally maps interpretations to overlays and risk. Traceability is good. Minor issues: a few “what would reverse” entries are vague or circular; material-risk ratings are inconsistent in calibration (e.g., SUP-715 medium while SUP-712 high is defensible but not always argued); SUP-713 and SUP-706 handling is reasonable but could more cleanly separate label mapping from control satisfaction.

**Section 5 (Final Judgment)** supplies a primary label for every case, severity/confidence on the noncompliant ones, a coherent system-level verdict (operations moderately impaired, M0 obsolete), and explicit rejections of each side’s overreach. Classifications are mostly defensible (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION for 702/704, NONCOMPLIANT for 703/705/708/712/714/715, MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT for 707/709/710, etc.).  
Deductions:  
- SUP-713 as pure COMPLIANT underplays that M0 itself cannot accept the label (better as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT or MIXED with explanation).  
- SUP-706 as DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE is fair, yet the mutable-link problem is under-weighted relative to the 29 % missingness aggregate.  
- “MIXED” is avoided, which is good, but a couple of edge cases (705, 714) receive cleaner NONCOMPLIANT labels than the surrounding evidence of ongoing factual emergency / normalization pressure strictly warrants without heavier hedging.  
- Severity is supplied only for some cases; the instruction asked for severity and confidence more uniformly.

**Section 6 (Revised Architecture)** is one of the stronger parts: the five-layer split is sensible, correctly assigns control-flow to POWL, data-aware/time/amount rules to declarative or pandas checks, resource/COI to organizational controls, and overlays/scope to governance tables. Explicit scope rules for rejected leads and reactivations are present. No major logical flaws.

**Section 7 (PM4Py + data proposal)** is where the largest technical failures occur.  
- **Invalid construction**: the single `sanctions` Transition object (and conceptually similar reuse patterns) is placed inside multiple alternative children of the top-level XOR. POWL/StrictPartialOrder node identity must be unique in the object graph; sharing across exclusive branches produces a malformed model whose partial-order semantics are undefined or collapse. Separate Transition instances (same label) are required.  
- **Missing reject-after-checks path**: original M0 and CS-9 allow (and real processes need) full concurrent checks followed by XOR(approve, reject). The proposed M1 forces an early XOR; once the standard/pilot/emergency paths are chosen there is no later rejection. This is a semantic regression.  
- **Request_More_Info approximation is incorrect for the given evidence**: `LOOP([quality, request_info])` forces Quality_Review before any Request_More_Info. SUP-707 (and the aggregate of 47 cases) shows requests *before* Quality_Review. The approximation therefore cannot replay the very desirable behavior it claims to accommodate. A more defensible encoding (loop of optional request before/around a quality activity, or a silent-exit loop higher up) was available and should have been used or the limitation stated more candidly.  
- Unused `skip_info`, multiple Quality_Review instances created ad-hoc, and the pilot/emergency paths correctly defer quality but inherit the shared-node and early-XOR problems.  
- The accompanying policy-overlay table and event-schema proposal are excellent (stable notification_id, rule/version IDs, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution, immutable dual timestamps, COI_Disclosure_ID). These redeem part of the section, but cannot offset a non-executable or incomplete POWL.

**Overall dialectical fairness and traceability** are high; the judgment does not merely split the difference and correctly identifies supported vs. unproved claims. However, the combination of (a) insufficient prosecution concession on model defects, (b) broken POWL object identity, (c) incomplete control-flow coverage (no post-check reject), and (d) a loop that fails the concrete SUP-707 pattern constitutes more than “minor” error under the grading rubric. These are material to “model semantics” and “quality of the proposed model/data revision.”

Hence 7.0. A near-flawless submission would have needed a correctly constructed, non-sharing POWL that preserves reject-after-checks, a loop (or explicit approximation note) that actually fits the observed Request_More_Info ordering, and a prosecution brief that openly concedes M0’s gaps while still carrying its burden.
