## Section-by-section

### 1. Target definition — good, with a structural tension
Strengths: Primary target is explicitly default *among activated* accounts framed with right censoring; rejects are not relabeled; alternative multi-state target is real and distinguished; estimand language is mostly careful.

Issues:
- Prediction time is an early prefix (3rd event / 7 days), but the population/estimand is defined by *eventual* activation, which is typically still in the future at that prefix. That is a legitimate optional formulation per the prompt, yet the write-up underplays the operational mismatch with “every credit application” and the fact that deployment-time scoring would be applied to cases whose activation status is unknown. Section 8 later patches this; Section 1 should have stated the two-time-scale problem more sharply (prefix time vs activation time vs 90d clock).
- “Time to 90-day default” is slightly muddled wording for a 90-day horizon binary-with-censoring problem (acceptable if treated as survival with admin censor at 90d, but not crisp).

### 2. Cohort construction — mostly complete; arithmetic/rule inconsistencies
Strengths: Full 48,000 is partitioned; rejects/withdrawn/open/transferred/immature are not collapsed into non-default; primary cohort = activated (including immature as censored) is coherent with the chosen target; 60/40 transfer split is applied.

Issues:
- Maturity cutoff is wrong: 90d + 35d latency before 15 Feb 2026 is ~13 Oct 2025, not “after 15 November 2025.”
- Inclusion text says cases must allow full maturity, yet the primary cohort deliberately keeps 3,600 immature activated cases. That contradiction should have been resolved (either drop the strict inclusion rule or restate it as “for complete-case metrics only”).
- Exclusion says “transfers without linkage,” but primary analysis drops *all* transfers; fine as a choice, but the rules text and table dispositions are not fully aligned.
- “Linked, outcome available” is assumed rather than justified (partner feed missingness is a stated risk).

### 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy — strong
Strengths: Separates admin censoring, delayed labels, activation selection, transfer, permanent missingness; primary survival-on-activated strategy matches the target; IPCW / include-linked-transfers / bounds sensitivity are appropriate; testable vs untestable assumptions are called out.

Minor: Partner-originated permanently missing feeds are thinner than the prompt’s emphasis; “non-informative censoring conditional on activation date” is asserted with only light test plan.

### 6. Leakage audit — excellent

### 7. Validation design — material flaw
Strengths: Argues correctly against random case split; policy-change and migration awareness; survival-appropriate metrics (C-index, Brier, calibration, subgroups, PSI/KL); expanding-window backtest idea; label maturity in evaluation discussed.

**Major error:**  
- Train = 1 Jan 2024 – 31 Mar 2025  
- Test set 1 = 1 Jul 2024 – 30 Sep 2024  

Test1 lies *inside* the training start-date window. That is not a valid held-out temporal test. In the code, `assign_split_group` checks `<= TRAIN_END` first, so Test1 is unreachable (empty). This alone prevents a high score: the validation scheme as specified is broken.

Secondary: Test2 is entirely post-policy (good for shift) but also straddles migration without a clean pre/post-migration split; Train ends before policy change so within-regime post-train holdout is weak once Test1 is invalid.

### 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary — excellent
Clear “can / cannot claim,” selection vs counterfactual vs association, three credible deployment feedback loops, and a sensible use boundary (early-warning triage  automated underwriting). Matches the quality bar.

### 9. PM4Py skeleton — ambitious, not reliable
Strengths: Right libraries and flow (read/parse  `format_dataframe`  case summary  prefix cutoff  cohort counts  temporal split intent  prefix filter  leakage checks  `get_prefixes_from_log` / DFG / variants / temporal features on train-only); comments flag survival/IPCW connection without inventing a PM4Py survival API; length and concreteness are in range.

Issues (implementation correctness):
- `activation_time` assignment via `np.where(..., groupby().min(), ...)` is index-misaligned and will not work as written.
- Split logic makes `test1` dead, as above.
- Cohort flags via independent `in x.values` can mis-handle odd traces; maturity/`label_available_at` are under-used relative to Section 3.
- Prefix path double-filters (manual cutoff then `get_prefixes_from_log` on already-truncated log) without reconciling short traces cleanly.
- Several PM4Py call shapes are optimistic/hand-wavy (`discover_dfg` unpacking, `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` args, etc.).
- No actual construction of `(duration, event)` from activation, nor a clean feature matrix join—acceptable for a skeleton, but the buggy case-level columns undermine the “concrete enough to implement” bar.
