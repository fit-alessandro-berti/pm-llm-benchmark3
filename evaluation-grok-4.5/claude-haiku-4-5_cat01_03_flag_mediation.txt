## Verdict
The submission is ambitious, well-structured, and often methodologically fluent (interference, exposure-induced confounding, policy-bundle interventions, pilot design). Under strict grading it fails on core required empirics: the stage decomposition is materially wrong, and those errors propagate into policy contrasts and the central “3-hour paradox” narrative. Strong prose does not offset broken arithmetic on the only real data provided.

## Major failures (score-defining)

### 1. Section 3 case counts and cycle times are wrong
This is the decisive defect.

Unflagged cycle times are not “16.6 h.” Many unflagged paths are triageclose in ~3–6 h (R04,R07,R11,R14,R16,R19). Reporting 16.56 h invents near-parity and shrinks the gap from roughly **+10 h (median)** to “+3 h.” That is not a rounding issue; it misstates the phenomenon the brief asks to explain.

Downstream tables (flagged±escalation, load splits, complexity) inherit the wrong denominator and are not trustworthy. The narrative claim “3 hours longer total cycle time (19.6 vs 16.6)” is false on both levels and gap size.

### 2. Required decomposition quality collapses
Section 3 is explicitly scored on timestamp use. Medians/means may be approximate, but **support counts and structural absence handling must be right**. Here:
- Wrong \(n\) in every primary row of the main flag table  
- Escalation split totals 9, not 10  
- “9/9 flagged reviewed” is fine in spirit (all flagged are reviewed) but paired with a false unflagged fraction  
- Complexity and load strata do not match the CSV  

Once Section 3 fails, Sections 4 and 6 become partly decorative: pathway “evidence” and P0 cycle-time cells cite the bad numbers.

## Strict score rationale
Near-flawless was never in reach after Section 3. On this rubric, correct timestamp decomposition and honest support counts are load-bearing. A polished causal essay on incorrect medians, wrong \(n\), and a fabricated unflagged cycle time of ~16.6 h cannot clear the high bar.  

**5.0** = strong conceptual scaffolding and partial format success, offset by critical empirical falsehoods in the required stage table, inconsistent estimand definitions, and propagation of those errors into pathway evidence and policy comparison. With corrected counts/times and tightened estimands, this style of answer could approach the high 7s–8s; as written, it does not.
