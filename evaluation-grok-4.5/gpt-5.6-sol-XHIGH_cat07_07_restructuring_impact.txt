### Deductions (hypercritical)

1. **Code/API looseness (Section 7)**  
   Handover/working-together/subcontracting conveniences do not return plain `dict` edge maps; treating them as `.items()` weight dicts is wrong for real PM4Py/SNA objects (typically graph-like structures requiring edge iteration with data). Even as “pseudocode,” this is a material inaccuracy in the section whose job is PM4Py fidelity. Several helpers are idealized.

2. **Wait-pressure index over-weighted (Section 1)**  
   \(\sum f_i \times m_i\) on medians is a rough exposure heuristic, not a queueing or cycle-time forecast. The answer notes non-additivity once, then leads the mechanism map with “~23%” as if it were a primary finding. That is methodologically softer than the tone implies.

3. **Approval framed as “outbound/return”**  
   Technical loops are true round-trips (caseworkerspecialistcaseworker). Supervisor/approvalimplementation is a forward edge, not a return. Bundling them as “outbound/return handovers rise 8192” slightly muddies the mechanism story.

4. **Listed-total handover comparison**  
   Summing the management table’s rows is directionally fair for “interfaces increased,” but the table is incomplete (no ImplementationQC, QCClose, etc.) and mixes stable automated edges with the edges that actually change. The caveat “if comparable and non-overlapping” is present but thin; a stricter answer would normalize only the contested edge classes.

5. **False precision / density elsewhere**  
   Scenario forecasts stay qualitative on cycle-time shape (appropriate) while Section 1 projects numeric confidence. Minor redundancy across §§2–4. A few table cells are prose-dense to the point of reduced scanability for a “map.”

6. **Minor completeness nits**  
   Mechanism map signatures are strong but only loosely tied row-by-row to named PM4Py calls (that link is deferred entirely to §7). Guardrail thresholds are illustrative—fine—but a stricter pilot section would state how baseline variance sets the 20%/2pp bands.
