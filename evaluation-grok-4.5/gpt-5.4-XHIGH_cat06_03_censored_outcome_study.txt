## Overall judgment
The submission is a strong, process-aware study design that correctly refuses the main failure modes (treating rejects/withdrawals/open cases as non-default, training only on the 21,600 export without qualification, random splits across policy/migration, casual use of post-outcome fields). Sections 1–3 and 8 are especially solid on estimands and selective observation. It is **not** near-flawless under a strict reading: the implementation skeleton re-introduces leakage/meta columns into the model matrix, the validation plan confounds policy shift with discrimination performance, and several operational details are looser than the prose claims.

## Section-by-section critique

### 1. Target definition — strong, not perfect
**Strengths:** Primary target is honestly restricted to eventual activation under the observed policy; rejects/withdrawals are not fake non-defaults; alternative competing-risk target is well contrasted; counterfactual “if all were activated” is correctly demoted.

**Issues:**
- The estimand explicitly conditions on **eventual activation**, which is not known at the landmark. That is defensible as a booked-account association estimand, but the wording still blurs “unit of analysis = early prefix” with “population = those who later activate.” Deployment on “every application” is then out-of-support; limitations discuss this, but Section 1 should state more sharply that the score is **not** identified as early-warning risk for the full funnel without a second model or selection correction.
- Landmark “first 3 events  Started  KYC  Credit Review” is an assumption, not a guarantee once rework/renames exist. Sensitivity \(k\) helps but does not fix the primary definition.

### 2. Cohort construction — strong
**Strengths:** Full 48,000 accounting table; maturity cutoff \(+90+35\); split of the 3,600 into not-yet-mature vs overdue/missing; as-of feature freeze; transferred/open not relabeled.

**Issues:**
- “Must eventually activate” is a **retrospective** inclusion rule. Fine for the chosen estimand, but the cohort-flow table should more cleanly separate (a) landmark-eligible prefixes at time \(L\) from (b) later activation/label filters used only for supervised fitting. Mixing them in one “primary-target inclusion” list invites leakage of future state into cohort logic if implemented carelessly (and the code partly does).
- Sub-counts for mature-missing vs not-yet-mature are described but not operationalized as first-class rows in the table.

### 3. Censoring / missing labels — very strong
**Strengths:** Clean separation of right censoring, delay, activation selection, transfer, permanent feed loss; IPW + bounds + linked-transfer sensitivity; testable vs untestable assumptions are mostly right.

**Issues (minor):**
- IPW is proposed on pre-prefix covariates + source_system, but activation and partner missingness may depend on post-prefix underwriting path; the positivity/overlap story is thin.
- Transfer as “primary target undefined” is consistent, but the 60% linkage sensitivity needs a clearer bias statement (linked  random subsample).

### 4. Prefix extraction — strong
**Strengths:** No padding with future events; exclude terminals inside the prefix; time-based landmarks; canonicalization + deterministic dedupe; keep document loops as signal.

**Issues:**
- Primary \(k=3\) can land mid-rework or on renamed/duplicate residue even after mapping; “pre-decision” is enforced by exclusion, which induces selection on short/fast-resolving paths.
- Elapsed-time rules are correct in prose; the code barely implements them (see §9).

### 5. Process-aware features — strong
**Strengths:** Static vs prefix vs historical reference; trailing rates gated on label observability before prefix time; risk_score last/first/delta without backward fill; explicit ban list.

**Issues:**
- Resource “experience at event time” is correctly flagged, but reconstruction method is hand-wavy (prior case counts only) relative to the known extraction-time contamination.
- Queue pressure / trailing default rates are high-risk leakage features; the availability rule is stated, not audited with a concrete as-of join key.

### 6. Leakage audit — strong on paper
Table covers required fields with sensible drop/repair decisions and the right rationale for `collection_status`, `final_limit`, nightly `documents_total`, extraction-time experience, etc.

**Gap:** Audit is not mirrored by a **whitelist** in code. A blacklist that omits `activation_time`, `cohort_state`, maturity flags, and `y_primary` is an incomplete control for the exact risks this section exists to catch.

### 7. Validation design — good intent, material design flaw
**Strengths:** Temporal split by case start; rejects random split for policy/migration/maturity reasons; maturity-aware evaluation denominator; PR-AUC/Brier/calibration/subgroups; post-migration as structural audit not fake default performance.

**Issues:**
- **Train = entirely pre-2025-07-01, test = entirely post-2025-07-01** makes the primary test a pure transportability stress test. Any drop confounds model failure with policy regime change. A stricter design needs at least one **within-regime** locked test (or expanding window with post-policy training freeze) plus a separate regime-shift probe. Validation is also entirely pre-policy, so hyperparameter choices never see the test regime.
- Post-migration window correctly blocked for mature `default_90d`, but Jul–Sep 2025 activations still have differential feed delay and residual immaturity at the edges; reporting only “observed label” performance without always publishing the excluded mature-missing rate by split is under-specified.
- Competing-risk metrics are named at a high level only.

### 8. Limitations — strong
Clear non-claims on counterfactual default; selection vs association; several deployment feedback loops (accept policy, queues, collections, partner routing, logging). Adequate decision-use boundary.

**Minor:** Could harder-separate “monitoring activated book” from any automated early decline use.

### 9. PM4Py skeleton — weakest part relative to the assignment bar
**Strengths:** Reads CSV; timestamps; `format_dataframe`; activity map + dedupe; case start/activation/terminal/maturity; temporal split (not random); `get_prefixes_from_log` + DFG/variants as audit; prefix row filter by event_index and terminal contamination; cohort_state counts; comments for IPW/competing risks; no fabricated survival API.

**Concrete defects (strict):**
1. **Feature matrix leakage / contamination (serious):**  
   `feature_cols = [c for c in model_df.columns if c not in banned_feature_cols]` leaves in `activation_time`, `cohort_state`, `label_observed_by_cutoff`, `activation_mature_by_cutoff`, `overdue_missing_label`, `split`, `y_primary`, etc.  
   - `activation_time` / activation flags encode the selection event the estimand conditions on.  
   - `cohort_state` / `y_primary` are outcome pathways.  
   Blacklist omits them. For a leakage-centric brief, this is a real implementation failure, not a nit.
2. **Whitelist never defined** — contradicts the rigor of §6 (“audit before every split”).
3. **`y_primary` duplication risk** if already inside `feature_cols` then concatenated again.
4. **Time-based prefix path is dead code** (`time_prefix` built, never turned into features or cohorts) despite §4 promising parallel elapsed-time design.
5. **Prefix audit log  modeling prefixes:** DFG/variants run on `get_prefixes_from_log`, while modeling uses a separate `event_index <= k` path—acceptable, but no check that the two define the same case set.
6. **No as-of enforcement** for historical reference features promised in §5 (trailing rates, reviewer prior volume).
7. **`documents_total`**: warn-only, no repair pattern.
8. **Transfer linkage table** absent despite being central in §3.
9. Length/quality: illustrative, but the final “model_df  train/val/test” step is exactly where leakage discipline must be tightest—and is not.

## Cross-cutting statistical concerns
- Primary analysis on activated+labeled cases is honest, yet the business question is full-funnel early warning; the piece handles this rhetorically better than it does as a closed decision system (pathway model remains underdeveloped relative to the primary).
- Permanent missing partner feeds: audit stratification is described, not designed as a first-class reported operating characteristic beside AUC.
- Policy change is both a split boundary and a causal shift in selection/outcome link; treating one post-policy block as *the* test overclaims “generalization” unless framed only as shift stress.

## What kept the score from collapsing
No catastrophic conceptual sins: no mass relabeling of rejects as non-default; full-cohort accounting; maturity lag; activation as selection not censoring; temporal validation philosophy; solid leakage taxonomy in prose; usable PM4Py-oriented skeleton structure.

## What prevented a high score under your rubric
Near-flawless required: (i) estimand/population/landmark fully aligned without future-conditioning ambiguity, (ii) validation that separates regime-shift probes from within-regime performance, (iii) code whose feature table **cannot** ingest activation/label/meta columns, (iv) time-based prefixes and as-of historical features actually implemented, (v) tighter coupling between leakage audit and feature whitelist. Those gaps are material in this assignment’s own quality criteria.
