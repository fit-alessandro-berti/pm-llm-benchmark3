**Major defects**

1. **DECLARE `precedence` is systematically inverted relative to both standard DECLARE and the author’s own prose.**  
   Standard DECLARE: `precedence(A,B)` means “if B occurs, A must already have occurred.”  
   The answer repeatedly documents the opposite reading (“if A occurs, it must precede B”) and encodes:
   - `precedence(Receive Clarification, Activate Vendor)`
   - `precedence(Receive Clarification, Reject Vendor)`
   - `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`  
   Under real DECLARE semantics these force a clarification response before every activation/rejection and a legal opinion before every activation. T1, T2, T3, T4, and T7 would **fail** DECLARE; the matrix marks them `pass`. That is a direct self-contradiction on a competency the prompt explicitly tests (“Distinguish response from precedence”).

2. **Correct templates were available and unused.**  
   The intended “if A occurs it is before B” pattern is log-skeleton `always_before` or DECLARE `nonsuccession(B,A)`, not `precedence(A,B)`. R5 is expressible with `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` + `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)`. The chosen encoding is not a minor notation slip; it is the wrong constraint family.

3. **Section 9 restates the same error.**  
   It claims `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is “vacuous when Obtain Legal Opinion does not occur.” That is response-style vacuity, not precedence-style vacuity. The discussion of vacuity is therefore unreliable.

4. **Test matrix is not an evaluation of the published model.**  
   Procedural / temporal / resource cells are largely defensible. DECLARE (and any overall verdict that depends on it) is not. T6 dual-resolution handling is conceptually fine; it does not offset the DECLARE/matrix break.
