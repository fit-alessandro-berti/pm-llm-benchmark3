## Grading rationale

The submission hits the required nine-section skeleton, defines labeled assumptions and two real objectives (tail/SLA and fairness/robustness), and does not pick a winner from base-case mean delay alone. Policy P3 is specified in operational terms, bottleneck migration under U1–U5 is discussed, and the validation plan correctly protects P1. Those are real strengths. Under strict grading they are not enough for a high score.

### Major deductions

**1. Offered-load / capacity analysis double-counts multi-skilled resources (serious)**  
Section 2 adds Ada+Ben+Deepa as if they were full-time Mechanical supply (1344 std-min) and separately adds Chen+Deepa+Emil+Ada as Electrical supply. The same person-minutes are treated as available to every skill they hold. Planned utilizations such as “Mechanical ~35%” and “Electrical ~47%” are therefore not meaningful joint loads. A correct bound would allocate each resource’s shift across skills (or use a simultaneous load vector). This is a core process-mining / OR mistake and undercuts the bottleneck story that the rest of the paper relies on.

**2. Python scaffold does not run and omits required mechanisms**  
- `self.rng.poisson(ea)` — `random.Random` has no `poisson`. The entry point crashes before any metric is produced.  
- Preemption is described in detail in prose and in A6, then explicitly left unexercised (`preempted_flag` always false). The task required handover, setup, parallel/sequential, **and preemption** to be implemented explicitly.  
- P3’s reserved slots, overflow triggers A/B/C, and sticky assignment are mostly narrative; the code path is essentially “sort by multiplier + light fairness penalty.”  
- `hash(policy)` / `hash(scenario)` for seeds is unstable across processes.  
“Scaffold is acceptable if omitted mechanisms are identified” softens this, but a hard crash plus a no-op on a central mechanism is still a large gap versus requirement 8.

**3. Internal consistency: text vs. code vs. table**  
The scenario table is presented as if informed by the simulator, yet the simulator cannot execute as written and does not implement the policies at the fidelity claimed in §3. Qualitative bands are therefore closer to unaudited expert judgment than to “seed-averaged estimates.” That weakens §4–§5.

### Moderate deductions

**4. Garbled objectives and constraints**  
Encoding damage turns O1/O2 into unreadable fragments (`completion_time SLA_p`, missing inequalities on utilization, broken fairness formulas). An auditor cannot implement O1/O2 from the text alone.

**5. Weak DES semantics**  
Greedy case-by-case forward assignment with `resource_busy_until` ignores true multi-case contention, break interruptions mid-service, overtime policy, and “at most one preemption.” Relative policy rankings may flip under a proper event queue—especially for P2 vs P3 on handover and preemption storms.

**6. U4 and service-time handling in code**  
Long-tail doubling is implemented with an ad-hoc extra draw, not by doubling `p_complex` on the mixture in A4. Planning draws a fresh random service time instead of E[service], biasing dispatch.

**7. Minor content nits**  
- “Appendix reasoning” referenced but absent.  
- Validation burst risk is asserted without a time-block offered-load calculation comparable to diagnostics.  
- Farah’s 4h shift and break structure are only partially stressed in the numerical bottleneck table.  
- PM4Py block is try/except-guarded and likely fragile on return types (`get_service_time`, handover network print).

### What still works (credit)

- Clear A1–A8; parallelism as a decision variable; two distinct objectives with robustness defined across U1–U5.  
- Daily case-type arithmetic and base mean service times are largely correct and auditable.

Competent structure and policy reasoning, but a foundational capacity error, non-executable code, and unimplemented preemption/reservation logic block anything near the top of the scale. With strict application of “minor issues lower the score substantially” and “very high only if nearly flawless,” the appropriate grade is the middle of the adequate band: **6.0**.
