## §1 Estimands — material conceptual errors

**Time origin / horizon** are fine (`Submit`, 72 h).

**E1 is incoherent.** It is introduced as  
\(S(72)=P(T>72)\) with \(T\) = time to “any internal resolution,” then immediately operationalized as a *breach event* process. Those are different objects:

- For KM/AJ with event = resolution, S03 (resolve at 76) is an **event at 76**, not a “breach event.”
- For a binary “breached by 72” estimand, S03 is a **known breach** with no need to place an event at 76.
- S09 (open at 92) **cannot** be both “already known to have breached” and handled only as ordinary right-censoring if E1 is the probability of breaching/not resolving by 72; the known \(T>72\) information must enter the estimand explicitly (e.g. as a definite contribution to \(\{T>72\}\), or via a landmark/binary at 72). Censoring at 92 is correct only for a continuous time-to-resolution survivor curve—not for a breach indicator the text keeps invoking.

**Withdraw at 27:** calling it a competing event that “neither breaches nor fulfils the SLA” needs a precise probability statement. Under competing risks, \(P(\text{resolved by }72)\) and \(P(T_{\text{resolve}}>72)\) are not interchangeable; the answer never defines the probability space cleanly.

**E2 formula is weak:**  
\(F(72)=P(T>72,\ \text{event = Resolve before right censoring})\)  
is not a standard CIF. Late-resolution cumulative incidence needs a clear time argument (CIF of resolution on \((72,\infty)\), or \(P(\text{resolve time}>72)\), with competing exits). Contributions for open/transfer are hand-wavy relative to E1.

**Transfer under E1 “PRIMARY = censor”** is defensible as one scenario, but not justified against informative censoring raised later—primary choice and later warning pull in opposite directions without reconciliation.

## §3 Bias discussion — one hard factual error, some logic slips

**Strong:** survivor bias with open IDs; transfers concentrated in escalated P1 (S08, S21, S32); cohort non-randomness.

**False statement:** “open cases that are still <72 h **(none in this extract)**.”  
Open and age <72 include at least **S10 (47), S15 (35), S24 (67), S29 (51), S31 (23)**. That is not a nit—it undercuts the risk-set / tail discussion.

**Competing risks bullet:** naive completed-case analysis does **not** generally “count [withdrawals] as successes”; if “completed-case = resolved only,” they are **dropped**; if “all terminals,” they are non-resolve terminals. “Counted as successes because they did not breach” confuses exclusion bias with mislabeling.

**“Left truncation”** is the wrong term for right-censored opens; delayed entry is not identified from the extract. Blurs a real issue (open &lt;72 vs open &gt;72) with incorrect survival jargon.
