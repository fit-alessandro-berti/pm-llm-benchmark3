## Section-by-section

### 1. Mechanism map — good skeleton, weak precision

**Strengths:** Tabular action → mechanism → signature is the right form. Covers nominal handovers, central queues, utilization, rework, local expertise, role clarity, resilience, and fairness. Utilization vs queue trade-off and segment/team filters are on-target.

**Defects:**
- Text is repeatedly broken (“local-caseworker tech handover frequency”, missing operators, empty cells in spirit). That is not minor in a mechanism map; arrows and inequalities are the claims.
- Little’s Law is wrong: `Wq = ·(util/(1-util))` omits arrival rate and mean service time. Citing a mangled formula undercuts the “connect structure to measures” brief.
- “Same-local-caseworker → tech loop disappears in North/South; survives as cross-team flow in East” is confused. Under the proposal, all three local techs merge into one shared service; East does not specially “survive” a local loop as cross-team flow. East’s distinctive story is overload relief and possibly larger *benefit*, not a different residual loop topology of that form.
- Management’s own table shows tech handovers *up* (36 → 41), so “nominal handover reduction” is only true for some edge types. The map never cleanly separates *local loop reduction* from *total handover count*, which is the central analytical tension later scenarios try to use.
- Role clarity → “same activity done by >4 roles” is not a standard or reliable `discover_organizational_roles` reading; RACI is asserted, not operationalized.
- Learning is collapsed into “local expertise loss” without a distinct skill-development / case-mix routing mechanism (hard cases pulled away → fewer learning opportunities), even though the question flags learning explicitly.

### 2. Scenario forecasts — useful spread, fuzzy quantities

**Strengths:** Optimistic / central / adverse is complete on the required dimensions. Central scenario is the best part: net touches vs nominal count, East gain vs North/South drift, rural/language fairness, reassignment and duplicate-approval warnings. Adverse super-hub and exception-handler spillover are credible.

**Defects:**
- Optimistic “local loops 18%, cross-team 50%” has unclear baselines and units (percent of what? reduction vs level?). Management data is in frequency per 100 cases; the answer abandons that unit without definition.
- Early-warning items under *Optimistic* (e.g. util >85%) read like failure signals for a success path; they belong in monitoring logic, not as optimistic-scenario content without framing.
- Several deltas (±0 d, −0.8 d, +1.4 d, rework ±pp) are asserted without tying back to current team baselines (North 6.8d / East 8.6d, Legal/Risk already 91–93% util). Ignoring that Legal/Risk are already near capacity makes the “broader detection → 12/100 and 10.5h wait” path under-modeled in all three scenarios.
- “Role clarity – clear RACI in shared queues” in the optimistic case is wishful and not linked to a log signature.
- Resilience is thin across scenarios (mostly queue tails), despite being a required mechanism theme.

### 3. Leading and lagging indicators — right idea, unfinished and partly miscategorized

**Strengths:** Mix of queue length, tail wait, utilization, reassignment, subcontracting depth, segmented cycle time, rework, CSAT, resource-similarity entropy. Explicit PM4Py-ish acquisition column.

**Defects:**
- Guardrail/target column is empty or incomplete for several lagging rows (“”, “not  >10 %”). An unfinished control table is a material gap in a forecasting/governance answer.
- Weekly SLA-miss as “leading” is debatable; it is usually a short-lag outcome. True leading signals (queue length, arrival mix to Tech_SS, priority inversion, `local_expertise_flag` decline, `handover_reason` shifts) are underused despite being in the log schema.
- No indicator for fairness of *priority* vs team/segment, or for learning (activity diversity / specialty exposure per local resource over time) beyond a vague entropy line.
- API hints are hand-wavy (`insert_case_service_waiting_time` “filter activity=…”), which foreshadows Section 7 errors.

### 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time — adequate list, shallow on fairness and composition

**Strengths:** Six risks span tail latency, high-complexity, knowledge loss, skill stagnation, accountability, central SPOF. Correct thesis: median/mean can improve while tails and latent capability worsen.

**Defects (strict):**
- Fairness across *teams and customer segments* is not a first-class risk; it is smuggled into “high-complexity starvation.” Composition bias (East improvement offsetting North/South degradation so the global average looks fine) is the classic average-cycle-time trap here and is missing.
- Little operational detail on how each risk appears in the given columns (`rework_flag`, `local_expertise_flag`, `customer_segment`, `reassignment_count`, `handover_reason`).
- Exception handling is one sentence; no link to cross-team cover already showing 18% rework and 8.9h wait in the baseline table.
- “Appears months later” for knowledge loss is asserted without a detection design (cohort lag, complexity-mix exposure).

Only six short bullets for a section that asked for depth on seven named themes is thin relative to Sections 1–2.

### 5. Pilot design — smart East focus, design flaws

**Strengths:** East-first matches the asymmetry in the data (E_Tech 82%, worse tails). Control group, pre/post windows, segmentation, guardrails, staff pulse, rollback triggers — all present. Avoids full rollout.

**Defects:**
- Dual treatment confounds inference: full East shared-service *plus* 20% random North/South high-complexity cases is two interventions. You cannot cleanly attribute East effects vs selected hard-case routing effects, nor estimate North/South full-cutover risk from a 20% high-complexity skim.
- Three months is short for learning/expertise erosion and fairness stabilization; the answer never says which effects the pilot *cannot* detect.
- No handling of Legal/Risk already at capacity when routing rules broaden specialty detection (management table: 9 → 12 per 100, wait 9.6 → 10.5h).
- Guardrails omit tail metrics by complexity/segment and specialist utilization overload (e.g. sustained >90%).
- “Revert within 10 days” is operationally bold and unexplained; adjustment triggers are binary stop-loss more than staged redesign (routing rules, staffing, local tech retention).

### 6. Recommendation — aligned, slightly generic

**Strengths:** “Pilot first, scale adaptively” satisfies the constraint against unmonitored full rollout. Explicit warning not to read early averages as long-run success. Mentions upskilling to counter capability erosion.

**Defects:** Scale-up *conditions* are vague (no explicit gate: e.g. Tech_SS p90, segment SLA parity, no CSAT gap widen, stable rework on high-complexity). “Modify” options (keep embedded tech in North/South, shared service only for overflow/East, dual path for high-complexity) are under-developed given the mechanism map’s own risks. Conditional language is present but not crisp enough for an executive decision memo.

### 7. PM4Py analysis sketch — largest technical failure

**Strengths:** Attempts the full checklist: handover, working-together, subcontracting, DFG/performance DFG, wait/service, similarity/roles, filters, rework/SLA. Before/after loop is the right comparison frame.

**Defects (heavy):**
- Prompt listed `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network`, etc. The code mostly ignores those and uses other module paths (`handover.apply`, etc.) without showing equivalence.
- `discover_organizational_roles(sim_matrix)` is almost certainly wrong input (roles from log/resource-activity, not a similarity matrix passed that way).
- `insert_case_service_waiting_time(..., activity=act)` does not match typical PM4Py usage as implied; waiting/service extraction is hand-waved.
- `eval(f"df_{cohort}")` is poor and fragile.
- `filter_variants` / variant length logic is confused (`len(l)>10` on variant values depends on structure; “keep frequent” is not clearly implemented).
- `pm4py.view_dfg_graph` and several imports look incorrect or incomplete.
- No real before/after *comparison* metrics (edge weight deltas, p50/p90 wait by segment, statistical or even simple grouped tables)—only “view” calls.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` from the relevant list is unused.
- SLA-miss block assumes an `outcome=='SLA_MISS'` encoding never specified; baseline used rates, not necessarily that literal.

As an “analysis sketch,” some pseudocode latitude is fair; as written it overclaims executability and would mislead someone implementing the monitoring system the recommendation depends on. That is a serious flaw in a PM-centered exam answer.
