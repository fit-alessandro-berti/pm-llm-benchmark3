## 5.2

The submitted design recognizes the core selection problem (activation-dependent labels) and avoids the most catastrophic errors (treating rejects/withdrawals/open cases as non-defaults, random splits, or naïve use of the 21 600-row export). That alone places it above failing submissions. Nevertheless, under strict grading it contains multiple material inconsistencies, unclarities, implementation defects and incomplete fulfilments of the required nine-section contract. These accumulate into a middling score.

**Section 1.** The primary estimand is cleanly stated as \(P(default_{90d}=1\mid\text{activation},\text{prefix})\). The alternative competing-risk formulation is appropriate and correctly differentiated. However the prediction-time definition (“first event after Credit Review or the 3rd event, whichever later”) is operationally ambiguous and never reconciled with the fixed-\(k=3\) prefixes used later. Horizon and maturity cut-offs are reasonable but the population wording mixes “all applications” with an activation-conditioned estimand, creating unnecessary vagueness.

**Section 2.** The arithmetic of the cohort-flow table is correct and accounts for all 48 000 cases, including the 60 % linkage split. This is a strength. The inclusion/exclusion rules, however, are poorly specified and internally contradictory: the text claims to exclude post-1-July-2025 cases “until sufficient volume exists for a separate stratum,” yet the validation design immediately uses July–August 2025 as the test window. The missing relational operator in the inclusion sentence and the lack of an explicit as-of predicate for every terminal state further reduce clarity.

**Section 3.** Distinguishing ordinary censoring, delayed labels, activation selection, transfers and permanently missing feeds is good. The proposal of IPW or a joint activation–default model is statistically defensible. Yet the section abruptly switches the primary analysis to cause-specific Cox / Fine–Gray models while Section 1 defined a binary target; the two formulations are never reconciled. Claims about partial testability are acceptable, but the sensitivity analyses remain high-level.

**Section 4.** Fixed-length and elapsed-time prefixes, short-case handling, rework collapsing and migration normalisation are all mentioned. The description stays generic; it never pins the prefix cut to the exact prediction event defined in Section 1, nor does it give a concrete rule for the document-loop collapse that preserves the as-of constraint.

**Section 5.** The catalogue of static, prefix-derived, queue and calendar features is adequate and correctly insists on historical reference data for resource/queue statistics. No feature is flagrantly leaky on paper. Depth is modest; control-flow features beyond a raw DFG count and rework counter are thin.

**Section 6.** The leakage-audit table is the strongest single element: every required column appears, availability rules are stated, and the keep/repair/drop decisions are justified. Minor quibbles (e.g., the precise midnight rule for `documents_total` could still admit same-day leakage under certain extraction semantics) do not undermine the table.

**Section 7.** A temporal split that isolates the July 2025 policy change is correct in spirit and the warning against random case splits is well taken. Label-maturity enforcement and the mention of calibration and subgroup metrics are present. Because the authors drifted into survival thinking, they prescribe time-dependent Brier and cumulative/dynamic AUC for what was defined as a binary target—another inconsistency. The October migration is essentially untested (test window ends 31 August), and no explicit rolling-origin or policy-regime interaction analysis is supplied.

**Section 8.** Limitations are acknowledged honestly: the estimand is conditional on historical activation, not counterfactual; three concrete deployment feedback loops are listed. This section meets the bar.

**Section 9 (code).** The skeleton is the clearest failure relative to the specification.  
- It is substantially shorter than the requested 80–160 lines.  
- The group-by aggregation that computes `activation_time` is syntactically invalid and will raise.  
- `label_ready` construction via `set_index` on a non-unique key is fragile.  
- Full-trace DFG discovery and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are performed on logs that still contain post-prediction events, directly contradicting the leakage-prevention narrative.  
- No real leakage-audit assertion, no competing-risk indicator construction, no proper censoring-time calculation, and almost no comments that mark modelling assumptions or future survival-model hooks appear.  
- Unused imports and a complete absence of any feature-matrix materialisation after prefix extraction leave the pipeline non-executable even as a sketch.  

Collectively these defects show that the data-engineering discipline demanded by the prompt has not been demonstrated.

**Summary judgement.** Conceptual awareness of selection bias, leakage and temporal validity is evident and the leakage table plus cohort arithmetic are solid. Against that must be weighed: (a) unresolved mismatch between the declared binary target and the survival analysis/metrics, (b) contradictory cohort and validation windows around the policy change, (c) vague prediction-time semantics, and (d) a non-working, incomplete code skeleton that fails to realise the as-of and audit claims made in prose. Under a hypercritical reading these are not minor polish issues; they are material flaws in statistical design coherence and implementability. Hence the score is firmly in the middle of the range rather than the upper tier.
