**6.2**

The submission is a competent, well-structured attempt that hits every required heading, maintains a mostly dialectical posture, produces a usable evidence matrix, reaches a non-split system judgment, and supplies a layered architecture plus a data-overlay table. It is far from flawless. Under hypercritical review the defects are material, repeated, and concentrated exactly where the rubric weights model semantics, evidence traceability, and precise handling of authority/scope.

### Major deductions
- **POWL model (Section 7) is semantically broken on a required feature.** The task explicitly demands “a repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism or a clearly explained approximation.” The code builds only a single optional XOR (skip vs. one `Request_More_Info`) placed *after* both sanctions and quality. That is neither repeatable nor faithful to the observed desirable trace (SUP-707: requests occur between sanctions and quality). `Operator.LOOP` exists in PM4Py and is the obvious construct; the authors note the limitation yet still ship a non-loop. The approximation is therefore indefensible. Placement, cardinality, and interleaving are all wrong. This alone collapses the “model semantics” criterion.
- **Evidence matrix contains a clear factual error.** SUP-704 (11 July) is inside the FD-7 window; the prosecution cell nevertheless claims “FD-7 expired or was over-applied.” That is simply false and undermines traceability.
- **Defense brief fabricates a fact.** It asserts that the auto-approve policy was “subsequently formalized.” The source materials state the opposite (“no permanent amendment existed”). Over-claiming is fatal to evidence discipline.
- **Prosecution fails the mandatory self-limitation.** The instructions require the prosecution to “acknowledge genuine model/data defects.” The brief largely treats M0/CS-9 as sacrosanct and attributes almost every gap to operational drift or bad logging; the required concessions are perfunctory or absent.
- **Classification softness on clear rule breaches.** SUP-712 violates an explicit CS-9 resource rule (requester  approver) that is stipulated in N1. Labelling it MIXED (with a control-flow-centric dominant reason) is evasion. SUP-715 is closer to MIXED/DATA, but the pattern of leniency is noticeable. “Do not use mixed as a substitute for analysis” is only half-respected.
- **Secondary model and scope issues.** Auto and emergency branches omit any info-request path; concurrency and silent transitions are inconsistently applied; early-reject is accidentally enabled (which happens to help SUP-709) but never justified as a deliberate scope decision. Reactivation and rejected-lead scope receive architecture language but almost no executable reflection.

### Lesser but still chargeable flaws
- Burden-of-proof paragraph is clean, yet the later judgment does not consistently apply the “immutable contemporaneous exemption” standard it announced (especially around SUP-705/714).
- Aggregate numbers (14 ineligible autos, 6 post-pilot, notification missingness jump, Internal Audit flags) are under-used in both briefs.
- Architecture diagram is attractive but the mapping “what belongs in POWL vs. DECLARE vs. pandas vs. org controls” remains high-level; several constraints that could have been DECLARE-style (sanctions before any activate, spend-cap responses, etc.) are left entirely to “pandas.”
- Code is non-executable in spirit without further repair: duplicate transition labels for Activate_Supplier, info_loop construction that cannot iterate, and no guard or silent exit that would let a LOOP terminate cleanly.
- Minor rhetorical inflation (“immutable tablets,” “governance collapses,” “force majeure”) appears on both sides and slightly erodes the required sober, process-aware tone.

### Residual strengths (why not lower)
Exact heading compliance, complete 15-row matrix, explicit rejection of each side’s overreach in §5, sensible primary classifications for the unambiguous cases (701–704, 707–711, 713–714), and a data-collection table that actually lists the required stable IDs, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution and WORM timestamps. The system-level verdict (“ops materially noncompliant *and* M0 obsolete”) is the correct non-split conclusion.

These strengths cannot offset the broken repeatable-loop construct, the matrix factual error, the invented formalization claim, and the failure of the prosecution to own model defects. Under the mandated hypercritical standard the answer sits at 6.2. A near-flawless submission would have used a genuine `Operator.LOOP` (or a rigorously justified multi-XOR nesting), kept every matrix cell factually tight, refused to invent post-hoc formalization, forced the prosecution to list M0’s missing loops/overlays as defects, and classified the explicit SoD breach as NONCOMPLIANT.
