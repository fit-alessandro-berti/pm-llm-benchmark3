# Grading rationale (strict)

## Summary judgment
The submission is a strong, coherent process-mining analysis that follows the required eight-section structure, respects aggregate limitations, separates demand/mix/capacity/backlog non-stationarity, refuses to treat recovery as steady-state throughput, and supplies runnable Python that largely matches the narrative. Under a hypercritical bar it is **not** near-flawless: a few analytical inconsistencies, over-reliance on a self-flagged broken field, thin uncertainty quantification on tiny regimes, and minor presentation/code–text gaps keep it well below 9–10.

## Section-by-section

### 1. Data audit and estimands — strong, not perfect
**Strengths:** Clear open-hours convention; correct refusal to treat median/p90 service as \(E[S]\) or flow time; backlog/carry-over and completionsame-day-arrival threats are explicit; lists well more than five threats; correctly flags that daily points are dependent.

**Flaws:**
- Visible ANSI artifact (`[0m`) in the math line is sloppy.
- Threat (10) on peak-field vs vector mismatch is excellent discovery, but the section does not yet draw the operational consequence that `peak_08_10_pct` may be unusable for inference (see §2).
- “Rough rostered labor-minute denominator” is introduced without enough warning that productive fraction is unidentified, so later “stress indices” inherit an unstated strong assumption.

### 2. Primary regime segmentation — good structure, evidence hygiene problem
**Strengths:** Explicit partition of all 28 days; outage and recovery isolated; intervention not sold as clean causal success; arrival/completion/WIP arithmetic checked out on spot audits (baseline WD arrivals 100.1, mix WD 114, totals 2519/2409); confidence column present; storyline matches the quality bar.

**Flaws (material under strict grading):**
- Regime evidence repeatedly cites “peak field 26–33% / 40–50%” **after** documenting that the only hourly ground truth contradicts those fields by large factors (e.g. 4/21 vector 9.6% vs field 47%). Using a field you have shown is irreconcilable as primary regime evidence is a serious consistency error. Complex %, WIP, service tails, and net flow already suffice; leaning on the broken field weakens the segmentation.
- Several regimes are 2-day (or 1-day) slices given point estimates to 1 decimal with no ranges/bootstrap/IQR; “low confidence” labels do not substitute for interval estimates when \(n=2\).
- “Median/p90 stress index” is not utilization and is labeled as such, but presenting it in the main table still invites misreading; definition (arrivals×median)/(720×FTE) mixes an arrival count with a completion-time quantile and ignores carry-over.

### 3. Alternative segmentation — acceptable, under-developed
Two-axis state model (volume/burst × mix/FTE, outage override) is a genuinely different operational story and the strength/weakness comparison is fair.

**Flaws:** Almost no quantitative regime summaries, no date membership table, no side-by-side metric contrast with primary. For a required “materially different segmentation,” this is more sketch than analysis. Thresholds (39% complex, etc.) are acknowledged as pragmatic but not stress-tested.

### 4. Pooled vs segmented — strong
**Strengths:** Three+ clear obscuring mechanisms (WD/WE composition, complex-mix doubling hidden by average, outage+recovery completion cancellation, intervention interpretation trap); Simpson-style composition math (71% of days vs 86% of arrivals); arrival-weighted vs equal-day percentages distinguished; recovery not treated as high productivity.

**Flaws:**

- Some pooled figures are given as if precise (29.9 WIP, 32.9% complex) without noting snapshot/rounding/weighting sensitivity.

### 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, stationarity — very strong with one hedge gap
**Strengths:** CV and Fano on hourly vectors; correct warning that bin-dispersion  process Fano from repeated realizations; 4/21 burst structure well described; daily sequence change-points (outage net +47, mix shift ~4/20); pooled overdispersion largely mixture/trend; lag-1 reported with sample-size humility on weekends; four vectors cannot support a full stochastic model.

**Flaws:**

- No simple weekday-only or pre/post-mix split of lag dependence beyond a couple of scalars.

### 6. Little’s-Law matrix — strong, minor verdict nit
**Strengths:** Correct refusal of pooled LL; median service never substituted for \(W\); open-hours units; both \(\lambda_A\) and \(\lambda_C\) shown; carry-over and boundary columns honest; baseline “approximate only,” outage/recovery/intervention “not defensible” align with the quality bar; qualitative gap vs 25 min median noted without false precision.

**Flaws:**
- High-mix weekday = `approximate only` while intervention weekday = `not defensible` is a thin distinction: both have large net drift and short windows; either both diagnostic-only or the criteria should be sharper.
- Implied times reported to 2 decimals on unstable windows still look more authoritative than the verdict column admits.
- No explicit statement that snapshot mean WIP over open hours is not the time-average \(L\) of the same population as the throughput proxy when overnight holding exists (mentioned elsewhere, under-weighted here).

3. **LL verdict boundary** between high-mix and intervention is under-motivated.
4. **Presentation/reproducibility polish** (ANSI glitch; code regime set  text regime set).
5. **Alternative segmentation** is conceptually good but not carried to the same quantitative standard as primary.
