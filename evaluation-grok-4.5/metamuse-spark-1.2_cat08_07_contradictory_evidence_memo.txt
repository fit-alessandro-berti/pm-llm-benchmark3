## Overall

Strong strategic memo: correct non-scaling stance, segment-aware hybrid, clear VOI logic, and good use of process-mining artifacts. Under strict grading it is clearly not near-flawless. Several consistency, precision, and overclaim issues keep it well below the top band.

## What works

- **Decision quality:** Rejects A and pure B for the right reasons; E(+D) fits conflicting evidence and the stated constraints.
- **Structure:** Required sections present; evidence table cleanly splits fact / interpretation / assumption with ≥8 items.
- **Metric conflicts:** Fitness vs precision, median vs tail, handovers vs resilience, visible vs downstream cost, and segment split are explained coherently and tied to DFG, alignments, resources, and segments.
- **Alternatives A–E:** Advantages, risks, and “reasonable if” conditions are decision-useful.
- **Final package:** Pause / scale / measure / thresholds is operational enough for an exec memo.

## Major issues (score-driving)

**1. Internal contradiction on what is deployed**  
Section 6 says deploy “**Full Redesign R**” for simple low-risk houses, then immediately **pauses post-billing activation** system-wide and restricts survey skipping. That is not full R; it is a modified subset. The memo never reconciles this. A decision memo that mislabels the intervention fails a basic consistency test.

**2. Unsupported “34% nationally”**  
Pilot simple-house share is 34%, and pilot regions were **richer** in apartments/old buildings than baseline. National simple-house share is therefore likely **higher** than 34%, not equal to it. The memo treats pilot mix as national mix in the executive recommendation while the evidence table lists that comparability as uncertain—self-contradiction between §§1/6 and §2.

**3. Causal overclaim**  
“Three design choices **drove** this,” “clearly reduced waiting,” “proves the core mechanism,” and similar wording outrun observational pilot evidence (non-random regions, incomplete attributes, sparse start times, truncated cost). Stronger hedging belonged in the executive memo, not only in the table.

## Moderate issues

**4. Broken key sentence in §3**  
“Fewer handovers  more robust” is corrupted/unreadable at the punchline of the resilience argument. That is a clarity failure on a required conflict explanation.

**5. Threshold design is partly arbitrary or odd**  
- Complaint gate **6.5%** vs baseline **6.1%** with no rationale (looseness smuggles acceptance of regression).  
- Model gate **fitness ≥ 0.85** is weaker than baseline fitness (0.87) and much weaker than pilot fitness (0.94); high fitness was not the problem—**low precision** was. Fitness as a go-live bar is poorly motivated.  
- VOI side numbers (rule accuracy “&lt;70%” / “&gt;85%”, cost “220–230 EUR”) are invented without sensitivity or source.

**6. “Average customer feels improvement”**  
Median improved; that is not the same as average/mean experience, especially with a worse tail and higher complaints. Sloppy distributional language.

**7. Compliance language slightly overshoots evidence**  
Alignments show billing-before-activation and missing permit checks; calling this flat “compliance risk” / “regulatory” without separating legal requirement vs intended control is a mild category error (prompt itself says “compliance-like”).

## Minor issues

- Exec memo is dense and near the 700-word ceiling; some metric dump belongs only in later sections.  
- M1 and M6 are “tied” then sequenced differently later—ranking is a bit mushy.  
- Queueing-theory appeal is decorative, not evidenced.  
- Option C is undervalued relative to E given the memo’s own universal billing block and coordinator redesign; the distinction between C and E blurs once activation-after-billing is killed for everyone.  
- “Estimated total cost rose” is stated more firmly in §1 than the incomplete measurement allows.

## Bottom line

This would likely pass a human exec review as a solid draft. Against a hypercritical LLM-judge standard—accuracy, internal consistency, and no smuggled certainty—it loses substantial credit for the **full-R contradiction**, **pilot-share-as-national** error, **over-causal executive voice**, **broken resilience sentence**, and **weak/odd thresholds**. Those are not polish nits; they affect whether the recommendation is implementable and honest about evidence.
