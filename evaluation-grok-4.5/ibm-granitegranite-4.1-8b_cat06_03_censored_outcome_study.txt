The submission follows the mandated 9-section structure and shows surface awareness of selective labels, temporal issues, and leakage, but it is riddled with internal contradictions, statistically indefensible choices, and a non-functional implementation that violate the quality criteria.

**Section 1.** The primary target is mis-specified. It is labeled a “counterfactual” yet the estimand and later sections collapse to ordinary conditional default among the observed activated accounts. Prediction time is left vague (“first few events, e.g. after KYC”) while the horizon is defined only after activation; the two are never reconciled. The claim that this answers the business request for “every credit application” therefore rests on unstated and untestable policy-invariance assumptions that the text itself later admits are strong. The multi-state alternative is described more cleanly, revealing that the primary choice was not the most defensible.

**Section 2.** The cohort-flow arithmetic is correct (48 k accounted for). However the text immediately declares that “the primary analysis will use the labeled activated cohort (21 600)”. This is precisely the weak design the rubric condemns: training only on the selectively observed activated cases “without qualification”. Non-activated trajectories are relegated to a sensitivity analysis rather than being treated as part of the primary estimand or properly censored/competing.

**Section 3.** The taxonomy of missingness is adequate, yet the proposed primary method (Fine–Gray on activation-with/without-default) presupposes that non-activated cases are retained as competing events—contradicting the cohort decision of Section 2. Interval censoring and IPW are mentioned but never operationalised with the given `label_available_at` field. Several assumptions are declared “testable” when they are only partially or not at all testable from the supplied data (transfer linkage completeness, partner-feed missingness).

**Section 4.** Prefix logic is confused with the activation event itself. The illustrative call sets `prediction_point='Account Activated'`, which destroys the early-warning semantics required by the business question. Fixed-count and elapsed-time prefixes are named but never cleanly separated from post-activation information; short-case padding and document-loop aggregation are described only rhetorically.

**Section 5.** Several proposed features (`employee_experience_at_activation`, `queue_time_before_activation`, `days_until_label`) are unavailable at a genuine early prediction time and re-introduce leakage. The distinction among static, prefix-derived and historical-reference features is asserted but not enforced.

**Section 6.** The leakage table covers the required columns and reaches mostly correct keep/repair/drop decisions. It is nevertheless incomplete: it never audits the activation-time features introduced in Section 5, nor the `label_available_at` timestamp itself when used as a covariate.

**Section 7.** The chronological split correctly isolates the July 2025 policy change and the October migration; the warning against random splits is sound. Metrics (C-index, time-dependent Brier) match a survival target. Subgroup and calibration plans remain high-level; no concrete handling of the 3 600 immature labels inside the test window is given.

**Section 8.** Limitations are acknowledged at a generic level (predictive association  counterfactual, three deployment shifts). The text still over-claims that the model estimates risk “for cases that would be activated” after having trained only on those that were activated.

**Section 9.** The code skeleton is the clearest failure. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, never calls `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`, and invents non-existent APIs (`prefix_view`, `filter_activity_univ`, `split_prefix_and_post`, custom `EventLog` constructor). The temporal split contains invalid pandas syntax (`.dt.to_period.isin`). Filtering is performed against the global extraction date rather than a case-wise as-of prediction time; activation is again treated as the prediction point. Cohort counts, label-maturity flags and a proper leakage audit are only printed as static tables, not computed from the data. The skeleton therefore cannot be executed or extended to the survival/competing-risk stage it comments on.

Collectively these defects—especially the persistent equation of “early prefix” with “activation”, the reduction of the primary cohort to the 21 600 labelled rows, and a fabricated non-runnable PM4Py pipeline—render the design misleading on the exact points the rubric flags as critical. A few locally competent paragraphs do not offset the fundamental inconsistencies.
