## Scoring rationale (strict)

The answer is structurally complete, often insightful on the speed/quality/compliance contradiction, and clearly tries to differentiate three redesigns by segment. Under hypercritical review it is **not** near-flawless. Material inaccuracies, unsupported quantification, and cascading capacity fiction pull the score down hard.

### Severe issues (major deductions)

1. **Unsupported volume/time base (critical)**  
   The DFG is “aggregated over 5,000 cases” with **no calendar period**. The answer repeatedly treats 5,000 as a weekly (or stable weekly-equivalent) load and builds reviewer utilization (“~2,500 manual-review activities per week”, “82%  95%”, “+560 cases/week”, quality-gate “2,200–3,500 cases/week”, “330 hours/week needed vs 76 capacity”, etc.). Resource pressure, bottleneck migration, hiring needs, and much of the cost/resource columns in the table rest on this invention. That violates the grounding constraint and the instruction not to pretend values not supported by the prompt.

2. **Invented / wrong flow counts**  
   - “820 reopened cases/week” (and similar) is inconsistent with the given 9.2% of 5,000  460 and with the DFG reopen edges.  
   - “320 of 1,110” abandonment framing and several loop totals are not cleanly recoverable from the given DFG.  
   - 70/20/10 GOOD/BORDERLINE/POOR splits, auto-resolve percentages, and many “target” medians (13 h, 22 h, 35–45 h, 40–60 h, 85 h, etc.) are presented with false precision.

3. **Violates “do not pretend to calculate exact values”**  
   Performance DFG gives selected edge times only. The answer chains them into precise path totals, weighted averages, “% cheaper”, fitness “0.98+”, and narrow implementation-week and EUR ranges as if they were identified. The comparison table and pilots are full of these. Qualitative signs were required; the answer repeatedly over-claims.

4. **Capacity arithmetic that collapses under its own assumptions**  
   Redesign A/C failure modes correctly *notice* that a universal pre-dispatch quality gate can explode demand, then “solve” it with invented automation fractions and FTE counts. Because inflow is already fictional, the bottleneck analysis is theater. Same for escalation-board “24–30 cases/week vs 820.”
