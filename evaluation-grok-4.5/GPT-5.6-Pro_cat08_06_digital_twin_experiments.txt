### 5. Warm-up, replications, uncertainty — excellent (small technical quibbles)

**Strengths:** Replay vs long-run modes; annual-cycle warm-up; Welch check; CRN with named streams and keyed draws; nested parameter × MC uncertainty; heavy-tail reporting (not means-only); right-censoring for external delays.

**Issues:**

### 6. Validation strategy — excellent

**Strengths:** Face validation with impossibility checks; replay with completion-only projection; distributional bands; utilization and dynamic bottleneck perturbations; independent-case ablation; blocked out-of-sample; pilot for unevaluable policies.

**Issues:**
- Provisional bands (e.g., median duration 5–10%, variant share ±2 pp) are presented as “starting tolerances” but are easy to game if many metrics are tested without multiplicity control.
- Bottleneck ±10–20% capacity perturbations are sound; expected qualitative responses are slightly too confident (“D5 can move legal work earlier without increasing legal throughput”) as if already known rather than hypotheses to confirm.

### 7. Decision criteria — very strong (threshold arbitrariness)

**Strengths:** Robust Pareto; legal/safety guardrails first; no exact optimality; applicant burden and quality metrics; monitoring/drift; reversible pilots preferred.

**Issues:**

- “Prefer at least 5% or 2–3 days” mixes relative and absolute rules without stating which wins when they conflict.

### 8. Python skeleton — good but not fully faithful/consistent

**Strengths:** pandas load (no fake `pm4py.read_csv`); `format_dataframe`; process-tree parse with limitation note; variants/DFG/performance DFG/arrival average; timing path that refuses to invent starts; modular DES skeleton; PolicySpec covering D1–D10; batcher stub; experiment runner with shared seeds; clear PM4Py vs engine vs experiment split.
