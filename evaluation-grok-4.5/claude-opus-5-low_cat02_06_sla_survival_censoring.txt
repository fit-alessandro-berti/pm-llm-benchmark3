# Grading rationale (strict)

## Summary judgment
The submission is analytically ambitious, mostly well-structured, and demonstrates real process-mining and competing-risk competence. It is **not** nearly flawless. Under a hypercritical standard, clear factual slips, over-precise claims on \(n=32\), occasional internal inconsistencies, and a few methodological soft spots keep it well out of the 9.5–10 band and below a clean 9.0.

## Strengths (why it is still high)
- Correct insistence on defining estimands before estimators; E1–E5 are useful and mostly well mapped to decisions.
- Strong treatment of open cases **above vs below** 72h; correctly notes that age  72 already identifies non-resolution for the SLA-horizon estimand.
- Competing risks vs censoring vs already-observed breaches are distinguished with concrete case IDs (S03, S06, S08, S09, etc.).
- Immortal-time / leakage discussion for `Escalate`, `Rework`, prefix length, and `last_event_gap_h` is appropriate; multi-state sketch is sensible.
- Sensitivity section correctly flags that withdrawal/rejection coding and transfer coding (especially in P1) can move conclusions more than the choice of KM vs AJ.
- Code block is substantial: builds the table, encodes multiple transfer rules, keeps open cases, stratifies, attempts AJ/KM from first principles, and invokes PM4Py-style variant/prefix logic with a fallback.
- Limitations section prioritizes the right missing data (event timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff/submission dates, compliance, assignment).

## Deductions (strict)

### Factual / internal consistency errors
- **P1 headcount error (material slip):** §2 states “3 of the 6 P1 cases” then lists eight P1 IDs (S04, S08, S11, S18, S21, S25, S29, S32). That is a direct arithmetic/labeling mistake in a stratum the author correctly identifies as assumption-fragile.
- **Stalled-case listing inconsistency:** Informative-censoring text emphasizes S26/S11/S25 while the feature threshold `last_event_gap_h  24` also catches S09 (31) and borderline S10 (19); the prose and the sensitivity design do not stay aligned.
- **Decomposition arithmetic presented as tidy when it is estimator-dependent:** “0.38 still active + 0.32 absorbed  E10.70” is narratively neat but depends on the hand-rolled AJ/KM implementation and on how transfers/late resolves sit inside \(S_{\mathrm{any}}(72)\). On \(n=32\) this precision is overstated.

### Estimand / encoding soft spots
- **T1 vs T3 distinction is weaker than advertised.** Once transfer is never coded as Resolve, \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\) is essentially the same object; risk-set differences are second-order. The write-up sometimes talks as if T3 meaningfully redefines E1 at \(\tau\) beyond the two pre-72 transfers, then admits the numerical bite is small—muddled.
- **E2 as \(\mathrm{CIF}(\infty)-\mathrm{CIF}(72)\)** is fine in principle but poorly identified here (heavy censoring/transfer in the tail). The text under-sells how speculative the late-resolution mass is relative to E1.
- **“Reject as SLA success”** is a defensible sensitivity, but the operational reading is asserted rather than tied to any stated contractual definition in the prompt.

### Statistical plan / evidence handling
- Primary recommendation (stratified Aalen–Johansen + all-cause KM for E3) is reasonable; secondary landmark/RMST direction is right.
- However, the submission **prints three-decimal CIFs and E10.696 as if stable facts** while simultaneously conceding that a bootstrap interval for A vs B would cover both signs. That is overconfident reporting on 13 resolution events.
- Cause-specific Cox / Fine–Gray are correctly down-weighted for event count, but the plan still under-develops variance estimation, ties, and how administrative cutoff informative censoring would be diagnosed beyond a narrative stress test.
- Naive completed-case critique is strong, yet the dramatic “31% vs 70%” contrast mixes a conditional-on-resolved proportion with a competing-risk absolute risk; pedagogically effective, statistically a bit rhetorical.

### Process-aware / leakage
- Feature table is good. The Mermaid model is plausible.
- Landmarking is correctly prescribed, then the text admits timestamps are missing and falls back to “degenerate” prefix-order landmarks—appropriate, but the earlier tone sometimes implies more identification than the data support.
- `entry_pattern` / AutoCheck compliance is correctly flagged as post-baseline confounding risk; good. Still, cohort-policy claims oscillate between “no demonstrable effect” and detailed per-protocol speculation that could mislead a skimmer.

### Code (§8) — strict
- Meets most checklist items (in-memory table, two estimands, three transfer scenarios, open cases retained, stratification, sensitivity, PM4Py-ish variant/prefix attempt, no survival timestamps fabricated for hazards).
- Hand-rolled AJ order of operations looks *directionally* right (use \(S(t-)\) then update), but there is no validation against a reference implementation, tie handling is naive, and transfer “breach” vs “competing” paths do not actually diverge in the CIF engine—so the “three scenarios” are partly cosmetic for E1.
- `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` / `get_prefixes_from_log` are wrapped in try/except (good) but the control-flow section is not cleanly integrated into the survival estimands; it mostly prints variants and string-derived features.
- Comments assert “Expected output… CIF0.304” as reproduced truth; graders cannot treat that as verified, and the solution’s authority depends on it.

### Operational interpretation
- Alert vs recovery-queue split (already past 72 vs landmark residual risk) is excellent.
- Fairness warning (model learning P1  transfer/low internal resolve  deprioritise P1) is insightful.
- Calibration plan is generic-good but not grounded in what is actually identifiable from this freeze (no timestamps, tiny strata).

### Format / prompt adherence
- Required H1 sections are present and in order.
- Distinguishes assumptions/estimates reasonably (A1, T1–T4).
- Still occasionally blurs observation vs estimate vs business redefinition (especially reject/transfer “success” language and the headline 70% figure).

## Why not higher (9.5)
Near-flawless would require: zero stratum headcount errors; estimands and transfer scenarios numerically distinct where claimed; uncertainty attached to every headline risk; verified survival estimators; tighter alignment between prose, tables, and code; less over-precise pooling on \(n=32\).

## Why not lower (7.5)
This is not a shallow or KM-only completed-case write-up. It correctly refuses non-informative censoring of withdrawal/transfer without discussion, centers process-aware leakage/immortal time, and keeps open cases in the risk set. Those are the core competencies the rubric rewards.
