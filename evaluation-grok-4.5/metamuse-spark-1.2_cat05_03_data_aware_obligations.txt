## Evaluation

### Strengths
Case-level judgments are mostly right under event-time semantics: TX-301/303/304/307/309/310 as noncompliant (or noncompliant-with-conflict for EX-88), TX-305 indeterminate, TX-306/308/311 compliant, TX-302 justified via strong EX-77 evidence and/or Enhanced_Review. Sections 1, 4, 6, and 7 show real grasp of blanks-as-no-update, no final-state leakage, evidence vs record time, R4 thresholds, and why DECLARE/trace-order checks fail on data-conditional rules.

### Major defects (score drivers)

**1. Formalization is badly broken (Section 2)**  
The required pseudo-logic is not reliably readable as logic. Comparison and connective symbols are missing throughout (`amount 100000`, `Order(e,c)  Order(c,rel)`, `evidence_time  Release`, `eC: r.activity=...`, `mMaterialChanges...`). As written, R1–R5 are not executable specifications; an LLM judge scoring “formalization quality” should treat this as a large failure, not a cosmetic glitch.

**2. Code does not meet the stated checking bar (Section 5)**  
- R4 is not actually computed: amount-at-review vs post-review increase is stubbed (`enh_amt = None`, “COARSE_PASS_NEEDS_R4_CHECK”) despite being central to TX-301/306/308/309.  
- R3 is effectively hard-wired and incomplete (`has_new_screen = False`; jurisdiction==`XZ` special case), not a general “screen after list update before release” check.  
- “Latest material data change” is not tracked as a first-class timeline object.  
- Same-timestamp safety is only partial (`last_Enhanced < release_time` ignores `event_index`).  
- Dead/half-written mask logic inside `diagnostics` signals unfinished work.  
Coarse `REVIEW_REQUIRED` labels are allowed, but the script still under-delivers on items 5–7 of the brief and is only weakly “useful” for verifying the narrative.

**3. Precision and consistency slips**  
- R1 “material change” is defined inconsistently (Create + any decision attribute vs R4-only >20% amount / trigger-affecting jurisdiction).  
- Findings table text is often corrupted (`80k125k`, `StandardEnhanced`, missing `<`/`≥`), which hurts auditability.  
- TX-302 dual label (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION *and* COMPLIANT) is defensible but sloppy; a single primary class with a note would be cleaner.  
- Exemption checks in code do not fully encode R5 (scope, expiry, Head_Compliance, evidence quality)—only thin timestamp hacks.

**4. Smaller analytical nits**  
- R4 listed as a “triggered rule” at release confuses invalidation mechanics with obligation triggers.  
- Control-flow section is strong (7 modes) but partly repeats points already made; still adequate.  
- No proof the notebook path runs under a real pm4py version after `format_dataframe` column renames.
