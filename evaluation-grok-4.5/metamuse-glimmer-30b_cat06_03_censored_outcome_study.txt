The answer shows surface familiarity with selection bias and leakage themes, but under strict grading it fails on statistical coherence, factual consistency, implementability, and several explicit requirements. It is not close to a strong design document.

### Critical failures

**Broken primary estimand and prediction-time logic.**  
Section 1 sets prediction time as “completion of KYC or k=3” then writes the unreadable constraint ``t0  time of Credit Review decision``. That is not a usable as-of rule. The estimand is written as conditional on eventual activation while features are taken from a pre-decision prefix; that is defensible in principle, but the write-up never cleanly separates (a) the prediction time, (b) the activation-selection event, and (c) the 90-day post-activation horizon. Horizon is tied to activation, yet the business use case is framed as scoring “every application,” so the primary choice needed a sharper operational story (e.g., decision support only among cases still open for underwriting, with activation probability modeled separately). It does not deliver that.

**Cohort math and maturity rules are inconsistent.**  
Section 2 claims training needs ``case_start  2025-04-30`` for maturity, while Section 7 trains on starts before 2025-04-01 and still tests into late 2025. Cases started in Nov–Dec 2025 cannot have mature 90-day post-activation labels by 2026-02-15 in general (activation itself can be weeks later, plus 90d + 7–35d feed delay). The design never enforces “activation + 90d + feed delay ≤ as-of” as a hard cohort filter in the flow table. The table dumps all non-activated and immature activated cases as “excluded from primary” without a usable analysis path for open/delayed cases, so “censoring strategy” is mostly exclusion theater.

**Censoring section is thin and partly wrong.**  
Calling delayed labels simply “excluded from primary” is not a censoring strategy; it induces another selection bias (slow-to-activate or slow-to-feed accounts differ systematically). IPCW for activation is named but not specified (propensity model, overlap, stabilisation, what estimand is recovered). Competing-risk and landmark survival are listed as sensitivities with no concrete state definitions, time origin, or handling of transfer linkage at 60%. Partner missing feeds are mentioned then ignored in the primary plan. Testable vs untestable assumptions are generic one-liners.

**Prefix section has a dangerous error.**  
“Short cases < k use full trace” can pull terminal Accept/Reject/Withdrawn outcomes into the prefix and is a classic leakage path. Activity renaming is hand-waved; duplicate events from migration are not handled. Elapsed-time prefixes are named without saying whether the case must still be open at the time landmark or how decision events inside the window are treated.

**Features and leakage audit are incomplete / sloppy.**  
The audit table misses several required themes in real depth: `default_90d` on event rows, `source_system`, post-activation collections events, and the policy-change selection shift as bias (not only feature leakage). “Resource experience computed at event time from historical log” is asserted without a join key or as-of method. `documents_total` “repair” is underspecified. Section 5 leans on PM4Py helpers without justifying as-of safety.

**Validation design contradicts label maturity.**  
Hold-out starts Nov–Dec 2025 while extraction is 15 Feb 2026: many activated accounts in that window will not have mature `default_90d`. Policy-change and migration are “covered” by calendar bins without blocked backtests, walk-forward folds, or explicit pre/post interaction analysis. Metrics are a standard classification laundry list; no proper scoring under censoring, no precision-at-k / alert utility, no calibration protocol tied to delayed labels. Saying random splits are bad is correct but not enough.

**Limitations are boilerplate.**  
Three deployment shifts are listed at slogan level. The answer never states a hard decision-use boundary such as “do not threshold this score to auto-reject” or “do not treat score as PD for non-activated applications.” Counterfactual language is gestured at, not disciplined.

### Section 9 code is disqualifying for a “concrete skeleton”

- Activation aggregation is nonsensical: a `groupby` lambda using `idxmax` on a boolean mask, then a second merge that can duplicate cases if multiple activation events exist.  
- ``case_agg['label_mature'] = df.groupby(...)...`` assigns a Series without aligned merge; this is not reliable pandas.  
- ``get_prefixes_from_log(..., parameters={'max_length':5, ...})`` is not shown as a verified API usage pattern; even as sketch, there is no join from prefixes back to labels, no prefix-end timestamp, no feature matrix.  
- Forbidden-column check only tests presence in the raw CSV, not whether features were built from post-`t0` rows—the actual leakage failure mode.  
- `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` on the full train log (not prefixes) do not demonstrate prefix-safe process features.  
- No cohort-flow accounting of all 48,000 cases in code, no terminal-state classification, no transfer/open/rejected handling, no temporal feature as-of filter, no train/val/test label-maturity enforcement.  
- Far below the “coherent data-engineering and audit pipeline” bar; closer to unrunnable pseudocode with PM4Py name-drops.

### What it gets right (limited credit)

It refuses to label rejects as non-default, names activation selection as selection rather than a free negative class, prefers temporal splits, lists major leaky fields, and sketches a multi-state alternative. Those are necessary insights, not a finished design.

### Strict score rationale

A strong answer would need: a crisp primary estimand with operable as-of rules; a cohort flow that preserves censoring/competing risks rather than deleting them; maturity-safe temporal folds; a real missingness/selection strategy; prefix rules that cannot ingest terminal outcomes; a leakage table that is operationally enforceable; and a skeleton that could be extended without rewriting the logic. This submission repeatedly breaks those requirements with internal contradictions and non-working code. Under hypercritical grading, that lands in the low-to-mid 3s—not zero, because the high-level bias story is directionally aware, but nowhere near a defensible study protocol.
