## Evaluation

### Overall

Under a strict rubric it is clearly not near-flawless. Presentation failures in the effects table, several unsupported precision claims, and a few technical slips pull the score down hard.

### What works
- **Boundary (Sec 1):** Gate-to-gate plus failure/obsolescence waste is appropriate given the log. Inside/outside split maps cleanly to `energy_kwh`, `transport_kgco2e`, scrap/packaging, rework, and partial-shipment second legs. Uncertainties (idle energy in waits, van empty-return allocation, forecast-driven obsolescence) are real and well stated.

Monitoring evidence is generally event-attribute concrete.
- **Rebound (Sec 5):** Among the best parts. Batch→wait/air/obsolescence, light packaging→returns, CNC_A overload displacement, Critical “gaming,” inspection rush→hidden rework, and hold-for-complete→cancel are all grounded and multi-criteria.

### Critical defects (score drivers)

Directional symbols and comparisons are corrupted throughout (`2416 kWh`, `1.80.70 kg`, `32%~21%`, `18%9%`, `438h`, `2.81.3`, bare “slight” / “****” cells). The required effects table is exactly where the judge needs clear energy/transport/service trade-offs. Recoverable in places from prose, but as submitted it fails a core deliverable on clarity and auditability.

**2. Indicator formula error.**  
`energy_kwh / (1 - scrap + rework rate)` mixes incompatible quantities (scrap kg vs rates) and is algebraically wrong as a “per good item” intensity. That undercuts an otherwise careful indicator set.

**3. Precision beyond the evidence.**  
Repeated numeric ranges are asserted without support: S4 “+5–8h” and “60–75%” (conflates stock→air path 18.5 with segment average 7.8), S8 “40–60%,” S9 “maybe 30–50% of Critical is precautionary,” S7 “+2–4h / 20–35%,” late-risk point estimates like “5–6%,” “10–12%.” The prompt allows qualitative/broad ranges; several cells read like false precision.

**4. Sloppy or unchecked arithmetic.**  
Examples: rework-packaging “620/11000 = 5.6%” (denominator is hand-waved); CNC volume “3670” does not clearly reconcile with DFG inflows to CNC; S3 “30–50% energy” mixes path averages with rework-loop avoidance in ways that double-count with S7.

**5. Kitchen-sink package.**  
Sec 6 stacks essentially S1–S12 (S10 “optional”). Phasing in Sec 7 helps, but the “coherent redesign” still under-prioritizes. A stricter answer would sequence a smaller core (e.g., S1+S4+S12 transport governance; S3+S7+S2 production quality/batch caps; S9+S8 Critical triage) with explicit non-goals.
