## Verdict
A strong, professional digital-twin design that hits essentially every required section and most hard constraints. It is clearly above a competent baseline. Under a hypercritical standard it is **not** near-flawless: there are real PM/timing subtleties missed, PM4Py API fragility, and a prose–code gap on the mechanisms the text itself treats as central.

## What is genuinely strong
- **Scope / state**: Correctly rejects independent-case DES. Entities (`ArchitectCluster`, `InspectionBundle`, `NoticeCohort`), AND-join before decision drafting, external timers vs municipal queues, calendars, and second-order bottleneck shift are the right level of ambition.
- **Missing data honesty**: Objections as latent proxies from notice duration; start/complete split not collapsed; performance DFG not treated as service time; flattened log understating dependence—all handled explicitly.
- **Non-stationarity & dependence**: Seasonal/weekday NHPP + architect bursts, district batching trade-off, correlated objection shocks, council/holiday calendars—aligned with the brief.
- **Experiments**: Full D1–D10, sensible combined packages, three stress scenarios, factor ranges, and trade-offs. Avoids false optimality; Pareto/robustness language is appropriate.
- **Uncertainty / validation / decisions**: Warm-up under seasonality, CRN, percentile bootstrap, heavy tails, OOS/rolling checks, fairness (district/architect), utilization guardrails—methodologically solid.
- **Skeleton intent**: pandas load (no fake `pm4py.read_csv`), `format_dataframe`, tree parse guarded, PM4Py discovery separated from DES placeholders—directionally correct.

## Critical deductions (why not 9+)

### 1. Process-mining timing logic is incomplete (substantive)
Parallel block is `+(Planning, Fire?, Env?)`. Waiting as `start  previous sequential event` (and the code’s simple `groupby.shift`) is **wrong under AND semantics**. Decision-drafting wait should be driven by **max completion of applicable prerequisites**, not the prior row in the flattened trace. The prose occasionally says “previous prerequisite” (singular) while §1 correctly requires an AND-join—internal inconsistency. For a PM-grounded twin this is not a nit.

### 2. PM4Py conventions / code robustness
- `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is not the stable public convention (`pm4py.get_variants` is); unlike the tree parse, this path is not hardened—`main()` can die on a standard install.
- `get_case_arrival_average` unit labeled as hours without verification (API units vary)—silent scale bug risk.
- Variant count handling is defensive soup, signaling API uncertainty rather than mastery.

### 3. Prose–code gap on mechanisms the answer insists matter
Text: Monday clerk portal diversion, legal council-week blocks, external missing-doc timers, inspection bundles, objection shocks, rework loops, AND-join.
Code: `ResourceCalendar`/`build_resource_pools` omit Monday capacity carve-out and council blocks; `RoutingModel` is a linear happy-path list (no missing-doc loop, no rework, weak legal/public-notice logic); `DependencyManager` is two key tuples; `run()` is a stub with no FEL/event types for external timers or joins. For a *skeleton* placeholders are allowed, but the gap is large relative to the claims of modularity and fidelity.

### 4. Identifiability under-sold
Layered calibration is good, but with widespread missing starts, service vs wait vs calendar idle is **poorly identified**. The plan underplays structural non-identifiability and the risk of “calibrating” to end-to-end targets with compensatory wrong internals (dangerous when comparing D3/D4/D6).

### 5. Smaller design / clarity issues
- D5 (“parallelize legal pre-check”) is reinterpreted as early triage/WIP-heavy pre-check—plausible policy reading, not a crisp operationalization of the named intervention.
- Decision thresholds (10% median, 15% p90, 70–85% util, 5-day architect fairness, etc.) are numerous and sharp relative to stated uncertainty; fine as provisional, still over-precise.
- `ArchitectCluster` / notice cohorts are first-class in §1 then largely disappear from estimation formulas and code paths.
- Process-tree string is treated carefully as prior (good), but redo labels like `'Completeness rework'` are awkward relative to standard `*` semantics—acknowledged only lightly.
- Experiment text promises crossing stresses with “best three” policies; matrix does not fully specify that combinatorial frame (analysis burden, multiplicity).

### 6. What was *not* wrong (credit)
Does not claim calibrated optimality; does not invent observed objections; does not ignore calendars or cross-case effects; does not use nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv`; structure matches the required eight sections.
