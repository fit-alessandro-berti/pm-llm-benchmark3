## Critical failures

### 1. DECLARE `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is unsound (major)

`precedence(A, B)` means: **if B occurs, then A occurred earlier**.  
Encoding R5 this way forces **every** activation to be preceded by a legal opinion, including ordinary low-risk cases (T1) and high-risk cases with no escalation (T2).

Under a real DECLARE checker:

| Case | Effect |
|------|--------|
| T1 | **fail** (Activate without prior opinion) |
| T2 | **fail** (same) |
| T4 | **fail** (same) |
| T5 | **fail** (same) |

The matrix marks T1/T2/T4/T5 DECLARE as **pass**. That is inconsistent with the model as written. R5 is **conditional on Sanctions Escalation**; a global precedence is not a conservative approximation of that condition—it changes the policy for the whole process.

Same class of error: `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` **and** `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` together require **both** approvals before every activation. That contradicts R2 (“exactly one”) and the procedural XOR. T1 would need Compliance Approval; T2 would need Manager Approval. Again the matrix does not reflect the model.

### 2. Test matrix does not match the stated models (major)

Beyond the above:

- **T7 temporal:** Escalation@6 → Opinion@40 = **34 h**. Profile mean 12 h, σ = 6 h, ζ = 2 → band **[0, 24] h**. 34 h is a correct **warning**. Good.
- **T7 Activate→Payment:** 45 − 43 = **2 h** vs mean 72, σ = 36 → lower bound \(72 - 2\cdot36 = 0\). 2 h is inside the band if the checker uses a floor at 0; some implementations still flag extreme early completions. - **T5 procedural = pass** is defensible only because clarification was excluded from the tree; that design choice is fine, but then overall “DECLARE fail only” needs a clear “earliest decisive violation” story tied to R3—thinly done.
- **T4:** Resource fail is right; claiming DECLARE/skeleton **pass** is fine for R9, but not after the broken global precedence rules.

A matrix that disagrees with its own DECLARE dictionary is a first-order defect on this task.
