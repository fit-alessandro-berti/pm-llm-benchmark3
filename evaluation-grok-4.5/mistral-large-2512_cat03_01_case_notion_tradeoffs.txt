**1. Analytical assumptions – fundamental definitional error**  
Convergence is defined as “artificial merging of distinct business processes into a single trace.” In standard OCEL flattening an event related to *k* objects of the lead type is simply *copied* into *k* separate traces; traces are never merged. The supplied definition inverts the established meaning (and the very phenomenon the later matrix and distortion sections try to discuss). Divergence is only marginally better. Because every subsequent risk rating and distortion claim rests on these terms, the error propagates.

**2. Object-type comparison matrix – systematically wrong risk ratings**  
- Order convergence is labelled “Low” even though the E2O column already shows multi-order events e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27. That is textbook high convergence / duplication.  
- Delivery convergence is labelled “High” even though no event is related to more than one Delivery object; the high rating confuses multi-object participation *inside* a delivery with multi-delivery participation of an event.  
- Invoice duplication is labelled “Medium” and cites e19/e22/e25; each of those events touches only a single Invoice.  
- Several “what is lost / preserved” cells and suitability scores are therefore built on false premises. Qualitative adjectives are used freely but are only loosely tethered to concrete identifiers.

Eight items are supplied and the mandatory coverage categories are ticked, yet the causal stories are repeatedly unsound:

- Several “hiding” claims are true as information-loss statements but are then over-interpreted as DFG-edge or performance distortions without showing the actual edges that would appear.
