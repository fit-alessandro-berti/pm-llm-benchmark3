## Evaluation

The answer is strong on the dimensions the rubric cares about most: it keeps A/B firmly nonconformant and low-ranked, refuses to let deadline EV “buy” a control waiver, prefers a conformant second-officer path, builds a real parallel composite (alternate screen ∥ wait for S4) with an explicit C fallback, and internalizes P-901 spillover. Structure matches the six required sections and uses the mandated conformance labels. That is clearly above a mediocre compliance-aware ranking.

It is not near-flawless under a hypercritical bar. Material issues:

### Process / model errors
- In the E continuation it blurs Rule 6 (≥70 or unresolved match → second officer) with Auto Clear (no alert and risk **&lt;40**). Saying that risk &lt;70 and a resolved match “could” open Auto Clear is wrong; 40–69 with a clear match is not the auto-clear path. That is a direct normative-process mistake.
- Treating F’s fast reject as partly **beneficial because it “avoids deadline breach” / is “within SLA”** misreads the economic object. The €20k–€35k figure is value of **execution** by the deadline, not value of any terminal decision. Rejection forfeits that upside and adds customer harm; framing it as deadline success is a conceptual error in the benefit column and weakens Section 3/4 consistency.

### Probability and quantification sloppiness
- Non-approval mass from 0.55–0.75 is dumped into “Reject (0.25–0.45)” while Hold/SAR is a separate normative branch—double-use of the complement.
- Conditional breach bands for D* (~0.35–0.70) and similar invented conditionals are presented with more precision than the prompt supports; they read like calibrated estimates rather than illustrative ranges.
- Equating P-901’s interruption loss to the same €20k–€35k band as P-884 is an assumption, not given; useful as a sensitivity sketch, overstated as system-level netting.

### Ranking / decision-quality nits
- E as global rank 3 is weakly motivated once C/D* already cover mandatory review: more evidence after a fresh validation cycle is almost pure deadline abandonment. As a residual ranking among {E,F,G,B,A} it is defensible; as “third-best next action” it overstates E’s attractiveness without saying it is dominated whenever a second officer is obtainable.
- Minor presentation defects (e.g. “R17S2” instead of a clear four-eyes inequality, mushy benefit cells mixing money and “clarity”) add friction but are smaller.

### What remains genuinely good
- Lexical priority of mandatory controls over observational forecasts; explicit anti-transport warning for A/B.
- D* parallelization, API-quota note, S4 35 vs 65 sensitivity, and t+30 checkpoint → C are the right operational shape.
- Section 4’s evidence bar for F and G is appropriately strict; Rule 10/11 handling is sound.
- Primary + fallback + rerank triggers in Section 6 are actionable and conformance-safe.

### Bottom line
A capable expert answer that would score highly on a lenient rubric. Under the instructed strictness—process fidelity, clean benefit definitions, and disciplined uncertainty—the auto-clear threshold error, the rejection-as-deadline-win slip, and the sloppy probability accounting block anything in the 8.5–10 band. **7.3** reflects “strong core, several real flaws,” not “nearly flawless.”
