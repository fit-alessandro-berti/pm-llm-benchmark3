## Deductions (hypercritical)

**1. Material data-consistency issue under-played (largest cut)**  
Cumulative arrivals  completions = +110 over 28 days while late-period `mean_wip` falls into the high teens/low thirties. Under any closed population with comparable WIP definition this is a serious accounting gap (uncounted exits, scope mismatch, snapshot bias, or start-of-horizon backlog not reconciling). The answer notes the tension once, then continues to lean heavily on net AC as “pressure” and on regime backlog narratives. A top-tier answer would bound how much net-count evidence can support, or mark several pressure claims as provisional pending start/end WIP. This is not a quibble; it directly affects estimands the task asks for.

**2. Mean vs median slippage**  
Regime tables often report “Median WIP” while Little’s Law rows switch to arithmetic mean of daily `mean_wip` (correct for L, but the switch is not always signposted). Pooled service “32.5” and other summaries mix median-of-medians with means without a consistent rule. Minor, but repeated.

**3. Over-precise reporting on tiny n**  
Post-intervention weekend and high-complex weekend are n=2; several intensities are still given to two decimals with only soft confidence labels. Ranges help, but the presentation still overstates precision relative to sample and serial dependence.

**4. Section 4 weekend WIP/FTE contrast is partly confounded**  
“Weekends 6.61 vs weekdays 5.66 WIP/FTE” pools low-complex and high-complex weekends/weekdays. The qualitative point survives, but the quantitative contrast is not a clean composition effect; the primary segmentation already showed the cleaner comparison. Slightly sloppy relative to the rest of the paper.

**5. Representative hourly vectors vs `peak_08_10_pct`**  
The 08–10 share mismatch (e.g. 04-07 ~17% from the vector vs 31% in the daily table) is correctly flagged, then largely set aside. Those vectors are labeled “representative”/“candidate”; treating their Fano/CV as if they were the named calendar days’ exact profiles, without downgrading confidence, is a mild over-read. The code’s `stated_08_10_share` highlights the issue but the prose does not fully integrate the reliability implication.

**6. Alternative segmentation thinner than promised “serious” alternative**  
The state-pressure alternative is conceptually good but weakly quantified (no parallel intensity/WIP/capacity columns). The case-mix-first sketch is only a paragraph. Acceptable, not excellent.

**7. Minor internal / presentational nits**  
- Per-FTE and open-hour rates sometimes appear as if staff_fte were constant within day (acknowledged in threats, still used pointwise on outage/intervention days).  
- “Four weeks” and calendar cuts are fine; a few regime boundary phrases (“to 2026-04-28, weekdays only” vs weekend rows) force the reader to re-parse.
