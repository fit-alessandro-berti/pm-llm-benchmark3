## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Scenario A: Smooth path**

- Chemistry waiting and service: I split by queue discipline. In the priority regime, B-417 likely starts Friday around 16:45-17:30, gets only 0.5-1.3 h done before the 18:00 close, and resumes Monday 08:00 with roughly 0.2-4.0 h left; that gives a usable chemistry result around Monday 08:15-12:00. In the FIFO regime, B-417 more likely waits behind the visible backlog and finishes Monday later, roughly late morning to mid-afternoon. I therefore carry Monday 09:00-15:00 as the smooth chemistry-result band.
- Microbiology incubation and readout: Primary incubation becomes technically ready 46-54 h after Friday 14:00, i.e. Sunday 12:00-20:00. If weekend on-call authorization is granted, readout can complete Sunday roughly 13:00-23:00. Without authorization, the result sits until Monday 08:00 staffing and is recorded about Monday 08:30-09:30.
- Parallel join time: I use `T_join = max(T_chem, T_micro)`, not a sum. On the smooth path the join is usually Monday 09:00-15:00. Microbiology governs when chemistry got favorable priority and no weekend callout occurred; chemistry governs under stricter FIFO or when microbiology was already read Sunday.
- QA waiting and service: Saturday QA time matters only indirectly by draining backlog. By the Monday join I expect the original queue to be partly reduced but not gone with certainty, so I use about 0.5-4 h QA waiting plus roughly 1-3.5 h of review. If review starts late enough on Monday, any unfinished QA work resumes Tuesday 08:00.
- Optional rework/investigation: None in this scenario.
- Final release or rejection: Electronic Release is continuous and adds about 0.25-0.75 h after QA acceptance. That gives a smooth terminal window centered on Monday afternoon/evening, with a Tuesday-morning spillover if QA starts late.

**Scenario B: Chemistry rerun**

- Chemistry waiting and service: The primary chemistry branch is as above, but a borderline result triggers a rerun with about 10-20% baseline probability, higher if the excursion is later confirmed. The rerun has priority and takes about 1.0-3.0 h of working lab time.
- Microbiology incubation and readout: Usually the microbiology branch is already usable by the time the rerun finishes, especially if the microbiology result is read Monday morning or earlier on Sunday.
- Parallel join time: Here the join is still `max(T_chem, T_micro)`, but the rerun usually makes chemistry the maximum. If the primary chemistry result is available by late morning Monday, the rerun can still finish Monday afternoon. If the primary result lands too late Monday and the rerun is long, the rerun pauses at 18:00 and resumes Tuesday 08:00.
- QA waiting and service: If chemistry finishes Monday afternoon, QA may still complete Monday evening on a light queue; otherwise QA moves to Tuesday.
- Optional rework/investigation: The rerun itself increases the chance of a QA deviation investigation, so this scenario overlaps with the QA-tail scenario.
- Final release or rejection: Most outcomes are still release, but the clock shifts right to late Monday or Tuesday, with Wednesday spill if the rerun crosses a closure boundary or triggers QA rework.

**Scenario C: Delayed microbiology readout or confirmation**

- Chemistry waiting and service: Chemistry usually resolves on Monday and is no longer the long pole unless FIFO is very unfavorable and microbiology has no deep issue.
- Microbiology incubation and readout: The mild form of this scenario is simple weekend staffing delay: technically ready on Sunday, but not read until Monday 08:30-09:30. The heavier form is a contamination indication at primary readout. Then another 20-30 h of continuous incubation is required, followed by another 0.5-1.5 h readout. If the indication is discovered Monday morning, the confirmation result is typically Tuesday 08:30-17:00, with a Wednesday-morning spill if technical readiness lands after staffed hours.
- Parallel join time: Again I use the maximum, not a sum. In the confirmation branch microbiology clearly governs the join because chemistry is usually already done by Monday.
- QA waiting and service: With a Tuesday join, QA starts Tuesday unless the queue is exceptionally heavy or the join occurs very late; the service itself is still usually only hours, not days.
- Optional rework/investigation: A microbiology anomaly materially increases deviation-investigation probability, so this scenario often feeds into a QA tail even when chemistry is clean.
- Final release or rejection: A plain Monday micro readout can still end in Monday evening release. The confirmation branch more often ends Tuesday evening to Wednesday, with Thursday spill if QA rework is then opened.

**Scenario D: QA query or investigation tail**

- Chemistry waiting and service: Usually already complete by the time this scenario becomes decisive.
- Microbiology incubation and readout: Usually already complete by the time this scenario becomes decisive.
- Parallel join time: The join remains `max(T_chem, T_micro)`, but after the join the process is no longer branch-dominated; QA becomes the clock.
- QA waiting and service: I still allow 0.5-4 h of queueing before first review, but the real delay here is rework. A Documentation Query adds about 4-12 working hours before QA can resume, so a late-Monday query often means Tuesday or Wednesday resumption. A documentation-only investigation adds about 8-20 working hours, i.e. roughly 1-3 working days once pauses are respected. A laboratory investigation adds about 3-7 calendar days.
- Optional rework/investigation: This is the core of the scenario. If both branches are normal, investigation risk is only about 4-10%; if there was a rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed excursion, the investigation probability jumps to about 30-55%.
- Final release or rejection: Most documentation-query and documentation-only investigation cases still end in release. Batch rejection becomes materially more plausible only in the deeper investigation/critical-defect tail.

**Scenario E: Temperature excursion confirmed (correlated-stress regime)**

- Chemistry waiting and service: The main effect is not queueing but path coupling: a confirmed excursion makes a borderline chemistry result more likely, so the rerun branch becomes fatter.
- Microbiology incubation and readout: The same confirmed excursion also raises contamination-indication risk, so the confirmation branch becomes fatter.
- Parallel join time: I do not multiply baseline anomaly probabilities as if the branches were independent. Instead, the confirmed excursion makes a “double trouble” path more plausible, where chemistry rerun and microbiology confirmation both occur, and the join is the later of those two repaired branches.
- QA waiting and service: QA also becomes more likely to open a deviation investigation in this regime, so the post-join delay thickens as well.
- Optional rework/investigation: This regime is exactly why the severe tail is wider than a naive independent model would imply.
- Final release or rejection: Release still may occur, but this is where the rejection tail becomes meaningfully thicker. A practical clock consequence is a shift from a Monday/Tuesday forecast toward Wednesday-Friday, with next-week outcomes no longer remote.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. Temperature-excursion confirmation: mainly observation uncertainty that creates dependence uncertainty. It can simultaneously raise chemistry rerun risk, microbiology contamination risk, and QA investigation risk.
2. Weekend microbiology readout availability: mainly calendar uncertainty. Sunday technical readiness is fairly predictable; whether a human reads it on Sunday versus Monday is not.
3. Chemistry queue discipline and actual service-start position: mainly queue uncertainty. The FIFO versus risk-priority split changes chemistry completion by several hours and can flip the governing branch at the join.
4. QA queue at the future join time: mainly queue uncertainty. The current five-batch queue is real, but the Monday state depends on Friday/Saturday completions plus overdispersed arrivals.
5. QA rework branch after first review: mainly path uncertainty. Documentation Query, documentation-only investigation, and laboratory investigation have very different time scales, from several working hours to nearly a week.
