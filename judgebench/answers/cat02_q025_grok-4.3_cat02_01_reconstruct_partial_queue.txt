# 1. Assumptions and notation
A1. All timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 min; an observed time t represents an actual instant in [t2.5 min, t+2.5 min].  
A2. Event order respects the supplied columns: a case enters the S2 queue at the instant S1 ends.  
A3. Resources are single-task; apparent overlaps are artifacts of rounding or logging delay and are resolved by earliest feasible start.  
A4. MISSING S2 start is bounded by max(S1_end, previous completion of the assigned S2 resource) and min(S2_end, next start of the same resource).  
A5. OPEN activities are right-censored at 14:00; elapsed service is recorded but final service time remains unknown.  
A6. C2 outage [10:55–11:10] is treated as unavailable; no case may start on C2 inside that window.  
A7. Simultaneous events at the same rounded minute are ordered S1 completion  S2 start  S2 completion within that minute for WIP counting.  
A8. Blank S2 fields for completed S1 indicate the case is still waiting at 14:00.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas
S1_wait = S1_start  arrival_S1 (observed or bounded).  
S1_service = S1_end  S1_start (observed or bounded for MISSING).  
S2_wait = S2_start  S1_end (observed or bounded).  
S2_service = S2_end  S2_start (observed, bounded for MISSING, or censored for OPEN).  
Station sojourn = wait + service.  
Case total sojourn (completed cases) = S2_end  arrival_S1.  
Arrival rate  and departure rate  computed per 30-min bin from observed starts and ends.  
WIP(t) = number of cases with arrival_S1  t and (S2_end > t or OPEN).  
Utilization = busy minutes / available minutes; bounds obtained by varying imputation extremes.  
Little’s Law L = W applied only on fully observed 30-min intervals with no censoring.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation
Before 10:55 S2 shows higher utilization and longer queues; after the C2 outage S1 utilization rises sharply while S2 utilization drops. Evidence (completed services, observed waits) indicates S2 is the primary bottleneck until 11:10; thereafter S1 becomes the tighter constraint. The maintenance interval visibly shifts the bottleneck from S2 to S1. Hypothesis: the 15-min outage plus subsequent queue build-up at S1 explains the afternoon backlog; this remains stable under all imputation scenarios.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis
Optimistic: MISSING starts placed at latest feasible instant  S2 waits shortened by 5–10 min, S2 utilization 59 %.  
Central: mid-point of feasible interval  values shown in Section 4.  
Pessimistic: earliest feasible start  S2 waits lengthened by 5–10 min, S2 utilization 66 %.  
Stable conclusions: S1–S2 bottleneck switch after 11:10, S1 peak WIP 7, total completed cases = 17.  
Unstable: exact S2 mean service time varies ±8 min across imputations; Little’s Law interval estimates change by up to 12 %.

# Parse status-aware timestamps (MISSING/OPEN/blank kept distinct)
for col in ['arrival_s1','s1_start','s1_end','s2_start','s2_end']:

                       'org:resource': r.s1_resource})

        events.append({'case:concept:name': r.case, 'concept:name': 'S2',

                       'org:resource': r.s2_resource})
event_df = pd.DataFrame(events)

                                  activity_key='concept:name',
                                  start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp',
                                  timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

# Case-level semantics valid only on fully observed COMPLETE cases
