# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

1. **Low-Risk vs. High-Risk Routing**: The selection between low-risk and high-risk approval pathways is represented as an exclusive choice ($\text{XOR}$). In a low-risk case, the pathway executes `Supervisor Review`. In a high-risk case, the pathway executes both `Quality Review` and `Regulatory Review` concurrently in parallel ($\text{AND}$ / partial order), permitting either completion order. Because risk classification is a data attribute not recorded in the event stream, the model represents this choice non-deterministically through control-flow branching.
2. **Optional Supplier Consultation**: The optionality of `Consult Supplier` is represented as an exclusive choice ($\text{XOR}$) between the visible activity `Consult Supplier` and a silent transition ($\tau$ / skip). This allows cases where supplier consultation is not required to advance to release synchronization without generating visible log events, while restricting execution to at most one consultation per case.
3. **Abstraction of Passing vs. Failing Trial Inspections**: Because trial inspection outcomes ("pass" vs. "fail") are not distinct event labels, the trial cycle is modeled using repeat-until loop semantics: $\text{LOOP}(\text{SEQ}(\text{Execute Trial}, \text{Inspect Trial}), \text{Adjust Parameters})$. The primary loop body $\text{SEQ}(\text{Execute Trial}, \text{Inspect Trial})$ executes first. A passing inspection corresponds to the loop exit transition (proceeding directly to downstream synchronization at `Release Material`), whereas a failing inspection corresponds to the redo transition `Adjust Parameters`, which enforces a subsequent $\text{Execute Trial} \to \text{Inspect Trial}$ cycle. This guarantees that `Adjust Parameters` occurs strictly after a failed inspection and never after the final, passing inspection.
4. **Duplicate Visible Labels Policy**: Visible label duplication is strictly avoided across both formalisms. Maintaining unique visible activity labels ensures model identifiability, prevents state-space and alignment ambiguity during process discovery and conformance checking, and preserves clear analytical mapping back to the operational workflow.
5. **Process-Tree Approximation Policy**: A **conservative under-approximation** policy is adopted for the Process Tree. Due to the strict block-structured nature of process trees, cross-branch dependencies cannot be expressed directly without duplicating labels or under-/over-approximating behavior. We enforce a two-phase hierarchy: an initial parallel block $+(\text{Contain Material}, \text{Analyze Root Cause})$ followed by a main concurrent processing block. This under-approximation enforces all mandatory precedences (preventing illegal early execution) at the expense of disallowing certain valid interleavings (e.g., delaying `Plan Verification` until `Analyze Root Cause` completes).
6. **Interpretation of "May Overlap"**: "May overlap" is interpreted as **true interleaving concurrency** (absence of precedence constraints). In the partial-order and Petri net semantics, concurrent nodes are unconstrained with respect to each other and may execute in any relative order or simultaneously; in sequential trace logs, their completion events may interleave arbitrarily.

# 2. Intended Dependency Specification

| ID | Source activity or submodel | Target activity or submodel | Relation | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DEP-01** | `Register Deviation` | `Contain Material`, `Analyze Root Cause` | Precedence (1-to-N Fork) | Every case starts with deviation registration, which enables immediate containment and root-cause analysis. |
| **DEP-02** | `Contain Material` | `Analyze Root Cause` | Concurrency ($\parallel$) | Containment and root-cause analysis proceed independently without mutual synchronization. |
| **DEP-03** | `Contain Material` | `Plan Verification` | Precedence ($\to$) | Verification planning may begin only after physical material containment is complete. |
| **DEP-04** | `Analyze Root Cause` | `Design Corrective Action` | Precedence ($\to$) | Designing corrective actions requires the root-cause analysis findings. |
| **DEP-05** | `Analyze Root Cause` | Approval Route Selection | Precedence ($\to$) | Risk assessment and routing to the required approval bodies occur after root-cause analysis. |
| **DEP-06** | Approval Route Selection | `Supervisor Review` **XOR** (`Quality Review` $\parallel$ `Regulatory Review`) | Exclusive Choice ($\text{XOR}$) | Low-risk cases route to supervisor review; high-risk cases require both quality and regulatory reviews. |
| **DEP-07** | `Quality Review` | `Regulatory Review` | Concurrency ($\parallel$) | In high-risk cases, quality and regulatory reviews may occur concurrently in either order. |
| **DEP-08** | `Analyze Root Cause` | Supplier Consultation Selection | Precedence ($\to$) | Engaging an external supplier is determined only after root-cause analysis is established. |
| **DEP-09** | Supplier Consultation Selection | `Consult Supplier` **XOR** $\tau$ | Optionality ($\text{XOR}$) | Supplier consultation is optional (occurs at most once per case) depending on case context. |
| **DEP-10** | `Plan Verification` | `Design Corrective Action` | Concurrency ($\parallel$) | Verification planning and corrective action design may proceed in parallel once their respective prerequisites finish. |
| **DEP-11** | `Plan Verification`, `Design Corrective Action` | Trial Subprocess (`Execute Trial`) | Synchronization (2-to-1 Join) | Trial execution requires both verification planning and corrective action design to be finished. |
| **DEP-12** | Approval Route | Trial Subprocess | Concurrency ($\parallel$) | Approvals and trial execution proceed concurrently; approvals do not gate trial execution. |
| **DEP-13** | `Consult Supplier` | Trial Subprocess / Approvals | Concurrency ($\parallel$) | Supplier consultation may overlap trial execution, trial adjustments, and approval reviews. |
| **DEP-14** | `Execute Trial` | `Inspect Trial` | Precedence ($\to$) | Trial inspection strictly follows trial execution within each trial iteration. |
| **DEP-15** | `Inspect Trial` (Fail) | `Adjust Parameters` $\to$ `Execute Trial` | Loop Redo ($\text{LOOP}$) | A failed inspection requires parameter adjustment and triggers a new trial execution cycle. |
| **DEP-16** | `Inspect Trial` (Pass) | Exit Trial Subprocess | Loop Exit | A passing inspection completes the trial loop; no further adjustments or trials are performed. |
| **DEP-17** | Trial Subprocess, Approval Route, Supplier Route | `Release Material` | Synchronization (M-to-1 Join) | Material release strictly requires passing inspection, completed approvals, and supplier consultation (if chosen). |
| **DEP-18** | `Release Material` | `Archive Case` | Precedence ($\to$) | Archiving the case is the final milestone, occurring strictly after material release. |

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

### Process-Tree Expression

```
->(
    'Register Deviation',
    +( 'Contain Material', 'Analyze Root Cause' ),
    +(
        X( 'Supervisor Review', +( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ),
        X( 'Consult Supplier', tau ),
        ->(
            +( 'Plan Verification', 'Design Corrective Action' ),
            *( ->( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' )
        )
    ),
    'Release Material',
    'Archive Case'
)
```

### Mapping to the Process Narrative
- `Register Deviation` is sequenced first.
- `+( 'Contain Material', 'Analyze Root Cause' )` represents the initial parallel investigation phase.
- The subsequent `+` operator executes three parallel streams:
  1. Approval route: `X( 'Supervisor Review', +( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) )`.
  2. Optional supplier consultation: `X( 'Consult Supplier', tau )`.
  3. Trial preparation and trial loop: `->( +( 'Plan Verification', 'Design Corrective Action' ), *( ->( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ) )`.
- `Release Material` synchronizes all three concurrent streams before concluding with `Archive Case`.

### Self-Contained Python Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# Root sequence: Register Deviation -> Initial Phase -> Main Processing Phase -> Release Material -> Archive Case
root = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)

# Start activity
reg = ProcessTree(label="Register Deviation", parent=root)

# Phase 1: Parallel Contain Material and Analyze Root Cause
init_par = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=root)
contain = ProcessTree(label="Contain Material", parent=init_par)
analyze = ProcessTree(label="Analyze Root Cause", parent=init_par)
init_par.children = [contain, analyze]

# Phase 2: Concurrent Processing Block (Approvals, Supplier Consultation, and Trial Stream)
work_par = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=root)

# 1. Approval Branch: XOR between Low-Risk (Supervisor) and High-Risk (Quality || Regulatory)
appr_xor = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, parent=work_par)
sup_rev = ProcessTree(label="Supervisor Review", parent=appr_xor)

high_risk_par = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=appr_xor)
qual_rev = ProcessTree(label="Quality Review", parent=high_risk_par)
reg_rev = ProcessTree(label="Regulatory Review", parent=high_risk_par)
high_risk_par.children = [qual_rev, reg_rev]

appr_xor.children = [sup_rev, high_risk_par]

# 2. Optional Supplier Consultation: XOR between Consult Supplier and Silent Transition (tau)
supp_xor = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, parent=work_par)
supp_act = ProcessTree(label="Consult Supplier", parent=supp_xor)
supp_tau = ProcessTree(label=None, parent=supp_xor)  # Silent transition tau
supp_xor.children = [supp_act, supp_tau]

# 3. Trial Preparation and Trial Execution Loop
trial_stream = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=work_par)

prep_par = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=trial_stream)
plan_ver = ProcessTree(label="Plan Verification", parent=prep_par)
des_act = ProcessTree(label="Design Corrective Action", parent=prep_par)
prep_par.children = [plan_ver, des_act]

trial_loop = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.LOOP, parent=trial_stream)
trial_cycle = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=trial_loop)
exec_tr = ProcessTree(label="Execute Trial", parent=trial_cycle)
insp_tr = ProcessTree(label="Inspect Trial", parent=trial_cycle)
trial_cycle.children = [exec_tr, insp_tr]
adj_param = ProcessTree(label="Adjust Parameters", parent=trial_loop)
trial_loop.children = [trial_cycle, adj_param]

trial_stream.children = [prep_par, trial_loop]

# Assemble Phase 2 children
work_par.children = [appr_xor, supp_xor, trial_stream]

# Concluding milestones
release = ProcessTree(label="Release Material", parent=root)
archive = ProcessTree(label="Archive Case", parent=root)

# Assemble Root children
root.children = [reg, init_par, work_par, release, archive]

# Assign final process tree model to variable 'tree'
tree = root
```

### Representational Loss and Approximation Analysis
Process trees enforce strict block structuring where every parallel fork $+$ must join symmetrically before the next operator can fire. In the intended process, `Plan Verification` depends solely on `Contain Material`, while `Design Corrective Action`, approval routing, and supplier consultation depend solely on `Analyze Root Cause`. Because process trees cannot represent asymmetric cross-branch dependencies without label duplication, this tree model imposes an **artificial synchronization barrier** after `init_par`. As a consequence, `Plan Verification` is over-constrained and prevented from starting until `Analyze Root Cause` completes, even if material containment was finished immediately. Similarly, `Design Corrective Action`, `Approvals`, and `Consult Supplier` are prevented from starting until `Contain Material` completes. This conservative under-approximation guarantees safety (preventing illegal execution before prerequisites are satisfied) at the cost of disallowing valid concurrent interleavings.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

### Compact POWL Description

```
PO=(
  nodes={
    Register Deviation,
    Contain Material,
    Analyze Root Cause,
    Plan Verification,
    Design Corrective Action,
    X( 'Supervisor Review', PO=(nodes={Quality Review, Regulatory Review}, order={}) ),
    X( 'Consult Supplier', tau ),
    *( PO=(nodes={Execute Trial, Inspect Trial}, order={Execute Trial --> Inspect Trial}), 'Adjust Parameters' ),
    Release Material,
    Archive Case
  },
  order={
    Register Deviation --> Contain Material,
    Register Deviation --> Analyze Root Cause,
    Contain Material --> Plan Verification,
    Analyze Root Cause --> Design Corrective Action,
    Analyze Root Cause --> Approvals_XOR,
    Analyze Root Cause --> Supplier_XOR,
    Plan Verification --> Trial_Loop,
    Design Corrective Action --> Trial_Loop,
    Trial_Loop --> Release Material,
    Approvals_XOR --> Release Material,
    Supplier_XOR --> Release Material,
    Release Material --> Archive Case
  }
)
```

### Self-Contained Python Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# 1. Base Activity Transitions
t_reg = Transition(label="Register Deviation")
t_contain = Transition(label="Contain Material")
t_root = Transition(label="Analyze Root Cause")
t_plan = Transition(label="Plan Verification")
t_design = Transition(label="Design Corrective Action")
t_sup = Transition(label="Supervisor Review")
t_qual = Transition(label="Quality Review")
t_reg_rev = Transition(label="Regulatory Review")
t_supp = Transition(label="Consult Supplier")
t_exec = Transition(label="Execute Trial")
t_insp = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
t_adj = Transition(label="Adjust Parameters")
t_rel = Transition(label="Release Material")
t_arch = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# 2. High-Risk Approval Partial Order (Quality Review || Regulatory Review)
high_risk_po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_qual, t_reg_rev])
# No edge between t_qual and t_reg_rev: they are concurrent and may complete in either order

# 3. Approval Selection: XOR(Supervisor Review, High-Risk PO)
approvals = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_sup, high_risk_po])

# 4. Optional Supplier Consultation: XOR(Consult Supplier, tau)
supplier_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_supp, SilentTransition()])

# 5. Trial Subprocess Loop: Loop(Sequence(Execute Trial -> Inspect Trial), Adjust Parameters)
trial_cycle = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_exec, t_insp])
trial_cycle.order.add_edge(t_exec, t_insp)
trial_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[trial_cycle, t_adj])

# 6. Top-Level StrictPartialOrder Workflow
powl_nodes = [
    t_reg,
    t_contain,
    t_root,
    t_plan,
    t_design,
    approvals,
    supplier_opt,
    trial_loop,
    t_rel,
    t_arch,
]

powl_model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=powl_nodes)

# Initial fork from Register Deviation
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_reg, t_contain)
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_reg, t_root)

# Asymmetric independent branches
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_contain, t_plan)
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_root, t_design)
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_root, approvals)
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_root, supplier_opt)

# Synchronization gating trial execution
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_plan, trial_loop)
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_design, trial_loop)

# Terminal synchronization before Release Material
powl_model.order.add_edge(trial_loop, t_rel)
powl_model.order.add_edge(approvals, t_rel)
powl_model.order.add_edge(supplier_opt, t_rel)

# Final case archival
powl_model.order.add_edge(t_rel, t_arch)

# Assign final POWL model to variable 'powl'
powl = powl_model
```

### Explanation of Intentionally Unconnected Node Pairs
1. `Contain Material` and `Analyze Root Cause`: Both depend on `Register Deviation` and proceed concurrently without cross-synchronization.
2. `Plan Verification` and `Analyze Root Cause` / `Design Corrective Action`: `Plan Verification` depends strictly on `Contain Material`. Leaving it unconnected to `Analyze Root Cause` allows verification planning to start and finish while root-cause analysis is still ongoing.
3. `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action`: These represent independent planning streams that execute concurrently once their respective prerequisites finish.
4. `Quality Review` and `Regulatory Review` (inside `high_risk_po`): Both approvals are required for high-risk cases but have no mutual precedence constraint, allowing them to proceed and complete in any order.
5. `approvals` and `trial_loop`: Approvals depend on `Analyze Root Cause` and synchronize at `Release Material`, leaving them concurrent with `trial_loop` so that approvals may overlap trials.
6. `supplier_opt` and `trial_loop` / `approvals`: Optional supplier consultation depends on `Analyze Root Cause` and synchronizes at `Release Material`, allowing it to overlap design, planning, approvals, and trial cycles.

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

### Preferred Representation: POWL
The **POWL (Partially Ordered Workflow Language)** model is the preferred canonical representation. 
- **Defensibility**: POWL natively combines partial orders (which handle asymmetric, non-block-structured synchronizations such as $\text{Contain} \to \text{Plan} \to \text{Trial}$ alongside $\text{Analyze} \to \text{Design} \to \text{Trial}$) with structured control-flow operators ($X$ for exclusive choices and $*$ for loops).
- **Exactness vs. Compromise**: Unlike the Process Tree, which is forced into an under-approximation that artificially synchronizes `Contain Material` and `Analyze Root Cause`, POWL represents the exact intended causal dependencies without requiring duplicate visible activity labels.

### Limitations of the Validation Plan
1. **Control-Flow Isolation (Data Blindness)**: The validation plan evaluates pure control-flow alignments. It does not validate case data attributes (e.g., verifying that a case flagged as `risk_level == 'HIGH'` in a data payload actually followed the `Quality Review` + `Regulatory Review` branch).
2. **Playout Finite Sampling**: Stochastic playout samples a finite subset of traces ($N=1000$). For models with unbounded loops and wide concurrency, playout cannot guarantee full state-space or trace-language exhaustion.
3. **Absence of Real-Time Duration Semantics**: The alignment and replay tests treat activity completion events as instantaneous atomic points, verifying partial ordering but leaving overlapping start-to-complete time intervals unverified.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **Non-Deterministic Representation of Data-Driven Branching (Semantic)**:
   The routing between low-risk and high-risk approval tracks and the decision to consult an external supplier are inherently governed by process data (e.g., severity scores, vendor warranty clauses). Because standard Process Trees and POWL models operate purely on control-flow abstractions, these branches are modeled as non-deterministic choices ($\text{XOR}$). Consequently, the model admits scenarios where high-risk deviations take the low-risk pathway without raising a model-level violation unless data-aware extensions (e.g., DPNs) are incorporated.
2. **Theoretical Liveness vs. Unbounded Loop Execution (Semantic)**:
   The repeat-until trial loop is modeled via the standard loop operator $*(\text{Trial\_Cycle}, \text{Adjust Parameters})$. While mathematically sound and terminating in finite playout, standard Petri net semantics permit an infinite execution sequence $(\text{Execute Trial} \to \text{Inspect Trial} \to \text{Adjust Parameters})^\infty$. The model does not enforce a hard upper bound on trial iterations, relying on real-world engineering convergence rather than formal structural termination guarantees.
3. **Process-Tree Under-Approximation Overhead (Semantic / Representational)**:
   The decision to avoid duplicate visible activity labels in the Process Tree necessitated a conservative under-approximation. In operational environments where material containment is resolved instantly while root-cause investigation is protracted, this approximation model predicts a false bottleneck by declaring verification planning blocked until root-cause analysis completes.
