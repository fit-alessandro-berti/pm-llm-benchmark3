# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Analysis clock.** Times are measured in minutes after 08:00. The nominal observation interval is \([08{:}00,14{:}00]\), 360 minutes.

**A2 — Rounded-event ordering.** For nominal queue/WIP calculations, events sharing a reported timestamp are processed in this order:

1. service completion;
2. downstream queue entry caused by that completion;
3. service start;
4. external arrival.

This “release before acquire” convention prevents a hand-off at the same rounded time from creating artificial overlap. It is only a bookkeeping convention; rounded times do not establish the true ordering within approximately ±2.5 minutes.

**A3 — Rounding uncertainty.** A reported timestamp \(t\) represents approximately \([t-2.5,t+2.5]\). Thus a difference between two independently rounded timestamps has uncertainty of about ±5 minutes, constrained to be nonnegative where process semantics impose an order. Tables primarily show nominal values; ±5 minutes is the approximate case-level sensitivity for a duration defined by two observed endpoints. Aggregated busy-time error is correlated and should not be treated as independent measurement noise.

**A4 — Capacity.** S1 has I1 and I2; S2 has C1 and C2. Each resource is single-task. C2 contributes no available capacity during 10:55–11:10. Reported apparent overlaps are not silently repaired.

**A5 — Queue definitions.**

- S1 queue entry is external `arrival_s1`; exit is S1 start.
- S2 queue entry is S1 completion; exit is S2 start.
- An activity remains in service through 14:00 if marked `OPEN`.
- A blank start after queue entry means still waiting at 14:00.
- Queue length excludes active service; station WIP includes waiting plus active service.
- Total process WIP includes every arrived case not yet completed at S2.

**A6 — Missing starts are intervals, not blanks or zero-duration events.** Resource feasibility and process precedence give the following nominal feasible ranges:

- P05 S2/C1: **09:45–10:15**. Lower bound follows P03’s C1 completion at 09:45; upper bound is its own completion. Queue entry was 09:25.
- P07 S1/I1: **09:25–10:00**. Lower bound follows P05’s I1 completion at 09:25 and is after P07 arrival; upper bound is its own completion.
- P11 S2/C2: **12:00–12:25**. Lower bound follows P08’s C2 completion at 12:00; upper bound is its own completion. Queue entry was 11:05.
- P15 S1/I1: **11:40–12:15**. Lower bound follows P13’s I1 completion at 11:40 and is after P15 arrival; upper bound is its own completion.

Endpoint rounding can widen these approximate limits by about five minutes, subject to nonnegative service and no true overlap.

**A7 — Central imputation.** The central scenario uses the midpoint of each A6 range: P05 10:00, P07 09:42:30, P11 12:12:30, and P15 11:57:30. The optimistic scenario chooses the latest feasible start (minimum service, maximum waiting); the pessimistic/high-busy scenario chooses the earliest feasible start (maximum service, minimum waiting). These labels refer to station busy time; their effect on waiting is reversed.

**A8 — Apparent conflicts are retained and flagged.** P07’s possible early S1 interval can overlap P05 on I1; therefore A6 uses the non-overlap bound 09:25. P15 cannot start before P13 ends at 11:40. Nominal observed S1 records also contain rounded boundary contacts, such as P12 ending when P14 starts at 11:30/11:35 only after a gap; none requires exact physical simultaneity. At S2, P10 starts on C1 at 11:45 after P09 ends 11:40.

**A9 — Open service.** P15 has at least 30 nominal minutes of S2 service by 14:00; P17 at least 5 minutes; P21 at least 30 minutes of S1 service. These are elapsed-service lower bounds, not estimates of final service durations.

**A10 — Utilization denominator.** Utilization is busy time divided by resource-minutes available. S1 availability is \(2\times360=720\) resource-minutes. S2 availability is \(720-15=705\), accounting for C2 maintenance. Busy time includes elapsed portions of open work and imputed portions of completed/missing-start work.

**A11 — Analysis cohorts.** Waiting/service summaries use all cases with identifiable relevant endpoints, with separate bounds for missing/open observations. Completed-case total sojourn is reported only for P01–P14, the cases completed at both stations by 14:00.

**A12 — Little’s Law.** \(L=\lambda W\) is used only as a finite-window flow-conservation check, not as proof of steady state. The main check uses the complete-case cohort P01–P14 and integrates its WIP from first arrival to last completion, avoiding right censoring within that cohort.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case \(i\), let \(a_i\) be external arrival, \(s_{1i},e_{1i}\) the S1 start/end, and \(s_{2i},e_{2i}\) the S2 start/end.

\[
W_{1i}=s_{1i}-a_i,\qquad B_{1i}=e_{1i}-s_{1i},\qquad T_{1i}=e_{1i}-a_i
\]

\[
W_{2i}=s_{2i}-e_{1i},\qquad B_{2i}=e_{2i}-s_{2i},\qquad T_{2i}=e_{2i}-e_{1i}
\]

For a fully completed case,

\[
T_i=e_{2i}-a_i
  =W_{1i}+B_{1i}+W_{2i}+B_{2i}.
\]

For missing starts, these quantities become intervals. For example, P05’s S2 start \(x\in[09{:}45,10{:}15]\), so \(W_2=x-09{:}25\in[20,50]\) and \(B_2=10{:}15-x\in[0,30]\); its station sojourn remains exactly 50 nominal minutes.

At time \(t\):

\[
Q_1(t)=\#\{i:a_i\le t<s_{1i}\},\quad
A_1(t)=\#\{i:s_{1i}\le t<e_{1i}\},
\]

\[
Q_2(t)=\#\{i:e_{1i}\le t<s_{2i}\},\quad
A_2(t)=\#\{i:s_{2i}\le t<e_{2i}\}.
\]

Then \(WIP_j(t)=Q_j(t)+A_j(t)\), and total process WIP is the number arrived but not S2-completed. Missing starts generate lower/central/upper queue and active-service trajectories.

Time-weighted mean WIP is

\[
\bar L=\frac{1}{H}\int_0^H L(t)\,dt,
\]

computed exactly from piecewise-constant nominal event intervals. Peak WIP is the maximum under A2; rounding can alter short-lived peaks near tied events.

For a subperiod of duration \(H\),

\[
\lambda_j=\frac{\text{queue entries to station }j}{H},\qquad
\mu_j=\frac{\text{completed services at }j}{H}.
\]

These are realized window rates, not estimated stationary intensities.

Resource busy time is the union length of service intervals per resource. With no unresolved overlap after A6 constraints,

\[
U_j=\frac{\sum_r \text{busy minutes}_r}
          {\sum_r \text{available minutes}_r}.
\]

For missing starts, lower/central/upper busy estimates use latest/midpoint/earliest feasible starts. Open intervals are truncated at 14:00, making utilization “observed-window utilization,” not utilization over final service durations.

For Little’s Law, the complete-case cohort has

\[
\bar L=\frac{\sum_i T_i}{H},\quad
\lambda=\frac{N}{H},\quad
\bar W=\frac{\sum_i T_i}{N},
\]

so \(\bar L=\lambda\bar W\) follows exactly from area accounting when \(H\) spans that cohort’s first arrival through final completion. This checks definitions and integration, but not stationarity.

\]

\]

This is a valid accounting consistency check for that completed cohort. It is not evidence that the full process was in steady state; arrivals are plainly nonstationary and eight cases remain unfinished.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Observed evidence**

1. **S1 is the first visible constraint.** External arrivals are front-loaded: 14 arrive by 10:00, while only seven S1 services complete in that period. S1 waits rise from 5–20 minutes for early cases to roughly 85–105 minutes for later observed starts. By 10:00, S1 has accumulated substantial WIP.

2. **The pressure then propagates to S2.** S2’s waits grow from 5–15 minutes early to 45–80 minutes among later completed cases. At 14:00, S2 still contains six cases versus two at S1, even though the stations have comparable window utilization.

3. **The bottleneck is therefore time-varying rather than a single immutable station.** In the early morning, S1 is the dominant queue-forming stage because the external arrival burst reaches it first. Around late morning and early afternoon, S2 becomes the dominant downstream backlog: S1 continues releasing approximately three cases per hour while S2 is already carrying accumulated work.

4. **Observed-window utilizations are similar.** Both stations fall around the mid-70% range centrally, with broad 70–80% ranges. Window-average utilization alone does not identify the bottleneck because:
   - arrivals are highly nonstationary;
   - capacity idle early or late cannot serve work that has not yet arrived;
   - S2’s outage and long service times occur during a congested period;
   - missing starts affect busy-time estimates.

5. **At the end of the window, S2 is the operational bottleneck.** Four cases are waiting and two are active at S2, while S1 has one active and one waiting. This is direct state evidence, not an inference from averages.

**Maintenance interval**

C2 is unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10. No reported C2 service overlaps the outage: P06 ends at 10:50 and P08 starts at 11:15. Thus the records are consistent with the stated maintenance.

The outage removes 15 resource-minutes, only about 2% of S2’s six-hour two-resource capacity, but its timing matters. S2 already has queued work, and P08 waits from 10:20 to 11:15. Without the outage, a feasible but unproven counterfactual is that C2 could have started P08 between 10:55 and 11:10, reducing that wait by up to roughly 20 minutes relative to the reported 11:15 start after allowing for rounding. The full delay cannot be attributed to maintenance: C2 was busy until 10:50, and dispatching/setup behavior is unobserved.

**Interpretive hypothesis**

A defensible narrative is: **S1 creates the morning backlog under the arrival burst; S2 becomes the limiting stage after that backlog is released, with maintenance temporarily worsening S2 congestion.** The data do not justify claiming that one station has permanently lower intrinsic capacity. Estimated completed-service means are broadly overlapping once missing starts and censoring are considered.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

The three start-imputation scenarios are:

| scenario | P05 S2 | P07 S1 | P11 S2 | P15 S1 | interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| optimistic / minimum busy | 10:15 | 10:00 | 12:25 | 12:15 | Zero-duration service is mathematically feasible at reported precision but operationally extreme; maximizes inferred waiting |
| central | 10:00 | 09:42:30 | 12:12:30 | 11:57:30 | Midpoint of resource-feasible range; descriptive, not a claim of truth |
| pessimistic / maximum busy | 09:45 | 09:25 | 12:00 | 11:40 | Maximizes service and minimizes inferred waiting while avoiding same-resource overlap |

Approximate sensitivity:

| quantity | optimistic | central | pessimistic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 observed-window busy time | 510 min | 545 min | 580 min |
| S1 utilization | 71% | 76% | 81% |
| S2 observed-window busy time | 510 min | 538 min | 565 min |
| S2 utilization | 72% | 76% | 80% |
| Mean S1 wait among 21 started cases | about 73 min | about 72 min | about 70 min |
| Mean S2 wait among 16 started cases | about 42 min | about 41 min | about 39 min |

The exact queue-WIP area also changes because a later imputed start transfers time from active service to queueing while leaving station sojourn unchanged.

**Stable conclusions**

- External demand is strongly front-loaded.
- S1 waits increase markedly through the morning.
- S2 develops a persistent downstream backlog and has six cases at 14:00.
- At 14:00, S2 has more WIP and more waiting cases than S1.
- C2’s outage is represented consistently and occurs during congestion.
- P01–P14’s total-sojourn mean is about 147 minutes regardless of internal-start imputation.
- The process is not stationary over the full six-hour window, so a full-window steady-state Little’s-Law interpretation would be misleading.

**Sensitive conclusions**

- Exact station utilization ranking is not identifiable: S1 and S2 ranges overlap almost completely.
- Exact mean service times are sensitive to missing starts.
- Short-lived peak queue/WIP values depend on imputation and on ordering events within rounded five-minute timestamps.
- The amount of P08’s delay attributable specifically to maintenance is counterfactual and cannot be observed.
- Final service durations for P15, P17, and P21 cannot be estimated from elapsed time alone without a service-time model.

**Right-censoring effect**

Discarding open/waiting cases would preferentially remove the longest waits and late services. In particular, P16 has already waited 85 minutes for S2, P22 has waited 110 minutes for S1, and P15/P17/P21 remain active. Completed-case averages therefore describe completed work, not all work that arrived. Kaplan–Meier or survival modeling could handle censoring in a larger sample, but 22 highly time-dependent cases are too few to support a reliable model here.

**Rounding effect**

A nominal five-minute wait may represent a true interval near zero to ten minutes. Apparent zero-duration service in an extreme imputation is thus a mathematical bound, not a plausible operational estimate. For long waits of 50–110 minutes, ±5-minute endpoint uncertainty does not alter the qualitative conclusion. Rounding is much more consequential for resource conflicts, exact maintenance-boundary attribution, and instantaneous peaks.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from io import StringIO
import pm4py

# ---------- 1. Construct supplied table in memory ----------
raw = """case,arrival_s1,s1_start,s1_end,s1_resource,s2_start,s2_end,s2_resource,state_at_14_00
P01,08:00,08:05,08:25,I1,08:30,09:00,C1,COMPLETE
P02,08:05,08:10,08:35,I2,08:40,09:20,C2,COMPLETE
P03,08:10,08:30,08:55,I1,09:05,09:45,C1,COMPLETE
P04,08:20,08:40,09:10,I2,09:25,10:05,C2,COMPLETE
P05,08:25,09:00,09:25,I1,MISSING,10:15,C1,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P06,08:35,09:15,09:45,I2,10:10,10:50,C2,COMPLETE
P07,08:45,MISSING,10:00,I1,10:20,11:00,C1,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P08,09:00,09:50,10:20,I2,11:15,12:00,C2,COMPLETE
P09,09:05,10:05,10:35,I1,11:00,11:40,C1,COMPLETE
P10,09:15,10:25,10:55,I2,11:45,12:10,C1,COMPLETE
P11,09:25,10:40,11:05,I1,MISSING,12:25,C2,COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P12,09:35,11:00,11:30,I2,12:15,13:00,C1,COMPLETE
P13,09:50,11:10,11:40,I1,12:30,13:20,C2,COMPLETE
P14,10:00,11:35,12:00,I2,13:05,13:50,C1,COMPLETE
P15,10:10,MISSING,12:15,I1,13:30,OPEN,C2,OPEN_IN_S2
P16,10:25,12:05,12:35,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P17,10:45,12:20,12:50,I1,13:55,OPEN,C1,OPEN_IN_S2
P18,11:00,12:40,13:10,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P19,11:15,12:55,13:25,I1,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P20,11:30,13:15,13:45,I2,,,,WAITING_FOR_S2
P21,11:50,13:30,OPEN,I1,,,,OPEN_IN_S1
P22,12:10,,,,,,,WAITING_FOR_S1
"""
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw), dtype=str).fillna("")

DAY = "2026-05-12 "
OBS_END = pd.Timestamp(DAY + "14:00")

# ---------- 2. Status-aware parsing ----------
def parse_cell(x):
    """Return timestamp plus explicit status; MISSING, OPEN and blank stay distinct."""
    x = str(x).strip()
    if x == "MISSING":
        return pd.NaT, "MISSING"
    if x == "OPEN":
        return pd.NaT, "OPEN"
    if x == "":
        return pd.NaT, "BLANK"
    return pd.Timestamp(DAY + x), "OBSERVED"

time_cols = ["arrival_s1", "s1_start", "s1_end", "s2_start", "s2_end"]
for col in time_cols:
    parsed = df[col].map(parse_cell)
    df[col + "_ts"] = parsed.map(lambda z: z[0])
    df[col + "_status"] = parsed.map(lambda z: z[1])

# Missing-start resource-feasible nominal ranges.
bounds = {
    ("P05", "S2"): (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"09:45"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"10:15")),
    ("P07", "S1"): (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"09:25"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"10:00")),
    ("P11", "S2"): (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"12:00"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"12:25")),
    ("P15", "S1"): (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"11:40"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"12:15")),
}

# optimistic=min busy/latest start; central=midpoint; pessimistic=max busy/earliest start
def scenario_start(case, station, observed, scenario):
    if pd.notna(observed):
        return observed
    key = (case, station)
    if key not in bounds:
        return pd.NaT
    lo, hi = bounds[key]
    return {"optimistic": hi, "central": lo + (hi-lo)/2, "pessimistic": lo}[scenario]

def build_scenario(name):
    x = df.copy()
    x["s1_start_used"] = [
        scenario_start(c, "S1", s, name)
        for c, s in zip(x["case"], x["s1_start_ts"])
    ]
    x["s2_start_used"] = [
        scenario_start(c, "S2", s, name)
        for c, s in zip(x["case"], x["s2_start_ts"])
    ]
    # OPEN ends are truncated only for observed-window busy/WIP, never as final completion.
    x["s1_end_busy"] = x["s1_end_ts"]
    x.loc[x["s1_end_status"].eq("OPEN"), "s1_end_busy"] = OBS_END
    x["s2_end_busy"] = x["s2_end_ts"]
    x.loc[x["s2_end_status"].eq("OPEN"), "s2_end_busy"] = OBS_END
    return x

scenarios = {s: build_scenario(s) for s in ["optimistic", "central", "pessimistic"]}

# ---------- 3. PM4Py-compatible interval-event DataFrame ----------
# Only usable observations with known/imputed starts and observed completions are included.
# OPEN intervals are omitted from this completed interval log because 14:00 is censoring,
# not a true completion.
central = scenarios["central"]
events = []
for _, r in central.iterrows():
    for st, act in [("s1", "S1"), ("s2", "S2")]:
        start = r[f"{st}_start_used"]
        end = r[f"{st}_end_ts"]
        resource = r[f"{st}_resource"]
        if pd.notna(start) and pd.notna(end):
            events.append({
                "case:concept:name": r["case"],
                "concept:name": act,
                "start_timestamp": start,
                "time:timestamp": end,
                "org:resource": resource
            })
interval_df = pd.DataFrame(events)
interval_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    interval_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)

# convert_log_to_time_intervals is applicable to the completed interval projection.
# Direct pandas below is safer for station queues because queue entry differs by station,
# and because OPEN/blank/MISSING carry censoring semantics PM4Py should not be asked to infer.
interval_log = pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals(interval_df)

# ---------- 4. pandas station waiting/service and WIP ----------
def minutes(delta):
    return delta.dt.total_seconds() / 60

def station_metrics(x, station):
    if station == "S1":
        entry, start, end = "arrival_s1_ts", "s1_start_used", "s1_end_busy"
        completed = x["s1_end_status"].eq("OBSERVED")
        available = 720.0
    else:
        entry, start, end = "s1_end_ts", "s2_start_used", "s2_end_busy"
        completed = x["s2_end_status"].eq("OBSERVED")
        available = 705.0  # 2*360 less C2 outage
    wait_mask = x[entry].notna() & x[start].notna()
    service_mask = x[start].notna() & x[end].notna()
    waits = minutes(x.loc[wait_mask, start] - x.loc[wait_mask, entry])
    services = minutes(x.loc[service_mask & completed, end] -
                       x.loc[service_mask & completed, start])
    busy = minutes(x.loc[service_mask, end] - x.loc[service_mask, start]).sum()
    return {
        "station": station,
        "arrivals": int(x[entry].notna().sum()),
        "completed": int(completed.sum()),
        "mean_wait_started_min": waits.mean(),
        "mean_completed_service_min": services.mean(),
        "busy_min_in_window": busy,
        "utilization": busy / available
    }

metric_rows = []
for scen, x in scenarios.items():
    for st in ["S1", "S2"]:
        row = station_metrics(x, st)
        row["scenario"] = scen
        metric_rows.append(row)
metric_table = pd.DataFrame(metric_rows)

def state_at(x, station, t):
    if station == "S1":
        entry, start, end = "arrival_s1_ts", "s1_start_used", "s1_end_busy"
    else:
        entry, start, end = "s1_end_ts", "s2_start_used", "s2_end_busy"
    entered = x[entry].notna() & (x[entry] <= t)
    waiting = entered & (x[start].isna() | (t < x[start]))
    active = x[start].notna() & (x[start] <= t) & x[end].notna() & (t < x[end])
    return int(waiting.sum()), int(active.sum())

def time_weighted_wip(x, station):
    if station == "S1":
        cols = ["arrival_s1_ts", "s1_start_used", "s1_end_busy"]
    else:
        cols = ["s1_end_ts", "s2_start_used", "s2_end_busy"]
    times = {pd.Timestamp(DAY+"08:00"), OBS_END}
    for col in cols:
        times.update(x[col].dropna().tolist())
    times = sorted(t for t in times
                   if pd.Timestamp(DAY+"08:00") <= t <= OBS_END)
    area_q = area_a = 0.0
    peak_q = peak_wip = 0
    for left, right in zip(times[:-1], times[1:]):
        q, a = state_at(x, station, left)
        dt = (right-left).total_seconds()/60
        area_q += q*dt
        area_a += a*dt
        peak_q = max(peak_q, q)
        peak_wip = max(peak_wip, q+a)
    return {
        "station": station,
        "mean_queue": area_q/360,
        "mean_active": area_a/360,
        "mean_wip": (area_q+area_a)/360,
        "peak_queue": peak_q,
        "peak_wip": peak_wip,
        "q_at_1400": state_at(x, station, OBS_END-pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1))[0],
        "active_at_1400": state_at(x, station, OBS_END-pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1))[1]
    }

wip_rows = []
for scen, x in scenarios.items():
    for st in ["S1", "S2"]:
        r = time_weighted_wip(x, st)
        r["scenario"] = scen
        wip_rows.append(r)
wip_table = pd.DataFrame(wip_rows)

# ---------- 6. Meaningful two-hour rates ----------
cuts = [
    (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"08:00"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"10:00")),
    (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"10:00"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"12:00")),
    (pd.Timestamp(DAY+"12:00"), pd.Timestamp(DAY+"14:00"))
]
rate_rows = []
for lo, hi in cuts:
    # Half-open periods [lo,hi), except include no special 14:00 event here.
    ext = ((central["arrival_s1_ts"] >= lo) & (central["arrival_s1_ts"] < hi)).sum()
    s1c = ((central["s1_end_ts"] >= lo) & (central["s1_end_ts"] < hi)).sum()
    s2c = ((central["s2_end_ts"] >= lo) & (central["s2_end_ts"] < hi)).sum()
    rate_rows.append({
        "period": f"{lo:%H:%M}-{hi:%H:%M}",
        "external_arrivals": int(ext), "external_per_h": ext/2,
        "S1_completions": int(s1c), "S1_per_h": s1c/2,
        "S2_completions": int(s2c), "S2_per_h": s2c/2
    })
```
