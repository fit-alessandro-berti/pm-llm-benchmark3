# Why flagged cases triage faster but close slower: a mediation-aware process analysis

A preliminary caution that frames everything below: the "flag" is not a single manipulable cause. It is a deterministic function of `risk_score` (in these data every case with `risk_score >= 0.64` is flagged and every case with `risk_score <= 0.61` is not), and it triggers a *policy bundle*: priority triage lane, near-mandatory manual review, and altered escalation propensity. "Set flag := 0" is therefore ambiguous between at least (a) mislabeling the case while the organization keeps its post-flag behaviors, (b) suppressing the display so humans act on their own judgment (policy P1), and (c) dismantling individual components of the bundle (P2–P4). These are different interventions with different consequences, and I keep them separate throughout.

## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework choice, stated up front: I primarily use **interventional (randomized-interventional) direct and indirect effects** and **policy/stochastic-intervention contrasts**, not natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects require cross-world counterfactual independences (the mediator value a case *would have had* under the other flag state, combined with the actual flag state) that are not credible here for three reasons: (i) the flag is deterministic in `risk_score`, so "flag = 0 holding risk fixed" has no positivity for high scores except near the threshold; (ii) exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding — priority triage changes queue state, which then affects both whether/when review happens and the outcome — breaks the identification of natural effects even with full covariate adjustment; (iii) shared queues create interference, so a case's counterfactual mediator depends on other cases' treatments, making natural effects ill-defined at the unit level. Interventional analogues (set the mediator distribution to that observed in a reference stratum) and controlled/policy effects (P2, P3, P4 directly manipulate a mediator or its capacity) remain meaningful. Where a mediator only exists conditional on a prior event (manual-review *waiting* exists only if review occurs), I use a **separable-effects** style decomposition: conceptually split the flag into a "routing component" (who gets review) and a "priority/queue component" (how long they wait), which P2 and P3 physically instantiate.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P1 vs P0 (suppress flag display, keep score and human information) | system-level; all cases arriving in a period, cluster = time block | mean total cycle time | total policy effect (system-level, interference-robust because defined on clusters) | yes, as a cluster-level policy contrast | no concurrent control system; temporal confounding (case mix, load drift) |
| E2 | P1 vs P0 | same clusters | arrival-to-triage-done time | component (stage-specific) total effect | yes | same as E1; triage priority is the most mechanically tied to the flag, so smallest obstacle |
| E3 | P0 vs P0-with-review-assignment drawn from the unflagged-cases distribution given (risk_score, complexity) | case-level, flagged cases near the threshold | total cycle time | interventional indirect effect via **manual-review occurrence** | approximately, near threshold only | positivity away from threshold (all flagged get review); U_underlying_risk drives human review of unflagged cases |
| E4 | P3 vs P0 (protected review capacity; routing unchanged) | flagged cases + spillover set of unflagged cases | total cycle time | controlled/policy effect isolating **manual-queue-wait** mediation (separable "queue component") | yes (physically implementable) | interference: protected capacity may be taken from the shared pool, harming unflagged cases |
| E5 | P4 vs P0 (change escalation criteria or senior capacity; review unchanged) | reviewed cases | total cycle time | controlled/policy effect isolating **escalation occurrence + escalation-queue** mediation | yes | escalation is chosen using post-review information correlated with U_underlying_risk (exposure-induced confounding) |
| E6 | P2 vs P0 (keep priority triage, review only on human judgment) | flagged cases | total cycle time | interventional indirect effect via review occurrence, physically instantiated | yes | predicting the human-judgment counterfactual review rate; behavioral adaptation of triage staff |
| E7 | P2 vs P0 | flagged cases | decision quality: rejected-case error rate, post-hoc audit discrepancy | total effect on outcome quality | partially — needs an audit gold standard | selective labels: we only observe outcomes under the decision actually made |
| E8 | P3 vs P0 | **unflagged** cases sharing the review queue | manual queue wait of unflagged cases | spillover (interference) effect | yes, as a cluster-level contrast | requires queue-state data; effects mix with load fluctuations |

E1 is the total policy effect; E2 the triage-completion effect; E3 mediation by review occurrence; E4 mediation by review waiting; E5 mediation by escalation; E1/E4/E6 cover total cycle time; E7 covers decision quality; E8 makes interference an explicit estimand rather than a nuisance.

## 2. Mechanism graph

Directed adjacency list (`U_` = unobserved):

```text
U_underlying_risk   -> risk_score, complexity, manual_review, escalation, decision_outcome, manual_service, senior_service
U_case_difficulty   -> complexity, triage_service, manual_service, senior_service, decision_outcome
complexity          -> risk_score, flag, manual_review, manual_service, escalation, decision_outcome
risk_score          -> flag
flag                -> priority_lane, manual_review, escalation
priority_lane       -> triage_wait
arrival_load        -> triage_wait, manual_queue_wait, escalation_queue_wait
U_queue_state       -> triage_wait, manual_queue_wait, escalation_queue_wait
flag (other cases)  -> U_queue_state                      # interference edge
triage_wait         -> manual_queue_wait                  # earlier triage-done => joins review queue at a different (possibly more congested) time
triage_service      -> manual_queue_wait
manual_review       -> manual_queue_wait, manual_service, escalation
manual_service      -> escalation, decision_outcome
escalation          -> escalation_queue_wait, senior_service
senior_service      -> decision_outcome
U_reviewer_behavior -> manual_service, escalation         # reviewers may treat flagged cases differently
triage_wait, triage_service, manual_queue_wait, manual_service,
escalation_queue_wait, senior_service -> cycle_time
manual_review, escalation, decision_outcome -> (audit/compliance, not timed)
```

Time-ordered process-mechanism diagram:

```text
t0 arrival:      U_underlying_risk ==> risk_score --> flag ; complexity (intake rule) --> flag
                 arrival_load, U_queue_state snapshot
t1 triage queue: flag --> priority_lane --> triage_wait  [flagged jump the queue;
                 unflagged wait longer BECAUSE flagged jumped: interference]
t2 triage svc:   triage_service (short for flagged: pre-sorted lane? simpler screening?)
t3 routing:      flag ==> manual_review (near-mandatory) ;
                 unflagged: human judgment(U_underlying_risk, complexity) --> manual_review
t4 review queue: manual_queue_wait <-- shared queue(U_queue_state, arrival_load,
                 volume of flag-induced reviews)   [flag inflates its own queue]
t5 review svc:   manual_service(complexity, U_underlying_risk, U_reviewer_behavior)
t6 escalation:   escalation(flag, review findings, U_underlying_risk) --> scarce senior team
t7 senior queue: escalation_queue_wait (capacity-bound, overnight gaps visible in data)
t8 close:        cycle_time = sum of all waits + services + residual;
                 decision_outcome(review depth, senior input, U_underlying_risk)
```

Interference arises because triage, review, and senior queues are shared: flagging one case pushes it ahead of others at triage (their `triage_wait` rises) and adds a near-mandatory review job that lengthens `manual_queue_wait` for everyone, including other flagged cases — the policy partly congests itself. Exposure-induced confounding: the flag shortens `triage_wait`, changing the queue state the case meets at review; that queue state affects both downstream waits and (via reviewer time pressure) decisions, so it is a mediator–outcome confounder caused by exposure — this is precisely what invalidates natural-effect identification. Treatment-confounder feedback appears dynamically: today's flag-driven review volume becomes tomorrow's `arrival_load`/queue state, which influences how aggressively humans route unflagged cases to review, which feeds back into queue length. (≈150 words)

## 3. Stage decomposition

Handling of absent stages: a stage that did not occur contributes **no observation** to that stage's statistic (recorded `NA`/excluded), never a zero. Zeros would fabricate infinitely fast reviews and mechanically shrink flagged-vs-unflagged gaps. The pattern is exactly "acceleration at triage, delay downstream": flagged cases wait a median 0.20 h for triage versus 2.50 h unflagged, and their triage service is shorter (0.60 vs 0.95 h) — consistent with the priority lane. Everything after triage runs the other way: flagged cases *always* enter review and then sit a median 6.75 h in the review queue (vs 1.75 h for the few reviewed unflagged cases), and 60% are escalated with a further ~7 h senior-queue wait. The escalated-vs-not contrast (21.9 vs 8.8 h) shows most flagged excess travels through the escalation branch. None of this is automatically causal: the flag is deterministic in `risk_score` and correlated with `complexity` (5/10 flagged are High vs 3/10 unflagged; all Low cases are unflagged), so flagged cases would plausibly need more review and escalation even unflagged — case R20 (score 0.61, unflagged, High complexity) went through review and escalation on human judgment alone and took 19.2 h, nearly matching flagged escalated cases. ## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag *itself* (vs the risk it encodes) change routing and timing? | Local-threshold (regression-discontinuity-style) comparison in the band risk_score 0.55–0.67, where both flag values exist (R17 .55, R09 .58, R20 .61 unflagged; R15 .64, R08 .67 flagged) | complexity, arrival_load; band on risk_score | continuity of underlying risk and case mix at the threshold; no manipulation of scores around it | McCrary-style density check of scores near 0.62; covariate balance (complexity, load) across the threshold | discontinuity in *complexity* at the threshold would mean the intake rule is entangled with the score and the design collapses to confounded comparison |
| How much of flagged delay is congestion vs treatment? | Queue-state-aware analysis: reconstruct instantaneous queue length for review/senior queues from start/complete timestamps; model each wait as f(queue length at entry, flag, complexity) | queue length at stage entry, arrival_load, time-of-day/shift | timestamps mark true queue entry (triage_done ≈ joins review queue); service is single-case at a time | within identical queue-length states, compare flagged vs unflagged waits; check waits scale with reconstructed queue length | if flagged cases wait longer *even at equal queue states*, delay is treatment/prioritization policy, not congestion — shifts remedy from capacity (P3) to routing (P2) |
| What would P1/P2 actually do? | Stepped-wedge or randomized-encouragement rollout: randomize *time blocks or teams* (not cases) to flag-display-off (P1) or discretionary-review (P2); encouragement = "review optional" nudge for flagged cases | cluster fixed effects, load, case mix | no cross-cluster contamination (separate queues per team); stable case mix across arms | compare pre-period trends across clusters; check compliance (how often discretion actually skips review) | if discretionary arm reviews ~100% anyway, humans replicate the mandate: occurrence mediation is moot and delay must be queue-mediated |
| Which mediator carries the effect: occurrence, review wait, or escalation? | Interventional mediation analysis within the threshold band: estimate cycle-time change if flagged cases' review-occurrence distribution (then separately, wait distribution) were set to the unflagged stratum's, holding the other fixed | risk_score band, complexity, queue state at triage_done | no unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding *given queue state*; mediators manipulable separately (P2 vs P3 make this physical) | compare model-based interventional estimate with the P2/P3 pilot results | if setting the wait distribution (not occurrence) explains most of the gap, P3 dominates P2 and mandatory review can be kept |
| Is the "effect" an artifact of measurement or case mix? | Negative controls. Negative-control outcome: triage *service* time and residual-to-close should be barely flag-sensitive under pure-queue mechanisms (observed residual ≈1.0 h in both — passes). Negative-control exposure: risk-score variation *within* flag=1 (0.64–0.93) should not shift priority-lane behavior if the lane keys only on the binary flag | complexity | the control outcome shares confounders with the real outcome but no causal path from flag | flag "affecting" residual-to-close, or score-within-flag "affecting" triage wait, indicates confounding/measurement artifacts | a flag effect on the negative-control outcome would undercut all causal readings of the stage contrasts |
| Could hidden risk explain everything? | Sensitivity analysis: E-value / bias formulas for how strong U_underlying_risk -> (flag via score) and U -> cycle time must be to explain the ~10.7 h median gap; benchmark U against observed complexity (which moves cycle time ~15 h Low→High) | complexity, risk_score as observed proxies | bias formulas' structural form; complexity is a reasonable benchmark for U's plausible strength | does the flagged/unflagged gap survive within High complexity? (R09/R20 unflagged High: 18.4/19.2 h vs flagged High median ≈21 h — gap largely closes) | if a U no stronger than complexity suffices to nullify the gap — which these data suggest is close to true — the flag's own causal contribution beyond routing is small |
| Do policies help the *system* or just flagged cases? | Interference-aware design: randomize at queue-cluster level, define exposure mapping (own flag, fraction flagged in queue at entry); estimate direct + spillover under partial interference | cluster, queue composition at entry | partial interference (no spillover across clusters/shifts) | vary flagged density across clusters; test whether unflagged waits respond to flagged density | if unflagged cycle times worsen 1:1 as flagged improve, P0-style prioritization only redistributes delay — total-effect estimand E1 must arbitrate |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current | flagged fast (0.2 h), unflagged slow (2.5 h) | review queue saturated by mandatory flagged volume; senior queue ~7 h | flagged ≈16.5 h median, unflagged ≈5.8 h; system mean high | scrutiny concentrated where score says; misses low-score risk (score is imperfect) | flagged displace unflagged at every shared stage | observed baseline | keep only as control arm |
| P1 suppress flag display | flagged lose lane: triage wait rises toward ~2.5 h | review demand falls toward judgment-driven (~40–60%?), queues shorten for all | flagged likely faster overall despite slower triage; unflagged slightly faster; genuinely risky cases may be missed at triage | highest: removes the alerting function entirely, not just its costs | frees capacity broadly | weakest — no observed analogue; human-judgment counterfactual unknown | do not pilot first; too blunt |
| P2 priority triage, discretionary review | unchanged (fast flagged triage) | review volume drops by the fraction of flagged cases judgment would skip; queue waits fall for remaining | flagged non-reviewed drop toward ~2–4 h; reviewed flagged also gain from shorter queues | moderate: skipped reviews may miss problems; all observed Rejects passed review | positive spillover to unflagged review waits | moderate: R20 shows judgment does route risky unflagged cases correctly | pilot with randomization + audit (chosen below) |
| P3 protected review capacity for flagged | unchanged | flagged review wait falls (target 6.75 → ~2 h); unflagged unaffected *only if* capacity is additional, harmed if carved out | flagged ≈ −4 to −5 h; escalated flagged still ~22 h unless senior queue also fixed | low: nothing skipped | negative if capacity is reallocated, neutral-positive if new | moderate: wait-vs-occurrence split not yet identified | good second pilot; costs money; leaves senior bottleneck |
| P4 change escalation criteria/capacity | unchanged | senior queue (~7 h wait, n small) shortens or demand falls | escalated flagged (21.9 h) improve most; non-escalated untouched | criteria tightening risks missing severe cases (all 4 Rejects were escalated) | senior team scarcity means small absolute capacity changes matter | weak: n=8 escalations total | defer until P2/P3 evidence on volume reaching seniors |

**Pilot: P2 (discretionary review for flagged cases) with randomized encouragement.**
- **Eligibility:** flagged cases in the score band 0.64–0.80 (keeps the highest-risk band 0.80+ on mandatory review), all complexities, both load states.
- **Assignment:** case-level randomization 50/50 at triage-done: "mandatory review" (P0) vs "reviewer-discretion" arm; randomize within time blocks to permit cluster-level spillover estimation.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail:** if review-queue length exceeds 150% of trailing 4-week median, discretion arm pauses (all revert to mandatory) to prevent congestion states outside the analyzable range.
- **Primary outcomes:** total cycle time; review-queue wait for *all* cases (spillover). **Secondary:** escalation rate, decision reversals, audit-detected misses.
- **Quality guardrail / audit:** 100% delayed post-closure audit of discretion-arm cases that skipped review, by reviewers blind to arm.
- **Minimum process data:** per-stage start/complete timestamps, queue-entry timestamps, instantaneous queue lengths, resource identifiers and rosters/shift calendars, risk_score, complexity, arm, discretion decision and its stated reason, audit result.
- **Stopping conditions:** stop for harm if audit-detected miss rate in the discretion arm exceeds control by a prespecified margin (e.g., >2 percentage points) at interim looks; stop for futility if discretion arm reviews >90% of cases anyway; stop for success once cycle-time and spillover effects are estimated with prespecified precision. """

# ---- 1. parse all timestamps ("-" becomes NaT) ----
wide = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV), na_values=["-"])
TS_COLS = ["arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "manual_start", "manual_done",
           "escalation_start", "escalation_done", "close"]
for c in TS_COLS:
    wide[c] = pd.to_datetime(wide[c], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M", errors="coerce")

# ---- 2. reshape wide -> event level; absent stages produce NO events ----
STAGES = [
    ("Register",     "arrival",          "arrival",         None),
    ("Triage",       "triage_start",     "triage_done",     "triage_resource"),
    ("ManualReview", "manual_start",     "manual_done",     "review_resource"),
    ("Escalation",   "escalation_start", "escalation_done", None),
    ("Close",        "close",            "close",           None),
]
records = []
for _, r in wide.iterrows():
    for act, s_col, c_col, res_col in STAGES:
        if pd.isna(r[c_col]):
            # Stage did not occur: emit nothing. We deliberately do NOT
            # fabricate zero-length events, which would bias DFG statistics.
            continue
        records.append({
            "case:concept:name": r["case"],
            "concept:name": act,
            "start_timestamp": r[s_col],
            "time:timestamp": r[c_col],
            "org:resource": r[res_col] if (res_col and pd.notna(r[res_col])) else None,
            "flag": int(r["flag"]),
            "complexity": r["complexity"],
            "arrival_load": r["arrival_load"],
            "risk_score": r["risk_score"],
            "outcome": r["outcome"],
        })
events = (pd.DataFrame(records)
          .sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "start_timestamp"])
          .reset_index(drop=True))

# ---- 3. PM4Py formatting with explicit start-timestamp key ----
import pm4py
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)

# ---- 4. frequency and performance DFGs per flag group ----
for flag_val in (0, 1):
    sub = log_df[log_df["flag"] == flag_val].copy()
    n_cases = sub["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    freq_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
    perf_dfg, psa, pea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub)
    print(f"\n=== flag={flag_val} (n={n_cases} cases) ===")
    print("Frequency DFG (edge: count):")
    for edge, cnt in sorted(freq_dfg.items()):
        print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {cnt}")
    print("Performance DFG (edge: mean hours between activities):")
    for edge, perf in sorted(perf_dfg.items()):
        # depending on the PM4Py version, perf is a float or a stats dict
        mean_secs = perf.get("mean") if isinstance(perf, dict) else perf
        print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: {mean_secs/3600.0:.2f} h")

# ---- 5. explicit stage waiting/service times; absent stages stay NaN ----
def hours(a, b):
    return (wide[b] - wide[a]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

wide["triage_wait_h"] = hours("arrival", "triage_start")
wide["triage_svc_h"]  = hours("triage_start", "triage_done")
wide["manual_wait_h"] = hours("triage_done", "manual_start")      # NaN if no review
wide["manual_svc_h"]  = hours("manual_start", "manual_done")
wide["esc_wait_h"]    = hours("manual_done", "escalation_start")  # NaN if no escalation
wide["esc_svc_h"]     = hours("escalation_start", "escalation_done")
last_done = wide[["triage_done", "manual_done", "escalation_done"]].max(axis=1)
wide["residual_h"] = (wide["close"] - last_done).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
wide["cycle_h"]    = (wide["close"] - wide["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

STAGE_METRICS = ["triage_wait_h", "triage_svc_h", "manual_wait_h", "manual_svc_h",
                 "esc_wait_h", "esc_svc_h", "residual_h", "cycle_h"]

# ---- 6. stage summaries with support counts ----
def summarize(df, label):
    print(f"\n--- {label} (n={len(df)}) ---")
    med = df[STAGE_METRICS].median(numeric_only=True)
    supp = df[STAGE_METRICS].notna().sum()
    for m in STAGE_METRICS:
        print(f"  {m:15s} median={med[m]:7.2f} h  support={int(supp[m])}/{len(df)}")
    if len(df) < 5:
        warnings.warn(f"Subgroup '{label}' has n={len(df)} < 5: "
                      "estimates are extremely fragile; do not over-interpret.")

for fv, g in wide.groupby("flag"):
    summarize(g, f"flag={fv}")
for cx, g in wide.groupby("complexity"):
    summarize(g, f"complexity={cx}")
f1 = wide[wide["flag"] == 1]
for lv, g in f1.groupby("arrival_load"):
    summarize(g, f"flag=1, arrival_load={lv}")
summarize(f1[f1["escalation_start"].notna()], "flag=1, escalated")
summarize(f1[f1["escalation_start"].isna()],  "flag=1, not escalated")

print("\nManual-review occurrence: flag=1: {}/10, flag=0: {}/10".format(
    int(wide.loc[wide.flag == 1, "manual_start"].notna().sum()),
    int(wide.loc[wide.flag == 0, "manual_start"].notna().sum())))
print("Escalation occurrence:    flag=1: {}/10, flag=0: {}/10".format(
    int(wide.loc[wide.flag == 1, "escalation_start"].notna().sum()),
    int(wide.loc[wide.flag == 0, "escalation_start"].notna().sum())))

# Optional PM4Py helper: insert_case_service_waiting_time computes, per case,
# the total SERVICE time (union of activity start->complete intervals, so
# overlapping activities are not double-counted), the WAITING time (portions
# of the case sojourn during which no activity is in service), and the
# SOJOURN time (arrival of first event to completion of last). It is a
# descriptive decomposition of the case timeline, not a causal quantity.
try:
    svc_wait = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        log_df, timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    cols = [c for c in svc_wait.columns
            if "service" in c or "waiting" in c or "sojourn" in c]
    per_case = svc_wait.groupby("case:concept:name")[cols].first()
    print("\nPer-case service/waiting/sojourn (hours):")
    print((per_case / 3600.0).round(2).to_string())
except Exception as exc:
    print(f"insert_case_service_waiting_time unavailable: {exc}")

# ---- 7. causal-interpretation warning ----
warnings.warn(
    "DFG performance differences between flagged and unflagged cases are "
    "DESCRIPTIVE. The flag is a deterministic function of risk_score and is "
    "correlated with complexity and unobserved risk; review and escalation "
    "queues are shared, so cases interfere with one another. Nothing computed "
    "here identifies natural direct or indirect effects.")
print("\nDone. All stage statistics exclude structurally absent stages (NaN); "
      "absent stages are never treated as zero-length observed stages.")

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** if a sensitivity analysis or later audit shows unflagged high-score-band cases (like R20) systematically harbor unrecorded severity that flagged cases lack, then the flagged delay reflects genuine case difficulty rather than the policy bundle, and P2 (skipping reviews) would be removing scrutiny exactly where it is needed — I would withdraw the P2 pilot in favor of P3/P4 capacity fixes.
2. **Queue interference:** if the queue-state-aware analysis shows flagged cases wait long at review *even when the queue is empty* (i.e., waits are batching or shift artifacts, not congestion), then "protected capacity" (P3) would buy nothing; the remedy becomes scheduling, and my congestion-based reading of the 6.75 h review wait reverses.
3. **Decision quality:** if the P2 pilot's audit finds skipped reviews miss problems at a materially higher rate (all four observed Rejects went through review and escalation — the scrutiny may be exactly what catches them), the cycle-time savings are bought with error, and I would recommend P0/P3 despite longer cycle times.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** if `triage_done` does not mark entry into the review queue (e.g., cases sit in an unlogged holding step, or clocks include non-working overnight hours — several long "waits" such as R13's 18:06→04:06 span the night), then the wait/service decomposition is misattributed and the "delay is queueing" conclusion could reverse toward "delay is off-hours calendar time."
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap:** if richer data confirm that no unflagged cases exist above score ≈0.62 and no flagged below it anywhere in the population, the within-band threshold design has no support; all flag-vs-no-flag contrasts become extrapolation, and I would only make claims about physically implementable policy contrasts (P2–P4), not about "the effect of the flag."
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** if suppressing or de-mandating the flag causes reviewers to treat borderline cases *more* slowly or defensively (e.g., they escalate more because the safety net of mandatory review is gone), the assumed monotone benefit of P1/P2 reverses — pilot compliance and escalation-rate monitoring would detect this and flip the recommendation toward P3.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** flagged cases are triaged dramatically faster (median wait 0.20 h vs 2.50 h) yet close far slower (16.45 h vs 5.75 h). The excess sits entirely downstream: 100% of flagged cases enter manual review and wait a median 6.75 h for it (vs 4/10 and 1.75 h unflagged), and 6/10 are escalated into a senior queue with ~7 h waits. Service times differ little; queueing and routing dominate. Flag status is confounded: it is deterministic in risk_score and correlated with complexity, and the one unflagged near-threshold high-complexity case (R20) took 19.2 h through the same review–escalation path.

**Plausible mechanism:** the flag operates as a policy bundle — priority lane (helps early), near-mandatory review (adds an occurrence effect), and volume that congests shared review and senior queues (adds a waiting effect and spills over onto other cases).

**Unidentified:** the split between occurrence-mediation and waiting-mediation; how much of the gap is underlying risk rather than policy; all natural direct/indirect effects, because of exposure-induced queue-state confounding, interference through shared queues, and near-zero overlap in flag given score.

**Most useful next evidence:** the randomized P2 pilot in the 0.64–0.80 score band with a 100% delayed audit. It physically instantiates the occurrence mediator, measures spillover on shared queues, bounds the quality cost of skipping review, and — whatever it finds — tells us whether to invest next in routing discretion (P2), review capacity (P3), or senior capacity (P4).
