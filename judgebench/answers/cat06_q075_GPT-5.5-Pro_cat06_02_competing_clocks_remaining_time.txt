## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Common branch arithmetic used in the scenarios:

At Friday 15:30, chemistry has only 2.5 clock hours before closing. With two analysts, that is 5 analyst-hours of remaining Friday capacity. However, the two jobs already in service consume 1.6–2.9 analyst-hours, leaving only about 2.1–3.4 analyst-hours for queued work before the Friday close. Under FIFO, B-417 is behind four samples, so it is very unlikely to obtain a usable Friday chemistry result. Under risk-priority, B-417 can probably move ahead of the three ordinary samples, but if the urgent sample remains ahead, B-417 still tends to start around 16:45–17:30, pause at 18:00, and finish Monday morning.

Microbiology incubation started Friday 14:00. The primary incubation becomes technically ready 46–54 h later, which is Sunday 20 September, about 12:00–20:00. If weekend authorization is granted, the recorded result can plausibly appear Sunday 13:00–23:00. Without authorization, the result waits for Monday staffing and is recorded around Monday 08:30–09:30. QA cannot work Sunday, so even a Sunday microbiology result does not permit Sunday QA.

The parallel join is therefore:

`join time = max(usable chemistry result time, usable microbiology result time)`

It is not the sum of the chemistry and microbiology branch durations.

**Smooth path.**

Chemistry waiting and service: Under risk-priority, B-417’s chemistry result is plausibly Monday 08:30–11:30. Under FIFO, the four-sample queue ahead makes Monday 12:00–17:30 more plausible, with a small risk of Tuesday morning if B-417 starts late Monday and has a long service time.

Microbiology incubation and readout: The clean primary microbiology result is technically ready Sunday 12:00–20:00. With on-call authorization, it can be recorded Sunday afternoon/evening. Without authorization, it is recorded Monday 08:30–09:30.

Parallel join: In the fastest smooth cases, microbiology is already recorded by Sunday or Monday 08:30–09:30 and chemistry finishes Monday morning, so the join is Monday morning. In FIFO chemistry cases, chemistry becomes the slower branch and the join moves to Monday afternoon. The join is not chemistry plus microbiology; it is whichever branch is later.

QA waiting and service: By Monday 08:00, QA has had 8.5 working hours since the current time: Friday 15:30–20:00 and Saturday 09:00–13:00. That capacity can process some or most of the current five-batch queue, but new arrivals average about three batches by Monday 08:00 and are overdispersed. A reasonable smooth-path QA wait is 1–6 working hours, with service commonly around 1–3.5 h.

Optional rework/investigation: None in this scenario.

Final release: Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 h after QA acceptance. Smooth completion is therefore often Monday afternoon/evening, but an overnight QA spill can put release on Tuesday morning.

**Chemistry rerun path.**

Chemistry waiting and service: The first chemistry result follows the same priority/FIFO split as above. A borderline result causes a rerun with priority and 1–3 h of additional lab work. If the first result appears early Monday, the rerun can finish Monday. If the first result appears late Monday, the rerun may hit the 18:00 chemistry close and resume Tuesday 08:00.

Microbiology incubation and readout: Unless there is also a microbiology anomaly, the microbiology result is usually already usable by Monday 08:30–09:30 or earlier via on-call.

Parallel join: The join is usually governed by the chemistry rerun rather than microbiology. A representative join is Monday midday to Tuesday morning, depending on whether the rerun fits into Monday lab hours.

QA waiting and service: A rerun is an anomaly for QA purposes. The prompt says that if a rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed temperature excursion exists, Deviation Investigation probability rises to 0.30–0.55. So even after the rerun result is usable, QA may not be a simple 1.5 h review.

Optional rework/investigation: Documentation query remains 0.15–0.30. Deviation Investigation is materially more likely. A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours. A lab investigation adds 3–7 calendar days.

Final release or rejection: If the rerun resolves normally and QA accepts, Electronic Release follows quickly. If the rerun is tied to a confirmed critical defect or adverse deviation, Batch Rejected becomes plausible, with 0.5–1.0 h recording after the defect is confirmed.

**Microbiology delayed readout or confirmation path.**

Chemistry waiting and service: Chemistry may be complete Monday morning under risk-priority or Monday afternoon under FIFO. In this scenario, chemistry is not necessarily the slow branch unless FIFO is severe or a rerun also occurs.

Microbiology incubation and readout: Without weekend authorization, a technically ready Sunday result waits until Monday 08:00 staffing and is recorded around Monday 08:30–09:30. That is a common delay but not necessarily a severe tail. If a contamination indication occurs, confirmation requires another 20–30 h continuous incubation plus 0.5–1.5 h readout. If the indication is found Monday morning, confirmation readout is mostly Tuesday 08:30–17:00. If on-call finds it Sunday, an optimistic confirmation can appear Monday, but late Sunday readout can still push confirmation into Tuesday.

Parallel join: For clean delayed readout, the join is Monday morning if chemistry is fast, or Monday afternoon if FIFO chemistry is slower. For contamination confirmation, the join is usually Tuesday, because QA must wait for the usable confirmed microbiology result.

QA waiting and service: A Tuesday join may face a different queue than Monday: some Monday backlog may have drained, but new arrivals continue at about 0.35/h during QA opening hours and are overdispersed. A normal Tuesday QA wait might be shorter than a Monday-morning burst, but anomaly-related investigation risk is higher.

Optional rework/investigation: A contamination indication raises investigation probability to 0.30–0.55, especially if the temperature-excursion flag is also confirmed. Documentation-only investigation pushes completion into Wednesday or Thursday; lab investigation pushes it into the following week.

Final release or rejection: A clean confirmation can still lead to release. Confirmed contamination or a critical deviation can lead to rejection, but the prompt does not give enough evidence to assign a precise rejection probability.

**QA documentation query or documentation-only investigation path.**

Chemistry waiting and service: Pre-join chemistry can be smooth, rerun, or FIFO-delayed. The QA tail can occur after any of these.

Microbiology incubation and readout: Pre-join microbiology can be clean or anomalous. If both branches are normal, Deviation Investigation probability is only 0.04–0.10; if a rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed temperature excursion exists, it rises to 0.30–0.55.

Parallel join: The join still occurs at the later of the usable branch results. The QA delay begins only after this join.

QA waiting and service: Initial QA may start hours after the join because of the five-batch starting queue, overdispersed arrivals, and variable review service. A Documentation Query adds 4–12 QA working hours before QA can resume. If the query is raised late Monday, even a moderate delay returns the case on Tuesday. A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours, commonly moving terminal completion to Tuesday or Wednesday.

Optional rework/investigation: This is the defining feature of the scenario. The important distinction is that these are working-hour delays, so evenings, Sunday, and non-QA periods create calendar-time gaps.

Final release or rejection: Most documentation-only tails still plausibly end in Electronic Release. Rejection is more tied to confirmed critical defect paths than to ordinary missing documentation.

**Severe correlated anomaly / laboratory investigation path.**

Chemistry waiting and service: A confirmed temperature excursion makes a chemistry borderline result more likely. If a rerun occurs, chemistry may move from Monday to Tuesday and creates a stronger QA deviation signal.

Microbiology incubation and readout: The same confirmed excursion also makes contamination indication more likely. If contamination confirmation is needed, microbiology commonly becomes the join-limiting branch until Tuesday.

Parallel join: In the severe path, the join is often Tuesday rather than Monday, because the usable microbiology or rerun-confirmed chemistry result arrives later.

QA waiting and service: QA investigation probability is high once any of rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed temperature excursion exists. Initial QA queue uncertainty still matters, but it becomes smaller than the investigation tail.

Optional rework/investigation: A laboratory investigation occurs in the minority of opened investigations, about 20–40% conditional on investigation. It adds 3–7 calendar days, so a Tuesday investigation start can move terminal handling to Friday through the following Tuesday. This is the main severe-tail mechanism.

Final release or rejection: Terminal event can still be release if the investigation clears the batch. Batch Rejected becomes materially more plausible if the lab investigation confirms a critical defect.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Temperature-excursion flag status** — mainly observation uncertainty, dependence uncertainty, and path uncertainty. It can simultaneously raise chemistry rerun risk, microbiology contamination risk, QA investigation probability, and rejection risk.

2. **Microbiology branch calendar and result path** — mainly calendar uncertainty and path uncertainty. Primary incubation is ready Sunday, but routine readout is Monday unless on-call is authorized. Contamination confirmation moves the usable result toward Tuesday.

3. **QA queue at the future join** — mainly queue uncertainty and service-time uncertainty. The current five-batch queue may partly drain by Monday, but overdispersed arrivals and variable review times can move B-417 from same-day release to Tuesday or Wednesday.

4. **Chemistry queue discipline and Friday close** — mainly queue uncertainty and calendar uncertainty. Risk-priority can put B-417 near Monday morning completion; FIFO can push chemistry to Monday afternoon or, with rerun/long service, Tuesday.

5. **QA query/investigation type** — mainly path uncertainty and calendar uncertainty. A short Documentation Query adds hours; documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours; laboratory investigation adds 3–7 calendar days and creates the severe tail.

The single most forecast-changing new observation is **whether the upstream temperature-excursion flag is confirmed or cleared**.

If the flag is cleared, I would reduce the correlated probability of chemistry rerun, microbiology contamination indication, and QA deviation investigation. The forecast would concentrate around the smooth/ordinary-QA range: roughly Monday evening to Tuesday midday, with Electronic Release strongly dominant.

If the flag is confirmed, I would widen and shift the forecast later. Batch Rejected also becomes more plausible, though still not numerically estimable from the supplied facts alone.

A secondary update trigger is **the primary microbiology readout result**: a clean readout collapses the microbiology branch to Monday timing, while a contamination indication immediately moves the join toward confirmation timing and substantially raises the QA investigation tail.
