The reconstruction is unusually careful and mostly numerically reliable, but it is not nearly flawless: one core flow-to-WIP identity is internally inconsistent, and that inconsistency is reused in the bottleneck narrative.

The case bounds, completed sojourns, imputation ranges, capacity denominators, and midpoint snapshots that I recomputed are coherent. Missing, open, and blank statuses stay distinct; station sojourn and WIP are correctly treated as invariant to the wait/service split; open elapsed work is included in busy time without being treated as a completion; and the restricted Little’s Law check is appropriately caveated. Format and PM4Py obligations are met.

The serious flaw is the “net WIP” column. Under the stated post-batch state, WIP change from the left snapshot to the right snapshot is arrivals in (left, right] minus departures in (left, right]. The table instead uses [left, right) flow balance and labels it net WIP. Those are not the same when events sit on a boundary. S2 moves from WIP 0 to 4 over 08:00–10:00, not +3, and from 4 to 4 over 10:00–11:10, not +1. Section 5 then says early S1 WIP “grows by seven,” while the same answer’s snapshots go from 1 to 7, and it says S2 “begins to accumulate” in a subperiod whose endpoint WIP is unchanged. The late S1 drain of five and S2 rise of two do match the snapshots, so the time-varying conclusion survives, but it is partly supported by a mislabeled identity.

Smaller issues: the Little’s Law windows end exactly on departures, which improves the residual and is not disclosed as an endpoint choice; the 12:00 S2 “one in service” state is midpoint-imputation-dependent and can look like idle capacity beside a queue; and the adversarial rounding envelopes are so loose that the reported 61–98% S1 utilization range adds little, even though that limitation is acknowledged.
