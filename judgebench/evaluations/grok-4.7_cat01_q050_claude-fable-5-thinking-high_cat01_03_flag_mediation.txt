7.3

The stage table is accurate and the causal framework is unusually careful, but several concrete errors and design contradictions keep this well short of nearly flawless.

Strengths that hold up under checking: flag/score separation is exact in these data (≥0.64 flagged, ≤0.61 not); the median table matches the timestamps, including support counts and the rule that absent stages are missing rather than zero; occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation; natural effects are rejected for cross-world dependence, exposure-induced queue confounding, and interference; required headings, estimands, pathways, and reversal topics are present; word limits are met.

Material problems:

- Section 4 states that M2 handles all high-complexity reviews. R17 is high-complexity and was reviewed by M1. That is a false data claim used as bottleneck evidence.
- Section 5 says the within-high gap “largely closes” against a flagged-high median of ≈21 h. Flagged-high cycle times are 16.4, 19.7, 24.1, 25.2, and 26.6, so the median is 24.1 h, not 21. Unflagged high also includes R17 at 8.6 h, which is omitted. About half the crude median gap remains. The inference that a confounder no stronger than complexity nearly nullifies the flag’s contribution is not supported by the cited contrast.
- Reversal condition 1 is logically reversed. If unflagged cases harbor severity that flagged cases lack, that does not imply flagged delay is genuine case difficulty.
- P3 is internally inconsistent: a 4–5 h review-wait cut cannot leave escalated flagged cases at ~22 h, because those cases also sit in the review queue (median wait 7.5 h).
- The pilot contradicts the answer’s own interference analysis. A 50/50 case randomization inside shared queues contaminates the cycle-time contrast and holds treatment fraction fixed within blocks, so it does not identify the spillovers the design claims to estimate. The audit covers only skipped discretion cases, yet the stopping rule compares miss rates with control. That comparison is not identified.
- P1 is treated as removing both the priority lane and automatic review. The prompt separates display suppression from automated routing; that ambiguity is not analyzed.
- The triage-service negative control differs by flag (0.60 vs 0.95 h) but is reported as if only the passing residual matters. The “flagged density” evidence is asserted while the same row says it cannot be confirmed, and arrival load is not flagged density.
- “0.25 h flagged-dominated” is the mixed high-complexity median, not the flagged-high triage wait (0.20 h).

The mechanism discussion and timestamp decomposition are strong. The sensitivity conclusion and the pilot—the two places that convert description into a recommendation—are not reliable as written.
