### Major deductions

**1. QA throughput is materially overestimated.**  
Using reviewer count divided by the **median** service time is not a “Little’s-law approximation.” Capacity depends on the **mean** service time, and the stated median/P80 imply appreciable right-skew.

For illustration, a lognormal matching median 1.5 h and P80 3.5 h has a mean around **2.5 h**. That implies roughly 3.4 completions over Friday’s remaining 4.5 hours plus Saturday’s four hours—not the answer’s approximately 5–5.5. With about three expected arrivals, Monday’s starting backlog could remain around 4–5 batches even before accounting for current in-service work. Consequently, “near-empty by Monday afternoon” and a 0–3 h waiting envelope are insufficiently justified. Overdispersion does not repair a systematically optimistic capacity estimate.

**2. The claimed doubling of joint-anomaly probability is mathematically wrong.**  
The illustrative conditional probabilities imply:
\[
P(R)=0.30(0.30)+0.70(0.13)=0.181,
\]
\[
P(C)=0.30(0.20)+0.70(0.07)=0.109.
\]
Thus the independence comparison using the **same marginals** is approximately **0.0197**, versus the mixture’s **0.0244**—about a **24% increase**, not roughly double. The answer compares against unrelated baseline marginals and repeatedly propagates the incorrect conclusion.

**3. S5’s probability is grossly inconsistent with its definition.**  
S5 requires confirmed excursion, chemistry rerun, contamination, and laboratory investigation, yet receives **3–8%** probability.

Using the answer’s central illustration, excursion plus both anomalies alone has probability:
\[
0.30\times0.30\times0.20=1.8\%.
\]
Requiring investigation and its laboratory subtype reduces that further; even using 0.55 and 0.40 gives approximately **0.4%**. The stated band might describe a broader laboratory-investigation tail, but not this specifically defined conjunction.

**4. The percentile-like ranges are not adequately supported.**  
Overlapping scenario bands are permitted, but the answer never converts them into a coherent mixture supporting its P50/P80/P90 claims. The optimistic QA estimate and inflated S5 probability further undermine those summaries.

There are also calendar-conversion mistakes:
- **78 h** is Monday **21:30**, not Monday 20:00.
- **95–125 h** spans Tuesday **14:30** through Wednesday **20:30**, not simply Wednesday.
- Tuesday 08:30 is **89 h**, not 88.5 h.

**5. The overtime sensitivity contradicts the downstream calendars.**  
The ledger says Friday/Saturday chemistry completion could pull the smooth terminal event approximately one day earlier. But microbiology is not technically ready until Sunday, and QA is closed Sunday. Accelerating chemistry ordinarily advances Monday completion by hours—not to Sunday.

**6. Rejection and rework assumptions are insufficiently grounded.**  
The assumed 30–60% criticality fraction and 90–97% release probability are not supplied or established. An assumed rejection branch is acceptable for sensitivity analysis, but these numbers are presented too confidently.

Documentation queries and investigations also lack explicit allowance for **return-to-QA waiting and resumed review service**, despite the required decomposition.

**7. Some updates and scenario boundaries are overstated.**  
Clearing the excursion does not “confirm” smooth Monday release or establish independence of remaining anomalies. S2 says “then normal QA” while simultaneously overlapping investigation scenarios. S5’s Wednesday earliest completion is inconsistent with a laboratory investigation taking at least three calendar days after Monday-or-later QA.

### Overall
Strong temporal reasoning earns substantial credit. However, the incorrect dependence calculation, implausible severe-path probability, optimistic QA capacity estimate, and unsupported quantile calibration prevent a high grade.
