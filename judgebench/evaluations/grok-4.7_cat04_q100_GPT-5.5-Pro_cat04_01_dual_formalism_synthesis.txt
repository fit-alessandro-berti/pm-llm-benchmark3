8.5

The models are substantively correct, but the write-up is not nearly flawless.

The process tree is a genuine conservative under-approximation: registration, containment/analysis concurrency, plan-after-contain, design-after-analysis, mandatory risk choice, optional supplier, binary repeat-until, and release/archive joins are all preserved, while approvals and supplier consultation are incorrectly delayed until both work products join. That loss is identified accurately, and A4 is a real discriminating witness. The POWL partial order directly encodes the cross-branch dependencies without duplicate labels; the loop, XOR risk route, silent supplier skip, and release join are the right constructs. Forbidden traces F1–F3 are correctly rejected, and the earliest violations are identified.

Deductions:

- A2 and A5 overclaim overlap. In A2, Regulatory Review follows Inspect Trial, so it does not overlap the trial cycle. In A5, Consult Supplier likewise follows inspection. The verdicts remain right; the rationales do not.
- Section 4 does not actually cover every intentionally incomparable pair. Contain/Design, Contain/Risk, Contain/Supplier, and Analyze/Plan are left implicit.
- D13 treats “failed Inspect Trial” as a source activity even though failure is only a positional abstraction.
- The phase-join paragraph slightly misstates its safety benefit: premature release is already blocked by the release join; the join’s real effect is excluding early approval/supplier interleavings.
- The validation plan names the right checks but does not specify a concrete replay/alignment procedure, and the self-critique mostly restates limitations already disclosed rather than residual risks in the POWL interleaving claim.
