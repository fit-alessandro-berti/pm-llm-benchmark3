The required section structure is present, but the reconstruction is not reliable. Several mandatory case bounds contradict the resource timelines, key station counts and summaries are wrong, uncertainty is not actually propagated, and the Python does not implement the requested analysis.

The special-case reconstruction fails its own constraints. P05 cannot start S2 at a time implying a 45-minute service: C1 is occupied until 09:45, so its wait is roughly 20–50 minutes, not 10–15. P07’s stated S1 wait and 30-minute service are mutually incompatible and overlap I1’s preceding case. P11’s imputed S2 interval overlaps P08 on C2; its feasible wait is about 55–80 minutes, not 20–25. P15’s elapsed S2 service at 14:00 is 30 minutes, not 80, and its S1 wait range contradicts the stated service time. P16’s censored S2 wait is 85 minutes, not 95. P22’s elapsed S1 wait is omitted.

Station results repeat that carelessness. S2 has 14 completions, not 17; two missing S2 starts and four S2 waiters, not three and three. The S1 waiting median is not 55, observed S1 service reaches 20 minutes, and observed S2 service has median 40 and range including 25, not median 45 and range 40–55. Reported busy minutes do not produce the stated utilization percentages. The WIP formula counts system WIP while the table presents it as station WIP.

The bottleneck claim is unsupported. Observed S1 waits already exceed observed S2 waits well before 10:55, and both resources remain heavily occupied after the 15-minute outage. Calling the S2-to-S1 switch stable across scenarios is not earned. Little’s Law, subperiod rates, rounding bounds, and completed-case sojourn times are named but not calculated. The code truncates the data, converts MISSING, OPEN, and blanks identically to missing timestamps, drops open work, and replaces the required pandas calculations with a placeholder.
