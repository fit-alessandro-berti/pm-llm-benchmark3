However, several estimands and design details are less well-defined than the answer suggests.

### Main deductions

1. **The waiting-mediated estimand remains operationally ambiguous.**  
   E5 proposes replacing the manual-wait distribution under a fixed review-occurrence regime with that under P3. But additional capacity changes resource assignment, service timing, peer queues, and possibly escalation—not waiting alone. Calling this well-defined “as a cluster-capacity intervention” establishes a capacity-policy effect, not automatically a waiting-specific indirect effect. A precise joint stochastic intervention and reference regime are still needed.

2. **Several mediation estimands lack explicit counterfactual contrasts.**  
   E4 and E6 describe replacing mediator distributions but do not fully specify the reference outcome regime, the conditioning histories used to draw mediators, or how downstream mechanisms respond. E6 also combines escalation occurrence and waiting. That is acceptable as a joint mechanism estimand, but it should not be presented as a uniquely isolated escalation-mediated contribution. These are promising estimand sketches rather than fully specified estimands.

3. **The causal graph sometimes treats measurements as causes.**  
   `arrival_load` is described as a coarse congestion snapshot, yet the adjacency list gives it direct arrows to subsequent waits alongside latent queue-state causes. Unless the recorded load label itself changes organizational behavior, it should primarily be represented as a measurement of congestion. The graph also omits the explicit within-case timing-to-queue-entry connections central to the explanation; the prose supplies them, but the requested mechanism graph should show them.

4. **The pilot insufficiently resolves shared-queue carryover.**  
   Cases take many hours and frequently cross shifts. Randomizing saturation by shift therefore does not create clean interference clusters. The answer recognizes this issue generally, but the pilot does not specify queue-level clustering, washout, or a concrete carryover exposure model. Moreover, its 25% versus 50% saturation design estimates effects within mixed-policy environments, not a clean full-P2 versus full-P0 policy contrast. That limitation should be explicit.

5. **There are small but meaningful inaccuracies about overlap.**  
   The risk-score ranges do not “barely overlap”: they **do not overlap at all**—flagged cases range from 0.64 to 0.93, versus 0.28 to 0.61 for unflagged cases. Statements that “resources are segregated by flag” are also too broad: triage resources are segregated, but review resources overlap. These distinctions matter for identification.

6. **Some diagnostics are interpreted too strongly.**  
   Disappearance of a flag–waiting association after adjustment for realized queue state is compatible with congestion mediation, but it does not establish it: queue state is post-treatment, and adjustment can introduce selection or remove other pathways. Likewise, the proposed future-volume negative-control exposure needs stronger justification against feedback and shared temporal causes.

### What earns substantial credit

- The numerical stage decomposition appears correct and properly distinguishes absent stages from observed zero durations.
- P1–P4 are meaningfully differentiated, particularly display suppression versus backend routing and incremental versus reallocated capacity.
- Occurrence mediation is separated from waiting mediation.
- Cross-world assumptions, treatment-induced confounding, interference, resource assignment, and quality tradeoffs receive serious attention.
- The Python meets the requested event-log and DFG requirements without fabricating optional stages.
- The answer appropriately distinguishes approval/rejection from decision correctness.

**Overall:** excellent process-aware reasoning and execution, but not nearly flawless. The main weaknesses are incomplete operational specification of mediated estimands and an interference-aware pilot whose actual assignment boundaries do not yet match the shared-queue process.
