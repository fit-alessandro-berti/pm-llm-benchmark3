**Grade: 6.0 / 10**

The core interval arithmetic is mostly correct. However, there are several verifiable numerical and logical errors, hedged classifications, and a code block that does not deliver everything it was asked to.

**What holds up**
- The clock-offset direction and the order of operations are correct. Resolution is applied before offset correction, so the true time is [R_low − b, R_high − a].
- The UTC intervals for OPS-BER, VENDOR-NY, SENSOR, HUB, MOBILE-LON, the T-404 inferred start and the T-409 weak-offset case all check out.
- The T-405 fold analysis is correct. Only the Fold 0, Fold 1, Fold 1 assignment survives, and the resulting durations (720–1080 s, 2040–2400 s, 2940–3300 s) are right.
- The SLA tolerance and zeta matrices are largely correct, including fine edge cases. Examples are T-403 TP1 at ζ=3 (1141.5 > 1140 s) and T-409 S1 at +2 min (1021.5 > 1020 s).
- The governance section is solid and adopts a sensible "enforce only on certain violations" policy.

**Errors and weaknesses**
1. **Numerical error in T-401 S3 and TP4.** The upper bound of the feasible duration is given as 86941.5 s. The correct value is 87001.5 s (09:00:01.5 on 06-02 minus 08:51:00 on 06-01). The classification is unaffected, but the stated interval is wrong and appears in several tables.
2. **Wrong counterfactual in Section 6 for T-401.** The answer says better NTP would shrink the duration interval to [798.5, 856.5] s and give a certain pass.
   - With zero offset, the minute-floor alone gives about [838.5, 901.5] s.
   - That still exceeds 900 s, so the interval is miscalculated and the certain-pass conclusion is wrong.
3. **Wrong counterfactual in Section 6 for T-407.** The answer says narrowing latency to [0, 5 s] would give a "deterministic pass." If receipt is near the send time, investigation start comes about 17 minutes later. That is a certain S1 violation, not a pass. The analysis never notes that the outcome flips at a latency of about 2 minutes, which is the obvious latency sensitivity.
4. **Hedged classifications for T-407.** The findings table gives "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION (or INDETERMINATE)" and "CERTAIN_PASS (or INDETERMINATE)" instead of committing to one primary classification and one alternative. The causal assumption (receipt not after start) is applied in the primary intervals rather than treated as a sensitivity case.
5. **Incomplete code.**
   - `SLA_RULES` and `classify_interval_sla` are defined but never used, so S1–S3 and the tolerance sweep are not computed.
   - The DST fold alternatives are built but then hard-selected, so the union is not carried through the analysis.
   - The "Critical Diagnostic Comparison" prints are hard-coded strings, not computed from the results.
   - The closing claim that midpoints "produce false positives and false negatives" is not demonstrated by the two examples shown. They show classification differences, not errors in the midpoint sense.
   - The PM4Py diagnostic output is mapped to cases by position, which is fragile.
6. **Partial handling of the T-406 entry-delay alternative.** Both interpretations are defined, but the alternative is only worked through for S2 in the discussion. Its S1 interval and its profile rows are missing.
7. **T-405 and T-409 causal assumptions.** The raw T-405 union appears only in a notes column, and the ingestion-order assumption gets no sensitivity check. The T-409 weak-offset case is truncated by a causal bound and classified POSSIBLE_VIOLATION, with no INDETERMINATE alternative given for such weak evidence.
8. **Thin Section 6.** Several entries are generic, such as the T-405 "monotonic sequence numbering" suggestion. The T-404 S1 flip is also not discussed in Section 4.
