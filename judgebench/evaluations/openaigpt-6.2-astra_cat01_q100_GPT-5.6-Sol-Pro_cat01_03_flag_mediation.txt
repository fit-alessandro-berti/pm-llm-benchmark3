**8.2/10.** The answer gets the central process-mining task right: the stage means and support counts are consistent with the timestamps, skipped stages are not treated as observed zero-length services, and the policy contrasts distinguish changing the display, routing, and capacity from changing underlying risk. The main deductions concern what the proposed mediation estimands and pilot would actually identify.

- **The escalation comparison needs the decision-point denominator.** Escalation occurs in 6/10 flagged versus 2/10 unflagged *arrivals*, but in 6/10 versus 2/4 *reviewed cases*. The first comparison matters for senior-team workload; the second shows how little evidence there is that flagged cases are more likely to be escalated **once reviewed**. Neither contrast is causal. This distinction is especially important because the objective explicitly asks to separate review occurrence from escalation.
- **The waiting-mediated effect is not fully defined.** E5 specifies a P3 waiting-time distribution but not a joint scheduling rule that generates feasible waits across cases while preserving the other relevant mechanisms. Randomizing incremental capacity identifies a *capacity-policy effect*; it does not automatically isolate waiting from changes in reviewer assignment, service, escalation, or peer outcomes. The adjacency list also omits the own-case link from triage completion time to the downstream queue state encountered, although the prose correctly describes it.
- **Two identification claims are too strong.** If workers infer flag status from the score, a randomized P1 experiment still identifies the effect of *hiding the display while leaving the score visible*. It does not identify the effect of making workers unaware of status. Likewise, an encouragement ITT identifies the effect of the encouragement, not necessarily the effect of removing the mandate. These distinctions matter even with flawless randomization.
- **The pilot is informative but not a clean P2-versus-P0 system comparison.** The 25% and 50% saturation arms can identify within-saturation assignment effects and differences between those saturation levels. Neither supplies a pure P0 queue environment. Cases visibly span shifts, yet the pilot does not specify how it will handle inherited backlog. Its quality gate also lacks an operational non-inferiority margin, and the 20% wait guardrail lacks a stated comparator.
- **There are concrete missed data checks.** The score ranges do not “barely overlap”; they are disjoint (maximum unflagged 0.61, minimum flagged 0.64). Resource segregation is exact for **triage**, not review. Moreover, R01 and R03 overlap on M2 from 18:18 to 18:42. That does not prove a logging error, but it makes the meaning of “service” and the capacity represented by M2 an immediate question, not just a generic future diagnostic.

A particularly useful missing result is the **descriptive additive accounting**: weighting optional-stage means by occurrence, flagged cases gain 2.59 hours through triage but incur 5.75 more hours of pre-review waiting per arrival and 2.90 more hours of pre-escalation waiting per arrival. That makes the timing reversal substantially clearer without claiming that changing policy would recover those hours.
