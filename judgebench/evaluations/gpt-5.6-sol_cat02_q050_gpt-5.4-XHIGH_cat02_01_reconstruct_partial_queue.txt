This is a strong, well-structured reconstruction, but several issues prevent a higher score under strict evaluation:

- **Rounding uncertainty is not propagated into the principal bounds.** The utilization, mean WIP, queue peaks, missing-start ranges, and censoring lower bounds are largely nominal-time results. For example, a reported 12:10 arrival can occur as late as 12:12:30, so P22’s actual waiting lower bound is approximately 107.5 minutes, not strictly 110. Durations ending at the exact 14:00 cutoff have roughly ±2.5-minute timestamp uncertainty, not the ±5-minute two-rounded-endpoint band.
- **The reported utilization ranges reflect missing-start scenarios but largely omit timestamp-rounding uncertainty.** Thus they are not full “plausible utilization ranges” as requested.
- **The Python is likely not reproducible with standard PM4Py APIs.** Common PM4Py versions do not accept `activity_key` in `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` commonly uses argument names such as `case_arrival_rate_column` and `case_finish_rate_column`, not those supplied. This could cause the required code block to fail at runtime.
- **The Little’s-Law check is weakly justified.** The selected interval is short, still transient, and the waits of cases starting in it were accumulated substantially before the interval. Its cohort definition is therefore not cleanly aligned with the interval-average queue and start rate. A finite-window boundary correction or refusal to apply Little’s Law would be more defensible.
- The claim that completed total sojourn shows **“monotone growth” is false**: P08 is 180 minutes and P09 falls to 155 minutes. The broader upward trend is valid, but not monotonic.
- The outage argument infers queue growth from arrivals and completions. Those quantities directly determine **total WIP change**, not queue change; service starts must also be considered. The claimed queue increase happens to be compatible with the schedule, but the stated reasoning is incomplete.
- Some precision is excessive or slightly inconsistent: S1 nominal mean WIP is about **5.78**, not 5.79, and exact peak claims should be qualified more explicitly under rounded-event ordering.
- The column named `total_case_sojourn_completed_only` nevertheless contains lower bounds for incomplete cases, which is a minor labeling inconsistency.

Strengths include excellent adherence to the heading format, explicit missing-start scenarios, correct distinction between waiting and service, inclusion of censored cases, mostly coherent case-level arithmetic, useful temporal rate decomposition, robust WIP reasoning, and a persuasive early-S1/late-S2 bottleneck interpretation.
