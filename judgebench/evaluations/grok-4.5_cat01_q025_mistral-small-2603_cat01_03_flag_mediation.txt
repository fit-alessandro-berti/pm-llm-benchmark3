## Section-by-section critique

### 1. Intervention definitions and estimands — weak / inconsistent
- Meets the minimum of seven rows and names an interventional framing, which is directionally appropriate.
- Then immediately violates that framing: `IE_escalation` is labeled a **natural direct effect** while the text says NDE/NIE cross-world objects are being avoided.
- `TE_cycle | P0 vs baseline` is **not well-defined** (baseline is not among P0–P4 and is never specified).
- Several contrasts are whole-policy total effects relabeled as “indirect” (`P2 vs P0`, `P3 vs P0`) without nested interventional mediators, stochastic interventions, or a clear decomposition target. That fails the “well-defined interventions” bar.
- “Decision quality” is never operationalized (reject rate ≠ quality; no false-positive/false-negative framing).
- Interference is barely reflected in estimand definitions (units are “all cases” / “flagged cases” with no exposure mapping).
- Main obstacles are generic; positivity, exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding, and shared-queue spillovers are under-specified.

### 2. Mechanism graph — incomplete and partly wrong
- Required variables are only partially used; important edges are missing or reversed:
  - `arrival_load → priority_lane` is unjustified; priority is policy/flag-driven.
  - `triage_wait → triage_service` confuses sequencing with causation.
  - Policy edge `flag → manual_review` is not cleanly represented; human judgment / residual risk paths are thin.
  - No explicit cross-case interference structure (case \(i\)’s routing → shared queue state → case \(j\) waits).
  - `underlying_risk` barely touches outcomes/decision; `risk_score` has no path to behavior except via `flag`.
- The “time-ordered diagram” is a flat linear chain, not a process-mechanism diagram with optional stages, queues, or capacity.
- The interference paragraph is acceptable but shallow on treatment–confounder feedback and time-varying queue state.

### 3. Stage decomposition — severe failure (fabricated / incorrect)
This is the worst section and alone justifies a large penalty.

**Hard errors vs the CSV:**
- Flag counts are wrong: data are **10 flagged / 10 unflagged**, not 13 / 7.
- Almost every subgroup row repeats the **same** numbers (12, 24, 420, 180, 300, 120, 60, 1092). That cannot be real medians/means across high vs normal load, complexity strata, or escalation vs not.
- Unflagged cases **do** escalate (R09, R20) and **do** get manual review (R05, R09, R17, R20). Treating flag=0 manual/escalation as blanket `NA` and implying no downstream path is false.
- Spot checks (e.g., R01 total cycle ≈ 19h42m, not a uniform 1092; unflagged cycles far above “156”) show the table was not computed from timestamps.
- Residual-to-close and path-dependent totals are not handled carefully for cases that skip stages.

The verbal claim “faster triage, slower downstream” is directionally right, but the **required timestamp decomposition is not credible**. For a process-mining task that emphasizes timestamps, this is disqualifying.
