### Major Flaws

1. **Invalid queueing throughput approximation (`1 / median` instead of `1 / mean`) for a heavily right-skewed QA service distribution**
   - In Section 1 and Section 3, the candidate models QA throughput as `reviewers / median service (1.5 h) = 0.67 batches/h` per reviewer, concluding that the 5-batch Friday backlog nearly clears over the weekend and leaves only `~1–3` batches Monday morning (`0–3 h` wait, central `0.5–1 h` morning, `~0` afternoon).
   - Given a median of `1.5 h` and an 80th percentile of `3.5 h`, the QA service distribution is strongly right-skewed. Even an exponential distribution matching those quantiles (`median = 1.50 h`, `P80 = 3.48 h`) has a **mean of ~2.16 h**, and a lognormal fit has a **mean of ~2.49 h**.
   - Using the actual mean (`~2.2–2.5 h`), a single reviewer on Friday evening and Saturday morning has a service rate of only `~0.40–0.46/h` against an arrival rate of `0.35/h` ($\rho \approx 0.76\text{–}0.88$). Across the 8.5 weekend hours, the expected net backlog reduction is less than 1 batch, leaving **~4.0–4.6 batches** in the queue at Monday 08:00 (before even accounting for arrival overdispersion).
   - With 2 reviewers on Monday (`~0.80–0.93/h` capacity vs. `0.35/h` arrivals), the backlog takes **~7–10 hours** to clear—meaning substantial QA congestion persists through **Monday mid-to-late afternoon**. Expected QA wait for a Monday 10:00 join is closer to **3–4.5 hours** (not `0.5–1 h`), and even a 14:00 join still faces **~1.5–2.5 hours** of wait, pushing many smooth FIFO-chemistry paths close to or past the Monday 20:00 QA close into Tuesday morning. Thus, both the S1 central range and the P50/P80 forecasts are noticeably too optimistic.

2. **Order-of-magnitude probability error in Scenario S5**
   - In Section 2, Scenario S5 is explicitly defined as: `Severe correlated tail (excursion confirmed -> rerun + contamination + lab investigation)` and assigned a probability band of **`~0.03–0.08`** (3%–8%).
   - Using the candidate's own Section 3 numbers (`P(excursion) = 0.30`, `P(rerun | excursion) = 0.30`, `P(contamination | excursion) = 0.20`), the probability of `(excursion + rerun + contamination)` is $0.30 \times 0.30 \times 0.20 = 0.018$. Multiplying by the conditional probability of an investigation (`0.30–0.55`) and the conditional probability that it is a lab investigation (`0.20–0.40`) yields **$0.0011\text{–}0.0040$ (0.1%–0.4%)**—off by more than an order of magnitude from `0.03–0.08`. (The `0.03–0.08` band would only make sense for `excursion OR any anomaly -> lab investigation`, which contradicts S5's explicit conjunction.)

3. **Mathematically inconsistent baseline in the dependence calculation**
   - In Section 3 (S5), the candidate computes $P(\text{rerun} \cap \text{contamination}) \approx 0.30(0.30 \cdot 0.20) + 0.70(0.13 \cdot 0.07) \approx 0.024$ and compares it to `~0.013` under independence, claiming dependence makes the joint event **"roughly double."**
   - However, the conditional parameters chosen in that formula imply *marginal* probabilities of $P(\text{rerun}) = 0.30(0.30)+0.70(0.13) = 0.181$ and $P(\text{contam}) = 0.30(0.20)+0.70(0.07) = 0.109$. The independent product of those marginals is $0.181 \times 0.109 = \mathbf{0.0197}$, not `0.013` (which came from unadjusted midpoints $0.15 \times 0.085$). Comparing a correlated joint probability with inflated marginals against an independent baseline with lower marginals overstates the dependence multiplier (`~1.22x` for those marginals vs. the claimed `~2x`).
