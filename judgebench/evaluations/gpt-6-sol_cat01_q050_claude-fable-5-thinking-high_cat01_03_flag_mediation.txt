**6.5/10.** The answer is unusually thorough, follows the requested structure, and largely gets the timestamp decomposition right. It also correctly distinguishes review occurrence from review waiting and treats shared queues as a source of interference.

Several causal claims warrant a substantial deduction:

- **P1 is misdefined.** Suppressing display of the flag does not necessarily remove automated priority-lane or mandatory-review routing. Much of the P1 comparison assumes those rules disappear.
- **Policy effects are mislabeled as mediation effects.** P2 and P3 can change staffing behavior and other cases’ queues; neither cleanly isolates an indirect effect through review occurrence or waiting.
- **The threshold-design discussion confuses overlap with regression discontinuity.** A deterministic flag on opposite sides of a score threshold is the basis for a local RD design, not a reason that design necessarily lacks support.
- **The proposed negative controls are not reliably negative controls:** the flag could change triage service or residual-to-close time.
- **The pilot conflicts with the interference argument.** Case-level randomization within time blocks does not, by itself, identify cluster-level spillovers.
- **Some interpretations overreach the data.** Start and completion timestamps alone do not establish true queue-entry times or complete queue states, and the review-capacity and cycle-time projections are more precise than the evidence supports.

This is a strong descriptive process analysis, but its intervention definitions and identification claims are not consistent enough for a very high grade.
