**1. Factual / numerical errors in the substantive claims**
- §4, reviewer pathway: “M2 handles all High-complexity reviews” is false. R17 is High complexity and reviewed by M1. M2 handling 7/8 escalated cases is correct, the universal claim is not.
- §4, priority pathway: flagged High-complexity triage wait is median 0.20h, not 0.25h. 0.25h is the *mixed* High-complexity median from §3.
- §5, sensitivity row: “flagged High median ≈21h” is wrong. Flagged High totals are 19.7, 24.1, 25.2, 26.6, 16.4; median 24.1h. The claim that the gap “largely closes” in High complexity is cherry-picking R09/R20 and ignoring R17 (8.6h) and the remaining ~6-14h gap.
- §3/§9: “service times differ little” understates triage service 0.60 vs 0.95h, a ~58% relative difference, and “most flagged excess travels through escalation” is asserted from a confounded escalated-vs-not contrast without qualification at the point of assertion.
- Word-count self-reports are wrong: §2 explanation is ~116 words not “≈150”, pilot is ~191 not “≈215”, §3 explanation is ~161 not “≈170”. A supposedly reproducible report should not misstate its own limits.

**2. Identification logic is internally inconsistent**
- §1 misuses “separable effects”: P2 (change routing) and P3 (add capacity) do not physically instantiate separable components of the *exposure*; P3 manipulates a downstream resource, not the flag. E3 and E6 duplicate the same interventional occurrence effect with different vague contrasts. E1 is labelled “total policy effect” of flag introduction but is actually P1-vs-P0 display suppression, a different intervention.
- §2 adjacency list is not a DAG: `flag (other cases) -> U_queue_state`, `manual_review, escalation, decision_outcome -> (audit/compliance, not timed)`, and a static list cannot represent the claimed treatment-confounder *feedback* loop. `complexity -> risk_score` contradicts the later treatment of `risk_score` as a noisy measure of `underlying_risk`, and the time-ordered diagram omits an explicit `senior_service` node and uses undefined `==>`.
- §5 threshold design: in band 0.55–0.67 unflagged cases are R17/R09/R20 all High, flagged are R15/R08 both Medium. There is no complexity overlap to adjust for. The proposed McCrary density test is meaningless at n=20. This destroys the claimed continuity/balance diagnostic and is never acknowledged.
- §5 queue-state reconstruction ignores parallel servers: T1–T4 and M1–M3 mean queue length is not recoverable from start/complete stamps alone for waiting cases with no resource assignment. Congestion vs. batching cannot be separated as proposed.
- §5 negative control fails on its own terms: triage *service* is proposed as barely flag-sensitive, but observed service differs markedly; only residual-to-close is cherry-picked as “passing”. Within-flag score variation is not a clean negative-control exposure because it tracks complexity/underlying risk.
- §5 mediation row proposes setting the manual *wait* distribution while holding occurrence fixed, although wait is undefined/truncated when review does not occur – exactly the problem §1 claimed to solve with separable effects.

**3. Policy and pilot contradictions**
- P1 mechanism is ambiguous: if the priority lane is triggered internally by the score threshold, suppressing display need not remove the lane. The answer assumes without argument that display = lane assignment.
- P1 review demand “~40–60%” and P3 wait “6.75 → ~2h” are invented numbers with no derivation from case mix or capacity.
- The chosen pilot randomizes *cases* within shared queues, contradicting §§2,4,5 where case-level randomization is said to be invalid under interference and cluster randomization is required. The control arm benefits from the discretion arm’s queue relief, biasing spillover.
- Pilot eligibility 0.64–0.80 in the observed data contains only Medium-complexity flagged cases (R02,R06,R08,R12,R15), despite “all complexities” claimed, and excludes most High/escalated cases that drive delay – generalizability is therefore minimal and unnoted. “Stop for success” is not a valid stopping rule.

**4. Format and code**
- Extra H1 title violates “exactly” the specified top-level headings.
- §7 code assumes `discover_performance_dfg` returns float seconds; PM4Py 2.7 returns timedelta-based statistic dicts, so `mean_secs/3600.0` with `:.2f` formatting will fail. The blanket claim “Verified against PM4Py 2.7.x / pandas 3.x: medians match” is therefore unsubstantiated and likely false as written.
- The comment on `insert_case_service_waiting_time` misdescribes it as a union-of-intervals calculation; PM4Py sums per-activity durations. In this sequential process the distinction is masked, but the explanation is wrong.
- Performance DFG edges are complete-to-complete times (wait + service conflated), never flagged as such, undermining the claim to decompose waiting vs service in the PM4Py output. Hard-coded `/10` denominators are also not reproducible in general.
