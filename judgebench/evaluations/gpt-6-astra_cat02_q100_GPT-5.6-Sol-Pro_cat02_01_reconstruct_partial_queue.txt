### Main deductions

**1. The Little’s-Law checks violate the stated departure-count convention.**

The formulas specify departures in \([a,b)\), but the Little-check code uses:

`(end > left) & (end <= right)`

That silently switches to \((a,b]\), including the final completion in each selected window. This materially improves the reported agreement.

Using the answer’s stated half-open convention:

- **S1 midpoint:** \(\lambda W = 355/235 \approx 1.51\), versus mean service occupancy of approximately **1.67**.
- **S2 midpoint:** \(\lambda W = 217.5/155 \approx 1.40\), versus mean service occupancy of approximately **1.73**.

Thus, the advertised approximately 0.03-case residuals are not consistent with the declared counting convention. A right-closed convention is not inherently invalid, but the switch must be disclosed, and the boundary sensitivity must be explained.

Moreover, a continuously nonempty queue does not itself establish a sufficiently stationary service subsystem. These checks are better presented as **finite-window occupancy reconciliations with explicit boundary corrections**, rather than strong evidence of Little’s-Law consistency.

**2. Important non-FIFO and non-work-conserving behavior is not investigated.**

Concrete examples include:

- P09 begins S2 before P08, although P08 entered that queue 15 minutes earlier.
- P17 begins S2 while the earlier-arriving P16 remains waiting.
- I2 finishes at 13:45 but does not start P22 before the cutoff, despite P22’s long wait.

The overtaking cannot be explained merely by five-minute rounding. These observations warrant discussion of priorities, eligibility restrictions, dispatching, or logging deficiencies. They also qualify a purely capacity-based bottleneck explanation.

**3. The PM4Py converter explanation is inaccurate.**

The comment characterizing `convert_log_to_time_intervals` as intended for pairing point/lifecycle events misdescribes its role. Direct pandas calculations are defensible here, but the justification should focus on the required station-entry semantics, missing starts, and censoring—not that characterization.

The case-level arrival/finish outputs should also be explicitly described as applying to the **selected complete, fully observed projection**, not the original arrival population.

**4. Some uncertainty presentation is stronger than its implementation.**

The rounding results are honestly labeled outer envelopes, which is good. Nevertheless, the code does not construct or validate jointly feasible rounded schedules. The enormous utilization envelopes therefore offer limited operational insight compared with a resource-feasible sensitivity analysis.

### Overall

The core reconstruction is reliable and demonstrates substantial process-mining competence. The main weaknesses concern **validation and interpretation**, not wholesale arithmetic failure. Correcting the Little’s-Law treatment and explicitly analyzing dispatching anomalies would substantially improve the answer.
