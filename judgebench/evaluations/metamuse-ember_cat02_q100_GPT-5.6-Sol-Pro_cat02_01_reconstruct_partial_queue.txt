The core reconstruction is numerically strong - I verified the missing-start intervals, waits, services, sojourns, mean WIP, busy times, rates, snapshots and Little calculations and they are essentially correct, with censoring and missingness properly separated. That prevents a low score. It is not nearly flawless, however, and the following defects preclude a very high score under strict grading:

**Minor but real inaccuracies / unclarities**

1. **A2 notation and scope:** the interval formula contains a double-comma LaTeX error `[-5),,D+5]`. More substantively, alignment of the external-arrival clock with the operational clocks, and exactness of the 10:55-11:10 maintenance interval despite the general rounding regime, are used but not labelled as separate assumptions. A ±2.5 min envelope for the outage duration is never considered.

2. **Marginal vs joint rounding:** case-level envelopes e.g. P07 wait 35-80 plus service 0-40, P05 wait 15-55 plus service 0-35, cannot be added jointly - their maxima exceed the sojourn envelope. The text calls station envelopes outer bounds later, but does not warn in §3 that the case ranges are marginal only. This can mislead a reader adding wait+service.

3. **Maintenance net-WIP statement:** “between 10:55 and 11:10 S2 receives P10 and P11… net +1 from immediately before” depends on counting the 10:55 boundary event and on “before” meaning before that batch. The snapshot table itself shows 4 → 4 from 10:55 post-batch to 11:10 post-batch, with no reconciliation of the apparent 0 vs +1.

4. **Little’s Law check overstated:** the windows 09:50-13:45 and 11:15-13:50 are post-hoc, queue-nonemptiness throughout is asserted not demonstrated, “quasi-saturated” is not established (mean in-service ≈1.67-1.73 of 2), and $\overline R\approx\lambda_D\overline B$ is close to a finite-window accounting identity given boundary-crossing services are acknowledged. It is a useful arithmetic check, not independent validation of steady-state assumptions.

5. **Column semantics:** `completed_case_total_sojourn` is repurposed for incomplete cases as “age at 14:00”. It is labelled in the cells, but the header/contents are inconsistent; a separate elapsed-age column would have been cleaner.

6. **Other presentation slips:** S2 median wait is given only as a scenario range 35-47.5 without its midpoint 40; notes such as “sojourn is fixed at 125/50 min” are reported-clock statements while surrounding columns include ±5 rounding, without restating that distinction in the note.

**Reproducibility caveats**

7. The Python is well-structured and largely runnable in intent, but relies on version-sensitive PM4Py signatures (`start_timestamp_key`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`), uses fragile `np.where` on pandas Series, `groupby(...).first()` for PM metrics, leaves `T0` unused, and pins no PM4Py/pandas version. No execution output is shown to prove the PM4Py calls succeed in the claimed environment.

In sum: excellent process-mining reasoning and almost all arithmetic survives checking, but the above assumptions, boundary, joint-envelope, Little’s-Law and code-robustness issues are enough, under hypercritical standards, to deny a 9.5-10 score.
