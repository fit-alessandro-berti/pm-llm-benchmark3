The strongest part is the parallel-branch analysis. The main deductions are:

- **The dependence calculation uses the wrong comparison.** The illustrative conditional rates imply marginal rerun and contamination probabilities of 18.1% and 10.9%. Independence at those *same* marginals gives a joint probability of about **1.97%**, versus **2.44%** in the mixture—not 1.3%, and not a doubling. More seriously, the stated **3–8%** probability for S5 cannot follow from that model: excursion **plus both** branch anomalies has probability **1.8%** before requiring a laboratory investigation.
- **Several time ranges do not match their clock labels.** Monday 20:00 is **76.5 h**, and Tuesday 14:00 is **94.5 h**, so the stated P80 endpoints do not match 78–93 h. Likewise, **95 h is Tuesday 14:30**, not Wednesday. S5’s Wednesday earliest terminal time is incompatible with a three-calendar-day investigation that cannot start before Monday. And S3 uses usable-result times as terminal times without allowing even the minimum release or rejection-recording time.
- **The QA estimate is too confident for the available evidence.** Dividing reviewer-hours by the median service time is not Little’s law and can overstate throughput. The calculation is useful as an optimistic backlog check, but it does not establish that Monday afternoon will be nearly queue-free or that waiting is bounded by three hours. The forecast also gives no coherent path-weighting calculation to connect its overlapping scenarios to P50, P80, and P90. A priority rerun may still wait for a busy analyst, and the documentation-query path does not explicitly budget the return to QA review.

The assumed 30–60% criticality fraction and the 2–8% rejection band are therefore unsupported. Clearing the excursion reduces the rework and investigation risks; it does not confirm a smooth Monday release or establish independence. S2 also says “normal QA” while claiming overlap with the QA query/investigation scenario, leaving its probability band ambiguously defined.

The broad conclusion—**Monday release on a smooth path, Tuesday or later with rework, and a multi-day investigation tail**—is useful. The answer earns credit for that decomposition, but it presents its quantiles and severe-tail probabilities with more confidence than its calculations justify.
