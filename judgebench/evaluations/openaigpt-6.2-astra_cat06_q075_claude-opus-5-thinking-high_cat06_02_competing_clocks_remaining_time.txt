**5.0/10.** The answer earns credit for its process model: it separates waiting from service, uses the maximum of the two laboratory completion times, and projects the QA backlog to the future join rather than assuming five batches will still be ahead. Its strongest insight is that a Sunday microbiology readout can advance confirmation testing but cannot enable a Sunday QA review. The numerical forecast, however, contradicts that model in several consequential places.

| Issue | Why it costs credit |
|---|---|
| **Calendar and chemistry arithmetic** | Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00 is **62 hours**, not 14. Most Monday clock dates are nevertheless correct, so this is not a 48-hour error in the final median. More materially, a mean service time of 2.83 hours is treated as a minimum when claiming that both newly started Friday jobs must pause. A job starting at 15:54 can finish at 17:24 and free an analyst for another job. In the stated priority case, B-417’s Monday residual can range from **0.2 to 4.0 hours**, not 0.6–3.6. |
| **Incorrect scenario earliest times** | The answer’s own S3 timeline permits an indication at Sunday 13:00, completion of confirmation incubation at Monday 09:00, and a recorded result at 09:30. With chemistry complete, **Monday release is plausible**; Tuesday 11:00 is not the earliest. Conversely, without on-call authorization, the stated latest confirmation readiness is Tuesday 15:30, which cannot cause the claimed Wednesday spill *because readiness fell after Tuesday 18:00*. An early Monday rerun or four-hour documentation query can also finish well before the table suggests. |
| **Median contradicts the cumulative forecast** | The answer puts only **40%** of terminal events by +78 hours but gives a primary P50 range of +71 to +78 hours and a point estimate of +74. Those claims cannot both hold for the same distribution. Calling Tuesday morning a “secondary P50 lobe” confuses a second concentration of outcomes with a median. Also, +78 hours is Monday 21:30, not 20:00, and +130 hours is Thursday 01:30, not late Wednesday. |
| **Dependence recognized but miscalculated** | A latent excursion state is a defensible shortcut. The numerical comparison is not: it uses a 0.07 investigation probability in the independence benchmark even though a known anomaly puts that probability at 0.30–0.55, **whether or not the two test anomalies are independent**. The claimed order-of-magnitude increase thus mixes two effects. At the answer’s central settings, its S5 intersection is about \(0.30[1-(1-0.30)(1-0.18)](0.42)(0.30)\approx1.6\%\) under conditional independence, not the reported 3–8%. |
| **Unsupported rejection and severe-tail forecasts** | No probability of a confirmed critical defect given an indication is supplied. The assumed 30–50% drives the 3–7% rejection estimate and should be presented as a sensitivity case, not a forecast finding. Nor does the process make pre-Wednesday rejection nearly impossible. The October tail also adds possible laboratory work *after* the stated 3–7-day laboratory investigation, risking double-counting. |
| **Inconsistent scenarios and updates** | S4 is defined as having normal test branches but is then said to overlap freely with reruns and indications. Its table gives a 20–35% query band alongside the supplied 15–30%. A clean microbiology result does **not** eliminate S5, which can arise from an excursion plus a chemistry rerun and laboratory investigation. Likewise, clearing the flag does not restore low investigation risk if either test is anomalous. |

The answer also puts a substantial preamble outside the required six-section structure. That is a minor deduction compared with the timing and probability errors. The smooth-path conclusion—Monday afternoon or evening, with a risk of slipping into Tuesday—is defensible. The reported median, confirmation-path bounds, and rejection odds are not.
