The required structure, missing-start bounds, and qualitative bottleneck story are mostly sound, but several core aggregates are arithmetically wrong, mutually inconsistent, and not reproducible from the supplied code.

The case-level bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are defensible, open service is distinguished from final service, and total sojourn for P01–P14 is correctly treated as imputation-invariant. The time-varying bottleneck reading and the decision not to treat the full window as steady state are also reasonable. Those strengths do not offset the quantitative failures below.

- P12–P14 S1 waits are given as 85–95, but P13 is 80 minutes (09:50–11:10).
- The listed P01–P14 sojourns sum to 2050, not 2055. The Little’s-Law figures 146.8 and 5.87 inherit that error; the identity itself is fine, the arithmetic is not.
- S1 station WIP is imputation-invariant and equals 2085 case-minutes, so mean WIP is about 5.8, not 4.3. The nominal peak is 8, not 7. S2 mean WIP is about 3.7, not 3.3.
- Mean active cases must be busy minutes divided by 360. Reported S1 active WIP of 0.7–0.8 directly contradicts reported utilization near 76% on two resources, which implies mean active WIP near 1.5. This sanity check was missed.
- Busy-time ranges are also inconsistent with the case services. Fixed S1 service plus P21’s 30 elapsed minutes gives about 535–605 busy minutes (roughly 74–84%), not 510–580. The S2 range 510–565 omits P17’s 5 elapsed minutes despite the text saying open work is included.
- Started-case mean waits are overstated: S1 is about 65–69 minutes across imputations, not 70–73. The latter matches adding P22’s censored 110 minutes to the numerator while still dividing by 21.
- The two-hour rate table has no single boundary convention. S1 counts of 7/7/6 put 12:00 in the middle bin; S2 counts of 3/5/6 put 12:00 in the last bin. External arrivals of 14 in 08:00–10:00 likewise depend on an unstated endpoint rule.
- Completed-service means do not “broadly overlap.” S1 is about 25–29 minutes and S2 about 34–38 even at the imputation extremes. That capacity difference is relevant and was dismissed incorrectly.
- Section 7 would not reproduce Sections 4–6. The pandas WIP logic is closer to correct than the prose, while the one-interval PM4Py projection forces case waiting time to zero and therefore does not have valid service/waiting semantics.

The stable conclusion that S2 holds the larger end-of-window backlog survives. The reported utilization ranking, mean WIP, peaks, rates, and sensitivity numbers do not.
