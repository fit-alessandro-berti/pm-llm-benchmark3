Other consequential problems:

- **Little’s Law:** There are 17 S1 arrivals between 08:30 and 12:30, not 16. Direct integration of S1 occupancy gives mean WIP about **6.77**, not 6.10. The interval contains both an arrival surge and the cessation of arrivals, and the answer neither matches the residence-time cohort to the interval nor accounts for boundary WIP. A defensible check would sum each case’s *clipped residence time* and compare it with the area under the occupancy curve, explicitly identifying this as finite-window accounting rather than steady-state validation.
- **Temporal interpretation:** The claimed S1 queue clearance at 13:30 directly contradicts P22’s waiting status. The requested subperiod arrival **and departure** rates are not supplied for both stations. The outage certainly removes 15 resource-minutes, but the data do not establish a precise bottleneck switch at 11:30 or isolate the delay caused by maintenance. One completion in 40 minutes also does not equal one case per hour.
- **Uncertainty:** The nominal missing-start brackets are useful, but the midpoint services of 15–20 minutes are not supported by the cited historical medians. The answer alternates between zero and five minutes as the minimum service without consistently distinguishing a bound from a scenario. It states the rounding rule but does not propagate it into the reported utilization ranges or qualify nominal censoring bounds against the fixed 14:00 cutoff.
- **Reproducibility:** The script hard-codes only the central scenario, never computes queue-length or WIP trajectories, and omits waiting-only station records from its station summary. It therefore cannot reproduce the sensitivity table, occupancy claims, or Little’s-Law calculation. The PM4Py projection sensibly excludes incomplete cases, but the code assumes `case:service_time` and `case:waiting_time` output columns rather than the usual `@@` enrichment columns, risking a `KeyError`. It also does not explain that first activity start is not external arrival, so projected case waiting is not total waiting from arrival.

The answer earns credit for correct completed-case sojourns, sensible resource-based nominal brackets for all four missing starts, and distinguishing elapsed open service from final service. Its qualitative picture—early pressure at S1 and later accumulation at S2—is plausible. The low grade reflects that the requested quantitative evidence for that picture is repeatedly wrong or not reproduced by the code.
