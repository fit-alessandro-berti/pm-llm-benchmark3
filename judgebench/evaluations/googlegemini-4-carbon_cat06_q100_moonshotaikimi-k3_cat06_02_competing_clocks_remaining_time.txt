### Detailed Critique

#### 1. Severe Queueing-Theory Error: Using Median Instead of Mean for Service Capacity
* **The `1 / Median` Trap:** The prompt specifies that QA service time has a *median of 1.5 h* and an *80th percentile of 3.5 h*—a classic right-skewed distribution (specifically, an Exponential distribution with mean $\mu_S \approx 2.17\text{ h}$ has median $2.17\ln 2 = 1.50\text{ h}$ and P80 $2.17\ln 5 = 3.49\text{ h}$; a Lognormal fit has mean $\approx 2.49\text{ h}$). In Section 1, the response sets QA throughput to `reviewers / median service (1.5 h) = 0.67 batches/h` and calls this a *"First-order Little's-law approximation."*
* **Why This Breaks the QA Forecast:** Both Little’s Law ($L = \lambda W$) and server capacity ($c / \mathbb{E}[S]$) depend strictly on the **mean**, never the median. With $\mathbb{E}[S] \approx 2.17\text{–}2.49\text{ h}$, a single reviewer processes only **$0.40\text{–}0.46\text{ batches/h}$**, not $0.67\text{ batches/h}$. Against an arrival rate of $\lambda = 0.35\text{ batches/h}$ ($\rho \approx 0.76\text{–}0.88$, exacerbated by substantial arrival overdispersion), a single reviewer over the 8.5 weekend QA hours (Fri 4.5 h + Sat 4.0 h) reduces the net backlog by **less than 1 batch** ($\approx 3.4\text{–}3.9$ completions vs. $\approx 3.0$ new arrivals, plus any batch already in service at 15:30). Thus, Monday 08:00 begins with **~4–5+ batches still in queue**, not the nearly depleted backlog claimed in Section 3.

#### 3. Flawed Dependence Probability Math (Section 3 & Section 5)
* In Section 3 (S5), the response attempts to quantify common-cause correlation:
  $$\text{P(rerun} \cap \text{contamination)} \approx 0.30\cdot(0.30\cdot 0.20) + 0.70\cdot(0.13\cdot 0.07) \approx 0.024 \text{, versus } \sim 0.013 \text{ under independence — roughly double.}$$
* **Conflating Marginal Shifts with Correlation:** Under the mixture parameters the candidate invented, the implied marginal probabilities are $P(\text{rerun}) = 0.30(0.30) + 0.70(0.13) = 0.181$ and $P(\text{contam}) = 0.30(0.20) + 0.70(0.07) = 0.109$. Under independence *with those same marginals*, $P(\text{rerun})\cdot P(\text{contam}) = 0.181 \times 0.109 = \mathbf{0.020}$, not $0.013$ ($0.013$ comes from multiplying different unadjusted base midpoints $0.15 \times 0.085$). Thus, true correlation in the candidate's model increases the joint probability by **$1.24\times$ (24%)**, not "roughly double"—the rest is purely an upward shift in marginal means.
* **Undefined Parameters:** The four conditional probabilities (`0.30`, `0.20`, `0.13`, `0.07`) appear out of thin air in Section 3 without being defined or justified in the Assumption Ledger.
