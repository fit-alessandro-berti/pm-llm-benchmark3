### 4. Several identification proposals are invalid

Particularly serious problems include:

- **No score overlap:** All flagged scores exceed all unflagged scores in this dataset. Score-bin comparisons do not establish comparable treatment groups. A local-threshold design would require substantially more data and explicit assignment and continuity assumptions.
- **Circular queue analysis:** Regressing cycle time on manual waiting time and interpreting coefficient attenuation as mediation is invalid. Waiting time is part of cycle time, is post-treatment, and is only defined for reviewed cases.
- **Invalid negative control:** Approval is potentially affected by the intervention, so it is not a negative-control outcome. Restricting to approved cases and then comparing approval rates is also incoherent.
- **No genuine interference design:** Effect heterogeneity by arrival load does not identify spillovers or prove interference.
- **Contradictory behavioral design:** The proposed test of changing reviewer behavior assumes reviewer behavior is stable across policies.

The sensitivity-analysis entry does not specify an implementable model or interpretable sensitivity parameters.

### 5. Policy recommendations and safety measures are unsupported

P1 is assigned “High” evidence strength and “Low” quality risk despite no observed P1 intervention. Its predicted FIFO triage and reduced review load do not follow from display suppression alone.

**Rejection rate is not decision quality.** Increased rejection among high-risk cases could represent improved detection rather than harm. The pilot’s quality stopping rule could therefore stop a beneficial intervention or miss a harmful increase in false approvals.

Individual randomization could provide useful evidence, but with shared queues it estimates effects under a mixed-policy allocation—not automatically the effect of implementing P1 system-wide. The answer does not define this distinction or adequately monitor unflagged spillovers.
