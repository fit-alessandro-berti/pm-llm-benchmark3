The submission is strong: coherent OCEL 2.0 semantics, thorough validation, required sections, and generally accurate flattening/OC analysis. It is not nearly flawless.

Main deductions

- Flattening is treated as a PM4Py `EventLog`. `for trace in flattened_log` plus `trace.attributes` will crash if `ocel_flattening` returns a DataFrame, which some versions do. `discover_dfg` unpacking is also version-sensitive and was not isolated the way the constructor was.
- The model is labeled “source-scoped,” but E08 is linked to O100 even though the narrative only says “Pack Package P2 with I2” (unlike E07, which explicitly relates to both orders). That is a defensible choice, but it is richer than the stated policy.
- M2 is tagged `completes_settlement` while the process calls M1 and M2 “partial payments.” The caveat is present, but the qualifier still overclaims.
- Log-skeleton and OTG predictions are thin or hedged (“if based on O2O…”). DFG traces are much more precise than the other required artifacts.
- `discover_otg` / `discover_etot` are used as mandated, but unlike the constructor they are not isolated; if those names are missing the pipeline dies after a valid OCEL is built.

What holds up

- All 17 events, 12 objects, payment amounts as event attributes, timezone-aware timestamps, denormalized E2O rows, and the required O2O predicates.
- Direct vs propagated relations are explicit; dispatch/confirm/payment are not silently attached to orders.
- Flattened Order/Package/Delivery/Invoice traces, shared-event copying (E06/E07/E11), and the weakness of Payment flattening are right for this relation policy.
- Item-level OC-DFG (I2 skips Reserve; P1/V1 as convergence points) and the “no unique OCPN” caveat are correct.
- Referential-integrity checks and the eight-plus failure modes match the brief.

Minor modeling/API nits and one real executability risk keep it well below a top score.
