9.0

The interval arithmetic, timezone/DST handling, classifications, and sensitivity cells are overwhelmingly correct and internally consistent. A few minor flaws keep it short of near-flawless.

Verified strengths: clock-offset direction, resolution-before-correction, CEST/EDT/BST conversion, the single surviving T-405 fold assignment, T-404’s full 18–25 minute back-inference, and the SLA versus zeta=1/2/3 profile distinctions all check out. Easy boundary mistakes are avoided, including T-402 S1 as a certain breach while its profile result is only possible, and T-408’s 55m03s profile edge as possible rather than certain. Midpoints stay secondary, missing `Alert_Received` stays indeterminate, and the required sections and columns are present.

Deductions:
- T-409’s reported Investigation→Containment upper bound of 43m01.5s is the unconstrained pairwise extreme. Under the stated causal conditioning that start is not before the alert, that extreme is not feasible; the ordered maximum is about 35m03s. Classifications do not flip, but the feasible interval is overstated.
- The code hard-imports PM4Py and passes `zeta` as a keyword without isolating that call, so a version or environment mismatch blocks the interval engine as well as the diagnostic adapter. `datetime.fromisoformat` on space-separated minute strings is also version-sensitive.
- Section 4’s material alternative models (T-405 common offset, T-406 entry-delay semantics, T-407 proxy sublabels) are not reproduced by the code, so the executable artifact is narrower than the prose.
