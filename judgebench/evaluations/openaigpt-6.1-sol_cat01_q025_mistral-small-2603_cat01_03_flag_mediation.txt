### 2. The estimands are not properly defined

The table repeatedly labels policy effects on mediator variables as “indirect effects.” For example, **P2’s effect on review occurrence is not itself an indirect effect on cycle time**. An indirect-effect estimand needs an outcome and a specified mediator intervention or distribution.

Other serious problems include:

- Calling P4’s effect on escalation occurrence a **natural direct effect**, contradicting the stated framework.
- Introducing an unspecified “baseline.”
- Calling P1’s effect on decision quality a direct effect without defining which pathways are held fixed.
- Declaring all contrasts well-defined despite unspecified P4 criteria/capacity and unresolved P1 automation.
- Equating suppressing flag display with “set flag to zero.”
- Treating Approve/Reject as decision quality without ground truth.

The framework paragraph does not supply the missing estimand definitions.

### 3. The mechanism graph misrepresents important causal relationships

The graph omits `decision_outcome`, direct flag-to-review routing, underlying-risk effects on downstream processing, and queue/resource mechanisms. It also omits waiting-time contributions to `cycle_time`.

The explanation incorrectly describes baseline risk-score/complexity confounding as **exposure-induced confounding**. Such confounding requires a mediator–outcome confounder affected by exposure. Reviewer responses could create that problem, but the answer neither specifies nor graphs it adequately.

The time-ordered diagram presents optional stages and intake characteristics as a largely mandatory serial chain.

### 4. The identification strategies are often invalid

Several proposed tests would not identify their claimed mechanisms:

- **Adjusting for manual waiting and observing coefficient attenuation does not establish mediation.** It can introduce post-treatment selection/confounding and part–whole associations with cycle time.
- Comparing effects across coarse arrival-load categories does not identify interference.
- Comparing approval rates is **not a negative-control outcome**: approval is plausibly affected by the policy.
- Conditioning on approval introduces outcome-based selection.
- The threshold design lacks an explicit threshold, sufficient local support, manipulation diagnostics, and continuity of potential outcomes.
- The sensitivity analysis supplies no quantitative model or sensitivity parameter.
- Reviewer stability is assumed while reviewer behavior change is the phenomenon supposedly being tested.

P1 need not restore FIFO or reduce automatic review: hiding a display does not necessarily disable automated routing. The claimed “High” evidence strength and low quality risk are unsupported.

The pilot also:
- Uses rejection rate as decision quality.
- Treats increased rejection among high-risk cases as necessarily harmful.
- Omits a clear treatment rule for automated flag-dependent actions.
- Does not distinguish a mixed-policy individual-randomization effect from full P1 deployment under shared-queue interference.
- Lacks independent accuracy/compliance assessment and sufficiently specified capacity safeguards.
