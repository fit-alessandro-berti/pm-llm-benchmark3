6.5/10. The POWL model is well structured, the code is largely plausible, and the witnesses correctly identify several dependency violations. The main weakness is the process tree: it is **substantially less faithful than necessary**. After joining the planning and design streams, a process tree could place the risk route, optional supplier consultation, and trial loop in parallel, then join them before release. That would preserve many valid interleavings rejected by this answer while remaining a conservative under-approximation. The claim that a tree cannot represent their overlap is therefore too broad.

There are also validation imprecisions. A converted Petri net may contain silent routing transitions beyond the supplier skip, so the proposed silent-transition count is unreliable. Token-based replay fitness is not, by itself, a binary acceptance test for the negative traces. Finally, completion-time traces demonstrate permitted orderings, not necessarily actual temporal overlap.
