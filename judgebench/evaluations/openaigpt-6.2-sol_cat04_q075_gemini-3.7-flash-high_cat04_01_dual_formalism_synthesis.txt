The core models are sound as design proposals: the process tree is a genuine conservative under-approximation, and the POWL dependency graph captures the intended finite, completed **visible-trace** language. - **The semantic comparison contradicts the tree analysis.** Section 3 correctly says that the tree forces `Consult Supplier` to wait for containment. Section 6 nevertheless calls supplier consultation “Exact” across both models, even though the row explicitly includes its timing. Only the at-most-once choice and its synchronization at release are exact. Likewise, calling the risk route exact is valid for the branch structure, not for its full timing: the tree delays approvals until containment finishes.

In particular, W-05 cannot establish that `Execute Trial` *starts immediately* after planning and design. The answer correctly acknowledges this limitation later, making the earlier claims avoidable.
- **The POWL explanation does not fully meet the “every pair” requirement.** It repeats the planning/design pair and omits several intentionally incomparable pairs, including containment versus approvals or supplier consultation, and design versus approvals. A concise grouping of incomparable pairs, explicitly distinguishing them from pairs ordered by a transitive path, would have satisfied the requirement. It checks duplicate labels only in the POWL net. Counting silent transitions does not verify their behavior, although the positive witnesses without consultation do exercise the skip path. The comment that soundness checking verifies **1-boundedness** also conflates distinct properties.

Likewise, the loop treats the final inspection as passing **by abstraction**; it cannot verify that the recorded inspection actually passed. The assumptions and self-critique recognize this, but the comparison table’s business claims go beyond what the models establish.

The central modeling choices and witness table are strong. The biggest improvement would be to apply the answer’s own visible-trace abstraction consistently in the comparison and validation sections.
