7.5

The construction is strong and mostly faithful, but the expected-analysis sections contain several concrete inaccuracies that a strict reading cannot overlook.

The in-memory model is semantically consistent: all 12 objects and 17 events are present, timestamps and amounts match the narrative, E2O rows are merge-derived from `events`, O2O directions are coherent, and the validation block actually checks uniqueness, orphans, denormalized activity/timestamp agreement, and duplicate relations. The source-faithful versus derived-relation distinction is explicit, and the required pipeline stages and section structure are all there. Flattened Order, Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment predictions are largely right, including the important point that O100 never sees e08.

The analysis is not equally reliable. The OC-DFG “Item lane” is stated as Add Item → Reserve Stock → Pack Package, but I2 is not in e06, so I2’s directly-follows edge is Add Item → Pack Package. That same missed variant should have affected the Petri-net discussion. The Order lane likewise drops the Add Item → Add Item edge that section 4 correctly reports. The OTG list omits Order–Package even though e07 relates O100/O200 to P1, while still claiming to identify notable absences. Calling e02/e03/e05 divergence because an order “fans out” into items misuses the term; e05 is a single Add Item on O200, and OC divergence here is repeated activities on one object (O100’s two Add Item events, V1’s two Record Payment events). The OC-DFG paragraph also treats e06/e07/e11 as nodes; those graphs are activity-typed, and Pack Package aggregates e07 with e08. Finally, `completes_settlement` for M2 is not a stated fact—both payments are described as partial and no invoice total is given—yet section 8 presents it as a narrative check, and section 1 never flags the assumption.
