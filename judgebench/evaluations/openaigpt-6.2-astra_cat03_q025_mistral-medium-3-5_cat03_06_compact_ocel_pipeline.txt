**4.0/10.** The four tables are substantially correct: they capture all 17 events, all 12 objects, the payment amounts, the shared reservation and packing events, and the stated O2O relationships. The activity-by-object-type audit also matches the E2O rows. But the pipeline stops before discovery, and several central predictions contradict the tables.

1. **The validation rejects the correct dataset.** After the left merge, `merged.isna().sum().sum() == 0` fails because `amount` is legitimately null for every nonpayment event. Null attributes are not evidence of a failed join. A reliable check would merge on the three matching keys with `indicator=True` and `validate="many_to_one"`, then assert that every indicator is `"both"`.

2. **The PM4Py setup is unreliable.** The standard class import is `from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL`; the proposed fallback does not resolve that issue. The two frequency helpers are not standard top-level PM4Py calls. The requested frequencies can be computed directly with `events["ocel:activity"].value_counts()` and `objects["ocel:type"].value_counts()`.

3. **The principal flattening predictions are wrong.** PM4Py flattening follows direct E2O rows for the selected type; it does not traverse `o2o`. The exact event-ID traces make the discrepancy clear:

   | Cases | Traces from the submitted tables |
   |---|---|
   | O100; O200 | `1,2,3,6,7,8,11,17`; `4,5,6,7,11,16` |
   | P1; P2 | `7,9`; `8,10` |
   | D1; D2 | `9,13`; `10,14` |
   | V1 | `11,12,15` |

   Thus **E6, E7, and E11** are copied across Order traces. Neither Order contains a dispatch, payment, or confirmation. O100 has a `Pack Package → Pack Package` edge, and neither Package has a confirmation. The Invoice trace *does* show two distinct payment events against the same V1, with a `Record Payment → Record Payment` edge; it does **not** establish how either amount was allocated between Orders. The Delivery and Payment predictions are correct.

4. **The object-centric interpretations confuse types, instances, and causality.** An OC-DFG edge connects activities along an object-type-specific lifecycle, not O100 or O200 to P1. The OTG is presented as a directed process chain with a “terminal” Delivery without justification. E7 touches **three types and five instances**, not four types. A discovered object-centric Petri net does not have a V1-specific place with arcs from O100 and O200 to M1 and M2, nor does this sample establish the claimed waiting and parallelism semantics. Even the interaction count overlooks D1, which interacts with P1 at dispatch.

5. **The modeling policy contradicts the implementation.** Section 1 claims dispatches and confirmations directly involve Orders and Items, and payments directly involve Orders. The rows encode none of those links. The implemented selective policy is defensible; the explanation is not. The final “sanity check” that both payments must appear in both Order traces would actually reject the implemented policy. Other claims are similarly overstated: copying a shared event inflates flattened counts but does not itself demonstrate concurrency, and adding relations does not necessarily produce a complete OC-DFG.

The answer earns substantial credit for the dataset and for including the requested discovery calls. The decisive deductions are for **nonexecution and incorrect interpretation of its own data**, not for choosing a sparse downstream relation policy.
