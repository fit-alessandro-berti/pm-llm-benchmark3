The process states *“M1 and M2 are partial payments settling V1.”* Both are partial. Encoding M2 as `completes_settlement` asserts full settlement and a known invoice total (never given). Task language allows either qualifier, but choosing “completes” invents a business fact. Both should be `partially_settles`, or the completion claim must be labeled as an extra assumption.

**2. Asymmetric packing E2O (policy tension)**  
e07 is linked to Orders because the text says so; e08 (Pack P2/I2) is not, though I2∈O100 and P2 is O100’s remaining fulfillment. Under a consistent “operational touch” rule, packing an order’s last item is at least as order-relevant as packing a cross-order package. The choice is defended, but it produces a distorted O100 lifecycle (one Pack, no second pack) that the answer treats as acceptable loss rather than a modeling smell. This weakens the “source-faithful” claim.

**3. OTG description blurs graph semantics**  
“Order–Order” and “Item–Item” edges are justified via multi-object events, while other edges read like O2O type pairs. PM4Py’s OTG vs interaction/co-occurrence graphs are not the same artifact. The insight is fine; the structural claim is underspecified and easy to mis-audit.

Task prescribed those names, so usage is fair, but assuming a DataFrame-like `interactions` without a type guard is brittle for “executable-looking” code. Minor than (1)–(2), still a quality ding.
