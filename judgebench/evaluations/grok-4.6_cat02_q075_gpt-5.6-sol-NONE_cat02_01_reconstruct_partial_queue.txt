6.5

The write-up is methodologically ambitious and mostly well structured, but it is not close to flawless. Definitions, missing-start bounds, and the bottleneck narrative are strong; several core numbers are wrong, internally inconsistent, or conceptually mislabeled.

**What is actually good.** A1–A12 are clear and appropriate (event order, rounding, resource-feasible missing-start intervals, elapsed vs final open service, 705-minute S2 denominator, Little’s Law as finite-window accounting only). The four missing-start windows are correct: P05 ∈ [09:45, 10:15], P07 ∈ [09:25, 10:00], P11 ∈ [12:00, 12:25], P15 ∈ [11:40, 12:15]. Station sojourn is correctly treated as invariant to start imputation. Open/waiting cases are not dropped from the state description. Maintenance is checked against the C2 Gantt and not over-attributed. Format compliance is essentially complete, and the Python block hits the required API checklist.

**Factual and arithmetic errors.** The 14 listed sojourns sum to **2050**, not 2055; mean sojourn is **146.4 min**, not ~147 with an identity built on 2055. The median of those 14 values is **145**, not 155. P13’s S1 wait is **80 min** (09:50–11:10), so P12–P14 is 80–95, not 85–95. The two-hour rate table cannot be reproduced under one endpoint convention: including 10:00 in the first bucket (14 S1 arrivals, 7 S1 completions) would put **six** S2 completions in 10:00–12:00 (P04–P07, P09, P08), not five.

**Station WIP / busy time do not match the log.** Fully observed S1 service is 505 min; plus P21’s 30 elapsed minutes and P07/P15 ∈ [0, 35] each gives **535–605** busy minutes (74–84% of 720), not 510–580 (71–81%). S2 is similarly shifted (~515–570 vs 510–565). More serious: S1 “mean active 0.7–0.8” is busy/720, i.e. utilization, not the number in service. With two servers, mean activity is busy/360 ≈ **1.5**. Time-weighted S1 queue from all wait intervals including P21 and P22 is about **4.1–4.3**, not 3.5–3.7; mean S1 WIP is therefore ~5.6, not 4.3. S2 queue area is in the right ballpark, which makes the S1 error look like omitted open/waiting cases rather than a uniform approximation. Section 4 would not match a run of Section 7.

**Python.** Parsing, scenario imputation, and pandas WIP are on the right track, and the completed-case PM4Py projection is correctly labeled case-level. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is called on data that are already intervals and then ignored. The printed tables are not shown, so the document’s metrics are hand numbers that conflict with both the raw log and the code’s own formulas.

**Net.** Process-mining framing, uncertainty language, and bottleneck interpretation would support a high score if the integrals, rates, and occupancy definitions had been checked against the case table. They were not. Under a hypercritical standard that treats internal inconsistency and a factor-of-two occupancy error as more than cosmetic, this is a solid but clearly flawed answer.
