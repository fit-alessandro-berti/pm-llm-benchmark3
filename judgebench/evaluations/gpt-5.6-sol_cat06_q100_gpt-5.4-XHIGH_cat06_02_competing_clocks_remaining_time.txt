1. **The microbiology-delay probability is materially wrong or poorly defined.**  
   Primary incubation always becomes ready Sunday. The table says the delayed-readout-or-confirmation scenario is “about 20–40% if plain weekend-readout delay is included,” which is inconsistent with the supplied authorization probability unless “delay” means only a terminal-impacting delay. That qualification is not made.

2. **Several “earliest plausible” scenario times are too late.**
   - A chemistry rerun can plausibly finish Monday morning: favorable priority could yield the primary result shortly after 08:00, followed by a 1 h rerun. Terminal completion could therefore occur around late morning or early afternoon, not necessarily 16:00–19:00.

   - A microbiology confirmation begun after an early Sunday on-call readout could also produce a Monday result. The table’s Monday 16:00–20:00 lower bound is not clearly justified.

3. **The microbiology confirmation tail is overstated.**  
   If the primary indication is discovered Monday around 08:30–09:30, adding 20–30 continuous hours gives technical readiness roughly Tuesday 04:30–15:30. The subsequent staffed readout should normally finish by Tuesday afternoon or early evening. The claimed “Wednesday-morning spill if technical readiness lands after staffed hours” is generally not supported by these bounds. Wednesday would require an additional unmodeled delay.

4. **Scenario probability bands are largely asserted rather than derived.**  
   Bands such as 35–55% smooth, 10–22% rerun, 6–15% confirmation, and 18–35% QA rework are plausible-looking but receive little arithmetic support. Because the excursion-related increases are unspecified, the answer should distinguish:
   - directly supplied baseline probabilities,
   - identifiable lower bounds,

   The 10–22% and 6–15% bands in particular create unsupported precision.

5. **The temperature-confirmed scenario is shifted too far right as a whole.**  
   Confirmation raises anomaly and investigation probabilities, but does not guarantee a rerun, contamination confirmation, or investigation. Labeling Wednesday–Friday as the central terminal range for the entire 20–40% excursion-confirmed regime likely conflates confirmation of the flag with realization of every downstream adverse path.

   The P50-, P80-, and P90-like ranges are plausible envelopes, but there is no approximate cumulative-probability argument showing why the thresholds occur at those points. The answer effectively labels broad scenario ranges as quantiles without demonstrating the mapping.
