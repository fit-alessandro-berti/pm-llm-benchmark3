ROLE
You are a process-mining researcher asked to explain why cases carrying a newly introduced high-risk flag have longer total completion times even though they receive faster initial triage.

OBJECTIVE
Separate plausible direct effects of flag handling from effects mediated through manual review, escalation, queueing, prioritization, and case mix. The flag is produced from a risk score and is not itself the underlying risk. Treat the intervention carefully: “set the flag to zero” may not be the same intervention as “change what the organization does after seeing the flag.”

The task is intentionally underidentified. There is no single correct mediation decomposition. Your answer will be evaluated by another LLM for well-defined interventions, process-aware mechanism reasoning, cautious treatment of cross-world assumptions, use of timestamps, and actionable intervention design.

PROCESS
The observed process is:

Register -> Triage
    -> [optional Manual Review]
    -> [optional Escalation]
    -> Close

Policy after the flag was introduced:
- Flagged cases enter a priority triage lane.
- Most flagged cases are then sent to manual review.
- Some reviewed cases are escalated to a scarce senior team.
- Unflagged cases may still enter manual review or escalation based on human judgment.
- Review and escalation queues are shared across cases, so one case's treatment can affect other cases' waiting times.
- The recorded `risk_score` is imperfect and may omit important case risk.
- `complexity` is assigned at intake by a separate rule and may both influence the flag and affect processing.
- `arrival_load` is a coarse snapshot of system congestion at arrival.
- Timestamps represent local clock time. A dash means the stage did not occur.
- Stage start and completion timestamps are both available for triage, manual review, and escalation.

INTERVENTIONS TO DISTINGUISH
At minimum, reason separately about these possible policies:
- P0: keep the current system;
- P1: suppress display of the flag but leave the underlying risk score and human information unchanged;
- P2: retain priority triage but remove automatic mandatory manual review;
- P3: retain current routing and add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases;
- P4: retain manual review but change escalation criteria or capacity.

Do not assume these policies have identical “direct” or “indirect” effects.
