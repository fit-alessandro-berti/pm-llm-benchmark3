**4.0**

The write-up is format-compliant and sometimes conceptually on the right track (K-337 is a decision-policy change; C1 is scarce; D2 substitution and assessment bunching are real spillover channels). Under a strict reading it is not a reliable counterfactual analysis. Process feasibility, temporal consistency, and minimal-change discipline fail in several places that the rubric treats as central.

**Process feasibility.** The most serious error is K-337. The case is high-risk and high-value, so a compliance review is still required after the latest assessment and before decision. The counterfactual path is `… → Assess2 → Decision`, and the derivation explicitly “eliminates … Compliance wait” and has M1 decide “immediately.” That violates rules 5 and 8. Suppressing `Request More Info 2` removes the third document/assessment loop; it does not remove C1 or four-eyes. The same omission reappears in the cycle-time claim (−6d / Monday 12:00 decision). A feasible path is Assess2 (Fri 10:30) → C1 (Friday afternoon or Monday, depending on the Thursday 5h C1 forecast and weekend) → Decision → Notify, with approve-with-conditions or reject—not a silent pass and not a skip of compliance.

**Internal contradictions and arithmetic.** K-104’s table puts C1 at Wed 10:00 and −12h; the derivation moves C1 to Tue 20:00. Those cannot both be the intended counterfactual. K-219 says D2 takes 8–14h from Mon 17:30 and then finishes Tue 20:30 (~27h). K-411’s table has assessment at Wed 02:00 and −10h, while the derivation keeps D2 finishing at the factual Tue 19:30 and starts assessment at Tue 19:30. The answer repeatedly treats invented clock times as if they were derived.

**Queue snapshots are mostly unused.** Monday 16:00 has D2 at ~5h, so D2 cannot start at Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30). Tuesday 08:00 has C1 at 16h, so a reserved final review is unlikely to start Tue 20:00 unless other work is displaced—the displacement is the point of the intervention, and it is not timed. Document check, not escalation, is K-104’s long pole (D1 overnight into Tue 05:40, A1 not until Tue 15:10). Early S1/C1 prep cannot pull assessment forward. The only honest levers are (i) S1 off the critical path, (ii) 1–3h saved on final C1 if the packet is usable, (iii) a somewhat earlier C1 start after Tue 19:10 if a slot was held. Guidance that preliminary work may save 1–3h is denied (“does not shorten final review”) even though the factual note is that C1 read from scratch.

**Weak or inverted causal stories.** K-219 misreads the resource note: D1 is faster on complex *corporate* files; this case is standard/handwritten. An earlier D2 start can still beat waiting for D1 if rework does not fire; stating “+8h” and “expected benefit: none” as the base case is not calibrated. K-411’s reversal test is backwards: an empty assessment queue is exactly when earlier readiness *would* shorten cycle time; congestion is when earlier readiness becomes extra queueing. Saying parallelization increases I/D *demand* is wrong—it is the same work, earlier and concurrent. “Wed 02:00” assessment assumes overnight servicing that is not justified from the snapshots.

**System-level analysis is directionally aware but mechanistically sloppy.** Reservation does not create new C1 demand for 100 K-104-like cases (they needed C1 anyway). The nonlinearity is holding C1 *before the case is ready*, idling certified time or jumping the queue. One C1 at 3–6h cannot absorb 100 high-risk reviews in a week; that hard capacity bound is gestured at, not computed. The 100-case write-up also slides into treating C1 as the bottleneck for *all* four policies; K-219 and K-411 are low-risk and do not use C1. D1/D2 substitution, avoided vs appeal-driven rework, assessment bunching, weekend external delay, and timestamp-vs-true-concurrency are all named, which is why this is not a still-lower score, but several signs in the capacity table are overstated or wrong (I/D load “increases”; A1/A2 jumping to 20–30h without a queueing argument).

**Judgment and ranking.** Ranking K-104 as most robust because it “smooths” the C1 bottleneck is the opposite of the reservation risk the prompt flags. The most dangerous *fast* intervention is K-337: it buys time by changing the decision rule on unresolved discrepancy in a high-value sanctions-like case. Calling K-411 “most dangerous” (workaround/audit) while ranking it #2 and treating K-337 as merely “low robustness” is inconsistent. Least-transportable = K-337 is a fair synthesis point; the rest of §7 over-generalizes C1.

**Other strict deductions.** Paths are schematic (`I/Doc`, missing `Notify`, little partial order). Expected outcomes mix ranges with unjustified point clocks. Best/base/worst bullets are slogans, not scenario math. The feasibility model collapses four different legal partial orders into one chain and under-describes originals, rework, and “preliminary ≠ final.” The assumption audit restates given rules (four-eyes twice) instead of auditing the load-bearing modeling choices (immediate D2 start, handwriting = complex, overnight assessment, C1 free Tue 20:00, compliance optional). Pilot rows exist but stopping rules are generic and do not trip on C1 starvation, appeal spikes, or false concurrency.

The skeleton of a competent answer is visible. The executed counterfactuals are not trustworthy enough to score higher.
