**6.8**

The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant causal assessment, but it is not nearly flawless. It correctly finds the crude-versus-stratified reversal, mostly refuses to treat stratification as identification, separates queueing from review service, and gives an actionable phased plan. Several conceptual and design errors keep it well below a high score under a strict reading.

**What is done well.** The executive verdict (`mainly confounded`, 65) is a defensible reading of the management contrast: Orion’s crude mean is 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h, yet Orion is faster in every complexity stratum (7.5 vs 9.6, 17.2 vs 19.4, 31.6 vs 35.0). Arithmetic in Section 4 is accurate and auditable (pooled-weight standardized contrast ≈ −2.5h; night vs day; compliance; wait/service; rework/appeals). The DAG uses the allowed names, marks supported vs assumed arrows, and notes rework/backlog feedback. The variable table covers confounder, mediator, collider, proxy, and completed-case selection. Section 5 includes the required hypothesis types with predicted observations. Section 6 has a small routing pilot and a queue/compliance-capacity phase. The Python block matches the spec (embedded CSV, crude/stratified means, stated pooled-complexity weights, overlap cells, n<3 warnings, variants, DFG, no fabricated timestamps). Residual uncertainties are specific.

**Material flaws.** (1) Calling `routing_rule` “instrument-like” is wrong: the rule *is* the assignment mechanism, not an IV. (2) The “front-door-like” test treats `compliance_required` as a mediator of group assignment. In this process it is almost entirely a case/policy attribute driven by complexity, not a downstream effect of Orion vs Nova; `compliance_performed`/compliance *wait* could be mediators, `compliance_required` generally cannot. (3) The randomized pilot is in a Nova-like equipoise stratum (medium, non-compliance, day/normal load). That is not the population management would move. Orion’s actual mix is mostly night, high-load, high-complexity, compliance-required, where positivity is essentially absent. The stop rule (“stop if Orion is worse”) is also misaligned with a proposal to move work *to Nova*. (4) DAG vs table disagree on `unobserved_reviewer_skill` (mediator downstream of group in the graph; possible confounder in the table). (5) Process semantics are incomplete: `wait + review_service` does not recover `cycle_time`. The residual is ~0.8h on the simple path and ~2.2h+ when compliance or Request Info occurs, so some compliance/other service is being missed. (6) Section 5 understates the authors’ own night comparison: within complexity, night is *faster* in all three strata, not merely “not consistently slower.” The standardized −2.5h is also presented more firmly than n=2 cells (Low Orion, High Nova) can support.

**Minor issues.** Graph prose drops explicit directionality; `compliance_performed -> review_service` is weakly motivated; `quality_outcome` is loosely called a collider; arrival load never gets its own numeric contrast; the executive’s total/direct/mediated sentence is slightly at odds with a “mainly confounded” headline.

**Bottom line.** Core causal discipline and evidence use are clearly above average, and the required structure is filled in. The IV/front-door mistakes, policy–pilot mismatch, and incomplete time decomposition are enough that a strict grader should not go into the 8–10 range.
