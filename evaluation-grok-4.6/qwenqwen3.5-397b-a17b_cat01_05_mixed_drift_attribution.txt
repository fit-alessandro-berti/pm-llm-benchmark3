**5.8**

The write-up has the right high-level story and mostly follows the required skeleton, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has a clear arithmetic error, several unmet hard constraints, inconsistent drift taxonomy, and a monitoring design that would systematically look in the wrong place.

**What it gets right.** March is treated as a semantic/provenance break (`Eligibility Screen`, duplicate `Open Case`, missing resources) rather than a process rewrite, and later months are not waved away as logging. Supplier Z is correctly tied to the May rework jump; queueing is separated from the modest Assess service-time change; July staffing and the priority rule are at least named as distinct from October mix; December is not credited solely to the campaign. Multiple mechanism-specific change points, rather than one global break, is the correct conclusion.

**Quantitative failures (costly).** Section 3 states Manhattan distance on the seven supplied rates and then reports March = 2, May = 14, October = 24. The actual L1 values are 3, 40, and 64. The Part A snippet would print 64 for January–October, so the prose and the code disagree. October is labeled “peak drift” even though November is worse on cycle time, complexity, and rework. Cycle-time shares (4.0 / 5.5 / 1.0 / 5.8 h) are forced to add to 16.3 h with almost no justification; Assess service time rose 0.6 h, not 1.0 h, and the residual “queueing” bucket absorbs every omitted effect, including priority redistribution the prompt says the monthly aggregate conceals. Relative-percent changes on small bases (missing resources +427%) manufacture drama the aggregates cannot support.

**Hard requirements missed.** The prompt asked for *at least two* candidate change points *each* for logging, behavioral, and performance drift; the answer gives one apiece. Persistent post-March label/resource defects, the May–July mapped-fitness erosion (0.90 → 0.86), the October `Senior Assessment` path, and the December reversal are unused. The monitor list never includes **outcomes**, which was explicitly required. Onset types never use “mixed” or “seasonal”; June 15 staffing and July 1 priority are collapsed into one interval, and September 10 supplier recovery is omitted as a test of the supplier hypothesis.

**Causal and conceptual slips.** Section 2 calls post-May movement “genuine behavioral drift”; Section 9 says rework is population/input quality, not assessor habit. Both cannot be the headline. Priority is listed as “evidence supporting” when the only datum is the rule’s start date. `Senior Assessment` was historically recorded inconsistently; the October jump (4 → 22 per 100) is underweighted as a logging alternative relative to mix and priority. Mapped fitness is treated as “process unchanged” recovery; the residual 4–6 point drop after mapping is the actual control-flow signal and is barely used.

**Process-mining / PM4Py rigor.** Discrimination leans on generic DFG/cycle/fitness checks and barely uses alignments, skeletons, or DECLARE. Part A does not validate the tables, embeds only a slice of the monthly data, and never computes a monthly DFG-fragment series. `format_dataframe(..., activity=..., timestamp=...)` does not match the PM4Py API (`activity_key`, `timestamp_key`). `filter_time_range` is called with invented keyword arguments. Duplicate `Open Case` removal is a comment, not a procedure. The change-point rule (`Δmedian_cycle > 3`) cannot detect March logging drift at all.

**Monitoring design.** Ten monitors cover most requested families except outcomes. Alert logic is a pile of uncalibrated “+2” / “>15%” / “>20%” cuts with a 3-month reference that still includes the March schema break. Year-over-year seasonality is proposed with only one year of data. The proposed *primary* population—standard, non–Supplier Z, post-mapping—conditions away the two largest known shocks. That is the opposite of a useful control: Supplier Z is a non-random regional subset, so the leftover cases are a biased “clean” series that will miss the drift the system is supposed to catch.

**Net.** This is a competent structured memo with the correct multi-mechanism timeline, not a rigorous diagnosis. The distance numbers, missed mandatory items, PM4Py API errors, and inverted primary segment keep it well below a high score.
