2.5

This answer has the required heading skeleton and a partial grasp of missing/open/waiting states, but it is not a defensible reconstruction. Core definitions, arithmetic, resource constraints, Little’s Law, and the mandated Python contract are all substantially wrong. Under a strict bar it is far from a high score.

**Definitions are already broken in §2.** S1 waiting time is defined as `S2_start − S1_end`, which is S2 waiting time. The same formula is then repeated for S2. S1 wait is `s1_start − arrival_s1`. That error alone makes the subsequent metrics untrustworthy, and it shows up in the table: P01/P02 S1 waits are reported as 0 instead of 5.

**The case table is full of elementary timestamp errors, including several that are not close.** Logged point estimates that can be read off the table include: P06 S2 wait 25 not 5; P09 S2 wait 25 not 0 (the note “zero queue wait” is false); P10 S2 wait 50 not 10; P12 S2 wait 45 not 5; P13 S1 wait 80 not 95; P14 S1 wait 95 not 105; P15 S2 wait 75 not 15; P16 S1 wait 100 not 115; P17 S1 wait 95 not 100 and S2 wait 65 not 5; P18 S1 wait 100 not 135; P19 S1 wait 100 not 110; P20 S1 wait 105 not 115. The WAITING_FOR_S2 elapsed waits are not merely off: P16/P18/P19/P20 are given as 245/230/195/175 against 85/50/35/15. P16’s 245 is the minutes-from-08:00 encoding of 12:05, i.e. a parsed clock value dumped into a duration column. Case-level sojourn times for completed cases are required and never appear.

**Missing-start handling violates the problem statement.** The task forbids silently replacing a missing start with a single value. P07 and P15 S1 service are both written as 25; P21’s 30 is elapsed service presented as if it were a finished service time. Feasible windows ignore the same-resource predecessor: I1 is occupied by P05 until 09:25, so P07 cannot start in `[08:55, 10:00]`; I1 is occupied through P13 until 11:40, so P15 cannot start in `[10:35, 12:15]`. A constrained reconstruction is roughly P07 start ∈ `[09:25, 10:00]` (wait ~40–75, service ~0–35) and P15 start ∈ `[11:40, 12:15]` (wait ~90–125, service ~0–35), plus rounding. P11 on C2 is bounded as `[11:10, 12:25]`, which collides with P08 (11:15–12:00). Under A2 the plausible window is after P08, about `[12:00, 12:25]`. The outage story that P11 was delayed only until 11:10 is therefore inconsistent with the same-resource calendar.

**A2 (FIFO, no overlap) is asserted and then contradicted.** P08 enters the S2 queue at 10:20 and P09 at 10:35, but P09 starts first (11:00 vs 11:15). That is station-level overtaking around the C2 outage, not FIFO. Rounding is mentioned in A1 and then almost unused: tables are point estimates with unjustified tenths (27.4, 39.6, 4.2, 3.8).

**Station metrics use wrong counts and a single undifferentiated 6-hour bin.** S1 completed services are 20, not 21 including P21 (P21 is open). S2 arrivals are cases that have left S1 (20), not 22. S2 completions are 14 (P01–P14), not 13. There are no arrival/departure rates by subperiod, no queue-length path, and no resource-level busy minutes. The simultaneous-event WIP convention is inverted: arrival-before-departure raises occupancy, it does not bound WIP from below. The 11:15–12:00 peak “7 S1 + 2 S2” is not supported; at 11:15 S2 already has two in service (P09, P08) and at least P10/P11 waiting. Combined utilizations 82%/65% are point values, not ranges, and 65% for S2 is low relative to near-continuous C1 occupancy after 08:30 plus C2’s busy blocks.

**Bottleneck interpretation fights the data.** S1 waits rise from ~5 minutes to ~100 minutes while I1/I2 show only 5-minute logged gaps (the rounding grain). That is not a “lightly utilized” morning. Both stations are congested; S1 is overloaded from early in the window (arrivals faster than two ~25–30 min servers), and S2 congestion worsens after the outage (P08’s 55 min wait, then P10 on C1, then a four-case S2 queue by 14:00). The outage’s main visible effect is delay of P08 from a possible ~10:50 start to 11:15, not a 5-minute P11 slip.

**§6 is qualitative and internally inconsistent.** Optimistic/central/pessimistic are named, not computed. Little’s Law is checked with `λ ≈ 0.2` cases/min (about 12/hour) and `W ≈ 70` giving `L ≈ 14` against “observed avg WIP = 3.8”, then declared to “hold best.” Window throughput is on the order of 14/360 ≈ 0.04 cases/min, not 0.2. Mixing completed-case W with all-case L, and calling a 4× gap a confirmation, is not a consistency check. Censoring remarks are also confused: P16/P18–P20 elapsed waits are lower bounds on final wait, but the numbers already used are large overestimates; in-window S2 completions do not “drop to 12.”

**§7 fails the explicit code contract.** `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank are all mapped to `NaN`, which the task forbids. Interval rows are duplicated (zero-length start event plus interval). Timestamps are integers, not datetimes, so `pm4py.format_dataframe` is not a meaningful log. There is no real station-level wait/service/WIP/scenario calculation—only `count(s1_end) * 25`. The required calls to `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are absent. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is invoked on a malformed frame. Printed output is not the reconstruction tables.

What remains is format compliance, a few correct early fully-observed cells (e.g. P03–P05 S1 point estimates, P07 S2 20/40), and the correct high-level idea that missing starts need bounds and that C2’s outage matters. That is not enough. The piece would mislead a reader about waiting times, WIP, utilization, the bottleneck, and Little’s Law, and it does not reproduce itself.
