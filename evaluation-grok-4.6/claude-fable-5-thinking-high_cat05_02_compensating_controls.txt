The answer is strong, structured, and largely faithful to the required headings and judgment criteria. It does not mechanically equate four-eyes form with objective success, avoids outcome bias on AP-208, and treats ED-1 as a conditional permission. The remaining defects are real and keep it short of near-flawless.

**Section 1** is the high point. Seven threat scenarios, with explicit prevent/detect/correct/fail mappings, match the control descriptions. One slip: O3 overstates that MG-4 is the “only” preventive control. An independent releaser who actually inspects the receipt is also preventive; FEP-1 is not merely formal.

**Section 2** defines a usable sufficiency test and correctly rejects “same expected loss” as the sole standard (tails, deterrence, accountability, ex-post bias, independence as a failure-mode change). The four equivalence grades are never applied case by case. Section 3 switches to `OBJECTIVE_*` without stating fully equivalent / partially equivalent / merely mitigating / ineffective. The test is specified, then left half-used.

**Section 3 classifications are mostly right**, and that is the core of the task:
- AP-202 / AP-208 `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` (including failed-but-lucky AP-208)
- AP-203 / AP-207 `OBJECTIVE_MET` on a complete ED-1 path
- AP-205 `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`
- AP-204 `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` despite distinct users
- AP-209 `INDETERMINATE` rather than a form-based pass

Defects in the case work:
- AP-201 is `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` with **High** confidence while the write-up admits m04 may have rubber-stamped. If review quality is unknown, high confidence in a partial-pass is inconsistent. Residual dollars being small does not make the control objective clearer.
- AP-201’s rationale also mixes “unjustified as ED-1” with “tolerable.” Those are different dispositions; the row never cleanly chooses.
- AP-206 calls m11’s approval **substantive**. The fact table only says m11 approved before release. Substance is documented for m02 and m15, not m11.
- AP-204’s “EUR 220,000 probable loss” overreaches. The log shows a fabricated receipt and late audit, not confirmed diversion or unrecovered funds.
- AP-202’s “all human checks post-settlement” treats settlement timing as known. Settlement is not in the event log; only non-recovery is stated.
- AP-208’s facts frame marks m21 independent. Only m04, m02, m11, and m15 are given as independent. Harmless to the FAILED verdict, still an invented attribute.
- ED-1’s L3+ grade is never checked. “All seven conditions verifiably hold” for AP-203/AP-207 is slightly too strong.

**Section 4** answers the requested timing question, then contradicts itself. A post-release, pre-settlement ratification is first “preventive with respect to the loss event,” then adopted as **hybrid (detective–corrective)** under “loss-anchored” semantics. If the anchor is loss and the transfer is still stoppable, the loss-anchored label is preventive or preventive-corrective, not detective-corrective. The AP-207 “substantial, not full, credit” rule also sits uneasily next to an unqualified `OBJECTIVE_MET`.

**Section 5** is generally careful (qualitative ranges, luck vs control on AP-208, receipt fraud vs extra eyes on AP-204). The AP-204 “independent releaser changes little” line underweights the collusion/shared-session mechanism the same answer treats as central. AP-209 then says pair rotation is the highest-value fix. Those two claims need a sharper split: rotation matters if the pair is the failure mode; extra eyes on the same packet do not fix O3.

**Section 6** meets the prompt: required monitoring families, contemporaneous evidence, and a clear alert ≠ proof distinction. Thresholds are labeled illustrative. Little to fault here.

**Section 7** is competent and unusually honest about `filter_four_eyes_principle` retention semantics. Required pipeline pieces are present and labeled as evidence, not a verdict. Code-level problems:
- `release + BusinessDay(1)` keeps the clock time. AP-201’s audit at 09:00 the next morning (facts: 25 hours later) fails that check even though it is within one business day in ordinary control language. The prose and the code can disagree on the same case.
- Vendor tenure is invented (AP-202 = 30 days, AP-205 = 5 years). Harmless to most flags, still not in the source table.
- The 37-payment u12/u13 history is the pairing fact that matters and is omitted from the facts frame; the code can only see two log cases.

The answer is a serious control-objective analysis, not a four-eyes counter. It is not tight enough on evidence vs inference, on one control-timing definition, or on the code/prose audit rule to score as nearly flawless.
