**3.2**

The write-up looks complete and uses the required heading skeleton, but both formal models are defective, the witness protocol is violated, and large parts of the commentary misdescribe the artifacts that were actually built. Under a strict reading this is a structured but technically unsuccessful solution.

**Process tree (deliverable A) is not faithful and is mischaracterized.** The compact expression is not a valid process tree and does not match the Python: it nests `Contain Material` and `Analyze Root Cause` twice and is not an operator tree. The code is a crude block approximation that (i) sequences the risk route and supplier XOR *before* `Design Corrective Action` and the trial, so approvals cannot overlap the trial and supplier cannot overlap design/approvals/trial; (ii) puts `Plan Verification` in a parallel sibling of the trial, so `Execute Trial` is *not* ordered after planning—the central non-block dependency is simply dropped; (iii) shares a single `tau` node between the low-risk sequence and the supplier XOR, which is an illegal two-parent tree. Parent pointers are never set, despite the stated API requirement. The declared policy is “permissive over-approximation,” but the construction is mostly sequential *over*-constraint plus one serious *under*-constraint (plan ↛ trial). The limitations paragraph then asserts the opposite of the code on several points: `LOOP(seq(Execute, Inspect), Adjust)` does **not** force Adjust to occur (that is standard `do`/`redo`); Release is sequenced after the parallel join, so the tree does **not** admit release before those branches finish; calling the approval-before-trial sequencing an “under-constraint” is the wrong direction.

**POWL (deliverable B) fails the representation.** `OperatorPOWL` and `SilentTransition` are imported and required “where relevant,” then unused. A `StrictPartialOrder` cannot encode XOR, optionality, or iteration: every node is mandatory and occurs once. The model therefore forces *all* of Supervisor, Quality, and Regulatory review **and** Consult Supplier on every case. The trial is encoded with `Inspect → Adjust → Execute → Inspect`, i.e. a cycle in a *strict* partial order, plus `Inspect → Release`, so Adjust is also mandatory after the “passing” inspection. That is not a POWL encoding of repeat-until; the correct pattern is `OperatorPOWL(LOOP, [body, redo])` plus XOR operators for risk and supplier. The compact POWL text repeats the same illegal cyclic PO. The concurrency paragraph is conceptually fine for *unrelated* pairs, but it sits on an invalid model.

**Witnesses do not meet the spec and do not match the models.** The required order is five allowed then three forbidden; the table is 4 allowed, 2 forbidden, 1 allowed, 1 forbidden. T6 and T8 are the **same** visible sequence with two different stories. No forbidden trace is a clean cross-branch violation (e.g. Plan Verification before Contain, or Execute Trial before Plan Verification). Labels in the table (`Plan Verify`) are not the model labels (`Plan Verification`). Verdicts are inconsistent with the constructed objects: T5 (`… Inspect, Adjust, Release …`) is rejected by their own `LOOP` (redo must be followed by another `do`); T2/T3/T4 omit reviews that their POWL makes mandatory; T6 is claimed as POWL-admitted “because paths are not mandatory,” which inverts `StrictPartialOrder` semantics.

**Comparison, validation, and self-critique inherit those errors.** Section 6 repeatedly swaps over- vs under-constraint and claims the tree “admits premature release.” Section 7 prefers POWL for the right *generic* reason, but not for *this* POWL. The validation plan is a plausible API checklist that was not executed and would not have caught silent-node aliasing, cyclic order, or identical T6/T8. The self-critique’s best point (risk XOR) is still wrong in detail: their POWL does not “admit mixed-risk traces,” it requires every review. The larger semantic failures—illegal cyclic PO, unused POWL operators, dropped plan→trial edge, shared `tau`—are not listed.

What remains creditworthy is only the dependency inventory, a recognizably motivated (if inconsistent) approximation discussion, and a trial-loop *idea* in the tree that happens to be closer to PM4Py `LOOP` than the prose admits. That is not enough for a high score when both executable models and the eight-trace contract fail.
