## Defects that should move the score a lot

**1. Required formulas are unreadable.**  
Section 1 writes `fork_release_c  arrival_c`, `fork_releasearrival`, `final_end  arrival`. Worked examples collapse into `queue 255=20`, `09:2509:15`, `09:2508:25`, `join_ready 540535`, `690680`. A deliverable whose defining equations are missing operators fails a basic clarity bar, even if a patient reader can reconstruct the arithmetic.

**2. The case table does not use the required column name.**  
The prompt requires `upstream_elapsed`. The table header is `upstream`.

**3. Downstream masking of *branch* improvements is assumed away, not analyzed.**  
The prompt explicitly asks whether final-station congestion masks branch improvements. The answer freezes `final_start - join_ready` and only discusses the reverse (CF3 making headline flow time look better). That is the less important direction.

The same timestamps already show that many F waits are *not* pure F1/F2 congestion:

- F03: F1 is idle from 09:28; `join_ready` is 09:45; `final_start` is 10:00. Fifteen minutes of wait with an idle resource.
- F04: F2 frees at 10:00; start is 10:10.
- F06: F2 frees at 10:30; start is 10:45.

So some downstream delay is occupancy and some is residual/policy delay. Holding downstream wait fixed therefore both overstates CF2 pass-through (F09 in particular: F1 is busy until 11:30, so a 12.5 min earlier join would likely be absorbed) and undercuts Recommendation 2’s claim that eliminating downstream wait saves 14.7 min/case. Section 7 then overstates the limit: queue vs policy is not fully unidentified; it is partially identified and the occupancy evidence was unused.

**4. CF2 secondary effects are incomplete.**  
The text flags F10’s shift to A and F06’s A/B tie. It omits F05, which also flips to A (B moves 12.5 min earlier, join only moves 10). CF1 F11 is also an exact A/B tie (`11:20` vs `11:20`), contradicting the implication that the only counterfactual tie is F06.

**5. F08 is mis-described.**  
C is not critical in F08 “because queue spikes” in the F04 sense. F08 is `C_queue=25` *and* `C_service=30` (the unique 30-minute C service). `25+30=55` vs A’s 50 is a 5-minute near-tie driven by both components. C queues are also not “one outlier”: 35, 25, 15, 10 are all elevated.

**6. Throughput and utilization are overclaimed.**  
Arrivals after F01 are every 15 minutes. Nothing in the log shows blocking or lost throughput. Shortening flow time is not the same as raising sustainable throughput. “A and C are already cheap” is also unsupported: A/B/C resources are not logged. A’s zero queue is consistent with slack *or* a dedicated resource; it is not evidence of cheap spare capacity.

**7. “Contribution to `join_ready`” is only partly delivered.**  
The prompt asked for average and tail contribution to `join_ready`. Mean/max queue+service is sojourn, not contribution to the max. The natural quantities — slack vs the second-finishing branch, or mean `fork_join_stage` conditional on being critical — are never tabulated. Near-tie margins are discussed in prose only.

**8. Smaller nits.**  
“A has the largest *service* time on average is false as a controlling metric” is broken English. A’s zero variance and F’s constant 20-minute service are observed but barely interpreted. Recommendation 2 still leads with capacity addition after the author has said the wait may be a release policy.
