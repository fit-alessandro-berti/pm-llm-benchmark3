This is a structurally complete, quantitatively careful answer that still falls well short of “nearly flawless.” The format is followed, the estimands are mostly well posed, staggered adoption is handled better than a naïve TWFE headline, median and p90 are not collapsed, and the rollout choice is the right one. The deductions below are for real defects, not missing ornament.

**Logical inconsistency on the fraud desk (most serious).** Section 2 correctly treats the shared desk as a SUTVA violation that can worsen *measured* control outcomes and flatter DiD. Section 4 then asserts that “fraud-desk waiting time is excluded from recorded cycle times, so true tail behavior may be worse than measured for everyone.” The prompt only says branch-level desk waits were not recorded separately. Cycle time is Register→Close; desk time is in the clock unless shown otherwise. That error is repeated in §5.5 and §7 (“unmeasured fraud-desk queue whose waits are invisible”). If waits are inside p90, tails are not invisible and the spillover story and the “hidden tail harm” story cannot both be true. This is not wording; it changes the tail conclusion.

**Stated identifier ≠ implemented identifier.** The primary design is sold as Callaway–Sant’Anna-style cohort/event-time contrasts that never use already-treated units as controls. The hand 2×2s versus Delta/Epsilon match that. The code is ordinary TWFE with binned event dummies, branch FE, and month FE. Under that specification, Alpha/Beta in July–August still help identify month effects when Gamma is treated. The writeup warns about naïve TWFE and then ships a close cousin.

**“Tracks intensity” overclaims the numbers they themselves computed.** Alpha event-time median DiDs (−3.6, −6.6, −4.0, −5.8, −8.1) and Beta’s (−1.6, −4.1, −5.5, −2.2, −3.4) are not monotone in `automated_fast_path_pct`. June is the high-load/staffing month; July–August is a common seasonal recovery. Calling Gamma’s two-point slope “the strongest internal replication” is too strong for a July–August rebound window.

**Arithmetic and labeling slips.** Control median deterioration of about +6.0 / +4.4 hours is the Apr–Aug versus Jan–Mar change, mislabeled as “April–June” (true Apr–Jun moves are larger). Beta’s crude dose slope is about −0.10 h per point, not −0.07. “High-complexity rose 5–8 pp in all branches” is false for Beta (+3 from January; it *fell* in the adoption month). Alpha load “~30 to ~43” is January versus June, not the May FTE cut. E3 says “more sensitive … than E3’s median analogue.”

**Capacity versus substitution is under-argued.** Fast-path share rises while May–June medians and p90s worsen under higher arrivals per FTE, then ease when volume falls. That is the central process question—reordering easy work versus creating capacity—and it is left mostly implicit in the staffing and substitution rows.

**Inference theater.** Confidence 60 on the median sits poorly next to “no statistically rigorous claim is possible from this panel.” Putting DiD contrasts in the `Observed:` rung inflates a design-dependent comparison into a fact. The scale-up rule (one newly treated branch, one control, −3% median DiD) is not an identifying design; it will ratify noise.

Section 1’s “absent from cycle-time attribution” is careful; later sections abandon that care.
