**7.3**

This is a strong, process-aware survival write-up that does the hard things well: estimands are defined before estimators, open cases are not dropped, competing events are not casually treated as censoring, and prefixes are handled as time-ordered process objects rather than baseline attributes. It is not nearly flawless. Several conceptual, factual, and internal-consistency defects are serious enough that a strict reading cannot go into the high 8s–10s.

**What is actually good.** Time origin, calendar-time scale, and τ = 72 are defined cleanly. The open-above-τ vs open-below-τ distinction is the right core insight and is applied to real IDs (S09/S11/S25/S26 vs S10/S15/S24/S29/S31). E1–E4 are operationally mapped, and the four worked cases (S03, S09, S06, S08) are mostly correct. The naive 4/13 ≈ 31% vs competing-risk E1 ≈ 70% contrast is the right diagnosis of completed-case bias. Immortal time on `Escalate`/`Rework` is explained with S13 rather than slogans. Transfer informativeness (all three are P1 + escalated) is correctly flagged as the P1-stratum landmine. The pooled AJ numbers (CIF_Resolve(72) ≈ 0.304, S_any(72) ≈ 0.38) are consistent with a careful risk-set calculation on the supplied table. Format compliance is complete, and the single code block meets the mechanical requirements, including a PM4Py path that does not leak placeholder timestamps into the clock.

**Conceptual error: T1 vs T3.** Section 2 claims T1 and T3 differ because T1 “removes S21/S08 from the risk set” while T3 “keeps them as permanent non-resolutions.” That mixes competing-risk AJ with Fine–Gray / composite-endpoint thinking. Under both T1 and T3, a transfer leaves the internal risk set and is not a `Resolve`. For E1 = 1 − CIF_Resolve(72) they are the same estimand; the code even implements T3 as `pass`. T2 is the coding that redistributes. The prose therefore misstates the one contrast it presents as central, then reports a three-way transfer sweep that is numerically two-way.

**Process model is incomplete relative to the log.** The Mermaid diagram omits at least two observed transitions: `AutoCheck → Review` (S15, S17) and `Rework → Resolve` (S27). For a section that is supposed to be prefix-derived, that is a process-mining defect, not a cosmetic omission.

**Sensitivity section is internally inconsistent.**
- The per-protocol B split lists 12 “compliant” IDs and four non-compliant IDs, and drops **S17** (`Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review>Resolve`). 12 + 4 = 16, not 17.
- Informative-censoring prose says S10 (gap 19) is the only below-τ stall that can move E1; the code uses `last_event_gap_h >= 24`, which **excludes S10** and includes only already-past-τ opens; the printout then names S10 as stalled anyway. The advertised E1 stress test is either vacuous or mis-specified, depending on which paragraph one believes.
- The “most numerically consequential” switch (withdrawal/rejection as censoring) is argued in prose and never computed.

**Uncertainty is discussed, not carried.** Headline figures are given to three decimals (0.304, 0.696, 0.292 vs 0.316) with no interval, SE, or bootstrap, despite n = 32, 13 resolutions, and a correct verbal claim that the A–B contrast is noise. E2 is printed as `cif_end − cif_72` as if CIF(∞) were identified; with four late resolutions and a heavily censored tail it is not. Saying KM is “exactly right” for E3 also sits badly next to the informative-censoring argument.

**Smaller factual/encoding slips.** “3 of the 6 P1 cases” is immediately corrected to 8 — sloppy, and P1 is the stratum they say is most fragile. The “observed non-resolution” tally omits S32 (unresolved at 72, transferred at 96). “Only 5 cases require extrapolation” understates that S08/S21 are assumption-driven at the horizon. T3 is not a distinct encoded scenario in code, so requirement (2) is only partly met in spirit.

**Net.** Estimand-first structure, case-level reasoning, leakage/immortal-time discipline, and a mostly correct hand-rolled AJ implementation are well above a median answer. The T1/T3 muddle, the broken stall/compliance sensitivities, the incomplete state graph, and point estimates without uncertainty keep it well short of “nearly flawless.”
