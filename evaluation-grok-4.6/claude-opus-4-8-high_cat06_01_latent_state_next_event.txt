**6.3**

The answer is process-aware in the right places and does not collapse into DFG copying, but it is not close to flawless. Several predictions are ill-formed, several `process_enabled` flags are wrong or unused, and a few probability ranges are internally incoherent with the very timing evidence the answer cites.

**What is actually strong.** C6-A vs C6-B is the best part: identical prefixes and the same triage resource are not treated as identical forecasts. High severity, injury, multi-device/same-lot linkage, and similar complaints correctly block Fast Close and open TI/RA/SA; the duplicate-like low-severity profile correctly points at post-review closure rather than the triage Fast Close shortcut. C6-C overrides the more frequent TI→Disposition DFG edge (119) with the country reportability rule. C6-D uses rule 7 and the worklist snapshot instead of treating missing evidence events as proof of absence. C6-E correctly lets a six-complaint lot signal override a nominally low-severity path. Completion-only logging is named throughout. The JSON shape, case order, and three-slot constraint are met.

**Task-form failures (not nits).** Three of fifteen predictions are disjunctions, not a single next logged activity: C6-D rank 2 (`Customer Evidence Requested or Evidence Received`), C6-D rank 3 (`Technical Investigation Completed (repeat/second pass) or Regulatory-to-Technical loop`), C6-E rank 3 (`Customer Evidence Requested (third cycle) or Technical Investigation Completed`). That is hedging, not a top-three distribution. It inflates coverage, makes the intervals uninterpretable, and violates “predict exactly three plausible next logged activities.”

**`process_enabled` is mostly decorative.** The write-up repeatedly claims to distinguish enabled work from the next observable completion, then marks `process_enabled: true` on 14/15 rows. The only honest `false` is C6-C Disposition. C6-A ranks TI and RA completions as enabled before Evidence Review has occurred, despite Evidence Received having only one listed successor (Evidence Review Completed, 356). C6-B marks Close — No Safety Issue enabled before review. C6-C marks Supplier Analysis enabled on a different-lot, unconfirmed-component signal. C6-E marks Supplier Analysis *Completed* enabled while the second package has been in hand for 40 minutes and review is still pending. Rank-2/3 items are often “likely soon,” not “likely next in the log.”

**Probability hygiene is weaker than the calibration note claims.** C6-A/B/C are essentially the same stencil (`0.45–0.70` / `0.10–0.30` / `0.05–0.20`); C6-D/E only shift the top band to `0.40–0.65`. That is not case-specific uncertainty. Concrete incoherence: C6-E cites 6–36 h Supplier Analysis and 3 waiting items, then still gives SA *Completed* `0.10–0.30` as the next logged event against a 1–5 h review that is the actual pending step. C6-C gives RA Completed `0.45–0.70` with 12 waiting, 2–14 h after assignment, and no worklist evidence it is even assigned (unlike C6-D). C6-A/B give Close/TI/RA non-trivial mass as the *first* next event, which requires skipping or hiding Evidence Review; the rank-1 text understands that problem and the rank-2/3 numbers ignore it.

**Case-level defects.**

- **C6-A.** Primary S3 is right; the unlogged-S4 alternative is fair. Supplier Analysis is over-claimed as mandatory: one complaint linked to two devices is not the same as “two or more apparently related complaints” on a lot, and the three similar complaints are not shown to share that lot. Rank 2 Close as next event at `0.15–0.35` is high if review is still the pending completion. “0 similar complaints…” is filed as contradicting evidence while it actually supports non-escalation.

- **C6-C.** Best obligation reasoning of the set, and Disposition correctly not enabled. Rank 3 Supplier Analysis is a stretch (different lots; rule 5 needs lot *or* supplier component). Unconfirmed root cause is listed as a material unknown, then not represented as a prediction (second TI / extra evidence). RA timing is optimistic relative to the 12-deep, 2–14 h queue.

- **C6-D.** Observation-model handling is the high point (attachment in source, R-R2 opened 12:40, migration 8–15% / ≤6 h). Rank 2 should pick one delayed event; given an existing attachment, Evidence Received is the coherent backfill, not a fresh Customer Evidence Requested. Rank 1 `0.40–0.65` is still high: assignment at 12:40 plus a 2–14 h clock means earliest plausible completion is ~14:40, and delayed evidence can still interleave.

Fast Close / no-investigation closure is correctly suppressed, but a third evidence cycle deserved its own slot rather than being glued to TI Completed.

**Cross-case section.** The prefix-vs-attributes paragraph is accurate and on-task. Ambiguity sources and DFG limitations are correctly described (observational, no attributes, no concurrency, no missingness). The calibration note is better than the numbers it defends. Unused operating evidence: the 16:00 notification batch is never applied (acceptable if no case is truly notification-ready, but C6-C/D are close enough that it should have been an explicit non-prediction). C6-A/B/E compete for the same two reviewers and four-item evidence-review queue at 14:00; that coupling is ignored.

**Net.** This is a competent process-monitoring write-up with real command of obligations, object linkage, and feed delay. Under a strict reading it is dragged down by disjunctive predictions, almost unused `process_enabled` semantics, templated intervals, and at least one timing contradiction (C6-E Supplier Analysis) that the answer itself flags and then ignores. Not a 5 — the control-flow reasoning is better than that — and nowhere near a high score.
