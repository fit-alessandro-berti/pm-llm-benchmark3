The answer is a strong, coherent policy design that passes the main quality tests: I-03 is refused on stage ineligibility rather than ranked by `p_now`; I-05 is suppressed as an unchanged repeat while I-07 is treated as evidence-changing but still not automatically given a slot; spillovers, calibration, and feedback loops are explicit. Arithmetic for the selected trio is auditable and internally consistent. It is not near-flawless.

**What is done well.** The decision rule is multi-criteria rather than a single probability threshold. Segment calibration is applied to alerting urgency without being naively converted into a larger causal effect. Direct-benefit intervals, swarm cost, false-alarm burden, and a spillover term are combined into tiers, and the adverse scenario is allowed to go net-negative. The allocation table covers all ten cases with defensible rationales: I-06 is held for imputation risk, I-08 is waited because the lower bound is negative, and one slot is reserved. Fairness safeguards are concrete (value-quartile cap, non-slot review, language-capable staffing, treatment logging, spillover incidence by group). Monitoring includes operational, predictive, causal, capacity, and fairness tracks with change triggers.

**Material gaps.**

1. **Operational sequencing is missing.** Two specialists deliver a 45-minute swarm and stop ordinary diagnosis work. Three selected swarms are therefore sequential, not parallel. I-09 has 2.5 h and I-02 has 3.0 h; the policy never states order, whether both specialists are tied up per swarm, or how 135 minutes of swarm time interacts with those deadlines and the residual diagnosis queue. That is a real operational hole, not a cosmetic one.

2. **Spillover monetization is too thin for how much it drives the conclusion.** The €15k proxy for six untreated diagnosis-stage cases is necessary, but there is no sensitivity on exposed-case value. Varying that proxy (or mixing in higher-value untreated work) can move base net from clearly positive to fragile and makes adverse much worse. Scenario variation on \(\Delta p\) does not substitute for that.

3. **I-09 is treated as Tier A too confidently.** The prompt flags Non-Native-Language Support as uncertain on *both* calibration and intervention effects. Using the supplied 0.18 lower bound as if it were a robust bound overstates identification. A better-calibrated alternate (I-01) is available; the write-up mitigates with language support but still places I-09 in the core three.

**Smaller issues.** Reserve option value is named but not even roughly quantified, so the release rule is circular. False-alarm burden is folded into the “Intervention cost” column. I-04 is intervened immediately despite 10 h remaining, Strategic overprediction, and a €2,000 false-alarm burden, without a wait-versus-now comparison. I-07’s 1.5 h clock makes later reserve-release largely infeasible; that should have been stated as a hard constraint. Public-sector non-monetary duties are thin. Several thresholds (0.05, 0.10, 60%, 70%) are invented without justification. Section 3 still treats the supplied ARR bands as usable causal numbers, with uncertainty handled mainly by scenarios rather than an explicit identification caveat.

No core case is mishandled and there is no single-threshold ranking. The remaining defects are specific enough that the answer is strong, not excellent.
