**7.1**

The write-up is a strong design note that avoids the usual failure modes (training only on the 21,600 mature activated rows, coding rejects/withdrawals/opens as non-default, using post-decision fields, or treating a random case split as confirmatory). Target semantics, selection, and as-of thinking are clearly present. It is not close to flawless. Several claims in the prose are statistically inexact, the primary estimand is never actually constructed, and the skeleton contradicts the design on material points.

**Target.** The all-application multi-state framing is a legitimate primary choice and is better than a convenience binary. The alternative (default | activated, mature labels only) is correctly distinguished from a counterfactual \(Y^{A=1}\). Two problems remain. First, a horizon snapshot \(P\{Z_i(H)=z\mid X_i(\tau_i)\}\) is **not** equivalent to a vector of cumulative incidences once `no_qualifying_event_by_H` is included. Still-open is a transient state, not a competing cause; calling Aalen–Johansen “equivalent” to that multinomial is wrong. Flattening reject / withdraw / transfer / activate-and-default / activate-and-survive into one absorbing-cause model also erases the required intermediate transition through activation, which the text itself says should exist. Second, operational defensibility is overstated. The default slice is \(P(\text{activate and default}_{90}\mid\text{prefix})\), not default risk and not an early-warning credit score. Under the observed policy, obvious rejects get a low default-component probability because they will not be activated. That inversion is mentioned only indirectly in §8 and is not treated as a decision-use constraint in §1. \(H=180\) and the “first three eligible events” landmark are also stipulated rather than derived (activation delay + 90 + feed lag is the obvious rationale and is never given).

**Cohort.** The 48,000-row table reconciles and refuses fake negatives. That is the core requirement and it is met. The 3,600 immature activated accounts are still lumped as “censored or selectively unobserved,” which collapses ordinary administrative immaturity, delayed feed, and possibly permanent missingness. There is no second flow for “mature for \(H=180\) after \(\tau_i\).” Many 2025 starts cannot reach \(\tau+180\), let alone activation+90+35, by 15 February 2026; those cases are not identified. Transfer linkage is approximate and then dropped from the implementation. Accepted-but-not-activated is not a first-class disposition.

**Censoring.** The five mechanisms are named and the testable / partially testable / untestable split is honest. The primary estimator is not a single coherent procedure. Cause-specific hazards + Aalen–Johansen already handle competing terminals; IPCW is for residual coarsening. Stacking both, while putting “observed follow-up” into the censoring model, is circular. Entering the 90-day transition “only when the label has matured” is closer to complete-case on the default arm than to a delayed-label likelihood; defaults are often recorded faster than survivals, so maturity is informative. Sensitivities (complete-case, MAR-IPCW, MNAR tipping, transfer as censor vs cause) are the right set, but they are listed rather than specified (weight model, delta grid, how bounds map to CIF).

**Prefixes and features.** Pre-decision cutoffs, preservation of document loops, migration-before-prefix, refusal of same-day `documents_total`, and separate event-count vs elapsed-time landmarks are all correct. “Eligible” / “information-bearing” is undefined. Tied timestamps are handled. Feature families cover the required axes and correctly freeze historical rates on training time. Queue/wait is only an inter-event proxy; that limitation should be explicit.

**Leakage table.** This is the most reliable section. The required elements are present, availability rules are as-of, and keep/repair/drop choices match the risks. `label_available_at` and `data_extract_time` are correctly barred from \(X\).

**Validation.** A start-time split, locked post-policy test, migration subperiod, rolling origin, maturity gating, and a real critique of random splits are all appropriate. The concrete test window is poorly powered for the default component: H2 2025 starts, with extraction on 15 February 2026, yield few activation+90+35 mature labels, especially after the October migration. Saying “leave them censored” does not make a locked default-cause backtest. There is also no in-regime held-out slice (train and tune are both pre-policy). Metrics (cause-specific Brier/IPCW, CIF calibration, coherence of state probabilities, AUPRC as a supplement) fit a competing-risk target better than a single AUC.

**Limitations.** Predictive association vs selection vs counterfactual is drawn cleanly, and deployment feedback (threshold shift, behavioral adaptation, staffing/queues, migration/linkage, collections intervening on the outcome) exceeds the minimum of three. This section is in better shape than the estimator that precedes it.

**Skeleton (largest gap).** The code shows the requested surface (`read_csv`, timestamp parse, `format_dataframe`, temporal split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, DFG/variants as diagnostics, a column-name leakage assert, a survival comment) and stays inside plausible PM4Py usage without inventing a survival API. It does not implement the study.

- \(H\) is never applied. `H_DAYS` is unused. `state` is status at extract, not \(Z_i(\tau_i+H)\). A rejection or activation after day 180 is still scored as that terminal. Censoring time and cause indicators are absent, so the “later connection” has no duration/event table to connect to.
- §4’s short-prefix protocol is reversed: only cases with \(K=3\) strictly pre-decision events survive the inner join. Early rejects/withdrawals — exactly the competing outcomes the target exists to capture — are dropped from the feature table.
- Transfer handling contradicts §1: any later `Account Activated` overwrites `transferred`. No linkage table.
- Inclusion “starts 2024-01-01–2025-12-31” is not coded; the filter is only `timestamp <= cutoff`.
- `prior_30d_event_count` counts the **same case’s** earlier events, not resource workload from a point-in-time historical table, and is not training-history-restricted.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` / DFG / variants run on the full as-of log, including Contract Signed, activation, and collections. That is not a prefix view; calling it “audit only” does not justify discovering control-flow on post-outcome events.
- The leakage assert is vacuous: forbidden columns were never selected. There is no check that `prefix_rows.timestamp <= prefix_ts`, no `documents_total` repair, no risk-score missingness flag, no versioned experience join.
- Raw `start_ts` / `prefix_ts` remain in \(X\), which lets a model key on calendar era beyond the intended policy/migration flags.

**Net.** Conceptually above a typical PPM write-up, and it would not produce the biased “defaults among approved accounts, randomly split” study the prompt warns against. Under a strict reading — honest estimand, as-of cohort, implemented target, no self-contradiction — the CIF/snapshot slip, the P(activate and default) decision gap, the immature H2 test, and a skeleton that drops early routes and never applies \(H\) keep it well short of an excellent score.
