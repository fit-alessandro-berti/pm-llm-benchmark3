**What is right (and why this is not a mid-range answer).**

**Defects that pull the score down.**

1. **Code does not actually deliver the audit it describes.** `get_variants` and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are called only to take `len(...)`. Monthly V1/V2/V3 shares—the central process evidence in the narrative—are not computed. Temporal features are a checkbox. `filter_time_range` is used for the “event-calendar” cohort without `mode="events"`; in common PM4Py defaults that filters traces contained in the window, which is the wrong object for a calendar-month activity/DFG readout. Duplicate counts are merged onto both cohort types by month string, so case-start rows inherit event-calendar duplicate totals.

2. **The key DFG edge is computed in a biased way and not flagged.** `median_part_lead_hours` on the event-calendar slice keeps only cases with both `Order Part` and `Part Received` in the same month. Cross-month waits are exactly what 130–155h leads produce. The prose never warns about this in the final code path.

3. **Table cells overclaim relative to the month-level numbers.** Concept-drift support says predicted risk “stays near 0.19–0.21 while observed matured evidence shows much higher breach risk” for July–October. July is 0.18 vs 0.19. October’s 0.23 vs 0.16 is not underprediction of a mature outcome. The narrative later gets this right; the summary table does not.

4. **A few analytical hedges are muddy.** Partner Z’s 0.45 is called “biased” without direction; given slower maturity and longer paths, the interesting hypothesis is downward bias, not a vague bias. June is allowed as population shift “if broadly defined,” which blurs a distinction the rest of the answer works hard to keep. September is labeled unsafe for training at 82% maturity—defensible conservatism, but blunt next to August “conditionally safe,” even though September is when Partner Z actually appears.

5. **Small presentation errors.** Mix identities are written without equals signs (`0.18*0.35  0.158`). Not fatal, but sloppy in a triangulation-heavy audit.

The diagnosis is the right shape and would be operationally useful. The monitoring design and risk section are coherent. The answer falls short of a high score because the required process-mining artifact is thinner and less careful than the prose, and because a few headline statements are less exact than the monthly evidence.
