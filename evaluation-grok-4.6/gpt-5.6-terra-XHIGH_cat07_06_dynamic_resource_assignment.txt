**6.7**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and unusually strong on explainability, socio-technical framing, and audit design. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, the policy and the actual batch contradict each other in several material places, the scorecard is never computed, and a few “clever” assignments create the problems the policy claims to prevent.

## What is actually strong

Sections 1, 5, and 6 are the best parts. Hard safety rules are separated from preferences. Historical handover/rework figures are treated as calibrated warnings, not automatic rules. The worker text is non-punitive and gives a real right of correction. The audit table covers every required indicator, adds useful extras (training outcomes, batch effectiveness, HHI-style concentration, association vs causation), and correctly refuses to treat rework as an individual scorecard.

The policy skeleton in section 2 hits every required component: eligibility, SLA urgency, skill match, continuity, workload/overtime, training, resilience, tie-breaks, and escalation. The C015 catch is genuinely sharp: Eva and Jonas have Security **0**, so the queue note about supervised training is infeasible. Quality batching rules, Security/Approval qualification, the four-assignment cap, and the 20% training arithmetic are all handled explicitly.

That is a high floor. It is not enough for a high score once the batch is checked against the policy.

## Material contradictions in the recommended batch

**Farid is assigned an infeasible load.** C002 (urgent, 2h), C011 (urgent, 4h), C010, and a C003 trainee task are four commitments for someone with 6.5 hours left and five already-open cases. The policy itself says an assignee must have enough forecast capacity to start or finish inside the SLA plan. Two urgent jobs with 2h and 4h remaining cannot both be true under that rule. C010 is the avoidable extra: Bruno already owns it, is skill 2 (legal finalizer), and the SLA is 20h. Moving it “to protect overtime” dumps non-urgent work onto the only person holding two burning SLAs. That is a first-order failure of the policy’s own priority order.

**David’s role on C003 violates the overtime rule.** David is `overtime risk = high`, 14 open cases, 3 hours left. Keeping ownership of a two-reassignment diagnosis loop is defensible. Making him supervise a skill-1 trainee is new work. The table’s claim that he “receives no additional unrelated work” is word-play. Supervision is work. It is also a bad training vehicle: the same answer cites >2 reassignments → 1.8× rework. Putting a trainee into that case to hit 22.2% is quota-satisfying, not safe development.

**Celine is silently triple-booked.** She keeps C007 (high, 6h, complexity 0.81, three reassignments), gives a “structured handover” on urgent C001, and supervises C009. None of that is costed against 6 hours and her existing six cases. The policy talks about substantive supervision; the batch treats supervision as free.

**Hours remaining are ornamental.** Eva keeps C005 (8h) and C016 (7h) with 5 hours left and eight open cases. Continuity on C016 is required by constraint 5 and is the right call. Not quantifying the clash with C005 is not. The algorithm advertises \(\widehat{Q}+\widehat{S}+\widehat{H}\) and then never produces a single number for any of the 16 cases.

**C001 is only half-justified.** Breaking high-complexity / high-handover continuity is allowed if SLA-infeasible. The 3.3h weak-skill penalty would have made that argument tight. The table does not use it. It just asserts a skill-1 Security path “would not be SLA-feasible.” Constraint 5 still prefers Celine + Gita/Hugo supervision if supervision is feasible. The specialist choice may be right for an enterprise 3h case; the write-up does not show the work.

**C013→Jonas is a leftover dump.** A 72h new eligibility case is given to the person already holding the quality batch and C014, which is what pushes Jonas to the cap. The table even lists crowding as a risk, then does it anyway. Celine or Amal were obvious slack.

## The algorithm is less operational than it looks

The score

\[
-w_L L + w_M M + w_C C + w_F F + w_D D + w_R R
\]

is published as an explainable scorecard, then abandoned. No weights, no component values, no rejected alternatives, despite section 2 requiring those records. Continuity is later coded as `1.0` or `-handover_hours`, which is dimensionally inconsistent with 0–1 skill and fairness terms. A 2.7h handover penalty can dominate or vanish depending on unstated scaling. That is not auditable.

Tie-breaking prefers “normalized workload, not simply fewer open cases,” but C013 is given to Jonas because he had the lowest **open-case** count. The prose and the batch do not match.

`legal_option()` rejects all Approval skill 1, while the policy text allows skill 1 to prepare with a level-2/3 finalizer. C010’s “trainee approval under supervision” note is therefore unimplemented in both the code and the roster.

SNA, roles, similarity, batches, service/waiting time, and performance DFGs are named correctly and used in a plausible audit sketch. They do not feed the live score. `discover_working_together_network` is rightly not treated as competence; that is one of the few places the PM4Py section is careful rather than decorative.

## Weaker comparison and explanation than claimed

Section 4 is directionally right and correctly says first-available is not deployable without a compliance wrapper. It never instantiates the two baselines on C001–C016. Without those rosters, claims about throughput, SLA, and fairness are essays, not comparisons.

Section 5 explains the *policy*, not *these* assignments. Workers living under Farid’s double-urgent queue, C001’s broken ownership, or C014 leaving David still would not learn why *their* case moved.

## Strict scoring

A near-flawless answer would have: feasible hours, OT-consistent supervision, numeric scorecards for the batch, instantiated baselines, and code that implements the same rules as the prose. This answer has the right architecture and several genuinely good judgments (C007/C016 continuity, C015 skill-0 refusal, Bruno/Iris protection, quality batching). It then overloads Farid, uses David as an unpaid supervisor to manufacture a training percentage, never computes its own model, and leaves hours-remaining unused.

That is a strong design memo with a broken dispatch list. **6.7**, not higher.
