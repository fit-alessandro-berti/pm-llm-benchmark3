**5.5**

The answer has the right skeleton—max-not-sum, calendar pauses, FIFO vs priority, excursion dependence, overlapping scenario bands—and would be a solid draft. Under a strict reading it is not close to reliable. Material process mistakes, internal contradictions, and broken hour-to-clock conversions would change operational conclusions.

**Dominant-clock error (the largest conceptual miss).**  
The rubric’s central insight is that the *smooth* path is a microbiology-plus-staffing problem with a weekend floor; chemistry is a spillover risk. This write-up inverts that. Incubation is 46–54 h from Friday 14:00, so a usable micro result cannot exist before Sunday midday, and without on-call authorization (the majority case) not before Monday 08:30–09:30. That is the structural clock. Chemistry then adds a residual 1–5 h, more under FIFO. Calling Scenario A “chemistry-dominated” treats the 3 h residual as the story and the 64 h weekend as scenery. The table also disagrees with the prose: the table says “else tie,” the decomposition says chemistry still wins without on-call.

**Contamination confirmation is modeled incorrectly.**  
An indication is observed at readout, not at technical-ready. Confirmation incubation of 20–30 h starts after a microbiologist has read the first incubation. The answer adds 20–30 h to Sunday 12:00–20:00 and gets a Monday-morning earliest usable result. That skips the first readout and, in the common no-on-call case, a whole extra overnight. Honest C2 timing is roughly: first read Monday 08:30–09:30 → second ready Tuesday morning to Wednesday afternoon. The leftover scratch line “central Sun16:00+25h= Tue?” shows the arithmetic was not finished.

**On-call time is double-counted.**  
The brief: once authorized, “dispatch and readout take 1–3 h.” The ledger uses that as a single block; the decomposition adds 1–3 h dispatch *plus* 0.5–1.5 h readout (Sunday 13:30–00:30). Same object, two models.

**Forecast-summary clocks do not match the stated hours.**  
This is the required decision table, and it does not add up.

- P80: 96–125 h is labeled Tue 15:30–Wed 16:30. Wed 16:30 is ~121 h; 125 h is Wed ~20:30.
- P90: 125–210 h is labeled Wed 16:30–Fri 26 Sep 18:00. Those clocks are ~121 h and ~194.5 h, not 125–210.
- Lab tail “Tue 29 Sep 12:00 (240 h)” is wrong by about a day (that clock is ~260 h; 240 h is Mon 28 Sep ~15:30).

Section 2 conversions are mostly fine; Section 4 is internally inconsistent. A forecast product that fails its own unit conversion is not defensible.

**Chemistry FIFO is too optimistic and incomplete.**  
Both in-service jobs finish Friday (0.4–0.9 h and 1.2–2.0 h, both < 2.5 h). Two waiting samples start and pause. Monday: finish those partials, serve the remaining two, *then* start B-417. Central start ~11:50–12:45, not 11:30–12:00. Long triangular draws (partial remaining up to ~2.7–3.6 h, then 4.5 h ahead, then 4.5 h on B-417) easily hit the Monday 18:00 close and spill to Tuesday 08:00. The write-up caps FIFO chemistry at Monday 17:30 and never opens a Tuesday chemistry path except via rerun.

**Earliest terminal is not earliest.**  
Monday 09:30–11:00 with “QA Q=0–1” is not a plausible envelope. Q₀ = 5, Friday has 4.5 h × 1 reviewer, Saturday 4 h × 1, plus ~0.35/h arrivals. Monday 08:00 queue is typically ~3–5, not 0–1. Two reviewers still leave a wait. True join under priority + min chemistry is ~08:36, but QA makes ~11:00–12:30 a more honest earliest. Scenario B’s earliest is wrong the other way: priority first-pass can finish ~08:36–10:00, rerun min 1.0 h, so a Monday-afternoon terminal is possible; “rerun started 15:00 → term 18:00–19:00” is a central FIFO story labeled as earliest.

**QA at the future join is asserted, not modeled.**  
“Q ≈ 2–4 from 5 − 2.3 Fri − 2 Sat + 2.7 arrivals − 2 Mon AM” mixes completions and arrivals across windows. More important: join time and QA wait are **negatively correlated**. FIFO chemistry joining Monday afternoon faces two reviewers who have been draining since 08:00; morning priority joins face the leftover weekend queue. Treating QA wait as an independent 1–3 h add-on overstates FIFO delay and understates Monday-morning congestion.

**Smaller but accumulating slips.**

- Working-hour example: query at Monday 15:00 + 8 QA hours is Tuesday **11:00**, not 09:00 (15:00–20:00 = 5 h, then 08:00–11:00).
- Smooth joint probability 30–40% is low. Even with the stated dependence, P(both labs clean) ≈ 0.75–0.80, times P(no query)×P(no clean-path investigation) is nearer 45–55%.
- Conditional lab probabilities (0.22–0.28, 0.12–0.18, etc.) are invented without a likelihood-ratio justification.
- Rejection “>95% release, <2–4% reject” is fabricated precision; the brief gives no critical-defect rate.
- Monday earliest described as “1-server QA min” when Monday has two reviewers.
- Scenario C is two different things, one of which (C1) is admitted to be Scenario A.
- Documentation completeness 0.82 is unused.
- Investigation type is correctly called the largest calendar swing, then ranked 5th without reconciling impact vs probability.

**What still works.**  
Assumption-ledger format, refusal to sum parallel branches, weekend chemistry/QA pauses, FIFO vs priority as a calendar cliff, overlap note on B∩D and C2∩D, excursion as a common cause rather than independent Bernoulli draws, and choosing the excursion flag as the highest-value next observation are all the right moves. Those keep this well above a failing forecast. They do not offset skipped process steps, a reversed smooth-path clock, and a summary table that disagrees with itself.

A nearly flawless answer would have said: microbiology plus the staffing calendar sets the Monday floor; chemistry discipline decides whether join is Monday morning or afternoon (with a Tuesday spillover tail); QA wait shrinks as join slips later; confirmation incubation is gated on the first readout; every reported hour would match its clock. This draft is not that answer.
