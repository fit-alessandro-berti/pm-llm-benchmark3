**4.5**

The write-up has the required eight-heading shape and a basically right qualitative story (weekday/weekend split, mix shift around 20 Apr, 15 Apr outage as non-stationary, 29 Apr staffing change, do not treat median service as flow time). That is not enough. Under a strict reading the answer is compromised by wrong numbers, an incomplete segmentation, and Python that cannot reproduce the claims.

**Segmentation does not do what the task asked.** Recovery is named (“Outage & recovery”) but only 15 Apr is placed there. 16 Apr (129 completions, `staff_fte` 7.0, WIP 52) and 17 Apr (112 completions, 6.5 FTE, WIP 35) are the recovery window; they sit in no primary regime. 14 Apr is also orphaned even though it is still a pre-shift weekday (`complex_pct` 24). Pooling all weekends hides the same mix break the weekday split is built on (early weekends ~21–24% complex vs late ~39–42%). That is a serious alternative they describe later, not a reason to treat weekends as one stationary regime in the primary table.

**Many published figures are simply wrong.** High-complex `staff_fte` is 5.93, not 5.86; `complex_pct` 44.6, not 45.3; `peak_08_10_pct` 45.9, not 45.3; 27 Apr p90 is 88, not 84. Weekend median service is 32.0 min, not 30.9; p90 68.3, not 71.0; `complex_pct` 31.6, not 30.4; FTE 2.63, not 2.56. Section 6 calls the high-complex window both 7 days and “9 days.” Completion Fano/CV figures in §5 do not match the hourly vectors printed in the same section (7 Apr completions Fano is ~1.56, not 2.12; 15 Apr ~2.06, not 4.95; 21 Apr ~0.90, not 7.62; 25 Apr ~0.72, not 1.11). Completions on the bursty weekday are *under*-dispersed, which undercuts the overdispersion narrative. Lag-1 autocorrelation 0.42 is stated as computed; nothing in the document shows a correct calculation.

**Little’s Law is conceptually careful and still sloppy in execution.** The matrix correctly refuses a law claim on the outage day and flags carry-over, snapshot WIP, and non-stationarity. Using arrivals/12 as λ is disclosed. Missing: that implied sojourn (144–280 min) is several times median service (24–36 min), i.e. waiting dominates; that a flat 12-hour λ ignores the 08–10 peak the rest of the paper emphasizes; and that “applicable only for this short recovery window” is not one of the three allowed verdicts.

**Section 8 fails the spec.** `outage_min` has 29 entries for 28 days and puts 180 on 13 Apr, not 15 Apr. 14 Apr arrivals are 105 rather than 107. Late-week `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are shifted. `df["regime"]` is never created before `groupby("regime")`. `Series.mean(ddof=0)` is not valid pandas. Pooled vs regime comparison tables required by the prompt are not printed. A threshold heuristic on WIP crossing 30 would fire first on the outage, not on 20 Apr as the prose claims. Comments say “15-hour snapshots.” The block would not run and would not match §§2–6 if it did.

**Over-claim and terminology.** A “10% peak redistribution → 15–20% WIP drop” is not supported by the aggregates. `mean_wip / staff_fte` is repeatedly called utilization; it is congestion per FTE. Arrivals per FTE is not utilization either.

The operational story (static 6.0 FTE looks fine when pooled and fails when segmented; do not read 16 Apr catch-up as a healthy high-throughput regime) is the strongest part. The errors above are not cosmetic: they are wrong arithmetic, missing regimes the prompt required, false dispersion statistics, and non-reproducible code. That caps the grade well below a competent pass.
