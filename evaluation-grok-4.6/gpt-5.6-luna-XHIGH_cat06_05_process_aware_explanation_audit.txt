The answer is substantively strong: it catches obvious and subtle leakage, keeps queue/age/complexity, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legal but future, and separates predictive, causal, actionable, and fairness standards. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several analytical shortcuts and a few conceptual slips keep it well below the top of the scale.

**Most serious flaw: leaked-model attributions are recycled as “credible drivers.”**  
Cases X and Y are correctly marked `recompute`, but the revised drivers still adopt the original SHAP *directions* (queue up/down, complexity up, elapsed down, region R4 up). Once `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, and especially `full_trace_variant_id` (+0.31) are removed, those local values are not identified. Domain knowledge can support queue, complexity, and slack; it cannot support “R4 increases risk.” Case Y therefore presents a contaminated association as a post-repair driver. A strict revision would list *candidate admissible features* and refuse direction for anything that is not independently justified.

**Section 1 is comprehensive but uneven.**  
Coverage of the top 12, all five patterns, and X/Y/Z locals is complete, and the 65% team-coincidence, nightly 23:00 snapshot, `<= extraction_date`, missing sequence index, and unversioned `risk_score_current` are the right subtle defects. Problems:

- Every sequence pattern is `critical`. The *Why* text distinguishes impossible vs future-legal, but severity does not, which blurs the distinction the rubric cares about.
- Causal overclaim on *admissible* features (`queue_length` “increases risk,” region as “delay driver,” complexity as a cause) is not a finding type. Leakage is audited; unsupported causal stories on valid features are deferred.
- There is no synthesis that ranks 1–4 and 7 make the *global ranking itself* an invalid early-warning ranking.
- `prior_claim_count` is understated: `claim_created_at <= extraction_date` typically includes the *current* claim on every row unless `claim_id` is excluded, not merely “can include” it or later same-day claims.
- `claimant_channel` as medium for missing lineage is fair; treating it more harshly than region is consistent with the notes, but the table never says channel is usually a registration attribute.

**“Unusable until lineage is repaired” conflates two classes.**  
`total_case_duration_hours`, complete-trace variant/appeal flags, and `final_decision` are not waiting on a join fix; they are the wrong objects for a t0 model. Repairable PIT bugs (queue as-of, document snapshot, risk-score version, prior-claim predicate, t0 team) belong in a different bucket. The answer knows the difference in Section 1 remediations and then flattens it in Section 3.

**Section 2 is a good essay, not a validation suite.**  
Enabled vs future-possible vs impossible, DFG ≠ norm, ties as partial order, and occurrence-indexed settlement decisions are the right ideas. Missing: named tests, fixtures (prefix after coverage check; tied close/assess; two decisions with and without appeal; closure between decisions; late-arriving same-day events), and expected pass/fail. “Alignment/footprint/declarative” are named more than operationalized.

**Section 4 is mostly disciplined, with a few judgment slips.**  
Narratives do not replay Partial, appeal, or `REG-COV-DOC-...-CLOSE`. X’s removal of undocumented channel is conservative and acceptable. Weaker points: elapsed (−0.03) is elevated to a main driver; Y’s only retained process signal is a small queue effect, and the write-up does not say the case may have little prefix signal after leakage is stripped; Z’s note is the best of the three but still lists `calendar_week` as an ambiguity rather than as available-but-regime-entangled (a different defect).

**Section 5 hits every required heading** and correctly refuses intervention claims from SHAP. It is a generic-but-competent plan; “explanation stability under alternate linearizations of ties” is the one distinctly process-aware item. Prioritization is sensible (store → recompute → conform → recalibrate → fairness → monitor).

**Net:** this is a strong auditor’s draft that would pass a real review with revisions, not a near-flawless one. The content bar of the prompt is met; the strictness bar is not, because the revised locals still lean on a poisoned explainer and a few taxonomies are fuzzier than the analysis they sit on.
