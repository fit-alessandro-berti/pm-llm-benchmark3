**7.4**

The answer is substantively strong and format-compliant, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has several real technical faults, some over-precise claims, and a few places where required distinctions are only implicit.

**What it gets right.** It detects the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, keeps queueing distinct from service, treats the group as a routing/skill/night bundle rather than a clean treatment, flags completed-case selection, and proposes a safer learn-while-limiting-risk plan. Arithmetic for the main means and the complexity-standardized contrast is correct. The six hypotheses, most falsification tests, the variable-role table, and the Python diagnostics match the requested design.

**Causal-graph errors.** `complexity → unobserved_case_risk` is inverted. Recorded complexity is a label or noisy measure of difficulty, not a cause of residual risk; the residual threat after conditioning on complexity should be a parent (or common cause) of routing and outcomes. The DAG also locks in `resource_group → shift` and leaves `shift` and `arrival_load` disconnected, even though every night case in the file is High load and the operational story equally supports `shift → routing_rule → resource_group`. Those are not cosmetic omissions: they change which variables are confounders versus mediators.

**Process-mining miss.** Residuals `cycle − (wait + service)` are not “largely fixed” Register/Classify/Close time. They split cleanly as ≈0.8 h (straight path), ≈2.2 h (compliance), ≈2.3 h (Request Info), ≈3.6–3.7 h (both). That is customer/compliance overhead outside the two named clocks. Calling it fixed overhead discards an identifiable mechanism.

**Over-precise adjusted effect.** The headline −2.5 h standardized gap is driven hard by High-Nova n=2 and Low-Orion n=2. Those two thin strata account for most of the standardized contrast. Medium, the only decently supported stratum, already favors Orion, so the direction is not fabricated—but reporting 17.14 vs 19.65 in the executive summary as if it were a stable adjustment oversells the evidence they elsewhere call unidentified.

**Required estimand language is thin in §1.** Total vs direct vs mediated interpretations appear later (roles, mediation hypothesis, wait/service split) but the executive assessment never names those estimands, which the prompt required at minimum. “Mainly confounded” at 62 also compresses two different claims: the crude Orion penalty is mostly case mix, and any residual group effect may be beneficial. The second claim is not identified; the verdict label is slightly stronger than the identification discussion.

**Hypothesis set and local sloppiness.** The required “genuine harmful Orion effect” is only the night/load capacity story, not a distinct “Orion reviews are slower or more bureaucratic” mechanism (which the service times would then contradict). “Night Nova mostly Low” is 2/4. “Both components favor Orion by 0.3–3 h” mashes a ~1.6–3.3 h wait gap with a ~0.1–0.4 h service gap. Conditioning on complexity is said to “open collider paths” without specifying the collider; the real selection/collider problem is `case_completed` (and possibly rework/quality).

**Code and plan.** The script meets the spec, states the weighting distribution, warns on n<3, and does not abuse placeholder timestamps. The three-phase plan is the right shape. Residual uncertainties are specific. Those parts are close to the standard the rest of the memo does not quite hold.
