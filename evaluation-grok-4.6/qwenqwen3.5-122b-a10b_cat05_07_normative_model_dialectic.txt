**5.1**

The write-up hits the required headings and gets several easy case calls right, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the rubric—dialectical fairness, model semantics, evidence traceability, authority/scope, and especially the revised POWL—the answer has material errors, internal contradictions, and a failing core deliverable.

### What holds up

Structure matches the spec. SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-708, SUP-711, and SUP-714 are classified on defensible grounds. The fact/dispute split in §1 is directionally correct. The architecture sketch in §6 (control-flow vs data constraints vs overlay governance vs scope) is the right shape. The text does notice that a strict partial order cannot encode a cycle.

That is not enough.

### Briefs are not the strongest versions of either side

The prosecution is required to acknowledge genuine model/data defects; the defense is required to acknowledge real risk and the limits of informal practice. Both do this only half-heartedly.

Prosecution overreaches and then contradicts itself. It counts SUP-706 among seven “materially noncompliant” cases, then §5 reclassifies it as `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`. It treats a missing CRM-mirrored notification as a process breach while N4 is stipulated. It barely uses the aggregate record (312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing attributes, notification missingness 2%→29%, six August post-pilot cases, Internal Audit on SUP-703/708). Those numbers are the strongest system-level prosecution evidence and are almost unused.

Defense concedes too much, too early. Calling SUP-703 and SUP-708 simply “noncompliant” inside the defense brief is not arguing the strongest process-aware case. A real defense would press that M0 contains no `Auto_Approve` transition at all, so every pilot trace is an M0 deviation *by construction*; that PD-3 expressly allows quality after activation; that CS-9 is silent on whether rejected leads and reactivations are M0 cases; and that CRM linkage, not missing work, explains notify gaps. Instead the defense minimizes SUP-708 as a “logging/ordering issue, not a safety breach” because no purchase landed first. That is the opposite of acknowledging genuine risk: activation before sanctions is the control failure; the empty purchase log is luck.

The matrix compounds this. Several “Prosecution interpretation” cells already say `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT`. That is the defense’s claim. The prosecution column should say the trace violates M0/CS-9. Collapsing the dispute in the matrix makes §4 look complete while emptying the dialectic.

Authority is under-analyzed. PD-3 is minutes plus email, never folded into CS-9; the Compliance observer first called it a “monitored exception” and later denied approving a weakening of CS-9. CS-9 still requires quality and manual approval *before* activation. That hierarchy fight is the center of the case and is mostly skipped.

### Case judgment: several calls are sloppy

Correct or acceptable: 701 COMPLIANT; 702 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (PD-3 window, eligible, quality next day); 703 NONCOMPLIANT; 704 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; 705 NONCOMPLIANT (expiry is documented; “the flood still existed” is not an instrument); 706 DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE; 707 MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT as a *dominant* label is fair if M0-nonconformance is still named; 708 NONCOMPLIANT; 709/710 MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; 711 DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE; 713 MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; 714 NONCOMPLIANT.

Errors:

- **SUP-712.** The trace *fits M0*. The breach is the CS-9 resource rule, which M0 does not encode. Folding that into a single `NONCOMPLIANT` erases the distinction the prompt required (“include cases that fit M0 but may violate non-control-flow rules”). This is a genuine mixed object: control-flow compliant, resource noncompliant. “Do not use mixed as a substitute for analysis” does not mean “refuse MIXED when both heads are true.”
- **SUP-715.** The dossier says no rule prohibits the spouse/reporting-line fact pattern; COI policy requires *disclosure*, and none was recorded. That is not the same as an activation-control breach. `NONCOMPLIANT` at medium severity overclaims. The honest label is a disclosure/governance finding, not a CS-9 control-flow failure.
- **SUP-702 matrix.** M0 issues are Auto_Approve, quality-after-activate, *and* Notify absent from the onboarding log. Only the first two are listed, even though N4 is the whole point of that case’s extra evidence.
- **System arithmetic.** “60% (9/15) of cases are compliant or justified” is false on the answer’s own labels: 1 COMPLIANT + 2 JUSTIFIED = 3/15. The other six “non-noncompliant” rows are model/scope or data defects. Inflating those into “compliant or justified” is a category error, then used to support the system finding.
- **“M0 is INVALID as a standalone governance tool.”** Overstated. M0 is a faithful CS-9 happy-path skeleton. It is incomplete for overlays, loops, resources, and scope. Invalid ≠ incomplete.

### Revised POWL is the largest failure

The prompt required one executable model that represents, at minimum: concurrent sanctions/quality on the standard route; manual route; low-risk auto route; **emergency/provisional route**; repeatable Request_More_Info or a *defensible* approximation; reject + notify-before-close.

The code does not meet that bar and is not safely executable.

1. **Not executable as written.** `M1` is built from a *list* of nodes, then `M1.nodes.add(decision_node)` is called. Lists have no `.add`. The subsequent mutation also leaves `checks → decision_approved` and `decision_approved → notify` in the relation while the node is removed. Stale edges, relation universe not updated.

2. **Illegal POWL sharing.** The same `manual_approval` object is in `manual_path` and `rmi_path`. The same `activate` object is in `manual_path` and `auto_path`. XOR children cannot share Transition instances. This is not a style nit; it is an invalid object.

3. **Mandatory emergency route is missing.** There is no path in which sanctions precede activate and quality/manual follow. That is a specified minimum. PD-3/FD-7’s whole point—quality after provisional activation—is also unrepresentable, because `quality` lives only in the pre-decision `checks` block. Auto-approve still requires quality *before* the XOR.

4. **RMI approximation is semantically wrong, not merely incomplete.** `rmi_path` is `{Request_More_Info → Manual_Procurement_Approval}` with **no Activate**, then XOR’d against the real approve paths. Choosing RMI yields approve-without-activate, then notify and close. That is not a loop approximation; it is a different, absurd process. `Operator.LOOP` exists in the same `Operator` enum they imported and was the obvious encoding (body = checks/decision work; redo = Request_More_Info). They noted the DAG limitation and then implemented a worse idea.

5. **Construction is confused.** They build `decision_approved`, embed it in `M1`, then try to wrap it in another XOR with `reject` by mutating nodes. Comments admit the XOR is a “simplification.” A clean model would be one XOR among standard / auto / emergency / reject, with LOOP or a silent-guarded optional RMI *before* the decision, and quality either concurrent (standard) or after activate (overlay routes).

6. **Policy/data proposal is a comment block.** The required overlay table is four loosely typed dicts. It does not specify stable `notification_id`, rule/version IDs on events, eligibility snapshots, alias/delegation tables, emergency/pilot authority IDs, or immutable effective vs record timestamps as a schema. Those are named in prose at the end, not designed.

So §7 fails both “executable” and “must represent at minimum.” That section is a large share of the judge’s score, and it is the weakest part of the answer.

### Net

This is a competent exam outline with several correct case labels and a reasonable layering story, damaged by a non-executable and semantically wrong POWL, a false 60% compliance claim, over-classification of 712/715, unused aggregate evidence, and briefs that do not actually fight at full strength. It is far from the near-flawless bar. **5.1.**
