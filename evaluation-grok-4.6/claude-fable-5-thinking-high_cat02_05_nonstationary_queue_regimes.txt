**7.4**

The answer is analytically strong, format-compliant, and clearly above a competent baseline: it separates demand, mix, capacity, and backlog; refuses to treat Apr 16–17 as a sustainable high-throughput regime; and is appropriately reluctant about Little’s Law. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict reading, several numerical, definitional, and inferential slips keep it well short of the top of the scale.

**What holds up**

Estimands in §1 are mostly well defined: daily λ and μ_eff as 12-hour window averages, S as active handling (quantiles only), L as snapshot WIP, W as open-hours flow time. Assumptions A1–A3 are labeled. The peak-window vs hourly-bin inconsistency is a real, well-checked data defect (Apr 7/15/25 align only if the peak is shifted one bin; Apr 21 matches neither alignment). The 28-day arrival/completion totals (2519 vs 2409) are correct. Regime rates in §2 match the table. Fano/CV on the hourly vectors are correct for population moments (Apr 7 Fano ≈ 2.96). The Mt-overdispersion vs genuine burstiness distinction is the right one. The Little’s Law matrix is the best section: transients are marked not defensible, overload is bounded rather than point-estimated, and median service is not substituted for mean flow time. The Python block meets the seven mechanical requirements and largely reproduces the narrative.

**Material flaws**

1. **Conservation is over-interpreted.** Mean 15-minute WIP is not a stock. Closing minus opening inventory equals arrivals − completions − exits; the time-average of snapshots does not. Comparing Σ(arrivals − completed) = 110 to mean_wip moving 18 → 19, or “Apr 18–28” net +106 to a rise “from 35 to 50,” is not an accounting identity. Apr 18 mean_wip is 16, not 35 (35 is Apr 17). The qualitative suspicion of abandonment, reroute, or inconsistent aggregation is fair; “roughly 90–100 cases unaccounted for” and the ±10–15% haze on baseline W are more precise than the estimand allows. This is the largest methodological error.

2. **Unidentified means are used as if known.** Median 24–26 min is 0.40–0.43 h, not 0.47–0.53 h. The uplift is an unlabeled skew assumption, then fed into erlangs and ρ ≈ 0.65–0.75 / ρ ≳ 1.05–1.20 in the primary table. Recovery “near-100% utilization” from 1.49 completions/FTE-h likewise requires an unidentified E[S]. Observed instability (arrivals > completions, WIP +2/day) does not need that calculation and is the stronger evidence; the table blurs observation and hypothesis.

3. **Table/label noise.** The baseline-weekday row header includes “Apr 6–14 & Apr 18–19*, Mon–Fri (7 d)” while the footnote correctly puts Apr 18–19 with weekends. Weekend share of volume is ~14% (359/2519), not “~16%.” Drain time 47/(10.0−8.8) is 39.2 open-hours, not ≈ 38.

4. **Reproducibility gaps.** Pooled corr(staff_fte, mean_wip) = +0.65 and the staffing/throughput “reversals” are not computed in the required code block. The advertised change-point heuristic (`staff_fte.diff() ≥ 0.9` within day-type) never flags the weekend 2.5 → 3.0 step that §2 treats as a regime boundary. Autocorrelations are implemented, but several headline statistics in §§1 and 4 are not.

5. **Small internal tensions.** §5 argues the Apr 20 jump cannot be resolved (mix vs coding), then treats physical deficit as ruling out a pure coding change. That is a reasonable hypothesis, not a resolution: undercounting, abandonment, or the same conservation problem could move completions without a true work-content shock. Full-pool Little’s Law is called a valid long-run average and simultaneously undermined by the conservation gap; “approximate only” is acceptable, but the two claims are not fully reconciled. Apr 21’s Fano is first called “genuinely bursty,” then correctly qualified as still partly a single-day profile.

**What is not wrong (and should not be punished)**

Not forcing one segmentation; treating outage and both drains as transients; using fluid ΔL/(μ−λ) instead of W on those windows; refusing four hourly vectors as a stochastic model; and the pooled-average staffing / early-withdrawal policies in §7 are all aligned with the quality bar.

**Net.** Deep, internally mostly coherent, and usable as an operational memo. Strictly graded, the conservation-as-stock error, unidentified E[S] in the main table, and the small trail of numeric/label/code mismatches prevent anything in the 9–10 band.
