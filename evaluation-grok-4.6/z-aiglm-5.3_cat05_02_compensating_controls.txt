**6.5**

The analytical core (sections 1–5) is strong: threat-scoped decomposition, a usable equivalence test, non-mechanical case classifications, consistent harm-referenced timing, and counterfactuals that distinguish coverage gaps from separation failures. Completeness and a few overclaims keep this well short of a high score.

**What works.** FEP-1 is treated as an objective, not a headcount. AV-2/MG-4/RA-7/ED-1 are mapped to prevent/detect/correct/fail across eight threats, including stated AV-2 blinds (receipt, collusion, who holds a credential). The equivalence test includes all required dimensions and a real argument that equal expected loss is not enough (adversarial timing, irreversibility, deterrence, evidence value). Case calls generally track facts: AP-203/AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` under ED-1; AP-201 as tolerable nonconformance; AP-202 as inverted controls (same-actor override of m09’s credential, conflicted late ratification, post-recall audit, unrecovered loss); AP-205 as `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`; AP-206 as `INDETERMINATE` on token timing; AP-208 as control failure without loss (fail-open outage, unsigned checklist, post-settlement ratification). AP-208 correctly does *not* fail ED-1 condition 3 (eight-month vendor ≥ 180 days). Hybrid semantics for post-release/pre-settlement manager action are stated and applied consistently. Section 5’s best points are the ones that refuse to treat “a second person” as a universal fix (AP-204 fabricated receipt; AP-208 blind release during outage).

**Material failures.**

1. **Section 7 is missing.** The required executable pandas/PM4Py block (embedded CSV, `format_dataframe`, `filter_four_eyes_principle`, blocked attempts, joined facts, evidence flags, “not a verdict” label) never appears. That is a hard required heading, not an optional appendix.

2. **Section 6 is truncated** mid-sentence in the alert-vs-proof discussion. Evidence-collection recommendations are not delivered as a finished argument, even though the prompt asked for them.

3. **AP-204 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` overreaches FEP-1’s stated purpose.** Two distinct humans prepared and released. The realized defect is a fabricated receipt — explicitly outside AV-2 and outside FEP-1’s single-actor initiate-and-authorize threat. Pair concentration and a shared workstation are serious *independence* doubts, but they do not by themselves prove this payment was a four-eyes objective failure rather than a coverage failure. `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` (or `INDETERMINATE` on collusion) would have matched the evidence quality better. The same stretch appears, more mildly, in treating clean AP-209 as only partially met largely because of AP-204.

**Smaller issues.** Several severity/confidence cells mix two scales in one cell. AP-201’s claim that ratification was “not structurally blocking” is a fair uncertainty, then used as if it slightly weakens the package without evidence either way. “Fully equivalent” for AP-203 is too strong given residual O3/receipt threats the answer itself lists. Monitoring thresholds are illustrative and labeled as such, which is fine; they do not compensate for the missing code or the cut ending.

Net: high-quality control reasoning on the cases that were finished, but not a complete required deliverable, and one important classification is harsher than the facts support.
