**6.0**

The legal and evidentiary core (sections 1–5) is strong: facts are separated from authority/scope disputes, the burden of proof is coherent, both briefs are mostly well-aimed, and the case calls are mostly decisive rather than a bland split. The revised POWL, however, fails a central semantic requirement of the task, and several required concessions and classifications are off. Under a strict standard that is not near-flawless work.

**What is done well**

Section 1 correctly treats CS-9 as the only controlled standing norm, PD-3/FD-7 as time-boxed overlays, and N4/N5 as data/model defects rather than silent amendments. The burden rule (noncompliance requires excluding exception, scope, logging, and mapping explanations; exceptions require contemporaneous authority plus eligibility plus collateral conditions) is the right standard.

Most case calls are defensible and well-traced: 701 compliant; 702/704 justified under in-force overlays; 703 and 708 correctly isolated as severe; 705 and 714 correctly refused “inertia” after expiry; 707/709/710 correctly treated as model/scope problems; 712 correctly pulled out of control-flow blindness. The system judgment—partial operational noncompliance plus a partially invalid model—and the explicit rejections of each side in section 5 are the right shape of verdict. The overlay table and data-collection proposal (immutable timestamps, `notification_id`, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution, `rule_version_id`, case types) are substantively good.

**Major model-semantics failure**

M1 forces `checks = (Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review)` **before every** XOR child. That cannot generate the traces that motivated the revision:

- auto-approve / PD-3: Quality may follow provisional activation (702, 703, 714);
- emergency / FD-7: Quality and manual approval follow activation (704, 705);
- reject / pre-screen: Quality need not occur at all (709).

Putting `Retrospective_Quality_Review` inside the emergency branch does not fix this; Quality is still mandatory *before* the decision, so emergency and auto routes are double-gated and cannot replay the observed orders. This is not a POWL expressiveness limit. It is a design error: deferred Quality should live *inside* those branches (or Quality should be optional/XOR-skipped on the common prefix). The task asked for a defensible approximation with limitations stated. The limitation statement only mentions data-aware predicates, not this control-flow bug.

A second technical defect: the same `activate` `Transition` instance is reused in `standard_route` and `auto_route`, then both are XOR children. In POWL, node identity is unique; shared children across XOR alternatives is ill-formed.

`Request_More_Info` is only allowed *before* both checks, so the model cannot even approximate SUP-707 (`Sanctions → RMI* → Quality`). That limitation is not stated. Reject after full concurrent checks also mis-scopes abbreviated pre-screen.

**Other defects that keep the score down**

The prosecution brief does not really concede genuine model/data defects, as required; it treats them as things the operation may not hide behind. The defense concedes 703/708 but underplays the 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases and the August normalization cluster.

SUP-711 is mis-binned. Same `source transaction ID` is stipulated; the underlying trace fits. That is a resolved logging artifact, not `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`. High confidence plus “indeterminate” is internally inconsistent.

SUP-713’s mapping was approved *a week after* the case. Calling it simply “approved label equivalence → COMPLIANT” skips the retrospective-authority issue the rest of the memo is otherwise careful about.

The matrix prosecution column collapses on 702/704 (“None under PD-3”) instead of stating the strongest remaining prosecution point (PD-3 never entered CS-9; observer later denied weakening CS-9). Harmless for the final call, but weaker dialectically than the prompt asked.

**Net**

High-quality dispute analysis with a broken revised control-flow model and several strict-standard misses. That is a mid-band score, not a high one.
