**7.4**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that hits the hard required distinctions: full-trace leakage vs prefix signals, `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` as semantically legal but temporally inadmissible, `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` as both future and process-inconsistent, preservation of queue/age/complexity, and predictive ≠ causal ≠ actionable ≠ fair. It is not close to flawless under a strict reading.

**Material issues**

The revised Case X/Y explanations still treat contaminated SHAP signs as if they were process facts. After `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, `full_trace_variant_id`, and (in training) `total_case_duration_hours` are removed, local attributions are not stable. Saying magnitudes “must be recomputed” while still listing `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (−0.03) as a credible driver is inconsistent. A strict revision should keep only *feature values known at t0* and refuse signed contributions until a prefix-only model exists.

The sequence explainer is handled pattern-by-pattern and never diagnosed as a method failure. Four of five patterns are impossible in a `t0` prefix; `Claim Closed → …` proves the miner ran on complete traces (and often on arbitrary tie order). That is a single horizon/methodology defect, not five separate feature bugs.

`total_case_duration_hours` is called “direct target leakage.” It is near-label leakage, not the 10-day breach indicator. Duration-to-close can diverge from time-to-settlement when appeals or close-lag exist. The conclusion (remove, retrain) is right; the characterization is sloppy.

“Appeals cause breaches” is rejected as unsupported causation but the definitional issue is missed. If the target is time to *final* settlement/closure, an appeal almost tautologically extends duration. If it is time to *first* decision, the appeal is after the outcome. That ambiguity matters for both leakage severity and the team’s causal story.

**Inconsistencies and softer errors**

`claimant_channel` is treated as a defensible t0 signal on an unstated “presumably at registration” assumption, while `initial_reserve` is parked for missing timestamps. Same evidence standard is not applied.

Section 3 is internally messy. Channel appears under both “defensible predictive” and “proxy”; region, which is equally available at registration, appears only under proxy—yet is then used as a Case Y credible driver. Repairable items (`prior_claim_count`, `initial_reserve`, `risk_score`) are double-listed without a clean now-vs-after-repair split.

Case Z notes week 52 as a generic calendar/policy proxy and never connects it to the stated holiday alignment. That is the obvious reading of week 52.

`queue_length_at_t0` as “low” understates a possible ±5 minute future snapshot. Not critical, but it is still a join-leakage class defect, not just “join quality.”

The validation suite is a good checklist, not a concrete suite: no Declare/LTL formulas, no replay cost model, no failing examples, no feature-store assertion syntax. Bitemporal storage is mentioned correctly and then left unimplemented.

Minor overclaim: “relatively high” / “relatively light” queues with no distribution. Minor under-audit: the team story that more same-day documents “reduce uncertainty” is never dismantled as a selection/collider story. Global vs local feature sets (`final_decision`, `claimant_channel`, SIU role) are itemized but not called out as an explanation-completeness failure.

**What is actually good**

Coverage of the top-12, all five patterns, and every local-only field is complete. The 65% `final_handler_team` agreement is correctly rejected. `prior_claim_count` SQL, nightly document snapshot lookahead, missing sequence index, extraction-date employee joins, and “deleting SHAP rows does not validate the score” are all handled. Legitimate congestion and prefix-age signals are kept. Region/channel are not purged for being correlated. Section 5 is correctly ordered: quarantine → PIT feature store → prefix recompute → conformance → recalibration → stability → fairness → drift gates.

Those strengths make this a good audit. The leftover SHAP reuse, the missing method-level sequence finding, the target/appeal semantic sloppiness, and the classification inconsistencies keep it well short of a high score under the stated standard.
