**7.2**

This is a substantively strong policy memo that meets the main quality bar (I-03 excluded on stage, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers treated as first-class, observational \(p\) not treated as a causal effect). Under a hypercritical standard it is not nearly flawless. Operational realism and a few coherence gaps keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually good.** The decision rule separates eligibility, effect, and capacity. Calibration bands are computed correctly and explicitly *not* treated as causal corrections to \(\delta\). Direct-benefit arithmetic for \(D^L/D^M\) checks out on every case. Charging full false-alarm burden is labeled as conservative, with the \((1-p)F\) alternative named. I-08 is correctly withheld on a negative lower bound despite €100k value. I-06 is held for imputation rather than rewarded for a 0.32 jump. The I-07 vs I-02 trade-off (€1,900 midpoint money vs 0.11 fewer direct breaches) is explicit. Unknown spillover victims are not invented as fake euro totals; \(W\) and the \(W<€147{,}778\) release boundary are the right way to keep the impact table auditable. Repeat-treatment vs repeat-*alert* is distinguished. Fairness and label-feedback discussion is concrete (non-monetary lane, treatment ledger, two-stage intensity/assignment design).

**Major operational flaw: I-07 is selected as if it will be delivered.** The rule says remaining resolution and validation work must remain feasible after a 45-minute swarm. I-07 is still in *Triage* with 1.5 h to SLA. After a 12:00–12:45 swarm, 45 minutes remain for Diagnosis Started → Diagnosis Complete → Resolution Work → Customer Validation. That is not a plausible path for a contractual resolution deadline. The memo hedges with “if feasible,” then names I-07 in the selected portfolio, sequences it first, and builds Section 3 on “all three selected swarms are delivered.” That is having the feasibility gate and not using it. A coherent rule would make I-07 a *conditional candidate*, not a booked slot, and would specify the replacement (I-02 is the obvious one) if the path test fails.

**Related coherence gap: waiting I-02 while consuming both specialists.** I-02 is already in Diagnosis Started, 3.0 h to deadline, no prior swarm. The prompt states that interrupted specialists stop ordinary diagnosis work, and that each slot spills 0.01–0.03 onto six untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Booking 135 elapsed minutes / 270 specialist-minutes on I-07 → I-09 → I-04 therefore delays the very work I-02 needs *and* can raise I-02’s breach risk while it “waits.” Generic \(3\times6\times q\) spillover math does not substitute for that case-level conflict. “Reassess I-02 before I-04” is not the same as the table’s unconditional **Intervene** on I-04.

**I-09 is ranked as Economic allocation 2 on the same \(\delta\) ranges the memo says are not identified causal effects.** For Non-Native-Language Support, both calibration *and* effect estimates are flagged as weak. Refusing automatic exclusion is right; treating 0.18–0.30 as comparable to I-04’s range for a scarce-slot ranking is not. A robustness penalty or an evaluation/equipoise frame was required, not a second economic slot.

**Smaller but real issues.**

- Calibrated Strategic Enterprise \(p\) for I-04 is ~0.36–0.43 with 10 h slack. Those bands are computed carefully and then almost unused: I-04 is still first economic pick on unadjusted \(\delta \times V\). Overprediction after a workflow redesign should have lowered priority or deferred dispatch, not just appeared as a footnote.
- Section 1’s \(D_i\) includes swarm cost; Section 3’s “direct economic benefit” excludes it. Adverse “direct net” figures then mix the two conventions (€400 / €2,800 / €5,400 include cost; the table range −€2,850 to €6,750 does not). Auditable arithmetic should not require reconciling definitions.
- Optimistic and base both sit on 0.69 direct breaches at the lower optimistic end, so the three scenarios are not cleanly separated.
- Adverse spillover \(q=0.03\)–\(0.05\) is extra-prompt and, as the memo admits, not identified from 15–30 delay minutes. Fine as sensitivity; the table’s “0.16–0.90 more breaches” still splices different responder × \(q\) pairs into one range.
- “One of every three discretionary slots” is a mechanical fairness quota. Public-sector obligation is discussed only as something *not* to treat as an automatic entitlement; I-01 is never given a non-monetary review path.
- Minimum evidence change is specified for *repeat* alerts, not for first dispatch. Monitoring triggers (5 pp calibration, 0.8 access ratio) are arbitrary.

**Not a weak answer.** It does not rank on \(p_{\text{now}}\), does not spend a slot on I-03, does not equate \(\delta\) with observational \(p\), and does not ignore untreated harm. The remaining failures are exactly the ones this task is designed to catch: booking an almost certainly infeasible urgent case, under-weighting specialist interruption of live diagnosis work, and using unstable effect ranges as if they were ranking-quality.
