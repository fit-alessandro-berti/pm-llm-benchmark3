The write-up has the required seven sections, uses mix-plus-disruption scenarios rather than volume-only stress, and generally avoids “add staff everywhere” and average-only optimization. It is still well short of a high score because several claims are inconsistent with the log, several policies would not fire in the scenarios they are meant to cover, and some numbers are invented.

**What is solid.** The four scenarios match the required disruption types and change case mix, not just headcount. Bottleneck write-ups repeatedly use utilization, median queues, selected DFG counts, and the stated variants (Urgent batch-wait bypass, Complex-panel multipliers, M2/repeat clustering). The split between a small always-on package (I6, I7, guarded I4) and contingent I1/I2/I3/I5 is the right architecture. Downstream overload is discussed for most picks. The trade-off table has every required column. Residual risk includes a redesign-induced failure. The dashboard separates leading station signals from lagging SLA/rework outcomes.

**Material errors and logic breaks.**

- Scenario 2 treats M1/M2 dedication as if it were the current routing (“I3-style dedications force Routine onto M1”). I3 is a candidate intervention, not a fact in the log. That contaminates the outage analysis.
- Medical-validator **queue** (22 h peak) is conflated with the Hold → Medical **edge** (48 h). Those are different objects.
- P1’s on-switch requires Technical validation queue **&lt; 14 h**. Peak median queue is already 16 h at 101% utilization, so Urgent-first is off in the winter-wave scenario it is written for.
- I7 is sold as a standing robust change and again as an on/off policy. That is an inconsistent operating model.
- I4’s trade-off row asserts Urgent cases “bypass accession correction.” The log does not say that. Accession errors are not a case-type bypass.
- Hold-count alerts at 50/80 per day are not calibrated: 610 peak Hold entries over three weeks is on the order of ~30/day, so the “warning” band barely intersects the observed process.
- Residual risk cites “pilot programs” and a **5–10%** repeat-assay increase. That evidence is not in the materials.
- I10 is effectively unused. Specialist-review absence is one of the four mandated stresses, and the peak Hold/specialist path (36 h queue, 48 h edge, 610 Hold entries) is the obvious place for a guarded overflow policy. Omitting it leaves the redesign thin on the disruption it itself named.
- P1’s “next bottleneck” is given as Registration/Pre-analytical. Urgent-first is specified at preparation and assay; the migration risk is assay fragmentation, Technical validation, and starvation of Routine/Complex-panel, not intake.
- Peak **9,900 cases / 3 weeks** is handled like a volume level rather than a higher weekly rate plus a mix shift (~3,300 vs ~2,300 cases/week). Period-length naivete makes some count comparisons weaker than rate comparisons (repeats, Holds, medical-validation share).
- Several trade-off cells lost their direction markers, so effects on Urgent/Routine/Complex-panel are hard to read.
- A blended **70 h** median cycle-time target in the dashboard slides back toward the average the prompt told you not to optimize; the case-type p90/SLA lines are the right objects and should have been the only outcome targets.

**Narrower gaps.** Scenario 1’s “sequence” is close to a full-process list rather than a tight migration path. I8 is never accepted or rejected despite the explicit hint that it unmasks Pre-analytical and assay. Little’s-law or even rough WIP math is missing at 98–101% stations. “Acceptable” is never stated as scenario-specific bands (e.g., Urgent p90 is already 33 h against an 18 h contract). Recollection→cancel growth in the peak DFG is unused.

The skeleton and the instinct to separate robust gates from contingent dispatch are competent. The grade is held down by misread edges, an unusable flagship trigger, fabricated residual numbers, uncalibrated dashboard thresholds, and a missing specialist-overflow lever.
