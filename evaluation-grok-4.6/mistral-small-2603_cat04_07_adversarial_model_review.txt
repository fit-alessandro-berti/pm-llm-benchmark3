## Metric interpretation

The table shape is right and the refusal to average scores is right. Several cells are not.

Training fitness does not “reward overfitting to noise” in the usual sense here. Model S’s 0.992/0.981 pair is the signature of a language that is too large, not of a model that memorized noise. Holdout fitness is not “overestimated” by the absence of negative traces; fitness only scores replay of observed cases. What the missing negatives destroy is the interpretation of precision and of C’s holdout collapse.

Duplicate labels are treated as if they made the net unsound. They do not. Soundness is option-to-complete, proper completion, and no dead transitions. ## Model S: some real flaws, one fabricated one

The strongest case for S is underwritten. S is a sequence around a four-way interleaved optional block and a binary approval choice. That is intentional missing-event tolerance, which is exactly what the discovery team claimed.

The strongest case against S is then blurred. The prompt required a distinction between intentional noise tolerance and accidental semantic flaws. Optionalizing Credit Check and Technical Review is the former, and it is unacceptable for enforcement (F1, B3). Allowing Expedite Review to interleave before the mandatory checks is the latter: a structuring mistake, not a noise model. ## Third alternative

This is the other centerpiece, and it does not survive contact with its own code.

The prose sketch is a defensible *family*: procedural backbone plus DECLARE-style guards, with missing attributes called out. The implementation does not realize that family and does not satisfy the API contract `model_third = …`.

Concrete failures:

1. **Mandatory checks are encoded as XOR.** `X(Credit Check, Technical Review)` admits exactly one of the two activities. P2 requires both, in either order. The code inverts the requirement.
2. **`Correct Dossier` sits on the happy path, after approval.** Every accepting trace must correct after the approval choice and before release. That falsifies P1’s intent, P5, and P6 at once.
3. **`Operator.OPTIONAL` is not a process-tree operator.** Optional behavior is `X(a, τ)`. The snippet is not self-contained PM4Py.
4. **Supplier then Expedite is a hard sequence**, so Expedite cannot precede Supplier Review, contradicting P4.
5. **`model_third` is never bound.** The net is built from `root`.
6. **DECLARE cardinality 1** on each mandatory check contradicts any legal recheck after correction.
7. **Precedence `from Expedite to {CC, TR}`** is at best ambiguous and most naturally read backwards.
8. **The formal `Block`/`MandatoryBlock` DSL is not POWL and is not implemented.** The code and the mathematics describe different machines.

This is not “another justified strategy.” It is a strategy statement plus a model that would reject the desired happy path and force a correction onto every case. The prompt’s hard constraint — do not fix missing attributes by making everything optional — is honored in prose and then replaced by something worse in code.

A serious third model, given the missing `external/internal` and `affected-check` fields, would keep CC∥TR mandatory in the control flow, keep Expedite after both checks and unordered with Supplier, keep a binary approval, represent Supplier and correction as *explicitly uncertain* optional structure, and push the missing attributes into post-hoc guards that fail closed or fail into review — not into lambdas that pretend the attributes exist.

## Comparative audit and validation

Section 8 never puts B1–B8 in the required four-way table. The extra “adversarial” traces are mostly restated probes, not new distinguishers, and several “third accepts” rows assume the prose model rather than the code.

Section 9 talks like an evaluation program and then measures the wrong thing. `precision_alignments` on a negative log is not a true-negative rate. Precision asks how much of the *model* language appears in the log. The quantity you want on curated forbidden traces is fitness / exact replay: they should not be in the language. The rejection rule `precision_negative < 0.95` is therefore incoherent. The “stratified” split is an unstratified shuffle. The comment promises 100 negatives and lists four. Imports such as `discover_process_tree_inductive` and `pm4py.algo.evaluation` are invented relative to the API the prompt already gave. Stakeholder-comprehension protocol is named, not specified. No fabricated numbers — that is the one validation virtue.

## Governance and dissent

Retiring C for enforcement, fencing S as exploration-only, and refusing to deploy either original as policy is the right board posture. The go-live gates for a third model are directionally right and then cite the broken negative-precision statistic.

The dissent is short of the 150–250 word floor, and it contains a logic error: a third model that *accepts* curated negatives is under-rejecting, not over-rejecting. The dissent also restates the missing-attribute limitation already conceded in §7 instead of pressing the harder objection: given F4’s missing events and unseen combinations, a strict control-flow model will flag operationally live work, and F8’s conservative stance (unseen = noncompliant until approved) is a real alternative to the recommended hybrid.

## Bottom line

What is good is the meta-reasoning: metrics are not a scoreboard, use case changes the trade-off, C is a memorized catalog, S is not an enforcement model, and a third artifact is required. What is required to be *true* is often false: S/C membership for B5/B6/B8, soundness versus duplicate labels, precision on negatives, and the actual third model. Under a hypercritical bar, a polished wrong audit of the process trees and a non-executable `model_third` cannot score in the upper half.
