**7.4**

The final design is unusually complete and hits every required section, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it is a strong expert draft with several real methodological holes, some underspecified experiment machinery, and a few API/code nits.

**What is actually good.** Scope is a resource-constrained, calendar-aware DES with cross-case coupling (queues, architect bursts, district bundles, latent objection windows), not a replay of independent cases. The service/wait treatment is the best part of the document: completion-only timestamps are not silently split, waiting is treated as an emergent calibration target, and the throughput-vs-FTE table correctly shows that the supplied “service” medians cannot all be true touch times. Objections stay latent (mixture/proxy + parameter sets). Optimality is explicitly refused. PM4Py usage stays inside the requested convention (`pandas` ingest, `format_dataframe` with the three standard keys, `parse_process_tree`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`). The skeleton is modular and matches the required computations.

**Material gaps (these are why this is not a 9+).**

1. **Right-censoring is ignored.** Complex-variance p90 is 260 days. A 24-month terminating run will leave a large unfinished tail. There is no rule for whether duration/SLA KPIs drop incomplete cases, restrict arrivals to an entry window, or use survival estimators. That biases every tail metric the decision rule depends on.

2. **The experiment design is not operational.** E1–E11 × S1–S4 × K≈12 × up to 150 reps of a 24-month, 400-case/week model is asserted rather than budgeted. The 2^(9−4) screen has no generators, resolution, or alias structure. Sequential stopping is not specified inside the nested parameter-set design. CRN is named but not designed (separate streams for arrivals vs routing vs service, and what happens when D2/D5 change the event graph).

3. **Capacity reconciliation is only half-closed.** The 4–9× infeasibility argument is right as an identity, but the two rival resolutions (true service is ~15–60 min vs HR undercounts staff/task ownership) imply opposite intervention rankings (D2/D4 vs adding hidden capacity). That structural fork is mentioned, then folded into generic “K parameter sets” instead of being a first-class model variant.

4. **Validation bars are too soft for the claims.** Per-case replay correlation ≥ 0.6 and “2 of 3 bottlenecks match” would let a poorly identified twin pass the gate and still drive D10. Holdout is 6 months against 260-day tails, so out-of-sample power for the process that matters most is weak.

5. **Smaller factual/engineering nits.** `discover_performance_dfg` is printed as if edges were `{mean: …}`; in common PM4Py versions the values are already aggregated floats, so the demo would report NaNs. Clerk load omits “Request missing documents.” Public-notice × council/holiday interaction is thinner than the problem statement. SLA cutoffs 21/60/90/150 and several eligibility shares are invented defaults. Weekend portal arrivals (the excerpt itself has a Saturday 18:21 submission) are not specified in the NHPP.

The reasoning quality is high and the constraints are respected. It is not a near-flawless simulation design.
