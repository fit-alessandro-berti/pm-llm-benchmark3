The dialectical core (authority hierarchy, expiry, overlays vs informal practice, case-level distinctions) is strong. The PM4Py revision and several classification choices are not. Under a strict reading of model semantics, evidence hygiene, and “do not use MIXED as a hedge,” this is clearly not near-flawless.

## What holds up

Sections 1–3 set a usable burden of proof: CS-9 remains the controlled standard; PD-3/FD-7 can justify a case only inside written window, eligibility, and quantitative limits; desirability and “factual emergency” do not create authority. That principle is applied consistently to SUP-702/704 vs SUP-705/714, and both briefs concede the other side’s strongest points (CRM/label/duplicate artifacts; SUP-703 and SUP-708 as real risk). The matrix is complete and mostly traceable. Layer 2–5 of the architecture (eligibility snapshots, overlay register, scope for leads/reactivations, identity/COI outside POWL) is the right split of concerns.

## Material defects

**1. LOOP semantics are wrong, and the comment contradicts the code.**  
In PM4Py/process-tree POWL, `LOOP(A, B)` is do-`A`, then exit or redo-`B` then `A` again. `A` runs **at least once**. `LOOP(rmi, skip)` is therefore `Request_More_Info+`, not `0..n`. The comment calls the children `(body, exit)` and claims zero-or-more cycles. That is a different algebra. Consequence: the revised model **cannot replay SUP-701**, the only fully CS-9-clean happy path. An approximation that excludes the normative baseline is not a defensible approximation; it is a broken one.

**2. RMI is placed where the only observed RMI case does not occur.**  
SUP-707 is `Sanctions → RMI → RMI → Quality → …`. The model forces RMI **before** the route XOR, i.e. before sanctions. The text says this “covers the common pre-decision pattern.” The corpus has one RMI pattern, and it is mid-check, not a prefix. So the LOOP is both the wrong operator instantiation and the wrong locus.

**3. Shared `Transition` objects across XOR children.**  
`sanctions`, `quality`, and `activate` are the same objects in `std_route`, `auto_route`, `emerg_route`, and `reject_route`. POWL children of `XOR` must be node-disjoint; reuse yields a DAG, not a tree, and makes Petri-net unfolding/semantics ill-defined. The standard fix is same-label distinct objects, or factoring a single sanctions prefix. Neither is done.

**4. The model under-approximates while claiming to over-approximate.**  
Section 6 says every legitimate authorized trace should fit. It does not:

- Standard path with zero RMI: rejected by `LOOP(rmi, skip)`.
- Auto-approve with quality **before** activate (allowed by PD-3’s “may” delay, and stricter than the pilot): fits neither `auto_route` (forces `activate → quality`) nor `std_route` (requires manual, not auto).
- M0’s actual reject path — both checks, then `Reject_Supplier` — is gone. `reject_route` is sanctions-only. That is a regression from the normative model being replaced.

So the “over-approximation + data guards” architecture is asserted, not implemented.

**5. MIXED is used as a hedge after the author already names a dominant class.**  
The instructions forbid that. For SUP-712 the text says the dominant issue is the CS-9 distinct-human rule. That is **NONCOMPLIANT** (control-flow fit is irrelevant once a binding resource rule is broken). MIXED is for co-equal conflicting readings, not “M0 is silent so we split the label.” SUP-715 is weaker still: CS-9 does not prohibit the relationship; only a disclosure rule is unmet. Dominant class is closer to COMPLIANT-with-residual-disclosure, or a narrow non-control-flow finding — not MIXED-at-medium-severity as if it sat next to SUP-703.

**6. SUP-711 is internally inconsistent.**  
Classification `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` with “severity none, confidence high” and “pure migration duplicate” is a contradiction. High confidence that both rows share one `source_transaction_id` means the process is **COMPLIANT** and the extra event is a log artifact. Indeterminate is the right bucket for SUP-706 (late mutable CRM link), not for a resolved duplicate.

**7. Section 1 never isolates “unproved.”**  
The prompt asked for supported / disputed / **unproved**. Alias identity on 712, contemporaneity of 706’s notification, validity window of 710’s prior cert, and why 703’s operator selected “pilot” are unproved, not merely disputed-authority items. They are dumped into the matrix “missing evidence” column instead of governing the burden-of-proof taxonomy.

## Lesser but real issues

- Post-hoc QA_Clearance mapping (approved **after** SUP-713) is treated as a clean model/scope defect with **high** confidence. Content equivalence is good evidence; contemporaneous authority is not. Confidence is overstated.
- `auto_route` encodes delayed quality as mandatory order, not as a permitted lag. PD-3’s master-data validation is omitted without a corresponding DECLARE/data rule.
- System label “partial-to-noncompliant” is slightly mushy next to an otherwise sharp expiry rule; the six August auto-approvals deserved a quantified residual-noncompliance statement, not only a mention.
- Defense brief quotes the observer’s “monitored exception” more cleanly than her later denial that she weakened CS-9 (that tension is in §1, then softened).

## Bottom line

The legal/process argument is better than most answers to this prompt: it does not split the difference, and it treats written windows as hard. The artifact the prompt weights equally — an executable POWL that actually accepts the standard route, a 0..n info-request, full-check rejection, and pilot orderings — does not do those things, misstates its own LOOP, and reuses nodes across XOR. That, plus MIXED/711 taxonomy slips, caps the score well below the 8–10 band.
