The final write-up is structured correctly, applies event-time roles rather than current master data, normalizes aliases before SoD, treats `svc_qms` as non-human, and does not infer collusion from repeated pairs. Those are the right analytical commitments. It is not nearly flawless.

**Classification error.** CHG-509 as `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` is too generous. D-QM-02 requires the principal to be absent. Asha’s recorded leave ends 14 August; 15 August is only a weekend, and the prompt separately states there was no holiday. Weekend ≠ evidenced formal unavailability. The honest labels are `INDETERMINATE` or `UNAUTHORIZED` with an absence-condition failure. Baking `WEEKEND_AS_PRINCIPAL_UNAVAILABLE = True` into the checker hard-codes the generous reading instead of testing the stated condition.

**Code is conceptually complete and operationally sloppy.** The required ingredients are present (StringIO, `format_dataframe`, alias map, four-eyes calls, interval checks, handover/working-together, optional different-resource filter, no auto-collusion). Defects:

- The reconstructed CSV mis-times CHG-506 `Draft_Change_Request` as `08:30Z` instead of `10:00Z`.
- `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called with a two-activity list; current PM4Py takes two activity strings. The `try/except` will swallow the miss and the “uses four-eyes” requirement will not actually run.

Those are not polish nits. The rubric asked for the quality of the PM4Py/pandas checks, and the checks are wrapped so they can fail silently.

CHG-501’s late QMS row is correctly treated as documentation risk, but severity is soft for a HIGH-risk approval whose system-of-record entry post-dates the act. **Net.** Strong control reasoning and good case narrative, undermined by one material delegation-condition miss and PM4Py/network code that would not do what the section claims. That is well above a weak answer and well short of a near-flawless one.
