The write-up is well structured, often sophisticated, and much closer to the required method (eligibility sets, competing predictors, identifiability) than a label-only answer. It is not close to flawless. The central task is reconstructing decision sets; several of those sets are wrong, one important decision is missing, and a few high-level claims overstate what the tables actually show.

## Format and method

The seven required headings are present, the diagnosis table has the specified columns, and confidence is used as an ambiguity score rather than as rhetoric. Section 2 is the right object: decision times, eligible jobs, and FIFO / priority / SPT / appointment / skill predictions. Distinctions among observation, hypothesis, and recommendation are generally respected. That is the floor for a high score, not the score itself.

## Material errors in decision reconstruction

**QA omits A08 from two contended decision sets.** A08 arrives at 08:40 (priority 1, est 25) and is waiting through calibration and through A04. It belongs in the 08:58 and 09:05 rows. At 09:05 the table predicts priority → A06. That is false: A08 is already present and is the unique priority-1 job, so priority predicts A08. The later prose that A08 waited 86 minutes shows the author knew the job existed and still left it out of the sets. The *diagnosis* (FIFO + appointment, priority inert) survives, but the reconstruction the rubric asks for does not.

**QD misstates both skill eligibility and D-F availability.** D10 is *electrical*, not mechanical+electrical. D-F (firmware, electrical) can serve it; D-X is not the unique feasible resource. The 11:48 row’s skill note “D-F unavailable (meeting)” is factually wrong: the meeting is 12:00–12:20. The same error is repeated in the QD summary (“D07/D10 need the mech+elec resource”). The 12:16 note on D10 does *not* license backdating the meeting to 11:48.

**D-F becoming free at 11:36 is a decision opportunity and is not analyzed.** After D03, D-F is idle until the meeting. D10 (est 12) is waiting and would finish at or before 12:00; D05 (est 30) cannot finish before the meeting under non-preemption. Idling rather than taking D10 is a FIFO/SPT/priority violation on that resource. A confidence of 80 for “FIFO within the eligible set,” and the claim that the within-queue rule is tested at only four instants, are therefore too high. The missed idle also needed to appear in §3 and §5 (dedication to D05, unofficial wrap-up, refusal to finish exactly at a meeting start, unrecorded unavailability).

These are not nits. They are exactly the failure mode the quality bar warns against: start-order storytelling with incomplete eligibility sets.

## Smaller but real inconsistencies

- **Appointment semantics are applied cleanly in QA and then loosened elsewhere without enough signaling.** Treating appointment time as a release date makes FIFO at 09:20 equal A07 (eligibility clock 08:35 vs 09:15). That is defensible, but A06 is still listed as eligible, and a reader using recorded arrival (08:29) would call FIFO = A06. The ranking key `max(arrival, appointment)` is never stated.
- **QB 09:48 should not get a FIFO check.** Strict FIFO including the interrupted job keeps B01; FIFO among never-started jobs picks B03. Marking `B01/B03 ✔*` blurs the only rule being tested.
- **“Priority, deadline and `est_min` are strongly correlated” in QC is overstated.** C05 is priority 1 and earliest-deadline, but it is the *longest* job. Priority/EDD and SPT diverge on C05; they are not interchangeable except at the 10:54 override. The “two most informative instants are observationally equivalent” sentence in the diagnosis table is muddy relative to the better §2 discussion (10:10 / 10:18 / 11:34 *do* separate SPT from EDD/priority).
- **Scan-delay at 10:10 is not handled locally.** C05’s recorded arrival is 10:11; a 2-minute scan lag can put it in the 10:10 set. SPT still picks C02, so the headline diagnosis holds, but priority/EDD predictions at the first QC decision are incomplete.
- **Exact `est_min` = realized duration is true in every queue, not just QC.** Using that fact to discount only the SPT diagnosis is selective. The prompt also *defines* `est_min` as the estimate visible at arrival, so the “back-filled, therefore circular SPT” story is an extra assumption that sits in tension with the data dictionary.

## Why the score is not higher

A near-flawless answer would have complete eligibility sets, correct skills and calendars, every free-resource instant (including deliberate idleness), internally consistent appointment rules, and confidence numbers that fall when a resource sits idle against every named discipline. This answer performs the right *kind* of analysis and then makes several errors of the kind that analysis is meant to prevent. Under a strict reading, that caps the grade well below the 8–10 band.
