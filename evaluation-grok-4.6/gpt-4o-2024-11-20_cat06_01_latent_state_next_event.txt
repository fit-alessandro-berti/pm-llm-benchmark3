### Why this cannot be mid-to-high under a strict rubric
The quality bar is explicit: integrate obligations, objects, queues, and feed delay; do not treat absence as non-occurrence; do not confuse frequent DFG successors or *eventual* terminals with the next log line. This answer systematically predicts downstream completions (investigations, disposition, authority) that are often **not yet enabled** and **not temporally next**, mis-labels C6-A, nulls alternatives on the noisiest case, and drops the strongest object-centric obligation on C6-E. Those are central failures, not nits.

A nearly flawless answer would have put both A and B in S3 with Evidence Review Completed first, used lot/injury only to shape *post-review* branches and alternatives, treated D’s worklist + missing evidence as first-class observation-model predictions, and put Supplier Analysis on E’s (and A’s) obligation list without claiming its *completion* is the next event. This is not that answer.
