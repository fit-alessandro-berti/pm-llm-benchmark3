The final write-up is technically strong on the parts that matter most: loop/`+`/`X`/`tau` semantics, explicit observation boundaries, and valid two-way counterexamples. Languages are characterized correctly (A has one check per iteration after the `A∥C` join; B shuffles a single check against the whole loop). `A-only-*` and `B-only-*` traces are genuine, complete, and inside the P3 bound. Verdicts match those facts: raw/P1/P3 not equivalent; P2 equivalent only after the stated collapse of checks, approvals, and A/C order.

What keeps it from a top score is a cluster of smaller defects, not a broken core argument.

- The P1 observer is a bit ad hoc (adjacent `C,A` swap plus a separate compliance predicate) rather than a clean attempt-wise homomorphism. The conclusion is still right, but the notion is less precise than the rest of the note.
- Section 2 has broken math markup (`n-1` with a stray backtick), and a few rows restate the same loop/rework fact.
- The “simpler” model is only one operator node smaller than A. That is honestly compared, and the P1-core target is defensible, but the simplification is thin.
- The PM4Py BFS design is careful (silent labels, exact final marking, monotone bounds, marking+prefix state), yet nothing was executed: no unique-trace dump, no soundness/simplicity numbers, no footprint/EMD output. Section 8’s checks also depend on earlier helpers.
- Footprints/EMD are discussed at the right level of caution (no invented weights), but remain speculative.

Overall: rigorous enough on equivalence, traces, and bounded-vs-unbounded, not quite tight enough in formalization, execution, and economy to be nearly flawless.
