**7.2**

The final JSON is a strong process-aware forecast, not a DFG lookup. It keeps control-flow obligations, object/lot relations, queues, and a completion-only observation model in view, and it is especially good on C6-A vs C6-B. It is not near-flawless. Calibration is optimistic in several places, `process_enabled` is used loosely, and a few obligation judgments are incomplete or slightly scrambled with future states.

### What is actually good

The required schema is complete: five cases in order, three predictions each, supporting/contradicting evidence, unknowns, observation caveats, and a real cross-case section.

C6-A vs C6-B is the main test, and it is handled correctly. Same prefix, different latent obligations. A is a mandatory multi-investigation fork (high severity, injury, same-lot / related-complaint signal). B is a post-triage duplicate-closure path. Fast Close is correctly withheld for B because the fast-close window is a triage outcome, not an evidence-cycle outcome.

The observation model is used as intended. Missing events are not treated as proof of missing work. C6-D is the clearest example: the worklist snapshot, the source-system attachment, and the migration flag are combined instead of trusting the analytical prefix. `process_enabled` vs next logged event is at least *attempted* (B2, C2, D3, E3 marked false with a reason).

Queues and the 16:00 batch are not decorative. Review 1–5 h, tech overtaking, regulatory priority, supplier 6–36 h, and the notification cutoff actually change rankings.

### Material problems

**1. Several “next logged” probabilities are too high for the clocks given.**

- C6-D rank 1, Regulatory Assessment Completed at 0.35–0.60. R-R2 opened the case at 12:40; it is 14:00; the stated completion window is 2–14 h after assignment. The earliest plausible completion is ~14:40, and 12 items are waiting. The “already completed but delayed” story is weak inside a 2–14 h window that has only run 1.3 h. That interval is not honest.
- C6-C rank 1 at 0.40–0.62 has the same problem. Reportability priority is a reason to *rank* RA first, not to put a midpoint near 0.5 on it being the next event when assignment is unobserved and the queue is 12 deep.
- C6-A rank 2, Technical Investigation Completed at 0.15–0.30 as the *next* log event, with 7 waiting, one of three engineers out, and historical medians of 8.5–9 h. Possible if work started at triage and jumped the queue; too high as a next-event mass. Together with rank 1, A’s midpoints sit near 0.95 and leave almost no residual for RA, supplier analysis, or anything else.

**2. `process_enabled` is not applied consistently.**

C6-C and C6-E mark Supplier Analysis Completed as `true` while the prose says “if opened.” That is exactly the distinction the field was supposed to enforce. C6-A rank 2 does the same thing more quietly: primary state is still S3, TI is only a suspected obligation, but completion is marked enabled. The calibration note describes the distinction better than the booleans do.

**3. Primary states are hybridized with future states.**

C6-A’s primary label is S3, then immediately “effectively transitioning toward S4.” C6-E is S3, then “effectively headed into S4.” The alternative field already exists for that. Mixing current abstraction with likely next abstraction blurs the state estimate the task asked for.

**4. C6-E underplays reportability.**

Six complaints on one lot is not only a supplier-analysis trigger. Rule 4 also covers a possible reportability signal. The write-up makes Supplier Analysis mandatory (correct) and TI only “possible,” and never puts Regulatory Assessment on the obligation list. That is an incomplete reading of the same lot-signal used so carefully in C6-C.

**5. Rank 2/3 items are often near-term outcomes, not next events.**

Close — No Safety Issue, Disposition Recorded, and Supplier Analysis Completed are repeatedly scored as if they compete with an imminent review/assessment completion. The text admits they are not enabled yet; the intervals still treat them as serious next-event candidates. The calibration note tries to paper over this. It does not fix the numbers.

**6. Small factual sloppiness.**

“~28% (101/356) of reviews trigger another cycle” uses the wrong denominator (receipts→reviews, not reviews). The right local count is 101 against the observed review successors (~342), which is nearby but not what was written. That is a symptom of using the DFG carefully in spirit and a bit casually in arithmetic.

### What is not wrong

No case is a mechanical DFG successor dump. Fast Close is not abused. Supplier Analysis is not forced on C6-C’s different-lot failure mode. Authority Notification is not promoted into a next-event slot that would require an implausible chain before 16:00. Residual mass is at least *described* as leftover, even when A’s intervals do not behave that way.

### Bottom line

This is clearly above a generic or frequency-only answer. Under a strict bar it is still a 7-range paper: the process semantics are right, the A/B contrast is right, and the observation-model talk is right, but the probabilities are not as disciplined as the prose, `process_enabled` leaks, and C6-E’s obligation set is incomplete. That is enough to keep it well short of 9–10.
