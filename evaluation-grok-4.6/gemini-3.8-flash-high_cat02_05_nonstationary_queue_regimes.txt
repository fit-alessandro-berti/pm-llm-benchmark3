## Material numerical and logical errors

**1. The productivity “drop” is false, and it is used as a central pooling moral.**  
§1 Threat 3 and §4 claim completions per FTE fell from ~16.7/16.9 to **14.7**. The same answer’s own regime table contradicts this: R1-WD is \(99.6/5.9 \approx 16.9\); R4-WD is \(100.6/5.9 \approx 17.0\). Completions per FTE are **flat**. Apr 6 is \(94/6.0 = 15.7\); Apr 27 is \(105/6.0 = 17.5\). The real segmented story is mix and service-time inflation under a saturated completion ceiling, not a collapse in cases per FTE. Using a nonexistent 14.7 figure to stage Simpson’s paradox is a core analytical failure, not a typo.

**2. Little’s Law table does not match the data it cites.**  
- Pre-outage Apr 6–14 is **781 arrivals / 776 completions**, net **+5**, mean WIP **\(\approx 18.7\)**. The matrix states Arr 802, Comp 799, net +3, WIP 18.8, \(W \approx 152.1\).  
- Full overload Apr 20–28 is **892 / 786**, net **+106**. The matrix states 914 / 820, net **+94**. +94 is the **weekday-only** imbalance (R4-WD), pasted onto a 9-day window that includes the weekend.  
The Python in §8 would recompute the correct sums from the same rows. Narrative and code are inconsistent on the two multi-day windows that most need care.

**3. Outage inventory reconstruction is self-contradictory.**  
Hourly nets on Apr 15 sum to +47. Starting at ~14 yields a peak near 69 **and a close near 61**. Closing at 47 requires a different initial condition (or conflating `mean_wip` with terminal queue). The text uses the opening that produces peak ~69 and the closing that belongs to another path. That is not a labeled bound; it is an impossible trajectory.

**4. Staffing math contradicts its own target.**  
§7 says raise weekday FTE to **7.2 to keep \(\rho\) below 85%**, then computes \(4945/5184 \approx 0.95\). 85% would need \(\approx 8.1\) FTE at the assumed workload. “Ideally 7.5 FTE for \(\rho \approx 0.91\)” still misses the stated 85% goal. The policy paragraph is arithmetically incoherent.

**5. Mean service times are invented, then treated as data.**  
Only median and p90 are supplied. Baseline “~28 min/case” and overload “~43 min” are unstated distributional assumptions. They are then fed into \(\rho\), staff-minute deficits, the 7.2 FTE recommendation, and the 5.2 FTE “catastrophe” (\(\rho = 1.32\), 28 missed cases/day). The caution in §1 about not substituting median for mean is abandoned in the operational section.
