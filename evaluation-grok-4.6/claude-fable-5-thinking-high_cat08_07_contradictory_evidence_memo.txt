6.2

The memo is strategically competent and mostly well structured, but it is not close to flawless. Several claims overreach the evidence, two operational recommendations contradict each other, and the executive section violates an explicit length constraint.

**Format and constraint failures.** Section 1 exceeds the 700-word cap (roughly 730–760 words). That is a hard instruction, not a stylistic preference. The write-up also leans on exam-only language in places (“violates the constraint”), which does not belong in a management memo.

**Material overclaims.** “The other two-thirds of cases deteriorated” is false as stated. Apartments, business, and rural cases all improved on median duration; only old buildings worsened on the median. The damage is concentrated in tails, first-time-right, complaints, and rework, not in a blanket deterioration of two-thirds of volume. “Easy 60% / hard 40%” is invented. “Manual activation doubled” is imprecise (7% → 15%). “Likely value-destroying outside simple houses” treats an incomplete *aggregate* cost flip (EUR 198 → 207, missing complaint cost after week 4) as if cost were segmented. It is not.

**Logical contradictions.** Section 6 both pauses “any expansion of the pilot to new regions” and scales the full R flow for simple houses “across all regions.” Those cannot both be the plan. Worse, it reinstates surveys for most segments *and* reassigns capacity from the survey team (52% utilization) to second visits. If surveys are coming back, that bench is needed; cannibalizing it is operationally incoherent.

**Process-mining slips.** High fitness is not “not good news”; high fitness *plus* low precision is the diagnostic. Collapsing precision (0.79 → 0.51) is not evidence that metric gains are “unmeasured variance rather than design.” That confuses model underfitting of the allowed language with statistical confounding. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is essentially unused despite being part of the required artifact set. “Flower-like” is asserted, not shown.

**Decision quality is good but leaky.** E is the right family of choice, and the A/B rejection is well argued. The hybrid still scales survey-skipping for “simple low-risk houses” immediately, while ranking M6 as the highest-value measurement because the risk rule is unvalidated and attributes were incomplete before week 3. The memo notes routing risk, then ignores it in the rollout. Thresholds (FTR within 2pp, complaints within 1pp, precision ≳ 0.70) are crisp, but they sit on top of an identification problem the plan does not gate.

**What is strong.** The evidence table is the best part: twelve items, generally clean fact / interpretation / assumption splits, and real use of alignments, DFG edges, resource networks, and segments. Metric-conflict explanations are mostly right on median-vs-tail, handovers-vs-resilience, and visible-vs-downstream cost. VoI ranking is coherent, especially M6/M1/M4 as decision-changing and M7 as a control rather than a strategy test.

**Net.** This is a solid executive synthesis with the correct directional recommendation. Under a strict standard it is marked down hard for the word-limit breach, the region-scale contradiction, the survey-capacity error, unsegmented cost causal claims, and several quantitative simplifications that a decision-maker could act on incorrectly.
