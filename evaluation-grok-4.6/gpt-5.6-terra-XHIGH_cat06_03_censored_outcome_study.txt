**7.2**

The design is substantively strong on the points the rubric cares about most: it refuses to treat omitted applications as non-default, does not train uncritically on the 21,600-row export, keeps an as-of view, and separates activation selection from ordinary censoring. It is not nearly flawless. Several logical, definitional, and implementation gaps are material under a strict reading.

### What holds up

The multi-state target is a legitimate primary choice. The estimand is explicitly policy-conditional, rejected/withdrawn/open cases are competing or pending states rather than zeros, and the counterfactual “default if everyone were approved” is correctly labeled as unidentified. The five missingness mechanisms in §3 are distinguished cleanly, and the assumption table (testable / partially testable / untestable) is the right epistemic posture. The leakage table covers every required field with availability rules that match the known data risks. The validation prose correctly rejects a random case split and treats the July 2025 policy change and October 2025 migration as transportability breaks, not extra i.i.d. holdout. The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points, builds case-level endpoints as of a cutoff, splits by start time, and never invents a survival API.

### Material logical problems

**1. The “business-facing default quantity” can invert early-warning rankings.**  
After defining a pathway vector, §1 presents \(P(\texttt{DEFAULT\_90D}\mid H_l)\) as the quantity the business should use. Under the observed policy this is \(P(A_{90}=1\mid H_l)\,P(D_{90}=1\mid A_{90}=1,H_l)\). High-risk prefixes that the current policy rejects have a low joint probability *because they are not activated*, not because they are safe. An early-warning ranker on that scalar will deprioritize the very applications a lender wants flagged. The staged factorization in §3 would have been the honest operational output; calling the joint pathway probability the business default quantity is a real design error, not a wording quibble. “Unconditional” is also the wrong word for a probability still conditioned on \(H_l\) and \(\pi_{\text{observed}}\).

**2. Joint maturity is applied to every pathway.**  
Requiring \(l+90+90+35\le C\) before *any* label is used is conservative for default, but it discards fully observed rejections, withdrawals, and transfers whose absorbing event occurred weeks after a later landmark. That contradicts the staged analysis promised in §3 (pathway model first, default only on the activated branch). The code’s `classify_pathway` implements the crude joint rule and therefore throws away usable competing-event information.

**3. The implemented analysis is complete-case, not the stated primary strategy.**  
Prose commits to IPCW / weighted likelihood for missing outcome feeds and bounded MNAR sensitivity. `analysis_frame` then keeps only the six fully observed states and drops `MISSING_OUTCOME_FEED` and `ADMIN_CENSORED`. The IPCW connection is a comment. Relative to the required skeleton — “concrete enough to implement” the data-engineering and audit pipeline — the primary missing-label strategy is not actually wired through.

### Design–code and spec failures

**4. Length.** The block is ~250 lines against a stated band of approximately 80–160. That is not a rounding miss; it is outside the spec.

**5. Validation in §7 and in code are different studies.**  
Section 7 specifies train / validation / pre-policy test / two-week policy-transition / migration-as-diagnostics-only. The code assigns a 3-way split by start date and dumps every remaining mature row into `test`, mixing May–June 2025 (same policy) with the thin 1–15 July 2025 post-policy slice. The finer design is not what would run.

**6. Eligibility is not the stated start-event rule.**  
Inclusion requires a valid `Application Started`. The code uses `groupby(case)[timestamp].min()` after the cutoff filter. A case whose first *visible* event is not the start, or whose start name did not survive `ACTIVITY_MAP`, is still admitted.

**7. `get_prefixes_from_log` / `discover_dfg` are dead.**  
They satisfy the “demonstrate” clause only as unused QA objects. Features come from pandas `.head(K)`. That is acceptable as engineering, but it does not show how prefixes from PM4Py would be constrained to pre-barrier events or aligned to landmarks.

**8. Cohort flow is extract-status, not analysis-status.**  
The 48,000 are accounted for, which meets the letter of §2. The table does not quantify how many of the 5,100 opens are `PENDING_90D` versus administratively censored, nor how many of the 3,600 immature activations are delayed versus permanently missing, nor how many landmarks survive the 15 July 2025 maturity cut. Those are the numbers that determine whether the primary estimand is estimable.

### Smaller but real issues

- No `Accepted → not activated` state, despite the given control flow (Accept, then Contract Signed, then Activate). Those cases collapse into `PENDING_90D`.
- Code implements only \(K=3\). Baseline and elapsed-time landmarks are described and then omitted, including the `documents_total` same-day reconstruction rule.
- Feature code is much thinner than §5: no loop durations, waiting times, policy/migration indicators, or as-of queue features, so the skeleton does not demonstrate the historical-reference discipline the design insists on.
- Same-timestamp ties are flagged and dropped from fitting, but there is no stated rule for *scoring* those cases in production.
- Post-policy mature default labels exist for roughly a two-week landmark window. “Limited evidence” is true; there is no sample-size implication for calibration or subgroup claims after 1 July 2025.

### Rubric alignment

This is far above a weak answer (labeled-export-only, rejected-as-non-default, leaked post-decision fields, random split). It is also not a 9–10: the operational target summary is misleading under selection, the maturity/IPCW design is not what the code does, the validation scheme is internally inconsistent, and the skeleton misses the length bound and several stated as-of rules. Under strict grading those are enough to keep the score in the low 7s.
