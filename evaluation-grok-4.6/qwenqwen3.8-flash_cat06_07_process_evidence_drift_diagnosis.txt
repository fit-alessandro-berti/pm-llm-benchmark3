**7.6**

The final six sections get the main audit right: April is a schema/pipeline incident, June is a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, July–October contain real parts-process deterioration, August–September overlap process, concept, covariate, and (in September) population shift, and October’s 0.16 observed breach rate is not comparable to matured months. Metrics are triangulated with variant rates, DFG edge times, Partner Z shares, and maturity. Falsifiers, staged remediation, and alert gating are present. Those are the load-bearing conclusions, and they hold.

The remaining issues are enough, under a strict bar, to keep this well below a high score.

**1. The required PM4Py sketch is not a working sketch.** `pm4py.filter_time_range` takes `(log, dt1, dt2, mode=...)`. Passing `"time:timestamp"` as the second argument and inventing `timeframe="month"` would not produce an event-calendar cohort. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is called only to count rows, and `discover_performance_dfg` is called only to count edges; the actual duration statistic is a homemade consecutive-event join. That join is not the same object as a performance DFG and will miss non-adjacent follows. For a task that explicitly asked for those functions as audit instruments, this is not a cosmetic code nit.

**2. Feature timing is blurred at the prediction point.** The model scores immediately after diagnosis. Case-level `Order Part → Part Received` duration is not a prefix feature. The answer repeatedly treats “add part-delay features” and “same feature buckets (e.g., part-delay bucket)” as if they were valid at scoring time. Supplier-level SLA known at diagnosis would be legitimate; realized case delay is leakage or a post-hoc explanation, not a training feature. The write-up never draws that line, so the retraining/feature-repair plan is not operationally sound.

**3. The “same-path” test is internally inconsistent.** Concept drift here is a change in `P(breach | diagnosis prefix)`. Conditioning on the realized part-delay bucket absorbs the main mechanism (longer waits) and would make genuine process-driven concept drift look like “no concept drift.” The table says concept drift is supported if same-path, same-bucket rates rise; that expected result does not match the hypothesis being tested. A two-layer test (variant/prefix only, then delay-conditional residual) is what the evidence actually needs. That confusion sits in the diagnostic core, not the appendix.

**4. Several smaller unclarities compound this.** “Part-supplier delay” is left ambiguous between case duration and supplier SLA. Variant typing by activity membership can mis-assign traces that contain both remote and parts activities. Monitoring thresholds (unknown >1%, missing queue >2%) are asserted without tying them to the January–March baseline or to false-alarm behavior after the May patch. The documented absence of an outcome-definition change is not used to bound label-definition drift. None of these is fatal; together they show the argument is not fully tightened.

**5. What is not wrong, and is not being deducted as if it were.** April’s collapsed AUROC/ECE with stable matured V3 rates and stable part-edge times is correctly read as pipeline, not process. June’s drop in overall breach with stable variant-specific rates is correctly read as mix. August 0.28 vs predicted 0.19 is not blamed on 7% immaturity. October is not treated as recovery. Partner Z is not naively called unfairness. Recalibration is not offered as a cure for April or for immature October labels. Those distinctions are why the score is not in the middle of the scale.

A near-flawless answer would keep this diagnosis, fix the time-range API, use performance-DFG statistics as performance-DFG statistics, and split prefix-conditional tests from delay-conditional tests so feature repair cannot leak future process time into a diagnosis-time model. This draft does not.
