**3.2**

The write-up mimics the required outline and avoids the worst “train the 21,600 and call everyone else non-default” design, but it is not a coherent study. Core estimand, cohort arithmetic, competing-risk logic, calendar, leakage repairs, validation, and the PM4Py skeleton are all wrong in ways that would produce a misleading model.

### 1. Target definition
The primary target (90-day default **given activation**) is a legitimate *observational* estimand, but it is not operationally specified. Prediction time is circular (“the first event that meets the prefix criteria”) and sits **before** activation is known. Fitting only on cases that later activate while scoring early prefixes is conditioning on a future event. That is either (a) a booked-account monitor whose prediction time should be activation, or (b) an early-warning screen that needs a different target. The text never chooses.

The estimand sentence (“conditional risk … under the assumption that activation is the relevant policy decision point”) does not match an early-prefix model. Population at inference time is unspecified.

The alternative is worse. Treating reject/withdraw/transfer as “competing outcomes **(non-default)**” is the convenience coding the prompt forbids. A CIF “of default **among activated cases**, with competing risks for rejection/transfer” is internally contradictory: once the sample is activated, rejection is no longer a competing event for post-activation default. “Causal pathway” is unjustified.

### 2. Cohort construction
Inclusion says start events **before** 1 Jan 2024. The observation window is 1 Jan 2024–31 Dec 2025; that rule empties the study. “At least one prefix event before the prediction time” is tautological.

The flow table mislabels the 21,600 **labeled** activations as “Activated cases.” The 3,600 immature activations are also activated, so booked accounts are 25,200, not 21,600. Transfers are “excluded” in the rules and “censored” in the notes. Still-open vs not-yet-mature vs selectively unobserved are not given distinct as-of rules. There is no cutoff logic of the form: include a case at time \(t\) only if the prefix is complete by \(t\) and the label is known by \(t\) (90 days + 7–35-day feed). All 48,000 are asserted to have usable prefixes with no evidence.

### 3. Censoring and missing labels
The prompt required a clean split among ordinary right-censoring, delayed labels, activation selection, transfer, and permanently missing feeds. The answer collapses the 3,600 into “ordinary right-censoring” and then redefines delayed labels as “missing at **prediction** time.” At an early prefix, **no** case has its own 90-day label; delay is a **training/maturity** problem after the horizon.

Primary analysis (Fine-Gray on **activated** cases with rejection/transfer as competing risks, censoring still-open cases) is not a valid model for the stated primary target. Those competing events are not in the activated risk set. Sensitivity “binary default vs non-default, ignoring competing risks” is underspecified and, if applied beyond booked accounts, again codes undefined outcomes as non-default.

Assumptions are thin. Transfer linkage is only **partially** testable (60% linked ≠ MAR). Partner missingness is closer to MNAR and mostly untestable; no concrete handling (exclude `source_system`, bound analyses, IPW under stated assumptions) is given. No distinction between “undefined under the observed policy” and “censored but defined.”

### 4. Prefix extraction
Fixed length 3 plus “if ambiguous, first event after 24 hours” is not two well-defined schemes; it is one confused hybrid. Dropping cases with \(<3\) events or \(<24\)h systematically removes early reject/withdraw paths. Document loops are named but not handled (the 3rd event may be another document request). Migration duplicates are ignored.

Using `discover_dfg` to “map renamed activities” is not how a DFG works. Canonicalization needs an explicit synonym table around 15 Oct 2025. Same-day `documents_total` leakage is not addressed here.

The snippet invents APIs (`csv_importer.import_dataframe`, `min_length`/`max_length`, a `length` column on prefixes). The required `format_dataframe` path is absent. `get_prefixes_from_log` returns a log, not a filterable frame.

### 5. Process-aware features
The list is generic and misclassified. `risk_score` and `documents_total` are not resource/queue features. “Aggregated risk_score over the prefix” is prefix-derived, not historical reference. Missing: as-of queue/workload, handoffs, waiting time, calendar/policy-regime flags (pre/post 1 Jul 2025, pre/post migration), and any reference-time feature that needs other cases or HR history. No rule that every feature is computed on events \(\le\) prediction timestamp.

### 6. Leakage audit
Dropping `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and future events is directionally right; the **availability rules** are not.

- `final_limit` appears at the credit decision, **before** activation; the leak is post-**prefix**, not post-activation.
- `collection_status` exists only **after** activation; “must be before activation” is the wrong gate for an early-prefix model.
- Same-day `documents_total` is not fixed by “use the value at the prefix timestamp”; you need the prior snapshot or events strictly before \(t\).
- Resource experience is stored at **extract** time; it cannot be “repaired” from this event file.
- `label_available_at` is an as-of **filter**, not a feature that must precede prediction time. Labels are supposed to arrive long after the prefix.
- Latest `risk_score` “before the prefix” drops a valid on-event update.

`default_90d`, `data_extract_time`, and `source_system` are unaudited.

### 7. Validation
The calendar is a year off. Policy change is **1 Jul 2025**, migration **15 Oct 2025**. Train/val sit entirely in 2024 (all pre-policy). Test dumps 1 Jan 2025–14 Feb 2026 into one bucket, mixing pre-policy, post-policy, and post-migration, and includes starts that cannot mature by 15 Feb 2026 (90 days + feed lag). No rolling-origin backtest, no maturity gap between train and “deployment,” no calibration plan.

The required explanation of why a **random case split** is misleading (future policy/migration leaked into the past, mixed selection regimes, non-causal time order) is missing.

Metrics are sloppy: “AUC for cumulative incidence” is unspecified; log-rank is not a predictive metric; no time-dependent AUC, IPCW Brier, calibration, or competing-risk proper scoring. Subgroups omit channel, region, amount, and policy period.

### 8. Limitations
A few correct caveats, but the decision-use boundary is not drawn: \(P(\text{default}\mid\text{activated},\text{prefix})\) must not be used to score or reject **all** applications. The three “deployment” risks are mostly pre-existing data issues. Performative effects are missing: the score changes who is accepted, who loops for documents, and therefore future labels and prefix distributions.

### 9. Code
This is not an implementable pipeline (~40 lines, required 80–160) and ignores the stated APIs.

- No `pm4py.format_dataframe`.
- Old/nonexistent CSV importer; unused `log_converter`.
- `EventLog` treated as a DataFrame.
- Case metadata is **global** (`min`/`max` over the whole log), not `groupby(case)`.
- `is_activated` compares **case IDs** to activity names `"Contract Signed"` / `"Account Activated"`.
- Merge keys do not match (`case_id` vs `case:concept:name`).
- No temporal split in code (despite the note claiming one). `split_train_test` is not even shown as a rejected baseline.
- No stripping of post-prefix events/columns.
- Label filter `label_available > "2024-01-01"` is not a maturity rule.
- No cohort-count audit against the 48,000, no real leakage check, no comments tying in survival/competing-risk stages.
- `get_prefixes_from_log(..., min_length=1, max_length=3)` is not the usual PM4Py signature.

### Bottom line
Themes (selection, competing exits, leakage, temporal validation) are named, then implemented incorrectly. The design still conditions on future activation, mis-counts the booked population, applies the wrong competing-risk model, places the policy break in the wrong year, “repairs” data that cannot be repaired, and ships non-runnable PM4Py. That is well below a defensible study, even before applying a high bar for nearly flawless work.
