**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and methodologically above a weak baseline: it uses `max` rather than a sum for the parallel join, pauses work at calendar close, mixes FIFO vs priority, treats the temperature-excursion flag as a common-cause shock, and does not collapse the forecast to one false-precise timestamp. Those are the right instincts. Under a strict reading they are not enough, because the numeric and calendar scaffolding has several material defects.

**Systematic date error.** Friday 18 September 2026 implies Monday 21, Tuesday 22, Wednesday 23, Thursday 24, Friday 25, Saturday 26, Sunday 27, Monday 28. The write-up labels the next Monday as 22 September and then shifts every subsequent weekday by +1, including calling 26 September a Friday (it is Saturday) and 27 September a Saturday (it is Sunday). Elapsed-hour figures such as ~72 h to Monday afternoon are those of Monday **21** 15:00, not the 22nd. The required clock dates are therefore wrong throughout Sections 2–4 while the hour arithmetic silently uses the correct weekdays. That is a serious failure on a task whose explicit grading axis is calendar-time reasoning.

**Impossible earliest path.** Under both stated disciplines the first free chemist (remaining 0.4–0.9 h → free ~15:54–16:24) takes the urgent sample, not B-417. The earliest B-417 start is when the second analyst frees (~16:42–17:30). Minimum chemistry service is 1.5 h, so finish is after 18:00; the brief forbids overtime. Friday chemistry completion is not a low-probability tail — it is incompatible with the queue model. Section 3 even admits that priority work is paused Friday evening, then Section 2/4 still keys earliest release (Mon 09:00–11:00, 65–68 h) to “Fri chem finish + empty QA.” The rare 5–10% “fast track” start at 15:54–16:12 repeats the same contradiction. A defensible earliest bound is Monday late morning to early afternoon after a Monday join and a non-empty QA queue, several hours later than claimed.

**Contamination clock is internally inconsistent.** Additional 20–30 h confirmation incubation is added to Sunday technical-ready time in one place (`Sun 12:00+20 h = Mon 08:00`) and implicitly to a Monday readout in another (`usable Mon 09:00–Tue 10:00`). If confirmation starts only after the indication is read, the join moves to Tuesday; if it is an automatic extension of the primary incubation, Monday afternoon is more typical. The scenario that was required to carry this path does not pick one mechanism and compute it cleanly.

**Local arithmetic errors.** The documentation-query example (Tue 10:00 + 8 working hours) has 10 QA hours left until 20:00, so resume is Tuesday evening, not Wednesday morning. S4’s tail “Fri 26 Sep 18:00, ~171 h” does not match either the true Friday (25th, ~170.5 h) or the mislabeled 26th (~194 h). P80 is described both as the band Wed–Fri and as “80% by Friday evening,” which blurs a percentile cutoff with a wide date range. Unconditional documentation-investigation probability (~0.05–0.15) looks low once the 0.30–0.55 investigation rate on anomalous branches is mixed in.

**Smaller but real looseness.** Priority start “~16:10” still steals the urgent sample’s slot. Smooth-path text says chemistry is usually the slower branch by 1–5 h, yet driver 1 is weekend microbiology authorization — not contradictory, but the write-up never reconciles when that driver is inert (FIFO chemistry already joining Monday afternoon). P90’s upper 310 h does not reach Friday 2 October. “Europe-compatible calendar” is unexplained filler.

**What still works.** Assumption ledger, overlap disclosure, FIFO/priority split, Friday 18:00 / QA 20:00 / Sunday QA closure, future QA backlog as initial 5 plus overdispersed arrivals minus Fri/Sat completions, excursion-induced positive dependence, electronic release as the modal terminal event, and the excursion-flag resolution as the highest-value next observation are all the right calls. The weekend is not ignored and stages are not naively summed.

Those strengths describe a competent sketch. The off-by-one date spine, the structurally impossible Friday chemistry finish, and the sloppy S3b/query arithmetic keep it well short of a high score under the instructed standard.
