The answer is complete, correctly structured, and substantively engaged with the multi-objective problem. It does not invent a single optimum, separates hard rules from preferences, and produces a full assignment table that respects the letter of most constraints (max 4, high-overtime protection, Security/Approval rules, 20% training share, skill-0 block on Eva/Jonas). That is not enough for a high score under strict grading: the policy and the batch are only loosely coupled, several assignments fight the problem’s own evidence, and the PM4Py sketch contains real errors.

**What is solid.** Section 1 states the right objectives and mostly the right hard rules. Section 2 includes every required scoring family, eligibility filters, quota logic, and exception handling, and it treats the policy as decision support rather than an autopilot. Section 4 compares all seven requested dimensions without claiming dominance. Section 5 is appropriately non-punitive. Section 6 covers every mandated audit indicator with log fields and targets. **Assignment quality is the main failure.** The table reads as expert judgment, not as the output of the algorithm in Section 2. No scores, candidate lists, or weight applications are shown, so it is impossible to verify that the recommended batch is what the policy would actually select.

The worst call is **C001**. The log evidence says keeping a weak-skilled owner adds a median **3.3 hours of service time**; remaining SLA is **3 hours**, the note says the case needs a specialist, and complexity/handover are both high. **Capacity is asserted, not checked.** Hours remaining never bind. R6 (6.5h) is given two urgent cases (2h and 4h SLA) plus trainee diagnosis; R3 (6.0h) is given urgent high-complexity security, high-complexity rework with three prior reassignments, and supervision of C003; R8 is pulled into C001, C009, and C015; R1 (already 9 open cases, 5.5h) gets a high-priority negotiation plus two other cases. The `policy_deviation_rate` column check is sloppy. These are not cosmetic.

That is clearly above a failing design, and clearly not near-flawless.
