5.6

The write-up is complete, on-format, and operationally literate, but it is not a near-flawless process-mining redesign. It repeatedly restates peak-period utilization as if that were a derived migration result, underuses the DFG and variant evidence, and contains several concrete process errors.

**What is adequate.** Four scenario types match the required axes (mix shift, machine failure, validator/specialist absence, courier compression). Several conflict hints are respected: Urgent-first is capacity-capped; I8 is treated as a bottleneck-shifter, not a cure; I10’s transport/traceability cost is named; case types are not all prioritized at once. The table has the required columns. Leading vs lagging indicators are distinguished. One residual risk is attributed to the redesign itself.

**Process-mining evidence is thin relative to the brief.** The frequency DFG is barely used. The answer does not quantify the peak-period path shift (technical validation → medical validation and hold-for-specialist rising sharply while volume falls), the collapse of the Urgent batch-wait bypass (74% → 58%), Complex-panel’s 3.1× medical-validation and 2.4× specialist-review odds, or Repeat-after-failure clustering after night shifts and M2. Performance edges that dominate cycle time—recollection 42h/60h, batch wait 9h/22h, specialist hold 18h/48h—are almost unused. Bottleneck “migration” often just reprints the given peak utilizations (pre-analytical 96%, technical validation 101%, medical queue 22h) rather than tracing how a specific relief action overloads the next station.

**Concrete errors and sloppy policies.**
- Routine cases already often skip medical validation (normal DFG: 11,700 technical validation → release vs 3,900 → medical validation). Treating “let senior techs release Routine without medical validation” as a Scenario C innovation misreads the log.
- I4 is a poor Scenario C control. Cross-training technical validators into accession correction attacks the wrong station when the shock is medical/specialist absence, and it would further starve technical validation (already 101% in peak). The 4-week cross-train lead time is ignored; I4 is treated as an on/off switch.
- “Hold Routine releases to avoid flooding M2” confuses I9 (batch reporting at release) with withholding Routine from assay. Holding releases does not protect M2.
- Scenario A is essentially the historical peak mix with a round-number +30% volume, not a distinct stress design. Weekly peak volume in the log is closer to +40%.
- I2 and I3 are not analyzed despite being the most dangerous manager proposals. Dedicating Routine to M2 (I3) is especially poorly handled: the log already ties repeats to M2 and night shifts. Inventing “dynamic batching” does not substitute for rejecting or tightly conditioning those interventions.
- I5 is placed in the *robust* package. That is a temporary capacity add, which the brief warns against treating as the default answer.
- The 60% Urgent assay cap, and the “normal 4% / peak 8%” defect bands, are asserted without calculation. I6 is called a centerpiece intervention but scored Neutral for every case type in the table—internally inconsistent, and wrong given 42–60h recollection loops.
- Rerouting assay/technical staff to registration in a courier storm assumes skills the log does not grant (only I4 describes cross-training, and in the other direction). During a late dump those downstream stations will not stay idle.

**Underweighted constraints.** Specialist review is the worst peak queue (36h median, 48h hold-to-medical) and is structurally tied to Complex-panel and many Urgent paths, yet the redesign centers intake smoothing and a generic evening medical shift. Accession correction (two people, 14h peak queue) is almost unused. The dashboard omits the two most important leading signals for this process: Urgent age-in-system versus the 18h penalty, and specialist-review queue. The residual risk that managers will “not buy another assay machine” is a capacity cop-out, not a process vulnerability created by the design.

**Net.** This is a structured, partly grounded operations memo with real bottleneck-migration language, not a rigorous reading of the event log. The conceptual mistakes (existing medical-validation skip, I4 as a medical-absence policy, hold-release vs hold-process) and the unused variant/DFG facts are enough to keep it well below a high score under a strict rubric.
