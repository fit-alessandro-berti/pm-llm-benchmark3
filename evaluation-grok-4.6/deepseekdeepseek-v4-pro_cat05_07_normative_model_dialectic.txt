6.4

The written briefs and case work are serious and mostly disciplined; the required executable model is not. Under a strict reading of the rubric, that gap, plus several steelman and semantics misses, keeps the answer well below the near-flawless band.

**What holds up.** Sections 1–5 separate authority, scope, logging, and control-flow more carefully than a split-the-difference review would. The burden rule (contemporaneous authority for exceptions; no post-hoc rationalization) is the right one. The defense correctly treats Request_More_Info, QA_Clearance, the CRM gap, the duplicate register, rejected-lead scope, and reactivation as model/data/scope problems rather than misconduct. The prosecution is right that PD-3/FD-7 expiry is real, that belief is not authority, and that SUP-703 and SUP-708 are control failures, not M0 bugs. The matrix is complete, including resource/COI cases that fit M0. Case calls on 701, 702, 703, 704, 706–712, and 714 are defensible.

**Dialectical fairness is not at full strength.** The prosecution never presses its strongest formal point: PD-3 is minutes and email, never folded into CS-9, and the Compliance observer later denied approving any weakening of CS-9. A steelman would treat even in-window Auto_Approve as formally unauthorized against the only controlled standard, then distinguish managed exception from compliance. Instead the brief *grants* PD-3 as concurrent authority and mis-heads SUP-702 (and SUP-715) as “PD-3 violations.” SUP-715 is a standard-path COI/disclosure case, not a pilot case. That is sloppy traceability. The defense overclaims SUP-708 as “the only case of its kind” on a 15-row extract and treats 14/312 as the full eligibility failure while downplaying nine missing attributes. System-level “materially compliant in aggregate” then splits the difference the prompt forbade: expired-exception normalization plus failed eligibility gates are a system-control finding, not just a few outliers beside an obsolete model.

**Several conclusions are overconfident or category-bleed.** Stipulated fact 10 already convicts SUP-703/708. SUP-713 as COMPLIANT / High is too clean: the mapping was approved a week *after* the case. Equivalence of content supports “not business misconduct,” not high-confidence contemporaneous model fit; MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT or a qualified COMPLIANT is tighter. SUP-705 as NONCOMPLIANT is legally coherent, but calling the emergency “still factual” while also saying that cannot matter is finer than the evidence. SUP-712’s “aliases suggest awareness” is speculation. Invented Layer-2 numbers (EUR 50k auto cap, 5-day quality window, 12-month cert, 48-hour ratification) needed a brighter “design proposal, not source” label.

**The POWL is the decisive failure.** The prompt required one *executable* PM4Py construction that actually represents concurrent standard checks, manual vs auto vs emergency routes, a Request_More_Info mechanism, reject, and notify-before-close. This block would not run and would not mean what the commentary claims.

- `Operator` is never imported. `OperatorPOWL.XOR` is not the PM4Py API; M0 already showed `Operator.XOR`.
- `info_loop_choice` and `activation_merge` are used in edges but omitted from `M1.nodes`.
- A back-edge on `StrictPartialOrder` is not a loop. The text admits POWL POs cannot cycle, then adds the cycle anyway. The defensible encoding is `OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, …)`.
- `Request_More_Info → Provide_More_Info` invents an event the log does not contain. SUP-707 would still deviate.
- `checks_block` (sanctions ∥ quality) sits *before* the XOR for every path. Auto and emergency traces that activate before Quality_Review therefore cannot fit the graph. That is the main authorized deviation the revision was supposed to express. Comments punt it to “Layer 2,” which is an evasion, not an approximation.
- The same `quality` / `manual_approval` / `activate_standard` objects are reused across exclusive branches and the merge XOR. Emergency also introduces a second `Sanctions_Check` after the mandatory checks block, implying a double sanctions fire.
- `Activate_Supplier_Provisional` is a new label. Conformance against the given log (always `Activate_Supplier`) will miss the emergency path the model pretends to add.

Layering (POWL vs DECLARE/pandas vs IAM vs policy registry) and the overlay/event schema are the right *kind* of revision: stable `notification_id`, eligibility snapshots, rule/version IDs, alias resolution, immutable effective vs record time. Those do not rescue an invalid control-flow object.

**Net.** Strong legal/process commentary with real case discrimination, undercut by a weaker-than-required prosecution steelman, a few evidence overreaches, a hedged system verdict, and a non-executable POWL that still forces Quality_Review before every decision. That is a good draft review, not a near-flawless one.
