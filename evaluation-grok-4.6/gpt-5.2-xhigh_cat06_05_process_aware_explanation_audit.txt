**7.4**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that hits the main quality bar: it catches obvious leakage and several subtler lineage defects, keeps congestion and prefix-age, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as later-feasible rather than impossible, and generally refuses causal/actionable overclaim. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, the misses below are enough to keep it well short of the top of the scale.

**What it gets right.**  
Section 1 is complete against the coverage contract (top-12, all five patterns, local-only fields). The critical calls on `total_case_duration_hours` (near-label leakage), `full_trace_variant_id`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_decision`, and `last_resource_role` are correct. The SQL as-of bug, nightly 23:00 snapshot, closure-populated `final_handler_team` without SCD2, and untimestamped `risk_score_current` are the right second-order defects. The same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern is correctly treated as a data artifact plus a normative impossibility, not as a real DFG rule. Queue length and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are preserved rather than discarded. Region/channel are kept as proxies under fairness review instead of being purged. X/Y as `recompute` versus Z as `invalidate` is a defensible split.

**Concrete inaccuracies.**  
Case Y’s `removed_or_repaired_drivers` includes `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, which is not a Case Y local driver. That is a straightforward attribution error. Case X’s removed list adds `final_handler_team` and `total_case_duration_hours`, which do not appear in the Case X explanation (they can only be smuggled in via the opaque +0.21 residual). That blurs local versus global leakage and makes the JSON look padded.

**Process-awareness gap at this specific `t0`.**  
The prediction time is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Suggested repairs such as `num_document_requests_so_far_at_t0`, `appeal_seen_in_prefix`, and `prefix_variant_id_at_t0` would be nearly degenerate: document cycles, appeals, and variant divergence have not started, so those prefix features are likely constant and not an “early warning.” The Case X narrative notices that those activities are unobserved, but the remediations still treat `t0` as a generic prefix time. That is a real process-mining miss, not a wording nit.

**Appeal vs impossibility is good, but not sharp enough.**  
The table correctly marks the appeal pattern as future-only and later-legal, unlike the closed-before-assessment pattern. At this `t0`, however, appeal is not merely unobserved; it is not enabled (only after Partial/Deny). Section 2 eventually says this; Section 1 still leans on “has not occurred.” Case X also jointly leaks `final_decision = Partial` and `appeal_seen_anywhere = 1`—the decision that enables the appeal—yet the revised writeup never uses that coupling.

**Standards are implied, not stated.**  
The task asked to explain the different standards for predictive validity, causal interpretation, operational actionability, and fairness review. Section 3 encodes those constraints inside class-wise “may / may not claim” bullets, which is substantively close, but it never sets the four standards out as distinct tests. A reader can still confuse “keep after fairness review” with “safe to show a claimant as a reason.”

**Smaller technical slips.**  
`queue_length_at_t0` is allowed “symmetric tolerance but never future,” which is internally contradictory. `initial_reserve` is treated as possibly mutable without lineage evidence. Case Z’s note omits `calendar_week = 52`, the one temporally admissible local driver and a holiday/regime confounder the global notes already flagged. “Claim Closed is effectively impossible as an immediate next step” understates reachability: it is not enabled until after a final decision. The fraud-pattern row conflates “observed sequence is future-only” with “activity may be enabled-next.” Case Y’s narrative that low queue “should lower risk” is a mild causal slide the later boundary then has to walk back.

**Section 2 and 5.**  
The validation suite is credible: as-of contracts, alignment versus DFG, partial order on timestamp ties, decision-round for repeated settlements, and explanation gating by observed / enabled / future-possible / impossible. Recommendations cover the required plan (feature store, timestamps, prefix recompute, conformance, calibration, stability, fairness, drift). These sections are the best part of the answer; they do not offset the JSON error, the degenerate-prefix repair, or the missing four-standard frame.

The audit is usable and mostly right. It is not a 9–10: those require no misattributed drivers, a crisp epistemic framework, and remediations that respect how early this `t0` actually is.
