**6.3**

The answer is in the right decision-theoretic family: it refuses I-03, separates I-05 from I-07, treats ARR as distinct from `p_now`, prices spillovers, and is honest that the program is near breakeven. That is not enough. The rule as written is not deployable, the allocation contradicts the rule, and several operational facts are wrong. Under a strict reading those are structural defects, not nits.

## Section 1 — Decision rule

The architecture is sensible (gates → robustified EV → congestion-aware portfolio → reserve). The text is not a rule an operator could execute.

Comparison and arithmetic operators are missing in the places that matter: `EV_dir = ARR_mid × V  450  FA_burden`, `EV_net = EV_dir  S`, `time-to-deadline  90 min`, `p_adj  0.5`, `p_adj  0.70`. The calibration table gives SME an explicit `+0.065` and Strategic Enterprise a unsigned `0.085` for *overprediction*. Later arithmetic (`I-03` `p_adj ≈ 0.74`) shows a minus was intended. Intent is not a deployable offset. A signed table is the whole point of Step 0.

Green/Amber/Red is defined on *pessimistic* ARR and `EV_dir > S`, then abandoned. Section 2 ranks on midpoint `EV_dir` and midpoint break-even `V_q = EV_dir / 0.12`. I-08’s lower-bound EV is negative (`0.05 × 100000 − 450 − 5000 = −450`) and would be Red/Amber under Step 2; it is still `B+` and on the reserve watchlist. The congestion brake (“after 2 slots, clear `1.5 × S`”) is never applied to the only case that would be slot 3.

`S = 6 × p_cong × V_q` with `V_q` taken from the live diagnosis queue is the right object. Using the listed-portfolio average €29.2k as the prior is the wrong population: that average is the ten *alerted* cases, including ineligible I-03 and long-horizon triage, not the six untreated diagnosis-stage cases that actually bear spillover.

The 90-minute remaining-SLA gate is a crude proxy and is applied inconsistently (see I-07). Remaining work after diagnosis (resolution + validation) is never in the eligibility logic.

NNLS is “widened, not shifted” on `p` only. The problem states that *intervention-effect* estimates are uncertain in that segment. ARR is still taken from the supplied range as if it were a known identification interval. That is exactly what the prompt forbids.

## Section 2 — Allocation

**Factual error:** I-06 is `Diagnosis Started`, not `Triage`. Calling it triage understates urgency and overstates option value. A 15-minute imputation hold is still reasonable; treating a diagnosis-stage case as a long-runway triage case is not.

**I-03** is correctly killed. That is the one non-negotiable item and it is done well, including the “don’t perform theater” routing to breach management.

**I-09 as slot 1 is not robust.** It has the highest midpoint EV among urgent diagnosis-stage cases, but it is the segment with the weakest calibration *and* the weakest effect evidence. Lower-bound EV remains positive (`0.18 × 20000 − 1250 = 2350`), which is not the same as “EV robustness.” Putting the highest-uncertainty case in the first slot, without shrinking ARR or forcing the Amber sign-off the rule itself defined, is incoherent with Section 1 and Section 5.

**Scheduling is infeasible.** Two specialists deliver *the* swarm; a swarm is 45 minutes. Slot 1 at ~12:10 and slot 2 at ~12:40 overlap by ~15 minutes. Either both specialists are on one case (no overlap allowed; slot 2 cannot start before ~12:55) or the staffing model is unspecified. “Stagger ~30 min” does not resolve this.

**I-07 is internally contradictory.** The 90-minute gate at 12:00 with 1.5 h remaining makes the case expire immediately, yet the row says “expires ~12:15” and the reserve checkpoint is **12:20**. That checkpoint is after both the implied expiry and the gate. I-07 is also still in *Triage*: even an instant swarm leaves ~45 minutes for diagnosis completion, resolution, and customer validation before SLA. Defaulting to no deploy is the right action for the wrong reasons (spillover economics rather than remaining-process infeasibility).

**I-06 vs the congestion brake.** Slot 3 must clear `1.5 × S`. At the `V_q ≤ €22k` gate they set for I-06, `S ≈ €2640` and `1.5S ≈ €3960`. Midpoint `EV_dir = €2625` fails that test. The allocation therefore violates the rule that supposedly produced it. Making I-06 conditional on verification is right; making it slot 3 under the written brake is not.

I-02 as a time-sensitive second alert is defensible. Tying “window closes ~15:00” to the SLA clock rather than to `Diagnosis Complete` is not. Eligibility dies at DC, which can occur well before the deadline once diagnosis has started.

I-04/I-08 wait-with-trigger is the strongest part of the portfolio logic (option value, high FA, SE overprediction). It is qualitative: no estimate of the value of waiting versus the risk that DC arrives unobserved.

## Section 3 — Expected impact

Midpoint and upper-half arithmetic for I-09/I-02/I-06 is mostly auditable and mostly correct (`0.24/0.15/0.225`, gross €11.9k, net of cost+FA €8.9k, spillover `0.36 × €20k = €7.2k`). The honest “near breakeven, brakes do the work” summary is the right epistemic stance.

Defects:

- Optimistic I-06 uses ~0.26 breaches and €3940 benefit (`≈0.2627 × 15000`). Small, but this table is supposed to be auditable.
- Adverse assigns I-09 a 0.10 effect against a stated lower bound of 0.18, *and* treats I-06 as a phantom. The prompt’s “at least one case gets little or no benefit” is already satisfied by the phantom. Haircutting I-09 below its range is an extra knob, not a scenario.
- Adverse spillover is `0.36` on two slots only if `Δp = 0.03`; that mapping is implicit.
- Minus signs are missing on the Adverse net row (`€5.5k to €9k`) and in the mixture `0.25(5,500)`. The mixture equals `+€1.3k` only if that term is `−5500`. As printed, the table can be read as Adverse being positive.
- Full FA charging is a conservative convention they disclose; mixing it with a later “probability-weighting FA adds €1.0–1.6k” move is used to talk the program above breakeven. Learning value and “public-sector trust” are then stacked on the same side. That is advocacy, not a sensitivity.

`V_q = €20k` central is asserted, not derived from the spillover population.

## Section 4 — Repeated alerts

This is the best section. Cool-down, third-alert suppression, enumerated material events, and “re-entry restores eligibility, not entitlement” are operationally usable. The I-05 vs I-07 contrast is exactly what the rubric asks for, including the point that taking I-07 seriously and still declining is success.

Residual mess in Step 1: “repeat with no prior swarm and `p_adj ≳ 0.5`” would admit I-05 (third alert, never swarmed, SME-adjusted `p ≈ 0.64`) until the later sentence auto-suppresses third-plus alerts. The override is the intended policy; the `or` chain does not say that cleanly.

Max two swarms per incident is stricter than the prompt’s “normally forbidden unless a new event,” which is fine if stated as a hard cap.

## Section 5 — Fairness

The risk list is complete: value starvation, NNLS, differential calibration, label feedback, concentrated spillover. Safeguards are concrete (25% ARR-only floor, €50k winsorization, disparity band `[0.8, 1.25]`, treatment flags, doubly-robust training, spillover ledger, next-day boost).

They are not applied to *this* decision. Winsorization does not touch I-08’s score. The equity floor is weekly, so today’s high-V bias is unconstrained. The 10% randomized holdout is restricted to amber cases, so it will not identify effects on the green cases the policy always treats, and with four slots per day it will not yield usable precision. Language-matched swarm staffing is a wish, not a constraint on slot 1 going to NNLS.

The deeper tension is unaddressed: Section 2 spends the first slot on the least-identified segment while Section 5 warns that silent under-service of NNLS is the risk. Over-service under unquantified effects is the actual choice.

## Section 6 — Monitoring

The five monitoring tracks and pre-registered change rules are above average for this prompt. Several claims are overconfident.

Propensity-matched treated-vs-untreated estimates of ARR, on a policy that selects on EV, stage, and urgency, are confounded by design. Naming doubly-robust methods does not fix the lack of overlap on green cases. “10 consecutive interventions ⇒ halve ARR priors” is an underpowered tripwire. PSI/ECE/Brier weekly on Public Sector and NNLS will be noise at the sample sizes the prompt already flagged. None of this is fatal, but it is written as if it solves identification.

## Net

What the rubric calls a strong answer is present in outline. What a deployable, internally consistent policy requires is not. The decisive failures are: unsigned/illegible formulas; I-06’s wrong stage; slot 3 violating the 1.5×S brake; overlapping 45-minute two-person swarms; I-07’s checkpoint after expiry; and treating NNLS ARR as known while making I-09 first. Those are enough to keep the score well below the near-flawless band.
