**6.3**

The submission is formally complete, unusually well written, and methodologically self-aware. It still fails the central requirement that classifications follow from the stated interpretation. Under the requested strictness, that is a large deduction, not a nit.

**What it does well**

Sections 1–3 and 5–7 are strong. Authority is treated as issue-specific rather than as a single stack; later-in-time and “strongest wording” are correctly refused as automatic trump cards; A6 is stated clearly; emergency necessity is confined to mitigation; ERP times are subordinated to better contemporaneous evidence; and facts / interpretations / assumptions are separated. The hierarchy table is dimension-specific as required. The defeasible catalogue is real logic, not decorative `DECLARE` rules. Section 5 identifies genuine premise-changes. Section 6 keeps policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and unprovability apart. Section 7 is a usable replacement clause and does contain an anti-normalization safeguard.

Those virtues are why this is not a mid-low score. They do not make the assessment nearly flawless.

**Material logical failure: the opinion does not apply its own rules**

This is the decisive defect.

- Rule 2 requires *both* independent budget-owner approval *and* Finance-Controller approval before commitment at ≥ EUR 50k. The `unless` clause overrides only `finance_approver_identity`, and only after 07-15 07:20, only ≤ EUR 150k.
- I5 and the hierarchy table say the same thing in prose: the CFO may move *who signs Finance*; the budget-owner leg is “not the CFO’s to move.”
- Rule 8 says mitigation does **not** change classification.
- I2 / C5 say B2 (Plant + Finance) is not an “ordinary” step ECD-2 can displace.

P-505 and P-507 are then classed **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** anyway.

On P-505 the writer admits the budget-owner leg “remains formally unsatisfied” and relabels it a “residual defect rather than a breach,” using non-required Compliance clearance as invented “independent challenge.” That is a post-hoc patch. It contradicts Rule 2, Rule 8, and the writer’s own I5.

On P-507 the patch is worse: the budget-owner leg simply disappears. The write-up says “the only open item is the Finance leg.” That is false under the catalogue. 110k triggers A2 in full; B2 still requires Plant_Approve; neither is on the record. R12 does not cover Plant or budget ownership. The case is scored as the “best-executed” file by forgetting two controls the method still treats as live.

Two of eight primary labels therefore do not follow from the stated norm hierarchy. That is not a close call about residual risk. It is an internal contradiction on the main deliverable.

A consistent application of *this* memo’s rules would have been NONCOMPLIANT / low severity / strong mitigation for both files (or a rewritten Rule 2 that actually extinguishes the budget-owner leg). Either would have been defensible. Splitting the difference only when the file “looks good” is leniency bias, not interpretation.

**Other accuracy and discipline problems**

*P-502 date treated as fact.* The table does not date P-502. The opinion asserts as fact that commitment was “07-14 at 10:15,” which is the only way D1 is inapplicable. That date is an inference from narrative order and “same day.” It belongs in §1 as an assumption. If the act is on/after 07-15 07:20, the Finance analysis changes and the remaining live issue is A3. Section 5’s alternative never mentions this. Presenting an inferred date as a clock fact is an evidential error.

*P-501 misreads B4.* B4 lets commitment precede the PO in a ≤6h stoppage and lets a *delegated* Shift Lead substitute for an *absent* Plant Manager on Plant approval. It does not say a Shift Lead is an “unauthorized actor” merely by committing. ENG-1 requested, SHIFT-1 committed — A1 is fine. The live defects are B2/R6 (Plant/Finance after the fact at 38k) and failure of B4’s *substitution* conditions for Plant_Approve. NONCOMPLIANT can still be right; the controlling story is the wrong limb. Confidence “High” overstates a messy actor theory.

*R10 silently rewrites C3.* C3 requires two distinct IC members, verbal-then-record ≤4h. The catalogue adds `auth_i ≠ committer`. That is an A1 overlay, not a clause of ECD-2. It should have been marked as interpretation. Using the rewrite to say ECD-2 “never attached” on P-503 is argumentatively convenient; A1 already dooms P-503 without it.

*B2 timing is undertagged in the case rows.* “Before commitment (purposive reading)” in the catalogue is honest; the case table then treats late Plant/Finance as automatic breaches as if the SOP text were explicit. That is a hidden interpretive step.

*INDETERMINATE is defined out of existence.* I9/I11 are coherent, but they convert missing artifacts into findings rather than into uncertainty. That is a choice. It sits poorly with P-505’s unverified skew, P-507’s single-system PDF time, and P-506’s alleged extension — exactly the cases where the brief asked for visible uncertainty at classification level, not only in a later alternatives section.

*D1 is narrowed, then applied unevenly.* I5 correctly refuses to let an uncontrolled email waive A1/A3. The same memo then lets D1 plus “independent challenge” do the work of A2’s second leg on the two sympathetic files, while P-508 is held to the full two-leg A2 because it is EUR 5k over a ceiling in that same email. The 150k reading on P-508 is actually one of the better pieces of the paper; the contrast makes the P-505/P-507 generosity more obvious.

*Smaller slips.* ON-44 is generalized into a standing “principle” for 07-18 clock skew (the outage is 07-14). P-501’s outage-window timestamps are not stress-tested at all. Precedent (“directives as overlays”) is correctly cabined for Compliance, then ignored when the writer needs D1 to do analogous overlay work on Finance. “No case qualifies as INDETERMINATE because every case has one independently decisive finding” is circular once the writer has chosen which finding is decisive.

**What is not wrong**

P-503 as high-severity NONCOMPLIANT on A1 is correct and robust to the scope fight. P-504 is the cleanest emergency-normalization call in the set. P-506 correctly refuses to treat an unrecorded verbal extension as an instrument. P-508’s reading of D2 as an added condition, not a raised sufficiency ceiling, is the better textual reading and is properly paired with a credible alternative. A6 is not used as a cure. Not every emergency buy is blessed. Those points matter.

**Net**

This is a high-quality brief with a broken last mile: the formal method is stricter than the two “good incident” outcomes the writer wanted. Under a normal review this would still be a solid 8. Under the stated standard — classifications must follow the interpretation, minor defects count, only near-flawless work is very high — a paper that misclassifies 25% of the docket against its own rules cannot sit in the 8–10 band.
