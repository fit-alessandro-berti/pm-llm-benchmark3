**7.1**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured review that mostly does what the prompt asked: both sides are steelmanned, facts are separated from authority/scope/logging disputes, MIXED is not used as a dodge, and the case calls are mostly traceable. It is not close to flawless.

**What holds up.** Sections 1–3 are the best part. The burden-of-proof rule is clear (applicable rule, scope, reliable evidence, no valid overlay). The prosecution correctly treats CS-9/M0 as the standing baseline, expiry as real, informal “normalization” as non-authority, and desirable RMI as non-amendment. It also admits genuine model/data defects instead of pretending every M0 mismatch is misconduct. The defense correctly separates trace deviation from business misconduct and is right on concurrency/overlays, rejected-lead ambiguity, reactivation silence, CRM missingness, duplicate register, and label drift. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including 712/715 (M0-fit, non-control-flow breach). The system judgment does not split the difference: 703/708/705/714/712/715 and the ineligible/post-expiry auto population stay noncompliant; 707/709/710/711/713 stay model/data/scope.

**Case-level problems.** 702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is too generous against the author’s own standard. PD-3 requires automated master-data validation before activation; that event is not in the trace. “Eligibility fields complete” is not the same fact. Medium confidence does not resolve the contradiction. 713 as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT is a stretch; the live issue is delayed label mapping plus audit equivalence, i.e. data/alignment, not scope. 711 as DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE is overly timid once both rows share one source transaction ID; the business trace is a standard compliant case plus a migration duplicate. 708 as High rather than Critical underweights activation-before-sanctions on a high-risk supplier (the same class Audit flagged). 715 as NONCOMPLIANT is acceptable only as a disclosure failure; the text sometimes slides toward treating the spouse relationship itself as the breach, which CS-9 does not state.

**Model/code problems (the largest deduction).** The required “executable” POWL is conceptually right (standard S∥Q, manual path, auto path with post-activation quality, emergency path, reject, notify≺close) and the overlay/collection tables are the right layering. The encoding is not safe.

- `OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[rmi])` is the wrong arity. PM4Py loop is `LOOP(do, redo)` (typically two children). This is likely to assert or be undefined, so the block is not reliably executable.
- `SilentTransition(label="No_Request_More_Info_…")` is not a silent tau. A non-null label can become a required log activity.
- The standard “0 or more RMI” encoding is `LOOP(tau, Request_More_Info)`. That is not what was implemented, and the limitation note does not fix it.
- M1 drops reject-after-full-checks. M0/CS-9 allow `S∥Q` then `Reject_Supplier`. Here reject is only a sanctions-then-reject variant, and the standard/auto/emergency arms force activation. That is a control-flow regression, not disclosed as an approximation.
- RMI is cloned per branch and only concurrent inside each arm; that is a defensible approximation, but it is not equivalent to “cycles before a decision” across a shared check phase.

**Smaller defects.** 709 is “Low to Moderate” in the matrix and “Low” in the judgment. PD-3 authority is argued as shaky in the prosecution brief, then relied on to clear 702. The architecture section is clean but slightly tidy about what DECLARE can enforce (business-day SLAs, spend caps) versus what must stay in pandas. None of that is fatal; together with the code/semantics gaps it keeps the score well below the top band.

The analysis would sit in the high 8s if the POWL were valid and 702/713/711 were tighter. Under a strict reading of executability, model completeness, and burden-of-proof consistency, **7.1** is the right score.
