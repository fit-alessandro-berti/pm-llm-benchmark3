**7.6**

This is a strong, coherent modeling write-up: the POWL model is essentially the right object, the tree’s loss is not disguised, the witnesses are well chosen, and the validation plan is more honest than most. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are real inaccuracies, a few logical slips, and some specification gaps that should have been caught.

## What is actually right

The control-flow reading is the correct one for this prompt. Completion-only labels, risk and supplier as exclusive/silent control-flow rather than data, and the trial as `LOOP(ET → IT, AP)` are the right abstractions. The loop encoding is exact for requirement 8: the language is `(ET·IT)(AP·ET·IT)*`, so a passing inspection is the last body execution and `Adjust Parameters` cannot follow it.

The POWL edges match the narrative’s non-series-parallel shape:

- `ARC → RISK` and `ARC → SUP` only
- `PV → TRIAL` and `DCA → TRIAL`
- `RISK`, `SUP`, and `TRIAL` unordered with each other
- all three joined at `RM`

Nesting is also correct: `QR`/`RR` live inside `high_risk`, not the root PO, so they are not executed twice. Unconnected pairs are justified in 4.3 rather than waved at.

The tree

```
RD → ((CM → PV) ∥ (ARC → DCA)) → (Risk XOR ∥ CS? ∥ trial loop) → RM → AC
```

is a genuine conservative under-approximation. It never lets `ET` precede `PV` or `DCA`, never lets release fire without the chosen route, and never ends a trial on `AP`. What it drops is early `RISK`/`CS`. A5 is a clean witness of that loss.

A1–A4 and F1–F3 are the right eight rows: low risk, high risk, supplier, two-iteration trial, an approximation witness, and a cross-branch violation (`ET` before `DCA`) rather than a trivial start/end error. F2 and F3 name the earliest broken condition.

## Real errors and overclaims

**Section 3.3 contradicts the model and Section 6.** The tree is *not* inexact for requirement 7’s trial-start rule. The first parallel block ends exactly when both `PV` and `DCA` have occurred, which is exactly when `ET` becomes enabled. Approval∥trial is also exact in the tree (they share the second `+`). Requirement 7 is represented exactly; requirements 5–6 are over-synchronized. Calling (c) a “deviation that removes traces” is false. Section 6 later says “none for this rule,” so the document is internally inconsistent on a core claim.

**Duplication is mischaracterized.** Assumption 6 says copying `Consult Supplier` or the approval subtree would make “at most once” unenforceable. That is only true if copies sit in AND or sequential positions. XOR-unfolding (mutually exclusive copies for different interleavings) preserves “at most once.” The good reasons to refuse duplication are analysis corruption and explosion; the at-most-once argument is overstated.

**The intended-dependency table is incomplete in the place that matters.** D4 and D7 record two independences. The whole point of the task is the *other* ones: `RISK ∥ PV`, `RISK ∥ DCA`, `SUP ∥ PV`, `SUP ∥ DCA`, `SUP ∥ RISK`, `SUP ∥ TRIAL`. Those are first-class requirements (5–7), not implementation details. D9 and D12 are also definitional identities (`RiskRoute` *is* the XOR), not relations. Section 2 was supposed to state the specification independently of either model; it only half-does so.

**The approximation policy is under-justified.** The chosen seam preserves approval∥trial and sacrifices early supplier/approvals. The other conservative tree — fold `RISK`/`SUP` with `DCA` after `ARC`, then sequence the trial — does the reverse. Both are under-approximations; they are not equivalent for a compliance user. Presenting one seam as *the* conservative policy, without that fork, oversells uniqueness.

## Smaller defects

- Verdict vocabulary in the witness table mixes “allowed / forbidden / rejected.”
- “Three incoming silent arcs” at `RM` is a guess about a particular conversion, not a fact.
- `play_out(..., parameters={"noTraces": ...})` is sketchy against current pm4py parameter names.
- “Tree language ⊂ POWL = intended” is plausible and A5 supports one inclusion direction, but it is asserted as settled rather than scoped to the abstractions in §1. Self-critique 2 admits this; it remains a hole in §6.
- Assumption 1’s “overlap is unobservable” and the later use of `+` / unconnected POWL nodes are compatible, but the write-up could have said more cleanly that the *language* is all interleavings and that true concurrency is simply not in the observable alphabet.

## What is not wrong

I would not ding the models themselves. The Python is self-contained, parent/child wiring is explicit, LOOP child order is correct, `SilentTransition` is used only for the supplier skip, and every required POWL edge is added with `add_edge`. Interpreting “may overlap” as missing order rather than a true-concurrency axiom is the only reading that fits completion-only traces. Preferring POWL as canonical is the right call.

The remaining problems are not “the model is unsafe.” They are overclaim, inconsistency between sections, and an incomplete independent specification. That is enough, on a strict rubric, to keep this well clear of the 9–10 band.
