The submission has the required heading skeleton and a veneer of uncertainty language, but it fails the quality bar on the cases and quantities that matter. Several required analyses are missing, the special-case reconstructions are wrong, and the Python does not reproduce the narrative.

**Critical reconstruction errors.** P05’s S1 interval is fully observed (09:00–09:25); reporting S1 service as `[0, 25]` is simply false. P05’s missing S2 start is bounded by C1 still serving P03 until 09:45, so S2 wait is on the order of 20–50 min and service on the order of 0–30 min — not wait `[0, 10]` and service `[40, 60]`. P07 cannot start S1 before I1 finishes P05 at 09:25, so S1 wait is about 40–75 min and service about 0–35 min; the table’s wait `[10, 25]` and service `[50, 75]` ignore the resource calendar stated in A5. P11 cannot start S2 before C2 finishes P08 at 12:00 (and after the outage), so service is about 0–25 min; assigning both wait and service as ~80 min treats station sojourn as if it were wait *and* service at once. That is exactly the confusion the prompt forbids.

**Internal inconsistency.** Identical 5-minute nominal gaps get different rounding windows (P01 S1 wait `[2.5, 7.5]` vs P02 `[0, 10]`). Differences are sometimes ±2.5, sometimes ±5, sometimes ±10, with no rule. A1 orders ties by case id; §2 uses right-open intervals; the two conventions are never reconciled. S1 mean wait ~52 min does not follow from the case table or the raw timestamps (observed S1 waits average about 65 min). S2 is given 21 queue entries and 5 waiters; S1 completions are P01–P20 only, and the S2 waiters at 14:00 are P16, P18, P19, P20 — four, not five.

**Missing required quantities.** There are no case-level total sojourns for completed cases, no arrival/departure rates by subperiod, no queue/WIP path over time, and no actual Little’s-Law arithmetic. Calling 09:00–12:00 “steady state” is indefensible: that is the buildup interval. Utilization and WIP figures are asserted without a resource-minute ledger or a tie convention applied to a timeline.

**Bottleneck reading is too neat.** S1 waits already rise from 5 min to ~70–100 min before the outage; morning S1 is not “balanced” or “minor queues.” At 14:00 S2 holds more WIP, but that is a late-window snapshot, not proof that S1 was manageable. The 15-minute C2 outage is a delay for P08 and successors, not a demonstrated “tipping point” or “severe” capacity collapse.

**Sensitivity is theatrical.** Optimistic / central / pessimistic are described, not computed. No scenario table, no rounding-only vs imputation-only split, no demonstration that open/waiting cases were retained in the means. Claiming P07/P11/P15 means move by “up to 15 min” is empty when those bounds are already wrong.

**Python fails the spec.** `parse_time` maps blank, `MISSING`, and `OPEN` to the same `NaT`. Means are complete-case `.mean()` over those NaTs — no WIP, no bounds, no scenarios. Missing-start activities are dropped from the log. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are called on the full formatted log, not the claimed projection, inside `try/except`, with no printed case-level table and no explicit case-vs-station labeling of numbers. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is skipped (the comment is acceptable), but the “compact result tables” are a few unprotected averages. The code cannot have generated §§3–6.

The writeup is structured and sometimes locally correct (P16–P22 flags, elapsed vs residual service for opens, C2 outage existence). That is not enough. The core evidence-handling is unreliable.
