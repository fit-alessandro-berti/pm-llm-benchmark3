**6.2**

The abductive core is competent: the June collapse is treated as a stacked logging/ETL artifact, August sign-off is treated as model obsolescence, residual enhanced-review gaps are left open, and stable KPIs are not used to exonerate anyone. That is the right causal skeleton. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading of the spec and the evidence, the write-up has material factual errors, fabricated provenance, a missed required analysis, and a Section 6 implementation that ignores the APIs it was told to use.

**Section 1 — Evidence ledger.** Twelve rows and the required columns are present, and the staged alignment recoveries are used correctly. Several cells are sloppy or false. Item 10 claims `Manual Override` “appears after [the] Aug 12 interface patch” while also counting 20 June–July traces; those traces predate the patch, so the chronology is wrong. Items 5 and 12 invent sources the prompt never supplied (“verified ETL commit history,” “automated system log field null-count analysis”). That is a data-provenance failure, not extra rigor. Item 7’s “210 occurrences” is arithmetically right (150+60) but is used as if it were a single DFG relation. Item 8 does not actually weaken employee noncompliance; relaxing the model absorbs sign-off and leaves the missing-check residue untouched. The omitted high-value attribute, the 11/70 screenshot audit, and the 1 September incomplete-detection warning are buried instead of standing as first-class items.

**Section 2 — Ranking.** The June-drop ranking (label > technical event > order) follows the staged fitness path 32% → 58% → 81% → 89% and is the strongest part of the answer. Two conceptual faults remain. First, the prompt asked to distinguish “cause of the conformance drop” from “real operational concern”; the table never says this in those terms. Second, “real behavioral process change” at 30 is inconsistent with accepting the 1 August policy as a genuine process change. Official sign-off is a real process change that made the tree obsolete; ranking the former near the floor only makes sense if that hypothesis was silently redefined as unofficial drift, which is never stated. Posterior 80 on model obsolescence is fine for August–September and too high as an explanation of the June break. Expected observations are often restatements of already-seen alignments rather than out-of-sample predictions.

**Section 3 — Causal graph and narrative.** The narrative is within the word band and correctly says stable outcomes neither prove pipeline error nor rule out bypass. The “adjacency list” is ASCII art, not an adjacency list, and it is incomplete: `Gateway_release` / `recorded_events` are invented node names, `reviewer_behavior` has no inbound cause, and the required mismatch `true_process ↛ normative_model` is drawn as if policy updates the model. The 89% recovery sentence overstates cleanliness: after label map, queue drop, and source order, log moves remain (`Manual Override`, missing checks). Treating all 55 missing-check traces plus overrides as “potential employee noncompliance” in the same breath ignores the prompt’s explicit statement that bypass versus omitted events is unaudited.

**Section 4 — Counterfactuals.** Eight rows, right columns, and the first four repairs correctly predict the given fitness steps with unchanged business KPIs. Row 7 is internally contradictory: deleting `Manual Override` as “logging noise” fights both IT’s statement and the answer’s own ranking. Row 6’s “~95%+” after a hard gate is hand-waving. Falsifiers are uneven; some are good (source order still shows `Notify → Decision`; low-value cases carry sign-off), others are just “fitness does not move.”

**Section 5 — Diagnostics.** Four phases, and the isolation idea (clean provenance first, then residual alignments, then attribute-aware model comparison, then case audit) is sound. The spec required frequency DFGs to be used or discussed; they are absent from the table. `pm4py.filter_between` is the wrong operator for claim-value stratification. Phase 2 never says how move-on-log versus move-on-model will be separated, which is the whole point of the supplied alignment summary. Phase 4’s “55 cases” audit is right in spirit and still underspecified (sampling frame, provenance of missing `Compliance Check` versus override).

**Section 6 — Code.** This section fails the written contract. The prompt required `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, and `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`. None appear. The tree is hand-built, converted with `pt_converter.apply`, and the log is a pandas expansion. Shared `ProcessTree` nodes are reused in the candidate tree; PM4Py trees are parented, so this is a real construction bug. Alignment *diagnostics* are never computed—only `fitness_evaluator.apply`. DFGs are discovered once and not compared. `precision_evaluator.apply` is printed as a float; that API typically returns a dict. Order repair is a name-swap heuristic, not source-sequence ordering. Staged preprocessing (label only, then drop queue, then reorder) is collapsed into one function, so the code cannot reproduce the diagnostic ladder the prose relies on. Caveats about control-flow optionality versus the EUR 50,000 rule are the one part that matches the spec.

**Section 7 — Conclusion.** The primary explanation of the measured collapse is right, and the “do not claim” list is responsible. Then the answer overreaches. Residual noncompliance is given **80%** confidence after the ledger rated those traces 70 and after the prompt said no reliable audit exists. “ETL code commit records” are cited again as if they were in the dossier. `Manual Override` (20+35) is framed as circumvention of control gates; the only behavioral testimony in the prompt is routing correction. Word count also overshoots the 250–400 band. Process-owner action to version a data-aware model is correct; the confidence calibration is not.

**Section 8 — Governance.** The six rules hit the six required topics and are usable. They are also generic and do not close the specific hole this case exposed: a label can change while the payload stays the same, and a control-flow “optional sign-off” will pass low-value sign-offs. No mention of keeping source sequence numbers as a first-class ordering key in the mining contract, only “don’t use warehouse time.”

**Net.** Stronger than a confused blame-the-staff write-up, far from a clean process-mining diagnosis. The staged-repair reading is the reason this is not in the 4–5 range. Invented evidence, the Manual Override timeline error, the missing DFG phase, the ignored PM4Py API contract, and an 80% noncompliance claim the prompt forbids are the reason it cannot go above the low 6s.
