**7.6**

The final seven-section answer is strong on portfolio logic, DFG grounding, and the anti-frequency argument, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has several material gaps and a few stretched claims.

**What holds up.** The matrix does not rank by frequency. A1 is correctly treated as high local labor / low process leverage with real downstream arrival-rate risk. A4, A5, and A6 are justified from the right artifacts: the 31h mismatch wait and 82% critical-path variant, the 3,300/2,800 missing-doc loop and 52% repeat rate, analyst centrality plus the 20h/28h chain, and the 3-FTE / two-senior specialist bottleneck. A11 is deferred until controls are reliable. The 380 mismatch → contract-prep edge is a genuine control finding, not decoration. P1–P4 have real trade-offs, not renamed copies of the same four modules. Resource shift (execution → exception review) is treated as a design constraint, not a footnote.

**Recommended portfolio is the main weak point.** P4 (A5, A4, A6, A9) underweights the largest synchronization delay in the log: 58h external valuation, 19% late, required in 71% of equipment finance. The write-up admits this and parks A7 in year 2, but “partner dependency” is not a full process-level rebuttal when the prompt’s thesis is that low-frequency sync points dominate. A9 is a defensible control/labor pick, but the prerequisite story is thin: A5/A4/A6 do not actually clean committee-decision data. Terms-match quality is more tightly coupled to packaging integrity and classification (A8/A2) than to statement extraction.

**Concurrency and variants are underused.** The prompt states KYC and financial-document review are concurrent and that this must not be ignored. Section 4 still treats waits as if they stack. Committee’s weekly high-exposure cadence is almost absent, so residual structural wait is understated. Micro / SME / High-exposure and product variants appear only as scattered pilots (Micro/WC for A6, renewals for A9), not as a routing or control-path design.

**Control coverage in year 1 is incomplete.** Compliance’s “one control-strengthening module” is satisfied by A9 (and A4’s audit trail). The live control failures are only partly covered: provisional signing (6.5%) has no hard KYC gate without A10; four-eyes (5.8% at month-end) is hoped away via “less rush” rather than system segregation. That is an optimistic causal leap.

**Rollout and integration claims are a bit tidy.** Calling A6 a deep core-banking slot is an unargued assumption; if it is wrong, the two-slot constraint was used to exclude better modules. A9 in “months 6–12” sits awkwardly against “two deep integrations in the first six months.” Gate language (“materially reduced”) is too soft given the numeric baselines sitting in the table.

**Smaller unclarities.** A1-as-non-module “lite dedup” papers over the same customer-master integration they used to rank A1 down. P2’s A8 is labeled deep without the same rigor. Several effect statements (“four-eyes eases,” “strong improvement on the mainstream variant”) outrun the evidence.

The piece is a competent process-level recommendation with the right architecture. It is not a 9–10: the biggest wait is not in the chosen four, concurrency/variants are thin, year-1 controls leave known violations open, and a few dependency/integration claims are asserted rather than earned.
