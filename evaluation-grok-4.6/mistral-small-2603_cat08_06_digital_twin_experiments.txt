**4.2**

The write-up looks complete and copies the prompt’s structure, resources, and intervention list, but it is not a reliable digital-twin design. Process-mining mechanics, identification of service vs wait, initialization, tail uncertainty, and the PM4Py skeleton are wrong in ways the prompt explicitly warned against.

**1. Model scope**  
The entity table is a glossary, not a simulatable state. There is no process-tree marking, AND-join / pending-review set, inspector location or tour, batch-window, no-show/readiness, violation history (required for D2), or council-week clock. Cross-case dependence is named (architect and district bundling, objections) but not given rules: hold time, geographic radius, who waits for the bundle, or how an objection actually couples cases. “Related applications share rework if one is rejected” is invented. A twin that compares policy under contention needs those variables; listing `related_cases: []` does not.

**2. Parameter estimation / PM grounding**  
This section is the weakest part of the prose.  
- `W = L /` is a broken Little’s Law and is not a substitute for timestamped waits.  
- Start times are missing for many human activities, so the log mostly identifies *sojourn*, not service. “Infer from queue lengths” is not an estimator.  
- `get_start_activities` / `get_performance_spectrum` do not identify arrivals or service distributions. `discover_variants` is not the usual PM4Py entry point (`get_variants` / high-level `pm4py.discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg`).  
- Calendar-aware *net working time* is never estimated, so fitted “service” will include nights, weekends, and council blackouts.  
- Rework text is stipulated as unreliable; planning to mine it treats missing labels as observed.  
- Objections are correctly called a proxy in places, then treated as a regression on duration—the same contaminated proxy.  
- Architect bursts need a clustered / batch arrival model; a hotter Poisson does not produce same-week clumps.

**3. Assumptions**  
Several numbers are presented as if estimated: 10% inspection rework (the table is 9% of *all* variants, not P(rework | inspection)), applicant p90 = 15 days (not in the data), public notice jumping to a fixed 60 days. Log-normal for every municipal activity is false for portal/system tasks and for a 14-day statutory notice. Independence of routing from load and season is implicit and unsafe. No-shows, prior violations, and 40% environmental non-permit time are ignored.

**4. Experiments**  
Baseline + D1–D10 is present, but D10 is “all factors, as above,” i.e. not a fixed combined policy. Stress-3 is just another policy mix, not a stress. Missing stresses the prompt’s physics actually require: heavier Monday/architect bursts, objection-rate spike, longer applicant tails, extra council/holiday closures. There is no CRN design, no computational budget, and no statement of which factor levels enter D10. Trade-off text is generic.

**5. Warm-up and uncertainty**  
This is methodologically unsafe. Complex-variance p90 is ~260 days; a 3-month warm-up from an empty system never fills the long-case stock. An 18-month horizon then yields few completed tail cases. A digital twin should be initialized from a reconstructed live snapshot, not empty + short warm-up. “30 replications ⇒ 95% CI ±5%” is asserted, not piloted, and is implausible for p90 under heavy tails. Truncating at p99 *to avoid outliers* directly contradicts tail SLAs. Parameter (input) uncertainty is only one-at-a-time sensitivity; stochastic and structural uncertainty are not separated. Common random numbers for intervention contrasts are absent.

**6. Validation**  
The heading list matches the prompt; the methods do not. Aggregate “replay” is not token replay / alignment. KS tests on n ≈ 31k will reject every serious model; no practical-significance alternative is given. Random 20% holdout is wrong for non-stationary demand—use a *temporal* holdout. “Real queue lengths” are not in the log. Validating that D4 “reduces bottlenecks” is impact evaluation, not model validation. Cross-case patterns and work-outside-calendar are not validation targets. The start/wait split they cannot identify is not listed as unvalidatable.

**7. Decision criteria**  
Thresholds fight the empirical table: rework ≤15% while missing-docs alone is 19%; end backlog ≤50 against ~1,700 arrivals/month; complex median ≤150d vs current 140d. Fairness ≤10% across districts ignores stated D3/D5 travel. Applicant effort (D1/D7) and inspection-risk (D2) are not criteria. There is no Pareto / regret rule, only a wish list. They correctly avoid claiming optimality; that is one of the few constraints they meet.

**8. Python skeleton (disqualifying for “PM4Py conventions”)**  
The prompt required `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.parse_process_tree` (or a clear limitation), variants, DFG, performance DFG, arrivals, and wait/service *if timestamps allow*, plus modular arrival / routing / calendar / experiment placeholders.

What was delivered:
- Internal `dataframe_utils.format_dataframe` instead of `pm4py.format_dataframe`.  
- `from pm4py.algo.discovery import discover_variants, discover_dfg, discover_performance_dfg` — that module/API does not exist. Unused/wrong imports (`generic_tree`, `sojourn_time_get`).  
- A hand-rolled parser that **swaps operators**: `+` → XOR, `X` → OR. In process trees `+` is AND and `X` is XOR. Parallel planning/fire/environment becomes exclusive/mis-parsed; the AND-join before decision drafting disappears.  
- The tree string in `__main__` is not the given tree: loops flattened, `Completeness rework` no longer the redo child, public notice no longer optional.  
- With only completion times they **sample random lognormals/exponentials** instead of inter-event sojourns. That is collapsing wait and service without a real estimator.  
- Arrivals: `groupby().min().diff()` is not sorted in time; rate units are seconds vs day-scale demand; no case type, district, or architect burst process.  
- `Queue` is used and never defined. `run_experiment` references undefined `sim_engine` and `t in ...`. Utilization is computed as calendar availability.  
- **No routing component**, though required. No batching/dependency object. Experiments reuse one engine without reset. Calendars are hardcoded Mon–Fri 08–16 and cannot represent fire Tue–Thu, inspector Tue–Fri, legal council weeks, or 40% environmental reservation.

**Constraints:** objections-as-proxy and no optimality claim are mostly honored. Calendars appear in prose only. Non-stationarity and cross-case dependence are discussed and then almost absent from the executable design. Service/wait are collapsed. PM4Py usage is inconsistent with the stated conventions.

A competent outline with systematically wrong PM operators, a non-identifiable wait/service split, an empty-system warm-up shorter than the tail, tail truncation, and a skeleton that would not run under real PM4Py is not near-flawless. Strictly, 4.2.
