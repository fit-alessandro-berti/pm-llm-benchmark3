**3.0**

This answer has the outline of a digital-twin design but fails the required specification on structure, process-mining grounding, simulation methodology, and code. It is a generic DES write-up dressed in the problem’s vocabulary, not a usable design.

**Format.** The required eight numbered sections are not produced. Validation is a second “5”; decision criteria and the skeleton become 6 and 7. Applicant/third-party response is not a standalone assumption class. Stress scenarios are missing from the experiment matrix. Those are not cosmetic: they are the contract.

**Section 1 is not a state model.** Entities and “stakeholders” are listed, but the twin is never specified as a discrete-event system with an event calendar, token/case objects, AND-join wait-for-reviews, loop counters, or policy flags. Queue length is wrongly equated with waiting time. Cross-case dependence is named (architect, objections, district travel) and then left unoperationalized: no bundle membership, no shared latent objection object, no inspector tour/batch state, no architect burst window. Calendars that the brief treats as first-class (council weeks, fire Tue–Thu, environmental 40% reserved, Monday clerk dedicated to portal) do not appear as state. Out-of-scope is undefined.

**Section 2 does not estimate this log.** There is no stratified plan by case type/variant, no NHPP with the given 1.7× seasonal and 2.2× Monday multipliers, no compound arrivals for the top-20 architects (31% of volume), and no treatment of fire/environment routing when the threshold and sensitivity attributes are incomplete. Rework “estimation” is described as time gaps between consecutive events — that is duration, not a loop probability. The required wait/service split is not handled: missing start timestamps are waved at with “interpolation or imputation,” which is the wrong method and violates the constraint not to collapse wait and service without explaining the limitation. Objections are not treated as a latent process proxied by public-notice sojourn. PM4Py usage is a sentence, not a plan (`format_dataframe`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, tree parse, arrival statistics).

**Section 3 is circular.** Almost every bullet is “assume X is accurately captured.” That is the opposite of what the known gaps require. Needed assumptions (external applicant delay ≠ municipal service; geometric rework; AND-join before drafting; objections as unobserved Bernoulli inflating notice duration; flattened log understating dependence; emergency-case selection bias; calendars from HR not the log) are absent or contradicted.

**Section 4 is an intervention list, not an experiment matrix.** Factors and levels are missing. D4 is pluralized; D5 is recast as extra legal FTE instead of a routing/timing change. D9 is written as if objection events were observed. There are no stress cells (e.g., 2× peak, inspector outage, council week ∩ autumn peak, objection-rate shock, architect mega-burst). Combined policy is only D10. Trade-offs are one-liners with no KPIs.

**Warm-up/uncertainty is methodologically wrong for this process.** A 500-case warm-up then “steady state” is invalid under documented non-stationary demand; this must be a terminating, calendar-horizon design (e.g., 18–36 months) with time-based truncation, not IID steady-state theory. Run length in calendar time is unspecified. One hundred replications and “bootstrapping” are asserted without a variance or sequential-stopping rule. Common random numbers for intervention comparison are absent. Heavy tails get “use appropriate methods.” Input uncertainty vs. stochastic uncertainty is not separated.

**Validation is a checklist.** Replay vs. open-loop simulation is confused. No quantitative gates (KS/MAPE on cycle time by type, seasonal and Monday spike reproduction, utilization by pool). Temporal holdout is not specified. The objection subprocess cannot be validated against events that do not exist; that caveat is missing. Conformance (fitness/precision on the tree) is not used.

**Decision criteria contain a factual error.** Targeting “median < 12 days for Completeness check” confuses the 12-day simple-path *case* median with a 25-minute activity. An SLA of 95% within 60 days is incompatible with Complex variance (median 140d, p90 260d) unless it is type-specific — and type-specific SLAs were required. Fairness, robustness, backlog, and rework lack metrics and a multi-objective rule (Pareto / constrained optimization). Invented thresholds are not tied to the 18-month summaries.

**The Python skeleton fails the PM4Py contract and is internally incoherent.**
- Does not call `pm4py.format_dataframe` on `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`.
- Invents `pm4py.process.mapping.parse_process_tree` instead of `pm4py.parse_process_tree`.
- Wrong import path for `EventLog`.
- `json_normalize` on two columns drops the case id and destroys the log.
- Derives `case_type` by splitting `P001` and activity names by `_`; both are false for the excerpt.
- Computes none of: variants, DFG, performance DFG, arrival average, wait/service columns.
- Placeholder engine is imported rather than defined; PM4Py handling is not separated from the simulator in working form.
- Arrival rates of 1000–1700/day are an order of magnitude above ~57/day from 31,200 cases / 18 months.
- Inspector `hours=4` confuses 4 FTE with shift length.

**Constraint violations.** Independent-case thinking is not fully avoided because dependence never becomes mechanism. Calendars are named, not modeled. Wait/service are collapsed. Objections are treated as trackable. Invented PM4Py APIs are used. Nothing here is calibrated or validated, which is fine only because the rest is too weak to over-claim.

What remains is a high-level reminder that arrivals, queues, calendars, and D1–D10 exist. That is not enough for a digital twin that must compare interventions under non-stationary demand, rework, and cross-case coupling.
