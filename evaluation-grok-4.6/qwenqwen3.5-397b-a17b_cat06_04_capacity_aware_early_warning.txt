**6.0**

The answer has the right *shape* of a portfolio policy: stage eligibility, an evidence gate, a reserve slot, explicit I-03 refusal, a real I-05 vs I-07 distinction, and some treatment of spillovers, calibration, and feedback. Under a lenient rubric that would be a high-6 / low-7. Under the requested strict standard it is not close to excellent, because the quantitative core does not cohere with the stated rule and several technical claims are wrong.

**Decision rule (mixed).**  
Eligibility before `Diagnosis Complete`, cool-down plus a material-event re-entry test, and a reserved slot are deployable and correctly motivated. The rule is weaker than it looks. Calibration is applied to *absolute risk reduction*, not to `p`, even though the reliability notes are about probability bias. Over-predicted Strategic *risk* is not the same object as an overstated swarm effect; collapsing them into a flat 0.7/1.1 multiplier is a conceptual shortcut, then used inconsistently later. Time-to-deadline never enters the rule, which is an operational hole (I-07 has 1.5 h; I-08 has 8 h). The €1,500 spillover penalty is not derived from the stated 0.01–0.03 × six-case model, so ranking and the impact table are on different ledgers. I-06-style missing features get no verification step in the rule itself.

**Allocation (materially inconsistent).**  
I-03 is correctly barred. I-05 is correctly suppressed. I-07 is correctly kept eligible because of the new log. Those are the required qualitative landmines, and they are handled.

The selected book — I-04, I-08, I-09, one reserve — does not follow the author’s own robustification. After the stated Strategic 0.7 haircut, I-08 is roughly  
`0.10 × 0.7 × €100,000 − €450 − €5,000 ≈ €1,550`  
before spillover, and near zero or negative once the rule’s own spillover penalty or the lower ARR bound (0.05) is applied. Midpoint I-08 without haircut is only about €4.5k of direct EV; the lower bound is already negative (`€5,000 − €5,450`). Calling this Tier 1 because “value is highest” and “EV remains positive” is value-hunting, not the robust EV policy advertised. I-09 is promoted as Tier 1 for a *high* midpoint ARR after the rule said to use the *lower bound* on Non-Native-Language Support. I-01 (well-calibrated Public Sector, ARR 0.18–0.35) and a verified I-06 are more coherent uses of scarce, spillover-creating slots than I-08. Diagnosis-stage urgency (I-02 at 3 h) is under-weighted relative to long-horizon triage cases.

**Expected-impact table (not auditable; arithmetic is wrong).**  
This is the largest failure. Intervention cost is written as “€2,000 (Labor + Burden).” Three slots cost €1,350 in labor; selected false-alarm burdens are €2,000 + €5,000 + €800 = €7,800. The prompt’s own EV identity includes that burden. Omitting ~€7.8k while claiming the €2k line includes burden makes every net figure unreliable.

The Base note jumps from a gross `€11.4k + €10k + €4.8k = €26.2k` to an unexplained “€14,000” after “segment reliability discounts.” The stated 0.7 / lower-bound recipe does not produce €14k (`0.7×11.4 + 0.7×10 + 0.18×20 ≈ €18.6k`). Spillover is first computed as a probability-weighted expectation (`3 × 6 × 0.02 × €20k = €7,200`) and then arbitrarily halved “assuming not all spillover cases breach,” which double-counts that 0.02 is already a probability. Adverse uses ~€7,200, which is the *base* 0.02 math, not the stated 0.03 adverse increment (~€10.8k at the same value). Optimistic “upper half” benefits (€18–24k) look like midpoints, not the upper half of the given ranges (upper bounds sum to ~€38k). The table therefore cannot be audited, which the task required.

**Repeated-alert policy (strongest section).**  
Cool-down, suppression of refresh-only alerts, and re-entry on a new artifact are clear. I-05 vs I-07 is drawn correctly and used in the allocation. Escalation when the clock is short is reasonable. This part meets the bar.

**Fairness (right topics, one harmful safeguard).**  
Starvation of low-value cases, Non-Native uncertainty, segment calibration, selective-label feedback, and spillover incidence on untreated diagnosis work are the right risks. The proposed quota and segment-wise spillover audit are concrete.

The feedback-loop fix is incorrect: writing the *pre-intervention predicted probability* in as the training label does not recover untreated potential outcomes; it trains the model to imitate itself. The actual problem is that treated outcomes are a different regime. A serious safeguard is a treatment flag, withholding treated cases from `Y(0)` training, or an identification strategy (holdout, delay, instrument). Forcing a non-Strategic slot and then filling it with the *worst-calibrated* segment (I-09) also sits poorly next to the uncertainty discussion.

**Monitoring (adequate, loose).**  
Operational, calibration, causal, capacity, and fairness monitors are all named, and there are change triggers. “Stepped-wedge” is conflated with a 10% eligible holdout; weekly segment Brier scores will be noise in Public Sector and Non-Native samples; the 0.10 Strategic error trigger is already in the problem statement; data-quality (I-06) and realized interrupt/false-alarm costs are missing. Usable, not tight.

**Net.**  
This is a complete six-section policy memo that avoids the classic weak pattern (rank by `p_now`, treat I-03 as urgent, treat I-05 like I-07, ignore spillovers). It is not decision-theoretically coherent. The rule, the book, and the impact table disagree; I-08 is selected for headline value against the author’s own robustness math; the one table that had to be auditable is not; and the main causal-feedback control is technically backwards. Those are not cosmetic defects. A nearly flawless answer is in the 9–10 range; this is a structured mid-tier answer with broken arithmetic and an internally inconsistent portfolio.
