The answer is structurally complete and many UTC conversions and zeta=2 SLA calls are arithmetically right, but it is not close to a strict pass. It overstates certainty on weak evidence, omits required interval logic in the code, and misses several material sensitivity flips.

**What is actually solid.** The offset convention `true = resolved_recorded - offset` is stated correctly and applied in the right order (resolution, then clock). HUB ±1.5 s, OPS-BER `[T−120, T+149.999]`, SENSOR nearest-10 s, VENDOR-NY EDT, and LEGACY-BER minute-floor ±60 s are consistent. T-405’s only surviving fold (Alert CEST, Start CET, Complete CET) is the right causal cut. Core calls such as T-402 S1 certain violation, T-408 S2/S3 certain violations, and T-401 S1 possible violation are correct at the stated bounds.

**Classification errors and missing INDETERMINATE.** The rubric defines INDETERMINATE for weak, unbounded, inconsistent, or untestable evidence. This write-up never uses it.

- T-407 has no `Alert_Received`, no ack, and a causal bound the prompt marks as unlogged. Binding receipt to `[Alert_Sent, Investigation_Start]` and calling S1/TP1 a possible violation treats an assumption as data. That pair should be INDETERMINATE, or at least reported under both “causal bound on / off.”
- T-409 S1 is given as `[-481.5, 1021.5]` and then discussed only as crossing 900 s. A start that can precede the alert is internally inconsistent under the weak NTP substitute; that needed an explicit causal clip and/or INDETERMINATE, not a routine possible-violation line.
- T-406’s entry delay is fixed as “occurrence was up to 5 minutes earlier.” That is one legitimate reading, but the prompt flags the record-vs-occurrence distinction as substantive. Under record-only delay, S2 max is about 2611.5 s and is a certain pass; under their occurrence shift it is a possible violation. Presenting only one story without the alternate interval is a silent midpoint-style collapse of an assumption.

**Sensitivity is incomplete and therefore wrong as a section.** Several classification changes at zeta 1 or 3 are simply not reported:

- T-408 TP3 `[3897, 3903]`: certain violation at ζ=1 (`[1980, 3420]`), certain pass at ζ=2/3.
- T-408 TP4: certain violation at ζ=1/2, **certain pass at ζ=3** (`86400±3·21600 = [21600, 151200]` contains ~138300). Omitting this is the largest sensitivity miss.
- T-402 TP3 `[3717, 3723]` is a certain violation at ζ=1, certain pass at ζ=2.
- T-403 TP3 overlaps the ζ=1 band and is a possible violation there.
- T-402/T-404/T-406 TP2 all become possible violations at ζ=1 after being certain passes at ζ=2.

The listed SLA-tolerance flips for T-401/402/403/404/405/407 are mostly right, including the tight T-404 S1 case that remains possible at +2 min. That does not excuse the missing TP matrix.

**Section 6 numbers do not hold.** “Reduce OPS-BER offset from 120 s to <28 s” does not make T-401 S1 certain. The duration *upper* bound is set by the **lower** offset (currently −90 s) plus minute-floor, not by the +120 s end; a symmetric ±28 s bound still leaves max duration ≈929 s. T-408 TP2 cannot be made certain by shrinking HUB’s ±0.5 s measurement while the ±1 s clock bound remains (pairwise slack is ~3 s vs a 3300 s band edge). After they themselves resolve T-405’s fold, asking for “which DST fold” is the wrong residual evidence; the leftover gap is minute resolution and ±60 s.

**Section 5 fails the stated spec.** The script rebuilds a dataframe and applies timezone arithmetic, then stops. It does not derive duration intervals or emit interval classifications. DST is hardcoded as `UTC−2` / `UTC−1`, not alternative `fold` intervals that are then filtered. The midpoint log is only commented as “lossy,” not labeled in data. `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` is guessed, wrapped in `except`, and never compared to the interval table. T-404 still has `Investigation_Complete` and no inferred start; T-407 still has `Alert_Sent` and no received event, so the profile pairs the judge cares about are not even in the PM4Py log. That is not an executable reconstruction of the analysis.

**Smaller but chargeable defects.** Negative durations are left in the T-409 row with a rationale that ignores them. T-407’s inferred received interval is written as if it were observed. Unions are never shown, even as discarded DST branches. Governance is a generic checklist—on-topic, but it does not say whether overlap is enough to enforce, beyond “define a policy.”

The piece looks careful because the tables are dense. Under a strict reading it is a mid-quality interval pass with a stubbed mandatory code path, a missing evidence class, and a sensitivity section that skips the cases that actually move.
