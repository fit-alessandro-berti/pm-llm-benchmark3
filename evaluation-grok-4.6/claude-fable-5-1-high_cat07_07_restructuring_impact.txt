8.2

The answer is unusually strong on causal reasoning, arithmetic interrogation of management’s own tables, East-versus-North/South heterogeneity, fairness/learning, and pilot logic. It still falls short of near-flawless because of several concrete technical and methodological defects, plus a few over-precise claims.

**What holds up.** The opening arithmetic is correct: 343 vs 359 handover events per 100 cases; ~588 h vs ~722 h of handover-attributable wait; Legal/Risk volume +33% against 91–93% utilization; approval load per remaining supervisor ≈1.75×. Those calculations correctly reframe “nominal handover reduction” as a change of type, not of volume, and correctly flag an unstable Legal/Risk queue. The mechanism map ties each restructuring action to a PM measure and a signed expectation. Scenarios are differentiated by capacity, routing quality, and survival of informal triage, not by a generic good/bad switch. East is treated as the team most likely to gain, North/South as the ones that can be levelled down, and “75% of volume is North/South” is numerically right (1580/2100). Leading vs lagging indicators are actually time-separated. The nine hidden-average risks cover the required set. The recommendation is “modify, then pilot,” with expansion conditional on guardrails and an explicit list of invalid inferences from early means.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Before/after leakage.** Cohort membership is assigned by *first* event, then restricted to cases that eventually hit Close. Long cases that start before the change and finish after are labelled “before” while most of their events occur under the new model. The run-in drop applies only to the after set. A clean design would keep before = last event before change, after = first event after run-in, and drop in-flight cases.

2. **PM4Py object types are wrong.** `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, and `discover_subcontracting_network` return NetworkX graphs, not `{ (a,b): weight }` dicts. `len(how)` is then node count, not edge count, so “distinct_handover_edges” is miscomputed. `for pair in wt` iterates nodes. Those snippets would not run as written and would report the wrong SNA metrics.

3. **Return-loop detector is incorrect.** Collapsing `"00"`/`"11"` once does not fully compress runs (`"1110"` does not become `"10"`). The `"101"` test on a partially compressed bitstring both false-positives and false-negatives. This is the code’s main operationalization of “clarification loops,” so the bug is not cosmetic.

4. **Part-time Legal/Risk capacity is ignored.** The prompt states those specialists are part-time. Utilization uses 37.5 h/week for every resource. That overstates available time and understates how infeasible the 9→12 referral increase already is. The 0.5 h/event fallback is disclosed but still makes the utilization leading indicator unreliable in the likely no-start-timestamp case.

5. **Team filter at case level.** `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` keeps a case if *any* event matches. Cross-team cover (6 per 100 today) therefore contaminates East/South/North cells. First-event (or intake) team should be used.

**Smaller issues that still cost.** Several scenario figures (resources per case 4–5→6–7, East 8.6 d→7.2 d, waiting share >80%) are presented with unearned precision. Which supervisor is removed is never specified, so the 1.75× load is an aggregate that could be milder if East’s seat is the one cut. Invented `TSS_*` names and `SLA_DAYS` are undocumented relative to the log. `get_variants` return-type handling is a hedge, not an API. Learning is correctly described as 6–18 months, yet the 12-week pilot cannot observe it; that tension is noted but not fully absorbed into the go/no-go rule.

**Format and constraints.** All seven required headings are present and used as specified. The write-up does not treat the restructure as inherently good or bad, does not hang on mean cycle time, does not flatten East into North/South, and does not recommend full rollout. Those constraints are met.

The substance of sections 1–6 is close to what a careful review should look like. The grade is held down by the case-split contamination, the NetworkX/dict mismatch on the required SNA calls, the broken return-loop metric, and the part-time capacity miss.
