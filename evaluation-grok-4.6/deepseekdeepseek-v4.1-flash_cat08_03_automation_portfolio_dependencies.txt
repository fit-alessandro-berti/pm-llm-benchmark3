The write-up is complete, process-aware, and correctly refuses a frequency-first ranking. It still has several factual slips, control-logic tensions, and sequencing contradictions that a strict reading cannot treat as polish.

**What holds up.** All twelve candidates are scored on process leverage, not volume. A1 is correctly demoted: registration is already largely digital and a 1.5h wait. A4, A5, and A6 are the right bottleneck cluster (31h entity-mismatch wait and 82% critical-path share; 3,300/2,800 missing-doc loops at 52% repeat; 5,200 normalizations at 75 min and 20h wait). Resource-network facts are used (3 compliance specialists, 9 analysts, legal month-end overload). The two-deep-integration constraint, human-in-the-loop exceptions, and “no downstream bot without prerequisites” rule are acknowledged. Four portfolios, including a recommended A4/A5/A6/A9 set, match the required shape.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Wait times are attached to the wrong edges.** The 20.0h figure is median wait *before* Normalize, not wait on `Normalize → Credit analysis` (credit analysis waits 28h). The 22.0h contract wait is mostly upstream KYC/decision/synchronization, not the 35 min of legal drafting A9 removes. Service-time automation is repeatedly sold as if it clears multi-day queues. For a process-mining brief, that is a core error.

2. **A9 is the control module, then withheld from the riskiest product.** Credit-line renewal has 14% contract-remediation loops vs 8.5% overall. Gating A9 away from that variant in year 1 undercuts the claim that A9 satisfies “at least one first-wave control module.” Assisted check-only mode for renewals would have been consistent; exclusion is not.

3. **Upstream control path is left open while a downstream generator is funded.** A2 (8% misroutes, wrong four-eyes/collateral path) and A3 (KYC hits) are out of the four-slot portfolio. A9 still depends on clean KYC and decision data. Gating “clean low-risk cases” papers over the constraint rather than meeting it. Four-eyes violations (5.8% of High-exposure SME at month-end) are deferred to “manual / year-2 A12” with no operational control in the first wave.

4. **Integration timing contradicts the six-month cap.** Prose assigns A6 and A9 as the two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months; the phase table puts A9 in months 6–9. Rank order (A6 above A5) also fights the stated dependency that A6 needs complete documents.

**Lesser issues.** Back-office utilization falling after 15:00 is unused. Micro vs SME vs High-exposure is thin except where High-exposure is a gate. A7’s 58h external wait is the longest delay in the log; dropping it is defensible given partner control and the compliance slot, but the recommended effects section still talks as if end-to-end cycle time moves a lot while that synchronization point is untouched. Overlapping Phase 0/1/2 windows blur ownership.

The portfolio instinct is right. The artifacts are not handled tightly enough—especially waits, control targeting, and prerequisite order—to score higher under a strict standard.
