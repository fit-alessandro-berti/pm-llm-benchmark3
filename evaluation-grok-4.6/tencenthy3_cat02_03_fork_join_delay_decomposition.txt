**7.5**

The core fork-join decomposition is correct, internally consistent, and aligned with the required format. Case-level numbers, critical-branch labels, the additive identity \(T=U+S+D+F\), and the argument that \(\sum_i w_{c,i}\) double-counts overlapping wait all check out. The analysis correctly refuses to treat A’s 50-minute mean service as the controlling branch and correctly treats C’s large sync wait as evidence of early finish, not influence.

**What holds up**

- All 12 rows of the required table match the timestamps. Worked cases F02, F04, F07, and F11 are exact.
- Critical frequencies 2 / 8 / 2, service and queue summaries, and CF1/CF2 savings (15 min and 92.5 min) are right, including zero-benefit cases and the A-cap after B-queue cuts.
- Assumptions are labeled. Limits of inference cover causality, unobserved capacity, preemption, and the queue-vs-policy ambiguity.
- The Python block hits the required checklist: in-memory table, custom max-based join, deterministic A>B>C ties, three counterfactuals with per-case and aggregate savings, interval log, `format_dataframe`, a performance call, and an accurate comment that DFG semantics do not recover sync wait.

**Material faults**

1. **Final-station congestion is mis-measured.** Occupancy on the assigned resource fully explains F05 (10), F08 (15), F09 (15), and F11 (20), plus 5 of F10 — about 65 minutes, not “F05, F08, F10; maybe 30.” F1 is continuously busy 11:10–12:10 (F07–F09–F11). CF3 and Recommendation 2 therefore understate how much of \(D_c\) is resource wait and overstate the case for “do not add final capacity.”
2. **The F1/F2 assignment rule is missed.** Resources alternate exactly with case order (odd→F1, even→F2). That is direct evidence that downstream delay is at least partly a release/assignment policy, not a shared two-server queue. Section 7 mentions the policy question in the abstract but never uses this pattern.
3. **`pm4py.get_service_time(log, act)` is a fragile / likely-wrong call.** Common PM4Py entry points take a log or dataframe and return a mapping, not `(log, activity)`. Section 6 is specified as a runnable artifact; this is a real reproducibility risk. `discover_performance_dfg` is mentioned only in a comment, never called.

**Lesser issues**

- “Adding \(\sum(q+s+w)\) and \(S\) overcounts by a factor of four” is sloppy next to the otherwise clean double-count proof.
- CF3’s 50% cut is an arbitrary proxy, even though it is labeled.
- Average utilization (~42% over 07:55–12:40) is used as if it ruled out congestion; it does not, given the F1 afternoon chain.

The delay accounting, critical-path ranking, and first two counterfactuals are strong enough that this is still a good answer. It is not near-flawless: the required downstream/capacity analysis is factually incomplete, and that error flows into the redesign.
