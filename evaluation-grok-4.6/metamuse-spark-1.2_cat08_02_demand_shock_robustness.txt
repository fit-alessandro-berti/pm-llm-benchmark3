**6.9**

The answer is a strong, data-aware operations redesign: it keeps mix and disruption distinct from volume, uses the DFGs and station metrics as evidence, treats bottleneck migration as the main design problem, and refuses average-only optimization. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative claims are unnormalized or invented, and two of the scenario policies are internally inconsistent with the same evidence the author otherwise uses well.

**What is actually good.** Weekly mix loads in S1 (Urgent 414→957/wk, Complex 299→660/wk) are correct. Core DFG ratios are mostly right: assay→repeat 6.9% vs 15.5%, technical→medical 25% vs 47%, technical→release 74% vs 56%, recollection rate 4.2% vs 8.0%. The four required stress types are present and can co-occur. The robust/contingent split is the right architecture. Rejecting always-on I1, I2=24, I9, and unconditional I10 is justified by the stated conflict hints. Residual vulnerability #2 (automation flood) and #4 (cannot serve all SLAs at once) match the grading criteria. Leading vs lagging is conceptually correct, and the “queues fall / p90 stays high ⇒ bottleneck moved” rule is the right operating doctrine.

**Process-mining errors.** Peak vs normal frequencies are often compared as raw period counts (8 weeks vs 3 weeks). “Hold 290→610 (+110%)” and “batch wait 760→1,180” understate intensity; weekly they are roughly 36→203 and 95→393. The author normalizes volume at the top, then drops that discipline in the DFG narrative. Accession “blocks 420 cases” is a 3-week inflow, not a queue. S2’s “25–40% effective capacity drop” is not in the log.

**Repeat-after-failure is not Repeat assay.** The case type (8–9% of mix, double assay use) and the DFG activity (1,140 / 1,260) are related but not interchangeable. S4’s claim that relieving the front door “re-clogs assay with repeat-after-failure cases” conflates pre-analytical recollection with analytical rework. R1 later distinguishes them; S4 does not.

**Broken causal chains.** In S3, adding evening medical capacity does not make assay “block because they cannot hand off to technical.” Medical queueing is downstream of technical completion unless a WIP cap is assumed (it is not). The correct migration story is that I5 is starved by technical at 101%, not that I5 clogs assay. In the trade-off table, Urgent benefit from R1 is tied to the 5/8h register→receive edge, which is not the recollection loop (42/60h). I10 is treated as trading a 48h hold for “12h transport”; the candidate intervention is a transport delay plus partner processing, not a 12h TAT.

**Invented parameters presented as analysis.** No evidence supports “30–40% of Routine have complete metadata,” “one cross-trained validator keeps technical above 95%,” WIP 450 / 600, batch 16–18, or a 65% bypass cap. Technical and medical headcount are unknown; moving one person from a 101% pool cannot be shown to land at 95%. These are design choices, written as if computed.

**Policy logic fails on the author’s own peak.** P-S1 requires Urgent breaches *and* Routine WIP <450. At peak mix and a 62h Routine median, Little’s law puts Routine WIP near ~500, so the policy is off during the winter surge it is built for. The 30% Urgent slot cap is also roughly the peak Urgent share (29%). Cases that need an 18h path versus a 72h path cannot share capacity 30/70 and still hit the contract; priority has to be disproportionate, not proportional. P-S2 then dumps Routine onto M1 while M2 is degraded, which attacks the same scarce machine Urgent and Complex need. Forcing Urgent into batch-8 in that state trades schedule smoothness for the 18h SLA with no quantitative check.

**Smaller defects.** R1 is said to work in S2 and then to fail in S2. The “minimum” dashboard is a large control room, not a few leading indicators. Courier delay / registration overtime caps never specify whether the 18h clock starts at register or receive. Specialist-Friday + M2 clustering is a real residual risk; the “600 cases / p90>240h” figure is a loose ballpark.

The package is still clearly above a generic “add staff and prioritize urgent” answer. The deductions are for avoidable evidence handling and for policies that would not fire, or would fire wrongly, in the peak the log already describes.
