The write-up is complete in outline and gets most case outcomes right, but it is not close to a high score. Internal contradictions, a clear misclassification, broken formal logic, and code that cannot actually check the rules are enough to keep it in the mid range under a strict reading.

**Case reasoning.** Ten of eleven labels are defensible. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are clean NONCOMPLIANT calls. TX-305 is correctly INDETERMINATE (settlement must not be copied back). TX-310’s NONCOMPLIANT reading of EX-88 is reasonable given R5 and the requester-witness. TX-311 is correctly COMPLIANT once `event_index` is applied. TX-306 and TX-308 as COMPLIANT are a *possible* reading if “material” is the R4 >20% test.

The serious case error is **TX-302 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**. An `Enhanced_Review` already sits after the only known state and before release. R1 is satisfied on the facts; EX-77 is surplus. That is COMPLIANT, not a justified exception. Treating the exemption as the deciding path is a conceptual miss, and the ambiguities section repeats the same premise.

Worse, **Section 1 contradicts Sections 3–4**. Section 1 defines any amount/risk/jurisdiction/customer update as a material change. Under that definition plus R1’s “after the latest material data change” test, TX-306 (amount update after review) and arguably TX-308 fail. The narrative then redefines materiality via R4 to save those cases. That is not a minor nuance; it is two incompatible theories of the same rule.

**Formalization.** The rule table is usable. The pseudo-logic is not. R2 is ill-formed: `not (enhanced_review ; compliance_approve)` is the wrong connective, and `not (T_e < T_c)` uses unbound times. R4 does not encode decreases, unknown-from amounts, or “invalidates amount-dependent obligations.” R3 and R5 are sketched, not operational. A judge looking for formalization quality should mark this down hard.

**Code.** This is the weakest required section. Failures against the spec:

- R2 flag is `not r2_trigger and (reviews…)`, so every R2-triggering case is automatically false and many non-R2 cases fail too.
- R4 compares to `Create_Transaction` amount, not the immediately prior amount (wrong on TX-309’s second hike).
- R3 is never evaluated; the exemption register is never joined.
- `event_index` is not used in comparisons, despite R7 and TX-311.
- Diagnostics lack evidence timestamps and unknown-value flags.
- Unused Petri/alignment imports look version-fragile and do not constitute a DECLARE check.
- The implemented R1 test treats every update as material, so the code would not reproduce the TX-306 COMPLIANT story.

The forward-fill, `StringIO` log, `format_dataframe`, and “don’t copy the future” comments are the only solid parts. “REVIEW_REQUIRED” as a human-judgment bucket is acceptable; shipping broken flags is not.

**Other sections.** Temporal-semantics prose (blanks ≠ null, no final-state leakage, event vs record vs evidence time) is the best part. Section 6’s six failure modes and Section 7’s logging recommendations match the brief. They do not offset the formalization/code defects or the TX-302 / materiality inconsistency.

A nearly flawless answer would have classified TX-302 as COMPLIANT, defined “material change” once and used it everywhere, written executable R1–R4+exemption logic with unknown flags, and given working (if coarse) data-aware checks. This is a structured, often-correct essay with a broken checker and one important case error.
