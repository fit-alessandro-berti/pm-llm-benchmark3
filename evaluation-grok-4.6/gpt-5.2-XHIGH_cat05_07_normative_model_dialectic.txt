The answer is a high-quality, well-structured review that hits the required headings, separates stipulated facts from disputed propositions, and does not simply split the difference. It is not nearly flawless. Several authority, classification, and model-semantics problems are material.

**What is strong.** Section 1 is the best part of the answer: CS-9 vs M0 vs PD-3 vs FD-7 vs CRM vs practice are correctly placed, and the burden-of-proof rules for NONCOMPLIANT, JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, and MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT are usable. **Prosecution is not at its strongest.** A maximal CS-9 brief would treat PD-3 as incapable of amending a controlled standard: minutes plus a later-recanted observer note, 312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 ineligible, 9 missing attributes, and six August continuations as a population-level control failure. **Authority is used inconsistently.** Section 1 says PD-3’s power to weaken CS-9 is disputed and that JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION needs explicit overlay authority plus an eligibility snapshot. Section 5 then classifies SUP-702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION with medium-high confidence without an explicit finding that steering-committee minutes were enough. **Some case labels over-resolve.**
- SUP-715 is control-flow compliant; the only issue is undisclosed COI, and the relationship is not even prohibited. MIXED (or a resource-rule fail with compliant flow) is the accurate label. Wholesale NONCOMPLIANT overstates the control-flow case.

- SUP-713 mapping was approved *after* the case. Semantic equivalence can support MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT, but high confidence is too high.
- MIXED is avoided even where two independent dimensions really diverge. That is cleaner than using MIXED as a cop-out, but it forces false single labels.

**Defense skips a requested process-aware point.** Concurrent `Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review` in M0 already allows sequential interleaving; extra order is not a deviation. The defense never says this, despite the prompt.

**The revised POWL has a real language error.** M0’s XOR sits *after* both checks, so `Register → (Sanctions ∥ Quality) → Reject → Notify → Close` is a legal M0 trace. M1’s reject child is sanctions-only, and `standard_route` always activates. Full-check rejection is inexpressible. That is a regression, not an approximation.

Other model/data defects, smaller but not trivial:
- `Master_Data_Validation` is a mandatory visible task on the auto route, so every current Auto_Approve log would fail M1; no silent/XOR-skip and no note that this is to-be instrumentation.
- Reactivation is in the prose architecture, not in M1, and Section 7 does not state that limitation (it does state the LOOP limitation, which is correct: `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)` is a defensible 0..n encoding).
- No activity-synonym table despite SUP-713.
- DECLARE/deadline/cap/SoD rules stay informal; the overlay table is good but not operationalized.
- `Request_More_Info` is absent from emergency/reject children.
- On the auto PO, `Quality_Review` is unordered with `Sanctions_Check` (only `Register → Quality`), so quality-before-sanctions is in the auto language.

**Bottom line.** Dialectics, evidence handling, and the layered governance proposal are clearly above average. The answer is not a 9+: it softens the prosecution, applies JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION before settling PD-3’s authority, mislabels at least one resource-only case, and ships a revised POWL that cannot reject after the standard checks. Those are structural, not cosmetic.
