**4.7**

The submission has the right governance posture and a complete 11-section skeleton, but it is not close to a process-mining-expert review. Material errors in the probe matrix, process-tree literacy, and the third model dominate.

**What is actually strong.** Sections 1, 10, and 11 do the job the prompt cares about: they refuse to crown a winner by averaging scores, split exploration from enforcement, treat F7/F8 as use-case claims rather than facts, and write a real dissent (validator burden, missing attributes, checklist fallback) instead of a token objection. Several metric caveats are fair: unpublished seeds and alignment costs, informal 1–5 comprehension, runtime confounded by net size, and Model C’s holdout collapse as overfitting to enumerated variants. The validation *program* (stratified splits, negative suite, perturbation, cost sensitivity, reject-despite-good-aggregates rule) is directionally right and does not invent new numbers.

**Probe matrix and language of S/C — failing grade.** This is the factual core, and it is wrong in multiple cells.

- Model C does **not** accept B2. The only Auto Approval branch is `Register → Credit → Technical → Auto → Release`. There is no `Technical → Credit → Auto` variant. That omission is the point of B2/F4; calling it “Yes (explicit branch)” misses the central enumeration failure.
- Model S does **not** accept B6 or B8. Its alphabet has no `Correct Dossier`, and `+(X(Credit,τ), X(Technical,τ), …)` allows each check **at most once**. Two of the “five concrete behaviors S admits” (S4, S5) are not in \(L(S)\). The requirement was five *admitted* traces; after dropping those, the set is short and padded with activities the model cannot fire.
- `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` is a mandatory exclusive choice, not an optional skip. Section 8’s claim that S “could have none” (or that both approvals are optional) is a basic process-tree error. S actually satisfies P5; it fails P2/P4/P7 for other reasons.
- Model C’s language is essentially **eight concrete traces**. The review never states that sharply. Instead it speculates about a Register→Expedite shortcut that those branches do not contain.

**Metric interpretation is uneven and one cell is conceptually false.** Alignment precision is not “classification precision on traces that contain the checks.” It is escaping-edge / allowed-vs-logged behavior on the *log as recorded*. F5 means there was no designed negative suite; it does not mean PM4Py dropped traces without checks. Token-based generalization is not “compression of repetitive patterns,” and saying C’s low generalization “could improve coverage of new combinations” is backwards: branch unrolling *reduces* unseen combinations. Missing-event bias (F4: omitted Supplier Review inflating S’s fitness) and “generalization rewarded by permissiveness” are required pathologies and are only half-covered.

**The third alternative is the largest failure.** The prose backbone (Register; both checks in either order; Supplier gated on external; Expedite after checks; XOR approval; correction with `affected_checks`) is a reasonable *intent*. The artifact is not.

- `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` both consume `R` and produce `M`: that is XOR, so **P2 is not encoded**. One check empties `R` and the other is dead.
- Manager/Auto consume `{S, E, M}`: that is an AND-join, so Supplier and Expedite become **mandatory**. Classic Petri-net mistake, opposite of P3/P4.
- Initial marking on `R` with `Register Dossier` producing `R` either skips P1 or makes Register dead.
- `Correct Dossier` is a self-loop on `R` only, so correction is impossible after any check.
- Required `model_third` is never assigned. Imports and constructors (`pm4py.models.petri`, `input_places=[None]`, `pm4py.util.set_log_format`, `pm4py.metrics.alignment.fitness`) are not PM4Py. Section 9 even contains a list comprehension with `detects violation?` in the source.
- The validator is used to paper over the net, then itself returns `True` if no approval is found in the no-correction path, and can unbound `approval_idx`. That is not “post-hoc policy checks” of a sound backbone; it is an unsound net plus buggy Python.

A competent third model was available in the offered POWL/process-tree fragment: AND of the two mandatory checks, then optional Supplier and Expedite, XOR approval, a loop on correction that honestly repeats the check block because the affected-check attribute is missing. That would be safer than S and more maintainable than C. This submission describes that idea and then implements a net that falsifies it.

**Other spec misses.** Section 8 does not tabulate B1–B8, only P-style rows plus three extra traces (one of which restates B4). Duplicate-label “33” is discussed, but impact analysis stays generic. Stakeholder-score methodology is noted as informal, not actually specified as a protocol until §9, and even there the “5-question Likert” is a sketch.

**Net.** Framing, use-case split, and dissent are graduate-level. Conformance reasoning, process-tree semantics, and the executable third model are not. Under a hypercritical bar, that caps the grade well below “good review with nits”: the board cannot trust the probe table or deploy `model_third` as written.
