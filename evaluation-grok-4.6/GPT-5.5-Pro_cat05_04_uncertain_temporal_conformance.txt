**8.2**

The analysis is technically strong: clock-offset direction, resolution-before-correction, zone conversion, DST fold filtering, inferred-start arithmetic, and almost all duration bounds and classifications check out. It still falls short of a top score because of taxonomy slippage, a few presentation inconsistencies, and several places where weak evidence is treated as firmer than the stated rules allow.

**What is actually correct.**  
The offset formula is right (`true ∈ [clock_min − b, clock_max − a]`). HUB `[t−1.5s, t+1.5s]`, OPS-BER CEST conversions, VENDOR-NY EDT floor-minute ±180s, SENSOR nearest-10s then ±5s, MOBILE-LON nearest-minute ±300s, and LEGACY-BER fold `0/1` handling are all consistent with the metadata. T-405 ingestion order correctly kills every combo except alert in the first fold and start/containment in the second. T-404 start `= complete − [18,25] min` retains the full Minkowski range. T-408 S2/S3 are genuine certain violations (~55 min and ~38h25m). Sensitivity matrices match the duration intervals (including awkward boundaries such as T-404 S1 still PV at +2 min because 1023 > 1020, and T-402 S1 still PV at +5 min). Midpoints are quarantined to §5 and labeled lossy.

**T-407 is the largest conceptual miss.**  
`Alert_Sent` is not `Alert_Received`. The required taxonomy has INDETERMINATE for missing/untestable receipt. The write-up admits this in prose, then puts a single POSSIBLE_VIOLATION row in the mandatory findings table under a “plausible but unlogged” causal cap. That collapses a semantic gap into a numeric interval. The table should have carried an INDETERMINATE primary row and a clearly marked conditional alternative. As written, §3 contains **zero** INDETERMINATE classifications even though the prompt built that class for exactly these cases.

**Weak bounds are over-promoted to certainty.**  
T-409 S2 and several T-406/T-407 profile pairs are labeled CERTAIN_PASS because the *constructed* interval sits inside the band. That is only as certain as the offset/delay story. T-409’s `[−12, +12] min` is a “weak engineering estimate,” not NTP. CERTAIN_PASS with a confidence footnote is cleaner than INDETERMINATE, but the table still reads as if the rule is settled. Same issue for T-407 Alert→Containment: the pass is entirely an artifact of the receipt proxy.

**Small numerical/semantic inconsistencies.**  
Section 2 uses `59.999` minute ends; several §3 durations are then written as exact minute spans (`720.0–1080.0`, etc.), i.e. a closed 60s minute. Harmless for these classifications, but it is sloppy relative to the claimed semantics. T-409 S2 uses investigation times *before* the alert for the max duration, while S1 trims negatives to 0. The dual convention is disclosed, but the causally trimmed S2 interval is never shown. T-406’s occurrence-vs-record delay is handled well in prose; the main table still commits to the wider occurrence reading without a parallel row.

**§5 is good but not clean.**  
The interval engine matches the narrative, DST alternatives are enumerated, and PM4Py is isolated. The midpoint log, however, feeds **inferred** `Alert_Received` (T-407) and `Investigation_Start` (T-404) into `format_dataframe` / `conformance_temporal_profile` as if they were observations. That is a second lossy step on top of midpoint collapse, and it is not flagged as strongly as the midpoint warning. The `try/except` around PM4Py is allowed, but it also means the required comparison may never actually execute.

**§6/§7 are better than §3.**  
Sufficiency requests are specific and typed. Governance (UTC + raw + offset telemetry + resolution + lifecycle + fold + correlation IDs + overlap policy) is on point. Those sections do not rescue the missing INDETERMINATE rows or the T-407 enforcement framing.

Net: rigorous interval work with verified arithmetic and a usable sensitivity story, undermined by one material taxonomy error, over-certain labels on weak evidence, and a few endpoint/inference inconsistencies. Under a strict reading that is well above competent and well short of flawless.
