**7.2**

The answer is complete on structure and meets the hard constraints (cross-case dependence, calendars, service/wait split, latent objections, no optimality claims, no `pm4py.read_csv`). It is not near-flawless. Under a strict reading the control-flow, capacity, PM4Py, and validation details have enough errors and vagueness to block a high score.

**What holds up.** Scope, entities, queues, and calendars match the brief. Architect bursts, inspection bundles, objection contagion, and AND-join contention are first-class, not bolted on. Missing starts, missing objections, unstructured rework, and flattened related cases are treated as identification problems. The experiment table covers B0, D1–D10, a combo, and three stresses with trade-offs. Warm-up/WIP init, drain/censoring, CRN, paired CIs, cluster bootstrap, and heavy-tail caution are the right uncertainty stack. Decision rules use median, tails, SLA by type, fairness, utilization, rework, backlog, and robustness, with an explicit pilot-not-optimum stance. The skeleton builds a DataFrame, calls `format_dataframe` with the required keys, tries `parse_process_tree` with a real limitation note, and separates PM4Py from DES placeholders.

**Control-flow is looser than the given tree.** The tree is `+(Planning, Fire?, Env?)` then optional public notice, then inspection, then drafting, then optional legal. The design treats drafting as a join on “Planning + Fire/Env/Public-notice + Inspection” as if those were parallel siblings. Public notice and inspection are sequential in the tree; P001 is consistent with a 14-day notice then scheduling. Over-parallelizing changes waiting-time attribution and D5’s meaning. D5 itself is unclear: “legal starts after Planning draft” mixes planning review with decision drafting and never states what legal overlaps.

**Fire capacity is internally inconsistent.** “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is implemented as two servers on 3/5 days (~48 h/week if 8-hour days). Two FTE is ~80 h/week. That understates fire capacity by ~40% unless hours are stretched on Tue–Thu or headcount is raised. Inspectors (Tue–Fri) have the same FTE-vs-open-days issue, milder. This is a bottleneck-calibration error, not a wording nit.

**Arrival math is sloppy.** 31,200 / 18 months ≈ 57/day is fine. The 1.7× factor is versus January–February, not versus the annual mean. Treating peak days as ~1.7 × 57 (90–100) inflates seasonal intensity unless Monday and burst effects are doing all the work—and that is not shown. `t//30 % 12` and `t%7` in the skeleton are too crude for the seasonality the text insists on.

**PM4Py in the plan is not reliable.** `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_token_replay` is not the usual public name (`...token_based_replay`). `pm4py.statistics.traces.pandas.case_statistics.get_median_case_duration` and `pm4py.statistics.variants` are stale internal paths. The skeleton is more careful (`get_variants` plus fallback, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`) but still version-hedged rather than pinned. Inductive rediscovery on a 9-row excerpt is not a serious executable stand-in for the given tree.

**Validation overclaims.** KS/Anderson–Darling on ~31k cases will reject any calibrated model; there is no large-n or practical-significance caveat. “Prediction intervals cover holdout with <15% median error” mixes coverage and point error. Fitness “>0.9 on the top 80% variants” is an arbitrary bar with no alignment-cost reading. Face validation is named, not operationalized (who signs off, what mismatch kills a run).

**Warm-up vs sojourn.** Sixty days is short next to Complex median 140d / p90 260d. Seeding WIP from the log helps; 0/30/90 sensitivity is not a substitute for showing open-case mix by activity and type after warm-up. A 90-day drain still censors the long tail. Nested MC (100 parameter draws × 30+ replications × many cells) is proposed with no budget, sequential CRN plan, or reduced design.

**Code is a sketch, not a faithful excerpt.** P001/P002 are truncated (missing-doc loop, public notice, scheduling, drafting, legal, several P002 steps). `service_hours_est` stays NaN; `waiting_days_est` is copied sojourn. `ResourceCalendar.is_available` always returns True. `ArrivalGenerator` returns a count, not cases. That is acceptable only as a stub; it does not demonstrate a working wait/service split or calendar logic.

**Assumptions and effects are often invented numbers wearing scenario clothing.** D1 −30 to −50% missing-doc loops, D2 60–70% of Simple eligible, rework service +20%, SLA cutoffs (21/60/90/180d), utilization 65–85%, D10 must beat the best single by 3d, ±15% demand, ±30% service: some are labeled illustrative, but there is little SME elicitation or log-identifiable bound for the ones that drive rankings (D2 eligibility, D1 effect, objection mixture). D7’s “Mon 2.2x1.3x Wed” is unreadable. Redo `*(check, 'Completeness rework')` is never mapped to a second completeness check versus a dummy redo activity.

**Net.** Strong advisory outline with the right twin architecture and honest missing-data stance. Not a calibrated, tree-faithful, API-accurate design. The remaining gaps are exactly where a municipality would mis-rank D2/D3/D4/D5.
