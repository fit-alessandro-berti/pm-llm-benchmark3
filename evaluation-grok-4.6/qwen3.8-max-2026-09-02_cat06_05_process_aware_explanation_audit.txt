**8.3**

The final five-section audit is strong on the criteria that matter: it catches obvious target/future leakage, subtler lineage defects, preserves queue and prefix-age signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legitimate-but-unavailable rather than as the same-timestamp impossibility, and keeps predictive, causal, actionable, and fairness standards apart. It does not treat full-trace SHAP as an early explanation, and it does not delete region or channel as if correlation were disqualifying.

It is not nearly flawless.

**Section 1** covers every required item and several extra defects (extraction-dated employee experience, unitemized base mass). Most severities are defensible. Weaker points: `final_handler_team` is a closure-populated field with no valid-time, closer to the critical complete-trace group than to ordinary high-severity repairables; the “unusable until repaired” later grouping also dumps permanently inadmissible fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `final_decision`) into a repair bucket; reverse causation for appeals (denial/delay producing appeals) is only implied; the nightly document snapshot is framed mainly as post-`t0` leakage and underplays the equally real stale-snapshot case when `t0` is after 23:00.

**Section 2** is a real validation suite, not a slogan list, and it correctly refuses to treat a frequent DFG edge as a rule. The enabled-state story is slightly sloppy: `Damage Assessment Completed` is a future event at `t0`, not an already enabled completed activity, even if fast-track assessment is the next legal step. “Future-possible” and “not admissible as completed” for `Claim Closed` are both used without a clean single classification.

**Section 3** is the right conceptual core. Overlap between predictive and process-state signals is acknowledged. The class table still blurs “repair to a prefix analogue” with “never use at `t0`,” which is the main conceptual leak in an otherwise careful taxonomy.

**Section 4** JSON matches the required schema, does not dump the Case Y variant string or Case X’s Partial/appeal into the narratives, and correctly marks X/Y as recompute and Z as invalidate-and-recompute. Inconsistencies: `claimant_channel` is a credible driver even though the audit’s own table only allows it after timestamp verification; Case X’s +0.21 unexplained mass is not represented in `removed_or_repaired_drivers`, so the 0.82 is treated as if the listed leaks were the whole contamination; Case Y’s narrative still hedges on reserve instead of staying strictly inside verified `t0` facts.

**Section 5** hits every mandated workstream and is prioritized. It is more a competent program than a distinctive one; explanation-stability and calibration are named but not specified tightly (what stability metric, what calibration check, what fail threshold).

Net: a credible, process-aware audit with the hard distinctions in place, reduced by several small classification, completeness, and internal-consistency defects. Not 9+ under a strict bar.
