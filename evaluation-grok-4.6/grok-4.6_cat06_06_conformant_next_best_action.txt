The answer is strong on the criteria that matter most: it refuses to let SLA forecasts buy out hard controls, keeps A/B at the bottom, treats SAR and rescreen as non-substitutes for independent review, and makes P-901 a first-class cost. The six-section form, labels, and composite D‖S4 plan with an explicit C fallback are the right shape. It is not nearly flawless.

**Logical defects**

The fallback time-gate is internally inconsistent and operationally incoherent. The table escalates to C “when remaining SLA ≈ S2’s lower-bound review window” (~35 minutes left). S2’s review is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute, so that trigger cannot capture any on-time tail. Section 5 correctly says to call S2 *now* if S4’s ETA is 65 minutes; section 6 then restates the late 35-minute-remaining gate. Those cannot both be the decision rule. Because the primary plan already accepts a late payment, the only reason to promote C is residual SLA option value — and the stated trigger spends that option.

D1 overreaches on rule 6. Alternate screening “does not substitute for the mandatory second review while the current conditions remain.” A conflicting vendor result does not, by itself, retire an unresolved 0.46 primary match or a live score of 72. The write-up hedges with “may drop,” then still treats “screen clears + fresh score < 70 → second officer optional” as a main continuation. That is the closest the answer comes to the failure mode the rubric warns about (rescreen as a substitute). The score-refresh premise is also unsupported: the prompt only says the vendor may reduce match uncertainty, not recompute risk.

The rank-1 tree omits the most important first-line branch. After Evidence Validated, rule 5 requires Analyst Review. If R17 already recommends reject or SAR-hold on the validated pack, waiting out S4 is the wrong continuation; the plan proceeds as if second-officer wait is unconditional.

**Labeling and column problems**

D is `CONFORMANT NOW` while C is `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`, yet both are conformant only if the second officer actually reads the file. That is the same condition. Confidence means three different things: ranking uncertainty (rows 1–5), invalidity of the act (A/B), and “medium–high” for E despite the prose calling E a bad *next* move. Expected benefit/harm cells are narrative blobs, not ranges, so the table is harder to audit than the prompt asked for.

**Smaller inaccuracies**

Rule 5 is applied in the C row and in the plan text, but dropped from D’s mapping. “Criminal” harm and “late-payment friction usually below a false rejection” are not in the case data. Section 5 then wobbles on whether D and C *flip* when S4 is late or only flip if leadership wants the SLA tail — which leaves the rank-1/rank-2 order underdetermined in the scenario the sensitivity analysis exists to resolve.

**What still holds**

Dominance in §3 is right: the €100k–€500k+ unlawful-execution tail plus non-monetary harm beats any combination of P-884’s €20k–€35k SLA save and the customer-harm band. Observational SLA/approval rates are not treated as interventional. F and G are correctly conditional, and the evidence required to enable them is specified. A and B are not rehabilitated by a favorable forecast. Those are the expensive mistakes, and this answer does not make them. The remaining errors are why it is clearly short of a 9.
