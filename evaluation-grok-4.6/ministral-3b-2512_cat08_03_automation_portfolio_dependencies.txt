**4.0**

The answer has the right *shape* (required sections, frequency-is-not-impact thesis, A11 deprioritized) but is not a reliable process-mining recommendation. Material data errors, invented effects, a budget misread, and a rollout that contradicts the chosen portfolio keep it far from a high score.

**1. Candidate matrix — systematically sloppy**

Frequency is treated as “cases/day” for all twelve candidates. 12,000 originations per day is not what the table describes; that unit error runs through the whole memo and inflates every “direct benefit” claim.

Several cells misread the artifacts:

- A4 does **not** “eliminate 38% of KYC delays.” 38% is the *rework/exception rate of Resolve entity mismatch itself*, not the share of KYC delay it removes.
- A8 is scored on 2.7k (the Credit analysis → Committee packaging DFG arc), not the 3,900 packaging cases.
- A1’s main control risk is duplicate false positives / blocked goods apps, not “misrouting.” Misrouting is A2.
- A3 false negatives are described too mildly later as delays; the brief says they are severe.
- First-year suitability is inconsistent with the rest of the paper (A4 “High (but low frequency)”; A7 “Low” despite the 58h wait being the largest synchronization delay in the log).

Qualitative columns exist, but they are not grounded tightly enough in service time × wait × rework × scarce FTE.

**2. Dependency map — required headings, weak bindings**

The five required themes appear, but several links are wrong or over-specified:

- A5 is missing-document capture/nudging, not a fix for “low-quality PDFs.” A6’s PDF failure mode is not cured by A5.
- “Only A1 and A9 can be deeply integrated in Year 1” is invented. The constraint is *two* deep core-banking integrations in the *first six months*, not a whitelist of those two modules.
- A8’s prerequisites (clean KYC, normalized statements, collateral if required) are stated here and then ignored in the rollout.

Human-escalation capacity is named but not quantified against the 3-FTE specialist pool or the two-person handover bottleneck.

**3. Portfolios — constraint failures and overclaiming**

- Quick-win (A1/A5/A10) correctly fails the “at least one control-strengthening module” rule; it is also only three modules with an unused slot.
- Bottleneck (A3/A4/A6) omits the dominant synchronization point: **Wait for external valuation (58.0h median, 2,350 cases)**. A “synchronization” portfolio that skips A7 is a process-level miss. It also skips the 2,800-case missing-document loop.
- “Reduces credit analyst workload by **75%+**” is false. Someone almost certainly confused the **75-minute** normalization service time with a 75% cut in analyst work. Normalization is 5,200 × 75 min; credit analysis is 8,900 × 110 min and remains judgment-heavy for high-exposure cases.
- Compliance-first is the only four-module set, then Section 7 says four modules **exceed** a budget of “at most four.” That is a hard constraint error. Four is legal.

**4. Process-level effects — fabricated and mis-attributed**

Section 4 is the weakest analytic section. Almost every “after” number is unsupported, and several are attributed to modules that cannot produce them.

- A1+A5+A10 do not cut KYC wait 4.0h → 2.0h. None of those modules sits on KYC.
- A5 would more likely *reduce* back-office “request missing documents” work and RM-triggered loops; the write-up says it *overloads* back office. That is the opposite of the resource note (12 FTE, utilization already &lt;65% after 15:00).
- A4 → “0h” mismatch wait is fantasy. A4 is an assistant; 1,160/1,750 mismatch cases **loop back to KYC**. The DFG loop is ignored.
- Valuation wait 58h → 20h appears under a portfolio that **does not include A7**.
- A3 does not move provisional-signing violations from 6.5% to 1.1%. 1.1% is the *existing* remediation rate in the control findings, recycled as a forecast.
- Compliance-first is credited with halving financial-review rework (24% → 12%) with no document or normalization module.

Waiting time and service time are mixed throughout. DFG relations, variant positions, and concurrency of KYC vs financial review are barely used despite being required.

**5. Risks — adequate list, weak compensation design**

The risk table hits the required categories, but compensating controls often point at modules **not in the recommended set** (especially A12). Four-eyes is pinned on A8/A9; four-eyes applies to credit analysis vs committee packaging in high-exposure cases, not contract generation. A3 false negatives are again underplayed as operational delay rather than control failure. Model-drift and audit-trail requirements are named, not operationalized (override rates, sampling, challenger rules).

**6. Rollout — contradicts the recommendation and the budget**

Recommended set: **A3+A4+A6**.

Actual Year-1 plan: A3, A8, A6, A9, then A2, A4, A10 — **seven to eight modules** against a four-module cap.

Worse sequencing:

- A4, the alleged critical-path lever (and part of the chosen portfolio), is deferred to months 7–12.
- A8 is piloted in months 1–3 even though Section 2 says it needs A6 (and often A7) first. Garbage-in/garbage-out is acknowledged and then scheduled.
- Gate criteria are arbitrary percentages with no measurement design.
- The two-deep-integration rule is not mapped cleanly onto the first six months (Phase 1+2 = four modules, one labeled “deep”).

A rollout that does not implement the recommended portfolio is a structural failure.

**7. Final recommendation — right slogan, broken argument**

“Do not automate Register first” is the correct management message and is the main reason this is not lower. The supporting proof is not.

- “Entity mismatches block 82% of critical paths” is false. The note is: *when present*, mismatch is on the critical path in 82% of *those* cases. Mismatch occurs in ~1,750/12,000 cases (~15%). That is high leverage per case, not 82% of the book.
- The compliance-wave rule is waved through as “indirectly” satisfied. A3/A4/A6 are primarily triage and labor tools. A9 (control-rule checks) or A12 would have been the honest control module; neither is in the chosen four (or three).
- Two of the three modules land on a **3-FTE specialist pool** already showing a two-person handover bottleneck. Shifted demand is acknowledged as a “sacrifice,” not designed for (staffing, surge rules, after-hours utilization).
- Unused budget slot, unused 58h valuation wait, unused four-eyes/month-end finding, unused legal-ops seasonality, unused weekly committee cadence (packaging automation cannot beat a weekly batch).

**Net:** Competent outline, unsafe details. Too many invented magnitudes and internal contradictions for a bank risk/process decision. Not a 2–3 because the anti-frequency thesis and the A11 prerequisite logic are sound. Nowhere near 7+ because the artifacts are repeatedly misread and the plan does not implement the portfolio it sells.
