The answer is strong process-aware work, not a DFG lookup. Primary states are right for all five cases, A vs B is correctly split on obligations rather than prefix, missing events are not treated as absent tasks, and the uncertainty is mostly honest. It is not close to flawless.

**What is actually good**

- **C6-A vs C6-B** is the required discrimination: same visible prefix, different normative state. High/injury/multi-device/lot vs low/no-injury/single-device/duplicate is used to open TI/RA/possible SA on A and push B toward non-investigation closure.
- **C6-C** correctly treats repeated malfunction as a *possible reportability signal* (Rule 4) and keeps Supplier Analysis optional because lots differ.
- **C6-D** is the best case: worklist evidence that RA is open, migration as an observation process, source attachment as delayed-log evidence, and `process_enabled: false` on a restored `Evidence Received`.
- **C6-E** correctly makes SA mandatory from the six-complaint lot and uses completeness 0.72 to keep a third evidence cycle alive.
- Intervals are wide, DFG is treated as observational, starts are treated as invisible, and supporting / contradicting / unknowns are separated.

**Why it is not an 8+ under a strict bar**

1. **Next logged event vs later milestone is systematically blurred.** Rank-1 choices are mostly the right immediate completions. Ranks 2–3 often score *downstream* work as if it were a competing next log line: `Close — No Safety Issue` on B (8–25%) while review is still open and the feed is *normal*; `Disposition Recorded` on C (10–30%) and D (5–25%) before RA has completed; `Authority Notification` on C (3–18%) which still needs RA + a reportable finding + disposition + the 16:00 batch, with 12 items already in the RA queue at 14:00. `process_enabled: false` shows the dependency, but it does not make those probabilities coherent as *next observations*.

2. **Probability bands are templated.** Four of five cases sit on roughly `0.30–0.55` / `0.10–0.30` / `0.05–0.25`. Clock time and queues are listed as evidence, not used to move the numbers. ERC on E is still `0.30–0.55` after only 40 minutes in a 4-item review queue. RA on D is `0.25–0.50` after 1.3 hours in a 12-item, 2–14 h activity.

3. **C6-A under-ranks Regulatory Assessment.** Injury, high severity, and R-T4 (reportable specialist) plus RA’s reportability priority make `Regulatory Assessment Completed` a real competitor with TI. The answer puts TI in the top three and leaves RA out, while also calling TI currently process-enabled. That is an assumption: investigations are typically opened from review, not shown as already running.

4. **A few normative slips, not catastrophic.** C6-C’s S5 alternative (“RA not required”) is weaker than “RA finished but unlogged”; Rule 4 is triggered by the *signal*, not by a later “yes, reportable” decision. C6-E slightly overstates that the lot cluster blocks ordinary no-safety closure rather than only Fast Close and pre-SA disposition. C6-B never really tests Fast Close as an S1 residual, even though the case still satisfies Rule 2 on paper.

5. **16:00 notification batch and remaining working time are underused.** They appear once in C’s rank-3 sentence and almost nowhere in the actual ranking, which is exactly where they should bite.

Net: disciplined, case-sensitive, and well above a mechanical DFG answer. The recurring next-vs-later confusion, cloned intervals, and the A/RA miss keep it well short of near-flawless.
