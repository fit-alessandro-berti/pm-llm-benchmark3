**6.4**

The final write-up is well structured, format-compliant, and clearly above generic Lean/Six Sigma advice. Diagnosis uses many concrete artifacts (DFG volumes, performance tails, 14.1% vs 3.3%, 31%→9% cost erosion, fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, 26% repeated evidence, segment medians and complaint rates, resource constraints). The three redesigns are materially different, trade-offs are stated, and exact ROI/Pareto dominance is not claimed. That is the ceiling of the credit.

The score is held down by repeated **path-to-outcome misattribution**, which is a core process-mining error rather than a wording quibble.

**Redesign A overclaims the wrong mechanism.** Segment outcomes of 22%/18% vs 5%/6% belong to a *reviewed/repair fulfillment* mix, not to “manual or fraud review, then still Fast approval → Dispatch.” A mostly inserts a compliance hold in front of the same replacement path. That can cut R1/R2 breaches and some ineligible approvals; it does not automatically buy the repair-path quality rates, nor does eligibility/risk review fix the artifact the prompt ties to complaints: **incorrect replacement part selection** (dispatch→reopen mean 96h / p90 180h, EUR 410 high-value error). Citing those complaint deltas and the EUR 410 figure as A’s improvement mechanism is not supported.

**Redesign C makes a category error on the repair loop.** A pre-dispatch gate sits on *replacement*. Rework repair is Quality check → Rework repair on the *repair* path (12.5% vs 4.0%). C cannot “also reduce rework repair” as a primary effect. The claim that the gate should move fast-path complaints “from 14.1% toward” the 3.3% repair-quote path is the same mistake: 3.3% is a different variant (quote + customer approval + repair + QC), not a quality-gated replacement.

**Performance DFG is used sloppily in the experiment.** Fraud/risk → Fast approval p90 of 38 hours is treated as a “p90 wait” stop rule. That edge is sojourn between two activities, not queue wait. After A routes high-value *off* the fast path, “high-value fast-path complaint rate → 10–12%” is the wrong success metric; the residual fast-path slice is selected and small. The 120-hour Premium high-value p90 go-threshold is invented (prompt gives a 96-hour *median* on the reviewed/repair path).

Smaller issues compound this: B treats the 140-hour unclear-defect median as if the portal were the main driver (loops are only one component); R1 (EUR 400) is repeatedly fused with “high-value device”; stop/go is specified mainly for A while B and C are sketched; “dominated?” is conceptually muddy (current = “No”; A “not dominant for low-risk” even though A is designed not to change that segment).

So: strong evidence inventory and three real design archetypes, but the causal story that links DFG/conformance/rework stats to expected outcomes is repeatedly wrong in the places that matter most. Under a strict rubric that is well short of nearly flawless.
