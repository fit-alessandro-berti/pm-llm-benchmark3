**5.2**

The write-up is complete in outline and sometimes operationally literate, but it is not a reliable process-mining redesign. Several claims contradict the log, several policies are internally inconsistent, and frequency evidence is mishandled in a way that would mislead an operations team.

**Format and coverage.** All seven required sections exist, scenarios cover the mandated disruption classes, and the trade-off table has the required columns. That is table-stakes compliance, not excellence. Completeness does not offset broken logic.

**Process-mining hygiene is weak.** Normal totals (8 weeks, 18,400 cases) are repeatedly treated as comparable to peak totals (3 weeks, 9,900 cases). “Recollection 760 → 780” and “cancels 280 → 540” are the clearest examples. Weekly, recollection and cancellation both get much worse in the peak; the answer understates that. It also never converts DFG counts into rates, so it misses the strongest evidence in the file: technical validation releases ~74% of cases in the normal period versus ~50% in the peak; batch-wait incidence roughly triples; assay-to-repeat roughly doubles; specialist-hold volume jumps from 290 to 610 on a much shorter window. Invented utilizations (M1 “~140%”, medical validation “150%+”, courier “130%+”) are not derived from the capacity table and are presented as if they were measurements.

**The M2-outage policy is a hard logic error.** Scenario 2 is an M2 outage. I3 *routes Routine work to M2*. Activating I3 when M2 is down sends Routine cases to a machine that does not exist. The same bundle is then scored as *reducing* repeats. The prompt states the opposite: M2 already has the higher failure rate, repeats cluster on M2, and I3 increases repeats if Routine is dumped onto it. Section 6 later admits the M2/repeat risk that Section 5 denies. That is not a wording slip; it is an incoherent control policy.

**Intervention logic is inconsistent.** Section 3 “avoids” I1 because it starves Routine and Complex-panel work; Section 4 turns I1 on in the Urgent Surge. The I1 trigger is medical-validation queue time, which is the wrong station for an assay-dispatch rule. I6 and I7 are sold as always-on, then re-listed as “scenario-specific” for Courier Chaos, so that scenario has no incremental policy. I4 is made always-on even though technical validation is already at 101% in the historical peak; the “no net loss of technical-validation capacity” assumption is the operating condition the data already falsifies. I5 is activated in a specialist-absence scenario, i.e., extra shifts of people who are not there. I8’s stated conflict—unblocking registration only to expose pre-analytical check and the assay line—is not analyzed, despite being given in the prompt.

**Bottleneck-migration analysis is mostly narrative.** Some station-level facts are used correctly (pre-analytical 96%/9.5 h, assay 98%/18 h, medical validation 22 h, specialist hold 48 h, courier spikes, Urgent batch-wait bypass falling from 74% to 58%). But migration paths are generic “A backs up B.” Technical validation is said to congest *because* medical validation is slow; those stations are sequential, and the log does not establish blocking. Urgent Surge analyzes I1 as if it were already in the as-is process. Downstream overload is discussed for some moves and omitted for others (I7 smoothing can remove recovery troughs and pin pre-analytical/assay at high utilization; I10 full-case outsourcing would unload assay, yet the table lists assay as the next bottleneck from transport delay).

**Dashboard thresholds are not decision-useful in a peak.** Warning/critical bands sit at or below historically normal peak values (assay >90/95% while peak utilization is already 98%; medical queue 18/24 h vs peak median 22 h; specialist 30/48 h vs peak median 36 h; repeat >10/15% vs a peak assay-repeat share already near 15%). The board would be red on day one of any winter wave and would not distinguish expected peak from a breaking peak. Urgent p90 <18 h is also the wrong statistic for an 18-hour contractual cut-off.

**What remains salvageable.** Case types are not collapsed into one average. Recollection loops consuming clerk capacity, batching versus Urgent fragmentation, and specialist review as a Complex-panel choke point are the right mechanisms. Residual vulnerabilities include at least one redesign-induced failure (I4, I10 traceability). Those points keep the answer above a failing score. They do not offset the M2/I3 contradiction, the unnormalized DFG arithmetic, the I1/I3/I4 policy collisions, or the unused routing evidence that actually explains why the peak is not just “more volume.”

Under a strict reading, this is a structured but unreliable operations memo: right skeleton, several controls that cannot work as written, and process-mining evidence used more as decoration than as constraint.
