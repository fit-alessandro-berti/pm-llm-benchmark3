**5.0**

This is a competent-looking design that avoids the worst traps (treating rejects as non-default, training only on the 21,600 rows without comment, random splits) but is not close to a defensible, internally consistent study. Under a strict reading of the brief—early warning from the first few events, honest label maturity, leakage-free prefixes, and a runnable PM4Py skeleton—the errors are structural, not cosmetic.

### What is actually right
The core statistical instinct is correct: `default_90d` is defined only after activation; rejected, withdrawn, and open cases must not be recoded as non-default; activation is a selection mechanism, not a nuisance label. The 48,000-row flow (≈22,800 never activated + 21,600 matured activated + 3,600 immature activated) adds up. Competing-risks is a real alternative, not a slogan. Collection status, future events, and the label itself are correctly treated as forbidden features. A random case split is correctly rejected.

That is the floor for a serious answer, not the ceiling.

### 1. Target and prediction time — the design is the wrong product
The lender asked for an **early-warning** model from the **first few events of every application**. The primary design predicts **at Account Activated**, after KYC, document loops, Credit Review, Accept, and Contract Signed. By then the underwriting decision is done. That is post-booking default scoring, not prefix-based process monitoring.

The brief explicitly allows “default among accounts that are eventually activated” as a *target*. It does not allow moving the *prediction time* to activation. A valid activated-cohort design still scores **early prefixes** of cases that later activate (with selection stated as a limitation). This answer instead feeds the model almost the entire trace. That also makes “every application” operationally impossible: the model cannot be applied until activation has already occurred, which Section 8 then has to walk back.

The 90-day clock starting at activation is right. The estimand is reasonably written. The alternative (competing exits before activation) is the better match to the business question and is treated as second-class. A policy-counterfactual target is named and then abandoned, which is acceptable only if the primary target were operationally aligned. It is not.

### 2. Cohort construction — arithmetic yes, taxonomy no
Counts reconcile. The table still collapses distinct mechanisms the brief asked to keep separate:

- **Still-open (5,100)** are not competing terminal outcomes. Many are **pre-activation administrative censoring**; some can still activate after 15 Feb 2026.
- **Transfers (2,900)** are not simply “non-activated / exclude.” They can reappear under a new id; linkage exists for only ~60%. That is missing follow-up / identity breakage, not a clean competing event.
- **Partner-originated missing feeds** are “selectively unobserved” among *activated* accounts, not a footnote on the 3,600. They can sit inside both the labeled export (if a feed eventually arrived) and the immature bucket (if it did not).
- The 3,600 mix **horizon not reached** and **horizon reached, feed delayed**. The table treats them as one censored class.

Inclusion is “has an Account Activated event.” That is operationally clear and statistically incomplete.

### 3. Censoring — named, then flattened
The five mechanisms are listed. The analysis then treats delayed labels, ordinary right-censoring, and possibly permanent missingness as the same thing: “right-censored at extraction.”

That is wrong. If the 90-day window has **ended** and the feed has not arrived, the event time is not censored at 15 Feb 2026 in the survival sense; the binary 90-day outcome exists and is **unobserved**. Delayed labels want waiting, truncation by calendar time, or inverse-probability of observation. Survival censoring is for cases whose 90-day clock is still running. Permanent missingness for partner feeds is closer to MNAR selection than to non-informative censoring.

Sensitivity analyses (drop censored; partner missingness bounds) are directionally right. Declaring partner permanence “fundamentally untestable at extraction” is fair. Then Section 7 proposes backtests that **assume censored = non-default**, which is the convenience coding Section 3 forbids.

The 15 Nov 2025 cutoff is already slightly wrong (15 Feb − 90 days ≈ 17 Nov 2025) and, more importantly, **ignores the 7–35 day feed delay**. Last fully mature activation is closer to mid-October 2025, not mid-November.

### 4–5. Prefixes and features — late, leaky, and process-thin
Fixed-length and elapsed-time prefixes are mentioned, then defined as “everything ≤ activation.” Contract Signed is explicitly kept. Activity renaming is “use whatever string is in the log,” which makes pre- and post-15 Oct 2025 prefixes non-comparable and ignores duplicate events from the migration. Short rejected traces never enter the primary model, so the prefix machinery does not serve “every application.”

Process features are a standard PPM list (loops, sojourn, queue time as time-to-first-review). Resource/queue is not queue length or contemporaneous workload. Historical reference data is mostly calendar dummies. `final_limit` and `decision_reason` are treated as legitimate prefix features because prediction sits at activation. For early warning they are post-decision and should be dropped.

### 6. Leakage audit — required rows present, several decisions wrong
The table hits every mandated field. Keep/repair/drop is the failure:

| Field | Verdict in the answer | Strict verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Keep | Drop for early prefixes; it is the underwriting result |
| `decision_reason` | Keep | Drop; populated at Accept/Reject |
| `collection_status` | Drop | Correct |
| Future events | Drop | Correct |
| `documents_total` | Repair | Correct idea |
| Updated `risk_score` | Last pre-activation | Should be last **as of the prefix cutoff**, not activation |
| Resource experience | Keep with a flag | Drop or rebuild as-of; extraction-time experience is not a process feature |
| `default_90d` / `label_available_at` | Drop as features | Correct |

Keeping the two post-decision fields is consistent with the mistaken prediction time and inconsistent with the brief.

### 7. Validation — temporal in form, unusable in substance
Splitting by time and calling out policy and migration is necessary and done. The **test set is activations ≥ 1 Nov 2025**. Extraction is 15 Feb 2026. Almost none of those accounts have a matured 90-day outcome plus feed. The holdout cannot be scored. That is a first-order design error, not a quibble.

A maturity-respecting split would put the test window on case starts (as required) or activations whose labels are fully observed by 15 Feb 2026 (roughly through early/mid-October, after a feed-delay buffer), with an explicit embargo. Training only on pre-1 July 2025 activations is a reasonable **shift stress test**, not a primary operational split: the live policy is the post-July regime, and that is exactly the data you must learn from once it is mature.

Metrics (KM / cumulative incidence, time-dependent Brier, calibration on known labels, subgroups) are appropriate in principle. Then the backtest again imputes censored cases as non-default. Random-split criticism is fine.

### 8. Limitations
The association vs selection vs counterfactual distinction is stated. Deployment DGP changes are plausible. The section does not draw the sharp reject-inference boundary: a model fit only on activated accounts **must not** be used to score applications at origination without an explicit selection model. That is the decision-use boundary the lender will actually hit.

### 9. Code — too long, not executable, wrong APIs, wrong split
The skeleton is a wall of Python (~2× the 80–160 line envelope) that would not run and does not implement the spec:

- Split is by **activation time**, not **case start time**.
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` is not applied to the main log as the first structuring step.
- Case-level **terminal** events, competing outcomes, and as-of **cutoff** maturity are not built; only `is_activated` / `label_known`.
- `label_events.loc[case]` indexes the frame index, not the case id.
- `info_df.reset_index()` does not restore `case:concept:name`; the merge is broken.
- `groupby.apply(get_flags)` passes a group, not a row.
- Doc-loop code uses `loc[i]` as if it were positional.
- `df.loc[case, "source_system"]` assumes the index is case id.
- Feature frame has **no case id** and is merged on `right_index=True` — features attach to the wrong rows or to nothing.
- Leakage audit looks for an **activity** named `"Collection Status"`; in the schema it is a **column**.
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(..., max_length=...)` and `first_prefix_log['activities']` are not reliable PM4Py usage; prefixes are not a dict with an `'activities'` key.
- Survival / competing-risk hook comments are essentially absent.
- Duplicate-event repair and as-of `data_extract_time` are absent.

A skeleton does not need a fitted model. It does need to be a coherent pipeline. This is a sketch of one with several show-stopper bugs.

### Scoring rationale
Avoiding the “21,600 convenience cohort” and the “rejects = 0” lie is worth a lot; that is why this is not in the 2–3 range. The remaining failures are exactly what the rubric said to punish: prediction time that abandons early prefixes, a test set with no mature labels, post-decision fields retained, delayed labels laundered into survival censoring, still-open/transfers misclassified, and a PM4Py pipeline that is neither the requested length nor implementable. Hypercritically, that is a mid-pack answer, not a strong one.
