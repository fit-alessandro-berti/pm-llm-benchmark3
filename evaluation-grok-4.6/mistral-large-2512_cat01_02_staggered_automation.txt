**4.8**

The answer has the right skeleton and the right high-level instinct (no single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim; expand only with an evaluation). Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless. Quantitative errors, reversed signs, unreproduced statistics, and a design that is described one way and estimated another way are disqualifying for a lead-scientist memo.

## Format and coverage

The ten required headings are present and the main tables have the requested columns. That is the strongest part of the write-up. It is not enough.

Section 1 is acceptably descriptive and avoids an explicit causal claim, but it mis-times the complexity shock as “post-March” when `high_complexity_pct` jumps in May, and it never comments on the 4–6% `automated_fast_path_pct` already recorded while `automation=0`. Section 3’s justification is short of the requested 150–250 words and overclaims that the specification “controls for branch-specific trends.” Section 4 is damaged in a basic way: the beneficial/harmful/evidence cells lost their direction markers, so several rows are unreadable. Section 9 does not implement several results asserted in Section 5.

## Estimands

The table hits the required themes but is conceptually loose.

- ITT-2 is labeled ITT while the population is “adopter branches only.” That is an ATT-like contrast, not ITT.
- ATT-1 is a pooled continuous intensity association, not an ATT.
- “Identifiable from supplied data? Yes, but confounded” is a contradiction. If selection and co-occurring shocks confound the contrast, the estimand is **not** identified from these data without extra assumptions. Marking ITT-1/ITT-2/ATT-1/HET-1/DYN-1 as “Yes” inflates what the panel can support.
- There is no clean intensity/IV estimand on cycle time. Actual use is attached only to rework.
- VERSION-1 uses take-up as the outcome, which is not the policy contrast leadership asked about.
- Interference and treatment version are mentioned, but SUTVA is not built into any identified estimand except a spillover row marked unidentified.

## Identification

Staggered adoption / event study is a reasonable primary design in prose. The operationalization is not.

The primary row says comparison groups include never-treated and not-yet-treated units. The code sets `relative_time` only for Alpha/Beta/Gamma and then drops `relative_time` missing, so **never-treated branches never enter the “ITT event study.”** `never_treated` and `post` are created and unused. Calendar-month shocks (March fraud desk, May product change, late-summer arrival decline) are therefore aliased with relative time. That is not Sun–Abraham, Callaway–Sant’Anna, or even a two-group DiD.

Covariate choice is also sloppy. `arrivals` is partly a post-treatment variable: the routing campaign is concurrent with go-live. Conditioning on it can block a mediator or open a collider.

Alternative 2 specifies “branch × month FEs” with a branch-month treatment. Those FEs absorb the treatment. That is a specification error, not a conservative alternative.

Synthetic control is correctly flagged as weak with two donors, then left unused. No honest discussion of TWFE negative weighting appears in the actual estimator, only in marketing language for the event study.

## Process mechanisms

The right mechanism list is there: fast-path substitution, specialist congestion, learning, case mix/routing, staffing, rework, tails, survey selection. The evidence column does not respect the data.

The dominant pattern is a **common May–June deterioration** (complexity, rework, p90, CSAT) and a **common July–August easing** as arrivals fall in every branch, treated or not. Gamma’s post-July improvement sits on top of that system-wide recovery. Alpha/Beta’s first treated months sit on top of extra routed volume, an FTE loss at Alpha, and the May shock. The memo repeatedly treats Gamma as a success story and early adopters as mixed, without centering the common shock.

Rework is not a clean adopter increase: April dips, then the May jump is visible in Delta and Epsilon too. Escalation’s first discrete jump is March in **all** branches, i.e., the fraud desk, before Alpha/Beta go live.

## Quantitative discipline

This section is the largest failure.

**Arithmetic.** Beta post `rework_pct` for Apr–Jun is \((10.4+12.7+12.7)/3=11.93\), not 11.3; the −1.5pp / −11.7% figures are wrong. Gamma Jan–Mar `csat_1to5` is \(4.057\), not 4.02. The never-treated Jan–Mar vs Apr–Jun block is not reproducible: pooled median is about 37.4 → 43.7, not 38.3 → 44.5; p90, rework, escalation, and CSAT are off in the same table. A memo that asks leadership to trust ranges cannot miss the control means.

**Windows.** Gamma is given a distant Jan–Mar pre-period even though it is treated in July, which discards the relevant May–June baseline. Alpha/Beta post windows stop in June and therefore omit the August improvement that the narrative later leans on. Section 9 then uses a different rule (all pre months; post = adoption through +2 months). The tables and the “reproducible” script do not compute the same contrasts.

**Sign errors.** Gamma’s pre-period median \(36.4\to36.5\to39.6\) is called improvement; it is deterioration. The never-treated \(+16.2\%\) movement is called “improvement” in the bounding exercise; the table itself is a worsening. Section 7 cites “Gamma’s improvement” on p90 as the strongest supporting evidence, while Section 5’s own table says Gamma p90 \(+7.5\) hours / \(+9.7\%\).

**Logic.** Control-group deterioration is listed as **counterevidence** for a median benefit. In a DiD reading it is the opposite: it is what generates the relative-gain story. The “bounds” are not bounds; they are a confused DiD labeled backwards, plus raw adopter pre/post labeled as an upper bound with no identification argument.

**Unreproduced numbers.** Section 5 reports correlations, p-values, a clustered intensity coefficient with CI \((-0.48,-0.02)\), and a February placebo \(p=0.87\). Section 9 does not compute the correlations, the placebo, or the bounds. With five clusters, a clean \(p=0.04\) on the intensity model should not be sold as evidence. Invented or off-stage p-values are a serious problem in a section titled “reproducible.”

**Intensity.** Pooled correlations of `automated_fast_path_pct` with outcomes mix between-branch selection (the slow branches are the ones that later have high take-up) with within-branch change. Branch FE helps; month FE is still missing, so later months with high take-up are also the months after the product change and the summer easing.

## Threats and effect assessment

The threat list is complete enough and includes the required items. Several bias directions are wrong or too confident. Shared fraud-desk congestion is more likely to make controls look worse and **overstate** adopter DiD benefits, not understate them. Selection on historically weak branches plus extra load and Alpha’s FTE loss should, if anything, bias raw operational contrasts against finding benefits; the memo says the opposite. “Small number of branches” is an inference problem, not a signed bias on the point estimate.

Section 7 then overreaches. Confidence 60 on median and 80 on escalation is not defensible with five branches, no case-level data, and a common shock. The “safe” leadership sentence that automation “may reduce median cycle time by up to 10%” is exactly the over-compression the prompt warned against, and it conflicts with Alpha/Beta’s own first-quarter post means (median **up** \(+3.1\%\) and \(+5.4\%\)). Escalation \(+2\) to \(+4\)pp is the least bad descriptive claim, but it still bundles the March desk, the May mix shift, and any automation increment. CSAT is correctly called inconclusive; p90 should have been called **not identified**, not “mixed with Gamma improving.”

Internal inconsistency continues: Section 5 Alpha rework change \(0.0\); Section 7 counterevidence “Alpha’s increase \((+1.3\)pp).”

## Rollout and claim ladder

`expand with an embedded evaluation` is a reasonable headline. Treating Delta first and holding Epsilon is the right instinct. The decision rule undoes the identification work: “if Delta’s median falls 10% and CSAT does not drop, scale to Epsilon” is a before-after rule that the May and July–August comovement already shows can fire on seasonality and mix. There is no requirement that Delta beat Epsilon, no case-level randomization of automated vs manual triage, and no redesign trigger for the one stable process signal (higher escalation / possible tail congestion). Guardrails (\(p90 +20\%\), CSAT \(-0.2\)) are wider than shocks already observed under no incremental treatment.

Section 10 is ordered backwards. **Observed** facts are stronger than a **Conditionally causal** 10% cycle-time claim that the same memo cannot identify. The first bullet should not be a causal reduction; the escalation bullet uses causal language (“increased”) under `Observed:`. The intensity bullet is closer to `Suggestive:` than anything above it.

## Code

The CSV is complete and the script would run. It does not validate a \(5\times 8\) balanced panel. Trend plots are separate figures, as required. Clustered SEs and the small-cluster warning are present. Causal caveats are a trailing print, not comments on the models.

The labeled “ITT event study” is within-adopter relative-time dummies plus a few covariates. That is not the primary strategy in Section 3. The intensity model has no calendar time. Pre/post windows disagree with Section 5. No load metric (`arrivals/fte`), no control-group contrast, and no placebo appear in code, despite being argued in text.

## What a strong answer had to see

Endogenous targeting of weak, high-volume branches; availability \(\neq\) use; a March shared-desk shock; a May mix shock; routing and FTE movements coincident with go-live; median and p90 that do not move together; July–August recovery in **untreated** branches; five clusters; and survey data without denominators. The memo names most of these and then mishandles the numbers and the signs. The policy headline is acceptable; the evidential ladder is not.

The grade is 4.8 because structure and the expansion-with-evaluation instinct are real, but a process-mining / causal-policy answer that reverses pre-trends, mis-adds control means, cites a p90 improvement that its own table contradicts, claims identification while calling the contrast confounded, and estimates an event study with the controls dropped cannot be scored as competent scientific work under a strict rubric.
