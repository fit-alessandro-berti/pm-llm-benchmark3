**7.8**

The report is complete, well structured, and mostly aligned with the evaluation emphasis: March is treated as semantic/provenance drift, later movement is not collapsed into the migration, rework and queueing are tied to supplier and staffing, priority and seasonality are handled separately, and monitoring is built to tell recurrence from new drift. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up.** Timeline construction is the strongest part. Logging metrics step in March while cycle time and the normalized DFG stay flat (L1 = 3); rework and supplier-Z share step in May; June–August is a queueing ramp with flat service time; October is mix plus a senior-assessment jump that outruns complexity; December is concurrent recovery. That is the right mechanism-specific change-point story, and Section 3.5 correctly rejects a single global break. DFG L1 arithmetic checks out (3 / 40 / 59 / 28 / 64 / 36). The fitness-gap discussion is conceptually right: mapping recovers vocabulary, not frequencies, routing, or waiting. Cycle-time shares are given as ranges with stated assumptions. The eight-plus hypotheses, discrimination table, and 12-monitor design are usable, and the seven IF–THEN–BECAUSE rules match the mechanisms.

**Concrete errors.** Section 2 calls the March raw–mapped gap a “37-point” drop between 0.55 and 0.90; that difference is 0.35. “DFG distance of only 3 per 100 cases” conflates total L1 edge mass with a per-case rate. Section 6’s “±9pp precision” at n = 30 is about one standard error, not a confidence interval (~±18pp at 95%). Section 1 states “supplier-Z-affected share” as an observation; that is an interpretation of an ambiguous column, only flagged later.

**Causal slips.** Section 9 asserts July–August deterioration “is superadditive,” while Section 4 admits aggregates cannot prove superadditivity. Sequential worsening is not interaction. “Rework load peaked exactly when capacity was cut” is also overstated: rework had already doubled in May (12→24) before 15 June. Residual cycle-time is labeled “queueing,” so the decomposition is partly definitional even though the service-time fact supports a large waiting component.

**Format and PM4Py.** The spec asked for one code block with two parts; two fences are provided. `format_dataframe` is called with already-standard XES names, so it does not actually standardize a raw log; `normalized_copy` assumes those names exist pre-format. Dedup is any exact `(case, activity, timestamp)` match, not the specified `Open Case` technical duplicates. `fast_variant_pct` is supplied and barely used. The hypothesis “rank” column and the dual 75/60 evidence cell in Section 1 break the requested single-valued fields.

**Net.** The causal skeleton and monitoring architecture are well above a median answer. The grade is held down by a wrong headline gap, an interaction claim the text itself retracts, several quantitative wording errors, and avoidable spec/API misses.
