**4.3**

The answer avoids the worst failure modes (it does not blindly treat the 21,600-row export as the study population, does not train with a random split, and does not use `final_limit` / `collection_status` as features). That is not enough. The core estimand is internally contradictory, several censoring mechanisms are collapsed into the wrong risk set, the validation window cannot yield mature 90-day labels, and the PM4Py skeleton would not implement the design it claims to implement.

## 1. Target definition — conceptually broken

The primary target is named “time-to-default after activation with right-censoring **and competing risks**,” but the population is then restricted to cases that **eventually reach Account Activated**, and the estimand is written as a **binary 90-day probability**, not a survival functional. Those are three different objects.

Rejection, withdrawal, and most transfers occur **before** activation. They are not competing events on a post-activation default clock. Once the risk set is “activated accounts,” the only coherent competing events are post-activation (charge-off for other reasons, transfer after booking, account closure, permanently missing feed). Calling pre-activation terminals “competing risks” while simultaneously **excluding** those cases from the population is a contradiction, not a competing-risk design.

Worse, prediction time is the first *k* events / 7 days of the **application**, while inclusion requires **future** activation. That is selection on a post-prediction event. The quantity being estimated is roughly

\[
P(D_{90}=1 \mid \text{prefix}, \text{eventually activated}),
\]

which is not “default risk for every application,” not a well-defined counterfactual under the current accept/reject policy, and not identified from prefixes alone. The write-up says it “acknowledges selection bias” but never writes the selected estimand, never states the ignorability assumption that would justify fitting only on activators, and never says that a deployed scorer applied to all live applications would be scoring a different population than the one that generated the labels.

The alternative multi-state target is the more honest match to the business wording, and it is dismissed in a sentence. No activation-conditional binary target among **mature** booked accounts is cleanly separated from the survival version. Horizon is 90 days post-activation, but prediction time is calendar time after start — the clock that matters for the outcome is not the clock used to form prefixes.

## 2. Cohort construction — arithmetic only

21,600 + 3,600 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 = 48,000. That is the only fully correct part of the table.

- “Inclusion: all 48,000 / Exclusion: none” contradicts the primary population (activated only).
- Still-open applications are labeled “right-censored.” They have **not entered the default risk set**. Censoring them on a default clock treats “never (yet) activated” as “activated, outcome pending.”
- Rejected is annotated “competing outcome **(no default)**.” That is the convenience coding the prompt forbids, smuggled into a parenthetical.
- Transfers: 60% linkage is mentioned and then ignored. The 40% unlinked are not split out; re-identification under a new case id and double-counting are not handled; transfer-as-competing-event vs transfer-as-missing-follow-up is not chosen with a rule.
- Partner-originated permanently missing feeds do not appear in the flow at all.
- There is no as-of maturity rule: activation time + 90 days + the 7–35 day feed lag, using `label_available_at` and extract date 15 Feb 2026. Without that rule, “21,600 labeled” is treated as if it were a well-defined complete-data cohort rather than a selected, delayed-observation extract.
- Late-2024/2025 starts that activate slowly can be immature even if the case started long ago; start-date windows are not a substitute for outcome-maturity windows.

## 3. Censoring and missing labels — mechanisms mashed together

Ordinary administrative censoring, delayed outcome arrival, selection through activation, transfer, and MNAR partner feeds are different. The primary strategy treats immature activated, still-open, and missing partner feeds as the same right-censoring device. That is incorrect:

- Delayed labels are **not** standard right-censoring unless you stop the clock at `min(extract, label_available_at)` and only declare a 90-day outcome when the feed has had time to arrive.
- Partner-originated accounts have an **elevated** chance of a permanently missing feed. That is informative missingness, not independent censoring.
- Activation is a **selection mechanism**, not a censoring mechanism. Non-activated cases do not have an undefined-but-censored default time under the observed policy; they have **no potential default under this product** unless a counterfactual policy is stated.
- Sensitivity analyses (“all censored = default / all = non-default”) are crude Manski-style corners and are not labeled as such. They also reintroduce the coding the study was told not to use. There is no IPAW / selection model, no complete-case mature-activated analysis, no exclude-partner sensitivity, no bound that respects “outcome undefined if never activated.”
- “Policy change is testable” is not a missingness assumption. The actual untestables — counterfactual default given reject, MNAR partner feeds, unlinked transfers — are under-discussed.

## 4–5. Prefixes and features — thin, and one API is invented

`pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is length-based (`length`), not `max_length`, and it does not take `max_time=7d`. The prose invents a time-prefix API. Filtering to `start+7d` and then taking length-3 prefixes is an unspecified hybrid; it is unclear whether one prefix per case or all prefix lengths are used.

Short cases “padded with NA” is not a process-aware solution; standard PPM uses per-length models or all-available-events prefixes. Migration **duplicates** are never handled, only renaming. Cases that have already terminated (reject/withdraw) before the nominal prediction time are not given a rule.

Features: `discover_dfg` is a process **model**, not a case-level feature; using it as “frequency of paths in the prefix” is a misuse. Required cohort/calendar structure — pre/post 1 Jul 2025 policy regime, pre/post 15 Oct 2025 migration, day-of-week, queue time — is almost absent. Last `risk_score` at or before prediction time is not listed. Resource experience “at event time” is not in the schema; only extract-time experience exists. Historical reference features are a shrug (“if available”).

## 6. Leakage audit — best section, still incomplete

Required columns are mostly present and mostly dropped/repaired in the right direction. Failures:

- `default_90d` itself is repeated on events and is a direct label leak; it is not in the table.
- `label_available_at` “Keep (for censoring)” is acceptable only if it is barred from \(X\); that prohibition is not written.
- Comparison operators in the availability rules are missing (`time:timestamp  prediction_time`).
- `data_extract_time` and post-activation activities (Contract Signed, Collections Contact, Default Recorded) are not audited as event-level leaks.

## 7. Validation — temporal in name, unusable in fact

A case-start split is the right *family* of design. This particular split is not.

- Test cases start 1 Oct–31 Dec 2025. Migration is **15** Oct, so the “post-migration” test includes two pre-migration weeks.
- A case started in Q4 2025 almost cannot have a matured 90-day-after-activation label plus 7–35 day feed by 15 Feb 2026. The test set is either empty of honest labels or selected toward instantly activated accounts. That selection is not discussed, let alone fixed (earlier test window, or eligibility = `label_available_at <= extract`).
- Train is entirely pre-policy. That is a useful **shift stress test**, not a primary backtest of a model that must operate after 1 Jul 2025. No walk-forward, no embargo for delayed labels, no maturity filter inside each fold.
- C-index is only aligned with a true survival target; the written estimand is 90-day default. Missing: time-dependent AUC / Brier at 90 days, AUPRC (2,700 / 21,600), calibration by policy regime, and any metric that does not silently drop non-activators.
- Random-split critique is correct but shallow (policy + maturity). It misses that a random split also leaks through shared resources, same-day document snapshots, and transfer linkage.

## 8. Limitations — generic, not decision-boundary quality

The activation-conditional vs counterfactual distinction is stated and is the one clear success. Deployment risks (feedback on activation, later policy change, label delay) are boilerplate. Missing the ones this process actually produces: reviewers substituting the score for KYC/credit judgment; document-request loops changing because the score fires; partner-feed coverage changing when the score is used for transfer decisions; double-scored transferred identities.

## 9. Code — would not run as a design audit

Concrete defects:

- `activated & defaulted` and `activated - defaulted` on `ndarray`s from `.unique()` are not set algebra; the cohort print is wrong.
- Open cases defined as `time:timestamp > CUTOFF_DATE`. The extract should not contain those events. Open = no terminal activity by cutoff, not “has future timestamps.”
- No `label_available_at`, no activation timestamp, no 90+delay maturity flag, no immature-activated count of 3,600.
- `default_90d == True` on a CSV column is brittle and uses the label as if it were complete.
- `get_prefixes_from_log(..., max_length=...)` is the wrong signature; input is a filtered DataFrame, not a justified EventLog conversion.
- `insert_case_service_waiting_time` on a single-timestamp prefix log has no justified service/wait semantics (the prompt warned against this).
- Post-prefix columns (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, `default_90d`) are never dropped from a feature table; the “leakage audit” is a hardcoded dict.
- Final comment: “cases not in `activated` are **excluded**” — that is selection, and it contradicts the competing-risk story. `discover_heuristics_net` is not a multi-state outcome model.

The skeleton is also shorter and less complete than the requested 80–160 line engineering/audit pipeline (no temporal-feature extraction actually applied, no DFG/variants with a justified use, no `split_train_test` even as a foil).

## Verdict

This is a formatted recitation of the right vocabulary (as-of, competing risks, temporal split, leakage table) wrapped around a selected activated-only survival analysis that mislabels pre-activation terminals as competing risks, censors never-activated cases, cannot evaluate Q4 2025 starts, and ships non-executable PM4Py. Under a lenient rubric it might look like a mid-range student design. Against the stated quality bar — honest target semantics, process-aware cohorting, separated missingness mechanisms, and a concrete audit pipeline — it is well below a defensible study plan.
