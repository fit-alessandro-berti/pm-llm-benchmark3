The memo is strategically sound and hits the required structure: Option E (not A or B), a real trade-off, segment-aware rollout, and explicit stop/go thresholds. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated; the five metric conflicts are explained in process-mining terms; A–E are evaluated; M1–M8 are ranked with decision-changing implications.

It is not near-flawless. The evidence table is hard to read in several rows (`31.038.5`, `82%76%`, `€184€161`, `0%38%`), which undercuts a required section. Notes such as “skip rule often wrong” and “manual activation creates billing disputes” are treated as facts rather than interpretations. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is listed and then ignored. Those are real defects in clarity and evidential hygiene, not cosmetic ones. The recommendation would still be defensible if the table were repaired and the remaining artifacts were interpreted.
