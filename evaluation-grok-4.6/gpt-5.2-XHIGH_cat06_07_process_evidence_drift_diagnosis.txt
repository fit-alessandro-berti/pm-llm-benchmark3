**7.4**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that correctly refuses a single-cause story and gets the four load-bearing months right: April is a schema/pipeline incident, June is a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September are overlapping process, concept, and population shifts, and October is dominated by immature/censored labels. Predictive metrics are repeatedly triangulated with DFG/variant evidence, and remediation is staged rather than “retrain everything.” It is not nearly flawless. Category errors in the diagnosis table, a silent bug in the required process-evidence sketch, and several over-tidy causal claims keep it well below a high score under a strict reading.

## What the answer gets right

The central diagnostic pattern is correct and well evidenced. April overpredicts (mean risk 0.28 vs breach 0.19) with UNKNOWN activity 17%, missing queue 14%, and 18% unmapped variants — a feature-corruption signature, not a real risk spike. August underpredicts (0.19 vs 0.28) while `Order Part → Part Received` jumps 42h → 130h and V3 breach goes 0.35 → 0.64 — the opposite signature, and the answer says so explicitly. June’s V2 jump (34% → 52%) with stable diagnosis→Remote Fix times (3.8–4.6h) is treated as an intervention/mix change. October’s 0.16 observed breach is not treated as comparable to mature months; the backlog’s bias against long paths is identified. Falsifiers are stated for each drift type. Alert gating (maturity/data-quality suppress retraining) is the right architecture.

## Material flaws

**1. The diagnosis table mis-assigns two months.**

April is listed under process drift as “logging/schema.” Schema rename, UNKNOWN labels, and duplicate events are pipeline/log-quality failures, not a change in the real process. The row text even admits process drift cannot explain April, yet April still appears in `Months implicated`. That is a category error, not a nuance.

June is listed under covariate drift because routing mix changed. The prediction is made immediately after diagnosis; remote routing is a *post-prediction* assignment. That is process/mix (and possibly a policy-induced change in \(P(Y\mid X)\)), not a demonstrated shift in diagnosis-time \(P(X)\). Section 2 correctly says June need not be concept drift; Section 1 still files it as covariate drift. The two sections disagree.

Using “mean score does not track base rate” in June *and* September as covariate-drift evidence is also sloppy. June is overprediction after a mix shift; September is underprediction after supplier delay plus Partner Z. Those are different mechanisms.

**2. The required PM4Py sketch would not extract the performance edge it claims to extract.**

`discover_dfg` is unpacked as `(dfg, start, end)`. `discover_performance_dfg` is not, despite the same 3-tuple API. Then:

```python
part_edge_median_h = (perf_dfg[edge] / 3600.0) if edge in perf_dfg else None
```

If `perf_dfg` is a tuple of dicts, `edge in perf_dfg` is always false and the parts-wait monitor — the main process-evidence the narrative depends on — silently becomes `None` every month. That is a functional bug in the one artifact the prompt required, not a stylistic nit.

Further sketch defects:

- `start + MonthEnd(1)` lands at midnight on the last calendar day, so late-day events on month-end are dropped.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is called and discarded; it contributes nothing auditable.
- Monthly output has `top_variants` but not V1/V2/V3 shares or diagnosis→Remote Fix time, i.e. it does not operationalize the monitors the prose just designed.

**3. Several causal claims are tighter than the evidence.**

- August’s V3 share rise (22% → 35%) is treated as a real mix shift. Partner Z only starts in September. The answer never asks whether longer parts paths, delayed closure, or variant-mapping rules inflated V3 *share* rather than just V3 *duration*.
- June miscalibration (0.20 vs 0.15) is attributed to routing. Residual May lineage debt (cached “events before diagnosis,” ECE still 0.050) is an equally live competing explanation and is not tested against June.
- AUROC drop in Aug–Sep is called “consistent with changed outcome relationship conditional on the same pre-diagnosis information.” It is also consistent with mix shift, population shift, and selective label missingness. The later tests would separate these; the diagnosis table already leans on AUROC as concept-drift support.
- July is called “potentially safe” for training while V3 breach has already jumped 0.35 → 0.46. That undercuts the claim that Jul–Sep is the concept-drift window.

**4. Recalibration advice is slightly incoherent.**

Immediate containment is good (freeze retraining, gate on maturity, recompute April). Then the short-term step is a *global* recalibration on Jan–Mar and May–Jul. July is already inside the supplier-break regime. A global calibrator fitted through July will not fix V3 underprediction in August and can smear June’s intercept shift into earlier months. The mixture-of-experts formula later in the section is the right idea; putting it after a global Platt/isotonic pass on mixed regimes is the wrong order.

**5. Minor unclarities.**

- Confidence “Medium–High (Jul–Aug); Medium (Sep)” for concept drift is reasonable, but process-drift confidence is “High” for a row that still includes April.
- Partner-Z “High (shift exists); Medium (impact size now)” is well hedged; the table still puts Oct in population shift as if impact were already measurable, while Oct labels are 55% mature and Z matures more slowly.
- “Eventual variant” is correctly flagged as post-hoc in monitoring, but test 3 still uses it as if it were a valid diagnosis-time slice without quantifying leakage from path duration.

## Section-by-section

**§1 Table.** Right columns, right overall mapping, but the June/April category mistakes and the reuse of score–base-rate gaps as generic covariate evidence are the weakest part of an otherwise careful table.

**§2 Timeline.** Best section. The April-vs-August contrast (overprediction from corrupt prefixes vs underprediction from parts delay) is the key insight. October is not homogenized with August. Deduct for not explaining the pre-Z V3 mix jump in August and for underplaying May residue as a June confounder.

**§3 Tests.** Meets the required set. Competing-hypothesis predictions are mostly well posed, especially fixed-vintage backtesting vs pipeline recomputation vs V3-only calibration shift vs Partner Z reweighting. Test 5’s \(\alpha_m,\beta_m\) sketch is the right way to separate “same path, new risk” from mix-only change. Not flawless: “pure covariate drift ⇒ V3 calibration stable” ignores that missing Partner Z features can look like intercept shift inside V3.

**§4 Decision.** Proportionate in spirit (don’t train on April/Oct; shared backbone plus segment calibrators). Unsafe-month list is correct. Staging is muddled: global recalibration on a mixed May–Jul window, then later a variant mixture that should have been the short-term design.

**§5 Monitoring.** Alert dependencies are the strongest design choice in the answer: maturity and schema alarms *suppress* retraining rather than trigger it. The Python block hits the required APIs and the event-calendar vs case-start distinction, then fails to unpack the performance DFG and drops month-end events. For a “sketch,” that is still a failing extraction of the metric the whole audit turns on.

**§6 Risks.** All four required failure modes plus two more, each with a real mitigation. This section is close to the standard the rest of the answer is aiming for. The June-overreaction and Partner Z “unfairness without strata” items are particularly well judged.

## Why not higher

A 9+ would keep April out of process drift, keep June out of covariate drift unless diagnosis-time \(P(X)\) is actually shown to move, unpack `discover_performance_dfg`, and not recommend training/calibration windows that include the month concept drift is said to start. This answer is the right *kind* of analysis — overlapping causes, timed, falsifiable, with gated monitoring — executed with enough conceptual and implementation errors that it cannot be scored as nearly flawless.
