**4.8**

The response has the right genre and several defensible case calls, but it is not close to a publishable independent review. Material factual errors, invalid POWL semantics, a failed single-model deliverable, and several instruction violations keep it well below a high score.

**Headings and stance.** The seven required sections are present. Both sides are argued at length, and the author does not pretend M0 is either sacred or irrelevant. That is the main credit. It is not steel-manning. The prompt required the prosecution to concede genuine model/data defects and the defense to concede genuine risk and the limits of informal practice. The prosecution instead treats CRM mutability as “evidence tampering,” calls missing Notify a reason to reject the case, and treats a desirable pre-decision info loop as unauthorized conduct. The defense misstates SUP-708, treats post-expiry SUP-714 as a live overlay, and suggests operational necessity can outrun an expired written directive. That is not the strongest version of either brief.

**Evidence errors that a reviewer cannot treat as nits.**

- SUP-702 is dated 2026-06-15, inside PD-3 (2026-06-01–2026-07-31). The matrix still has the prosecution reading as “Noncompliant (pilot expired).” That is false and poisons the matrix.
- SUP-702’s onboarding trace also omits `Notify_Requester` while CRM has a stable `notification_id`. The matrix never records that second issue.
- SUP-703 is a HIGH-risk ineligible foreign supplier, not a missing-eligibility case. The reverse condition (“eligibility data confirms LOW-risk DE group”) rewrites the file.
- SUP-708 is `Quality_Review` *before* `Activate_Supplier` and `Sanctions_Check` *after*. The defense brief says the opposite and then uses that invented order as a model-expressiveness problem. 708 is a sanctions-before-activation failure with no overlay.
- SUP-715 is not a CS-9 requester/approver identity breach. The CS-9 resource rule is requester ≠ manual approver (that is SUP-712). 715 is quality reviewer and manual approver as spouses, plus a missing COI disclosure. No rule prohibits the relationship. Calling it a CS-9 control-flow/resource violation, at High severity and High confidence, is a category error.
- SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours. Weak, late, mutable linkage is a data-quality problem. It is not tampering.

**Scope, authority, and MIXED.** PD-3’s authority is contested in the record (minutes/email, not folded into CS-9; observer later denies having approved a CS-9 weakening). That contest is listed, then underused. FD-7 expired automatically on 2026-07-12; SUP-705 is 2026-07-13 with no written extension. Classifying 705 as MIXED is exactly the hedge the prompt forbade. The dominant legal fact is expired written authority; residual flood recovery can lower confidence or severity, it cannot keep the case in a split bucket. System-level “~30% / ~20% / ~50%” figures are computed on a curated 15-row exhibit and treated like population rates. That is methodologically unsound. The actual aggregates (312 Auto_Approve, 14 ineligible, 9 incomplete, 6 August leftovers, 47 info-request cases none of which activated early, Notify missingness 2%→29%) are mentioned and then not used as the compliance base rate.

**Model semantics.** Concurrent `checks` in M0 means *unordered*, both before the XOR—not “strict concurrency” that FD-7 or SUP-708 somehow refute. Auto_Approve is outside M0’s alphabet, so eligible pilot traces deviate *by construction*; that is a modeling fact, not automatically misconduct. More serious: the author asserts POWL cannot model loops. PM4Py POWL has `Operator.LOOP`. The “approximation” then adds a self-edge on a `StrictPartialOrder`, which must be acyclic. That object is not a valid POWL. `Request_More_Info` is also encoded as `SilentTransition(label=...)`. If the activity is logged and desirable, it must be a labeled `Transition`; silent nodes are redo/skip scaffolding inside a LOOP, not the business event.

**Section 7 fails the stated deliverable.** The task asked for *one* executable POWL that contains, at minimum, concurrent standard checks, manual route, low-risk auto route, emergency/provisional route, a Request_More_Info mechanism or an honest approximation, reject/close, and notify-before-close. The code builds three uncomposed models and prints their node lists. `auto_path` sends *every* XOR child, including reject and the manual branch, into post-activation quality, and it drops the concurrent quality check from the standard path. `emergency_branch` contains both `Provisional_Activate` and `Activate_Supplier`, i.e. two activations, which FD-7 does not describe. `request_loop` is never attached to the root. `pandas` is used and not imported; `dataframe_utils` is imported and unused. A conformance checker could not consume this as a revised normative model.

The overlay table and data proposal are the best parts of the revision, and still incomplete: no CS-9 baseline row, no requester/approver inequality, no COI disclosure, no master-data validation from PD-3, no daily monitoring from FD-7, no activity-label map, no bitemporal effective vs record time, no rule/version IDs on events, no delegation evidence, no withdrawals. Saying rejected leads are simply out of scope is a choice, not a derivation; CS-9’s abbreviated pre-screen is equally compatible with an explicit reject path after sanctions.

**What is actually right.** SUP-701 COMPLIANT; SUP-702 as a justified PD-3 exception (classification only); SUP-703 and SUP-708 as severe noncompliance; SUP-704 as FD-7; SUP-712 as a CS-9 resource breach invisible to M0; SUP-714 as post-expiry normalization; 709/710/713 as scope or label problems; 711 as a duplicate-log artifact.
