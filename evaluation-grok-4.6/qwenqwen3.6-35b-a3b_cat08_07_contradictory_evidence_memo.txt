**6.1**

The final memo is complete, correctly structured, and strategically in the right family (hybrid E rather than national scale or blanket rollback). Fact/interpretation/assumption separation is mostly maintained, the five metric conflicts are explained in causal terms, A–E are compared, and M1–M8 are ranked with decision impact. That is a competent synthesis. Under a strict standard it is not close to flawless.

**Logical errors.** Two of these are decision-relevant. First, Section 6 says to “execute M4 within the first 2 weeks,” but M4 is a 60-day downstream-cost follow-up; that timeline is impossible. Second, the memo’s “next steps” treat apartments returning to baseline p90/complaints as a green light to expand survey-skipping—while the same memo routes apartments back onto the baseline process. That test is circular: baseline-like outcomes would be expected and would not validate the skip rule. Section 6’s M6/accuracy thresholds are better, so the two recommendation blocks disagree.

**Factual slips.** Alternative B claims rollback “loses EUR 23 visible cost savings *per simple case*.” EUR 184→161 is the *overall* mean visible cost, not a simple-house unit saving. The memo’s “remaining 66% (apartments, old buildings, business customers)” omits the 11% rural segment that is needed to reach 66%. Coordinator load is repeatedly treated as a global 93% fact; the source is 93% in *two of four* regions. Option C is described as forfeiting merged validate/schedule gains; C only suspends skip and post-billing activation.

**E is redefined without saying so.** Prompt E is “deploy R for simple low-risk houses” (where skip and merge actually helped: −6 days, FTR +3pp, complaints −1pp). The write-up pauses skip *everywhere*, which is closer to C applied only to simple houses. That may be defensible, but the memo never justifies throwing away the skip where the segment table says it worked.

**Process-mining use is uneven and slightly wrong.** Fitness vs. precision is directionally right, but move-on-log is treated as evidence that “the model allows” bad sequences. Move-on-log is log behavior poorly explained by the model; low precision is the model allowing *unseen* combinations. Temporal-profile and DFG edge shares (the actual frequency shifts, billing→complaint, failed visit→second visit under high utilization) are mostly unused. SLA’s split (worse overall, better for simple houses) is ignored even though it supports hybrid routing.

**Presentation breaks the decision.** Comparison operators and arrows are missing throughout (`14.2  10.8`, `is  EUR 184`, `returns to  31 days`, `drops to  6.5%`, `at  85%`). In a decision memo the go/no-go thresholds are therefore unreadable. One Facts row also smuggles causation (“coordinator ownership reduced handovers”).

**VoI is decent, not tight.** Ranking M6/M4 high is reasonable. M7’s write-up mostly restates *already observed* deviations rather than incremental information value. M5 is undervalued given that the same memo treats 93% utilization and absence-stalls as a scale-limiting risk.

Net: right call, right skeleton, several errors that would matter to an executive reader. Not a low-quality miss; not near-flawless.
