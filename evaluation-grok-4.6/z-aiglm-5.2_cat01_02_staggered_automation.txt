**6.5**

The write-up is well structured and hits the required scaffold, but it is not close to flawless. Several material inconsistencies and one integrity problem keep it well below the top band.

**What is strong.** Section 1 stays descriptive and correctly separates availability, take-up, targeted selection, and concurrent shocks. The estimand table covers ITT, intensity, median vs tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, and dynamics, and the SUTVA/treatment-version notes are on point. The mechanism table is the best part of the answer: fast-path substitution vs capacity creation, fraud-desk congestion, learning, staffing, rework displacement, and survey selection are all framed as testable signatures. Pre/post arithmetic for the three adopters is mostly accurate, median and p90 are not collapsed, and the claim ladder correctly rejects “automation reduces cycle time by X%.” Small-cluster fragility and CSAT nonresponse are treated with appropriate caution.

**Quantitative discipline is weaker than it looks.** The DID tables are largely right, but Delta’s post-period escalation is 9.2, not 9.3, and several reported contrasts carry false precision (Gamma’s two-month ATT of −4.75h / −8.85h). More serious: §5.3 reports a specific intensity slope of about −0.15 to −0.25 hours per fast-path point, and an implied 7–12h effect at 50% intensity, as if those numbers had been estimated. They were not. The code that would produce them is only sketched later, and month FE plus a trending take-up variable could easily yield a very different coefficient. That is invented evidence, not approximation.

**Identification and implementation contradict each other.** The primary design is sold as Callaway–Sant’Anna / event-study, with an explicit rejection of naïve TWFE. The quantitative section never uses Gamma as a not-yet-treated control for the April cohort, never pools the two never-treated branches, and never reports relative-time coefficients. Section 9 then estimates exactly the TWFE the text disavowed; the `rt_*` dummies are built and unused, and the promised covariates (`fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, arrivals) never enter the models. Naming CS/SCM with two donors and five clusters is mostly branding.

**Process interpretation has a hole.** Arrivals per FTE is first-order and barely used. Alpha’s April–June load rises while FTE falls and p90 hits 119.9; that is a utilization story, not just a listed confounder. Pre-adoption `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% is noticed and then dropped. Encoding artifacts (“48.343.3”, missing minus signs) make several mechanism cells harder to trust than they should be.

**The rollout choice is the right class, the design is not.** “Expand with an embedded evaluation,” Epsilon-on / Delta-holdout, fraud-desk spillover monitoring, and hard guardrails are sensible. Then the plan waits for a 2027 pre-period even though eight 2026 months already exist, and the embedded RCT randomizes only low-complexity cases—the one stratum that cannot identify tail amplification, specialist congestion, or escalation, which the same answer treats as the main risks. The scale-up rule (“statistically significant 5h median reduction”) also targets a different estimand than the branch-level ITT the rest of the memo is about.

**Net.** This is a competent policy memo with the right refusals and a real process vocabulary. Under a strict bar it is still a 6.5: too much unrun regression, too much estimator name-dropping relative to what is actually computed, and an evaluation design that fails the author’s own mechanism analysis.
