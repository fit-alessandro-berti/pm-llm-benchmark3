**6.4**

The write-up is structured, often sophisticated, and avoids the usual trap of mapping every same-user case to failure. It is not nearly flawless. Required items are missing, several claims overreach the record, and the checking code can silently invert the primary test.

## What is actually strong

The analysis generally reasons from objectives, timing, independence, coverage, and recoverability rather than activity counts. AP-203 and AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` under a completed ED-1 package, AP-205 as `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`, AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` despite a clean two-user path, and AP-204 as an objective failure despite formal four-eyes are the right *kinds* of judgments. The hybrid treatment of post-release / pre-settlement MG-4 (detective of process, preventive of loss) is clear and defensible. The argument that equal expected loss is not a sufficient equivalence standard is one of the better passages.

That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading of the brief.

## Material reasoning defects

**Section 1 misstates AV-2.** The brief says AV-2 checks bank-account ownership (and 7-day account changes). The decomposition asserts that AV-2 “cannot verify true bank account ownership.” That collapses a stated check into a coverage hole. The real residual is weaker: AV-2 does not establish who controlled a compromised account, commercial reasonableness, receipt of goods, or collusion. Treating ownership as wholly uncovered distorts later sufficiency judgments.

**ED-1 is repeatedly framed as a preventive control.** The brief is explicit: ED-1 is a *permission with conditions*, not a disappearance of the four-eyes objective. Calling ED-1 “PREVENTIVE” in the threat matrix blurs the fact that prevention, if any, comes from the embedded AV-2 / MG-4 / RA-7 conditions. Token existence is not itself a fraud control.

**AP-204 is over-decided.** The record says u12/u13 deny collusion, design documents are missing, and the pair *may* be a dedicated two-person cell. A fabricated receipt *did* get paid, so an objective failure is arguable on outcome and independence doubt. Stating “pair collusion” at **High** confidence converts an unresolved independence problem into a finding of concerted fraud. That violates the instruction not to equate a control event (or a suspicious pattern) with a sufficient conclusion.

**AP-202 is slightly over-narrated.** Credential use, supervisory conflict, late ratification, and an employee-linked destination make `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` obvious. “Active fraud” plus unresolved “whether m09 shared the credential” cannot both sit at High confidence without qualification.

**“Corrective capacity” is used loosely.** In AP-201, AP-203, and AP-207 the column credits pre-settlement MG-4 — a preventive/hybrid barrier — as corrective capacity. RA-7 is detective and only partly corrective inside the recall window. AP-201’s audit at ~25 hours is outside that window; calling residual corrective capacity “Moderate” because a manager had already ratified mixes layers.

**Equivalence still leans on the metric the text rejects.** After arguing that \(E[\text{loss}]\) is the wrong sole standard, “Fully Equivalent” is defined as replacing FEP-1 with “zero increase in net loss exposure.” The added phrase “unmitigated risk pathways” does not resolve the inconsistency.

**Shared service accounts**, named in FEP-1 as non-independent actors, never appear in the decomposition.

## Completeness failures against the required brief

Section 6 was told to include **same-user rate** and **audit-before-recall-window**. Neither is specified as a rule. RULE-01 tracks emergency-token rate, which is not the same-user rate. RULE-03 flags *any* ratification after `Release_Payment`, which would alarm legitimate ED-1 patterns (AP-201, AP-207) without a recall-window or pre-settlement distinction.

Section 5 covers five cases but frames AP-208’s “most plausible loss mechanism” as unvalidated misdirection after the facts state the payment was legitimate. The exposure argument is fair; the wording treats a counterfactual pathway as the case’s loss mechanism.

## Code: required behavior, silent polarity risk, and bugs

The script meets the mechanical skeleton (in-memory CSV, UTC parse, `format_dataframe`, `filter_four_eyes_principle`, blocked-attempt detection, joined facts, labeled “not a verdict”). It fails several requirements and contains defects that matter in an evidence extractor.

1. **Repeated-pair flags are required and absent.** Tenure is computed and never emitted. Pair concentration is never computed at all.

2. **Four-eyes polarity is asserted, not checked.** Default PM4Py `filter_four_eyes_principle` / LTL four-eyes behavior is commonly *keep traces that satisfy* four eyes (`positive=True`: empty resource intersection), not traces that violate it. The text states as fact that 2.x/3.x return same-resource traces. The manual same-user fallback runs only if the API throws. If the API succeeds with the opposite convention, `Nominal_FEP_Violation` flips for every completed payment and the script will not notice. An evidence tool that can invert the primary statutory test without a cross-check is a serious flaw.

3. **AP-205 `Same_User` is False.** There is no `Release_Payment`, so `rel_user is None` and the same-user predicate fails. The blocked attempt is detected; the same-actor attempt is not.

4. **`settle_time is pd.NaT` is not a valid NaT test.** Several cases have no settlement timestamp. The branch is luckily not load-bearing for AP-202’s late manager, but it is still wrong (`NaT is not None` is True; identity comparison to `pd.NaT` is unreliable).

5. **AP-208 `Token_Time = 14:50` is invented.** The case facts say only that a token was active. Fabricating a pre-prepare timestamp in an “evidence, not verdict” table is exactly the kind of silent enrichment the brief warns against.

6. Blank system resources are handled; version caveats exist, but they document the *wrong* polarity with undue certainty.

## Net

This is a competent control-objective memo with the right overall case pattern and a few genuinely good distinctions (AP-209 vs AP-204, ED-1 vs raw FEP-1, hybrid MG-4). Under the stated grading rule it cannot be high: it misstates AV-2 coverage, treats ED-1 as a preventive control, overclaims collusion, omits required monitors, and ships checking code that can invert four-eyes results and drop required pair evidence. Those are not nits in a conformance review.
