**7.2**

This is a strong, well-structured operations-research writeup that meets the required heading contract and shows real process-mining competence. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several mismatches, unauditable jumps, and design contradictions keep it well below the top of the scale.

## What holds up

The load arithmetic is careful and mostly auditable. Daily case count (42), branch counts (M 18.09, E 24.30, S 12.85), uniform-mixture means, the 2,065–3,376 distribution-free band, 2,490 / 2,670 capacity, and the 109% / 102% / 121% ratios all check. U1–U5 bottleneck migration is coherent: morning surge → Intake then diagnostics; U2 as a mix shock; Chen absence as an E/S capacity hole; U4 as both extra mean work and worse residual-time error; U5 as infeasible for routing alone. Treating raw minutes versus person-minutes as a lower bound, because every multiplier is ≥ 1.0, is the right inequality.

Assumptions are labeled, independence of priority and type is flagged, U2 is given both the renormalized reading and the harsher “extra ~7 cases / ~467 minutes” sensitivity, and overhead is split into elapsed delay versus staffed time. Two objectives exist, and Objective B is actually a robustness/fairness object, not a relabeled mean delay. The recommendation does not rest on base-case average flow. Specialization versus pooling is treated as a real tension (handover/setup and dual-resource occupancy versus fixed-queue fragility), and late-day coverage collapse (Emil, no Mechanical after 18:00) is identified.

Section 7 is one of the best parts: required decision attributes are present, PM4Py case-level service/waiting is not confused with stage queue wait, and parallel traces are recognized as a contamination source for handover-of-work. Section 9 is appropriately modest.

## Material defects

**1. Section 4 is not computed from the model that Section 8 claims to be the evidence.**  
The required comparison table is “engineering risk bands” with very wide intervals (e.g. base P1 SLA 88–96% for H, U5 55–78%). Section 2’s arithmetic does not produce those SLA percentages. The DES that could have produced them is never run into the table. That is the core deliverable, and it is not auditable. “Values may be qualitative” is not a license to detach the table from the only operational model in the document.

**2. Narrative policy and code are not the same policy.**  
These are not comments versus implementation details; they change dispatch:

- Preemption is specified with a net-benefit test against restart, setup, and transition. The code preempts the longest remaining lower-priority diagnostic after a wait threshold if no free qualified resource exists. No comparison of P1 time saved versus 10% restart plus setup/handover appears.
- Hybrid P2 parallelization is specified as “saving > 15 minutes and next-hour diagnostic load < 90% of capacity.” The code uses `diag_wip < 180` remaining base minutes and ignores the 15-minute test.
- Pooling is specified as parallel only when projected parallel completion beats sequential. The code always parallels multi-branch work.
- Stress mode is a major part of the recommendation (seven triggers, Emil-after-16:00, P3 freeze on the flex resource). It is not a simulated policy and is not in the comparison table.
- Overtime is a deliberate stress action in §5 and an always-on 30-minute calendar extension in the sim. Always-on OT systematically shrinks policy differences and softens late-day failure.

The task allowed a scaffold *if omitted mechanisms were identified*. Here a “transparent DES” is claimed while the omitted or altered mechanisms are not listed.

**3. Hybrid primary pools contradict the bottleneck analysis.**  
Section 2 correctly makes Emil the late shared bottleneck for Intake, Electrical, Software, and Validation, and Deepa the only regular Mechanical/Electrical cover into early evening. Policy H’s primary Validation set is still Ben / Farah / Emil. After 16:00 that is Emil alone, while Deepa (1.00 Validation, on shift until 18:00) is overflow-only. The recommended policy therefore *manufactures* the Validation/Emil collision the capacity section warned about. That is a logical flaw, not a taste difference.

**4. Several simulation metrics are biased in exactly the scenarios that matter for “robust.”**  
Incomplete cases are scored as finishing at the horizon, so `mean_delay` and `p90_delay` are downward-biased when `censor_rate > 0` (U1/U3/U5). Utilization can include clearance days; fairness CV is arrival-day only — the two fairness-related numbers are not on the same clock. `get_service_time` is pointed at break- and preemption-split segments, so activity service is fragmented; that caveat is not stated, unlike the better note on `insert_case_service_waiting_time`. Six seeds are too few for the p10/p90 SLA columns they print; those quantiles are decoration.

**5. Smaller but real issues**

- Time-block tables charge Validation to the arrival block. The caveat is present; the 187% and 160% late-block ratios are still used as if they were contemporaneous offered load.
- Objective A scales CVaR by \(T/60\), Objective B by \(T/120\), with weights that are never stressed. “Robust” is defined, but the scalarization is not shown to be stable.
- SILO Electrical excludes Ada (qualified at 1.35) and hybrid Validation excludes Deepa/Ada without a capacity argument.
- One-minute global pair search over a 4-day horizon, plus an unpruned `low_priority_busy_times` list scanned inside `policy_allowed`, is a fragile way to generate the only quantitative evidence. A capacity approximation plus a smaller DES would have been more reproducible.
- Zero-length Start/Resume events and re-assignment after preemption reset `planned_at_assignment`, so preempted work is logged as a new Start. That quietly damages any later pause/resume mining.
- Carryover assumes Chen returns the next day. That makes U3/U5 look more recoverable than a true full-day skill loss. The bound without return is missing.

## Verdict

The piece demonstrates the intended competence: offered load by skill and block, speed and calendars, parallel versus sequential, handover/setup/preemption, fairness versus SLA tails, and bottleneck migration under shocks. It does not meet a near-flawless bar because the policy comparison is not derived from the stated model, the implemented rules are not the recommended rules, and the recommended hybrid fights its own capacity diagnosis. Those are structural faults, not copyedits.
