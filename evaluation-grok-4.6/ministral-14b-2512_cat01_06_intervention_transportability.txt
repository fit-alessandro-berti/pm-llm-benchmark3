**4.0**

The answer has the right managerial instinct—do not copy Site A wholesale, segment to digital claims, treat locks and specialization as binding, and run a stratified pilot—but it fails multiple explicit requirements and several first-order structural facts. Under a hypercritical rubric that is enough to keep it well out of the high band.

**Process structure is mis-specified.** Site B is not a sequential `I → OR → D → A` machine. Rules 2–3 and the DFG already allow `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after `Register` (`Register → Identity Check` 2,050; `Register → Originals Receipt` 1,860). The live constraint is that `Document Check` cannot start when originals are required. Calling Site B “sequential” and marking prerequisites as *low* transport relevance inverts the problem. The portable object is a *partial order* (overlap what is already legally enabled), not “create Identity and Document tasks together after Register for every case.”

**Site A’s 9h movement is treated as an effect.** The prompt forbids this. Mix shift (48% → 40% high-complexity), +2 verification FTE, API latency, huddles, timestamp capture, and no control jointly unidentified the before/after. Assigning median-cycle transport confidence 70 and then projecting **10–15h** gains at B—larger than A’s already-confounded 9h—is unjustified. The complexity drop is even worse: parallelization does not change case mix; marketing did.

**Required artifacts are incomplete or non-compliant.**
- The transport DAG omits required nodes (`identity_capacity`, `document_capacity`, `assessment_capacity`, `API_latency`, `appeal`) and most `S_` selection nodes. Capacities are the bottleneck-shift mechanism; leaving them out is not cosmetic.
- No scenario has **median down and p90 up**, which was mandatory. The natural version (digital cases speed up; originals/legal/assessor queues fatten the tail) is never built.
- Section 8 does not call `pm4py.parse_process_tree(...)`. It invents `ProcessTree`, `StartEvent`, `ChoiceEvent`, `ParallelSplitEvent`, and a bare `POWL().add_node/.add_edge` API. Site A’s after tree duplicates Assessment and drops Notify. `check_soundness` is called on the wrong object shape. The code would not run and is not faithful to the requested constructors.

**Candidate POWL contradicts the intervention text.** `register >> document_check` and `originals_receipt >> document_check` are both always-on; legal review is forced for every case; Assessment is said to wait for identity and document “*or* Originals Receipt”; Notify is missing; the supplement loop does not return cleanly through Assessment. A single strict order cannot encode the 62%/40% data-dependent branches without XOR/LOOP. That is exactly the fidelity the question asked for.

**Mechanisms are often the wrong ones.** Rework at B is a legal-supplement loop (360 requests, 345 back to Document Check), not Identity/Document “conflicts.” Pre-assessment parallelization does not automatically raise supplements 30%. Site B pools (4 / 7 / 3 / 2) are under-used: three assessors and two legal reviewers are the plausible congestion points if verification is sped up. Copying A’s WIP=6 and proposing cross-training smuggles co-interventions in as if they were the treatment.

**Pilot arithmetic and outcomes do not close.** 3,400 cases / 26 weeks × 4 weeks × 38% digital ≈ 200 *total* digital cases, not 200 *per arm*. Appeals are 90-day; a 12-week, 4-week-per-stratum design cannot measure them. Splitting tiny specialist pools so reviewers never touch both arms is operationally implausible and changes the system being studied. Randomizing originals cases “if receipt < 12h” misunderstands the already-legal `I ∥ wait-for-originals` structure. Assumption 3 (“originals delay is 12h for digital claims”) is internally contradictory: digital claims have no originals receipt.

**What remains creditworthy:** correct heading/column skeletons; recognition of several co-interventions; refusal of a unique transport effect; a usable modifier list (originals, locks, specialization, timestamp back-entry); a *conditional go* aimed at digital, low-complexity cases; and a clear mechanism-vs-implementation split via overlap, cycle time, and queues. Those are real strengths. They do not offset inverted Site B concurrency, an unidentified 9h treated as ATE, a missing required scenario, an incomplete DAG, an illegal/always-on Document edge, and non-executable PM4Py.

A nearly flawless answer would have (i) written A’s trees with `parse_process_tree` exactly as given, (ii) built B as `Register` then `{Identity ∥ Originals}` then `Document` only when allowed, with optional legal LOOP, (iii) decomposed A’s 9h into mix/FTE/API/wait/overlap residual with low transport confidence, and (iv) included a median-better / tail-worse congestion scenario. This answer does not.
