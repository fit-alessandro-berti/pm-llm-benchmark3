**7.3**

The final JSON is a strong, process-aware analysis: it uses control-flow obligations rather than DFG counts, treats missing events as incomplete observation rather than absent work, keeps uncertainty honest, and explains why C6-A and C6-B must diverge. It is not nearly flawless. Several conceptual and labeling issues keep it well below a top score.

**What is done well**

- **Obligations over frequencies.** High severity and injury correctly force Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment on C6-A and C6-D (rule 4). C6-B’s Fast Close branch is correctly treated as already left. C6-E’s six-complaint lot correctly makes Supplier Analysis mandatory and blocks the DFG path Evidence Review → Close. C6-C’s country rule is read as a reportability signal, not as a free pass to disposition.
- **Object-level caution.** C6-A’s two devices on one complaint plus “similar” complaints are kept as a *suspected* Supplier Analysis trigger, not a certain one. C6-C’s shared failure mode across *different* lots is not collapsed into rule 5’s “same lot” wording.
- **Observation model.** Completions-only logging, the 48-hour delay window, and C6-D’s worklist snapshot are used as designed. C6-D rank 3 with `process_enabled: false` is the right kind of distinction.
- **Queues and timing.** Review (1–5 h), Technical Investigation congestion, Regulatory Assessment priority, Supplier Analysis (6–36 h), and the 16:00 notification batch are actually used, not listed for show.
- **Uncertainty.** Intervals are wide, midpoints are roughly coherent, residual mass is acknowledged, and DFG limitations (marginal vs conditional, concurrency, rare-but-mandatory Supplier Analysis) are stated clearly.

**Issues that lower the score**

1. **Horizon is not fixed, so several probabilities are poorly defined.** Rank 1 is usually “the immediate next completion.” Ranks 2–3 are often later successors, or the same successor if a review/assessment event is hidden. Under a strict next-event reading, C6-A’s P(Evidence Review Completed) should be higher than 0.52–0.78, and P(Technical Investigation Completed) as *the next* event is too high at 0.06–0.20 (review must finish first, then a congested 8.5 h median investigation). Under a same-day/next-day horizon, C6-A rank 3 is if anything too *low* because the investigation is mandatory. The calibration note names a horizon after the fact; the numbers were not built under one rule.

2. **`process_enabled` is used inconsistently.** The flag is true for Disposition Recorded on C6-C and C6-D while the primary state still has Regulatory Assessment open, so disposition is not currently enabled. It is also true for C6-A Technical Investigation Completed at S3, before review has opened investigations. The flag is really being used as “real process action vs feed artifact,” which only matches C6-D rank 3. Requirement 6 asked for a cleaner split between what the process allows now and what the log will show next.

3. **Rank 2 vs rank 3 is often weakly supported.** C6-A’s [0.08, 0.22] and [0.06, 0.20] almost fully overlap; C6-C and C6-E ranks 2–3 overlap similarly. Ranking them 2 vs 3 is then a narrative choice, not a probabilistic one.

4. **Small state/label errors.** C6-A’s alternative “S2/S3 boundary” is wrong: Evidence Received has already occurred, so the case is not in S2. C6-D rank 3 names two different activities and two different stories (delayed backfill vs retroactive repair) as one prediction. C6-C’s alternative packs S5 and S4+Supplier into a single string.

5. **A missed operational coupling.** C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E are all in the same Evidence Review system at 14:00 (4 waiting, 2 active). Treating their review times as independent slightly overstates how soon all three review completions can appear.

6. **C6-B Close as the next logged event.** [0.15, 0.35] is reasonable as a near-term *outcome* after review, but high if the quantity is the single next event, which still has to be Evidence Review Completed unless that event is hidden.

The answer avoids the usual failure modes (blind DFG successors, missing-event = missing-task, fake precision). The remaining problems are real: mixed prediction targets, a muddled enabled-vs-observed flag, and rankings that the intervals do not actually separate.
