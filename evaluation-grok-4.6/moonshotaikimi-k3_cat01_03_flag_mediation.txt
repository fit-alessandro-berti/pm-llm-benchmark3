**8.2**

The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant answer: interventions are treated as a policy bundle rather than a magical `do(flag)`, occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation, cross-world NDE/NIE are correctly rejected, stage times are computed from timestamps without coding absent stages as zero, and the P2 pilot is process-aware. The stage table checks out against the CSV (means and supports are accurate to the reported rounding).

It is not near-flawless.

The largest substantive flaw is the local-threshold design. Flag is not a univariate cutoff on `risk_score`: complexity also enters (R09 is 0.58/High/unflagged; R15 is 0.64/Medium/flagged; R20 is 0.61/High/unflagged). Treating ~0.62–0.63 as “the” threshold and proposing a standard fuzzy RD is misspecified, and the observed scores barely overlap anyway, so the design is not implementable on these data. That is a real identification error in a required row, not a quibble.

Smaller issues compound. “10× faster” triage is wrong (2.5/0.2 = 12.5×). The unflagged high-load vs normal-load wait gap is offered as interference evidence, but `arrival_load` already encodes congestion, so it does not identify displacement by flagged cases. The E-value “RR>3” is asserted, not calculated. A within-complexity flagged-vs-unflagged contrast is never tabled, even though that is the obvious check against case-mix. A few mediator arrows disappeared in the prose (“reviewcycle time”). The PM4Py snippet is conceptually right about absent stages and DFGs, but it assumes `flag` survives `format_dataframe` and that `insert_case_service_waiting_time` accepts the formatted frame.

Those defects keep it well below a top-band score under a strict rubric. The causal framing, timestamp discipline, and policy distinction are still clearly above a merely competent answer.
