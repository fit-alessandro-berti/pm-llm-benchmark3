**6.4**

The final answer hits the required heading set, stays appropriately agnostic, differentiates East from North/South, and treats fairness, learning, tails, and pilot logic as first-class issues rather than afterthoughts. The mechanism map, three-scenario structure, leading/lagging split, hidden-average risks, and “do not infer success from early means” recommendation are the strongest parts. It is not close to flawless.

**Structure and coverage.** All seven mandated sections are present and mostly populated with the required sub-elements (optimistic/central/adverse; leading vs lagging; ≥6 hidden risks; pilot ingredients; conditional recommendation; PM4Py sketch). That is necessary but not sufficient. Several required ideas are named more than they are analyzed: stacking Technical Review, Legal/Risk, and difficult Quality Check onto one pool is the central staff objection, yet it is only weakly isolated as a distinct mechanism. Bots, priority, Quality Check, and exception paths are underused given the log schema.

**Mechanism map.** The best insight is that “nominal handover reduction” may be a relabeling (local loops become queue pulls) rather than a true cut in handoffs. Linking each action to a PM signal is the right form. Weaknesses: causal chains are mostly parallel bullets, not sequenced mechanisms (informal local advice → fewer unnecessary referrals → lower rework). Resilience is thinner than utilization/rework. Character corruption (`6  3`, `caseworker  tech  caseworker`, `6.8d6.5d`) makes several claims unreadable without reconstruction.

**Scenarios.** The three-way split is directionally right: East relief vs North/South expertise loss, average vs tail, role ambiguity. But several numbers (5% extra referrals, 1–2% / 2–3% rework, 30d+ P90) are invented and stated as if estimated from the given tables. The adverse P90 jump is extreme relative to East’s current 22.8d and is not derived. Optimistic “early warning signs: Minimal” is empty. Encoding damage again collapses cycle-time claims (`22.8d18d`, `16–18d  21d`).

**Indicators.** The table is usable and tied to real log fields (`reassignment_count`, `handover_reason`, `rework_flag`, team/segment). SLA miss is reasonably treated as lagging. Gaps: no explicit comparison operator (edge deltas, wait-time ratios, similarity drop thresholds), and several “leading” metrics would need a defined baseline window that is never specified here.

**Hidden-average risks.** This is the cleanest section: tail latency, complexity, local knowledge, fairness, learning, accountability, and exception variants are all present and connected to observables. Still slightly generic; it does not use the actual current table (Legal/Risk already at 91–93% utilization, East E_Tech at 82%) as hard evidence that the *same* scarce pool cannot absorb broader detection (9→12/100) plus QC.

**Pilot.** Scope, control (South), 6+12 week windows, segmentation, feedback, and rollback thresholds are all there. Two real design errors: (1) “East 90th percentile reduction > 10%” is a success KPI, not a guardrail; (2) piloting East *and* North together undercuts the answer’s own claim that those teams should respond differently. A staggered East-first design would have been consistent with the analysis. Trigger levels (8h wait, 12% rework) are arbitrary.

**Recommendation.** Correct type (pilot/modify, not full rollout) and correctly warns against reading early averages. Conditional logic is present but coarse; it does not say what would justify extending to South versus reverting to a hybrid embedded model in operational terms.

**PM4Py sketch — the largest factual failures.** Required function names appear, but several uses are wrong or conceptually confused:

- `insert_case_service_waiting_time` does not take a list of activity pairs; it annotates consecutive-event service/waiting times (optionally with business hours).
- `get_service_time(..., 'Technical Shared Service')` is not a resource-name API.
- `discover_performance_dfg` is unpacked as `(perf_dfg, perf_metrics)`; typical return is `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`.
- `'local caseworker'` / `'Technical Shared Service'` are org constructs, not `concept:name` values (Intake, Local Technical Assessment, Specialist Review, …). Handover networks are resource-to-resource; waiting-time/DFG analysis is activity-to-activity. Mixing them is a methodological error.
- Subcontracting in PM4Py is an A–B–A resource interruption pattern, not “local teams sending work to a faceless pool.” That is a misuse of the metric.
- `log_before` / `log_after` and `variant_list_of_interest` are undefined; there is no timestamp or period split. SLA misses are asserted, not computed.

**Net.** Strong exam-shaped organizational forecast with the right headings and the right cautions; not a reliable PM method sketch and not precise enough in quantification or presentation to score high under a strict rubric.
