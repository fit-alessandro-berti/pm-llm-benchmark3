**5.7**

The write-up is complete, uses the required headings/tables, and gets several structural points right: GPIP-4 integrity controls are not automatically displaced by ECD-2 or the CFO email; A6 is stated; rules are mostly applied at commitment time; P-501 is correctly treated as pre-ECD-2; P-504 is correctly treated as out of scope; P-506’s verbal extension is correctly rejected. That is not enough. Under a strict reading there are material legal, consistency, formalization, and evidential errors.

**Internal contradiction (most serious).** The hierarchy, R2, R7, A4/A6/A7, and D3–D4 all say Finance/Compliance/SoD survive ECD-2 and that late approval does not cure. P-502, P-507, and P-508 are then NONCOMPLIANT for missing or late Finance. P-505 is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION for the same A2 failure, with the incoherent gloss that A6 is “waived by continuity.” That also breaks the method in §1 (ambiguity resolved against the actor). P-505 should have been NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE on the clock/expiry question, not a justified exception.

**Wrong default rule.** The catalogue states `normally(Commit before PO)`. The default is the opposite (B1: Create_PO before Commit). Emergency/ECD-2 are the exception. That is a core formalization error, not a wording quibble.

**A2 is under-specified and then misapplied.** A2 requires an independent budget owner *and* a Finance Controller before commitment. The facts say IC members are not clearly budget owners. D3 gives the CFO Finance-interpretive authority, which is the best path to a justified Finance outcome — and is then both undersold in the hierarchy and inconsistently used. Finance_Approve is treated as if it exhausted A2.

**Evidential mistakes.** ON-44 is 14 July 08:30–13:00. §6 attributes that ERP outage to P-505 (18 July; different “clock may be nine minutes fast” point) and P-507 (17 July; a *document-system* queue, not ON-44). High confidence is attached to P-505 despite an expressly uncertain clock. Contemporaneous supplier ack at 17:59:31 is relevant; it does not license rewriting A6.

**Designed ambiguity is flattened.** INDETERMINATE is never used. C6 (Ops vs Internal Audit), A7 (whether COO+IC is a CRO exception), historical treatment of incident directives as overlays, and P-503’s three-day backup stock are noted then largely forced into NONCOMPLIANT. Seven of eight cases share one label. The prompt asked not to treat every deviation as noncompliant; the only exception is the inconsistent one.

**Process/timing slips.** P-502 is assessed without noting the CFO email did not yet exist (15 July 07:20 vs 14 July commitment). P-501 credits R6 (“IC not required”) even though ECD-2 was not in force; B6 (form within 24 hours) is actually met and ignored. R4’s actor constraint fuses B4 Shift-Lead substitution with ECD-2’s two-IC rule. P-507’s PDF at 12:40 vs the four-hour C3 clock is not worked through in the case row.

**§5 and §7 are only adequate.** Four alternatives exist and are directionally fair, but the most needed alternative — P-505 flipped to NONCOMPLIANT/INDETERMINATE — is missing. The policy clause is usable and has an anti-normalization safeguard; it still does not cleanly resolve A7 vs signed incident directives, or the CFO’s limited Finance role versus Compliance/SoD.

Net: a competent scaffold with one broken classification, one inverted default rule, misassigned outage evidence, and too little use of INDETERMINATE. Not a collapse, and not close to flawless.
