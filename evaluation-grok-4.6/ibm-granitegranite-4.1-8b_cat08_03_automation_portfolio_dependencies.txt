**4.4**

The answer is complete, well-sectioned, and rhetorically fluent, but it fails the brief on the exact dimensions it is supposed to be judged on: process-level prioritization, dependency accuracy, control literacy, and constraint-respecting portfolio design. The polish conceals material errors.

**Recommendation contradicts the case.** Management was explicitly told frequency is misleading; entity-mismatch (31h wait, 38% exception, critical path in 82% of occurrences), missing-document loops (52% repeat), external valuation (58h), and analyst-normalization load dominate end-to-end performance. The chosen “QuickWin” set (A1, A5, A9, A10) still centers the highest-frequency activity and two downstream closing steps. A5 is a defensible process pick. A1, A9, and A10 are not a process-level first wave. The write-up even cites the “frequency is misleading” line, then automates registration first and defers the critical-path exceptions to year 2 — the outcome the prompt warns against.

**Hard constraint violations.** Budget is “at most four modules in the first year,” yet Phase 3 (months 6–12) adds A4+A6 or A2+A3, i.e., six year-1 modules. That alone makes the rollout internally inconsistent. The plan also treats A9/A10 as light API work after two “deep” integrations; contract generation with control-rule checks is not a shallow add-on. Phase 2 calls A10 “optional” and then counts it as mandatory to fill the four-slot budget.

**Downstream automation without reliable upstream controls.** The prompt forbids activating downstream bots while KYC, decision data, and control completeness are still weak. A9 “depends on clean KYC and decision data”; A10/A11 sit behind KYC clearance; provisional signing is already 6.5%. Automating contract generation and e-sign/reminders in months 3–6, with no KYC, classification, or entity-mismatch work, can accelerate non-compliant signing and dump dirty packs onto Legal — who are already idle when KYC is slow and overloaded at month-end. Calling A10 a control-strengthening module is a stretch; reminder/e-sign adoption does not enforce KYC-before-sign and may worsen provisional-signing leakage.

**Control concepts are repeatedly wrong.** Four-eyes applies to Credit analysis vs Committee packaging on High-exposure SME (5.8% month-end violations). The answer assigns four-eyes to A3, A6, and A10. A6 is statement extraction, not credit analysis. A3 is a KYC hit pre-classifier. A10 is e-sign. In §5, a KYC false negative is defined as “blocked loan / long remediation.” That is a false *positive*. A false negative is a missed hit — the severe outcome the candidate note flags. These are not wording slips; they show the control model is not understood.

**Dependency map has invented or inverted edges.** A4 does not require A3; hit triage and entity-mismatch resolution are different exception classes (DFG: KYC → Resolve entity mismatch 1750; mismatch → KYC 1160; mismatch → contract 380). Collateral need is product-driven (71% equipment, 18% working capital) after credit analysis, not a consequence of A6. A8’s 8.5% “packaging rework” is misquoted — 8.5% is *contract* remediation (14% on credit-line renewal); packaging exception rate is 15%. External wait is 58h in the table and “23h” in the map. Final section inflates Request missing documents from 3300 to “33k.”

**Resource-demand shift is mostly ignored, then contradicted.** The social-network note is explicit: speeding clean inflow into the 9 analyst FTEs overloads them unless normalization (A6) improves. A1+A5 do exactly that and omit A6. Back office is the “most visible queue” but utilization falls below 65% after 15:00, so A1’s labor save is partly political, not capacity-critical. The 3 compliance specialists and two-person handover bottleneck are described, then left out of the recommended wave. Legal month-end bunching after a late KYC release is not modeled. “Additional 23 FTE” is either a catastrophic staffing claim or a hyphen dropped from “2–3.”

**Section 4 invents unsupported precision.** The matrix correctly refuses fake ROI; the effects tables then publish 12–15%, 30–35%, 45% wait cuts, “within 12days,” and similar figures with no throughput, WIP, or little’s-law reasoning. Several claims are directionally implausible (A10 “eliminates” 44h signing wait; A7 “dramatically” cuts an *external* 58h wait; A8 “protects four-eyes”). Concurrent KYC / financial review, committee weekly cadence, product/exposure variants, and the remediate-control loop (650/510) are almost unused.

**Portfolios are labeled, not stress-tested.** The bottleneck set (A4, A6, A7, A8) has no module that clearly “strengthens controls, not only reduce labor,” so it may be non-compliant as a first-year package. It also omits A5, which the text itself says the set needs. Compliance-first (A2, A3, A9, A12) is the most coherent of the three, then is discarded for a politically easier bundle. Trade-off language is present; the trade-off chosen is the one the case asks the analyst *not* to make.

**What remains creditworthy:** required seven-section shape; all twelve candidates tabulated; A5 and A4 correctly flagged as high process leverage; some GIGO caution on A8; audit-trail language; three named portfolios with sacrifice notes. That is competent scaffolding, not a sound recommendation.

Net: a fluent deck that would not survive a process-mining or compliance review. The central choice, the year-1 constraint handling, and the control logic are all wrong.
