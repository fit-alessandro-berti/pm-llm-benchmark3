The answer is structurally complete and directionally aligned with the evaluation emphasis, but it is not close to flawless. **Factual and quantitative defects.** The March median is reported as `+0.5%` versus January. The true change is `31.2 / 31.5 − 1 ≈ −1.0%`. That is not rounding; it invents a deterioration (or at least a non-decrease) in a month whose mapped control-flow and cycle time are essentially unchanged. The service-time share is likewise overstated: Assess service rises by about `0.6h` against a Jan–Nov median increase of `17.5h`, i.e. ~`3%`, not `5–10%`. The L1 distances and most other deltas are correct, which makes the errors more damaging: the quantitative apparatus looks careful and then misstates the one month that is supposed to isolate logging from performance.

The cycle-time decomposition then presents four “shares” that can sum above 100% and are treated as roughly additive, even though later sections correctly insist on mediation (supplier rework × thin staffing × priority). That is not a harmless range; it is an identification claim the aggregates cannot support. No variance, no case weights, no interaction residual.

**Causal timing is softer than the prose suggests.** May already moves median `32.0 → 36.5` and p90 `71 → 84`, with `supplier_z` `18 → 43` and rework `13 → 24`, *before* the 15 June staffing cut.
