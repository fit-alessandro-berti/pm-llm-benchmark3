**7.5**

The final seven-section memo is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the event log. It uses T0–T4 metrics, DFG counts, and conformance figures; it does not treat every deviation as failure; and it correctly refuses to equate throughput, reconstructed scans, or after-the-fact audit work with real-time compliance. The adaptation table is the strongest part of the answer. Several issues keep it well short of a high score.

**Section 3 fails a core requirement.** The prompt asks which adaptations are true resilience, which are risky shortcuts, and which are ambiguous. A, D, E, H, I, and J appear in more than one bucket. Conditional wording does not fix this: the taxonomy is overlapping rather than partitioned, so the central distinction is blurrier than the table in section 2.

**L is misplaced as resilience.** Audit remediation is cleanup. Listing it under “true resilience,” even with a hedge, fights the case facts and the explicit warning that post hoc remediation is not contemporaneous control.

**T4 operational recovery is overstated.** Wholesale-bulk median cycle time is still 58h vs 36h baseline and p90 is 120h vs 72h; pharmacy p90 is 74h vs 49h. Saying throughput “looks close to baseline” is not accurate. Hospital recovery is real; the other segments are not.

**Material DFG structure is under-read.** Temporary release does not replace scanning: 1,450 traces go `Temporary release control -> Dispatch without scan`. More packs skip release (1,800) than receive it (1,650). Dispatch-without-scan inflow (~3,250) exceeds phone POD (2,600), so a large set likely lacked both scan and phone confirmation. About 2,100 T2 cases end in spreadsheet invoice hold. T4 `Proof-of-delivery remediation` (1,200) only sends 800 onward, and compliance-hold incidence roughly doubles versus baseline. These are central to concealed noncompliance and the hidden tail; they are mostly unused.

**Control-gap rates are used without a denominator caveat.** Missing dual-control and missing temperature documentation are reported on all cases. If controlled and cold-chain items are a minority, 2.9% and 4.8% understate the failure rate on the regulated subset.

**Internal design inconsistencies.** Pharmacy fill is targeted at ≥80% in one indicator and a 70% floor in another. “Reconstructed scans = 0” conflicts with later scanning of contemporaneous evidence. Manual-Emergency “restrict to hospital-critical and emergency pharmacy” sits poorly with keeping governed deferral (I) as capacity management. Stabilization p90 targets at 1.25× baseline (hospital ≤19h) are looser than T4 hospital actuals (16h) and are not derived from the log.

**Smaller defects.** Fast-lane “fill 91%” is treated as resilience without a baseline fill rate (the prompt’s commentary supports the reading, but it is still an inference). C’s disposition is a hybrid of retire and modify. Several numeric gates (snapshot ages, max ages, wholesale fill ≥70%) are invented without being labeled as policy choices rather than data.

The recovery model, indicator set, recommendations, and management trade-off are usable and on-brief. The grade is held down by the messy resilience taxonomy, the overstated T4 recovery, and the missed DFG path structure—not by missing sections or wholesale factual invention.
