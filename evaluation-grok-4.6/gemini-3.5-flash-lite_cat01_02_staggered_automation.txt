6.0

This is a competent, well-structured consulting write-up that understands the assignment’s spirit—endogenous staggered rollout, availability ≠ use, concurrent shocks, small-N fragility, and the indefensibility of “automation reduces cycle time by X%.” It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has material identification, quantitative, and reproducibility failures.

**Format and coverage.** The ten required headings are present, tables use the specified columns, and the mandated estimand/mechanism/threat families are mostly named. Section 1 stays descriptive and hits the five requested distinctions. That is the ceiling of the formal compliance. Several hard requirements are then only half-met: Gamma is an adopter and is almost absent from the pre/post evidence; relative-time indicators are built and unused; branch trends are not printed month-by-month; the code does not implement the primary design.

**Estimands (Section 2).** The set is directionally right and the interference/treatment-version note is one of the stronger passages. The rows themselves are sloppy. TOT-01 is labeled both a 100% fast-path contrast and a LATE, which are different objects; 100% is also an extrapolation far outside the observed 25–50% intensity and is process-incoherent because escalation remains a designed path. CEX-01 collapses rework and escalation into one outcome. SAT-01 answers identifiability with “Yes (descriptively), No (causally),” which is not an identification statement. ITT-01’s “Yes (with strong assumptions)” is too generous for five selected branches and a March–May shock bundle. There is no explicit transport estimand for expansion to historically healthier never-treated branches, which is the actual policy contrast.

**Identification (Section 3).** Calling Callaway–Sant’Anna / event study the primary design is conceptually defensible and the TWFE warning is correct. It is not credible as an *implemented* strategy on this panel: two treated cohorts, one late cohort with two post months, two donors. Synthetic control with a two-unit donor pool is a named alternative, not a working one. The IV alternative is not “credible”: adoption date is chosen on poor pre-period performance, so it is not a valid instrument for intensity, and exclusion fails because availability changes routing, escalation, and local work rules independently of `automated_fast_path_pct`. The write-up admits these failure modes and then still treats the menu as a serious identification plan.

**Mechanisms (Section 4).** This is the second-best section. Fast-path substitution, specialist/fraud-desk congestion, learning, case-mix, staffing, rework displacement, tail amplification, and survey selection are all there with the right qualitative signatures. Weaknesses: evidence cells cherry-pick Jan vs Aug for Alpha; they never flag that `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6% when `automation=0`; and several “expected harmful” signatures are then over-attributed to automation in later sections even though the same signatures appear in never-treated units after March/May.

**Quantitative discipline (Section 5) — the largest substantive miss.** Arithmetic for Alpha/Beta Jan–Mar vs Jul–Aug is mostly accurate. The design of the comparisons is not.

- The spec asked for pre/post **for each adopter**. Gamma, the only clean late adopter after the May complexity shock, is omitted. Gamma’s May–Jun to Jul–Aug drop in median and p90 is larger than Delta/Epsilon’s contemporaneous recovery and is the single most informative descriptive contrast in the table.
- Jul–Aug is labeled “steady state” and is the most favorable window for treated medians. April–June (Alpha median 48.3/49.5/53.5, p90 to 119.9) is dropped. That is window shopping.
- Never-treated comparison uses Jan vs Aug endpoints. Epsilon 41.4 → 40.8 hides June 49.4. The dominant pattern is an organization-wide May–June deterioration and a July–August partial rebound, not a treated-only stabilization.
- “Suggesting a protective effect” is causal interpretation inside the facts block.
- The intensity result “−0.8h median and +0.4h p90 per 10ppt, after branch FE and arrivals” is not produced by the code, and the code has **no branch FE**. That is a fabricated specification.
- The p90 bound of “+5% to +12%, robust” is the central empirical error. Raw treated p90 rose, but so did control p90 after the May product change and the March fraud desk. A simple treated-vs-never-treated DiD on the same Jul–Aug window can flip the tail conclusion toward *less* deterioration among adopters, not automation-induced tail inflation. The write-up even cites parallel tail degradation in Delta as counterevidence, then still reports worsening with confidence 75. That is internally inconsistent.

**Threats and effects (Sections 6–7).** The threat list is complete and mostly well motivated. Two directional mistakes matter. Shared fraud-desk interference that worsens controls typically *overstates* treated-vs-control benefits (or understates harm), not the reverse. Survey and aggregation threats are well stated. Effect assessments then overreach: escalation +2.0 to +4.5 ppt at confidence 85 ignores that every branch jumps ~2 ppt in March, before Alpha/Beta adoption and in never-treated units. CSAT “leadership may safely make” language is too strong given 20–45% response with no counts. Median’s −8% to +2% band is more honest than the p90 band.

**Policy (Section 8).** `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the right bucket. Delta-next / Epsilon-holdout, case-level logs, fraud-desk queue instrumentation, and P90/CSAT circuit breakers are sensible. The assignment mechanism is theater: “randomized branch-week” with two remaining branches is not a design. Requiring a “statistically significant” median gain in one newly treated branch contradicts the paper’s own five-cluster warning. Transportability is underplayed: Alpha/Beta were selected as high-volume, historically slow sites, so their ATT is the wrong object for Delta/Epsilon.

**Code (Section 9).** The CSV embedding and clustered OLS skeleton are fine. Failures against the spec: no real panel validation; trends reduced to first-vs-last; pre/post coded as calendar April for all branches, so Gamma’s “post” includes untreated months; `rel_month` never enters a model; both regressions are pooled OLS without branch or month FE, i.e. not the primary staggered design; `warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')`; no p90 model despite numeric p90 claims; causal caveats exist only as end prints, while Section 5 already treats the missing intensity coefficients as results.

**Claim ladder (Section 10).** This is the best short section: the “Not supported” bullet is exactly right, and the observed/suggestive split is fair. The conditionally causal bullet still smuggles a −2 to −4 hour number that was never cleanly identified.

**Net.** The answer knows the vocabulary of modern staggered-adoption evaluation and refuses the single-number leadership ask. It would not survive a methods review: it omits the best adopter contrast, cherry-picks the median window, asserts an unreproduced intensity model, and states a tail-harm conclusion that a control comparison does not support. That combination caps the grade well below the “nearly flawless” range.
