**6.1**

The final write-up has the required nine headings, defines estimands before estimators, and is conceptually stronger than a typical completed-case SLA analysis. It is not close to flawless. Several claims about *this* table are wrong, the process model does not match the log, and the code does not actually estimate either survival estimand.

**What is solid.** Time origin (`Submit`), horizon (72h), and the split among right-censoring, competing terminals, and already-observed non-resolution are stated clearly. The four requested cases (76h resolve, 92h open, 27h withdraw, 68h transfer) are handled correctly under Estimands 1 and 2. Transfers get three distinct assumptions (competing, independent censoring, worst-case), and withdrawal/rejection are not waved through as non-informative censoring. Immortal time and leakage for `Escalate`/`Rework` are identified. Method choice is data-specific (Aalen–Johansen primary; KM rejected; multi-state treated as overparameterized at N=32). Limitations are the right ones.

**Factual errors on the supplied cases.**

- The 72h “known unresolved” lower bound of **8/32 omits S32** (transferred at 96h, therefore still unresolved at 72). That bound is 9/32 if the claim is “observed still unresolved at hour 72.”
- Section 6 treats **S32 as competing B cases out of the 72h risk set**. Transfer is at 96h, so under T1 it does not remove S32 before 72. The 72h transfer that *does* bite is **S08 at 68h (cohort A)**, plus S21 at 26h. The stated cohort-reversal story is therefore mis-aimed.
- **AutoCheck-first list drops S22** (`Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Withdraw`).
- “**3 of 8 P1 cases experienced Transfer or Escalate before 72h**” is false: every P1 prefix contains `Escalate`, and only S08 and S21 transfer before 72 (S32 transfers after).
- Cohort A open-case count is written as “3 … (S09, S10, S11, S15—actually 4).” The correction is right; leaving the contradiction in the finished text is sloppy internal consistency.

**Process-mining defect.** The Mermaid diagram is advertised as capturing observed control flow without inventing transitions, but it **omits several observed edges**: `AutoCheck→Escalate` (S18, S29), `AutoCheck→Resolve` (S01), `Screen→Resolve` (S16), `Screen→Escalate` (S04, S11), `Screen→Reject` (S05, S23), `Screen→Withdraw` (S12). That is a direct failure of prefix-to-model fidelity.

**Section 8 vs. the spec.** The table is complete and open cases are kept. Three transfer encodings exist. But the two estimands are not encoded as distinct analysis objects (Est. 2 is only loosely implied by flags). There is **no implemented CIF/KM/risk-set estimator**—only status counts plus a comment. `T3` in code (`event=5` at transfer time) does not implement the prose rule of forcing a breach at 72 when `t>72`. `format_dataframe(..., timestamp_key="time:timestamp")` with all-`NaT` is likely non-executable; that undercuts the required PM4Py variant/prefix step even though avoiding fake event times is the right instinct.

**Net.** Strong scaffolding and the right survival-analysis instincts, undermined by repeated misreads of the 32-row extract, an incomplete process model, and code that prepares data instead of analyzing it. Under a strict bar that is mid-range, not high.
