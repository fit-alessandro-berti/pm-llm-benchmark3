**6.5**

The answer is structurally complete and avoids the usual failure modes (unlawful shortcuts, treating rescreen/SAR/self-certification as a substitute for second review, ignoring P-901). Conformance labels are used correctly, A/B are treated as hard filters, and the high-level preference for a conformant second-officer path is right. It is not close to flawless. The ranking is built on a broken time budget.

**Central error (ranks 1–2, fallback, §5–6).** Action C states a second-officer review takes 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute. Rank 1 writes “40–80 min total” as if S4’s arrival (35–65 min) *is* the decision. That only works if review+execution after arrival is ~5–15 minutes — i.e., the recording interval, not an independent review of a €480k file with risk 72 and an unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match. The answer never states or defends that assumption; it just elides review duration.

The stated fallback is therefore inoperative. Switching to S2 at t=50 plus a 35–75 minute review finishes at ~90–140 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. You cannot wait until minute 50 to learn that S4 is late and still use S2 to hit the SLA. A coherent plan would *immediately* tighten S4’s ETA and pre-commit to C if that ETA leaves insufficient review slack (on the order of the first 10–20 minutes, not 50). Because this arithmetic is the only reason D-parallel-S4 outranks C, the table order is not earned.

§5 repeats the same mistake: “S4 at 65 min ⇒ 25 min slack” treats arrival as nearly decision time. The directional sensitivity (early S4 ⇒ spare P-901; late S4 ⇒ interrupt) is right; the thresholds are not.

**Other material issues**

- Invented quantities presented as if given: screen-clears “p0.4–0.6”; rank-1 false-reject/hold narrowed to €8k–€40k from the prompt’s €15k–€80k; “S4 almost surely Reject or SAR” if the screen worsens the match. The prompt gives no such probabilities.
- Stale risk score (12 minutes old) is almost unused except as a rerank trigger. It is a live uncertainty on whether rule 6 still binds at the same strength and on whether a refresh should precede any decision.
- Counterfactuals (§2) are compact traces, not the requested distinction among what the process permits, likely continuation, P-884 benefit/harm, spillover, compliance/non-monetary risk, and intervention non-transportability. Transportability is mentioned once; SAR-does-not-authorize-execution is underplayed.
- F above E is poorly justified given P(ultimate approval | conformant review) = 0.55–0.75. Immediate reject is more likely than not a false reject; the low confidence flag does not fix the rank. E is correctly treated as deadline-destructive, but a hold-for-evidence path is not obviously worse than locking in a probable false reject.
- No parallel, non-reliant G inquiry. Director lead time (20–60 min) overlaps S4’s window. Seeking authorization *without* treating override as the plan is an available information option; ranking G only as a standalone disabled path undersells it.
- Rank 1 benefit “€12k–€32k *if* decision before 90 min” is circular: the headline benefit assumes the timing conclusion the plan does not establish.
- Eight-row table (D twice) is allowed in spirit but blurs “rank A–G.” Pure D as a separate rank 3 is nearly dominated by the rank-1 composite and adds little.

**What is actually good.** Exact label set; A/B last with high confidence they are unacceptable; forecasts not allowed to override rules 6–7 and 9–10; F/G gated on missing evidence (rejection threshold; director + incident ID); API quota and P-901 +0.12–0.28 treated as real costs; observational 0.55–0.75 not naively intervened; medium confidence and sensible rerank triggers (S4 ETA, screen return, score/match change).

**Net.** A strong *template* with a wrong operational plan inside it. Under a lenient rubric this might sit around 8. Under the required hypercritical standard, a recommendation whose fallback cannot meet the deadline it is designed to protect cannot score high.
