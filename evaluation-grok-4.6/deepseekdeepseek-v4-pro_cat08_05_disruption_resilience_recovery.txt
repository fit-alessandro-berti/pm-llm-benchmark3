**6.7**

The final write-up is complete, on-format, and conceptually aligned with the brief: it does not treat every deviation as failure, does not equate throughput with resilience, and treats T4 audit work as hidden compliance debt rather than restored control. Those are the main things the task asks for. It is not close to flawless.

**What holds up.** The A–L table is the strongest part. C and G are correctly treated as concealed control loss, B and a bounded I as genuine resilience, and A/H as appetite-dependent. K and L are not confused with operational recovery. The recovery model has the required ingredients (trigger, triage, risk-tiered minimum controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, reconciliation). Leading/lagging indicators cover the mandated dimensions, including fairness. The management statement is decision-usable.

**Material gaps (temporal reasoning and grounding).** T3 is the weakest point and it matters. The log is built as four regimes; T3 is the hybrid state (intake back, inventory sync and scanning unreliable) where concealed noncompliance is most likely. The timeline gives T3 metrics but almost no dominant variants. The recovery model jumps from full manual mode to “systems returned,” with no degraded-mode rules for untrusted scans, dual-write, or when to distrust the restored WMS. That is a design hole, not a polish issue.

Grounding is thinner than the source allows. Move-on-log / move-on-model / temporal-profile deviations are named once and then largely dropped. DFG volumes that would size the argument (1600 skip-auth vs 2500 manual auth vs 1000 fast lane; 1800+1450 into scanless dispatch vs 2600 phone POD; 2300 post-invoice audit cases) are barely used. Claims such as F producing “only 2.9% vs potential much higher” are not in the data.

**Unclarities and small logical slips.** F is marked Keep while the cell specifies new mandatory controls (that is Modify). G is marked Retire while the real proposal is a replacement evidence path. L is sometimes the concealment and sometimes the cleanup of concealment. “Inventory check against snapshot 2h old” can be read as requiring a stale snapshot. Section 1 under-delivers “dominant process variants” for T0, T1, and T3. Compound-word sloppiness (`Hospitalcritical`, `afterthefact`, `highconformance`) is pervasive and makes several cells harder to parse.

**Net.** A competent advisory memo that would pass a lenient rubric in the mid-to-high 8s. Under a strict reading—accuracy, temporal completeness, DFG/conformance use, and consistent keep/modify/retire logic—it is clearly short of excellent.
