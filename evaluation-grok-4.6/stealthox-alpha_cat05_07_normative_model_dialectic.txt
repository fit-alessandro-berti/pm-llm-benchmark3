**7.0**

The legal/dialectical core (sections 1–5) is strong: both briefs concede what they must, expiry and authority are handled as real constraints rather than a compromise, and case labels are mostly evidence-traced. It is not nearly flawless. Special-character stripping, several genuine POWL/DECLARE errors, and a few burden-of-proof slips keep it well below a high band.

**What holds up**

Stipulated vs disputed facts are separated cleanly (P1–P8). The burden rule—noncompliance needs in-force rule, scope, reliable evidence, and no valid overlay; exceptions need contemporaneous written authority—is applied with real bite on SUP-705/714 and real restraint on SUP-709/710.

Prosecution is actually prosecutorial (CS-9 as the only controlled instrument, expiry as the tell, SUP-703 as the catastrophe case, 712/715 as hidden SoD/CoI, 47 unauthorized RMI cycles). Defense is actually defensive (M0 under-encodes CS-9, PD-3/FD-7 as governance acts, CRM missingness as a pipeline signature, trace deviation ≠ misconduct). Each side’s required concession is specific, not ceremonial.

The 702/714 and 704/705 pairs are the right test of overlay discipline, and the judgment does not split them. Rejecting M0-letter enforcement that would paint 702/704 red and 712/715 green is the correct system-level move.

**Material defects**

*POWL does not match the prose or the overlays.* Auto and emergency routes add `act → qual` (and emergency `act → man`). PD-3/FD-7 *permit* deferred quality/approval; they do not require it. A low-risk case that completes Quality_Review before activation cannot replay `auto_route`. The write-up even says the routes “intentionally permit” post-activation quality, then encodes the opposite.

*The M0 reject-after-checks path is gone.* M1’s only rejection child is `Sanctions_Check → Reject_Supplier`. `Register → Sanctions → Quality → Reject → Notify → Close` is a legal M0 trace and a normal business exit; it fits none of the XOR children. Front-loading route choice as XOR of complete recipes also decides auto vs manual vs reject *before* that route’s checks, which is the wrong moment.

*Standard-route sequencing is an unexplained weakening.* M0 has checks *then* the approval decision. M1 makes `checks_std ∥ Manual_Procurement_Approval` with a join only at activation, so an approver can sign before seeing sanctions/quality. CS-9’s letter allows that reading; M0 does not. The change is never justified.

*DECLARE formulas are wrong or self-contradictory.* `responded_existence(Quality_Review, Activate_Supplier)` is “if quality then activate,” which forbids reject-after-quality and does not require quality on activation. The intended constraint is the reverse, plus a time window. `response(Sanctions_Check[clean], Activate_Supplier)` forces activation after a clean check and fights both the reject path and the (correct) precedence constraint sitting next to it. `absence(Register_Supplier, 2)` is nonstandard.

*RMI placement cannot replay the cited “desirable” traces.* LOOP(τ, RMI) between register and routing is disclosed, but it is not the most defensible approximation: SUP-707 is `Sanctions_Check → Request_More_Info* → Quality_Review`. A loop around the evaluation fragment would fit; a prefix-only loop will be scored as non-fitting. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation is never an activity, only a missing-evidence note on SUP-702.

*Burden and labels slip in the margins.* SUP-702 is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at medium-high confidence while a stated PD-3 precondition (master-data validation) is unevidenced—that should cap confidence or yield a narrower label. SUP-713 as COMPLIANT on a mapping approved a week later is generous relative to the same “in-force at execution time” rule used to convict 705/714. Unused `rmi = Transition(...)` is dead code.

**Clarity**

Operators were stripped throughout: `2%29%`, `Trace deviation  business misconduct`, `checks  manual`, `event_ts  overlay window`, `spend  20000`, `requester_actor  approval_actor`, `QA_ClearanceQuality_Review`. In the overlay table those symbols *are* the rules, so the “machine-checkable” artifact is not actually checkable.

**Net**

Case-level reasoning and overlay governance are the high-value part of the answer and would grade higher in isolation. The required model/data revision—the dimension that distinguishes a process-aware review from a policy memo—contains errors a PM4Py/DECLARE-competent reviewer cannot treat as nits. That, plus the encoding failures in the formal table, is why this is 7.0 rather than the 8–9 the briefs otherwise approach.
