**7.5**

The final six-section writeup is substantively strong: it correctly splits April (pipeline/schema), June (routing mix, not concept drift), August–September (overlapping process, concept, and population effects), and October (label-maturity confounding). It does not collapse the year into one drift label or treat October’s 0.16 observed breach as comparable to mature months. That is the core of the task, and it is done with specific numbers and proportionate remediation.

It is not near-flawless. Several real inaccuracies and conceptual slips keep it well below a high score.

**What holds up.** The diagnosis table assigns the right months to pipeline failure, process drift, concept drift (V3 from July), population shift (Partner Z from September), and censoring (mainly October). Within-variant stability through June is used correctly to reject early concept drift. The timeline, competing-hypothesis tests, staged contain/recalibrate/repair/retrain plan, alert-dependency design, and the five failure modes all match the required structure and the main evidence.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Variant-stratified calibration at scoring time is not valid as stated.** The model scores immediately after diagnosis, before `Dispatch` / `Remote Fix` / `Order Part`. Calibrating or applying a variant-specific map at inference leaks the future path. The text never flags this; “variant-path probabilities” appears only later and does not rescue the short-term plan.

2. **October’s mean predicted risk is treated as nearly the true rate.** “The model’s mean pred 0.23 is closer to the eventual truth” is a hypothesis, not an observation. That violates the instruction to separate evidence from speculation.

3. **Test 4’s competing hypothesis is timed incorrectly.** August’s V3 breach (0.64) cannot be tested against a Partner Z population-shift alternative; Partner Z starts in September. The right contrast for August is mix vs process/concept, not population.

4. **Covariate drift is under-specified.** Almost all pre-September mix change is parked under process drift. A six-type audit should say more about whether June’s routing change also shifted *features available at diagnosis* (triage, queue, remote-eligibility), versus only downstream variant frequencies.

5. **Internal tension on July.** July is both “early concept drift, include only if intended” and part of the immediate isotonic pool. Recalibrating on July starts absorbing the new V3 base rate; that is not reconciled.

6. **“Benign” June is too soft.** Observed breach fell because V2 rose; ECE doubled and mean risk stayed ~0.20 vs 0.15. That is a real prior/mix problem, not a non-event.

7. **The PM4Py sketch would not run as written and misuses the performance DFG.** After `format_dataframe`, columns are typically `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp`, so later `activity` / `case_id` / `timestamp` access and `drop_duplicates` are wrong. `discover_performance_dfg` does not return median hours via `.get((src, tgt))`. `get_variants` values are often integer counts, so `len(v)` is wrong. `filter_time_range` is used without a `mode`, so the event-calendar vs case-start distinction is not actually implemented. For a required code block, that is more than cosmetic.

**Smaller nits.** “34–42 h pre-Jun” should be pre-July (June is already 42 h). Several “expected AUROC ~0.81 after fix” lines are over-precise. Immediate “do not naively recalibrate” sits awkwardly next to a June–July recalibration plan.

The analysis would be a clear pass in a normal review. Under a strict reading—correct core diagnosis, but multiple conceptual, evidential, and implementation errors—**7.5** is the right score.
