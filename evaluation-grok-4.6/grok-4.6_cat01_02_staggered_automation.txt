**7.8**

This is a strong, format-complete answer with verified arithmetic and genuine causal-process sophistication. It is not nearly flawless. Several identification, consistency, and specification issues keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is done well.** Section 1 stays descriptive and cleanly separates availability, intensity, targeted selection, co-occurring shocks, and aggregate measurement. The estimand table is the right object: ITT-like availability, endogenous dose, median vs tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, HTE/dynamics, plus a useful IV/exclusion note. SUTVA, routing interference, the shared fraud desk, and bundled treatment versions are named rather than waved away. The primary design (cohort-time ATT / event-study, not naïve TWFE) is the correct default; Gamma as a late, piloted, lower-complexity site is not pooled with Alpha/Beta. Process mechanisms are the best part of the memo: the March +2pp escalation common shock, the adopter-month +1pp escalation step, the first-month rework dip and rebound, AFP already 4–6% pre-go-live, and the raw-vs-DiD sign flip on p90. The pre/post windows are explicit; facts are labeled as facts. Almost every reported contrast I recomputed matches (Alpha/Beta/Gamma raw deltas, never-treated +5.22h / +16.05h, DiDs, Gamma May–Jun vs Jul–Aug, April-only Beta ≈ −1.5h). The recommendation—Epsilon next, Delta as holdout, case- or week-level randomization, no extra routing, desk-level spillover data, hard p90/CSAT/desk-wait stops—is the right joint operations-and-learning policy. The claim ladder correctly refuses “automation reduces cycle time by X%.”

**Identification flaws.** The primary strategy conditions on arrivals/FTE and FTE. Routing is part of the adoption bundle, so load is post-treatment; adjusting for it overcontrols and contradicts the later (correct) remark that the ATT is automation-plus-extra-demand. Alternative B then asks for ignorability of AFP given the same post-treatment load. The 7-hour median bound is not a sensitivity analysis so much as an analogy to the May shock; it is not derived from a specified confounding model. Calling never-treated summer recovery “the” counterfactual for Gamma is stronger than the two post months and the selected May–Jun trough can bear. May–Jun is Gamma’s worst window; using it as the “pre-comparable” period inflates raw improvement even though the DiD partly corrects this. Routing is treated as stealing volume from controls; the prompt only says extra cases were sent to adopters.

**Internal inconsistencies.** Section 7 marks CSAT as not identified (confidence 20) because nonresponse can manufacture the series; Section 10 then puts “no first-order CSAT movement” on the conditionally causal rung. That does not follow. Escalation as a ~1pp availability effect is the cleanest timed association in the panel and deserves the highest confidence, but 55 is still a bit high once fraud-desk composition is unobserved. Median confidence 40 is generous next to the residual bundle the author themselves lists. The April Alpha median dip of 0.6h is inside Jan–Mar noise (46.9–50.4) and should not be used as mechanism evidence.

**Specification and code gaps.** Section 1 is over the 250-word cap; Section 8 is well over 400. Relative-time dummies are built and printed, then never used; the estimated “descriptive panel model” is the TWFE the narrative rejects, plus a two-dummy cohort-post model that is not an event study. `applymap` is deprecated. Control contrasts equally weight Delta and Epsilon despite different volume and mix. The decision rule’s “interval excludes zero after mix and load adjustment” again overcontrols and ignores joint error across median, p90, desk wait, and CSAT.

**Bottom line.** Estimands, process reading, numerical honesty, and the rollout choice are well above average. Post-treatment adjustment, a convenient Gamma window, a CSAT contradiction, unused event-time structure, and word-limit breaches are enough that this is not a 9+. A single X% claim is correctly rejected; a tighter memo would have estimated the design it recommended and kept CSAT off the causal ladder.
