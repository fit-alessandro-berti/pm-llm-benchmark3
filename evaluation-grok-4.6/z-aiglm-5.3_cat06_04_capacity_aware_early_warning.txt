**7.0**

The six-section write-up meets the problem’s quality bar: I-03 is refused on stage/effect grounds despite \(p=0.82\) and €40k; I-05 is suppressed as a stale refresh while I-07 is re-entered on new evidence; allocation is not a \(p_\text{now}\) ranking; observational \(p\) is not treated as an interventional effect; calibration, false-alarm burden, and spillovers appear in the rule and the impact table. That is why the score is not in the 4–5 range. It is not higher because the rule and the chosen portfolio are not internally consistent, several “hard” gates are invented and then applied as if they were constraints, and some impact arithmetic is less auditable or less conservative than the prose claims.

**Decision rule.** The structure (stage/runway/repeat/data gates → segment \(p_\text{adj}\) → credibility-weighted effect → EV minus cost, false-alarm burden, and a spillover shadow price \(\lambda\)) is the right kind of object. The observational/causal distinction is stated cleanly, and \(\min(\delta, p_\text{adj})\) is a correct cap. Deployability is weaker than advertised. \(\lambda \in\) €1,200–2,400 rests on an unstated €20k average for six unnamed diagnosis-stage cases. \(p_\text{fail}\approx 0.1\)–\(0.3\) is not a case-level procedure. False-alarm burden is subtracted as a separate term *and* folded into a driver-failure mode, so the score is not a single expected-value expression. Tier 1 is an OR of “clears \(\lambda\) under a conservative score” and “expiring option with scenario-weighted EV near \(\lambda\)”—an escape hatch that later admits cases the robust rule would reject. The ~3 h Triage / ~2 h Diagnosis-Started runway floors are policy inventions, not implied by the 45-minute swarm.

**Allocation.** The table is complete and the 3+1 split matches both management preference and the claim that the fourth case fails \(\lambda\). I-06 as a data-integrity hold, I-08 as wait-for-information (high value, weak effect, €5k false-alarm burden, 8 h runway), and I-04 as high-EV with deferred execution are sound.

The live contradiction is I-02 and I-09. Their own conservative scores (~€1.6k and ~€1.4k) are below \(\lambda\), yet both are “intervene now.” That is expected-value close-calling dressed as robustification. After the NNLS 0.6 haircut, I-09’s mid EV collapses to roughly I-01’s, while I-01 is Public Sector with a clean +0.19 jump and a usable 7 h option to wait; taking I-09 then needs the exploration/fairness argument, which is mentioned only as a side note. I-07’s “Eligible now? No” is overstated: short runway reduces *effectiveness* of a still-eligible Triage swarm, it does not trigger the problem’s stage gate. Serial execution (two specialists jointly, one swarm at a time) is a reasonable reading but is treated as fact.

**Impact table.** Optimistic/base/adverse rows, visible \(\sum \delta_i V_i\), and 3×€450 costs are auditable. Treating “breaches avoided” as \(\sum\delta\) (0.74 / 0.58 / 0.20) rather than counting cases is correct. Gaps: Section 2 discounts SE/NNLS effects; Section 3 uses raw range points—the two sections do not describe the same portfolio. False-alarm €0.3k / €0.9k / €2.4k is not derived in the final text. Adverse spillover of €9–13k stacks \(\Delta p=0.03\) on six cases *and* 15–30 min delay; those are likely the same congestion channel. They never state the count implication of their own base math: 0.58 direct minus \(3\times6\times0.02=0.36\) spillover ≈ **0.22 net breaches**, so the economic case is almost entirely I-04’s value, not fewer breaches. In adverse, spillover expected counts exceed direct effect (net *increase* in breaches). A 25/50/25 blend yielding ~€9k is fine as illustration, not as a calibrated posterior.

**Temporal policy.** Cool-down, suppression ≠ ineligibility, and the I-05 vs I-07 contrast are the right distinctions. The general rule that three consecutive elevations earn a tier bump sits badly next to “I-05: third alert, no escalation.” Either “action threshold” is a hidden score cut that should have been specified, or the I-05 treatment contradicts the rule.

**Fairness and monitoring.** All five required risks have operational safeguards (value floor, NNLS exploration cap, calibration layer, treated-label handling, spillover DID/credits). That is well above a principles dump. Weak points: a 10% randomized no-swarm holdout on high-stakes SLA work is proposed without a real ethics/power bound; weekly segment DID on four slots/day is almost certainly underpowered; conflict between the EV ranker and the 20–25% equity floor is unresolved.

Net: a strong, on-spec policy memo with real decision-theoretic content, undermined by a robust-looking rule that does not govern the two contested slots, a few invented hard gates, and impact numbers that are only partly the same model as the allocation.
