**5.8**

The write-up hits the main qualitative marks the rubric cares about: I-03 is refused on stage grounds, I-05 is suppressed as a refresh artifact while I-07 is treated as an evidence-changing re-entry, effects are not treated as known causal quantities, calibration is segment-specific, and spillover is in the objective. The six required sections are present and mostly operational. That is not enough for a high score. The deployable rule is internally inconsistent, the allocation violates the rule that was just stated, and there are material arithmetic and decision-theoretic errors on the cases that actually matter.

**The decision rule cannot be executed as written.** Eligibility says a second alert with no prior swarm is eligible. The novelty modifier then sets `NH = 0.0` (suppress) for “a repeated alert with no new event and no prior swarm.” I-02 and I-09 are exactly that case. The paper still assigns I-02 a slot with `NH = 1.0`. An operator following §1.1 and §1.4 would not reach the same action. The core priority formula also overloads `p_adj` for both causal risk reduction and the false-alarm probability; the table uses two different quantities, the formula text does not. That is not a deployable scoring rule.

**Allocation contradicts the stated ranking rule.** After filters, cases are to be taken by `PS_adjusted` descending, subject to a marginal spillover test. I-08 is scored at €3,275 versus I-01 at €1,478, yet I-01 is swarmed and I-08 is waited. Waiting I-08 can be defended as option value (8 h, Triage, thin effect range, €5,000 disruption), but that override is not in the rule. The marginal spillover test is announced and then never applied to the live book. Under the paper’s own spillover arithmetic (~€1,440 per slot), I-08 still clears the test more comfortably than I-01. I-09 at Diagnosis Started with 2.5 h left is marked WAIT; that is a process-risk the rule does not price. The reserve is reasonable; the ranking that feeds it is not.

**Arithmetic is wrong on the binding cases.** I-08: `0.075 × 100,000 − 450 − 0.655 × 5,000 = 3,775`, not `3,275`. The reported figure is the false-alarm term, not the net. That error understates the case they chose to bench. I-06: `0.191 × 15,000 − 450 − 144 = 2,271`, not `2,121`. I-09: `0.156 × 20,000 − 450 − 248 = 2,422`, not `1,882` (so the DQ-scaled score is not €941). I-04 is €6,890 in §2 and €6,920 in §3. Optimistic spillover is defined as 0.005–0.015 per slot and then computed at 0.01. “Direct economic benefit” is already net of swarm and false-alarm cost, while a separate intervention-cost column sits unused in the net; the table is not cleanly auditable.

**Two conceptual mistakes are serious.** First, spillover is described as something that “would also occur at lower magnitude even without intervention, since congestion exists.” Swarm spillover is caused by taking specialists off the diagnosis queue; it is not a background congestion term. Second, inaction on I-02 is costed at `0.575 × €25,000 = €14,375` and compared to a €2,120 intervention EV. The relevant quantity is the *preventable* slice, `Δp × V`, which they had already computed. Comparing full expected breach cost to incremental intervention value is not a valid argument that “opportunity cost of inaction supports the intervention.”

**What remains good is real, but it does not offset those failures.** Calibration and causal discounts are directionally right even if the 0.65–0.85 factors are invented to two decimals. The I-05 / I-07 contrast and the cool-down / re-entry logic are the strongest operational pieces. Fairness and monitoring are unusually concrete (value cap, equity tracking, label contamination, RD / slot-scarcity designs). Those sections are why this is not in the mid-4s. They cannot raise the score further while the scoring rule disagrees with itself, the live allocation disagrees with the scoring rule, and the pivotal I-08 number is simply wrong.
