**6.5**

The answer is complete, well structured, and hits every required heading. It separates hard compliance rules from preference trade-offs, does not claim a unique optimum, and is generally aligned with the socio-technical brief. It is not close to flawless.

The most serious technical error is in the scoring formulas. As written, `workload_score` and `resilience_score` are `1 - a + b + c + d` rather than `1 - (a + b + c + d)`. Because `hour_load`, overtime penalty, batch load, queue pressure, and specialist-utilization risk are all “bad” terms, the published formulas reward overload and bottleneck concentration. That is not a wording quibble: an implementer following the pseudocode would invert two of the policy’s stated objectives.

The recommended queue also fights the historical evidence the answer itself cites.

- **C001** (urgent, 3h left, complexity 0.88, “needs specialist”): assigning skill-1 R3 as performer is allowed under the Security rule, but the answer’s own statistic is that keeping a weak owner adds a median 3.3h of service time. That is incompatible with a 3h SLA. Supervision by R8 does not cancel that. Constraint 5 prefers continuity when supervision is feasible, but a competent policy would treat predicted SLA miss as an override, not just a “risk to monitor.”
- **C003** (repeated diagnosis loop, already 2 reassignments): giving it to trainee R1 creates the >2-reassignment state the answer says multiplies rework and appeals. That is a poor case to burn on the 20% training quota when **C009** already covers diagnosis training for the same person. The trade-off is disclosed, but it is still a bad trade.
- **Hours remaining are not checked.** R6 gets two urgent, high-complexity cases (C002 at 2h SLA and VIP C011 at 4h) plus C010 on 6.5h. R3 gets urgent supervised Security plus high-complexity Rework plus C009 supervision on 6.0h. R5 gets high Negotiation, high-complexity Diagnosis, and C003 supervision on 5.0h. R8 supervises an enterprise Security review while also taking C014 and C015 on 5.5h. The policy talks about hours; the table does not show the work fits.

Smaller but real gaps:

- The 5% training weight would not produce C003/C009; those look like a post-hoc patch, and C010/C012 (explicit training notes) are unused.
- Section 4 never instantiates first-available or specialist-only on this snapshot, so the comparison stays generic.
- Weights are presented as precise without justification.
- The PM4Py sketch covers the requested functions, but `rework_rate_by_skill` is not a rate, several APIs are guessed, and activity names (`Technical Diagnosis`) are not reconciled with skill keys (`Diagnosis`).

What keeps the score from falling further: hard constraints are mostly respected (skill 0, Security/Approval rules, high-OT avoidance, batch cap, 22% training share, continuity on C007/C016), the worker explanation is usable and not punitive, and the audit-indicator list is thorough. Those are necessary, not sufficient, for a high score.
