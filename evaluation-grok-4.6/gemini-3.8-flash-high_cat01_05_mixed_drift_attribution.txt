**6.2**

The answer is complete, well-structured, and substantively aligned with the intended diagnosis (March semantic/logging shock versus later real operational drift; supplier rework; staffing queueing; priority tails; seasonal mix). That is not enough for a high score. Under a strict reading it has repeated causal overreach, manufactured parameters, and several process-mining errors that a competent analyst should not make.

**Causal restraint.** The problem states that the evidence is non-unique and that there is no hidden correct decomposition. The answer repeatedly claims alternatives are “refuted,” calls the May shift “definitively driven” by Supplier Z, and assigns evidence strengths of 86–95 to mechanisms that are only identified from monthly aggregates plus a known timeline. September residual rework, December improvement, and the July–August p90 explosion all have overlapping explanations (backlog, mix, staffing, priority, seasonality, interventions). Treating them as settled is a core failure on the stated rubric.

**Manufactured precision.** The aggregates cannot support \(\rho \approx 0.95\), Kingman’s \(G/G/c\), a 2.0h complexity differential, or “~6.0h extra” dual-assessor senior review. Assessor headcount is never given, so utilization is unidentified. The cycle-time split then presents a residual (everything not assigned to service/rework/mix) as if queueing were measured at 78–88%. The timeline’s “queue wait accounts for ~88% of delay” also conflates share of the *increase* with share of delay. “Throughput variance increases” is not in the data.

**Process-mining errors.** Duplicate `Open Case` events are log moves against a model that does not contain that activity, not “minor silent transitions.” Silent transitions are tau/model moves. \(L_1\) on seven selected rates is not “an exact lower bound on the full graph edit distance”; GED is a structural edit metric, and omitted-edge assumptions do not convert a partial rate vector into GED. The October `Assess -> SeniorAssessment` jump (7 to 22 per 100) far outpaces the high-complexity share change (26% to 41%). Given the prompt’s warning that senior assessment was historically logged inconsistently, that jump needed a logging/semantic alternative; it is treated as almost pure case-mix.

**Weaker quantitative and timeline choices.** January–February rows in the baseline-delta table are noise. The second logging “change point” (2026-07-01) is only the peak of a March step-change plus slow missingness creep. “Stable intake mix” in June–August is false (Supplier Z 38–46%, complexity still rising). December’s 8pp rework drop is attributed to the quality campaign despite simultaneous drops in volume, complexity, and Supplier Z. Ranking “pure logging-semantic migration” first among *competing* hypotheses for the whole phenomenon is misleading; it explains March, not May–November.

**Monitoring design.** The proposed “primary monitoring population” (non-Z, standard complexity, no duplicates, non-null resource) is a useful *control* slice, not the primary operational population. It would miss the supplier shock, seasonal complexity surge, and priority effects that the diagnosis itself treats as central. Decision rule 4 (`P90/median > 2.5`) never fires on the observed crisis (August 2.44, November 2.47). Part A’s “change-point” rule compares every month to the January–February baseline, so March–December are almost all flagged as logging/rework/congestion; that is a control chart against a fixed baseline, not onset detection.

**What remains good.** Section 2’s conceptual point is right: mapped fitness recovery does not prove the business process was unchanged, and post-May mapped-fitness decline plus rework/DFG/cycle-time movement cannot be logging alone. DFG \(L_1\)/\(L_2\) arithmetic is correct. The multiple-mechanism-specific change-point argument is the strongest part of the paper. Discrimination and segmentation tables use the right artifacts (DFGs, variants, alignments, temporal profiles, skeletons, DECLARE) and name real confounders (non-random Supplier Z assignment, missing resources, July 1 overlapping June 15). The monitor set covers the required surfaces, and seasonality/FDR/hierarchical gating are directionally sensible.

Those strengths make this a competent draft, not a near-flawless analysis. The decisive deductions are causal overclaim, invented queueing parameters, and avoidable PM mischaracterizations.
