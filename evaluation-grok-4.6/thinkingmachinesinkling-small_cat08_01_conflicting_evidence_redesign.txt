7.2

The final write-up is a strong, artifact-grounded advisory piece, not a generic Lean/Six Sigma list. Diagnosis, path differentiation, and trade-off language are clearly above typical answers. It is not nearly flawless. Several design and quantitative mistakes are material under a strict reading.

**What is actually good**

Section 1 does the job the prompt asked for. It names the frequency DFG volumes (3,600 auto, 2,550 fast approval, 2,300 dispatch), the performance DFG times, token replay (fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces), alignment deviations on R1–R3, the temporal-profile link from dispatch to complaint, the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split, the 31% / 9% pre- vs post-rework cost finding, segment complaint rates, and the reviewer 82% cliff. The contradiction is stated as heterogeneity hidden inside a high-frequency cheap path, not as “fast is bad.” That is real process-mining reasoning.

A / B / C are different mechanisms, not parameter tweaks: conformance-hard routing plus a pre-dispatch gate; fulfillment-mode switch to repair; loop/bottleneck attack with portal, triage, and sampling. Sacrifices are named. The table does not invent ROI or declare strict Pareto winners. Failure modes point to plausible bottleneck migration. The experiment sequence (learn inflow elasticity, then harden rules, then change fulfillment) is coherent.

**Where it breaks**

Redesign B is the largest substantive error. It routes “high-value device, Premium, or unclear defect” and then adds 14% + 21% + 22% as if those sets were the intended scope. Unclear / missing-evidence cases are an evidence-completion problem (140 h median, repeated requests, abandonment), not a repair-quote problem. Sending that cohort to Repair quote skips the activity the log says they actually need. The same paragraph also implies a volume shift on the order of half the caseload onto three technicians whose current repair-device throughput is a few hundred cases, not thousands. Failure modes later admit overload; the redesign itself does not size or stage the shift. That is incomplete operational logic, not a minor caveat.

Several numbers are used more tightly than the prompt supports. The table’s “–30–50 h” for Redesign C treats a 30–45% reduction in *repeated evidence requests* as a 30–50 hour cycle-time cut. Those are different quantities. Phase 3’s stop rule comparing service-credit cost to “EUR 410 replacement-error savings” treats an expected cost *of a wrong replacement* as if it were per-case savings. R1 is a EUR 400 threshold, repeatedly collapsed into “high-value device.”

There is a clear misread of the Quality check resource. “345 close vs 2,320 dispatch close” is used as evidence the inspectors are idle. Those are different fulfillment paths; dispatch typically never enters Quality check. Underuse is in the resource note, not in that edge comparison.

The experiment plan undercuts its own first pilot. A portal built to break missing-evidence loops is piloted on Standard low-risk cases *with complete evidence*, which is the cohort least able to show a 26% second-request effect. Redesign C’s flow says high-value cases get mandatory Manual/Fraud review, then the sacrifices paragraph says C “does not fully enforce R1/R2 by structural design.” Those two claims cannot both be right. R3 wording in B and C is also sloppy (“only after first reopening,” escalation as a substitute path).

Customer-experience scoring is inconsistent with the same artifacts the diagnosis uses. Premium cases have strict expectations and, for low-risk Premium, credit exposure above 24 hours. Calling Redesign B’s 96–120 h repair default “excellent” CX for high-value Premium overweights the 5% complaint rate and underweights wait-time and credit risk. The sacrifices paragraph is more honest than the table.

Smaller issues: A and C both lean on the portal, so C is less cleanly distinct than advertised; A’s “skip per R2 but include a basic check” is internally muddy; Monday/campaign fraud peaks and Manual → Repair quote slowness for Premium are cited and then mostly left undesigned.

**Net**

This is a defensible, non-generic synthesis with a high-quality diagnosis and three recognizably different bets. It would be in the 8–9 band if B did not mis-route the unclear segment, if C’s hour savings were not invented, and if the first pilot tested the loop it claims to fix. Under a hypercritical bar those are not nits. **7.2.**
