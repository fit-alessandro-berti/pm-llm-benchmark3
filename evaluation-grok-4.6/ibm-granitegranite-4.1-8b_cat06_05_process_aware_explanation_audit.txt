The answer is structured correctly, catches the obvious post-closure leakages, and does not treat SHAP importance as causation. It is still well below a high score: it misclassifies several future process patterns as admissible at `t0`, invents lineage and case facts, and then writes revised narratives that reuse leaked outcomes.

**Section 1 — coverage is broad, taxonomy is sloppy, and several calls are wrong**

The table hits every required global feature, sequence pattern, and local-only field, and the critical calls on `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, and `last_resource_role` are right. That is the floor, not a distinction.

Serious errors:

- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is labeled “temporal leakage (acceptable).” It is not leakage. It is the prefix-age / remaining-slack signal the prompt explicitly asks to preserve. Calling a valid past-only timestamp feature leakage collapses the taxonomy.
- Three of five sequence patterns are marked acceptable at `t0`: `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`, `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`, and `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`. Immediately after the first coverage check, the prefix is `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`. Evidence collection, damage assessment, and settlement have not occurred. Those patterns are complete-trace / future-event features, not “process-state (acceptable).” This is the central process-awareness failure.
- `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is treated only as an order reversal to be flipped and re-signed. At `t0` *both* activities are still future. Same-timestamp inversion is an additional data-quality defect, not a reason to keep a reversed future pattern with the opposite SHAP sign.
- `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically legitimate after Partial/Deny (unlike the closed-before-assessment pattern), but it is still unavailable at `t0`. The remediation “Appeal Opened before `t0`” is process-impossible: appeal is not enabled until after a Partial/Deny decision. The answer therefore fails the required distinction between a legal later fragment and an impossible ordering artifact.
- `initial_reserve` is declared critical leakage because “reserve is set after assessment.” No lineage note says that. “Initial” reserve is commonly captured at registration. Inventing a post-assessment story and then deleting the feature is an unsupported audit finding.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is described as including claims that may *close* after `t0`. The SQL defect is `claim_created_at <= extraction_date`, i.e. claims *created* between `t0` and extraction. Wrong mechanism, right severity.
- `final_handler_team` remediation allows a “forward-lookahead field if timestamped.” A lookahead of the closing team is still leakage.
- `calendar_week` is sent to a fairness review. It is a calendar/policy-drift confounder, not a protected-class proxy. Region belongs in fairness review; week does not, at least not under the same standard.
- `queue_length_at_t0` at Medium with “no remediation” is slightly over-severe for the best-justified congestion feature (±5 min join tolerance is Low).
- Duplicate `claimant_channel` row and a ghost “Appeal pattern not shown for Z” row are sloppy.

**Section 2 — right checklist, weak process semantics**

The six required checks appear, and the note that a DFG edge is not a hard rule is in the right direction. Limitations are thin and sometimes harmful:

- Mined DFG, “RFC,” and the normative model are conflated. A frequent DFG edge can be a logging artifact (exactly the same-timestamp closed/assessment pair).
- Alphabetical tie-breaking on simultaneous events would *manufacture* `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. That is a bad control.
- “Enabled vs future-possible” is described but not applied: at `t0`, Documents Requested / fast-track assessment / possibly fraud are enabled or soon-enabled; settlement, appeal, and close are only future-possible; close-before-assessment is impossible. The later sections ignore this.
- Alignment, footprint, prefix-language, and declarative (existence/response/not-succession) checks are named only in passing or not at all, so the suite is a feature-filter list rather than a process-conformance design.

**Section 3 — classes exist; membership and claim-boundaries fail**

The five required classes are present, and the answer correctly refuses to delete `claimant_region`. Then it undoes the taxonomy:

- After repair, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` and a timestamped team-at-`t0` become *prefix* features. The text says repaired features “remain postmortem” and “cannot appear in any early-time explanation.” That contradicts the class name and the lineage notes.
- Prefix `full_trace_variant_id` and filtered `num_document_requests_total` are treated as useful process-state signals. After coverage check, almost every case shares the same prefix (`REG-COV`), and document requests have typically not started, so both are near-constant at `t0`.
- Queue length is banned from any causal reading. Congestion is the *most* intervention-plausible signal here. The right boundary is “SHAP ≠ causal effect; action needs an intervention model,” not “observed states cannot be drivers.”
- `initial_reserve` is simultaneously a critical future leak (Section 1) and a fairness proxy “if not at t0” (Section 3). Inconsistent.

Predictive vs causal vs actionable vs fairness is stated in the abstract and then violated in the case write-ups.
