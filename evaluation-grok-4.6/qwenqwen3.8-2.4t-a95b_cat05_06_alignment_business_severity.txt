**7.5**

The final write-up follows the required headings, covers all 12 cases, separates technical cost from business risk, and is generally well reasoned. It is not near-flawless. Several policy and consistency errors keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is strong**

The materiality framework correctly treats severity and confidence as different axes and uses the requested dimensions (control objective, exposure, reversibility, timing, harm, recurrence, evidence quality, model scope). Silent `tau` on CL-605 is identified as fitting. CL-609 and CL-610 are correctly called out as zero-cost, fully synchronous policy failures. Missing events are not treated as proof of non-occurrence. Patterns match the extra monthly evidence (retry connector, external-adjuster gap, label mapping, out-of-scope denial/withdrawal, data-aware auto-approval). Remediation covers investigation, payment gating, data-aware rules, evidence linkage, log hygiene, and model-scope repair. Section 6 uses more than five cases and hits cost-function design, scope, hidden moves, data conditions, label granularity, telemetry, external evidence, and irreversibility. The PM4Py block hits the required API steps and keeps `raw_cost` and `business_severity` separate.

**Material errors and imprecision**

P3 is misapplied to **CL-602**. P3 is a sequencing rule: *Pay Claim before the applicable approval*. The supplied alignment is `S(Manual Approve) → S(Pay Claim)`. The failure is a missing `Fraud Screen` under P2, plus weak verbal evidence—not pay-before-approval. CL-604 is the actual P3 case. Calling CL-602 a P3 failure blurs the two most important control incidents.

**CL-609 vs CL-610** are both scored 4, which flattens a real difference. CL-610 is a P1+P2 fraud-relevant bank-change bypass. CL-609 is an 18k MEDIUM auto-approval that violates P1, but P2 does *not* force Fraud Screen + Manual Approve (not >50k, not HIGH, no bank change). The P1/P2 gap is never stated. That is a context-sensitive miss.

**CL-608** at severity 3 is high relative to P4. Notification at +3 hours in CRM/portal is inside the 24-hour window; the defect is a mutable link, not a missed control. Low confidence is right; severity 3 overweights evidence weakness on a completed high-value payment.

**CL-612 amount is wrong in the code** (`amount_eur: 0` vs EUR 2,000 in the narrative). That breaks the aggregation the question asked for. **CL-607** invents `risk: LOW` with no basis.

Payment-gating language (“unless required Fraud Screen and Manual Approve are recorded”) can be read as blocking the legitimate Auto Approve path. Priority 3 only partly repairs that.

**P6** is stretched onto withdrawals. P6 names denied pre-screen leads and re-opened claims, not withdrawals. The model-scope point is fair; the policy citation is not.

**Net**

This is a complete, usable analysis with the right structure and the right big picture (cost ≠ risk; zero-cost policy failures; telemetry vs control failure). Hypercritically, the P3 error on the largest case, flattened 609/610 ranking, CL-608 over-score, and the CL-612 amount bug are enough to deny anything near 9–10.
