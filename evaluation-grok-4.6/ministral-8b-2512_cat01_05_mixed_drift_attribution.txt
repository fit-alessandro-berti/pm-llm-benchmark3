**3.8**

The answer has the right skeleton and the right headline story (March is mostly semantic, later movement is real and multi-causal). Under a strict reading it is still a weak submission: it misses hard requirements, reverses or invents several quantities, and then builds monitors and change-point rules on those errors.

**Format and coverage.** Headings match. Section 2 is about 130–160 words against a 180–280 requirement, and it is the section that most needed careful prose. Logging drift is given only one change point; the prompt asked for at least two in each of logging, behavioral, and performance. Part A was required to *embed* the monthly and DFG tables; it reads nonexistent CSVs instead.

**Temporal synthesis.** Distinct shocks are collapsed. May 5 is a sudden supplier break (`supplier_z_pct` 18→43, `Assess→RequestInfo` 12→24) and is labeled gradual. June staffing, the July 1 priority rule, the September Z recovery (`Z` 38→24, rework 27→20, p90 110→96), October seasonality, and the December intervention are not cleanly separated. The October–December row treats recovery as one mechanism, even though the prompt says December mixes load drop *and* overtime/campaign. January–February stability is never established as the reference regime.

**Quantitative failure.** This is the largest defect.

- The “baseline-relative” table mixes January deltas with sequential deltas and gets the arithmetic wrong. March median cycle is 31.2 vs 31.5 (−0.3 h), not +0.7 h. July is +12.7 h vs January, not +8.4 h. October high-complexity is +20 pp vs January; December is +13 pp, not −7. `supplier_z` in October is +12 pp vs January, not −18.
- Reported Euclidean DFG distances (0.12 / 0.25 / 0.18 / 0.30) are not recoverable from the given rates on either a 0–100 or 0–1 scale. January vs March on the *normalized* fragment is tiny (order 1–2 points). Attributing that distance to “Eligibility Screen emergence” is incoherent: the fragment is already label-mapped.
- The cycle-time split (case mix 30%, rework 40%, service 15%, queueing 15%) contradicts the only service/queue evidence in the prompt: Assess service moves 4.8→5.4 h while reconstructed queueing “increased much more.” Queueing is understated; point percents are manufactured precision.

**Process-mining and causal restraint.** “Eligibility Screen mislabeled as Validate Eligibility” is backwards. Senior Assessment is treated as if it were the priority class; the July rule reorders the queue, it does not by itself create the October 9→22 Senior edge. That edge tracks `high_complexity_pct` (28→41) and the known historical under-recording of Senior Assessment—so it is population plus possible semantic cleanup, not a clean behavioral jump. Supplier-driven rework is called “directly attributable” with no Z-stratified DFG, only an ecological coincidence. “Not all years show this [seasonal] pattern” is invented; the prompt says the opposite. Evidence scores of 90–95 are too high for deliberately non-unique aggregates. Section 2’s “median_cycle_h stagnation” misreads the note about *service* time.

**Discrimination and segmentation.** The Z vs non-Z limitation “small sample for non-Z” is inverted (non-Z is the majority). High vs low complexity lists “takes longer” as a *behavioral* signature; that is the population signature. The primary monitoring population is chosen as post-May Z and post-October high-complexity cases—the mediator/collider slices. That maximizes conditioning bias. The primary frame should be the full case population (or a pre-declared stable core), with Z and complexity as diagnostic strata.

**Monitoring and rules.** Several thresholds are dead on arrival: mapped fitness never falls below 0.86, so `<0.85` never fires; January p90 cycle is already 69 h, so “cycle_time > 50 h (P90)” is always true; “Assess duration P90 > 70 h” confuses a ~5 h service time with end-to-end cycle time. Rule 6 tests `raw > mapped` by 0.1, which never occurs, and then interprets a *logging* gap as behavioral drift. Rule 7 is a one-off December retrospective, not an operational rule. Rolling 12-month seasonal baselines cannot be formed from a single year. Bonferroni on two of ten monitors is not a multiple-testing design. Duplicate `Open Case`, `fast_variant_pct`, supplier mix, and a clean service-vs-queue pair are missing.

**Code.** Wrong or non-existent PM4Py paths (`pm4py.statistics.patterns.discovery.get_variants`, `get_temporal_profile`, `format_dataframe(..., parameters={...})` without `activity_key`). Required names `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.get_variants`, and `pm4py.discover_temporal_profile` are not used. `iloc[2]` is labeled as March. The change-point rule `mapped < 0.8` finds nothing. `f"{month}-31"` is invalid for several months. `import pm4py` is missing where `pm4py.*` is called.

The qualitative plot—March provenance, May rework, June queues, July priority redistribution, October mix, December mixed recovery, no single global change point—is roughly what a good answer should say. Almost every place the write-up had to be exact, it is not.
