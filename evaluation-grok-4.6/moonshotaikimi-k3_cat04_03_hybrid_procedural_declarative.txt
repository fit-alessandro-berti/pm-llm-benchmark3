7.1

The deliverable is a complete, well-structured hybrid model that hits every required heading, surfaces the R5/R6 conflict instead of papering over it, and gives a coherent layered story for T1–T8. It is not close to flawless. Several artifacts would mis-fire if treated as executable PM4Py, and a few claims about the APIs are overconfident.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 is the right shape: explicit assumptions, three resolutions, a chosen policy (B), and a real compliance risk (authorization lives off-log). The mapping table covers R1–R12 and, importantly, refuses to stuff ternary/resource rules into binary DECLARE. The process tree is a legitimate design: three full outcome branches avoid the RV-then-AV correlation bug; the clarification loop is a disclosed block-structured approximation; R5/R6 is left *neutral* in the tree rather than silently compiled as Legal-only. The DECLARE dictionary uses the requested key shape, sticks to templates the author can explain, and the response/precedence, alternate/chain, and noncoexistence/nonsuccession distinctions are correct (T8 really does require noncoexistence, not a single nonsuccession). Temporal arithmetic is right: T7’s 34h vs 12±2·6 is the intended warning; T6’s 10h emergency window is handled as a hard 24h check, not as ζ. The verdict matrix is the best part of the answer: layered pass/fail, T6 under both resolutions, earliest decisive events called out.

**Log skeleton is the largest technical miss.** The six keys are populated, but the author asserts “PM4Py subset-inclusion / whitelist” as if that were the official meaning of `conformance_log_skeleton`. That is not a safe claim. Under the usual *constraint* reading (pairs in the model must hold in the trace), putting both `(CMD,RSS)` and `(RSS,CMD)` in `always_before`, and almost every activity pair in `equivalence`, is contradictory or immediately fatal. Under a discover-trace-then-difference reading, the giant equivalence/always_before sets become a mechanical necessity—but then they are barely constraints at all, which sits badly next to “populate only constraints you can defend.” Either way, the skeleton is not a clean normative model.

Worse, `activ_freq` omits `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification`. If PM4Py treats a missing activity key as a deviation (a common pattern), T2 fails the skeleton for a bookkeeping reason, not a policy reason. A defensible model would have given those labels an explicit unbounded (or generously bounded) count set. `never_together` is the one fragment that is clearly right; it is overloaded with extra structural pairs that duplicate the tree.

**Executable-layer bugs.** The R3 backstop is inverted. `all(tx2 >= rc …)` is the *success* condition (answer before outcome) used as a failure test; run as written it would fail T2. `g.attrs` is not how case attributes appear in PM4Py event logs. The four-eyes note hedging on `positive` is honest but leaves the snippet non-authoritative. Duplicate labels (`Obtain Legal Opinion` in three slots, second `Verify Bank Account`) are workable for alignments after Petri-net conversion, but the answer understates that some process-tree algorithms assume unique labels.

**Smaller modeling nits.** The tree still admits `Sanctions Escalation` then `Manager Approval` (and opinion without escalation); that is disclosed, but it makes the “procedural pass” column weaker than the backbone narrative suggests. T5 temporal as `pass` is generous; `not evaluated` on the incomplete RQ→RC pair would have been more precise. `succession(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` is an extra obligation (A5) that is defensible but will fail any activate-then-abandon case that is not in the test set.

**Net.** This is a strong exam answer: conflict-aware, layered, and internally consistent *if you grant the author’s skeleton semantics*. It is not nearly flawless. A score in the high 8s–9s would require a sparse, constraint-oriented skeleton that matches PM4Py’s checker, complete `activ_freq`, and backstop code that does not invert R3.
