**5.5**

The write-up follows the required seven-section shape and is stronger than a frequency-first plan: it treats entity-mismatch as a critical-path blocker, flags analyst overload if inflow is cleaned without normalization, keeps A11 off the first wave, and argues that A1 would mostly unmask downstream queues. That is real process-level reasoning. It is not close to flawless.

**Budget and internal consistency fail.** The brief allows **at most four modules in year one**. Section 6 deploys A5, A9, A12, then A4, A6, then A8 and A7/A3 — seven items. Section 7 then recommends a *different* four (A4, A6, A8, A12). A first-year plan that does not implement the recommended portfolio, and that ignores the binding budget, is a structural error, not a drafting slip.

**Downstream automation without prerequisites.** A8 is defined as GIGO if KYC, normalized financials, collateral (when required), and credit notes are inconsistent. The recommended set drops A5 (3,300 missing-doc loops, 52% repeat, RM-triggered incompleteness) and A7 (58h external wait, 71% of equipment cases). Committee pack assembly sitting after a 58h valuation hold does not unlock cycle time; it polishes a synchronization point that has not been relieved. That violates the instruction not to automate a downstream bot while upstream artifacts remain unreliable.

**Overclaimed effects and invented precision.** “Collapse” the 31h mismatch wait, 25–35% cycle-time cut, 8.5%→~5%, 52%→~35%, 38%→~20%, 5.8%→&lt;1% are unsupported. A4 is a medium-feasibility assistant with limited coverage facing a 3-FTE specialist queue; most of the 31h is capacity and handover, not missing UI. A10 cannot erase a 44h customer wait. Weekly committee cadence for high-exposure cases is ignored, so end-to-end claims are inflated even if packaging is instant.

**Matrix vs. choice.** A5 is ranked above A8 on first-year suitability, with stronger feasibility and a clearer DFG loop, then discarded without a tight trade-off. A9 would have met the “strengthen controls” rule *and* cut contract/decision mismatch (8.5% overall, 14% renewals) with high feasibility; A12 is used as a compliance checkbox while A9 appears only in the contradictory rollout. Product/segment variation (equipment vs working-capital collateral, high-exposure four-eyes, renewal contract risk) is mostly flattened into one process.

**Weaker use of the mining evidence.** Back-office utilization falling after 15:00 is the cleanest argument that A1 is a political win with little queue value; it is barely used. Legal ops idle when KYC is slow and overloaded at month-end is relevant to A9 timing and is not carried into resource-demand effects. Concurrency of KYC and financial review is mentioned, then not designed (recommended set leans KYC/normalization/pack, not the document-request loop that starves the concurrent financial path).

**What still scores.** Dependencies, escalation capacity, audit/override/four-eyes, and the case against frequency-only prioritization are competent. Risks are the right categories. A11 is correctly deferred. The answer is a mid-quality consulting draft with several material constraint breaks — not a near-miss on a 9–10.
