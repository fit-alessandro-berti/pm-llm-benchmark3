## Drift types are systematically confused

**Covariate drift is assigned to the parts-delay spike.** The model scores immediately after diagnosis. `Order Part → Part Received` is a *downstream* sojourn. That is process-performance evidence, not a change in \(P(X)\) at the prefix. Treating 130–155 h part waits as covariate drift is a category error. Real covariate signals in this file are different: June’s mix shift (V2 34%→52%), Partner Z’s unmodeled rural distance and device age, and April’s *corrupted* features (which are pipeline-induced, not natural covariate drift).

**Concept drift is tied to the wrong mechanism and the wrong numbers.** The table claims mean predicted risk moved “0.18 → 0.31.” Predicted risk never reaches 0.31; that is September’s *observed* breach rate. Predicted risk is 0.18 / 0.19 / 0.21 / 0.23 from July–October. V2 adoption is also the wrong culprit: matured V2 breach stays ~0.09–0.12. A V2 mix increase should *lower* the base rate (that is June: observed 0.15). The actual concept-like signal is V3’s matured rate 0.33–0.35 → 0.46 → 0.64 on the *same* variant after supplier failure — a change in \(P(Y\mid\text{path})\) overlapping process drift, not “V2 adoption + Partner Z.”

**Process drift starts too late and ignores June.** The DFG edge is already 65 h in July and 130 h in August; the remote-routing policy on 1 June is a real variant-mix / routing change. The note that “V2 does not show similar delay” does not contradict process drift; it localizes it.

**Label delay is mixed with activity-label noise.** Unknown-activity share (0.8–3%) is a data-quality/schema issue, not censoring. October’s 55% maturity is the *overall* cohort, not “V3 maturity.” The claim of “no significant label delay in V1 or V2” is unsupported; the audit says the backlog hits *longer* paths, which implies V3 is *more* censored, not that other variants are clean. October V3 rates are explicitly “insufficiently mature,” which the answer later ignores.

**Population shift includes a false contradiction.** “No shift in V1 or V2 usage” is false. V1 falls 45%→22% and V2 swings 35%→52%→36%. Partner Z is a population shift; variant mix is a separate, earlier process-mix shift.

## Diagnostic tests

The seven headings were copied from the prompt. The implied likelihoods are often inverted.

- Maturity curves / fixed-vintage tests are for **whether October’s 0.16 is a censoring artifact**, not for “stable V3 rates under covariate drift.”
- Activity-label normalization and deduping test **April pipeline repair**, not outcome-label delay. The answer treats activity labels and breach labels as interchangeable throughout.
- Under genuine post-diagnosis process deterioration, a prefix model should be **miscalibrated** (under-predict V3), not “align with higher risk.”
- Under October-style censoring, calibration should look like **over-prediction** (0.23 vs 0.16), not “appear stable.”
- DFG sojourn times are observed from the event log and are almost independent of outcome-API backlog. “Under label delay, DFG edges may appear stable despite delays” is incoherent.
- Conditional-on-path tests must hold **the same variant over time** (V3 Jan 0.32 vs V3 Aug 0.64). Comparing V3 to V1/V2 is just the baseline mix, not concept drift.
- Transport/reweighting is the right *name* for Partner Z; “performance should improve” is too vague, and using the same tool as a fix for supplier lead times is the wrong causal lever.

## Recalibration vs retraining

There is no usable safe/unsafe training map, which the prompt required.

A competent staging is roughly: Jan–Mar safe; **April unsafe** (corrupted prefixes/features); May usable after lineage repair; June usable for mix-aware recalibration, not as proof of new \(P(Y\mid X)\); Jul–Sep V3 needs mature labels before any “new concept” retrain; **October unsafe** until the outcome backlog clears. The answer instead blocks May–June from training, then jumps to full retrain on September–October — the two months most contaminated by Partner Z’s slower labels and the API backlog. That is the opposite of containment.

Variant-specific models are mentioned as a slogan, without asking whether V3’s jump is a missing lead-time feature versus a true change in the diagnosis-time mapping.
