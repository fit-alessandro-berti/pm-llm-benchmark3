**5.3**

The write-up has the right skeleton and several sound process-mining instincts, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. Definitions and regime logic are mostly competent; the quantitative layer is repeatedly inaccurate, internally inconsistent, and in a few places conceptually wrong.

**What is actually strong.** Opening-hours convention, the service-time vs flow-time distinction, carry-over, and the refusal to treat 15 April as a normal throughput day are correct. The primary cut (baseline / outage-recovery / high-mix / intervention) is a defensible reading of the series. Recovery is not sold as a sustainable high-throughput regime. The staffing-based alternative is a real competing story, and the confounding of extra FTE with outage clearance and mix is the right objection. The Little’s Law matrix design matches the prompt. The operational section correctly flags a policy that looks fine in a grand average (hold 6.0 FTE) and fails after the mix shift.

**Arithmetic and internal coherence fail the quality bar.** Pooled arrivals **88.9** and completions **92.1** are wrong (about **93.5** and **86.0**). The pooled sign is inverted: the four weeks accumulate backlog, they do not drain it. That error alone undermines the pooled-vs-segmented claim. Baseline weekday arrivals/completions/WIP are off (**~100 / ~99.6 / ~20.3**, not 99 / 98 / 19.2). Disruption completions **88** match neither the five-day mean (**~77**) nor the weekday mean (**~100**). High-mix weekday arrivals/WIP are **114 / ~44.3**, not 113 / 43.4. The “pooled demand understated by ~13%” line does not follow from the author’s own table (113 vs 88.9 is ~27%). 7.0-FTE mean WIP is **~43.7**, not ~47.

**Little’s Law is not calculated on the windows named.** The baseline table says **138 min** while the notes use **19.2 / (98/12) ≈ 141 min**; those cannot both stand. The **283 min** high-mix figure is the 20 April single-day calculation (**37 / (94/12)**), not Apr 20–28. Apr 20–28 weekdays have rising WIP (**37 → 50**) and arrivals well above completions, so the implied wait is larger and the window is not stationary. Marking that window **Stationarity = Yes** while the verdict is only “approximate” is a direct contradiction. “Applicable” for Apr 6–14 is too strong given missing 07:00 stock and weekday/weekend mixing inside the same row.

**Hourly burstiness is miscomputed and under-used.** Ordinary-day hourly mean is **101/12 ≈ 8.42**, not 9.6; the 9.6 figure is the bursty day’s mean. Fano **2.76** is therefore not a valid ordinary-day index (order-of-magnitude contrast with Apr 21 still holds, which is why this is not fatal). CV is barely discussed. Daily-count overdispersion is not computed. The code never implements the required transparent change-point/segmentation **heuristic**; regimes are hard-coded. Dispersion in code is essentially one family (variance/Fano). Pooled summaries required by §4 are not printed, so the wrong pooled numbers are not generated by the “reproducible” block.

**Required comparisons are thin.** Weekday/weekend composition is asserted in §1 and then mostly skipped as a quantitative contrast; weekend rows are folded into date windows or quietly dropped. The prompt’s coding-change vs real mix caution is almost ignored; high `complex_pct` is treated as a structural complexity regime. `peak_08_10_pct` is used to recommend moving staff into 08:00–10:00, without reconciling it to the hourly vectors (Apr 7: 08–10 is **17/101**, not 31%; Apr 21’s spike is **10–11 and 13–14**). That is exactly the sort of aggregate/vector clash a process-mining audit should flag.

**Smaller but countable defects.** Assumptions are not systematically labeled. Observation/estimate/hypothesis are mixed in the regime table (“median inflates… likely due to rush/pressure”). 15–19 April as one recovery regime over-states weekend days that already look like low-volume steady operation. Simpson’s example is directionally right and still numerically sloppy. Four-week implied **248 min** does not match the author’s own L and λ.

Structurally this is a mid-tier competent essay. Strictly, the broken pooled balance, window-mismatched Little’s Law, false stationarity call, missing change-point heuristic, and repeated table arithmetic keep it well below “nearly flawless.”
