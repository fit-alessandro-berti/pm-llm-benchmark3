**5.8**

The write-up looks complete and often says the right high-level things (do not code rejects/withdrawals as non-default; activation is selection, not a convenient negative; random splits are invalid; post-decision fields leak). Under a strict statistical-design standard it is not close to reliable. Several core objects are internally contradictory, and the implementation skeleton fails the spec it was asked to satisfy.

### Target and estimand
Choosing “default among eventually activated accounts” is allowed, but the section never stabilizes what is being predicted. The title is survival / time-to-default; the target is then a binary `default_90d_after_activation`; the estimand conditions on the **future** event of activation; the operational task is early warning from the first few events of **every** application. Those are different questions. At prefix time activation is unknown, so a model fit only on eventual activates is a selected-sample predictor. That can be a defensible *associational* estimand, but the text still sells it as an early-warning system and later (Section 8) even as a basis to “decline” applicants — which is exactly the counterfactual use it disclaims.

Prediction time is also not identified. Section 1 uses “after the first *k* events, strictly before Contract Signed / Activated / terminal events.” Section 2 uses “last event before any activation-decision event,” which can be **Contract Signed** and therefore leaks the accept decision. Section 4 then allows elapsed-time prefixes of 7–60 days with no stop at Accept / Activate. Three incompatible as-of rules.

Alternative 1 (multi-state) is the right *family* of targets, but the CIF is defined on “90 days post-activation” for applications that never activate. That time origin does not exist. Alternative 2 then **censors** rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases at last event — which treats a terminal competing outcome as incomplete follow-up and contradicts the paper’s own claim that default is undefined without activation.

### Censoring taxonomy (the largest statistical error)
The 18,900 matured non-defaults are **complete 90-day survivals**, not right-censored observations. Calling 87.5% of the primary cohort “ordinary right censoring” and then prescribing Kaplan–Meier / Cox under MCAR is a basic failure of survival semantics. In the cohort-flow table the primary analysis has “Censored/unobserved: 0.” The survival apparatus is therefore ornamental: the design is complete-case binary classification of labeled activates, mislabeled as time-to-event.

Delayed labels (3,600) are not “standard right censoring.” The outcome may already be determined; the feed has not arrived. That is delayed ascertainment / missing labels, possibly informative if defaults are posted faster than non-defaults. Still-open cases are not right-censored for a *post-activation* clock; they never entered the risk set. Fine–Gray with “still-open” as a competing event is misspecified (administrative incompleteness ≠ a competing terminal state). Transfer is sometimes a competing event, sometimes lost-to-follow-up, sometimes a new case via a 60% linkage table; those are not interchangeable, and “include ~1,740 linked transfers as new cases” breaks the 48,000 accounting.

IPCW via a logistic model for `label_available` is a cartoon of the actual mechanism (activation date + 90 days + 7–35 day feed lag is mostly calendar, not a static propensity). Testing MAR with `collection_status` uses a post-activation leakage field. “No unmeasured confounding” is not an assumption of a *predictive* Cox model; that sentence confuses causal identification with ranking/calibration.

### Cohort construction
The 48,000 decomposition is correct if left alone: 21,600 + 3,600 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100. The transfer story then double-counts or leaves the flow inconsistent. Inclusion depends on `label_available_at` without a hard check that 90 days post-activation have elapsed by the extract date (the 3,600 are described as immature *or* delayed). Sensitivity cohort 4 censors never-activated cases on a clock that starts at Application Started while the primary outcome clock starts at activation.

### Prefixes and leakage
The leakage table (Section 6) is the strongest part: `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, updated `risk_score`, extract-time resource experience, and `label_available_at` are all treated in the right direction.

The prefix design does not implement those rules. Fixed-*k* extraction is `events_ordered[0:k]` with no truncation before Accept / Contract Signed / Activated / collections. For activated cases, *k* ∈ {10, 15, 20} will often include the decision and sometimes the label-bearing tail. Elapsed-time prefixes have the same hole. Short-case handling (“use all *n* events before activation”) quietly replaces “first few events” with “almost the whole underwriting trace.” That is not an early-warning prefix.

### Validation
Rejecting a random split is correct. The proposed calendar cut is not. Application starts run only through 31 Dec 2025; a “held-out Jan 2026 – present, ~1,600” cohort is invented. Train/val/test headcounts (~10,800 / 3,600 / 3,600) do not add to 21,600, and the maturity notes are confused (e.g. train cases “activated by 30 Nov 2024,” test cases “activated by end of Feb 2026” after the extract date). The same section both insists on a temporal split and recommends fitting one Cox model on all 21,600 with a policy dummy. Claiming a “reasonable” C-index of 0.70–0.75 and a 0.65 promotion rule is made-up performance. If the primary analysis truly had censoring, Graf/IPCW Brier and time-dependent calibration would be required; they are absent because the authors have already thrown the censored cases away.

### Limitations
The association vs selection vs counterfactual distinction is mostly right, and the three deployment feedback loops are good. They are undercut by Section 8’s own permission to use the scores to decline applications, and by silence on reject inference, 40% unlinked transfers, and permanently missing partner feeds as *decision-use* constraints.

### Implementation skeleton
This is well below the stated bar (one coherent ~80–160 line pipeline using specified APIs).

- Far longer than 160 lines, with unused imports (`dfg_discovery`, `case_statistics`, `xes_converter`) and a likely non-API `pm4py.algo.filtering.log.log_start_attributes`.
- Required `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is never called; prefixes are a slow Python loop.
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` is applied only after landmarks, on four columns, so proposed case attributes never enter the feature table.
- No programmatic ban on post-prefix columns; no assert that `final_limit` / `decision_reason` / `collection_status` / `default_90d` stay out.
- Prefixes are the first *k* events of activated cases, so Account Activated / Contract Signed / later events can enter features — the leakage the audit forbids.
- `time_to_event` is `label_available_at - activation`, i.e. outcome **ascertainment** delay, not time to default; non-defaults sit near 90 after clipping, defaults are systematically late by 7–35 days.
- *k*=5 and *k*=10 are stacked as separate rows with disjoint column names, then merged 1-to-many; the analysis table is malformed.
- Dedup creates `time:timestamp_rounded` and then drops duplicates on the unrounded timestamp.
- `NaT` label times are neither “mature” nor “not yet mature,” so the 3,600 can vanish from the flow.
- `pm4py.split_train_test` is not even mentioned as a rejected baseline.
- No elapsed-time prefixes, no DFG/variant/temporal-feature call with a justified interpretation.

### Net
This is a fluent template of a good design, not a sound one. It avoids the worst forbidden shortcuts (pseudo-non-defaults, random splits, using collections fields as predictors in the *prose* audit) but misstates censoring, conditions on the future, lets prefixes run past the decision, invents validation cohorts, and ships a leaky, off-spec PM4Py sketch. Those are not polish issues; they are the objects the rubric said would be graded.
