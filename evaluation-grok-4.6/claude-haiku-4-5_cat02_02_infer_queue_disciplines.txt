**3.6**

The write-up looks like a complete process-mining diagnosis: it uses the required seven-section skeleton, talks in decision sets, and spends real effort on hybrids, scan delay, and data-collection design. That shell is not enough. The core reconstructions are repeatedly wrong, and several headline claims are the opposite of what the event log shows.

## 1. The central method is stated and then not executed

The task’s quality bar is explicit: reason from eligible waiting sets at each start or preemption, and do not treat every FIFO violation as priority. The answer announces that method and then fills the tables with the wrong waiting sets. Once the eligible set is wrong, every “FIFO vs priority vs SPT vs appointment” comparison is theater.

Worst case is QA, which is also the easiest queue.

Observed QA order is `A01 → A03 → A02 → A04 → A05 → A06 → A07 → A08`.

At **08:20**, when A01 finishes, the waiting set is already:

- A02 (08:04, P3, 20)
- A03 (08:08, P3, 10, appointment 08:20)
- A04 (08:13, **P1**, 7)

The observed choice is **A03**. Appointment beats both FIFO (A02) and priority/SPT (A04). This is the single most informative QA decision. The answer never treats it as a live contest among A02/A03/A04.

At **08:30/08:32**, waiting are A02, A04 (P1), and A05. Observed: **A02**. That is FIFO, and it is a clean rejection of priority and SPT. The answer writes “None” waiting and “A02 only arrival.” That is false.

At **08:58**, A02 and A03 are already finished. Waiting are A04, A05, A06, A07, **A08 (P1, arrived 08:40)**. Observed: A04, which is simply the oldest remaining job. The answer’s strongest supporting episode is “A04 (P1) jumps ahead of A02, A03.” A02 finished at 08:52 and A03 at 08:30. A04 jumped nobody.

At **09:05**, A08 has been waiting for 25 minutes. Observed: **A05 (P3)**. Priority is rejected again. The answer says A08 has “not yet” arrived.

At **09:20**, A08 is still waiting. Observed: **A06**, five minutes after its appointment. Appointment/FIFO beat P1.

At **09:32**, A07 (P3) is taken over A08 (P1). Another priority falsification, omitted.

So QA is appointment insertion plus FIFO on the residual queue. Priority is not merely under-identified; it is contradicted at 08:20, 08:32, 09:05, 09:20, and 09:32. The diagnosis “non-preemptive priority, confidence 78” is therefore an inversion of the evidence, and the A08 “P1 selected over P2 and P3” claim is invented from start-order storytelling.

The appendix later notices A03 before A02, then fails to retract the Section 1 table. That is not careful uncertainty; it is an unreconciled self-contradiction.

## 2. QB timeline is scrambled

B02 preempting B01 at 09:16 is real, and the “urgent arrival” note supports it. Almost everything after that is mis-timed.

B01’s second stop is **09:48**, reason “dispatcher reassignment.” The next job is **B03**, not B05. B05 runs **10:00–10:14**, after B03, and B01 only resumes at **10:14**. The repeated claim that B05 “interrupts B01 again at 10:00” is false.

That error matters. At 09:48 the waiting set is B03 (P2), B04 (P3), and B05 (P1, arrived 09:46). Selecting B03 is a **priority failure** unless one leans hard on a 2-minute scan delay that makes B05 invisible at the exact decision second. The answer uses that escape hatch, which is allowed, but then still sells “P1 preemption confirmed again” via the 10:00 story. You cannot treat B05 as both not yet visible at 09:48 and as the job that preempted B01.

A further inconsistency: Section 2 says that once B01 resumes at 09:31 it “runs to completion,” then the same section has it stopped at 09:48. Residual work is also ignored as a decision at **10:14**, when B01 is resumed while B04, B06, and B07 are waiting.

There is also a small but revealing arithmetic slip: “B03 est 12 min < B04 est 8 min.” That is backwards.

QB is messy and likely hybrid/discretionary. Calling that out is fair. Calling it “non-preemptive priority with P1 preemption, 62%” while misstating the second interruption is not.

## 3. QC eligible sets and deadline arithmetic are wrong

Two hard errors drive the QC story.

First, C05 arrived at **10:11**. At **10:18** it is waiting. The answer says it has not arrived. So the 10:18 row never tests the obvious competitors: C05 (P1, deadline 10:50) versus observed C03. Both pure priority and EDD fail there; SPT (C03 = 14) fits.

Second, C07’s deadline is **12:00**, not a time already blown at 10:54. Slack is approximately:

- 10:54: ~48 minutes
- 11:34: ~8 minutes
- 11:40: ~2 minutes

C07 is still feasible at 11:40. Serving C01 (36 minutes) from 11:40 is what makes C07 miss. That is evidence **against** EDD and against priority, not a “mode switch after a catastrophic miss.” The “−54 min” / “−94 min” / “−100 min” figures look like wait time or clock arithmetic detached from 12:00.

The skipped **10:32** decision is equally damaging. Waiting then include C05 (P1, deadline already at risk) and C06 (est 6). Observed: C04 (P3, 22). That single row hurts priority, EDD, and SPT at once. A serious reconstruction cannot omit it.

C02 first at 10:10 is consistent with SPT among the opening backlog; C01 last among that backlog is given in the note. That is a better primary hypothesis than “deadline-aware priority.” Deadline pressure is a local comment on C05, not the policy that generated the sequence.

The sentence “C04 is served before C05 if pure priority applied; instead deadline governs” is also incoherent: C04 **was** served before C05, and deadline pressure would have preferred C05 too.

## 4. QD is the only queue that is mostly right — and still sloppy

Skill eligibility plus resource calendars do dominate QD. D05 waiting for D-F, D06’s 11:30 appointment on D-X, and D10 taking D-X while D-F is still in a meeting are correctly read. The late appendix point that D-F may refuse D05 at 11:36 because 24 minutes remain before the 12:00 meeting is one of the few genuinely sharp observations in the document.

Even here the decision-set work is careless. At **11:25** D-M becomes free with D04 (P3, 10), D08 (P1, 8), and D09 (P3, 20) all waiting. Observed D04 is FIFO and a rejection of both priority and SPT. The table lists those jobs and then says D04 has “no competition for D-M at this moment.” That sentence is false, and it is what lets the author keep “SPT/SEPT secondary within skill pools.” The secondary claim is not supported.

## 5. Identifiability, confidence, and “dangerous inference” are built on the broken reconstructions

Because QA is misread, the synthesis is upside down.

- **Most identifiable** is not “QA = priority.” QA is the cleanest appointment+FIFO trace in the file.
- **Least identifiable** is closer to QB or QC, where preemption reasons, residual work, and SPT/deadline/priority coincide. QD’s *routing* constraint is highly identifiable; only within-resource tie-breaks are not. The answer half-sees this and still ranks QA as the safe automation candidate under the wrong rule.
- Confidence 78/71/72 on QA/QC/QD is rhetorical precision on top of wrong eligible sets. The rubric asked for confidence to track evidential ambiguity, not polish.

The warning that inferring automated preemptive priority on QB would be dangerous is reasonable as operational advice. It does not rescue the factual errors that precede it.

## 6. What is actually good

Sections 5–6 are the strongest material: scan delay, noisy or post-hoc `est_min` (the near-perfect estimate/actual match is worth flagging), hidden resource state, SLA mode switches, and a prioritized ask for decision time, eligibility set, queue-entry time, remaining processing time, and structured preemption reasons. Those are the right fields for *these* ambiguities.

Section 4’s *form* is also right: pick an ambiguous fork and name a discriminating observable. Test 1 (B05 visibility at 09:48) is a real test. Test 2 (C06 vs C07 at 11:34) would have been a real test if C07 had actually been 94 minutes late.

Format is only approximately compliant: headings are `##` rather than the required `#`, column names are paraphrased, and a long appendix is added after the mandated Section 7. The appendix is where the author begins to notice QA problems and then leaves the official diagnosis in place.

## 7. Why not lower, and why nowhere near high

This is not an empty or off-topic answer. It knows what a dispatching-discipline argument is supposed to look like, handles QD’s hard skill constraints in outline, and writes a usable measurement plan. That is why it is not in the 1–2 range.

It is also not a 6+. Under a strict reading, inventing waiting sets, relocating interruptions, and computing a deadline as already missed while it is still feasible are not “minor unclarities.” They are failures of the exact competence the benchmark asked to see. The most readable queue is assigned the wrong primary rule with high confidence; the quality bar’s main pitfall — start-order treated as priority — is committed in textbook form.

**3.6** reflects a professionally structured, locally insightful memo whose decision reconstructions are too often factually false to count as a competent process-mining diagnosis.
