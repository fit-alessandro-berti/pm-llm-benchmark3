**4.6**

The write-up gets the headline pattern right and is therefore not a failure: crude Orion > Nova reverses inside every complexity stratum, so a 50% dump of Orion work onto Nova is a bad idea. That is not enough. Under a hypercritical reading the answer has material factual errors, misses required analyses, overclaims identification, and ships non-working process-mining diagnostics.

**Causal verdict is too confident and the wrong object of inference.** `mainly confounded` / 70 is a plausible label for the *crude harmful association*, but the task asked for total vs direct vs mediated interpretations of an Orion effect. Section 1 never states those estimands. It treats within-complexity means as if they were “direct comparisons,” which they are not. Stratification is not identification: Low–Orion and High–Nova each have *n* = 2, Orion–Day has *n* = 1, Medium–Orion–Day and High–Orion–Day are empty, and only completed cases are observed. The honest option `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` is barely entertained. “Evidence against” is written against a causal *delay*, not against the chosen verdict, so the required for/against structure is missed. Arrival load, completed-case selection, and quality are almost absent from the executive claim.

**The graph is internally inconsistent and omits required structure.** `case_completed` is called a collider but has no parents. `complexity → shift` is not a supplied fact (arrival time and coverage policy assign shift). Simultaneous `shift → resource_group` and `resource_group → shift` is left as an unexplained cycle. `arrival_load`, `compliance_performed`, `unobserved_reviewer_skill`, and `unobserved_case_risk` are unused. Quality depends only on completion. Queue wait has no arrows from load, group capacity, or complexity. That is not a usable identification graph.

**Section 3 does not fill the estimand column.** Entries say “operational condition” and “sample limitation.” Those are not estimands. Compliance is labeled mediator/confounder without saying relative to assignment vs complexity. A proxy is named, a selection variable is named, but collider bias is never actually specified.

**Section 4 contains clear numerical mistakes and skips required rows.** “Night wait (19.5h) > Day wait (5.1h)” is false: 19.47h is Orion–High mean wait and 5.06h is Low–Nova mean wait. Orion mean wait is about 12.8h, not ~16h; Orion mean service is about 7.0h, not ~6h. Day vs night *cycle times* are never computed. Compliance-required vs not-required *cycle times* are never computed. “Quality unaffected” overstates two appeals (C14, C24). The “interpretation allowed” column treats confounding as established rather than as a descriptive pattern.

**Section 5 fails a hard requirement.** There is a harmful-Orion hypothesis and a selection hypothesis, but there is no standalone hypothesis that Orion is *beneficial and masked by mix* (the reversal is folded into “confounding”). The shift test’s predicted observation is backwards: if night queues cause delay, raising night share should not “drop” cycle time. “Cases requiring compliance assigned to Nova” is not a negative control; those rows already exist. Tests are named without tight predicted outcomes.

**Code does not do what the prompt specified.** Pooled-complexity standardization is a reasonable contrast, but traces are tallied overall, not by group; only the first five strings are sent to PM4Py; `parse_event_log_string("Register>Classify>...")` is not a group event log; no Orion vs Nova DFG comparison is produced. Warnings cover only group × complexity, not the empty/one-cell shift overlaps that actually kill positivity.

**What remains salvageable:** correct crude and complexity means, opposition to the 50% reassignment, a capacity phase plus a small routing pilot, and five named uncertainties. Those keep the score out of the 2–3 range. They do not offset the factual shift/wait error, the missing required comparisons and hypothesis, the broken graph, the estimand evasion, or the non-compliant PM4Py block.
