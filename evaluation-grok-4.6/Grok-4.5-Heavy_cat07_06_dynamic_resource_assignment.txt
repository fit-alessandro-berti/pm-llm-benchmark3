**6.0**

The write-up looks complete and professionally structured, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has material assignment errors, an internal contradiction with its own hard rules, a training target that only holds because of a bad case choice, and a comparison/PM4Py section that stays too generic for the snapshot it was given.

**Format and framing.** Sections 1–7 match the required headings. Hard vs soft constraints are separated, the policy is not throughput-only, and the text correctly refuses a unique optimum. That is the strongest part of the answer. Even here there is slippage: the 20% training share is written as a hard constraint, while the prompt makes it conditional on acceptable SLA risk. Several numeric cutoffs (`~4–6 h`, `≥ ~0.7`, “complexity-proxy threshold”) are invented and then never defined tightly enough to audit.

**Policy design.** The algorithm lists every required score and an explainable greedy loop. That is necessary, not sufficient. Weights are arbitrary (resilience at 0.05 is decorative), training is double-counted in both the skill score and a separate training score, and the only repair pass is for the training quota. There is no backtrack when the greedy urgency sort piles multiple tight SLAs onto one person. Tie-breaks and exception logging are stated, but supervision—the mechanism the scenario is built around—almost never appears in the actual decisions.

**Assignments: real errors, not nits.**

- **C001 contradicts the author’s own constraint 5.** High complexity (0.88), high handover, owner Celine has Security 1, and Gita/Hugo supervision is feasible. The stated rule is: prefer continuity unless the owner is skill 0/1 *and* supervision is infeasible. Supervision is feasible, so Celine+supervisor should have been the default. The given reason (“skill 1 insufficient”) ignores the explicit skill-1–2 + R7/R8 path. Worse, the historical evidence the prompt supplied—owner change on high-complexity work adds a median **2.7 h** of waiting—is never applied to a **3 h** SLA. Routing to Hugo is likely an SLA miss. Capacity conflict with C007 could justify a specialist move, but that is not the reason given.

- **C003 is the wrong training case.** It is a repeated diagnosis loop, reassignment_count = 2 (the next hop crosses the >2 band with 1.8× rework / 1.4× appeal), and Celine is an idle Diagnosis-3, low-OT alternative. Putting a skill-1 trainee on it is the opposite of the continuity/rework evidence. C009 already is the marked training diagnosis case. Using both is redundant and risky.

- **Training quota is fragile.** Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, C015 (9). Only C003 and C009 are marked training → 2/9 ≈ 22%. If C003 is rejected, the share is 1/9 and the 20% rule fails. **C010 is explicitly “trainee approval under supervision”** and constraint 7 allows skill 1 to prepare; no supervised pair is used. **C012 is “suitable for trainee”** and is not discussed. **C015** is handled correctly (Eva/Jonas are skill 0, not 1–2), but that is the only place supervision is reasoned about. The table contains **zero supervised pairs** after a policy that advertises them.

- **Farid is a single point of failure on the urgent tail.** C002 (urgent approval, 2 h) and C011 (urgent VIP negotiation, 4 h) both stay with him, then C010 is added because Bruno is high-OT. Farid has 6.5 h left. Two urgent items plus a third approval is not a serious reading of hours-remaining or resilience. Eva (5.0 h) also gets two high-priority items (C005, C016). The max-4 cap is satisfied; capacity and bottleneck avoidance are not.

Most other rows are defensible: keep C007/C016/C002/C005/C008/C011, park high-OT Bruno/David/Iris, send C006 to Jonas, keep C015 with Hugo. That is why this is not a failing answer. It is why it is also not a high score: the cases the prompt marked as traps are exactly the ones mishandled.

**Section 4.** Dimension-by-dimension prose is directionally right and correctly avoids declaring a winner on every axis. It does **not** instantiate first-available or specialist-only on C001–C016, which is the obvious way to compare given a full snapshot. Claims that the proposed policy has the “strongest fairness” and “best resilience” sit poorly next to Farid’s urgent concentration and three people at 3 assignments while three others get nothing. No even rough quantitative deltas (expected extra waiting, reassignment counts, specialist queue length) are offered.

**Section 5.** Tone is appropriate and non-punitive, but it is a generic leaflet. It never walks a worker through an actual move in this batch (why C001 left Celine, why C003 is a training case, why Farid has three items). For a question that asked for explainability to workers, that is a miss.

**Section 6.** All required indicator families are named and tied to log columns. This section is competent. It is also a checklist: no formulas, no alert thresholds, no stratification plan beyond a sentence, no discussion of how `assignment_reason` must be written to make deviation replay possible.

**Section 7.** Breadth is fine; precision is not. `filter_case_performance` filters throughput time, not SLA remaining—the example is simply the wrong API. `discover_batches` and `get_variants` are listed in the prompt and unused, despite an explicit Quality-Check batching rule and a “batch candidate” case. There is essentially no *learning* from the log (service time by skill, empirical handover delay, weight calibration from rework/appeal). “Policy replay” is a comment, not a sketch of state reconstruction from `workload_snapshot` / `current_owner`. Acceptable as atmosphere; weak as an implementation sketch.

**Net.** A strong student outline with the right socio-technical posture, undermined by inconsistent application of its own constraints, a bad training assignment that props up the 20% target, unused supervision, an overloaded urgent owner, and a comparison/PM4Py tail that does not use the data the question provided. That is a mid-range answer, not a high one.
