**6.5**

The memo chooses the right family of action (E, not A or B), follows the required six-section structure, and is more decision-oriented than a dump of metrics. Under a strict reading of the grading criteria—especially fact/interpretation separation, fidelity to the evidence, and value-of-information logic—it is not close to flawless.

**Invented and mis-specified economics.** The executive memo treats “EUR ~23 per simple case” and “EUR ~1.5M in annual savings” as decision-grade facts. The €23 is the *overall* visible-cost gap (€184 → €161), not a simple-house unit saving; no segment cost, no national volume, and no full downstream cost exist in the brief. Presenting a fabricated national P&L in the first page of an exec memo is a serious failure of the very discipline the prompt demands.

**Pilot mix treated as national mix.** Simple houses are 34% *of the pilot*, and the pilot is *over*-weighted toward apartments and older buildings. Section 6 then scales “34% of national volume.” That error cuts both ways: nationally the simple-house prize is likely larger than they claim, and aggregate pilot deterioration is a biased read of what unmodified R would do on a typical mix. A careful memo would use that selection bias; this one either ignores it or parks it as a vague “assumption.”

**Fact / interpretation / assumption leakage.** The three-column table is the right device and then repeatedly misfiles rows. “Cost data excludes complaint-handling after week 4” and “pilot regions had more apartments/older buildings” are given data limitations, not assumptions. Billing-before-activation is a *designed* feature of R, not a discovered interpretation. “Unauthorized” paths and “the workflow engine lost its strict control structures” conflate a low-precision *discovered model* with the live control system. Low precision is also partly the intended flexibility of skip-rules and deferred activation, not just “process drift.”

**Over-smoothing of segments.** Apartments are labeled “severe failure.” Their median still improved (−2 days). The conflict is faster typical path / much worse tail, FTR, and complaints—the same pattern the memo elsewhere explains well. Rural (11%) is barely in the decision; business is waved into the “high-risk” bucket without dealing with billing-dispute mechanics. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56), pre–week-3 missing attributes, and 6-week right-censoring on 30-day complaints are material measurement threats and are omitted.

**VoI ranking is only half-disciplined.** M6/M4 as high-value is defensible. M7 is mostly a *control*, not an information experiment; scoring it as high VoI because it “triggers hard IT blocks” mixes action with learning. M5 is ranked mid-pack as a test, then section 6 implements 3-person pods immediately—acting as if the answer is already known. M1 (stratified RCT of skipping) is ranked near the bottom; given that skip-rule validity is the binding scale/no-scale uncertainty, that is hard to justify except as “we have already decided E.” False-negative gates are inconsistent (5% in the VoI write-up, 3% in the final table). Several numeric gates (p95 ≤ 35 days vs baseline 45; complaints ≤ 5.5% vs 6.1%) are stricter than baseline with no rationale.

**Other strict deductions.** Rollback “employee morale” is invented. Baseline €184 is not “field survey cost.” “Eliminating” bottlenecks under hybrid overclaims: coordinator utilization is still 93% in two regions, and scaling ownership without a staffing model can reproduce the tail. Date is 2023 in a 2026 setting. Language (“catastrophically,” “severe failure”) outruns the tables.

What remains good is real: A is correctly rejected without a national scale; B is correctly rejected because simple houses win; C–E are compared with conditions; the five metric conflicts are explained with actual mechanisms (fitness vs precision, mix/queues, SPOF vs handover count, failure demand, topology); alignments, DFG shifts, utilization inversion, and social-network centralization are used, not just cycle time. That is a solid B-level strategy memo, not an A. The factual inventions and category errors are exactly what this rubric is designed to punish.
