The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant drift diagnosis. It correctly treats March as semantic/provenance drift, keeps later rework and delay as real process change, and uses mechanism-specific rather than global change points. The remaining deductions are for manufactured precision, a few conceptual stretches, and small technical slips.

**What is strong.** Sections 1, 4, 9, and 10 show real causal restraint. March is not over-read as a business-process break; May is tied to Supplier Z rework; June is tied to queueing rather than longer `Assess` service; July priority is labeled plausible, not proven; October is treated as seasonal mix plus residual capacity strain; December is left confounded. The DFG distances are computed correctly, the raw-vs-mapped discussion is accurate, and the monitoring design can separate recurrence from new drift. The Python/PM4Py plan is usable and mostly API-faithful.

**Precision the aggregates cannot support.** The cycle-time table reports 15/50/5/30-type point shares. The prose calls them illustrative, but the table still looks identified. That violates the explicit instruction not to manufacture precision. Ranges or qualitative ranks were requested and would have been safer.

**Weaker or slightly off attributions.** Listing 2026-10-01 as a logging change point is a stretch. The senior-assessment jump is better read as case-mix, priority, and historically inconsistent recording, not a second schema break. Evidence strengths of 95/85/80 are a bit high given non-unique monthly totals. `fast_variant_pct` and the `CollectDocs -> Assess` decline are underused; both would have helped the rework/backlog story.

**Technical nits.** “distinct labels rise 911” is a broken 9→11. The change-point code forward-fills missing DFG months, so July can inherit a +5 `Assess -> Request Info` move from May and get a behavioral flag it barely earned. The recovery-ratio formula stays high whenever raw fitness remains crushed, so it can overstate how complete mapping is after May.

**Why not lower.** The required headings, tables, word bands, hypothesis coverage, monitor set, and seven decision rules are all there. The main interpretive claims are right, and the monitoring/segmentation plan is operationally useful. This is a high-quality answer with several avoidable flaws, not a near-flawless one.
