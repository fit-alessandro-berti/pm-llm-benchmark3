**8.0**

The final brief is a strong, on-format process-mining redesign. It uses the frequency DFG, performance DFG, token replay (fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces), alignment diagnostics, low precision, temporal profiles, rework stats, segment outcomes, and resource constraints; it treats the fast-path cost/speed advantage as conditional rather than global; and the three redesigns are distinct mechanisms (policy-encoded routing, inspection placement, demand-side loop removal), not parameter tweaks. Exact ROI and strict Pareto dominance are mostly avoided. That is the ceiling. The score is not higher because several inaccuracies and overclaims remain.

**Evidence and PM reasoning.** Diagnosis is the best section. The 31%→9% pre/post-rework gap, 14.1% vs 3.3% complaint split, 890-edge evidence loop (14h + 34h / p90 88h), Dispatch→complaint 96h/180h p90, 68% fitting traces, and Premium low-risk 8h vs 46h with only a 6%→3% complaint gap are used correctly and support the central claim that the slow path is not uniformly better. Precision-as-unmanaged-variant-space is a real conformance insight.

Defects: Fast approval→complaint (140, 72h) is folded into “discover it shipped the wrong thing.” The temporal profile ties *incorrect replacement* to Dispatch→complaint, not to the Fast-approval complaint edge (those cases did not take that dispatch edge). “4–8 days” also misstates 72h (3 days). “~400 of ~460 reopens originate on the fast path” mixes 140+260 *edges* with 9.2% *cases* (complaint edges sum to 510). “2×” understates “at least twice.” The R3 escalation bypass is plausible but not demonstrated: 310 reopens already go to Manual review, and board handling may follow a prior review. “Conformance cannot be policed case-by-case” overstates what low precision implies; token replay is already that policing.

**Redesign logic.** A/B/C are materially different and grounded. A correctly preserves the R2 Premium low-risk exception and does not dump low-risk accessories onto the 46–52h manual path. B uses idle inspectors, parallelizes the gate, and excludes Premium low-risk from a serial 6–18h add (24h credit). C uses the 30–45% portal effect and the stated digital-literacy/abandonment risk.

Gaps: A’s capacity story is too soft. Disabling auto-approval for all high-value work (35% of volume) is a much larger shock than the prompt’s +15–35% rule-update inflow; leading with the smaller number understates the 82% utilization cliff. High-value flow *after* mandatory review (repair vs replacement vs fast approval post-review) is underspecified; R1 allows Fast approval *after* review, so A is stricter than the rule without saying what that extra restrictiveness buys. B’s “near-zero marginal labor cost” conflates 6–18h *lead time* with inspector *labor*; campaign-period conflict with actual quality checks is only lightly flagged. B is also sold as shrinking the 31%→9% gap, which includes eligibility/compliance complaints a part-match gate will not catch.

**Trade-offs, table, experiments.** Sacrifices are explicit and segment-specific. The table has every required column and stays qualitative. “Conditionally dominated … by A+B” still edges toward a dominance claim the data cannot support. Stage 1 (B-lite, no inflow shift, 30-day post-close window) is the right first experiment; stop/go on utilization >82% and portal abandonment is well specified.

The weakest experimental claim is Stage 1 “toward single digits” from an 18% Standard high-value fast-path complaint rate. A gate that does not add R1 review cannot be assumed to produce reviewed-path (5–6%) outcomes. Downstream-shift detection (post-close complaints, reshipment, rework, abandonment, re-mined R1–R3) is otherwise the right test.

**Net.** Excellent structure and synthesis; not near-flawless. The remaining misses are causal over-attribution of complaint edges, understated A load, and overstated B cost/complaint effects.
