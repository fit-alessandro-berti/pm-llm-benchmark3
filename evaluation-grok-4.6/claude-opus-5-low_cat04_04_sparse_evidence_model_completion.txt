The answer is unusually complete and methodologically serious: the three candidates are real competing hypotheses, every major construct is tied to named evidence, missingness is not treated as free choice, and §§9–10 would actually reduce uncertainty. ### What is strong
The evidence ledger is the right artifact. Variants, D1–D7, and S1–S7 are all present; the DFG-cycle paragraph correctly refuses to infer concurrency from completion-order reversal. A5’s disclosure that `XOR(SC, τ)` conflates stocked skip, S5 loss, and unknown causes is exactly the abstraction the prompt asked for. Conservative-as-enumeration, balanced-as-POWL, permissive-as-tree satisfies the PT/POWL requirement and matches the inductive biases. The CC/ER/SC/EX decision rules in §4 are the best part of the modeling: impose an order iff it is exception-free *and* domain-compatible; refuse an order when a counterexample or S6 exists. The S1 alternative in §10 is a genuine recommendation-flip, not a hedge. The validation harness states the playout limitation instead of pretending sample languages prove inclusion.

### Factual errors that should not be in a high-scoring answer
**SC case count is 23, not 20.** Cases with Supplier Confirmation are V1+V2+V5+V6+V7 = 12+7+2+1+1 = 23; absences are 7. The author repeats “20/20 SC cases,” “20 traces containing SC,” and “empirical bet on 20 cases” as the justification for the balanced `CC < SC` edge. The invariant is real (CC precedes SC in *all* SC cases), but 20 is the DFG count `("Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration")` — i.e. immediate succession, not case support. Conflating a directly-follows weight with a case count is a process-mining error, not a typo, and it is used as if it were the decisive statistic.

**Permissive language size is 38, not 40.** `+(CC, ER, ×(SC,τ), ×(EX,τ))` yields 4! + 3! + 3! + 2! = 24+6+6+2 = 38 traces. “24 orderings … ×4 option combinations, i.e. 40” is incoherent arithmetic. The conservative tree is also miscounted: ~25 leaves, not “~23”; “12 duplicate labels” does not match 7+7+5 occurrences of CC/ER/SC.

**Balanced language is exactly 12, not “≈ 12–16.”** Linear extensions under `CC < SC`, `CC < EX`, `ER < EX` are five both-present traces, three SC-only, two EX-only, two neither. After the careful case analysis in §4, leaving a four-trace band is careless.

### Model-design problems
The permissive tree admits `RO, EX, CC, ER, …`, which violates D6/S6. The author calls this “clearly undesirable” and “an artifact of the representation,” then in the same section defines the forbidden boundary *without* EX-after-both-checks. That is not a defensible permissive hypothesis; it is a block-structure concession that lets the model generate behavior no domain rule endorses. The prompt asked for generalizations “consistent with the broad domain rules.” The alternative they themselves mention — a POWL that drops only `CC < SC` and keeps the `< EX` edges — is the model they should have shipped. Disclosing a defect does not make the candidate fit the brief.

Conservative “underfitting risks” are mostly overfitting symptoms (variant lock-in, false alarms on legal interleavings). The log-vs-true-language distinction is recoverable from context, but the section title and the quality criterion (“identifies overfitting and underfitting”) are blurred. A5/A6 also overclaim: S7 says ties are broken by ingestion index; the text treats zero-duration pairs as known and uninformative. No tie was observed.

### Smaller unclarities
“Verified in §8” is not verification; §8 is unevaluated code. `log_fitness >= 1.0` on a one-trace log is a reasonable acceptance test, but silent/duplicate transitions can still make alignment diagnostics version-sensitive, and that caveat is thinner than the duplicate-label discussion in §3. Precision bands in §7 (“high-0.7 to low-0.9”) sit awkwardly next to the promise not to invent metrics. The “~8–20 further interleavings” figure is never derived.

### Net
This is a strong exam answer with a real methodological spine. Under the required standard it cannot sit in the 9–10 band: a repeated misread of the SC support count, a wrong language cardinality, and a permissive model that violates the author’s own compliance rule are not polish issues. They are the difference between a careful discovery argument and a nearly finished one.
