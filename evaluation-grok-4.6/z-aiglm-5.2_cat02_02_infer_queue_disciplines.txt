**4.5**

The write-up follows the required seven-heading skeleton and is locally strong on QA and QD, but it fails the task’s core requirement: reconstruct **actual** eligible waiting sets at decision epochs. Several headline claims are factually false, one counterfactual is about a decision that never occurred, and another cannot discriminate the hypotheses it names. Under a strict standard that is not a high score.

## What is actually solid

**QA is mostly right.** Arrival order matches start order except A03, and A03’s 08:20 appointment is the clean override. Later choices (A02 over P1 A04 at ~08:30; A05 over P1 A08 and shorter A07 at 09:05; A07 over P1 A08 at 09:32) really do kill priority and SEPT as primaries. Treating QA as FIFO with appointment override is the correct call.

**QD is mostly right.** Per-resource sequences on D-M / D-F / D-X are FIFO among skill-eligible work, D05 is correctly tied to D-F as sole fully qualified resource, and D08/D10 losing to lower-priority eligible jobs is genuine evidence against priority. Skill-based routing plus FIFO, with a hard-ish appointment on D06, is the right primary story.

**Format and some PM hygiene.** There is a diagnosis table with the specified columns, at least three decision times per queue, named competing rules, some uncertainty language, and a sensible instrumentation list. The piece does not naively relabel every FIFO violation as priority on QA/QD.

## Critical factual errors

**QC eligible sets are wrong, repeatedly, including in the summary table.**

At 10:10, C03 (10:05, P2) and C04 (10:07, P3) were already present. The table only lists C01 and C02. That omission is decisive: **priority predicts C03, EDF predicts C03, SEPT predicts C02.** The observed start is C02. The first QC decision is evidence *against* priority-as-primary, not a neutral SEPT tie-break among P3 jobs.

C05 starts at **10:54**, after C04’s 10:32–10:54 segment, not at 10:32. Section 1’s “strongest supporting episode” — “C05 (P1) starts at 10:32, jumping C04 and C01” — is false on time and false on jumping C04. The note “deadline already at risk when selected” is consistent with a *late* pickup, not timely priority/EDF.

At 11:40, C04 is already finished. The reconstructed choice “C01 vs C04” did not exist. Waiting work was **C01 vs C07** (P2, est 18, deadline 12:00). Observed C01 contradicts priority, SEPT, and EDF simultaneously; only FIFO / “clear the withheld backlog job” survives. Building both the diagnosis and the §4 test on C01 vs C04 is a hard analytical miss.

So `primary_discipline = Priority` for QC is not a defensible reading of the decision sets. The opening four starts C02(8) → C03(14) → C04(22) → C05(40), with C01 (36) held and the note “oldest job, executed last among initial backlog,” plus C06(6) inserted only after C05, point to an **SEPT/batch hybrid with an explicit C01 hold and a tardy C05 exception** — not priority first.

**QA §2 SEPT cell is inverted.** At 08:20, A03’s estimate is 10 and A02’s is 20. SEPT predicts **A03**, not A02. Writing “est 20<10” is an arithmetic error in the required comparison table. Priority is a P3–P3 tie, not “predicts A02.”

**“Strongest contradicting episode” is misused.** A07 before A08 *supports* FIFO and *refutes* priority. D06 waiting until 11:30 *supports* the stated appointment component, it does not contradict SBR+FIFO. Those cells should hold pressure against the claimed primary, or they are empty.

## Weaker but still material problems

**QB is under-reconstructed and over-smoothed into “P1-only preemptive priority.”** The 09:16 B02 preemption is real. The answer then skips the episodes that break a clean rule: at **09:31 B01 (P3) resumes over waiting B03 (P2)**; at **09:48 B01 is stopped again for B03 under “dispatcher reassignment”** (so P2 also interrupts P3); at **10:14 B01 resumes again** over B04 and P2 B06; B05 (P1) does **not** preempt B03; B07’s 10:30 appointment is ignored. Resume-preference plus discretionary dispatcher interrupts is at least as good as “P1-only preemption,” and B04 before B06 already falsifies strict priority. Three decision times is the minimum, but they are the convenient ones.

**QD identifiability problem is overstated.** D08 is mechanical on a mechanical resource; D10 is electrical on D-X while D07 is also eligible there. Skill does **not** mask those P1 losses. The real residual puzzles are D-F idle ~11:36–12:00 (D10 would fit before the 12:00 meeting; D05’s 30 min would not) and D-X idle 11:20–11:30 for the appointment. Those are barely treated.

**§4 tests fail as discrimination.**  
(1) If B06 is moved earlier than B04 **and** remains higher priority, **both** FIFO and priority pick B06. That counterfactual does not separate the two theories. The actual 10:28 episode already separates them; the extra observable should be eligibility/hold-state or remaining work, not a logically vacuous arrival swap.  
(2) The QC test is about a phantom C01–C04 epoch. The real 11:40 C01 vs C07 comparison would have been a sharp FIFO-vs-EDF/priority/SEPT test.

**§5 does not do what was asked.** Four items appear, but they are not ranked by plausibility in any operational sense, and diagnosis impact is thin. High-leverage exceptions that the log itself flags — dispatcher reassignment, C01 held by name, C05 selected after the deadline is already shot, possible unrecorded D-F unavailability — are not in the ranked list.

**Internal inconsistency.** The table gives QD 90 and QA 85, then §7 calls QA most identifiable. Confidence 70 on QC is rhetorical certainty on a wrong primary.

## Process-mining bar

The quality bar asked for decision-set reasoning, refusal to equate every FIFO violation with priority, and respect for observational equivalence (skill vs appointment vs urgency vs SPT). The paper meets that bar on QA/QD and fails it on QC/QB: omitted waiters, a non-existent choice, and a priority label that the first QC start already contradicts. Exact numbers matter less than **who was eligible**. That is the mistake that should dominate the grade.

A nearly flawless answer would have kept QA/QD, labeled QC as an SEPT/backlog hybrid with a late C05 exception, treated QB as an undocumented preemption/resume/dispatcher hybrid, and used real residual episodes (09:31, 10:14, 11:40 C01 vs C07, D-F idle) as the discrimination tests. This is well short of that.
