**7.0**

The design is conceptually strong: it refuses the biased 21,600-row export, does not relabel rejected/withdrawn/open cases as non-default, keeps an as-of prediction point, separates activation selection from ordinary censoring, and uses a temporal rather than random split. Those are the main things the rubric rewards. It is not nearly flawless. Several statistical and implementation errors are material.

**What is right.** The primary estimand \(P(T_D\le 90\mid A=1,X)\) is honest given how `default_90d` is generated. The alternatives (policy-conditional \(P(A=1,Y=1\mid X)\), multi-state competing outcomes) answer different questions and are labeled as such. The cohort table exhausts all 48,000 applications and correctly puts the 3,600 immature activated accounts in the primary cohort as censored, not as zeros. Leakage handling of `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-activation events, same-day `documents_total`, as-of `risk_score`, extraction-time resource experience, and label fields is correct. Validation discusses policy change, migration, calibration, subgroups, and why a random split is misleading. Limitations distinguish association, selection, and counterfactuals, and name real deployment feedback (selection truncation, intervention, missingness).

**Material flaws.**

1. **Survival coding contradicts the stated target.** `survival_time_days = extract - activation` is never capped at 90 days and is not time-to-default. Matured non-defaults are coded as event-free at hundreds of days; defaults are marked at extraction lag rather than at \(T_D\). `event_indicator` is initialized as a boolean (False → 0) for immature cases with no default event, then only patched for matured rows. That is not a careful right-censoring construction, and the later pandas `loc` assignment of the full `default_90d` series onto a subset is a classic misalignment bug.

2. **The test window cannot support a 90-day post-activation outcome.** Starts from 15 Oct–31 Dec 2025, with extraction on 15 Feb 2026, plus 7–35 day feed delay, leave most test cases immature. The text notices maturity in passing and waves at survival metrics, but still presents this as the primary test fold. A backtest that respects `activation + 90 + feed delay ≤ cutoff` should have been the main scheme, not a footnote. Splitting on **start** time also lets processes begun before 1 Jul 2025 finish after the policy change, so the “pre-policy train / post-policy val” cut is leaky.

3. **Prefix semantics drift between design and code.** Section 1 specifies the first three named activities (Started → KYC → Credit Review). The skeleton takes `event_idx < 3` after a guessed rename map. Extra or duplicate events (the migration risk the prompt flags) can make the third event something else. `get_prefixes_from_log` is only used for a DFG/variant print; features are built separately, which is fine, but the audit does not check that prefixes stop before decision/activation.

4. **Cohort incompleteness.** Transferred cases are not split into the ~60% linkable vs unlinked. Partner-originated permanently missing feeds are discussed in prose but never appear as a flow row, so the 21,600 “labeled” count is treated as clean observed outcomes when the prompt says those feeds are selectively missing. Still-open cases are called “excluded” from the primary model without a clear as-of rule for applications that are simply not yet through KYC/review.

5. **Smaller but real unclarities.** IPCW is asked to do too much (ordinary censoring, delayed 90-day labels, and MNAR partner feeds). The two-stage product \(P(A=1\mid X)P(Y=1\mid A=1,X)\) is deployed as if it answers “every application” without spelling out that the second factor conditions on a **future** event at prediction time. `feat` joins `matured` and `event_indicator`; the leakage check only looks at a hard-coded forbidden set. Activity names in `ACTIVITY_MAP` and terminal labels `Rejected`/`Withdrawn`/`Transferred` are assumed, not derived from the log.

The writeup would be in the 8–9 range if the survival times, evaluation windows, and cohort splits had matched the target definition. Under a strict bar, those mismatches keep it at **7.0**.
