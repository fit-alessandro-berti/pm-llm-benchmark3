**7.6**

This is a strong, process-aware survival analysis: estimands are defined before estimators, open cases are retained, competing risks are not casually recoded as censoring, and the main Kaplan–Meier / Aalen–Johansen arithmetic I reconstructed from the table matches the reported headline figures (`F_resolve(72) ≈ 0.304`, `S(72) ≈ 0.380`, E4 partition sums to 1, RMST ≈ 50.4 h, P1 `S(72) ≈ 0.469` and transfer CIF ≈ 0.281). Format compliance, case-level walkthroughs, immortal-time / leakage treatment, and the naive-completed-case bound argument (`0.308` outside `[0.562, 0.719]`) are all well above a typical pass. It is not nearly flawless.

**Material statistical problems**

The Section 1 interval for E1 is incoherent. After correctly noting that only five cases are unknown at 72 h and that E1 is boxed in `[0.562, 0.719]`, the write-up still attaches a “Greenwood-type” SE ≈ 0.09 and a log-log 95% interval of roughly `0.50–0.85`. That interval includes values the same section has already ruled out. Greenwood variance also belongs to the all-cause survival curve, not to `1 − F_resolve`; the Aalen–Johansen variance is different. Publishing an informal CIF interval that contradicts the hard bounds is a real error, not a rounding nit.

“Assumption-free” is oversold in the same place and again in Section 7. The lower bound `18/32` treats every withdrawal, rejection, and transfer as a known non-resolution. That is correct *for this E1 definition*, but it is not free of coding assumptions. Under T3 the known-failure count drops (S08 and S21 become on-time disposals), so the logical floor is `16/32 = 0.500`, not `0.562`. The T1/T2/T3 table is careful; calling `0.562` assumption-free is not.

S08’s contribution row attributes the T1 movement in E2 (`0.380 → 0.435`) to “a censoring at 68 h, which alone raises…” T1 also censors S21 at 26 h, and that earlier removal has a larger effect on `S(72)` than S08. As written, the sentence is either false (if “alone” means S08) or unclear (if it means the T1 policy). In a case-level table that is a substantive unclarity.

**Smaller conceptual and presentation flaws**

E1’s gloss (“what fraction will have received an internal resolution”) describes `F_resolve`, not `1 − F_resolve`. The next sentence flips to the complement. Readers can recover the intent, but the estimand and the operational sentence are misaligned.

E3 is defined as `F(∞) − F(72)` and then estimated at the last observed time, with S13 forcing `S → 0` and dumping residual mass onto late resolutions. The author flags fragility, but still reports `E3 ≈ 0.30` / `0.44` as if they were stable tail probabilities. That is better than hiding the issue, not as good as treating E3 as a bound or as unidentified.

A3 (incident-cohort sampling) is labelled and then largely ignored in the reported numbers. A single freeze of mixed open and closed cases can be length-biased; saying “no left truncation is present” in §1.1 sits in tension with A3. The limitation is parked in Section 9 instead of being allowed to qualify the point estimates.

Several stratified figures (cohort CIFs, per-protocol E1/E2, dormancy-recoded `0.229`) are given to three decimals with `n = 32` and, in the prose tables, without SEs. Section 4 correctly says the extract cannot detect effects smaller than ~35–40 points; Sections 6–7 still invite a more determinate reading than that warning supports.

**Code (Section 8)**

The script meets the checklist: in-memory table, two estimands, three transfer encodings, open cases kept, cohort/priority stratification, a dormancy sensitivity, PM4Py prefix/variant features, no use of fabricated durations, named `lifelines` fallback. The KM/AJ/RMST implementation is the right discrete-time skeleton.

It is still oversold as unconditionally executable. It depends on PM4Py returning a DataFrame, on remapping positional case IDs, and on `@@index_in_trace` existing after `get_prefixes_from_log`. Those are version-sensitive internals. The naive-KM print also compares `1 − S` to the AJ *incidence* `0.304` and then to E1 in one crowded line, which repeats the complement confusion from Section 1. Greenwood is implemented only for all-cause `S`, which is fine if unused for E1; the narrative used it for E1 anyway.

**What remains excellent, and why this is not higher**

Sections 2–5 and 7 do the work the prompt asked for: three-plus transfer stories, concrete IDs for survivor bias and competing risks, open-below-72 vs open-above-72, escalation as a time-varying/post-treatment object, `last_event_gap_h` as a freeze-date separator, a data-faithful multi-state diagram, estimand-to-decision matching, and an explicit perverse-incentive warning on E2. Under a normal rubric this would sit near the top of the range. Under the requested standard, the incoherent E1 interval, the “assumption-free” overclaim, the S08/T1 wording, and the CIF-variance slip are enough to keep it well clear of a high score.
