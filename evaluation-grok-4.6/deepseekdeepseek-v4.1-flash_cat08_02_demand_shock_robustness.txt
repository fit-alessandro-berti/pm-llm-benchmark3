**6.5**

The final write-up hits the required seven-section format and is substantively an operations redesign, not a “add staff everywhere / chase the mean” plan. Scenarios vary by mix and disruption, interventions are mostly drawn from I1–I10, downstream overload is discussed, and Urgent / Routine / Complex-panel are not treated as simultaneously first-class. That is the floor for a passing answer. It is not close to flawless.

**What is solid.** S1–S4 match the mandated disruption types and are tied to peak queues (specialist 36h, medical 22h, assay 18h, tech validation 16h/101%, pre-analytical 9.5h/96%) and to variants (Urgent batch-wait bypass 58% in peak; Complex-panel 3.1× medical / 2.4× specialist). Bottleneck migration is the strongest part: I1 starving Routine/Complex and fragmenting machines; I2 pushing work into assay; I3 dumping Routine onto higher-failure M2 and amplifying the repeat loop; I4 stealing from an already overloaded technical-validation pool; I5 moving the queue to specialist review; I8 exposing pre-analytical/assay; I6 inflating recollection. I9 is correctly kept out of the backbone. WIP caps, trigger on/off rules, a complete trade-off table, redesign-caused residual risks (I4→tech validation, I8→pre-analytical/assay), and a leading vs lagging dashboard are all present.

**Process-mining evidence is sloppy in a way that matters.** Normal vs peak DFG frequencies are compared as raw counts across unequal windows (8 weeks vs 3 weeks). “Assay→repeat 1,140 vs 1,260” and “recollection 760 vs 780” look almost unchanged; as rates they are not (repeat on the assay arc is on the order of ~7% vs ~15%; pre-analytical→recollection ~4% vs ~8%). A process-mining argument that does not normalize volume is not robust evidence. Share-of-path evidence that *is* in the log (tech→medical rising from roughly a quarter of tech-validation outflows to ~40%+ in peak) is unused.

**Concrete inaccuracies and policy errors.**
- Parallel SLA wording is wrong: “Routine p90 68h→120h, breaching 72h” treats 68h as a breach; it is not. Urgent p90 is already 19h in the *normal* period; that baseline miss is not designed against.
- I10 is specified as Complex-panel overflow. Activating it for Routine when assay queue >24h is not in the intervention set and is operationally unjustified (different assays, contracts, traceability).
- “Auto-release eligible Routine after technical validation” is described as a shortage policy. The DFG already has tech→release as the dominant path (11,700 normal; 4,050 peak). The real policy is “do not drag Routine into medical/specialist capacity”; that distinction is blurred.
- Dashboard “M2 repeat <7.5%” hijacks *overall rework* (7.5% / 11.8%), which is not the assay-repeat rate. Cancel “green <3%” is stricter than normal operations (~3.1%).
- Triggers such as “Complex-panel WIP >20” and “recollection queue <5” have no units (cases vs hours) and are arbitrary relative to daily volume.
- I8 is placed in the backbone even though registration is not the binding peak resource (85%, 2.8h). Gating it does not make it a first-order robustness lever.
- Sequences and arcs are often concatenated (“61%42%”, “assayrepeat”, “Recollectionreceive”), which is not cosmetic when the claim *is* the path.

**Weaker robustness than the structure suggests.** “Acceptable” is never stated as a design objective in the redesign package; it is only implied later by dashboard bands, and Complex-panel “green” is the historical normal p90 (118h)—exactly the historical-average trap the prompt warns against. The known every-second-Friday specialist gap is not given a calendar pre-activation, only a queue trigger. I6 is treated as a cycle-time fix; it mainly saves downstream capacity—the recollection edge is still 42h/60h.

Complete, scenario-aware, and better than generic bottleneck advice. Not rigorous on the log, and several policies are imprecise or slightly wrong. Under a strict reading that is a mid-6, not an 8+.
