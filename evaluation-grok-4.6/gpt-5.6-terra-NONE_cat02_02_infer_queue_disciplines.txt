**6.2**

The write-up is methodologically ambitious and often sophisticated, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict standard the required diagnosis table and several reconstructed decision sets contain factual errors and a headline contradiction that a careful reader cannot ignore.

**Internal consistency (serious).** Section 1 labels QA as “appointment-aware non-preemptive priority,” with secondary “priority among ordinary waiting jobs,” confidence 72. Sections 2, 3, and 7 then show the opposite: A02, A05, and A07 are taken ahead of waiting priority-1 work, and the ordinary-job rule is FIFO. The strongest *contradicting* episode in the same table is simply a contradiction of the authors’ own primary label. That is not residual ambiguity; it is an unreconciled draft. QD’s secondary “resource-local priority” has the same problem: D-M’s 11:25 choice of D04 over P1/shorter D08 is FIFO.

**Factual misreading of QB (serious).** B01’s segments are `09:02–09:16; 09:31–09:48; 10:14–10:28`. The second stop is **09:48** (dispatcher reassignment). **10:14 is a resumption**, after B05 completes `10:00–10:14`. The Section 1 supporting episode (“interrupted again at 10:14 for B05”) inverts the timeline. Section 2 then invents a 14-minute unexplained gap before B04. There is no gap: B01 is in service until 10:28. Section 3 repeats the false 10:14 interruption. Confidence 88 is therefore poorly calibrated; the “clearest” supporting story is partly false. A resume-first rule at 10:14 (B01 over P2 B06) is the interesting test, and it is missed.

**Wrong or incomplete eligible sets.** At 09:20, A05 is finishing, not waiting. Claiming A06 starts “ahead of A05/A07” (Sections 1 and 3) is incorrect; competitors are A07 and A08. At 08:30, A06 has already arrived (08:29); the note that it is “not yet recorded until 08:29” is incoherent. At 10:18, C05 (arrived 10:11, P1, est 40) is omitted. That omission makes 10:18 look like a priority/SPT tie; with C05 included it is SPT, *not* priority. The Section 1 claim that C02–C03–C04 “closely follows SPT among available work” is also overstated: at 10:32, C06 (est 6) is available and C04 is not SPT.

**Incomplete decision reconstruction on QD.** D-F is free **11:36–12:00** after D03 and before the meeting. D05 (30 min) does not fit before 12:00; D10 (P1 electrical, 12 min) would. The idle interval is a first-class decision opportunity and is not analyzed. D-X’s idle 11:20–11:30 for D06 while P1 mechanical D08 waits is mentioned only indirectly. The skill-routing primary is still the best label, but local-rule confidence is too high.

**What is actually good.** The answer does reason from decision sets, labels assumptions, keeps SPT on `est_min`, treats scan delay as a two-minute bound rather than a universal excuse, and generally does not treat every FIFO violation as priority (except in the QA header). QB’s 09:16 preemption, QC’s C02-at-10:10 SPT evidence, C05-as-SLA-override, C01-over-C07 as a real hybrid-breaker, and D05’s unique-skill/meeting lock are the right episodes. Sections 4–6 are the strongest parts: the discrimination tests are well posed, the exception ranking is sensible, and the collection plan is tailored to these ambiguities rather than generic. Section 7’s warning that **strict priority** is the most dangerous claim to infer from this log is correct.

**Net.** This is a strong process-mining *approach* executed with several errors in the artifacts that matter most: the required summary table, a core QB timeline, and more than one eligible set. Those are not cosmetic. A near-flawless answer would have labeled QA as appointment-aware FIFO, read B01’s third segment as a resumption, put C05 in the 10:18 set, and treated D-F’s pre-meeting idle as a decision.
