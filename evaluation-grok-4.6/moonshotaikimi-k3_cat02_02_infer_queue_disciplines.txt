**7.2**

The write-up is format-complete, decision-set based, and generally careful about observational equivalence. It is not close to flawless.

**What is actually strong.** Eligible-set reconstruction is the right method and is done for many real decision instants, not just start order. QA is the best section: A03 at 08:20 (appointment over older A02 and over P1/short A04), A02 at 08:32, and A05 at 09:05 are genuine discriminators against priority and SPT. The note that several later QA picks are simultaneously oldest/highest/shortest is correct and keeps confidence honest. Realized duration equals `est_min` for every job is an important, correctly used fact: estimate noise cannot rescue skipped short jobs. C05’s P1 / earliest-deadline / already-late equivalence, and B02’s priority–SRPT equivalence, are handled properly. The three counterfactual tests are well posed.

**Material analytical problems.**

1. **QD’s 11:20 idle is under-analyzed and sits badly with the QA appointment story.** When D-X becomes available at 11:20, work-conserving FIFO/priority/SPT all have someone to start (D03, D07, D08, …). The resource idles 10 minutes for D06’s 11:30 appointment. That is the cleanest appointment-*reservation* episode in the data. The answer buries it as a side comment on the 11:30 start and still describes QD appointments as release-time semantics—the same weaker reading it correctly gives QA, where A05 is launched at 09:05 even though that makes A06 late. QA is no-lookahead; QD idles a free skilled resource for an upcoming appointment. Those are different rules. Missing that contrast is a real inference error, not a presentation nit.

2. **The D-F idle / D10 story is imprecise.** D10 (P1, est 12, electrical) could have run 11:36–11:48 on D-F without delaying D05 at all, because D05 cannot start until 12:20 anyway. Classic HOL blocking of a job that *could* start is not the same as refusing a filler that does not postpone the blocked head. “Reserved for D05” or “D10 pre-routed to D-X” is the coherent extra assumption; “HOL blocking” as stated is sloppy.

3. **QC’s “SPT primary” is a stack of patches, not one discipline.** The 10:10 backlog C02→C03→C04→C01 is a real SPT signature. Then C04 over already-present C06 (est 6, and the estimate was exact) needs a frozen snapshot; then C05 needs an SLA/priority interrupt; then C06 is allowed to jump leftover C01 (so the snapshot was *not* frozen); then C01 beats C07, which is just two-job FIFO and is oversold as an aging/anti-starvation policy. Four mechanisms for seven jobs. Confidence 60 is in the right direction; listing SPT as the primary discipline still overclaims.

4. **QB is similarly over-specified.** FIFO + P1 expedite + insert-and-resume + remaining-work preemption threshold + an off-policy reassignment is a five-parameter story on seven jobs. The contradictions to a priority *baseline* and to SPT are real; committing to this particular hybrid as the diagnosis, even at 55, is more machinery than the likelihood can bear. The 09:48 B05 pass-over also depends on treating recorded arrival as visibility time; the opposite reading of “scans delayed up to two minutes” makes B05 knife-edge and deserved more than a ±2 shrug.

**Smaller issues.** The Python only checks `est == realized`; it does not build decision sets. QD confidence 75 is high given how many placements are skill/availability-forced and given the unresolved D-F/D-X idles. “Aging flush” for C01 vs C07 is rhetorical inflation of pairwise FIFO (the text later admits this, which is why it is only a minor hit).

**Bottom line.** Competent process-mining reasoning with the right overall shape (QA ≈ FIFO + due appointment; QD ≈ skill/availability-constrained FIFO plus appointments; QC and QB not uniquely identified). Deducted hard for the QD appointment/idle miss, the HOL mis-statement, and two primary labels that only work after stacking exceptions.
