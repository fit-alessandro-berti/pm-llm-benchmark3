**7.4**

This is a strong, process-aware causal memo that does most of the hard things the prompt asked for: it catches the crude-vs-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, separates queueing from service, treats the group label as a bundle, and proposes tests with predicted observations. Under a hypercritical bar it is not nearly flawless. The defects below are specific, not generic caveats.

**Factual errors in the required decomposition.** Section 4 is supposed to be auditable. Two headline contrasts are wrong. Night vs day mean cycle time is given as 22.4 h vs 13.9 h; the 17 night cases average **21.74 h**, not 22.4 h (day is ~13.98 h, so that side is only loosely rounded). Compliance-required vs not is given as 26.3 h vs 12.8 h; the 13/17 split is **26.78 h vs 11.95 h**. Those are not rounding slips, and they sit in the one table the reader would use to check the author’s arithmetic. Nearby claims that *are* correct (crude +6.72 h, stratum gaps −2.14/−2.21/−3.43, Kitagawa +9.03/−2.31, wait-driven residual, residual “other” ≈ 0.8/1.4/1.5 h) make the errors more striking, not less: the author could compute, and still published wrong margins.

**Local overclaim against the author’s own identification limits.** The executive verdict is disciplined (`mainly confounded`, 70 on the crude harmful gap, ~40 on a residual benefit, positivity and n=2 called out). Then the same section predicts the 50% move will “most likely fail or backfire” with “roughly +2 to +3.5 h per moved case.” That range is just the stratified Orion advantage treated as transportable. The later text correctly says that contrast is not identified. The recommendation is still directionally right; the quantitative forecast is not earned. The same pattern appears for shift: Nova night-vs-day cells of n=1 or 2 are used to conclude that “night shift per se does not appear to lengthen cases.” That is not a finding the sample can support. “Nova day High-load cases are only about 0.8 h slower than Normal-load” is unspecified (which complexity?) and does not match the Low-day split (~0.55 h).

**Weaker spots in an otherwise good causal graph and H-table.** Total / direct / mediated structure is present in §§3–5 but is not actually named in §1, which the prompt required “at minimum.” `shift → routing_rule` and `shift → resource_group` duplicate the same story; a `resource_group → shift` (coverage) arrow is at least as consistent with the process text and is omitted. H3 (genuine Orion harm) is required and the *test* is good, but the *supporting* column cites Orion’s High wait of 19.5 h as a bottleneck while Orion’s High wait is shorter than Nova’s 22.75 h—the cited fact cuts against the hypothesis. Residual time is called “identical” across groups; two Nova cases are 0.7 h, not 0.8 h, which is rounding in the source fields, not identity.

**What remains excellent, and why this is not a 6.** Format is complete. Complexity is handled as a confounder *and* an effect modifier without pretending the 3-level label blocks the back-door. Wait vs service vs “other,” compliance as mix rather than a group-triggered mediator, rework inflating residual time rather than recorded service (against the field definition—correctly flagged as measurement), completed-case selection as a collider, and resource group as treatment/proxy/bundle are all real process-mining work. Standardization uses an explicit common distribution; overlap/positivity is diagnosed at complexity×shift×group; the Python block is diagnostic, embeds the CSV, warns on n<3, and uses PM4Py only for frequency DFGs without fake performance timestamps. The phased plan (instrument → overlap-hour quasi-random Low/Medium pilot with Low as negative control → certified/compliance capacity without relabeling → small hold-out reassignment) is the right operational answer.

The memo would be in the 9s if the decomposition table had been as careful as the identification language. The published wrong means, plus forecasts that outrun the author’s own caveats, are enough to keep it well below a high score under the instructed bar.
