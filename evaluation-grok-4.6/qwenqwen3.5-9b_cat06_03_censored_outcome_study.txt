**5.0**

The answer avoids the worst failure modes (it does not recode rejects/withdrawals as non-default, and it rejects a random split), but it is not a statistically tight or implementable design. Several core pieces contradict each other, the validation window cannot produce mature labels, and the PM4Py skeleton would not run as written.

**Target and estimand.** \(P(D_{90}\mid A)\) is an allowed formulation, but it is not aligned with the stated prediction time. Scoring at `Application Started` / KYC while defining the population as cases that *later* activate conditions on a future event. That is not an early-warning model for “every credit application”; it is an associational model on the selected activator subpopulation. The write-up never states the operational rule this implies (score only after activation is known, or explicitly estimate a two-stage \(P(A\mid\text{prefix})\,P(D\mid A,\text{prefix})\)). The 90-day clock starts at a future activation time, so the horizon from the prefix is random; that is ignored. Section 4 then moves the cutoff to “the last event prior to Account Activated,” which can include Accept and Contract Signed and is no longer “the first few events.” Sections 1, 4, and 8 therefore describe three different studies.

**Cohort.** The 48,000 arithmetic is correct, and excluding non-activators from a \(P(D\mid A)\) label set is coherent. The flow table still fails the prompt: transferred/partner cases are not marked selectively unobserved despite the 60% linkage table and missing outcome feeds; still-open cases are dumped as N/A rather than pre-terminal and possibly later activated; the 3,600 immature activated cases are listed but never given an as-of maturity rule using `label_available_at` and the 7–35 day feed delay. The exclusion “started after 1 January 2024 (outside window)” is simply wrong. There is no left-truncation / extract-date as-of rule.

**Censoring.** The taxonomy is named, then mishandled. The 3,600 immature labels are administrative end-of-study plus delayed feeds, hence strongly tied to activation date and to the July 2025 policy regime. Treating them as generic non-informative censoring, and inventing a “paused because low risk” story, is incorrect. Kaplan–Meier plus “an ML classifier with lifelines” is not a primary analysis strategy. The proposed sensitivities change the estimand (recode transfers as default; switch to Fine-Gray on a cohort already dropped from the primary set) rather than stress-testing MAR/MNAR, linkage, or delayed-label assumptions. Testability is reduced to one sentence on independent censoring.

**Prefixes and features.** Duplicate events from the October 2025 migration are unaddressed. Same-calendar-day `documents_total` leakage is not. Required cohort/calendar features (policy regime, migration window, start-month) are absent. Resource experience is “repaired” to event time with no method, even though the warehouse only stores extract-time values. `risk_score` is frozen at the first event rather than last value at or before the as-of time.

**Leakage table.** Several keep/drop calls are wrong. `label_available_at` is not a predictor; it is a label-timing field and keeping it is leakage. `data_extract_time` is a snapshot artifact, not a scoring-time feature. Future-event and post-decision fields are correctly flagged in the table, then reintroduced by the Section 4 stopping rule.

**Validation.** A single start-date cut at 30 June 2025 is not a backtesting scheme. Test starts through 15 February 2026 cannot have matured 90-day post-activation outcomes (extract date is that same day). There is no train/validation/test split, no maturity-restricted test cohort, no gap around the policy change (June starts can activate under the new policy), and no handling of migration effects on cases that *started* before October. Metrics omit the delay/censoring structure (no IPCW/Brier for censored labels) and the 12.5% base rate.

**Limitations.** The text claims the study “captures early signals of activation failure” after excluding every non-activated case, and that temporal validation makes the model “robust to policy shifts.” Both are false. The association / selection / counterfactual distinction is asserted more than drawn.

**Code (Section 9).** This is well below the required skeleton.

- `df[df['activity'].str.contains('Account Activated')]` keeps only activation *rows*, discarding every prefix event. The rest of the pipeline is empty or meaningless.
- `pm4py.format_dataframe(..., case=, activity=, timestamp=)` is not the API (`case_id`, `activity_key`, `timestamp_key`). After formatting, filters on `'activity'` are likely invalid.
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log_formatted, case='case', prefix_length=5)` does not match `get_prefixes_from_log(log, length)`.
- Required case-level start, activation, terminal, censoring, and label-maturity as-of the cutoff are not built.
- No train/validation/test; no 48k cohort accounting; prefixes are taken from the uncleaned log that still has leakage columns and post-prefix events.
- `audit_df = ...[['Account Activated']]` would KeyError and is not a leakage audit.
- Unused `PRED_TIME`, no migration normalization, no maturity filter, ~70 incomplete lines rather than a concrete 80–160 line engineering path.

The piece has the right headline instincts and the required headings. It is not close to a defensible, as-of, leakage-free study, and the implementation would not execute.
