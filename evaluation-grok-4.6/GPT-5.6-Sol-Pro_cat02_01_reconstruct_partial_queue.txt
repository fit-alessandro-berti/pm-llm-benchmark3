**8.5**

The write-up is technically strong: event semantics are kept distinct, missing starts are interval-censored rather than filled in, open and waiting cases are retained, utilization includes elapsed busy time, and the time-varying S1→S2 bottleneck is the right conclusion. Almost every headline number I recomputed (sojourns, wait/service ranges, WIP areas, rates, snapshots, Little residuals, resource busy minutes) matches. The remaining deductions are for real gaps and a few unclarities, not for arithmetic.

**What holds up**

Assumptions A1–A12 are explicit and operational. The half-open `[A,S)` / `[S,E)` convention plus post-batch tie-breaking is stated and then actually used. Feasible windows for P07/P15/P05/P11 are the correct resource-and-precedence intervals. Station sojourn and completed-case sojourn are correctly treated as invariant to the wait/service split. Right-censoring is not converted into fake completions. The Python block meets the spec: status-aware parsing, a PM4Py interval frame only for fully observed intervals, `format_dataframe`, pandas station metrics under three imputations, case-level-only use of `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, and a justified skip of `convert_log_to_time_intervals`.

**Material issues**

1. **End-of-window S1 is under-interpreted.** After 13:45 I2 is idle while P22 has already waited 95 minutes and is still waiting at 14:00. That is the clearest non-work-conserving episode in the log. It is not mentioned. It matters for the “S1 has drained” story, for S1 utilization, and for whether late S1 is capacity-constrained or dispatch/logging-constrained.

2. **Systematic idle-while-queue gaps are treated only in passing.** Same-resource 5-minute holes persist while station queues are long. Those holes may be rounding, setup, or true idleness; they change how one should read the 74–84% / 73–81% bands. The outer rounding envelope swallows some of this numerically, but the operational interpretation is not carried through.

3. **Section 5 over-attributes S2’s structural disadvantage to the outage.** “This makes S2 the lower-capacity station under sustained flow” points at the 15-minute C2 outage. The stronger, already-stated evidence is the ~30 vs ~40 minute service medians (roughly 4/h vs 3/h) even with both calibrators up. The outage is a temporary extra squeeze, not the reason S2 is slower under sustained flow.

**Smaller issues**

- The rate table’s “net WIP” column is net *change*, not WIP level.
- Midpoint S1 utilization is 79% in §4 and 79.2% in §6.
- Little’s-law windows are defensible but post-selected; P21 (and P15 on S2) sit in \(L\) and not in departure \(W\), so the 0.03 residual is partly by construction.
- A simple window-identity check — mean system WIP 9.51 versus 22 arrivals × mean elapsed sojourn / 360 — would have been cleaner than implying that only the service subsystem can be checked.
- Individual-resource narrative omits C2’s midpoint utilization (~74.6% of 345 minutes).

**What is not a problem**

Using reported-clock scenarios as the main range and keeping all-adversarial rounding as an outer envelope is the right hierarchy. Peak S1 queue remaining 7 across imputations is correct (the 11:30 snapshot is before P15’s earliest feasible start). Completed-case mean sojourn 146.4 is properly labeled completion-conditioned. Format constraints are met.

The analysis is well above a typical competent reconstruction, but under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless: one salient end-state fact is missed, idle-with-queue is under-analyzed, and the bottleneck paragraph contains a causal slip.
