4.3

The write-up has the required skeleton and a few correct case-level bounds, but it fails several explicit requirements, reports station metrics that are not supported by the data, and treats the Python section as a sketch rather than a reconstruction. It is not close to the quality bar.

**Requirements that are missed or only half-done.** The task asked for queue length and total WIP *over time* with a stated convention for simultaneous rounded events. A1 orders completions before starts to “maximize capacity”; that is not a WIP counting rule, and section 4 only gives undocumented mean/peak figures. Case-level total sojourn for completed cases is defined and then never reported. Arrival and departure rates by subperiod are reduced to one all-morning average plus one burst remark. Resource busy time is not broken out by I1/I2/C1/C2. Sensitivity analysis names three scenarios but computes none of them. P18–P20 (right-censored S2 waits) are dropped from the case table even though they are not “fully observed unremarkable cases.”

**Case table: locally good, globally sloppy.** The interval bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 match the obvious resource-availability constraints (C1 free at 09:45, I1 at 09:25, C2 at 12:00 after P08, I1 at 11:40). OPEN/WAITING rows are correctly treated as lower bounds on elapsed time. That is the strongest part of the answer. Problems: A4 sets the latest missing start to the completion time and never states the next-job constraint (numerically non-binding here, but the method is incomplete). “Central” is the *earliest* feasible start—an optimistic utilization corner, not a midpoint—while section 6 reuses “central” for the same corner and then defines “pessimistic” as that corner plus an unspecified “5-minute rounding penalty.” The aggregated “5–95 / 20–30 / 5–65 / 25–50” block mixes heterogeneous cases into a single range and hides that later fully observed S1 waits are 100–105 minutes.

**Station metrics are not defensible.** S2 waiting “mean ~45m” is too high. Observed completed S2 waits average about 32 minutes even after inserting the earliest-start imputations for P05/P11; including elapsed waits for P15/P17 still lands near the high 30s. S1 “~60m” is a bit low versus the known waits (~65m). Busy time “~560m” and “~532m” are exactly (completed services) × (mean service): 20×28 and 14×38. That discards P21’s elapsed S1 work and P15/P17’s elapsed S2 work, and it is precisely the completed-only utilization the quality bar forbids. The 75–80% and 72–78% bands are therefore both too narrow and wrongly constructed. C2’s 15-minute outage is never clearly included or excluded from the denominator.

**Bottleneck story is directionally plausible and internally inconsistent.** S2’s longer service times, 14 vs 20 completions, and six cases still in S2 at 14:00 support S2 as the tighter *output* constraint after mid-morning. The S1 capacity figure 2×(60/28)≈4.3/h does not. I1/I2 are occupied almost continuously, later cycles are ~30 minutes plus systematic 5-minute gaps, and S1 still has a ~100-minute wait at 14:00; effective S1 throughput is near 3.3–3.5/h, not 4.3. You cannot claim S1 capacity exceeds the 3.7/h arrival rate and also observe a queue that never clears without explaining the gap (changeover vs rounding). The C2 outage is over-weighted: P08 already waited from 10:20, and at most one 15-minute slice of that wait is the outage.

**Little’s Law.** It is correct to refuse a steady-state claim on a growing queue. The 08:00–10:00 check then applies cohort S1 sojourn (~76 minutes) to the window arrival rate and “predicts” L≈8.9, which must exceed time-average WIP because much of that sojourn lies after 10:00. That is a useful caveat, not a consistency check. The finite-window identity (time-average WIP equals arrivals per unit time times *time spent inside the window*) is never computed.

**Section 7 fails the specification.** `parse_time` maps MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same `NaT`, which is the opposite of “without treating MISSING, OPEN, and blank as the same thing.” The interval log keeps only complete start/end pairs, so missing starts, open activities, and waiting cases never enter PM4Py. Pandas computes raw differences and `print`s `.head()`; there is no WIP path, no utilization, and no uncertainty scenarios. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are applied to a complete-case projection and labeled case-level, which is the one PM4Py requirement that is met, but PM4Py waiting will start at the first S1 *start*, not at `arrival_s1`, and that semantic limit is not stated. The comment on `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is acceptable; printing only `head()` is not “compact result tables.” The script cannot reproduce sections 3–6.

**What survives.** Missing-start bounds for the flagged cases; OPEN vs final service; no invented bypasses; unstable-queue caution; S2 slower than S1 on fully observed services; heading structure. Those points keep the grade out of the failure floor. The utilization method, the S2 wait summary, the missing WIP convention, the uncomputed scenarios, and the status-collapsing code are enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.
