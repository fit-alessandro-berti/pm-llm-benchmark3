The submission is complete in structure, covers every required section, and the in-memory construction is largely coherent. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several analysis claims contradict the constructed relations, and a few modeling choices conflict with the stated narrative.

**What is solid.** All four DataFrames, 17 events, 12 objects, timezone-aware timestamps, payment amounts as event attributes, O2O coverage, relation-level activity/timestamp copy, and the required validation checks are present. The `involved` vs `propagated` qualifier split is a clear, reviewable way to separate source facts from inference. The pipeline shape matches the spec (flatten + DFG + skeleton for the four types; OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, OCPN, interactions). Failure modes and semantic checks are mostly inspectable and on-topic.

**Material inconsistencies (these drive the score down).**

1. **Package flattening is wrong relative to the E2O table.** Confirm Delivery is related only to `Delivery` (and propagated `Order`s), not to `P1`/`P2`. Flattening on Package therefore yields `Pack Package → Dispatch Package` only. The write-up still puts Confirm Delivery on the P1/P2 traces “propagated from D1/D2.” That is O2O reasoning smuggled into an E2O flattening. The same answer later correctly says PM4Py flattening does not walk O2O.

2. **Invoice flattening invents events that cannot appear.** V1 is only on E11, E12, and E15. Confirm Delivery never touches `Invoice`, with or without the `propagated` flag. The hedge about those events appearing “if propagated relations are kept” is false.

3. **Alternative A misstates the source-minimal Order log.** Dropping `propagated` rows does **not** make Order traces stop at Issue Invoice. `Close Order` remains a direct Order relation, as do Create/Add/Reserve/Pack. The predicted DFG is therefore wrong.

4. **`completes_settlement` for M2 is not licensed.** The process states that M1 and M2 are both partial payments against V1. No invoice total is given, so treating M2 as completion invents a business fact. The allowed qualifier pair does not require using completion.

5. **E8 vs the stated qualifier policy.** Pack P1’s Order links are justified by the narrative (“relates to both Orders”). Pack P2 is not given that wording, yet O100 is tagged `involved` (source fact) rather than `propagated`. The policy in §1 is not applied consistently.

**Smaller defects that still matter at this bar.** ET–OT is described with event *instances* (E6, E7) as if they were graph nodes; `discover_etot` is type-level. OC-DFG/OCPN commentary similarly slides between typed structure and instance hubs (P1 as a “place”). The constructor is isolated for version drift, but the mandated discovery names are not. Payment DFG is stored in a different shape from the other `dfgs` entries. The `created` qualifier blurb also talks about close. Flattening in code never filters `propagated`, while parts of §4 talk as if that filter might be on.

**Net.** This is a competent, well-organized solution with real referential integrity in the tables and a usable pipeline. The expected-analysis sections—the other half of the grade—contain several claims that do not follow from the model just built. That is enough to keep it well below the near-flawless band.
