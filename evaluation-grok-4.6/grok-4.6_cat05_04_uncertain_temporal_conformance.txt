8.3

The analysis is numerically strong, assumption-transparent, and structurally complete. Interval construction, offset direction, timezone conversion, DST reduction, inferred start, and the send≠receive refusal are all handled in the intended way. The main SLA and profile classifications I recomputed match the reported arithmetic. Sensitivity, evidential sufficiency, and governance are aligned with the required distinction between possible and certain outcomes. Midpoints are confined to a labeled lossy comparison.

What keeps this well short of a top score is a set of avoidable precision, semantics, and presentation defects.

**Interval endpoints are slightly sloppy.** After clock correction the feasible sets should be closed (or closed to 59.999s). The writeup keeps half-open `HH:MM+1min` notation even after offset expansion, so several reported bounds are 1 µs–1 ms conventional rather than exact. That does not flip any class here, but it is the same family of silent cleanup the prompt forbids for midpoints. T-405’s unfiltered unions also drop the date on the second component and therefore fail the “explicit ISO timestamps” requirement.

**T-405 without ingest order is mis-typed.** The prompt already tells you ingest order rules out fold combinations; using it is correct, and the surviving triple is the right one. The counterfactual is not. If fold tags are left unconstrained, the timestamps remain a finite union of intervals. Duration arithmetic on that union is still possible and would be `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` (negative and 12–18 min branches), not `INDETERMINATE`. Calling the unconstrained case indeterminate overstates evidential collapse.

**T-409’s weak offset is treated as a hard bound in the primary table.** The writeup admits the estimate may be non-binding, but §3 still reports `[-481.5, 1021.5]` and `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` as the main result. Under the stated `INDETERMINATE` rule (“too weak / untrusted clock”), the headline row should have been indeterminate, with the ±12 min band only as a conditional. Putting the conditional in the main grid and the caveat in prose is the wrong emphasis for weak evidence.

**T-406 delay handling is conservative for S2 and anti-conservative for S1**, which is acknowledged only on the S2 side. The occurrence-shift reading is a legitimate primary choice; it should have been stated that widening the start earlier makes S1 look better, not merely that it is “more conservative for containment.” The zeta=3 parenthetical (“below 60?”) is a visible moment of confusion, even though the final class is right.

**Section 5 is not as tight as the narrative.** The script is the right shape: raw frame, `zoneinfo` normalization, fold alternatives, inferred start, interval classes, explicit lossy midpoint log, `format_dataframe`, `conformance_temporal_profile` at zeta 1/2/3, and a version-isolated diagnostic extractor. But the midpoint-vs-interval loop never actually prints the interval class (the comment claims it will), unused locals remain, exclusive intervals are collapsed to closed minus 1 µs, and a `RuntimeError` if the DST filter does not yield exactly one triple is brittle. The qualitative claim that midpoints would pass T-401/T-404/T-405/T-406/T-409 S1 while intervals stay possible is correct and is the point of the exercise.

**Coverage nits.** S3 is omitted entirely on cases with no close rather than marked not testable. The signed T-404 duration is taken as exact true-time with no source clock, which is stated but never bounded. Severity/confidence are reasonable but not operationalized.

None of these break the core incident-timing argument. T-402 S1 and T-408 S2/S3 are genuine certain misses; the 1.5 s and 3 s knife-edges are not over-read; zeta and grace are treated as policy, not facts; PM4Py is not sold as an uncertainty engine. The work is well above a competent pass. It is not nearly flawless under a strict reading of the spec.
