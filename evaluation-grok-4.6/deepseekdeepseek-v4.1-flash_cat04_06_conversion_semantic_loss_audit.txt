**7.4**

The final write-up is a strong invariant-first conversion audit: it keeps POWL authoritative, does not invent a POWL→BPMN API, treats a process-tree intermediate as allowed to fail, and refuses to equate a returned BPMN object with semantic fidelity. Format compliance is essentially complete, and the N-poset / non-series-parallel argument for pipeline B is the right reason a faithful tree may be impossible.

It is not nearly flawless.

The preservation table overclaims. `conversion failed` is used as if Petri→tree actually raised, but the code was not executed. That term is for a caught exception, not a theoretical prediction. The honest cells were `not tested` or a split between “faithful tree impossible” and “API outcome unknown.” Using `expected preserved` elsewhere shows the author knew the difference and then ignored it for pipeline B.

Several baseline “negative witnesses” are properties, not traces (especially I2). The witness suite also misses a decisive illegal interleaving: Legal Review after Release. “Release before Legal Review” is ill-posed on the silent-skip path.

The token game is a custom firer on plain dicts, not PM4Py `Marking` / library semantics. `get_enabled_transitions` after `dict(im)` is a real integration risk. Stopping at the first exact final marking hides post-final extra behavior; `max_label_count` on every label can make bounded trace-set equality look better than it is.

Footprints and reachability are printed, not compared as semantic objects. `play_out` is guessed behind `hasattr`. Those are secondary, but they pad “validation” without adding much proof.

What remains is still clearly above a mediocre answer: correct source semantics, a defensible primary pipeline, realistic failure handling, layout-versus-behavior discussion, and residual uncertainty that does not pretend passing tests are equivalence. The score is held down by predicted results presented as audit outcomes and by an unvalidated, slightly brittle explorer.
