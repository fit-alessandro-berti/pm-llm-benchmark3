**5.8**

The write-up is unusually well structured and, in places, genuinely strong: assumptions are labeled, the uniform-mixture means check out, branch demand and the 2 720.6 / 2 490 offered-load bound are arithmetically correct, parallelism is treated as a state-dependent decision, U2’s two readings are flagged, and the recommendation is not “pooling wins on base-case mean delay.” That is not enough to save the grade. Under a strict reading there are material calculation errors, an internal contradiction at the point of recommendation, a capacity-gap argument that does not follow, and a simulation/table pair that cannot have produced the numbers shown.

**Invented precision and a broken evidence chain.** Section 4’s two-decimal SLAs, delays, and Gini figures are admitted to be “scaffold plus judgement,” yet they are written as if they were the §8 experiment. The DES cannot be the source. Completions are processed *after* assignment at the same timestamp; a resource with `busy_until[r] <= t` is treated as free while still in `in_service`, so a new task overwrites the completing one and the finished activity never releases its successor. Ready work at a completion instant is the normal case, not an edge case. The pipeline would stall, deferral would explode, and you would not observe an orderly base-case C result of P1 SLA 0.89 and ~12% deferral. The table is therefore a narrative artifact with false precision. That is a serious methodological failure in a document that asks to be audited.

**The recommendation contradicts its own table.** C is said to be “the only policy that respects the ≤90% utilisation fairness constraint in the base case.” The same table gives C `max_util = 0.93`. J2 also penalizes anything above 0.90. You cannot claim constraint satisfaction and print a violation in the same breath.

**Handover savings do not close the §2.1 hole.** Cutting overhead from 24 to 18 min/case is ~250 resource-minutes. The §2.1 shortfall is 2 720.6 − 2 490 ≈ 231 minutes of *direct service* at multiplier 1.0, *before* any handover is added. Reducing an add-on cost does not fill a primary-work deficit. The line that handover discipline is “about the entire capacity gap” is the load-bearing justification for C and it is wrong. The honest statement is that overhead control trims effective ρ from something like 1.5 toward 1.4 and still leaves the day unclosable.

**Same-day horizon is allowed to masquerade as SLA science.** P3’s clock is 24 hours; many P2 clocks expire after 20:30. Counting unfinished-at-horizon cases as SLA misses is a modeling choice, not a finding that “none reaches 0.90 P2 anywhere.” Section 9 later admits the model is blind to carryover; section 4 still treats the artifact as a result. ρ > 1 implies same-day *clearance* is infeasible. It does not imply every routing policy fails every multi-hour SLA.

**Capacity arithmetic is sloppy where it matters.**
- 10:00–12:00 on-shift minutes are Ada+Ben+Chen+Deepa+Farah = 600, not 570. Breaks in that window are none. ρ is ~0.98, not 1.03; the “tight from 10:30” story is overstated.
- Electrical+Software “1 350 raw minutes” treats Deepa’s 450 minutes as fungible Software capacity. She cannot do Software. The squeeze is real; this bound is not the one that shows it.
- U5’s ρ ≈ 1.69 is 3 442 / 2 040 with the outage waved at. The outage blocks Intake for 30 minutes; it is not a 450-minute-scale capacity deletion on top of Chen.

**Code does not implement the policy that is recommended.** `tier1_wait` is hard-coded to 0, so C’s wait-based tier opening never fires. The write-up’s third parallelization clause (sequential breaches the deadline, parallel does not) is absent. C-triage is in the table and the mode-switch logic and not in `POLICIES`. Preemption does not rewrite the already-emitted interval, so the mandatory event log is wrong whenever a restart occurs. Reservation release rules are a thin subset of C.2. A scaffold may omit mechanisms if it names them; it may not claim those mechanisms are implemented and then omit them.

**Smaller but disqualifying-under-strictness issues.** A8’s “tasks never span breaks” is a strong extra assumption that manufactures lunch-time holes and is only lightly stress-tested. [A6]’s pessimistic handover-blocks-the-server reading is fine as a switch, but every utilization number inherits it while the prose sometimes talks as if utilization were a policy-invariant fact. Software morning demand “4.0 branches ≈ 154 std-min” is a bit loose (08:00–12:00 software is 4.0 branches × 38.5 ≈ 154, yes, but ES/S mix and Chen’s dual claim are the real constraint). Policy B’s “fairness collapse” is asserted at Gini-derived 0.71 without showing the utilization vector. Several §4 cells (900+, 1000+) are atmosphere, not estimates.

**What still deserves credit.** O1/O2/O3 are real objective functions, not slogans. The ME/ES latency-versus-synchronization trade-off is the right one. Stranded Mechanical capacity under A/U2 is the correct specialization failure mode. B’s least-slack score can rank a tight P2 above a loose P1; that critique is valid. The pm4py sum-versus-union warning is exactly the trap the spec asked for. Section 6 (estimate gaming, multi-day P3 starvation, skill erosion, true preemption cost ≈ 15–20 min, Validation as the migrated bottleneck, interpretability of a five-threshold rule) is the best part of the document. Section 9’s pilot (day-level randomization, P1 safety carve-out, do not fire Chen to test U3) is competent experimental design.

**Net.** This reads like a strong analyst’s memo whose quantitative spine was not executed. The prompt rewards coherent calculations and defensible uncertainty, not a polished policy story sitting on an unsound DES and a self-contradictory utilization claim. Nearly flawless would require the table to come from a correct simulator, the 90% claim to match the numbers, and the capacity-gap sentence to be withdrawn. It is well above a shallow or confused answer, and well below the bar implied by “utmost strictness.”
