**8.3**

The final five-section audit is substantively strong: it covers every required item, preserves `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as future-but-semantically-legitimate rather than impossible, and keeps predictive / causal / actionable / fairness standards distinct. It is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up.** The leakage table is complete and mostly well-calibrated. Complete-trace fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) are correctly marked critical. Subtler lineage bugs are caught: prior-claim SQL (`<= extraction_date` vs `< t0`), nightly document snapshot, closure-populated `final_handler_team` without a historical timestamp, ±5 minute queue join, mutable untimestamped `risk_score_current`, and Case Z’s missing sequence index. Section 2 states the right process facts at `t0` (only registration and coverage completed; appeal not enabled; repeated settlement only after Partial/Deny plus appeal) and correctly warns that a frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule. Revised Case X/Y narratives do not replay leaked outcomes. Case Z is rightly sent back for recomputation.

**Deductions.** Several issues are small but real.

1. **Wording error on `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`.** Calling it “full-trace leakage *if used later*” inverts the issue. Leakage is using the full-trace pattern *at this `t0`*. If the fraud event is already in a later prefix, the pattern can become admissible.

2. **Credible-driver vs lineage-unverified tension.** `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` are tabled as “verify lineage first,” then listed as Case X/Y `credible_drivers`. That overstates what is currently defensible.

3. **`calendar_week` on Case Z is mis-bucketed.** It is known at `t0`. The problem is proxy/drift interpretation, not temporal ambiguity. Listing it beside missing sequence indexes and mutable scores conflates two different failure modes. The “do not delete every correlated feature” rule is slightly violated here.

4. **`final_handler_team` is underscored as high.** It is populated at closure with no as-of timestamp. The 65% coincidence with the `t0` team does not make it available at `t0`; critical would be more consistent with the other closure fields.

5. **Prefix-variant repair is oversold.** Replacing `full_trace_variant_id` with `prefix_variant_id` is methodologically right, but immediately after the first coverage check the prefix is nearly constant (`Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`) and would not explain case-level differences. That limitation is not stated.

6. **Section 2 is a checklist, not a suite.** Alignment, footprint, DFG, and declarative checks are named with limitations, but there are no concrete fixtures, expected pass/fail outcomes, or an operational test sequence.

7. **Class membership is muddy.** `prior_claim_count_before_t0` appears as defensible, proxy, and unusable; Case Y then lists it under both `credible_drivers` and `removed_or_repaired_drivers`. The repair logic is recoverable, but the presentation is unclear.

8. **The four standards are implied, not stated.** Predictive validity, causal interpretation, operational actionability, and fairness review are present in “may claim / may not claim” form, but never set out as distinct evaluation bars as the task asked.

None of these reverse the main conclusions. They keep the answer clearly above a routine audit and clearly below a clean 9+.
