**7.6**

The answer is a strong, format-compliant expert write-up: it separates timing, routing, and decision-policy interventions; keeps four-eyes and mandatory rework rules intact; treats snapshots as forecasts; and is unusually clear that K-337 changes the decision standard rather than the clock. Those are the main things the rubric asks for. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless.

**K-104 overstates the local gain.** The intervention is a reservation, so any large advance of `C_f` is preemption, not a free start. Monday–Tuesday C1 load is 16–20 h, and the factual Wed 13:10 start is what an 8-hour-day drain of that backlog predicts. A Tuesday-evening slot therefore requires jumping a large queue. Holding other behavior “approximately fixed,” the only robust local effect is 1–3 h of reusable preparation plus any true idle gap—not the headline 4–16 h (best 10–16 h). Preliminary C1 work is also left unscheduled against that same backlog: C1 cannot prepare this case without displacing someone else. The capacity section later admits this; the case table and derivation do not apply it to the numbers.

**K-337 arithmetic is sloppy.** Friday-evening close vs factual Wed 09:10 is ~110 h; Monday-morning close is ~48 h. “Early Monday, saving 72–110 h” is wrong (Monday morning is ~48–56 h). “Monday or Tuesday, saving 48–96 h” is wrong for Tuesday (Tue–Wed is ~14–24 h) and 96 h is not a Monday figure. The table’s Friday/Monday envelope is better than the derivation that is supposed to justify it. Incomplete evidence should also have been allowed to stretch C1 beyond the “evidence complete” 3–6 h band.

**K-411’s 8–14 h best case is weakly supported.** A ~16:30 join vs 19:30 still sits behind Tuesday’s grown A2 queue and the training-day note. Same-day A2/M1 is a thin tail, not a serious upper bound. The 0–4 h ordinary case is the credible one.

**K-219 under-models “slower.”** Handwriting is handled as a discrete A2-issued RMI branch, while the clean pass still uses D2’s 4–8 h standard band. A slower first pass without formal rework is the obvious middle scenario and is missing. Rework is also kept on D2; sending the hard handwriting back to D1 is the natural alternative and is ignored. Overnight D2 start is possible but not implied by this case’s own morning D1 start.

**100-case discussion is qualitatively right and quantitatively thin.** Saturation, D1↔D2 substitution, rework feedback, A2 bunching, weekend external delay, and false concurrency are all named. What is missing is the obvious capacity check: 100 C1 reservations or 100 D2 handwritten checks in a week is an order-of-magnitude overload of a one- or two-person pool, not a mild nonlinearity. That weakens the interference analysis relative to the rest of the piece.

**Smaller nits.** Working-hour vs continuous drain is used when convenient (D1 overnight in K-104, not in K-219) without stating the rule. Assumption 4 flags M1 nights but not C1’s apparent daytime pattern. The eight assumptions are well labeled; they do not rescue the local/system contradiction on reservations.

Format, structure, constraint discipline, and the K-337 policy point are well above average. The grade is held down by optimistic best cases, one clear time-arithmetic error, and a local counterfactual that does not fully respect the “others fixed” instruction the answer itself announces.
