This is a strong, process-aware audit that hits the rubric’s “strong answer” bar: it catches both obvious leakage and lineage bugs, keeps queue/prefix-age/complexity, treats `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` as semantically late-legal but `t0`-inadmissible, treats `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` as impossibility plus tie-break failure, and keeps predictive / causal / actionable / fairness standards apart. It is not nearly flawless.

**Section 1** is the high-water mark. Coverage of the top-12, all five patterns, and the X/Y/Z-only fields is complete, and employee-experience (not even required as a displayed local driver) is correctly flagged. Severity calls are mostly right: duration, appeal-anywhere, final team, full-trace variant, final decision, last role, and the closed→assessment pattern belong at critical; the SQL `extraction_date` bound and same-day 23:00 snapshot belong at high; queue, frozen complexity, and elapsed-at-`t0` are correctly preserved. The 65% `final_handler_team` coincidence is handled as a non-join, not as partial justification.

Defects here are still real. Several “Why” cells are missing the temporal operator (`timestamps  t0`, `event time  t0`), which is sloppy in an audit whose whole point is point-in-time legality. `initial_reserve` is graded `low–medium`, which is not in the required `{critical, high, medium, low}` scale. Sequence-pattern severities are slightly uneven: `Documents Requested → Documents Received` and `Damage Assessment → Settlement` are the same class of post-`t0` pattern as appeal→decision, yet only the latter is critical. Team causal copy (“appeals cause,” “final team determines”) is only partly prosecuted in the table; most of that work is deferred to later sections.

**Section 2** is credible and usable: PIT replay, Declare-style constraints, tie handling, complete-trace detectors, enabled vs impossible vs future-possible, and indexed second decisions. The DFG-is-not-normative warning is exactly what the prompt asked for. Limitations are stated, not just namedropped. Residual gap: the suite is described well but never specifies concrete fail-closed artifacts (e.g., what happens to a local explanation that cites a future-possible but unobserved fraud opening).

**Section 3** is conceptually clean and follows “do not delete every proxy.” The five-way split and the four epistemic standards are correct. The repairable-vs-forever-invalid split (prior count / as-of score vs duration / appeal-seen / variant / final decision) is the right distinction.

**Section 4 is the largest miss against the written spec.**  
- Case Y puts `prior_claim_count_before_t0` in **both** `credible_drivers` and `removed_or_repaired_drivers`, then tells the reader to treat the “credible” driver as unreliable. That is not a coherent `t0` explanation.  
- The prompt forbids reproducing leaked future outcomes in revised narratives. Case X’s narrative still names “later appeals” and “the eventual partial decision.” Case Y’s narrative names document loops, appeal, and a second decision. Those are auditor asides stuffed into the case narrative field.  
- “Elevated risk score” in Case X is ambiguous next to Case Z’s mutable `risk_score_current`.  
- Neither X nor Y notes that `base value and other features` is itself contaminated, so the retained 0.82 / 0.71 figures are not just “recompute for purity”—they are currently uninterpretable. Status `recompute` / `invalidate` is still the right trichotomy.

**Section 5** covers every required workstream (feature store, PIT timestamps, prefix recompute, conformance CI, post-removal calibration, stability, fairness, drift). Priority order is sensible. The line “Predictive use  operational lever” is garbled (likely a dropped ≠), which again matters because the distinction is the recommendation.

Net: expert-level leakage diagnosis with a few structural contradictions, an explicit narrative-instruction violation, and recurring missing operators. That is well above a weak SHAP-as-gospel answer, and well short of a 9+.
