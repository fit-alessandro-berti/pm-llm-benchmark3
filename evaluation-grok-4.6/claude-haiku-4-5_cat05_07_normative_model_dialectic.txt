The write-up is a competent legal-process brief dressed up as a conformance review. Sections 1–5 are mostly well structured and often right on the hard cases. Sections 6–7, plus evidence hygiene, are not close to the standard the prompt set. Under a hypercritical reading this is a mid-pack answer, not a strong one.

## What is actually good

The heading contract is met. Both sides are argued at real length. The brief does not collapse into a 50/50 split: SUP-703 and SUP-708 are treated as genuine control failures; PD-3/FD-7 expiry is treated as dispositive; SUP-707/709/710 are treated as model or scope problems rather than misconduct. The evidence matrix is complete for all 15 cases and usually separates M0 deviation from policy overlay from data defect. Allowed labels are used; `MIXED` is not used as a dodge. The layered-architecture *idea* (POWL vs DECLARE/log-skeleton vs pandas vs org controls) is the right shape.

Those are necessary features. They are not nearly sufficient.

## Evidence traceability fails the assignment

The prompt scores evidence traceability. The answer invents material that is not in N1–N5 or the case table:

- an “email chain” / “email evidence” that a permanent amendment was imminent
- a “CRM mapping job in backlog”
- “flood-status memos”
- a PD-3 amount cap of EUR 50,000 (PD-3 has no cap; EUR 20,000 is an FD-7 rule)
- “~80” notification-defect cases (the sources give a 2%→29% missingness rate, not a headcount)
- “process documentation says pre-screen acceptance gates M0” (the file only records a process-owner / model-owner dispute and an ambiguous scope document)

That is not paraphrase. It is fabricated support. In a review whose job is to mark propositions *supported / disputed / unproved*, this is a first-order defect.

There are also false or sloppy citations of the actual record:

- Prosecution: “Notify_Requester is a mandatory step (M0 and **CS-9**).” CS-9 never mentions notification. M0 does. Collapsing them inflates the notification theory of liability.
- Defense on SUP-705: “Did transactions occur before **sanctions clearance**? Unknown from the trace.” The trace is `Sanctions_Check` then `Activate_Supplier`. That timing is known. The open question is spend versus *retrospective approval*, not sanctions.
- SUP-704 is written as if criticality were unconfirmed. The case text already calls it a critical pump supplier under FD-7.
- EUR 180,000 on SUP-703 is compared to “the limit” / the FD-7 cap. FD-7 does not govern that case. The spend is still aggravating; the comparison is legally careless.
- The 15-row table is treated as a 27% operational compliance rate. Those rows are a designed conflict set, not a sample. The usable aggregates are 289/312, 14 ineligible, 9 incomplete, 47 info-request cases, 6 August auto-approvals.

## Model semantics are wrong in ways the prompt made central

The revised POWL does not do what the prompt required, and several claims about POWL are false.

1. **Emergency/provisional route is absent from the POWL.** The spec lists it as a minimum construct. It appears only as commentary and DataFrames. A model that still forces `checks → decision → activate` cannot replay SUP-704 even when FD-7 is valid.

2. **Authorized delayed quality is also absent.** PD-3 and FD-7 allow Quality_Review after provisional activation. In the code, `checks` (sanctions **and** quality) still precede every decision. SUP-702 would remain nonconforming to the “revised” model.

3. **“POWL cannot natively express arbitrary loops” is false.** `Operator.LOOP` is the standard construct. The answer’s XOR of one `Request_More_Info` versus a skip is a weaker approximation than the language already provides, and the limitation note is therefore not a careful approximation—it is a capability error.

4. **The XOR is ill-formed.** The same `activate` object is a node in both `approved_manual` and `approved_auto`. Shared identity across XOR children is not a legal POWL tree. Construction may not throw; the model is still invalid.

5. **`SilentTransition(label="_skip_request")` is not a silent skip.** A labeled silent transition is either rejected or becomes a visible activity the log will never contain.

6. **Request_More_Info is placed after both checks.** SUP-707 is `Sanctions_Check, Request_More_Info, Request_More_Info, Quality_Review`. The approximation cannot fit the very case used to justify the loop.

7. **Reactivation is a second, unconnected model with new labels** (`Reactivate_Supplier`, `Sanctions_Check_Reactivation`, …). It cannot be used for conformance on SUP-710, whose events are ordinary `Sanctions_Check` / `Activate_Supplier`.

8. **The prompt asked for one executable POWL block.** The answer emits multiple blocks, unused duplicates (`request_or_no`), print-dump schemas, and empty DataFrames. That is scaffolding, not a conformance model.

9. **N2’s automated master-data validation before activation is omitted** from both the POWL and the overlay rules.

So the “revised normative architecture” talks correctly about layers and then ships a control-flow object that is incomplete, partly invalid, and still blind to the authorized overlays that motivated the revision.

## Scope and authority: better, not clean

Expiry is handled well: PD-3 and FD-7 are time-boxed; informal continuation is not an amendment; SUP-705 and SUP-714 are noncompliant unless a written instrument appears. Resource rules are correctly located in CS-9 rather than M0. Scope ambiguity on rejected leads and reactivations is real and is mostly treated as unproved rather than as guilt.

The misses matter. Prosecution was required to *acknowledge* genuine model/data defects; it mostly files them under “not an excuse.” That is advocacy, not the mandated dual burden. Defense was required to accept limits on informal practice; it does so on 703/708 in the end, but only after inflating “reasonable belief” and “imminent amendment” beyond the file. Burden-of-proof text also wobbles: retrospective judgment is called invalid, then post-expiry cases are said to need retrospective authorization.

## Case calls, graded tightly

Most headline labels are defensible (701 compliant; 702 justified; 703/708 noncompliant; 707 model defect; 706/711 data; 712/715 noncompliant on non-flow rules). Several are softer than they look:

- SUP-702 is `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` on PD-3, but the onboarding trace still lacks `Notify_Requester`. The CRM ID makes this data-qualified, not a clean overlay win. The matrix underplays that.
- SUP-713 as `COMPLIANT` is generous. Equivalence is post-hoc by a week. Substantive equivalence ≠ contemporaneous authority.
- SUP-711 as `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` is timid. Once the duplicate register is a same-transaction-ID migration clone, the remaining sequence is ordinary M0. The residual uncertainty is low.
- SUP-708 as `SEVERE` while also recording that no pre-clearance transactions occurred overstates operational harm. The sequencing breach is real; the severity argument needed that distinction in the final table, not only in a footnote.
- SUP-715 is a disclosure/COI-policy breach. The text sometimes treats the spousal/reporting-line fact as if it were itself forbidden. The source says it is not explicitly prohibited.

## Dialectical fairness

This is the strongest dimension and still not excellent. Each brief is long and pointed. Each side’s overreach is later named. But fairness is damaged by invented exhibits, by CS-9/M0 fusion, by the 15-case “rate,” and by a defense that spends more energy on atmospheric justification than on the actual limits of minutes, emails, and expired instruments.

## Net

A nearly flawless answer would have kept every proposition inside the record, encoded emergency and delayed-quality routes in POWL, used `Operator.LOOP` (or a stated *correct* bound), avoided shared XOR nodes, and put amount/risk/identity rules only in the overlay tables. This answer does the essay well and the model/evidence job poorly. That is a 5.9, not a high-7, and nowhere near the top of the scale.
