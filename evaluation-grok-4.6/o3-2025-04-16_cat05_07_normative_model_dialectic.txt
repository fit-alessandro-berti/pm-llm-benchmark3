The answer is structurally complete and gets many case calls right, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. It fabricates authority, misstates POWL semantics, and ships a revised model that cannot express the routes it claims to add.

**Fabricated and sloppy evidence.** The prosecution invents “CS-9 §9” on tamper-resistant records; that section is not in the materials. PD-3 is inflated into a “signed” competent-authority overlay even though it is minutes/email, never folded into CS-9, and later disclaimed by the Compliance observer. SUP-703 is scored against the FD-7 €20,000 cap, which does not govern that case. Defense invents a management intent to amend CS-9. Those are not stylistic slips; they are unsound traceability.

**Required dialectical posture is only half-met.** Prosecution treats M0 as essentially correct and treats logging failure as guilt, instead of conceding real model/scope/data defects (no loop, no resource rule, contested lead/reactivation scope, CRM break). Defense waves at 703/708 and then argues informal FD-7 continuation and “don’t punish initiative.” The final section mostly splits the difference and never cleanly rejects the weakest claim on each side.

**Case work is uneven.** 701, 703, 704, 706–709, 712, and 714 are defensible. Several are not. SUP-702’s trace omits `Notify_Requester`; the matrix never says so. SUP-713 is called COMPLIANT even though the QA_Clearance mapping was approved after the fact. SUP-715 is called NONCOMPLIANT though no rule bars the spousal relationship and the only proven gap is a missing COI disclosure. SUP-711 is a duplicate log row with a shared transaction ID, not an indeterminate process. “0 minutes lag” under FD-7 is also wrong: FD-7 still requires Sanctions_Check **before** activation.

**The revised POWL is the decisive failure.** POWL has `Operator.LOOP`; saying it does not is a basic semantics error. The “loop” is a **cycle inside `StrictPartialOrder`**, which is not a partial order and is not a valid approximation. `Operator.AND` is not the PM4Py/POWL idiom (M0 correctly uses an unordered `StrictPartialOrder`; the process-tree operator is `PARALLEL`). `Provisional_Activate` does not exist in the log. Worse, `checks_parallel` still forces Quality_Review **before every** decision, so the auto-approve and emergency routes cannot replay PD-3/FD-7 (quality and/or manual approval after activation). `Request_More_Info` is also placed after both checks, so it cannot replay SUP-707, the very “desirable loop” the brief says must be modeled. Eligibility, amounts, identity, and authority are correctly pushed out of POWL in prose, but the control-flow layer itself does not deliver the minimum behaviors the prompt required.

**Architecture is a sketch, not a design.** Layering POWL / DECLARE / pandas / governance is the right idea. Scope rules for rejected leads, reactivations, and withdrawals are named and then not specified. The overlay table omits resource/COI/reactivation rules. Silent, labeled loop bookkeeping and a new provisional activity paper over log/model mismatch instead of stating a defensible skip/XOR structure.

In short: competent outline, several correct classifications, and a broken normative model sitting on invented citations. That is mid-range work, not a strong review.
