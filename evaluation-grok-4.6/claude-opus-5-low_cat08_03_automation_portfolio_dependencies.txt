**7.8**

This is a strong, process-literate portfolio memo: it refuses frequency-first ranking, keeps downstream bots behind control prerequisites, and uses the DFG loops, resource network, and control findings as real constraints rather than decoration. It is not close to flawless. Several load-bearing claims are sloppy, one constraint is misread, and the recommended Wave 1 is less consistent with the author’s own diagnosis than they admit.

**What is done well**

The opening instinct is right: elapsed time is waits and synchronization, not touch time. A1 is correctly demoted with a Little’s-law / WIP argument, the after-15:00 back-office utilization figure, and the 72% already-digital note. Sequencing is mostly sound: A4 before A3 (3 FTE, two-senior handover concentration, leftover-exception density), A6 before A1/A2 (analyst overload warning in the brief), A12 before A9 (shared rule library), A10 only after a hard KYC gate, A8 only after the four packaging inputs, A11 last. The three named portfolios have honest sacrifice statements. Q is correctly rejected for missing the compliance mandate and for industrializing provisional signing. DFG arcs (3300/2800, 1750/1160, 650/510, 2400→2350→1980, 7250 vs 3940) are used as operational objects, not citations. Concurrency of KYC and financial-document review is flagged so cycle-time gains are not double-counted. Resource-demand shifts (analysts, specialists, legal month-end, RM friction) are treated as first-class.

**Material analytical flaws**

The lede overstates the problem size. Summing every median wait to “250–300 hours” adds mutually exclusive variant activities (31h mismatch on 1,750 cases, 58h valuation on 2,350, 8h missing-docs on 3,300) as if they sat on one path. A typical path is closer to ~170–190h even before concurrency; worst-case tails can reach the cited range. “Registration is ~1% of elapsed time” survives either denominator; the 250–300 frame does not. That is a bad error in a process-mining argument.

Waiting-time *mass* (frequency × median wait) is never computed, yet the final section calls A4 and A12 among “the four largest controllable sources of elapsed time.” Approximate masses: signing ~321k case-hours, credit analysis ~249k, contract ~167k, financial-document review ~166k, valuation ~136k, normalize ~104k, packaging ~101k, mismatch only ~54k. A12 has no tabulated wait at all. A4 is justified by critical-path presence, rework, and scarce specialists — that is a fair *different* argument — but it is not a largest-elapsed-time argument. A12 belongs in Wave 1 because Compliance required a control module, not because it moves the clock.

That same mass view exposes the biggest portfolio hole. The author says the process problem is waiting and synchronization, then leaves the two longest waits (58h, 44h) out of year one. A10’s deferral is earned (6.5% provisional signing). A7’s is not. Classification already exists at 11,820 cases; A7 does not require an automated A2 to trigger valuation after classify instead of after `Credit analysis → Collateral valuation request` (2,400). Partner dependency and slice-specificity (71% of equipment finance, 18% of working capital) are real, but they do not make A7 a Wave-2-only item. The unexamined set **A7 + A6 + A5 + A12** fits the author’s own thesis better than B: largest external synchronization point, analyst constraint, largest rework loop, and the mandatory control module — still without A10’s legal prerequisite. Portfolio D pairs A7 with A10 and is rejected for A10’s sake; that is a stacked deck.

**Variant blindness**

Micro / SME / High-exposure and WC / equipment / renewal are used as facts, not as design. High-exposure is bound by weekly committee cadence and four-eyes (5.8% month-end); equipment finance is bound by the 58h external wait; renewals by the 14% contract-remediation loop; a large share of credit analyses never hit normalize (5,200 / 8,900). A6 is ranked #1 as if it sat on the main path for the book. It does not. A one-portfolio-for-all Wave 1 is a process-level miss given how much variant structure the prompt supplied.

**Internal inconsistencies**

Section 2 says A1 and (partly) A10 need deep core-banking integration. Portfolio Q is then described as integration-light. Q is A1+A2+A5+A10 — two deep modules, not one. The dependency “spine” `A1 → A2 → A5 → A6 → …` is written as a hard quality chain; Wave 1 then ships A5 and A6 with neither A1 nor A2. Existing human classification can feed A5; the spine should have distinguished hard gates from quality enhancers. A6 is ranked above A5 while the same matrix lists A5 as a prerequisite for A6.

The two-deep-integration limit applies to **the first six months**, not the year. Holding “one slot in reserve for Wave 2 (months 6–12)” misreads the constraint. After month 6 the cap lifts. The Wave 1 choice can still be defended on risk and prerequisite grounds; the reserve-slot story cannot. Wave 2 inside year one also collides with “at most four modules in the first year” unless A12 is treated as almost free — the hedge is present, the rollout calendar still spends six-plus modules before month 12.

A4 as “workflow layer only” is optimistic. Entity resolution usually touches customer-master / KYC systems of record; claiming Wave 1 uses “at most one” deep slot (and maybe zero if A6 is read-mostly) is convenient, not evidenced.

**Smaller defects**

Classify’s 8% exception rate is treated as an 8% misroute rate. “Double-digit-percent median reduction” for B is asserted without a critical-path decomposition by variant. Format is not exact: a long framing note sits above the seven required top-level sections. Committee cadence is used to limit A8 but not to qualify B’s cycle-time claim for High-exposure. Financial-document review’s 18h × 9,200 wait-mass is only indirectly attacked via the shared 9-FTE analyst pool; that link should have been explicit.

**Bottom line**

The memo would be usable in a real steering committee. It is not a 9: the opening metric is wrong, A7 is under-weighted relative to the author’s own theory, variants are not designed for, and integration/budget constraints are partly misapplied. Those are structural, not cosmetic.
