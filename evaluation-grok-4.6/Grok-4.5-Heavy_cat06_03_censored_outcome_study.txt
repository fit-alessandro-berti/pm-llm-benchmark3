7.0

The design is conceptually strong and avoids the classic failure modes (rejected-as-non-default, random split, uncritical use of the 21,600-row export). It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several inconsistencies, incomplete as-of rules, and implementation bugs are material.

**What is actually good.** The primary estimand is correctly restricted to matured activated accounts and is not sold as a universal default probability. Rejected, withdrawn, open, transferred, and immature cases are partitioned rather than recoded as non-events, and the 48,000-row flow table is arithmetically exhaustive. The missing-label taxonomy, leakage table, temporal-split rationale, and decision-use boundary are the right objects. The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points and does not invent a survival API.

**Target and estimand (Section 1).** The write-up contradicts itself. It denies a counterfactual reading and then invites operators to treat the score as “default risk *if activated*” on every application. That second sentence is exactly the untestable claim later disavowed. A strict answer would keep \(P(Y=1\mid\text{prefix},\text{activated under historical policy})\) and state that scoring non-activated cases is extrapolation, not a well-defined “if activated” risk. Horizon is defined from activation, not from \(\tau\), so time-to-outcome is heterogeneous; that is never named.

**Cohort and censoring (Sections 2–3).** Complete-case analysis of the 21,600 is defensible *only* as an activation-conditional association, and that qualification is present. It is still a convenient cohort: maturity filtering further selects fast-to-activate, early-start cases, and that induced selection is not put in the flow table. Still-open is defined by `end_time` near extract; stalled applications with an old last event would be dumped into “Other.” Delayed labels are correctly distinguished in prose, then treated as ordinary right-censoring in the survival sensitivity (`event = 0` if immature). That biases the 90-day endpoint whenever the horizon has elapsed but the feed has not. IPW is listed as if it were a routine robustness check; without unconfoundedness it does not recover default-if-activated. Transfer linkage and partner MNAR are acknowledged but not given an explicit primary rule beyond “exclude.”

**Prefixes, features, leakage (Sections 4–6).** The stated rule—\(k\)th event before Accept/activation—is the right rule and is not implemented. `get_prefixes_from_log` is run on full activated traces, so short happy paths can put Accept, Contract Signed, or Account Activated inside a \(k=4\)–\(5\) prefix. The later `timestamp <= pred_time` filter is tautological. Elapsed-time prefixes are specified and then absent from the code. `default_90d` / `label_available_at` are aggregated with `"first"` after a time sort; given “repeated on events when known, otherwise missing,” early rows are the ones most likely to be NA, so the labelled cohort can silently collapse. `post_policy` is a dead feature: train and validation both end before 1 Jul 2025, so the indicator is identically 0 in every fitted row and cannot do the work claimed in Sections 5 and 7. Resource experience and `documents_total` are handled correctly in the table; `collection_status`’s “valid availability rule” states when the field exists, not when it may be used.

**Validation (Section 7).** Refusing a random split is correct. The proposed windows still have holes. Almost no application *started* after the 15 Oct 2025 migration can mature a 90-day label by 15 Feb 2026, so “post-migration hold-out if sufficient cases exist” is mostly empty and should have been said plainly. Late test starts that *are* mature are selected for short time-to-activation; that is not discussed. Training only pre-policy and testing post-policy is a transport check, not a deployment-relevant fit, and the text blurs the two. Metrics are appropriate for the stated binary target.

**Code (Section 9).** Useful as a sketch, not as a pipeline that would reproduce the design. Unused import, empty `ACTIVITY_MAP`, prefixes only on train, no pre-terminal cut, unsafe label aggregation, and a survival stub that conflates delay with censoring. Cohort prints will not recover the six-row table in Section 2 because `terminal_type` is too crude.

**Net.** This is a competent, honest PPM design with the right scientific posture. It is not rigorous enough in the places the prompt said it would be judged: as-of discipline, label-delay versus censoring, selection into the 21,600, and a skeleton that actually enforces the rules in the prose. Those are not nits; they are the difference between a careful memo and a study that can be implemented without reintroducing leakage or a biased estimand.
