**4.6**

The answer has the right skeleton and several defensible case calls, but it fails mandatory briefing constraints, misstates authority, underuses the strongest evidence, and then ships an invalid POWL. That is not a near-miss. It is a complete-looking brief with broken semantics.

## Failed mandatory constraints

The prompt required the prosecution to acknowledge genuine model/data defects and the defense to acknowledge genuine risk and limits on informal practice. Neither brief does that.

The prosecution treats CRM missingness as an accountability vacuum and never concedes that M0 cannot express `Request_More_Info`, that rejected-lead and reactivation scope are ambiguous, that `QA_Clearance` is a post-hoc label mapping, or that duplicate `Register_Supplier` rows share a source transaction ID. Those concessions were not optional color. They were the price of a fair prosecution.

The defense is worse. It minimizes SUP-703 as UI error and SUP-708 as a “sequence artifact,” then asserts that SUP-714 used a policy that was “universally acknowledged as beneficial and subsequently formalized.” That last claim is false. The record says no permanent amendment existed. “Universally acknowledged” is also false: the Compliance observer later said she had not approved weakening CS-9. The defense therefore argues from invented ratification.

## Authority and scope are handled loosely

PD-3’s contested status is the central authority problem in the file. The answer never uses it. PD-3 was minutes and email, not an amendment to CS-9. The observer allowed a “monitored exception” and then denied having approved a CS-9 weakening. A serious brief would have treated 2026-06-01–2026-07-31 as a time-boxed exception with disputed legal force, not as an ordinary valid overlay that simply “expired.”

Calling **CS-9 obsolete** in the system judgment is an authority error. M0 is incomplete relative to CS-9 and to practice. CS-9 remains the formally approved standard. Collapsing them lets the defense smuggle informal practice into the baseline.

Scope is named (SUP-709, SUP-710) but never specified. Section 6 was required to give explicit rules for rejected leads, reactivations, **and withdrawals**. Withdrawals are absent. The lead/reactivation rules never become operational criteria.

## Evidence errors and unused aggregates

The matrix is complete, which is the strongest part of the answer. It is not reliable.

- SUP-704’s prosecution cell says FD-7 “expired or was over-applied.” FD-7 was in force on 2026-07-11. That is a fact error, not a steelman.
- SUP-702’s prosecution cell says PD-3 was “non-binding,” while the judgment treats the same case as a justified exception. The two sections do not share a theory of authority.
- PD-3 required automated master-data validation before activation. No case, brief, or model mentions it.
- Aggregates are almost unused: 312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 with missing eligibility attributes, 47 `Request_More_Info` cases with no premature activation, notification missingness 2%→29%, six August post-pilot cases, Internal Audit flags on SUP-703 and SUP-708.
- SUP-712 is a written CS-9 SoD rule (“requester and manual approver must be different humans”), not an unmodeled preference. `MIXED` is a dodge. The dominant finding is **NONCOMPLIANT** on a resource rule that M0 simply failed to encode.
- SUP-715 is closer to genuine `MIXED` (no explicit spousal ban; missing COI disclosure). Using `MIXED` twice, once correctly and once to avoid a clear CS-9 hit, looks like a template.

Several classifications are still reasonable: 701 compliant; 703/708/714 noncompliant; 704 justified if FD-7 is accepted; 705 noncompliant after automatic expiry; 706/711 data-quality; 707/709/710/713 model-or-scope. That is not enough to rescue the dialectic. The prompt forbade splitting the difference; the system judgment then declares operations “materially noncompliant” *and* CS-9/M0 “obsolete.” That is the split, with harsher words.

“High confidence” is not earned. Mutable CRM links, alias identity, post-hoc label mapping, and an ambiguous scope document are exactly why confidence should be case-stratified.

## The revised POWL is not a valid model of the required process

This is the decisive technical failure.

**Shared nodes.** `sanctions`, `quality`, `activate`, and `manual_approval` are reused across XOR children. POWL children must be disjoint. Shared `Transition` objects make an invalid object graph, not a compact encoding.

**`Request_More_Info` is not repeatable.** `XOR(τ, Request_More_Info)` is optional-once. SUP-707 has two cycles. PM4Py exposes `Operator.LOOP`; the comment even gestures at loops, then does not use one. The approximation cannot replay the motivating case.

**Placement is wrong even for the optional-once version.** The info block is ordered *after both* sanctions and quality. SUP-707 is `Sanctions_Check → Request_More_Info → Request_More_Info → Quality_Review`. The proposed standard route cannot produce that trace.

**The XOR is in the wrong place.** M0 does checks, then chooses approve vs reject. The revision does `Register → XOR(standard, auto, emergency, reject)`. Rejection after completed checks is inexpressible; rejection with no checks is always legal. That inverts CS-9 and cannot represent SUP-709’s actual path (`Register, Sanctions, Reject, Notify, Close`) as a checked reject.

**Route objects are sloppy.** Two different `Activate_Supplier` transitions exist, while other activities are shared. Auto-approve and emergency paths are always enabled in the XOR; the text correctly says POWL cannot enforce eligibility, then does not isolate that limitation to data guards. Quality is forced to complete before the XOR can exit to `Notify_Requester`, which is an extra control-flow constraint PD-3 does not impose.

The overlay table is the only part of §7 that is close to the prompt: stable `notification_id`, rule/version IDs, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution, delegation evidence, immutable timestamps. It cannot compensate for a model that is neither valid POWL nor a defensible approximation of the required behaviors.

## What a strong answer had to do and this one did not

A strong prosecution would have conceded M0/N4/N5 defects, then still convicted 703, 708, 712, 714, 705, and the 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases on surviving CS-9 rules. A strong defense would have conceded those risks, then confined its win to scope, logging, labels, and in-window overlays. This answer writes both speeches, then lets each side overclaim.

Net: competent outline, several fair case labels, one useful overlay table, and then factual invention, unused controlling evidence, a CS-9/M0 authority collapse, and an executable-looking model that is semantically illegal and cannot generate the loop it was asked to represent. Under a hypercritical rubric that reserves high scores for near-flawless work, that is a mid-fail, not a high pass.
