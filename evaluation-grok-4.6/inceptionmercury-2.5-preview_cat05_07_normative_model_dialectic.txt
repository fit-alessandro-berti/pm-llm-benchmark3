4.8

The answer is structurally complete and gets several core case calls right (701–705, 703 vs 702, 704 vs 705, 708, 714). That is not enough. Under a strict reading it fails the task’s hardest requirements: strongest-possible briefs, internally consistent evidence, and a revised POWL that actually encodes the overlays the analysis accepts.

**Section 1.** The burden of proof is wrong. Material risk is a severity input, not an element of noncompliance. A sanctions-before-activation failure is noncompliant even if no spend occurred. The write-up also treats PD-3 as a settled overlay and never records the live authority dispute: CS-9 is controlled and approved; PD-3 is minutes plus email, never incorporated, and the compliance observer later denied approving any weakening of CS-9. “Case traces are immutable” is false given N4’s mutable CRM links. The stripped “Requester  Approver” phrasing is unclear.

**Section 2 (prosecution).** Not the strongest case, and it violates an explicit instruction: the prosecution must acknowledge genuine model/data defects. It does not concede concurrent-check limits, the missing RMI loop, CRM missingness (2% → 29%), duplicate register with the same source transaction ID, or label equivalence. It underuses the best prosecution facts: PD-3’s weak instrument form, the observer recant, 14 ineligible plus 9 incomplete Auto_Approve records, six August post-pilot cases, and Internal Audit’s identification of SUP-703 and SUP-708. Luck is not a control (SUP-708 had no pre-clearance spend). Informal “still flooding” / “permanent approval imminent” is exactly how exceptions become the system of record.

**Section 3 (defense).** The 708 claim that “M0 ignores order” is a factual error. In M0, `checks` (Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review) precedes `decision`, and the approved branch is Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier. Sanctions-before-activation is in the normative model. A strong defense would argue limited harm, not a missing order constraint. The brief also fails to press its best evidence: 289/312 Auto_Approve cases appear PD-3-eligible; 47 RMI cases never activated before evidence arrived; SUP-702 has a stable `notification_id`; SUP-711 is a duplicated row, not a double registration. “Must acknowledge genuine risk” is only a closing sentence, not an argument.

**Section 4.** The matrix contradicts the briefs. Section 3 rejects treating 703 as a model defect and 714 as normalized policy; the matrix then does both. SUP-702’s M0 issues are understated (Auto_Approve, Quality after Activate, Notify absent from the onboarding log). SUP-703’s eligibility is not “missing.” SUP-713’s mapping was approved a week *after* the case; calling that a clean semantic match buries the retroactive-authorization problem. SUP-715 is not a CS-9 segregation failure; the sources say the relationship is not prohibited and the gap is undisclosed conflict of interest. Equating 715 with 712 is a category error. “What would reverse” is often empty or inverted.

**Section 5.** Primary labels for 701, 702, 703, 704, 705, 706, 707, 708, 709, 712, 713, and 714 are mostly defensible. The rest is not.

- Severity is required per case and is not systematically given.
- SUP-711 is not indeterminate: same source transaction ID plus an otherwise M0-fitting path is a log duplicate on a compliant case.
- SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT at the same grade as 712 overreaches; control-flow fits CS-9/M0, and the live issue is disclosure.
- SUP-710 is too clean as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT. CS-9 requires Quality_Review and manual approval for *activation*. Reactivation is an activation with a missing quality event; scope ambiguity does not automatically extinguish that rule.
- SUP-709 still executed `Register_Supplier`. That weakens “outside M0 / pre-screen only.”
- The 60/20/20 system split does not match the fifteen classifications (roughly 2 compliant, 2 justified, 6 noncompliant, 3 model/scope, 2 data). Those percentages are not derived from the matrix.
- The judgment correctly refuses to treat expiry as policy, then criticizes the prosecution for “equating” 703 and 705 while itself labeling both NONCOMPLIANT without a usable severity distinction.

**Sections 6–7 (decisive failure).** The architecture sketch is directionally right (POWL vs data constraints vs overlays vs org rules) and then the executable model reconstructs the same mistake M0 is accused of: it still forces `checks = Sanctions ∥ Quality` *before* the XOR, so Quality remains a predecessor of Auto_Approve, emergency activation, and reject. That model cannot replay SUP-702, SUP-704, or SUP-709, i.e. the very justified and scope cases the opinion accepts. Further defects:

- `Request_More_Info` is instantiated and never added to any POWL node. A comment is not an approximation. `Operator.LOOP` is the native encoding and is unused.
- `Emergency_Provisional_Activation` is an invented activity that does not appear in the log; emergency traces use `Activate_Supplier` before Quality/Manual.
- `M1.order.add_edge(decision, t_notify)` is ill-formed: `t_notify` is not a child of `M1` (`notify_close` is). Close is not correctly ordered from the decision.
- The overlay table omits SoD, COI disclosure, RMI, reject-after-sanctions, reactivation, and a post-window prohibition on Auto_Approve. Inventing “no Quality if recent certification” states a policy CS-9 does not contain.
- Rejected-lead scope as “pre-screen is NOT in M1” does not deal with leads that already registered.

The opinion does not split the difference as a political compromise, and it is right that expiry and ineligible Auto_Approve are real control failures. It is not a reliable independent review: it misstates M0 on 708, contradicts itself on 703/714, fabricates a system-level mix, and ships a revised POWL that cannot represent the processes it recommends.
