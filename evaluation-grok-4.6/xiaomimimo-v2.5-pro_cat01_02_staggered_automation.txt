The write-up is structurally complete and conceptually strong: it separates availability from intensity, treats selection and co-treatments as first-order, keeps median and p90 apart, refuses a single “X%” claim, and pairs a defensible staggered event-study design with a learning-oriented rollout. Those are the right scientific instincts. Under a strict reading of the rubric, several concrete failures keep it well below a high score.

**Format.** Section 1 is over the 150–250 word cap (about 300). Section 8 is well over 400 words (roughly 550–650). Both are explicit constraints.

**Quantitative discipline.** The main pre/post tables are mostly accurate, but not clean. Epsilon’s pre-period arrivals per FTE is given as 31.5; the Jan–Mar mean is about 30.3 (31.5 is March only). The sensitivity upper bound of ~9.4 hours for Gamma vs Delta mixes Gamma’s Jan–Jun→Jul–Aug change with Delta’s Jan–Mar→Apr–Aug change — that is not a valid DiD. That invalid contrast then props up the 0–9 hour median range. Jan–Mar is also labeled “pre-fraud-desk” even though the fraud desk starts in March (the text earlier states this correctly). The −13.2 hour p90 bound is not reconstructed from matched windows. The pooled −0.52 intensity correlation is largely a Gamma level effect, not a within-branch dose gradient.

**Section 9.** The code is self-contained and appropriately cautious, but it never computes branch-clustered uncertainty even though Statsmodels supports it. The spec asked for clustered SEs *and* a small-cluster warning, not a warning in lieu of the estimator. Printing conventional t-stats and starring |t|>1.96 sits poorly next to the “do not interpret these as tests” caveat.

**Smaller analytical nits.** The event-study baseline t = −1 is June for Gamma (after the product shock and the FTE cut) and March for Alpha/Beta (fraud-desk onset); that contamination is not discussed. Week-alternating randomization is a good learning idea, but within-branch interference and four-week power are underspecified relative to the firm decision rule.

**What still works.** Estimands E1–E7 cover ITT, intensity, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, and dynamics, with SUTVA and treatment-version notes. The threat table is thorough and directional. Confidence scores are appropriately low. The claim ladder is correctly ordered and the policy choice (`expand with an embedded evaluation`) matches the identification problem. Those strengths make this a solid answer, not a near-flawless one.
