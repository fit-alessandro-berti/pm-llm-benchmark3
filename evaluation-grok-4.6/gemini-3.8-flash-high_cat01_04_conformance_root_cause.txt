## Ranking and causal logic (major)

**Model obsolescence cannot be a leading cause of the June collapse.** `Supervisor Sign-off` is an 1 Aug policy. Jun–Jul fitness already fell to 32% and recovered to 89% *before* that policy exists. Ranking “obsolete normative model” at #3 (posterior 94) as an explanation of “the” deterioration conflates two periods. A precise answer would split: Jun–Jul drop ≈ logging/telemetry/order; Aug–Sep residual after the same preprocessing ≈ stale model plus leftover deviations.

**Rank vs posterior is incoherent.** Rank 5 (noncompliance, 75) sits above rank 6 (real behavioral change, 85). If the table is ordered by contribution to measured fitness, say so in the rank column consistently; if by posterior, the order is wrong. Double-counting is also real: mandated sign-off is both a true process change *and* model obsolescence.

**Standard Review sign-offs do not contradict obsolescence.** The ledger/table treat 60 `Standard Review → Supervisor Sign-off` traces as contradicting the obsolete-model hypothesis because “policy assumed enhanced review focus.” The policy is value-based (`> EUR 50,000`), not path-based. Those 60 traces are exactly what a control-flow-only stale model would mis-penalize, and they support obsolescence plus the need for data-aware guards. This is a domain-logic error, not a nit.

**Causal graph asserts the unresolved dispute.** Required nodes are mostly present, but `policy_change` never points at a stale `normative_model`—the actual obsolescence mechanism. Missing checks are wired `reviewer_behavior → unlogged_or_bypassed_checks` with no competing `extraction_defect → omitted Compliance Check` path, even though the brief says no audit has distinguished them. `true_process → business_outcomes` attributes the full 26.0h→27.4h cycle-time rise to sign-off; 0.5h of that rise is Jun–Jul, before the policy.

**“Illusion” / “measurement noise” erases a real process change.** Section 3 calls the collapse an illusion and then treats the vast majority of deviations as “harmless measurement noise.” Label/telemetry/order artifacts are measurement problems. Unversioned sign-off is not noise: the work happened; the model was wrong. That conflation undercuts the required distinction between logging defects and model obsolescence.
