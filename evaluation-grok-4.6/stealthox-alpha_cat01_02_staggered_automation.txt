**6.4**

The answer is substantively ambitious and often careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading, repeated arithmetic slips, reversed bias directions, an estimator that does not match the design it names, and an internally inconsistent rollout plan keep it well below the top of the scale.

## What works

The piece correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It separates availability from uptake, selection from contemporaneous shocks, and median from tail behavior. The estimand table is the strongest section: ITT-like availability, endogenous dose, dynamic effects, and an explicit SUTVA/interference estimand are the right objects. The main Jul–Aug vs matched-calendar-window DiD table is arithmetically accurate for the windows it defines. Mechanisms are process-aware rather than generic DiD talk. The claim ladder is well ordered. The code is self-contained, validates the panel, avoids PM4Py-on-aggregates, and states assumptions as comments.

Those strengths are why this is not a low score. They do not offset the defects below.

## Material inaccuracies

**1. False full-post median for Alpha.** Section 5 says Alpha’s raw median is “−3.3h late post but +1.9h full post.” Alpha Apr–Aug mean is 48.5 vs Jan–Mar 48.9, i.e. about **−0.4h**, not +1.9h. +1.9h is roughly Beta’s full-post change. This error sits in the regression-to-the-mean paragraph, where the raw-vs-relative distinction is doing real argumentative work.

**2. Case-mix shock is misstated, and the implied DiD bias is reversed.** Alpha complexity rises 28→36 (+8pp). Epsilon, a never-treated control, also rises 30→38 (+8pp). Beta, an adopter, rises only 25→28. “Larger complexity jump in adopters” is false. Section 6 then says that if adopters were hit harder, “DiD overstates improvement.” If treated units take a worse shock, treated post outcomes worsen and DiD **understates** benefit. The routing and Alpha FTE-loss shocks are likewise load increases that should bias **against** finding improvement; Section 3 dumps them into one “overstates improvement” failure mode with RTM. Bias signs are not optional in an identification write-up.

**3. “Small monthly case counts” for p90 is wrong.** Arrivals are ~700–1,300 per branch-month. Sampling variance of a monthly p90 is not the problem. The real issues are mixture of process variants, composition change, and the fact that a branch-month quantile is not a case-level QTE. Section 1 also says aggregates come “without … counts” while `arrivals` is sitting in the CSV.

**4. Named estimator ≠ estimated estimator.** The primary strategy is sold as Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham group-time ATTs that “never use an already-treated branch as a control.” The code is OLS with branch FE, month FE, and binned event-time dummies. Month FE still uses already-treated units to pin down calendar shocks; that is the TWFE contamination the prose claims to have escaped. With two cohorts and two never-treated units this is not a quibble: it is the difference between the design and a conventional event-study TWFE.

## Design and quantitative overclaim

The Jul–Aug post window is the most favorable slice for Alpha/Beta (after the May–Jun p90 spike and after uptake has risen). The text flags “honeymoon risk” for Gamma and does not equally flag **late-window selection** for the April cohort. “Median DiD stays in [−6.9, −2.8]h for every adopter and permutation we could construct” is stronger than the small set of windows shown. The p90 “relative −8 to +8h” band is not produced by any displayed permutation (shown DiDs are about −5 to −8h); it reads as an unearned robustness interval.

The naive “h per +10pp” ratios (−1.6, −0.6, −1.6) treat three confounded DiDs as a dose slope. The later caveat that uptake is endogenous is correct; putting the ratios in a results table still invites a per-10pp reading the rest of the memo rejects.

Pre-trend discussion is too thin even while calling the test uninformative. Alpha’s Jan–Feb–Mar median path is 50.4→46.9→49.4, not a stable −1.0h drift. Three pre-points plus a March fraud-desk break cannot support the confidence later attached to a 5–10% median effect.

Not-yet-treated evidence is incomplete: only Alpha’s median vs Gamma is reported. Beta’s early-post raw median rose while Gamma’s rose more; that is not the same as showing the pattern is “consistent” for the April cohort.

## Rollout plan contradicts itself

“6 additional clean pre-months (Sep–Feb)” cannot coexist with first go-live on **2026-11-01**. The November branch gets two extra pre-months, not six. If both branches need Sep–Feb untreated, the first adoption is March 2027, not November 2026. That is a direct conflict between the measurement requirement and the assignment calendar.

Epsilon-first is a defensible scientific choice and an aggressive operational one given unresolved tails; the memo does not really hold those in tension. The −3h median decision rule with two new clusters and wild-cluster bootstrap is still underpowered, which the threats section knows and the decision rule forgets.

## Clarity and spec nits

Tables are repeatedly garbled (`automation 01`, missing “≠”, broken arrows). That is not cosmetic in an estimand table. Section 3’s justification is at or over the 250-word cap. Several “expected signature” cells are slogans rather than process predictions. Confidence 55 on median and 65 on escalation sit poorly next to an E8 that is “not identifiable” and a SUTVA story the authors themselves treat as likely violated. If interference is a first-order threat, those confidences are too high.

## Net

The scientific bottom line is reasonable: no universal X% claim; medians possibly better relative to a deteriorating comparison group; tails not claimable; CSAT not identified; expand only with an experiment. That is the right shape of answer. Strictly graded, too many of the supporting numbers, bias directions, and design details are wrong or inconsistent for a high score.
