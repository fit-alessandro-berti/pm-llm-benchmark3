## Third model: better than S/C, not safe as specified

The backbone is the right *kind* of alternative: mandatory initial `+(Credit, Technical)`, XOR approval, Release last, correction in a loop rather than unrolled variants. That is genuinely safer than S and more maintainable than C.

It is still sloppy in ways the prompt warned against:

- Post-correction checks are re-optionalized with `τ`—the same trick condemned in S—because the missing “affected check” attribute was solved by making every recheck skippable.
- `ChainResponse('Correct Dossier', 'Credit Check' OR 'Technical Review')` is not a well-formed DECLARE constraint; Chain Response is immediate and binary. Immediate-succession also fights the parallel block (τ can fire first).
- `*(A,B)` with `A = +(X(SR,τ), X(ER,τ))` forces a pass through the optional-review block after every correction and allows a second Supplier/Expedite after a dummy recheck. That is new policy-ambiguous behavior, never audited.
- Correction is impossible until *both* initial checks complete. If Credit fails, Technical must still run first. Unmentioned.
- Data rules cite `Order.Type` and `System.DeficientItems`, which the log does not contain. Acknowledging the missing attributes does not make those rules operational.
- “Phase 2 must execute” is false: A can be two τs.

So `model_third` is a sketch of a better model, not a deployable semantics.

Section 6’s checklist is complete and the ranking is defensible. That is one of the stronger parts.

The reject rule “TNR = 100% or simplicity ≥ 0.5” would fail their own third model unless the undefined external rules are in the measured artifact.
