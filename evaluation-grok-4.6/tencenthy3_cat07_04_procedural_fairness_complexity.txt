**6.4**

The answer hits every required heading and constraint, and the core fairness reasoning is largely sound. It still falls well short of a high score because of factual sloppiness, incomplete use of the given evidence, methodological gaps, and a technically unreliable PM4Py plan.

**What is solid.** Raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination. Case mix is not used to wave disparities away. `risk_score_band` is treated as possibly biased. Rights-preserving steps are correctly framed as a source of burden rather than something to delete. The eight fairness dimensions, the disparity-versus-routing-versus-uncertainty framing, the sensitivity plan for endogenous covariates, and the mitigation/trade-off pairs all match the prompt.

**Material accuracy problem.** Section 3 misstates the variant table. “Group B accounts for 24% of V4” and “Group C accounts for 31% of V5” describe composition of a variant. The table gives *within-group* shares: 24% of B cases follow V4, 31% of C cases follow V5. The follow-on phrase “versus near-zero for others” only makes sense under the correct reading, so the author understood the pattern but wrote the opposite quantity. That is a real error on the central path evidence.

**Incomplete use of the provided numbers.** The assessment underuses several tables it was given: 90th-percentile cycle times (C 38.8d, B 34.2d vs A 21.4d), V6 appeal-path shares (D 13%, B 8%), Submit → Identity Verification waits, Supervisor Review → Decision Draft waits (B 1.9d, C 1.6d), and D’s adjusted appeal gap (+5.7 pp, the largest). C’s *largest* adjusted cycle-time gap (+4.1 days) is not given enough weight relative to the smaller approval gap.

**Methodological unclarity.** Matching on `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested` is proposed without saying that this absorbs the main phenomenon of interest. Conditional on need, V4/V5 differences largely vanish by construction; the fairness question is whether those needed steps impose extra wait, extra handovers, and worse downstream treatment. The plan never cleanly separates (a) burden *because needs differ* from (b) worse treatment *given the same need*. Saying resource concentration is “independent of individual case characteristics” is overstated: B and C are concentrated on specialists *because* of those characteristics. The inequity is under-provision and extra handovers, not the fact of specialist assignment.

**Section 8 is internally inconsistent.** It calls the process both “procedurally unfair in identifiable respects” and “indeterminate.” The prompt asked for a calibrated choice among fair / unfair / indeterminate. The caution is appropriate; the double verdict is not.

**Section 7 is only a sketch, and several calls are wrong.** The prompt allowed pseudocode, but the listed APIs are used incorrectly enough to weaken the plan: `filter_case_performance` is invoked with one bound (it needs a min/max window); `filter_paths_performance` is treated as an arc extractor rather than a performance-window filter; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is given invented attribute-name arguments; `get_service_time` / `discover_declare` / `discover_log_skeleton` are called with no parameters and no comparison logic. Channel and region subgrouping is promised in Section 2 and then omitted from the code. Handover, working-together, and role-discovery objects are created and never interpreted.

**Smaller defects.** Arrow/path labels collapse to whitespace (“Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment”, “generalspecialistgeneral”). In-person channel friction (B 38% vs A 9%) and dual-role specialists who also do supervisor review are mentioned as facts but not developed as mechanisms. Monitoring advice warns about metric gaming but never specifies a concrete adjusted metric or review cadence.

The piece would be a strong draft after correcting the variant-share wording, using the unused statistics, tightening the matching logic, picking one bottom-line verdict, and turning the PM4Py section into executable, correctly parameterized comparisons. As written, it is competent but not close to flawless.
