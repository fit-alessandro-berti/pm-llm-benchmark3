**6.4**

The write-up is complete, follows the required heading contract, and respects the main constraints: it does not treat raw approval gaps as proof of discrimination, does not treat case mix as a full excuse, does not treat `risk_score_band` as neutral, and does not propose removing rights-preserving steps. Those are real strengths. Under a strict reading, they are not enough.

The most serious analytical error is the handling of “adjusted” residuals. The preliminary model does **not** control for language need or accommodation request. The leftover +2.6 / +4.1 days and the extra supervisor / missing-docs probabilities for B and C therefore still include the legitimate support paths. The answer treats those residuals as remaining unfairness after complexity control. That collapses three different things: delay because a support step is required, delay because that step is under-staffed, and delay on otherwise comparable standard paths. The question asked for exactly that distinction.

Data use is mostly faithful but not clean. Saying V4 “adds 19.2 days” and V2 “adds 14.9 days” is false: those are median cycle times for the variants, not increments over V1 (5.8 days). Supervisor wait of 1.9d vs 1.0d is turned into “priority bias” without engaging the stated regional backlog, extra handovers, or case complexity. Outcome parity is described with adjusted gaps and “inequity in final decision-making,” which mixes dimensions the prompt separated. Important resource facts are underused: five accommodation specialists of whom two also do supervisor review, three overloaded regional officers, and the explicit absence of SLAs on support steps.

Section 2 never actually defines disparity vs discrimination vs legitimate routing vs uncertainty; it lists techniques. Matching omits month (used in the given model) and never says whether language/accommodation should be stratifiers, mediators, or excluded because they are the fairness-relevant process. The interesting adjusted fact—Group B still +9.5pp “Request Missing Documents” after controlling for documentation completeness—is barely used.

Section 7 is the weakest technical part. `format_dataframe` is applied to an already-read log; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is given a fabricated `start_event`/`end_event` API; `filter_variants(..., variants=['V4'])` treats the scenario’s table IDs as PM4Py variant keys (they are activity tuples); log-skeleton is asked to encode a DECLARE-style response constraint. Several listed functions (`filter_event_attribute_values`, `filter_paths_performance`, `get_service_time`, organizational-role / resource-similarity networks) are unused even though they map directly onto the resource-concentration story.

The bottom line is directionally reasonable—B/C look worse on burden and timeliness; D’s outcome gap is not settled—but “procedurally unfair” is stronger than the residual evidence supports, and the memo never states crisply what **cannot** be inferred (intent, dignity, explanation quality, true fraud rates, whether risk is biased). Solid exam answer; not close to flawless.
