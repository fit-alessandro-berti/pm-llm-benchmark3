**7.1**

The final six-section writeup is a strong, on-spec answer: it uses the required structure and conformance labels, ranks unlawful shortcuts last, refuses to treat re-screening, self-certification, a SAR, or an override as a substitute for independent second-officer review, and treats P-901, API quota, and S4 timing as first-class constraints. It is not close to flawless.

The most important factual error is the **timing of Action C**. The prompt gives S2 review as **35–75 minutes**, plus **5–15 minutes** to record and (if approved) execute, against a **90-minute** deadline. The table instead treats review as “~35–50 min” and execution as plausible by “t40–65.” That is not an “earliest plausible” bound; it is an optimistic central range. The true window is **40–90 minutes**, so C is deadline-feasible mainly in the lower half of its own range and is tight or late in the upper half. That bias makes C look more operationally dominant than the evidence supports and weakens the C-versus-D/S4 comparison.

The **benefit/harm columns are not on a common scale**. C is scored mainly on €20k–€35k deadline savings (then informally probability-weighted to €5k–€25k). F is credited with avoiding €100k–€500k regulatory tail risk as if that were F’s distinctive benefit. Under that frame F would dominate every conformant review path, which the prose later denies. The dominance argument in §3 is the right one; the table does not implement it consistently. The probability-weighted euro figures also sit uneasily next to the prompt’s warning that observational predictions may not transport under intervention.

**Ranking F above E is too aggressive.** F is only `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`, and the prompt says the rejection threshold is **not** established. E is `CONFORMANT NOW`. Putting a possibly premature rejection of a case with 0.55–0.75 predicted approval ahead of a definitely legal hold is a real tension with the compliance-first ranking rule, even though the later prose hedges it.

Smaller defects: the stale 12-minute-old risk score is barely used in the recommendation; rank 1 is a stealth C+optional-D composite while D is also ranked separately; Auto Clear’s inapplicability is left implicit; and “filing a SAR does not authorize execution” is under-emphasized relative to the spec.

What keeps the score well above a mediocre answer is the control reasoning: A/B are correctly treated as deontically blocked, not as high-EV options; D is not allowed to replace second review; G is an unenabled exception, not a plan; S2-versus-S4 and the 35-versus-65 ranking shift are explicit; and the primary/fallback/rerank trigger in §6 is usable. Those are the hard parts of the task, and they are mostly right. The grade is held down by the C timing distortion, inconsistent monetization, and the F-over-E rank.
