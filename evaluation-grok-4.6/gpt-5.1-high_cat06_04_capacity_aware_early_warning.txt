**6.2**

The memo is complete, hits the required failure modes (I-03 ineligible; I-05 suppressed vs I-07 re-opened; spillovers priced; effects not treated as known causal quantities), and the six-section structure is usable. It is not decision-theoretically coherent enough, nor operationally tight enough, to score higher under a strict bar.

**The allocation contradicts the stated rule.** Section 1 requires \(EV^{\text{net}}>0\) in the base case *and* non-negative EV under a modestly adverse draw, and defines Tier 1 as “robust under mild adverse assumptions.” I-07 fails that filter. Midpoint direct EV is about \(0.26\times5000-450-50=€800\); subtracting the memo’s own ~€2,400 spillover per slot makes the *marginal* contribution about **−€1,600**. Adding I-07 to {I-04, I-09} therefore *lowers* base-case net monetary impact. I-09 also fails the robustness clause: the adverse scenario the authors themselves write down assigns it little or no effect. Selecting both as “Intervene now / Tier 1” is an override, not an application of the rule. Urgency and fairness can justify an override, but then the EV threshold was not the decision rule.

**Calibration is described and then not used.** Segment offsets on \(p_{\text{now}}\) never enter the score that actually ranks cases. Scoring uses unadjusted \(\Delta p_{\text{mid}}\times V\). If Strategic Enterprise overprediction infects the *effect* estimates, I-04 is overstated; if SME underprediction infects them, I-07/I-05/I-10 are understated. That mapping is never made, so the calibration apparatus is ornamental.

**Priorities are not auditable.** An EV formula is given, then replaced by unlabeled tiers. The claimed value cap / concave transform and segment floors are not applied in the table, so I-04 vs I-08 vs I-09 vs I-02 cannot be reconstructed. A deployable rule needs the number that was compared.

**Reserve logic fights the data-quality flag.** I-06 is correctly held for imputation risk, then named first in line for the unused slot. I-02 has complete data, is already in `Diagnosis Started`, has 3h left and comparable (if not better) direct EV. Preferring the imputed spike for the reserve is backwards.

**Operational realism is thin where it matters most.** Two specialists deliver four slots; I-07 has 1.5h to deadline and is still in Triage, with diagnosis, resolution, and customer validation still ahead. A 45-minute swarm can consume half the remaining SLA clock. Sequencing (I-07 first, I-04 later) is never specified. The “≤12h” eligibility window is arbitrary and binds on nobody in the snapshot.

**Section 3 sugar-coats the adverse case.** Base arithmetic is mostly reconstructible (midpoints 0.19/0.26/0.24; ~0.7 breaches; ~€17.5k gross; €4.2k direct cost; 0.18–0.54 extra expected breaches). Adverse is not: benefit €5–10k minus €4.2k minus spillover €8–15k is roughly **−€2k to −€14k**, not “close to zero or mildly negative.” The ~€20k spillover-case value is also an untested global constant.

**I-09 as a learning instrument is in tension with scarce capacity.** Using a slot to “generate evidence” in the sparsest segment is a real consideration, but it is exactly the case the prompt flags as having unreliable effect estimates. That belongs in a cautious/experimental tier with a pre-registered outcome review, not in the same “intervene now” bucket as I-04.

What remains is still a competent policy sketch: cool-down vs new-evidence (I-05 vs I-07) is the right distinction; I-03 is refused; false-alarm burden on I-08 is used to wait rather than chase €100k; fairness and label-feedback risks have concrete safeguards (treatment logging, boundary randomization, segment reliability plots, spillover-by-group monitoring). Those are why this is a 6 rather than a 4. The grade stays well below “strong” because the memo does not actually follow its own decision theory when it spends the slots.
