The answer has the right skeleton: six required sections, lawful shortcuts ranked last, a parallel conformant second-review plan, and explicit attention to P-901. Under a strict reading it is still far from reliable. Several claims are invented, internally inconsistent, or conceptually wrong, and those errors sit in the places a decision-maker would actually use.

**Format and ranking.** Actions A–G are all ranked and the four conformance labels are used correctly. That is the strongest part of the submission. The rank order is only partly defensible. Putting a 6–24 hour evidence request (E) third, above any remaining same-day second-review path, is a poor next-action ranking: S4 is 35–65 minutes away and C is still available. E is a later fallback, not the third-best move now. The table also sells D as executing at T+70–85m with “€0 regulatory risk,” then Section 5 admits T+100–110m if S4 arrives at 65 minutes. “Earliest plausible continuation” is written as the happy path (Approve → Execute), not as a branched continuation. Benefit cells double-count avoided SLA loss, avoided false rejection, and avoided regulatory fines as if all three are realized payoffs of D and C. Harm for E is silently narrowed from €15k–€80k to €15k–€40k.

**Fabricated quantities.** The trees attach probabilities the case does not give: screening clears / risk <70 with P≈0.60; persist P≈0.25; delay P≈0.15; S2 approves P≈0.65; director reachable P≈0.50 and refuses P≈0.70. **A clear technical error.** Section 3 treats match confidence 0.46 as P(regulatory loss) and computes `0.46 × [€100k–€500k] = €46k–€230k`. Fuzzy-name confidence is not the probability of a fine, of a true hit, or of an unlawful execution being sanctioned. That calculation cannot support a “strict dominance” claim. Unlawful execution is a control breach even if the name match is a false positive; the right argument is invariance of Rules 6, 7, 9, and 10, which Section 4 actually has and then undermines with this arithmetic.

**Timing and spillover are biased toward the preferred plan.** Second-officer review is 35–75 minutes in the prompt. The D tree shortens S4 to 25–35 minutes and later invents a 15–20 minute S2 “handoff.” The C diagram treats P-901 as certain to breach; the prompt only gives +0.12–0.28 on breach probability. “S4 is unencumbered by urgent cases” is not in the case. Because of that, the 35-versus-65 minute sensitivity is too weak: the answer keeps D strictly rank 1 in both worlds. If S4 is 65 minutes, C becomes materially more attractive for P-884’s 90-minute window; a careful ranking should move, or at least tighten, not declare strict dominance and call a 10–20 minute miss “minor” while assuming a 35-minute review.

**Fallback is the wrong branch.** The recommended fallback collapses three different observations—dirty screen, ambiguous screen, S4 later than T+50m—into Action E or a SAR hold. If S4 is merely late, jumping to a 6–24 hour customer-evidence cycle is dominated by waiting for S4 or, conditionally, interrupting S2.
