**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and hits the stated “strong answer” tests (I-03 ineligible, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers and calibration treated as first-class). It is not close to flawless. Decision-theoretic claims, operational constraints, and the author’s own rules are repeatedly left unapplied or contradicted.

## What is actually sound

Stage ineligibility for I-03 is treated as a hard gate, not a ranking footnote. Alert persistence is defined with a material-change test, and I-05 vs I-07 is compared on evidence change rather than on `p_now`. Segment offsets have the right sign (SME up, Strategic Enterprise down). Observational `p_now` is distinguished from incremental effect. The three-scenario table is mostly auditable. Feedback-loop and spillover-on-untreated-groups discussion is more concrete than typical.

Those are necessary conditions, not a high score.

## Material coherence failures

**The prioritization score is defined and then abandoned.** Section 1.4 specifies \(S_i = \Delta p^{\text{rob}} V_i - (450+FA_i) - \text{SpillPenalty}\) and says remaining slots go to the highest \(S_i\). Section 2 never computes \(S_i\), never states \(\delta_{\text{spill}}\) or \(\bar V_{\text{diag}}\) used for ranking, and substitutes unlabeled tiers. The allocation cannot be audited against the stated rule. That is a core decision-theoretic failure, not a presentation nit.

**The fairness rule is violated by the same-day allocation.** Section 5 reserves at least one of four slots for high-risk / high-uplift cases “regardless of value” when \(p_{\text{cal,mid}}>0.70\) and \(\Delta p_{\text{low}}>0.15\). I-07 is the only live case that clearly meets that test (SME-adjusted risk about 0.76–0.81, \(\Delta p\) 0.20–0.32, 1.5h left, new log). It is Wait. A slot is instead earmarked for I-04 (10h, Strategic Enterprise overprediction, €2,000 false-alarm burden, first alert). Calling the fairness constraint a “daily” rule does not rescue this: I-07 cannot wait until a later review.

**The stop-loss is already binding on the chosen book.** The rule caps further swarms if untreated diagnosis-queue expected extra breaches reach 0.5. Three slots at the top of the stated spillover band give \(3 \times 6 \times 0.03 = 0.54\). The selected portfolio is therefore on the wrong side of the author’s own congestion brake, with only a later prose hedge (“defer I-04 if congested”). A coherent policy would have made the two-swarm book the base plan and I-04 strictly optional.

**I-04’s action is not a legal value of the required column.** The spec asks for intervene / wait / do not intervene. I-04 is labeled “Intervene” while the rationale is “schedule later today” and the reserve logic holds a slot until 15:00. That is a committed third spend dressed as flexibility. It also collides with the I-06 flex story: if the imputation artifact clears, I-06 is Diagnosis Started with 4h left and should outrank a 10h first-alert Strategic case on urgency. The text never ranks those two under a single rule.

## Operational realism gaps

**Specialist bottleneck is ignored.** Two seniors deliver the swarm; a swarm takes 45 minutes; I-09 (2.5h) and I-02 (3h) are both “intervene now.” The policy never says whether a swarm consumes both specialists (serial) or one (parallel), never sequences the two live swarms, and never prices ordinary diagnosis work stopped on other cases beyond a generic spillover term. Four “slots” are treated as four independent tokens.

**The 60-minute time gate is too weak and is not applied as a remaining-stage feasibility test.** I-07 is still at Triage with 1.5h to SLA. After a 45-minute swarm the case would still need Diagnosis Started → Diagnosis Complete → Resolution Work → Customer Validation → Closed. Swarming is very likely futile for deadline recovery even if diagnosis accelerates. The answer waves at “whether the swarm can be executed fast enough” and then keeps I-07 eligible. A deployable rule would hard-exclude swarms that cannot plausibly finish the remaining path before the deadline.

**Non-monetary obligations are almost only the regulatory reserve.** Public Sector (I-01) is Wait on moderate value, with no treatment of statutory / reputational loss as distinct from €9,000. The prompt required that discussion; a preference to hold one slot until 15:00 is not enough.

## Uncertainty handling is better than average, still sloppy

Calibration intervals are described and then not shown in the allocation table, so it is unclear whether I-02/I-04 were ranked on raw or adjusted risk. Sparse-segment protocol (lower-bound effect, downgrade tier, human verification) is stated, then I-09 is first-wave Tier A with no verification step in Section 2. That is the highest-uncertainty row in the book.

Adverse spillover uses \(\delta \in [0.03,0.05]\). The brief’s adverse case is 15–30 minutes of delay, not a 0.05 probability shock. The extra 0.02 is invented, and it is what makes the adverse net-impact look disastrous. Optimistic/Base “direct economic benefit” is a point, not a range, despite the column name and despite “upper half of the range” being an interval.

I-08’s midpoint net is larger than I-02’s, and fails only on a lower-bound / false-alarm test that is never written as a hard gate. The Wait is defensible; the ranking logic is not shown.

## Smaller defects that still count under a strict bar

“Diagnosis episode” is undefined. Tiers A–D are not mapped to thresholds. \(p_{\text{cal}}\) clipping is mentioned, then unused. The exploration / lottery idea in Sections 5–6 is reasonable but sits next to a fully deterministic live allocation with no exploration this cycle. Weekly/monthly recalibration triggers are specific; they do not say who owns the decision, what sample size is required before moving Strategic Enterprise offsets, or how to handle the known post-redesign overprediction as a one-off vs a continuing regime.

## Bottom line

This is a competent policy memo that would pass a coarse rubric because it avoids the classic failure modes (rank by `p_now`, swarm I-03, treat I-05 like I-07, ignore spillovers). Under a decision-theoretic and operational standard it does not implement its own score, contradicts its own fairness and stop-loss rules, leaves the two-specialist constraint unspecified, and treats I-04 as both committed and optional. That is well below “nearly flawless.”
