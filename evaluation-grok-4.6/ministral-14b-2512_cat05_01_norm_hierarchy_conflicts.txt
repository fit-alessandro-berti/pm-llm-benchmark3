**3.8**

This is a complete, well-scaffolded memo that fails the thing it exists to do: apply the sources at the time of each act, on the evidence given. The form is professional. The casework is not reliable.

## What is actually good

Section 1 correctly refuses a single “newest / loudest document wins” rule, separates assumptions from facts, and states the right temporal principle: rules attach at commitment, later approval does not automatically cure, emails are not controlled instruments, and ERP time is weaker than contemporaneous records. A1’s purpose (conflict), A2/A3’s purpose (budget and supply-chain risk), and A4’s narrow emergency waiver are identified. P-503 and P-504 are the only case rows that are both correctly classified and roughly correctly reasoned. The P-508 segregation analysis (BUY-8 is not an Incident Command authorizer) is sound. Section 7 at least names residual non-waivable controls, recording, expiry, and an anti-normalization brake.

That is not enough. The judge criteria are consistency, process awareness, scope/time, evidential discipline, and argument quality. Those are exactly where the memo breaks.

## Structural and conceptual defects

**D1 is repeatedly smuggled into ECD-2.** The CFO email is an uncontrolled interpretation of *Finance* controls. The memo treats “EUR 150,000” as if it were an ECD-2 clause (“ECD-2’s EUR 150k threshold,” hierarchy row saying ECD-2 overrides Finance “up to EUR 150k (D1),” R1/R4/R5). That collapses three different things: C1–C4 (timing and register), D1–D4 (Finance gloss plus a warning not to strip integrity controls), and A7 (only the CRO issues GPIP-4 exceptions). The CRO never ratified ECD-2. Once that merge is made, every later Finance/Compliance call is contaminated.

**The hierarchy contradicts itself.** Section 1 and the GPIP-4 row call A1/A2/A3 a non-waivable floor. The ECD-2 row and the pseudo-logic then let ECD-2+D1 override Finance, and the P-502 write-up lets C2 erase A3. You cannot announce a floor and then walk through it whenever an incident document is silent. Silence is not a waiver; A4 and C5 exist to block that inference.

**“Specificity trumps generality” is mis-aimed.** D1 is not more specific GPIP-4 law. It is a cheaper instrument. Specificity can rank *within* a valid source. It cannot promote an email over a board policy the email itself says it is not meant to gut (D4), on subjects the CFO is not empowered to waive (D3).

**R7 is false.** Recording joint Incident Command authorization within four hours (C3) does not retire B6’s emergency form. Different instruments, different clocks, different purposes. The catalogue also drops A4, A5, A6, B2’s €25k floor, B5, and C6’s scope fight — i.e., the rules that actually decide half the docket.

**Comparison operators are stripped** (“Purchase EUR 50k”, “Purchase EUR 100k”, “requester or committer final business approver”). In a threshold regime that is not cosmetic. R1/R2 as written do not state the rule.

**Pseudo-logic is internally at war.** Line 1 lets ECD-2 waive Compliance for a new supplier; line 5 says that waiver exists only if CRO or an explicit ECD-2 waiver, “not present in C1–C6.” A formalized catalogue that asserts both is not a catalogue.

## Case-by-case (this is where the score dies)

**P-501 — wrong class, fatal timing error.** Commitment is 07-14 **08:50**. ECD-2 starts at **09:00**. ECD-2 cannot authorize, waive, or “justify” that act. The memo’s controlling interpretation is therefore impossible. Local B2 still requires Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve at €25k; A5 lets that stricter local floor stand. Both approvals are after commitment. B5 says the local emergency path does *not* waive Finance. B4’s Shift Lead substitute requires an *absent* Plant Manager *and* active delegation; the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. A6 bars treating 15:10/15:12 as a cure. Calling Plant timing a “logging defect” is a category error: the approval is late as a *control*, not merely as a stamp. Emergency form timing is fine; that does not salvage the rest. Primary class should be **NONCOMPLIANT**, not JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. High confidence makes this worse.

**P-502 — over-classified, factually sloppy.** C2 lets you use a supplier off the register. A3 is a global pre-commitment Compliance control for any new supplier. C4’s “supplier-onboarding requirements” *might* be read to reach A3; the file itself says the organization has never treated an incident overlay that way. The honest primary is **INDETERMINATE** or **NONCOMPLIANT**, not **COMPLIANT / High**. Finance_Approve at 13:05 is not “timely” under A2 (commit 10:15). “Post-commitment Compliance is noncompliant but not required” is not a legal sentence. R7 is marked satisfied; no emergency form appears in the case file.

**P-503 — right class, incomplete.** NONCOMPLIANT is correct: A1 (PROC-3 is requester, committer, and one authorizer), A2, A3, no CRO exception. But the live scope contest is almost unused. Three-day backup stock is exactly the Operations-vs-Internal-Audit fight in C6. If ECD-2 does not attach, you do not even reach the overlay; you are on GPIP-4 + SOP in full. “Satisfied rules: None” is also too clean — C1 timing would hold *if* the directive attaches.

**P-504 — correct.** Out of ECD-2, not a B4 emergency, off-register, no IC authorization. This is the one row that is clean.

**P-505 — forbidden reasoning plus ignored better evidence.** The problem statement bans treating a late ERP stamp as conclusive when better contemporaneous evidence exists, and bans treating post-hoc approval as automatically curative. The memo does both. Supplier acknowledgment at **17:59:31** is inside the window; ERP 18:06 is the thing you are told not to privilege (and the July 14 outage is the wrong outage anyway; the nine-minute clock note is case-specific). If commitment is inside ECD-2, the live issue is A2 Finance at 19:00, possibly D1, not “expiry + A6 cure.” If the memo insists on 18:06, the act is *after* ECD-2 and D1 cannot travel with it — still not a justified exception via A6. Section 4 says JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; Section 6 says accept as compliant / “late evidence of a timely act.” Those cannot both be the analysis.

**P-506 — right class, inverted timeline.** Finance_Approve **06:05**, Commit **06:20**. The memo lists those times and then writes “Finance approval after commitment.” That is a hard factual error. The real defects are: ECD-2 is dead; verbal “extension” is not an A7 instrument and is unrecorded under C3; Create_PO after commit with no live emergency path; Plant_Approve after commit against B2/A2’s independent budget-owner requirement. Low confidence is appropriate; the justification is not.

**P-507 — inverted the decisive sequence.** Compliance_Clearance **08:50**, Commit **09:00**. The memo prints those times and calls Compliance *after* commitment. That single inversion flips the case. Compliance and the >€100k/new-supplier trigger are satisfied. C3 recording is a signed PDF at 12:40 (3h45 after 08:55), inside four hours; ERP 14:30 is the weaker stamp. The actual open question is missing Finance, which is the one place D1 is even *arguably* competent (€110k ≤ €150k, CFO owns Finance). Primary class is not “NONCOMPLIANT because Compliance was late.” That is not a close call. It is a misread of the row the author just typed.

**P-508 — wrong threshold.** D1’s “IC is enough for operational approvals” is capped at **€150,000**. This buy is **€155,000**. D2 adds Legal *as well*; it does not extend the Finance waiver above the cap. Legal at 16:00 and independent IC authorizers are good. No Finance event remains a live A2 breach unless you invent a power D1 does not grant. COMPLIANT / High is not defensible on the memo’s own D1 quotation.

Net: 2 of 8 cases are solid (P-503, P-504); 1 is the right stamp with a broken reason (P-506); 5 are wrong class and/or wrong facts (P-501, P-502, P-505, P-507, P-508). That is a failing assessment docket wearing a passing outline.

## Alternatives, evidence, and the policy rewrite

Section 5 meets the four-case minimum and then argues the wrong direction. The P-502 “NONCOMPLIANT if A3 survives” reading is the *stronger* one given A4, A7, C5, D3, and the org’s own lack of precedent; the memo calls it weak. The P-508 alternative cites the €150k line against a €155k purchase and still calls the Finance-survives reading weak. Missing alternatives that would actually move the primary class: P-501 (ECD-2 not yet in force), P-505 (17:59:31 vs 18:06), P-506 (unwritten extension as proof failure vs breach), P-507 (D1 Finance + PDF as timely C3).

Section 6 mis-bins issues. P-501 is not a logging defect. P-505 cannot be both “post-expiry” and “late evidence of a timely act.” P-507 is not a Compliance-timing breach. Recommending retroactive Finance/Compliance as if that restored compliance re-imports the A6 mistake. The required distinctions (policy breach / logging defect / late evidence of a timely act / inability to prove) are named and then not applied.

Section 7 is the second-best part of the memo and still smuggles the email into A7 (COO+IC may waive Finance to €150k), weakens C3 (“failure to record does not invalidate”), and never cleanly settles the €25k local vs €50k global stack or whether IC members are budget owners. The 72-hour revert plus short CRO-ratified extension is a real anti-normalization clause; credit for that.

## Bottom line

A compliance assessment that inverts two timestamp pairs, applies a directive before it exists, uses A6 as a cure after being told not to, and spends D1 past its own cap cannot be scored as merely “uneven.” The methodology preamble is better than the work it produces. Under a strict rubric this is a 3–4 paper: complete headings, two good cases, and a usable policy sketch, standing on systematically unsafe application of time, scope, evidence, and exception authority.
