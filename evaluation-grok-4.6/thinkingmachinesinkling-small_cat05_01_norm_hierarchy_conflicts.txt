**6.4**

The response is complete, correctly headed, and methodologically self-aware. It separates assumptions from facts, refuses a single total ordering, applies rules at the time of commitment, treats A6 as non-curative, and does not collapse every emergency into compliance. Sections 1, 2, 6, and 7 are the strongest parts. The grade is held down by material internal contradictions, one clear factual misread, and several process errors that a careful analyst should not make.

**What is actually good**

- Authority is handled as layered and issue-specific: GPIP-4 A1/A3 as structural, ECD-2 as a scoped overlay for timing and onboarding, D1 as a Finance interpretation rather than an A7 exception, ERP outage as evidential not normative.
- Time and scope are usually applied at the act, not at case close: P-501 pre-09:00, P-506 after expiry, P-504 outside “critical lots,” P-505’s 17:59 acknowledgment vs 18:06 ERP.
- P-502 and P-508 are the best rows. New-supplier Compliance after commitment is correctly fatal under A3/C5/D3. P-508 correctly keeps Legal/Compliance as satisfied and still fails A2/B2 above EUR 150,000.
- Alternatives in §5 are real opposing premises, not restatements. The P-502 and P-505 alternatives identify the exact lever that would flip the label.
- §7 hits the required points and includes a concrete anti-normalization safeguard.

**Material flaws**

1. **P-503 fact error.** The case is an *approved* supplier. The controlling-interpretation cell says “unapproved supplier.” The same row then lists C2 as satisfied because the supplier is approved. C2 is inapplicable, not satisfied. A3 still bites on the EUR 100,000 threshold, so the label can survive, but the row is internally false.

2. **D1 is applied inconsistently.** P-505 is `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` largely because D1 is taken to stand in for Finance during the window. P-507 is `NONCOMPLIANT` for missing Finance/budget even though it is in-window, has timely IC authorization, is under EUR 150,000, and has a *stronger* A3 record. If D1 is too weak for P-507, it is too weak for P-505. If it is strong enough for P-505, P-507 should move. That is a controlling-rule failure, not a nuance.

3. **A1 is used in two incompatible ways.** The method treats IC authorization as *not* final business approval (so D1 cannot satisfy A2’s budget-owner limb). P-503 then treats PROC-3’s role as requester/committer/IC authorizer as an A1 breach. Those cannot both be true without an explicit fork. Either IC auth is the approval, in which case A1 fires and A2’s Finance limb may be substituted, or it is not, in which case A1 is not triggered and the real defects are missing A2/A3/B2.

4. **P-505 is over-classified.** Contemporaneous evidence can put commitment before 18:00; that only saves C1/C3. There is still no Plant_Approve, no identified independent budget owner, and D4 warns against stripping integrity controls. The row’s own contingency (“NONCOMPLIANT if post-18:00”) and medium confidence are an `INDETERMINATE` fact pattern. Choosing `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` as primary overstates the exception path.

5. **False deviations and uneven A3.** P-501 lists “no IC authorization” as a deviation at 08:50, when ECD-2 C3 was not yet in force. That is not a breach. Section 1 assumes unapproved = “new supplier” for A3, then never applies A3 to P-504. Either the assumption is operative or it is not.

6. **Formalism is sloppy.** Catalogue triggers drop the comparison operators (`Amount  EUR 50k`, `stoppage 6h`). Pseudo-logic mixes notation with prose (`ECD2 does not override(X, compliance) per C5 and D3`). B2’s missing “before commitment” language is flagged once and then enforced as if it were as explicit as A2.

**Net.** This is a serious, usable memo, not a superficial one. Under a strict rubric it is clearly not near-flawless: one wrong supplier fact, one broken D1 pattern across two flagship cases, and an A1 contradiction are enough to keep it out of the 8–10 band.
