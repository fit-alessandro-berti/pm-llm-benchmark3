**6.3**

The JSON is complete, on-schema, and clearly above a DFG-frequency baseline. Primary states are mostly right, A vs B are not collapsed, migration is not treated as proof of absence, and the intervals are appropriately wide. Under a strict reading of the rubric, that is not enough.

**Required distinction is mostly abandoned.** `process_enabled` is `true` on all 15 predictions. Delayed past events (C6-D `Evidence Received`), downstream loops that are not yet enabled (`Customer Evidence Requested` before review completes), and terminations that still require an unseen review (`Close — No Safety Issue`, `Fast Close`) are all marked enabled. The task explicitly asked to separate a process-enabled action from the next log observation. The caveat paragraph states the distinction; the structured field does not use it.

**Ranks 2–3 are often not “next” events.** For A, B, and E the mass after rank 1 is placed on successors of `Evidence Review Completed` (another evidence request, close, fast close). That is defensible only as a mixture over a hidden completed review. The write-up gestures at that, but the probabilities are presented as competing next observations without a clean latent-state mixture. Midpoints for B and E overshoot a coherent top-three distribution (~1.19 and ~1.19).

**Control-flow errors and overreach.**
- C6-C: Rule 5 needs the same lot or a supplier component. The prefix is *different lots*, same failure mode. Supplier Analysis is correctly called “suspected” in the state, then ranked #2 next completion at 0.10–0.25. That overweighting is inconsistent and weakly licensed.
- C6-E: a lot now tied to six complaints is a possible reportability/safety signal. Rule 4 is not applied. Technical Investigation / Regulatory Assessment never appear, while a 6–36 h Supplier Analysis completion is forced into the top three.
- C6-B alternative `S1 TRIAGED` after an executed evidence cycle is the wrong abstraction. Fast Close remains *permitted* by Rule 2; it is not the S1 state.
- C6-E alternative `S2` after a logged `Evidence Received` (13:20, feed normal) is not supported.
- C6-D dumps S5/S2/S3 into one “alternative.” C6-C’s alternative that Technical Investigation “may be delayed” is confused: that completion is already in the prefix.

**Operating conditions are used unevenly.** Evidence-review queues and the 48 h / 8–15% delay model are used well. The 16:00 notification batch is unused even where a hidden `Disposition Recorded` is hypothesized (C6-C, C6-D). C6-D’s 0.45–0.75 on `Regulatory Assessment Completed` is high: R-R2 opened at 12:40, assessment is 2–14 h *after assignment*, and it is 14:00. The high end assumes an unusually fast finish *and* that this is the next logged event.

**Smaller unclarities.** Supporting and contradicting lists are relevant but sometimes restate the prefix rather than pressure-test the ranking. “Unknowns” are better. The A/B cross-case paragraph is the strongest part of the answer and is actually process-aware.

This is competent specialist reasoning with several material semantic slips. It is not near-flawless.
