**6.4**

The final write-up is complete, format-compliant, multi-hypothesis, and mostly well calibrated. It is not close to flawless. The central process-mining claim is only partly right, and there are several real logical and factual slips.

**What it gets right.** The six required headings are present and filled. Handover, working-together, subcontracting, time, and role-discovery are each given an “implies / does not imply” reading. Centrality is not treated as automatically good or bad. Five hypotheses include the three mandated ones, each with mechanism, support, limits, and log predictions. Risks stay qualified (absence evidence is used; no misconduct is asserted). The analysis list actually names the relevant PM4Py calls. The recommendation splits immediate / monitor / do-not-touch and does not delete Mira without a replacement. The executive summary is in range and readable.

**Material defects.**

1. **The required contradiction is mis-specified.** In PM4Py, working-together is case co-occurrence. Mira→Carla frequency 350 on ~4,100 cases is ~8%, which *is* the 8% co-case rate. That is a denominator effect, not “weak” counting of short events. Transient routing explains *why* she is a handover/subcontracting hub without being a stable teammate; it does not explain the low percentage. Section 1’s “still counts as co-occurrence but weakly” is unclear and, as written, wrong. Section 6 then repeats only the “steps out” story. Section 4 later proposes filtering to Mira’s cases, which shows the author half-sees the issue, but the main interpretation never states the arithmetic.

2. **Hypothesis A uses supporting evidence as if it were limiting.** Unaffected ordinary claims during her absence *fit* an exception desk. Treating that as a contradiction is inverted.

3. **Factual error.** “Bot_Rules still handles ~4,090 cases without her” is false. Bot_Rules appears on 4,090 cases, many of which *also* involve Mira (920 Bot_Rules→Mira handovers). The right contrast is that most Bot_Rules cases never reach her.

4. **Hypothesis distinctness is thin.** A (exception desk), B (key-person), and C (broker) share the same mechanism—post-bot routing of hard cases. Labels differ more than causal stories.

5. **Vendors and bots are mentioned, not used.** Vendor_Pay as a sink fed by Alice/Bruno, not Mira, is a control fact (she is off the payment-release path). Vendor_Verify’s 2.1-hour waits and the Temp_1–Vendor_Verify subcontracting loop are almost unused. Automation is backdrop, not an organizational actor.

6. **Smaller unclarities.** “Assign Specialist” is treated as a settled activity even though it is absent from the 14-activity list (Hypothesis E notes this, then the rest of the text ignores it). The summary’s “She is not a formal team lead” is stronger than the “no ground-truth label” constraint. “High-variance cycle-time contribution” is never unpacked (variance of what, relative to waiting vs service).

A careful organizational reading that missed the working-together denominator, inverted one hypothesis test, and misstated Bot_Rules’ case set cannot be scored as nearly correct. Completeness and calibration keep it well above a failure, not near the top of the scale.
