**6.0**

The write-up is complete, correctly headed, and mostly aligned with the brief: it stays neutral, looks past averages, treats East differently from North/South in the central case and the recommendation, and refuses a full rollout. Under a strict reading it is still not a strong forecast. Several required causal points are mis-specified, one scenario contradicts the given data, the pilot is internally inconsistent, and the PM4Py sketch is only loosely grounded in the actual API and log schema.

**Mechanism map.** The table covers the required themes, but several links are wrong or too thin. Role clarity is listed as an expected *gain* from a shared approval queue; the rest of the answer (and the staff risks) correctly treat shared queues as blurring ownership. Learning loss is hung on “keep intake local,” which is the wrong cause; the learning risk is routing all hard work away from local caseworkers. Resilience is described as leftover local intake capacity, not as the loss of local specialist cover when N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech and one supervisor are pooled. The map also accepts “nominal handover reduction” uncritically. Management’s own routing table *increases* caseworker→specialist (36→41) and approval handovers (42→49). The only clear cuts are intake→caseworker and cross-team cover. A careful map would have said that.

**Scenario forecasts.** The three-way split is the right shape, and the central case (median a bit better, tail worse, East helped, North/South hurt on complex work) is the one defensible forecast. The optimistic case invents a 2.0h specialist wait with no capacity story; that is better than both today’s 3.2h local-tech wait and management’s 4.0h estimate. The adverse case then says East’s complex cases are the fairness victims. That fights the problem statement: East is the overloaded team (E_Tech 82%, 8.6d / 22.8d). If the shared pool fails, East may still be no worse than today; North/South are the ones who lose a working local loop. Legal/Risk at 93%/91% are almost absent from all three scenarios, even though the plan *adds* volume (9→12/100) and dumps difficult QC into the same queue. A 15% utilization lift is arithmetically impossible for those two roles. That is the single largest omitted mechanism.

**Indicators.** Leading vs lagging is the right cut, and several log fields are used well. Two mappings are incorrect: `discover_organizational_roles` is not a utilization measure, and `discover_performance_dfg` does not by itself produce SLA misses. Skill decay via `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is a stretch. `handover_reason`, `local_expertise_flag`, `priority`, and handover-type waiting times — the metrics the current state is already reported in — are barely used.

**Hidden risks.** Six of the seven named risks are there and clearly written. **Exception handling is missing**, despite being listed in the required set. Resilience as a hidden-average risk is also thin.

**Pilot.** Scope, holdout, window, segmentation, guardrails, feedback, and triggers are all present. The design then contradicts itself: it both merges N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech *and* keeps North “fully local.” If N_Tech is in the pool, North is not a clean control; if North is a clean control, the shared service is not the proposed service. Legal/Risk saturation is not a guardrail. Fairness triggers are weaker than the later recommendation implies.

**Recommendation.** “Pilot first, do not trust early averages” is the only recommendation consistent with the brief, and the “do not infer” list is sound. It is weakened by the same Legal/Risk omission and by leaning on a pilot that is not actually identified.

**PM4Py sketch.** The function *names* match the prompt, but the code does not. There is no `phase` column; before/after should be a timestamp or case-id cut. `read_event_log` on CSV, `get_service_time(..., filter_activity=...)`, and `filter_event_attribute_values(..., comparison='==')` are not the PM4Py contracts. Specialty and `customer_segment` filters are required and not coded. SLA misses are a comment. `discover_dfg`, `filter_variants`, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are unused. Working-together and subcontracting are computed once, not compared. For a section that exists to show how the forecast would be *measured*, that is a material miss.

Net: a competent structured memo with the right headings and the right high-level stance, but not a careful reading of the numbers, the routing table, or the library. Too many causal and technical errors for anything in the high band.
