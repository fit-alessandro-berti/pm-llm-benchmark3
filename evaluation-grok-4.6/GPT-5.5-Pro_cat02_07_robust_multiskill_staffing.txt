**6.4**

This is a complete, well-structured operations write-up with accurate offered-load arithmetic and a serious discrete-event scaffold. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several comparison claims are under-identified, and the recommended policy is not the policy encoded in the code.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 states distributional assumptions, labels the U1 P1-mix funding choice, and gives two distinct objectives (lexicographic SLA-tail risk vs worst-case regret plus fairness). Section 2’s expected service times (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74), block loads, day total of 2721 base minutes, and the 2490/2670 capacity bounds are auditable and correct. U1 case counts (~53.2) and P1 volume (~9.1), U2’s 12:00–16:00 Electrical shift (~341 → ~405) with near-unchanged day total after renormalization, and U4’s ~242 extra diagnostic minutes are also right. Bottleneck migration by scenario is the right qualitative picture: morning Intake/E-S under U1, Chen/Deepa/Emil under U2/U3, tail-driven preemption cost under U4, and triage-only feasibility under U5. Sections 6, 7, and 9 are professionally specified, including the important caveat that `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is case-level and must not be treated as stage queueing.

**The central integrity failure is policy–code mismatch.** The recommended hybrid is specified as primary specialist pools, reserved P1 slots, overflow triggers, Validation congestion control, and explicit stress modes. The simulator does none of that. Hybrid eligibility is the same “any qualified resource” set as full pooling. There is no reservation state, no queue-wait overflow threshold, no Validation trigger, and no U1/U3/U5 degraded mode. The only hybrid-specific machinery is a fairness term, a poorly named slow-skill penalty, and a different parallelization heuristic. Section 8’s own rubric allows a scaffold only if omitted mechanisms are identified; they are not. Section 4 therefore cannot be a comparison of the three *stated* policies. It is, at best, a comparison of fixed queues vs two close pooling scores, dressed in narrative ranges.

**Section 4 is not evidence.** The table is not produced by the code it cites. Bands are so wide they overlap (base mean delay 500–750 vs 500–720; many P2 SLA intervals essentially identical), yet the text still declares a clean hybrid win on robustness. That is motivated reasoning, not decision analysis. Resilience is scored without a numeric definition even though Objective B was defined as worst-case regret. P1 SLA for hybrid-base is given as 80–95%, which is compatible with both meeting and missing the 90% target. A robust recommendation cannot rest on intervals that do not decide the constraint.

**Specialization is partly a straw man.** Fixing Software to Emil only, despite Chen’s 1.25 Software capability, and excluding Ada/Chen/Farah from Validation, makes Policy 1 fail for construction rather than for specialization as such. A fair specialist policy would still be brittle (Emil starts at 12:00; Chen’s absence; late Mechanical coverage), but the implemented version exaggerates that brittleness and then uses it to “dominate” specialization.

**Capacity framing is slightly oversold.** 2721 vs 2670 is base minutes versus raw paid minutes. The direction is right—handovers, setups, coordination, multipliers, and skill incompatibility push the system into overload—but the headline comparison ignores speed multipliers on the demand side and treats 30 minutes of overtime as always-on calendar time rather than a scarce decision. In the code, every shift is extended by overtime a priori, so “planned utilization > 90% is unfair” is not the quantity being computed. Utilization is further diluted by dividing busy time by a horizon that can run into the next day, when there are no new arrivals and idle time is cheap. Those two choices can move `max_resource_utilization` a lot; the table’s 85–92% vs 95–100% contrasts are therefore not trustworthy.

**Several mechanisms are internally inconsistent.** Textual preemption requires material P1 SLA gain, remaining work above a threshold, and a benefit–penalty test; the code preempts the eligible lower-priority diagnostic that would finish latest, then stops. Restart-penalty math happens to be equivalent (10% of remaining base, later multiplied), but the decision rule is not. Handover, team-transfer, and setup minutes are charged as resource busy time, not only elapsed delay; that is a defensible modeling choice, but it is never labeled, and it inflates both utilization and the “overload” story. Coordination is applied as an 8-minute Validation ready-time gap, yet `coordination_min` is always written as 0.0 in the log. Electrical and Software are collapsed into one setup family, so E↔S switches incur no setup; that matches one reading of “team,” and contradicts a natural reading of “skill family.”

**Smaller technical defects.** Poisson counts are drawn from a NumPy generator while case attributes use `random.Random`, so a “seed” is not a single sample path. `random.Random.poisson` does not exist; the fallback is correct but the stream split is not. Parallelization for hybrid P1 is effectively always-on because expected parallel critical path is far below 0.85 × 120. Dispatch is purely myopic idle-resource assignment; reservation cannot emerge from that. Unfinished cases, if the safety cap ever binds, are dropped from SLA denominators, which would bias attainment upward. The PM4Py block is wrapped in `try/except`, so the required demonstration can silently become a skip.

**Recommendation quality.** Choosing hybrid for Objective B plus a P1 constraint, rather than for base-case mean delay, is the right *kind* of answer. The trigger table is operationally useful. But a primary policy that is not implemented, not identified as unimplemented, and not separated from pooling in the only executable experiment is not a robust recommendation. At most the text shows: specialization is brittle; unconstrained pooling is more flexible and harder to audit; some continuity/fairness penalties are desirable. That is a hypothesis, not a result.

**Net.** Format compliance, load algebra, uncertainty taxonomy, and process-mining instrumentation are well above average. Strictly graded, the piece still fails the standard it set for itself: comparable, auditable policy evaluation under U1–U5. The analysis is a strong design note attached to a weaker, incompletely identified simulator.
