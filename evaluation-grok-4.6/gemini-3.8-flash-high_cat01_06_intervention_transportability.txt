**6.0**

This is a complete, well-structured response that correctly refuses a blanket copy of Site A, treats the before/after evidence as confounded, preserves statutory ordering, and builds a genuinely concurrent Site B model rather than a sequential simplification. It is not nearly flawless. Several errors are structural, not cosmetic, and they propagate into the recommended subgroup, the effect sizes, and the pilot.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 covers the required features and correctly ties concurrency to document readiness, case locks, specialization, AND-join tail effects, and the 3-assessor bottleneck. Site A’s 9-hour median drop is not treated as an identified treatment effect; co-interventions (FTEs, mix, API, huddles, timestamps) are unpacked. The candidate design withholds `Document Check` until originals arrive, keeps Assessment behind both checks, and adds WIP/huddle/telemetry controls. The POWL sketch and code keep identity and document unordered relative to each other, with Assessment as a join, plus an optional legal loop. Recommendation `conditional go` is the right class of answer.

**1. Digital channel is treated as the no-legal subgroup.** Originals-required (62%) and legal-review (40%) are cross-cutting, not a partition. Digital volume is ~38%, but a large share of digital cases can still be public-sector/high-value. The pilot strata 38% / 37% / 25% cannot add to the actual margins (62% originals, 40% legal). Section 9 then sends “Fully Digital, Standard-Complexity Claims (approximately 38%)” first and asserts those cases “require no legal review.” That is false on the given numbers. The first-wave population, the legal-congestion scenario, and the expansion rule are all mis-specified.

**2. Incremental effect on originals is largely already in the baseline.** Site B already allows `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after Register. The DFG supports that (Register→Identity 2,050; Register→Originals 1,860; Identity→Originals 1,020). Overlapping identity work with the 18h mail delay is not the Site A intervention and is not a new Site B treatment. Scenario 2’s `0.38×7h + 0.62×3h` therefore credits ~2h of site-wide median reduction that should not be attributed to the proposal. The transferable analog of Site A is parallel identity and document work on cases whose documents are already in hand (and, secondarily, not spawning an unexecutable Document Check).

**3. Required “average up, tail down” scenario is fudged.** Scenario 3’s median is `-1.0 to +2.0 h` while p90 blows out. That is “maybe better, maybe worse, tail worse,” not a clear mean/median gain with tail deterioration. Kingman’s formula is also written as a vague `V ∝ …` rather than a waiting-time relation, and utilization `ρ ≈ 0.85` is asserted without using the 3,400-case / 3-assessor evidence.

**4. Pilot arithmetic and SUTVA do not survive the answer’s own diagnosis.** Target N = 840 Track-1 cases in 8 weeks of intake is infeasible: 3,400 / ~26 weeks ≈ 131 completions per week; 38% digital ≈ 50/week ≈ 400 in 8 weeks, not 840. Interference control partitions identity/document reviewers but leaves the 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers shared—the exact bottleneck and loop the rest of the memo says will contaminate cycle time. A treatment-induced assessment queue will hit controls, so the design cannot separate mechanism failure at Assessment from a treatment effect. “Pearl S-admissibility reweight by Site B’s P(x)” is also confused: the pilot is already at B.

**5. Transport DAG has a wrong edge and weak mechanism coding.** `digital_documents → originals_required` is not a causal mechanism; they are complementary site/case attributes. `legal_capacity → rework` is underspecified (supplement incidence is a process rule, not a capacity arrow). `treatment_parallelization → rework` is discussed in prose and missing from the graph. Selection nodes are present, which is good, but signs/modifiers are not. The narrative is slightly over the 180–280 word cap (~300).

**6. Table-level factual slips.** Daily huddles are a Site A co-intervention, not a pre-period feature; putting them in “Site A before” mixes treatment with confounders. “Beneficial signature” is used for “zero benefit,” for benefits of the constraint itself, and for the interaction—column meaning drifts. Completions 4,800→5,200 are tagged “marketing expansion”; the prompt only says mix shifted toward simpler digital claims.

**7. PM4Py fidelity is close but not executable as written.** Nested POWL, XOR intake, LOOP supplement, and Assessment join edges match the spec and the manual’s intended constructors. Two defects remain. `soundness_result, _ = pm4py.check_soundness(...)` will fail if the high-level API returns a bool. `StrictPartialOrder.add_edge` may need to be `po.order.add_edge` depending on version. Duplicate-label Transitions for redo Document Check/Assessment are the right idea. The limitation comments (guards, capacity, locks, durations) are appropriate.

**8. Other design overreach.** Component-level CMS lock splitting is treated as part of the intervention; Site B is only guaranteed to be able to pilot under statutory constraints, not to re-engineer locking. Without that patch, Arm 1 is the IT-lock failure scenario they themselves rate as harmful. Pre-legal checklists and CONWIP numbers are reasonable but presented with more precision than the evidence supports.

**Net.** Analogical structure, confounding, and “do not parallelize legally impossible work” are all present, which is why this is not a low score. It loses heavily because the recommended 38% entry cohort is the wrong set (digital ≠ non-legal), originals “gains” are partly already occurring, the tail scenario and pilot power/SUTVA do not do what the prompt asked, and the DAG/code have concrete mistakes. Under a hypercritical bar this is a solid B-level memo, not an A.
