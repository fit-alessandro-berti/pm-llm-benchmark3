**3.5**

The write-up has the required heading skeleton, labeled assumptions, and a recognizable process-mining vocabulary, but it is not a reliable reconstruction of the supplied log. Several of the cases that were required to be treated carefully are misread or miscomputed, and those errors propagate into utilization, WIP, throughput, bottleneck, and sensitivity claims.

**Evidence handling.** P05 and P11 have observed S1 starts (`09:00`, `10:40`); only S2 starts are `MISSING`. The answer treats both as missing S1 (and, for P05, missing S1 *and* S2). P15 has a recorded S2 start (`13:30`) and is treated as if S2 start were missing. Highlighted waits are often simply wrong: P16 S1 wait is 100 min, not 75; P17 is 95, not 70; P19 is 100, not 95; P20 is 105, not 95; P21 is 100, not 90; P06 S2 wait is 25 min, not 5. P21 elapsed S1 service is 30 min to 14:00, not 15; P15 elapsed S2 service is 30 min, not 45.

**Censoring.** Blank S2 rows and P22 are right-censored waits. Elapsed waits at 14:00 are about 85, 50, 35, 15, and 110 minutes respectively. Reporting them as “5 min” (and P22 service as 0 without a lower bound) discards open cases in exactly the way the quality bar forbids. That is a conceptual failure, not a rounding quibble.

**Missing-start logic.** The text promises resource-feasible intervals, then violates them. P07 on I1 cannot start before P05 ends at 09:25, so S1 wait is roughly [40, 75] min, not [15, 75], and service is not a point 60 min. P15 S1 must sit in the I1 gap after 11:40 and before 12:15; a 135 min “service” is S1 sojourn (or worse), not active service. P05 S2 cannot start before C1 finishes P03 at 09:45; P11 S2 cannot start before C2 finishes P08 at 12:00 (and after the C2 outage). Table ranges, footnotes, and “central” times contradict each other.

**Station metrics.** S2 has 14 completed services (P01–P14), not 15. S1 busy time from observed intervals plus constrained missing starts plus P21’s 30 min elapsed is about 535–605 min on 720 min of capacity (~74–84%), not 660 min / 91–97%. Subperiod arrival and completion counts do not match the clock (e.g., S1 “10 arrivals / 10 completions” in 08:00–10:00; S2 “6 completions” by 10:00 when only P01–P03 have finished). Peak S2 WIP of 3 is inconsistent with four S2 waiters plus two open S2 cases by early afternoon. Rounding is applied as ±2.5 min on *differences*; two independent ±2.5 stamps give up to ±5 min.

**Bottleneck.** Early S1 congestion is real, but the “persistent S1 bottleneck / S2 has idle capacity” story is over-certain. S2 service is systematically longer (~40 vs ~30 min), C2 loses 15 min, S2 waits rise (P08 55, P14 65, P16 elapsed 85), and late S2 queue + in-service WIP exceeds S1. A time-varying combination is the defensible reading; the answer asserts a single primary bottleneck while also saying S2 “cannot keep up.”

**Little’s Law.** The 08:00–10:00 check uses an ill-defined cohort, mixes system WIP with a 10-case λ, and then excuses a 25 vs 58 min gap as “handoff.” That is not a station-level check in an interval where the assumptions hold.

**Python (spec).** The in-memory table codes P05 `s1_start` as `None`. `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank are collapsed in parsing and only partly recovered from a state flag. The PM4Py frame is a point log (`S1_Start`/`S1_End` with identical `start_timestamp` and `time:timestamp`), not interval events, so `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is semantically invalid. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is never called. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is neither used nor justified away. The `scenario` argument is unused; optimistic/central/pessimistic numbers are not computed. WIP treats completed-but-missing-S2-start cases as still in S2 until 14:00. `complete_cases` is computed and then ignored.

Structure and assumption labeling keep this above a failing fragment, but the reconstruction is not trustworthy on the cases and quantities the task made decisive.
