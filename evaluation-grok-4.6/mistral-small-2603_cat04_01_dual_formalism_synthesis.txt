**3.0**

The write-up has the required skeleton and a high-level reading of the narrative, but the two models are not faithful, not executable, and not even internally consistent. The witness table then grades those broken models incorrectly. That is a failure of the core task, not a cosmetic issue.

**Models would not run.** `Operator.SILENT` is not a process-tree operator; silent nodes are `ProcessTree(label=None)`. `OperatorPOWL.SEQUENCE` does not exist; POWL sequence is encoded by edges. `LOOP` is used with one child instead of the do/redo pair. `Transition("...")` and assigning `root.order = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)` onto an `OperatorPOWL` are not the documented construction. Parent pointers are never set. The compact tree string is not a process tree; it is informal arrows that also disagree with the Python.

**The process tree misses the control flow.** The intended loop is `LOOP(→(Execute Trial, Inspect Trial), Adjust Parameters)`. Their body can take `τ_pass` *instead of* `Inspect Trial`, and the only inspect path is `Inspect → τ_fail → Adjust`. A passing inspection is not a generated visible path. `Release Material` is then wrapped in `XOR(τ, →(τ, Release))`, so release is optional. In the Python tree, approvals sit in a sibling of `SEQUENCE(Analyze, Design, …)`, so reviews need not follow root-cause analysis, and `Execute Trial` is not synchronized with `Plan Verification`. The informal tree instead sequences reviews *before* design/trial, which forbids the required approval–trial overlap. They announce a conservative under-approximation and label duplication, then build neither: the tree both drops required behavior and admits unsafe behavior, and no visible label is duplicated.

**The POWL model erases every choice.** All of `τ_low`, `τ_high`, `τ_supplier`, `τ_pass`, `τ_fail` and their visible activities are ordinary nodes of one partial order, so they are all mandatory. Both risk routes and supplier consultation always occur. `inspect → τ_pass` and `inspect → τ_fail` plus `adjust → execute` put a cycle in a `StrictPartialOrder`, which is illegal. There is no `OperatorPOWL` XOR or LOOP. Release therefore waits on supervisor *and* quality *and* regulatory *and* supplier. The “why unconnected nodes may stay concurrent” paragraph mostly restates edges and does not justify the intended non-edges (plan vs design, approvals vs trial, supplier vs those streams).

**Witnesses do not test the language they claim to test.** T3 and T4 are marked allowed but contain no approval route; intended they are forbidden. The required “allowed + supplier” row is therefore invalid. T6 is offered as the approximation witness, but their own tree still requires the risk XOR, so the stated PT verdict is not entailed by the code; they also invert under- vs over-approximation. T8 includes `Adjust Parameters` and then claims a *passing* inspection. No allowed row shows approvals overlapping the trial, which is the point of the non-block-structured design. T7 is the only clean cross-branch negative (plan without contain), and even it also drops approvals.

**Sections 6–8 paper over this.** The comparison table asserts “exact in POWL,” “PT serializes approvals before trial,” and “duplication used conservatively.” Those claims contradict the Python, the informal tree, or both. The validation plan lists the right headings, then invents `pm4py.discover_conformance` and assumes conversion of a cyclic, choice-free POWL. The self-critique never names the actual semantic failures: missing XOR/LOOP in POWL, a trial encoding that cannot end on a passing inspect, optional release, and wrong intended verdicts on T3/T4/T8.

What remains is a correctly headed document, a dependency table that is only partly right, and the correct *preference* for POWL in principle. That is not enough for a modeling answer whose job is executable, assumption-true control flow and honest counterexamples.
