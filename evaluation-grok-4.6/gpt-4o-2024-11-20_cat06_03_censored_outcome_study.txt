The answer has the right section skeleton and avoids the worst failure modes (it does not recode rejects/withdrawals as non-default, and the 48,000 cases are fully allocated). It is otherwise internally contradictory, statistically sloppy, and the required implementation is non-runnable and non-compliant.

## Target and estimand

The primary target is stated as \(P(\text{default}_{90d}\mid\text{activation})\), which is a defensible selected-outcome estimand. The rest of §1 immediately undoes that choice.

- **Population and estimand do not match.** The population is “all 48,000 applications,” while the target is defined only after activation. That is either a selected subpopulation analysis or a two-stage/selection model. It is not both.
- **Competing risks are grafted onto a conditional-on-activation estimand.** Withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are not competing events for default-after-activation; they are the selection mechanism that determines whether the default outcome is even defined. Conditioning on activation *and* “accounting for competing risks at earlier steps” is not a coherent estimand.
- **The alternative target has the counterfactual logic backwards.** A multi-state / competing-outcome target on observed process states does *not* require counterfactuals. The formulation that *does* is “what would default have been if rejected or withdrawn cases had been activated.” Calling the multi-state target the counterfactual one, and the activation-conditional target the assumption-light one, inverts the question’s taxonomy.
- **Prediction time is underspecified.** “Fixed prefix or elapsed time (e.g., after first Credit Review)” is a menu, not a design. There is no rule for cases that terminate before that point, and no statement that prediction is strictly before Accept / Contract Signed / Activate.

## Cohort construction

The flow table sums to 48,000 and does not dump omitted cases into non-default. That is the only solid part.

- Open cases are **excluded** in the prose and **retained as censored** in the table.
- Transfers are uniformly “right censored.” The brief requires treating transfer as a competing terminal event *or* a missing-follow-up mechanism, and notes that only ~60% are linkable. Neither the competing-event reading nor informative missingness for the unlinked 40% is handled.
- The 3,600 immature activations are reduced to “7–35 day feed delay.” Many are immature because the 90-day horizon itself has not elapsed by 15 Feb 2026.
- There is no as-of label rule of the form: include a labeled activated case only if \(\text{activation}+90\text{d}+\text{feed delay}\le\text{cutoff}\) (or \(\texttt{label\_available\_at}\le\text{cutoff}\)).
- Under the stated primary target, rejects/withdrawals/opens are **selectively unobserved / outcome undefined**, not competing outcomes. The table is written for a different estimand than §1.

## Censoring and missing labels

The five missingness types are named, then the analysis plan ignores the distinctions.

- The primary strategy is a competing-risk model of default vs non-activation. That is a different study from §1.
- “Matured-window … but imputing/informing uncensored likelihoods” is not an identification strategy. A matured-window analysis uses only labels that would have been known at the cutoff. Imputation of delayed or selected outcomes needs an explicit model (IPCW, Heckman, MNAR partner-feed model). None is given.
- Permanently missing partner feeds are listed and then dropped from the primary plan.
- Testability is almost empty. Untestable quantities are not named: counterfactual default under activation for never-activated cases; MNAR partner outcome feeds; outcomes for unlinked transfers; independent censoring of still-open cases.

## Prefixes and features

- Collapsing document-request loops **destroys** the main rework signal. Loops should be retained in the prefix and encoded as features.
- No as-of cut of the event stream: no rule that only events with \(\texttt{time:timestamp}\le t_{\text{pred}}\) enter the prefix, and no handling of same-calendar-day `documents_total`.
- Short-case handling (“keep incomplete prefixes”) ignores the operational constraint that a \(k\)-event predictor is not applied to cases that already terminated with \(<k\) events.
- Migration: “standardize names” with no synonym map and no duplicate-event rule after 15 Oct 2025.
- Resource experience cannot be “adjusted to event time” from an extraction-time snapshot unless a historical HR series exists. The brief says it does not. That is not a repair.
- Cohort/regime features (pre/post 1 Jul 2025 policy, pre/post migration) are essentially absent. Queueing/waiting time is named only as resource counts.

## Leakage table

Required columns are declared, then **misaligned**. `Drop` is written in the availability-rule column; rationales sit under Keep/Repair/Drop. That is not a usable audit.

Intent for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and future events is correct. Gaps:

- `default_90d` repeated on events is never listed as a feature-leak.
- `label_available_at` is dropped as a feature (fine) but never used as a maturity gate.
- Resource-experience “repair” is infeasible, so the correct action is drop or an explicit proxy with bias caveats.
- No rule for `data_extract_time` / `source_system`.

## Validation

A start-time split is the right *idea*. The proposed cut is not a valid backtest.

- Train Jan 2024–Jun 2025 / val Jul–Oct 2025 / test Nov–Dec 2025 puts the test set almost entirely in the immature-label region. A case started in Dec 2025 cannot have a matured 90-day post-activation outcome by 15 Feb 2026.
- “One pre-policy, one post-policy, and one mixed split” is promised; one split is delivered.
- **Why a random case split is misleading is never explained** (policy break, migration, label-delay correlation with calendar time, within-period leakage). That was an explicit requirement.
- No subgroup protocol (channel, region, partner-originated, pre/post policy).
- Metrics (C-index, Brier, time-dependent AUC) assume a survival/competing-risk target that §1 did not actually lock in. No selection-aware or calibration-by-regime plan.

## Limitations

Three deployment shifts are named, which is the minimum. The section does not state the decision boundary the rest of the brief asked for: this study cannot score “default for every application,” cannot support accept/reject policy as if it were a counterfactual risk model, and cannot treat partner-missingness as ignorable. “Predictive, not causal” is boilerplate.

## Implementation skeleton

This section fails the specification, not just style.

- `pm4py` is used and never imported.
- Unused/obsolete factory imports (`csv.factory`, `log_to_features.factory`).
- **`pm4py.algo.transformation.prefix_extraction.factory.apply` is fabricated.** The brief required `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` and forbade inventing APIs.
- No case-level start, activation, terminal state, or `label_available_at` maturity table as of a cutoff.
- After `df = df[df['time:timestamp'] <= cutoff]`, `censored = (case_end > cutoff)` is identically zero. The censoring flag is dead code.
- The “temporal split” filters **prefix event timestamps**, not **case start time**. Later events of early cases leak into test; cases are not held intact.
- No train/validation/test; no leakage-audit assertion; post-decision columns (`final_limit`, `collection_status`, `default_90d`) are never stripped before feature extraction.
- Far below the 80–160 line concrete pipeline; no hooks for survival/competing-risk modeling; no justified use of DFG/variants/temporal/waiting-time helpers.

A skeleton that does not run, splits the wrong unit, fabricates PM4Py, and skips cohort/maturity/leakage construction cannot support the design claimed above.

## Net

Partial process literacy (don’t call rejects non-default; count all 48,000; drop obvious post-outcome fields) is not a study design. The estimand, the cohort rules, the missingness strategy, and the code describe three different analyses. Under the stated rubric—statistical design, process-aware cohort construction, leakage prevention, honest target semantics, and a PM4Py-oriented skeleton—this is well below a competent pass.
