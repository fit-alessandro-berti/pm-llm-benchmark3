**6.0**

The memo is structurally complete and strategically directionally sound: it refuses national scale of full R, refuses a blanket rollback, and picks a segment-conditioned hybrid. That is the right neighborhood. Under a hypercritical standard it is not close to flawless.

**Strategic synthesis.** The core call (protect simple-house gains, stop the skip rule and post-billing activation on hard segments, measure before wider simplification) is defensible. It is also mis-specified. Stated option E is “deploy R only for simple low-risk houses … and run measurement before wider scale.” The write-up instead scales coordinator ownership *nationally* and then grafts C’s suspensions onto complex segments. That is C+E, labeled E, and it under-argues C, which is the more conservative reading of the same evidence. National coordinator scale sits poorly next to 93% utilization, absence-stalls, and a more centralized working-together network. Rural (p90 +9 days, FTR −5pp, complaints +2pp) is omitted from the pause list. Combined validate/schedule is casually bundled with the predictive skip rule as if they were one control.

**Facts vs interpretations vs assumptions.** The three-way split exists, but it leaks. “Cost data excludes complaint-handling after week 4” is a coverage *fact*; “true cost may be higher” is the assumption. Low precision is recast as “poor process discipline,” which over-claims. Mix-shift (pilot regions heavier in apartments/old buildings) is tabled as context and then ignored as a confound on *aggregate* deltas; segment tables already show heterogeneity, so headline worsening is partly composition. Sparse start timestamps, incomplete attributes before week 3, generalization 0.70→0.56, and DFG/temporal-profile detail are barely used. Invented bars (90% rule accuracy, 95% scale gate, 5% FNR) are written as if they were evidence-derived.

**Process-mining literacy.** Fitness vs precision is roughly aimed and then wrong in the mechanism. Frequent *move-on-log* (billing before activation; repeat visit without survey) means those behaviors are *not* replayed by the model; they do not illustrate “the model permits deviant paths.” Low precision is unused permitted combinations (the PM team’s own gloss). Treating alignments as precision failures is a real conceptual error in a memo that is supposed to use the artifacts. Temporal profiles (billing→complaint as a new long tail; failed-visit→second-visit delay under high coordinator load) and resource reallocation (survey idle 78%→52%, second-visit 66%→92%) are under-exploited relative to what the prompt supplied.

**Metric conflicts.** Four of the five required tensions are explained clearly and in business language (median vs tail, handovers vs resilience, visible vs downstream cost, house vs apartment). That section is the strongest part. It is still docked for the precision/alignment mix-up.

**VOI.** Ranking all of M1–M8 is good; the top of the list (M6, M1, M4) is reasonable. It is not decision-consistent. If coordinators go national now, M5 is not a sixth-priority resilience study. If billing-before-activation is the compliance issue the constraints flag, M7 is a cheap immediate control, not mid-pack. “If the rule is invalid, suspend skipping *entirely*” contradicts the memo’s own segment split. Lower items mostly restate purpose; they do not say what estimate would flip E→C, C→E, or un-pause skipping.

**Presentation.** Percentage vs percentage-point on handovers; stripped operators (“EUR 184  161”, “  6.1%”); thresholds that cannot be audited. Minor, but they add noise to an executive artifact.

Net: a usable draft that would survive a first steering meeting and would not survive a careful process-mining or causal review. Right shape, several load-bearing inaccuracies.
