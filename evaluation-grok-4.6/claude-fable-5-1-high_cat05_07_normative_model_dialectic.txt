**7.5**

The answer is unusually complete against the required headings, and it is dialectically serious: both briefs concede real ground, the burden-of-proof rule is operational rather than ornamental, and the layered revision mostly puts rules in the right evaluation layer. It is not nearly flawless. Under strict scoring, several concrete errors and semantic overclaims keep it well below the top band.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 separates stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes with unusual discipline. The four-part test for NONCOMPLIANT versus JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is a real standard, not a mushy split: written authority at event time, contemporaneous eligibility, compensating controls, and a clean rule that model-inexpressibility is not a CS-9 breach. The prosecution’s normalization sequence (FD-7 → SUP-705; PD-3 → SUP-714/August) is the right strongest case. The defense’s additive-loop argument for SUP-707 and the CRM-missingness argument for SUP-706 are also the right strongest case. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource failures (SUP-712, SUP-715). M1 actually includes a real `LOOP` for `Request_More_Info`, a distinct provisional label, an escalation arm, and an explicit “shape vs permission” split. Those are the parts that deserve credit.

**1. The system-level count is false.** Section 5.2 says “six are model/scope/data findings, two are justified exceptions, three are fully compliant and five are noncompliant.” That sums to 16, not 15. The table the same section just published is:

- COMPLIANT: 701, 711, 713 (3)
- JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: 702, 704 (2)
- NONCOMPLIANT: 703, 705, 708, 712, 714, 715 (6)
- DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE: 706 (1)
- MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT: 707, 709, 710 (3)

So the sample is **6 noncompliant and 4 model/scope/data**, not 5 and 6. This is not a rounding nit. It is the one quantitative summary of the 15-case review, and it misstates the noncompliance rate the judgment then leans on. An evidence-first review that cannot add its own labels is a serious integrity defect.

**2. Auto-route notification order contradicts the pilot the model claims to encode.** PD-3 and SUP-702 have notification near activation and `Quality_Review` up to two business days later (CRM notify at +2 hours; quality the next business day). M1 nests `Quality_Review` *inside* the auto path and only then sequences `Notify_Requester`. Alignment therefore requires Quality before Notify. The self-check quietly rewrites SUP-702 into that illegal-for-the-facts order. That is a control-flow error, not a data-rule leftover. The emergency path happens to match SUP-704’s notify-late shape; the auto path does not match the overlay it exists to represent.

**3. Mandatory unlogged activities repeat the logging fallacy the briefs attack.** `Master_Data_Validation` and `Emergency_Authorization` / `Provisional_Activation` / `Confirm_Activation` are required POWL nodes. The text admits historical PD-3/FD-7 traces will not fit until the log is rewritten. For `Notify_Requester`, the same answer refuses to treat a known collection gap as noncompliance. For MDV, it does the opposite: an activity that never appeared in the log becomes a hard model move, so the 289 “eligible” cases fail M1 by construction. That is philosophically inconsistent. The defensible move was optional/tau in POWL plus a pandas obligation, which is exactly the pattern used elsewhere.

**4. `Request_More_Info` is oversold.** `LOOP(tau, RMI)` concurrent with both checks *does* generate SUP-707, but it also allows RMI after `Quality_Review` is already finished, with no re-review. Calling that “the semantics SUP-707 requires and not an approximation” is false. Layer 2 then invents `Info_Received`, which is not in M1 at all. So the “real loop” still does not make the “none activated before evidence arrived” claim checkable in the POWL.

**5. Several classifications are sharper than the record.**  
- **SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT:** the source says no rule prohibits the spousal/reporting-line relationship; the proven fact is missing disclosure. Treating a disclosure gap as process noncompliance, at medium severity, overreaches CS-9. A secondary organizational finding on a COMPLIANT control-flow case was the tighter call; refusing MIXED does not require inflating the primary label.  
- **SUP-702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** depends on a burden-shifting rule that case handlers may rely on a competence-doubtful overlay. That rule is declared, not derived. It is coherent, but it is also the most contestable move in the paper, and it is what lets the judgment reject the prosecution’s 312-case claim. The brief should have flagged more clearly that this is a chosen standard, not a fact.  
- **SUP-711 as COMPLIANT (high confidence)** rather than a resolved data-quality item is slightly too clean; the raw trace does not fit M0/M1 until R-DEDUP-1 is applied.

**6. Architecture/layer leaks.** C1’s “result = clear” is a data predicate parked in the DECLARE layer. C7 references an event the POWL never emits. The auto path has no post-activation fail/revoke/deactivate arm, so a failed post-activation quality review still runs to Notify/Close on an active supplier. Emergency has no sanctions-fail exit before `Provisional_Activation`; only a later data rule can stop it. These are exactly the holes POWL-plus-overlays were supposed to close.

**7. Smaller but real nits.** SUP-706’s date is not in the case table; CRM-migration treatment is inferred. Defense’s “14/15 sanctions-before-activation” treats the non-activating reject as a success, which should have been said. Overlay table “Compliance signature absent” flattens a written “monitored exception” plus later disclaimer. Self-check column names (`case_id` / `is_fit` / `fitness`) are version-fragile; the `try/except` hides a failed semantic demonstration rather than proving the claimed fits. `seq()` plus nested XOR/LOOP is plausible PM4Py, but the only executable proof is that guarded print.

**Net.** This is a high-quality legal/process argument with a real model, not a template dump. Strictly, it is still a 7.5: the sample arithmetic is wrong, the auto-route happy path is ordered incorrectly, unlogged gates are treated inconsistently, and a few labels outrun the sources. Those are not formatting issues; they are the exact dimensions the judge was told to score.
