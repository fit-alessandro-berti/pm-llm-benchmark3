**5.5**

The answer has the right skeleton for this task—availability vs intensity, targeted staggered adoption, median vs tail, small-cluster caution, and a refusal of a single “X% cycle-time” headline—but it is not close to flawless. Quantitative discipline, identification hygiene, and the required reproducible analysis all fail in material ways.

**What is done well.** Sections follow the mandated headings and table schemas. Section 1 stays descriptive and separates availability, `automated_fast_path_pct`, selection, co-occurring shocks, and CSAT/aggregation limits. The estimand list covers ITT, intensity, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, and a dynamic contrast. Naming staggered DiD / Sun–Abraham rather than naive TWFE is the correct instinct. Mechanisms include substitution, specialist congestion, learning, routing, staffing, rework displacement, tails, and survey selection. The claim ladder is well ordered, and “expand with an embedded evaluation” is a defensible policy class.

**Quantitative errors are disqualifying for a high score.** Claimed “3-month pre / 3-month post” windows are not the windows actually used, and several published contrasts are wrong or irreproducible. Alpha pre median is reported as 48.4 vs. Jan–Mar \((50.4+46.9+49.4)/3=48.9\); Alpha post 47.5 does not match Apr–Jun (50.4), Apr–Aug (48.5), or any other transparent window they define. Alpha p90 post 108.2 is the five-month post mean, contradicting the three-month rule. Never-treated “35.3 → 39.2” / “40.2 → 42.8” mixes different spans (roughly early-year vs Jul–Aug). The intensity result “+10pp auto → −0.8h median / +1.5h p90” is not derived in the write-up or the code. Sensitivity ranges are therefore built on opaque arithmetic, which the rubric treats as a first-class failure.

**Section 9 does not do what it claims.** The embedded CSV corrupts Alpha January (`50.4/101.3` becomes `46.9/94.7`). Relative time is built from each branch’s *first calendar month*, not first `automation=1` month, so event time is meaningless. The “panel model” is bivariate OLS of median on the adoption dummy—no branch FE, no month FE, no covariates. Statsmodels is called incorrectly (`OLS(..., cov_type=..., cov_group=...)` belongs on `.fit(cov_kwds={'groups': ...})`); the required clustered models would not run. That is not a cosmetic bug in a section whose purpose is reproducibility.

**Identification and process logic have real holes.** E7 claims case-level complexity heterogeneity is identifiable; the file is branch-month aggregates, so it is not. Routing-campaign dummies are almost collinear with Alpha/Beta adoption and are a bad control. Interference is listed as a threat, then Section 8 recommends randomizing *half the cases inside* Delta/Epsilon—the design that most directly violates SUTVA given shared FTEs, specialist queues, and the fraud desk. The scale-up rule also abandons the paper’s own preferred contrast (vs counterfactual trend) and reverts to a 3% drop vs own pre-period, with broken inequalities (“p90 growth 2%”, “CSAT 3.8”). Utilization (`arrivals/FTE`) is never computed despite a 30→43 cases-per-FTE shock at Alpha. The common March +2pp escalation jump (fraud desk) is underused.

**Net.** Conceptually above average, executable science well below the bar. Under a hypercritical standard—wrong numbers, non-running code, a false identifiability claim, and a rollout that contradicts the interference analysis—this cannot score in the high range.
