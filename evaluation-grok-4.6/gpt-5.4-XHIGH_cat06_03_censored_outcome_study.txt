**6.2**

This is a conceptually competent design that still fails a strict bar on estimand–deployment coherence, validation, and especially the implementation skeleton. The write-up correctly refuses to treat the 21,600-row export as the population and correctly refuses to recode rejects, withdrawals, and open cases as non-defaults. That is necessary, not sufficient. Several errors would produce a misleading study if the text and code were implemented as written.

## What is actually sound

The primary estimand is the right *scientific* refusal of the informal business request: booked-account `default_90d` under the observed activation policy, not a fabricated all-application default label. The competing-outcome alternative is a genuinely different question, and the counterfactual “default if forced to activate” is correctly parked as untestable. The 48,000-row accounting identity is exact. The five-way split of missingness (open-process censoring, administrative delay, activation selection, transfer, permanently missing partner feeds) is the core distinction the prompt is testing. The leakage table in §6 is, as a table, mostly the right keep/repair/drop calls. Temporal splitting and the random-split critique are directionally correct.

Those strengths are why this is not a 3–4. They are not enough for a high score under a hypercritical standard.

## §1 Target definition — selected-sample estimand presented as an early-warning target

The stated estimand is

`Pr(default_90d = 1 | prefix at L, eventual activation under the policy in force at L)`.

Two problems are fudged.

First, “eventual activation” is future information at a first-3-event landmark. This is a selected-sample parameter, not an operational early-warning parameter for “every credit application.” §8 later admits this, but §1 still sells the formulation as “operationally defensible” because the warehouse field is only defined after activation. That defends *label semantics*, not *operational use at L*. At L the lender does not know who will activate. A score trained only on those who later activate is not a well-defined action score for the live funnel unless paired with an activation / competing-risk model. Calling that pairing a mere “alternative” leaves the primary target misaligned with the business question it claims to serve.

Second, the horizon is not a 90-day horizon from prediction time. It is a binary endpoint 90 days after a *future random origin* (activation), plus a 7–35 day feed lag. Time from L to label is heterogeneous, policy-dependent, and collides with extract-date maturity. The write-up never names this variable-origin endpoint problem. That is a target-definition error, not a later implementation detail.

The claim that k=3 “should correspond to” `Application Started → Identity/KYC Check → Credit Review` is an untested process assumption. Extra system events, incomplete rename maps, or a repeated KYC before first review all break it. A first-hitting-time landmark at `Credit Review` would have been the process-aware choice. k=3 is a convenient event-count proxy presented as if it were the business landmark.

## §2 Cohort construction — 48k is accounted for; the analysis cohort is not

The table is a source-file inventory, not a cohort-flow. The required as-of study flow is at least:

all starts → reach landmark L with no terminal contamination → later activate → label mature and observed.

None of those sequential cuts is quantified. After k=3 and “no decision/terminal in prefix,” the 21,600 labeled activated cases will shrink by an unknown amount. After requiring maturity in a July–September 2025 test window, it will shrink again, selectively. A cohort-flow table that cannot say how many cases remain in the *estimation* sample is incomplete.

The 3,600 unlabeled activated cases are correctly split *in principle* into not-yet-mature vs overdue/missing. The split rule (activation ≤ 2025-10-13) uses a uniform 35-day worst-case lag. Cases that activated after 13 October but whose feed arrived in 7–20 days are dumped into “not yet mature” even when they may already be missing-not-delayed. The code then overrides this if a label is present, so labeled late activations are kept, but the unlabeled remainder is a mixture. The text never says that the 3,600 composition is unidentified from the given margins.

Accepted-but-not-activated cases are invisible. The process allows `Accepted → Contract Signed → Account Activated`. An accepted file sitting unsigned is neither a pre-activation terminal nor an activated unlabeled account. The code’s `classify_case` will call it `open`. The narrative table has no such row. Warehouse categories and process-mined states are treated as interchangeable. They are not.

## §3 Censoring and missing labels — good taxonomy, weaker identification

The taxonomy is the strongest section. The primary analysis (fit only on activated, observed, mature labels; name the population) is honest. Bounds / IPW / linked-transfer sensitivities and the testability ranking are appropriate.

Gaps that matter:

- There is no design for activation as a *selection equation* even as a diagnostic (who reaches activation given the prefix). Without it, “historically activated accounts” is a changing, policy-dependent composition, and §7’s post-policy test set is not the same estimand as the training estimand.
- Time-to-default after activation with right censoring is listed in the prompt and then essentially discarded. The 3,600 unlabeled activations are exactly the reason to write a censored-time target. Choosing a complete-case binary among observed labels is simpler and more biased if missingness is informative — which the text itself says partner feeds likely are.
- IPW is specified for *label observation among mature activations*, not for activation. That is the smaller missingness problem. The larger one is left untouched except by the competing-risk “sensitivity.”
- Best/worst-case bounds that recode all overdue missing labels as 0 or 1 are crude; with partner-elevated permanent missingness they can be almost vacuous. No discussion of how wide those bounds are expected to be, or that 12.5% default among *observed* labels is already a selected rate.

## §4 Prefix extraction — rules are mostly right; PM4Py usage is not

Excluding short traces instead of padding, excluding prefixes that already contain accept/reject/transfer/activation, keeping document loops, and canonicalizing names before DFG/variants are correct.

Defects:

- Fixed-k and first-review landmarks are conflated.
- Dedup on `(case, canonical activity, timestamp, resource, source_system)` will miss the “occasional” migration duplicates that are the dangerous ones (shifted timestamps, renamed resources, duplicated with a new `source_system`).
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, k)` is treated as “the k-event prefix log.” In PM4Py this API returns the *truncation of every case to at most k events* (short cases included), not a clean length-k pre-decision cohort. Variants and DFG are then computed on that mixed object, *before* the later `n_events == 3` and no-terminal filters. The printed “Unique k=3 prefix variants” is therefore not a k=3, pre-decision, modeling-cohort statistic. That is an audit that does not audit the analysis sample.

## §5 Features — reasonable list, no discipline about what the code will actually ship

The static / prefix / historical-reference split is correct, as is the ban on trailing rates that use labels not yet observable at the prefix clock. Variant/DFG indicators are only safe if computed on prefixes of the modeling cohort; §9 does not do that.

The list never commits to an allowlist. That omission becomes the §9 leakage bug.

## §6 Leakage audit — the table is stronger than the pipeline

The required rows are present and mostly right. `documents_total`, extraction-time tenure, retroactive `risk_score`, and `label_available_at` are handled correctly *as prose*.

Missing from a strict audit:

- Case-level denormalization: if `default_90d` / `final_limit` / `collection_status` are written back onto early event rows once known, “we did not aggregate them” is not a control. A serious pipeline nulls or drops those columns *before* prefixing.
- `data_extract_time` and raw `start_time` / `prefix_time` as features (calendar identity, extract artifact).
- `cohort_state`, `activation_time`, `label_observed_by_cutoff`, `activation_mature_by_cutoff` — all constructed later and all illegal as predictors. The table never mentions the features the code actually passes downstream.

“Execute the audit before every split” is asserted, not implemented.

## §7 Validation — the most important design error after the code leak

A random split is correctly rejected. The proposed calendar cut is not a finished backtest.

- Train (starts ≤ 2025-03-31) and validation (2025-04-01–2025-06-30) are entirely *pre* policy change. The locked test (2025-07-01–2025-09-30) is entirely *post* policy change. That can be a stress test only if no test-set information is used to adapt. The same section then says to “recalibrate probabilities separately for the pre- and post-2025-07-01 regime if calibration drift is material.” There is no post-policy calibration window that is not the test set. Recalibrating on test is leakage. Not recalibrating means the locked metric is an unadapted regime-shift number, which is fine only if sold as such. The text wants both.
- Test-window maturity is not confronted. Conservative full maturity requires activation by 2025-10-13. A case that starts 30 September and activates on a normal multi-week cycle is often *not* mature by 15 February 2026. Evaluation “only on activated holdout cases with observed labels” therefore retains fast-to-activate, fully fed accounts and drops slow/partner/missing-feed accounts. That is a second selection layer on top of activation. It is mentioned as a count to report, not as a bias in the headline PR-AUC/Brier.
- Starts 2025-10-01–2025-10-14 fall into an undocumented `unscored_gap` in the code. §7 never defines that interval.
- The post-migration window is correctly barred from primary `default_90d` scoring, then still implied to be useful for “structural audit” of a default model that cannot be labeled there. DFG/variant drift is all it can support.
- No discussion of test-set size or uncertainty after all filters. Three post-policy months of *starts*, after activation and label filters, can easily be a small, composition-shifted slice. PR-AUC on that slice is not a serious confirmation plan.

Metrics for a complete-case binary (PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier, calibration, subgroups) are appropriate *to the chosen complete-case estimand*. They do not evaluate the 3,600 unlabeled activations or the 22,800 never-activated applications. For a study whose point is uncertainty and selection, that is an evaluation hole.

## §8 Limitations — better than most, still too tidy

The association vs selection vs counterfactual distinction is stated. Several performativity channels are listed.

The “safe decision boundary” is not safe: “use the primary model for risk stratification within the historically activated portfolio.” At the declared landmark the account is not in that portfolio yet. If the score is only used after activation, the first-3-event prefix is the wrong feature time. If it is used before activation, the selected-sample model is the wrong estimand. The text offers a pathway model for “every application” but never specifies how the two scores would be combined for the actual accept / document-request / early-warning decision. Without that, §8 is a disclaimer, not a decision-use boundary.

## §9 Code — this is where the answer breaks the assignment

The skeleton is long (~250 lines vs the requested ~80–160), partly dead (`time_prefix` is built and never turned into a cohort or feature table), and not safe to train on.

The decisive defect:

```text
feature_cols = [c for c in model_df.columns if c not in {final_limit, decision_reason,
    collection_status, default_90d, label_available_at}]
train_df = model_df.loc[split=="train", feature_cols + ["y_primary"]]
```

After the filter to activated cases with observed labels, `cohort_state` is a deterministic recoding of `default_90d` (`activated_default90` / `activated_nondefault90`). It is not in the ban list. A model fit on `train_df` as written has direct label leakage. The same matrix still contains `activation_time` (future), `label_observed_by_cutoff`, `activation_mature_by_cutoff`, `overdue_missing_label`, `split`, `y_primary` itself, and raw timestamps. This is not a cosmetic omission. The prompt’s quality bar is leakage prevention. The code fails that bar.

Other implementation defects, more briefly:

- No explicit start-window filter to 2024-01-01–2025-12-31, despite §2.
- `ACTIVITY_MAP` is a stub; all downstream “canonical k=3 = first review” claims depend on it.
- Terminal state is the first of `{Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, Account Activated}`. `Accepted` / `Contract Signed` without activation become `open`.
- Time-based prefixes are unfinished: no terminal filter, no `n`/`t` landmark table, no feature freeze.
- Leakage “check” is a print of how many prefixes contain forbidden *activities*, plus a reminder that `documents_total` exists. No column allowlist, no as-of snapshot test, no assertion that banned attributes are absent from `train_df`.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` / `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` run on the unfiltered truncated log and are not connected to `model_df`.
- Resource-experience repair, trailing as-of rates, and IPW are comments only. Acceptable in a skeleton; not a substitute for a non-leaking feature table.
- `np.select` timezone-aware cutpoints plus `unscored_gap` are undocumented relative to §7.

The code does do several assigned things: `read_csv` + timestamp parse, `format_dataframe`, case-level activation/maturity flags, temporal rather than random split, prefix row restriction by `event_index`, and a cohort-count print. That is why this is not a 4. It is not close to a clean 80–160 line engineering argument.

## Net

The answer understands the trap (do not train blindly on 21,600; do not call omitted cases non-default; do not random-split across policy and migration). It then weakens that understanding at every place where design must become a closed, as-of, non-leaking procedure: landmark semantics, sequential cohort counts, post-policy validation without test-set recalibration, maturity-induced selection in the test metric, and a feature matrix that reintroduces the label. Under a lenient reading this is a solid draft. Under the required standard it is not nearly flawless, and the implementation would not survive its own leakage audit.
