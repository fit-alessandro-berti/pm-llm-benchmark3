**8.5**

The audit is complete, process-aware, and hits the published quality bar: it flags both obvious target leakage and subtler lineage bugs, keeps congestion and prefix-age as legitimate signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as feasible-but-future rather than as the same-timestamp impossibility, and consistently separates predictive association from causal or operational claims. The five required sections are present, the table covers every mandated item, and the Case X/Y/Z JSON matches the specified schema without putting leaked outcomes into the revised narratives.

What keeps it short of near-flawless is a set of real conceptual slips, not just wording.

The biggest miss is the relationship between appeals and the *settlement* target. If the 10-day SLA is time to first `Settlement Decision Recorded`, then `appeal_seen_anywhere` and a second decision are not merely future information; they are often *post-label*. “Appeals cause breaches” is then backwards as well as leaked: an appeal can only follow Partial/Deny, so the feature can be a consequence of an already-recorded (possibly timely) decision. The answer treats this as timeline-extending leakage and never draws the post-outcome distinction. The same gap appears for `total_case_duration_hours`: calling it a near-reproduction of the label is too strong if duration is measured to close (including appeal) rather than to first settlement. It is still critical leakage; it is not necessarily a deterministic transform of the target.

`Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is oversold as “near-tautological protection.” On a complete trace, almost every settled case contains that pair, breached or not. Negative importance is therefore not explained by “the decision happened,” and may be an artifact of how the pattern miner encodes timing or of cases that never decide. Severity `high` is defensible; the mechanism is not.

A related overstatement: `num_document_requests_total` is “identically 0” at `t0` only if the log obeys the normative order. That is a model assumption, not a data fact, and it sits awkwardly next to Case Z’s `same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6`, which shows that document *artifacts* can exist around `t0` even if the `Documents Requested` activity has not fired. The request-count vs upload-count distinction is never made.

Case X’s status `recompute` still leans on the *signs* of remaining SHAP contributions from a model trained with leaked features. The text warns that attributions will shift after decontamination, then uses those same directions as “credible drivers.” After joint estimation with `appeal_seen_anywhere` and `final_decision`, even `queue_length_at_t0` and `initial_damage_complexity` cannot be trusted at face value until refit. Case Y’s `invalidate` is the more honest status; X should have been closer to it, with qualitative process facts rather than inherited SHAP signs.

Smaller issues: `initial_reserve` is graded `low` with no lineage in the notes (pending timestamp checks, `medium` is safer); “months of process time” for an appeal is loose against a 10-day SLA; the Y variant `REG-COV-DOC-DOC-DMG-DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE` is process-legal (repeated DEC after appeal) as well as temporally illegal, and that dual nature is left implicit.

Section 2 is otherwise strong (PIT replay, truncation invariance, footprint vs alignment vs declarative limits, enabling/impossible/future-possible, repeated-DEC rules, and an explicit “frequent DFG ≠ normative” gate). Section 3’s five-way taxonomy and the refusal to delete region/channel are exactly what the task asked for. Section 5’s P0–P3 plan covers every required control. Those parts are close to the standard of a very high score; the SLA/label and complete-trace-pattern mechanism errors are why it does not get one.
