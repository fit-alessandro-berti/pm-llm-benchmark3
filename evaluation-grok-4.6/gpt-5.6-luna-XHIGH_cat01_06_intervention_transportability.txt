**7.2**

The answer is substantively on the right side of the problem: it refuses to treat Site A’s before/after contrast as an identified treatment effect, refuses a wholesale copy of A’s task graph, gates originals, keeps Legal Review downstream, and designs a mediator-aware stratified pilot. Format compliance is high. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several quantitative, causal-graph, DFG, and model-fidelity defects are material.

**What is strong.** Section 1 covers the required features and the right distinctions (avoidable precedence vs statutory gates, cross-training vs specialized pools, enabling IT vs the one-owner lock, mix shift as confounder). Section 3 is the best part of the writeup: the nine-hour median move is repeatedly treated as a confounded association; API, +2 FTE, marketing mix, huddles, and timestamp drift are carried as co-interventions; transport confidences on the cycle-time rows are appropriately low. Sections 6, 7, and 9 give a coherent operational answer: digital-complete concurrent ID/document work; blocked-not-executed Document Check on the originals path; versioned supplement loops; WIP/admission control; case-level randomized (or stepped) pilot with T1/T2/T3; stop rules on legality, p90, and rework; `conditional go` with a small first stratum. That is the correct recommendation class.

**Quantitative and analogical defects (largest score hits).**

1. Eligible-share arithmetic does not match the scenario label. “Digital, non-legal, *lower-complexity*” is computed as \(0.38\times0.60\approx23\%\). High-complexity is 48% at B, so the labeled cell is closer to 12% if independence is assumed, and unknown if digital, legal, and complexity are associated (they almost certainly are). The parenthetical “measure the actual intersection” does not repair a mislabeled planning number.

2. Scenario 1 is internally inconsistent. The text calls A’s six-hour pre-Assessment wait reduction a “loose upper anchor,” then posts an eligible median-cycle range of **−6 to −10 h**. That exceeds the stated anchor and quietly re-imports A’s confounded nine-hour headline. An optimistic bound can be wide; it cannot contradict the sentence that justifies it.

3. The 38% residual after “originals required = 62%” is treated as a digitally complete, immediately parallelizable package. Site B only says originals are legally required for 62%. The complement need not be registration-complete digital files. Sections 4–5 and the first-wave recommendation all rest on that slide.

4. The DFG is underused where it would have disciplined the scenarios. Register→Identity Check is already 2,050/3,400; Register→Originals Receipt 1,860; Identity Check→Document Check 1,160 (≈34%, close to the non-originals share). Site B *already permits* identity and originals receipt after Register. The incremental originals-path treatment is mostly “start identity immediately / don’t serialize it in front of the 18 h receipt wait,” not the introduction of a new partial order. The gated-originals scenario (−2 to −5 h median, +5 to +20 h p90) does not separate already-legal concurrency from new task creation, so it overstates structural novelty and under-identifies the actual lever.

5. “Transport confidence” is not a stable estimand. Rows on mix, staffing, huddles, no RCT, and timestamp provenance score 80–95, while cycle-time rows score 15–35. Some of those high numbers are confidence in a *negative* transport claim, which is defensible only if the column is redefined mid-table. As written it inflates certainty and is unclear to a second grader.

**Causal graph.** Required node names and S-nodes are present, and the 180–280 word note is in range and conceptually right (shared critical-path mechanism; site-specific originals gate, lock, specialized pools, legal loop; similar trees ≠ transport). Missing or misspecified edges matter: `assessment_capacity` should hit `waiting_before_assessment` (that is exactly B’s “median waiting before Assessment”); `identity_capacity` and `document_capacity` should hit `true_overlap`, not only waiting; `digital_documents` has no path to waiting except via overlap; `IT_case_lock` and `role_specialization` stop at overlap; `treatment_parallelization → rework` is unmediated while `true_overlap → rework` is absent. Legal capacity → rework without a supplement/loop node is crude. This is a sketch, not a transport graph that would actually identify effect modification.

**PM4Py / model fidelity.** Site A trees, Petri conversion, a non-sequential Site B POWL, explicit `(identity, assessment)` and `(document_track, assessment)` edges, and comments on data/resources/locks/durations all meet the prompt’s skeleton. The legal/supplement fragment does **not** match Site B’s stated rules. Legal Review should be a choice among approve / reject / Request Supplement, with the supplement sending work back through document check and assessment into Legal Review again. The code does Legal Review once, then `XOR(τ, LOOP(supplement_cycle, τ))`, then a separate Legal Approve/Reject. `Legal Review [supplement]` is not a decision node; redo is silent. Relabeling Document Check as `[digital]` / `[originals]` / `[supplement]` fragments the activity the rest of the memo treats as one task. `hasattr` guards around `check_is_workflow_net`, `check_soundness`, and `play_out`, plus a fallback import `pm4py.objects.powl.to_petri_net`, mean the required soundness/playout steps may no-op; `parameters={"no_traces": 3}` is not the usual PM4Py playout key (`noTraces`). The prompt asked for constructors consistent with a supplied manual and a working check, not a best-effort wrapper.

**Smaller unclarities.** Interference with 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers is acknowledged via WIP slots but still underestimated; case-level randomization on those pools is almost all spillover. Pilot “200 per arm in each digital/originals × legal cell” is tight against 3,400/6 months and a 12–16 week window, especially in the digital×legal cell; the 100-case floor and extend rule only partly save it. Queue huddles are listed as a Site A *after* queue-discipline feature, which conflates a co-intervention with a standing WIP rule. Section 2 is at the 280-word ceiling and still does not say how one would identify the graph from B’s DFG.

**Net.** This is a competent transport memo with the right decision (`conditional go`), the right first subgroup class, and a serious attempt at every required artifact. It is not a 9+ answer: the quantitative scenarios argue more precisely than they compute, the DFG is decoration rather than identification, the causal graph is incomplete, and the POWL legal loop is the sequential-looking simplification the prompt warned against, just moved into the legal branch. Strictly, that is mid-7 work.
