- External arrivals in 08:00–10:00 are **13** (P01–P13 if 10:00 is excluded; 14 if it is included), never 12. The 12 / 9 / 1 split is internally consistent with a total of 22 and therefore silently drops one morning arrival.

I1’s observed complete work is 215 min; plus P21’s 30 min elapsed is 245; plus central P07/P15 (30+30) is **305**. The 245–280 band matches no clean combination of those pieces. C1 *does* include P17’s 5 min elapsed, so the two stations are treated inconsistently. That is exactly “inferring utilization from completed cases alone,” which the quality bar forbids.
- A4’s evidence list is cherry-picked and partly wrong. C1 goes P07 end 11:00 → P09 start 11:00 (zero gap), which falsifies “every back-to-back start equals previous end +5.” The claimed C2 gap “12:00 → 12:15” is **C1’s P12 start**, not a C2 start. These are not rounding quibbles. They sit in the case table, the station table, and the assumption that later capacity arithmetic treats as fact.

## Internal inconsistencies and category errors

**A4 is an assumption, then sold as observation.** Section 5’s “capacity arithmetic” is labeled “Observation (not hypothesis)” and converts 28+5 and 40+5 into \(\mu_{S1}\approx 3.6/h\), \(\mu_{S2}\approx 2.7/h\). The 5 min gap is compatible with mandatory changeover, with 5 min rounding, or with tick-aligned logging. Realized S2 throughput of 2.8/h over 09:00–14:00 is *above* that notional capacity; a 0-gap C1 handover already shows the +5 rule is not physical. That tension is never faced. Effective capacity is a scenario, not a measurement.

**Central imputation is not the rule A5 states.** A5 says earliest feasible start +5. The table uses that for P07/P15 (09:30, 11:45) but 0-changeover earliest starts for P05/P11 (wait 20 / 55, service 30 / 25). The P05 note even admits 09:45 vs 09:50, then prints the 0-gap pair.

**Little’s Law S2 is computed with two windows.** \(L=1340/335=4.00\) is right. Then \(\lambda=20/320\) produces a cosmetic \(\lambda W=4.19\) and a “5% boundary effect.” The same \(\Delta=335\) gives \(\lambda W=4.00\) exactly. The code uses one span; the prose invents a discrepancy. For an answer that spends a paragraph refusing the wrong form of Little’s Law, this is a real slip.

**Optimistic 0-service imputations** are correctly called implausible in the notes, then still used to push I1 utilization to 0.61 and to advertise a 22 pp range. The *feasible* range, given the rest of the service distribution, is much tighter. The stability table therefore overstates how unstable I1 utilization is, even though the qualitative warning (do not staff from I1 vs I2) is fair.

Minor additional nits: P18–P20 are bundled with “fully observed unremarkable cases” though S2 is blank; “queues grow monotonically” is false for S1 after arrivals stop; unfinished cases “will all exceed” the completed-case mean sojourn — P22’s elapsed time is already only 110, and the claim is a projection, not a fact; A2 lists “queue entry at the next station” as distinct from the previous completion even though the scenario defines them as the same instant.

## What survives

The conclusions that *should* survive are mostly the ones the author marks stable: 20 S1 completions vs 14 S2 completions, system WIP area 3425 min (mean 9.5, end-of-window 8, peak 13), S2 still building a queue of 4 at 14:00, S1 largely drained, C2 outage ≈ one-third of a service and not the cause of the backlog, completed-case sojourn mean 146 biased low by right-censoring. The time-varying bottleneck (S1 first because it is first; S2 as the binding constraint once both are loaded) is the right reading of the counts. That is why this is not a mid-score reconstruction.

It is a 7 rather than an 8+ because a strict grader cannot treat wrong arrival splits, wrong peak \(L_1\), an unreproducible wait summary, an I1 busy-time that drops an OPEN activity, a false universal changeover, and a self-inflicted Little’s-Law residual as “minor polish.” Those are the quantities the prompt asked to estimate or bound.
