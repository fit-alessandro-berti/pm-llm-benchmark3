**6.5**

The final nine-section design is substantively on-target: it refuses to treat rejected/withdrawn/open cases as non-default, does not train only on the 21,600 labeled rows, keeps an as-of prefix, and uses a temporal split. Those are the main failure modes the prompt warned against. It is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and statistical gaps are large enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.

**What is actually strong.** The primary estimand is a competing-risk cumulative incidence of “activated and then default within 90 days,” defined for every application, with a clearly different conditional-on-activation alternative. The cohort table sums to 48,000 and assigns omitted groups to competing events, censoring, or missing follow-up rather than to a fake negative class. The leakage table hits every required field with the right keep/repair/drop calls. Validation correctly rejects a random split because of the July 2025 policy change, the October 2025 migration, and delayed label maturity.

**Target and time scales are not fully specified.** Section 1 defines event \(E\) on the activation clock (90 days after \(A\)) and reports \(F_E(H)\) on the prediction clock (\(H=365\) from \(t_p\)). That two-clock construction is legitimate, but the write-up never states the event time used in the CIF (calendar time of default vs \(A+90\) vs first competing terminal). “Activated-no-default” is called a competing event without saying it is observed only at \(A+90\). IPCW is stacked on cause-specific hazards without saying what it is for (Aalen–Johansen already handles independent censoring; IPCW is for metrics or missingness). This is more than wording: a reader could not implement the likelihood from the text alone.

**The stated estimand and the code labels disagree.** `HORIZON_DAYS` is never applied. `summarise_case` classifies from the full extraction window, so a 2024 case that activates late and defaults inside 90 days of activation is labeled `activated_default` even if that occurs after \(t_p+365\). That is a horizon violation relative to Section 1, not a minor omission.

**Prefix rules collapse in both design and code.** \(t_p=\min(\text{start}+7\text{d},\;k\text{th event},\;\text{decision}-\epsilon)\) is a single hybrid cutoff, not the two prefix schemes the prompt asked for. For traces with fewer than four events, `kth_time = last_event+1s` so \(\min(\text{start}+7\text{d},\;\text{last_event}+1\text{s})\) is almost always the last event; the 7-day elapsed prefix is not extracted. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then ignored; features are built from all pre-\(t_p\) events. Short-case handling therefore does not match Section 4.

**The primary temporal split makes the policy feature unidentified.** Train is all of 2024 and validation is 2025 H1, both before 1 July 2025; test is entirely post-policy. A `policy_regime` dummy is then constant in train/val and constant-at-the-other-level in test, so it cannot be estimated and cannot adjust the relationship the prompt flagged (same prefix, different selection/outcome after the threshold change). Rolling-origin backtesting is mentioned but not specified at the level of which origins have mature 90-day labels. Test starts through December 2025 against a 15 February 2026 extract will be dominated by immature activation outcomes; that is noted in prose and not built into the split or the case table.

**Missingness is partly conflated.** The 3,600 rows are “not yet matured” in the snapshot; partner feeds that are permanently missing are a separate mechanism with no count. The design and code merge them as `activated_immature_or_missing`. Transfer linkage (60%) is a sensitivity in text only. `documents_total` is correctly flagged in the audit and never repaired in the skeleton. Resource experience is correctly called extraction-time leakage and then dropped from the implementation rather than shown as an as-of join.

**The skeleton is in the right shape and still thin as an audit pipeline.** It does the required CSV parse, `format_dataframe`, case-level as-of fields, start-time split, prefix filter, DFG/variants, a column-name leakage assert, and a competing-risk TODO without inventing a PM4Py survival API. The leakage check cannot catch post-decision fields used inside aggregations; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is commented out inside a try/except; no `time_to_event` / cause / censor indicators are created; Accepted is used only to truncate prefixes and is not a modeled state, so accepted-but-not-activated cases are silently folded into still-open. Transferred cases that re-enter under a new ID are not discussed as a double-counting risk against the 48,000 starts.

Net: statistically much better than a convenience labeled-cohort design, but not a tight, internally consistent study protocol. The grade reflects those inconsistencies rather than missing section headers.
