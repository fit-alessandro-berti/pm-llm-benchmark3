**4.0**

The writeup is well-structured and the POWL *sketch* is conceptually close to the right non-block-structured design. Under a strict reading it still fails several core deliverables: executable fidelity, alphabet completeness, witness correctness, and honest language comparison.

**Fatal model errors**

- `Design Corrective Action` is a required visible activity. It is absent from the compact tree, the Python tree, and the compact POWL node set. Dropping a label is not a permissive over-approximation. Over-approximation is `L_intended ⊆ L_model` on a shared alphabet. Here the languages are not even comparable. The prose only says the activity was “omitted from the direct trial prerequisite,” which disguises a missing activity as a scheduling approximation.
- A faithful block-structured over-approximation was available and not unreasonably complex, e.g. `→(Register, +(→(Contain, Plan), →(Analyze, +(→(Design, trial-loop), risk-xor, supplier-xor))), Release, Archive)`. That keeps every label, waits for Design before the trial, and still admits extra interleavings on Plan vs. trial. The submitted tree is weaker than that and is not defended as such.
- The tree also lets `Execute Trial` start after `Analyze Root Cause` alone, with no join on `Plan Verification`. That *is* extra behavior, but the answer never exhibits it.

**POWL code would not run**

- `OperatorPOWL` supports only `Operator.XOR` and `Operator.LOOP`. Parallelism is a `StrictPartialOrder` with no edge. `OperatorPOWL.PARALLEL` does not exist.
- `OperatorPOWL.XOR` / `OperatorPOWL.LOOP` are also wrong; the operator enum lives on `Operator`, which is never imported.
- High-risk concurrency should be `StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_qual, t_reg_rev])` nested under the XOR. As written this is an immediate `AttributeError` or constructor exception, so “standalone Python” is not satisfied for the POWL half.
- The compact POWL text is internally inconsistent (Design appears in edges, not in `nodes`) and is not a real POWL term.

The *edge set* in the Python sketch is the strongest part of the answer: Register splits to Contain/Analyze; Plan after Contain; Design, risk XOR, and supplier XOR after Analyze; trial after Plan *and* Design; Release after trial, approvals, and supplier; Archive last. That is the intended partial order. It does not rescue non-executable constructors.

**Witness section fails the stated tests**

- T1–T5 all contain `Design Corrective Action`. The tree cannot emit that label, so the “Process-tree verdict = Allowed” cells are false.
- T5 is illegal in the *intended* language: it contains Supervisor Review **and** Quality Review **and** Regulatory Review. Both models use XOR on the risk route, so T5 is forbidden in all three columns. It does not expose tree over-approximation, and it does not even show approval/trial overlap (reviews finish before `Execute Trial`).
- No allowed row isolates the actual tree extra behavior (`Execute Trial` before `Plan Verification`, or a trace without Design).
- T6–T8 are all same-branch or missing-step errors. None is a cross-branch violation such as trial before Plan, trial before Design, or Release before a selected `Consult Supplier`. The prompt required one such counterexample.
- T6 is truncated with `...`, so it is not a fully specified witness.

**Internal contradictions**

- §1 and §3 advertise a permissive over-approximation; §6 says the tree *overconstrains* the Plan/Design split and “may restrict flexible interleaving.” The constructed tree does the opposite.
- Approval/trial overlap is intended, not an over-approximation; §6 marks it as permissive extra behavior.
- “May overlap” is defined as both true concurrency and mere absence of order. Those coincide only after the representation is fixed.
- LOOP is a non-deterministic redo, not an enforcement of “adjust on failure.” That is acceptable as an abstraction, but §6 overclaims it.

**What is actually fine**

Assumptions hit the required bullets. The dependency table is mostly the right specification (D1–D10). Trial-as-`LOOP((Execute, Inspect), Adjust)` is the correct control-flow encoding of pass/fail without data labels. Optional supplier as XOR-with-τ, risk as XOR(Supervisor, Quality ∥ Regulatory), and Release as a multi-predecessor join are the right ideas. Format, heading set, and preference for POWL are appropriate. The validation plan names the right tools, though it cannot be executed on the submitted POWL object and never targets the real bugs (missing label, XOR violation, cross-branch join).

**Self-critique is too soft**

The three listed weaknesses are generic (data-free loops, visual POWL clutter, tree false positives). They do not mention the dropped activity, the illegal OperatorPOWL API, T5, or the missing cross-branch negative witness. That is a semantic failure, not a syntax nit.

Net: a complete-looking document with one conceptually good partial order, wrapped around a broken tree alphabet, non-runnable POWL constructors, and a witness table that does not test what it claims. That is well below a high score under a strict rubric.
