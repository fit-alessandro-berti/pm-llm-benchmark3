**3.2**

The answer follows the required heading skeleton and correctly notices that K-337 is a decision-policy change, not a pure timing tweak. That is not enough. The counterfactuals, queue logic, ranking, and synthesis are repeatedly wrong in ways the prompt itself warned against.

**Feasibility model is incomplete and internally broken.** The partial order omits `Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check`, `Request More Info ≺ Document Check ≺ Assessment`, re-compliance after rework, and the four-eyes constraint as a feasibility restriction. `Triage < {Identity Check, Document Check}` is too strong when originals are required. `Identity Check  Document Check < Compliance Review` is malformed. `Decision > Compliance Review` flips the inequality convention used everywhere else. Conditional constraints are a thin restatement of a few rules, not an operational model. Classifying K-104 as “timing only” is false: slot reservation reallocates scarce C1 capacity.

**The case table does not construct feasible minimal-change paths.** None of the four `counterfactual path` cells is an explicit activity sequence. That is a direct format miss.

- **K-104 is the worst error.** The intervention is *at Triage* (Mon 08:20–08:50). The answer starts “preliminary compliance” at **Tue 19:20**, which is the *factual* escalation time. The local change is therefore not the specified intervention; it is a slightly earlier Wednesday C1 booking after assessment. Early-packet incompleteness, S1 overlap with identity/document work, and the distinction between preliminary screen and final review after Assessment are not operationalized. “Decision risk: minimal” is overconfident given the stated incomplete-packet risk.
- **K-219 treats D2 as idle.** Originals arrive Mon 17:30; the Mon 16:00 snapshot gives D2 a **5 h** queue. Starting D2 at 17:30 ignores queues the prompt called forecasts, not idle capacity. Rework is bounded at **+10 h / +14 h** despite 8–36 h external collection *plus* a new Document Check *plus* a new Assessment. The sign of the effect is acknowledged, the magnitude is not.
- **K-337 assumes the unresolved discrepancy is “not material.”** The intervention forbids silently ignoring it and explicitly allows approve-with-conditions *or reject*. The path never branches. Cycle-time −48 to −72 h is an undifferentiated guess: no Friday C1 wait, no working-hours constraint, no four-eyes restatement, no reaffirmation of compliance after Assessment 2.
- **K-411 starts I2 and D2 at Tue 09:35.** Tue 08:00 queues are I2 **2 h** and D2 **4 h**. Concurrent *task creation* is not concurrent *service start*. Cycle time is a point estimate (−4 h) while the same row invents a +2 h assessment delay, which is inconsistent.

Queue snapshots are almost unused. Downstream times are subtracted as lumps, which the prompt forbade.

**Derivations are too thin to be counterfactual reasoning.** Each subsection restates the table and emits best/base/worst hours without propagation through the named queues. K-104 “only timing changes” contradicts reservation. K-219 never compares D2 4–8 h (and handwriting clarification risk) with D1’s factual 4 h clean check. K-337 best (−48 h) and base (−50 h) are essentially the same number; worst is a decision change, not a comparable time. K-411 best and base are identical. Four-eyes is barely checked. Earlier *readiness* is not distinguished from earlier *final completion*, especially for K-104 (prep does not finish compliance) and K-411 (earlier checks still face Wednesday assessment congestion).

**Capacity table inverts the K-411 interference story.** Parallel verification makes cases ready for assessment sooner, so the assessment queue should **grow**, not shrink, and “None” as the growing queue is the opposite of the prompt’s own complication. K-104 shrinking the **Identity** queue is unexplained. K-337 growing the **Decision** queue while shrinking an “External” queue is sloppy. The 100-case writeup is ~180 words against a 250–400 requirement. It never faces the hard nonlinearities: 100 C1 reservations against a single reviewer; 100 suppressed rework loops dumping high-risk files onto C1 earlier; D2 substitution raising *rework* that returns to Assessment, not a clean D1→D2 swap. “Weekend effects remain unpredictable” is a shrug, not analysis.

**Robustness ranking is self-contradictory.** Rank 1 is Suppress RMI, labeled **Low** robustness, **Low** reversibility, and high reversal risk. The ranking criterion was robustness across assumptions, quality/compliance, spillovers, and learning—not cycle time. Ranking the policy override first because it “prioritizes throughput” is exactly the error the evaluation rubric flags. Section 7 then calls that same intervention “most robust” *and* calls early C1 reservation “most dangerous,” which cannot be reconciled with putting reservation second and calling RMI non-contentious. At scale, suppressing the second loop *creates* C1 contention.

**Pilot design inherits the bad ranking.** Randomizing a decision-policy change on high-risk, high-value files with unresolved discrepancies, with an arbitrary 10% appeal stop, is not a serious compliance pilot. The second arm uses “manual selection based on volume,” which destroys identification. Logging rows do not validate four-eyes, incomplete early packets, or real-time vs batch timestamps.

**Synthesis and assumptions.** The synthesis repeats the inverted robustness claim, understates K-337’s quality danger, and picks K-411 as least transportable because of “system locks.” The least transportable local counterfactual is C1 slot reservation or suppressed rework on 100 similar files: one reserved slot is not a policy. Several assumption labels are mis-typed (complexity distribution as behavioral; risk-committee approval as behavioral). Missing are the assumptions the answer actually relied on: queues can be jumped, D2 can start immediately, reserved C1 slots are honored, and overnight/weekend service is unlimited.

**Net:** format-aware, conceptually leaky, temporally inconsistent, and ranked backwards. It would not pass a strict process-mining review.
