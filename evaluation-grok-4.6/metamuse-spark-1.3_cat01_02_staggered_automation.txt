**6.6**

The write-up is complete, format-compliant, and conceptually stronger than a typical causal-policy memo: it separates availability from take-up, refuses a single X% claim, keeps median and p90 apart, and recommends a stepped expansion that also improves identification. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict bar the gaps below are enough to keep it well out of the 8–10 range.

**What is solid.** Section 1 stays descriptive and hits the required distinctions. The estimand table covers ITT, dose, median, tail, rework, escalation, CSAT, and heterogeneity. Identification names staggered event-study DiD as primary, flags naive TWFE weighting, and gives synthetic control and ITS as alternatives with real failure modes (two donors, May–June slope break). The mechanism table is the best part: fast-path substitution vs specialist congestion, learning, routing, staffing, rework displacement, tail amplification, and survey selection. Main pre/post arithmetic is mostly correct (Alpha median Jan–Feb 48.7 → Jul–Aug 45.6; Gamma Shock→Mature DiD vs Delta about −5.1h median and −9.4h p90). Threats, outcome-wise confidence, the claim ladder, and `expand with an embedded evaluation` are the right shape of answer.

**Material logical flaws.** (1) The primary design is not the analysis that is actually specified. Relative time is built and then unused; the estimated models are two-way FE on a binary `automation` dummy and on `automated_fast_path_pct`. That is the naive TWFE the identification section warns against, with no cohort/event-study coefficients, no not-yet-treated coding, and no pre-trend estimates. (2) Section 5 asserts that intensity OLS “yields a negative coefficient” and that p90 is “smaller/positive in early months.” Those signs are not shown, and the code has no early/late split, so the early-month p90 claim is invented. (3) “Month FE absorb the March fraud-desk jump and May–June complexity surge” overstates identification: complexity, staffing, and routing are *not* common shocks of equal intensity. (4) The 30–50% “discount” is not a sensitivity analysis; it is an unmotivated haircut.

**Quantitative and process misses.** Never-treated and pre-period rows already show `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0`. That is a treatment-version and measurement issue and is never interrogated. Table 5.1 dumps Alpha/Beta months 3–4 and Gamma months 0–1 into one “Mature” column; the text notes this, the table still invites a false comparison. Epsilon rework Shock→Mature is about +0.2pp, not +0.3pp. E1’s population is “treated months in treated branches,” which is not an ITT. Relative-time construction (`period difference .apply(lambda x: x.n)`) is version-fragile and leaves never-treated as NaN, so the coded panel cannot implement the stated event-study even if someone ran it. Clustered TWFE on 5 clusters is fine as a warning demo; it is not evidence for the ranges in Section 7.

**What leadership would still get wrong.** Median confidence of 60 is high for two post months on Gamma, endogenous selection of Alpha/Beta, bundled routing, and missing fraud-desk waits. The memo correctly refuses “automation reduces cycle time by X%” and correctly treats CSAT as unidentified. It still implies a more transportable median ITT than the design can support, and it never cleanly says that fast-path substitution is not shown to be new capacity.

The recommendation (Delta first, Epsilon as live control, case-level queues, pre-registered guardrails) is the right policy given the data. The grade is held down by the strategy–code contradiction, unshown regression claims, a fake sensitivity, and the ignored 4% pre-adoption “automated” path.
