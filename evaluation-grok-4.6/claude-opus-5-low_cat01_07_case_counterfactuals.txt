**6.4**

The writeup is structurally complete and conceptually strong on the criteria the prompt actually cares about: partial orders, four-eyes, readiness versus completion, K-337 as a decision-policy change, and bottleneck migration. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, the quantitative core is sloppy, several timestamps are infeasible, and a few claims contradict the author’s own paths.

**Fatal and near-fatal quantitative errors.** K-104’s best case is advertised as ~42 h / 16 h saved, but the path still has `Decision`/`Notify` on Wednesday morning. Mon 08:10 → Wed 09:10 is ~49 h, a ~9–10 h save. 42 h would require a ~Wed 02:10 close that the same row forbids. The 42–52 h band also excludes the author’s own ~57 h worst case. K-219’s derivation says “cycle ~28 h vs 24 h factual”; the factual close is Wed 09:00 from Mon 09:00, i.e. **48 h**, not 24 h. That is a basic elapsed-time failure, not a rounding issue. K-337’s 118 h best-case save is ~9 h high (Fri ~20:15 vs next Wed 09:10 is ~109 h). These are not decorative numbers; they are the headline counterfactual effects.

**Infeasible or non-minimal timings.** C1 doing a Mon 14:00–16:00 prelim, with a 20 h compliance queue at 08:00 and two urgent cases arriving at 16:00, is queue-jumping dressed up as a reservation. Possible only if C1 aborts live work; the path presents it as a normal start. K-219’s A2 start at Tue 10:00 ignores the Tue 08:00 A2 queue of 6 h; a case ready at ~05:00 is more likely to start mid-afternoon. K-104 also shortens A1’s assessment (19:10 → 18:40) without the intervention touching assessment — extra mechanism, not minimal change. Overnight C1/M1 completions are used when convenient, then morning batches are invoked when they flatten gains; the shift calendar is not applied consistently.

**Overconfident invented parameters.** 25–35% D2 handwriting rework, ~10% sanctions conversion, 85% unchanged K-104 decisions, and a 25/55/20 K-337 outcome split are written like estimates. None are in the case file. The prompt asked for ranges and scenarios; fake precision is worse than a wide interval. The K-219 “probability-weighted −2 to +10 h” mixes an expectation with a range and flips the sign convention inside one sentence. A 30% rework tail at +18 to +36 h makes the expectation clearly positive (slower), which the table then softens to “roughly neutral.”

**Feasibility nits that matter.** Rule 6 lets Compliance issue `Request More Info`. The K-337 path assumes C1 will certify residual risk on Friday; it never states that the intervention also binds C1, so the second loop can reappear one activity later. S1’s Mon 09:30 start has no queue evidence. C8’s “±50%” queue law and C11’s shift rule are smuggled into the constraint block as if they were normative process rules. Assumption 8 is tagged with two labels; the spec asked for one.

**Ranking quietly rewrites K-104.** The specified intervention is packet **and** provisional C1 reservation. The robustness table and synthesis promote “packet, reservation optional,” then rank that cleaned-up version first. That is motte-and-bailey. On the intervention as specified, K-104’s scarce-server reservation is the least scalable lever in the set; K-411 (artefact removal, no quality shift) is the more robust object. Ranking a high-risk evidentiary-standard change (K-337) above a reversible routing trial (K-219) is defensible only if cycle time is still doing too much work in the ranking, which the author denies.

**Format breaches.** Section 4’s 100-case writeup is ~600+ words against a 250–400 limit. Section 7 is over 320. These are not cosmetic when the spec said “exactly” and gave word bands.

**What remains good, and why this is not a 4.** The feasibility model is the right shape (not one fake global sequence). K-219 and K-411 correctly treat M1/A2 as absorbers. K-337 is correctly refused as a “save two days” story. Interference, D1/D2 equalisation, C1 ρ→1, weekend/external dominance, and false concurrency are the right system points. The pilot table is usable. That is a strong qualitative answer with a broken stopwatch. On a hypercritical rubric, a broken stopwatch on a timing counterfactual is disqualifying for the top band.
