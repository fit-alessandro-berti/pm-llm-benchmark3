**4.0**

The submission has the required skeleton and some competent meta-reasoning, but the three models—the actual deliverable—are internally inconsistent, fail their own inductive claims, and would not do what the prose says. On a hypercritical reading that is a core failure, not a cosmetic one.

**Models do not implement the stated hypotheses.** Conservative is defined as replaying observed variants and blocking the rest. The tree is `RO → XOR(CC≫ER, ER≫CC) → XOR(τ, SC) → XOR(τ, SC≫EX, EX) → App → Rel → Arc`. That language cannot generate the two traces `CC, SC, ER, …` or the expedite trace `ER, CC, EX, SC, …`. Those are 3/30 cases. A conservative model that drops observed behavior is not conservative; it is an underfitted sequential abstraction. The same tree also *overfits*: `opt_sc` followed by `seq(sc, ex)` admits a second `Supplier Confirmation`, and `XOR(τ, EX)` after a skipped SC admits the unobserved `CC, ER, EX, App`. So the model is simultaneously too tight and too loose.

Balanced repeats the same structural mistake. `AND(CC, ER)` then optional SC then optional EX cannot replay `CC, SC, ER` or `EX` before `SC`. The write-up claims two SEQ children realize “EX before/after SC”; the code is `xor(τ, seq(ex, τ), seq(τ, ex))`, which is just optional EX. The before/after-SC hypothesis is not implemented. Choosing a hard AND-join before SC also contradicts D5 *and* the only traces that speak to D5. That is the one place the log actually informs the SC synchronization question, and both tree models ignore it.

Permissive is the right *kind* of object (a partial order) and then breaks optionality. Every node in a `StrictPartialOrder` is mandatory. There is no `OperatorPOWL`/`SilentTransition` XOR. The text says optionality is “handled by omitting nodes in specific instances,” which is not a semantics POWL has. As written, every case must contain both SC and EX. Fitness on this log would be on the order of the two expedite traces (both happen to contain SC), not 1.00. The model also lets SC start immediately after `Register Order`, which is weaker than D5 (after *either* check). Permissive may loosen constraints; it may not silently make optional work mandatory.

**API / executability.** `ProcessTree(operator=Operator.TAU)` is not valid PM4Py (`Operator` has SEQUENCE/XOR/PARALLEL/LOOP/OR, not TAU; silence is `label=None`). `StrictPartialOrder("Production-POWL")` is not the node-list constructor; `po.transitions.add` is the wrong collection. `fitness_alignments(...)['averageFitness']` is the wrong key. `check_soundness(...)[0]` is unsafe if the function returns a bool. The validation script would not run as a self-contained check of these candidates.

**Evidence ledger is only half-done.** Grouping all 30 cases as V1–V30 hides the only diagnostically important variants: skip-SC (7 cases), `CC < SC < ER` (2), and the two EX placements (after SC vs between the checks and SC). Those should have been separate rows. The DFG remark is correct—bidirectional arcs across *different* traces do not prove concurrency—but it stays generic and never uses the SC↔ER edges (freq 2 vs 13) as evidence. “EX always precedes Approve” is sloppy: EX is usually absent. Reliability labels are reasonable; the modeling implications are not carried into the trees.

**Section 6 is factually wrong, not just sloppy.** Conservative is said to admit “none within length ≤ 8,” then the next rows admit and exclude traces, including `CC, ER, EX, App`, which the *code* admits and the *prose* excludes. `ER, CC, SC, App` is labeled unobserved; it is the second-most frequent variant (7/30). Permissive lists `CC, ER, SC, App` as “never observed”; that is the mode (12/30). A behavior audit that cannot recognize the log’s majority traces cannot be trusted. Excluded traces are dumped into the same table with an “(Excluded)” prefix instead of a clean second table. Conservative does not get two clearly excluded traces with an earliest violated constraint.

**Matrix invents the numbers the prompt forbade, and they are false.** The spec asked for qualitative or interval assessments. The answer prints `1.00`, `≈0.97`, `1.00`. Conservative and permissive are not perfectly fitting; balanced is closer to 27/30 exact-fit than 0.97. Precision/generalization comments would be plausible *if* the models were the ones described. They are not. “XOR explosion” is mentioned without the required discussion of duplicate labels and scalability—while the conservative tree accidentally *does* duplicate `SC`.

**Shared assumptions collapse the distinction they were asked to keep.** Missing SC (S5, 5–10% integration loss) and genuine skip of stocked parts (S4) are both compiled into an unconditional silent XOR. That is an allowed abstraction only if disclosed *and* not then treated as a domain fact. D3 is medium-high and is encoded; D5 is low-medium and is dropped even where the log supports it. S1 is said to drive conservative sequencing, but conservative already allows both CC/ER orders because both are frequent—so the S1 “preference” is rhetorical.

**What is actually good.** Headings match the spec. At least one tree and one POWL are attempted. D1–D2 hard spine is respected in all three designs. The concurrency caveat, the completion-timestamp assumption, and the “playout ≠ inclusion” limitation are stated. Sections 9–10 are the strongest pages: start+complete timestamps, external/stocked, integration-loss flags, and a distinguishing future-trace plan are the right next measurements. Confidence numbers are at least explicit.

Those strengths do not rescue the object-level work. The three candidates are not competing, executable hypotheses; they are three prose stories with broken encodings, a self-contradictory audit, and metrics that cannot be true of the nets as written. That is a 4, not a mid-range “good try.”
