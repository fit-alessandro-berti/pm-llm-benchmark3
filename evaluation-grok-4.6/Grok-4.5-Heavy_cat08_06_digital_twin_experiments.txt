**7.4**

The answer is a competent, complete design that hits the required section contract, keeps PM4Py off the runtime path, refuses to invent objection events, and does not claim optimality. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless. Several issues are conceptual, not cosmetic.

**Simulation design.** Scope, entities, calendars, AND-join at decision drafting, architect bursts, district bundles, and a latent objection factor are the right ingredients. Two design choices undercut intervention comparison. First, the parameter plan treats waiting-time distributions as things to *estimate and store*, while a digital twin that ranks D1–D10 must let municipal waiting *emerge* from contention, calendars, and batching, and treat only applicant/third-party delays as exogenous. The DES placeholders lean emergent; Section 2 leans parametric. That ambiguity is material: if waits are replayed, D3/D4/D6/D7 cannot be identified. Second, there is no workload–capacity reconciliation. Roughly 31,200 cases in 18 months, planning median 3.5 h (p90 14 h), and 8 FTE (two Simple-only) already imply planners are near or beyond saturation once means, rework, and reserved time are considered. Without that check, fitted service times plus HR calendars can produce a model that cannot reproduce the empirical waits, or that attributes non-queue delays (handoffs, incomplete files) to phantom congestion.

**Warm-up and tails.** “At least one full seasonal cycle … recommended 180 days” is internally false. Mar–May and Sep–Nov peaks define a ~12-month cycle, not 180 days. Complex-variance p90 is 260 days; a 180-day empty-start warmup cannot populate the long-case mix. Welch-style stabilization is the wrong instrument for a forced seasonal regime. The answer never specifies the observation convention after warmup (arrivals vs completions vs open WIP), so right-censoring of heavy tails is unhandled. Forty to fifty replications and CRN are reasonable; they do not repair a biased horizon.

**Process-mining grounding.** Avoiding `pm4py.read_csv`, using `format_dataframe`, and separating discovery from the engine match the stated conventions. The tree-limitation note is too generic. The narrative places rework after planning, legal check, and appeal; the string only loops completeness and inspection. That gap is not sharply treated as a required enrichment. In code, service/waiting columns are a string placeholder. Consecutive completion timestamps still allow sojourn features and a documented incomplete split; that was an explicit compute requirement.

**Experiments and assumptions.** The matrix covers baseline, D1–D10, and four stresses, with plausible trade-offs (D2 leakage, D3 first-case delay, D5 wasted legal work, D10 bottleneck shift). Effect sizes (D1 40–60%, D3 20–40% travel, D9 20–40%) are free parameters passed off as if they will be “calibrated” from a log that cannot identify D9 at all. S4 largely restates D10 plus non-stationarity already in the baseline. The OOS split (last 4–6 months) cannot test both seasonal peaks and is a weak holdout for this demand process.

**Uncertainty, validation, decisions.** Sensitivity, mixtures for tails, and “fit for ranking, not point prediction” are the right posture. Face/replay/distributional/utilization/bottleneck/OOS are all named. Cross-case reconstruction is not a first-class validation target. SLA cutoffs (30/90/120/180) and the 10%/15%/8 pp decision rule are arbitrary relative to the variant table (Complex median 140 d, p90 260 d makes a 180 d SLA a different object than a Simple 30 d SLA). No-shows and inspection readiness appear in the log summary and then fade from the state model.

**Code.** Modular and convention-faithful enough for a skeleton; too hollow where the prompt asked for actual statistics. `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` / `get_case_arrival_average` / `parse_process_tree` are plausible. Nothing executable demonstrates sojourn construction, NHPP arrivals, or calendars.

Net: strong report structure and the right digital-twin instincts, with a self-contradictory warmup, an unresolved waiting-time identification problem, missing capacity math, and a skeleton that ducks one of the few concrete compute requirements. That is clearly above a pass and clearly below “nearly flawless.”
