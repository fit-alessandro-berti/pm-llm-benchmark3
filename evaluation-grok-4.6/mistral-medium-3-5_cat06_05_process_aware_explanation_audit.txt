**1. The required Appeal vs impossibility distinction is not held consistently.**

**2. At least one retain-rule is temporally inverted.**
`Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is labeled “Process-possible / Low” with remediation “Retain if timestamped at/**after** `t0`.” After `t0` is leakage. The only admissible observation is fraud already opened at or before `t0`. **3. Sequence patterns are generally under-flagged for this prediction point.**
After coverage check, evidence cycles, damage assessment, settlement, and appeal are *future-possible*, not prefix-observed. `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` as “Defensible / Low” is process-unaware: under the normative model those activities are not enabled before `t0`, so a global sequence explainer that scores this pattern is almost certainly using the complete trace. Related: replacing `num_document_requests_total` / `full_trace_variant_id` with prefix analogues is incomplete without noting those prefix features are nearly degenerate (almost always 0 / `REG-COV`).

`elapsed_since_registration_at_t0 = 1.2 h` contributes *down*. The write-up calls the claim “slightly older” and talks about “reducing slack time.” 1.2 hours is a *fresh* prefix; remaining slack on a 10-day target is almost intact, which is why risk falls. “Reducing slack” is the old-claim theory and would raise risk. Direction and mechanism contradict each other. This is a direct failure to preserve prefix-age *semantics*, not just to keep the feature.

**5. Case Y uses an unrepaired feature as if the value were known.**
`prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` appears in `credible_drivers` *and* in `removed_or_repaired_drivers`, and the narrative still says “multiple prior claims.” After the SQL fix the count may not be 3.
