**6.6**

The answer is process-aware and clearly above a DFG-copy baseline, but it fails two *required* distinctions in a way that is not cosmetic. That caps the score well below the “nearly flawless” band.

**Core methodological failures**

The task asks for the next logged event and a clean split between what is process-enabled now and what may merely appear next in the feed. The answer repeatedly blurs both.

In C6-A, rank 2 is `Technical Investigation Completed` with `process_enabled: true`, while the reasoning says it is “Process-enabled only after review completes” and, worse, “Not the immediate next log at 14:00, but second-observation likelihood.” That is an explicit violation. Ranks 2–3 are mostly the *path after* Evidence Review, not alternative next observations from the 14:00 prefix. The same lookahead leak appears in C6-B (`Close — No Safety Issue`, `Customer Evidence Requested`) and C6-E rank 3. Midpoints are then presented as a top-three next-event distribution even though two of the three mass points are not next events under the primary state.

`process_enabled` is therefore unreliable as a flag. It is used correctly in a few places (C6-C SA; C6-D delayed CER/ER; C6-E SA) and incorrectly in the A/B lookahead predictions. The answer knows the distinction and still fails to apply it consistently.

**Case-level defects**

*C6-A.* Primary S3 is right; SA as a suspected rule-5 obligation is right; Fast Close is correctly blocked. But if the alternative is “review already finished and not logged,” the next *observation* should still usually be the missing review completion, not TI. TI as immediate next is also poorly calibrated: 7 waiting, one engineer down, historical median ~9h. The 0.12–0.28 band is too high for an immediate next log. Concurrent TI started at triage (the 126 IT→TI pattern) is a better S3/S4 overlap than the one they wrote, and they did not use it cleanly.

*C6-B.* The A-vs-B contrast is the strongest part of the submission. Low / no injury / single device / 0.93 duplicate / 0.98 completeness correctly opens a non-investigation close path that A does not have. Still, Close is not enabled until review completes, Fast Close is a stale S1 DFG successor (they cite 286/972, mixing the wrong denominator), and R-T4 is treated as mild contradiction without a real update.

*C6-C.* Best of the five on enablement. Ranking RA above the more frequent TI→Disposition arc (119 vs 74) is genuine rule-4 / country-rule reasoning, not frequency matching. The alternative that RA is unnecessary is allowed, but then Disposition at 0.18–0.38 is generous if repeated-malfunction reportability is taken seriously. SA at rank 3 with `process_enabled: false` is appropriate given different lots.

*C6-D.* Observation-model handling is the other high point: worklist opening at 12:40, source attachment, migration flag, and rule 7 are integrated, and delayed evidence is correctly marked not process-enabled. Then it wastes the third slot. CER and ER are the same catch-up mechanism; Evidence Review Completed, Disposition Recorded, or a delayed review event would have been a real alternative. Attachment existence does **not** establish that CER, ER, *and* Evidence Review all completed operationally — that is over-inference. RA completion as rank 1 (0.28–0.55) is also early: 1h20m into a 2–14h task sitting behind 12 queued items. They notice the timing problem and still leave RA on top with a high band.

*C6-E.* Second-cycle S3 is correct; six same-lot complaints make SA mandatory; 0.72 completeness justifies another evidence loop. Rank 1 is fine as the next *enabled* completion, not as an imminent one (40 minutes into a 1–5h review queue). Rank 2 SA completion is a coherent hidden-concurrency story, but TI after lot escalation is underspecified relative to a third CER. Low severity does not automatically demote TI when the lot has already become a cluster.

**Local sloppiness**

DFG arithmetic is casual (286/972; 173/356 mixes an incoming count with outgoing branches). Several “contradicting_evidence” bullets do not contradict the forecast; they are blocked paths or expected delays. Two-decimal intervals (0.48–0.72, 0.03–0.12) feign resolution the write-up otherwise disclaims. **What remains genuinely strong**

Control-flow obligations, object/lot context, queues, and feed delay are actually used. C6-A and C6-B are not cloned. Absence is not treated as proof of non-occurrence. Intervals are wide on purpose. The cross-case section on prefix identity vs obligation divergence, DFG non-causality, and next-event vs delayed observation is better than the case predictions that are supposed to instantiate it.

That combination — expert commentary with a broken next-event / enablement implementation — is why this is a mid-6, not an 8–9. Under a hypercritical reading, a near-flawless answer would have emitted three mutually alternative *next observations* per case, set `process_enabled` from the current normative state, and refused to score “the event after the event after now.” This one does not.
