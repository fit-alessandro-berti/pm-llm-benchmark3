The answer is complete, on-format, and conceptually aligned with the task (policy vs timing vs routing; four-eyes; rework rules; interference). It is not close to flawless. Several timing claims fail the stated test of distinguishing earlier readiness from earlier completion, and a few structural/capacity statements are internally inconsistent.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 correctly refuses a single global sequence and, crucially, classifies K-337 as a **decision-policy** change rather than a time hack. K-337’s path keeps Identity/Document before each Assessment, keeps Compliance after the last *used* Assessment, and does not let the unresolved discrepancy vanish. Side-effect and 100-case discussion of C1 starvation, D1↔D2 substitution, assessment-queue migration, weekend external delay, and false concurrency is the right interference vocabulary. The ranking that puts K-337 last on robustness is the correct qualitative call. The eight-assumption audit exists and is mostly well-typed.

**Temporal consistency errors (most damaging).**

- **K-411 overstates cycle-time gains.** Factual Document Check ends Tue 19:30; Assessment already starts **Wed 09:30** after an overnight wait. Parallel I2/D2 at Tue 08:00 queues (~2 h and ~4 h) can plausibly finish documents mid/late Tuesday afternoon, i.e. earlier *readiness*, not an earlier *calendar finish*. Overnight gating plus A2’s Wednesday queue make a **0–2 h** end-to-end move the default; **2–5 h** (table) and **3–6 h** best (derivation) are not jointly justified. The table text “assessment enters the Wed morning queue instead of later” is false: it already did. The derivation itself notes saturation can nullify gains, then ignores that bound in the headline range.
- **K-219 likewise treats slack as throughput.** Originals at Mon 17:30 plus D2’s ~5 h Mon-16:00 queue imply a start near **Mon 21:00**, not “within a few hours of 17:30.” Factual Assessment ends Tue 22:30 and **Decision is Wed 08:30**. No case in the set shows M1 signing on Tuesday. An earlier Tuesday assessment can easily leave Notify unchanged. A **6–12 h** cycle-time win is an upper bound that assumes evening/night M1 (and often A2) capacity that is not in evidence. The overnight D1 fact (Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40) supports *document* night work; it does not license manager-side pull-in.
- **K-104 local gain is queue-jumping, not extra capacity.** C1 is 20 h / 18 h / 16 h deep Mon–Tue. A “provisional slot” after Tue 19:10 Assessment can move this file from Wed 13:10 toward Wednesday morning **only by displacing other work**. The 3–10 h band (especially the 10 h tail) is the gain from preemption plus 1–3 h less final reading, presented too much as a free local improvement. Preliminary C1 blocks of 1.5–3 h on Monday, while C1 already has a 20 h backlog, are not a minimal, capacity-feasible tweak.

**Feasibility / partial-order flaws.**

- The K-104 order `Assessment < Escalation` and `Escalation ≤ PreliminaryCompliance` encodes the **factual** late escalation, not the intervention. The intervention puts an S1 packet at **Triage** and allows preliminary compliance **concurrent with Identity/Document**. The table path then *adds* residual post-Assessment escalation on top of early S1 and reserved C1 — that is not minimal change.
- `≤` vs `<` is undefined (timestamp equality vs mere non-ordering).
- Identity∥Document when originals are not required is stated in prose but not used cleanly in the K-104 order (in fact those tasks were already queue-serialized by D1, which is fine to keep, but then do not imply new parallelism).
- K-411 “quality slightly improved via earlier visibility” has no mechanism: standard digital docs, no defect in the fact, no new check.

**Capacity table contradicts itself.** Local demand for K-104 is **net extra C1 time** (prelim 1–3 h minus 1–2 h saved later). The same row then says the compliance queue “may shrink.” Reordering reserved cases is not shrinkage. At 100 similar high-risk files, C1 is a singleton with ~3–6 h reviews; 100 cases are already far beyond weekly C1 hours **before** reservations. The rollout paragraph notices fragility and starvation but never states that the 100-case K-104/K-337 scale is infeasible on C1, which is the central nonlinearity.

**Smaller but real issues.** D2 handwritten service “4–10 h” is invented between the standard/complex bands. K-337’s worst case “C1 still Tue afternoon” is weakly motivated once the file is ready Fri 10:30 and Thursday C1 wait is only 5 h (Monday is a fair base; Tuesday needs an extra congestion story). “One assessor in training” is pinned on A2 without support. Table vs derivation hour bands for K-411 do not match. K-104 expected quality is one-sided (“stable or slightly better”) while incomplete packets are a first-order risk. Pilots use “sufficient power” with no n, no multiple-testing note, and no cap that randomization itself will not swamp D2.

**Bottom line.** This is a competent process-mining writeup that hits format, four-eyes, and the policy-change insight on K-337. Under a strict bar it loses a lot of points for optimistic arithmetic, a wrong K-104 partial order relative to the specified intervention, and treating reservations/earlier readiness as if they were additional system capacity. Those are not cosmetic; they are the core of minimal-change, temporally consistent counterfactuals.
