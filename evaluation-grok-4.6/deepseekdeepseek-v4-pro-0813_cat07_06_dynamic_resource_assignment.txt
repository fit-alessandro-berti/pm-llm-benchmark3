**6.0**

The write-up is complete, on-format, and mostly constraint-aware, but it is not close to flawless. Several assignment and algorithm choices are internally inconsistent or at odds with the historical evidence the scenario itself provides.

**What holds up.** Hard vs soft constraints are separated cleanly. The policy skeleton includes every required component (eligibility, urgency, skill, continuity, workload/overtime, training, resilience, tie-breaks, exceptions). The queue table covers C001–C016 with the requested columns. High-OT staff (R2, R4, R9) are spared non-urgent work. Security skill-0 is correctly refused for Eva/Jonas. Continuity is respected on C002, C004, C005, C007, C008, C011, C016. Training share is computed (2/9 ≈ 22%). The comparison does not claim a single optimum. Audit indicators match the brief. The worker note is non-punitive.

**Material assignment errors.** R3 has 6.0 hours left and is given C001 (urgent, 3h SLA, complexity 0.88) as primary, C007 (high, 6h SLA, complexity 0.81) as primary, plus two diagnosis supervisions. Those two primaries cannot both fit the remaining day; C007’s SLA alone consumes the entire remaining window. R6 (6.5h) is given two urgent cases (2h and 4h SLAs) plus a diagnosis training case. R5 (5.0h) is given two high-priority cases including 0.79-complexity diagnosis plus an approval. Hours remaining appear in the score and then are ignored in the roster, so the claim of lower SLA risk than the baselines is not supported.

C001 keeps a Security skill-1 owner as performer on an enterprise, specialist-needed, 3h case. That follows a literal reading of continuity-when-supervision-exists, but it ignores the stated +3.3h service-time penalty for weak-skill continuity—larger than the SLA itself. R8 as primary (with R3 assisting) is the obvious SLA-safe alternative and is not chosen.

C003 is moved from a Diagnosis-3 owner onto a skill-1 trainee, pushing `reassignment_count` through the >2 threshold that the scenario links to 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeals, on a case already in a diagnosis loop. That assignment exists mainly to hit the 20% training quota. The quota is then resting on a low-quality training case.

**Algorithm and comparison gaps.** Continuity “penalty” is computed in the loop and never added to `score` (dead code). The exception branch is inverted: when the owner is weak *and* supervision is *not* feasible, non-owners still get a large penalty—the opposite of constraint 5. Weights and case-effort estimates are unspecified, so capacity filtering is decorative. First-available and specialist-only are compared only as qualitative stereotypes; the answer never instantiates those policies on C001–C016. The worker explanation is generic and does not tell R3 why they are at the cap, or R2/R4/R9 why they received nothing.

**PM4Py sketch.** Required functions are named, but `filter_case_performance(..., None, None)` does not audit SLA deadlines (`sla_deadline` is a timestamp, not a duration). Role/similarity/handover discoveries are called and then unused for learning or assignment. This is an audit stub, not a working learn-or-audit pipeline.

A high score would require a schedule that is feasible in remaining hours, C001 routed to a Security specialist under the 3h constraint, no training assignment that deliberately crosses the >2-reassignment threshold, executable (not dead) scoring, and a concrete baseline comparison. This is a competent design memo with several load-bearing mistakes.
