**5.0**

The writeup avoids the worst failure modes (treating rejects as non-default, training only on the 21,600-row export without comment, random splits). That is the floor for a passing design, not a high score. Under a strict reading of the brief, the statistical estimand, as-of discipline, validation maturity, and especially the PM4Py skeleton all have material errors.

**Target (uneven).** Framing the primary estimand as \(P(\text{default}_{90d}\mid\text{activated},X)\) is honest and operationally narrower than the lender’s request. The alternative multi-state target is the right contrast. The section then undermines itself: the population is “all 48,000 applications,” while the estimand is defined only after activation, and later IPTW is sold as recovering a *population* loss among approved portfolios. Those are different questions. Early-warning use on every application is a prediction-time quantity; conditioning on *eventual* activation is not observed at the third event. That circularity is noted too lightly.

**Cohort (mostly solid, incomplete).** The 48,000-row flow is arithmetically correct and correctly refuses to recode reject/withdraw/open as non-default. Weak spots: “Open” is treated as if it were a terminal activity; partner-originated permanently missing feeds are not a row; transfers are not split into linked vs unlinkable; and the 21,600 labeled export is not clearly reconstructed from the full event file versus taken as given.

**Censoring (conceptually mixed).** The five mechanisms are named. The primary strategy is the wrong tool for the stated primary estimand: if the target is already conditional on activation, IPTW/“treatment” weights are unnecessary for that functional and quietly reintroduce a counterfactual/transport problem the primary target was meant to avoid. IPSW for *observation among activated accounts* (delayed feed, partner gaps) would have been the coherent fix. Fine-Gray with rejection/withdrawal/transfer competing with default is process-incorrect: those events live on the application clock; default lives on a post-activation clock. Sequential states are not same-time-scale competing risks. Delayed labels are distinguished in prose and then not built into the availability rule (`label_available_at` ≤ analysis time).

**Prefixes (design better than mechanics).** Fixed-count and elapsed-time prefixes, rename mapping, and loop aggregation are appropriate. The design never requires prefixes to stop *before* Accept / Contract Signed / Account Activated. Padding a short trace with `<TERMINAL>` after activation leaks the path that defines the label. Global calendar cutoffs are repeatedly conflated with per-case prediction time.

**Features (acceptable, leak-prone details).** The control-flow / timing / rework / calendar split is in the right shape. “Distance to terminal states” and DFG features are unsafe unless discovered only on prefixes as-of prediction time. Resource tenure is “repaired” with HR logs that are not in the schema. Rolling default-rate features need an explicit matured-as-of rule and do not get one.

**Leakage table (good list, weak rules).** Required columns are covered and the keep/repair/drop calls are mostly right (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events). The `risk_score` rule (“max before cutoff”) still admits post-prefix updates if cutoff is a study date rather than the prefix end. Resource experience cannot be repaired from data that do not exist.

**Validation (serious maturity error).** A start-time split and the critique of random splits are correct, and policy/migration dates are used on purpose. The test window (starts Oct–Dec 2025) is largely unevaluable for a 90-day post-activation outcome plus a 7–35 day feed by 15 Feb 2026. Saying “only score mature cases” quietly empties the test set. A maturity-lagged rolling origin (train earlier, evaluate mid-2025 with labels observed by extract) was the obvious design. Training with *no* post-1 Jul 2025 data is a defensible shift probe, not a complete backtest, and is not treated as such.

**Limitations (one of the stronger sections).** Association vs selection vs counterfactual is stated; three deployment feedback loops are plausible. Still missing: IPTW does not identify reject-case default without untestable assumptions, and the primary model must not be described as an early-warning default probability for every application.

**Code (fails the assignment’s central engineering test).** Roughly ~70 lines (under the 80–160 band) and not an implementable pipeline:

- `prefix_events = events[events["time:timestamp"] <= PREDICTION_CUTOFF]` keeps *almost the entire trace* for 2024 starts, including Accept, Activate, collections, and default. Temporal features and waiting times are then built on leaked events. That directly violates “prevent post-prefix columns and events from entering the feature table.”
- `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then unused; prefixes are never joined to labels.
- Prediction time is a single calendar date, not the last prefix timestamp.
- The leakage “audit” asserts prohibited columns are *absent from the CSV*. They are in the schema, so the assert is either always false or never a real check. The columns are never dropped.
- `default_90d` is taken as `"first"` (often missing on early events). Maturity uses a 90-day gap to 2025-09-30 and `label_avail.notna()`, not `label_available_at <= cutoff`, so delayed labels leak.
- The activation-time lambda is fragile; `terminal_event="last"` depends on intra-group order; `LABEL_MATURITY_DAYS` and `datetime` are unused; `discover_dfg(..., limit=3)` in comments is not a real PM4Py signature.
- No cohort-flow counts for the six 48k buckets.

**Net.** Honest target language and a complete 48k table keep this out of the failure band. The skeleton that was supposed to *prove* as-of discipline implements the opposite; the test split ignores label maturity; IPTW and Fine-Gray are misaligned with the process. That is not near-flawless work.
