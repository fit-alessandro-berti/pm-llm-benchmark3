**4.6**

The write-up follows the required headings and gets several hard identity/authority points right, but it is not close to a reliable investigation. Material misclassifications, invented delegation conditions, unused business-day logic, and a non-compliant PM4Py/pandas section keep it well below a high score.

**What is actually solid.** Alias normalization for `b.koch`/`u_bk` is applied before SoD, so CHG-508 is correctly a same-human RR1 break. CHG-501 is a defensible `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` under D-QM-01 (signed PDF predates the event; Asha is on leave; scope is production equipment). CHG-502 and CHG-510 approvals are true RR1 failures. CHG-507 is a clean unauthorized HIGH approval: Eva is Independent Verifier on 9 Aug, not QM, and D-QM-02 does not exist yet. CHG-510 correctly rejects D-IC-04. The later QMS row cannot authenticate an earlier delegation; the signed PDF can. RR6 is respected in the collusion section (pairs are indicators, not proof). Controls in §6 cover the requested surface area.

**Case reasoning is too coarse and several labels are wrong.**

- **CHG-503** should not be a high-confidence `UNAUTHORIZED` SoD hit. RR5 requires *reliable* session mapping. WS-DM-04 is shared, there is no re-auth, CCTV is missing, and badge-in only shows Diego entered the room. That is `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` / `INDETERMINATE`, with a possible RR2 issue *if* Diego is later proven to be the actor. Treating `svc_qms` as Diego and closing the case is exactly the over-inference the brief forbids.
- **CHG-506** is not proven unauthorized action by Ben. He is on certified sick leave, denies the act, and the IP is an office workstation. That is credential-use / identity ambiguity first (`DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` or `INDETERMINATE`, secondary `SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN`). “Delegate availability” as a completed RR3 fail is too strong.
- **CHG-505** is collapsed into one `UNAUTHORIZED` row. Farid’s 13:00 approval sits inside D-IC-03 (signed, CI-44/cooling, no subdelegation). That is the RR4 emergency path, i.e. a likely `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` on approval. The retrospective is the broken control: D-QM-01 is scoped to **production-equipment QM approval**, not cooling-system retrospective; Ben is on sick leave at 14 Aug 16:00; and “within two business days” of 11 Aug is 13 Aug, so 14 Aug is late on a strict reading. None of that is in the findings table.
- **CHG-509** is not a clean `VALID_SUBSTITUTION`. D-QM-02 requires the principal to be absent. Asha’s leave ends 14 Aug; 15 Aug is only a weekend. Weekend ≠ documented absence. Scope and calendar interval match; the absence condition is unproven. `INDETERMINATE` or failed-condition unauthorized is the honest call.
- **CHG-504** as `AUTHORIZED` is acceptable on standing QM authority, but the brief says security cannot exclude credential sharing during the VPN window. Residual identity risk is missing from the row.
- Technical SoD vs control objective is not separated. CHG-502 (LOW self-approval) is scored the same High severity as HIGH-risk QM failures.

**Matrix and rule formalization have factual errors.** Diego is the **principal** of D-IC-03 and a standing IC from 1 Jul 2026; listing him as “IC Delegate” is wrong. D-IC-03 does **not** require Diego to be absent; inventing that condition contradicts CHG-505, where Diego is implementing while Farid approves. Eva’s IV→QE cutover at 11 Aug 23:59 is blurred. RR1–RR6 are paraphrased, not formalized as event-time predicates. Business-day counting is defined and then not applied.

**§5 is a large, explicit miss.** The brief asked for executable checks, not a sketch. The code never calls `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`; it never calls `discover_handover_of_work_network`; it never uses `filter_activity_done_different_resources`; it never runs pandas interval overlap against roles/delegations at each approval (the dict is dead). Timestamps are not parsed; `format_dataframe` is called without XES column mapping on renamed fields. Worst: **every** `svc_qms` row is mapped to Diego, including CHG-509, where badge + FIDO at WS-IV-02 point to Eva. Judgments that must stay manual are mentioned only in passing. Network output therefore cannot be trusted.

**§4 and §7 are directionally right but thin.** Reciprocal Ben↔Clara training approvals, Asha credential-sharing, and D-IC-04 as ignorance vs evasion are underplayed. The three investigations (Ben’s sick-leave account, CHG-503 attribution, Clara/Ben pairing) are reasonable, but CHG-509’s absence condition and the cooling retrospective scope/lateness problems belong in the same list.

Net: a structured, partly competent GxP write-up that would mislead a reviewer on the ambiguous-identity cases and does not deliver the required process-mining evidence. That is mid-fail against a near-flawless bar, not a 7–8 “good enough” paper.
