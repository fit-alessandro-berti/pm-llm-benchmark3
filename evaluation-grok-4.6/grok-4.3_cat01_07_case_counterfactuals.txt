**3.6**

The answer has the right skeleton and a few real insights (K-337 is a decision-policy change; C1 reservations displace others; D2 can induce rework; earlier readiness is not the same as earlier completion). Under a strict reading of the brief, that is not enough. The counterfactuals break prerequisites, invent calendars, and then scale those errors into a system analysis that is numerically impossible.

**Process feasibility.** K-337 is high-risk and high-value. Final compliance must follow the latest assessment and precede decision; four-eyes is not optional. The stated path is `Ass2 → Decision → Notify`. Compliance is absent. The derivation then asserts that “four-eyes and Compliance still satisfied,” which cannot be true of that path. This is the single most important failure in the answer: the one case whose intervention changes the allowed decision rule is also the case whose counterfactual silently drops a mandatory control. K-104’s path likewise drops Escalation Advice after an explicit triage packet to S1.

**Temporal consistency.** Several claimed clocks are incompatible with the log and the snapshots.

- K-337: Assessment 2 ends **Friday 10:30**. The answer moves Decision to **Tuesday 11:00–14:00** and “eliminates Thu–Mon external delay.” The second RMI is Friday–Monday, not Thursday–Monday. Even a legal path (Ass2 → C1 → Decision) lands Friday afternoon or Monday, not Tuesday.
- K-104: “Identity and Document finish earlier relative to Assessment.” The intervention does not touch those activities. Readiness for Assessment is still after D1’s Tuesday 05:40 completion and A1’s queue. A reserved C1 slot at Tuesday 20:00–23:00 is a queue jump through a 16–20 h compliance backlog, not a mechanical consequence of earlier ID/docs.
- K-219: Tuesday 08:00 snapshot has D2 at **4 h**. Starting D2 at 08:00 ignores that queue. “As soon as originals arrive” is Monday 17:30, when D2 still has ~5 h from the Monday 16:00 snapshot. The answer then has a **slower** D2 pull Assessment **earlier** (20:00 → 18:30–19:30). That arithmetic only works if service-time inflation is ignored.
- K-411: both checks already finish Tuesday (Identity 13:20, Document 19:30). Saying they “finish Tue 19:30 instead of Wed 09:30” confuses document completion with assessment start. The interesting effect is earlier *readiness* into a congested A2 queue; the write-up names that, then still posts a 4–9 h saving whose worst case is still a saving.

**Uncertainty and invented precision.** Queue estimates are treated as soft in the constraint block, then overwritten with unsupported point values: 60–70% of compliance work pre-done, clarification *p* = 0.22, D2 utilization 55% → 78%, 12–18 extra loops, 8–12% appeal exposure. Best/base/worst bands are internally inconsistent (K-219 table “6 to +4 h” vs derivation best 10 h; K-104 “worst” still 8 h when an incomplete packet can erase the gain). Cycle time is written as if it were a duration (“Cycle time 8 to 18 h”) when the surrounding text means a reduction.

**Capacity math.** “100 similar cases in one week” is treated like a dozen. One certified reviewer cannot absorb 100 K-104-like reservations at “30–40% of C1 capacity.” One hundred K-219-like D2 jobs is hundreds of hours, not a move from 55% to 78% utilization. One hundred suppressed weekend loops remove thousands of external hours, not 180–240. One hundred earlier K-411 arrivals do not add only 35–50 h of assessment backlog. Sign of interference is sometimes right; magnitude is not. K-337 at scale also **advances** C1/M1 demand into Friday (and appeal work later); the table’s “queue likely to grow: none / affected non-target cases: none” is false.

**Format and ranking.** The 100-case write-up is ~140 words against a 250–400 requirement; the synthesis is ~140 against 220–320. Required topics are named, not analyzed. Ranking K-337 first overweight cycle-time certainty and underweights quality, defensibility, and irreversibility—the brief forbids ranking on time alone. “Most dangerous despite speed” is assigned to K-104 (spillover). The intervention that can change the decision, appeal risk, and loss exposure is K-337. Assumption 1 (C1 honors reservations without harming others) and assumption 2 (clarification rate invariant to load) contradict Section 4. Assumption 6 (quality comparable on incomplete evidence) is what props up the K-337 rank and is not defended.

What remains is a well-outlined, partially literate interference story sitting on infeasible paths and unscalable numbers. That is not a high score.
