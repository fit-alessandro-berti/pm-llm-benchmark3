**7.4**

The final write-up is structurally complete, dialectically serious, and mostly well-traced. It does not merely split the difference, and the hard cases (SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-712, SUP-714) are classified on the right grounds. It is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up**

Sections 1–3 separate stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes and give a usable burden rule: raw M0 mismatch is not automatically noncompliance; an exception needs time-effective, scoped authority; informal necessity does not amend CS-9. Prosecution and defense are both argued at strength, and each side is forced to concede what it must (model/data defects vs SUP-703/708 and the limits of unofficial practice). The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource/COI failures. The system judgment is coherent: enforce CS-9, retire M0 as a sole artifact.

**Material defects**

The revised POWL is the weakest part of a section the rubric weights heavily. `Master_Data_Validation` is made mandatory on the auto route even though no observed eligible pilot trace contains that activity. Under that model, SUP-702 would fail replay. PD-3 requires automated master-data validation as a control, not necessarily as a logged activity; that limitation is not stated.

The auto and emergency routes are also over-constrained. PD-3/FD-7 *permit* Quality (and, in FD-7, manual approval) after provisional activation; they do not *require* those steps to follow activation. The model adds `activate → quality` (and emergency `activate → manual`), so a case that completed quality before activation under a valid overlay would be rejected. That is a control-flow semantics error, not a harmless approximation.

`SilentTransition(label=None)` is not a safe PM4Py constructor. Typical `SilentTransition.__init__` takes no arguments. A section that demanded executable construction should not ship an unforced TypeError.

**Classification and authority nits**

SUP-715 as `NONCOMPLIANT` overreaches. The materials say no rule prohibits the spousal relationship; only disclosure is required, and control flow is clean. The dominant issue is an organizational disclosure gap. `MIXED` or a scoped noncompliance limited to COI process would have been tighter.

SUP-713 as `COMPLIANT` with medium-high confidence is too clean. Equivalence is attested, but the mapping was approved *after* the case. That is at least a contemporaneous-label/data issue.

SUP-702’s CRM notification is accepted without noting that N4’s CRM cutover is 2026-07-01 and the case is 2026-06-15. The case text supplies CRM evidence, so the classification can stand; the date collision should have been flagged.

SUP-711 is labeled `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` even though the duplicate is explained by a shared source transaction ID. The residual process is otherwise standard. `COMPLIANT` with a data-quality note, or a narrower data defect that does not leave the case “indeterminate,” would have been more precise.

**Smaller issues**

Layer 1 in §6 lost its ordering arrows and reads as a bag of activities. DECLARE/log-skeleton rules are described, not specified. PD-3’s contested compliance sign-off is recorded in §1 and then treated as sufficient authority for SUP-702 without restating that residual dispute in the judgment. The LOOP encoding (`τ` do / `Request_More_Info` redo) is a reasonable 0..n approximation and is disclosed; that part is fine.

**Net**

Strong review document, weak-to-uneven normative revision. The legal/process reasoning is well above average; the model/data artifact has enough semantic and executability faults that a high score is not justified under a strict standard.
