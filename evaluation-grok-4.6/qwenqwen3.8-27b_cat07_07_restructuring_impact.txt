**7.2**

The response is complete, correctly formatted, and balanced. It hits every required heading and sub-element, treats East as a distinct case, refuses a full rollout, and keeps averages from dominating the analysis. Under ordinary grading this would score higher. Under the required standard it does not, because several analytical gaps are real, not cosmetic.

**What it does well.** The mechanism map ties each structural change to a concrete event-log signature. The three scenarios are internally consistent and use the supplied handover and team tables rather than generic “it depends” language. Leading vs lagging indicators are separated and mostly operationalizable. Section 4 names the right hidden risks (tail, complexity, local knowledge, fairness, learning, accountability, exceptions, absence). The recommendation is conditional and explicit about what early averages must not be taken to mean.

**Substantive flaws.**

1. **Capacity arithmetic is missing where the data already falsifies the management story.** Legal/Risk sit at 93% and 91% utilization. Routing more cases (9 → 12 per 100) *and* targeting a 15% utilization lift is infeasible without added headcount (0.93 × 1.15 > 1). The text says they are “near capacity” but never does this calculation, so the first-order bottleneck is under-specified.

2. **The management table is not a handover reduction.** Summing the listed handover types, the proposed mix is *higher* than the current mix (local loops are replaced by more frequent central hops, and approval volume rises 42 → 49 while a supervisor is removed). The answer notes offsetting central handovers qualitatively and never checks the table’s own totals.

3. **The pilot tests a different intervention than the proposal.** Keeping local supervisors, retaining local tech for informal advice, and randomizing only 50% of North/South cases is prudent risk control, but the write-up does not say clearly that a clean result therefore cannot validate the *actual* redesign. Worse, a single shared queue serving fully treated East plus a North/South experiment is contaminated by construction: East volume moves the queue that the randomized cases wait in. That interference is not discussed.

4. **Guardrails are not calibrated to the baseline.** An 8h p90 on Technical Shared Service is asserted without a current p90 (only a 3.2h local-tech *median* is given). Several other thresholds (0.5pp rework, 24h legal p90, 2-day fairness gap) are similarly free-standing.

5. **Optimistic cycle-time gains are asserted, not derived.** Almost every proposed handover is slower than its local counterpart (3.2h → 4.0h technical; 2.4h → 3.0h approval; 9.6h → 10.5h legal). Median cycle-time improvement then has to come from fewer loops, East relief, and standardization. That can be true, but it is not shown, and it sits uneasily next to already-saturated shared specialists.

6. **The PM4Py sketch covers the required function names but not the problem’s own metrics.** It never computes handover frequency per 100 cases, median wait *after a named handover*, or rework *conditional on the preceding handover*—the exact quantities in the scenario tables. `get_variants` is treated as a Series with `.index`; `filter_event_attribute_values(..., attribute_values=...)` and several other signatures are guessed. Allowed as “style,” but as an analysis sketch it stops at dumping graphs rather than estimating the effects the rest of the memo forecasts. `local_expertise_flag` aggregated with `max` is the wrong case-level summary if the flag is event-level.

**Smaller issues.** Forecast ranges have unearned precision (40–42, 3.5–4.0h). Optimistic “early warning signs” largely repeat the other scenarios. Queueing near saturation (Little’s law, utilization → wait convexity) is implied but never used, despite being the natural model for a shared service absorbing East’s 82% E_Tech load plus Legal/Risk.

The memo is usable as a briefing. It is not nearly flawless: it leaves the sharpest implications of the given numbers on the table and designs a pilot that cannot cleanly identify the policy it is meant to test.
