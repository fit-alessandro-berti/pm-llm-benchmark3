**4.5**

The response has the right scaffold and the right headline conclusion, but it fails the standard it set for itself: quantitative discipline. Several numbers that leadership would actually use are wrong, one never-treated comparison is built on a median/p90 mix-up, and at least one regression result is invented.

**What is solid.** The write-up correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It separates availability from `automated_fast_path_pct`, flags targeted selection of weak high-volume branches, keeps median and p90 apart, and treats the fraud desk, routing campaign, staffing, and May complexity shift as time-varying confounders rather than background color. The estimand list, mechanism table, threat list, embedded-evaluation recommendation, and claim ladder are conceptually in the right family. That is not enough.

**Quantitative errors that change the story.**

- Alpha Apr–Aug median is \( (48.3+49.5+53.5+47.9+43.3)/5 = 48.5 \), not 46.5. Versus a Jan–Mar mean of 48.9 the change is about **−0.4 h**, not −2.4 h. The “best-case 2.5-hour median gain” in §5.5 and the ±2.5 h language in §7 and §10 are six times too large.
- Epsilon Apr–Aug **median** is \( (42.1+46.6+49.4+44.8+40.8)/5 = 44.7 \), not 88.5. The +48.2 h “collapse” is p90 values treated as medians (`102.2`, `107.5`, …). Never-treated medians actually moved about +4–6 h, in line with Delta (+6.0 h), not a unique disaster.
- Alpha p90 post is 108.1, not 106.1. Delta p90 is ~71.8 → ~89.3, not 74 → 82. Epsilon rework is not flat at 13.5; Apr–Aug averages ~15.6.
- Gamma’s −3.4 h median is computed on a **different window** (Jan–Jun vs Jul–Aug) than the table header (Jan–Mar vs Apr–Aug). On the stated window Gamma is about 37.5 → 41.1 (**+3.6 h**). The nicest adopter contrast—Gamma Jul–Aug versus May–Jun, compared with Delta/Epsilon over the same months—is not computed.

**Invented precision.** §5.3 reports “+1.8 hours per 10 percentage points (SE ≈ 0.9, p ≈ 0.05)” and “+1.2 after controls.” No regression is run in the narrative, and a branch-FE fit is unlikely to produce that sign cleanly: within Alpha/Beta, intensity rises while August medians fall. Using that coefficient again in the claim ladder treats a made-up number as evidence.

**Spec and identification misses.** E7 defines “baseline median > 90 hours”; no branch median is near 90 (that is the p90). Interference and treatment-version (official go-live vs 4–6% pre-period fast path, shared fraud desk) are not pinned to estimands. “Gamma enters at t=+3” confuses calendar time with event time. **Dynamic treatment effects** are a required threat and do not appear as a row. §9 estimates the naïve TWFE the prompt told the author not to use without HTE/timing, builds relative-time dummies that are never used, and the `pd.cut` call has 5 labels vs 4 bins and prints `{b}` from the wrong loop. §8 overshoots the 250–400 word cap and proposes case-level randomization of a branch-level system without facing shared queues.

**Bottom line.** The policy stance—do not announce an X% effect; expand only with an embedded evaluation—is defensible. The numbers, windows, and “estimated” intensity effect are not. For a task scored on estimands, identification, process interpretation, **quantitative discipline**, and uncertainty, that is a mid-fail, not a near-miss.
