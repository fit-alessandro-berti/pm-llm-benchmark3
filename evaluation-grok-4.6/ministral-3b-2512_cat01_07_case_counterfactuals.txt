The answer has the required skeleton and a few correct high-level instincts (K-337 is a decision-policy change; C1 is scarce; D1/D2 substitution creates spillover). It then fails the actual task: feasible, minimal-change, temporally grounded counterfactuals. Under a strict reading that is disqualifying.

**Feasibility model.** The partial order is not a partial order. Relations are written as spaces, and `Identity Check ≺ Document Check` is asserted as a general fact. That is false: when originals are not required the two checks may run concurrently; originals constrain only Document Check. Constraint 2 then garbles the same point (“both must complete before Assessment unless Originals Receipt precedes Document Check”). Assessment before Decision, final vs preliminary compliance, resource non-overlap, and four-eyes (same *person* on final Assessment and final Compliance Review) are missing or misstated. Constraints were also required in a second fenced block and are not. Intervention typing is the strongest part of §1: K-337 is correctly flagged as a policy change.

**Case table — control-flow failures.** Three of four paths are illegal or non-minimal.

- **K-104** omits `Assessment` entirely and then sends the case to Escalation/Compliance. A high-risk/high-value case cannot skip Assessment; final compliance must follow the latest Assessment. The path is also sequential I→D even though originals are not required. Cycle time “12–24 h” is unlabeled (level vs delta) and implausible as a level (factual span is ~58 calendar hours).
- **K-337** is the clearest minimal-change miss. The intervention forbids only the *second* RMI, after Assessment 2. The CF path drops RMI1, DC2, A2, **and Compliance Review**. High-risk/high-value still requires compliance after the latest Assessment. The table then asserts “reject with conditions” as if determined; the prompt allows approve-with-conditions or reject, and forbids only silent ignore.
- **K-411** is specified as concurrency, then written as a total order I→D. That hides the intervention.
- **K-219** is the only roughly legal path, but the later derivation starts D2 at Tue 09:30 — the same instant as D1 — which contradicts “as soon as originals arrive” (Mon 17:30). Outcome “2–8 h” is again unlabeled.

Four-eyes is repeatedly checked as “M1 ≠ C1.” The rule is Assessment vs Compliance, not Decision vs Compliance. Compliance/four-eyes are also invoked for K-219 and K-411, which are not high-risk/high-value and have no compliance step.

**Temporal reasoning is decorative.** Queue snapshots are almost unused. Derivations copy factual timestamps and draw arrows to the *same* times while claiming earlier starts (`Wed 13:10 → Wed 13:10`, `Tue 19:20 → Tue 19:20`, K-337 Decision still `Wed 08:30`). That is not propagation. The answer never separates “ready earlier” from “finishes earlier,” never subtracts reserved C1 wait from residual service after a preliminary read, and never nets D2’s earlier start against its slower (4–8 h vs 3–6 h) and more error-prone handwriting work. Invented rework probabilities (20–50%) are uncalibrated. Best/base/worst is only best/worst.

**Wrong causal attribution.** K-104’s intervention is early escalation plus a provisional C1 hold. The write-up instead “parallelizes” Identity and Document — which were already allowed — and then claims those queues shrink. D1’s Monday 13 h backlog, not control flow, is why documents started late. Reserving C1 does not shrink I/D queues; it rationing-starves other urgent compliance work. **Capacity and ranking.** The interference table has the right columns and some right words (C1, D1↔D2, assessment bottleneck) but the signs are off. K-337 is not demand-neutral: it frees D1, A1, and a weekend of external wait, and it *advances* C1 use. K-411 does not shrink I/D queues; it raises coincident utilization. The 100-case note is thin and internally inconsistent with the ranking.

Ranking K-337 first because it “preserves compliance rules” and “increases defensibility / reduces appeal risk” inverts the prompt’s own complication. Deleting the second loop is the *least* robust legally: it changes the decision rule, residual discrepancy, appeal and loss exposure. K-411 is a defect workaround restoring intended concurrency; treating logging risk as more dangerous than starving the only certified reviewer (K-104) or forcing a decision on incomplete high-risk evidence (K-337) is a quality-of-judgment error. The pilot inherits this: K-104 eligibility requires “originals required” though K-104 has none; the K-337 stopping rule treats “rework loops fall 50%” as an outcome when the treatment *forbids* the loop.

**Assumptions.** Eight items exist, but several are restated norms (four-eyes is structural, not behavioral), and the audit never states the load-bearing CF assumptions (other cases held fixed; preliminary work saves 1–3 h; D2 start at originals arrival; weekend calendar).

**Net.** Format compliance is superficial. The answer does not construct process-feasible counterfactual paths, does not do queue-consistent timing, misstates four-eyes, and ranks the policy-changing intervention as the safe one. That is well below a competent process-mining review.
