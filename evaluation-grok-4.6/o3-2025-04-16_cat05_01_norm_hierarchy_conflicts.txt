**5.8**

The memo is complete, on-format, and often process-aware, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has a central authority contradiction, a broken formalization, at least one fact error, and several case-level overclaims. Those are not cosmetic nits.

**What it does well.** It separates assumptions from facts, applies controls at the time of commitment rather than at case close, refuses to let later approvals cure A6 defects, and prefers contemporaneous artefacts over ERP stamps for P-505. P-501 is correctly placed before ECD-2; P-504 is correctly treated as out of scope; P-506 is correctly treated as an expired directive plus a late Plant approval; P-508 is correctly failed for missing Finance above the CFO’s own €150k ceiling. Section 5 is the strongest part: several alternatives are real, and the “ECD-2 as A7 by custom” and “ERP stamp is dispositive” readings are properly labelled weak.

**Fatal consistency problem.** Section 2 says the CFO email “can interpret but not repeal” GPIP-4 A2, and that ECD-2 “probably cannot override” Finance unless the CRO acts. Section 4 then uses that same email to classify P-505 and P-507 as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` for missing Finance. The hierarchy and the holdings cannot both be true. The pseudo-logic makes this worse: line 7 ties `justified_exception` only to an ECD-2 override, while the actual JE holdings rest on a CFO path that the formal system never carries through. A compliance assessment whose rule layer and case layer disagree on the main contested authority is not a reliable assessment.

**Formalization is sloppy in a required section.** Threshold operators are missing throughout the catalogue (` 50 k €`, ` 25 k €`), so R2/R3/R6 are not actually readable as rules. R1 is missing the prohibition (“Committer  final approver”). R7 conflates B4 timing with B6’s 24-hour form. Rule 3 is written as if new-supplier and >€100k were conjoined; A3 is a disjunction. That is a material mis-encode of the global policy. P-502 then cites “R2 Finance,” which is the budget-owner rule in the author’s own table. The formal layer looks precise and is not.

**Hierarchy problems.** The instruction was not to let the global document automatically defeat every other source. Assumption A-1 does almost exactly that. The issue-specific supplier-register line then contradicts the main table: the table says ECD-2 C2 can override the register inside scope, while the illustrative ordering writes `GPIP-4 = SOP > ECD-2` and, in the same breath, that C2 wins inside scope. A5 (local may be stricter) is applied in some cases and conceptually cancelled by A-1.

**Case-level defects.**
- P-505 lists R2 (budget owner) as satisfied and “uncertain” at once. There is no independent budget-owner record. That is not a satisfied rule.
- P-507 asserts “segregation satisfied” and, in the next column, “Role of committer” as missing evidence. You cannot have both.
- P-503 “satisfied rules: none” is too absolute (C1/C3-style steps did occur) and omits the missing Finance event from the deviation list while advertising A1/A3 as controlling.
- P-501’s “missing evidence” is formal delegation after the facts already state the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. That is a proved substitution failure, not an evidence gap.
- No case is `INDETERMINATE` despite designed disputes on ECD-2 scope, A7 equivalence, CFO reach, and the P-505 expiry edge. Confidence tags and Section 5 make uncertainty visible, but the method’s own fourth bucket is never used. P-505 in particular is a knife-edge that the author’s own Medium confidence almost admits.
- P-502’s High confidence overstates the case. C4’s “supplier-onboarding” language is the best available argument that A3 is inside ECD-2’s overlay; the memo never wrestles with it.

**Section 5 fact error.** Alternative 3 puts P-508 outside a 24-hour reading of “critical lots.” P-508 is the only pump that can restore the *affected* line. That is not the P-503 backup-line / three-day-stock problem. Treating them as the same scope failure is a misread of the record.

**Remediation and policy draft.** The logging-versus-breach distinction for P-505 and P-507 is sound. The rest is thin: no case-level action for P-502 Compliance, P-503 SoD/Compliance, or P-504’s out-of-scope commit; and a request for retrospective Plant countersignatures that A6 already says cannot cure timing. A7-bis is the best-written part of the paper (explicit waiver, expiry, repository lodging, anti-normalization). It still leaves the €50–150k Finance/budget-owner default and new-supplier Compliance underspecified, and it invents a CFO+GC substitute that the source texts do not grant.

**Net.** This is a structured, often defensible analyst memo with real dialectical range. It is not a 7+ product once internal contradiction, unreadable/wrong formal rules, overclaimed SoD/budget-owner findings, and a scope error on P-508 are counted. Those are the exact dimensions the task said would be judged.
