**3.3**

The answer has the required skeleton and a few locally correct instincts (A6 is not curative; contemporaneous evidence can beat ERP clocks; P-503 and P-504 are noncompliant). That is not enough. Under a strict reading it is a well-formatted analysis that repeatedly inverts the record, the authority map, and the clocks. Those are not presentation nits. They are outcome-determinative errors.

**1. Authority and hierarchy are asserted, then contradicted.**

The method section correctly notes that ECD-2 is not an A7 instrument and that the CRO never ratified it. The hierarchy table then places ECD-2 at the top and lets it override GPIP-4 A1, A2, and A3. That cannot be squared with A4 (emergency waives competitive tendering only), C5 (ECD-2 does not mention SoD, Finance, Compliance, the €100k threshold, or the CRO), or D3 (CFO may interpret Finance, not Compliance or segregation). The problem expressly forbids treating the newest or loudest text as automatically supreme. The answer does exactly that, then applies the opposite rule in some cases.

The table is also internally broken. ECD-2 is said to override GPIP-4 Finance/Compliance/SoD *and* to be unable to override local SOP “unless explicitly waived,” while C1, C2, C4, and B7 are the clauses that *do* speak to local timing and supplier onboarding. D is listed as able to override ECD-2. D is an uncontrolled email interpreting Finance during the ECD-2 window; it is not a higher norm. “NORTECH-FACT” is a fabricated source name.

C6 is treated as settled in Internal Audit’s favor. That is an interpretive choice, not a fact. It should have remained a contested scope premise, especially for P-503.

**2. The rule catalogue misstates the instruments it claims to formalize.**

Triggers drop the comparison operators (`Purchase EUR 50,000`, `Purchase EUR 100,000`), so R1/R2 do not even encode the thresholds. A3 is *above* €100,000 or a new supplier, not “€100,000 OR new supplier” as a single blurred gate. C5 is coded as a *waiver* of Compliance. C5 is a silence. R6 treats A1 as waivable in emergency; no source says that. R4 collapses B4’s six-hour plant emergency, ECD-2’s sterilization-lot scope, and C3’s two-person IC rule into one trigger. D1 is built in as a general Finance waiver without its date, its uncontrolled status, its €150k cap, or D4’s integrity caveat.

The pseudo-logic restates the same over-claim: ECD-2 and D1 normally defeat pre-commitment Finance and Compliance. That is the opposite of a disciplined `overrides_for(issue, scope, time)` model.

**3. Casework contains multiple inverted facts. Several classifications therefore cannot stand.**

**P-501 — wrong facts, wrong window, wrong result (COMPLIANT, high confidence).**  
Commit is 08:50; Create_PO is 12:20. The answer says the reverse and treats B1 as satisfied. ECD-2 starts at 09:00; this commitment is *before* the directive. B4 can excuse PO-before-commit and, *if* the Plant Manager is absent *and* has actively delegated, a Shift Lead substitution. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. Plant and Finance approvals are afternoon, post-commitment. A6 is not a cure. A2 is not triggered at €38k, but B2 is. This is a straightforward NONCOMPLIANT (or, on a very generous reading, INDETERMINATE on whether B2 is pre-commitment). COMPLIANT is not defensible.

**P-502 — D1 applied before it existed (COMPLIANT).**  
The CFO email is 15 July 07:20. This purchase commits at 10:15 on 14 July. D1 is not available. New-supplier A3 Compliance lands at 18:30, after commitment. C2 waives the approved-register rule, not GPIP-4 Compliance. C4’s “supplier-onboarding” gloss is arguable; it is not a free pass, and C5 cuts against a silent waiver. Finance is also post-commitment, and A2 is live at €62k. Primary should be NONCOMPLIANT or, if C4 is read maximally, INDETERMINATE. COMPLIANT / high confidence is unjustified.

**P-503 — right label, confused reasons.**  
NONCOMPLIANT is the strongest call: backup line, three-day stock (scope fight under C6), >€100k with no Compliance, PROC-3 as requester/committer/one of two authorizers, no Finance/Plant events. But the write-up says “ECD-2 does not waive Finance approval (D1)” while D1, issued that morning, *does* purport to treat joint IC authorization as sufficient operational approval up to €150k. The clean noncompliance path is Compliance (D3/C5/A3/A4), SoD (A1), and doubtful scope—not a denial that D1 speaks to Finance.

**P-504 — acceptable result, sloppy framing.**  
NONCOMPLIANT is correct. The purchase is outside ECD-2’s sterilization-lot scope; cafeteria spoilage is not a B4 emergency; B3 is breached; there is no two-person C3 authorization. Citing “R4/R5” as if this were a failed sterilization emergency misstates the defect. It is out of scope, not a near-miss emergency.

**P-505 — the expiry issue is the case; it is barely analyzed (COMPLIANT, high confidence).**  
ECD-2 dies at 18:00. The live evidence is a supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 versus an ERP commit at 18:06, with a possible nine-minute-fast clock. That is the whole problem. The answer treats Compliance as a waived ECD-2 issue even though A3 is not triggered (€88k, approved supplier), and it labels the 17:59 acknowledgment as `Record_Emergency_Form`. No Plant_Approve or emergency form is in the file. Finance is 19:00. If 17:59:31 is accepted, the live questions are D1’s weight for Finance and A1, not a clean COMPLIANT. **P-506 — Finance timing inverted.**  
Finance_Approve 06:05 *precedes* Commit 06:20. The answer says the opposite and hangs R1 on a non-fact. ECD-2 has expired; the “verbal overnight extension” is an unrecorded buyer claim. **4. Alternatives, evidence, and the redraft are thin and sometimes parasitic on the same errors.**

Four alternatives is the minimum, and two of them argue from false premises (P-507 “late Compliance,” P-508 “Legal never obtained”). P-501—the most obviously misclassified case—is not reconsidered. Alternatives that would actually move a careful reader (C4 as onboarding-includes-Compliance; D1 as valid Finance interpretation only after 15 July 07:20; C6’s 24-hour versus backup-line reading; 17:59:31 versus 18:06 on P-505; PDF 12:40 as timely C3 support on P-507) are mostly absent.

Section 6 does not reliably distinguish breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and unprovability. P-507 is a late-record-of-timely-authorization problem; it is filed as a Compliance breach. P-508 is told to “obtain Legal_Clearance” it already has. P-501 is given no remediation.

Section 7 is the least-bad part: it sees the need to pin scope, thresholds, SoD, new suppliers, retrospective records, and expiry. It then lets the CFO waive CRO control above €250k, which recreates the A7 hole, and it never states a hard anti-normalization rule (automatic sunset plus mandatory post-window reconstruction under full GPIP-4, with no custom-and-practice accretion). That is a draft, not a repair.

**5. What “process awareness” required and this text did not do.**

A defensible write-up had to apply norms at the moment of the act; keep D1 off the 14 July file; keep ECD-2 off the 08:50 file and off the 19 July file; treat C5 as silence rather than a waiver; treat D3 as a hard limit on what an email can do; refuse A6 as a cure; and use the outage note only to re-weight clocks, not as an exception. This answer states several of those maxims and then violates them in the table that matters.

Right headings and mixed labels prevent a collapse into the 1–2 range. The factual inversions on P-501, P-506, P-507, and P-508, the anachronistic use of D1, the C5 inversion, and high-confidence COMPLIANT calls on broken timelines make anything near the top of the scale impossible.
