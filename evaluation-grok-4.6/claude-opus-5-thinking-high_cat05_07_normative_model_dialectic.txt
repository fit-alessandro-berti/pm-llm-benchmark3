7.2

The opinion is unusually complete and mostly careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict standard the defects below are enough to keep it well short of the top of the scale.

**Burden-of-proof special pleading.** Section 1.5 requires a written instrument from a body empowered to vary the control before an exception can be accepted, and it expressly says authority to run a pilot is not authority to disapply CS-9. PD-3 is then recorded as minutes/email, never incorporated, with `authority_basis = NULL`. SUP-702 still comes out `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That is a direct contradiction, not a nuance. Charging the defect “upward” answers a personnel question; it does not satisfy the case-level test the author wrote. The same test is applied rigorously to SUP-705 and SUP-714. Eligible in-window pilots are thereby legalized while expired copies of the same pattern are condemned. That is a split dressed up as institutional analysis.

**Soft system verdict against the author’s own aggregates.** Fourteen clearly ineligible auto-approvals, nine with missing eligibility attributes, six post-expiry August cases, plus SUP-703/708/712, are then summarized as the operation “not materially noncompliant as a whole.” The governance failure is real; calling the operation as a whole not materially noncompliant underweights the author’s own evidence and the Internal Audit flags.

**Overstated empirics.** “Correlation between M0 deviation and actual risk is… arguably negative” is false on this sample: SUP-703 and SUP-708 both deviate and are high-risk. “Material harm exposure” is stretched over SUP-712 (a segregation failure, not shown loss). Forty minutes is weak clock-skew evidence; treating D9 as a live, confidence-cutting dispute is generous.

**POWL wording slip.** XOR *is* optionality. “Zero tolerance for optionality” is imprecise even though the surrounding semantics (concurrent `checks`, block-before-block edges, no loop) are otherwise right.

**Model gaps the text does not own.** The architecture/limitations section is honest about unguarded XORs, the RMI over-approximation, and missing time/amount/identity, but it underspecifies other defects:
- `Reuse_Valid_Certification` sits on the *standard* route after a shared intake XOR, so a new `Register_Supplier` case can skip `Quality_Review` in control flow.
- `Request_More_Info` can fire after activation or rejection.
- Withdrawal is only an exclusive alternative to whole routes, so mid-process withdrawal is inexpressible.
- On the auto route, `Quality_Review` is unconstrained relative to sanctions.

Those are exactly the “state the limitation” cases the prompt asked for.

**Classification nits.** SUP-711 as `COMPLIANT` rather than a data/log class is argued, but the object of the label (trace vs business) blurs. SUP-710 as pure `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` downplays CS-9’s activation-time quality rule; medium confidence does not fully fix that. Avoiding `MIXED` is compliant with the prompt, not a virtue when 702 is doing mixed work under another name.

**What remains strong.** Dialectical structure is real, not performative: both briefs concede the points that should be conceded (703, 708, 14 ineligibles, unevidenced master-data validation, 707/709/711/713, CRM missingness). POWL concurrency is read correctly; M0-fitting SoD/CoI cases are not hidden. The matrix is traceable. Layers 0–5, the overlay tables, and the event-schema proposal are the right decomposition. The Python construction is executable in form, uses LOOP/`SilentTransition` correctly for `*`/`+`, keeps notification mandatory, and does not pretend POWL can enforce eligibility.

Those virtues are why this is not a middling score. The authority inconsistency, the softened system finding, and the unowned model holes are why it is nowhere near a 9–10.
