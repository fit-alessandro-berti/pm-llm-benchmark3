**7.0**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the required seven-section format. It stays inside the event log, DFG, path table, batching/transport evidence, and SP1/SP2/Q1/R1 findings. It does not pick the lowest-emission option for Critical-downtime, does not treat all air or dedicated-van moves as equally unjustified, and does not invent a fake lifecycle inventory. Those are the main things the prompt asked for.

It is not close to flawless.

The biggest analytical gap is interaction risk. The “coherent package” is essentially S1–S12 stacked together. Batch wait (4–38h), consolidation wait (12–24h), inspection-gate queueing, fewer partial shipments, and air-to-ground substitution (about 24h slower) can add on the same order. The text never tests whether that combined delay stays inside promised slack for Standard-stock Service-contract (median 18h, p90 41h) or Made-to-order Service-contract. Separate qualitative arrows are not a multi-criteria assessment of the package.

The effects table is also sloppy. Shipping interventions (S1, S4, S5) are given energy movements even though `energy_kwh` is a production/machine attribute and transport is already measured as `transport_kgco2e`. S7’s “small ↑ from extra inspection” is not in the evidence. Several cells contain mixed arrows (`↓ if …; ↑ if …`), which makes direction unfalsifiable.

The recommendation is a kitchen-sink, not a sequenced redesign. S1 and S4 largely duplicate each other. S12 is governance, not a process change that reduces impact. There is no first-wave versus later-wave cut based on where the footprint actually sits (Critical 11% at 38 kgCO2e/order; MTO Regular 22% at 18.5 kWh; stock-air 5% at 18.5 kgCO2e). Lifecycle reasoning stops at a gate-to-gate list; burden shift into unused batch stock, cancelled work, and return logistics is named but not traced through the paths.

Minor unclarities: “toward single digits” for SP1 can fight the slack rule; “no material increase” restates the goal instead of defining a band; S10’s resilience story flips between “less rush helps” and “customers will expedite.”

Strong on coverage and grounding. Weak on package-level trade-off math, indicator hygiene, and selectivity. That is a solid answer, not a near-perfect one.
