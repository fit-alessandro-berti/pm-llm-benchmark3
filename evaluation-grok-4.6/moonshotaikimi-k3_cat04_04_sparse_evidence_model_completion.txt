The analysis is strong: the three candidates are genuinely different hypotheses, language inclusion is exact (\(|L|=7\subset 14\subset 18\)), DFG bidirectional edges are not treated as concurrency, missingness is separated from optionality with a defensible calculation, and D5 is encoded as a disjunctive precedence rather than a join. The ledger, decision rules, audit, matrix, and data-collection plan are complete and internally consistent.

What keeps it well short of a high score is that a required deliverable is sloppy. Section 5 still contains leftover `if False` draft code and then builds the outer chain from `list(tail.children)`. In PM4Py that collection is often a set, so the intended R→core→A→P→Z order is not guaranteed. Section 8 rebuilds the same model correctly with `_chain`, which means the published `model_permissive` and the validated one are not the same artifact. `trace_fits` also calls `parse_event_log_string` with no fallback after the log constructor already admitted that API may fail.

Those are not cosmetic nits. Under a strict reading they are implementation defects in the permissive candidate and in the validation harness. The reasoning would be near the top of the range; the code is not.
