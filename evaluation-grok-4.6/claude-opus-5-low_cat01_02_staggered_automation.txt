**6.5**

This is a conceptually strong, well-structured policy memo that still fails a strict standard on quantitative discipline, fidelity between claimed and executed analyses, and a few identification facts. It is far from a weak answer. It is also not nearly flawless.

## Format and coverage

The ten required headings, table schemas, rollout choice, and claim-ladder prefixes are followed. The estimand list, threat list, and mechanism list meet or exceed the stated minima. Word counts in §§1 and 3 are acceptable; §8 is dense but within spirit. Format compliance is not the problem.

## What is done well

The availability vs. intensity vs. selection vs. co-shock vs. measurement distinction in §1 is the right opening. Estimands E1–E9 are unusually complete: ITT-like availability, dose, tail, loops, diversion, CSAT-among-responders, event time, load heterogeneity, and shared-desk interference. Treatment-version heterogeneity (25% untrained vs. 50% trained) and the upward bias from a contaminated “never-treated” control are correctly named. Rejecting naïve TWFE on Goodman–Bacon grounds, and preferring not-yet-/never-treated group-time comparisons, is the right primary design for this panel. Mechanisms are process-literate: substitution vs. capacity, rework relabelled as escalation, p90/median widening, sticky later-month values, survey selection. The recommendation — Delta first, hold Epsilon, case- or team-level randomization, fraud-desk instrumentation, hard tail/CSAT guardrails, scale only if median improves *and* the tail does not worsen — is the correct dual objective (operations + identifiability). The claim ladder is properly ordered and refuses the sentence leadership wants.

## Material quantitative errors

Section 5 announces that the numbers are “arithmetic facts.” Several are not.

- Alpha post-period `high_complexity_pct` is 33.8, not 34.6 (Δ is +5.8, not +6.6).
- Gamma Jan–Jun complexity is 23.7, not 24.5.
- Gamma May–Jun → Jul–Aug load change is about +0.9 arrivals/FTE, not +2.9. That is a large relative error on a covariate they treat as central.
- Delta median is written as “+5.1 (34.5→40.5)”. Those two statements contradict each other; 40.5 − 34.5 = 6.0. The +5.1 figure is then fed into the Alpha/Beta DiD.
- Epsilon Apr–Aug p90 averages ~97.2, not 95.2; Delta’s post p90 is ~89.3, not 88.9. The April-cohort p90 DiD is therefore misstated (closer to −6.5h than −5.1h).

None of these flips the qualitative story, but they are exactly the mistakes this section promised not to make. Approximate calculation was allowed; internal inconsistency and a 3× error on load were not.

## Empirical overclaims

The strongest-evidence cell for `median_cycle_h` says the within-adopter dose–response is “monotone at all three adopters.” It is not. Alpha medians by fast-path share are 48.3 (25%), 49.5 (32%), 53.5 (39%), 47.9 (46%), 43.3 (48%). Beta rises from 47.3 to 49.0 before ending at 45.0. Only Gamma’s two post points are monotone. Section 5.3 already notes that p90 peaks mid-dose, then §7 quietly upgrades the median path to monotone by using first/last treated months only. That is selective arithmetic in the service of the headline.

Alpha/Beta are repeatedly described as having deteriorating pre-trends. In the supplied window they do not. Alpha medians are 50.4, 46.9, 49.4; p90 is 101.3, 94.7, 99.4. Beta drifts up by ~1h. Selection was on *historical levels*, which motivates regression to the mean, not a visible pre-period slope. Conflating those two underwrites the claim that event-study leads “are not flat.”

Gamma is called the “stable conditions” adopter. It lost an FTE in June, received a routing bump after July, and entered via a readiness pilot. That overstates how clean E1 is for Gamma.

## Identification: proposed vs. performed

S1 is specified as Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham group-time ATTs. The Python never estimates group-time ATTs, never uses the relative-time dummies it constructs, and reports TWFE plus a `post_Apr` / `post_Jul` dummy. Relative-time indicators are printed as counts and then abandoned. The prose also says “S2 and S3 are run as robustness.” They are not run anywhere. That is a false statement about the analysis, not a nuance.

S1’s adjustment list includes both month effects and a “March fraud-desk step.” Calendar FE already absorb that common step. Small, but sloppy.

The case-level 50/50 randomization inside Delta is a good learning design and a different estimand than branch availability (E1). The memo never says clearly that the RCT answers efficacy of routing a case through the engine, not the ITT of standing the system up in a branch — the decision leadership is actually making. Stepped-wedge is offered as fallback, which helps, but the estimand switch is underspecified.

## Process-science gap

For a lead process-mining scientist, utilization is under-worked. Alpha load rises from ~31 to ~40 arrivals/FTE while the median is roughly flat and p90 blows out. In a queue that pattern is exactly what a utilization shock produces, with or without automation. The memo notes staffing and routing as confounders but never computes a load-implied counterfactual or discusses convex waiting time. That is the natural process interpretation of the April cohort, and it is left qualitative. The 4–6% baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` in never-adopters is observed and then not interpreted (partial prior automation, skip-triage variants, or measurement floor).

Rework and escalation are correctly flagged as possibly sticky/carried-forward, then still given the highest confidences in the table (55 and 60). If those series are partly imputed, those numbers should fall.

## Code

The script is self-contained, validates the panel, prints trends without subplots, computes declared pre/post windows, clusters by branch, and prints the right warnings. Assumptions live in comments. That part meets the spec. It does not implement the primary estimator advertised in §3, does not estimate the event-study it built dummies for, and the intensity specification in code (full panel, month FE) is not the n=12 treated-only slope narrated in §5.3. The code and the memo are cousins, not the same analysis.

## Bottom line

Estimands, threats, interference, TWFE critique, median-vs-tail separation, and the embedded-evaluation rollout are well above average. Strict grading turns on the thing the author invited scrutiny of: quantitative facts, pre-trend description, and honesty about what was actually estimated. Those are not clean. A single organization-wide “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is correctly refused; several of the narrower numeric claims that replace it are still a bit too neat for the arithmetic and the series.
