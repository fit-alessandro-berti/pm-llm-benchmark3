**7.3**

The diagnosis is largely the one the prompt rewards: April is treated as a schema/pipeline break, June as a policy-driven mix change rather than concept drift, August–September as overlapping process, induced concept, and population effects, and October as label-maturity confounding. Metrics are triangulated with DFG edge times, variant shares, and source mix. Remediation is staged and mostly proportionate. That is not enough for a high score under a strict rubric, because the answer still contains a serious methodological error in the required monitoring sketch, several smaller technical defects, and a few over-clean claims that the tables do not fully support.

**The case-start cohort is implemented incorrectly.** `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_contained")` keeps traces whose *entire* lifecycle lies inside the window. It is not a case-start cohort. Open cases and any repair that finishes after month-end are dropped. Those are exactly the V3 / Partner Z / backlog cases this audit is about. The accompanying note even claims the opposite: “start cohort counts cases that began in window (different interpretation for open cases).” The code would systematically reproduce the censoring bias the rest of the memo warns against. Worse, variants and performance DFGs are computed only on the event-calendar slice. Event-window filtering fragments traces, so `get_variants` on that log does not recover the case-level V1/V2/V3 mix in the prompt. Edge times that cross a month boundary (Order Part in July, Part Received in August) are also biased. For this problem, that is not a cosmetic API nit.

**The PM4Py sketch has further correctness issues.** `int(v)` on `pm4py.get_variants` is very likely wrong: the usual return is variant → list of cases, so this should be `len(v)` and would TypeError on a list. Dates are passed as raw strings with no `Timestamp` conversion. `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as a mean tuple while every process claim in the memo is about *medians*. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is required by the prompt and left as a commented ellipsis. Duplicate handling is a generic `(case, activity, timestamp)` drop; it does not reflect the audit finding that cleaning applied only to the new source after 20 April. As a “sketch” this is still too sloppy for the section that was supposed to demonstrate competent process-evidence extraction.

**A few empirical claims are slightly too tidy.** April is not a pure pipeline month with “no material” within-variant rate movement: matured V1/V2/V3 rates all tick up (0.15→0.18, 0.11→0.13, 0.30→0.34). Small, and not enough to explain AUROC 0.67 / predicted mean 0.28, but “no material change” oversells it. June is correctly not called concept drift, but mean predicted risk stays at 0.20 while the mix-implied rate is ~0.16 and ECE is already 0.060. That is more than a “modest” mix effect: the scores did not move with the policy. September’s Partner Z observed rate of 0.45 is flagged as immature, but the memo never states the directional implication of informative censoring: longer V3 paths are less mature, so 0.45 vs 0.27 (and the headline 0.31) are likely *downward*-biased, not just noisy. October is handled well overall; this gap matters more for September.

**Taxonomy is mostly right and occasionally mushy.** Putting June under both process drift and covariate drift is defensible, and calling the V3 SLA break “concept drift induced by process change” is the correct P(y|x) framing. The covariate row then becomes a residual bin (April artefacts + June mix + Partner Z), which blurs the table’s job of separating mechanisms. Confidence “High” on population shift is fair for *presence* of Partner Z; it is too high if read as a claim that Partner Z *explains* the September breach jump, which the contradicting cell itself says may be mostly V3 exposure under an already-shifted concept.

**Sections 3, 4, and 6 are strong but not airtight.** The seven tests map onto the required list and each has two competing predicted outcomes. Fixed-vintage evaluation, clean-recompute of April, same-path conditional tests, and transport checks are the right separators. Missing is an explicit informative-censoring / path-length-dependent delay test, which the outcome-API note makes first-order. The retrain/recalibrate staging is sound: quarantine April, block low-maturity targets, stratified V3 calibrators, do not treat June as a reason to retrain. “Safe: January–March plus post-May mature months” still under-specifies that a model trained only on pre-July data will remain wrong for V3 unless new delay/supplier features or post-crisis mature labels are included; the later paragraph partly repairs this. The five failure modes are the right ones and the mitigations match the monitor hierarchy.

**Net.** This is a good audit memo with the correct multi-cause timeline and mostly disciplined recommendations. It is not nearly flawless. The required process-mining code mis-implements the cohort distinction the author spends the rest of the essay insisting on, and a handful of claims are cleaner than the monthly tables. Under a lenient reading this would sit in the high 8s. Under the requested standard it does not.
