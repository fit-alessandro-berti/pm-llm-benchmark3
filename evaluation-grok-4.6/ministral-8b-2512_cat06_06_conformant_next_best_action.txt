**5.2**

The answer has the right skeleton: all six required sections, A/B last, G treated as an unenabled exception, screening not treated as a substitute for independent review, and a conformant second-review plan as the primary recommendation. That is not enough. Under a strict reading it is a formatted, directionally sensible memo with several process-model errors, invented quantities, and internal contradictions that would mislead an operator.

**1. Ranked action table**

The table is the weakest part of an otherwise recognizable structure.

- Nine rows are used to rank seven actions. Action C appears twice (ranks 2 and 6) with an unexplained “spillover mitigation” distinction that is never specified in the table. Rank 3 is just a slower serial version of rank 1. This is not a ranking of A–G; it is a padded list.
- Rank 1 is labeled `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS` but no conformance condition is stated. Launching alternate screening and waiting for S4 is allowed now. That label is reserved for cases such as F (rejection threshold) or a completed exception path. Misuse of the controlled vocabulary.
- Ranks 2 and 6 say “then execute.” Second-officer review can approve, reject, or hold/SAR. Building execution into the plan name treats a contingent outcome as the action.
- Benefit column is copied as €20k–€35k onto plans that only sometimes meet the 90-minute deadline. S4 at 65 minutes plus review (5–15) plus booking (5–15) is 75–95 minutes before any screening overrun. Rank 3 (screen, then wait 65 minutes) is even worse if sequential. Same benefit number for materially different deadline odds is incoherent.
- Harm for P-901 is monetized as “€10k–€28k.” The case gives only a 0.12–0.28 breach-probability increase, not a euro equivalent. That figure is fabricated.
- Rank 1 false-rejection harm starts at €0; rank 2 starts at €15k for the same downstream decision risk. Inconsistent.
- Confidence “None” is not a confidence level. “High” on rank 1 is unsupported given S4’s 30-minute window, a 12-minute-old score, and non-transportable observational predictions.
- F’s benefit is listed as €0. Avoiding unlawful execution is the main benefit of a justified reject; omitting it distorts the comparison with E and with waiting.

**2. Counterfactual continuations**

This section fails the required counterfactual discipline (permit / likely continuation / P-884 benefit / P-884 harm / spillover / compliance / intervention uncertainty). It is a set of invented probability trees.

- **Critical process error:** “If fuzzy match resolves (confidence <0.46), risk score may drop below 70 → Auto Clear possible.” Auto Clear is allowed only with **no alert and risk < 40** (rule 3). A drop below 70 is not Auto Clear. Unresolved-match “resolution” is a determination, not a confidence falling below the current 0.46. The same sentence then contradicts itself (“still requires second review if score 70”). This error is repeated as a reranking trigger in section 6.
- A defensible related point is never made: if screening **both** resolved the match **and** moved risk below 70, rule 6’s second-officer trigger might lapse; Auto Clear would still be unavailable. That is the actual control-path question.
- Approval/rejection/hold splits (60/20/20, 55/25/20, 40/60, override 20/80, “30% false negative”) are presented as if known. The brief gives observational approval 0.55–0.75 and warns that estimates may not transport under intervention. The answer ignores that warning and over-precisifies.
- Rank 5: if the match “resolves” after 6–24 hours, “Deadline saved.” The deadline is 90 minutes. That branch is impossible. This is a basic horizon error, not a nuance.
- Hold+SAR is mentioned, but not that filing a report does not authorize execution (rule 8) or that the customer must not be tipped (rule 11). Those constraints belong in the continuation, especially beside Action E.

**3. Benefit and harm reasoning**

The dominance claim that A/B are never justified by a favorable operational forecast is correct and is the one part of the normative reasoning that is sharp.

Everything else is thin or inverted:

- Deadline loss (€20k–€35k) is the **smallest** quantified bucket. False reject/hold is €15k–€80k; unlawful execution is €100k–€500k plus non-monetary harm. The write-up still talks as if capturing the deadline is the thing being “balanced.” That quietly reintroduces SLA-maximization after claiming regulatory dominance.
- Because deadline loss is dominated by false-clear/regulatory harm, interrupting P-901 to chase P-884’s SLA is much harder to justify than the ranking implies. The text does not run that comparison.
- Customer harm and regulatory harm are ranges with different legal status; they are added and compared as if they were commensurate point estimates.
- No serious treatment of a **conformant miss** (wait for S4, accept SLA loss, keep four-eyes) versus a **conformant interrupt** (burn P-901). That is the actual live tradeoff.

**4. Conformance constraints**

- A is mapped to rule 7 (four-eyes). Immediate execution skips second review and a valid approval path (rules 6 and 9). Four-eyes is a pairing constraint on two reviews, not the primary defect of skipping review entirely. Rule 10 is a non-available workaround, not a rule A “violates” by executing.
- **F is mapped to rule 11 (SAR non-disclosure).** F is a documented rejection on an unresolved match. That is not a SAR disclosure. Wrong rule, and it shows the author is pattern-matching labels rather than applying them.
- What would make F valid is never stated operationally: a recorded, policy-sufficient rationale that the unresolved 0.46 match plus risk 72 plus destination risk meets the **rejection** threshold (rule 6 requires second review before approve/execute, not necessarily before reject). What would make G valid is stated better (director + incident ID), but “a genuine incident identifier would still need to be created and justified” is not developed: manufacturing an incident to hit a deadline would be an invalid exception even if a director answered the phone.
- The required paragraph on why a good numerical forecast cannot promote A or B is mostly deferred to a slogan. Hard constraints versus terms in a utility function should have been explicit here.

**5. Resource spillovers**

- S2 versus S4 is described at the right level of mechanism (interrupt P-901 vs wait), then the sensitivity analysis goes wrong: if S4 is 65 minutes, rank 3 is said to become “closer to Rank 2’s spillover risk.” Late S4 raises **P-884 deadline risk**. It does not create P-901 spillover. Those are different externalities.
- P-901’s own 70-minute deadline and a 45-minute interrupt are not computed. Taking S2 now is not a mild spillover; it is a large hit to another high-value case. The fallback “if S4 is not available in <60 minutes, interrupt P-901” is therefore more aggressive than the author admits.
- API quota: “3 left, using one is acceptable” is an assertion, not an analysis of opportunity cost.
- Waiting for G is listed as a time sink in the table and then barely analyzed here, despite the prompt calling it out.
- No plan for **starting the screen immediately** (information value for whoever reviews) while **deferring** the S2 decision until an S4 ETA update. That is the obvious real-time policy; the ranking treats S2/S4 as static alternatives.

**6. Final recommendation and confidence**

Primary recommendation (screen in parallel, wait for S4, then independent review) is the right *family* of answer. The supporting claims are not.

- Confidence **85%** is incompatible with a stale risk score, a 35–65 minute S4 window versus a 90-minute deadline, 0.55–0.75 observational approval, and non-transportable predictions.
- Reranking trigger: “if fuzzy match resolves, risk score drops below 70 → Auto Clear possible.” Repeats the rule-3 error in the one place an operator would act on it.
- Fallback to S2 at 60 minutes is not conditioned on P-901’s remaining slack, on whether screening changed rule-6 triggers, or on the fact that a deadline miss (€20k–€35k) may be cheaper than P-901’s spillover plus a rushed review of a 0.46 sanctions match on €480k.

**Net**

This is a competent exam-shape answer with the correct political ranking (no unlawful shortcuts) and a defensible headline plan, undermined by a genuine conformance mistake (Auto Clear at risk < 70), a horizon mistake (deadline “saved” after 6–24 hours), a wrong rule attached to F, fabricated money and probabilities, a duplicated action, and plans that assume execution. Those are not nits in a conformance-and-counterfactual task. A near-flawless answer would keep the same primary rec, fix the control logic, refuse invented numbers, rank each of A–G once, and treat missing the SLA as often preferable to either nonconformance or burning P-901. This is well short of that.
