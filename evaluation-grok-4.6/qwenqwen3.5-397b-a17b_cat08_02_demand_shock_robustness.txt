**6.0**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly stays inside the required seven sections. It avoids the obvious traps (always-on Urgent-first, bigger Routine batches, naive M1 dedication, I9). It also treats mix shift, SLAs, and p90—not just average cycle time. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Process-mining errors.** Normal and peak DFGs cover unequal windows (8 vs 3 weeks). Citing raw arc counts as if they were comparable is wrong. “Repeat assay rises from 1140 to 1260” looks mild; weekly repeats roughly triple and the per-case rate roughly doubles (~6% → ~13%). The same distortion appears on hold-for-specialist and recollection. A process-mining answer that mishandles frequency normalization is not robust evidence use. The important variant that Urgent batch-wait bypass falls from 74% to 58% in peak is never used, even though it is the clearest log evidence that the Urgent fast path collapses exactly when it is needed.

**Broken policy design.** P2 cannot fire in the state it is meant to fix: peak accession queue is 14h *and* technical validation is 16h / 101% utilized, so “accession > 10h AND tech val < 5h” is empty. I4 is thereby a dead letter in winter. R3’s “spill to M1 if M2 queue > 4h” is also always-on in peak (assay median queue 18h), so “dynamic dedication” collapses into mixed routing—the thing I3 was trying to avoid. Thresholds are not calibrated to the tables they cite.

**False bottleneck migration.** Clearing medical validation does not pull more work into assay. Those assays have already run. The Scenario A second-order story is factory-pull language that the log does not support. Scenario C overstates medical validators “sitting idle” during specialist absence; they still receive a large direct tech-val → med-val stream. Scenario B is partly circular: it assumes I3 routing that is not current-state.

**Ungrounded and inverted details.** Dashboard “queue depth” bands (5 / 8 / 10 *cases*) are not convertible from the given *hour* queues and are too small to be leading at peak rates. R4 invents a 5pm technical-validation close. The trade-off table has R3 increasing repeats “if M2 spill occurs”; spill *to M1* should reduce M2-driven repeats. R1’s claim that early reject loads pre-analytics harder is asserted, not shown; early reject can also cut wasted bench/assay work and later recollection chatter. Repeat “>15% of capacity” is sloppy arithmetic on un-normalized counts.

**What still works.** Four scenarios match the required axes. I1/I2/I3/I4/I10 are treated as conditional, with downstream overload named for the core package. The table has the required columns. Residual risk includes a redesign-induced vulnerability. Leading vs lagging is distinguished, and Urgent 18h is used as a hard constraint rather than a mean.

Those strengths keep it above a failing score. The DFG period error, two non-firing “dynamic” policies, and the med-val→assay pull story keep it well below a high one.
