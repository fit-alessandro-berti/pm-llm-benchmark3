**6.2**

The memo reaches the right strategic call (hybrid E, not national scale or full rollback), follows the required six-section structure, and generally keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions in separate columns. The five metric conflicts are explained in a way that matches the process-mining story (fitness/precision, median vs tail, handovers vs resilience, visible vs downstream cost, segment split). A–E are evaluated with conditions, and M1–M8 are ranked with an explicit “how this could change the decision” link.

It is not close to flawless.

**Made-up precision.** “M6 and M2 together resolve >70% of the central uncertainties” is an invented quantity. Utilization “80%,” rule accuracy “85%,” and a cost gate of “EUR 175” are presented as if they were evidence-based cutoffs. EUR 175 is also poorly specified: it is compared to overall baseline visible cost (EUR 184) while the rollout cohort is only simple houses, whose baseline cost is unknown.

**Wrong comparison frames.** Thresholds that ask the simple-house tail to converge “within 2 days of baseline p90” treat an overall baseline as the right counterfactual. The same error appears in the cost gate. Segment-to-overall comparisons are exactly the confound the memo should have refused.

**Selection and data-quality bias are underweighted.** Pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings; attributes are incomplete before week 3; complaint cost is truncated after week 4; start timestamps are sparse. Those are first-order facts for any scale/rollback/experiment choice. They barely appear in the evidence table and do not drive the executive argument that overall pilot-vs-baseline metrics are mix-biased.

**Evidence table is thinner than it looks.** Nine rows meet the count, but several important prompt items are missing as facts: DFG edge shifts, temporal-profile deviations, survey-team utilization 78%→52% vs second-visit 66%→92%, SLA improved for simple houses, NPS, and the coverage limits above. A few “facts” carry evaluative parentheticals. Item 6’s “assumption” (alignments might be recording artifacts) is a weak reading given the explicit redesign, DFG, and complaint-theme shift.

**VoI ranking is only partly defensible.** Putting M6 and M2 first is reasonable. Ranking a randomized skip-rule experiment (M1) sixth, below M5 and M7, undervalues the highest-leverage causal test of the main tail mechanism.

**Prose quality is below exec-memo standard.** Compound terms are smashed together throughout (`firsttimeright`, `skiprule`, `postbilling`, `workingtogether`, `visiblevstotal`). Relative percentages are sloppy (“handovers dropped 65%,” “complaints rose 61%”). The 5% conformance threshold is worded as “remain 5%,” which is not a well-formed gate. Generalization (0.56) is listed and then left unexplained.

The recommendation is usable. The analysis is not tight enough, or clean enough, to score much higher under a strict reading.
