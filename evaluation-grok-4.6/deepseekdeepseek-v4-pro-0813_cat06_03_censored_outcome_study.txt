**6.5**

The nine-section write-up is complete, on-policy, and conceptually in the right family: it does not treat rejected/withdrawn/open cases as non-default, it does not train uncritically on the 21,600 labeled rows, it keeps an as-of view, and the cohort arithmetic is exact (18,900 + 2,700 + 3,600 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 = 48,000). Those are the main failure modes the prompt warned against, and they are avoided. The remaining problems are real design and statistical defects, not cosmetic ones.

**Target and clocks are internally inconsistent.** The primary estimand mixes two time scales: application-start time with an arbitrary \(F=365\) day administrative horizon, and a 90-day clock that starts only at activation. The write-up never resolves whether a default at activation+90 after day 365 counts, whether CIF is evaluated on the application clock or the activation clock, or how landmark time \(s\) enters the probability. The formal statement also sneaks in a restriction (“has not already reached Rejected/Withdrawn/Transferred before \(s\)”), which is landmark conditioning, not the population CIF advertised in Section 1. \(F=365\) is especially poorly chosen: cases starting in late 2025 have only weeks of follow-up by 15 February 2026, so that horizon is not identified in the data.

**Missing labels after the horizon are not ordinary right-censoring.** Once activation+90 has elapsed and `default_90d` is still absent, the 90-day endpoint is missing, not censored in the usual survival sense. Censoring at extraction or at “last known non-default status” after the horizon does not identify a 90-day CIF; it is a missing-data problem and needs IPCW, bounds, or exclusion with a missingness model. The text sometimes sees this and sometimes falls back to “censor it.” That is a methods error, not a wording issue.

**Cause-specific hazards are treated as if they deliver CIF.** Section 3’s primary estimator is “Aalen–Johansen or cause-specific hazard models.” Cause-specific Cox does not estimate cumulative incidence unless all causes are combined. Fine–Gray appears later as if it were interchangeable, and Fine–Gray with prefix/landmark covariates is not free.

**The validation plan cannot evaluate the stated estimand.** Train ends 30 June 2025 (no post-policy data). The test window is 1 October–31 December 2025, i.e. the period in which 90-day outcomes plus 7–35 day feed delay are largely immature by extraction. A 365-day CIF is impossible there; even a 90-day-after-activation CIF is only sparsely observed. Rolling-origin and label freeze are the right words, but they are not reconciled with \(F=365\) or with the 3,600 immature activations.

**Process-aware leakage is not fully closed.** Discovering the DFG and variants on complete training traces includes Contract Signed, Account Activated, collections, and default — exactly the post-prediction path the study is supposed to keep out of the early-warning view. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then ignored; features are built in a separate pandas loop. `final_limit_available` / `decision_reason_available` are kept as features, which encodes that a final decision has already occurred. Outcome columns (`default_event`, `outcome_status`) sit in the same frame as predictors; the leakage assertion would not catch that. Activity rename keys are invented without being marked as placeholders. Dedup on exact `(case, activity, timestamp, resource)` will miss near-duplicate migration events. Multiple prefixes per case are never treated as clustered observations.

**Smaller but material gaps.** Partner-originated permanently missing feeds are folded into the 3,600 immature/delayed row instead of being called out as a distinct missingness class. Transfer-after-activation vs transfer-as-competing-exit is blurred. `collection_status` is correctly banned as a feature but never used as a possible event-time marker. The skeleton overruns the requested ~80–160 lines and is partly decorative PM4Py. None of this sinks the design — the target choice, cohort table, leakage grid, assumption testability, and deployment/DGP discussion are genuinely strong — but under a strict reading the statistical object being estimated is not clean enough, and the backtest cannot measure it.
