**6.8**

This is a complete, constraint-aware digital-twin design: DES rather than replay, cross-case coupling, calendars, latent objections, waiting as an emergent output, PM4Py isolated from the engine, and no optimality claim. It is not nearly flawless. Several modeling, identification, and experiment-design errors would change intervention rankings if left unfixed.

### What is actually strong
The boundary, entities, and first-class dependencies (architect bursts, geographic inspection bundles, cluster-level objections) match the process, not a naive i.i.d. case generator. Holding empirical waits out as validation targets instead of injecting them is the correct queueing discipline. Objections are treated as unobserved and swept, not as log events. The experiment table includes baseline, D1–D10, non-additivity for D10, and three real stresses. Uncertainty treatment (CRN on differences, outer parameter loop, tail metrics/CVaR, unfinished-case censoring) is the right family of methods. The skeleton uses pandas + `format_dataframe`, avoids `pm4py.read_csv`, documents the process-tree string’s lack of guards, and refuses to label inter-event sojourn as service time.

### Material flaws

**1. Planner pool is very likely misspecified.**  
“Two planners only handle Simple renovation” means those two are *restricted away from complex work*, not that Simple is sealed into a 2-FTE sub-pool. Simple is 45% of volume. Isolating it to 2 of 8 planners invents a bottleneck the log probably does not support and will distort D2/D6/D10. The correct default is: 2 FTE cannot take non-Simple work; the other 6 can take any type (or estimate the mix from `org:resource` × `case_type`).

**2. Clock definition is inconsistent with the data and with the SLA question.**  
Section 7’s primary metric is “municipal clock (excl. applicant wait)”, while variant medians (12d/22d/48d/…) and the completeness-with-missing-docs path are dominated by applicant delay (~5d median from request). Distributional validation against those figures on a municipal-only clock will look like misfit, or worse, will be “passed” by comparing different quantities. Statutory permit time is almost always applicant-experienced elapsed time. Report **both** clocks, and validate each against the matching observable. D1 in particular can game a municipal-only clock by pushing work before Submit.

**3. Effect sizes for interventions are mostly missing.**  
E1–E9 are on/off switches. A twin that compares policies needs parameterized mechanisms: e.g. D1 multiplies P(missing docs | type) by a factor with a prior; D7 reshapes the weekday profile toward a stated capacity; D8 has a false-negative rate; D9 shifts the public-notice tail by a stated amount; D5 must say *when* legal may start and whether it can fail closed. Without magnitudes, the matrix is a list of labels, not a design of experiments. D5 remains control-flow theater: legal sits after drafting in the tree; “pre-check” is not pinned to a start event, information set, or rework channel.

**4. Warm-up advice contradicts itself.**  
The text correctly rejects classical steady state, then applies Welch/MSER-5 and drops 4–6 weeks. In a seasonal, calendar-driven system that is the wrong tool. Seed WIP from a month-matched snapshot and either (a) run a full seasonal cycle as warm-up, or (b) start on a real calendar date with no discard and report by season. 4–6 weeks does not cover March–May or council-week structure. Nested 50×40×14 long runs are also proposed with no budget, so the protocol is not executable as written.

**5. The given process tree is treated as more complete than it is.**  
The narrative allows rework after planning, legal, and appeal; the tree only loops completeness and inspection. Complex variance (8%, median 140d, p90 260d) is council- and appeal-driven. Rare variants are 7% with a 220d p90. Simulating the tree plus two geometric loops will understate the tail that D6/D9/D10 are supposed to move. That mismatch needed an explicit extra-tree layer (appeal, planning bounce, “other”) rather than a parenthetical rework probability.

**6. Stated empirical mechanisms are dropped.**  
Site-inspection no-shows are in the timing table and never appear in state or the skeleton. Neighborhood objections can hit *related* cases; the latent model is mostly i.i.d. Bernoulli (`rng.random() < rate`) plus a comment about clusters. Architect-burst intensity is month × weekday × Hawkes/batch overlaid without a statement of how double-counting Monday architect dumps is avoided.

### Smaller but real issues
- Decision rule “no case type worsens” plus recycling observed medians as SLA targets is a rigid Pareto filter; many useful tail interventions would fail it. Quality/safety of skipped inspections is not a first-class metric despite being D2’s actual risk.
- S2’s “+1 council week/month” has no baseline council calendar, so the shock is unscaled. S3 confounds D9 with high objection prevalence.
- Token replay validates a simplified tree that cannot explain 7% of traces; large-n KS tests will reject even a good model (Wasserstein is mentioned, but the test plan still leads with KS).
- Text promises per-source RNG substreams; the code uses `master_seed + r`. Only D2 has an executable policy hook.
- Service times independent of congestion, public-notice as a 24/7 “system resource” rather than a statutory calendar delay (holidays/council), and `prior_violations` / area threshold left as unparameterized latents all affect D2/D8 identification.

### Constraint check
Hard constraints are met: not independent-case only; calendars present; service/wait not silently collapsed; objections not treated as observed; no fake `read_csv`; no optimality claim. PM4Py usage is mostly conventional (`format_dataframe`, DFG, performance DFG, variants, `get_case_arrival_average`, `parse_process_tree`). That is why the score is not lower. The grade is held down because the twin would not be safe to rank D1–D10 without repairing pool semantics, clocks, intervention magnitudes, warm-up, and the appeal/rare-variant tail.
