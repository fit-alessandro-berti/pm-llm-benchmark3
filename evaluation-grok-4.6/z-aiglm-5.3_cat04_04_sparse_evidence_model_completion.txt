## Why the score is still low

**1. Missing required artifact (decisive).**  
Sections 1–10, the evidence table, the three five-part candidate writeups, the audit tables, the comparative matrix, the recommendation, and the uncertainty statement were only outlined. A grader cannot award credit for a document that does not exist.

**2. The “code” is unfinished and unexecuted.**  
No Petri-net conversion, soundness check, alignment, playout, or suite result is shown. The script stops inside the negative-suite list. Claims about fitness 1.0, language membership, and API behavior are therefore unverified.

**3. A material PM4Py risk in the final construction.**  
The balanced and permissive models wrap the middle in `OperatorPOWL(Operator.SEQUENCE, [RO, middle, Approve, Release, Archive])`. In PM4Py, `OperatorPOWL` is the operator node for **XOR / LOOP**; sequencing is normally a `StrictPartialOrder` with a chain of edges. That is not a cosmetic style choice. If `SEQUENCE` is rejected or given the wrong semantics, both non-conservative candidates fail to convert. A linear `StrictPartialOrder` over five children would have been the safe encoding.

**4. Optionality-inside-a-PO is underspecified at the API level.**  
Putting `XOR(S, τ)` and `XOR(X, τ)` *as nodes* of a partial order, then adding edges into those nodes, is reasonable *if* silent completion still respects the order. The note assumes language is unchanged. That is plausible but not demonstrated, and it is exactly the kind of POWL detail that should have been checked by converting the net and replaying the suite.

**5. Conservative vs. missing `Supplier Confirmation` is overstated, then only half-corrected.**  
A lost S on V1/V2/V5 still looks like V3/V4 and *fits* the XOR-of-variants model. Brittleness is real only for the two expedite shapes (`C,E,X` and `E,C,X`). The early “one missing S collapses fitness” claim is too strong; the later hedge is better, but the operational story never lands in a finished section.

**6. Permissive is only two traces wider than balanced.**  
That fork (C-trigger vs. either-trigger) is defensible, but the prompt asked for a *permissive* envelope of unobserved-but-rule-consistent behavior. Independent optionality of X without S is already in the balanced language. A reviewer can fairly say the three candidates are not spaced as widely as the task’s inductive-bias story requires, even if the fork is conceptually clean.

**7. Several planned “facts” stay informal.**  
The 19:11 binomial, the 0/8 vs 2/15 trigger argument, and the “5–10% missing cannot explain 7/30 no-S” comparison are the right *kind* of reasoning, but they remain back-of-envelope and never appear as a ledger the reader can audit.

## Bottom line

The final *ideas* would have supported a strong 7–8 if they had been turned into the specified document, with working ProcessTree + POWL code and a finished suite. What was submitted is an incomplete internal draft with at least one serious API design risk and no required sections. That is not a high-quality answer to this task.
