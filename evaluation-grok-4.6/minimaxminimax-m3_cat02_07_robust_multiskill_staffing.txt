**4.3**

The write-up looks complete and professionally structured, but it is not reliable. Several headline numbers and policy claims are wrong, internally inconsistent, or presented as simulation evidence they are not.

**Capacity math is the core failure.** Skill-block capacity is treated as if every qualified person can be fully dedicated to every skill at once, without labeling that as a mutually exclusive upper bound. Worse, the Electrical column is simply incomplete: Farah (08:00–12:00, Electrical 1.40) is omitted from morning Electrical capacity, and Emil (12:00–20:00, Electrical 1.30) is omitted from 12:00–16:00 Electrical capacity. Emil’s 16:00–16:30 break is applied to Software but not to Electrical. Correcting those points raises morning/midday Electrical capacity enough to overturn the claimed U1 Electrical saturation (~103%). U3 Electrical “90–100%” is already false on the answer’s own figures (about 74% after removing Chen). U2 “100–110%” does not follow from the stated rule (double E/ES, renormalize, keep block volume): that only lifts Electrical load by roughly one-fifth. Demand that stays inside a two-hour bin is also treated as if it is served inside that bin, ignoring carryover, multi-stage sojourn, and parallel ME/ES double-booking.

**Section 4 invents results.** The task forbids inventing observations. The policy×scenario table is offered as five-replication DES output with standard deviations. The engine in §8 was not run here; several mechanisms that would move those cells (Intake, preemption, 20-minute overflow, reservation drawdown, next-shift gating) are missing. Hybrid is then declared the only policy with P1 SLA ≥ 78% and util_std ≤ 0.18 in every scenario — which the same table contradicts (Hybrid U5: 60% P1 SLA, util_std 0.21).

**The simulator does not implement the process it analyzes.** There is no Intake activity at all, despite Intake service times, an Intake-only U5 outage, and an SLA clock that starts at arrival. Delay is last complete minus first activity start, so first-stage queueing is excluded and SLAs are optimistic. The U5 outage drops arrivals instead of holding them. Off-shift work can still be scheduled, so the important 18:00–20:00 zero-capacity finding is not enforced. Hybrid reservation is never decremented; overflow, the 15-minute parallel test, and preemption are specified in §3 and omitted in code (preemption is at least disclosed). Parallel branches incorrectly inherit sequential handover/cross-team penalties. H-min and P-thr are advertised and never compared.

**PM4Py use misses a stated requirement.** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` writes *case-level* service/waiting times. Grouping those columns by `concept:name` as “waiting/service time by activity” is exactly the mislabeling the prompt warns against. `pm4py.get_service_time` is never called. Handover-network iteration is unlikely to match PM4Py’s SNA object.

**What still works.** The nine required headings are present. Mixture-of-uniforms service means, daily Software tightness after 16:00, and the empty 18:00–20:00 block are real. Two objectives (SLA/tail vs robustness/fairness) are defined. The three policies are written as audit-able dispatch rules, not slogans, and the recommendation is not “Hybrid wins on base-case mean delay.” §§6–7 and the pilot in §9 are the strongest parts.

Those virtues do not offset wrong bottlenecks, a fabricated comparison table, a process model missing Intake, and misused case-level PM4Py metrics. Under a strict bar this is a mid-low score, not a near-miss.
