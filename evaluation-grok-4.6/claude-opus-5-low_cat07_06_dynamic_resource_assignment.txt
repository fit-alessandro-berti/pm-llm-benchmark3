**7.3**

A strong, unusually complete design that still has enough concrete errors, internal contradictions, and unverifiable leaps to fall well short of “nearly flawless.”

**What holds up.** Hard/soft constraints are separated correctly, and H1 is interpreted the right way: skill 0 is authorization, not a supervision upgrade. That is why C015 is *not* given to Eva or Jonas as executors — the notes invite that mistake, and the answer refuses it. The algorithm has every required scoring term, greedy-over-ILP is justified on explainability grounds, and supervision is treated as a real capacity cost rather than a comment. Continuity on C007/C016, unloading David/Iris, H3 split on C010, and H2 specialist routing on C001 are all legally coherent. Sections 5–6 are the best parts: the worker note is non-punitive, and the audit table includes failure modes (especially A13/A14). The self-fulfilling-log warning in §7 is sophisticated.

**Constraint accounting is wrong.** S4 requires ≥20% of *normal/low* assignments to support *documented* training priorities. The answer lists 9 such cases and only one documented-priority match (C009), then still reports “33%, above the 20% floor” by padding with C010 (Jonas/Approval; his priority is Security) and C015. That is a failed soft constraint reported as a success. Worse, C015 is Security observation for Jonas — it *is* on-priority — yet §3 calls C010 *and* C015 “off-priority.” The same paragraph cannot both be true.

**Factual slips in the baseline comparison.** There are two Quality Checks (C006, C012), not “all three.” There are three Diagnosis cases, not “both Diagnoses.” First-available for C001 is Celine (6 open, Security=1), not “Celine or Iris”; Iris has 11 open and would not win that rule. These are small, but they sit in a required comparison section.

**Misused historical evidence.** The +3.3h figure is for keeping a *weak* owner. Eva is Diagnosis skill 2 (competent) on C016. Applying that median, even “partially,” inflates the quality risk of the continuity choice the policy itself defends.

**The published scorer is not shown to produce the table.** Weights, `expected_service_time`, `queue_delay`, and `accept_threshold` are never instantiated on this snapshot. Several calls look like qualitative overrides: C003 becomes a third reassignment against the 1.8× rework evidence and S3; Celine is given C003 + C007 (6h SLA, complexity 0.81) + C009 supervision on 6.0h remaining; C010 still touches high-overtime Bruno on a *normal* case (S1 requires urgent + no safer alternative; Eva is Approval 2). The text flags tightness but does not show the score losing to the next-best eligible pair.

**Capacity and H6 bookkeeping are loose.** Jonas is “3 new, at the ceiling of 4” in one place and “3 + 1 prep” in another. Hours are never estimated per case, so S6 (“must fit hours remaining”) is asserted, not checked. Farid’s two urgents and Eva’s C005+C016 are acknowledged as residual SLA risk; that honesty is good, but it also means the policy’s highest weight (SLA) is left exposed on the two scarcest people.

**PM4Py sketch is directionally right, methodologically sloppy.** The required discovery/filter/performance calls are present and used for the right audit questions. Using `groupby` timestamp `diff` as service time conflates waiting and work — exactly what `get_service_time` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` exist to avoid. Role-object attributes and a few signatures are version-fragile. Fine as a sketch; not a clean implementation.

**Net.** This is a serious multi-objective policy with real socio-technical judgment, not a throughput maximizer, and it does not pretend there is one optimum. Under a normal rubric it would score higher. Under the required strictness, the S4 misreport, the C015 contradiction, the baseline miscounts, the 3.3h misuse, and the gap between pseudocode and the assignment table are enough to keep it out of the 8–10 band.
