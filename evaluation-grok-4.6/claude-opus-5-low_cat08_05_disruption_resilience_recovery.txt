8.3

This is a strong, well-grounded analysis with careful DFG arithmetic, a sharp conceptual distinction between control substitution and control deletion, and a redesign that is actually operable. It is not nearly flawless. Several precision, attribution, and classification problems keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What works.** Temporal structure is handled correctly: T2 is treated as a different process, not a noisy version of the baseline; T3 is correctly flagged as the false-confidence window; T4 is read through the `Invoice close → Audit remediation` edge rather than through cycle time. Core counts are right: 1,600/5,100 = 31% skipped authorization; 1,650/3,600 ≈ 46% hit temporary release; 1,800 + 1,450 = 3,250 unscanned dispatches; 3,250 − 2,600 ≈ 650 with no POD; 450/1,500 = 30% backlog cancellations; 300/1,400 ≈ 21% reconciliation cancellations; 2,300/2,100 leaves a 200-case open tail. The constraints are respected: hospital protection is not treated as automatic virtue, throughput is not counted as resilience when controls vanish, and after-the-fact remediation is not equated with contemporaneous compliance.

**Classification failure in section 3.** The prompt asks which adaptations are resilience, which are risky shortcuts, and which are ambiguous. I, J, and L are placed in both the “true resilience” list and the “ambiguous” list. The surrounding prose explains the hedge, but the required identification is then internally inconsistent. K is also called “true resilience” even though the table correctly notes that reconciliation occurs after dispatch decisions and that one in five manual reservations was fictitious. Post-hoc record repair is recovery work, not resilience.

**Causal over-attribution.** Skip-authorization (C) is repeatedly blamed for the 9.5% traceability-exception rate and for move-on-model deviations (missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature). Those deviations map to E/G/H/F, not to an authorization skip. C is a real compliance hole; it is a different hole. This is the most important analytical slip in the piece.

**Overstatement.** “Wholesale-bulk effectively stopped” is false: median 96h is severe degradation, not a stop. “The most safety-sensitive customers received the least verified orders” is stronger than the DFG supports (1,000 fast-lane cases, 920 of them to snapshot, no proof that hospital orders were globally the least checked). Hospital-critical T4 is called “at baseline (8h / 16h)” when baseline p90 is 15h. Contemporaneous scan evidence in T2 is called “~0%”; the log shows no *system* scan, not the absence of every paper artefact. T3’s 11,400 cases over four calendar days are compared to a 21-*business*-day baseline of 2,000/day and then used to assert backlog burn-down; that daily-rate claim is under-qualified.

**Unacknowledged DFG leakage.** Manual pack is 3,600, but 1,650 + 1,800 = 3,450. Temporary release is 1,650, but only 1,450 leave to dispatch-without-scan. Fast lane 1,000 → 920, manual auth 2,500 → 2,300, skip 1,600 → 1,500. An analysis this quantitative should have flagged incompleteness instead of writing as if the graph were closed.

**Format and design nits.** An extra H1 precedes the seven required top-level sections. Recovery thresholds (30 minutes, 40% scan rate, 8h snapshot TTL, 20%/10% sampling, 15-minute triage SLA) are asserted rather than marked as illustrative. P1/P2 is an XOR, so a hospital-critical controlled-substance order is not routed into the controlled lane; product-class gates mostly save this, but the interaction is underspecified. Deferral cap is left as “X%.”

**Section 4–7 remain high quality.** Named degraded modes, dual operational/evidence exit gates, non-waivable floors by product class, contemporaneous evidence packets, and invoice-close blocking for gated product are the right redesign. Indicators cover the required domains, including fairness and hidden post-close obligation. The management paragraph is decision-useful, though one long block rather than a tight statement.

Net: excellent process-mining reading and a credible recovery model, marked down for a blurred A–L taxonomy, a real C/traceability conflation, and repeated local overclaim. Not a 9.
