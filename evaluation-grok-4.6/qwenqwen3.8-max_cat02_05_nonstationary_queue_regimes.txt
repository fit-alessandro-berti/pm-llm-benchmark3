**7.4**

The final write-up is a strong process-mining analysis: it matches the required eight-heading format, keeps regimes operational rather than unique, refuses to treat recovery as steady capacity, and handles Little’s Law with the right caveats. It is not nearly flawless. Two published statistics are wrong, one of them is inconsistent with the accompanying code, and a few claims are less precise than the rest of the document.

## What is actually good

The estimands are defined carefully: opening-hours clock, arrivals vs completions, median/p90 as handling-time quantiles rather than mean flow time, snapshot WIP with unknown overnight coverage, and FTE as a capacity proxy. Assumptions are labeled. The threat list is real, especially carry-over, non-independence of daily rows, and the mismatch between `peak_08_10_pct` and the hourly vectors.

The primary segmentation is defensible and operationally useful: baseline weekday/weekend, outage window, post-outage recovery, high-mix weekday/weekend, and intervention/recovery. Recovery and the 11:00–14:00 outage are not folded into a “high throughput” regime. Capacity pressure is given as median/p90 planning proxies, not fake point utilizations. The alternative (pre/post mix-change around 20 Apr) is materially different and the comparison of strengths is honest.

Pooled-vs-segmented comparisons hit the required three distortions (day type, mix, outage/intervention). The sign reversal in `completed − arrivals` is the right Simpson-style point: the pooled −4/day looks like chronic shortage, but baseline is balanced and recovery/intervention are net-positive because they are burning backlog.

Little’s Law is the best section. Windows, units (opening-day λ × 12), throughput proxy, and boundary conditions are stated. Median service is not substituted for sojourn. Only baseline weekday/weekend are called `approximate only`; outage, recovery, high-mix growth, intervention drain, and the grand pool are `not defensible`. That is the correct conservative call.

The Python block meets the spec: in-memory tables, pooled/regime summaries, Fano and CV plus lag autocorrelation, a transparent single-split heuristic marked as non-truth, Little’s Law only on named windows, PM4Py confined to comments, and compact prints.

## Defects that block a high score

**Published arithmetic errors.** Post-mix `complex_pct` is given as 43.9 in both §3 and §4. The 14 post-change days sum to 606, so the mean is 43.3. The 43.9 figure is the pre+week-3 total (615) divided by 14. Small relative error, but it is a hard number in two summary tables.

**Burstiness table is wrong and self-contradictory.** Ordinary weekday completions are reported as CV 0.40 and Fano 1.31. The completion vector `[4,7,10,14,15,11,9,8,7,6,5,3]` has sum of squares 971, not 946 (the 5² term was dropped). Population Fano is about 1.56 and CV about 0.43. The code uses NumPy and would print the correct values, so the prose table does not match the “reproducible” output. For a quantitative process-mining answer that advertises reproducibility, that is a real consistency failure.

**Serial dependence is underspecified in the document.** Lag-1/lag-7 are described qualitatively and deferred to code. The section asked for analysis of the daily sequence, not a promise that a script will compute it. A reader who does not run the code never sees the dependence magnitudes.

**A few unclarities remain.** Apr 15 residual hours are bundled with 16–17 recovery in one row, which blurs an outage-damaged afternoon with a backlog-drain regime even though the intensities are split. WIP decline is written `43  36  32` with the separators missing. The peak-field vs hourly-vector conflict is noted mainly via 21 Apr; the same mismatch exists on the “ordinary” day (08–10 share 17% vs table 31%) and deserved the same explicit treatment. Apr 13–14 are called baseline while mix and WIP are already drifting; that choice is reasonable but not defended against a slightly earlier break.

## What was not a problem

Correcting the high-mix weekend net from a draft `+4` to `−6` in the final tables is not penalized. Using only `approximate only` / `not defensible` (never `applicable`) is conservative, not incomplete. Treating 16 Apr’s extra FTE as outage recovery rather than the 29 Apr intervention is right. Not forcing a single segmentation is right.

## Bottom line

This is competent, well-structured queueing analysis with the right regime story and the right Little’s Law discipline. Under a hypercritical standard it cannot sit in the 9–10 band: two final-table numbers are false, and one of them contradicts the code that is supposed to underwrite the paper.
