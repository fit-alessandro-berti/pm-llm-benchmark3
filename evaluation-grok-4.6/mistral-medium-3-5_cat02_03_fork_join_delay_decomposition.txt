The double-count write-up is directionally right but loose. The clean statement is: for every branch \(i\),

## Counterfactuals: the main conceptual failure

The task asked for next-branch limits, zero-benefit cases, and **whether F congestion masks branch gains**. The write-up treats “critical ⇒ full intervention size” and “not critical ⇒ zero,” which is the textbook mistake.

**A service −20% (50 → 40)**  
- F05 is not A-critical: **0**.  
- F01: B already finishes at 08:55; A can only give **5** minutes, not 10.  
- F11: B slack is 10, so **10** is feasible *at the join*.  
They also assert “downstream unchanged.” If `join_ready` moves, `final_start` need not; that is exactly masking.

**B queue −50%**  
Savings equal \(\min(\tfrac12 q_B,\; \text{join}-B_{\text{end}}+\tfrac12 q_B\text{ slack to the next end})\), not always \(\tfrac12 q_B\):

- F10: B moves from 11:20 to 11:10, A is still 11:15 → **5**, not 10.  
- F05: B moves to 09:57.5, A holds 10:00 → **10**, not 12.5; then F1 is occupied until 10:20 anyway → **flow-time saving 0**.  
- F03 does **not** flip to A (B still 09:35 vs A 09:30). Their “e.g. F03 becomes A-critical” example is false.

**Add F capacity / drop downstream to 0**  
They set “zero-benefit cases: none.” That only holds for a **fiat** deletion of `final_start − join_ready`. It does **not** hold for adding a server. F1/F2 are idle in front of several waits (F01, F02, F03, F07, …). Assignment is strictly alternating F1/F2, not an obvious pooled two-server queue. F04 is join-ready at 09:52 while F1 is free. Calling every gap “queueing that capacity removes” is unidentified, which they almost say in §7 and then ignore in §4–5.

## Redesigns and limits

B-queue reduction and F capacity are plausible headlines, but they rest on the wrong frequencies and on treating F delay as pure occupancy. The more evidence-based pilots—confirm whether F waits are policy vs contention; use C’s large sync slack (true mean ~30 min) rather than cutting A; watch near-ties F01/F10—are not recommended. §7 is the strongest section and still undersells what the F timeline already shows: several “downstream queues” are **not** two-server congestion.
