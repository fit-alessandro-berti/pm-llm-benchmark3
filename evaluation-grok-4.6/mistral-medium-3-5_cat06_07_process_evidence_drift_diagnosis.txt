**6.5**

The answer gets the main story right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing mix change without concept drift, August–September is overlapping process/concept/population change, and October is not a usable performance month. That is the core triangulation the task asks for. Under a strict reading, it is still well short of near-flawless.

**What is actually strong.** The six-section scaffold is complete. Section 2 correctly refuses to treat April / June / August–September / October as one failure. June is handled well: V2 rises, observed breach falls, AUROC stays roughly intact. October is not treated as a real improvement. Alert design correctly blocks “label-delay → retrain.” Section 6 names the right failure modes (immature labels, calibrating a pipeline bug, ignoring supplier-driven change, Partner Z “unfairness” without strata).

**Material diagnostic errors.**

1. April is listed as **process drift** because 18% of variants are unmapped. That is not a process change. It is the same rename/`UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` incident already assigned to pipeline failure. Treating corrupted activity labels as a new variant mix is a category error, and it is repeated in the table’s “months implicated.”

2. **Covariate drift is mis-specified.** A June jump in V2 share is process/mix shift. Partner Z is population shift, plus missing relevant attributes. Classic covariate drift is a change in \(P(X)\) for the model’s actual features. The table’s “High” confidence and “add rural/device-age features” action collapse three mechanisms into one and then prescribe a September fix for a June phenomenon.

3. **Label delay is scoped too narrowly.** Maturity is already 93% in August and 82% in September; Partner Z labels mature more slowly. Using September’s 0.31 as the maturity-curve target in Section 3 is circular. Calling June–September “safe” for training in Section 4 contradicts the same evidence. July is also stated as “100% mature” in the narrative; the table says 98%.

4. **Observed and hypothesized evidence are mixed.** The write-up says AUROC “drops sharply for V3; see Section 3,” but no variant-level AUROC is given. Section 3 only proposes a test. Stable V1/V2 *base rates* are then used as if they showed the model still works for those variants; they do not.

5. **The missingness test is conceptually wrong.** Rural distance and device age “were not present in the original model.” Higher Partner Z missingness on features that were never collected does not distinguish covariate drift from pipeline failure. That is one of the required tests, and the expected-result logic is false.

6. **Supplier delay is only half-analyzed.** At prediction time (immediately after diagnosis) realized `Order Part → Part Received` is future information. Calling for “supplier delay features” without insisting on *pre-prediction* proxies (rolling vendor KPI, not the case’s own later wait) risks leakage. July (65 h, V3 breach 0.46) is a concept/process onset in the narrative and missing from the concept-drift row.

**Internal contradictions.** The table’s concept-drift action is “retrain on post-Aug data,” which includes October and a contaminated September. Section 4 then freezes October and is more cautious. April–May are “unsafe unless recomputed” in one place and still mixed into process-drift months in another.

**Code is not a reliable audit sketch.** After mapping `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` to `None` and `dropna`, `unknown_activity_share` is identically zero — the data-quality signal is destroyed, then reported. `pm4py.get_variants` does not return a frame with a `variant` column you can `.value_counts()`. `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as a dict of `{edge: {"mean": ...}}`; typical returns are a tuple `(dfg, start, end)`. `filter_time_range` is used without a mode, so event-calendar vs case-start cohorts are not actually identified. The problem asked for different interpretations of those two cohorts; the code creates both frames and then mostly ignores the distinction. Duplicate handling is only timestamp-exact and never quantified. This is not inventing a fake drift detector, but it would not run as an auditable monthly extract.

**Smaller but real defects.** Broken numerals throughout (`0.460.64`, `Order PartPart Received`) make the audit artifact sloppy. May’s 20%→22% V3 move is over-read as covariate drift (April’s unmapped mass already distorts shares). Predicted-vs-observed is not used systematically, even though it is the cleanest triangulation in the table: April over-prediction with garbage prefixes; June 0.20 vs 0.15 mix miscalibration; August–September under-prediction as V3 breaks; October 0.23 vs 0.16 as classic immature/long-path censoring. October’s remaining labeled cases are also selected against V3 — that selection bias is barely developed.

**Net.** The answer would pass a professional review as a first draft of the right narrative. It would not pass as a finished audit: wrong bucket for April’s unmapped variants, confused drift taxonomy, an invalid missingness test, unsafe training months, and non-working process-evidence code. That is why it sits in the mid-6s, not the 8–10 band reserved for tight, internally consistent, evidence-clean work.
