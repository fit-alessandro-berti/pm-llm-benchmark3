The answer is well-structured and often sophisticated, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it contains several material inconsistencies, one serious resource-feasibility error, and multiple overclaims that a second-pass reviewer would catch.

**What it gets right.** The write-up follows the required skeleton, keeps mandatory control-flow and four-eyes constraints, and correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a time compression. It also distinguishes earlier *readiness* from earlier *completion* (especially K-411), treats queue snapshots as forecasts, and has a conceptually sound interference story: C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework tails, Assessment bunching, weekend external waits, and timestamped vs apparent concurrency. **Serious flaws.**

1. **K-104 invents C1 idle time that the snapshot forbids.** C1’s queue is 20h / 18h / 16h on Mon–Tue. The derivation still has C1 doing 2–4h of preliminary reading “in idle gaps” on Tue 06:00–15:10. A singleton with a 16h backlog does not have those gaps. Preliminary work can only occur by displacing other cases — the stated complication — not by quietly using spare capacity. Calling K-104 “timing-only” is also wrong: the intervention adds activities and a priority reservation.

2. **K-337’s clocks contradict each other.** Assessment 2 ends Fri 10:30. The same subsection assumes both a 5–7h C1 queue *and* Compliance Fri 11:00–16:00, with finish Fri 16:00. Those cannot be true together. Fri 10:30 + 5h wait is a ~15:30 start; 3–6h review lands Friday evening or later. The −4.0 to −4.5 day claim further assumes Friday-night or Saturday Decision/Notify. Factual Decisions are morning/afternoon manager slots (Wed 08:30 after Tue 19:30 compliance). A Monday Decision is the more coherent counterfactual and would cut the saving to roughly two days. That possibility is not in the range.

3. **Decision gating is under-modeled in K-219 as well.** Factual Decision is Wed 08:30 after a Tue 22:30 Assessment. A faster D2 that only pulls Assessment into Tuesday afternoon may still hit the same M1 morning batch. The no-rework “Tue 20:00–Wed 00:00” finish is an unstated calendar-hour assumption, so the −8 to −12h branch is inflated.

**Other deductions.**

- The 100-case C1 discussion has the right sign and the wrong magnitude: 100 provisional holds on one certified reviewer is not a move from 5–8h to 30–40h; it is a multi-week backlog. The qualitative interference point survives; the numbers do not.
- K-219’s “same decision if rework” is too strong. A different reader plus a clarification loop can change conditions or outcome, not just delay.
- Best-case K-104 (−10h, Wed 08:30) needs an honored jump of a saturated C1 queue on a day when two urgent compliance cases were added. That is not a nearby world.
