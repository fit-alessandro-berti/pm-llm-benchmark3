**What it gets right.** The ledger is dense and mostly well-sourced. The code path is coherent: process tree → Petri net, variant expansion, DFG, alignments, transparent normalization that does *not* delete `Manual Override`, optional sign-off candidate, and the EUR 50,000 caveat. Section 8’s six rules match the requested governance surface.

**Reliability column is mis-specified.** The answer redefines reliability as “how strongly an item supports the stated inference,” which duplicates the supports/weakens columns and is not what the schema asked for. That shows up concretely: E12’s memo is a reliable artifact with *weak* bearing on H5, yet it is scored 35. E4’s 70 is closer to true reliability (the percentages may be unreproducible). The scale therefore cannot be read uniformly.

**Posterior scores blur P(H) with P(H caused the drop).** H3=98 and H6=95 are defensible as “these events happened and they break raw fitness.” H1=80 is not defensible *as a cause of the June collapse* given the author’s own claim that most of the drop vanishes under recording repairs. Sign-off is a real later process/model issue; stuffing that into an 80 for “real behavioral change” inflates H1 relative to the narrative. The disclaimer that scores need not sum to 100 does not fix the mixed estimand.

**Over-weighting Assumption A1.** Declaring E16 reliability 100 is fair only as *arithmetic tension under a completeness assumption*. The case totals match, so omitted *cases* are unlikely; omitted *event types* inside listed cases, lifecycle expansion, or a different fitting definition (cost threshold vs language membership vs visible-move count) could still produce 93% vs 100% and 32% vs 0% without a corrupt report. The Apr–May line already hints at this: 93% fitness plus “Request Info irregularities” is ~40/600. That is an alternative (loop not encoded, or Request Info misplaced in the *full* log) the answer underplays while using 32%→89% as a 57-point “reported” effect in Section 3—the same numbers it says cannot be trusted.

**Causal graph is richer than it is clean.** Required nodes are present, and extras like `model_update_failure` and `event_completeness` are useful. Fine as abduction; slightly sloppy as the requested artifact.
