**6.3**

The answer is complete, on-format, and conceptually aligned with the hard parts of the rubric: it keeps four-eyes and final compliance, treats queues as forecasts, distinguishes readiness from completion, and correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several timing claims are unsupported and one path misstates legal concurrency.

**Format and constraints.** Sections 1–8 exist with the required headings, tables, and labeled paragraphs. The partial order and conditionals are mostly right. The intervention taxonomy (K-104 timing/scheduling, K-219 routing, K-411 orchestration, K-337 policy) is the strongest part of the paper. Missing from the feasibility model is a named place for Escalation Advice / S1, and there is no working-hours or calendar constraint even though the factual log contains overnight document work.

**Temporal consistency is the main failure.** No case gets a clocked counterfactual trace, so later ranges cannot be audited against queues. For K-104 the compressible suffix is only post-Assessment: D1 still faces the Monday 13 h queue and still finishes about Tue 05:40; A1 is still gated by that result. Only S1 overlap, C1 wait, and 1–3 h of C1 orientation can move. A 46 h cycle (notify ~Wed 06:10) and a “same-day final review after A1” (A1 ends Tue 19:10) contradict the Tuesday C1 backlog of ~16 h and the Monday addition of two urgent compliance cases. Getting C1 Tuesday night is preemption, not a free reservation. The stacked band 46–60 h, then 60–70 h, mixes this-case delay with harm to others.

**Process-feasibility slip on K-219.** Identity ends Mon 16:20; originals arrive 17:30. Service overlap of Identity and Document Check is impossible under the intervention. Writing `Identity Check || document work where possible` imports the no-originals pattern into an originals case. D2 start “as soon as originals arrive” is also underspecified: Monday 16:00 D2 wait is 5 h, and the write-up never says whether D2 works Monday evening, unlike the factual overnight D1 interval on K-104.

**Interference attribution is sloppy.** Displacement delays *other* cases first. Giving K-104 a 70 h treated-case outcome and K-411 a 29–36 h treated-case outcome “if earlier arrival displaces other work” reverses that causality unless a reservation is cancelled or the workaround throws an exception. K-411’s range also mixes cycle times (24–30, 29–36) with a delta (“near-zero change”) in one clause.

**What is actually good.** K-411’s base case—that A2 and overnight idle can absorb earlier dual-check readiness—is the right minimal-change reading. K-337’s path after Assessment 2 is legal: first rework loop is complete, C1 still follows the latest assessment, A1 ≠ C1, and the residual discrepancy is forced into approve-with-conditions or reject. The 100-case discussion is the right shape: C1 reservations do not create certified hours; D2 inherits D1’s bottleneck plus a heavy-tailed clarification risk; suppressed rework can reappear as monitoring, appeal, or loss; parallel release without a throttle becomes an A2 wave; timestamps can fake concurrency. The ranking (K-411 > K-219 > K-104 > K-337) matches robustness rather than raw speed. Pilots are usable.

**Residual nits.** M1’s calendar is never treated as a possible last gate (several factual decisions sit on the next morning). K-104’s table `I1 || D1` describes an allowed partial order, not the realized schedule the intervention would still inherit from D1. “Referral” is undefined as an outcome. Section 3 explains mechanisms in prose but still does not derive hours from snapshot waits plus unchanged prefixes.

The piece reads like a strong exam answer that understood the assignment and then approximated the clocks. That is well above a fail and well below near-flawless.
