The write-up hits every required section, keeps three (plus one) relations internally consistent, and does not treat local IDs as meaningful or invent events. The ACT / TYP / SEM assignments are mostly right: X1≅X2 under renaming, X5/X6 match X1 on labels but not on incidence or externality, X7 is label-equivalent to X1 but not on Dispatch E2O or qualifiers, and X8 is a reverse-logistics motif. Borderline pairs, robustness perturbations, and purpose-specific recommendations are coherent.

It is not close to flawless.

The preferred “reproducible” signature is not reproducible as written. `count_local_objects` increments per incidence, not per distinct object, and the comment admits as much. `Package` appears on both Pack and Dispatch, so `counts.get("Package", 0) > 1` is true for the **baseline**. Combined with `or`, `split_pkg` becomes true for X1/X2/X5/X6/X7 and the published table would not be produced by the published code. That is a first-class failure on the one artifact the prompt asked to be operational.

Related design sloppiness: the min-over-bijections wrapper does not change `motif_signature`, which already ignores identifiers. Graph-canonicalization theater is unused. Qualifier handling is selective without a crisp rule: `removed_before_dispatch` splits a cluster, while X4’s `partial_settles` / `completes_settlement` is demoted to a “subtype” and discarded. Grouping X3 with X4 is defensible, but then “business-semantic using multiplicities and O2O qualifiers” is only half-applied. Qualifier names in code (`repack_replaces`, `replaces_item`) do not match the prompt’s wording.

The PM4Py section is a plausible sketch, not an exact account of the key (activity sequence plus related-object information, typically types and/or IDs depending on version). Stubs (`...` for bijections and extraction) and a guessed `lead_object_type` / `max_objs` API keep that part from being executable.

Clusters and prose are strong; the signature that is supposed to make them checkable is not.
