**7.6**

The final 7-section write-up is complete, well structured, and largely faithful to the required format. It does not treat every baseline deviation as failure, does not equate hospital throughput with resilience, and correctly refuses to treat after-the-fact audit remediation as real-time compliance. Those are the hard constraints, and they are met.

The main weaknesses are precision and unused evidence, not conceptual confusion.

**What is strong.** Temporal framing in section 1 is coherent: T0 as a capable control environment, T2 as service-preserving control collapse, T3 as incomplete recovery, T4 as operational recovery with a hidden compliance tail. The resilience / shortcut / ambiguous split in section 3 is the right analytical cut, and it is tied to the right objects (skip-auth, dispatch-without-scan, phone POD, stale snapshot, reconstructed scans, audit tail). The A–L table covers every required column and usually lands on a defensible keep/modify/retire call. The recovery model in section 4 actually contains the mandated pieces: triggers, triage, minimum controls by risk, manual evidence, backlog rules, and a reconciliation/remediation flow. Indicators and recommendations are concrete and decision-usable. Section 7 states accept / do-not-accept / disconfirming evidence.

**Where it falls short under a strict reading.**

1. **DFG underuse.** The frequency graphs are cited more as scenery than as quantitative evidence. The answer does not compute the obvious path shares: skip-auth is ~1,600/5,100 of triaged work; temporary release covers only 1,650/3,600 packs, so a large share bypassed release entirely; dispatch-without-scan is 1,800+1,450, but only 2,600 get phone POD, leaving a residual custody/POD hole; backlog prioritization cancels 450; reconcile-manual-reservation cancels 300; stabilization compliance holds rise to 1,300 from a baseline 700; audit remediation is 2,300 in / 2,100 out, which is direct evidence the queue persisted. Those are the abductive facts. Leaving them uncalculated makes the interpretation thinner than the source material allows.

2. **Incomplete T2 temporal reporting.** Hospital medians/p90s are stated; Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk *medians* (54h / 96h) are not. That is a direct miss against “refer to the before/during/after metrics.”

3. **Recovery SLAs are invented, not anchored.** 30-minute triggers, P1 8–12h, 98% evidence, fitness ≥0.95, 2% exception caps, and fairness “2× cancellation” are plausible, but they are not derived from baseline p90s, observed exception multiples (trace ~12×, cold ~12×, dual-control ~14.5×), or the actual T2/T4 residuals. A redesign can propose numbers; a rigorous one should show why *these* numbers follow from *this* log.

4. **Classification noise.** L is simultaneously true resilience, a risky shortcut, and ambiguous. The dual nature is real, but listing it in all three buckets without a single governing rule (e.g., “resilience only as a time-boxed recovery stream; noncompliance if used to declare operational close”) is muddy. K’s label “Keep and accelerate” also fails the required keep/modify/retire vocabulary.

5. **T3 is thin.** No T3 DFG was given, so some inference is fair, but “hybrid manual/system” is asserted rather than abducted from the T3 metric pattern (trace 5.4%, rework 9.0%, p90 still ~2× on bulk). The stabilization DFG is not used to infer what T3 must have left unresolved.

6. **Small overclaims.** Hospital fill of 91% is treated as strong resilience evidence, but no baseline fill rate is provided—only the word “remained.” Fast-lane authorization bypass is stated more confidently than the DFG supports (fast lane → snapshot 920/1,000; skip-auth is a separate path). Spreadsheet invoice hold is named, then barely analyzed as a concealment mechanism.

This is a competent, usable advisory document with the right conceptual spine. It is not nearly flawless process-mining reasoning. The deductions are for missing quantification, unanchored redesign parameters, and a few classification/metric gaps—not for misunderstanding the case.
