**5.6**

The memo has the right skeleton and makes the three qualitative calls the prompt treats as table stakes (I-03 ineligible, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers priced). It is not decision-theoretically coherent, not internally consistent, and not careful enough with the numbers it asks the reader to trust. Under a strict reading that is a mid-pack answer, not a strong one.

**Decision rule is a midpoint machine dressed up as robust policy.** Eligibility by stage is correct. Everything else collapses uncertain ranges into single points: SME +0.065, Strategic Enterprise −0.085, effect midpoints, spillover 0.02, untreated value €20,000, hurdle Net EV > 0. That last step is a single-index threshold. It is better than a raw `p_now` cutoff, but it is not an uncertainty-aware rule. The prompt required robustification of known calibration problems; Public Sector and Non-Native-Language Support are left at raw `p_now`. Non-Native is the segment where both calibration *and* effect estimates are least reliable, so leaving it unadjusted is a miss, not a footnote.

`P_FalseAlarm = 1 − p_adj` is the wrong object. The observational score is not P(breach | no swarm), not P(breach | swarm), and not the probability that the alert’s reason is false. Subtracting `(1 − p_adj) × FA` therefore mixes a biased propensity with a cost that may be incurred whenever the swarm was unnecessary, including cases that would have closed on time *and* cases whose risk was real but not swarm-responsive. The prompt already warned that the prediction is not the interventional probability; the rule ignores that.

The spillover charge is the load-bearing parameter and it is invented. `6 × 0.02 × €20,000 = €2,400` is presented as if computed. The €20k figure is not the mean of the live book (~€29k), not the mean of live diagnosis-stage cases, and not tied to the two specialists’ actual interrupted work. No sensitivity is shown. I-01’s own midpoint EDB is ~€1,840 — it is excluded by a few hundred euros of an unvalidated externality. Change the average untreated value or use the low end of 0.01–0.03 and the feasible set changes. A rule that cannot survive that is not deployable.

**The allocation breaks the rule that supposedly produced it.**

- The rule says high-EV cases with >6h to SLA should usually wait. I-04 has **10h**, calibrated `p_adj ≈ 0.40`, false-alarm burden €2,000, and a Strategic Enterprise overprediction problem. It is nonetheless a hard intervene, justified with an unexplained “early swarm leverage” clause. I-08 (8h, same stage) is waited. The case with *more* option value is the one they lock in. That is the opposite of optionality.
- I-06 is labeled “Low EV.” Under the published formula it is not: midpoint EDB ≈ €2,780, Net EV ≈ +€380. Waiting because of imputation can be right; calling it low-EV is a calculation error.
- Repeated-alert language (“suppress unless new event or `p_adj` jump >0.15”) would also park I-02 (+0.08) and I-09 (+0.07), both second alerts with no new event and no prior swarm. Those two are selected for slots. Either the rule is not the rule, or “repeated” silently means “third refresh like I-05.” A deployable persistence policy cannot contain that ambiguity.
- Fairness section 5 invents a dedicated SME/Public Sector slot and then section 2 does not use it. I-07 is 1.5h from breach, has new evidence, and sits in the underpredicted SME segment; it is discarded solely by the €2,400 hurdle. Safeguards that do not bind on the live book are decoration.
- I-04 as a firm consume-now decision is a poor operational call even aside from inconsistency. After the mandated Strategic Enterprise down-adjustment this is a minority-probability, high-disruption, high-slack triage case. The adverse scenario later *assumes this is the case that gets zero effect*. Selecting it anyway, rather than waiting with I-08, shows the scenario analysis did not feed back into the action.

Reserving one slot is defensible. There is no release policy (when the reserve expires if nothing regulatory has appeared), no re-query rule for I-06 when the live queue field returns, and no account of whether two specialists can actually deliver three 45-minute swarms plus a possible fourth before 15:00 without wrecking the diagnosis queue they are supposed to protect.

**Expected-impact arithmetic is not audit-clean.** Base-case direct benefit `(0.15×25k)+(0.19×60k)+(0.24×20k)=€19,950` is fine. Optimistic “~0.73 breaches avoided” matches neither upper bounds (0.22+0.28+0.30=0.80) nor upper-half midpoints (~0.69). Costs are defined differently in each row: optimistic is labor only, base is labor plus *expected* false-alarm cost, adverse is labor plus I-04’s full false-alarm bill. Those are not comparable “intervention cost” cells. Adverse congestion is priced only as extra breach probability; the specified 15–30 minutes of aggregate delay is not costed. Non-monetary obligations (public-sector duty, regulatory arrival) do not appear in the net-impact interpretation at all, despite being required. “Solidly positive (+€9k)” in base next to “−€8.5k” in adverse, with only moderate confidence, is not paired with any robust selection rule (maximin, regret, fewer slots). The natural robust action — swarm the time-critical diagnosis-started cases and wait on I-04 — is not taken.

**Temporal policy is the strongest section and still thin.** The I-05 / I-07 contrast is correct and clearly written. Cool-down of 2h and escalation inside 4h are arbitrary constants with no link to SLA remaining, stage, or imputation flags. I-06’s missing-feature failure mode is not given a re-entry trigger. Maximum swarms per case is left as the prompt’s default.

**Fairness and monitoring are checklist-complete and only partly operational.** All five required risks are named and each has a concrete-sounding control. The controls are not used in today’s allocation (value cap / SME slot), are vague (“value cap”), or fight the capacity story (10% randomized denial of high-EV cases when four slots are already scarce; no ethics or power calculation). Inverse-probability weighting is the right family of idea; saying “IPW or a causal uplift model” is not a training protocol. The proposed above/below-threshold breach comparison treats a multi-factor EV-plus-wait-plus-suppression policy as if it were a single cutoff, and confuses treatment effects with label drift. Recalibration triggers (ATT < 0.10, delay > 15 minutes) are more point thresholds. There is no plan to refresh the 0.065 / 0.085 offsets, no logging of *why* the model fired, and no specialist-utilization metric even though interruption of two seniors is the physical spillover mechanism.

**Net.** Correct format, correct refusal of I-03, correct I-05/I-07 distinction, real attempt to price externalities. Fatal for a high score: the live portfolio does not obey the stated rule, the hurdle that eliminates half the book is an untested constant, optimistic counts do not reconcile, calibration/effect uncertainty is collapsed before choice rather than after, and the fairness/monitoring apparatus is not applied to the decision it is supposed to discipline. That is a 5–6 answer, not a 8–10.
