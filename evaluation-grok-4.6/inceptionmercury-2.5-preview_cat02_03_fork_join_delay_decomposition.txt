## Counterfactuals miss slack and masking

The prompt forbids equating service with influence and asks whether downstream congestion **masks** branch speedups. Both are largely ignored.

**A −20% service (10 min):**
- Applied to F01, F04, F08. F04 and F08 are **C-critical**; the A-critical cases are F01 and **F11**.
- Even on F01, B is only 5 min shorter than A, so a 10 min A cut saves **5 min**, not 10. Treating savings as \(0.2\times S_A\) per “affected” case is the exact fallacy the task warned about.
- “Secondary effects: None” is false: earlier \(J\) can be absorbed into \(D_c\) if F is not free.

**B queue −50%:**
- Includes F11, which is not B-critical.
- No slack check. F05’s next branch (A) is only 10 min behind a 12.5 min B-queue cut; F10’s slack is 5 min vs a 10 min cut. Naive 50% queue cuts overstate savings.
- “Reduced downstream queue if join moves earlier” can go the **other** way: if `final_start` is held by F1/F2 occupancy, \(D_c\) **rises** and flow time does not fall. F05 is the clear example (join 10:10, F1 busy until 10:20).

**Downstream capacity:**
- \(\sum D_c\) is **176 min**, not 203 (wrong \(D\) on F04/F08/F11).
- Zeroing every \(D_c\) with two resources is infeasible: F09/F10/F11 join-readies already overlap three ways around 11:30. Adding F3 is not “all 12 cases, full \(\sum D\)”.

§5’s two redesigns (cut B queue; add F capacity) are directionally reasonable but rest on these numbers. Throughput, tails, utilization, and new bottlenecks are asserted, not measured.
