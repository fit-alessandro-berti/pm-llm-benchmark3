**7.5**

The final write-up is a strong process-mining diagnosis: it reconstructs eligibility sets at decision times, does not treat every FIFO violation as priority, and usually keeps appointment, skill, urgency, and SPT observationally distinct. Format compliance is good, confidence scores are mostly calibrated, and the QA/QB/QD core stories are internally consistent.

The analysis is not nearly flawless. Several slips and one real coherence problem keep it well below a top score.

**Substantive issues**

- **QC “deadline/priority override” is timed inconsistently.** C05 is already infeasible at 10:18 (`est_min` 40, deadline 10:50). If SLA-endangered escalation were a rule, C05 should have been chosen at 10:18 or 10:32, not only at 10:54. The note that the deadline was “already at risk when selected” is used as support for an override, when it actually weakens that explanation. Calling the primary discipline an SPT–deadline hybrid therefore overstates a one-off late pick of C05.
- **Noisy/strategic estimates are treated as a live exception without checking the data.** Every job’s realized duration equals `est_min` exactly. Ranking estimate noise as a plausible alternative is unsupported and should have been discarded.
- **Required comparisons are incomplete.** The task asked for FIFO, priority, SPT, appointment, *and* skill-based predictions at each decision. QA/QB tables omit skill-based (even as “vacuous / all general”). QC replaces appointment/skill with EDF rather than reporting both.
- **A clear QD decision opportunity is missing from the table.** When D-X becomes available at 11:20, D03, D06, and D07 are eligible and the resource idles until the 11:30 appointment. That idle is stronger appointment evidence than starting D06 at 11:30 (where FIFO also picks D06). The write-up mentions the idle in prose but starts the D-X table at 11:30.
- **One SPT prediction is wrong.** At 10:14, SPT is uniquely B07 (`est_min` 6), not “B07 or B04.”
- **Preemption/resumption cost is assumed away**, not analyzed, despite being a required accounting item. Resume-remaining-time is reasonable, but it is never tested against a restart-cost or setup-cost alternative.
- **QA’s “strongest supporting episode” is muddled.** Serving A04 at 08:58 does not discriminate FIFO from priority or SPT. The sharp FIFO test is 08:32 (A02 over A04 p1 / shorter) and 09:05 (A05 over A08). The identifiability note that A03 “could look like SPT if A04 were missed” is hypothetical; in the reconstructed set A04 is present, so 08:20 uniquely favors appointment over SPT.

**What is done well**

Decision-set tables for QA, QB, and most of QD are careful, including A04 already waiting at 08:20 (so A03 is *not* SPT). QB’s mix of p1 preemption, resume-to-head, dispatcher reassignment, and later FIFO is handled with appropriately low confidence. QD skill constraints (D05 only D-F; D07 only D-X) and the D-F 11:36–12:00 hole are identified. Section 4’s two tests are well posed. Section 7’s claim that strict priority is the most dangerous label to infer from these traces is the right cross-queue warning.

**Net:** high competence and mostly faithful use of evidence, but not tight enough on QC mechanism timing, estimate diagnostics, required prediction columns, and a few local predictions to score higher under a strict bar.
