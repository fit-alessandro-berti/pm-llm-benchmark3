The write-up is well structured and mostly competent, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several required quantities are wrong or only loosely supported.

**What is solid.** Assumptions A1–A9 are explicit and usable. Missing-start bounds for P05/P07/P11/P15 are correctly constrained by the previous job on the same resource, queue entry, and nonnegative service. OPEN work is treated as elapsed/censored rather than completed. Waiting cases are kept. Completed-case sojourns (mean 145 min) and the hand-computed busy-time ranges are arithmetically right. The bottleneck story — S1 early, S2 late, C2 outage real but limited — is the right qualitative reading and is separated from hypotheses. The three imputation scenarios and the stable/unstable split are appropriate.

**Material errors.** The subperiod rate table does not add up. Counting P14 in both 08:00–10:00 and 10:00–12:00 yields 23 S1 arrivals against 22 cases. S2 completions in 10:00–12:00 are listed as 8 and include P10 (12:10) and P11 (12:25); the late bucket then drops those cases and reports 3 instead of about 5–6. S1 completions in 10:00–12:00 include P15 at 12:15. That is not a rounding-boundary quibble. It is a broken throughput reconstruction for a quantity the prompt asked for.

**Smaller but real defects.** Peak S2 \(L_q \approx 6\) is about one too high (central path peaks near 4–5; even late missing starts peak near 5). Peak S1 \(L_q\) of 8–9 is only reachable at the pessimistic edge, and 9 is not. System-wide WIP over time is never really built, only per-station peaks. Little’s Law mixes an in-window arrival rate with cohort sojourns that spill past 11:00, then compares that to in-window time-average \(L\); the 15% gap is mostly that mismatch. Markdown ate several ranges (“5110”, “585”), which makes the station table harder to trust. The C2 outage’s effect on P08 is closer to 25 min of extra wait than “~15 min” if C2 would have taken the job at 10:50.

**Code vs. prose.** The Python block hits most of the required PM4Py checklist and correctly refuses to treat `insert_case_*` as station metrics. It also prints `wip_s2` under the S1 peak heading and drops OPEN elapsed service from busy time, so the runnable utilization/WIP output does not match the (better) hand analysis.

The process-mining framing is good enough that the main conclusions would survive a corrected rate table. The errors are still too concrete, and too central to the requested metrics, for a high score.
