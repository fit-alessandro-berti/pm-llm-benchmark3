**2.8**

The write-up has the required seven headings and some process-mining vocabulary, but the quantitative core is largely false. Under a strict reading that is not a format-only pass.

**Data were misread, then treated as observations.** P05 is fully observed at S1 (`09:00–09:25`) with a **missing S2 start** and S2 end `10:15`. The answer inverts this: it treats S1 start as missing and S2 as a `10:15–10:15` zero-length job. That single error corrupts I1/C1 occupancy, P05 waits/services, and every downstream WIP/utilization claim that depends on C1 between 09:45 and 10:20. The Python table repeats the error and adds another: `s2_start` has 23 values for 22 cases, and P21 is given an S2 `OPEN` timestamp even though P21 is `OPEN_IN_S1` with blank S2 fields.

**S1 waiting times are systematically invented.** Reported S1 wait is `s1_start − arrival_s1`. That is 5 / 5 / 20 / 20 / **35** / **40** / (40–75) / **50** / **60** / **70** / **75** / **85** / **80** / **95** / (90–125) / **100** / **95** / **100** / **100** / **105** / **100** / 110 minutes. The table instead prints `[20, 30]` (or similar) for most later cases, including required rows P11, P16, P17, and P21. P11’s S1 wait is 75 minutes, not `[15, 25]`. P21’s S1 start is known (`13:30`); it is not an interval `[12:50, 13:30]`. P15’s S1 **finished at 12:15**; treating S1 service as 105–120 minutes of elapsed time to 14:00 confuses a completed S1 interval with the open S2 activity. That is exactly the service-vs-sojourn confusion the rubric forbids.

**Station sojourn is defined incorrectly.** `S1_sojourn = S1_svc + S2_wait + S2_svc` is case throughput after S1 starts, not S1 sojourn (`S1_wait + S1_svc`). S2 sojourn is never defined as `S2_wait + S2_svc`. Case-level total sojourn for completed cases is not actually tabulated.

**Subperiod rates are not computed from the clock.** Arrivals by `arrival_s1` are 13 / 8 / 1 in the three two-hour windows, not 7 / 4 / 11. The 11-arrival afternoon figure looks like a case-ID slice (P12–P22), not a time window. The bottleneck story that “arrivals exceed S1 completions after 12:00” is therefore unsupported: after 12:00 there is **one** external arrival (P22) and S1 is draining a morning backlog, while S2 still has four waiters plus two open jobs at 14:00.

**Utilization and WIP numbers are not reconcilable with the log.** I2 is busy for most of 08:00–10:00 (P02, P04, P06, and part of P08), on the order of ~80% of the window, not 42%. S2 service times are systematically longer (~40 min vs ~25–30), C2 is out 10:55–11:10, and end-of-window WIP sits mainly on S2. A defensible reading is a **time-varying mix with S2 structurally tighter**, not a clean late-morning flip to S1. Rounding is applied inconsistently (`[5, 10]` vs `[0, 5]` for two reported 5-minute gaps). Mean WIP values such as 2.1 and “rounding moves means by &lt;3%” are unjustified precision.

**Required Little’s-Law check is missing.** A7 only states when the law *might* apply. No interval is given with L, λ, W, and a comparison.

**Section 6 does not do the imputation work it describes.** Optimistic/central/pessimistic are named, not computed. Missing starts are not carried as competing feasible intervals through the metrics. The “stable” finding is the same unsupported S1-bottleneck claim.

**The Python block would not execute the required analysis.** Mixed `MISSING`/`OPEN`/datetime types make the wait/service subtractions invalid; the WIP predicate compares those sentinels to timestamps; `pm4py.insert_case_*` is called on an aggregated case table, not a valid event projection; `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is only mentioned in a comment (allowed) but the pandas substitute does not implement uncertainty scenarios; printed “summaries” group on columns that do not exist.

What remains is a correctly headed template plus a few locally right fragments (P01–P04 service order of magnitude, P22’s ~110 min S1 wait, P17’s 5 min elapsed S2, the idea of resource-bounded missing starts). That is not enough to offset inverted missingness, fabricated waits, wrong period counts, a reversed bottleneck, a no-op Little’s-Law item, and non-runnable code.
