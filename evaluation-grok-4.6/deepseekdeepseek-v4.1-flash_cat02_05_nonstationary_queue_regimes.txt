**7.1**

The final write-up is a strong, internally consistent regime analysis with the required structure, mostly correct arithmetic, and the right refusal to treat recovery or pooled averages as a steady queue. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is solid.** Estimands and units are defined correctly (12 opening hours, 720 FTE-minutes, arrivals vs completions as distinct intensities, median/p90 as handling time not flow time). The primary cut by weekday/weekend × operational phase, with outage and catch-up split out, matches the data: R1 is stable (~100 arrivals, WIP ~20, complex ~21%); 15 Apr is a capacity break (58 completions, 180 min outage); 16–17 Apr is backlog clearance (completions 120.5/day, WIP 52→35), not a new throughput regime; late weekdays show a mix/demand shift (complex ~45%, WIP ~44, median 35 / p90 83) with arrivals exceeding completions; the short post-29 Apr window is treated as low-confidence and confounded. Pooled vs segmented numbers check out (2519 arrivals, 2409 completions, weekday 108 vs weekend ~45, weighted complex 33.8% hiding 20.9% vs 44.6%). Hourly Fano/CV values are correct under population variance. Little’s Law is applied only as \(W=L/(\cdot/12)\) in hours, never by substituting median service, and pooled/outage/recovery windows are marked not defensible. The uniform-~5 FTE policy is a real pooled-vs-segmented reversal. The Python block is self-contained and hits the mandated checklist.

**Material misses.** The data-audit section never checks the hourly vectors against `peak_08_10_pct`. Those vectors reproduce daily totals, but 08:00–09:59 (bins 08–09 and 09–10) is ~17% / ~18% / ~10% / ~12% on the four representative days versus daily peaks of 31 / 32 / 47 / 22. That contradiction belongs in §1 and affects any claim that uses both sources. Little’s Law is only half-specified: if snapshots are opening-hours occupancy, \(L/\lambda\) is open-hours sojourn, not calendar flow time; overnight waits are excluded from \(L\) while cases still age. That boundary condition is required by the prompt and is left vague. Net backlog over the sample is +110 cases and is never stated.

**Smaller defects that still count.** Several weekend “regimes” have \(n=2\) and R8 vs R9 is a 21.5→20.5 WIP wiggle, too weak to carry an intervention story. \(U_{p90}\) is tabled like utilization (values >2) even though p90 is not a mean. Regime service is the mean of daily medians, not a case-weighted pooled median. Apr 29 is assigned entirely to intervention despite an unlogged start. Hourly Fano on 12 bins is reported without uncertainty. Lag-7 (the weekly cycle the lag-1 of ~0.4 is partly made of) is computed in code and omitted from the prose. Table cells are cramped (`U_med0.59`). `groupby.apply` for weighted complex mix is version-fragile.

Correct core queueing, honest non-stationarity, and complete format keep this well above a middling score. The audit hole, the open-hours vs calendar sojourn gap, and the small-sample over-segmentation keep it far from a high score under a strict bar.
