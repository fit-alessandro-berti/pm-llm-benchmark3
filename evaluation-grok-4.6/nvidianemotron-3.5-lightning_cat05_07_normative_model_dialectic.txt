**6.0**

The write-up is complete, follows the required headings, and gets most case-level authority calls right. It is not close to flawless. Process-mining semantics, the revised POWL, and several factual claims are wrong enough that a strict judge should not go much higher.

**What holds up.** Section 1 separates stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes and states a usable burden of proof. Expiry is treated as binding: SUP-705 and SUP-714 are correctly noncompliant; SUP-703 and SUP-708 are correctly severe. SUP-704 and SUP-702 are defensible as time-boxed exceptions. SUP-706/711 as data-quality, SUP-707 as a missing loop, and SUP-709/710 as scope gaps are the right dominant labels. SUP-712 is correctly noncompliant on CS-9’s human-identity rule even though it fits M0’s control flow. The matrix covers every case and the required columns. The layered split (POWL vs data predicates vs DECLARE-style overlays vs organizational rules vs scope SOPs) is the right architecture in prose. The data-collection list (stable `notification_id`, rule version, eligibility snapshot, aliases, delegation, overlay flag, immutable timestamps) matches the prompt.

**Dialectical requirement is only partly met.** The prosecution brief does not really concede M0’s measurement defects (forced quality-before-decision on every path, no loop, no resource/COI predicates, no reactivation/reject-lead scope). It treats those as “not a free pass,” which is argumentatively convenient and not the acknowledgment the task required. The defense brief does the opposite of what was required: it recasts SUP-703 and SUP-714 as communication/model problems and does not squarely admit genuine risk or the limit of informal practice. Using SUP-708 as an example of “check-order flexibility” is indefensible.

**Model-semantics errors.** Unordered nodes in a `StrictPartialOrder` already allow either order of Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review. Treating sequential observation of those two activities as a conflict with M0 is a basic partial-order mistake. M0 *does* require both checks before the XOR, including before Reject_Supplier; the scope discussion around SUP-709 never makes that point cleanly.

**Factual and authority errors.** SUP-715 is overclassified. The source says no rule prohibits the spousal relationship and that the COI policy requires *disclosure*. The answer invents a prohibition-without-waiver and treats the case like a CS-9 hard control-flow/resource breach. SUP-713 is called COMPLIANT despite mapping approval *after* the case. PD-3’s contested authority (monitored exception vs later “I did not weaken CS-9”) is parked in Section 1 and then ignored when SUP-702 is given high-confidence justification. The 6/15 = 40% “operational noncompliance rate” is invalid: the fifteen cases are a curated hard set, not a sample. “PD-7” and “2 business days daily monitoring” are sloppy. The overlay table sunsets CS-9 on 2026-05-31, which contradicts N1 and would erase the standard during the pilot.

**Section 7 fails a core requirement.** The revised POWL still puts `checks` (sanctions **and** quality) before the XOR, so it cannot replay the auto-approve or emergency routes the prompt required (quality after provisional activation). `emergency_branch` then requires Sanctions_Check a second time. `Request_More_Info` is an orphaned `LOOP` with one child, never attached to `M_revised`; in PM4Py a loop is a do/redo pair. `OperatorPOWL` is imported from `pm4py.objects.powl.operator` rather than `pm4py.objects.powl.obj`, so the block is unlikely to run. Silent transitions are not used to approximate deferred quality or optional inquiry. Comments admit POWL cannot enforce data rules, which is correct, but that does not excuse a control-flow skeleton that cannot express the three routes it claims to represent.

Net: a strong exam-style brief with a usable case matrix, undermined by a wrong concurrency reading, an invented COI rule, a statistically meaningless system rate, a CS-9 sunset that never existed, and a revised model that does not implement the behaviors it was required to implement.
