4.2

The write-up is structurally complete and conceptually pointed in the right direction (multi-criteria scoring, hard vs soft constraints, worker-facing language, audit metrics). Under a strict reading it is not a reliable policy design: the core assignment table misreads the skill matrix, violates stated constraints, and the “executable” parts would not actually run or audit the policy.

**Section 1 — adequate frame, underspecified policy.**  
Hard/soft separation is mostly right (Security/Approval rules, overtime, max-4, continuity). Missing as first-class rules: Quality batching (constraint 8), explicit supervised pairs, and any numeric weights or priority order. Without those the later scoring formula is not auditable.

**Section 2 — the algorithm does not implement the constraints it claims.**  
The required ingredients are named, but:
- Weights \(w_1\ldots w_5\) are never set, so the policy is not determined.
- Resilience/bottleneck avoidance is not a real step (no specialist-queue or single-point-of-failure check).
- Pseudocode uses `current_open_cases < 4`. The constraint is **≤4 new assignments in this batch**, not “fewer than 4 open cases.” That filter would wrongly exclude almost everyone.
- No supervision-pairing procedure, no Quality-batch rule, no urgency-first scan. Exception handling is a one-liner.

**Section 3 — several assignments are factually wrong.** This is the decisive failure.

R6_Farid is **Approval 3 / Negotiation 3 / Diagnosis 1**, owns C002 and C011, has 5 open cases, 6.5h, low overtime. The table inverts that profile.

| Case | Given assignment | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| C002 | R2_Bruno (“only competent Approval”) | False. R6 is the **current owner and skill-3 specialist**; R5 is also skill 2. R2 is high overtime, 12 cases, 4.0h. Urgent 2h work should stay with R6. |
| C011 | R5_Eva | R6 is owner **and** Negotiation 3 on a VIP urgent case with low handover. Breaking that continuity is unjustified. |
| C003 | R6_Farid (“skill match”) | R6 Diagnosis = **1**. A repeated diagnosis loop (complexity 0.66, 2 reassignments) is a poor trainee case. R3 is Diagnosis 3 with spare capacity. |
| C010 | R2_Bruno | Normal priority, 20h left. Constraint 4 forbids loading high-OT R2 when R5 (skill 2) or R6 (skill 3) exist. |
| C014 | R4_David | Normal, 25h, complexity 0.52, medium handover. Constraint 5 does **not** force continuity. Constraint 4 plus 14 cases / 3.0h / high OT says move it (R3 or R8, Rework 3). |
| C006/C012/C015 | marked as training | R10’s training priority is **security**, not Quality (and R10 is already Quality 3). R8’s priority is **approval**, not Security. C015’s note is “training for Eva/Jonas under supervision”; Eva/Jonas are Security **0**, so they are ineligible even supervised — that data conflict is never discussed. |

R1 (Diagnosis training priority, owns C004 and C009) and R9 get nothing, with no explanation. No supervised pair appears anywhere, despite the required table columns. A correct urgency-first pass would lock C002 and C011 to R6 before any diagnosis training work.

**Section 4 — comparison is generic.**  
The seven dimensions are the right ones and it correctly avoids a single optimum. It never **applies** first-available or specialist-only to C001–C016, so the comparison is not evidenced. Specialist-only would actually win on C002/C011/C001; first-available would pile onto R10/R6/R3. That work is missing.

**Section 5 — tone is fine, content is a template.**  
Non-punitive, but it does not explain *these* assignments. Given R1/R9 idle and R2/R4 overloaded, a worker-facing note needed concrete reasons.

**Section 6 — checklist coverage, sloppy mappings.**  
The indicator list matches the brief. Several PM4Py bindings are wrong: `filter_case_performance` filters case **duration**, not `sla_deadline`; a performance DFG has no `reassignment_count`; activity-based similarity is not a bottleneck metric.

**Section 7 — would not execute.**  
`from pm4py.objects.log import import_log` and `from pm4py.analysis import discover` are invalid. The filter/DFG snippets do not match those APIs. `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_batches`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_variants`, and SNA viz are barely or not used, despite being listed as relevant.

**Net:** a plausible essay about a scoring policy, not a correct, explainable, auditable assignment for this queue. The R6 inversion alone would send urgent Approval and VIP Negotiation to the wrong people and put a trainee on a diagnosis loop. That is a policy failure, not a polish issue.
