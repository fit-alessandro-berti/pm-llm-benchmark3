The answer is well-structured and hits the required format almost exactly: the eight headings, both fenced constraint blocks, one row per case, labeled derivation paragraphs, the two specified tables plus pilots, a synthesis in range, and eight labeled assumptions. It also gets the main conceptual points right: K-337 is a decision-policy change rather than a timing trick; four-eyes and the high-risk compliance gate are preserved; queue snapshots are treated as forecasts; earlier readiness is distinguished from earlier completion; and local speedups can migrate congestion.

Those strengths are undercut by three consistent feasibility/accounting problems.

**K-104 overcommits scarce C1.** Tuesday 08:30–09:30 preliminary work is written into the main counterfactual path even though C1’s queue is 16 h at 08:00, with urgent compliance work already added. Wednesday 10:00–13:00 likewise ignores an 8 h forecast. Displacement is mentioned as a side effect, but the path still treats reserved slots as if they appear on demand. A stricter minimal-change story would keep the S1 packet and a *provisional* later slot, and would put any C1 prep in the best-case branch only.

**The 100-case C1 analysis double-counts.** High-risk/high-value cases already consume final C1 time in the factual world. The incremental load is reservation/preemption plus optional preliminary minutes, not “preliminary screen plus final review” of 4–6 h, and “50 cases” is the wrong scale for a 100-case rollout of this case type. Net C1 hours could even fall if prep shortens the final review; the real nonlinearity is priority reordering of a singleton reviewer.

**K-411 conflates earlier queue entry with overlapping service.** Creating both tasks at Triage lets Document join D2’s queue around 09:35. With D2 at ~4 h at 08:00, a plausible start is ~12:00, after I2’s 10:00–11:50 interval—concurrent *waiting*, not concurrent *service*. Starting D2 at 11:00 is a quiet queue jump. The assessment-bottleneck discussion is good; the path times are still too optimistic, as is same-day M1 at 18:30.

Smaller issues: the Tuesday C1 screen sits *after* both checks, not during them; a 20% D2 clarification rate is asserted rather than scenario-tagged; overnight D2 work is defensible (D1 already worked overnight) but should be flagged. Uncertainty ranges, reverse-evidence paragraphs, ranking logic (K-411 > K-219 > K-104 > K-337), and the pilots are all credible. The answer is strong, not near-flawless.
