**6.4**

The write-up hits the required six-section form and the main quality traps: I-03 is refused on stage, I-05 is suppressed as a refresh with no new event, I-07 is re-opened because of the new log, allocation is not a `p_now` sort, and spillovers are treated as real scarce-capacity costs. That is a competent policy memo, not a ranking exercise. It is not close to flawless.

The decision rule is only partly coherent. Eligibility is gated on stage, material change, and a 1.0 h deadline floor, then ranking is almost entirely midpoint effect × value − cost − burden − haircut spillover. Segment calibration is applied to `p_now` “before any ranking,” but `p_now` barely enters the score, so SME under-prediction and Strategic over-prediction do little work on today’s slate. The 1.0 h floor also fights the surrounding text, which says a 1.5 h case is already too tight. Cool-down, 0.10 re-alert delta, 0.6 spillover haircut, and 4 h silence are operationally usable but presented as if derived rather than stipulated.

The live allocation is the weakest decision. I-04 is the right first slot. The third slot is not. I-02 is deployed despite a weak effect band (0.08–0.22), Strategic over-prediction, and a negative adverse net—the same robustness logic used to bench I-08, which has a higher base EV. I-06 (better effect band, acceptable Public Sector calibration, imputation as a *conditional* wait) and even a two-slot book are more consistent with the stated rule. I-09 is simultaneously a scarce-slot pick and the case assumed to get zero effect in the adverse row; that tension is noted, not resolved. Two specialists and sequential 45-minute interruptions are not translated into a daily throughput constraint beyond a copied 6×Δp spillover term.

The impact table is auditable and the base arithmetic for the chosen three cases checks out, but optimistic spillover is set at 0.005 per case-slot, below the problem’s 0.01–0.03 band. Untreated harm is valued at a free €15k average that largely determines whether nets are positive; there is almost no sensitivity on that price. Adverse is directionally right (one null effect, congestion) and correctly shows the book can lose money.

Sections 4–6 are stronger than the portfolio. The I-05 / I-07 contrast is explicit and correct. Fairness items requested are all present, with concrete controls (floor slot, calibration cards, spillover ledger, holdout). The 15% randomized holdout, however, is incompatible with “four slots, reserve one”: withholding a high-value swarm for learning is not costed. Several triggers (ECE 0.05, PSI 0.2, 20% effect shift) are generic.

Net: decision-theoretic in intent, operationally specific, and right on the three knockout tests, but the allocation does not follow its own robustness rule, calibration is mostly decorative, and at least one scenario input contradicts the brief. That caps the grade well below the top of the scale.
