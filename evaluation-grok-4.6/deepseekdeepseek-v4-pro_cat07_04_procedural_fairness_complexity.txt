**6.8**

The final write-up follows the required heading structure, covers every mandated dimension, and mostly obeys the hard constraints (raw rates ≠ discrimination; case mix ≠ automatic justification; risk scores not treated as neutral; support steps treated as a source of burden; no proposal to remove rights-preserving steps). Sections 1–6 and 8 are substantively competent. The score is still well below the “nearly flawless” band because of concrete errors, overreach, and incomplete inference control.

**What holds up.** Adjusted vs raw outcomes are distinguished cleanly. The residual gaps (B −5.5 pp / +2.6 days; C −2.2 pp / +4.1 days; extra supervisor and missing-document routing) are correctly treated as *unexplained by the listed controls*, not as proof of animus. Resource concentration (7 bilingual staff; 5 accommodation specialists who also do supervisor review) and the 3.8d / 4.7d support-step queues are the right focal disparities. Mechanisms mix legitimate fraud routing with capacity, handover, and covariate-bias stories. Mitigations all include trade-offs. The bottom line is only mildly overstated relative to the evidence.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Section 7 is not a working PM4Py plan.** `pm4py.get_variants` returns a variant→traces mapping, not a frame you can `.groupby(['applicant_group','variant'])`. `get_all_case_durations` returns a list of floats, not a mergeable table. No `variant` column is ever written, yet later code filters `subset['variant']=='V1'`. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with a single timestamp; the log has only `time:timestamp`, so service/wait splits are not identified, and `@@service_waiting_time` is not a reliable column name. `discover_dfg` is unpacked as if it returned markings. Several functions the prompt named (`filter_variants`, `filter_case_performance`, `filter_paths_performance`, `get_service_time`) are unused. As “code or pseudocode showing how to use” those APIs, this section fails.

2. **Over-interpretation of tables.** The claim that B/C “likely” wait longer *inside* V2 is not in the data (no group×variant durations). Calling the residual delay “irreducible” is too strong: the preliminary model did not control language preference or accommodation, so the residual *is* partly those steps, not a leftover after they are accounted for. That point is made well in §2 and then blurred in §3.

3. **“What can and cannot be inferred” is never stated as a discipline.** Limitations are listed, but the text still slides from “capacity bottleneck is observed” to “does not currently meet reasonable standards of procedural fairness.” The evidence supports *structural delay and unequal burden* for B/C; it does not support a global fairness verdict, intent, or a calibrated claim about decision quality. “Probable unfairness” plus that closing sentence is stronger than §5’s own caveats allow.

4. **Smaller analytic gaps.** No explicit design for comparing *within-variant* approval or appeal rates (only cycle time is discussed). Appeal/rework is treated as burden without separating overturn-worthy error from access-to-appeal. Matching advice is generic and does not address post-treatment bias if documentation completeness is itself affected by language. Formatting (missing hyphens throughout) is sloppy but secondary.

Strong policy read, weak process-mining execution, and a bottom line a notch firmer than the identification strategy. That is not a high score under a strict rubric.
