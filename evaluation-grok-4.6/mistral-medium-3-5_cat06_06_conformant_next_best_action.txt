The answer has the right skeleton—six required sections, A/B ranked last, conformance labels used correctly, and a preference for a second-officer path—but it is not an expert operational recommendation. Several errors sit in the primary plan and in the process model itself. Under a strict reading those are not nits.

**Primary plan is operationally incoherent.** Rank 1 is labeled “D + C (parallel)” but is not parallel C. It waits for S4, then interrupts P-901 only if S4 is late by >45 minutes. That fallback ignores the resource state. P-901’s deadline is **70 minutes from now**; the +0.12–0.28 spillover was estimated for interrupting **now** for ~45 minutes. At t=45, P-901 has ~25 minutes of slack. Starting a 35–75 minute S2 review then makes a P-901 breach nearly certain, not a modest probability bump. The same delay also pushes P-884’s review to t≈80–120 against a 90-minute deadline, so the plan can miss **both** deadlines and still impose the spillover it was trying to avoid. An operational-decision answer that puts this in Rank 1 is not high-quality.

**Deadline benefit is over-credited.** Ranks 1–3 all list €20k–€35k deadline benefit as if it were in range. S4 arrives in 35–65 minutes; a second-officer review, if comparable to S2, is another 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute. Waiting for S4 then reviewing is often 70–140+ minutes. Treating that as capturing the on-time execution value is unsupported. Rank 2 (C now) is actually the only second-review path with a realistic shot at the 90-minute window. Ranking a slower wait-first plan above immediate S2 would be defensible as P-901 protection—if the timing arithmetic were honest. It is not.

**Material process error in the top continuation.** After an unresolved fuzzy match, the tree says “Mandatory hold/SAR if risk ≥70 → Reject or SAR (no execution).” That is false. Risk ≥70 or an unresolved fuzzy match **requires Second Officer Review** before approve/execute. After that review the case may be approved, rejected, or held while a SAR is filed. SAR is not mandatory, and a SAR does not bar a later conformant approval. This error is in the Rank 1 trace, so it distorts the recommended policy.

**Section 5 contradicts the table.** If S4 is available in 35 minutes, the answer says D+wait “becomes more attractive (Rank 2).” That leaves C on top even when waiting avoids P-901 harm and still has some chance of finishing. If the 35-minute world makes waiting better, waiting should **overtake** C, not stop at Rank 2. The 35 vs 65 sensitivity analysis is exactly what the prompt asked for, and it is internally inconsistent.

**Better composite is missing.** The prompt explicitly allows composites. The natural high-value plan is **D in parallel with immediate C**: start the scarce-but-available rescreen now and put S2 on the file now, so new screening results arrive mid-review without delaying four-eyes. That dominates Rank 2 (C alone) on information and dominates Rank 1 on P-884 time. It is never ranked.

**F over E is poorly justified.** F is only `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`; the prompt does not establish that the rejection threshold is met. E is `CONFORMANT NOW`. Observational P(approval) is 0.55–0.75, so F is more likely a false rejection than a true one. E burns the deadline and is a prolonged hold, which is costly, but it preserves option value and stays inside the process. Ranking uncertain-conformance rejection above a legal hold needs an explicit dominance argument. Section 3 never supplies one.

**Section 3 misstates the tradeoff.** It says conformant paths “trade off deadline risk against regulatory risk.” Conformant second-review paths have low unlawful-execution exposure. Their real tradeoff is P-884 deadline loss vs false-reject/hold cost vs P-901 spillover. Regulatory dominance is the right argument **against A/B**, and that part is fine. Using it as the conformant comparison blurs the actual decision.

**Several required distinctions are thin or absent.**
