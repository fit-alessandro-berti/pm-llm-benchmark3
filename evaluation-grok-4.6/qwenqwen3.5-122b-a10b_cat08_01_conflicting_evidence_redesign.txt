**6.0**

The write-up is complete, on-format, and clearly better than a generic Lean list: it cites frequency/performance DFG edges, fitness vs. share of fitting traces, R1/R2, the 14.1% vs. 3.3% reopen split, the 31%/9% cost inversion, the 26% evidence-repeat loop, the 140h unclear-defect tail, reviewer utilization, and replacement-error costs. Trade-offs and a staged pilot with downstream-shift checks are present. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Diagnosis is serviceable but shallow as process-mining reasoning.** Fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces is quoted, then barely used. Alignment moves (fast approval before evidence completeness; dispatch of high-value devices without risk review; complaint after close) are named, not turned into a variant or rule-by-rule diagnosis. R3 is effectively ignored, as are model precision, the temporal-profile finding that Manual eligibility → Repair quote is already too slow for Premium, the 350 Fast approval → close-without-dispatch cases, underused quality inspectors, and the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate. “890 looping back from evidence requests” is wrong: that edge is mostly first entry to manual after an evidence request, not a rework loop. Calling 1,400 intake→manual cases “forced” overstates what the DFG shows.

**The three redesigns are not a synthesis; they are a paraphrase of the three implementation options the prompt already offered** (rule update, evidence portal, pre-dispatch gate). They are mechanically different, so they meet the letter of “materially different,” but they fail the spirit of non-generic redesign. Stronger answers would have built segment×defect architectures (replace-first vs. repair-first, Monday fraud capacity, inspector reallocation, status-visibility for Standard, R3-compliant reopen handling).

**Redesign A contains a material policy error.** Route 2 sends *all* Premium cases off the fast path, including Premium low-risk accessories (18% of volume, 8h vs. 46h, service-credit exposure above 24h). R2 *excepts* that segment from evidence-before-dispatch. This violates the explicit instruction not to treat the slow path as generally better. The same section both claims it will *reduce* manual inflow by filtering low-risk cases and warns that a rule update *increases* manual inflow 15–35%—the prompt’s own warning. Whether high-value cases still receive Fast approval *after* review (what R1 actually requires) or “bypass Fast approval” entirely is stated both ways. Dumping high-value volume onto two fraud specialists is not sized.

**Redesign C misstates compliance.** A part-compatibility gate does not satisfy R2 (evidence completeness) and does not fix missing risk review. Invented cycle times (~32h, ~60h, ~20h) are presented with more precision than the log supports.

**The table and experiments are adequate, not tight.** “Dominated? N/A” for the as-is process is a dodge. Conformance / % fitting traces / R1–R3 never appear as go/no-go metrics despite being the diagnostic core. Service-credit exposure for Premium accessories is unmonitored—the exact harm Redesign A would create.

Net: a competent executive memo that uses the artifacts, then spends them on prompt-echo designs with one serious segment error, one compliance non sequitur, and several internal contradictions. That is a mid score, not a high one.
