**4.5**

The diagnosis of the main waiver-join defect is mostly right; the repair package, which is a core requirement, is not.

**What is actually correct.** The net is a workflow net and is not sound. `t_join_waived` is a one-input silent step, not a synchronization, so the waiver branch can reach `p_decision` while the analysis token is still in `p_analysis`. The required waiver sequence `t_register → t_split → t_waive_review → t_join_waived → t_authorize` is legal, and the marking table correctly shows both branch tokens after the split, `t_authorize` enabled together with `t_analyze`, and `sink:1` with a leftover that is **not** `fm`. Repair B (add `p_analysis_done → t_join_waived`) is the right one-arc fix, and the Section 5 net implements that edit. The write-up also correctly refuses to treat soundness as proof of policy fit.

**Fatal repair errors.** The task required **at least two valid, behaviorally distinct repairs**. Only B is valid.

- **Repair A** adds `p_waived` as a **third AND-input** to `t_join_normal` while leaving `p_review_done → t_join_normal` in place. Standard review never marks `p_waived`; waiver never marks `p_review_done`. Both completions deadlock. The removed preset of `t_join_waived` is also left with its output to `p_decision`, so the silent join becomes an unguarded token source. Calling this “aligns with intent / no new risk” is false.
- **Repair C** does not make analysis optional. It strips `p_analysis_done` from `t_join_normal` and AND-joins the two exclusive review outcomes. Both paths deadlock and the analysis token is stranded. The policy-change story describes a different net than the listed edits.

That is not a near-miss on minimality. It is XOR/AND confusion on the two alternatives that were supposed to demonstrate distinct repair families. The fallback in Section 8 is therefore a broken net. The cost model is stated, but comparing costs of non-repairs is empty.

**Marking argument is incomplete.** The table never shows the equally important proper-completion failure: analyze **then** waive, then join and authorize, leaving `p_analysis_done:1, sink:1`. Soundness already fails when the mandatory work **is** done. After the given counterexample, `t_analyze` is still enabled; the leftover can move, but it still cannot be consumed into `fm` because `t_join_normal` waits forever for `p_review_done`. That consumption argument was required and is only gestured at. The Executive Verdict’s extra “deadlock on the standard-review path” is invented: `p_review` is an XOR, and `t_join_normal` is a correct AND-join for that branch.

**Section 6 would not run.** `pm4py.get_enabled_transitions` returns `Transition` objects, not names, so `transition_name in enabled` is always false. `net.transitions` is a set, not a name-keyed map. The helper does not meet “check enabledness rather than silently moving tokens” in any executable sense. The required API calls are present and the qualitative soundness contrast is reasonable; the operational half of the section is not.

**Smaller defects that still count under a strict bar.** Section 2 marks the analysis branch as symptom-free even though that token is exactly what remains after premature authorization. Silent joins are discussed, but not that `t_join_waived` is not a join at all. Positive regression traces omit the analyze-then-waive leftover. Trade-offs for A/C describe imaginary behavior.

**Net.** This is a competent identification of one defect plus one working patch, wrapped in the right headings. It is not a successful marking-and-repair solution: two of three repairs are ill-formed, the second required valid repair is missing, the fallback is unsafe, and the firing helper is incorrect. That is well below near-flawless.
