**Why it is not near-flawless.**

1. **Python spec is only partly met.** The code computes per-case CF columns but prints only aggregates, so it does not “summarize per-case and aggregate time savings.” `discover_performance_dfg` returns dicts; `perf_dfg.head()` will throw and be swallowed by `except`. PM4Py is therefore decorative, not a working performance check.

2. **Section 4 is thinner than the task asked.** CF2/CF3 never get a full per-case savings table in the prose (only case lists and totals). CF3 is a blunt 50% cut of `downstream_queue`, not a capacity/schedule model of F1/F2, even though F11 masking is used elsewhere. Secondary effects are sketched, not quantified.

3. **Small unclarities.** CF2 uses 12.5-minute times without noting the 1-minute clock. The CF1 assumption cell is garbled (`A service 50  40`). The double-count identity writes \(3\cdot join\_ready - \sum E_i\) as if clock times were already minute offsets. A sharper fact is left implicit: A’s service exceeds B’s in all 12 cases (50 vs max 45), which is the cleanest evidence that service ≠ influence.

Correct, coherent, and competent, but not tight enough on the required code/output contract or counterfactual tabulation to score in the high 8s–10s under a strict bar.
