**8.0**

The submission is a complete, well-structured governance review that hits every required heading, reasons from concrete traces, separates exploration from enforcement, and proposes a genuinely better third design. It is not nearly flawless. Several technical and semantic slips, plus incomplete enforcement code, keep it well below a top score under a strict reading.

**What is strong.** Use-case scoping is clear and correctly refuses to average the metrics. The Model S / Model C analyses are mostly accurate: S’s `+(X(a, tau), …)` block really does optionalize Credit/Technical and lets Expedite precede them; C really is an 8-trace allow-list that rejects B2, B5, and B6. The B1–B8 matrix is correct on exact language acceptance, and the exact-vs-alignment distinction is stated. Pathologies are ranked by decision impact rather than treated as a scoreboard. The third backbone (`MAND · CORR* · POST · APPR`) is safer than S and more maintainable than C. Validation design (seeded split, negative suite, perturbations, cost sensitivity, reject-despite-good-aggregates) is the right shape. The dissent is a real allow-list argument, not a token objection, and sits in the 150–250 word band.

**Material defects.**

1. **Token-based generalization is mischaracterized.** TBR generalization is about how often model parts are used in replay, not a direct reward for permissiveness. Unused enumerated branches explain C’s 0.412; calling the metric “generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness” conflates it with precision/fitness. That is a real conceptual error in the metric-pathology section.

2. **P6 is not actually enforced.** The tree allows C-only / T-only / both after every `Correct Dossier`, but `affected_check` handling is a `pass` stub. Semantics are described in prose; the required external check is not implemented. Combined with a structurally optional Supplier block, the “hybrid” is a backbone plus a partial policy layer, not a closed enforcement artifact. Dual status (“accepts, but uncertain”) for B6 is muddy for a governance board.

3. **Third-model restrictions are larger than advertised.** Correction is frozen before Supplier/Expedite; every correction must recheck at least one mandatory activity; Auto Approval after correction is allowed without argument. These are acknowledged, but they are policy choices, not forced by the log. A POWL or attribute-conditioned family would have addressed P3/P4/P6 with less residual optionality. Duplicate C/T labels remain, so alignments can still map rework ambiguously.

4. **Validation code is partly theatrical.** Alignment-cost “profiles” are name-only dicts that would not change PM4Py costs. `parameters=profile` is a no-op. Several helpers are sketches. That is acceptable as a program design, but it overclaims “self-contained” executability next to `model_third`.

5. **Smaller nits.** Informal 1–5 comprehension is treated skeptically and then reused as if it still informs retirement. Runtime and duplicate-label rows are fine, but the undocumented “33” is discussed at more length than it can support. Section 8 repeats the probe matrix instead of adding much new discriminatory behavior beyond A1–A4 (A2 is the one that earns its keep).

The piece would be in the high 9s if generalization were stated correctly, P6 were executable, and the third model did not lean on optional Supplier plus an unfinished checker. As written it is a strong board memo with a few wrong or unfinished parts, not a near-flawless review.
