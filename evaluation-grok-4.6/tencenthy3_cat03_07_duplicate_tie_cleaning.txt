**6.4**

The write-up is complete, on-template, and conceptually on the right side of the hard constraints (do not drop e05/e14, do not treat equal activity+timestamp as a merge key, do not order by ingestion, keep original timestamps, do not treat a total order as physical truth). The decision tree, pair table, and failure-mode list are the strongest parts. It is not close to flawless.

**Serious inconsistency (ordering).** Section 3 states a semantic policy: both picks before pack, e06 before e07, e08 < e09 < e10, invoice then authorize then concurrent captures then summary. The `sort_key` in §6 does not implement that. For the 10:00 group every eid falls through to `rules.get(..., 0)`, so the tie-break is `source_seq`. That yields **e07 (ERP 30) → e03 (WMS 100) → e06 (WMS 110) → e05 (scanner 552)**: pack summary before the I1 pick, and the I2 pick after pack. That is exactly the “false story” the same answer warns against, and `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` would then bake it into durations. Domain ranks are only wired for the 10:15 and 12:00 clusters, and even there e11 is omitted (invoice vs authorize is left to `source_seq`/eid). Prose and code disagree on the main deliverable of §3–§6.

**Conservative pipeline is slightly too eager.** e03/e04 and e11/e16 are reasonable *candidates* to merge, but dropping the scanner and billing *rows* in the “minimum-change” path is not the most conservative reading of facts 1 and 6. Each source remains authoritative for different attributes; collapsing them into a WMS- or ERP-primary event removes those sources from activity/ET–OT counts even if prefixes are kept. The table allows “or keep both linked” only in passing; §4 then commits to 15 events as if that count were settled. e06/e07 is handled more carefully than e11/e16, without a clear reason.

**Implementation quality is below the spec.** `custom_merge_verified_pairs` does not implement the stated relation-union or the §8 provenance schema; it only copies some secondary columns and deletes rows. The pre-check `assert ... payload_hash ... != ...` compares NumPy arrays and can raise `ValueError`. Timestamps are assumed to be datetimes (`.timestamp()`) although the log is given as strings. Evidence-based merging is a required comparison dataset in §5/§7 but is never built as a third deep copy. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is only shown on a synthetic extra e01, which satisfies the letter of “justified scenario” and avoids abusing the API on the real log, but it does not exercise any candidate group that actually exists.

**Smaller inaccuracies.** E2O arithmetic treats e06 as five relations; the table lists four (O1, I1, I2, P1), so raw ≈44 not ~45 and conservative ≈39 not ~40. Harmonization is terminated as “retain both unchanged (with label refinement),” which is contradictory. “Order-DBG” is a typo. “Six” patterns are then seven. `have_common_object=True` is discussed correctly for e13/e14 (shared O1/V1 is not identity of M1 vs M2), which is one of the few places the answer is both precise and aligned with the prompt.

**What remains solid.** Categories in §1 match the required distinctions. The pair table covers every mandated pair, refuses to merge e05 and e13/e14, and does not add Payment objects onto e15. §3’s semantic-vs-deterministic split and the microsecond warning are right. §5’s two aggressive scenarios and the damage discussion are directionally correct. §7 names concrete edges whose meaning actually flips (I2 pick, packing loop, false dispatch loop, doubled invoice, collapsed partial payments). §8–§9 hit the required schema fields and the eight named failure modes.

Net: a competent OCEL-cleaning design with good conservatism in the *narrative*, undermined by a sort key that violates its own rules, an over-aggressive “conservative” row-drop, a helper that does not match the merge/provenance policy, and several countable nits. That is well above a failing answer and well short of a high score under a strict rubric.
