**6.4**

The answer is format-complete and conceptually aligned with the intended rubric (policy vs timing, readiness vs completion, four-eyes, queue forecasts, interference). Under strict scoring that is not enough. Several quantitative claims are internally inconsistent, a few feasibility constraints are waved through, and the 100-case analysis never does the capacity arithmetic it pretends to do.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 correctly separates K-337 as a decision-policy change and K-219 as a routing change, rather than treating every intervention as “do the same path faster.” The K-104 and K-411 derivations state the right mechanism: upstream earliness is absorbed unless the binding downstream queue actually yields. Four-eyes and “final compliance after latest assessment” are preserved. The robustness ranking is defensible because it does not rank by cycle time alone. The pilot table has the required rows and a real stopping rule.

**Material timing errors.**

- K-337’s headline saving is undercounted. After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) the factual remainder to Notify (next Wed 09:10) is ~118 h, not 65–90 h. “Decision Fri 15:00 vs Wed 09:00” is ~114 h, yet the same cell claims a max of 90 h. The derivation only adds RMI2 (~70.5 h) plus Doc3/Assess3 service (~6 h) and forgets the Mon–Wed idle gaps (Doc3 wait, overnight before Assess3, C1 wait, overnight before M1). Order of magnitude survives; the reported range does not.
- K-411 contradicts itself. The table and best-case paragraph cap the gain at 0–4 h / Notify Wed 10:30, including a case that “catches [a] Tue evening Assessment gap.” If that slot exists, Assessment can finish Tuesday evening and the saving is an overnight (~12–18 h), which the reverse-evidence paragraph itself admits (~15 h). The published range is therefore too narrow on the upside and inconsistent with the author’s own scenario.
- K-219’s −16 h best case (Notify Tue 17:00) assumes M1 will sign the same afternoon. Factual K-219 and K-337 decisions sit on the next morning after late-day assessment; K-104 is the exception because compliance ended at 17:40. If M1 is morning-loaded, a much earlier Document/Assessment still Notify-s Wednesday and the gain collapses. That fork is not in the outcome range.
- K-104’s best case (C1 Tue evening, Notify Wed 08:00, −11 h) is not reconciled with Monday’s 20 h C1 queue, Tuesday’s 16 h queue, or the rule that final review cannot start before Assessment ends Tue 19:10. Immediate post-assessment C1 would actually point to a Tuesday-night finish (larger saving) *or* no movement if the backlog is real. The stated −3 to −11 h band occupies neither pole.

**Feasibility gaps.**

- Preliminary C1 work during Identity/Document on Monday is treated as available capacity. C1 is a single certified reviewer with a 20 h Monday queue and two urgent cases added by 16:00. Prelim hours are not free; they are preemption. The answer lists displacement as a side effect but still times the treated case as if the pre-read occurs in parallel and then shortens the final review by 1–3 h. That is not a minimal-change, resource-feasible path; it is a hidden priority change.
- Partial order dumps optional/case-specific nodes (`Escalation Advice`, `Compliance-prelim`) into the general model as if they always sit between Triage and final compliance. `Assessment < {RMI | CR | Decision}` is not a partial order. The accompanying constraint list is better than the relation set; they do not match.

**Capacity section is qualitatively right and quantitatively loose.** Bottleneck migration, D1/D2 substitution, weekend lumpiness, and false concurrency are the right phenomena. Then the rollout math breaks. One C1 cannot expend “100–200 h” of prelim time in a week; two document clerks cannot absorb 100 extra D2 jobs at 4–8 h each. The text says “exhaust C1” and “floods D2 (2-5h 15h+)” — the second phrase is garbled, and neither is turned into a hard infeasibility (≈40–50 C1 hours/week vs hundreds of demanded hours). “100 similar cases in one week” is not a slightly worse queue; it is a different operating regime. That is exactly the interference test, and it is only half done. Section 4 also looks slightly over the 250–400 word cap.

**Calibration and invented precision.** Queue snapshots are correctly called forecasts, but several numbers are presented with fake resolution: K-337 60–75% conditional / 25–40% reject; K-219 25–40% clarification; ±50% queue error; 85% C1 utilization threshold. These should be unlabeled scenarios, not implied frequencies. Assumption 2’s ±50% is arbitrary. Assumption 1 (prelim saves 1–3 h without anchoring) is doing a lot of work for K-104’s benefit estimate and is not stress-tested in the base case.

**Lesser issues.** “D1 queue 13h lower” reads as if 13 h were the reduction rather than the Monday snapshot. S1 Monday availability is assumed, not checked. Overnight D2 work is inferred from D1’s night interval; that resource-specific transfer is never flagged. K-104 still places a full Escalation node in the CF path even though the intervention already moved S1 to Triage; a short reaffirmation would be the minimal change.

**Net.** The skeleton of a good counterfactual write-up is here, and the dangerous intervention is correctly identified as K-337 rather than the slow one. Strictly, though, the piece repeatedly fails the standard it claims: durations that do not follow from ready times, queues, and resource uniqueness; a best-case that disagrees with its own alternative; and a system-wide story that never compares demanded hours to pool hours. That is a solid draft, not a nearly flawless expert answer.
