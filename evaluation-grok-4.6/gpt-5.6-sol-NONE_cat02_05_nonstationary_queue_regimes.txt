5.8

This is a methodologically ambitious write-up that understands the queueing issues the prompt is testing, then repeatedly fails basic evidence handling. Under a strict standard that is not a high score.

**What is actually strong.** The estimands are defined correctly: opening-hours intensities, not Poisson rates; medians/p90s are not mean service or mean flow time; completions are not same-day conversion; capacity pressure is not a claimed \(\rho\). Outage and recovery are not treated as a high-productivity regime. Little’s Law is refused where boundaries, throughput, or stationarity fail, and the open-hour formula \(W=12L/X\) is stated with the right units and overnight caveat. The factorial and backlog-state alternatives are real alternatives, not a relabeling. The weekend WIP/FTE reversal, recovery-as-inventory, and “don’t staff from pooled daily totals” policy are the right operational points. The Python block matches the spec: in-memory tables, regime summaries, Fano/CV plus lag-1, an explicitly non-authoritative split heuristic, LL only on chosen windows, and a clear PM4Py-is-not-applicable-to-these-aggregates comment.

**Fatal or near-fatal evidence errors.** The audit’s headline totals are wrong. The 28 rows sum to **2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions (net +110)**, not 2,780 / 2,679 (net +101). The 99.3 / 95.7 daily averages are therefore false. The later “2,421 weekday arrivals” figure is the same error (true weekday arrivals are 2,160). Publishing a wrong closed-system balance in §1 is not a rounding nit; it is the one stock-conservation fact the table actually identifies.

The claim that representative hourly vectors “do not always sum to the corresponding daily totals” is also false. All four days match exactly (101/99, 105/58, 115/101, 51/43). The mid-sentence “105 rather than the daily 105?” is not uncertainty; it is an invented data defect, which the prompt forbids.

**Internal inconsistency in the primary segmentation.** “11–14 Apr weekend days (4 days)” is calendar-wrong: 13–14 April are Monday–Tuesday, and 6–14 April contain only two weekend days. The 41.8 / 41.5 / WIP 13.8 figures are the **four** days 11–12 plus 18–19, which the same table also lists as a separate post-outage weekend. The code and the LL mask then use only Sat–Sun inside 11–14 (two days), while §6’s \(L\approx13.75\), \(W\approx3.96\) again uses the four-day average. The primary regime table, the LL matrix, and the “reproducible” code do not implement the same partition.

Related arithmetic: 6–14 April net is **+5**, not +4; 20–28 April arrivals are **99.1/day**, not 97.0. The 97.0 figure cannot coexist with completions 87.3/day and net **+106**. Discharge nets are written unsigned (“net 13”, “Net 30”, “net 5”) after net was defined as \(A-D\). “WIP falls 3632”, “WIP changes 5235”, and “WIP 2219” are unreadable as written.

**Required §4 comparison is unfinished.** The weekday/weekend table, which the prompt explicitly requires, leaves the weekday row as “121? No… must be checked computationally.” That is not a quantitative comparison. Deferring to unread code does not repair a published table.

**Smaller but real issues.** Pooled mean WIP 30.3 and FTE 5.25 do not match the row means (~29.86 and ~5.14). Weekend WIP/FTE 6.73 is a bit high versus the daily ratios (~6.6). Four-day and two-day windows are given “High” or “Moderate–high” confidence that the sample cannot support. Fano/CV on 12 diurnal bins is correctly caveated as mixed seasonality-plus-noise; that part is fine. The one-split SSE heuristic is appropriately not sold as truth, but it is also too coarse for a series that the authors themselves segment into outage, recovery, mix shift, and intervention.

**Net.** Process-mining competence and format compliance are well above average. Internal consistency and use of the supplied evidence are not. Wrong system totals, an invented hourly mismatch, a self-contradictory weekend regime, a broken required table, and irreconcilable mix-window arithmetic are enough to keep this far from the top of the scale. The code would likely print better numbers than the prose; the graded artifact is the prose, and it does not check itself.
