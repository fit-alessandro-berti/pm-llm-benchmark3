**6.3**

The submission is structurally complete, case-aware, and clearly above a DFG-copying baseline: C6-A vs C6-B are differentiated on obligations rather than prefix, migration is used in C6-D’s state inference, `process_enabled: false` is applied to premature disposition, and the cross-case DFG critique is conceptually right. It is not close to flawless. Two systematic reasoning errors and one direct control-flow inconsistency keep it well below a high score.

**1. “Next logged” is repeatedly treated as “soon after the obvious next step.”**  
The task asks for the next observed completion/termination, not the following milestone in a happy path. In C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E, rank 1 is correctly `Evidence Review Completed`, but ranks 2–3 are written as what happens *once review finishes* (`Technical Investigation Completed`, `Close — No Safety Issue`, another `Customer Evidence Requested`). That collapses competing next-event hypotheses into a sequential story. Under the evidence-cycle rule, a new `Customer Evidence Requested` is enabled by review, not by `Evidence Received`. Marking those skip-ahead events `process_enabled: true` contradicts the same enablement distinction the answer uses correctly for `Disposition Recorded` in C6-C/D.

**2. C6-E contradicts its own obligations.**  
Rule 5 makes Supplier Analysis mandatory for a lot now tied to six complaints; the write-up states that obligation, notes a supplier certificate in the second package, and even mentions SA in contradicting evidence—then puts `Technical Investigation Completed` in the top three and omits `Supplier Analysis Completed`. Low severity and no injury do not mandate technical investigation; the lot cluster does mandate SA. Predicting the historically more common investigation completion here is exactly the DFG pull the rubric warns against.

**3. C6-D under-uses the observation model for the next *logged* event.**  
The case is the intended test of feed delay: migration-affected, customer attachment in the source system, evidence events absent. A delayed `Customer Evidence Requested` / `Evidence Received` / even `Evidence Review Completed` surfacing in the analytical feed is a first-class candidate for the next *observed* record, distinct from the next process-enabled action (RA, already opened at 12:40). That distinction is confined to the caveat and never appears among the three predictions. Rank 2 `Customer Evidence Requested` is framed as a *new* request, not catch-up of a missing event. Alternative `S5 DISPOSITION_READY` is also poorly supported: the worklist already shows RA open and incomplete, so “never formally required” is incompatible with both Rule 4 and the snapshot.

**4. Several alternatives and probabilities are looser than the case evidence.**  
- C6-C alternative S5 (“RA not mandated”) fights Rule 4 plus the country reportability signal; `Disposition Recorded` at 0.15–0.40 as *next* event is high given a 12-deep RA queue and TI completed only at 11:45. `Supplier Analysis Completed` is marked enabled though lots differ and SA may never have been opened; typical 6–36 h makes it an unlikely immediate completion.  
- C6-B supporting evidence still cites Fast Close preconditions after the case has already left S1 via an evidence cycle; Fast Close and `Close — No Safety Issue` are conflated.  
- C6-A rank-2 TI is justified with “once Evidence Review has progressed,” which is not a next-event argument; a better concurrent story (high-severity TI opened at triage while evidence ran) is not developed. SA is listed as likely open but unused in the forecast, which would be fine if ranks 2–3 were true next-event alternatives rather than later steps.  
- Interval midpoints on C6-A sum to ~1.00, which sits awkwardly next to the claim that intervals encode structural uncertainty rather than a partitioned next-event distribution.

**What remains genuinely strong.**  
Primary states S3/S4 are mostly right; object/lot/injury/severity are used; queue comments are present if thin; unknowns are mostly material; C6-D does *not* treat missing evidence as proof of no evidence; calibration language is honest in spirit. Those virtues are why this is not a mid-fail. Under a hypercritical bar, two methodological breaks on “next logged” plus an internal SA/TI contradiction are enough to block anything in the 8–10 band.
