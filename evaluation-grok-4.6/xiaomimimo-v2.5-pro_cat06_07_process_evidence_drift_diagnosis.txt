**6.3**

The final six sections get the *main* causal decomposition right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing/mix change that does not require concept drift, August–September are overlapping process-performance, concept, and population shifts, and October is not a comparable performance month. That is the difference between a weak answer and a competent one. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading there are factual errors, unmet requirements, a misleading October inference, and a required code sketch that does not actually use PM4Py correctly.

**What is solid.** The drift table covers all six required types and does not collapse ten months into one label. April is tied to the rename, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue features, unmapped variants, and prefix construction at the wrong event. June is tied to the remote-routing policy and the V2 share jump, with V2’s matured breach rate still low. July–September V3 breach (0.46 / 0.64 / 0.61) is triangulated with the `Order Part → Part Received` median (65 h / 130 h / 148 h) while V1/V2 stay roughly flat and remote-fix latency does not move. Partner Z is treated as a September population shift (share, V3 intensity, missing rural/device-age features, slower label maturity), not as “the model became unfair.” October’s 0.16 observed rate is not treated as a recovery. The staged remediation and the four required failure modes are directionally right. Monitoring lists the required families and correctly says a label-maturity alarm must not auto-trigger retraining.

**Factual and logical errors.** May is labeled “early concept drift for V3.” Matured V3 breach in May is 0.33 versus 0.30–0.34 in January–April. That is not concept drift; it is residual pipeline noise plus a 38 h parts edge that has not yet broken the outcome. October’s claim that mean predicted risk 0.23 “might be more accurate” is wrong even as a hedge. A mix-implied rate using last mature variant rates (roughly V1 0.19, V2 0.12, V3 0.61 on a 23/35/41 mix) is already ~0.34, before Partner Z’s extra risk. The 0.16 figure is downward-biased by immature long-path cases; 0.23 is still an underprediction of a still-broken V3 regime, not a substitute truth. June–August is then offered as a “safe” training window, which smuggles a non-stationary supplier break into one sample. “Retrain from September onward once mature” sits next to the admission that September is only 82% mature and Partner Z matures more slowly. Duplicate-event removal after 20 April is in the prompt and almost unused in the final diagnosis.

**Unmet prompt requirements.** The task asked to separate observed evidence from hypotheses *and state what would falsify each explanation*. The “contradicting evidence” column is not that; several hypotheses (April over-prediction cause, June miscalibration cause, October 0.23 as closer to truth) are written as conclusions. Diagnostic tests were required to give expected results under *at least two* competing hypotheses. Tests 4 and 5 mostly state one expected pattern. Presentation is sloppy throughout (`unknownactivity`, `queuefeature`, `remoterouting`), which is not cosmetic in an audit table.

**Code is not a free pass.** `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode='cases')` is not a valid mode (`events`, `traces_contained`, `traces_intersecting` are). Unpacking `perf_dfg[edge]` as triples `(_, _, t)` does not match PM4Py’s performance-DFG return type. Dedup on `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` ignores the audit finding that duplicates were source- and date-specific. Cohort comments name the two filters but do not state the interpretive point the audit needs: event-calendar months are right for DFG edge times; case-start months are right for predictive metrics and label maturity, and mixing them is how October gets misread. The sketch avoids inventing a drift detector and does not fit a model, which is the one code requirement it cleanly meets.

**Net.** This is a good exam answer that would pass a human grader who is scoring the causal story. It is not a near-flawless audit: it mislabels May, misreads October’s 0.23, skips explicit falsifiers, only partly dualizes the tests, and ships broken PM4Py in the one mandatory artifact. That caps the score in the mid-6s.
