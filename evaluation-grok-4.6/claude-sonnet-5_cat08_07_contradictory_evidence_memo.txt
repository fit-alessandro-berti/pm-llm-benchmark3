**6.4**

The memo is strategically competent and hits the required skeleton, but it is not close to flawless. The recommendation (E) is defensible. The execution has inaccuracies, overconfident causality, a weak VoI concept, and several process-mining misses that a strict judge should not wave through.

**What works.** The memo is decision-oriented rather than a recap. It refuses A and B for the right reasons: tails, rework, activation/permit failures, and coordinator fragility block national scale; the simple-house lift blocks a full rollback. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated. All five metric conflicts are addressed. A–E are scored with conditions. M1–M8 are all ranked. The final section names pause / scale / measure / stop-go rules.

**Material flaws**

1. **Redesign is mis-specified.** R is not “three bundled changes.” The prompt also merges validation/scheduling for simple cases. That merge is the cleanest mechanism for the simple-house median win. Omitting it makes the causal story too tidy (skip + post-billing = harm; coordinator = good) and leaves the strongest low-risk lever unnamed in the exec memo and the scale list.

2. **Causality is overclaimed for bundled, poorly measured interventions.** Alignments *coincide* with skip and post-billing activation; they do not isolate them. Attributes are missing before week 3, cost is truncated after week 4, and start timestamps are sparse. Phrases such as “implicated directly,” “as designed incorrectly,” and “no segment where [post-billing activation] looked net-positive” treat a package experiment as if components had been identified. Simple-house complaints *fell* 1pp; post-billing was not reported by segment. Halt-everywhere can be a prudent control; it is not a finding.

3. **Fitness / precision is partly wrong, and generalization is ignored.** High fitness means observed traces replay; it is not evidence that the process “became more variable.” A permissive model of a *simpler common path plus a few chaotic variants* also yields high fitness. Low precision is the over-permissive flower-model problem the mining team already stated. Declaring this “not a modeling artifact” is too strong: generalization fell 0.70 → 0.56, which is the usual sign of an unstable, overfit discovery. That number is dumped in the table and then unused.

4. **The cost conflict is explained with a false mechanism.** Visible cost falling because of “fewer visits, less checking” contradicts the log: second visit 11% → 18%, failed visit 13% → 19%, manual activation 8% → 19%. The honest account is mix (cheap simple houses dominate the mean), survey-cost displacement, and a truncated complaint window—not fewer visits.

5. **VoI ranking confuses control with information.** After deciding to pause billing-before-activation, M7 is a control chart, not the highest-value experiment. Decision-flipping uncertainty is whether skip is salvageable (M6/M1), whether full cost reverses the economics (M4), and whether coordinator ownership survives absence (M5). Ranking M7 first and M5/M8/M2 as afterthoughts does not match a scale / carve-out / rollback choice. M8 is undersold: the complaint mix already moved from wait-time to “installed but dead” / “billed before live,” which is exactly why median speed is not a sufficient success metric.

6. **The action plan fights its own evidence on resilience.** The memo correctly flags 93% coordinator utilization, second-visit saturation (66% → 92%), and stall-on-absence. It then scales coordinator ownership immediately and parks M5 in the second wave. That is internally inconsistent. A 93% utilized single-owner role is not a “clean win” you roll out while deferring the backup design.

7. **E is recommended without facing its operational precondition.** Segment/risk tags are incomplete before week 3. A hybrid that routes on “simple low-risk house” cannot be run safely on that instrumentation. The alternatives table mentions tagging only in passing. It is also unclear whether simple houses get *full* R or a stripped R (merge + coordinator, no post-billing, skip optional). E, C, and “modified R” are blurred.

**Smaller but real errors**

- Evidence table drops the minus signs on the simple-house row (“p90 8.0 days, complaint 1pp”), which inverts the only winning segment if read literally.
- Skip rule is restated as “medium/high” risk; the prompt is medium-risk addresses.
- Formatting collisions (“EUR 184161”, “66%92%”) make core numbers unreadable.
- Temporal profile, working-together centralization, and SLA (worse overall, better for simple houses) are required artifacts and are barely used.
- Pilot mix (more apartments / older buildings) is filed as a confused assumption about *segment* results. The real issue is *aggregate* confounding plus a 6-week vs 10-week window with completion-only timestamps. Tail, 30-day complaints, and p95 = 62 days deserved an explicit censoring/comparability caveat.
- The 90% risk-rule agreement gate is invented false precision. “Near-zero” M7 incidence is undefined. No sample-size, observation-window, or mix-adjustment rule is given.
- “Network-wide” halt of a four-region pilot practice is sloppy scope.

**Verdict.** Right alternative, right anti-scale / anti-rollback posture, usable structure. Not executive-grade under a strict reading: component miscount, causal overreach, a broken cost story, a VoI concept error, and a rollout that ignores the resilience and measurement gaps the memo itself documents. That is a solid-but-flawed synthesis, not a 8–10 memo.
