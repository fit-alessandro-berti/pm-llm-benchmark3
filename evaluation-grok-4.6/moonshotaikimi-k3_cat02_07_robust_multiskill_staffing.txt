**6.5**

The write-up hits the required nine-heading format, names assumptions, compares three operational policies, and correctly refuses to pick a winner from base-case mean delay. The central capacity insight—per-skill pools double-count shared people, while aggregate staff-minutes are already above 100%—is the right first-order result. Several material defects keep it well below a high score.

**Arithmetic and load logic.** Software branch count is 12.85, not 13.2 (S+ES = 7.86+4.99). Pooled Electrical capacity is about 1,750 effective minutes (Ada 333 + Chen 450 + Deepa 450 + Emil 346 + Farah 171), not 1,471; Software is about 981, not 891. Those errors inflate naive skill utilizations. The binding 2,900 vs 2,490 (~116%) calculation is roughly right, but the cumulative block table assigns each arrival its *full* case work to the arrival window. A 09:50 arrival cannot consume ~69 minutes by 10:00; morning ratios are overstated and evening pressure is under-attributed to when Validation actually runs.

**Internal contradictions.** Resilience is defined as P1 SLA ≥ 0.85 *and backlog clearing with overtime*, then C is rated High in base and U5 while the same text says the base day leaves 3–5 cases unfinished and U5 is “capacity-bound.” C’s base P1 range 0.92–0.97 also sits above the author’s own structural ceiling: complex-tail ME/ES P1 paths are already 111–125 minutes before queueing, so mid-90s P1 attainment is optimistic. Parallelization is specified as a state rule and then “i.e.” equated to a wait-saved > 15 min test; those are not the same rule.

**Policy comparison is stacked.** P-A is a weak strawman. Farah is qualified for Software and is the only morning Software body besides Chen, but she is omitted from the Software fixed queue—so U3/U5 “collapse” is partly by construction. Validation is restricted to Ada/Ben/Deepa, which yields *zero* Validation capacity after 18:00 even though Emil is on shift and 1.00 at Validation. A competent specialist design would not do that; P-A is set up to fail.

**Section 4 is not evidence.** The policy×scenario table is invented ranges, not output of §8. Wide bands (e.g. A/U5 mean delay 380–600) make dominance claims hard to audit. That is allowed as “estimates,” but then asserting C “dominates or matches in every scenario” overstates what the table can support.

**Notation and code.** Objective formulas are corrupted (`_p w_p`, missing minus signs, broken set notation). The DES scaffold is ambitious and mostly transparent (explicit handover/setup/preemption/parallel constants, multi-seed summaries, interval log, case-level vs stage-level wait labeled). It was not run against the table. `discover_handover_of_work_network` typically returns a graph, not a dict of edges; `dict(ho_net)` is likely to fail. Top-level `get_service_time` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are used as the prompt asked, but there is no fallback if the installed PM4Py surface differs.

**What still works.** O1 vs O2 is a real objective split; surge triggers are operational; secondary effects (gaming, P3 starvation, Farah skill erosion, Validation migration after 16:00) are on point; §7 attributes and PM4Py mapping are complete; §9 correctly says expected arrivals plus ranges cannot identify 24 h P3 under persistent overload, and the pilot/backtest design protects P1.

Strong structure and the right qualitative story; too many calculational, definitional, and design errors for a high mark under a strict bar.
