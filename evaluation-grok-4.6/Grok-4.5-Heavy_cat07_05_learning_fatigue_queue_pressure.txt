**7.4**

The answer is complete, well structured, and constraint-compliant. It does not collapse the pattern into fatigue, it treats early complexity and late queue pressure as first-order facts, it rejects raw late-shift punishment, and it refuses to treat deferred quality checks as a throughput win. Those are the hard requirements, and they are met. The remaining problems are real, and they keep this well short of a high score.

**Mechanism table.** The seven-row layout and the four-column design are correct. Several cells, however, mix mechanisms. Night first-hour complexity of 56% is placed as circadian support; it is case-mix and backlog, not clock time. Junior 12.4% error under later high WIP is placed as fatigue support; that cell is the experience × pressure interaction already used for queue pressure. Senior 15.9 → 11.8 min is used as learning evidence, but “later events” in the source table pools mid-shift warm-up with the high-WIP speed-up. The “against” columns partly rescue this, yet a strict table should not file the same residual as support for two different mechanisms. The learning test (within-resource FE, low-WIP restriction, experience interaction) is the strongest identification idea in the document. The fatigue test under matched low WIP is conceptually right and practically weak: Day/Late last hours are exactly when queues rebuild, so that cell is sparse and the design is under-powered. That limitation is not stated.

**Integrated explanation.** This is the best section. The three-phase account (early mix/selection, mid warm-up plus simpler residual work, late pressure plus workarounds) matches the joint movement of complexity, priority, WIP, service time, and errors. The claim that pure fatigue would lengthen service time, not shorten it, is the right discriminator. Two slips remain. First, “selection and pressure are the largest movers” is an unearned ranking; there is no variance decomposition. Second, sequence position is treated as if it were elapsed exposure. It is not. Faster workers and those left with easier residual work reach bins 51–75 and 76+ more often. That collider/survivorship problem is never named, so the late “speed-up” is still partly compositional even after the case-mix discussion.

**Proposed analyses.** Coverage of the required PM4Py surface is broad, but the code is thinner and less accurate than the prose. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is case-level. Using it as the prelude to within-resource, first-20-versus-later *event* contrasts blurs the unit of analysis the whole exam is about. Event service time is `time:timestamp − start_timestamp`; that calculation is never written down. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., list(range(0,30)))` treats WIP as consecutive integers and will drop real-valued WIP. `get_service_time` and `discover_performance_dfg` are invoked without a log conversion or a statement of what they actually return. `cluster_log` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are name-dropped rather than tied to a mechanism test. The OLS/logit sketch is the right instinct and still omits clustered errors, the endogeneity of sequence position, and the fact that high sequence bins are selected.

**Interventions.** All eight are scored with benefit, risk, and monitors, and the ranking (3 and 7 first, 8 never) is sound. Intervention 1 still under-states the main objection: if the error spike is WIP, not clock hours, a hard cap relocates the spike. Intervention 6 is handled carefully. Intervention 8 is correctly refused. No constraint is violated.

**Ethics.** Selection, measurement lag, management-created queues, Bot_Risk under load, and the perverse effects of punishing raw late errors are all present. The section is slightly repetitive and still does not say that `error_flag` / 7-day rework are delayed case outcomes, so late-bin rework cannot be attributed to late-bin work without a lag structure.

**Dashboard.** It exceeds the eight-indicator minimum and includes residual service time, pressure exposure, batching, handovers, fairness, and automation health. Alerts are operational. The specification assumes the residualization that section 3 never fully specifies, so the dashboard is more confident than the identification strategy.

The answer reads like a strong practitioner memo. It is not a nearly flawless identification argument, and the PM4Py fragments are not executable analysis. Those are the reasons it cannot sit in the high 8s or 9s under a strict rubric.
