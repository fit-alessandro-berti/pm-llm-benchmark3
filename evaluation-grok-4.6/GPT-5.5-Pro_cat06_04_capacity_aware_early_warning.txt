**6.5**

The memo is structurally complete and avoids the failure modes the prompt flags (I-03 is correctly ineligible; I-05 is not treated like I-07; effects are not equated with \(p_\text{now}\); spillovers and feedback loops are named). It is still not close to a high score. Under a strict reading it is a coherent draft with several decision-rule violations, unused robustification, and sloppy scenario math.

**Decision rule is only partly deployable.** The gates (stage, new evidence, reserve, Diagnosis Started before Triage) are the strongest part. The rest is looser than a live policy can use: “roughly 0.08,” “tighter time-to-SLA band,” and “very high robust lower-bound” are not operational cutoffs. More serious is the break between the stated rule and the numbers. Calibration is announced as a mechanical adjustment to SME / Strategic / Public / NNL probabilities, then never enters the score, the table, or the scenario arithmetic. The score that is actually computed is unadjusted midpoint ARR × value − €450 − full false-alarm burden. That ignores the prompt’s own warning that \(p\) is observational, treats the supplied ARR band as if it were a usable causal interval after a disclaimer, and subtracts the entire false-alarm burden as a sure cost rather than an expectation. Spillover and reserve option value appear in the formula in §1 and disappear from the §2 scores.

**Allocation is internally inconsistent with the rule’s own robustness language.** I-03, I-05, I-08, and the I-06 data hold are the right calls. The selected book is not. I-09 is the weakest identification case in the set (sparse calibration *and* uncertain effects) and is nonetheless first in line, using the midpoint 0.18–0.30 band as if it were as reliable as a complete-data Strategic or Public case. That contradicts “look at the lower bound, midpoint, *and* segment reliability before spending a slot.” I-04 is the other weak commit: Triage, 10 h of runway, Strategic overprediction, €2,000 disruption cost, and a lower bound that is only comfortable before queue externalities. Spending the third scarce slot on it at 12:00 fights the reserve preference and the “do not automatically fill slots” rule. The parenthetical that I-06 may replace I-04 is the better policy; writing I-04 into the 12:00 portfolio and then unsaying it is not a clean decision. I-07 is handled better than I-05 on evidence, but 1.5 h to SLA in Triage with SME underprediction and a large stated ARR is not a “wait” case. A 45-minute swarm plus two pulled diagnosticians can miss the window; the cheaper log review needed to be timed *now*, with an explicit re-entry to a slot if the log is a blocker. Two-specialist interruption (3 × 45 min × 2 = 4.5 specialist-hours off ordinary diagnosis) is almost unmodeled except as a monitoring metric.

**Expected-impact table is auditable in places and invented in others.** Midpoint direct EVs for I-01–I-10 check out. Optimistic low end is identical to Base because “upper half of the range” is implemented as [midpoint, max], so the optimistic column does not dominate. Spillover euros rest on an ungrounded €20–40k untreated-case value and then multiply the lowest probability by the lowest value and the highest by the highest, which inflates the envelope until Base is “ambiguous” by construction. Adverse 0.54–0.90 breach-equivalents is not implied by “15–30 minutes aggregate delay”; 0.90 is an undeclared jump to ~0.05 per untreated case per slot. The Adverse narrative also slips: 0.18 remaining breaches is the world where I-09 (not I-04) gets zero effect, while the euro example assumes I-04 fails. Calibration bias is again absent, so Strategic overprediction never haircuts I-02/I-04 in any scenario. The sequential-stop language is the best sentence in the section and should have been the allocation rule in §2.

**Temporal policy is directionally right, still underspecified.** Episode / suppress / cool-down / re-entry is the correct ontology, and the I-05 vs I-07 contrast is the required one. There is still no numeric cool-down, no owner for the I-07 log review, and no rule for what happens if I-07 is still in Triage at T–60 minutes. “Rest of the episode” after a swarm is too vague for a forbidden-repeat constraint.

**Fairness is complete as a list and weakly binding as a policy.** All five required risks are named and the safeguards are concrete enough to audit. They are not applied to the 12:00 book. The proposed low-value capacity floor would have forced an explicit waive or a cheap action with a clock on I-07/I-05; neither appears in the allocation table. “Do not mechanically penalize NNL” is used to justify giving the first slot to the least-identified case, which is the opposite of a robustness-and-review safeguard.

**Monitoring is the second-best section and still overclaims identification.** The five-layer split, intervention flags in the training record, live missingness for I-06-type fields, and “stop the next slot if observed spillover exceeds its direct benefit” are the right control system. Matching / doubly robust estimation on eligible-but-untreated cases will not recover effects if the policy itself selects on residual value and urgency—the confounding the design creates is not discussed. Randomized encouragement on critical SLA work is waved through with only “where ethically acceptable.” The 0.05 recalibration trigger is arbitrary next to already-known SME/Strategic biases of that same magnitude.

Net: decision-theoretic vocabulary and the hard qualitative tests are met; the quantitative policy does not implement the robust rule it states, the third slot is not justified at 12:00, scenario math is partly homemade, and fairness/calibration are commentary rather than constraints. That is a clearly above-average memo, not a near-flawless one.
