The answer has the right skeleton and several sound case judgments, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. Required elements are missing, several factual claims are wrong, and the checking code would not reliably produce the evidence it claims to produce.

**Section 1.** Six threat scenarios is the right move, and the AV-2/collusion gap is correctly named. Two conceptual problems remain. First, ED-1 is treated as if it were an independent P/D/C control; it is a conditional permission that *requires* AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7. Marking the “whole package” P/D/C on almost every row is circular and hides which condition actually binds. Second, AV-2 is overstated on insider diversion (row 1): ownership/7-day-change checks do not stop a long-standing mule vendor or a compromised-but-unchanged account. Row 4 marking AV-2 as “–” is also off; the validator still runs in an emergency and can still block.

**Section 2.** The six-axis test is usable, and rejecting “same expected loss” as the only standard is correct. The prompt explicitly required **collusion resistance** as a seventh axis. It is not defined. Independence is not a substitute: AP-204/AP-209 are exactly the case where two IDs can still fail the objective. “Partially equivalent” is also underspecified (any one weakened axis + “low to moderate” risk), so later case labels cannot be audited against the test.

**Section 3 — the serious factual errors.**
- **AP-201 recall is wrong.** Settlement is 10:00 on 4 May; audit starts 09:00 on 5 May. That is inside a 24-hour post-transfer window, not after it. “Recall window closed; only legal recovery” is false and drives residual/corrective cells. Wording “MG-4 after preparation” is also false: ratification is after *release*.
- **AP-209 recall is wrong in the same way.** Settlement 18:00 on 4 May, audit 10:00 on 5 May — still inside the window. “Recall window closed early evening” is invented.
- **AP-203** says ED-1 conditions 1–6 are satisfied. Condition 7 is satisfied too (same-day audit). That is a sloppy reading of the rule the answer is applying.
- **AP-201 / AP-207** sit on a thin “OBJECTIVE_MET / PARTIALLY_MET” line without saying whether Release_Payment is already authorization. If release is the authorizing act, same-user release is a completed FEP-1 breach; MG-4 after release is compensating, not nominal prevention. The classifications are defensible only if that semantics is stated. It is not.
- High confidence on AP-201 while listing “proof the manager did not rubber-stamp” as missing evidence is inconsistent.

The non-mechanical pattern is the strongest part of the paper: AP-204 distinct-user **FAILED**, AP-209 distinct-user **PARTIALLY_MET**, AP-201 same-user **PARTIALLY_MET**, AP-205 **LOSS_PREVENTED**, AP-206 **INDETERMINATE**. Those calls match the brief. They do not cancel the recall-window mistakes.

**Section 4.** Hybrid for post-release / pre-settlement MG-4 is an allowed reading, but the semantics are incomplete. The log never shows that ratification can *stop* settlement (AP-201 still settles after m04). Without a stop/cancel path, that act is detective, not corrective.
