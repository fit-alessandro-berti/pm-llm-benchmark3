The write-up has the right architecture: correct headings, a defensible multi-break segmentation, recovery treated as transient rather than a high-throughput regime, a real alternative segmentation, a careful Little’s-Law matrix, and a complete Python block. That is not enough for a high score. Required quantitative claims are wrong in ways that change the story.

**Material calculation errors**

Pooled throughput is simply false. Over 28 days there are 2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions (means **90.0** and **86.0**), a net accumulation of about 110 cases. The answer reports 93.7 and 94.0 and then builds a Simpson argument on “near-zero” pooled balance. The true pooled picture is imbalance, concentrated in the high-complexity weekdays.

Regime utilisation is computed as if the centre were open 10 hours, while the prompt, the 12 hourly bins, and the answer’s own formula all specify 07:00–19:00. Every §2 \(\rho\) is inflated by 6/5. Written formula and computed numbers do not match; §4’s pooled \(\rho \approx 0.94\) then switches back to a 12-hour convention. Qualitative overload in H-wd still holds under a 12-hour lognormal mean (\(\rho \approx 1.21\)), but the advertised levels (0.82 vs 1.44, outage 1.17) are not the quantities defined in the text.

Smaller misses compound this: H-wd completions are 100.6, not 103; H-wd P90 mean is 83, not 81; pooled WIP is ~29.9, not 28.5; hourly Fano/variance figures do not match the vectors (ordinary weekday variance ~24.9 not 22.6; 04-21 Fano ~9.3 not 11.2; weekend Fano ~1.18 not 0.95). The “~55 case / 5 weekday” backlog story uses the wrong day count (H-wd is 7 weekdays, net ~94) and calls a +13 mean-WIP move “consistent” with a much larger cumulative deficit without reconciling intra-day averages vs inventory.

**Conceptual slips**

Outage \(\rho>1\) “because completions were limited” gets the identity backwards: offered-load \(\rho\) uses arrivals; low completions during a 180-minute outage are a throughput collapse, not extra utilisation. Weekend Little’s Law marked `applicable` is too generous: Saturday/Sunday WIP is still partly Friday carry-over, so \(W \approx 234\) min is not a weekend sojourn time. Lag-1 correlations are inflated by the same level shifts already used as regimes; they are weak evidence of within-regime memory. The four-vector limitation is noted via the 04-21 vs `peak_08_10_pct` clash, but not developed into why hourly counts cannot identify an inter-arrival process.

**What remains solid**

Threats in §1 are well chosen. Primary cuts (weekday/weekend × pre-outage / outage / recovery / high-mix / intervention) match the visible breaks. Recovery is not sold as a new steady state. Alternative two-phase mix-only cut is a genuine rival, with a fair strength/weakness comparison. Little’s Law is withheld where WIP is trending. Median service is not used as mean flow time; the lognormal mean is labelled as an assumption. The pooled-staffing policy in §7 is the right “looks fine in aggregate, fails in H-wd” lesson. The script would actually recompute several of the prose numbers correctly, which makes the handwritten tables internally inconsistent with the reproducibility section.

A high score required the arithmetic in §§2, 4, and 5 to be trustworthy. It is not.
