**6.5**

The JSON is complete, on-schema, and process-aware in the ways the rubric rewards: A vs B are not cloned, obligations are tied to rules rather than DFG counts, missing events are not treated as proof of absence, and uncertainty is expressed as ranges. That is the ceiling. Several concrete defects keep it well below a high score.

**Factual error (C6-E).** Supporting evidence correctly states a lot linked to six complaints. Contradicting evidence then says “the four other complaints are across different lots.” That is C6-C’s object pattern, not C6-E’s. The same case therefore both applies Rule 5 and undercuts it with the wrong facts. Under a hypercritical read this is not a wording slip; it is an internal contradiction on the obligation that should dominate C6-E.

**Next-event vs later-on-the-path.** For C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E, ranks 2–3 are mostly *successors of* Evidence Review Completed (another evidence request, Close, Disposition, Supplier Analysis, Technical Investigation). Those are plausible *later* steps, not competing hypotheses for the *immediate* next log record, unless review is already done and hidden. On **normal** feeds that story is weak. The prompt asks for three plausible next logged activities; sequential path-continuation is a softer form of DFG-following.

**C6-A ranking is the weakest process call.** After high severity, reversible injury, same-lot multi-device linkage, three similar complaints, and R-T4, the live fork is investigation (TI / RA / Supplier Analysis), not another evidence cycle. Rank 2 as Customer Evidence Requested overweight completeness 0.86. Rank 3 as Technical Investigation Completed ignores the stated TI queue (7 waiting, one of three engineers out) and the 8.5 h median; as an *immediate* next event it is temporally implausible. Regulatory Assessment is absent from the top three despite being mandatory.

**C6-D calibration.** Migration-affected reasoning is the right idea, but 8–15% delayed events (even with a flag) does not justify 0.30–0.55 that a *specific* past completion is the next thing observed. TI Completed is already in the feed; ranking a causally earlier Evidence Review as #1 needs a stronger insertion model than is given. `process_enabled: true` on that backfill also blurs the required distinction between an enabled live task (RA, already opened by R-R2) and an observation-model echo.

**`process_enabled` is unused as a distinguisher.** Every prediction is `true`. The enablement-vs-observation point is only prose. For delayed/backfilled events that is exactly where `false` (or a split) would have earned credit.

**Smaller nits.** C6-A’s S4 alternative is thinly supported (~2 h since receipt, normal feed). C6-B ranks 2–3 (Close vs Disposition Recorded) overlap the same closure path. C6-C’s Supplier Analysis is correctly hedged in the state, then still ranked #2 on a same-failure-mode / different-lot pattern that Rule 5 does not clearly trigger. Intervals are honest in width; several midpoints are still optimistic given queues (especially RA at 12 waiting).

**What remains solid.** State labels are mostly right (S3 for A/B/E, S4 for C/D). Fast Close is correctly withheld once an evidence cycle has started. C6-C uses the country reportability rule instead of severity alone. C6-D uses the worklist snapshot without inventing a completion. Cross-case text on A vs B, DFG limits, and interval semantics is disciplined. Those strengths are why this is not a mid-pack score; the factual mix-up, sequential “next” events, and C6-A/C6-D calibration errors are why it is nowhere near flawless.
