**7.4**

The final write-up is complete, correctly formatted, and clearly better than a generic ops memo. It uses the logs rather than vibes: weekly mix decomposition (Urgent ~2.3×, Complex ~2.2×, Routine essentially flat), DFG share shifts (TV→MV 24.5%→47.2%, hold 1.8%→8.4%, batch-wait 4.4%→13.3%, repeats 6.6%→13.7%), queue/utilization pairs, variant facts (bypass 74%→58%, M2/night repeat clustering, Complex 3.1× MV / 2.4× hold), and the I1–I10 conflict set. It does not flood the system with standing capacity, does not chase the historical mean, does not claim every case type can be first, and does discuss downstream overload. Residual vulnerabilities include redesign-induced harm (M1 as a new single point of failure; I6 cancellation rebound). That is a strong process-mining answer.

It is not nearly flawless.

**Material evidence errors**

1. **I8 is attached to the wrong clock.** `Register order → Receive sample` (5h/8h) is sample logistics after the order exists, not clerk processing. Registration queues are 0.7h/2.8h. Automating complete digital metadata trims clerk time and accession errors; it does not “remove part of the 5–8h register→receive edge.” The migration story (load lands on pre-check/assay) is right; the time-saving claim is a DFG misread.

2. **Urgent 18h is “proved” by adding mixed-type median edges.** Peak `Technical validation → Medical validation` (24h) plus `Medical validation → Release` (5h) = 29h is treated as a structural impossibility for Urgent-with-MV. Those medians are across *all* cases on the edge, and Complex is 3.1× more likely to need MV, so the 24h figure is Complex-weighted. Medians of sequential edges also do not add to a path median. MV queue 22h still makes Urgent-with-MV extremely fragile; stating it as 29h > 18h arithmetic is overstated and is reused as a headline in S3.

3. **Pre-analytical “defect share” 7.3%→13.5% double-counts.** Direct pre-check exits to recollection + accession are 7.0% and 12.3% of received. Folding `Manual accession correction → Request recollection` on top recounts cases already in the 500/420 accession branch.

**Smaller reasoning flaws**

- Registration overtime after 9h is asserted as the cause of the ~140/wk accession load. The log does not support that causal link; labeling, physician data, and pre-analytical quality are equally plausible.
- MV entries ~2.6× versus utilization 58%→93% (~1.6×), and hold entries ~5.6× versus specialist utilization ~2×, are never reconciled (WIP, dwell, informal extra hours, or hours-per-case). Saturation is still real; the capacity math is incomplete.
- Several thresholds (Complex “internal 96h,” bypass ≥85%, WIP 96–120h) are invented. That is allowed as design, but they are written with the same confidence as log-derived figures.
- P-B assumes a partner that can absorb the same winter wave; the answer flags co-variance, then still scores Complex as “++” without a credible TAT model once 12h transport *plus* partner queue is included.

**What holds up**

Scenarios vary mix and disruption, not just volume. Bottleneck sequences are chained (intake → pre-check → accession → prep/batch → assay → TV → MV → hold) with explicit I1/I2/I3/I5/I6 overload couplings, M2 catch-up waves into TV/MV, and S4’s delayed clerk-loop. R1 (aging/WIP/quota instead of strict Urgent-first), trigger-gated I4, dual-trigger batching, and rejecting I9 are the right reading of the conflict hints. The trade-off table has every required column. The dashboard separates leading queues/SLA-risk/repeat-by-machine from lagging p90 and breach rates, with bands tied to observed peak medians.

Those strengths are why this is not a mid-range score. The two DFG misuses (I8 vs courier time; mixed medians as an Urgent critical path) and the defect-rate double-count are why it is well short of 9+. Under the stated strictness, that is a 7.4.
