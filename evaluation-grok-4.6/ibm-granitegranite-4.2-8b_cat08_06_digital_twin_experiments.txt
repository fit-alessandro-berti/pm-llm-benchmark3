**4.2**

The answer has the required eight-section shape and is not a naive independent-case sketch, but it is far from a sound digital-twin design. Under a strict reading of the brief, the PM4Py contract, empirical grounding, and internal consistency all fail in ways that would mislead a municipality.

**What is actually solid.** Scope is drawn at submission-to-decision, calendars and queues are first-class, service and waiting are at least *named* separately, objections are treated as unobserved, D1–D10 plus three stress cases appear, and warm-up/replications/bootstrap/sensitivity plus a multi-layer validation story are present. The text correctly refuses to claim optimality. That is why this is not a 2.

**PM4Py and code (hard requirement miss).** The brief required `pm4py.format_dataframe` on `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp`, `pm4py.parse_process_tree` *or* an explicit limitation note, and computation of variants, DFG, performance DFG, arrival average, and service/waiting columns. The skeleton does none of that. It invents `pm4py.log.normalize`, `pm4py.models.process_model`, `extract_process_model`, `extract_dfg`, and `normalized_log.cases()`. It falsely states that `pm4py.parse_process_tree` does not exist; it does, and the given tree string is never parsed. Variants, performance DFG, and timestamp-derived waiting columns are absent. That is not a style issue; it violates an explicit constraint.

The engine is also not coherent. `Calendar.is_available_week` indexes weekday+d off the end of the day list, tests day names against a list of date tuples, and always reads `interruption_weeks[0]`. Bundling writes `self.bundles[bundle_id]` while `bundle_id` is still `None`. `ArrivalGenerator` calls `calendar.get_first_day_of_month`, which `Calendar` does not have. `SimulationEngine.run` never drives the arrival process. Peak months in the log are multiplied by 1.7 *again* after rates are estimated from that same log, double-counting seasonality.

**Process-mining grounding errors.** Applicant response is set to median 0.2d / p90 5d, against the log summary (median wait 5d from request, heavy tail). The conversion `5d/24h = 0.208d` treats five *days* as five hours. Simple-renovation median lead time is invented as ~8d; the variant table gives 12d (clean) and 22d (missing docs). Public-notice duration is specified three incompatible ways: `14d + Gamma(1.5, 7)`, raw `Gamma` with “median ~10d”, and later “median ~6.36d / p90 ~35d”, none of which matches “14d fixed minimum / 60d with objections”, and the Gamma draw can fall *below* the statutory minimum. Fire capacity is capped at one case per FTE-day despite a 2.5h median service time. When a start timestamp *exists*, waiting is set to 0; that collapses wait and service after the brief forbade doing so without a limitation note.

**Cross-case and dynamics.** Architect bursts (top 20 IDs, 31% of cases, same-week clusters) never enter arrival generation. Inspection bundling is only an intervention (D3), although the as-is rules already allow architect–district grouping. The text quotes that objections can affect several related cases and then *denies* cross-case objection dependence. Case state is a single status string; the AND of planning / fire / environment in the tree is not a synchronization model. Completeness-loop rework in the tree is mixed with narrative rework after planning, inspection, and legal, with the extra assumption that rework duration equals the original activity and is independent of history.

**Experiment and decision logic.** The experiment table is malformed: stress rows bleed into D10, and “every policy × historical × stress” is asserted without a design (factors, blocking, what is held constant). D1’s “60% fewer missing-doc loops” is an uncalibrated effect size treated as fact. Decision inequalities are reversed in places: “select if median lead time ≥ baseline − 2d” and “utilization ≥ baseline +5%” would prefer slower, more loaded systems; the prose then argues the opposite. SLA and fairness cutoffs are invented and then treated as operational thresholds. Four-month warm-up plus recording months 5–12 on a six-month seasonal cycle does not give a full observed season.

**Uncertainty and validation.** Bootstrap CIs, lognormals, and sensitivity on the objection proxy are the right *vocabulary*, but the lognormal p90 formula (`exp(μ + σ√2 ln 10)`) is wrong, 50 replications are not justified for Complex-variance p90s, and “replay P001/P002 and tweak `rework_prob` if paths differ by 10%” is overfitting to two traces, not log replay. KS p > 0.1 as an acceptance rule on non-stationary heavy tails is not a serious distributional test.

The piece reads like a complete consulting memo, which makes the factual and API errors more costly, not less. It is usable as a section outline; it is not a trustworthy simulation design.
