The answer follows the required 10-section skeleton and gets the main causal story right: March is mostly semantic/provenance, later months are real process change, and there is no single global change point. That is not enough. Under a strict reading the deliverable has systematic presentation failures, at least one incorrect computed quantity, and several methodological stretches that a process-mining review would reject.

**What holds up.** The timeline correctly separates logging (March), supplier-driven rework (May), staffing/queueing (June), priority redistribution (July), seasonal mix (October), and a mixed December recovery. Section 2 is the strongest piece: mapped fitness recovering conformance does not prove the process was unchanged, and post-May DFG/rework/cycle patterns cannot be logging alone. The change-point list is mechanism-specific rather than one-shot. The hypothesis table covers the eight required families. The monitoring set spans schema, order, variants, rework, wait, service, mix, completeness, conformance, and outcomes, and it does not use one universal threshold.

**Quantitative and presentation errors.** Several numbers are corrupted in a way that makes the report unusable as written: “Supplier Z share 1443–46%”, “Cases 760790”, “raw fitness 0.920.55”, “4.85.4h”. Those look like stripped arrows (`14→43`, `760→790`, `0.92→0.55`, `4.8→5.4`). A diagnostic memo that can be read as claiming a 1443% supplier share fails basic quantitative hygiene.

The September Manhattan DFG distance is wrong. Against the January fragment the seven absolute gaps are \(2+3+8+8+3+4=28\), not 31. The other month distances check out (Mar 3, May 40, Jul 59, Oct 64, Dec 36), which makes the September error look like careless arithmetic rather than a different measure. The task explicitly asked not to manufacture precision the aggregates cannot support; publishing a false 31 is the opposite of that.

Cycle-time shares are only loosely constrained. Upper bounds (30% + 40% + 4% + 60%) exceed 100% with no joint-consistency check. “95% confidence” on the March logging attribution is theater: there is no formal change-point test, and the prompt already said none was applied.

**Methodological weaknesses.** The second logging change point on 2026-04-01 is invented. Nothing in the timeline or the series marks 1 April; missing-resource and duplicate rates simply plateau after the March shock. That is a plateau, not a second onset. Treating it as a candidate change point inflates “two CPs per class” without a rule that would have found April independently.

DFG-distance analysis states Manhattan and omitted-edge assumptions, then stops. It does not say which edges dominate (October’s 64 is mostly `Assess→SeniorAssessment` 4→22 and the request-info loop). Without that, “DFG distance rose” is an opaque scalar, not a control-flow diagnosis.

The primary monitoring population excludes Supplier Z and the October–November peak. That is useful as a *control cohort* for residual drift, but calling it the primary monitoring population is operationally backwards: the high-risk, high-volume cells are exactly where SLA and rework matter. The text never resolves that tension.

Several monitors would fire from May through December on the same known shocks (rework >20%, mapped fitness <0.85 is close in July, queue wait >1.5×). There is no rebaseline-after-accepted-shock rule, no distinction between “recurrence of a known seasonal signature” and “new drift,” and no handling of the fact that fitness, DFG distance, variants, and rework are highly correlated—so BH on 11 monthly tests is mostly cosmetic.

Acceptable as a sketch, not as a manual-faithful plan.

**Format.** Headings and table schemas match the spec. Section 2 and Section 9 appear to sit inside the requested word bands. Completeness is not the problem. Accuracy, inspectability, and operational monitoring design are.

Net: a competent multi-mechanism narrative with the right high-level decomposition, undermined by garbled figures, one incorrect distance, an invented April change point, an under-specified DFG analysis, and monitors that cannot tell recurrence from new drift. That is clearly above a failing reconstruction and clearly below a near-flawless one.
