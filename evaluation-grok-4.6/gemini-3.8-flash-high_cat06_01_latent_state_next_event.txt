**6.4**

The answer is process-aware, structurally complete, and clearly stronger than a DFG-copying baseline: C6-A vs C6-B is handled as a context problem rather than a prefix problem, C6-C uses the country reportability rule, C6-D uses Rule 7 and the worklist snapshot, and C6-E treats lot linkage as overriding low severity. The JSON shape, three-prediction constraint, and interval style are all in spec. It is not close to flawless.

**Internal control-flow inconsistency.** `process_enabled` is used correctly for C6-B rank 2 and C6-C rank 2 (Close / Disposition blocked until Evidence Review / Regulatory Assessment complete). C6-E rank 2 then marks `Customer Evidence Requested` as enabled and as the next logged event, even though Rule 3 is Evidence Review Completed → (optional) another evidence cycle. That is the same “downstream outcome treated as next event” pattern they correctly rejected for C6-B. This is a real logic error, not a wording quibble.

**The advertised S4 alternative is not used consistently in the rankings.** For C6-A, S4 (investigations already running since triage) is the alternative state, but Evidence Review Completed is still given 0.65–0.82. If technical/regulatory work has been running since 09:08, high-severity queue-jumping is allowed, and task starts are invisible, those completions are more competitive than 0.10–0.22 / 0.04–0.14. For C6-E the alternative is again S4, yet Technical Investigation Completed is omitted from the top three while a sequential third evidence request is ranked second. That does not match how they operationalized concurrency on C6-A.

**Obligation extraction is uneven.** C6-A treats Supplier Analysis as mandatory from “two devices on one complaint” plus three “similar” complaints whose lot identity is not given; Rule 5 needs related complaints on the same lot/component, so this is over-read. C6-E correctly makes Supplier Analysis mandatory for a six-complaint lot, then under-reads Rule 4: a clustered lot is a possible reportability/safety signal, so Regulatory Assessment should at least be suspected, as it was for C6-C’s repeated-malfunction rule. C6-B’s alternative `S5 DISPOSITION_READY` is weakly supported: evidence arrived at 12:16, feed is normal, and Evidence Review is still listed as open.

**Observation model is overclaimed, then over-concentrated.** The 8–15% / 6 h delay applies to cases created in the last 48 h, which includes A–E. Calling a `normal` flag a “guarantee” that absence equals non-occurrence (C6-A, C6-C) is too strong; C6-D is the flagged case, not the only delayed one. On C6-D, delayed `Evidence Review Completed` at 0.15–0.28 is too specific and too high: the missing chain is Customer Evidence Requested / Evidence Received / Evidence Review, and the stated delay rate does not justify putting that mass on one downstream completion.

**Next-event and remaining-time are mixed.** Several “contradicting” items are duration facts (40 min since C6-E receipt; 80 min since R-R2 opened C6-D; 2 h regulatory lower bound) that affect *when* an event appears, not *whether it is next*. C6-D rank 1 even converts the 2 h minimum into an “expected” 14:40–16:00 completion, which is the earliest end of a 2–14 h window, not the expectation. C6-B cites 68 ERC → Close — No Safety Issue traces as support while ignoring that ERC → Technical Investigation (173) and ERC → Customer Evidence Requested (101) are the dominant DFG successors; the case-specific override is right, the selective count is not.

**Smaller defects.** Fast Close being forbidden is listed as contradicting evidence for C6-A even though it was never predicted. C6-D invents a completed obligation named “Customer Evidence Submission.” Midpoint coherence of the top-three intervals is fine; the problem is not arithmetic, it is that the intervals are tighter than the alternatives and queue/feed caveats the text itself introduces.

The cross-case discussion is the best section (prefix-identical A/B, object-centric obligations, DFG concurrency flattening, no false point probabilities). Those insights are why this is not a mid-fail. They do not cancel the ranking/enablement contradictions or the overconfident observation-model language. Under strict grading this is a strong but clearly flawed domain answer, not near-ceiling work.
