**3.0**

The submission follows the required eight-section outline and names the right themes (non-stationary demand, calendars, cross-case coupling, objections as missing, intervention comparison). That is almost the only reason it is not lower. Under a strict reading it fails the problem’s explicit constraints, misreads the supplied empirical tables, and delivers a Python skeleton that violates the stated PM4Py conventions and would not run.

**Simulation design is conceptually thin and factually wrong in several places.** The twin is described as a bag of entities and tables, not as a discrete-event system. There is no event calendar, no seize/release semantics, no synchronization barrier for the AND-split `+(Planning, Fire?, Env?)`, and no operational rule for “Decision drafting waits for all applicable review/inspection results.” The process order is inverted: the given tree places Legal check *after* Decision drafting, but Section 1 makes drafting wait on legal results. Resource calendars are scrambled: fire reviewers’ Tuesday–Thursday window is applied to planners; clerk Monday availability is later coded as closed. Inspection batching, architect bursts, and neighborhood objections are named as state variables but never given mechanisms (batch-formation window, hold-for-bundle vs. travel saving, latent objection process). D4/D5/D8 are treated as if extra capacity or triage *cuts service time in half*. That is a queueing error: those interventions change wait, routing, or overlap, not the activity’s work content. D3’s “8d → 3d travel” confuses the 8-day *queue wait* before Site inspection with travel. D10 is described as including parallel legal checks, which is D5, not D1+D2+D3+D4.

**Process-mining grounding is poor.** Parameter estimation never specifies a defensible split of sojourn into wait vs. service when only completion timestamps exist. The text is contradictory: it both subtracts `end − start` “if start times are missing” and invents `waiting = end − (median service + 5 min)`. That is exactly the collapse the prompt forbids, and the limitation is not explained. Objections are converted into a deterministic `14 + 46 × 1{objection}` rule, i.e., treated as observed via the proxy rather than as a latent, partially identified process. Variant shares are reused as rework probabilities: the 9% *inspection-rework variant group* becomes “9% rework after Planning review”; the 8% *Complex variance share* becomes a rework rate. Fire routing is tied to the D2 €50k value threshold; environmental routing is tied to D3/D5, which the brief links to *travel*, not environmental sensitivity. Arrival math is wrong by an order of magnitude: 31,200 / 18 months is ~1,733 cases/month, not 173. Applicant “Receive missing documents” is called heavy-tailed Gamma with median = 0 and p90 = 0.

**Experiment planning and uncertainty treatment are not credible at this scale.** The matrix is a list, not a factor–level design; there is no CRN/paired comparison, no isolation of mechanisms, and the first “stress” scenario *adds* inspectors. Warm-up of 100 cases is far too short for a process whose complex tail is 260 days and whose demand is seasonal; “10% of baseline arrival rate” is not a time horizon. Run length is not specified as a seasonal multiple. One `random.seed(42)` for 100 replications either repeats the same path or is underspecified. “90% credible intervals” plus MCMC calibration plus inverse-probability weighting are named, not designed. Decision weights sum to **120%**. Utilization ≥ 85% is treated as a target rather than a congestion risk. Median is prioritized over p90 even though the brief is dominated by tails and SLA.

**Validation mixes the wrong tasks.** Face validation is described as matching historical KPIs (that is operational validation). “Replay 10% of the log” is not token replay/alignments and not a generative check. Case-level RMSE/R² against a holdout assumes a predictive remaining-time model, not an open-loop DES with its own arrivals. There is no plan to validate seasonality, calendar utilization, architect-batch dependence, or the fact that D2/D1 have no historical analogue.

**The Python skeleton fails the required checklist almost completely.** It does not build a DataFrame from the excerpt; it does not call `pm4py.format_dataframe`; it does not call `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. It invents non-existent modules (`pm4py.objects.concept.data.ConceptData`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.inductive.tree`, `process_tree.convert_from_string`) and a fictional XES rename (`concept:name[2]`, etc.). “Variants” are `case_type` counts, not activity-sequence variants. “DFG” is activity frequencies, not directly-follows. There is no performance DFG and no `get_case_arrival_average`. Service time is whole-case cycle time; waiting time diffs timestamps *across cases of the same activity*. `generate_arrivals` ignores the weights it computes and just copies log rows. `Case` has no `total_completion_time`, yet the engine reads it; `cases` is a list and then used as a dict. Clerk calendars mark Monday closed. Routing and calendars are not real placeholders so much as broken stubs.

The piece looks like a complete design document and is not one. Structure without a workable model, honest identification plan, or convention-faithful code is not a high-scoring answer.
