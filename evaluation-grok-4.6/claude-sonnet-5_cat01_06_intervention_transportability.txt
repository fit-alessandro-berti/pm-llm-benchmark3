**Process comparison.** The required features and columns are present, and the design implications are mostly right. Presentation is sloppy in ways that matter: “48%40%”, “9678h”, “11.8%12.4%”, and “OriginalsDocument” are unreadable without the missing separators. **Transportability graph.** All listed nodes appear, and the shared-vs-site-specific prose is mostly sound. Two edges are wrong. API latency does not increase `identity_capacity`; it shortens service time. `originals_required` does not change `document_capacity`; it gates start eligibility. `S_legal_requirement -> legal_capacity` confuses a statutory demand with a staffing stock. The write-up then mislabels effect modifiers as selection nodes: S-nodes are population indicators, not the modifiers themselves. The heading “Why a similar diagram transportability” is missing the claim it is supposed to make.

**Evidence transfer.** This is the strongest section. Confounding is handled honestly and the 9-hour figure is not treated as a treatment effect. Using `n/a` for several confidence cells violates the 0–100 column contract. Concatenated metrics again reduce auditability.

**Modifiers.** Coverage is adequate and the failure signatures are usable. **PM4Py code.** Site A trees, conversion, soundness calls, and limitation comments meet the checklist on paper. The Site B model does not implement the candidate intervention: originals are hard-gated for every case, so Identity∥Document—the actual treatment—is never encoded.
