**5.3**

The write-up has the right skeleton and the right high-level posture (no single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim; expand only with an embedded evaluation). Under a strict reading it is still not a reliable causal-policy memo. The quantitative core is sloppy, several bias signs are backwards, required elements are missing or only gestured at, and the identification story is not the analysis that was actually run.

**Format and coverage.** Sections 1–10 exist with the requested headings and table schemas. Section 1 stays mostly descriptive and hits availability vs use, non-random selection, co-shocks, and CSAT/aggregation limits. Word counts are roughly in range. That is the floor, not a virtue.

**Quantitative discipline is the largest failure.** The stated window is Jan–Mar vs Jul–Aug, but the numbers do not follow that window.

- Epsilon median post is **42.8**, not **45.8**; the **+13.6%** figure is Delta’s change copied onto Epsilon. Epsilon’s like-for-like median change is about **+6%**, not a twin of Delta.
- Beta p90 post is the Jul–Aug mean **102.4**, not August-only **99.2**.
- Gamma’s “pre” median **38.2** / p90 **78.4** match Jan–Apr, not the advertised Jan–Mar window (**37.5** / **77.4**).
- Epsilon p90 uses March and August point values, not the stated averages.

Those are not rounding differences. They inflate how badly never-treated branches deteriorated and make the adopter–control contrast look cleaner than it is. The later claim that adopter **absolute** median time “increased” also contradicts the Alpha table (−6.7%). The −0.35 intensity correlation and the “routing adds 5% cycle drag ⇒ ~−10% automation benefit” bound are asserted, not shown.

The window choice also buries the strongest process contrast: Gamma’s own pre-adoption May–Jun shock (median 45.9, p90 101.0, rework 14.6) then July drop (38.4 / 87.1 / 12.6). Using winter months as Gamma’s baseline makes adoption look like a 1-hour median blip.

**Causal logic errors.** Selection/RTM is signed wrong. Picking historically weak branches and then watching them recover **overstates**, not understates, an automation benefit. Alpha’s May FTE loss should bias cycle-time comparisons **against** finding a benefit if unadjusted; the table says the opposite. Anticipation that improves the late pre-period typically **shrinks** the post jump, not “inflates immediate impact.” Conditioning the “conditionally causal” claim on routing and staffing being “balanced across groups” is false on the prompt’s own facts (targeted +10%/+5% routing; asymmetric FTE shocks).

Post-treatment covariates are treated as innocent controls. Arrivals are partly the routing campaign aimed at adopters; FTE and even recorded complexity can be downstream or concurrent policy. The memo never separates confounders from mediators/colliders.

**Estimands and identification.** The estimand list is adequate in count and includes ITT-like availability, intensity, median, tail, escalation/rework fragments, CSAT, and a timing contrast. It does **not** do what was asked on interference and treatment versions (shared fraud desk, specialist queues, routing that reallocates demand, availability ≠ use, fidelity ramping from ~25% to ~50%). “Identifiable from supplied data?” is also used as a synonym for “unconfounded,” which it is not.

The primary design is described as a staggered event study, which is the right family. The text still underplays already-treated contamination / heterogeneous timing (the reason naïve TWFE is unsafe). Then Section 9 estimates exactly that naïve `automation + C(branch) + C(month)` TWFE. `rel_month` is built and never used. No event-study coefficients, no not-yet-treated vs never-treated split, no Goodman-Bacon / cohort decomposition even as a caveat with N=5.

**Process interpretation.** The mechanism table covers the required rows but several cells are garbled (“Alpha median slightly, but P90”, “13.214.5”). The March **+2pp escalation** jump in **every** branch—the fraud-desk signature—is barely used. April rework dips then reversals (Alpha 13.2→11.2→14.5; Beta 12.8→10.4→12.7) are flattened into “rework rose.” Tail vs median is separated in principle and then muddied by the bad control arithmetic.

**Section 9 misses stated requirements.** Panel “validation” is a `read_csv`. Trends are printed for **median only**, not the five outcomes. Pre/post contrasts are median-only. Clustered SEs and the small-N / aggregated-median warnings are the one part that is done properly. Assumptions are not written as explicit identifying comments; they are a footer.

**Rollout section.** `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the defensible choice. Delta-then-Epsilon staggering, case-level holdout, fraud-desk monitoring, and a numeric kill-switch are directionally right. They are also thin: pre-period length is unspecified, case-level randomization ignores shared queues (the interference problem again), p90>110 is a weak guardrail (Alpha already printed 119.9), and Epsilon’s three-month stagger is short relative to the learning/intensity path the memo itself emphasizes.

**Claim ladder.** The “Not supported” rung is correct and is the one sentence leadership needed. The top “Observed” rung overstates control degradation and calls an untested difference “significant.” The “Conditionally causal” rung is not conditionally identified under the condition given.

Net: a complete template with a sound refusal to invent a single percentage, undermined by incorrect arithmetic, reversed threats, an event-study that was not estimated, and missing interference/treatment-version work. That is not near-flawless, and the quantitative errors are disqualifying for a high score.
