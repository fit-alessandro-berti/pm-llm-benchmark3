The answer gets the intended story mostly right—April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a mix/routing change rather than a new outcome mechanism, August–September are overlapping process/concept/population effects, and October is not a comparable performance month. ## What is actually strong

Section 2 is the best part. January–March are treated as a stable baseline (AUROC ~0.81, predictions matching a ~0.18–0.19 base rate). April is triangulated correctly: 18% unmapped variants, 17% `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, 14% missing queue features, predicted risk jumping to 0.28 while the matured breach rate stays ~0.19, AUROC/ECE collapsing together. That is pipeline-induced feature corruption, not a new business concept. June is handled with the right restraint: V2 rises to 52%, overall breach falls to 0.15, within-variant rates barely move, remote-fix latency is flat—so this is process mix / covariate shift, not concept drift. August–September are not collapsed into one slogan; supplier latency, V3 prevalence, and Partner Z are allowed to overlap. October’s 0.16 observed rate is not treated as an improvement.

The test list in Section 3 is complete against the prompt (maturity curves, vintage backtests, remap-and-recompute, stratified calibration, DFG/performance-DFG, path-conditional outcome checks, transport/reweighting, lineage). Section 6’s four failure modes are the right ones and the mitigations are proportionate. The label-maturity monitor is correctly forbidden from auto-triggering retraining.

## Material errors

**April is not label delay.** The table’s “Label delay/censoring” row names April with “Very High” confidence and then cites `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue features, and the diagnosis rename. Those are activity-schema and feature-lineage failures. April has **100% label maturity** and a 34-day median delay, indistinguishable from Q1. The immediate action “backfill April labels” is therefore the wrong object. The narrative later says April is a pipeline catastrophe; the required diagnosis table says the opposite. That is not a wording nit. The whole exercise is about not mixing outcome-label maturity with other breakage.

**V3 mix is misstated.** The July–August story says V3 rises to “35% (July) → 42% (August).” The table in the prompt is July 22%, August 35%, September 42%. July is the start of the supplier shock (65 h, V3 breach 0.46) with mix still near baseline; August is the explosion (130 h, V3 35%, V3 breach 0.64). Collapsing them with the wrong shares blunts the process evidence.

**October is over-assigned to concept drift.** Concept drift is listed as August–October at “High” confidence, while matured V3 rates are explicitly unavailable in October and only 55% of labels exist, biased toward shorter paths. You can hypothesize that the August–September mechanism continues; you cannot treat October metrics as a third month of confirmed concept drift.

**May is sanded off.** The pipeline row claims “May–October pipeline stable.” The prompt says the May patch remapped the activity but did **not** fully recompute cached features (including events-before-diagnosis), and duplicate removal only applied after 20 April. May’s AUROC 0.78 and ECE 0.050 are residual pipeline, not a clean recovery. Training on May–June as a “safe recalibration window” is therefore too casual: May is still dirty, and a June recalibration (more V2, lower base rate) would push scores the wrong way into an August V3 shock.

## Conceptual slippages

Covariate, process, and concept drift are allowed to overlap—the prompt says they will—but the table does not keep the mechanisms distinct enough.

- June’s V2 policy is process drift that **induces** covariate shift in path mix. Listing it as primary covariate drift, then again as process drift, without saying which features actually moved, is circular.
- The supplier delay is process-performance drift on `Order Part → Part Received`. It becomes concept drift **at the mandated prediction point** (right after diagnosis) only because future lead time is not in \(X\). That mediation is never stated cleanly, so “isolate V3 and retrain” is treated as interchangeable with “monitor the supplier edge,” which it is not.
- Partner Z is population shift **and** a different variant mix **and** missing features (rural distance, device age) **and** slower label maturity. The table’s action “test for unfairness” jumps to a moralized hypothesis the evidence does not yet support. Test F then sets “population shift” against “unfairness,” which is the wrong contrast. The useful contrast is: gap closes after reweighting on observed path/features vs. residual gap from missing covariates vs. a different \(P(Y \mid X)\) on the same path.

Falsifiers are mostly weak. “If April’s breach had been higher than May’s” does not uniquely implicate concept drift. A real falsifier is: after activity normalization, duplicate removal, and full feature recompute, April’s AUROC/ECE still fail to return toward March. “If V2’s breach had increased” is better. For October, the unused smoking gun is directional: **predicted risk rises (0.23) while observed breach falls (0.16)** under an API backlog that censors long V3-like cases. That pattern separates censoring from genuine improvement more sharply than “maturity is 55%.”

September’s 82% maturity and the note that Partner Z labels mature more slowly are under-used. The 0.45 vs 0.27 contrast is cited as population evidence without the selection-direction caveat: if anything, immature long-path labels **understate** Partner Z risk, so the gap is not an upper bound on “fairness.”

## Section 4 is staged but not safe

Containment should start from the **live** system: do not trust current scores on V3 / Partner Z, do not treat October’s 0.16 as a KPI, do not recalibrate on immature or dirty months. Instead the plan is organized as if “Immediate (April)” were a current phase.

Retraining a V3-only model on July–August memorizes a non-stationary supplier regime unless lead-time (or a supplier-risk proxy) is actually added. “Retrain V3 monthly” is heavier than the evidence. A variant-specific or hierarchically calibrated model is justifiable; a permanently separate V3 model is not yet compelled. January–March are the only unambiguously safe training months. April is unsafe for features. May is unsafe until lineage is repaired. August–September are usable only as **matured, variant-stratified** process evidence, not as a pooled refit. October is unsafe for outcomes. The answer only clearly excludes April and October.

## Section 5: architecture is fine, code is not

The monitor set is roughly complete. The dependency design is not: “AUROC drop + calibration error → retrain” is exactly what April would have fired. A concept-drift retrain must be gated on healthy data-quality **and** adequate maturity; that gate is not stated.

The Python block fails several explicit requirements:

- It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`. It invents `dataframe_to_eventlog` and `variant_to_df`.
- Diagnosis normalization is written into `diagnosis_activity` and then ignored; the log is built from raw `activity`.
- `drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity"])` is not a migration-duplicate strategy; it destroys legitimate rework and is applied to every month.
- “Event-calendar” and “case-start” cohorts are the **same** `filter_time_range` call. Their different interpretations are not noted. Case-start cohorts need case start timestamps; calendar cohorts need event-time filters. `{month}-31` is also invalid for 30-day months.
- DFG objects are treated as graphs with `.edges()`. Pm4py DFGs are dictionaries.

For a “sketch,” some abbreviation is allowed. Shipping an unused remap and two identical cohorts is not a sketch of the audit you just recommended.

## Net

This is a competent walkthrough that would pass a loose rubric because it refuses a single-drift story and keeps October out of the performance time series. It is not a careful audit. The required diagnosis table mislabels April’s activity-schema break as label delay, July/August V3 shares are wrong, concept/process/population are smeared, May and September’s partial defects are minimized, recalibration advice is unsafe, and the one concrete artifact does not do what the prompt asked. Those are not polish issues; they are the difference between narrating the right months and being able to act on them.
