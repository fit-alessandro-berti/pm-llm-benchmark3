**8.0**

The answer is a strong, process-aware compliance opinion: it separates facts from assumptions, refuses a single total ordering, applies rules at commitment time, refuses to let later approvals or ERP postings cure defects, and produces mixed classifications instead of an emergency-or-nothing result. It is not nearly flawless. Several interpretive moves are cleaner than the record supports, and a few case outcomes sit in mild tension with the method the author announced.

**What is done well.** Authority is handled by control dimension rather than by document prestige. ECD-2 is allowed to move local PO timing and the supplier register, and is not silently promoted into an A7 waiver of A1/A3. D is confined, at least in principle, to Finance/operational form after 15 July 07:20 and is not applied retroactively to P-502. Scope is actually used: the cafeteria freezer is out; the three-day backup pump is doubtful; post-expiry chemicals are outside the window. ON-44 is treated as an evidential, not a normative, fact. A6 is applied rather than recited. Missing actor lineage is allowed to block a positive finding in P-505 and P-507. The alternatives section is genuine: several of them would actually flip the label, and the author is willing to say which premise has to move.

**Methodological defects.** The most important one is the Finance/budget-owner substitution. D3 gives the CFO authority to interpret Finance controls; it does not make two Incident Command members the A2 pair of “independent budget owner” and “Finance Controller.” D1’s phrase “all operational approvals” is then asked to do more work than it can carry, especially next to D4 and the charter fact that IC members are not designated budget owners. Labeling this an assumption is honest; treating it as robust enough to underwrite a primary `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` on a EUR 155,000 unapproved-supplier buy is not. A stricter reading of the author’s own burden rule — Northbridge must prove the exception — would have left P-508 `INDETERMINATE` or, if D is denied A2 effect, `NONCOMPLIANT`.

That produces an internal inconsistency. P-505 and P-507 are held back because actor identity or a C3 record is missing. P-508 is released despite missing “exact C3 recording evidence,” missing controlled status for D, and missing any designation of IC as the incident budget owner. Those are not lesser gaps. C3’s four-hour record is a constitutive condition of the very pathway the author uses to justify P-508.

Two smaller doctrinal slips matter because they are used as load-bearing reasons. First, B5 is invoked to support a *timing* conclusion about B2. B5 says Finance is not waived; it does not say when Finance must occur. The pre-commitment reading of B2 is still defensible from the nature of an approval gate and from B4’s narrow wording, but B5 does not prove it. Second, C4’s express claim of precedence over “ordinary … supplier-onboarding requirements” is a better rival to A3’s *new-supplier* prong than the alternatives section admits. For P-502 that rival still loses on A2, because D did not yet exist; calling the onboarding reading “weak” is therefore too quick, even if the overall `NONCOMPLIANT` label is right.

**Case-level problems.** P-501 is a close case treated with more confidence than the text of B2 warrants. The SOP never says Plant/Finance must precede commitment; the author’s alternative is not a fringe reading. `NONCOMPLIANT` is acceptable under a stated gate-timing interpretation; “medium–high” is not. P-505 overweights the absence of `Create_PO`. C1 permits commitment first; a same-day, end-of-window sensor buy can be incomplete in the file without that absence deciding the merits. The right residual issue is actor independence, not the missing PO. P-502, P-503, P-504, and P-506 are the solid findings: time-sliced correctly, and not rescued by emergency atmosphere. P-503 in particular is well layered — A1 and A3 still fail even if C’s scope and D’s Finance theory are granted.

**Taxonomy and wording.** Severity is not a stable metric: P-508’s “moderate residual governance risk” is a residual-risk comment, not the same scale as P-503’s “critical.” P-502’s “cannot prospectively authorize an already completed commitment” is the wrong adverb; the intended point is that D is not retroactive. Neither error changes a holding, but both are sloppy in a product that otherwise trades on precision.

**Sections 6–7.** The four-way split among policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and failure of proof is the right evidential grammar, and the monitoring list is tied to the failure modes actually found. The replacement clause covers the requested topics and includes a real anti-normalization brake. It is also heavier than the analysis that precedes it (four-signature 24-hour CRO fallback; 24-hour containment definition) and quietly hard-codes the IA-style 24-hour reading that Section 2 correctly refused to treat as C’s text. That is a policy choice, not a reconstruction of the governing sources.

**Bottom line.** This is a high-quality, judge-aligned opinion with real issue-specific hierarchy and almost no catastrophic errors. It is not a 9+: it leans on a generous D-as-A2 theory, applies its own proof burden unevenly to the one “justified” case, and overclaims on B5 and C4. Those are structural nicks, not formatting nits. An 8.0 is already generous under a hypercritical standard; a nearly flawless answer would have left P-508 open, calibrated P-501’s confidence to B2’s silence, and not dismissed C4’s onboarding language as weak.
