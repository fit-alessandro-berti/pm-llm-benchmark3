**5.4**

The answer is complete, well structured, and hits every required heading. It is not a good assignment policy under strict reading of the scenario. The write-up looks rigorous; the actual batch does not.

## Core failure: the recommended batch is internally inconsistent

The policy’s value is the C001–C016 table. That table violates the authors’ own hard constraint C7 (do not assign work a resource cannot finish in remaining hours) and contradicts evidence they themselves restated.

**Celine (6.0h, already 6 open cases) is the worst example.** She is given:

- C001 as *primary* on an urgent Security Review (3h SLA, complexity 0.88, “needs specialist”) at Security skill **1**
- C007 high-priority Rework (6h SLA, complexity 0.81, already 3 reassignments)
- C003 Diagnosis (3rd reassignment)
- C009 as supervisor

That is the batch cap of 4, treated as if count were capacity. It is not. Historical evidence in the prompt: keeping a weak-skill owner adds a median **3.3h of service time**. C001 has **3h** remaining. Expected outcome is an SLA miss on an enterprise Security Review, plus C007 competing for the same 6 hours. Supervision by Hugo does not remove Celine’s service-time penalty; it adds coordination latency across Team A and Risk.

This also fights the authors’ own P2 and urgent weights (SLA and skill dominate; training off). They then say C001 is **not** training because Celine’s priority is Negotiation. So the batch takes trainee risk on the most sensitive urgent item **without** a development payoff. That is the worst available trade-off, not a reasoned one.

**Eva (5.0h)** is assigned C005 (high, 8h SLA) and C016 (high, complexity 0.79, 7h SLA), plus C015 shadowing. Two high-priority jobs with SLA windows longer than her remaining day, on top of 8 open cases, again violate C7. Continuity on C016 is defensible in isolation; the joint load is not.

**Farid (6.5h)** gets two urgents (C002 2h + C011 4h) plus C010 sign-off. That is at least coherent, but the policy never sequences by urgency or re-checks residual hours after each assign. Sequential greedy scoring without a priority sort is how Celine/Eva get buried.

## Scoring math does not do what it claims

`SLA(c)` does not depend on `r`. Adding `0.35 * SLA(c)` (or any priority-specific constant) to every candidate **does not change the argmax**. SLA urgency only sneaks in via weight shifts on other terms. There is no resource-specific “can this person finish before the deadline given service time, open work, and hours left.” That is the quantity C7 needed, and it is missing from the score.

Skill for a supervised pair is taken as the **supervisor’s** skill (1.0). A Celine+Hugo pair therefore ties a Hugo-only specialist on skill, then wins on continuity (1.0 vs 0.1). That is why C001 happens. It is a formulation bug: it ignores trainee speed, coordination, and the 3.3h evidence. Continuity penalty for non-owners on high-handover work is so extreme (0.1) that it can dominate urgent Security even when the owner is skill 1.

Resilience is not operationalized (“slightly penalize”, “increase when slack allows”). Overtime is double-handled as a near-hard filter and a Load term that, in the worked formula, only subtracts for **medium** OT. When a high-OT resource is admitted under the urgent exception, the example Load score does not punish them.

No joint/batch optimization, no “assign urgent first,” no supervisor-hour accounting. The 4-assignment cap is necessary but nowhere near sufficient.

## Constraint handling is sloppy in places

- **C5 is restated incorrectly.** The prompt: prefer continuity *unless* owner skill is 0 or 1 **and no supervision is feasible**. The answer: unless skill is 0 or 1 **and** supervision/reassignment is needed for risk. Different predicate. C001 is then sold as C5-compliant; even under the prompt’s wording, 3h SLA + specialist-only notes should have overridden.
- **Constraint 6 vs skill 0.** Security may be done by skill 3, or skill **1–2** under Gita/Hugo. Skill 0 is “not allowed.” C015’s notes invite Eva/Jonas *with supervision*, so treating Eva as a **shadow** while Gita performs is a fair reading—if labeled as observation, not assignment. The table still lists a supervised pair and counts it toward the 20% training quota. C010 is worse: Hugo has **Approval 0**. The prompt allows skill **1** to prepare, not finalize. Skill 0 is outside that carve-out. Calling Hugo an “Approval trainee” pads training share.
- Training share **3/9 ≈ 33%** only holds if skill-0 shadows count. Two of three “training” rows are that pattern (C010, C015). C012 is explicitly “suitable for trainee” and is not used; the Bruno/OT reason is fine, but then C013 dumps Eligibility on Jonas instead of using slack for a cleaner training story.
- C015 moves a normal Security Review **off Hugo (skill 3, current owner, low OT)** onto Gita so Eva can shadow. Eva can shadow Hugo. The extra handover is unearned.

Several other rows are reasonable in isolation: C002/C011 with Farid, C004 with Amal, C005 with Eva, C007 with Celine *if she were not also on C001*, C008 with Gita, C014 off David, C006 off Iris onto Jonas, OT-high staff kept off normal work, batching C006/C012 legally.

## Comparison with baselines is generic

Section 4 names the right dimensions and does not claim a unique optimum. It never **runs** first-available or specialist-only on this queue. Without those assignments, claims about throughput, SLA, and fairness are essays, not a comparison. First-available would concentrate on Jonas/Farid/Celine; specialist-only would pile Security on Gita/Hugo and Diagnosis on Celine/David. Showing that side by side was the obvious way to make trade-offs concrete. It is absent.

## What is actually strong

Section 1 separates hard safety rules from preferences. Section 2 has every required ingredient (eligibility, SLA, skill, continuity, load/OT, training, resilience, ties, exceptions) and logs `assignment_reason`. The worker text is non-punitive and auditable. Section 6 covers all mandated indicators with definition, computation, and target; deviation replay is the right audit idea. Section 7 actually uses the listed PM4Py surfaces (handover/work-together/similarity, roles, service/wait, performance DFG, batches, variants, filters).

## Remaining inaccuracies (smaller but real)

- `pm4py.filter_case_performance(..., performance_metric="throughput_time")` is not the API; it is `min_performance` / `max_performance` in seconds. `some_threshold` is a stub.
- Random tie-breaks sit poorly next to an audit requirement; a logged deterministic key would be the explainable choice.
- SNA is described as informing resilience/supervisor pairing, then not used in the batch (C001 pairs Celine with Hugo rather than Gita without network evidence).
- High-OT exclusion is harder than the prompt’s “avoid.” That is a legitimate design choice, but it freezes Bruno’s Quality training indefinitely; the monitoring note is a patch, not a policy.

## Net

This is a fluent policy memo with a broken dispatcher. Format, explainability, and audit scaffolding are well above average. The scoring function does not rank on SLA, the recommended table overloads Celine and Eva, and the single most important case (C001) is assigned in a way the prompt’s own numbers say will miss. Under a hypercritical bar, that caps the grade well below “strong.”
