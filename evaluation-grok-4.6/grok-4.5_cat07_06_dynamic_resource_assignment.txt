6.6

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly aligned with the required headings. It correctly refuses a single optimum, separates hard safety rules from soft preferences, and is strongest in the worker explanation and the audit/PM4Py framing. It is not close to flawless. The live assignment set contradicts the policy in several places, the scoring model is only partly operational, and there are factual and logical defects that a dispatcher could not trust.

**Format and coverage.** All seven required top-level sections are present, and the assignment table has the requested columns. That is table-stakes compliance, not excellence. Comparison operators and set-membership symbols are missing throughout (`priority  {high, urgent}`, `complexity  0.75`, `all  4`, `skill  1`), so several “hard” rules are literally unreadable as written.

**Policy design.** The hard/soft split (H1–H9 vs S1–S6) is the best part of the design. Security, approval finalization, the batch cap, quality-batching, and the reassignment-fragility gate are encoded in the right spirit. Two design failures sit underneath that:

- **U does not choose a resource.** `U = 1/(1+sla_hours)` is constant for a case. Adding `5U` (or `8U`) to every option does not ask whether *this* person can finish given remaining hours, current open work, and other cases already taken in the same batch. There is no finish-feasibility or residual-capacity term, even though “hours remaining today” is in the snapshot.
- **F and R are slogans.** Fairness is “fewer opens, more hours, then multiply by OT.” Resilience is “don’t stack Security/Approval.” Neither is specified enough to reproduce a score. The published formula is therefore not an algorithm; it is a narrative with weights.
- **Pseudocode is order-dependent.** The overtime gate and H8/H9 filters run *inside* the per-resource loop and call `no_safer(opts)` before the option set is complete. Eligibility then depends on scan order. That is a correctness bug, not a sketch omission.
- **H7 is over-encoded.** The scenario says avoid loading high-OT people unless the case is urgent *and* no safer alternative exists. The policy treats non-urgent high-OT as a hard ban, including for work the person **already owns**. That is the wrong object: “do not pile on new work” is not “strip existing ownership and hand it to a saturated specialist.”

**Recommended assignments — material errors.**

The table is mostly legal (no skill-0 work, no unsupervised high-priority trainee, cap ≤ 4, C007/C016 continuity, C001 specialist on a 3h enterprise Security case, Eva/Jonas correctly blocked from Security because the matrix is 0). The operational choices still fail the policy’s own objectives.

1. **R6 is miscounted and overloaded.** Assignments are C002, C010, C011 — three cases, not “R6:4”. C002 (urgent approval, 2h) and C011 (urgent VIP negotiation, 4h) both correctly stay with Farid on skill and ownership. Adding **C010** (normal approval) onto the only person who already holds two competing sub-4h SLAs, with 5 open cases and 6.5h left, is the worst decision in the set. The comparison section then claims the policy is “strong on the urgent set.” That is inconsistent.

2. **C010 should not have been pulled off R2.** Bruno already owns it, Approval skill 2, 0 reassignments, low handover, 20h SLA. H7 does not require a handover. Legal finalizers are only R2, R5, and R6. R5 already has C005+C016. Moving C010 to R6 is exactly the specialist pile-up the text criticizes. The queue even flags “trainee approval under supervision”; the prepare+finalize path is defined and then deliberately unused.

3. **C014 → R8 fights resilience.** Hugo already takes C001 (urgent Security, 3h, complexity 0.88) and C015. Dumping normal rework on the Security bottleneck, when R1 has Rework 2, medium OT, and spare batch slots, ignores the R term and S4. This reads as specialist-only with an OT veto, not the stated multi-objective rule.

4. **Training quota is missed and under-analyzed.** 1/9 normal-low cases (C009) is 11%, not 20%. The skill-0 gaps for Eva/Jonas on Security are correctly called out; the symmetric gaps are not: R4 and R8 have training priority **approval** with Approval skill **0**. C010’s “trainee approval” note is therefore also a master-data problem, not just an OT problem. Stretch training (C012 is labeled suitable for a trainee) is not used, and the 0.85-score quota rule is never shown working.

5. **R3 is the quiet single point of failure.** C003 + C007 (high-complexity rework, 3 prior reassignments, 6h SLA) + supervision of C009 is a lot of “continuity and training” parked on one person. The risk is mentioned, not managed.

Several other rows are fine (C004, C005, C008, C011, C016, Quality batch C006+C012 on Jonas). That does not offset the load-allocation failures on the scarce Approval/Security people.

**Comparison.** The seven-dimension table is directionally right: first-available will breach H2/H3 (C001→R3 skill 1; C002→R10 skill 1 cannot finalize); specialist-only will queue Risk/Approval and ignore OT. But the baselines are never instantiated on C001–C016, so claims about throughput, SLA, and fairness are unfalsifiable. The proposed policy is also not scored; the table is post-hoc judgment. You cannot claim the algorithm produced these rows.

**Worker explanation.** This is the strongest section: non-punitive OT language, named supervision, explicit permission to override, and the Eva/Jonas matrix gap handled as a planning input. It still inherits the C010/C014 story, so the “transparent reasons” would not survive a shop-floor challenge from Farid or Hugo.

**Audit indicators and PM4Py.** The ten indicator families match the prompt and are the right socio-technical scorecard. The sketch cites every listed PM4Py symbol. It is still a sketch with broken pieces: `filter_event_attribute_values(..., [x for x in ... if x >= 0.75])` is not valid use; `filter_case_performance(..., 0, 1e9)` filters nothing; `eligible` / `score` / `audit_policy` trail into `...`. Useful as a lifecycle story (calibrate handover penalties, role similarity for backups, batch discovery for H6), not as implementable code.

**Net.** A competent design essay with a legally careful constraint layer and good explainability/audit thinking. Under a strict reading it fails the thing the question actually grades hardest: a reproducible policy whose *current-queue* decisions honor continuity, OT protection, resilience, and the training floor at the same time. The R6 count error, the C010 handover, the C014 specialist dump, the non-discriminating urgency term, and the order-dependent pseudocode keep this well below a high score.
