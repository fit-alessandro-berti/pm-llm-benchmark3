**3.0**

The write-up has the required skeleton and a few locally correct observations (QB’s first interrupt, D05’s exclusive skill match, the need for dispatch-time eligibility logs). It fails the actual task: reconstruct decision sets and test competing disciplines against them. Several “observed selections,” eligible sets, and preemption claims are factually false. The QA diagnosis inverts the evidence. Confidence numbers treat that inversion as near-certainty.

**QA is the clearest failure.** The log is non-preemptive appointment + residual FIFO, not priority.

- At 08:20 eligible work includes A02 (p3), A03 (p3, appointment 08:20), and A04 (p1, est 7). The observed start is A03. Priority and SPT both predict A04. This single epoch already falsifies the claimed primary rule.
- At 08:30–08:32 the same p1 job is still waiting; A02 starts. FIFO predicts A02; priority and SPT predict A04.
- At 09:05 eligible work includes A05 (p3), A06 (p2, appointment 09:15), A07 (p3), and A08 (p1). A05 starts. FIFO predicts A05; priority predicts A08; SPT predicts A07.
- At 09:32 A07 starts with A08 (p1) waiting.

A04 and A08 never preempt anyone. Every `service_segments` cell in QA is a single interval. A1’s calibration 08:52–08:58 is the gap after A02, not a priority event. A06’s “late” start is what non-preemptive FIFO produces after A05; the appointment is not a pull-forward.

The diagnosis table nonetheless says “non-preemptive priority,” confidence 90, strongest support “A04 preempts A05,” and the 08:32 row reports **observed selection A04**. The data say A02 ran 08:32–08:52. That is not a debatable interpretation. It is a misread of the event log. The 08:02 row also lists A02 as eligible before its 08:04 arrival. The prose claim that “priority 1 jobs always preempt lower-priority jobs” is false on every p1 job in the queue. The quality bar warned against reading FIFO deviations as priority; this answer does that and then fabricates the deviations.

**QB is only half-seen.** B02 interrupting B01 at 09:16 is real preemptive behavior, and the recorded reason is “urgent arrival.” That does not identify a standing preemptive-priority rule.

- At 09:31 B02 completes. B03 (p2) is waiting; B01 (p3) resumes. Pure priority would take B03.
- At 09:48 the second interrupt (“dispatcher reassignment”) starts B03 while **B05 (p1, arrived 09:46)** is waiting. Priority predicts B05. The answer’s 09:48 set is {B03, B04} and its note says B03 starts after B02 completes. Both are wrong: B01 ran 09:31–09:48, and B05 is omitted.
- At 10:14 B01 resumes again while B06 (p2) is waiting.
- At 10:28 B04 (p3) starts with B06 (p2) and B07 (appointment 10:30) waiting.

The residual pattern is closer to resume-the-interrupted-job plus discretionary reassignment than to preemptive priority. Secondary “skill-based (general)” is vacuous: one resource, every job `general`. B07 is a soft appointment, but it is not the strongest contradiction.

**QC’s deadline story is built on a false comparison.** At 10:10 C1 is first free. The backlog is C01 (p3, dl 13:00, est 36), C02 (p3, dl 14:00, est 8), C03 (p2, dl 12:30, est 14), C04 (p3, dl 14:30, est 22); C05 may or may not be visible given a 2-minute scan delay. Observed start: C02. That is SPT (or SEPT on the opening backlog), not EDD and not priority. C03’s deadline 12:30 is earlier than C02’s 14:00. The answer’s statement that C02 “has earliest deadline (14:00)” is false, and C01 is missing from the eligible set.

Later epochs also do not support a stable SLA/priority rule:

- 10:32: C06 (est 6) is present; C04 starts. SPT, priority, EDD, and FIFO all miss.
- 10:54: C05 starts after its 10:50 deadline — compatible with a late priority/SLA exception, which the note already flags, not with a queue-wide law.
- 11:34: C06 starts; C01 (older, same priority) is still waiting. The answer lists {C06, C07}, omits C01, and calls FIFO’s prediction C06. FIFO’s prediction is C01.
- 11:40: C01 starts over C07 (higher priority, tighter deadline, shorter estimate). That episode contradicts the stated hybrid and is the reason C01 looks like a deferred long job from an initial SPT batch.

“Deadline-driven (priority + SLA)” with confidence 80 overfits one annotated job and ignores the opening SPT choice the table actually shows.

**QD: skill constraints are real; preemptive priority is not.** D-M / D-F / D-X eligibility and D05’s unique (firmware+electrical) match are hard constraints. They are not a discovered priority rule. There is no preemption in QD.

- 11:00 is not a choice between D01 and D02; they require different resources.
- 11:21 is not D03 “preempting” D04. D-F cannot do mechanical work; D03 is p2, D04 is p3; D-M is still inside D01.
- 11:25 D-M starts D04 (p3, est 10) with D08 (p1, est 8) waiting. FIFO-within-skill predicts D04; priority and SPT predict D08. This is the discriminating mechanical decision. The answer never uses it.
- 11:20–11:30 D-X idles into D06’s appointment. That is appointment hold, not priority.
- 11:48 D-X starts D07 (p3) with D10 (p1) waiting. Again FIFO-within-skill, against priority and SPT.

The “strongest contradicting episode” is labeled as D08 preempting D09. D09 had not started. D08 vs D09 is consistent with FIFO, SPT, *and* priority, so it cannot support a priority secondary component. Confidence 95 is unjustified.

**Section 2 systematically violates the required comparison.** Eligible sets drop waiting jobs and add ineligible or not-yet-arrived ones. Skill-based predictions are missing from the QA/QB/QC tables. Several rows are not decision epochs (a job finishing is not “selecting” that same job). Competing rules are scored against the wrong set, so apparent “wins” for priority are artifacts.

**Section 3 cites supporting IDs that do not do what the text claims** (QA “preemption” of A05/A07; QD “preemption” of D09). Real contradictions (A03 and A02 over A04; A05/A07 over A08; B05 waiting at 09:48; C02 first despite later deadline; D04 over D08; D07 over D10; C01 over C07) are omitted or rebranded as minor appointment softness.

**Section 4 does not discriminate.** The B07 test asserts that no p1/p2 work is pending in 10:30–10:56; B06 (p2) occupies 10:36–10:56, and at 10:30 B1 is still inside B04. The D08 test treats a non-preemption as preemption and contrasts two hypotheses that this episode cannot separate. The actual discriminator already in the log is D04 vs D08 (FIFO vs priority/SPT).

**Section 5 is generic and mis-ranked.** Strategic `est_min` inflation is placed first even though the author’s own primary labels are not SPT. Prompt-given mechanisms that actually bite — ±2 min scan delay, A1 calibration, C1 busy until 10:10, D-F’s meeting, D-X’s 11:20 open, missing interrupt reasons, appointment window vs point — are barely used. Hidden “urgent” flags are already written on B01.

**Section 6 is the strongest part** (decision time, eligibility set, remaining estimate, preemption reason, policy version). It is still a template list rather than a map from the specific observational equivalences above.

**Section 7 compounds the misdiagnosis.** QD is “most identifiable” only as a skill-constrained *system*, not as preemptive priority. QC is reasonably called messy. The “most dangerous claim” is said to be FIFO; FIFO is the residual rule that actually fits QA and within-skill QD. The dangerous overclaim on *this* log is exactly what the answer makes: stable priority (preemptive or not) from a handful of order inversions, plus “priority dominates when observable.” That last sentence is inconsistent with the same author’s QA/QB/QD timelines once those timelines are read correctly.

**Net:** format compliance and scattered local hits cannot offset inverted QA, invented preemptions, wrong eligible sets, a factually false QC deadline comparison, a vacuous QB skill component, and 80–95 confidence on under-identified rules. This is not a near-miss. It is a structured write-up of an incorrect reconstruction.
