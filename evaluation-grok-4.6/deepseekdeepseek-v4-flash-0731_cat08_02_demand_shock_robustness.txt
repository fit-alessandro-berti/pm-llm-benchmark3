**6.5**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and hits every required section. The four scenarios vary by mix and disruption type, bottleneck migration is mostly sequenced with real DFG/queue numbers, I4 and I9 are correctly rejected, I1–I3 are refined instead of adopted naively, and residual risk includes failures created by the redesign. That is a solid process-design answer. It is not close to flawless.

**Material reasoning errors**

The most serious package-level contradiction is R3 under S1. Preferring M1 for Urgent, Complex-panel, and repeat-after-failure work assigns roughly 70% of S1 volume to one of two machines that are already at 98% combined utilization. The text flags M1 as a single point of failure only if M2 is down. It never checks whether the *default* routing already overloads M1 in the winter mix it claims to survive. R2’s dual-machine slot reservations and R3’s M1 preference also sit in unresolved tension.

Process-mining use is uneven. Peak DFG counts are sometimes handled correctly as same-window shares (8,530 / 9,730). Elsewhere they are treated as if they were comparable to the 8-week log. “Technical validation → Medical validation rising to 3,420” is simply wrong as a rise: the normal-period count is 3,900. Weekly rates go the other way (about 488/week → 1,140/week). Hold-for-specialist 610 vs 290 is likewise cited raw, which badly understates the weekly jump (about 36 → 203). For an answer that is supposed to be judged on process-mining evidence, that is a real methodological miss.

Accession correction is under-solved relative to the data. Two specialists, 91% peak utilization, 14h median queue, and the note that corrections unblock many cases. The package mostly hopes I8/I6 reduce inbound errors. That is not a design for the station the log actually shows as a key unlock.

The dashboard’s Urgent green band (`p90 < 18h`) is inconsistent with the lab’s own normal period (`p90 = 19h`). Stable operations would already be yellow. The contractual 18h rule is a case-level SLA; they already have `% overdue`, so the p90 band is both unnecessary and miscalibrated.

**Smaller but genuine issues**

- M2 “repeat rate > 15%” is treated as an instrument-specific trigger. The log has no M2-only repeat rate. System-wide peak assay→repeat is already ~15%, so the trigger would chatter in ordinary peak conditions.
- Repeat-after-failure as an *incoming case type* (8–9% mix) is conflated with repeats *generated* by M2. S2’s 15% mix therefore mixes two different objects.
- R4 (routine autoverification) is a large invented intervention. It is clinically plausible and they flag regulation, but it is not grounded in the candidate set, and they lean on it to paper over the 101% technical-validation bottleneck.
- Fast-fail still consumes the 96% pre-analytical pool. They discuss the recollection second wave well, but not that I6 can *increase* contention at the same scarce station.
- R5 is still a capacity add. Framing it as a “control system, not a capacity build-out” oversells. There is also no rough check that one evening medical-validation shift can dent a 22h median queue at 93% utilization.
- Registration overtime/error-after-9h, courier 7.5h queue mechanics, and “tech validators cross-trainable in 4 weeks” (train *toward* the bottleneck, not away) are sitting in the brief and barely used.
- A few trade-off cells are empty calories (“Neutral”, “Minimal direct effect”). Table arrows are dropped, which makes the S1 evidence cell harder to read than it should be.

**What is actually good**

Priority hierarchy is explicit; they do not pretend every case type can be first. Downstream overload is discussed for the main moves. I1/I2/I3 conflict hints are used rather than ignored. S3 correctly treats medical validation and specialist review as a coupled loop (`Hold for specialist → Medical validation`). S2’s recovery wave into technical validation is the right kind of bottleneck-migration point. Redesign-induced residual risk (front-end acceleration jamming validation; aggressive reject creating a 42–60h recollection echo) is specific and fair. Leading vs lagging on the dashboard is clean, and ON/OFF triggers have hysteresis.

Those strengths keep it well above a mediocre answer. The M1/S1 hole, the un-normalized DFG comparison, the accession gap, and the broken Urgent p90 band keep it well short of a high score under a strict reading.
