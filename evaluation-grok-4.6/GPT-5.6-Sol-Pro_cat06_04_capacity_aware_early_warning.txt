**7.5**

The answer is one of the stronger responses this prompt can produce: it refuses I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07 on evidence rather than alert count, treats effect ranges as unidentified, and keeps spillover and a reserve slot inside the allocation. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several decision-theoretic and operational breaks are material.

**Decision rule.** The eligibility, persistence, calibration-band, and no-repeat-without-new-evidence gates are deployable and correctly refuse to treat `p_now` as P(breach | swarm). The conservative floor \(D_i^L\) is computed accurately for every case. The problem starts when the rule also requires \(M_i^{\text{base}}>0\) and \(G_i^{\text{base}}>0\). Those objects are defined and then barely used. For I-07, \(M^{\text{base}}=800-6s_t\bar V_U\). At a mid spillover of 0.02 this is already negative if untreated diagnosis cases are worth more than about €6.7k — which is almost certainly true in this book. I-07 is nevertheless labeled **A1 / Intervene**. The later “urgency lane” can justify that, but the write-up never shows the exception firing, so Section 1 and Section 2 are not the same policy. LRM is written as a formula and then abandoned for raw time-to-SLA; that makes the most formal-looking piece of the rule decorative.

**Allocation.** The table is the right shape and the headline choices are defensible: I-03 out, I-05 suppressed, I-06 blocked on imputation, I-08 failing the robust-value gate, I-04 parked for option value, one soft reserve. Three issues keep this from being tight.

- Persistence is applied unevenly. I-01 and I-10 are first alerts and, under the two-refresh rule, are not yet actionable; I-04 and I-08 are still marked eligible. Either the persistence gate is real or it is not.
- I-02 as default third slot is fragile in the answer’s own arithmetic. Mid effect 0.15 versus \(6\times 0.02=0.12\) expected induced breaches is a thin margin, and Strategic Enterprise probabilities are the ones the model *overstates*. The hedge (“cancel if congestion is high”) is correct; making I-02 a named member of the 12:00 portfolio still over-commits relative to the rule.
- I-07’s 1.5 h clock is not reconciled with a 45-minute swarm that can start only after triage and must leave time for resolution and validation. The supplied effect range says a swarm still moves risk, so selection is allowed — but an operational policy has to say whether the case is already past last responsible moment. Sequential specialist capacity is also missing: two people imply one swarm at a time, so I-02 would not start at 12:00.

**Impact table.** The arithmetic is auditable and, on its own terms, correct: optimistic 0.65–0.84, base ~0.65, adverse 0.26–0.38; costs €1.35k; spillover 0.18–0.54 in base. Refusing to invent \(\bar V_U\) and reporting break-evens is honest. Weaker points: full false-alarm burdens are subtracted as if all three interventions were false alarms (flagged as conservative, still conceptually mixed); the table prices a three-swarm book and then argues for stopping after two, so incremental slot value is discussed in prose rather than shown; “unusually high” untreated values of €26k–€57k is the wrong adjective given visible diagnosis-stage values of €15k–€40k. Confidence labels are appropriately low.

**Temporal policy.** This is the cleanest section. Collapsing I-05’s 0.55→0.57 no-event third refresh into one episode, and re-opening I-07 because the new log is a new evidence version despite a *fall* in \(p\), is exactly what the prompt tests. Cool-down, commander-approved re-swarm, and re-entry without erasing the original timestamp are operationally usable. Minor nit: “two refresh cycles or 60 minutes” is redundant with a 30-minute refresh and could desynchronize if the cadence changes.

**Fairness and feedback.** Concrete, not slogan-level: dual lanes, 90th-percentile value cap, written override when urgency loses to contract value, no extra NNLS penalty, hierarchical borrowing, treatment/propensity logging, frozen pre-treatment scores, per-queue externality budget. Selecting I-07 while I-04 waits is a consistent demonstration. Residual gaps: Public Sector duty of care is not treated as a non-monetary constraint (I-01 is only a timing deferral); spillover concentration on untreated queues is described well but never mapped onto the actual live book (who the six untreated diagnosis cases *are*).

**Monitoring.** Broad and in the right causal shape: randomized near-tie assignment, interference at queue-block level, shadow scores, untreated-only calibration, champion–challenger for rule changes, and explicit tighten/loosen triggers. It is slightly generic (0.03 bias, 20-minute delay, monthly fairness) and does not specify how a champion–challenger policy is run when only four slots exist.

**Net.** The answer hits every “strong answer” checkpoint in the rubric and does not commit the classic failures (rank by \(p_{\text{now}}\), swarm I-03, treat I-05 like I-07, ignore externalities). It loses a large amount of credit because the formal marginal rule is not the rule that actually picks the book, I-07’s economic and timing status is unresolved, I-02 is over-selected relative to calibration and spillover, and persistence/LRM are stated more strictly than they are applied. That is a coherent, professional policy draft with several internal seams — not a near-flawless decision memo.
