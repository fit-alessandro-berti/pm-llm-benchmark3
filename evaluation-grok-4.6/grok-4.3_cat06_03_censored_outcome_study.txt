**4.8**

The answer has the right high-level instincts: it does not recode rejects/withdrawals as non-default, it treats activation as a selection mechanism, it keeps an as-of rule in prose, and it uses a temporal rather than random split. That is enough to escape the failure modes named in the prompt. It is not enough for a high score. The protocol is internally inconsistent, the maturity arithmetic is wrong, the cohort table does not implement the stated design, and the PM4Py skeleton would both fail and leak.

## Target and estimand

The primary estimand, \(P(\text{default}_{90d}=1\mid\text{activation},\text{prefix})\), is a legitimate quantity and is correctly distinguished from a funnel-wide competing-risk question. Two serious problems remain.

**Prediction time is not operational.** “The first event after Credit Review (or the 3rd event, whichever occurs later)” is not a well-defined stopping time. In this process the third event is typically Credit Review itself; the next event may be a document request, a second Credit Review, Accept, Reject, or Withdraw. After a document loop the “first event after Credit Review” is ambiguous (first review or last review?). At that clock time activation is still unknown, so a model of \(P(Y\mid A=1,X)\) cannot be used as an early warning on *every* application without either a second activation model or a change of estimand to \(P(Y=1,A=1\mid X)\) or \(P(Y=1\mid X)\) under a policy. Section 8 mentions the counterfactual gap but never closes this deployment mismatch.

**Maturity dates do not do what the text claims.** Extraction is 15 February 2026; the feed arrives 7–35 days *after* the 90-day horizon. Activation on 15 November 2025 yields a horizon of 13 February 2026 and a feed no earlier than 20 February 2026 — after extraction — even under the *minimum* delay. A conservative cutoff is approximately 13 October 2025 (90 + 35 days). The protocol asserts that 15 November “ensures” maturity including feed delay, then assigns all 21,600 labeled rows to that rule. Both the arithmetic and the identification are wrong.

The alternative target is directionally sound but underspecified (why 180 days? is activation a competing event, an intermediate state, or a mark on a multi-state process?).

## Cohort construction

The inclusion line is literally incomplete (“start timestamp  31 August 2025”). More importantly, **Section 2 excludes post-1 July 2025 starts until “sufficient volume” exists, while Section 7 puts July–August 2025 starts in the test set.** Those rules cannot both be in force.

The flow table is a relabeling of the prompt’s headcounts, not a cohort constructed under the stated as-of rules. The 21,600 labeled export is *not* “started by 31 August 2025 and activated by 15 November 2025.” Starts continue through 31 December 2025; some labeled accounts will have started after 31 August, and some pre-31 August starts sit in the 3,600 immature or 5,100 open bins. Late 2025 starts that the inclusion rule would drop never appear as a row, yet the table still totals 48,000. Transfer 60% → 1,740/1,160 is the only derived split that is computed rather than copied.

For the *primary* target, rejected, withdrawn, never-activated open, and most transfers are not “competing terminal states” or “ordinary right-censoring”; they are outside the activated risk set. The table mixes the primary estimand with the alternative.

## Censoring and missingness

The taxonomy names the right mechanisms and correctly flags the “rejected ⇒ non-default” assumption as untestable. The analysis plan then collapses.

A **binary 90-day default among activated accounts** (Section 1) is not analyzed by a **cause-specific Cox or Fine–Gray model on the activated subpopulation** (Section 3). After activation, reject/withdraw are no longer competing. Fine–Gray on that subset does not answer the stated estimand. IPW for activation is a real idea, but positivity after a July 2025 policy break, overlap of partner-originated cases with permanently missing feeds, and the fact that many prefix-time covariates also determine activation are not discussed. “MAR sensitivity weight” is not a method.

Delayed labels are handled by dropping them (`label_available_at` before extract). That is complete-case administrative truncation, not a delay model. Sensitivity analyses (complete case vs MAR imputation) do not probe the actually dangerous MNAR story the prompt gives: partner-originated accounts with *elevated* permanent feed loss.

## Prefixes and features

Fixed \(k=3\) plus a 14-day elapsed prefix is only a sketch. Fourteen days is arbitrary. Short-case “tokens” are irrelevant to the primary target (those cases never activate). Rework-cycle features are mostly not yet observed at event 3. Normalization of “several” renamed activities is reduced to a one-entry map. The feature list is otherwise competent (static vs prefix vs historical reference; regime indicator; last *as-of* `risk_score`).

## Leakage audit

This is the strongest section. Dropping `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, and `label_available_at` is correct; repairing `documents_total` to the previous midnight is the right as-of fix; extraction-time resource experience is correctly flagged.

It is not complete. `default_90d` is repeated on events when known and is never audited. `source_system` (partner missingness) is absent. The repair for experience assumes a historical table the warehouse may not have; the fallback (drop) is missing. Comparison operators are again stripped (“last event  prediction time”).

## Validation

A random split is correctly rejected. The proposed calendar split does **not** “respect” the October 2025 migration: it simply ends before 15 October, so migration robustness is never measured. The test window is two months of a new policy, with no post-policy validation fold for calibration, and with activation-by-15-November inducing selection on *fast* activations. Time-dependent Brier and cumulative/dynamic AUC belong to the survival/competing-risk formulation, not to the declared binary target. There is no IPW-aware metric, no competing-risk calibration, no pre- vs post-policy paired backtest, and no plan for the 3,600 immature labels.

## Limitations

Section 8 is the other relatively clean piece: predictive association ≠ counterfactual default under activation, plus three plausible DGP shifts (threshold change, applicant response, resource reallocation). It should have stated the valid decision use in one sentence: rank or monitor *booked* risk under a stable acceptance policy, not score rejects and not set policy.

## Implementation skeleton

This is well below the bar of “concrete enough to implement.”

- `activation_time` aggregates the *timestamp* series and then tests `"Account Activated" in x.values`. That predicate is always false, so activation times are all `NaT` and `mature` is all false.
- `label_ready` does `df.set_index(...)["label_available_at"].max()`, a global scalar, then maps it; it is not a per-case maturity time.
- `cutoff` is unused; train/test are not restricted to mature, labeled, activated cases.
- `pm4py.filter_log_on_case_id` is not a standard top-level API (unlike `format_dataframe`, `get_prefixes_from_log`, `discover_dfg`). Unused old CSV-importer and timestamp-filter imports are dead weight.
- `discover_dfg(train_log)` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time(train_log)` run on **full traces**, so they ingest Contract Signed, Account Activated, collections, and default — exactly the leakage Section 6 forbids. The comment “justified before prediction” does not make the call valid.
- Post-prefix columns (`final_limit`, `collection_status`, `default_90d`, `decision_reason`) are never dropped. The “leakage guard” is a stub that returns temporal features.
- Required pieces are missing: elapsed-time prefixes, a real cohort-flow print, a leakage-audit assertion, and comments tying the table to IPW / survival / competing risks. No outcome column is built.

A skeleton that cannot construct labels and that computes waiting times on post-outcome traces is not an audit pipeline.

## Net

Conceptually this is a major-revisions protocol, not a weak or dishonest one. The grade is held down by a false maturity guarantee, a cohort table that ignores its own inclusion rules, a primary analysis that does not match the primary target, a validation plan that contradicts the cohort and does not test migration, and a PM4Py block with blocking bugs and live leakage. Those are design errors, not nits.
