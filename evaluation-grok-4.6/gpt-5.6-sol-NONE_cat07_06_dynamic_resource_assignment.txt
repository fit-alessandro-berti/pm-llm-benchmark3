**6.0**

The write-up is polished and complete, and sections 1, 2, 5, 6, and 7 are substantially stronger than the actual assignments. That polish conceals several hard-constraint violations, a miscount of the training rule, and a policy that is described as multi-objective but executed as “keep the current owner.” Under a strict reading this is a competent design essay with a failed operationalization, not a near-flawless answer.

**Section 3 is the decisive failure.** The recommended table is the policy’s only concrete output, and it breaks rules the answer itself treats as non-negotiable.

- **C015 → Eva + Hugo is illegal.** Eva’s Security score is **0**. The problem states 0 = not allowed. Rule 6 allows only skill 3, or skill **1–2** under Gita/Hugo. Skill 0 is outside that set. Section 1 says “Skill 0 is ineligible.” The PM4Py sketch would flag this row (`skill_level.fillna(0).eq(0)`). The notes calling Eva/Jonas training candidates are a designed conflict (both are Security 0). The correct move is keep Hugo and escalate the matrix/training-plan inconsistency. The answer instead violates a safety rule and then writes “Security and Approval constraints are met.”
- **Constraint 4 is violated twice on David, and again on other high-OT staff.** David has 14 open cases, **3.0 hours left**, and overtime risk **high**. He receives **C003 and C014**, both **normal** priority with 18h and 25h slack. The rule allows high-OT load only when SLA risk is **urgent** and no safer alternative exists. Safer alternatives exist: Celine (Diagnosis 3, Rework 3, low OT) for C003; Celine, Hugo, Iris, Jonas, Amal, or Eva for C014. Continuity after two reassignments is a real consideration for C003; it does not license a second non-urgent assignment to the most overloaded person in the pool.
- **C006/C012 “batching” is incoherent.** Batching in this setting (and in `discover_batches`) means **one resource** grouping similar work. C006 is given to Iris and C012 to Jonas, then described as batchable “if operationally compatible.” That is not a batch. The better move is obvious: Jonas (Quality 3, 4 open, 6h, low OT) takes **both** eligible Quality checks. Iris is high-OT with 11 cases; handover cost on C006 is low; continuity is weak. The answer unloads nobody and invents cross-person batching.
- **The 20% training claim is cooked.** Normal/low cases are **nine**, not eight (C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, C015). C013 is labeled “Yes — cross-skill development,” but Celine’s documented priority is **negotiation**, and she is already Eligibility 2. That assignment does not count. C012 is explicitly “suitable for trainee” and matches Bruno’s Quality priority (skill 1, low, 48h slack); it is thrown away as “little immediate training value.” Real documented training is C009 (and, if anyone, a supervised Bruno on C012). The portfolio check overstates compliance.
- **C001 → Gita is weakly justified and anti-resilient.** Hugo is also Security 3, with fewer cases, more hours, and **low** OT. Gita already has 10 cases and 4.5h and is then given C001 (urgent, 3h, complexity 0.88) **and** C008. The stated reason is “R7 has skill 3.” So does R8. This concentrates the scarce Security queue on the busier specialist and uses Hugo only as a supervisor.

Secondary load problems follow from the same continuity-default: Eva gets C005, C015, and C016 on 5.0 remaining hours; Farid gets two urgents (C002 at 2h, C011 at 4h) with no sequencing note; Celine keeps the one continuity-critical case (C007: high, 6h, 3 reassignments, high handover) and is then given extra work (C013 plus a C009 checkpoint). Hugo and Jonas are under-used. The “≤4 assignments” cap is met; **capacity** is not.

**The algorithm is specified but not used.** Section 2 lists the right ingredients (eligibility, slack, skill, continuity, load/OT, training, resilience, ties, escalate-don’t-invent). The weights even sum to 1.00. Then section 3 never computes slack, never scores candidates, and never solves a portfolio. Almost every row is “current owner.” That is a heuristic, not the procedure advertised. “Select the best feasible portfolio” is left as a slogan; 16 cases × 10 resources × supervised pairs is a combinatorial problem with no method (greedy-with-repair, ILP, or even a worked example). SLA scoring is defined with handover and supervision delay, then unused. Several “hard” capacity/OT exceptions are never actually logged.

**Section 4 is directionally right and empirically thin.** The qualitative contrast with first-available and specialist-only is fair, and the answer correctly refuses a single optimum. It does not **apply** either baseline to C001–C016, so claims about throughput, SLA, and fairness stay unfalsifiable. First-available would in fact put work on Jonas/Farid/Celine; specialist-only would pile Security on Gita/Hugo and Approval on Farid. Showing those tables was the obvious way to make the comparison auditable. “Slightly more decision/review overhead” is hand-waving.

**Sections 1, 5, 6, 7 are the reason this is not a 4.** Hard vs soft constraints are separated cleanly. Historical handover evidence is used accurately. The worker text is non-punitive and treats the policy as decision support. The audit set covers every required indicator and adds continuity retention, supervision burden, and reason completeness. The PM4Py sketch hits the requested APIs and is explicit that role/similarity discovery does not mint credentials. Those parts are good.

They do not rescue a recommendation that assigns Security work to skill 0, double-loads a high-OT resource on non-urgent work, miscounts the training quota, and claims constraints are satisfied. A strict grade cannot treat a well-written policy document as nearly flawless when its own assignments would fail its own audit.
