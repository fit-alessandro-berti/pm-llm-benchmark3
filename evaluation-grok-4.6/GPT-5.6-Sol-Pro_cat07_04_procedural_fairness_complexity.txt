8.4

The answer is unusually complete, constraint-compliant, and well calibrated. It still has several real technical and interpretive defects that keep it well short of a near-flawless score.

**What it does well.** It uses the required eight headings, covers every mandated fairness dimension, and never treats raw approval gaps as discrimination. It distinguishes total experienced burden from like-for-like decision disparity, refuses to treat `risk_score_band` as neutral, treats language and accommodation routes as rights-preserving *and* as sources of burden, and never proposes deleting those steps. The bottom line is appropriately split: structural problems in timeliness and capacity, unresolved residual decision/escalation gaps, and no warrant for a finding of intentional discrimination.

**Substantive flaws.**

1. **Invalid waiting-time decomposition.** The Kitagawa-style formula is a *mean* identity. Medians do not decompose that way. Writing “matched mean or median duration” papers over a real statistical error. Given that almost all supplied timing evidence is median/p90, this is not a cosmetic slip.

2. **Over-reading the preliminary adjusted time gaps.** The given model does *not* control for language need or accommodation request. Residual cycle-time gaps of +2.6 (B) and especially +4.1 (C) still include necessary extra work on V4/V5, not only avoidable delay. Section 2 understands this (do not control away the support route); Section 3 then cites those residuals as leftover operational burden. That is an inconsistent causal reading of the same table.

3. **Incomplete path accounting for Group C.** 62% request accommodation but only 31% are on V5. “Largely explicable by need” is too quick. The other half of C is on other variants; that matters for whether the burden is the accommodation route itself or a broader pattern of extra review, rework, and tail delay.

4. **PM4Py plan is strong in design, weaker as executable analysis.** The strategy is right: case-level filtering, variant shares not raw counts, first-decision vs post-decision clocks, no fake service times, discovery ≠ norm. But `defaultdict` is unused; several discover/filter parameter names (`perf_aggregation_key`, `considered_activities`, DECLARE support/confidence args) are at best version-contingent; and the code never actually implements the matching/weighting, within-variant outcome comparison, or resource-standardized counterfactual that Section 2 says is required. Caveating “pin the API” does not convert a sketch into a finished plan.

**Smaller issues.** No uncertainty is attached to the preliminary “adjusted differences,” yet later prose sometimes treats 2.2 pp or 0.9 days as if the point estimates were known. Applicant-response time is acknowledged in the limitations and then under-used when interpreting the 3.8d / 4.7d specialist waits. Formula blocks are poorly marked for Markdown. None of these are fatal; together they add looseness.

**Net.** This is a high-quality fairness assessment with the right structure, the right refusals, and the right bottom line. It is not nearly flawless: one formal decomposition is wrong, the main adjusted time residuals are over-interpreted relative to the omitted support-need controls, Group C’s path mix is underspecified, and the PM4Py section overclaims relative to what the code actually does. Under a strict standard those are enough to drop the score well below the 9–10 band.
