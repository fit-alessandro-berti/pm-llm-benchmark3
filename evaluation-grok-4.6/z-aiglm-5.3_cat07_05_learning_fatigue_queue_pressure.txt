**Serious issues (these cap the score).**

1. **A category error is treated as a main finding.** Mean `case_complexity_score` (e.g. 0.43) is used as a mixing weight between the “low complexity” and “high complexity” cells. A mean score is not a high-complexity share, and the WIP table never defines the band cutoffs. The interpolation that produces “~8.0% vs observed 7.9%” and “almost no residual position effect” is therefore not identified. That claim is then promoted from a “consistency check” to the spine of Section 2. If high-complexity is roughly >0.5, a mean of 0.43 implies a *smaller* high-complexity share than 0.43, and the predicted error moves. Presenting informal arithmetic as if it decomposed the position curve is a logical flaw, not a rounding nit.

2. **The PM4Py snippets would not run as written.** `pm4py.discover_batches` returns a dict; `for activity, batch_list in batches` unpacks keys and fails. `discover_performance_dfg` returns three objects, not four (`perf_dfg, dfg, sa, ea = ...`). Those are not cosmetic API guesses; they are broken calls in the required analyses section.

3. **Cell matching is inconsistent.** First-hour quality is compared to the *low-WIP* high-complexity error rate (4.2%) while that hour has queue 46 (medium WIP; high-complexity cell 5.6%). Service-time “predictions” switch between medium- and high-WIP rows without a stated assignment rule (queue 62 is called medium, 88 high, with no sensitivity). “Almost exactly” is used for gaps that are not small under those switches (e.g. 18.8 vs ~17.7 min).
