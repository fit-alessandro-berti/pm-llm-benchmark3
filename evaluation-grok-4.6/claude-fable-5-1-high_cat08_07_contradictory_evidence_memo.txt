7.0

The memo chooses the right strategic posture (segment-gated hybrid, not national scale or full rollback), unbundles Redesign R, and keeps facts, interpretations, and residual uncertainty visibly separated. That is the core of the task. It is not nearly flawless. Several local errors, one process-mining misstatement, and an internal rollout contradiction are enough to keep it well below the top band.

**Format and consistency.** The executive memo is dense enough that it almost certainly exceeds the 700-word cap. More serious is an unresolved conflict on geography. The memo says scale the simple-house package now; the final section says scale it “in all regions where building-risk classification is available”; it also says pause “any geographic expansion of R beyond the four pilot regions.” If “R” means the full bundle, the wording does not say so. A decision memo cannot leave “national now” versus “pilot only until the gate” ambiguous.

**Overclaim on what is identified.** Stating that coordinator ownership and merged validate/schedule “work,” while skip-and-bill-early “cause most of the damage,” treats a bundled pilot as if components were identified. Simple-house gains are real; they are not a clean estimate of (1) or (2) alone. Coordinator load (93%), stalling under absence, and slower failed-visit → second-visit delays are attached to the ownership model. The later C/E discussion is more careful than the opening line, which is what executives will remember.

**Process-mining error in a required section.** Fitness does not rise “precisely because the model allows almost anything.” That is the precision story. The prompt’s own interpretation is that fitness is high because many cases follow the simplified coordinator path, while precision is low because the model still permits skip / manual activation / second visit / billing-start combinations. High fitness plus low precision plus low generalization is a richer diagnostic (permissive and poorly generalizing) than the “near flower” collapse. Using discovered-model precision recovering to 0.70 as a rollout gate confuses a mining diagnostic with a control-point KPI; prescribed-model conformance (already listed as M7) is the right instrument.

**Unsupported or overstated claims.** “Bimodal” is not in the evidence—heterogeneous segment effects do not imply two modes. “Improved on every metric” for simple houses overreaches: the segment table covers duration, first-time-right, and complaints, not cost, NPS, or SLA as measured. “Two thirds of the potential median-time gains” ignores that apartments, business, and rural also saw median improvements. M6’s claim that “all medium-risk cases were surveyed” in baseline is not supported (baseline survey edges are ~31%/29%). “True all-in delta is more likely worse than EUR +9” is a directional guess from right-censoring, not a fact. The 80% utilization cap, 5% false-negative rule, 98% permit bar, and 1pp/0.5pp tolerances are presented with false precision.

**Decision logic.** A, B, C, D, E are evaluated in the right spirit: A is correctly rejected given tail, rework, and billing-before-activation; B is correctly rejected because the simple-house cell is a real gain. E is the defensible choice. The remaining weakness is operational sequencing: scaling the simple-house path (including skip) depends on intake classification that the memo itself says is incomplete before pilot week 3 and that M6 is supposed to validate. Scaling on that classifier first recreates the mis-skip failure mode the rest of the memo is trying to stop. Rural (p90 +9 days, FTR −5pp) is also underweighted relative to apartments and old buildings.

**VOI.** The ranking is mostly sound (validate the rule, then randomize by stratum, then full 60-day cost, with M7 as a control rather than a learning item). Decision-change language is present. Deduct for the baseline-survey assumption under M6 and for ranking start timestamps too low given that durations are completion-to-completion only—the median “win” could be waiting-time composition, which would change how much of (1)/(2) is real.

**What still works.** The evidence table (12 items) is the strongest section: facts, mechanisms, and uncertainties are actually in different columns. Metric-conflict explanations for median vs tail, visible vs downstream cost, handovers vs resilience, and house vs apartment/old-building effects are correct in direction. Alignments, DFG edges, temporal profile, resource utilization, and handover vs working-together networks are used, not merely named. Immediate pause on billing-before-activation and missing permit checks is the right hard control. The 12-week, per-segment gate is the right shape of decision, even if several thresholds are arbitrary.

Net: high-quality strategic synthesis with a correct headline decision, undermined by a word-limit risk, a scale-versus-pause contradiction, a real fitness/precision mistake, and repeated over-precision about unidentified components and unvalidated classification. Under a strict bar that reserves 9–10 for near-flawless work, that is a 7.0.
