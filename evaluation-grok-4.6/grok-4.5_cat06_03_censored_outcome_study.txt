**6.8**

The design is substantively in the “strong” class: it refuses convenience non-default labels, conditions the primary estimand on activation, keeps mechanisms of missingness distinct, and insists on temporal rather than random splits. It is not nearly flawless. Several statistical, semantic, and implementation gaps are material under a strict reading.

## What is actually good

The primary estimand is named as associational and selection-conditional rather than as “default risk for every application.” The alternative multi-state target is a different question, not a relabeling trick. The 48,000-row flow is arithmetically consistent (9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 = 22,800 never activated; 25,200 − 3,600 = 21,600). Leakage discussion covers every required field. Assumption testability is split correctly into testable, partially testable, and untestable. Section 8’s use-boundary and performativity list are the most disciplined part of the writeup.

Those strengths do not cancel the defects below.

## Section 1 — target is honest, then slightly misspecified

Conditioning \(\theta\) on *label matured* folds an observability filter into the scientific estimand. The operational quantity is default among activated accounts; delayed feed arrival is a missing-data / as-of problem, not part of the definition of 90-day default. If delay is shorter when a default is recorded, \(P(Y{=}1\mid X, \text{activated}, \text{mature})\) is biased relative to \(P(Y{=}1\mid X, \text{activated})\). The text later treats delay separately, so the estimand and the analysis plan are not the same object.

Broken markup (`\ co{default\_90d}`) is sloppy in the one place the target must be unambiguous.

The deployment mismatch is under-argued. Training prefixes come only from cases that *will* activate; at scoring time the same \(k\)-prefix is observed for applications whose activation is still unknown. That is covariate shift plus a change in interpretation, not a small caveat. A selection model / IPAW “robustness check” does not repair it, and IPAW cannot identify default for never-activated cases because that potential outcome is not defined under the observed policy (which the authors elsewhere admit).

## Section 2 — cohort table is complete; transfer/partner ontology is not

The table accounts for all 48,000 and does not dump omitted cases into \(Y{=}0\). Good.

Permanently missing partner feeds cannot be an extra residue outside that partition. Those accounts are already inside the 21,600, the 3,600, or the 2,900. Calling them a “subset of above” without saying *which* row is hand-waving. If they are in the 21,600 they have labels; if they are in the 3,600 they are delayed/immature, not known-permanent MNAR.

Transfers are treated as “no activation on this ID,” which ignores the stated 60% linkage table: some transfers *are* later activated under a new key. There is no rule for which identifier is the unit, how linked outcomes are joined, or how to avoid double-counting.

## Section 3 — mechanisms are distinguished; one sentence is unacceptable

The five-way split of censoring / delay / activation selection / transfer / permanent feed loss is the right taxonomy. Sensitivities are relevant. “Rejects would not have defaulted” is correctly marked untestable.

Then the transfer row says: “linked subset for outcome sensitivity; unlinked  non-default.” That fragment can be read as coding unlinked transfers as non-default — the exact malpractice the rest of the memo forbids. If the intended symbol was “≠”, it is not on the page. In a study whose whole point is label semantics, this is not a typo; it is an ambiguity at the decision node.

IPAW is offered as robustness for activation selection and then disclaimed as non-causal. That is internally confused: IPAW toward the started-application population *is* a counterfactual/transport estimator and requires \(Y\) to be defined off the activated support. Either drop it from the primary strategy or write the potential-outcome assumptions.

No numerical bounds are given for MNAR partner loss (worst-case / Manski-style), only “bound it.”

## Section 4 — rules are mostly right; the implemented rule is weaker

Fixed-\(k\) and elapsed-time prefixes, no padding with future activities, synonym map, exact-duplicate collapse, and last `risk_score` at or before \(t_{\text{pred}}\) are correct.

The pre-decision requirement ( \(k\)-th event before Accept / Contract / Activate) is stated in prose and **not enforced** later. Activated cases can be `Started → … → Accepted` in three events; \(k{=}3\) then injects the decision into the “early warning” prefix. Exact-duplicate drop also misses migration duplicates that differ by a second or by resource.

## Section 5 — feature list is fine, and inconsistent with the split

Control-flow, timing, rework, and train-only DFG/variant references are appropriate. Extract-time experience is excluded. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is correctly restricted to prefix logs.

`policy_regime` is listed as a feature, but the training window is entirely before 1 July 2025, so the flag has zero training variance and is unidentified. That is a design contradiction, not a nit.

## Section 6 — audit table meets the spec

Required columns and required fields are present; keep/repair/drop choices are generally right. `documents_total` repaired as-of \(t_{\text{pred}}\) is correct in the table and then absent from the code audit (below). Treating “reject as non-default” as a data element is slightly category-confused but directionally right.

## Section 7 — temporal idea is right; the folds cannot do what is claimed

The critique of random splits (policy mix, migration mix, maturity-selected 21,600, time-varying activation) is the correct critique.

The proposed tests do not survive their own maturity rule.

- Conservative maturity is roughly \(t_{\text{act}}+125\) days. Extract is 15 February 2026, so activation must be \(\lesssim\) mid-October 2025.
- **Test B** (\(t_0\) 15 Oct–31 Dec 2025) is almost empty for the primary binary target. A same-day activation on 15 Oct plus a 7-day feed barely clears the extract; a 35-day feed does not. December starts are impossible. The text adds “evaluate only when mature” and still sells Test B as the migration default test. It is at most a process-shift / competing-risk slice.
- **Test A** (Jul–Sep 2025) is usable only for earlier, faster-to-activate cases. The policy-shift evaluation set is therefore selected on speed-to-book — a second selection layer that is never named.
- Train as-of 15 August 2025 is tight for March 2025 starts. Prose promises fold-specific as-of; the skeleton uses one global `ASOF`.

Metrics (PR-AUC at 12.5% prevalence, ECE, capacity recall, PSI, subgroup AUC) are appropriate to the chosen binary target.

## Section 8 — strongest section, small overclaim

Predictive association vs selection vs counterfactual is clearly separated. Three deployment DGP breaks are real. Calling activation a “collider/selection path” is only defensible under a specific DAG; as written it is slightly loose. This section is not where the grade is lost.

## Section 9 — concrete skeleton, requirement misses, silent leaks

The pipeline shape is right: `read_csv` → `format_dataframe` → as-of case table → start-time split → prefix frame → drop leaky columns → train-only DFG/variants → cohort counts → audit print → `LATER` survival hooks. `split_train_test` is correctly demoted.

Defects that matter:

1. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is wrapped and **never called** in `main()`. The live path is `groupby`/`cumcount`. The required demonstration is ornamental.
2. First-\(k\) events of *activated* cases are not truncated at the decision. `leakage_audit_check` only prints `REVIEW` if Accept/Activate appear; it does not remove them. Features will eat the decision whenever the path is short.
3. `documents_total` is not dropped, repaired, or audited in code, despite being a specified same-day leak.
4. Fold-specific as-of, elapsed-time prefixes, pre-decision filters, and test-fold evaluation are described and not implemented. `test_policy` / `test_migr` are computed and discarded.
5. `pd.read_csv` does not parse timestamps; the prompt asked for that explicitly.
6. Cohort report collapses reject/withdraw/transfer, so it cannot reproduce the 48,000 table it supposedly implements.
7. Length overshoots the 80–160 line envelope by a wide margin, while still omitting the checks that would make the extra lines useful.
8. `default_90d.max()` as a label reducer is crude; `label_available_at` is taken as `min` after a `to_datetime` only in that branch — inconsistent parsing.

## Net

This is a competent, process-aware design that would not commit the classic malpractice (train on 21,600 as if it were “every application,” call rejects non-default, random-split across the policy break, ship `collection_status`). It also contains a garbled transfer-label rule, an estimand that conditions on observability, a validation plan whose migration test cannot mature, a policy feature that cannot be learned under the stated split, and a PM4Py skeleton that does not enforce the prefix and leakage rules the memo itself writes down. Those are not polish issues. Under the instructed strictness they cap the score well below the near-flawless band.
