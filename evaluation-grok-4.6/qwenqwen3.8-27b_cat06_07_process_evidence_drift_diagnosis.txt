**7.7**

The final six-section write-up is a strong audit: it correctly treats April as a schema/pipeline break, June as a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September as overlapping process, concept, and population effects, and October as dominated by immature labels. It does not collapse the year into one drift type, and it does not treat October’s 0.16 observed breach rate as comparable to matured months.

What keeps it well short of a high score is a set of concrete inaccuracies and conceptual slips that a strict reading should penalize.

The diagnosis table is mostly right, but June is stretched into “population shift.” A remote-routing policy sending the same customers down V2 is mix/process (and, for the model, covariate) change, not a new population. Partner Z is the actual population shift. Listing June under three drift types is allowed by the prompt; calling it population shift is not.

Section 2 is the strongest part: month-by-month evidence, observed vs interpretation, and falsifiers. May-as-residual-pipeline is reasonable but slightly over-certain (6 May cutover plus incomplete April backfill could also leave a first-week artifact). July is handled well as the start of a V3/parts problem, not a global collapse.

Section 3 meets the required tests and usually states two competing expected results. Some “expected if X” cells are generic (“metrics remain poor”) rather than sharp, and a few tests hedge so much they would not actually discriminate hypotheses.

Section 4 is proportionate: quarantine April, freeze retrain on raw Aug–Oct metrics, recalibrate mix first, add lead-time/partner features, then retrain only on matured, lineage-clean data. The safe/unsafe month table is one of the better artifacts. Claiming July is “mostly safe” at 98% maturity is slightly generous if the 2% missing cases are long V3 paths.

Section 5’s monitor design and alert gates are sound: label-delay and data-quality alarms correctly block retraining. The code is not. `pm4py.format_log_to_dataframe` is not the public converter (`convert_to_dataframe` is). `filter_time_range` is called with an invented `time_range=` / `parameter="lifecycle:transition"` signature; the real API is start, end, and a mode. `discover_dfg` often returns `(dfg, starts, ends)`, so `.items()` on the return value is wrong. `get_variants` is typically a `{variant: count}` dict; running `value_counts()` on the keys would report every variant as 1 and discard frequencies. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is invoked with a guessed signature and swallowed by a bare `except`. Try/except fallbacks paper over this instead of using the APIs the prompt named correctly. That is a real failure on a required deliverable, not polish.

Section 6’s failure modes and mitigations are the right ones (immature-label retrain, calibrating a pipeline bug, ignoring supplier-driven V3 drift, Partner Z “unfairness” without strata). No major logical error there.

Net: excellent triangulation and remediation judgment, undermined by a mislabeled June population claim and several invented or broken PM4Py details in the one required code block. Not close to flawless.
