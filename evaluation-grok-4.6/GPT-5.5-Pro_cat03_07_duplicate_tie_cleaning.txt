7.6

The submission is a strong, constraint-compliant design that would score much higher under ordinary grading. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless: the conceptual pipeline is mostly right, but several implementation and consistency defects would actually reintroduce the failure modes the text warns about.

**What is solid.** The decision tree matches the required typology and defaults to retain/quarantine rather than destructive cleaning. The event table covers every mandated pair, gives a second scenario for e06/e07, and correctly refuses to collapse e03/e05 and e13/e14. Ordering policy separates semantic rules from deterministic fallbacks, refuses ingestion as a business clock, and explains why synthetic microseconds before a semantic order invent a false story. Conservative retention of 15 events / 39 E2O relations is arithmetically correct (17−2; 44−3−2). Aggressive `have_common_object=True` collapsing to 10 events / 28 relations is also exact: `{e03,e04,e05}`, `{e06,e07}`, `{e08,e09}`, `{e11,e16}`, `{e13,e14,e15}`. The argument that shared O1/V1 does not protect M1 vs M2 is the right object-centric point. Constraints on e05, e14, original timestamps, and “total order ≠ physical truth” are respected.

**Serious inconsistencies.**

1. Evidence-based cleaning is stacked on an already-processed conservative OCEL. `add_semantic_order` already ran `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` and mutated working timestamps. The second pass applies another index offset. That is exactly the “synthetic microseconds distort performance” failure mode listed in §9.

2. Attribute policy and code disagree. The table says keep WMS-enriched scan fields and billing tax/rendering fields. `merge_verified_pairs` keeps the canonical row and only JSON-logs conflicts. e11 wins every clash with e16, so dual authority from fact 6 is not implemented.

3. e03 < e05 is justified by “merged e03 inherits S-901.” The merger does not adopt scanner `source_seq`; it keeps WMS seq 100 and hardcodes an edge. The prose alternative (leave I1/I2 unordered before pack) is the more honest conservative stance and should have been the default.

4. §3 never names `source_priority`, yet the heap key ranks ERP < WMS < SCANNER < CARRIER < BILLING < PSP ahead of `source_seq` and `ingested_at`. That is a hidden cross-system story, not a neutral tie-break.

5. The audit schema in §8 is not produced. There is no canonicalization table, no archived raw E2O, no `rule_version`/`reviewer`. Provenance is a few columns on the survivor row. Reconstructing the raw view is described, not enabled.

**Smaller defects.** `refine_labels` uses a fragile `loc` length/alignment assignment and does not refresh activity on the relations table. There is no assertion that e05 and e14 still exist after library calls. Merging e11/e16 at “high” confidence is defensible given fact 6’s wording, but it is less conservative than the dual-authority clause and than the treatment of e06/e07. OC-DFG discussion often collapses into flattened DFGs; ET–OT and OTG get only brief remarks. `deepcopy` of a PM4Py OCEL is assumed to be safe.

**Net.** Conservative reasoning and the sensitivity counts are the best parts. PM4Py usage hits every required symbol and scopes `ocel_drop_duplicates`, but composition bugs and policy/code drift keep this well short of a 9–10.
