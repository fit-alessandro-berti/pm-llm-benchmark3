**What is actually good.** The ledger is broad, dated, and mostly well-calibrated. The ranking correctly treats label change, gateway insertion, and ETL reordering as the main *measured* collapse, and treats missing compliance checks as a residual operational question rather than the cause of the June crash. **Section 1 — ledger.** Too many items are slightly overconfident. “High-value status omitted” at 98 rates an *absence from a report* as if it were a measured causal fact. Outcome stability at 75 undersells a real median-cycle drift (26.0 → 27.4) that is consistent with added sign-off work. **Section 2 — ranking.** The six-way split is defensible and the collapse-versus-concern distinction is the right one. The posteriors are theater: 98 vs 96 vs 94 vs 92 implies a precision the mixed evidence cannot support. “Real behavioral process change” at 84 is also muddy—policy-driven sign-off is real and large in August, while the June collapse is mostly representational. **Section 3 — causal graph.** The required nodes are present and the prose is inside the word band, but the graph is incomplete in the one place the story needs a link: `policy_change` never touches `normative_model`. **Section 4 — counterfactuals.** Specific and mostly falsifiable. **Section 5 — diagnostics.** The phase isolation is the best part of the answer. Residual risk is correctly deferred until after normalization. In current PM4Py that API iterates a collection of traces; a raw `str` is iterated as characters and would not produce the intended log. The 45% residual-risk figure is better. Actions and “do not claim” constraints are sound. **Net.** Excellent abductive structure with a real process-mining brain. Not a 9+ under a strict bar: one likely non-executable log constructor, one missed exhaustive-variant fact, an incomplete causal graph, overprecise posteriors, and an incomplete preprocessing comparison.
