The answer has the required six-section skeleton and some of the right vocabulary (parallel join as a max, path bands, calendar pauses, dependence). Almost every number that matters is wrong, and several sections contradict each other. Under a strict reading this is a well-formatted but substantively failed forecast.

### Fatal temporal errors

**Microbiology ready time is off by a full day.** Incubation started Friday 14:00; 46–54 h later is **Sunday 12:00–20:00**, not Monday 14:00–20:00. That single mistake inverts the smooth-path clock. The result is technically ready on Sunday; the live question is weekend on-call readout versus Monday 08:00 staffing. QA does not work Sunday, and Saturday 09:00–13:00 is *before* the ready window, so even an authorized Sunday readout cannot start QA before Monday 08:00. The answer never sees this.

The same error reappears as “readout shifts from ~Saturday 09:00 to Monday 08:00.” Saturday 09:00 is only ~19 h after incubation start. Driver #4 is therefore mis-timed as well as mis-ranked.

**Remaining-hour arithmetic does not match the stated clock times.** Friday 15:30 → Monday 15:30 is **72 h**, not 36 h. Friday 15:30 + 40–42 h is Sunday morning, not Monday 17:00. The §4 table is internally inconsistent by roughly 1.5 days. That alone would sink a forecasting answer.

### Chemistry is not a two-job residual; it is a multi-job queue against a hard close

The log places **one urgent and three ordinary samples waiting ahead of B-417**, plus two jobs already in service. The answer starts B-417 at 16:20–16:40 as soon as current WIP ends. That erases four predecessors.

Even under risk-priority, the urgent sample still goes first. The earliest plausible start is late Friday, remaining laboratory time is ≤2.5 h, and service is triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5). B-417 **cannot finish Friday** except in a knife-edge min-service case that also jumps the urgent sample. The prompt already says unfinished work pauses until Monday 08:00.

Worse, the answer then writes completion at **19:10–19:30 “still within lab hours.”** Chemistry closes at 18:00. That is not a rounding issue; it is a calendar violation used as the baseline.

FIFO versus priority is not “a few minutes to hours.” It is whether three ordinary samples (and leftover Friday WIP) sit in front of B-417 across a weekend close. Under FIFO, B-417 may not start until Monday. Under priority, it may start Friday, pause, and finish Monday morning. The two disciplines change the join race, not a few minutes.

Rerun sensitivity (“adds ~1 h”) is equally wrong: a rerun that hits the 18:00 wall is a **weekend deferral**, not +1 h.

### Parallel join is stated, then applied to the wrong days

§3 smooth path: chemistry ~Friday 19:30, micro ~Monday 14:30, then “join **19:30** (chemistry becomes usable)” and QA starts Friday evening. That is not a max. If those (already wrong) finish times were taken seriously, join would be Monday afternoon.

The scenario table then puts the same smooth path on **Monday 15:30–16:45**. Decomposition and table do not agree. The rerun row has the same disease: table earliest Monday 13:00 / central 16:30–17:20; decomposition terminal **11:30 Monday**, and join is taken at chemistry finish while microbiology is still unread in the answer’s own model.

### QA queue, working hours, and the long tail are essentially omitted

Required handling of “uncertainty in the QA queue at the future join time” is replaced by “immediate start possible.” Five batches are already waiting; arrivals continue at 0.35/h with overdispersion through Friday evening and Saturday; Friday has one reviewer to 20:00 and Saturday one reviewer for four hours. By Monday 08:00 the residual queue is a first-class random variable. Two Monday reviewers do not make waiting negligible.

Documentation queries add **4–12 working hours**, not evening clock hours after 20:00. QA Monday closes at 20:00. A query opened Monday afternoon cannot clear Monday 20:00–21:30, let alone 23:00–00:30. Saturday 09:00–13:00 is unused.

A laboratory investigation adds **3–7 calendar days**. That is the actual P90/severe tail. The answer’s P90 is Monday night / Tuesday 00:30. That is not a conservative tail; it is roughly a central-to-upper smooth-ish path with a query glued on, and it ignores rejection, lab investigation, contamination confirmation, and correlated excursion effects.

Investigation probability **rises to 0.30–0.55** given rerun, contamination, or confirmed excursion. The answer never builds that correlated tail. Rejection is mentioned in the prompt and almost absent from the forecast.

### Path probabilities and dependence

“Smooth” 0.30–0.50 and “rerun” 0.10–0.20 are then said to **co-occur on the same path**. If smooth means no rerun, they are exclusive. The overlap note (“0.4 total”) is incoherent.

Delayed-microbiology is scored as 0.05–0.12 (raw contamination). The common delay is **not** contamination; it is Sunday readiness plus 0.55–0.75 probability of no weekend microbiologist. That is the modal staffing delay and is missing from the scenario table.

Temperature excursion is listed, then not used. No scenario raises both rerun and contamination together. “Modest factor” is not an operational dependence model.

Incubation-window sensitivity (“±2 h would move the ready window by a day”) is false.

### What is actually defensible (and why that is not enough)

A defensible envelope, using max-not-sum and the calendars as written:

- Chemistry usable: Monday morning (priority, started Friday, residual Monday) through Monday afternoon (FIFO leftover queue).
- Micro usable: Sunday evening only if on-call (0.25–0.45, then +1–3 h); otherwise Monday 08:30–09:30.
- Join: Monday 08:30 to Monday mid/late afternoon. QA cannot start Sunday.
- Smooth terminal: Monday midday–evening if the Monday QA queue is kind; Tuesday if it is not.
- Query: +1–2 QA working days.
- Contamination: another 20–30 h continuous incubation plus readout, typically Tuesday.
- Doc investigation: +8–20 working hours.
- Lab investigation / correlated excursion: **into the following week**.
- Earliest plausible terminal: Monday late morning, not Monday 15:30, and not 36 h.
- Central remaining time: on the order of **~65–80 h** (Monday afternoon/evening), not 40–42 h.
- P90: days, not +6–8 h past the median.

The answer’s “most plausible Monday ~17:30–18:00” and “P90 Tuesday 00:30” are therefore not approximate-but-defensible; they are the product of a missed queue, a closed laboratory treated as open, a 46–54 h window shifted by 24 h, and a 72 h weekend counted as 36 h.

§6’s choice of the temperature-excursion flag as the highest-value next observation is one of the few parts that is directionally right. It cannot rescue a forecast whose clocks, queues, calendars, and tails are all mis-specified.

**Grade 3.0:** required structure present; core remaining-time model is not competent.
