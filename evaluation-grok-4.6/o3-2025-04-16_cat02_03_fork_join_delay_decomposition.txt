**4.6**

The backbone is directionally right: max-based join, service ≠ influence, and non-critical acceleration can save nothing. That is not enough. Under a strict reading of the spec, the write-up has incorrect identities, missing required work, wrong aggregates, a broken PM4Py block, and at least two conceptual mistakes.

**Section 1 is not a usable specification.** Case/branch index notation is incomplete (`Cases, i {A,B,C}`, empty “for case .”). The no-double-count argument is mathematically wrong. The text writes `sync_wait,i = 3·join_ready – t(c,i_end)`. That is neither the per-branch wait nor the sum. The identity that should have been shown is

`queue_i + service_i + sync_i = fork_join_elapsed` for every *i*,

hence `∑_i sync_i` (or `∑_i (q_i+s_i+sync_i)`) counts the same clock interval three times. The prose gestures at this and then garbles it. The additive case identity `total = upstream + max_i(q_i+s_i) + downstream + final` is never written cleanly, even though it is the whole point of “without double counting.”

**Section 2 meets the table numerically and fails the required exposition.** Hand-checked minutes, `join_ready`, critical branch, downstream wait, and totals for all 12 cases match the log. That is the strongest part of the answer. The required detailed worked calculations for **F04, F07, and F11 are missing**; “similar and in the code” does not satisfy the format. Column names are abbreviated relative to the mandated list. Synchronization waits and fork–join elapsed time are defined and then omitted from the case view, so the reader cannot audit the no-double-count claim from the table.

**Section 3 ranks B first for the right qualitative reasons and then reports false statistics.** Critical-path frequencies 2 / 8 / 2 are correct. Mean B queue ≈ 19 and mean B service = 37.5 are correct. The required sync-wait numbers are not:

- mean `sync_A` = 132/12 = **11 min**, not 7
- mean `sync_B` = 52/12 ≈ **4.3 min**
- mean `sync_C` = 365/12 ≈ **30.4 min**

“7 min” is approximately `mean(B sojourn) − mean(A sojourn)` (56.7 − 50), which is not a synchronization wait. “B imposes 11 min on others” appears to be A’s mean slack mis-attributed to B. There is no comparison **table**, no tail contribution to `join_ready` (max/p75 sojourn or slack), and no case-level `q+s` distribution. The ranking B > A > C is still defensible, so the conclusion is luckier than the evidence display.

**Section 4 is only partly computed, and CF-3 is oversold.**

- CF-1: 15 min total, only F01 (−5) and F11 (−10), is correct. “Direct effect 10 min per case” is misleading even with the later qualifier.
- CF-2: true first-order saving is **92.5 min**, not 93 (half-queues of 5 and 25). After the cut, A is the new limiter in **two** cases strictly (F05, F10) and **ties** in two more (F02, F06). “Three cases” is wrong.
- CF-3: 176 min is the sum of observed `final_start − join_ready`. Claiming “no inter-case pre-emption risk under current load” is false if F is left at two servers: pulling every case to `join_ready` produces a **three-way overlap** F09/F10/F11 in 11:30–11:35. Adding F3 would fix that; “or pull scheduling” would not. Downstream **masking** of branch improvements is named and then not analyzed; all CFs freeze `down_queue` by assumption without labeling how strong that assumption is.

**Section 5 recommends the two levers the data actually support, then invents operations numbers.** B-queue reduction and extra F capacity are the right pilots. “Extra rig initially < 60% busy” is unsupported. **“Frees 176 min operator idle time” is a category error**: those 176 minutes are case waiting, not F1/F2 work. Service at F is 20 × 12 = 240 min either way. Adding F3 *increases* idle capacity; it does not harvest 176 minutes of labor.

**Section 6 would not run as specified and does not print what was asked.** Required calls are `pm4py.format_dataframe` and a top-level method such as `pm4py.get_service_time` / `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`. The code uses `log_converter.format_dataframe` (that converter exposes `apply`, not `format_dataframe`), `pm4py.algo.performance.dfg` (not a standard module), and an unofficial `service_times.pandas` import. `time_converter` is unused. PM4Py objects are computed and **never printed**. Counterfactual output is three aggregates only; **per-case savings are not printed**. The comment that DFG performance does not recover max-based sync wait is the one part of the PM4Py requirement that is actually met.

**Section 7 is the second-best section.** Causality, unobserved branch capacity, invisible preemption, and queue-versus-release-policy for F are all stated. Single-day sample and behavioral adaptation are fair. It still overclaims nothing that the timestamps *can* identify about F congestion, after Section 4 treated F waiting as pure slack that can be deleted.

**Net.** Correct 12-row arithmetic and a sound critical-path story are carrying an otherwise non-compliant answer. Required worked examples, required sync-wait analysis, required per-case counterfactuals, required PM4Py surface, and several stated numbers/claims are wrong. That is well below a high score under a hypercritical bar.
