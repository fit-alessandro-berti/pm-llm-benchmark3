**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and often process-aware, but it is not close to flawless. Under strict scoring, several designed distinctions are blurred, one high-signal control-flow error is baked into the C6-B forecast, and “next logged event” is repeatedly treated as “a nearby eventual event.”

**C6-B is the clearest failure.** Fast Close is an S1 triage shortcut (`Initial Triage -> Fast Close` is the only Fast Close arc in the DFG). This case already left that path: triage chose an evidence cycle. After evidence, the normative non-investigation outcome is **Close — No Safety Issue** (`Evidence Review Completed -> Close — No Safety Issue`, count 68). There is no historical Fast Close successor from the evidence cycle. Ranking Fast Close #2 at 0.22–0.40, and ranking it *above* Close — No Safety Issue (0.08–0.20), inverts the control-flow semantics the A/B contrast is built to test. The alternative state (“fast-close path operationally selected”) repeats the same category error. Noting that the case “was not closed immediately at triage” does not repair assigning it the second-largest next-event mass. Midpoints also sum to ~1.00, which is overconfident once other events remain possible.

**“Next logged activity” is systematically stretched.** The task asks for the next observed completion, not the next few milestones. With a normal feed:
- C6-A received evidence at 12:10 (now 14:00). Review is the enabled successor (1–5 h). TI/RA completions (median 5–9 h, long queues) are plausible later, or only now if work started much earlier—an assumption not in the prefix. Marking both `process_enabled: true` at S3 treats investigation opening as already licensed; S3 says review *may open* investigations.
- C6-E received the second package at 13:20 (40 minutes ago). A third `Customer Evidence Requested` cannot be the next log line unless the second review has already finished unlogged. That is unlikely on a normal feed inside the ordinary review service time.
- Closures on C6-B are likewise typically one event after review.

The write-up often *describes* this distinction well and then ignores it in the ranked list.

**Enablement is inconsistent.** C6-C correctly sets Disposition `process_enabled: false` under S4—this is the best use of the flag. C6-E then marks `Supplier Analysis Completed` false even though six same-lot complaints already create a standing obligation that does **not** wait for the second review. Opening/continuing SA is already justified; only *completion within minutes* is unlikely (6–36 h). C6-C also dismisses SA because lots differ, but the rules require SA when a **supplier or component** is implicated. Four shared-failure-mode complaints across lots are exactly that signal.

**Smaller but real slips.** C6-D’s #1 (RA Completed) is reasonable given the 12:40 worklist open, but 80 minutes elapsed versus “2–14 h after assignment” makes delayed evidence at least as competitive; Disposition is missing from the top three despite the S5 alternative. C6-C’s #3 (another evidence request after TI, with review already completed earlier) is a weak reading of an unconfirmed root cause. C6-B underuses R-T4 (reportable-case specialist) as a reason not to treat the case as an obvious duplicate. Activity names add parenthetical glosses; “Close - No Safety Issue” is not the stated terminal label. Two-decimal subjective ranges slightly overstate precision.

**What is actually good.** Primary states are mostly right (S3 for A/B/E, S4 for C/D). Alternatives are retained where the completion-only log and migration matter. C6-A vs C6-B is explained correctly in `same_prefix_different_state` even though the B forecast fails to implement that explanation. Queues, the 16:00 notification batch (implicitly unused, correctly), migration delay (especially C6-D), lot/object constraints, and DFG-as-observational-not-normative are handled with real discipline. Supporting/contradicting/unknowns and observation caveats are present and usually on point. Intervals are mostly honest and not fake point masses.

That is a competent, well-shaped answer with one trap missed, a recurring horizon error, and a few obligation/enablement contradictions. It is not near-flawless. **6.4.**
