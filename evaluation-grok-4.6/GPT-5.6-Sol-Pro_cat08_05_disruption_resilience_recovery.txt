**8.2**

The answer is complete, well-structured, and aligned with the required seven-section format. It honors every stated constraint: deviations are not treated as automatically bad; throughput is not equated with resilience; the T4 audit tail is treated as real work, not cosmetic recovery; and after-the-fact remediation is explicitly distinguished from contemporaneous control. Temporal reasoning is the strongest part of the piece. The T0–T4 narrative correctly identifies asymmetric recovery—hospital service first, ordinary service later, evidence last—and the T3 warning about trusting a restored but unsynchronized interface is a genuine insight, not a restatement of the prompt.

The resilience test in section 3 (service value, equivalent control purpose, contemporaneous evidence, no hidden transfer of burden) is the right conceptual instrument for this case. Classifications are mostly defensible: B/J/K as true resilience, C/G as unsafe shortcuts, A/I as allocation resilience rather than control waivers, D/E/F/H as time- and evidence-conditional, and L as necessary but non-substitutive. The recovery model is concrete enough to implement: mode triggers, hard feasibility gates before priority, product-specific minimums, append-only evidence rules, split fulfillment/remediation queues, and four closure states. Indicators and the management trade-off paragraph are decision-usable.

It is not near-flawless. Several issues are small individually and material in aggregate.

**Keep / modify is applied inconsistently.** A is “Modify” because it must be formalized and gated. B, J, and K are “Keep” even though the rationale immediately requires formalization, versioned sources, reason codes, fairness monitoring, or a change in *when* reconciliation starts. That is the same kind of change A received. The taxonomy therefore blurs the very distinction the table exists to make.

**DFG volume is underused, which weakens abduction.** The answer cites 1,600 skip-authorization edges and 1,000 fast-lane edges separately, but does not add them. Of 5,100 emergency-triage cases, 2,600 never entered `Manual authorization check`—a majority. That is the central authorization finding and it is left implicit. Likewise, 1,800 of 3,600 `Manual pack` cases went straight to `Dispatch without scan`, so about half of packed work skipped even the temporary release sample; and 2,600 of roughly 3,250 scanless dispatches reached phone POD, leaving a non-trivial set with neither scan nor phone confirmation. These are in the supplied graph. Treating G and F as qualitative patterns rather than measured splits makes the hidden-risk claims less precise than the data allow.

**Some metric reporting is uneven.** T2 and T3 rows lean on medians and omit the most damaging tails (Pharmacy p90 118h / 90h; Wholesale 180h / 160h). T4 daily volume is higher than baseline, which supports the hidden-workload thesis, but that intensity comparison is not made. T4 compliance-hold incidence is also higher than T0 and is not computed. None of this is invented; it is simply left on the table.

**A few statements are slightly over-clean.** “The fast lane went directly to an inventory snapshot rather than an explicit authorization activity” is true for 920 of 1,000 observed outgoing edges, not for the activity as a whole. Flow imbalances in the T2 DFG (snapshot, pack, release, and dispatch nodes do not fully balance) are ignored; a process-mining answer should at least flag incomplete or collapsed paths before treating edge counts as hard volumes. The caution in section 3 that aggregate edges do not prove case-level causality is correct and partly redeems this, but it arrives late and is not applied back to the table.

**No material factual errors.** Multiples (≈12× traceability, 12× cold-chain gaps, 14.5× dual-control gaps), fitness figures, fill-rate gap, and named DFG counts that *are* used check out. Redesign proposals (emergency whitelist, snapshot TTL, offline custody token, pharmacy floor, reconstructed-versus-contemporaneous labels) are inferences, not smuggled facts.

The result is a strong advisory memo with a clear operating philosophy and a usable recovery design, short of the tighter DFG arithmetic and tighter keep/modify discipline a near-perfect answer would have.
