**8.6**

This is a strong, disciplined causal-policy memo. Arithmetic on the disclosed windows is accurate, the availability/use/selection distinction is clean, median and p90 are never collapsed, and the recommendation correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict reading, several design, coherence, and over-precision problems remain.

**What is done well.** Section 1 stays descriptive and separates availability from intensity, endogenous targeting, co-occurring operational shocks, and aggregate/CSAT measurement limits. The estimand table is the right shape: ITT-like availability, endogenous completed-path dose, center vs tail, rework, escalation, population CSAT, dynamics, heterogeneity, and fraud-desk interference. Identification correctly rejects naïve staggered TWFE, refuses already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma, and treats contemporaneous load/mix as a total-package vs partial-out choice. Mechanisms are process-literate (completed-route collider, substitution vs capacity, tail amplification). The DiD-style contrasts I checked match the CSV. The claim ladder is ordered and honestly capped.

**Quantitative and inferential gaps.** The headline adopter table uses January–March vs July–August for Alpha/Beta, which skips the first three treated months—the period of peak arrivals, the May complexity jump, Alpha’s FTE loss, and the worst p90s. Framing this as “mature use” is defensible for learning, but it is also the most favorable post window. Apr–Jun relative contrasts are reported later and still favorable, which helps, yet the claim ladder leads with 2.8–6.9 / 5.3–7.8 as if those were the primary robust facts. Those figures are three descriptive DiD points with no standard error, no leave-one-control-out display, and no alternative-control range. Calling them a “span across windows” overstates robustness: it is mostly cross-adopter dispersion in one preferred window.

Gamma’s pre-window is May–June, i.e. the spike. The write-up notes control recovery, but still lets Gamma’s raw −8.45h / −15.6h sit in the main table as if it were an adoption contrast rather than mean-reversion after a common shock plus a readiness-selected launch. Beta is under-interpreted: complexity rises less than at Epsilon, Beta *gains* an FTE, and the raw median still worsens. That weakens the “relative p90 improvement = automation buffer” story more than the text admits.

The intensity table is only weakly attached to the script. Section 5 reports unadjusted and adjusted per-10pp associations; the code estimates only the covariate-adjusted TWFE. Those unadjusted numbers are therefore not reproducible from the artifact that is supposed to make the analysis reproducible. The coefficients are also still TWFE on five clusters—the design the identification section just rejected—so Section 5’s “dose associations” are not an implementation of the primary strategy.

**Identification vs code incoherence.** Relative-time dummies are built and then unused. No cohort-time ATT, no not-yet-treated restriction, no lead coefficients. The fitted models are the conventional two-way FE specifications the memo warns can take negative weights and mix treatment versions. Comments cannot substitute for the estimator the memo says the data require. The fourth “strategy” is a future RCT; it does not identify anything in the supplied panel and pads the identification table.

**Rollout design flaws.** `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the right bucket, but the design fights itself. Activating randomized subsets in *both* remaining branches retires the last never-treated units, which are the only credible sensors for organization-wide fraud-desk load. Within-branch manual cells do not replace that. Holding high-complexity and safety-sensitive cases on manual triage during the learning phase means the ITT is identified on the cases least responsible for p90, while the scale-up rule is written in terms of tail harm. Team-week assignment on a shared specialist/fraud queue also leaks treatment; case-level encouragement on eligibility would have matched E3 better. Guardrails (p90 +10% or +5h, rework +1pp, CSAT −0.10) are specific but not tied to sampling variation or to how monthly p90s are constructed.

**Smaller issues.** Confidence 55 on an effect whose own scenario interval includes +1 hour is slightly assertive. Escalation is the most identified directional finding; treating March’s common +2pp desk jump as serious counterevidence is conservative to the point of being slightly misleading, because controls flatten after May while adopters keep rising. CSAT Manski bounds are correctly computed and correctly kill a population claim; the respondent-mean interval (−0.2, +0.2) is more rhetorical than identified. Section 7’s safe claims are well worded; Section 5’s compressed “relative benefit” language is tighter than the identifying assumptions support.

**Net.** This would be a high-9 if the code estimated the group-time/event-study the memo advocates, if the main contrasts were not centered on the nicest post window, and if the next rollout preserved a holdout for spillovers and randomized the cases that generate the tail. As written it is excellent causal hygiene with a real strategy–estimator gap and a learning design that ducks the tail.
