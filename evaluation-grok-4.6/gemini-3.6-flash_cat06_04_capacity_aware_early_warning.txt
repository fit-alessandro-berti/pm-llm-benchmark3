**5.5**

The write-up looks like a complete policy paper, but it is not internally consistent and the live allocation is operationally weak. The required qualitative moves are present—I-03 is excluded on stage, I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is re-entered on new evidence, and spillovers are named—so this is not a `p_now` ranking. Those hits do not offset contradictions between the stated rule and the 12:00 decision, formula–calculation mismatches, unused calibration, and a portfolio that spends scarce, sequential specialist time on long-horizon, low-effect cases.

## Decision rule: specified one policy, ran another

The eligibility and persistence rules are deployable on the page and then violated in the table.

The suppression rule is explicit: a repeated unacted alert is suppressed unless there is a material new event or \(\Delta p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.15\). I-09 is a second alert, no prior swarm, no new event, and \(0.69-0.62=0.07\). I-02 is the same pattern (\(0.66-0.58=0.08\)). Under the author’s own rule both are ineligible. I-09 is nevertheless a Slot 3 intervention and I-02 is “eligible / wait.” That is not a small bookkeeping miss. The persistence rule is the mechanism that is supposed to stop alert-refresh noise from consuming capacity, and the case chosen for a live swarm is one the rule forbids.

Calibration is theater. Segment shifts are written down (\(p_{\text{SME}}+0.065\), \(p_{\text{SE}}-0.085\), NNL effect bounds \(\pm 20\%\)), then never used to rank. Every published \(EV_{\text{net}}\) is

\[
\text{midpoint}(\Delta p)\times V - 450 - \text{full false-alarm burden}.
\]

That is not the formula in §1.4, which claimed probability-weighted false-alarm cost and a spillover term. Recomputing I-04 with the stated formula (and \(p_{\text{adj}}=0.395\)) gives about €7.9k, not €8,950. The €8,950 figure is \(0.19\times 60{,}000-450-2{,}000\): full €2,000 burden, no spillover. The €1,800 spillover charge is a constant across cases, so even if it had been subtracted it would not have changed ranks. Writing it into the priority metric is presentation, not decision logic.

Other rule issues:

- The 0.75 h feasibility gate is too loose. I-07 is still in Triage with 1.5 h to a *resolution* SLA. A 45-minute swarm that has not even started diagnosis cannot be treated as executable on the same terms as a Diagnosis Started case.
- I-06 is “discount 25% and wait,” with no time-boxed verification. A data-quality gate that does not force a check before the next refresh is not a gate.
- \(p_{\text{adj}}\) is not a substitute for the problem’s warning that \(p\) is observational and is not the probability under intervention. The \(\Delta p\) ranges are used as if they were planning-grade CATEs, then scenario-banded. That is better than equating \(\Delta p\) with \(p_{\text{now}}\), but it is not the same as refusing to treat the ranges as known effects.

## Capacity allocation: value theater, wrong clock

I-03 is correctly killed. I-05 is correctly suppressed. The rest of the 12:00 book is hard to defend.

Reported ranks are wrong on the author’s own numbers. Eligible EVs are I-04 8950, I-08 4550, I-09 3550, I-02 2700, I-06 1780, I-01 1685, I-10 1490, I-07 800. I-01 is not “rank 4.” It is sixth. The I-01 rationale (“held back by the slot limit”) is therefore false: I-02 and I-06 outrank it on the metric they claim to be using.

The selected set **I-04, I-08, I-09 + reserve** spends two of three live slots on Strategic Enterprise triage cases with long clocks (10 h and 8 h) and, for I-08, the weakest effect in the eligible set (0.05–0.15) plus a €5,000 false-alarm burden. After the SE overprediction correction, I-08 is roughly a 0.35 case. There is substantial option value in waiting; that option is not priced. I-09 is time-critical and high-effect, which is the only clean “intervene now” argument in the set, and it is the case the persistence rule should have blocked and the case sitting in the sparsest calibration segment.

Meanwhile Diagnosis Started cases with closing windows are parked: I-02 at 3 h, I-06 at 4 h. I-07 is “re-evaluate then wait” at 1.5 h, which in practice is do-not-intervene. SME underprediction, which should *raise* the priority of I-07/I-10, never bites. Section 5 later invents a non-SE equity floor; today’s book is two SE swarms and one NNL swarm. The fairness rule is not applied to the decision it is supposed to constrain.

Two specialists deliver *each* swarm, so slots are sequential, not parallel. Three 45-minute swarms are about 2.25 h of both seniors, before coordination. Ordering I-04 → I-08 → I-09 (the slot numbers they printed) can push I-09 through Diagnosis Complete before the swarm starts. There is no sequence, no preemption rule, and no same-day reserve release (e.g., if nothing regulatory has appeared by 13:30, give the slot to I-02 while it is still in stage). Section 6’s reserve rule is a 30-day utilization tweak after 14:00, not a 12:00–15:00 policy. Management preference for a reserve is treated as a hard hold while real windows close.

Non-monetary obligations are almost absent from the allocation. Public-sector I-01 is a leftover EV rank. The possible regulatory arrival is handled only as a reserved slot, not as a constraint on which of the remaining cases can tolerate delay.

## Expected-impact table: auditable, overconfident, incomplete

The three-scenario skeleton is right, and several lines can be reproduced:

- Optimistic \(\Delta p\): \(0.28+0.15+0.30=0.73\), gross €37.8k
- Base: \(0.19+0.10+0.24=0.53\), gross €26.2k
- Costs: \(3\times 450=1{,}350\)

But the table quietly changes accounting conventions. Optimistic/Base false-alarm cost €4,733 is expected burden using \(p_{\text{adj}}\) (the first place calibration actually appears). Adverse switches to the full €7,800 nominal burden. Spillover uses an invented €15k untreated diagnosis value and additive \(n_{\text{slots}}\times 0.01\text{–}0.03\) on six cases, never stress-tested. Optimistic spillover is written as “6 × +0.03 × €15k,” which is defensible only if +0.03 is the *cumulative* 3×0.01 increment; the prose still calls this “minimal” disruption.

The larger decision-theoretic hole: net *breaches* are never shown. Under their own spillover model, Base extra breach mass is \(6\times 0.06=0.36\) against 0.53 direct, net ≈ 0.17. Adverse is \(0.28-0.54<0\). The portfolio can create more breaches than it avoids, and that is buried inside euro “net gain.” Calling Base confidence **High** is unjustified given SE overprediction, I-08’s thin effect, I-09’s sparse segment, imputation risk elsewhere, and congestion-dependent spillovers.

I-08 is the fragile leg and they know it (Adverse sets its effect to 0 and still keep it in the live set). A robust rule would have dropped I-08, waited on long-horizon value, and used capacity on shorter, better-identified effects.

## Repeated-alert policy: I-05 vs I-07 is the high point

The I-05 / I-07 contrast is the strongest part of the answer and matches the grading rubric. I-05 is a refresh on a static case; I-07 has a new log and should re-enter evaluation even though \(p\) fell slightly.

Everything around that contrast is thinner than it looks. The ASCII flowchart restates the rule that I-09/I-02 violate. There is no clock-based override when remaining SLA approaches swarm length, no stage-change re-entry, and no review of whether suppressed cases later breach. I-07 is admitted to the EV queue and then starved; with 1.5 h left that is an implicit hard reject, not a deferral.

## Fairness: correct risks, safeguards that do not bind

The five required risks are all named, and the mechanisms are the right family: label confounding from treating high-\(p\) cases, SE vs SME calibration distortion, NNL variance, value-based starvation, spillover landing on the untreated tail.

The safeguards are not operationalized.

- The non-SE slot floor is not used today.
- A constant spillover penalty does not re-rank.
- \(\varepsilon\)-greedy randomization on live critical incidents is proposed without an ethics/capacity constraint; burning 5–10% of monthly slots on medium-risk holdouts is not free exploration when each slot delays six other diagnosis cases.
- IPW / S-learners / “Intervention_Applied” flags are directionally right and still hand-wavy about identification (swarms are assigned by the same EV rule that will be in the propensity model).

## Monitoring: dashboard completeness, weak identification

The four-block dashboard is the right inventory: slot burn, added queue delay, segment ECE/Brier/PSI, CATE, allocation shares, spillover incidence. Triggers on ECE, 20-minute delay, and idle reserves are concrete.

Causal claims are oversold. There is no single eligibility threshold, so regression discontinuity around “the” threshold is cargo-cult. Randomized holdouts collide with the same scarce-slot and spillover problem as \(\varepsilon\)-greedy. There is no monitor for the things that actually failed in §1–2: suppression-rule compliance, whether swarms still land before Diagnosis Complete, whether suppressed cases breach, same-day sequence/preemption, and imputation-driven jumps like I-06.

## Net

This is a fluent, six-section answer that would beat a probability-threshold policy and that gets the three “strong answer” tells. It is not close to flawless. The persistence rule and the live selection disagree; the EV formula and the EV numbers disagree; calibration is defined and then ignored for ranking; I-01’s rank is misstated; I-08 should have been a wait; sequencing and same-day reserve release are missing; Base impact is overconfident; net breach externalities are underplayed; and the fairness floor is announced after the SE-heavy book is already locked. Those are structural defects, not polish issues.
