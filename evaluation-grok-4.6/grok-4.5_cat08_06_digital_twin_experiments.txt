**8.0**

A strong, professionally structured design that hits every required section and the main simulation/PM constraints. It is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading the defects below are enough to keep it well short of 9+.

## What is actually good

The twin is a shared-resource DES, not an i.i.d. case replay. Boundary, entities, queues, HR calendars, AND-joins, architect bursts, district batching, and a **latent** objection shock are all in scope. Missing starts are not silently treated as service. Objections are never treated as observed events. PM4Py is used only for log/model handling (`pandas` I/O, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree` with documented limits, variants, DFG, performance DFG, arrival rate, timing-quality flag). The experiment grid includes B0, D1–D10, extra packages, a steady-state contrast, and more than three stress cases. Warm-up, CRN, tail metrics, second-order uncertainty, and “no optimality” language are the right ideas. Decision metrics cover median, tail, SLA-by-type, fairness, utilization, rework, backlog, and robustness.

## Defects that cap the score

**1. Arrival model in the skeleton is miscalibrated (real inconsistency).**  
The prose correctly says peak months are 1.7× January–February. The code sets peak months to 1.7 and off-peak to 1.0 on top of a **mean** historical rate. That inflates annual volume by roughly a third. Monday = 2.2 vs Wednesday = 1.0 is also applied without renormalizing the week, so weekly volume is inflated again. Text and code disagree on a first-class requirement (non-stationary demand).

**2. Two arrival stories are superimposed without a clean generative model.**  
Section 2 specifies both a marked NHPP and an architect cluster process. It is not stated whether clusters **replace** the NHPP or sit on top of it. Implemented as written, cases can be double-counted. For a digital twin whose point is bursty, correlated demand, that is not a cosmetic gap.

**3. Required log excerpt is not actually built.**  
`ILLUSTRATIVE_LOG_ROWS` contains one event and a “truncated for brevity” comment. The spec asked for a DataFrame of the given excerpt. Analytics on a one-row log make `case_arrival_avg_per_day` NaN and make DFG/variant checks vacuous.

**4. Experiment table and formulas are damaged and, in one place, logically wrong.**  
Missing operators turn D1/D3 cells into fragments (“Missing-doc loop (e.g. 40–70% relative)”, “travel”, “First-case wait”). The composite score adds `norm(median)` and `norm(p90)` (lower-better) to SLA and `cost_inverse` (higher-better) without inversion. Utilization thresholds also disagree (flag >85% vs decide at >90%). A decision section that cannot be executed as written is a material flaw.

**5. Warm-up is short relative to the process the twin is supposed to rank.**  
Complex-variance p90 is 260 days. A 90-day warm-up (even with MSER-5 language) does not establish a credible backlog or calendar regime for the cases that dominate tail KPIs. Residual-WIP reporting does not fix initialization bias on p90/SLA.

**6. Design-to-code leakage on resources and interventions.**  
Monday portal-support clerk, Simple-only planners, legal/public-notice holiday gating, D4 FTE overlay, D5–D8 effects: specified in prose, mostly absent from `CalendarSpec` / `estimate_parameters_from_analytics`. `is_on_shift` returns `True`. D4 is a comment in `run_experiment`. Placeholders are allowed; claiming calendar-constrained, intervention-ready structure while the objects do not encode those constraints oversells fidelity.

**7. Holdout design is too coarse for the stated seasonality.**  
12-month train / 6-month test can miss an entire peak pair (Mar–May vs Sep–Nov). For a model whose main claim is non-stationarity, that is a weak OOS plan. Seasonal blocking or two half-year holds should have been specified.

**8. Minor PM4Py/engineering sloppiness.**  
`pm4py.get_variants(framed)` is unguarded while a fallback import (`variants_get`) is unused. Estimation barely uses the computed variants/DFG (hard-coded mix and 1.7× multipliers instead). CRN is “same seed” without the stream separation the text promises, so synchronization across policies is not actually designed.

**9. A few modeling shortcuts are under-argued.**  
Applicant delays are i.i.d. despite seasonal and architect-clustered demand. D2 equity (low-value vs high-value Simple renovations; districts with cheaper stock) is only covered by a generic fairness metric. Public-notice is correctly a duration mixture in words, then stuffed into `median_service_min` as 14×24×60 “service” minutes — easy to consume as FTE work.

## Dimension notes

| Lens | Strict read |
|---|---|
| Simulation design | Cross-case + calendars + joins are conceptually right; arrival/calendar implementation is not trustworthy |
| PM grounding | API choices are conventional; excerpt and unused discovery outputs weaken the “estimated from the log” claim |
| Experiment planning | Coverage is excellent; unreadable cells and an invalid scalarized score hurt executability |
| Uncertainty | CRN, tails, S5, latent objections are good; warm-up and holdout do not match the tail they emphasize |
| Assumption realism | Honest about missing starts, objections, flattened logs, no optimality — then the skeleton quietly hard-codes the things the assumptions say not to hard-code |

This is a high-quality consulting design with one serious parameterization error, a broken decision formula, an incomplete required excerpt, and repeated prose/code drift. That is an 8.0, not a 9.
