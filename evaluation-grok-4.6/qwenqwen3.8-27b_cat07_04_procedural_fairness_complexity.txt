**7.0**

The fairness write-up is strong and hits the required constraints: it does not treat raw approval gaps as discrimination, does not wave residual gaps away as case mix, does not treat `risk_score_band` as neutral, and does not recommend removing rights-preserving steps. Sections 1–6 and 8 are organized, numerically accurate, and appropriately calibrated. The grade is held well below the top of the scale because the analysis plan contains real methodological and API errors, and because several claims are left looser than the evidence and the required strictness allow.

**What is done well.** All eight mandated headings are present. The dimension table correctly separates raw parity from adjusted disparity, procedural burden, timeliness, routing consistency, support quality, appeal/rework, and resource allocation. The strategy section usefully distinguishes disparity, legitimate routing, discrimination, and uncertainty, and it is right that language and accommodation should be treated as rights-bearing strata rather than ordinary confounders to be partialled out. Path interpretation is mostly sound: V4/V5/V3 are recognized as policy-consistent, while B’s bilingual bottleneck and handovers, C’s specialist queue and dual supervisor role, and D’s residual approval/appeal gaps are correctly flagged as remaining concerns. Mechanisms, limitations, and mitigations cover the required items and include trade-offs.

**Material flaws.**

1. **The matching design is invalid as written.** The propensity model is fit on the event-level frame (`df`), then matching iterates `b.iterrows()`. Long cases are overweighted, the same case is matched many times, and SEs are pseudo-replicated. A case-level collapse is required before any PS or IPW step. This is not a cosmetic coding issue; it would bias the adjusted comparison the rest of the memo relies on.

2. **Several PM4Py calls are not merely “version-dependent”; they misuse the functions named in the prompt.** `filter_case_performance` filters by duration bounds, not `case_ids=`. `filter_paths_performance` filters path duration, not an arbitrary list of activity sequences. `get_service_time(..., activity=...)` is not a reliable signature. Discovering DECLARE after retaining only two activity names strips case context and does not test the intended constraints. The sensitivity loop is a stub (`print(name, covs)`). Presenting this as an executable plan overstates what the code would do.

3. **Invented or underspecified inputs.** The plan filters `risk_score_band == "High"` even though no such label is given. `audited_risk_band` is assumed into existence. These are small individually, but they make the “run this” section look more complete than it is.

4. **Strategy and implementation do not match.** Section 2 correctly says to stratify on language/accommodation need and not control those needs away. Section 7’s B-vs-A matcher never implements that design; it only matches on the preliminary-model covariates. The memo therefore recommends one identification strategy and codes another.

5. **Uncertainty is asserted, not used.** The preliminary model supplies point estimates only. The answer repeatedly treats −5.5 pp, +8.0 pp, and +4.1 days as established residual effects without saying that no SEs, CIs, or cell sizes were provided, and that Group C’s −2.2 pp could easily be noise. “Indeterminate” is the right bottom line; the body is more confident than the tables support.

6. **Minor unclarities.** “Not clearly procedurally fair” and “indeterminate” are compatible but slightly mixed. Quality/dignity gaps are correctly marked unmeasured, then sometimes allowed to color the burden narrative. Handover counts from event shifts treat every resource change as a fairness-relevant handover, including legitimate specialist transfers.

The substance of the fairness assessment would sit closer to 8.5 if Section 7 were omitted. With a broken case-matching design, incorrect PM4Py usage, and more certainty than the adjusted table can bear, **7.0** is the appropriate strict score.
