**5.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton and a mostly defensible missing-start reconstruction, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. Required quantities are missing, several published numbers are wrong, and the bottleneck story overreaches the evidence.

**What is actually solid.** Assumptions A1–A8 are explicit. The resource-feasibility intervals for P05/P07/P11/P15 are the right idea: P05 S2 cannot start before C1 frees at 09:45; P07 S1 cannot start before I1 frees at 09:25; P11 S2 cannot start before C2 frees at 12:00; P15 S1 cannot start before I1 frees at 11:40. Most fully observed waits and services are correct. OPEN work is treated as elapsed, not invented final service. Utilization is not computed from completed cases alone. The heading set matches the spec.

**Required outputs that are missing or only gestured at.** Case-level total sojourn (`s2_end − arrival`) is defined and never reported for the 14 completed cases (60, 75, 95, 105, 110, 135, 135, 180, 155, 175, 180, 205, 210, 230 min). Little’s Law is promised in §2 and never computed on any interval with \(L\), \(\lambda\), and \(W\). Waiting cases P18–P20 (and P22) are labeled `WAITING` without elapsed lower bounds (50, 35, 15, and 110 min). Rounding is named (±2.5 min) and then almost unused.

**Factual errors that should not appear in a finished table.** The subperiod throughput table is largely wrong. P21 arrives at 11:50, not in 12:00–14:00. S1 completions 10:00–12:00 are not P08–P16 (P15 ends 12:15, P16 ends 12:35). S2 completions 10:00–12:00 are not P07–P14 (P10–P14 finish after 12:00); the actual completions in that window are P05, P06, P07, P09, and possibly P08. S2 arrivals 12:00–14:00 are not “2 (P17, P18?)”; P14–P20 all enter S2 in that span. S2 peak WIP is not 6: after P20’s S1 end at 13:45, C1 still holds P14 until 13:50, C2 holds P15, and P16–P20 are waiting — WIP 7. The claim that after 12:00 “only one server works after P14 ends” is false: P15 is on C2 from 13:30 and P17 starts on C1 at 13:55.

**Internal inconsistencies.** Section 3 gives P07 wait [40, 75]; the pessimistic scenario uses start 09:55 and wait 70. Section 4’s S1 wait band “60–69” does not match §6 (63.8–65.5) or the arithmetic implied by those scenarios (about 63.5–66.5). Optimistic/pessimistic means look hand-waved. Central S2 wait/service for P05 is the *boundary* of the feasible set (start 09:45), not a midpoint, while the text still calls it central.

**Process-mining mistakes.** “Indirect blocking” (S1 cannot release until S2 has capacity) is invented. There is no after-S1 blocking; a finished S1 case simply joins the S2 queue. The bottleneck section treats a 6-hour mean-capacity comparison as decisive and then says S2 is the bottleneck “throughout,” which the prompt itself flags as the weak reading. Early arrivals are about 19 cases by 11:00 versus S1 capacity near 4.2/h — S1 is the binding constraint in the morning surge; S2 is binding later, after the S1 backlog lands and during the C2 outage. That is a time-varying combination. S1’s larger mean wait and higher peak WIP are acknowledged and then talked past.

**Code does not carry the analysis.** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is overwritten by `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, so the printed “service/waiting” columns are not produced by the first call. `compute_wip` ignores the stated completion-then-arrival-then-start order, does not handle `open_end`/`censor_start`, and can double-count when timestamps tie. P17/P21 fall out of `case_table`. Bounds are hardcoded, not derived from resource calendars. Optimistic/pessimistic scenarios are not executed. A reader cannot reproduce §4–§6 from the script as written.

The missing-start logic and the completed-case wait/service list are good enough that this is not a collapse. The station-level table, Little’s Law gap, sojourn gap, WIP error, blocking claim, and bottleneck overclaim keep it firmly mid-pack.
