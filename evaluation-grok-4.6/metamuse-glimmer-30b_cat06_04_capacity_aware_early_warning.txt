**5.5**

The write-up has the right skeleton: six required sections, I-03 correctly blocked on stage, I-05 suppressed as refresh-churn while I-07 is re-opened on a new log, calibration is adjusted by segment, and spillovers appear in all three scenarios. That is enough to clear the “weak answer” failure modes. It is not close to a high score.

The central failure is decision-theoretic incoherence. Section 1 defines a numeric score  
`[midpoint reduction × value − 450 − false-alarm] × confidence × urgency`.  
Under that rule the ranking is roughly I-04 ≫ I-08 > I-09 > I-02 > I-01 > I-06 > I-10 > I-07. The chosen book is I-01, I-04, I-07. I-07 is the *lowest*-scoring eligible case even after the stated urgency boost (~1.31× still leaves it around €1,050 versus ~€2,500–€3,900 for I-09/I-08/I-02). The text never says the formula is being overridden, nor adds a constraint that would justify the override. A deployable rule that the allocation then ignores is not a deployable rule.

Operational realism is weak in the same place. The swarm is a 45-minute *diagnostic* intervention that is useful only before Diagnosis Complete. The natural locus is Diagnosis Started (I-02, I-06, I-09). The portfolio instead spends three slots on Triage cases. I-07 is still in Triage with 1.5 h to the deadline; diagnosis, resolution, and customer validation still sit downstream. A swarm can easily consume the remaining clock without preventing breach. I-04 has 10 h left, a calibrated probability around 0.36–0.43 after the stated SE haircut, and a €2,000 false-alarm load — the option value of waiting is large and is not weighed. I-02 (already in diagnosis, €25k, 3 h left) is parked with a one-line shrug.

Other material nits:

- The score treats supplied reduction ranges as known causal midpoints, which the prompt forbids. Scenarios and a confidence column do not repair a point-estimate ranking rule.
- `p_adj` is defined and then not used in the score or in any explicit eligibility cutoff.
- Spillover cash uses an unstated €20k average on six unnamed diagnosis cases; no sensitivity, and no use of the three *known* untreated diagnosis values as a bound.
- Optimistic “upper half of the range” is implemented as the upper endpoint.
- Adverse “model’s reason for alerting was wrong” is pinned on I-04’s over-prediction of *p*, which is not the same as a null treatment effect.
- Reserve-slot release before 15:00 is unspecified.
- Specialist time (two seniors × 45 min × three swarms) is never converted into queue capacity, only into a generic euro spillover.

Sections 4–6 are the strongest parts (I-05 vs I-07 is clean; label bias + IPW, segment quotas, and a randomized-encouragement sketch are real safeguards). They cannot salvage a policy whose allocation does not follow its own rule and that spends scarce diagnostic capacity on the wrong stage.
