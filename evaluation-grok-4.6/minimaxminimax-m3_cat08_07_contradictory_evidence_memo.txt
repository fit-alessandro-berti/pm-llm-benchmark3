**7.4**

The final memo is strategically sound and format-compliant: it picks a defensible hybrid (E), refuses national scale and full rollback, separates facts/interpretations/assumptions in a dedicated table, explains the five metric conflicts, ranks M1–M8, and states actions, pauses, scale scope, and rollout thresholds. That core is well above average. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless.

**Factual and internal inconsistencies**

- In alternative A, “47% of cases (apartments + old buildings + business + rural)” is wrong. 29% + 18% = 47%; adding business (8%) and rural (11%) is 66%. An executive memo cannot misstate the affected population.
- Timelines conflict. Thresholds are framed as “one controlled quarter,” M4 needs 60 days after billing, and a go/no-go is set at week 8. Week 8 cannot deliver a credible 60-day cost picture.
- Coordinator scaling is ambiguous across sections: the memo says scale ownership “for all segments,” immediate action is only “retain” it in four pilot regions, and M5 is called a precondition for scale-out but ranked last and omitted from “measure next.”
- “Four measurements (M3, M4, M7, plus M1/M6)” is five. Small, but sloppy in a decision document.

**Separation of fact vs. interpretation**

The evidence table is the strongest section (10 items, three-way split, real PM artifacts). Section 3 still overclaims: the skip rule is treated as “tuned (or assumed to be tuned) on the simple-house distribution.” That is not in the evidence. The p90 ≤ 31 day gate for *non-simple* segments uses the *overall* baseline p90; segment baselines are unknown, so the threshold is methodologically loose. “0 unexplained missing-permit-check events” is an arbitrary hard zero.

**Coverage gaps (process-mining artifacts)**

Fitness, precision, alignments, DFG shifts, social networks, and segments are used. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is ignored. Temporal-profile detail is thin: the failed-visit → second-visit delay in high-utilization regions, installation → activation deterioration for apartments, and rural rescheduling / business billing-dispute notes are underused relative to the prompt’s instruction not to ignore those artifacts.

**What still works**

Conflicts are explained causally (happy-path vs. tail populations; allowed vs. observed behavior; cost shifted not removed; handovers vs. redundancy). VoI is decision-linked, not a shopping list: M4 can revive rollback; M1/M6 can restore skipping by stratum; M7 can keep activation suspended. The recommendation does not pretend one lever wins on all metrics.

Net: high-quality synthesis with several avoidable errors of arithmetic, consistency, and over-precision. Those keep it well short of 9–10 under a hypercritical bar.
