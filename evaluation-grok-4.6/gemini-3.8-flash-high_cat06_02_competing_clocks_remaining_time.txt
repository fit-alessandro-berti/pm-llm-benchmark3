**7.2**

The answer is structurally complete, uses \(\max\) rather than a sum, keeps queue wait separate from service, respects weekday/weekend calendars in most places, treats the temperature flag as a common cause, and does not collapse the forecast to a single point. Those are the right bones. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless: several numbers, quantile labels, and calendar claims do not survive a close check.

**What is actually strong.** Chemistry cannot finish on Friday: remaining capacity is 5 analyst-hours, the free times 15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30 are correct, and \(\mathrm{Triangular}(1.5,2.5,4.5)\) cannot fit in the residual Friday window. The join is written as \(\max(T_{\mathrm{chem}},T_{\mathrm{micro}})\) throughout. The observation that Sunday on-call readout does not help a *typical* smooth batch — because chemistry is closed until Monday — is the right kind of insight, even if it is overstated as “zero” benefit. QA backlog at a future Monday join is built from Friday/Saturday/Monday reviewer-hours versus \(0.35/\mathrm{h}\) arrivals rather than freezing the Friday queue of five. Documentation-query and documentation-investigation working hours are converted through the QA calendar (including the Monday 20:00 stop) with correct clock arithmetic in the short and medium paths. Scenario 5 correctly notices that an investigation finishing Saturday 16:00 misses Saturday QA (closes 13:00) and sits until Monday 28 Sep. Dependence is not assumed away. Overlaps among scenarios are declared instead of forced to 100%.

**Quantiles are scenario ranks, not mixture quantiles.** This is the main forecasting error. Smooth-path probability is given as 40–50%. That already implies that a large mass finishes later (rerun, query, contamination, investigation). A P50-like remaining time of 71–75 h (Monday 14:30–18:30) is the *conditional typical smooth path*, not the median of the mixture. With ~45% done by Monday evening and the next slab on Tuesday, the mixture median sits nearer Monday late–Tuesday midday. The P80-like band (Tuesday 11:00–18:00) has the same problem: stacking the stated bands, CDF by Tuesday evening is more like the high 60s than 80; a genuine P80 is more likely a documentation-query/investigation path into Wednesday–Thursday. The write-up even admits the P50 band “represents the nominal Smooth Path (~45% probability).” That is a mode, labeled as a quantile. “P90-like or severe-tail” is allowed to be wide; P50/P80 are not.

**Internal clock/elapsed inconsistencies.** Short-horizon conversions are mostly right (Monday 12:30 = 69 h, Thursday 24 Sep 16:00 = 144.5 h). The long tail is not. Tuesday 29 Sep 12:00 is ~260.5 h from Friday 15:30, not 250.5 h; Tuesday 29 Sep 15:30 is 264 h, not 250 h; Thursday 1 Oct 18:00 is ~314.5 h, not 306.5 h. Section 3’s Scenario 5 end (Tuesday 29 Sep 14:00 = 262.5 h) disagrees with Section 2’s table for a similar stamp. Earliest completion is also not self-consistent: Section 3’s smooth chemistry finish is Monday 10:30–13:30 and terminal 15:00–18:15, while Section 4’s earliest is Monday 12:00–13:30 under “zero QA queue wait.” Zero wait plus a 08:30–09:30 join and a sub-median QA service would be closer to late Monday morning. The earliest band is neither the optimistic envelope of the decomposition nor a coherent all-minima path.

**Calendar slip on microbiology.** Routine microbiology is Monday–Friday 08:00–18:00. Scenario 3 still sends a second readout to “Monday night.” A secondary incubation that becomes ready after 18:00 waits until Tuesday 08:00. The same scenario describes a Sunday on-call start (secondary ready Monday 13:00–23:00) and then drops it from the join formula, using Tuesday 09:30–16:30 as if the Monday-morning readout were the only case. That understates the earliest contamination path (Sunday readout + 20 h secondary can join Monday afternoon, with rejection recording still on Monday). The assumption ledger’s “on-call has zero impact on smooth release” is only approximately true: if residual chemistry is short (risk-priority start Friday, remaining ~0.5–1.5 h), Monday micro readout 08:30–09:30 *can* bind, and Sunday readout would save on the order of an hour, not zero.

**Path accounting is muddy.** FIFO delay appears inside the smooth blend *and* as part of Scenario 2 (“FIFO queue delay or borderline rerun”). Those are different mechanisms: FIFO without a rerun is still a clean-test Monday path; a rerun changes both the join clock and QA investigation probability. Bundling them inflates Scenario 2 and double-counts congestion. Scenario 4’s earliest Tuesday 14:00 is too late for a query-only path (4 working hours from Monday ~15:00 can finish Monday evening) because query and documentation investigation are stacked without saying so. Scenario 1’s “blended” chemistry window Monday 10:30–13:30 compresses a 45/55 discipline mix whose FIFO arm was just described as 13:30–15:30; the blend is shifted early. The 8.5 analyst-hours of Monday work ahead under FIFO is not derived; service times of the urgent and ordinary samples in front of B-417 are unknown and should have been an assumption-ledger row, not a silent input.

**Smaller but real defects.** (1) “A 15-minute delay on Friday pushes chemistry 64 h into Monday” does not apply to this prefix: Friday completion is already impossible, so the relevant sensitivity is hours on Monday, not a 62 h pause. (2) Two reviewers at median 1.5 h are ~1.3 batches/h, not “effective capacity ~1.0 batch/h”; the text elsewhere uses ~2 h mean service without stating that it is a skewness adjustment. (3) Conditional anomaly rates given a confirmed excursion are never set, only said to “increase,” so the 40–50% smooth band and the “>65% smooth if the flag clears” claim cannot be audited; the latter is optimistic relative to mid-range query/rerun/investigation rates. (4) Release 88–92% / reject 8–12% is tighter than the contamination and investigation inputs support. (5) QA wait of 1–3 h at join is a reasonable central guess, but overdispersion is named and then unused.

**Section 5–6.** The driver list is in the right family (calendar thresholds, latent excursion, investigation type, chemistry discipline, contamination). Ranking calendar first is defensible because most of the 70 h remaining is the weekend pause, not Monday service. Calling the Friday 15-minute / 64 h story the exhibit for that driver is the wrong exhibit. The next-event choice (resolve the temperature flag) is the correct one: it moves several path probabilities together; chemistry start/finish on Friday would mainly move hours, not the tail family. Secondary trigger (primary micro readout) is right. Inventing that engineering will close the sensor gap “Friday afternoon/evening” is unsupported.

**Net.** This is a competent scenario envelope with the right process-mining instincts, not an expert forecast you could defend line by line. The parallelism/calendar core is good enough that the piece is clearly above a mean-adding or weekend-blind answer. The quantile mislabeling, long-tail hour/date errors, the Monday-night readout, and the FIFO/rerun accounting keep it well short of a high score under a strict bar.
