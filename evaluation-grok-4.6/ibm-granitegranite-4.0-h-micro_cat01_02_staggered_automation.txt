**2.4**

This submission fails the assignment on structure, arithmetic, and the central empirical pattern. It is not a near-miss; it is an incomplete and factually unreliable memo.

**Missing required output.** Sections 9 and 10 are absent. There is no Python analysis and no claim ladder. Section 7 is not the required effect-assessment table; the rollout section is misnumbered as a second “7.” Identification (section 3) and effect assessment ignore the mandated table schemas. Section 1 is under the 150-word floor and already contains a counting error (“four branches” while listing five).

**Section 1 does not do the job.** Availability vs intensity is named, not distinguished. The answer never states that `automation=1` is branch-month availability, that `automated_fast_path_pct` is realized use, or that Alpha/Beta were selected *because* they were high-volume and historically slow. Routing (+10% to Alpha/Beta, +5% to Gamma), the March fraud desk, May product/complexity shock, FTE moves, manager discretion, and CSAT response rates of 20–45% with missing counts are flattened into a vague list. The closing sentence already slides into impact-assessment language.

**Estimands are malformed.** Estimand 2 treats automated-fast-path use as the intervention and `automated_fast_path_pct` as the outcome — a tautology. There is no heterogeneous or dynamic estimand, despite an explicit requirement. Interference (shared fraud desk, routing) and treatment-version problems (availability ≠ use; gradual fidelity) are not discussed. Marking almost every estimand “No” is crude: the point is to state what is identified under which assumptions, not to abdicate.

**Identification is a method mash-up.** “ITS with synthetic control” is not one design. Binary adoption is then said to target the *intensity* effect on median cycle time — internally inconsistent. Delta and Epsilon are never-treated, not “not-yet-treated”; Gamma is the actual not-yet-treated unit in April–June. Two donor branches cannot support a serious synthetic control. Alternatives are two-line sketches. There is no event-study / group-time ATT discussion, no negative-weighting warning, and no 150–250 word justification that engages these data.

**Mechanisms contain false statements.** Survey selection is missing. Beta is said to have lost FTE; Beta *gained* one in April. Rework is said to have “no direct data”; `rework_pct` is in every row. High-complexity is treated as an automation consequence rather than the May product change that also hits never-treated branches. The March +2pp escalation jump is common to all five branches and is not separated from any extra post-adoption rise.

**Section 5 invents a sample and mis-adds.** Pre is labeled “Jan–Aug” and post “Sep–Dec.” The panel ends in August 2026; September–December do not exist. The cells are single months (mostly March vs August), not window averages. Alpha 4→48 and Beta 6→50 are recorded as **+4** rather than +44. Controls are described as showing “no significant changes.” That is false: Delta median goes 34.4 → 42.6 → 39.0; Epsilon 41.4 → 49.4 → 40.8; both p90s spike in May with the complexity shock. Immediate post-adoption months at Alpha/Beta are *worse* (Alpha June median 53.5, p90 119.9), then recover in July–August *with the controls*. The memo never computes a transparent adopter-specific window, never contrasts with not-yet-treated Gamma in April–June, never shows a real intensity gradient, and asserts an undemonstrated sensitivity analysis. Median and p90 are not kept apart.

**Effect assessment reverses several series.** Alpha’s “−6.1 hours” is March vs August cherry-picking. Gamma rework *falls* after July adoption (14.6 → 12.6) after rising in May *before* adoption; the memo says it rose. Alpha/Gamma CSAT are said to decline; both end at or above January. Confidence of 60–80% is indefensible with five clusters, endogenous targeting, and a common May shock. Leadership is implicitly offered a cycle-time-reduction claim the identification and the raw paths do not support.

**Rollout is internally contradictory.** The chosen label is “expand with an embedded evaluation,” then the text recommends “immediate expansion to Delta and Epsilon.” Two months pre/post is too short given the documented learning ramp and monthly volatility. There is no holdout, no stepped-wedge or randomized phase-in among remaining units, and no decision rule that treats one never-treated branch as a contemporaneous control. The guardrails assume benefits already shown; they were not.

**What a competent answer had to see and this one did not.** Rollout is targeted at the historically worst, high-volume branches; treatment intensity ramps from ~25% to ~50%; a May complexity/product shock and a March fraud-desk shift hit treated and untreated units; staffing and inbound routing move at the same time; tails worsen more than medians right after April go-live; CSAT is a selected monthly mean. No single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” statement is defensible. The quantitative core of this answer is not merely imprecise — it is calculated on dates that do not exist and on contrasts that get the sign or the arithmetic wrong.
