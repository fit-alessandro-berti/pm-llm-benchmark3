**6.3**

The final six-section write-up gets the main audit thesis right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing mix change rather than concept drift, August–September are overlapping process, concept, and population shifts, and October is not a comparable performance month because of immature labels. That is the difference between a strong and a weak answer on this prompt, and it is stated clearly enough that the piece is not a collapse into a single drift label.

It is not close to flawless. Several conclusions are internally inconsistent, a few numbers are invented or wrong, and the monitoring sketch would destroy the event log.

**What holds up.** The diagnosis table covers all six requested types and usually separates support from counter-evidence. Mature V3 breach rates (0.30 → 0.46 → 0.64) are correctly treated as the cleanest process/concept signal. The stable remote-fix edge (3.8–4.6h) is used as a contrast to `Order Part → Part Received`. June is not over-read as model failure. October’s 0.16 observed breach is not treated as real improvement. Tests are framed as competing hypotheses. Alert design correctly blocks “label-delay ⇒ retrain.” The four requested failure modes in section 6 are present and proportionate.

**Material errors.**

1. **April is called training-safe.** Section 4 lists January–June as safe because of “stable pipeline.” That contradicts the rest of the memo. April is the month whose prefixes, unknown-activity spike (17%), missing queue feature (14%), unmapped variants (18%), and unrecomputed cached features make it *unsafe* until recomputation. Confidence for pipeline failure is also too low (Medium) given the audit trail.

2. **Label maturity is confused with scoring policy.** “Suppress predictions for cohorts with label maturity <80%” and “use the base model only on 100% maturity, pre-July data for any automated decisions” mix evaluation confounding with production inference. Immature *labels* contaminate metrics and training sets. They are not a reason to stop scoring live July–October cases. Containment should freeze bad features, isolate V3/Partner Z, and stop treating incomplete vintages as KPIs.

3. **The code drops the process.** `drop_duplicates(subset=[case_id], keep='first')` keeps one row per case and deletes the log. Duplicate handling should be on event identity, not case id. Event-calendar vs case-start cohorts are described in comments, not built. `filter_time_range(..., m + '-31')` is invalid for 30-day months. `get_variants(...).count()`, `case_start_timestamp`, `breach_time`, and `is_unknown` on the temporal frame are invented. The sketch never actually emits the one edge the case turns on (`Order Part → Part Received`).

4. **Factual and invented quantities.** V2 did not “triple” to 52% (baseline ~35%, April 29% with 18% unmapped). Stratified AUROCs such as “V3: Jan 0.81 → Jul 0.71 → Aug 0.65” and a V1 predicted risk of 0.18 are not in the data. Under a pure label-delay hypothesis, mature-label AUROC would *not* be expected to recover to ~0.75 for August–September, because mature V3 rates already exploded. June is also used as a DFG “supplier remediation” contrast, which is the wrong month.

**Smaller problems.** May is not given its own month in a supposed January–October narrative, so residual pipeline vs recovery is underspecified. Covariate drift is anchored on June even while the same row says June is mix change; a V2 share shift is process-variant mix, and Partner Z’s unused rural/distance features are population/missing-feature issues, not classic covariate drift on the trained feature set. April is weakly implicated under label delay (“feature backfill”), which blurs schema failure with censoring. Recalibration is called “conditionally safe” on July–October without a hard mature-only constraint, which undercuts section 6.

The triangulation is good enough to be useful. The contradictions on training safety, the maturity/scoring mix-up, the fabricated test numbers, and the case-level dedupe keep it well below a high score under a strict reading.
