6.4

The diagnosis is the strongest part of the piece and is genuinely grounded: it uses the frequency DFG (2550 / 2300), the performance DFG, token replay (fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces), alignment deviations on R1/R2, the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split, the 31%→9% cost erosion, the 26% repeat-evidence loop, the 140-hour unclear segment, and the resource constraints. The core contradiction is stated clearly and is not collapsed into “the slow path is better.” That is real process-mining synthesis, not Lean boilerplate.

Everything after that is weaker than it first appears.

**Redesign distinctness is overstated.** A is a kitchen-sink. It already contains segment-aware auto-rules, an evidence portal, a preference for Repair quote on high-value work, and a pre-dispatch quality gate. Those are the load-bearing ideas of B and C. B and C are therefore not three independent theories of change; they are residual emphases after A has already taken the useful pieces. “Materially different” is not satisfied by changing the title while recycling the same levers. A also quietly equates “high-value device” with “claim > EUR 400.” R1 is a value rule, not a product-class rule; treating them as identical is a conformance error.

**Several quantitative readings are sloppy.** Repair quote → Customer approval at mean 30 hours is mostly customer wait, yet C treats it as technician/part-price delay. The 24-hour service-credit threshold is stated only for Premium low-risk accessories; C applies it to “Premium cases (any value).” Fast-path complaint rates of 6–8% on accessories are not the same phenomenon as 18–22% on high-value devices, but A’s experiment later pretends a 20% relative drop on the low-risk cell is a meaningful test of the high-risk theory. C’s repair-default would push on the order of ~35% of volume (~1,750 cases) onto a path that currently clears ~460 customer approvals with three technicians and two inspectors, while also scaling the 12.5% high-value repair-rework loop. Calling this “rebalancing under-used QC” is not analysis; it is hand-waving. B’s “light Fraud screening in parallel” ignores a two-specialist Monday bottleneck.

**The table looks complete and is semantically messy.** Arrow polarity flips by column: ↑ means worse cycle time, better quality, and worse resource pressure. That is unacceptable in a Pareto-style comparison. Marking A as “dominated? No” is a dominance claim the prompt explicitly forbids; the disclaimer paragraph does not unsay the cell. Several cells (“neutral to modestly better,” “mixed”) are too vague to support a decision.

**The experiment does not test the design it leads with.** Stage 1 pilots A on low-risk accessories only. A’s distinctive claims are high-value diversion, hard R1/R2 routing, and a pre-dispatch gate for wrong replacements. None of those are identified in the low-risk cell. The first pilot therefore cannot falsify A’s theory of change. Sequencing A then C also double-books the same slack resource: A consumes idle quality inspectors for a pre-dispatch gate; C later needs those inspectors for a larger repair stream. The write-up never notices the collision. Control cohorts and 30–60 day downstream cost tracking are the right *philosophy*, but they cannot rescue a pilot aimed at the wrong segment.

**Smaller nits that still matter under a strict bar.** Fitness 0.91 “despite” 68% fitting traces and low precision mash three different conformance concepts into one sentence. A’s complexity is scored “medium” only by pretending the portal and gate are cheap add-ons to a two-week rule change. Escalation-board scarcity is listed and then ignored as a binding constraint whenever Premium priority is added. Residual 12.5% repair rework is treated as an A leftover rather than a cost C actively scales.

The answer is well-structured, non-generic, and unusually faithful to the artifacts in section 1. It is not nearly flawless. The redesigns leak into each other, the capacity math is unserious, the table is internally inconsistent, and the proposed first experiment does not test the intervention it claims to validate. Those are not cosmetic issues.
