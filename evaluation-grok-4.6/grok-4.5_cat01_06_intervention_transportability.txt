**6.4**

The answer is conceptually strong on transportability, co-interventions, statutory gating, bottleneck shift, and a segmented “conditional go.” It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several required artifacts are ambiguous, one quantitative claim is wrong, and the POWL model is internally inconsistent.

### What holds up
The core causal story is right: Site A’s before/after is a bundled, non-identified effect; a matching control-flow diagram does not travel; originals, specialization, the case lock, and legal/supplement loops are effect modifiers; Assessment remains an AND-join; and the naive “create both checks for every case” proposal should be rejected. The digital-vs-originals split, ID ∥ Originals Receipt (not ID ∥ Document Check) on the statutory path, and a median-up / p90-down scenario are the correct structural moves.

### Material flaws

**1. Process specifications are repeatedly unreadable.**  
Tables and the intervention spec lost the relational operators. Cells such as `p90 9678h`, `8%71%`, `4132h`, `1820`, `48005200`, and `11.812.4%` force the reader to reconstruct arrows. In §6 this is not cosmetic. “`Document Check  start before originals when legally required`” can be read as permission rather than prohibition. “`ID  Doc`”, “`Legal Review  Approve/Reject`”, and the priority line “`Request Supplement  Doc rework  aged cases`” are not well-formed constraints. A process-transport answer cannot afford an ambiguous partial order in the candidate design.

**2. The PM4Py/POWL model does not implement the intended language.**  
On the digital path, the same `Doc` and `Assess` objects are nodes of `digital_core` *and* of `loop_body` inside `legal_loop`. POWL children must be disjoint subtrees; shared objects are not “the same activity,” they are a malformed DAG. The originals path patches this (`Doc_o_b`, `Assess_o_b`) after an explicit comment, which shows the author saw the issue and left the digital branch broken. There is also dead code (`loop_body_o` assigned twice).

§6 and §8 disagree on the loop. The text is `LOOP(LegalReview, RequestSupplement → DocumentCheck → Assessment → LegalReview)`, which would execute Legal twice per iteration. The code is `LOOP(Legal, Supp → Doc → Assess)`, which is the better encoding. Required “consistent parent/child relationships” are not met. `try/except` around soundness and playout hides the likely conversion failure instead of demonstrating a sound net.

Legal vs skip-legal is a free XOR, which the comments admit, but the loop still cannot express “supplement may require repeating assessment *and* legal under a case-level guard.” That limitation is stated; the node-aliasing bug is not.

**3. Pilot power arithmetic is wrong.**  
B completes 3,400 cases in six months ≈ 130/week. Stratum D is ~38% ≈ 50/week. A 1:1 case-level RCT yields ~25 cases/week/arm. Reaching 400–600 per arm takes **16–24 weeks**, not “6–10 weeks.” Duration (10–14 weeks) plus 90-day appeal follow-up is also internally inconsistent if appeals are measured on the last enrollees. This is a direct miss on quantitative scenario/pilot rigor.

**4. Scenario ranges are asserted, not derived.**  
§5 says gains scale with “eligible share × true overlap × slack” but never maps A’s service times (ID ~3.5h, Doc ~7.2h ⇒ theoretical makespan save ≈ min of the two, plus wait) onto B (ID 3.4h, Doc 8.1h, originals delay 18h). The 2–4h / 3–6h bands are plausible, not shown. A’s wait drop of 6h is not decomposed into overlap vs huddles vs mix vs +2 FTE vs API. Confidence numbers (25–60) are directionally conservative but unanchored.

**5. Transport graph has small causal errors.**  
`S_legislation -> legal_capacity` confuses statutory *demand* for legal review with the *stock* of legal FTE. `true_overlap -> rework` is drawn without a signed mechanism; A’s rework barely moved. Selection on timestamping into `true_overlap` is correct; there is no node for measurement of cycle time itself, even though back-entry is a first-class threat. The required adjacency list is otherwise complete.

**6. DFG evidence is underused and slightly misread.**  
The 3,400-case relations are the only Site B path frequencies given. They are not used to estimate already-present incidental orders (e.g. `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` 1,020; `Identity Check -> Document Check` 1,160; `Originals Receipt -> Document Check` 2,108). Those counts are not a partition, so they cannot be added naively; the answer never says so. A stricter transport argument would calibrate “how sequential is B already?” from this table.

**7. Smaller logical nits.**  
WIP “6 open tasks” is copied from A’s cross-trained cap and applied per specialized role without justifying the number against 4/7/3/2 FTE. Case-level randomization “clustered by team week” mixes two assignment units. “Originals-non-blocking misclassified small set” in the optimistic scenario is undefined. High-complexity is 48% at both sites in the snapshots used for B, so treating complexity as a *site* difference is weaker than treating tail drivers (originals + legal) as the difference. Assumption 4 restates a conclusion (“A’s 9h is confounded”) as if it were a primitive assumption.

### Scoring rationale
On the rubric’s intended axes—partial-order analogy, refusal to copy a confounded 9-hour effect, no illegal Doc-before-originals start, bottleneck/interference, segmented pilot that separates mechanism from implementation—this would sit near the top of a normal band. Strict grading is dominated by: a non-sound / non-faithful POWL (model fidelity), an impossible pilot N/timeline (quantitative analysis), and systematically broken constraint notation (process structure). Those are not polish nits. They keep the score well below the “nearly flawless” band.
