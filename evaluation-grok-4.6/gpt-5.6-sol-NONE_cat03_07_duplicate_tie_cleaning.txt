**7.1**

The answer is complete, constraint-compliant, and conceptually stronger than most treatments of this problem. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several factual, logical, and implementation defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually strong.** The decision tree matches the required categories and is evidence-ranked rather than mechanical. Hard constraints are respected: e05 and e14 are kept, ingestion is not used as business time, original timestamps are retained as a separate field, and a total order is correctly described as a linear extension. The `have_common_object=True` discussion is the right one: e13 and e14 share O1 and V1 and would still collapse. Provenance design, failure modes, and the named DFG/OC-DFG reinterpretations are thorough.

**Factual error in the sensitivity table.** Raw E2O is **44**, not 47.

e01–e02: 2; e03–e05: 9; e06–e07: 6; e08–e10: 9; e11–e16: 15; e17: 3.

Conservative merges then yield **39**, not 42 (drop the three duplicated e04 relations and the two duplicated e16 relations). Evidence-based merge of e06/e07 must also drop e07’s two now-duplicate (e06, O1/P1) tuples, so E2O should fall again, not stay at 42. The write-up even says e03/e04 and e11/e16 reduce E2O, then refuses to apply the same accounting to e06/e07. That is an internal contradiction, not a rounding issue.

**Lifecycle rule vs 10:00 order.** Section 3 lists “lifecycle constraints on a shared defining object” as a semantic rule, then says e05 vs e06 is “not fully established.” e06 packs I2 and P1; e05 is the I2 pick. If shared-object lifecycle is a real rule, pick-I2 before pack-P1 is in the same family as e12 before e13. Calling `e03, e05, e06, e07` merely a “deterministic admissible extension” is either too timid or inconsistent with their own priority list. e08 ≺ e09 is treated the same way even though fact 4 gives the two labels distinct milestone meanings.

**`ocel_drop_duplicates` is mis-justified.** The tree defines exact technical duplicates as full source-key/payload/relation identity, and the text correctly says none exist. The code then runs `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(..., have_common_object=True)` on the **whole** log and labels that a “technical drop.” That is the overlap test they later warn against, and it is their own failure mode 8. A justified use would restrict drop to exact `source`+`source_event_key` (or full-row) matches, or skip the call. `call_supported` can also silently discard `have_common_object` if the installed signature differs, so the “justified scenario” is not even guaranteed to run as described.

**Design/implementation gaps.** `merge_verified_pairs` records an authority map and then never applies it, so e11 does not receive BILLING-authoritative fields despite the table policy. `evidence_based` is cloned from `conservative` after label rewrite, sort, and `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, so the scenarios are not independent and inherit a 15-event synthetic timeline. The DAG/toposort of section 3 is not implemented; the order is a hardcoded list. Section 3 asks for a separate `ordered_timestamp`; the code lets PM4Py mutate the primary timestamp. Quarantine exists in the tree and nowhere in the pipeline.

**Smaller unclarities.** Canonical e03 is reasonable for WMS enrichment but sits oddly with “same physical scan,” which is the scanner observation. Transferring e04’s `source_seq` onto e03 to beat e05 is legitimate only if stated as identity-transferred within-source order; as written it looks like a cross-source sequence comparison they elsewhere forbid. Aggressive cardinality is left as “10–13” when the same-activity/same-time/common-object groups already imply a definite lower bound near 10 if the library merges those groups. Word count is near the ceiling; the extra length is mostly restatement.

The piece would sit in the high 8s if the E2O arithmetic, the drop-duplicates scenario, and the section 3 rules were internally consistent. They are not.
