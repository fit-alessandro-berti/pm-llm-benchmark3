**6.4**

This is a process-aware, well-structured answer that does the main things the rubric rewards: it does not clone the DFG, it splits C6-A from C6-B, it treats missing events as non-proof, and it uses queues, batching, lot linkage, and feed delay. It is not close to flawless. Several required distinctions are applied inconsistently, and two case-defining signals are underused in the actual top-3 lists.

**What is actually good**
- Control-flow obligations are used as constraints, not flavor text: no Fast Close on C6-A/C6-E; RA required on C6-A/C6-C/C6-D given injury or reportability; Supplier Analysis treated as likely on the six-complaint lot.
- Observation vs. process is often handled correctly (C6-C/C6-D disposition and notification marked not enabled; C6-E extra evidence request marked not enabled but still observable if the review completion is delayed).
- Cross-case discussion is the strongest part: same prefix ≠ same state, DFG is unconditional and linearizes concurrency, intervals encode process uncertainty plus feed uncertainty.
- Activity names match the log vocabulary; intervals are wide enough to avoid fake precision; midpoints are roughly coherent as top-3 masses.

**Material flaws**

1. **C6-B inverts the required enablement distinction.** Fast Close is marked `process_enabled: true` while Close — No Safety Issue is `false`, with the same underlying story: review has not completed, so a closure label would appear next only if intermediate completions are delayed or merged. Fast Close is also listed as an “open obligation,” which it is not; it is a permitted terminal label at S1, and the DFG never shows it after an evidence cycle. Eligibility under rule 2 is not current enablement. This is a direct miss of the task’s own example, not a wording nit.

2. **C6-D’s distinctive evidence is not in the predictions.** The case is migration-affected, a source attachment exists, and evidence-cycle events are absent. Rule 7 plus the observation model say the next *logged* row may be a delayed Evidence Received / Evidence Review Completed / catch-up bookkeeping event. The answer parks that in caveats and suspected obligations, then spends ranks 2–3 on Disposition and Authority Notification. That is the wrong ranking of observation risk for the one case where feed corruption is explicit.

3. **C6-E omits Technical Investigation Completed.** After Evidence Review, the DFG’s common investigation completion is TI (173), not Supplier Analysis (which is slow: 6–36 h). A lot linked to six complaints makes TI process-plausible and, under the alternative S4 the answer itself proposes, possibly already running. Putting another (not-yet-enabled) evidence request above any technical-investigation completion overweights a delayed-log story and underweights the parallel-work story the state section already admits.

4. **Authority Notification is overweighted as the next logged event** on C6-C and C6-D (high end 0.25). For it to be next at 14:00, RA completion and typically Disposition Recorded must both be hidden, then the 16:00 batch fires. The batching point is good operational knowledge; the probability is not. On C6-C the feed flag is normal, so putting Disposition at 0.20–0.45 while marking it not enabled assigns a large mass to an 8–15% delay channel.

5. **C6-A rank 2 is weakly anchored.** RA as next completion is possible, but unlike C6-D there is no worklist start. The same operating note that high-severity work can overtake the TI queue is barely used, so TI is demoted mainly for staffing rather than for competing enablement. Another evidence cycle after review (completeness 0.86; DFG 101 recurrences) is ignored entirely, which is acceptable for *next* event only if rank 1 ERC is treated as almost certain — and it is not (0.45–0.75).

**Smaller issues that still matter under a strict reading**
- Alternatives for C6-A/C6-B (already past review by 14:00 after 12:10/12:16 receipts) are thin relative to the 1–5 h review queue; the template “always give S4/S5” is doing work the evidence does not.
- Primary-state writeups smear S3 and S4 (investigations “already underway” while the label is still EVIDENCE_REVIEW). The alternative field exists to carry that split; mixing it into the primary label blurs the abstraction the prompt defined.
- Several “contradicting evidence” bullets are just extra uncertainties (possible future SA, queue delay) rather than evidence against the stated next-event ranking.
- Rank-1 intervals on A and E are nearly copy-pasted despite different elapsed time since receipt (~1.8 h vs ~40 min) and different competing completions.

**Net**
The answer is clearly above a DFG-copy / missing-event-as-absence baseline, and the A-vs-B discussion plus migration caveat language would score well on a generous rubric. Under the requested standard — minor logical inconsistencies count, and a very high score requires near-flawlessness — the enablement contradiction on C6-B, the C6-D prediction list ignoring delayed evidence, the C6-E TI omission, and the inflated notification/disposition tails are enough to keep it well out of the top band.
