The assessment is complete, well-structured, and largely process-aware. It uses the required headings, separates facts from assumptions, refuses a single total ordering, applies rules at the time of the act in most places, and does not treat post-hoc approvals or late ERP timestamps as conclusive. Classifications are differentiated, alternatives name the premise that must change, and the evidential four-way split in §6 is the right kind of discipline. Those strengths are real. They are not enough for a high score under a strict reading, because several central claims do not cohere.

**Internal contradiction between the rule catalogue and the case results.** G2 is stated as non-waivable on the independent-budget-owner element; F1/F2 are expressly limited to the Finance-Controller element; IC members are not treated as budget owners. P-505, P-507, and P-508 then still come out as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` despite that element never being satisfied. Calling the gap “organizational” does not discharge G2. The pseudo-logic makes the same error in formal terms: the `unless` clause defeats *all* of G2 when joint IC authorization exists, while the comment and the later `overrides_for(F1, finance_element(G2), …)` line say the override is finance-only. That is not a residual ambiguity. It is the catalogue disagreeing with itself, and three case labels riding on the looser branch.

**Time-of-act slip on D1 and P-502.** The answer applies outage skew to P-502’s 12:40/12:55 stamps, which only makes sense if the purchase is 14 July. D1 is sent 15 July at 07:20. The assessment still credits D1 for P-502’s Finance element. Even a window-wide reading of the email needed to be argued; A6 cuts against using a next-day instruction to repair a missing pre-commitment control. The primary label stays `NONCOMPLIANT` because of A3, so the error is not outcome-dispositive, but it is exactly the timing failure the prompt warned against. The 14 July date itself is inferred, not stated as an assumption.

**P-501 is the most lenient defensible reading, presented as the main one.** B4 licenses commitment before the PO when a six-hour stoppage is predicted; it does not say approvals may follow, and B5 plus A4 cut the other way. The Plant Manager was reachable, with a four-hour runway, and never delegated SHIFT-1. Same-day Plant/Finance stamps at 15:10/15:12 are regularization, not pre-commitment control. `COMPLIANT` is therefore a stretch; `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` or low-severity `NONCOMPLIANT` would have followed more tightly from the hierarchy the author otherwise adopts. Section 5 records the stricter view, which is why this is a judgment defect rather than a silent one.

**Smaller defects.** Pseudo-logic operators are broken in several lines (`amount  50k`, missing comparators), so the formal layer is not actually inspectable. C3 *recording* is unproven for P-505 and P-508; under the author’s own burden rule those exception elements should count as unsatisfied, but they are parked as missing evidence while the primary label still leans on ECD-2. P-504’s “unregistered = new” mapping for A3 is declared, then allowed to inflate severity without a fallback classification if the mapping is rejected (B1/B3 would still fail).

Those pieces show a capable analyst. The grade is held down because the formal rules, the Finance-email timeline, and three of the eight labels do not line up, and because P-501 is classified as if the emergency path swallowed approval timing when the cited texts do not say that.
