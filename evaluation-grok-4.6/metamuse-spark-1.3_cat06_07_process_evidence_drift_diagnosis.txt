**8.2**

The diagnostic core is strong: April is treated as a schema/pipeline incident, June as an intended routing/mix change without path-level concept drift, August–September as overlapping performance, concept, and (from September) population shift, and October as dominated by immature labels. That decomposition is the main thing the question asks for, and it is carried through the table, timeline, tests, training gates, and failure modes. The remaining defects are real and keep this well short of a top score.

**What is actually good.** Predictive metrics are repeatedly triangulated with DFG edge times, variant shares, and variant-conditional breach rates (including the July mix identity: a V2-heavy caseload hides a V3 breach jump to 0.46). Falsifiers are stated in the table, not only supporting stories. Observed evidence is mostly separated from hypotheses. Remediation is staged and proportionate: repair April before any recalibration, do not retrain on October, do not treat June’s ECE as model death, do not launch a Partner Z model from immature, confounded gaps. Alert gating in section 5 correctly blocks retraining on maturity and data-quality alarms.

**Material issues**

1. **Required case-start cohort is implemented incorrectly.** Section 3 describes case-start vintages correctly; the code uses `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_contained")` and even comments “contained/started.” Those are not the same. `traces_contained` keeps only traces whose first *and* last events fall inside the calendar month. With 33–52 day median label delay, almost no field-service case finishes in the month it starts, so the “started” summaries would be near-empty or systematically stripped of long V3 paths—the exact cases the maturity and parts-delay monitors must see. Event-calendar vs case-start was an explicit requirement; the prose note at the bottom does not salvage the filter.

2. **Prediction-time information set is not respected in the retraining recommendation.** The mandated prefix is immediately after diagnosis, *before* Dispatch / Remote Fix / Order Part. A V3-specific model or variant-stratified calibrator cannot be applied at that prefix unless routing is already a known (or predicted) feature. “Actual `Order Part → Part Received` wait” at diagnosis is leakage; only expected wait / supplier regime is valid. The answer hedges with “expected wait” and prefers a shared model, but still recommends variant-specific models and calibration as if variant were in \(X\).

3. **One quantitative claim is misleading.** “Accounts for ~14pp of 42% V3 in September” is the share of *all* cases that are Partner Z and V3 (\(0.24 \times 0.58 \approx 0.14\)), not Partner Z’s incremental effect on the V3 mix. Versus a 36% non-Z V3 rate the extra mix is about 5pp; versus August’s 35% V3 the September rise is 7pp, only part of which is Z. That cell overstates Z as a mix driver, even though the surrounding argument (Z cannot explain August; non-Z V3 remains elevated) is right.

4. **Section 2 asserts an untested residual.** “Breach probability conditional on V3 rises beyond what wait alone predicted historically” is stated as fact. No wait-binned rates are in the data. Section 1 and the logistic/`wait×month` test correctly treat this as a hypothesis; the narrative does not, which blurs process-performance mediation and true remaining concept drift.

5. **Transport-test expected results are internally confused.** Under a pure mix/population-shift hypothesis, reweighting should restore the overall rate *and* the Z vs non-Z gap should shrink inside variant/wait strata. The answer says the Z-gap would *persist* under that hypothesis, which is the signature of unmeasured confounding/transport failure, not of mix shift. The supplier-concept branch is also garbled (“non-Z Sep overall Sep and reweighted gap remains large”). The test list is complete; the mapping from hypothesis to pattern is not.

**Smaller issues (still charged)**

- After `format_dataframe`, standard keys are `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp`. `cal["case_id"]` and friends are likely wrong. `discover_performance_dfg` values are often duration lists, not `{median: ...}` dicts.
- Dedup on `(case_id, activity_raw, timestamp)` misses the April pattern of the same event labeled both with a real activity and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`.
- August’s variant-matured mix implies \(\approx 0.31\) overall vs observed 0.28; that gap is consistent with 7% immaturity on long paths and is not mentioned, while October’s analogous paradox is.
- “Delay-adjusted V3 risk” in the concept-drift falsifier is ambiguous between parts-wait adjustment and label-delay adjustment.
- Listing July as “safe to train” sits awkwardly next to “onset of concept drift” on 10 July without an explicit pre/post split.

**What does not fail.** October’s 0.16 observed rate is not treated as comparable to mature months. No single drift label is applied to the whole window. Pipeline vs process vs mix vs concept vs population vs censoring are allowed to overlap in time. Section 6’s four failure modes match the requested risks and have usable mitigations.

The answer is a high-quality audit with the right month-level story and the right operational posture. It is not nearly flawless: a required cohort definition is wrong in code, prediction-time leakage/variant-model timing is under-specified, and two supposedly decisive quantitative/hypothesis mappings are sloppy. Those keep it in the low 8s under a strict rubric.
