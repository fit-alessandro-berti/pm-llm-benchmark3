## Material inaccuracies

**U2 load is internally inconsistent and overstated.** The prompt says E and ES demand doubles *and* proportions are renormalized. The simulator renormalizes type shares while holding block λ fixed, which yields 12:00–16:00 Electrical demand on the order of ~400 minutes, not 571. The 571 figure is what you get if you double E and ES contributions *without* reducing M/S/ME and without raising λ, so type weights no longer sum to 1. From that inflated demand the write-up concludes the specialist pair is at ~142%. Under the renormalized mix the same pair is near or below 100%. U2 is one of the scenarios used to reject A and to justify overflow; the severity of that shock is not earned by the stated model.

**F4 path math is not auditable and treats ME and ES as the same number.** “M ≈ 63, E ≈ 70, S ≈ 75, sequential ME/ES ≈ 108, parallel ≈ 90” is never derived from the means in the same section. Reconstructing it requires grafting on two or three 7-minute handovers and a team-change, which are not stated. Sequential ES is longer than sequential ME; quoting a single 108-minute figure is wrong. With a retained cross-skilled resource (Deepa on ME, Chen/Emil on ES) sequential means sit roughly in the 90–106 range *before* queueing, so “only ~10% slack, therefore P1 ME/ES must always be parallel” is too strong. Parallelization is still a good P1 rule under tails and handovers; it is not a mean-path necessity.

**CVaR is not equivalent to the weighted SLA score.** O1 is a scenario-weighted combination of SLA rates and a scaled p90. Calling that “equivalently CVaR of delay” is a category error, not a shorthand.

**U5 Intake outage.** “3 cases queued, each losing 30 minutes of the P1 budget” overstates the clock impact. ~3 arrivals in 09:30–10:00 is a fair expected count under U1, but delay is residual time to 10:00, averaging ~15 minutes, and most of those cases are not P1.

**Morning P1 “×2.6”.** U1 morning P1 volume is ~5.7 versus ~2.05 originally (~2.8×), not 2.6×, and 5.7 is not “per day” in the same sense as the base 5.4 daily P1 count.

## Prose vs code (the recommendation is not what was simulated)

The hybrid policy’s three selling points are only partly implemented.

- **Emil reservation is weaker than advertised.** In `eligible()`, P1 and overflow cases `return list(q)` *before* the Emil/Chen reservation logic. After a 45-minute P2 wait, Emil can be pulled onto Electrical while Software is queued. The write-up sells afternoon Software protection as a first-class mechanism.
- **“P1 always parallel” is a one-shot snapshot.** Parallelism is decided at Intake completion; if both branches are not free at that instant, the case is sequential forever. There is no wait-for-partner, no later upgrade, and no comparison of sequential same-resource vs parallel.
- **Preemption victim selection is reversed.** `min(vict, key=lambda x: (x[0], -x[1]))` with priorities encoded 1 < 2 < 3 preempts **P2 before P3**. The comment says “least important.” The code does the opposite.
- **Breaks are dropped after overrun.** A7 says: finish the activity, then take the break. If `busy_until` lands after the break window, the break never happens. Unavailability is understated on the exact resources that run hot (Chen, Emil).
- **Delay metrics censor unfinished work.** SLA treats horizon leftovers as breaches; `mean_delay` / `p90_delay` average only completed cases. That biases p90 downward in U3/U5, i.e. in the scenarios that are supposed to discriminate policies.
- **`format_dataframe` is not given `start_timestamp_key`.** Interval-aware PM4Py service/wait statistics are therefore not reliably demonstrated; the try/except also allows a silent skip.

These are not omitted-mechanism notes. They change who gets work, who gets preempted, and how bad the tails look.

## Overclaimed dominance

Section 4’s bands overlap heavily (base P1: B 90–95% vs C 92–96%; U2 P1: B 84–91% vs C 86–92%). That does not support “C holds the highest P1 SLA in every scenario” or “dominates under O1.” A honest reading is: A is dominated on shocks; B vs C is a trade of handover burden and reservation rigidity, unresolved at the reported precision. O2 as a *hard* P1-SLA≥90% constraint in every scenario is infeasible (they themselves show U3/U5 staffing collapse); using it as a ranking objective while calling the constraint “hard” is incoherent. Fairness discussion also slips: Farah’s “~20 h/week” is her contracted 4-hour shift, not under-utilization of available time.

Notation is repeatedly un-auditable because comparison and minus signs are dropped (`1  P90_delay`, `SLA_P1(s)  0.90 s`, `r  20 min`, `C  B-style`). In a document whose value is supposed to be reconstructable arithmetic, that is not cosmetic.
