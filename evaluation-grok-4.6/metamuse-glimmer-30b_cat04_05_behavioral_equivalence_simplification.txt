The write-up has the right high-level split (per-iteration check vs one check concurrent with the whole loop) and the right headline verdicts under P1/P2/P3, but it is not a reliable formal comparison. A persistent operator error, a failed counterexample obligation, and non-working comparison code keep it far from a high score.

**The decisive flaw is that the answer does not keep a single semantics for `+`.** After correctly treating `+` as AND/parallel (required by the problem text: “one check runs concurrently,” and by PM4Py), it keeps inventing a “Check-only branch/mode” that exists only if `+` is XOR. That contamination is not a slip in the scratch work; it appears in the structural table, the P2 caveat, counterexample B2, and the final recommendation. Under binary-loop semantics the first loop child always runs once, so Model B **always** executes at least one `Analyze Revision` **and** exactly one `Check Revision`. There is no Check-only language.

**Counterexamples do not meet the spec.** A1, A2, and B1 are valid and useful under AND. B2 (`Open, Check, Close`) is admitted by **neither** model if `+` is parallel. The required “at least two traces of B and not A” is therefore not delivered, and no inclusion proof is given in its place. A second B-only witness is easy (`Open, Analyze, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close` or `Open, Analyze, Rework, Check, Analyze, Close`); it is missing.

**Section 5 is not self-contained working code.** Transitions are never fired (`m2 = m.copy()`), `t.name` is used instead of `t.label`, `pm4py.apply_transition` is fictional, and the visited key `(marking, visible-count, analyze-count)` would collapse distinct traces even if firing were added. Soundness and bounded language comparison are asserted, not performed. Stating that BFS is exhaustive and `play_out` is not does not repair a non-executable explorer.

**Other deductions, still material under a strict bar:**
- Equivalence definitions are the right *kinds* of notions, but the math is broken (`M1 vis M2`, `{t| nA1  c}`, missing symbols). P1-equivalence as “equal intersections with the compliant set” is weaker and less clearly motivated than “same observations under P1’s rules.”
- P2’s verdict happens to be right for AND (`φ₂` really does identify the two languages after collapsing repeated checks and hiding approve/`tau`), but the stated reason (filter B’s Check-only traces) is false.
- Complexity counts for the trees look correct (12 / depth 4 / 6 visible / 1 silent / 0 duplicate leaves). Petri-net simplicity is not computed.
- The P1 simplification (force `Analyze → Check`) is a defensible operational tightening, with decent regression/negative traces. It is **not** shown to be structurally simpler than both originals (still 12 nodes, extra nested `->`). Calling it simpler without that comparison violates an explicit instruction.
- Footprints/EMD caveats are conceptually sound (ordering relations and a low EMD do not give trace equivalence; probabilities are not invented). The API usage is still sloppy.

**What is actually good:** loop-scope vs check-scope is identified; min/max check multiplicity is the right decisive difference for P1/P3; approve/`tau` are correctly treated as non-decisive; bounded comparison is not oversold as a proof; the conclusion that one simplification cannot preserve all three views is right.

A strong answer would have locked `+` = AND, dropped every Check-only claim, given two real B-only traces (including a two-analysis interleaving A cannot produce), and shipped a BFS that fires transitions, treats `label is None` as silent, and prints the bounded symmetric difference. This submission understands the models in outline and then repeatedly contradicts its own semantics.
