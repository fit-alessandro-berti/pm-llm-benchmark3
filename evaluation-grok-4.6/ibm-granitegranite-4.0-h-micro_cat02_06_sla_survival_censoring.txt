**3.0**

This answer has the required heading skeleton and some of the right vocabulary, but it fails the task’s explicit deliverables, contains several first-principles statistical errors, and in places invents or garbles the supplied cases. Under a hypercritical standard it is not a competent process-aware survival analysis.

**Section 1 is the first hard fail.** Time origin and horizon are stated, but the two estimands are internally inconsistent and operationally unusable. Estimand 1 folds competing terminals (withdraw/reject/transfer) together with “still unresolved,” so it is not a well-defined probability. Estimand 2 is named “cumulative incidence of resolution **after** 72 hours” and then defined as the chance of resolving **within** the SLA. Those are different quantities. Worse, the required case-level mapping is simply missing: there is no account of how a resolve at 76h, an open case at 92h, a withdraw at 27h, and a transfer at 68h enter each estimand. That walkthrough was the main test of whether the author can distinguish already-observed breaches, right-censoring, and competing events. Censoring is also misstated as “open at the 72-hour horizon,” which mishandles both S15-type cases (open at 35h: censored *before* the horizon) and S09-type cases (open at 92h: the 72h non-resolution is already observed). Resolve is treated as SLA success even though several resolves in the table occur after 72h (S03, S13, and in cohort B S20, S28).

**Section 2 does not encode the data.** The table applies “right censoring if still open at 72h” to resolved, rejected, withdrawn, and transferred rows, which is incoherent: those rows are already terminal. Sensitivity alternatives are “same as above.” The status label `transfer` does not match the data (`transferred`). The three transfer treatments are not three well-posed identifying assumptions. “Censor at transfer” is described as if transfers occur at 72h; “separate multi-state branch” is a model, not an assumption about the missing external outcome; “discard transfers” is a biased complete-case rule, not a competing-risk encoding. Better, standard options (transfer as competing event, worst-case never-resolved, best-case, IPCW if a transfer model is assumed) are not stated.

**Section 3 is factually wrong on the cases it cites.** S01–S15 are labeled “completed resolved cases.” They are not. That list includes rejects (S05, S14), withdraws (S06, S12), a transfer (S08), and opens (S09, S10, S11, S15). Cohort B is absent from the bias discussion. The required contrast between open cases *below* 72h (still in the risk window: S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) and open cases *already past* 72h (observed non-resolution: S09, S11, S25, S26) is never made. Cohort imbalance and incomplete non-randomized rollout are not analyzed. Informative censoring is asserted generically instead of being tied to the long inactivity gaps on late opens (S11 55h, S25 48h, S26 72h) versus near-zero gaps on recent opens (S15, S31), or to the fact that all transfers are P1 and escalated (S08, S21, S32).

**Section 4 recommends the wrong primary estimator and misstates what it does.** Kaplan–Meier is said to “handle competing risks effectively.” It does not; treating withdraw/reject/transfer as censoring makes the complement of KM an inflated incidence of resolution. That error is especially damaging because the quality bar explicitly forbids treating those outcomes as ordinary non-informative censoring. Landmarking is described as restarting the clock at 72h to study post-transfer outcomes, which is not what landmarking is for here; the relevant use is avoiding immortal time for post-baseline events such as `Escalate`. RMST is called “restricted mean time loss.” Methods are listed, not justified for *n* = 32, two policy cohorts, mixed terminals, and a single freeze. Multi-state is not compared as required.

**Section 5 commits the leakage error the prompt warned about.** Current stage and escalation timing are called baseline and “stable over time.” They are not. `Escalate` is a post-treatment, post-origin event; using it as a time-zero covariate is immortal-time bias. Escalation timing also cannot be computed: intermediate timestamps were not supplied. The Mermaid diagram is empirically false: it omits `AutoCheck` (the actual cohort-B control-flow change), omits `Rework`, and allows Withdraw/Reject/Transfer only from `Escalate`, contradicting S05, S12, S23 (screen-level reject/withdraw) and several review-level terminals.

**Section 6 only varies transfers.** The task required variation in transfer treatment, withdraw/reject coding, informative censoring, *and* cohort-policy effects. Possible reversals are vague (“importance of transfer outcomes”) rather than concrete (e.g., whether B looks better on resolution CIF, whether P1 looks worse once transfers count as internal failures, whether late-open gaps treated as informative flip tail-risk rankings).

**Section 7 is generic and casually causal.** A 0.3 alert threshold is invented with no link to either estimand, no distinction between already-breached opens and still-in-window cases, and no warning that competing-risk probabilities are not effects of intervening on reject/transfer. Fairness/calibration by cohort is almost missing despite the non-randomized policy change.

**Section 8 fails most of the seven hard requirements and fabricates data.** Only S01–S15 appear; all are forced to cohort A; S16–S32 are dropped. Priorities, durations, and gaps are wrong (S12 duration becomes 0; S11/S12/S13/S14/S15 priorities are permuted; several `last_event_gap_h` values are invented). `prefix`, `escalated`, and `rework_count` are omitted, then used, so the feature code cannot run. Only one estimand is encoded; transfer is misspelled so the map fails; three transfer scenarios are a comment. There is no stratified cohort/priority analysis, no CIF/risk-set construction beyond a single unstratified KM, and **no PM4Py log or variant/prefix feature**, which was mandatory. Lifelines is assumed with no fallback. This is not a partial implementation; it is an incorrect, non-executable sketch that violates “do not invent observations.”

**Section 9 is the only relatively acceptable part**, and even there cutoff/start dates are thin and the small-sample, missing-timestamp, and unknown post-transfer outcome issues are not connected back to which estimands remain identified.

**Net:** headings and jargon without identified estimands, without the required case arithmetic, with a false claim that KM handles competing risks, with exactly the immortal-time leakage the prompt forbade, and with a code block that corrupts the table and ignores half the extract. That is well below a passing process-mining survival analysis.
