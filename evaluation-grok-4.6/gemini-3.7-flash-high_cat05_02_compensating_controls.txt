6.2

The answer is structurally complete and often sophisticated, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has one important case error, several overclaims, invented control capabilities, and checking-code defects that would mis-flag the log.

## What is actually strong

The write-up follows the required headings, does not ask questions, and does not collapse the problem into “count the activities” or “sort by amount.” The best substantive move is refusing a mechanical FEP-1 mapping: AP-204 is form-conforming but objective-failed, AP-209 is form-conforming but indeterminate, AP-201 is a same-user convenience case that is only partly compensated, and AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-202 and AP-208 are clearly failed on independence, overrideability, timing, and recoverability rather than on outcome luck. Section 2’s dimensions (timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance) match the brief, and the argument that “same expected loss” is the wrong sufficiency standard is the strongest part of the paper. Section 4’s hybrid reading of post-release / pre-settlement manager action is reasoned rather than slogan-like.

## Section 1 — real inaccuracies, not just style

The five threat scenarios are reasonable, but the AV-2 description is padded beyond the given control. The source AV-2 checks duplicate invoice, sanctions, bank-account ownership, 7-day bank changes, operator limit, and basic PO match. The answer adds deterministic IBAN syntax/checksum catching and implies a broader data-entry net than the control actually has. That matters because later case judgments treat a “full pass” as stronger error coverage than the specification supports. ED-1 is also described as if it can “shut down typical impersonation vectors” and “eliminate discretion”; it is a conditional permission, and the brief explicitly says it does not make the four-eyes objective disappear.

## Section 2 — internally slightly at odds with itself

The four-way spectrum is usable, but “FULLY EQUIVALENT” is defined as “zero delta in unmitigated loss exposure.” That is an expected-loss / exposure-parity test, which the next subsection then rejects as insufficient. A strict sufficiency test should have led with design effectiveness, independence, and coverage, and treated loss parity as secondary. “Partially equivalent” is also blurred into “tolerable residual risk,” which smuggles acceptance criteria into equivalence.

## Section 3 — the grading core, and where confidence is too high

**AP-206 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` with High confidence is the largest analytical miss.** ED-1 condition 1 is binary: the token must be active *before* `Prepare_Payment`. The token is logged at 13:02; prepare is at 13:00. The brief itself says this may be a logging delay *or* a failed precondition. Acknowledging that fork and then declaring the objective met with high confidence is exactly “a control occurred, therefore it was sufficient.” The defensible labels are `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` or `INDETERMINATE`. Calling the 2-minute breach “core substance of ED-1” rewrites the rule.

**AP-203 / AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` is defensible but oversold.** The facts support a good ED-1 package on AP-203 and a plausible one on AP-207. “Fully compliant” and “complete preventive equivalence to FEP-1” go further than the brief allows. ED-1 is permissioned single-operator release, not restored four-eyes.

**AP-204 is directionally right, evidentially overstated.** A fabricated receipt plus no manager review plus a repeated shared-workstation pair is enough to refuse `OBJECTIVE_MET`. Treating the pair as proven “collusive/single-actor operation” is not. The brief says they deny collusion and that a dedicated two-person cell is possible. High confidence on collusion is not supported; high confidence that the *package failed to stop a defective payment* would have been.

**AP-201 is a fair `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`, but the rationale overreaches.** There is no emergency token, so ED-1 is unavailable. Independent m04 action before the stated 10:00 settlement plus a clean AV-2 pass on a low-value established vendor can make the residual risk tolerable. The text then says this “prevent[ed] unauthorized loss” and assigns High confidence, while the missing-evidence cell admits there is no proof m04 inspected artifacts. MG-4 without review is defined as weak. Residual risk is also called “negligible” and corrective capacity “high” in the same row as a 25-hour audit, which is already outside a strict 24-hour recall clock from release.

**AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` is the correct cautious call.** That is one of the few places confidence is appropriately reduced.

**Other case nits.** Manager m21’s independence is unknown; only m04, m09, m02, m11, and m15 are characterized. The code and implicit narrative treat m21 as independent. AP-208’s failure does not depend on that, but the evidence handling is sloppy. Almost every row is High confidence; that is incompatible with the brief’s missing org design, unknown review quality, and unresolved credential-sharing facts.

## Section 4 — semantics are fine, mechanics are invented

Hybrid gating is a legitimate chosen semantics. The supporting machinery is not in the record: a “guaranteed” 60–90 minute latency window, a tested stop-payment API, and cryptographic non-delegable override credentials. Those are recommendations smuggled in as if they were part of the control design.

## Section 5 — meets the letter, thinner than it looks

Five cases are present and probabilities stay qualitative. Two of the five (AP-206, AP-207) are “SAFE / identical protection” stories, which adds little residual-risk analysis. The more informative omitted cases are AP-201 (convenience same-user, pre-settlement ratify) and AP-209 (clean pair that looks like AP-204). AP-202’s counterfactual (“high certainty of prevention”) assumes an independent releaser would have refused; possible, not established. AP-208 correctly treats legitimacy as luck, not control success.

## Section 6 — complete list, weaker discipline

All requested monitoring families appear, and alert-versus-proof is distinguished. Then unnecessary apparatus appears: a “Gini segregation index,” a 70% pair-volume rule, Kerberos, SWIFT queue logs, packet captures. As a wish list that is harmless; as an evidence standard it blurs the same alert/proof line the section is supposed to defend.

## Section 7 — self-contained, but not a reliable checker

The script does the required mechanical things: in-memory CSV, UTC parse, `format_dataframe`, `filter_four_eyes_principle`, separate blocked-case handling, a joined facts table, and a “not a verdict” banner. Version caveats are actually useful.

It would still mislead a reviewer who trusted the flags.

1. **Settlement timing is incomplete, so manager-before-settlement is wrong for AP-201.** `Bank_Settlement` is absent from AP-201’s log; settlement at 10:00 lives only in the prose table and was never put in `case_facts`. The fallback sets `mgr_pre_settlement` only if the manager is also pre-release. AP-201’s manager is post-release and would be flagged as not pre-settlement, contradicting the case write-up and breaking ED-1 condition 6 in the checker.

2. **`is_same_user` ignores `Attempt_Release`.** AP-205 has no `Release_Payment`, so the flag is false even though u44 prepared and attempted release. The brief asked for attempted-but-blocked cases to be identified carefully; the same-user evidence for that case is dropped.

3. **Required flags are computed more than they are emitted.** Printed columns omit `bank_changed_7d`, emergency-token timing, and `repeated_pair`. Repeated pairing is hardcoded, not derived from the u12/u13 traces. “Emits preliminary evidence flags” is only partly done.

4. **Conforming-set hygiene is unsafe.** `keep_violations=False` is labeled “completed releases,” but cases missing `Release_Payment` are not removed. Depending on PM4Py version, AP-205 can appear conforming. The write-up knows missing activities are a version hazard and then does not guard the output.

5. **24-hour audit is not “one business day.”** RA-7 / ED-1 are one business day; recall is “usually” 24 hours. The code collapses both to `<= 24.0` hours, so AP-201’s next-morning audit fails a rule the policy may not impose.

6. **Facts are partly invented numbers, partly assumed independence.** Tenure-day conversions are harmless. `mgr_independent=True` for m21 is not harmless.

## Net

This is a competent control-objective memo with the right overall shape and several correct hard calls (AP-202, AP-204 form-versus-substance, AP-205, AP-208, AP-209). It is not a 8–10 answer: it upgrades an explicitly ambiguous ED-1 breach to objective met, invents AV-2 powers, overstates collusion and review quality, and ships a checker that would mis-time AP-201 and miss AP-205’s same-user attempt. Those are not cosmetic.
