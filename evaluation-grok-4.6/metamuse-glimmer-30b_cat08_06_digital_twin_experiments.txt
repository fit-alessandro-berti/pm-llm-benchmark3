**5.4**

The write-up is structurally complete and mostly constraint-compliant, but it is not a near-flawless digital-twin design. Several modeling and methodology errors are material, and several required artifacts are only half-built.

**What is actually solid.** All eight required sections exist. Resource calendars are first-class. Non-stationary demand is acknowledged (season, weekday, architect bursts). Missing starts are not silently treated as service times. Objection events are treated as latent, not as observed. The answer does not claim optimality. PM4Py usage is mostly conventional (`format_dataframe`, `convert_to_event_log`, `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`) and is kept out of the engine. D1–D10 plus three stress cases appear.

**Simulation design is still too shallow for the stated problem.** Section 1 lists entities but never specifies the event set, clock, or AND-join state. The process tree’s parallel block (`Planning` with optional Fire and Environmental) and the explicit rule that Decision drafting waits for every applicable result are not designed as synchronization state. Legal/planning/appeal rework is in the narrative but not in the tree; that mismatch is never reconciled. Cross-case dependence is the weakest part of the whole answer: neighborhood objections that “can affect several related cases” are reduced to an i.i.d. public-notice tail, and architect interaction is mostly inspection batching plus a burst arrival factor. That is still closer to independent cases with a few extras than to a twin of interacting applications.

**Parameter estimation and assumptions have real errors.** Calling a time-varying arrival process a “renewal process” is wrong. “Rework is memoryless and independent of prior waiting” is asserted, not justified, and fights the Complex-variance / council-calendar story. Estimation never anchors systematically on the supplied median/p90 tables, never reconstructs starts from calendars and queues, and never checks fitness of the given tree to the log. The 18-month mix, Monday spike, and D3/D5 travel effects are mentioned, then left as slogans.

**Experiment and decision design are thin.** The matrix is a one-row-per-lever list. Factors are single knobs; policies are not crossed with demand/calendar/objection stress except as three separate scenarios. Trade-off cells are truncated (“Completeness rework, time before submission”). SLA “thresholds/ranges” are never given numbers, despite the variant table supplying them. The closing rule (improve median and p90, disparity ≤10%, utilization <85%) is an over-sharp selection heuristic on an uncalibrated model.

**Uncertainty treatment would not survive review.** A 3-month warm-up and +2 months of tail drain are inadequate when Complex-variance p90 is 260 days. Thirty replications plus informal “Bayesian credible intervals from replication means” mixes paradigms and is weak for heavy-tailed p90s. CRN and LHS are named, not specified.

**Validation is a checklist.** “Replay validation” is used for trace-driven historical arrivals, not alignments/replay. The 12/6 split ignores the seasonal arrival design. Cross-case patterns, holiday/council calendars, and start/complete split quality are not validation targets.

**The Python skeleton fails several explicit requirements.** The log excerpt is not actually loaded (one stub row and a comment). There is no routing class or function, despite that being required alongside arrivals, resources/calendars, and experiment execution. There is no state for batching, architect clusters, or review joins. `inter_event_days` is a single proxy column, which is defensible given missing starts, but the engine placeholders are empty shells and do not show how calendars or non-stationary arrivals would be consumed.

Net: a competent outline of a DES study, not a rigorous, process-mining-grounded twin design. Under a strict bar that treats even secondary flaws as costly, this sits in the mid-5s.
