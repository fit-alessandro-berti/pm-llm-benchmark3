**5.6**

The write-up is complete, on-heading, and often sound on authority, scope, and case labels. It is not close to flawless. The required model revision—the criterion the prompt weights most heavily after dialectic—is semantically wrong, and there are several evidence and doctrine slips that a strict judge should not forgive.

**What holds up.** Section 1 separates stipulated facts from authority/scope disputes and states an actual burden (exceptions need in-force, in-scope authority; high-risk deviations are not inferred). Most classifications are defensible: 701 compliant; 703/708 noncompliant at high severity; 704 a true in-window FD-7 exception; 705 noncompliant after automatic expiry; 707/709/710 as model or scope defects; 712 as CS-9 resource noncompliance even though M0 is silent; 714 as post-pilot drift. The prosecution concedes logging/loop gaps; the defense concedes 703 and 708. The overlay-governance and data-collection lists (stable `notification_id`, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution, effective vs record time) are the strongest part of the revision.

**Dialectical fairness is only adequate.** Neither brief is the strongest version of that side. Prosecution underuses the best formal point: PD-3 was never written into CS-9, and the compliance observer later denied approving a weakening of CS-9—so even in-window auto-approve is contestable, not merely “no formal amendment.” It also underuses the aggregates (14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases, 9 missing attributes, 29% notification missingness). Defense overclaims “pending ratification” for 714; N5 is belief that a permanent amendment was imminent, not a recorded ratification process. Calling 713 a “pure” label-equivalence issue ignores that mapping was approved a week *after* the case. System judgment (“partial noncompliance” plus “M0 obsolete”) is closer to splitting the difference than the prompt allows, even though individual case calls are not.

**Evidence-traceability errors.** The matrix is wrong on SUP-702: the case already has a CRM notification at +2 hours with a *stable* `notification_id`. Listing that ID as missing evidence is a factual mistake. SUP-704’s prosecution cell (“noncompliant if post-expiry”) is confused—the case is inside FD-7. SUP-710 as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT is too generous without saying CS-9 still requires quality before *every* activation and is silent on relying on a six-month-old certificate. MIXED is avoided entirely; that is fine for 712/715 if the dominant reason is stated, but 702’s missing onboarding-log notify is never folded into the classification.

**Model semantics — the large failure.** The PM4Py construction does not implement the required behaviors.

- The claim that POWL cannot represent a loop is false. `OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, …)` is the standard encoding. The silent-transition “approximation” is not a loop, is not repeatable, and is not even placed after registration: `rmi_branch` has no incoming edge from `register`, so `Request_More_Info` is unordered with the start of the case.
- The same `Transition` objects (`quality`, `manual_approval`, `activate`) are inserted into multiple subtrees. That is an ill-formed POWL (shared node identity), not a compact encoding.
- Auto-approve still sits *after* global `checks`, so Quality_Review remains a predecessor of the decision. The distinctive PD-3 rule—quality up to two business days *after* activation—is not in the model at all.
- The emergency path inverts FD-7. `emergency_checks → activate` forces quality and manual approval *before* `Activate_Supplier`. Inventing `Provisional_Activate` and then still requiring a second activate after quality does not match the observed traces (`Sanctions_Check`, `Activate_Supplier`, `Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`). The comment in the code contradicts the edges.
- Rejection is only reachable after both checks, which silently takes the model-owner’s side of the SUP-709 scope dispute instead of encoding optional quality on the reject path.

So of the mandated features, standard concurrency and notify-before-close work; auto-approve, emergency order, and repeatable RMI do not. A revision that fails those three is not a passing technical deliverable.

**Net.** Strong outline and mostly right case law; weak strongest-case advocacy; one clear matrix error; a control-flow model that is neither well-formed nor faithful to the overlays it claims to represent. That is mid-pack work, not a high score under a strict rubric.
