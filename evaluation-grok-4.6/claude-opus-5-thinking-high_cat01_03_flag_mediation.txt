**8.0**

The answer is unusually complete, numerically accurate, and methodologically serious. Almost every required heading, column, estimand class, subgroup, pathway, design, policy, and code obligation is present. The stage arithmetic, path-weighted identity, and Kitagawa split check out against the log. That is rare. It is not nearly flawless.

**Logical inconsistencies.** P1 is defined as hiding display (`A_D`) while leaving score, human information, and—in E1—capacity unchanged. Section 6 then attributes to P1 the loss of the 2.24 h triage-wait and 0.35 h service advantages, plus a large cycle-time drop, which only follows if the lane and the mandate switch off with the label. The later hedge (“only if the mandate actually disappears”) does not repair the expected-effect cells. E4 likewise collapses two different objects: an interventional effect through waiting, with occurrence held at the P0 law, and a controlled direct effect of capacity. Those are not equivalent. “Separable effects map one-to-one onto P1–P4” is also wrong: P3 is a system-capacity knob, not a component of the flag.

**Overclaim from n = 20.** A sample gap between 0.61 and 0.64 is treated as a known deterministic threshold; T1/T2 vs T3/T4 exclusivity is treated as a known pool design; T3/T4 are called “demonstrably idle.” All three are sample patterns. They are useful hypotheses, not identified policy facts. The same overreach appears in `complexity → risk_score`, which the prompt does not give.

**Unclear or slightly misleading claims.** “Finish triage 0.60 h versus 0.95 h later” is easy to misread as time-from-arrival. The 1.79 h “enter review later” contrast (7.34 vs 5.55) is conditional on unflagged review and is restated in §9 without that selection. “Removes roughly 60% of mandated reviews” confuses a 60-point occurrence increment with a volume share. High-complexity and reviewed-only contrasts are labeled as if they were matching. The pilot stop rule—“wait reduction below 1 h (mechanism refuted)”—is either backwards (a large drop would support congestion) or badly phrased (reduction *of less than* 1 h). Resource-interval overlap is used well, but overnight calendar gaps are still baked into the headline wait means.

**Smaller defects.** Occurrence → wait is definitional, not causal. `cycle_time → U_review_queue_state` is a clumsy stand-in for occupancy. The performance-DFG block assumes `pdfg[edge]` is a dict with `"mean"` in seconds; that is version-fragile. Register/Close as zero-length milestones is defensible but should have been even more explicit when feeding DFGs.

**What remains strong.** Interventions are decomposed into executable components rather than “set flag = 0.” Natural effects are refused for the right reasons: exposure-induced confounding, interference, time-ordered mediators, and failed positivity. Occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation. Shared queues, reviewer beliefs, and outcome-as-routing-artifact are handled carefully. The identification table states what each design would identify and what would overturn it. The P3 pilot (added vs reserved slots, spillover guardrail, audit) is the right next experiment even if P2 is the better long-run routing change.

The piece is a high-quality research memo with a handful of real conceptual slips, not a near-perfect identification argument. Under a strict bar that is an 8, not a 9.
