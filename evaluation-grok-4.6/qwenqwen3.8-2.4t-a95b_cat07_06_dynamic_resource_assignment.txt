**Material flaws in the queue (section 3) — this is the core deliverable**

1. **C002 is the clearest error.** Urgent Final Approval, 2h SLA, current owner R6 (Approval 3, 5 open, 6.5h, OT low, handover cost *if changed* = low). The policy moves it to R5 (Approval 2, 8 open, 5.0h, OT medium) who is *also* given C016 (high complexity, high handover, 7h SLA). That creates an avoidable handover on a 2-hour SLA. The write-up’s own evidence says owner change on high-complexity work adds a median **2.7h waiting** — enough to miss the SLA. “Take load off R6 for VIP negotiation” is weak: R6 already owns C011, has the most remaining hours, and is the best legal approver. C002 should stay with R6.

2. **That error cascades.** C005 is taken from R5 (Negotiation 3, owner, customer waiting) and given to R1 (skill 2) so R5 can absorb C002+C016. If C002 had stayed with R6, R5 could have kept C016 *and* C005 — both cases she owns, both at skill ≥2. The swap loses specialist match on negotiation *and* approval.

3. **C003 vs the reassignment evidence.** Moving C003 from R4 (Diagnosis 3) to R3 pushes reassignment count from 2 to 3. The prompt states cases with >2 reassignments have **1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal**. The OT reason to leave R4 is real (14 open, 3.0h, OT high), but the write-up still parks the extra case on R3, who must keep C007 and supervise C009. The policy criticizes specialist bottlenecks, then builds one on R3.

4. **Hours are not checked against remaining capacity.** R5: 5.0h for urgent approval + high-complexity diagnosis. R8: 5.5h for urgent Security (3h SLA) + C014 + C015. R1: 5.5h for high negotiation + supervised diagnosis + C013. R3: 6.0h for high-complexity rework (6h SLA) + C003 + supervision. No duration model is applied; “monitor capacity” is not a substitute.

5. **C004/C013 is inconsistent with the R1 story.** C004 is taken off R1 (Eligibility 3, current owner, low handover, routine) to “protect R1 capacity,” then R1 is given three other cases including a new low-priority file (C013). The easy owned case is the one that should have stayed.

6. **C001 vs constraint 5.** High complexity + high handover + owner skill 1, and supervision *is* feasible (R7/R8). Constraint 5 says prefer continuity unless skill is 0/1 *and supervision is not feasible*. Overriding for a 3h SLA and “needs specialist” is arguable (weak owner adds ~3.3h service time), but the write-up treats a listed fairness/resilience constraint as casually soft without stating the exception rule up front. The structured-handover mitigation is asserted, not specified.

7. **Training stretch.** The 20% target is met only if skill-0 observation on C015 counts as a training *assignment*. That is a generous reading. C010’s own note (“potential trainee approval under supervision”) is unused; R4/R8 have Approval skill 0 so they cannot prepare, which should have been said explicitly rather than skipped.
