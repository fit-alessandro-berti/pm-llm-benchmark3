**6.0**

The JSON is well-structured, case-differentiated, and clearly above a DFG-copying baseline. It uses severity, reportability, object relations, queues, and feed delay, and the A-vs-B discussion is the strongest part of the writeup. It is not close to flawless. The required distinction between a currently enabled action and the next *logged* event is applied inconsistently, several ranks 2–3 are later outcomes rather than next observations, and a few normative calls are wrong.

### What is actually good
- **A vs B is not mechanical.** High/injury/multi-device/reportable vs low/no-injury/duplicate is the right split, and the cross-case note states it cleanly.
- **Missing completion ≠ missing work.** C and D treat RA as possibly in progress; D uses the worklist snapshot and migration flag instead of inventing a clean prefix.
- **Uncertainty is mostly honest.** Wide intervals, non-summing ranges, residual mass, and useful unknowns (lot linkage, whether TI/RA already started, reportability call).
- **DFG limitations are stated correctly** as observational, unconditioned, and non-normative.
- **Some `process_enabled` flags are right:** C3 Authority Notification, D3 Evidence Received, E3 third evidence request.

### Material process errors

**1. `process_enabled` is systematically sloppy (core requirement).**  
The task asks to separate a currently enabled action from the next log event. That boolean is true for several actions that are *not* enabled in the primary state:

- **B2 Fast Close / B3 Close — No Safety Issue:** Evidence Review has not completed. Neither termination is enabled now.
- **C2 / D2 Disposition Recorded:** High/medium-with-reportability-signal cases still need RA. Disposition is not enabled while that obligation is open.
- **A2/A3 TI/RA Completed:** Enabled only under the *alternative* concurrent-start story, which is then treated as if it were the main next-event model.
- **E2 Supplier Analysis Completed:** Enabled only if SA was already opened. Completeness 0.72 and a second evidence package make “opened after this review” at least as likely.

C3 and E3 show the author knows the distinction. Repeating the opposite pattern on the other ranks is not a wording slip.

**2. Next logged event vs eventual outcome.**  
Rank 1 is usually the right completion (Evidence Review or RA). Ranks 2–3 often jump to later terminals (Fast Close, Disposition, Authority Notification, TI/RA done) as if they competed with the immediate completion. Intervals of 0.10–0.25 for those as *next* log events are too high once the enabling completion still has to occur and be written.

**3. C6-B Fast Close is over-sold.**  
Fast Close is permitted on attributes, but the case already left S1 via an evidence cycle. The DFG successor after Evidence Review is Close — No Safety Issue / more evidence / investigation, not Fast Close. Calling Fast Close “the most process-appropriate terminal outcome” is the wrong normative call. Close — No Safety Issue should outrank Fast Close after review. R-T4 (reportable-case resource) is also a mild caution against a casual fast path and is underused.

**4. C6-A ranking fights the clocks and queues.**  
At 14:00, evidence arrived 12:10; review is 1–5 h after queue entry with 4 waiting. That makes Evidence Review Completed the dominant next *log* event. TI as next event requires a hidden start at triage: ~5 h elapsed, median ~9 h, 7 waiting, one of three engineers out. P(TI next) = 0.15–0.30 is too competitive with review. A second evidence request (completeness 0.86; DFG Evidence Review → Customer Evidence Requested = 101) is a more immediate fork than TI/RA *completion* and is only mentioned as contradiction, not as a top-3 prediction.

**5. C6-C Authority Notification as next event is not timed.**  
RA queue is 12, 2–14 h after assignment, then disposition, then the 16:00 batch. From 14:00 this is not a plausible *next* log event today. Framing it as rank 3 next activity (even with `process_enabled: false`) inflates a downstream compliance step.

**6. C6-E primary state underplays a mandatory obligation.**  
Six complaints on the same lot trigger Supplier Analysis; disposition must wait. Listing S3 as primary and S4 only as alternative is too weak. SA belongs in the primary obligation set as open/mandatory, with uncertainty only about *whether it has already started*, not *whether it applies*.

**7. C6-D Disposition as enabled/next is inconsistent with the worklist.**  
R-R2 opened RA at 12:40; it is 14:00; RA is 2–14 h. High + injury still requires RA. Disposition is not a coherent rank-2 next event under the primary state. A delayed `Customer Evidence Requested` is as plausible a feed artifact as `Evidence Received`, and is omitted.

### Smaller but real issues
- **A supplier rule is handled more carefully than E, but still soft:** two devices, same lot, plus three similar complaints is enough to keep SA live, not merely a footnote.
- **C2 Disposition `process_enabled: true` plus normal feed:** with a normal feed, an already-finished RA is the weaker story; S5 should not carry a mid-teens next-event probability.
- **No case is disposition-ready for today’s 16:00 batch**, which should have been a single explicit cross-case constraint rather than a one-off in C3.
- Intervals are appropriately wide, but midpoints are not a coherent next-event distribution once ranks 2–3 are later outcomes.

### Rubric read
This is competent process-aware work with a real observation model, not a frequency dump. The failures sit on the exact axes the prompt grades: enabled vs observed, next vs later, and a few hard control-flow calls (B after the evidence cycle, E’s mandatory SA, C/D disposition while RA is open). Under a hypercritical bar those are not polish issues. A nearly flawless answer would have kept ranks 2–3 as *currently plausible next log events* (or marked later terminals with much lower next-event mass and `process_enabled: false`), and would not have called Fast Close the right B outcome.
