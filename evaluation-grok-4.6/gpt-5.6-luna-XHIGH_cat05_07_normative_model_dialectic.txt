**7.0**

The written dialectic (sections 1–5) is unusually strong: facts and authority are separated cleanly, both briefs actually steelman their side, exceptions are not treated as amendments, and most case calls are traceable to a stated rule, window, or evidence defect. The score is held down because the required model revision—the other half of the rubric—contains errors a process-mining reviewer cannot treat as nits.

**What holds up.** Burden of proof is stated correctly and then used: noncompliance needs scope, an effective rule, a reliable violation, and no valid overlay; an exception needs affirmative eligibility, authority, and window; a missing field is not permission. PD-3 is not laundered into CS-9; the observer’s later remark is treated as evidence against an unqualified amendment, not as a veto of the minutes. FD-7 is time-boxed, and a continuing flood is not treated as a continuing instrument. Hidden-by-M0 failures (SUP-712) are distinguished from flow deviations. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are correctly treated as high-confidence safety/eligibility failures that model incompleteness cannot excuse. SUP-706/711 are not converted into invented omissions. The system-level finding—material operational noncompliance *and* an incomplete oracle—does not split the difference; each side is rejected on named cases.

**Classification issues, still mostly defensible.** SUP-705 as NONCOMPLIANT is legally cleaner than the facts are politically. The continuing emergency and low spend are real mitigants; calling that “not a valid extension” is right, but residual severity is overstated relative to SUP-703/708. SUP-715 as MIXED is the one label that brushes the instruction not to use MIXED as a rest stop. The analysis underneath is fine—flow and CS-9 separation are not shown to fail; disclosure is. The primary label should have been COMPLIANT on the normative flow with a separate organizational-control finding, or NONCOMPLIANT if disclosure is in-scope. Medium confidence plus MIXED is a hedge.

**Model semantics (material).** `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), Request_More_Info, SilentTransition()])` is not valid PM4Py. A loop has exactly two children. The comment “LOOP(do=τ, redo=Request_More_Info, exit=τ)” states the wrong arity. The usual encoding of zero-or-more RMI is `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)`. As written the block is not executable, which the prompt required.

**Revision quality (material).** Three design choices undo much of section 6.

1. RMI sits only between `Register_Supplier` and route selection. SUP-707—the case that motivated the loop—has RMI *after* `Sanctions_Check`. The prompt allowed an approximation if the limitation was stated. It was not. The new model still cannot accept the desirable loop it claims to restore.

2. `rejection_route` is a bare `Reject_Supplier`. That contradicts the architecture’s own intake path (`Register → Sanctions pre-screen → Reject`) and cannot replay SUP-709. M0’s opposite defect (quality forced before every reject) is replaced by a worse one (reject with no sanctions at all).

3. Control-flow is overloaded with activities that do not exist in the log: `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`, `Daily_Transaction_Monitoring`, `Retrospective_Approval`, and `Provisional_Activate`. SUP-702 and SUP-704—the best-justified overlays—would fail M1 on vocabulary grounds. Those obligations belong in the overlay/pandas layer the brief itself describes. Encoding them as mandatory transitions makes the “revised normative model” a worse replay artifact than M0 for the cases it was meant to legalize.

Smaller defects: `emergency_tail.nodes[i]` is brittle even if `nodes` stays a list; two unguarded auto XOR-arms over-generate (acknowledged, but still a POWL hole); notify-before-close is correctly sequenced but the emergency/pilot extras were the wrong place to be complete.

**Net.** Sections 1–5 would support a high-8 or low-9. Section 7 is a fail on executability and a fail on fit to the very traces used as evidence. Under a hypercritical reading that equally weights dialectical fairness, semantics, and the proposed revision, that caps the answer at 7.0.
