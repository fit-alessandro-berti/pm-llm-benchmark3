6.2

The write-up is unusually strong on method: it reconstructs eligibility sets, labels assumptions, refuses to treat every FIFO violation as priority, and repeatedly flags observational equivalence. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless. Several numerical claims are wrong, one cross-section conclusion contradicts the author’s own uncertainty analysis, and a fitted index is promoted beyond what seven decisions can identify.

**Factual errors.** The QB scoreboard “FIFO wins 6 of the 7 contested selections” is false. Pure FIFO predicts continue-B01 at 09:16, 09:48, and 10:00; it matches at most four contested picks (09:31, 10:14, 10:28, 10:36), or five only if 09:48 is redefined as “FIFO among fresh jobs” after an unexplained stop. That redefinition is special pleading and is inconsistent with FIFO at the other preemption. Section 7’s line that “three of the four queues let priority-1 jobs wait 45, 51 and 61 minutes” is also wrong: 45 and 61 are both QA (A04, A08); 51 is D10; QC’s C05 waited 43 minutes and is omitted, so the count of queues and the cited durations do not match.

**Internal contradiction.** Exception 2 correctly says that `est_min` = realized duration on all 32 jobs would collapse QC’s SPT reading and drop confidence to ~40. Section 7 nevertheless names QC the most identifiable queue. QA’s FIFO reading does not depend on the estimate field at all and is therefore more identifiable on the author’s own terms. Calling QC “most identifiable” while treating the estimate–actual identity as the most dangerous data-quality problem is a structural inconsistency, not a nuance.

**Overfitting presented as a discipline.** “Maximise age − est” is not in the candidate set. It is a one-parameter index fitted on six or seven decisions, and the advertised 6/7 hit rate includes the forced last job (C07). The only aging miss (C05) is then rescued by a second add-on (“SLA escalation”) inferred from a single annotated case. That is a three-component story (SPT + aging + mode switch) on n≈7, which the identifiability paragraph admits and the primary label ignores. “Genuine policy switch rather than a background rule” is stronger than one observation can bear: C05 is simultaneously p1, earliest deadline, and already infeasible, so priority, EDD, tardiness-shelving, and discretion are still equivalent there.

**Confidence and thin discrimination.** QA at 74 is too high for a rule that is separated from the rival index at exactly one time (08:32). The author says so in Test 4 and then leaves the number unchanged. The 08:58 “four-way tie” is correctly called uninformative in the table and then grammatically folded into the claim that “decisions where priority or SPT would differ from FIFO” are the FIFO wins — they do not differ at 08:58.

**Weaker use of supplied evidence.** D10’s note (“D-F still unavailable until 12:20”) is a recorded claim of unavailability. Treating 11:36–12:00 as a pure undocumented work-conservation violation underweights that note; it may still be an exception relative to the resource calendar, but it is not silence in the log. Assumption A3 upgrades a scan-tolerance bound and three 2-minute coincidences into a center-wide “dispatch latency of about 2 minutes.” A4 is introduced as an assumption and immediately called a fact. Appointment predictions are operationalized inconsistently (exact slot in QA; “due in 2 min” as APPT at QB 10:28).

**What remains solid.** Decision tables are the right unit of analysis. Priority is properly refuted in QA (A04, A08) and QD (D04 over D08, D07 over D10), not inferred from 08:58 or 11:35. QC 10:18 is correctly identified as the cleanest single discriminator against priority and EDD. The C07 counterfactual (both deadlines feasible if C07 precedes C01) is calculated correctly. Counterfactual tests name a real extra observable. The collection plan is tied to these ambiguities rather than copied from a template. None of that offsets the scoreboard errors or the QC/QA identifiability contradiction under the required standard.
