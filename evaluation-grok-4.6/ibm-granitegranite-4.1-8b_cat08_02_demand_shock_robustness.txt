**4.3**

The answer looks like a complete operations redesign, but it repeatedly reverses causal direction, invents mechanisms the log does not support, and then builds a “robust” package that would fail the very scenarios it defines. Format compliance is not the same as process reasoning.

**Scenarios are present, but thin.** The four required stress families appear, which is the one clean hit. S1 is only “peak mix, a bit worse.” S4 restates the already-observed peak courier queue (7.5 h) as if it were a new design case. S3 stacks a week-long medical-validator outage with a invented “Friday-night blackout,” then treats the peak-table specialist queue (36 h) as the computed absence effect. S2’s “20% throughput loss (runtime 15%)” is garbled. None of the scenarios is quantified from the DFG rates (recollection 4.2% → 8.0%, batch-wait incidence 4.4% → 12.7%, repeat-assay ~7% → ~15%, TV→MV share ~25% → ~42%, specialist incidence roughly quadrupling).

**Bottleneck-migration analysis is the core failure.** It mostly pastes peak utilization/queue cells into scenario tables and then misattributes them.

- Accession’s 14 h peak queue is blamed on “Urgent cases often need rapid accession tweaks.” The log never says that. Peak stress at pre-analytics is the recollection loop (780/9730 plus 110 from correction; recollection→receive 60 h), not urgent accession edits.
- Batch wait is called “previously idle.” It already exists in the normal DFG (760 cases; 9 h median on the wait→assay edge).
- S1 is analyzed as if I1 and I3 were already running (“Urgent-first dispatch pushes Urgent into M1… Complex-panel still go to M2”). That is intervention contamination, not as-is migration.
- Faster medical validation is said to raise repeat assays via “more TV→MV handoffs.” Repeats originate at assay run (and cluster after night shifts / M2). Speeding a downstream validator does not create wet-lab failures.
- I5 is said to overflow specialist review. The DFG is TV → Hold for specialist → MV. Specialist entry is a TV disposition, not a function of MV speed. The flow is backwards.
- Losing specialist capacity is written as utilization “87% → 43%.” 43% is the normal-period utilization. Halving a pool raises remaining utilization; it does not restore the off-peak figure.
- I4 is used to “absorb some Specialist work.” I4 trains technical validators for accession correction, not specialist review.
- S2 claims M2 degradation “releases” batch wait. If M2 stops consuming work, batch wait lengthens.
- Downstream-overload stories are often attached to the wrong station. The real conflict hints (I8 exposing pre-analytics/assays; I4 starving TV already at 101%; I3 dumping Routine onto high-failure M2) are underused or inverted.

**The robust package is not robust.** I3 is the clearest error. Peak assay utilization is 98%; there is no “10% M1 buffer.” Complex-panel orders need multiple assays, so putting Urgent *and* Complex on M1 is simultaneous priority of two heavy classes against Routine — forbidden by the problem and physically tight at 98% utilization. M2 already has the higher failure rate and is implicated in Repeat-after-failure clustering; I3 assigns *all* Routine to that machine, which is exactly the hinted failure mode. In S2 (M2 degradation) I3 is not a hedge, it is the worst routing. Calling I3 the fix for S2 is a direct contradiction.

I4 is the second structural error. Peak technical validation is already 101%. Moving two validators onto accession *removes* the scarcest peak resource. The write-up claims I4 “eases Technical validation load” in S3 and also lists TV as the next bottleneck. Those cannot both be true.

I5 is extra capacity, not reallocation, and is double-counted as always-on and as P3. I8 is a weak robust choice: registration is 68/85% with 0.7/2.8 h queues, while pre-analytics (96%, 9.5 h), benches (89%, 11 h), assays (98%, 18 h), and TV (101%, 16 h) are the real constraints. The given conflict hint is that I8 exposes those stations; the answer instead warns mainly about accession.

High-leverage options grounded in the log are missing. I7 (desynchronize 10:00/17:00 intake spikes) is the cleanest low-regret response to S4 and to pre-analytical saturation; it is absent. I10 is the obvious valve for Complex-panel × specialist-review (2.4× entry rate; 48 h peak specialist→MV edge; every-second-Friday gap) and is absent. Recollection’s 42/60 h loop — one of the largest performance edges — is never designed as a first-class object.

**Scenario policies include an inverted control and an unsafe one.** P2, on M2 degradation, “switch all Routine orders to M2.” That loads the failing machine. P3’s “release Routine with a pending-validation flag” is a patient-safety/regulatory break, not a lab control. P1/P5 restate the existing Urgent batch-bypass variant (already 74%/58%) without protecting Complex-panel from Urgent-first starvation (the I1 hint). Several triggers would be latched on for the entire peak (batch-wait >8 h vs 22 h peak edge; MV >20 h vs 22 h peak median).

**Trade-off table has the right columns and the wrong signs.** I3’s Routine risk is written as “M1 overfilled with Routine,” but I3 routes Routine *off* M1 onto unreliable M2. I8’s Complex effect is “Improves (Complex still need manual correction)” — that is not an improvement. I4 Urgent impact is “neutral” even though TV is the Urgent path’s already-saturated gate. Coverage scores do not match the footnote (High = 3 scenarios, then High is applied to two). Implementation priority leads with I3, the most brittle item in the set.

**Residual vulnerabilities are the strongest section, and still oversell.** Specialist Friday absence, M1 as a new single point of failure, I4 quality loss, and I8 metadata dependence are real. The redesign-caused TV collapse (I4 at 101% TV) and Routine hostage-to-M2 (I3+P2) are underplayed. “Mitigated by part-time specialist reviewers” is undeclared extra capacity.

**The dashboard would not steer a peak day.** Several “leading” bands are in **minutes** against **hour-scale** edges (batch wait yellow >10 min vs 6/14 h; assay latency yellow >10 min vs 9/22 h). Other bands are already breached in the *normal* or *peak* summaries (rework yellow >5% vs normal 7.5%; accession yellow >3 h vs peak 14 h; specialist red >6 h vs peak 36 h), so they are permanently red and useless. Urgent SLA-breach rate is a lagging outcome, listed as leading. Mix share (Urgent/Complex), M2 repeat/failure rate, Urgent age-to-18 h, courier-slot adherence, and known specialist calendar — the actual leading signals in this log — are missing.

**What is salvageable:** required section skeleton; refusal to run I2/I9 as standing policy; some awareness that one station’s relief can starve another; a residual-risk list that includes self-inflicted items; not optimizing only the 8-week average. That is a 4-range artifact: competent outline, unreliable machinery. It would not survive contact with S2, a Friday specialist gap, or the 98/101% assay/TV peak.
