## Primary segmentation is operationally wrong

This is the central process-mining failure.

The data have at least four distinct mechanisms: (i) weekday vs weekend volume, (ii) the 15 Apr outage and **16–17 Apr catch-up**, (iii) a **20 Apr case-mix / peak-shape break** (complex ~20–27% → ~40–47%, `peak_08_10_pct` ~26–34% → ~40–50%, completions persistently below arrivals), (iv) **29 Apr staffing** with completions again exceeding arrivals.

The proposed primary cut **merges 16–24 Apr as “Recovery & Post-Intervention.”** That label is false on both halves: recovery is essentially 16–17 Apr (129 and 112 completions), and the intervention is 29 Apr. Days 20–24 are the *start of backlog accumulation*, not recovery. Averaging them produces the bogus completion band “~99–106/day” and is exactly the error the quality bar forbids: treating outage recovery (and then the opposite of recovery) as one high-throughput-ish regime.

Further defects:

- **Apr 27–28 fall in a hole** (after the “recovery” end date, before the intervention start).
- All weekends are one regime, even though post-20 Apr weekends have much longer tails and higher WIP than 11–12 Apr.
- Staffing “360 staff-minutes/day” at 6 FTE is dimensionally wrong (that is ~per hour; daily offered capacity is 6 × 12 × 60 = 4320 minutes). The 80–90% utilization claim is therefore unsupported and, using median service × arrivals / open-hour capacity, looks more like ~55–75% on early weekdays.
- The mix-shift is demoted to an “alternative,” despite being the dominant non-stationarity after the outage.

Net flow, which the aggregates *do* support, is never used: roughly −47 on the outage day, ~+30 on 16–17 Apr, then on the order of **−100 from 20–28 Apr**, then partial unwind after 29 Apr. That accounting is the cleanest evidence the table gives, and it is absent.

Section 4 is promised in comments and `print`s that never become a report. Weekday/weekend composition, mix, and outage/intervention are named, but **no actual contrast table** is shown, and **Simpson-style reversal is asserted, not demonstrated**.

Dispersion statistics are computed on the **wrong mixture**:

- Daily Fano on all 28 days is mostly weekday/weekend level-shift, not within-regime overdispersion.
- Hourly CV concatenates four heterogeneous exemplar days.
- The change-point heuristic tests an arrivals mean shift at **29 Apr**, after arrivals had already stepped up with the mix change. That is a weak, mis-targeted detector presented with more authority than it deserves.

Little’s Law: the matrix’s cautious verdicts (`not defensible` / `approximate only`) are more reasonable than the rest of the paper — and then the code **computes L/λ anyway**, using median-of-daily-mean-WIP over mean arrivals, converting with ×12 open hours, and a weekend WIP comment of “~19” that does not match early weekends (~12–14). Boundary conditions (open-hours snapshots vs overnight freeze, arrivals vs completions as throughput, start-of-day backlog unknown) are mentioned in slogans, not worked through. Computing a 2.4-hour implied flow time while the same window is labeled “not defensible” is internally inconsistent.

The PM4Py comment names the requested functions and correctly says these rows are not an event log, then immediately sketches converting `daily_df` as if it had `case_id` / activity / timestamp. That is cargo-cult compliance.
