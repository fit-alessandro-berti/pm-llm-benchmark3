**7.4**

This is a high-competence answer that does the hard things well: it catches the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, keeps queueing distinct from service, treats the group label as a routing bundle, and proposes an intervention that learns instead of remixing the caseload. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several technical and rhetorical slips are material.

**Most serious error: the “DAG” is not a DAG.** Section 2 puts arrows in both directions between `complexity` and `unobserved_case_risk`. That is a cycle. Complexity is a coarsened measurement of risk, so the only defensible edge is `unobserved_case_risk → complexity`. Drawing the reverse edge, then discussing backdoor adjustment on that graph, is a real causal-graph mistake, not a wording nit. `shift → arrival_load` is also poorly motivated (time of day is a common cause, not a case-level shift label causing load). `resource_group → unobserved_reviewer_skill` muddles a stock of staff with the skill a case is exposed to. `case_completed → [selection…]` is a note, not an edge.

**Required distinction is only half-made.** The prompt required total, direct, and mediated readings of an Orion effect. Mediators are handled well in the tables, and Phase 1 names a “direct effect,” but there is no clean decomposition of Orion → cycle time into total vs. paths through wait, compliance, service, and rework. A committee reader could still confuse “adjust for complexity” with “shut the mediated routing path.”

**Several quantitative claims are slightly false.**
- “80–90% of *every* within-stratum contrast is waiting” is wrong for Low: wait is 1.61 / 2.14 ≈ **75%**. High is ~96%. The slogan overstates uniformity.
- “Near-constant offset across strata” is overstated (−2.14, −2.21, −3.43).
- H6’s claim that `wait + service ≈ cycle minus a small constant` is false. Residuals run from ~0.8 h on simple traces to ~3.7 h on Request-Info traces; that is leftover process time, not a constant.
- Orion Medium required vs not is a 3-vs-3 anecdote; calling compliance “essentially no penalty” is too strong given the bundled wait field.
- The “suspiciously compressed tail” (33.5 vs 36.0) cannot bear the weight H4 puts on it with two Nova High cases.

**Positivity and hypothesis testing are conflated.** H1’s predicted observation that “the matched sample retains <30% of cases” is a design fact already known from the overlap table, not a prediction that would confirm case-mix confounding. The matched *contrast* is the test; the attrition rate is a positivity diagnostic.

**Smaller unclarities.** The executive summary’s “92.9% night/high-load vs Nova’s 12.5%/25.0%/25.0%” invites the reader to treat Nova’s third number as high load (it is 50% night-or-load? no: night is 25%, high load is 50%). Confidence 70 sits awkwardly next to “overlap is fatal” and “not identifiable” for the opposite estimand. The final rec then says Orion “handles each stratum at least as fast as Nova,” which is the claim they just called unidentified. “Clinical/legal escalation” is the wrong domain. The day-vs-night row never reports day and night means, only collinearity. Rework leaving `review_service_h` unchanged is called “implausible”; an early stop plus a short second touch is a live process explanation.

**What still earns a high-7 rather than a mid-6:** arithmetic on the main contrasts and the pooled standardization is auditable and correct; selection, proxy, collider, and bundle roles are named; the six hypotheses cover the required types and most tests have a predicted pattern; Phases 0–2 are the right operational answer; the Python is diagnostic, warns on thin cells, states the weighting distribution, and does not invent timestamps.

It is a strong committee memo with one broken graph and a pattern of over-precise mechanistic claims. That is well short of near-flawless.
