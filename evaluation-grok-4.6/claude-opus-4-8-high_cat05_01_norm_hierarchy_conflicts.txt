**5.4**

The answer is well-structured and conceptually ambitious, but it does not earn a high score under strict grading. Sections 1–3 state a disciplined method; Section 4 repeatedly fails to apply that method. Several classifications are therefore not entailed by the author’s own premises, which is a core failure on a task that is scored for consistency, process awareness, and evidential discipline.

**Method is stronger than application.** The issue-specific hierarchy (integrity floor in GPIP-4; ECD-2 limited to timing/onboarding; CFO email limited to Finance mechanics; silence ≠ waiver; A6 non-cure; burden on the party claiming a waiver) is the right architecture, and the A7 assumption is properly flagged as decisive. That is then abandoned at the point of decision. The quality constraint “do not treat post-hoc approval as automatically curative” is quoted in Section 1 and violated in Section 4.

**P-502 is the clearest internal contradiction.** Under the author’s rules, this is a new-supplier purchase, so A3 requires Compliance_Clearance *before* commitment. Clearance is at 18:30; commitment is at 10:15. The row itself says A3 is “not curable (A6)” and that late clearance “mitigates, not cures.” The controlling interpretation nevertheless calls A3 “satisfied albeit late,” and the primary label is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. That is not a justified exception. It is an uncovered GPIP-4 breach. The secondary label “logging defect” is also wrong: a control performed hours after commitment is not a late record of a timely act. D1 is also inapplicable on the better reading of the facts (P-502 sits on 14 July, during ON-44 recovery; the CFO email is sent 15 July 07:20). Neither the date of D nor the A2 Finance approval at 13:05 (post-commit, €62k) is treated as a hard GPIP-4 defect. On the author’s own hierarchy this case should be NONCOMPLIANT, with at most a secondary logging note on the authorization *record* (which in any event was within four hours).

**P-507 and P-508 are classified inconsistently with each other and with P-502.** P-507 has pre-commit Compliance, in-window IC authorization, and no Finance event at €110k, which is inside D1’s €150k band; primary label NONCOMPLIANT. P-508 has the same missing Finance event at €155k, *above* D1’s band, plus Legal and SoD; primary label JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION because of a “strong integrity posture.” That is outcome-smoothing, not rule application. If A2 is a non-waivable floor, both are NONCOMPLIANT (P-508 more clearly, because D1 does not even purport to cover amounts above €150k unless D2’s “as well” is read as extending the Finance substitute—an argument the author never makes). If D1 validly restructures Finance, P-507 is the *better* candidate for JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, not P-508. Ranking P-502 (late Compliance) above P-507 (timely Compliance, missing Finance) inverts the author’s own integrity hierarchy.

**P-501 misstates B4.** A four-hour predicted stoppage meets the SOP emergency definition. The Plant Manager’s reachability and non-delegation defeat only the *Shift Lead substitution* for Plant_Approve, not the emergency itself. Calling this “no valid emergency” collapses two independent clauses. Commit-before-PO can be allowed under B4 while Plant/Finance still fail as pre-commitment controls under B2/B5. The NONCOMPLIANT label is still defensible on the Plant/Finance timing, so the error is in the reason, not necessarily the result—but the same error is then encoded in the pseudo-logic (`emergency_local(P) unless plant_manager_reachable...`), which means the catalogue does not match the SOP.

**Evidential discipline is uneven.** The principle that contemporaneous artifacts outrank ERP stamps is correct, and P-507’s signed PDF at 12:40 versus ERP 14:30 is handled well. ON-44 is then used as a general clock solvent. ON-44 is a 14 July 08:30–13:00 ERP outage with recovery lag of up to 12 minutes. It is not authority for a 18 July “nine minutes fast” clock (P-505), nor for a 17 July document-system queue (P-507). P-505 is rightly INDETERMINATE on the window edge, but the decisive-evidence cell should rest on the supplier call/ack and the *case-specific* fast-clock allegation, not ON-44. A3 is also wrongly treated as a relevant, satisfied control on P-505 (€88k, approved supplier: A3 is not triggered).

**Other process misses.** D1 is not applied as of its timestamp: it is available for P-503 (€140k ≤ €150k) as a Finance-leg argument and is ignored, while SoD/A3 still independently sink that case. Section 4 lists A2 as a P-506 deviation; Section 6 correctly notes Finance_Approve was *before* commit. Those two sections should not disagree about a timestamp. C3’s four-hour recording rule is computed correctly for P-502 only after an in-cell arithmetic wobble (“>4h? actually 2h45”). “Critical” scope is used well for P-503 and P-504 and then replaced, for P-508, by an impressionistic integrity score.

**What remains genuinely good.** The refusal of a single total order; the A7 non-equivalence assumption with reversal notes in Section 5; P-503/P-504/P-506 as NONCOMPLIANT on authority, scope, and expiry; the fact/interpretation/assumption split in Section 1; and Section 7’s replacement clause (CRO ratification, 24-hour objective criticality, non-waivable SoD/Compliance, no verbal extensions, anti-normalization review). Those pieces are why this is not a low score. They do not rescue a case table whose primary labels do not follow from the rules the author declared.
