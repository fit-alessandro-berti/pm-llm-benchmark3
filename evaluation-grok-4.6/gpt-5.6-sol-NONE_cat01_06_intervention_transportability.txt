**7.3**

The answer is a strong, well-structured transportability analysis that hits the required headings, tables, and most of the evaluation targets: it refuses to treat Site A’s 9-hour median as an identified effect, keeps the AND-join, refuses illegal Document Check starts, segments by originals/legal, and designs a mechanism-vs-implementation pilot. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading the flaws are numerous enough to block any high-8/9 score.

**What is actually good.** Section 1 is complete and operationally useful. The graph has the required nodes and a coherent mediation story (`treatment → true_overlap → waiting → cycle_time`) plus `S_` selection nodes. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, mix shift, huddles, timestamp inflation, no negative control) are not waved away. S4 supplies the required “mean better, tail worse” case. The modified intervention is the right object to transfer: lock-safe concurrent *enabled* work, dormant document readiness when originals are required, pool WIP, legal last. The recommendation `conditional go` matches the evidence.

**Logical and quantitative cracks.** The 23% first-wave share is `0.38 × 0.60` under an independence assumption the answer itself later flags; high-value/public cases are plausibly *more* likely to need originals, so the planning volume is an upper bound presented as a point estimate. S1’s “100% task-released / 4–7 h median” is not internally consistent with “only ~38% immediately dual-executable” unless the 62% originals group also contributes a large median shift, which S3 itself bounds at 1–3 h. Site B’s DFG is underused: `Identity Check → Originals Receipt` (1,020) already implies identity often finishes *during* the 18 h receipt wait, so the incremental value of “run identity early” is smaller than S3’s 1–3 h band. Waiting-before-assessment (20 h) is never decomposed by originals vs digital, so the 4–6 h Wave-1 target is not pinned to a subgroup baseline.

**Pilot design does not add up.** At ~130 completions/week, 16 weeks is ~2,080 cases. Wave 1 at ~23% is ~480 cases. That cannot support “≥250 completed cases/arm in each main stratum” once originals × legal × complexity is taken seriously, and it certainly cannot support T0/T1/T2 plus optional T3. Case-level randomization in shared 4/7/3/2 pools also violates SUTVA more severely than the interference paragraph admits. Appeals as a contemporaneous stop rule (+1 pp) is misaligned with a 90-day endpoint.

**Spec and model fidelity.** Section 6’s partial order never returns from repeated Assessment to Legal Review, so the supplement loop is not closed; Section 8 closes it differently (`LOOP` of a silent do-part plus `Legal Review [repeat]`). Those are not the same model. Splitting Approve/Reject/Document Check into labeled variants improves readability and weakens replay/conformance identity with the real log. `LOOP(tau, supplement_body)` is a workable encoding, not the natural “legal review, then decide-or-redo” loop. The code hedges on `check_soundness` / `play_out` signatures; `pm4py.check_is_workflow_net` as a top-level call is not clearly the public API. Resource, lock, and duration limitations are commented, which is required and correct.

**Smaller deductions.** `cycle_time → appeal` is undertheorized. Queue-huddle and API effects are sometimes mediators, sometimes co-treatments, with no single consistent placement. WIP caps (16/28/12/8) are invented without utilization math. “38% digital/no-original” equates “originals not legally required” with immediately complete digital evidence. Timestamp-reliability and quality rows in Section 1 are slightly padded. None of these are fatal; together they keep the piece clearly below “nearly flawless.”

The core analogical argument is right. The execution is not tight enough, especially on DFG-implied existing concurrency, sample-size arithmetic, and loop specification, to score higher.
