## Systematic numerical failure

The case table is the core artifact and it is not a table of the supplied event log.

- **Every branch queue is written as 0.** The data do not support that. B and C routinely start after `fork_release` (B queues of 5–30 min; C queues of 5–35 min, including 35 on F04 and 25 on F08). Zeroing queues erases the sojourn (`queue + service`) distinction the prompt explicitly required.
- **`join_ready` is often not the latest branch end.** Several rows use A’s completion (or another non-max end) instead of `max(A_end, B_end, C_end)`. Examples: F04 join is 09:52 not 09:45; F05 is 10:10 not 10:00; F07 is 10:55 not 10:30; F08 is 10:50 not 10:30; F09 is 11:15 not 11:00.

- **`final_service` is always 20 minutes** in the log. The table reports 45–52. That is not a rounding issue.
- **Totals are inconsistent with `final_end − arrival`.** Correct totals include F01 = 93, F02 = 115, F08 = 110, F12 = 125, not 87 / 100 / 115 / 105. F01 B service is 40 not 30; F06 C service is 20 not 30; F08 upstream is 20 not 10; F10 upstream is 20 not 15.

## Definitions: partial credit, then two conceptual errors

The identity

`total = upstream + (join_ready − fork_release) + downstream + final_service`

is correct. Two surrounding claims are not.

1. **Double-counting.** Summing the three `sync_wait_i` does **not** count `fork_release → join_ready` three times. Each `queue_i + service_i + sync_wait_i` *equals* elapsed fork–join, so summing those nine terms counts elapsed fork–join **three** times. Summing only sync waits adds *overlapping slack* (calendar-concurrent idle of finished branches). The write-up states the wrong identity and the wrong reason.
2. **Stage boundaries.** Downstream queueing is after `join_ready`. It is not a partition of fork–join time, and fork–join is not “competition for F1/F2.”

Those errors are not wording nits; they are the thing Section 1 was asked to get right.

## Aggregate ranking is the right headline on false evidence

B *is* the most influential branch, but not for the reasons given. Mean A service is longest (50) and A is almost never last once sojourn is used; B’s mean sojourn is about 57 vs A 50 vs C 31; B is last in 8/12 cases. The answer asserts B/C queues are 0, A sync is “negligible,” and B sync is 0. In the log, mean sync wait is about A 11, B 4, C 30. Treating the longest-service branch as non-influential is the one instruction they roughly follow, but they do it by inventing slack and queue statistics.

## Counterfactuals do not evaluate the interventions as specified

1. **A −20%.** Helps only when A is on the join path (F01 and F11, not “only F01”). On F01, A sojourn 50 vs B 45: a 10-minute A cut yields at most 5 minutes of join movement before B becomes critical. The 2-minute “saving” is arithmetic debris on a wrong baseline (87). The write-up never cleanly tests the required masking question: if `final_start` is resource-constrained, join moving earlier can **inflate** `downstream_queue` and leave `final_end` unchanged.
2. **B queue −50%.** This is the most damaging inferential error. B queue is **not** zero. Halving it would cut join_ready in multiple B-critical cases (on the order of 5–15 minutes before any F-station masking). Declaring the lever null is the opposite of the evidence.
3. **Final capacity.** Downstream waits of ~8–20 minutes exist; some align with F1/F2 occupancy (F03→F05, F06→F08, F07→F09→F11), some do not (F01 waits 8 minutes with F1 idle). The answer uses invented waits (F07 = 40), halves them by fiat, and quotes a 30–35 minute aggregate that is not a model of two servers.
