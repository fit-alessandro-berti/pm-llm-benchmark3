**5.5**

The final write-up is directionally right—Site A’s before/after result is confounded, a blind copy is not identified or legal, and a segmented “conditional go” is the correct class of recommendation—but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of process structure, quantitative transport, model fidelity, and pilot feasibility, there are several material errors, not just polish problems.

**What is solid.** Section 3 correctly refuses to treat the 9-hour median drop as a treatment effect and separates mix shift, FTE, API latency, huddles, and timestamp repair. The feature table hits the required axes. There is a genuine tail-worsening bottleneck-shift scenario. The pilot text on scheduled vs actual overlap is a real mechanism-vs-implementation test. The recommendation’s first stratum (digital, preferably non-legal) matches the binding constraints.

**Process-structure / analogical misses.** Site B already allows `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after `Register`; the DFG confirms both. For the 62% originals cases, identity (3.4h) typically finishes inside the 18h originals wait, so identity is often already off the critical path. The incremental Site A analogy is **not** “make Identity ∥ Document after Register for every case.” Document Check is legally unstartable until originals arrive. Calling Site B “implicit/sequential (IT lock)” collapses three different things—statutory gating, IT lock, and an already-partially-parallel identity/originals pair. Creating a `Pending` Document Check on originals cases still collides with the lock and invites false open-interval overlap. The transport DAG also misfires: `appeal -> cycle_time` is the wrong temporal direction; originals delay is not a direct waiting mechanism; legal supplement loops are under-graphed.

**Quantitative problems.** Optimistic −7 to −9h reuses almost the whole confounded Site A movement even after the text denies that identification. Parallelization can hide at most the overlapping verification work (identity ~3.4h, plus some handoff wait). Saving 7–9h from concurrency alone is not supported. “Eligible share 38%” vs median change is ambiguous (overall vs treated). The pilot is infeasible: ~3,400 cases / 6 months is ~1,130 cases in 8 weeks total, not 1,200 **per arm**. Shared pools of 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers make “queue isolation” wishful. Power text is garbled (“80% power at =3h”).

**Model fidelity is a fail.** Required PM4Py/POWL construction invents `PowlObject`, `PowlRelation`, `PowlModel` and a petri `convert_to_petri_net` import that are not the library’s POWL API (`Transition`, `StrictPartialOrder`, `OperatorPOWL`, loop/XOR). Worse, every `Assessment → Legal Review / Approve / Reject` edge is a strict order, so choice is encoded as mandatory successors; the supplement “loop” is a single edge, not a loop operator. That does not preserve Site B dependencies and would not be a sound claims workflow. Section 6’s textual POWL repeats the same XOR-as-order error.

**Format / consistency nicks.** Section 9 is under the 250-word floor. Cross-training is a failure-mode mitigation in §4 and “unnecessary” in assumption 6. Selection nodes are thin relative to the requested transport graph.

Right headline, incomplete causal/process anatomy, non-runnable and semantically wrong model, and an impossible pilot. That is a mid score, not a high one.
