**6.7**

The audit is substantively strong: it covers every required item, keeps the legitimate `t0` signals (`queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`), treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legitimate but future while treating `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as an impossible/same-timestamp artifact, and generally separates prediction, causation, actionability, and fairness. That is the core of a good answer. It is not close to flawless.

**Section 1.** Coverage and most severity calls are right (`total_case_duration_hours` as outcome-side leak, warehouse `final_handler_team`, nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, SQL `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, mutable `risk_score_current`, complete-case `last_resource_role`). Two problems recur. First, cutoff language is broken throughout (`timestamps \` t0\``, `as_of  t0`): the comparison operators are missing, so the remediations do not state whether the Coverage Check event itself is included. Second, severity is inconsistent across similar complete-trace patterns (`Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is critical; `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` and `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` are only high). The fraud edge is also over-generalized as always post-`t0`; if investigation opened at or before `t0`, a prefix version could be admissible.

**Section 2.** The suite hits prefix availability, order feasibility, same-timestamp ambiguity, complete-trace detection, enablement vs future-possible vs impossible, and repeated settlement after appeal. The DFG caveat is correct. Alignment, footprint, and declarative checks are barely named, and “`Damage Assessment Completed` is enabled at `t0`” confuses a completed event with an enabled activity. The same missing `≤ t0` operators make the prefix-projection rule unimplementable as written.

**Section 3.** The five-class split is the right taxonomy, and the may/may-not-claim lines are directionally correct. `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` belongs as much to process-state/slack as to “defensible predictive.” `initial_reserve` is treated as typically known at registration with no lineage support. Fairness guidance stays generic (no concrete checks for region/channel as repair-network vs socioeconomic proxies).

**Section 4.** Structure matches the spec; narratives do not replay leaked outcomes; X/Y are correctly `recompute`; Z is correctly an uncertainty note. The main conceptual error is treating leaked-model SHAP directions as still credible. Once duration, appeal, variant, final team, and final decision sit in the model, attributions for *remaining* features are entangled; admissible inputs are not the same as trustworthy local contributions. `initial_reserve` is promoted to a Case Y driver without an as-of guarantee. Putting `calendar_week=52` in Z’s “main_ambiguities” mixes a known-but-confounded calendar proxy with true lineage failures (missing sequence index, untimestamped score, last role).

**Section 5.** The plan includes every required workstream and is usable. It understates that the *global ranking itself* is invalid, not only the leaked rows, and it is thin on what explanation-stability and fairness tests would actually compute.

Net: a credible process-aware audit that would pass a review with revisions, not a near-flawless one. The missing cutoffs, leftover SHAP-from-a-leaked-model, reserve-without-lineage, and severity/enablement sloppiness are enough to keep it well below the 8–10 band under a strict reading.
