**2.6**

The write-up has the required eight headings and a mostly sensible narrative reading of the case, but the two models—the actual deliverable—are incorrect in ways that invert the process, and the witnesses contradict both the narrative and each other.

**What is acceptable.** Section 1 does resolve the required points: risk as XOR after root-cause analysis, supplier as an optional XOR, inspection outcome as a silent control-flow exit rather than a business label, a stated (if muddled) approximation policy, and “may overlap” as true concurrency. Section 2 lists many of the right precedence facts (containment before plan, analysis before design/consult/risk, trial after plan and design, release after a passing inspection plus approvals plus supplier-if-chosen). Preferring POWL for cross-branch edges is the right instinct.

**The process tree is not a model of this process.** The compact expression nests `Plan Verification` under `Analyze Root Cause`, places the trial `Loop` as a sibling of containment/analysis (so a trial can start immediately after registration), and puts consult, skip, and the risk route in a `Parallel` rather than a choice. That is the opposite of the stated dependencies. A process tree *cannot* express this non-block-structured split exactly—that is the point of the task—and the answer never says so. It instead claims “Exact” for split dependencies, approval/trial overlap, and release synchronization. The honest options (force extra sequence, allow extra interleavings, or duplicate subtrees for risk × supplier) are named in Section 1 and then not used.

The Python is not self-contained, not assigned to `tree`, and not valid PM4Py. Leaves are built as `ProcessTree(Operator.SEQUENCE, ..., name=...)`; the real constructor uses `label`, and activities are leaves with `operator=None`. `Activity` is imported from a module that does not export it. `trial_sync.children = [trial_loop]` overwrites the plan/design children just inserted. Several contradictory drafts are left in the answer; none is a single coherent tree.

**The POWL model inverts choice and cannot represent the loop.** `OperatorPOWL()` is not constructible that way; XOR/LOOP nodes are `OperatorPOWL(operator=..., children=...)`, and partial order lives on `StrictPartialOrder(nodes=...)`. The code treats `OperatorPOWL` as a mutable graph with `add_node`. Worse, edges from analysis go to *both* Consult and Skip, and to Supervisor *and* Quality *and* Regulatory; edges from Inspect go to *both* Adjust and Exit. Alternatives become AND. Release therefore waits for every review *and* consult *and* skip. The back-edge `Adjust → Execute` puts a cycle in a strict partial order; the loop must be an `OperatorPOWL(LOOP, [do, redo])`. `SilentTransition` is required by the spec and unused. The “why unconnected nodes may be concurrent” note covers three pairs and ignores the illegally connected ones.

**Witnesses fail the spec and their own table.** Allowed traces omit `Supervisor Review` (so there is no valid low-risk witness) and treat `Pass`/`Fail` as visible events, which the task forbids. T1 and T7 are essentially the same sequence with opposite verdicts. T6 is justified as “Plan before Contain” while the sequence is Register–Contain–Plan. T8 is justified as “Skip after Release” while Skip precedes Release. No row isolates a process-tree approximation (a trace the tree wrongly rejects or wrongly accepts). “Risk (Quality+Regulatory)” is not a visible activity label.

**Comparison and validation inherit the same errors.** Section 6 marks the hard requirements Exact and the business consequence “None.” Duplicate-label risk is listed N/A even though the tree introduces `Skip Consult` and `Exit Trial Loop`. The validation plan recites Petri-net conversion and soundness without noting that a cyclic “partial order” will not convert to a meaningful sound net, and the silent-behavior check assumes `SilentTransition`s the code never creates.

**Self-critique is too small for the damage.** Dummy skip labels and a static loop are real issues. The unstated problems are larger: XOR encoded as AND, a cycle in `StrictPartialOrder`, a tree that lets the trial race registration, invented constructors, and an internally inconsistent witness table. Residual weakness is not “the API does not enforce XOR”; the API provides `OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, ...)` and it was not used.

Format compliance and a usable dependency sketch keep this above a minimum, but under a strict reading the artifacts would neither run nor approximate the intended language. That is far from a high score.
