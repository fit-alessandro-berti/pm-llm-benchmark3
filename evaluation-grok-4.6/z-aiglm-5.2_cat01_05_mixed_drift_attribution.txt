**5.4**

The high-level story is mostly right: March is semantic/provenance drift, May is supplier-driven rework, June–August is capacity and queueing (with priority as a confounder), October–November is seasonal case mix, and December is an unidentified mix of load decline and intervention. Format compliance is also largely intact. That is not enough. Under a strict reading the answer has requirement misses, unreproducible numbers, and several causal mistakes.

**Wrong or unjustified quantities.** The stated DFG Manhattan distances (4 / 33 / 44 / 58) do not match \(L_1\) on the supplied rates. Using the six non-constant edges in the later code, the baseline distances are 3, 40, 59, and 64; using all seven edges does not recover the published figures either. The prose and the code contradict each other, so the “transparent” distance is not reproducible. The cycle-time split (mix 3–4h, rework 4–5h, service 0.6–1h, queue 7–8h) is not tied to a month and invents a complexity premium the aggregates do not contain. That violates the instruction not to manufacture precision.

**Failed checklist items.** Logging drift is given only one change point (March); two were required. Part A never validates the embedded tables. Seasonality is only implied by a few reference windows; multiple testing is not discussed at all. Part B does not compute *monthly* DFGs, only a coarse pre/post slice. `pm4py.format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` is not a real PM4Py signature. Open-case dedup by `(case_id, timestamp)` is the wrong rule for extra technical events. The change-point rule compares every month to January, so once a threshold is crossed it keeps firing; that is not a change-point detector.

**Causal and PM errors.** The July priority rule is treated as raising the high-complexity *share*; a queue rule cannot do that. New `Eligibility Screen` variants are listed as behavioral drift; they are the migration label. The known historical inconsistency of `Senior Assessment` is barely used, so the October 4 → 22 jump is over-attributed to population/behavior. “Mapped fitness ~0.90 *proves* the business sequence was intact” overclaims: mapped fitness itself falls from 0.92 to 0.86 after May, which is residual real non-conformance. `fast_variant_pct` (58 → 35) is ignored. Supplier Z vs other is a weak *primary monitoring population* because Z is a non-random region/complexity slice.

**What remains solid.** Sections 2 and 9 are the strongest: the raw/mapped gap is treated as provenance without waving away later DFG and waiting-time drift; interactions and revision conditions are stated. The hypothesis, discrimination, and monitor tables cover the required mechanisms and artifacts. Decision rules 1–3 and 6 are operationally usable. Those parts keep the score out of the failure range. They do not offset wrong distances, missing required analyses, and several mechanism mix-ups. This is a competent draft, not a nearly flawless one.
