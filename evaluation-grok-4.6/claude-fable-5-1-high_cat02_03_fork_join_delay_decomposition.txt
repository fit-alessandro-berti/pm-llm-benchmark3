## Defects (why not higher)

**Incorrect mechanism story for CF2.** The table values are right, but the prose says F02, F03, F05, and F09 savings are “truncated by A’s 50-min floor (A becomes critical or tied).” After a 50% B-queue cut, F03 still has \(E_B=55\) and F09 has \(E_B=52.5\); both remain B-critical and are limited by the 50% cut, not by A. Only F02 (tie at 50) and F05 (\(E_B=47.5\)) actually hit the floor among that list. That is a real qualitative error in the counterfactual write-up.

**CF3 / Redesign 1 over-attributes savings to pooling.** The 75-minute CF3a figure is mostly replacing observed 8–20 min lags with \(\max(JR+8,\) server free\()\), compared to **observed** ends rather than a like-for-like dedicated-assignment baseline. Incremental pooling vs odd/even is much smaller (third server adds only 5 min; both-busy time is 25/176). The body often says “pooling and dispatch plus handover,” but the redesign headline still sells ~6 min/case as a pooling win. Section 7 later corrects this; Section 5 does not fully.

**Small numerical/API nits.** B queue–service Pearson correlation is ≈0.54, not ≈0.57. The required column `upstream_elapsed` is labeled `upstream`. Tie-break text “A < B < C” is slightly ambiguous versus the code (A wins). `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(..., perf_aggregation_key="mean")` is a likely unexpected-kwarg against the usual 2.7 public signature (default is already mean). The F07 “280 vs 130” illustration adds \(\sum E+\sum W\) rather than only \(\sum W\), which muddies an otherwise clear double-counting argument.

**Residual judgment calls, not errors.** The 8-minute lag is labeled as an assumption. p90 on \(n=12\) is noisy; §7 says so. Near-ties of 5–7 min are reported honestly.
