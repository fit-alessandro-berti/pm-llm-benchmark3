**5.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton and several sound process-mining instincts, but it is not reliable enough to score higher under a strict reading. Internal consistency and fidelity to the supplied table fail in ways that are not cosmetic.

**What is actually strong.** The eight required headings are present. The estimands are defined, median service is not treated as mean flow time, and outage/recovery is correctly refused as a steady high-throughput regime. The Little’s Law matrix is the best part: completions as a throughput proxy, carry-over risk, and `approximate only` / `not defensible` verdicts are the right posture. The alternative, state-based pressure segmentation is a genuine alternative, not a relabeling. Section 8 at least attempts the mandated ingredients (in-memory tables, CV/Fano, lag-1 WIP correlation, a transparent shift heuristic, windowed L/λ, and a clear note that these rows are not a PM4Py event log).

**The numbers do not match the table.** There are 28 days, not 30. Pooled arrivals are about 93.5/day, not 88.2; completions about 86.0, not a 96.1% completion ratio (actual completions/arrivals ≈ 92%); mean WIP ≈ 29.9, not 31.2; mean `complex_pct` ≈ 32.9%, not 30.5%. Those pooled figures look like they were computed on an invented 30-day universe. That error then infects the “Arr/Staff” comparison (16.2 is not what this table produces).

Regime estimates are also loose or wrong. Apr 15–16 arrivals average 108.5, not 105.5. Post-intervention WIP is 43, 36, 32 — the claim that WIP “drops to 32–36” erases 29 Apr. Weekend arrivals average ≈44.9, not 43.6. Baseline “weekdays” Apr 6–14 includes a weekend if taken literally, and the quoted 99.6 / 98.4 / 19.6 set does not correspond to any clean subset. Apr 17 (112 completions, WIP 35, 6.5 FTE) is an obvious continuation of recovery and is left in a hole between regimes. Utilization “ρ ~ 0.85” is not derived from a stated formula; offered load using median service is closer to ~0.55–0.60 on baseline weekdays, so the 0.85 figure is either unexplained or a silent misuse of the median the authors elsewhere refuse to misuse.

**Section 8 does not reproduce Section 2.** That is a hard consistency failure. `loc[0:6]` is 6–12 Apr (first week, including Saturday–Sunday), not “baseline weekdays 6–14 Apr.” `loc[8:9]` is 14–15 Apr, so the outage slice includes a normal Tuesday and **drops 16 Apr**, the actual recovery day (129 completions, WIP 52, 7.0 FTE). High-mix `loc[14:21]` is 20–27 Apr and folds in a weekend; post-intervention `loc[22:25]` starts on 28 Apr, a day before the stated intervention. A reader who runs the script will not get the paper’s regime means. For a reproducibility section, that is disqualifying of a high score.

**Burstiness arithmetic is wrong.** 21 Apr hourly arrivals sum to 115, mean 9.58, not 9.1. Population CV is ~0.98 and Fano ~9.3 (sample Fano ~10), not CV 1.12 / Fano 11.4. 7 Apr Fano is ~3.0, not 4.4. The qualitative point (ordinary day vs overdispersed bursty day) survives; the advertised indicators do not.

**Several required subtleties are thin or missed.** The “Simpson” example is ordinary mixture masking (strain 88% vs clearance 105%), not a reversal of association. The coding-change hypothesis for `complex_pct` is named once and then treated as a real mix shift. The representative hourly vectors are incompatible with `peak_08_10_pct` (21 Apr’s 08–10 bins are not ~47% of arrivals; the spike sits later), and that data-quality tension is never flagged. Overnight freeze versus opening-hour sojourn is mentioned, then under-developed in the Little’s Law boundary discussion. Confidence labels have no rule.

The operational story — do not staff or SLA-plan off a pooled ~96% completion rate — is directionally right and would have been a high-scoring paper if the windows, code, and arithmetic had been internally consistent with the 28-day table. They are not. Under a hypercritical bar, that caps the grade at mediocre.
