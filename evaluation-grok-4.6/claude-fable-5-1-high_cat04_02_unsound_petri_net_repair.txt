**7.4**

The Petri-net diagnosis is strong; the write-up is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading of the spec, format misses, one non-executable API call, and a real slip about the original net’s language keep this well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually right.** The net is a WF-net and is unsound for the reason given: `t_join_waived` is a 1-input sequence step, not an AND-join, so the token `t_split` put on the analysis branch is never taken on the waiver route. Counterexamples A and B are legal, complete, and do the required work: they show tokens in both branches, follow waiver, show `t_authorize` both before and after `t_analyze`, and distinguish “`sink` is marked” from “`fm` is reached.” The leftover-token argument is the right one (`t_join_normal` still needs `p_review_done`, which the XOR at `p_review` will never produce). The hand enumeration of 14 markings / 17 edges (original) and 10 / 12 (R1), including the observation that `{p_analysis, sink}` is *not* a dead-end, checks out. R1/R2/R3 plus R1b/R1c are genuinely different; the edit-cost model is explicit; the cost-1 uniqueness argument under the stated intent is essentially correct; R1 code matches the chosen edit.

**Required-format failures.** Section 1 is over the 150-word cap (about 160–185). Section 8 is at or slightly over 250. Those are hard constraints, not style nits.

**Section 6 is not executable as written.** `pm4py.check_soundness(...)` returns a `bool`. `is_sound, diag = pm4py.check_soundness(...)` raises on unpack. The required calls are present, and the enabledness helper is done properly with `semantics.is_enabled` / `semantics.execute`, but a block that is supposed to run on both nets dies in `diagnose()`. Predicting that WOFLAN “should report” uncovered S-components, even with a version caveat, also goes closer to fabricated diagnostics than the prompt allowed.

**Section 7 contradicts Section 3.** The completing language of the original net contains **no** waiver trace: every waiver run ends in `{sink, p_analysis}` or `{sink, p_analysis_done}`, never `fm`. Saying the original “accepts” `Register Case, Record Review Waiver, Authorize Case` and that such traces “were fitting” is wrong on the criterion the answer itself used earlier. Token replay already leaves a residual token; alignments to `fm` already fail. The repaired net does not create a fitness *drop* on those traces so much as turn a non-completing run into a rejected one. That is a real conceptual error, not a wording quibble.

**Smaller gaps that still count.** (1) The diagnostic script never fires a *successful* repaired waiver-first complete sequence (`t_waive_review` then `t_analyze` then join), so it does not show that R1 keeps concurrency. (2) R1 makes the net non-free-choice (`t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived` share `p_analysis_done` with unequal presets); R2 stays free-choice. That structural trade-off is omitted. (3) “Not covered by any S-component” is true on the induced-subnet definition but is asserted, not derived. (4) “A sound WF-net must have exactly one dead-end” is necessary, not sufficient (livelocks); they do not treat it as sufficient, but they lean on it as if it closed the case.

The marking work and the repair table are well above average. The spec asked for a runnable diagnostic block, strict headings/limits, and honesty about what soundness does and does not mean. Those are exactly where this answer is sloppy.
