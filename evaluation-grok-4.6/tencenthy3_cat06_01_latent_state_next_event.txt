**6.1**

The final JSON is process-aware and structurally compliant, but it is not close to flawless. It correctly avoids naive DFG copying, keeps A and B from collapsing, uses rules 2/4/5/7, and (especially on C6-D) distinguishes a live enabled task from a late-arriving log event. Those are the right instincts. The score is held down by concrete control-flow errors, inconsistent use of `process_enabled`, and several poorly calibrated rank-2/3 forecasts.

**What is solid**
- C6-A vs C6-B is the required contrast: same prefix, different obligations (high severity / injury / lot vs low-severity likely duplicate). Cross-case text states this cleanly.
- Primary states are mostly right: A/B/E in S3, C/D in S4.
- Intervals are wide on purpose; midpoints are only loosely ranked; the calibration note does not fake precision.
- C6-D is the strongest case: migration flag, source-system attachment, and the R-R2 worklist are used as distinct evidence channels.
- C6-E correctly treats same-lot clustering as a Supplier Analysis obligation and does not Fast Close.

**Material flaws**

1. **C6-A misuses the 16:00 batch.** Contradicting evidence says review delay may push completion “beyond today’s 16:00 batch.” That batch is for Authority Notification, not Evidence Review. This is a straight mix-up of operating conditions.

2. **`process_enabled` is applied inconsistently.** On A/C/D/E, rank-2/3 events that skip the current marking are correctly marked `false`. On C6-B, both `Close — No Safety Issue` and `Fast Close` are `true` while the primary state is still S3. Close is not enabled until review completes. Fast Close is a triage-era outcome (S1 / DFG `Initial Triage → Fast Close`); after an evidence cycle it is not a cleanly enabled next event. The alternative state labeled “S1/early closure latent” is also the wrong abstraction.

3. **C6-D over-claims the evidence cycle.** An attachment in the source system is not the Requested → Received → Review cycle. Putting “Latent Customer Evidence Requested/Received likely occurred” in `completed_obligations` treats an inference as a completion. Rank 1 `Evidence Received` at 0.30–0.60 also over-weights an 8–15% delay base rate relative to an RA that is already opened on a worklist.

4. **Several rank-3 (and some rank-2) items are “event after next.”**  
   - A: `Technical Investigation Completed` is not enabled in S3 and is temporally implausible before review.  
   - C: `Authority Notification` needs RA completion *and* disposition (and then the 16:00 batch). Probability mass on that as the *next* log line is not justified. `Regulatory Assessment Completed` at 0.45–0.75 is also optimistic given 12 waiting and “2–14 h after assignment,” with TI only done at 11:45.  
   - E: `Supplier Analysis Completed` as next event, upper 0.25, ignores 6–36 h typical time and a review that has not finished. Six same-lot complaints are also a reportability signal (rule 4) that is only hedged as “possibly” TI/RA.

5. **Smaller unclarities.** C6-C barely uses “root cause plausible but not confirmed.” C6-B ignores that R-T4 is the reportable-case resource on a duplicate. Completed vs open obligations sometimes mix observed events with suspected ones. Rank-1/2 intervals on D almost coincide, so the ranking is weaker than the JSON implies.

The answer would sit in the high 7s if Fast Close/S1, the 16:00 error, and the D/E calibration problems were cleaned up. As written, those are not nits.
