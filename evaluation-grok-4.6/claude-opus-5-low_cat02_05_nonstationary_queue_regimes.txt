7.3

The answer is a strong, well-structured process-mining analysis that hits the required format and the quality bar’s main tests (regime separation by demand/mix/capacity/backlog, recovery not treated as sustainable capacity, Little’s Law withheld where the aggregates cannot support it). It is not close to flawless. Several numerical claims are false, one central inventory story is inverted, and a few inferences are tighter than the evidence.

**Factual errors.** R4 weekday WIP is **not** monotonically rising (37, 41, 46, 44, 43, 49, 50). Cumulative net and WIP do **not** track “almost 1:1” in the chronic-accumulation block: weekday net is about +94 while WIP only moves from 37 to 50. That is the opposite of a 1:1 stock identity, and it is a first-order clue that `arrivals − completed` and `mean_wip` are not closing on the same inventory. The answer never flags this conservation gap, yet later treats L and ΔB as interchangeable evidence about the same queue. R4 arrivals are also not a clean rise from 108 to 121 (111 and 106 interrupt it).

**Broken deduction.** Median-based ρ̂ ≈ 0.93 plus “E[S] > median” does **not** imply true ρ ≥ 1. That only follows if E[S]/median ≳ 1.075. The p90/p50 ratio makes that plausible, and the daily ΔB > 0 is the actual instability evidence; writing “ρ̂ ≈ 0.93 on medians → true ρ ≥ 1” presents a lower bound as a crossing of 1.

**Evidence handling.** The 21 Apr hourly vector is *representative*, and its 08:00–09:59 share is incompatible with that day’s `peak_08_10_pct = 47` if taken literally. Using both as the same observation to say the 08–10 metric “understates” burstiness is a category error. The qualitative multi-spike point can stand; the joint reading cannot. Primary segmentation also fails to partition the sample: 18–19 Apr is in no primary regime. R1-weekend Little’s Law in the code mixes pre-outage and post-recovery weekends. Clearance of “~18 weekdays” treats the unknown 06 Apr opening stock as zero after Section 1 correctly said it is unknown; 06 Apr L = 18 already implies a non-empty system, so +110 is a return to baseline, not emptiness. The 80–85% waiting share uses S̃₅₀ as if it were E[S], undoing the median/mean discipline applied elsewhere.

**Smaller defects.** R2’s “service behavior” cell is backlog arithmetic, not service. The Apr 15 Fano sentence starts a reversal claim and retracts it mid-parenthetical. “121–126 completions/day” in Section 2 drops 116. Weekend service is not meaningfully higher in R4 (35.5 vs 35.0). Weekday-only ACF/change-point scans glue Friday to Monday. Daily within-regime arrival Fano is never computed; those series are actually under-dispersed, which would have sharpened the multi-scale burstiness story. Staffing advice (hold 6.7–7.0 FTE) and backlog advice (drain ~6/weekday for 18 days) are not the same policy.

**What remains good.** Estimands, units, and the closed-overnight convention are defined carefully. The threat list is substantive. Recovery is correctly read as a backlog-fed drain, not extra capacity. Alternative A (starved vs saturated) is a real second cut, not a relabeling. The pooled-vs-segmented examples (day-type composition, between- vs within-regime mix slope, outage+recovery netting to “basically fine”) are the right kind of quantitative comparison. The Little’s Law matrix is the strongest section: operating vs wall-clock time, λ vs X as proxy, censored W in R4, pooled 4.0 h called meaningless. The “30% headroom, revert to 6.0 FTE” policy is an excellent segmented counterexample. The Python block meets the spec and does not pretend the daily table is an event log.

The piece reads like a high-end answer with several places where the narrative outran the table. Under a strict reading those are not nits.
