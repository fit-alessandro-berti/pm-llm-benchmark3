4.2

The write-up is well structured and looks like a decision-set analysis, but it repeatedly misstates who was eligible, misapplies the candidate rules, and then diagnoses policies that the same tables already refute. Under a strict reading of this task, that is a core failure, not a cosmetic one.

**QA is misdiagnosed.** Appointment-plus-FIFO fill-in is defensible; appointment-plus-non-preemptive-priority is not. At 08:32 the eligible set is A02 (P3, 20) and A04 (P1, 7); A02 starts. At 09:05 A08 (P1) is already waiting and A05 starts. At 09:32 A07 starts over A08. Those three episodes eliminate priority as even a secondary rule. The table never reconstructs 08:32 or 09:05, which are the most informative QA decisions. The cited “supporting” A06 story also misuses the two-minute *arrival-scan* tolerance as an appointment-lateness window. Worse, §4 claims A03 was “held or timed for 08:20 despite resource availability.” A1 is busy on A01 until 08:20. That idle-wait claim is false.

**QB is overcalled as textbook preemptive priority.** B02 preempting B01 is real. B05 at 10:00 is not a cut-in: B03 runs to completion. After B02, the resource *resumes* B01 at 09:31 while B03 (P2) waits, and resumes B01 again at 10:14 while B06 (P2) waits. Those are direct contradictions of re-evaluation by priority and are not in the decision table. The 10:28 row is factually wrong on two counts: B01 has *completed*, it is not paused; and a priority rule predicts B06 (P2), not B04 (P3). Writing B04 in the priority column is a rule-definition error. Confidence 85 is inconsistent with dispatcher-reassignment notes, resume-before-higher-priority, and an unhonored appointment.

**QC is not SPT, and the table contradicts the label.** Dynamic SEPT at 10:32 has C06 (est 6) waiting and C04 (22) is chosen. At 11:40 C07 (18) is waiting and C01 (36) is chosen; the table nonetheless lists C01 as the SPT prediction. That is filling in the observation, not the rule. C05 is not eligible at 10:10 (arrives 10:11); priority among the true backlog is C03, not C05. C07 is missing from the 10:54 eligible set. C02-first does support SPT *at that instant*, but C02 also has a loose 14:00 deadline, so the later claim that short jobs are the ones with endangered SLAs is not true of the opening episode. A frozen initial SPT order plus a later P1/deadline exception is a better hypothesis than “SPT with SLA safeguard,” and it is not considered.

**QD eligibility sets are not usable.** Skill constraints and unique-resource jobs (D05 on D-F only; D07 on D-X only) are the right *feasibility* story, but they are not a sequencing rule. D-M’s 11:25 choice of D04 over D08 (P1, shorter) is FIFO within mechanical skill and refutes priority/SPT; it is omitted. At 11:00 the table treats D03–D06 as waiting before they have arrived. At 11:30 it includes D03/D04 (already in service) and drops D05, D07, D09, D10. At 12:20 it lists D07 and D09 as waiting after they have finished, and D10 is already on D-X. §3 then explains D10’s wait by calling D-F “the only fully qualified firmware+electrical resource.” D10 is electrical only. That is D05’s constraint, not D10’s. D-X waiting idle 11:20–11:30 for D06 is the cleanest appointment evidence in the log and is treated only as a contradiction, not as a secondary rule.

**§4 does not discriminate.** The QC 10:10 choice already separates SPT (C02) from EDD/slack (C03); the interesting unresolved tests are 10:32 and 11:40. The QA 10:20/08:20 test is built on a false idle-resource premise; 08:32 already separates SPT from appointment/FIFO. **§5** lists real caveats but ranks scan delay as if it could repair multi-job order reversals it cannot. **§6** mostly restates the prompt’s field list. **§7** then calls QB “unmistakable” and QA “least identifiable” in part because the author imported a priority component the data reject. The warning that a pure-SPT reading of QC would be operationally dangerous is the strongest sentence in the memo — and it undercuts the author’s own primary label.

Format is compliant and the prose is confident. Confidence is not a substitute for correct decision sets. The required work product was reconstructed choice sets versus rule predictions; those sets are incomplete or wrong in every queue.
