**7.0**

The memo chooses the right strategic direction (selective hybrid, not national scale and not blanket rollback), uses the process-mining artifacts, and follows the required six-section structure. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several classification errors, overclaims, and operational ambiguities are material.

**What holds up.** Alternative E is the only option that respects both constraints at once: do not scale R while tails, rework, and billing-before-activation are worse, and do not discard the simple-house gain. Sections 3–5 correctly treat the result as heterogeneous rather than a single win/loss. Fitness vs precision, median vs tail, handover vs resilience, visible vs downstream cost, and house vs apartment/old-building splits are all explained with the actual numbers (0.94/0.51, p90 31.0→38.5, p95 45.0→62.0, FTR 82%→76%, complaints 6.1%→9.8%, DFG shifts, alignments, resource networks). The 6-week window is correctly read as likely *understating* tails. Guardrails (activation before billing, permit checks, do not cut survey capacity) are the right immediate controls.

**Fact / interpretation / assumption discipline is broken in places the prompt forbade.** The evidence table is the required artifact, and it does not stay clean.

- Row 9 parks *facts* in the assumption cell: pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings, and building-risk attributes were incomplete before week 3. Those are data-coverage facts, not assumptions. “Comparison is confounded” is the assumption; the mix difference is not.
- Row 1 invents seasonality, which is not in the evidence.
- The executive memo then states interpretations as settled fact (“improved the common path but broke the exceptional path”) and offloads the argument to “Details in Sections 4–6,” so the memo is not a self-contained decision document.
- Later, the same incomplete-attribute problem is ignored in the recommendation language: the simple-house path is called a “proven bundle” / “demonstrated winner” even though roughly the first third of a 6-week, non-randomized, mix-shifted pilot lacks segment/risk fields. That is the exact fusion of fact and interpretation the rubric bans.

**Operational recommendation is internally ambiguous.** “Scale coordinator ownership only for simple houses” is not the same sentence as “baseline path with coordinator support” for everyone else. C, E, and the rural finding are smeared together. Rural’s documented harm was *harder rescheduling after fewer handovers*, so extending coordinator ownership there can reproduce the failure mode the memo claims to contain. Business vs apartment vs old-building controls are also bundled as if they were one intervention. Immediate action (“keep the pilot running but constrain it”) is not fully reconciled with “pause skip and post-billing activation entirely.”

**Thresholds and VoI are sloppy.**

- Rollout gates are written as point values (“p90 31d and p95 45d… complaint 6.5%”) without inequalities, without saying whether they are overall or segment-weighted, and with unexplained buffers (6.5% vs 6.1%; 98% permit compliance, which was never a baseline metric).
- Coordinator utilization <80% is imported from a queuing-theory aside in §3, not from the log.
- M7 is primarily a *control*, not a measurement that resolves an uncertainty; ranking it as VoI confuses information value with operational enforcement.
- M4 is ranked first and called “cheap to collect retrospectively.” Full 60-day cost on late-pilot cases is not retrospective yet; that overstates both speed and cheapness.
- Cost-to-learn is named as a ranking criterion, then mostly unused: cheap items (M3, M7, M8) sit below an expensive RCT (M1) without a clear incremental-value argument.

**Smaller defects that still count.** The memo’s claimed “~310” words is decorative clutter. SLA improvement for simple houses is in the prompt and dropped. “Legal/billing-dispute exposure” is projected beyond the business-customer note. Start-timestamp sparsity is listed, then the Failed→Second delay is treated as if wait vs service were already identified. Alternative D is described as leaving “current pilot harm running if not paused,” which is a design choice for D, not a property of D.

The piece is a competent executive synthesis with the correct headline decision. It is not a clean separation of evidence from story, and the rollout mechanics are not specified tightly enough to act on without further interpretation. That is why it is well below a high score.
