7.6

This is a strong, process-aware analysis that mostly respects authority, timing, scope, and evidence. It is not nearly flawless. Several classification calls rest on an under-specified burden-of-proof rule, Section 5 underplays the one alternative that most matters, and a few case rows are cleaner than the record they themselves describe.

**What is actually solid.** The issue-specific hierarchy is the right architecture: ECD-2 can move local PO order and register status without becoming an A7 waiver of A1–A3; the CFO email is prospective Finance interpretation, not a GPIP override; ON-44 is evidential only and is not generalized past 14 July. Thresholds are applied correctly (`≥ 50,000` vs `> 100,000`; 24k under B2; 51k into A2; 155k into D2). “New” and “unapproved” are kept distinct. A6 is used as mitigation, not cure. Commitment is taken from the first binding undertaking, not the ERP stamp. Those choices are the core of the task and are handled well.

**1. Method is articulated, then applied unevenly.** Section 1 states that the party relying on an exception must substantiate it, *and* that a missing event is not proof the act never occurred. Both can be true, but the write-up never says which residual category follows when the only candidate approval is joint Incident Command authorization and Incident Command is not shown to be budget owner or Controller. In practice that residual is always `INDETERMINATE` (P-505, P-507, P-508). That is conservative, but it means D1 never does real work: it is neither accepted as a Finance-route substitution nor rejected as an unauthorized waiver. The same instrument is then used more aggressively in P-503, where joint authorization *is* treated as final business approval in order to fire A1. The “independently established breach” gloss is explicit, yet it still lets one overlay condemn one case and immunize two others. That is a logical flaw, not a mere judgment call.

Related unclarity: Plant_Approve is provisionally mapped to budget-owner approval, and that mapping is load-bearing for P-506, but the same mapping is only “prima facie” for A1 in P-501. If the mapping is firm enough to find a pre-commitment A2 breach on 19 July, unknown Plant/Finance identities on 14 July are also material to A1.

**2. Hierarchy table is good; one silent strengthening.** Preserving B4 against later GPIP-4 is correct: GPIP-4 does not require PO-before-commit. Treating B6 as something ECD-2 “probably cannot override” is a choice, not a source holding. B7’s undefined “ordinary local workflow steps” could include the emergency form. The table flags “ordinary” as undefined, then Section 3’s pseudo-logic `normally preserve({A1,A2,A3,B6})` hardens the choice. That is a small illicit promotion from uncertainty to rule.

**3. Rule catalogue is usable but incomplete at the points that decide cases.** R6’s refusal to import A2’s pre-commitment timing into B2 is textually justified and is one of the better moves in the paper. The pseudo-logic is less complete than the prose: `allow(post_commit_PO)` is written only for B4, while ECD C1 is left to `overrides_for`; there is no defeasible rule for “joint IC authorization counts as final business approval when it is the only business-approval event.” That omitted rule is exactly what separates P-503 from P-507/P-508. Twelve lines were available; the case-decisive A1/IC interaction should have been in them.

R15’s “preventive” pre-commitment reading of D2 is disclosed, which is acceptable. R14 is hedged until it is almost non-operational.

**4. Case assessments — specific problems.**

- **P-501 (`JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`, severity “none established”).** B4’s four-hour stoppage and pre-ECD timing are right; SHIFT-1 as committer is correctly not equated with Plant substitution. Two problems remain. First, Plant/Finance identities are unknown, so A1 is unproven, not “prima facie” satisfied. On the author’s own standard that a material unresolved issue yields `INDETERMINATE`, this row is over-classified. Second, “severity: none established” is the weakest cell in the table. Even on the author’s reading there was commitment before PO and before Plant/Finance; that is control exposure. Medium confidence does not repair a severity field that reads like an acquittal.

- **P-502 (`NONCOMPLIANT`).** This is the clearest correct call: new supplier, A3 after a corroborated 10:15 commitment, D not yet sent, A6 blocking the 18:30 clearance. High confidence on A3 is earned. The row is still slightly over-broad on A2: Finance at 13:05 is late, but Incident Command capacities are “unproven,” and ON-44 still covers that window. The A3 finding does not need A2, and A2 should have been left as secondary.

- **P-503 (`NONCOMPLIANT`).** Actor overlap is a fact; treating both co-authorizers as final business approvers is an interpretation, and confidence is appropriately discounted. Giving the backup pumps “provisional ECD scope” while “requiring engineering substantiation” that is not in the file is a fudge. Three days’ stock plus C6’s defined dispute is a scope defeater, not a tie to award to Operations. The NONCOMPLIANT result probably survives on A1/A3 even out of scope, so the error is in the controlling interpretation, not necessarily the label.

- **P-504 (`NONCOMPLIANT`).** Sound if SOP “maintenance” includes cafeteria. B3 plus no B4 safety predicate is enough. The row is one of the cleaner ones.

- **P-505 (`INDETERMINATE`).** Timestamp hygiene is excellent (ack 17:59:31 over ERP 18:06; ON-44 not used for 18 July). Once contemporaneous evidence is preferred, ECD expiry is no longer the main problem; A2 is. Finance_Approve at 19:00 is an affirmative later approval, i.e. the same structure as P-502’s late Compliance. The only distinction is a D1 candidate at 17:50. That distinction is real, but then the classification is entirely a D1-capacity question and should have been framed that way, not as lingering doubt about expiry.

- **P-506 (`NONCOMPLIANT`).** Expiry, no written extension, Plant after commit at ≥ 50k: this is consistent with A2 and A6. Good use of “verbal extension is not an instrument.”

- **P-507 (`INDETERMINATE`).** Recording vs ingestion is correctly split (PDF 12:40 vs ERP 14:30; deadline 12:55). A3 before commit is satisfied. A1 is not assessable on the named facts and is only gestured at as “independence.” That should have been an explicit unverified A1, parallel to P-508’s affirmative A1 pass. Unapproved supplier is allowed only if ECD scope holds; eight-hour need is outside B4’s six-hour test but inside C. That last point is right and should have been mirrored more skeptically onto P-503’s three-day stock.

- **P-508 (`INDETERMINATE`).** Best-evidenced high-value file (A1 separation, Compliance, Legal, in-window IC auth). `INDETERMINATE` is defensible because Legal ≠ Finance and D2’s “as well” is ambiguous. A stricter application of the author’s own exception burden would have been `NONCOMPLIANT` on A2: 155k is above D1’s 150k cap, there is no Finance event, and D3/D4 plus the charter gap are affirmative limits, not silence. Listing that as the primary, with `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` as the alternative, would have been tighter than parking three high-value files in the same residual bucket.

No case is `COMPLIANT`. That is not forbidden, but P-501 is being asked to do the work of the only “good” emergency path while still carrying identity and B2-timing holes.

**5. Alternatives section misses the load-bearing counter-reading.** Six cases are offered; the required minimum is four. Quality is uneven. P-501’s B2-pre-commit reading and P-503’s “C3 is not final approval” reading are genuine classification-changers. P-502 is not given a row. That is the most important omission in the paper. The strongest reasonable reclassification is: C4’s “supplier-onboarding requirements” includes A3, or past treatment of signed incident directives as overlays supplies A7-equivalence. Either moves P-502 from `NONCOMPLIANT` toward `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` (still leaving A2). The closing paragraph dismisses “all executive communications beat GPIP” — a weak straw man — instead of steelmanning C4’s actual words. P-508’s reverse reading (C2 cannot touch B3 without A7) is better, which makes the P-502 gap more conspicuous.

**6–7. Remediation and clause.** Section 6 correctly separates policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove compliance. P-502 vs P-507 is the right illustration. Recommending that unlisted forms be “unverified” rather than absent is consistent, but it also reveals why `INDETERMINATE` became a sink. Section 7 covers every mandated topic and includes a real anti-normalization safeguard (no verbal continuation, no auto-renewal, weekly emergency-share review). It also quietly tightens current law (25k becomes pre-commitment; SoD/Compliance become non-waivable even by CRO). As a *prospective* clause that is allowed; it should not be mistaken for a restatement of GPIP-4 as written.

**Grade rationale.** Deducted from a high ceiling for: (i) D1/A2 left in a residual `INDETERMINATE` loop while A1 on the same overlay is treated as established in P-503; (ii) P-501 over-classified and under-scored on severity given unknown approvers; (iii) Section 5’s failure to steelman P-502/C4/A7; (iv) B6 promoted from uncertainty to preserved rule; (v) pseudo-logic missing the A1/joint-authorization rule that actually drives a case. Factual chronology, source-scope discipline, and evidence-versus-norm separation remain clearly above a merely competent answer. Under a strict standard those remaining defects block anything in the 9+ band.
