The answer has the right skeleton and sees the central paradox, but it is not close to a high score under strict grading. It repeatedly misreads the artifacts, inverts the highest-value design choice, invents magnitudes the prompt does not support, and leaves compliance holes that a careful process-mining reading would have closed.

**Diagnosis is only partly grounded.** The 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split and the 31% → 9% cost erosion after complaints are used correctly, and fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces is a real distinction. After that the evidence use frays. Auto-screen cost is stated as “EUR 0.20 + EUR 34”; EUR 34 is the manual-review labor rate, not an auto-screen cost. “Premium customers (18%) have 22% complaint rates on the fast path” collapses two segments: 18% is Premium *low-risk accessories* (6% fast-path complaints); 22% belongs to Premium *high-value devices* (14% share). That is not a slip in a side fact — it is the attribute the whole redesign problem is organized around. Manual-review medians of “5–13 hours Premium vs 10–16 Standard” are not in the data; Intake → Manual is median 5.0 hours for the edge, not a segment split. Repair quote → complaint is 80 cases, but “mean 30 hours” is Repair quote → Customer approval, a different edge. Dispatch → complaint is described as a “96–180 hour cycle” by gluing the mean to the p90. Five artifacts are named, as required, but several are cited incorrectly, which is worse than citing fewer.

**Redesign A aims at the wrong risk.** Putting a 6–18 hour pre-dispatch gate on Premium/Standard accessories is inverted economics. Accessories already have the best fast-path outcomes (6–8% complaints, EUR 95 error). Premium accessories also have service-credit exposure above 24 hours; an 8-hour median path plus a 6–18 hour gate is a direct way to buy credits, not quality. The 30–45% catch rate is taken from the *evidence portal’s* effect on repeated requests and applied to wrong-replacement detection — a clear misuse. Claiming the design newly auto-approves 43% of cases ignores that 3600/5000 cases already enter Auto eligibility and 2550 already go Auto → Fast approval. Standard low-risk auto-dispatch without an evidence-completeness check is in tension with R2 (the exception is Premium low-risk only). The “digital literacy / abandonment” sacrifice is copied from the portal intervention A does not include.

**Redesign B is the strongest of the three and still leaky.** Parallelizing eligibility with evidence collection is a defensible reading of Request missing evidence → Manual (median 26h, p90 88h) and of the 22% unclear-defect cohort (140h, repeated requests, abandonment). That is real PM reasoning. Then the design undoes itself: “Fast approval if auto screen passes AND evidence is complete” still lets high-value / >EUR 400 claims skip Manual and Fraud/risk, which is exactly R1. Concurrent Manual + Fraud on a pool of 2 specialists and 7 reviewers, already unstable above 82% utilization and on Mondays, is treated as a cycle-time win; it is more likely a cost and queue transfer. “30–50% faster” is not supported. A 48h Premium repair-quote SLA is asserted against a p90 of 60h on Manual → Repair quote with no capacity add. Post-fulfillment audit does not stop the EUR 410 wrong-replacement event the performance DFG ties to long-tail complaints.

**Redesign C is not materially different enough.** Risk scoring, a predictive quality gate, mandatory QC on high-value replacements, and post-dispatch monitoring is an overlay on A+B, not a third mechanism. It leans on generic AI accuracy claims (“40–60% fewer complaints,” “>85% gate accuracy”) with no artifact behind them. The actual CX finding — Standard customers tolerate 72 hours *if visibility is good* — is applied to Premium status updates instead. Quality check’s 2 underused inspectors, the right resource to absorb a high-value pre-dispatch gate, are barely used as a design constraint. Escalation’s hard limit (one 45-minute daily slot, 120 cases) is mentioned only as a hoped-for load reduction.

**The comparison table does not do the job.** As written, directional signs are missing, so several cells are fragments (“for low-risk accessories”). Even reading intended arrows charitably, “Dominated? No” overclaims what the prompt says cannot be known. Current-state dominance is marked N/A rather than evaluated. B’s cost effect is scored as an improvement despite adding parallel specialist labor. A’s implementation complexity as “Low” ignores the stated 15–35% manual-inflow shock from rule updates.

**Failure modes are thin and partly invented.** Two modes plus one migration each meets the template, but A’s “85%” gate threshold is fabricated (82% is the manual-review figure), and the first-order A failure — Premium service credits from added latency — is absent. B does not list the R1 leak. Downstream shifting via the complaint → Manual loop (310 cases, mean 40h) and complaint → Escalation (120) is not treated as a failure path.

**The experiment sequence starts in the worst cohort and then invents KPIs.** Piloting the accessory gate on Premium low-risk first maximizes service-credit risk and minimizes quality upside; Standard accessories (72-hour tolerance) are the obvious first cell. Phase 5 treats “Fast approval path (3600 cases)” as the pilot frame; 3600 is Auto eligibility, not Fast approval (2550+240). Cost stop/go lines of EUR 150/160 per case are not in the prompt. Phase 6 asks for ROI and statistical significance after the same constraint forbids exact ROI/Pareto claims. Conformance (R1/R2/R3, fitting-trace rate, Fast-before-evidence, dispatch-without-risk on high-value) is not a first-class monitor. There is no holdout to separate real improvement from moving rework into Manual, Escalation, or credits. The one good line — test whether gains are only shifted downstream — is not operationalized.

**What a stronger answer had to do and this one did not.** Keep the cheap fast path for low-risk accessories; put evidence completeness and risk review in front of high-value replacement; treat the 22% missing-evidence loop as its own design, not a bolt-on; reuse idle quality inspectors on high-value pre-dispatch rather than delaying Premium accessories; size 7×36h review capacity and 2 fraud specialists before promising parallelism; and refuse invented percentages. The piece looks like a complete executive memo. Under a fact-check against the DFG, performance DFG, rules, and segment table, it is not.
