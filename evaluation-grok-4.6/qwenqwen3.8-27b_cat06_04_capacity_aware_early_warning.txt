**6.3**

The final six-section writeup is a complete, on-format policy. It refuses a swarm on I-03, treats I-05 and I-07 differently, keeps observational \(p\) separate from treatment effect, and puts spillover into both the score and the impact table. Those are the main quality-criteria items, and they are met. The answer is not nearly flawless. Several rule–allocation contradictions and untested modeling choices are large enough that a strict reading cannot go much above the mid-6s.

### What is actually strong

The decision rule is deployable rather than a single \(p\) cutoff: stage, 75-minute feasibility, repeat-swarm ban, imputation gate, \(\Delta p\) / new-event evidence, and segment calibration intervals. I-03 is correctly excluded despite \(p=0.82\) and €40k. I-05 is suppressed as a 30-minute refresh with no new event; I-07 is re-opened because of the new log even though \(p\) fell. The score \(S=\Delta p\cdot V-450-B-S_{\text{spill}}-U\) is the right shape. The live table covers all ten cases. The selected book is **I-09, I-02, I-04** with one slot reserved, and parking I-04 after the reserve block is operationally the right use of its 10-hour runway. Optimistic / base / adverse arithmetic is auditable (0.80 / 0.58 / 0.28 breach-equivalents; nets about +€20.9k / +€9.8k / −€3.3k to −€6.0k). Fairness and monitoring sections name concrete triggers, not slogans, and they do flag label contamination and spillover incidence.

### Decision-rule and allocation contradictions

The urgency exception is written as “time to SLA **< 3 hours**.” I-02 has **exactly 3.0 h**. Its \(S_{\text{mid}}\) is only ~€0.9k (below the €1.5k T1 bar) and \(S_{\text{low}}\) is about −€1.3k. Tier-1 status is therefore doing almost all the work for a case that does not satisfy the exception as written. That is not a rounding quibble; it is the difference between intervening and waiting.

Section 5 then invents a binding low-value floor: at least one of four daily slots (or 25% weekly) for \(p_{\text{cal}}\ge 0.65\) and time \(<3\) h, “regardless of value.” **I-07 meets that floor today** (SME-adjusted \(p\sim 0.76\)–\(0.81\), 1.5 h). The live book does not give I-07 a slot or the reserve. A safeguard that is announced and immediately ignored on the only day being decided is not a safeguard.

The I-07 timing is also internally impossible. Specialists are booked 12:00–13:30 on I-09 and I-02; the reserve is 13:30–14:15. The text still says I-07 can take the reserve “before 13:15.” Latest feasible swarm start for a 13:30 SLA is about 12:45. Under their own schedule, that option does not exist.

I-10 fails the stated first-alert gate (\(\Delta p=0.06<0.10\), \(p_{\text{cal}}\approx 0.525<0.55\), \(V=€11\)k \(<€50\)k) but is still scored and wait-listed. Small, but it shows the gates are not actually applied.

### Uncertainty is asserted more than used

Spillover is monetized at an invented **€15k** average for the six unnamed diagnosis-stage cases. The assumption is labeled, but there is no sensitivity. At €30k–€40k, \(S_{\text{spill,mid}}\) becomes €3.6k–€4.8k and I-02 / I-09 midpoint nets flip negative; only I-04 remains clearly attractive. A ranking that can reverse under a number the problem did not give needed a one-line stress test. It is missing.

I-09 is the first committed slot even though Non-Native-Language Support is the weakest calibration/effect cell. Section 5 later requires a 20% effect discount for that segment; the live \(S_{\text{mid}}\) for I-09 does not apply it (only a flat €400 \(U\) on \(S_{\text{low}}\)). Same pattern as the unused fairness floor: robustness language in the essay, point estimates in the book.

Adverse zeros I-02 (“model reason wrong”). Zeroing I-09 is the more natural adverse draw given sparse calibration, and it would have been harsher. Optimistic uses the **upper bound**, not the “upper half of the range” the prompt asked for. Both are small but they tilt the table in the policy’s favor.

The score is also over-parameterized (75 minutes, 0.10 / 0.55 / €50k, €1.5k / −€500, €300–€500 penalties, 60-minute cool-down). Those knobs are presented as if known. There is no check that the **rule** is stable if any of them move.

### Thinner spots the prompt asked to be explicit about

Non-monetary obligations are one sentence in the impact notes. I-01 is Public Sector, well-calibrated, first alert, \(\Delta p=+0.19\), effect 0.18–0.35, and it is left on the reserve list because €9k after spillover is slightly negative. Statutory / political cost of a public-sector breach is exactly the kind of non-monetary term the prompt required; it is not used.

Causal language in section 6 overreaches: treating reserve release as an instrument is not identification. Reserve use is a policy choice. Holdouts “when capacity allows” are also mostly fictional on a four-slot, three-committed day.

False-alarm burden is subtracted in full in every scenario, following the suggested formula, and they flag that this is conservative. That part is fine.

### Net

This is a competent operations-research memo that hits the required skeleton and the three “strong answer” tests. It is not a tight decision under uncertainty. The live portfolio depends on a misapplied urgency clause, a spillover value that was never stressed, and fairness/robustness rules that are not applied to the same cases they were written for. Under a hypercritical standard that is a mid-6, not an 8–10.
