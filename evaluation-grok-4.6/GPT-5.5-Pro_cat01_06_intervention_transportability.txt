**6.4**

A strong, format-compliant transportability memo that still fails a strict read on internal consistency, identification discipline, and a few model/API details. The analogical core is good. The quantitative and pilot apparatus is not tight enough to be nearly flawless.

## What is actually good

The answer correctly refuses a literal copy of Site A. It treats the before/after table as a confounded bundle (mix, +2 FTE, API, huddles, timestamps), keeps Assessment as an AND-join, and will not start Document Check before originals when the statute forbids it. The first stratum (digital / no-original / non-legal) is the right analogical move. Bottleneck-shift and legal/rework scenarios satisfy the required “mean up, tail down” case. Co-interventions are named rather than absorbed into “parallelization worked.”

That is the floor of a competent answer, not a reason to forgive the rest.

## Material flaws

**1. Identification discipline slips where it matters most.**  
Section 3 correctly refuses to treat the 9h median drop as a treatment effect (confidence 25). Section 5 then gives eligible cases **−5h to −9h** in the optimistic scenario, using Site A’s bundled median as if it were an upper structural bound. The prose says the 6h wait cut is a discounted anchor; the table still reprints the full 9h. That is a direct contradiction, not a rounding issue.

**2. Eligible share is not a single quantity.**  
38% is inferred as the complement of “originals required,” then 23% as 38% × 60% under independence, then the recommended first wave also drops high-complexity (~48%) and requires digital-at-register completeness. The operational first cohort is closer to ~10–15% if those filters are real, but scenarios, pilot power, and the recommendation still talk as if 23% (or 38%) were the design basis. High-value cases are also more likely to require originals; independence is used in the main table and only later demoted to an assumption.

**3. The pilot cannot do what it claims.**  
Site B completes ~1,300 cases in 8–12 weeks. A ~23% (let alone ~12%) stratum cannot yield 300 treated and 300 controls in that window, and 90-day appeals cannot mature inside “8–12 weeks plus follow-up” if expansion decisions are supposed to wait on them. Cluster assignment by week×team×stratum makes the effective *N* smaller still. “Or enough support for stable p90” is a fudge, not a design. Stop rules (true overlap <30%, p90 +10h, rework +2pp) are arbitrary and uncalibrated to baseline variance.

**4. T2 is partly as-is, and that is underplayed.**  
The DFG already has Register→Originals (1,860) and Register→Identity (2,050). Identity ∥ Originals Receipt is not a new partial order for much of the book. The incremental lever is mostly Identity ∥ Document Check on the no-original slice. The “minimal gain” scenario notices identity can hide under the 18h originals wait, but the intervention spec still sells T2 as a second wave rather than a documentation of current practice plus WIP/logging.

**5. Transport graph and confidence column are conceptually sloppy.**  
Required nodes are present, but `digital_documents → document_capacity` is not a mechanism, `cycle_time → appeal` is underspecified, and parallelization is not shown loading identity/document queues (the main interference path). “Transport confidence” sometimes scores *effect* portability (median 25) and sometimes scores a *method lesson* (no negative control 90, complexity shift 85). That inflates the table.

**6. Legal-loop POWL is not DFG-faithful.**  
DFG: Legal Review → Approve / Reject / Request Supplement. The model is LOOP(Legal Review, redo) then a separate XOR(Approve, Reject). Decisions are detached from Legal Review; a silent exit-then-decide path appears that the log does not show. Duplicate labels for Document Check / Assessment / Approve are acceptable as nodes, but the loop+XOR sandwich is a real control-flow error relative to the stated rules.

**7. PM4Py code is a sketch with papered-over APIs.**  
Trees, POWL edges, and the Assessment join are in the right shape. `check_soundness(net, im, fm)` in current pm4py typically returns a bool unless diagnostics are enabled; unpacking to `(sound, diagnostics)` will hit the `except` and never report soundness. `check_is_workflow_net` is not a reliable top-level name. `SilentTransition` is unused. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` in the pilot table is not a standard pm4py call. try/except around the required checks is not the same as performing them.

## Smaller cuts

- Optimistic all-case −3h to −6h is still large relative to a ~38% (or smaller) truly parallelizable share and a 3.4h shorter-branch cap.  
- IT-lock p90 +5h to +15h is asserted, not derived.  
- Digital-only confidence 60 is too high given specialized roles, the lock, and no API/huddle transfer.  
- Section 1 opening (“taken from the uploaded prompt”) and the GitHub footnote are noise.  
- Queue huddles are listed as a Site A after feature and a co-intervention, but the candidate B spec never says whether huddles are in or out of the base treatment (T3 tries to factor them; section 6 does not).

## Verdict

This is a good consulting memo: right qualitative recommendation (`conditional go`), right refusal to parallelize illegal work, right worry about locks, mix, and downstream queues. Under a hypercritical rubric it is not a 8–10 answer. The piece that the evaluation said it would grade hardest—causal transport with quantitative discipline and a feasible learning design—is exactly where the text talks carefully and then specifies carelessly.

The grade is held down by contradictory effect ranges, an infeasible pilot, an unstable eligible population, a legal-loop model that does not match the DFG, and checks that will not actually run. Those are not polish nits.
