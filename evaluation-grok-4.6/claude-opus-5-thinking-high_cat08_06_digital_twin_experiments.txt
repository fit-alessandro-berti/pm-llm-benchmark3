7.5

This is a strong, well-structured design that hits every required section and the hard constraints (emergent waiting, calendars as capacity, latent objections, non-independent cases, no optimality claim, plausible PM4Py usage). It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several methodological contradictions, scope mismatches, and realism gaps keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What holds up.** The twin boundary, entity set, and five coupling mechanisms are the right level of ambition. Waiting is treated as an output; applicant/statutory delays are the only sampled “waits.” Objections are a mixture inferred from notice duration, and D9 is correctly fenced as unvalidated/conditional. The experiment table has real trade-offs (D2 helping already-fast cases, D5 saturating legal, D3 fairness, D10 sub-additivity). CRN with entity-indexed streams, right-censoring, and the “do not mix CI/credible-interval labels” note are sound. The skeleton follows the stated PM4Py contract: pandas ingest, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree` with honest tree limits, variants/DFG/performance DFG/arrival average, timing columns that stay unidentified without starts, and a separate DES layer.

**Calibration target leakage.** Section 2.4 already uses variant shares as the “strongest constraint on joint routing.” Section 2.9 then lists those same shares, plus group median/p90 and pool utilisations, as summaries “that were **not** used for point estimation.” That is false on the author’s own plan. Utilisation is also used three ways: to close censored service times (§2.2), as a calibration target (§2.9), and as a validation test (§6.4). A digital twin that tunes to the same signatures it later “validates” will look calibrated and still be overfit. This is the most serious reasoning error in an otherwise careful uncertainty section.

**Scope does not match the model that is actually specified.** The text puts “all four rework loops (completeness, planning, inspection, legal/appeal)” inside the boundary. The given tree only loops completeness and inspection; planning rework never appears in `ReworkParams` or the engine. The process statement also separates legal rework from appeal, i.e. five mechanisms, not four. “13 nominal activities” is wrong: Issue and Reject are distinct labels, so the nominal set is 14. Small counting errors, but they sit in the section that is supposed to define the state space.

**Non-steady-state story is internally tense.** A33 correctly rejects a steady-state approximation, then §5.1 still leans on MSER-5/Welch after deseasonalizing. That is a workaround, not a resolved design: residual “flatness” after removing the seasonal mean does not restore the assumptions those truncation rules need, and the author never says what happens if year-2 vs year-3 WIP still differs (they say “report it,” but not whether the evaluation window is then invalid).

**Experimental design is computationally unreal and partly unfinished.** Thirty to 200 replications, plus ~40 outer parameter draws × ~20 inner runs, over a 6-month warm-up + 3-year horizon + cool-down, for a screening design, D1–D10, combinations, and five stresses, is a huge DES budget. There is no runtime model, no cheap surrogate, no statement of what gets dropped first. `build_scenarios()` also does not match §4: D5, D8, D9, C1–C3, and S4 are missing while the docstring claims coverage of “D1–D10, combinations, and the stress scenarios.” For a skeleton that is pardonable; claiming completeness is not.

**Process-mining nits.** `pm4py.get_start_activities` does not yield arrival timestamps; the prose pairs it with arrival estimation as if it did (the later `arrival_profile()` is the correct method). `pm4py.discover_batches` is proposed as the baseline for D3 without noting that batch discovery is weak or undefined on completion-only logs—the exact observability regime this log is in. Fitness ≥ 0.95 / “precision within a few points” is a slogan, not a protocol: a tree-driven simulator will often look highly fitting by construction, so this test can pass while timing and dependence are still wrong.

**Over-precise invented numbers.** Dataclass defaults (`base_rate_per_day=55`, objection rate 0.22 with CI 0.13–0.34, hazards `[0.35, 0.15, 0.06]`, `latent_difficulty_sd=0.6`, etc.) are not labeled as dummy values. Next to a long estimation plan, they read like results. The scheduler pool is introduced without an HR calendar, while the rest of the design insists calendars are first-class. Fee collection is excluded because it is “not on the critical path in the log excerpt”—two traces cannot support that claim.

**ABC/SMC is oversold.** With interval-censored service times, latent objections, latent area/sensitivity, and flattened cross-case dependence, the posterior the author wants is only weakly identified. Running SMC and then speaking of credible intervals and EVPI is formally consistent, but it papers over that several of those intervals will be prior-dominated. The text is honest about D9 and start timestamps; it is less honest that the whole calibration posterior inherits the same problem.

**What this still gets right, and why it is not lower.** Hard constraints are not violated. The municipality would get a usable comparison architecture, a shortlist logic based on regret and guardrails, and a code layout that can be filled in without throwing away the PM layer. The grade is held down by contradictions in the estimation/validation contract, incomplete fidelity between scope and mechanisms, and an experiment plan that is more textbook than operable—not by missing the assignment.
