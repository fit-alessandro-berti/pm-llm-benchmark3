**5.6**

The write-up is complete, on-format, and clearly above a generic Lean dump: it uses the DFG, rework rates, segment outcomes, R1/R2, resource caps, and the 31%→9% cost reversal, and the three options are structurally different (route, intake evidence, pre-dispatch gate). That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Process-mining errors.** The “~5 hour median” happy path is assembled by adding *mean* edge times (`0.8 + 1.0 + 3.0`). Those figures are means, not medians (medians are 0.5 / 0.4 / 1.8), and sequential means are not a case cycle time. Fast approval is also not a single 2,300-case auto path: 240 fast approvals already come from fraud/risk. R1 is mapped onto “Fast approval before evidence completeness,” which is an R2/alignment issue, not the €400 review rule. The 44-hour p90 cited as a fraud delay is the *Manual eligibility → Fraud/risk* edge, not fraud sojourn. These are not cosmetic.

**Redesign logic is coarser than the artifacts.** A treats “send all high-value to manual/fraud” as equivalent to the reviewed/repair path and asserts a jump from 20h to ~96h. Review-then-fast-approval already exists in the DFG; forcing the full quote/repair chain is an unargued leap and violates the instruction not to treat the slow path as uniformly better. A’s “faster Premium low-risk” lane mostly restates the R2 exception already in policy. C’s routing rule (`value > €400` **or** Premium) includes Premium low-risk accessories, then the same section says those accessories bypass the gate—an internal contradiction that would blow the 24-hour credit threshold the diagnosis itself flags. B claims both that the portal *reduces* the 320 evidence-loop abandonments and that it *increases* abandonment; failing-fast at intake does not retire those 320 cases, it relabels or hides them. All three designs are essentially the three interventions the prompt already named (rule update, portal, pre-dispatch gate), so synthesis is thinner than the brief asked for. Unused and material: R3, 12.5% vs 4% repair rework, escalation-board capacity, `Fast approval → Quality/compliance close` (350), part-price wait on quotes, and the Manual→Repair-quote Premium temporal violation.

**Forbidden dominance claim.** The table marks the current process `dominated? Yes`. The prompt forbids claiming exact Pareto dominance from this data. Current remains defensible for Standard low-risk if visibility is good; that cell is not supportable.

**Other concrete slips.** Failure mode C treats the 260 *complaint* arcs as the gate’s workload; the gate would sit on a large subset of ~2,300 dispatches, not on the failure outflow. Experiment order (A then C on Standard high-value) is partly incoherent: if A already lifts high-value off blind fast dispatch, C’s residual volume and learning value shrink, and that interaction is not discussed. Premium “24-hour SLA” is overstated (the artifact is service-credit *exposure*, not a hard SLA).

What remains good is real: contradiction is explicit, sacrifices are named, resources (82%, 15–35% inflow, 2 inspectors, digital literacy) constrain the designs, and the 90-day cohort cost check is the right test for displaced rework. Those strengths justify the mid-5s, not more. An executive-sponsor memo with wrong cycle-time arithmetic, a self-contradictory gate, and a banned dominance claim is not a high-scoring answer.
