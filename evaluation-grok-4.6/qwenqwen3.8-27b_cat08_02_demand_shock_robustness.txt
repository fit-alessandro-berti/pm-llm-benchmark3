**7.5**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured process redesign. It follows the required seven sections, stays mostly inside the given logs and candidate interventions, and consistently reasons about case-mix stress, bottleneck migration, and tails rather than the historical average. It is not nearly flawless.

**What works.** Scenarios S1–S4 match the required axes (mix shift, assay degradation/outage, validator/specialist absence, courier compression/delay); S5 is a useful compound stress test. Bottleneck sequences are grounded in the DFG and queues: peak recollection 8.0%, manual-correction-to-recollection ~26%, recollection-to-receive 60h, batch-wait 12.6% / 22h, assay-to-repeat 14.8% / 26h, technical validation 101% / 16h, medical 22h, specialist 36h / hold-to-medical 48h. Downstream overload is discussed for each lever. The package correctly rejects strict I1 and I9, uses protected lanes and WIP caps instead of ranking every case type first, and makes I4/I2/I3/I10 trigger-conditioned. The trade-off table has the required columns and does not pretend every bundle helps every case type. Residual risk includes redesign-caused starvation and M2 dependence. Leading vs lagging indicators are separated, with warning bands tied to peak medians.

**Defects that keep the score down.**

1. **Variant misstatement.** In S1, lower Urgent batch-wait bypass (58% vs 74%) is called exposure to “batch fragmentation.” Bypass *causes* schedule fragmentation; the peak pattern is more Urgent *batch waiting*. Section 3.4 partly corrects this, but the migration analysis is sloppy on a flagged variant.

2. **Resource-note misread.** “Cross-trainable in 4 weeks” is attached to technical validators as a role, not to accession-correction training. Treating it as I4 lead time is inaccurate.

3. **Imprecise mining arithmetic.** Tech→medical is reported as 34.5% vs 21.2% of *all cases*. That omits repeat→medical and hold→medical; medical→release is ~40% vs ~23% of cases. The direction is right; the load on medical validation is understated.

4. **Inference stated as fact.** Delayed/compressed samples are said to raise defect rates. The log shows higher peak recollection and correction, not a proven delay→defect mechanism.

5. **S5 sufficiency is optimistic.** With several stations at 87–101% utilization, lanes and triage are mostly zero-sum. The package never defines numeric “acceptable” service levels for Complex-panel or Repeat-after-failure, nor whether S5 can still meet Urgent 18h / Routine 72h without some capacity. I5 remains a capacity add, even if targeted.

6. **Presentation / trigger bugs.** Sequence arrows are missing. The batch-expansion trigger reads “M2 repeat rate 10%” with no inequality, so it can be read as an exact-value rule.

These are not fatal, but they are real accuracy, clarity, and robustness gaps. Under a strict reading they rule out a high-8 or 9.
