**7.4**

This is a strong, format-complete answer with accurate core arithmetic and the right causal instincts. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several identification, quantitative, and internal-consistency problems keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What works.** Section 1 cleanly separates availability, intensity, selection, co-occurring shocks, and measurement limits, and it avoids causal language. The estimand table covers ITT-like availability, intensity, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, plus dynamic and heterogeneous targets, and it is honest that almost nothing is point-identified. The primary design (cohort-specific comparative ITS / stacked event study) is the right family for two adoption dates and a ramping dose; rejecting naive TWFE and flagging IV exclusion are correct. Mechanisms are process-literate, especially fast-path substitution, fraud-desk interference, tail bifurcation, and survey selection. Pre/post and DiD arithmetic that I recomputed matches the tables (Alpha/Beta vs Delta/Epsilon median DiD ≈ −4.49 h, p90 ≈ −6.32 h, etc.). The rollout choice improves both operations and learning. The claim ladder’s top and bottom rungs are well judged.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Primary design is not what gets estimated.** Section 3 argues against naive TWFE because of staggered timing and event-time heterogeneity. Section 9 then estimates a branch-and-month FE model in the binary `automation` dummy—the specification just rejected—while relative-time dummies are built and never used. That is a real inconsistency, not a polish issue.

2. **Pre-trend parallelism is overstated.** Gamma’s Jan–Jun OLS median slope is about +2.1 h/month, but Delta/Epsilon over the same window are about +1.6 h/month, not “about +2.0.” The write-up makes the pre-trends look more parallel than they are, which props up the Gamma DiD.

3. **The headline DiD is sold too hard given a perfect confound.** The April routing campaign hits Alpha/Beta in the same month as adoption. Never-treated DiD therefore compares (automation + extra inbound flow + later staffing/complexity shocks) with branches that did not receive that routing package. Load/FTE adjustment does not fix possible composition change in *which* cases were diverted. Calling the result “avoided ~4 h median and 6–7 h p90 deterioration” as the main Suggestive claim, and assigning median confidence 60, is too confident for that design.

4. **Gamma’s “clearest” improvement is partly a common recovery.** July–August medians fall in Delta and Epsilon too, and August arrivals drop everywhere. Gamma’s extra drop is real in the DiD, but the claim ladder underweights the shared post-May mean-reversion and Gamma’s two-month post window plus readiness pilot.

5. **Section 5 intensity slopes are asserted, not shown.** Approximate effects per +10 pp (`−0.62` h median, `−1.01` h p90, etc.) appear without SEs, specification output, or a warning that residual intensity variation after two-way FE is essentially the within-adopter ramp. With five clusters those numbers are decorative.

**Smaller but real issues.** Section 1 is slightly over the 250-word cap. E1’s “0–4 months” horizon does not fit Gamma (2 months) or Alpha (5). Pre-rollout `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is noted and then dropped; that is a treatment-definition problem. The CSAT guardrail of 0.05 is not operable given missing response counts and 20–45% response rates, and a hard 3-hour absolute median gain for scaling to Epsilon fights the paper’s own “shock absorber” interpretation. The Suggestive bullet is already semi-causal. The p90 scenario interval (−7 to +3) mixes raw and DiD numbers without a stated bounding model.

**Bottom line.** Almost every required box is ticked, the arithmetic that can be checked is careful, and the policy recommendation is the right *type* of answer. Strict grading turns on over-claiming a contaminated DiD, a pre-trend slip that flatters identification, and code that does not implement the stated design. That is a very good exam answer, not a near-flawless one.
