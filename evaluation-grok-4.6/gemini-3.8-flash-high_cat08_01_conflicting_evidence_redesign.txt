**6.0**

This is a well-structured, evidence-dense executive brief with three genuinely different redesigns and real trade-off language. Under a hypercritical standard it is not close to flawless. Core mechanisms rest on unsupported arithmetic, selection bias, and invented precision, and it violates explicit constraints on Pareto claims and unearned numbers.

**What it does well.** The diagnosis actually uses the artifacts: frequency DFG (2550 auto→fast approval, 2300 dispatch vs 390 repair), performance tails (dispatch→complaint median 70h / p90 180h), token replay vs perfectly fitting traces (0.91 vs 68%), alignment moves on R1/R2, rework stats (14.1% vs 3.3%, 26% repeat evidence, 31%→9% cost reversal), and segment outcomes (Premium accessory 8h/24h credit vs Premium HV 22% fast-path complaints). The three redesigns are not parameter tweaks: A is a hard lane split, B is information/fulfillment quality, C is a fulfillment-philosophy and org-design change. Sacrifices, failure modes, bottleneck migration, and the “real vs shifted downstream” audit (long-tail observation, off-portal contacts, inventory reconciliation, lifecycle cost) are the strongest parts.

**Material quantitative and PM errors.**

1. **Redesign A’s main mechanism is arithmetically false.** “Removing ~2,150 accessory cases from manual review” and “>40% demand reduction” cannot be true. Accessories are 43% of *all* cases, not of the manual queue. Intake already sends 3,600/5,000 to auto-screen and only 1,400 to manual review. Many accessories are already on the 8–13h fast path. You cannot pull 2,150 cases out of a queue that does not contain them. The failure-mode section then *adds* “approximately 1,750” HV cases to mandatory review. Those two stories cannot both stand, and the prompt’s own warning (auto-rule updates may *raise* manual inflow 15–35%) is not netted against the claimed relief.

2. **Medians are added as if they were path times.** “Intake to fast approval median under an hour (0.5h + 0.4h)” is invalid. Median(A)+Median(B) ≠ median end-to-end.

3. **Edge counts are treated as cases.** 140+260+80+30 = 510 complaint *arcs*; unique complaint cases are given as 9.2% of 5,000 = 460. Dispatch 2,300 and repair 390 omit escalation inflows (65 and 45). Directionally right, technically sloppy.

4. **Redesign C transfers a selected-path rate to the whole population.** The 3.3% complaint rate is for cases that *already* traversed Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device. Forcing HV devices onto repair does not inherit that rate. The prompt explicitly warns not to treat the slow reviewed path as uniformly better. This is the central PM mistake in C.

5. **QC is misread.** Repair device → Quality check is already 360 of ~390 repairs; 12.5% HV rework happens *after* inspection. Re-using two “underused” inspectors will not cut rework to <4%. Scaling repair from ~390 to the HV mass (~35% of volume) saturates 3 technicians *and* 2 inspectors; C’s upside text treats that capacity as slack, while only the failure-mode section admits the bench constraint.

**Over-claims and invented numbers.**

- “For high-value devices, the fast replacement path is net-negative” is not in the data. After rework, fast approval is still **9% cheaper** in aggregate.
- Trace fitness “>95% / >98% / 100%”, 5–10 minute R1 sign-off, unclear-defect 140h → 36–48h, QC rework → <4%, and table cost bands (−15 to −22%, −18 to −25%, −10 to −18%) are not supported. A portal that cuts *repeat* evidence requests 30–45% does not compress a 140h median by ~70%.
- All 260 dispatch→complaint edges are treated as wrong-part selection. The temporal profile only says that tail is *linked* to part mismatch.
- B’s 10-minute HV “desk review” likely recreates rubber-stamp R1 violations rather than satisfying the rule.
- A “fixes” R2 by **amending the rule** to exempt all ≤€400 accessories. That is goalpost movement, not conformance improvement, and it is sold as “very low compliance risk.”

**Constraint violations.** The prompt forbids claiming exact Pareto dominance from this log. The table marks the current process **Dominated = Yes** and each redesign **Pareto-optimal**. Ranges are allowed; fake percentage ROI bands and “100% trace fitness” are not.

**Logical cracks in the experiment.** Phase gates at weeks 4 / 10 / 16 conflict with the (correct) 60-day post-close window needed to see complaint tails and reshipments. A GO criterion of “net cost −12% to −18%” in a 4-week accessory/HV pilot is unearned. The first pilot also mixes A’s express lane with B’s pre-dispatch gate, so a stop/go on “A” cannot isolate A.

**Smaller unclarities.** Low *precision* is attributed to outdated auto-rules; the prompt ties low precision to extra discovered paths that mix auto, risk, and repair. Complaint rate is repeatedly equated with scrap/error rate. C’s “negligible/zero” compliance risk is rhetoric. Swarming “2–4 hour” reopen resolution has no basis in the 31–42h median complaint-handoff times.

The brief would still help a sponsor see that the frequent path is a local optimum. It does not reliably size capacity, conformance, or cost, and two of the three “mechanisms of improvement” are not identified from the log.
