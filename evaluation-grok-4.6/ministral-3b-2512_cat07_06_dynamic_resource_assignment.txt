**3.5**

The submission has the required seven-section skeleton and some of the right vocabulary (SLA, continuity, training share, specialist bottlenecks). Under a strict reading it is not a workable, auditable, or constraint-respecting policy. The core deliverable—the C001–C016 assignment—contains hard-rule violations, false statements about the skill matrix, and inverted continuity logic. The algorithm cannot be implemented or audited as written. The PM4Py sketch is largely incorrect.

## Section 1 — objectives and constraints

Training is misclassified as a hard constraint. The brief makes the 20% training share conditional on acceptable SLA risk. Treating it as mandatory forces exactly the unsafe training assignments that appear later.

A 14-open-case cap is invented. The brief caps **new** assignments in the batch at 4, not stock on-hand work.

Inequality symbols are dropped (“Skill  2”, “Skill  3”), so the compliance rules are not even stated clearly. Hours remaining, Quality-Check batching, and “no safer alternative” as a precondition for high-overtime use are missing from the hard-constraint list.

Explainability and auditability are never listed as design objectives, despite being the point of the exercise.

## Section 2 — policy

This is a score catalogue, not an algorithm.

- There is no combination rule (weights, stages, or lexicographic order). Different numeric scales are added in the reader’s imagination only.
- Continuity is scored as `1 / (reassignment_count + 1)` on the **case**, not as a bonus for keeping the current owner. A new assignee on a never-reassigned case scores 1.0; the owner of a 3-reassignment case scores 0.25. That inverts the historical evidence.
- Handover-cost penalty is not restricted to owner changes, so it would also punish keeping the owner.
- Tie-break prefers the owner when skill is ≤1 **and** handover is **low**. Constraint 5 requires the opposite: prefer continuity on **high** complexity + **high** handover unless the owner is skill 0/1 and cannot be supervised.
- The 4-assignment batch cap is not enforced. Hours remaining are unused. Quality-Check batching (constraint 8) is absent. Case processing order is unspecified, so capacity contention is undefined.
- Fallback—“least overloaded generalist (skill 1)”—can violate skill-0 bans, Security/Approval rules, and the high-priority ≥2 rule.
- Resilience is named, not operationalized (no specialist reserve, no split of Security/Approval load).

An underspecified policy is not auditable. Section 6 therefore cannot do what it claims.

## Section 4 — baselines

The question asked for a comparison that does **not** crown a single optimum, and that is grounded in this queue. The answer does the opposite.

- No first-available or specialist-only assignment of C001–C016 is produced, so the comparison is unfalsifiable.
- **Expected throughput** is not a named dimension (required).
- The proposed policy is scored best on almost every row (“Fair”, “High”, “Robust”). The short trade-off note then admits high-priority skill mismatches—which the brief forbids, not “trades off.”
- First-available is given “High” SLA compliance; the brief already states that it raises reassignments and mismatch, which can **worsen** SLA on Security/Approval/high-complexity work.
- Their own plan (all Security on Gita, Hugo idle, David on Approval with skill 0) is **less** resilient than a careful specialist-only split of Gita/Hugo.

## Section 5 — worker explanation

Tone is appropriately non-punitive. Content is a generic template (“we picked someone who can handle the task well”) that cannot explain this batch: why Eva lost her negotiation, why Farid lost an approval he is best qualified for, why Gita received three Security reviews, or why a skill-0 approver appears. “Ask your team lead” outsources the explainability the policy was supposed to provide.

## Section 6 — audit indicators

The required names are present. Definitions are sloppy enough to make the audit wrong:

- SLA: `timestamp + sla_deadline` treats a deadline as a duration.
- Overtime: “high-risk resources with overtime hours remaining” confuses the OT-risk flag with remaining capacity.
- Skill mismatch via `required_skill = 0` does not measure assignee skill vs required skill.
- Handover is not tied to SNA / `org:resource` vs `current_owner`.
- No targets, no baseline deltas, no rule for supervised exceptions (which they used loosely).

You cannot audit deviations from Section 2 because Section 2 never specifies a decision rule.

## Section 7 — PM4Py

This is the other hard fail.

- The log is a **dataframe** with given columns; `format_dataframe` is listed in the brief and unused. An XES importer is assumed instead.
- `filter_case_performance` is used as a **priority** filter. In PM4Py it filters by **case duration**, not `case_priority`.
- `filter_event_attribute_values(...)` is called with invented parameters and does not detect skill mismatch.
- Import paths (`pm4py.algo.discovery.handover_of_work_network`, `activity_based_resource_similarity` as that module layout) do not match the public API the brief told them to use (`pm4py.discover_*`).
- Required functions never appear in a real role: `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_batches` (needed for C006/C012), `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_variants`, `view_sna` / `save_vis_sna`.
- `r.skill >= required_skill` assumes one skill per person. The matrix is activity-specific.
- The loop does not learn from the log and does not audit assignments against `assignment_reason`, `reassignment_count`, or `handover_cost_band`.

## What keeps it above a 2

Format compliance; recognition that multiple criteria exist; two continuity-correct rows (C007, C016); a non-punitive worker tone; indicator **names** that match the brief.

That is surface completeness, not a correct policy. A near-flawless answer would have: hard vs soft rules aligned to the brief; a staged, implementable algorithm with batch order and the 4-assignment cap; a legal assignment table (especially Security split Gita/Hugo, Farid keeping C002/C011, Eva keeping C005, David **not** approving, supervised training only where skill ∈ {1,2}); explicit baseline assignments on this queue; and working `pm4py.*` calls on a formatted dataframe.

This answer is a structured set of constraint violations and matrix errors. **3.5**.
