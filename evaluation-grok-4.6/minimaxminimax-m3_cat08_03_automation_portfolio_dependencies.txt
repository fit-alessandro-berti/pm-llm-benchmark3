**6.2**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and generally aligned with the required sections. It correctly refuses a frequency-first ranking, defers the activation bot, treats controls as non-transferable, and uses waiting time, loops, resource pools, and four-eyes findings. Those are real strengths. Under a strict reading they are not enough, because the final recommendation is built on several factual slips, internal contradictions, and process-mining misses.

**Factual and quantitative errors**

The anti-A1 argument depends on a bad labor comparison: “Receive + Register combined: ~3 min service × 12,000 → 600 hours.” Receive is 3 minutes; Register is 8 minutes at 11,880 cases. Combined service is about 2,200 hours, not 600. The direction of the argument can survive, but the headline contrast with 54k/104k/136k case-hours is sloppy, and it is the one place the answer tries to be precise about why management’s favorite module is weak.

Little’s Law is also used loosely. \(L=\lambda W\) is not the same claim as “throughput is bounded by the slowest synchronization.” That is bottleneck/ToC language. Small, but it sits in the final justification.

**Internal contradictions**

The dependency map says A6 “cannot reach its quality bar if A5 is not in place,” then the recommended year-1 portfolio ships A6 and parks A5 in year 2. There is no compensating control for low-quality PDFs, RM-generated incomplete instructions, or the 52%/84.8% missing-document loop that A6 is supposed to consume. That is a planning hole, not a stylistic one.

The quick-win effects section says the missing-document loop is “not targeted,” while A5 is inside that same portfolio. The hedge that A5 “does not break the loop structurally” is not reconciled with the matrix, which gives A5 high process-level benefit for exactly that loop.

“Downstream bottleneck risk” is scored as a hazard for A1 and as a benefit for A4/A6/A7. The column stops being comparable.

**Process-level overclaim**

A4 is treated as the highest-leverage unblocker. Entity mismatch occurs on 1,750 of ~12,000 cases (~15%). The 82% critical-path figure applies only when the activity is present, so A4 can move end-to-end time on roughly 12% of the book. The case-hour math in the same answer already shows A6 and A7 as larger wait pools; the narrative then ignores its own arithmetic.

Concurrency is almost unused despite an explicit instruction not to ignore it. KYC and financial-document review run in parallel for a large split of the DFG (7,250 vs 3,940 from classify, plus KYC → credit 4,200). Speeding the KYC/mismatch path does not reduce cycle time if the financial/normalization path is longer. That should have disciplined the A4-first story. It does not.

The BS portfolio is said to deliver the largest cycle-time cut while leaving customer signing’s 44h × 7,300 (~321k case-hours) untouched—the single largest wait pool in the table. Deferring A10 can be defended (identity, consent, KYC-before-sign), but then the “largest reduction” claim is not defensible. Credit-analysis wait (28h × 8,900) is similarly large and only partly a normalization problem; 4,200 cases already enter credit from KYC.

Committee packaging wait is attributed too cleanly to A7. Only 1,980 of 3,900 packs come from external valuation. High-exposure committee wait is also partly a weekly cadence, which no module removes.

**Variant, control-path, and integration thinness**

Product and exposure variants are mostly decorative. Collateral is required in 71% of equipment finance and 18% of working capital; contract remediation is 14% on credit-line renewals; four-eyes is a high-exposure rule. A stronger portfolio would scope A7, A12, or A9 by variant instead of treating the book as homogeneous.

A2’s misroute risk is scored in the matrix and then dropped. If classification is wrong, A4/A6/A7/A12 execute on the wrong control path. That is a real prerequisite, not a side note.

A6 is “moderate” core-banking integration in the dependency map and a “deep” slot in the rollout. Receive is already digital for 72% of cases; that fact never qualifies A1’s “high direct labor” rating. The table rates candidates but does not actually rank them.

**What still works**

The three portfolios are distinct and the sacrifice statements are mostly honest. A11 is correctly withheld. Resource-network points (3 specialist FTE, two-senior concentration, analyst centrality, back-office idle after 15:00, legal month-end) are used, not merely listed. Control findings appear in gates and compensating controls. The two-deep-integration constraint is respected in the phase plan.

Those are the reasons this is not a mid-5 or lower. The reasons it is not an 8+ are the bad labor number, the A5/A6 contradiction, the QW rework contradiction, the ignored concurrency instruction, and the inflated A4/cycle-time claims. Under the requested strictness, those are disqualifying for a high score.
