**7.0**

The final write-up is a strong causal-policy analysis: it refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, keeps median and tail separate, treats availability as distinct from realized use, and recommends expansion only with an embedded evaluation. Estimands, interference, and rollout design are generally well specified. It is not near-flawless. Quantitative discipline—the dimension this task weights heavily—has real errors and over-precision, and a few identification/policy claims sit in tension with the evidence the author just produced.

**What is done well**

Section 1 correctly separates availability, dose (`automated_fast_path_pct` ramping from a 4–6% pre-availability baseline), targeted selection of weak high-volume branches, and co-occurring shocks (March fraud desk, April routing, FTE moves, May complexity, Jul–Aug demand dip), without causal language. The estimand table covers ITT availability, dose, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamics, heterogeneity, interference, and the missing case-level contrast, with honest “not identifiable” calls on CSAT, dose, extrapolation, and spillovers. Identification is not naïve TWFE: never-treated staggered DiD is primary, TWFE is labeled descriptive, and Goodman–Bacon / small-cluster problems are named. Mechanisms (fast-path substitution, specialist congestion, rework displacement, tail widening, survey selection) are process-aware. The placebo (Delta pseudo-treated in May: about +3.9pp median / +4.6pp p90) is a genuine discipline check. The policy choice—Delta next, Epsilon as holdout, case-level randomization, desk ring-fencing, explicit scale-up rule—improves both operations and learning. The Python block matches the spec (embedded CSV, validation, no subplots, event time, clustered SEs, warnings).

**Material quantitative flaws**

The complexity-adjusted DiD table in §5.2 is wrong for mixed-complexity windows. April is still the *old* complexity at Delta (18, not 23) and Gamma (22, not 27). Window-average Δcomplexity is about +3.3pp for Delta in Apr–Jun, not +5pp; Gamma’s Apr–Jun → Jul–Aug complexity change is not zero. Those mistakes feed the headline adjusted figures (Alpha early “9.0% / 4.9%”, Gamma “4.5% / 3.9%”, Beta early “0.9%”). Recalculating with consistent window averages moves several of those cells by a few points. For a report that advertises transparent arithmetic, that is a serious miss.

The January 5-point slope (~1.0h median / ~1.9h p90 per complexity point) is then treated as a known elasticity. Cross-section residuals are large (Epsilon: 30% complexity but only 41.4h median versus Alpha 28% and 50.4h). Leave-one-point-out on that slope is never shown, yet the same slope is used to declare Beta’s median effect ~0 after adjustment. That “finding” is knife-edge, not a result.

Several series are piecewise constant to 0.1pp for months (rework, escalation). Reporting rework DiD of −1.4 / −2.5 / −2.1pp and escalation of +0.7 to +1.1pp as if they were measured effects overstates precision on rounded, likely synthetic aggregates. CSAT DiD of +0.07 to +0.13 with unknown 20–45% response is indistinguishable from noise; calling it a range of −0.10 to +0.25 is appropriately wide, but listing it alongside operational DiDs still over-weights it.

**Identification and logic tensions**

Unadjusted DiDs (−8 to −17pp median, −7 to −12pp p90) are then collapsed to a “point ~8%” package effect even after the author shows Beta’s complexity-adjusted median is ~0 and Gamma’s absolute median is essentially unchanged versus Jan–Mar (37.5 → 37.2h). Heterogeneity is documented and then averaged away. The “system-alone” 10–25% band applies untreated volume elasticities (~1.0 / 1.3–1.5) to high-utilization adopters as if queueing were linear; the text flags extra assumptions but still presents the band as a peer of the package range.

Primary strategy is named Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham and implemented as calendar-window mean DiDs. That is honest in the table footnote, but it is not the named estimator. Event-time dummies dump Gamma’s deep pre-period (rel −6…−4) into the same all-zero reference as never-treated branches—mentioned, not repaired.

Fraud-desk spillover is correctly signed (relative DiDs are upper bounds) but the implied magnitude is stretched: ~10–17 extra cases/month per adopter onto a five-branch desk is used to rationalize multi-hour control p90 residuals without a queueing bound.

**Policy recommendation**

`expand with an embedded evaluation` is the right class of decision. Sending Delta first (best baseline, lowest complexity) while E8 says effects at strong branches are not identified is operationally sensible and statistically a *different* population than Alpha/Beta—the write-up underplays that as a limit on what the RCT will teach about Epsilon. The ≥8% median / p90 no worse than +3% rule is not implied by the author’s own intervals or by Beta’s near-null adjusted estimate. Intra-branch specialist-queue interference under 50/50 case randomization is barely treated.

**Section 10**

The claim ladder is correctly ordered and the “Not supported” bullet is the right bottom rung. “Conditionally causal: 5–15% median” is still too compact given Beta, the adjusted-table errors, and the ±4–5pp placebo floor.

**Net**

This would be an 8.5–9 if the complexity-adjusted arithmetic were correct, slope uncertainty were shown, and the median “point estimate” / 8% scale-up rule were dropped in favor of heterogeneous, assumption-labeled ranges only. Under strict scoring, those are not cosmetic. The causal architecture and process reading remain clearly above average; the quantitative core is not reliable enough for a higher grade.
