8.3

This is a strong, constraint-faithful design: shared-resource DES, calendars, non-stationary clustered arrivals, latent objections, explicit service/wait separation, CRN pairing, and no optimality claim. It is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up.** The twin is correctly framed as interacting mechanisms rather than sampled variant durations. Two clocks (municipal vs applicant) prevent D1/D7 from gaming intake time. Observed vs imputed vs scenario fields, versioned rework, guarded joins, and “same architect/district ≠ automatic wait” are the right cross-case discipline. Saturday fire/inspection events vs stated calendars, and the planner offered-load bound, are real identifiability gates rather than silent fixes. Objections stay latent; historical waits are validation targets, not extra sampled delays. The experiment matrix covers B0, D1–D10, a joint factorial, extra combination C1, and four stress cases, with D2×D3 as a genuine spillover. Warm-up, censoring, follow-up arrivals, outer parameter / inner Monte Carlo loops, and heavy-tail handling are coherent. Decision criteria are multi-objective and explicitly municipal inputs.

**Defects that block a high score.**

1. **PM4Py convention error after an explicit signature check.** `discover_performance_dfg(..., perf_aggregation_key="all")` is not a valid high-level PM4Py signature; `perf_aggregation_key` belongs (if at all) on lower-level DFG adapters, and `"all"` is not a standard aggregation. That is a direct miss against the stated PM4Py-fidelity constraint. Timezone-aware UTC timestamps after `tz_convert` are also a known breakage risk in `format_dataframe` / DFG code paths.

2. **The capacity argument is correct but underplayed.** Mean ≥ median/2 is a weak bound. The same lognormal the text proposes for planning (median 3.5h, p90 14h) implies a mean near 6h and a much larger overload vs 8 planner FTEs. Using the loose bound makes the contradiction look like a possible 2× mismatch instead of a likely 5–8× interpretation problem.

3. **Prose/code gaps on interventions.** Section 1 says D2 must omit inspection scheduling when no inspection work remains; `RoutingPolicy` only drops `Site inspection` from drafting prerequisites and never waives scheduling. D7 shifts Monday volume but never models slot capacity, so appointment waiting can be hand-waved. Rework return points (planning / legal / appeal) are described, not encoded.

4. **Arrivals and objections stay verbal.** Seasonal/weekday/architect clustering is required, but there is no intensity model (e.g. marked NHPP + weekly clusters), and D9’s extension reduction is a free parameter with only a “no peeking” warning.

5. **Process-mining grounding is thinner than the DES protocol.** Variants/DFG/performance DFG/arrivals are present; alignments or fitness against the incomplete tree (missing planning/legal/appeal rework, `Completeness rework` absent in P001) are not used as estimation or validation instruments.

6. **Presentation nits that matter in this format.** The `sandbox:/mnt/data/...` download is a dead artifact; the GitHub cite does not justify the performance-DFG claim; the full D10 2⁴ × parameter-draw × 100–300-rep grid is not costed, so the experiment plan is not shown to be runnable.

No hard constraint is outright violated (independent cases, collapsed waits, observed objections, `pm4py.read_csv`, claimed optimum). The remaining issues are API inaccuracy, a softened workload contradiction, incomplete intervention mechanics, and PM methods used as diagnostics rather than as the calibration backbone. That is excellent applied design, not a near-flawless one.
