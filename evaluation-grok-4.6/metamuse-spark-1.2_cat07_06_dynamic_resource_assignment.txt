**C012 is a direct violation of the overtime rule.** Bruno is `overtime risk = high`, already at 12 cases with 4.0h left. C012 is low priority with 48h of slack, and the owner (Jonas) is a quality specialist with spare capacity. Constraint 4 forbids loading high-OT resources unless the case is urgent and no safer alternative exists. Calling this “minor overtime exposure justified by low priority” inverts the rule. Low priority is exactly when you must *not* use Bruno.

**Training share is inflated and partly invented.** The claim “4 of 9 = 44%” counts C010 and C015 as training-priority hits. They are not. Amal’s documented priority is diagnosis, not approval. Celine’s is negotiation, not security. Eva/Jonas cannot take C015 even under supervision (Security = 0). David/Hugo have Approval as a training goal but Approval = 0, so they are ineligible. Honest count is **C009 only**, or C009+C012 if one also accepts the OT violation: **11–22%**, not 44%. **C010 is a weak, costly exception.** Bruno already owns the case, is Approval = 2 (allowed to finalize), and has 0 reassignments / low handover cost. Splitting it into Amal-prepares / Farid-finalizes consumes two people, breaks ownership, and does not serve Amal’s stated development goal. **C015 likely contradicts the author’s own scorer.** Hugo is the owner, Security = 3, low OT, 0 reassignments, low handover. Moving the case to Celine (Security = 1, training goal ≠ security) drops skill-match and continuity a lot, and the +3 resilience bonus is weighted 0.05. The numeric policy would keep Hugo. The table overrides it for “secondary capacity” while also loading Celine with C003, C007 (6h high-complexity rework), C009 supervision, and C015 as trainee.

**Farid’s dual urgent load is ignored.** C002 (Approval, 2h) and C011 (Negotiation, 4h, VIP) both stay with him, plus C010 sign-off. Continuity/skill make each keep individually defensible; together they concentrate SLA risk on one person. A policy that advertises urgency weight 0.30 and resilience should surface that collision. **Celine and Hugo are over-committed relative to remaining hours.** Celine keeps a 6h high-complexity rework and still receives a new diagnosis, a supervision, and a Security training case. ## Policy specification problems

The algorithm is readable but not executable as written.

- Eligibility is missing the union (`candidates  supervised_candidates`).
- Step 0 admits only `skill >= 2` as direct candidates, while H5 talks about `skill >= 1`. Approval supervision is tightened to skill ≥ 3 even though the brief allows skill 2–3 to finalize.
- Continuity scoring is ambiguous: it is unclear whether the 7/5 bands apply only to the owner, and “0 if reassignment would be 4th+” is ill-posed for the incumbent (keeping the owner does not increment reassignments).
- Two decision systems sit on top of each other (weighted total *and* urgent/continuity overrides) without a worked example. Section 3 never publishes U/M/C/F/D/B totals, so the table cannot be audited against Section 2. Several rows look like post-hoc narrative, not rank output.
- Resilience is rhetorically central and mathematically almost inert (weight 0.05).
