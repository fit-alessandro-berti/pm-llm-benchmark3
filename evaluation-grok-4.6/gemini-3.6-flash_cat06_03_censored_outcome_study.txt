**6.2**

The write-up looks polished and avoids the worst failure modes (rejects-as-non-default, train-only-on-21,600-without-comment, random splits, post-outcome fields as features). Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless: the primary estimand and the primary analysis are different objects, the validation scheme contradicts itself and cannot test the migration, required PM4Py prefix extraction is missing, and several process rules leak future information.

## 1. Target definition — conceptually right, operationally unfinished

Choosing \(P(Y_{90}=1\mid X_{\le T_{\text{pred}}}, A=1)\) is statistically legitimate and the alternatives (multi-state, survival, \(Y(1)\)) are the right menu. The text correctly refuses to call reject/withdraw/open a non-default.

It does not actually do the required job of defending that choice as the *primary* operational target. The lender asked for default on **every** application, at the **first few events**, when \(A\) is still in the future. A score fitted on activated accounts and then applied to all live prefixes is reject inference in disguise unless the deploy-time population is restricted to cases that will be booked — which is unknown at \(k=3\). That train/serve mismatch is the central design problem and is only gestured at.

Other defects:
- Prediction time is not chosen: \(k=3\) or \(k=5\) *or* 24 hours (later 72 hours). An as-of study needs one primary clock.
- Population text (“all applications whose outcomes have matured”) does not match the estimand (activated accounts only).
- The 35-day feed lag is treated as part of the “horizon.” It is a label-availability constraint. The estimand remains 90-day default after activation, not 125-day default.
- The 180-day multi-state target mixes time origins. Default is defined from \(t_{\text{act}}\), not from start; an account activated on day 170 has no matured 90-day default by day 180.

## 2. Cohort construction — arithmetic good, semantics sloppy

The 48,000 row-add is correct (21,600 + 3,600 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100). Maturity as \(t_{\text{act}}+125\le\) 2026-02-15 \(\Rightarrow t_{\text{act}}\le\) 2025-10-13 is the right calendar.

Then the table undoes some of that care. Withdrawn is simultaneously a competing terminal state and “right-censored at withdrawal.” Those are incompatible. Default is not on the clock until activation; withdrawal is not censoring of \(Y_{\text{cond}}\). Unlinked transfers are likewise called both a competing event and an IPCW missing-follow-up problem. Partner permanent feed loss never appears as a distinct accounting line, even though it is a stated data risk inside the activated set.

There is no inclusion rule at the prediction point (minimum prefix length, must still be open, must have reached KYC/review). Appending a linked child case onto the parent identifier is asserted without a plan for double-counting, platform shift, or outcome attribution.

## 3. Censoring strategy — taxonomy strong, primary estimator wrong

The five-way split (administrative censoring, delayed labels, selection, transfer, MNAR partner feeds) and the testability table are the best part of the answer. Manski bounds and a pattern-mixture \(\delta\) for partner MNAR are appropriate sensitivities. Selection-as-untestable is correct.

The **primary** analysis is not. Inverse-probability weighting by \(1/\mathbb{P}(A=1\mid X)\) does **not** estimate the declared target \(P(Y\mid X,A=1)\). Under unconfoundedness it targets something closer to \(P(Y(1)\mid X)\), which Section 8 later says the study cannot claim. The product weight that folds selection, maturity censoring, and partner dropout into one \(w_i\) is not a well-specified IPCW/IPW estimand. In-flight applications are misclassified as ordinary right-censoring of default.

So the paper’s own honest position (“do not pretend \(Y\) exists without activation”) is abandoned by the estimator that is supposed to implement it.

## 4. Prefix extraction — look-ahead via padding

Fixed-\(k\) and elapsed-time prefixes are the right pair, and the migration rename + near-duplicate rule is reasonable as a sketch.

Padding short cases with `[CASE_TERMINATED]` encodes the terminal decision into the prefix. For \(k=3\), a reject at event 2 is no longer an early-warning problem; the case is over. Treating termination as a token is leakage for any model that is also supposed to score live applications. \(T_{\text{pred}}=t(e_m)\) on a finished case is not an early-warning time.

Rework handling is also process-naive. The stated path is Start \(\to\) KYC \(\to\) Credit Review, *then* document loops. At \(k=3\) those loop counts are mostly zero; the write-up never faces that the chosen prefix is too short for the rework features it advertises. Elapsed window is 24h in §1 and 72h in §4.

## 5. Process-aware features — required calendar/cohort block missing

Control-flow, timing, queue depth, and as-of underwriter approval rate are directionally right, and the last of those is carefully restricted to cases closed before \(t(e_k)\).

The prompt required **cohort/calendar** features. There is no policy-era flag, no migration-era flag, no start-month/day-of-week, no calendar distance to 1 Jul 2025. That is not cosmetic: the same prefix is stated to have a different selection probability and a different outcome map after the policy change.

`dfg_alignment_score` is unspecified and unsafe: a DFG fit on the full log (including destinies after \(T_{\text{pred}}\)) leaks future control flow, and “alignment” against a DFG is the wrong PM4Py object (alignment is against a net). Historical-reference features are not cleanly separated from prefix features as asked.

## 6. Leakage audit — table is good, incomplete

The required rows (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, updated `risk_score`, resource experience, label availability) are present and the keep/repair/drop calls are mostly right.

Gaps:
- `default_90d` / `raw_default_label` never appear. That is the actual label and must be excluded from \(X\).
- Accept/Reject (and `Account Activated`) in a long prefix *are* the selection event; not audited.
- Repair of resource experience invents `hire_date`, which is not in the schema.
- `label_available_at` is correctly banned as a feature and then never used as the maturity instrument it exists for.
- `source_system` is blanket-dropped in code; it is one of the few observables that marks partner-feed risk.

## 7. Validation — the timeline does not work

Rejecting a random case split is correct, and PR-AUC / ECE / Brier / subgroup-by-channel are appropriate for the binary selected-population target.

The scheme as drawn is internally false:

- The ASCII axis places **15 Oct 2025 before 13 Oct 2025**.
- The diagram labels validation as post-policy; the text puts validation in May–June 2025 (**pre**-policy).
- Test starts are 1 Jul–13 Oct 2025, with maturity requiring **activation by 13 Oct**. The migration is **15 Oct**. Almost no mature labeled case can have lived under the renamed schema. Claiming this test “evaluates system migration” is incorrect. Migration is unevaluable for a matured 90-day label given the extract date; that should have been an explicit limitation, not a checkbox.
- Split membership is by **start** time, not activation+maturity. Starts on 1 Oct that activate on 20 Oct enter `test_ids` with no label.
- Sample-size figures (~32k/5k/7.5k) are all-application starts, not the much smaller mature activated counts, and there is no power comment on post-policy defaults.
- No post-policy calibration window, no protocol for the known 1 Jul selection/outcome break (era interactions, separate models, or declared non-transport).

## 8. Limitations — the one nearly clean section

The can/cannot claims, the refusal of \(Y(1)\), and the three DGP feedback loops (threshold shift, applicant gaming, underwriter rerouting) are the right decision-use boundary. “Accurately predicts” in the banner is overstated for a selected-population association. The IPW primary analysis in §3 is inconsistent with the causal humility here.

## 9. Code — misses a stated requirement and re-imports leakage

What works: `read_csv` + timestamp parse, `pm4py.format_dataframe` on the named columns, case start/activation/terminal/maturity fields, temporal split by start, competing-risk labels with `NaN` rather than \(Y=0\) for non-activated cases, a leakage column assert, and a comment that survival/competing-risk models attach later. No fabricated PM4Py survival API.

What fails the spec and the design:

- **`pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is never called.** The prompt required it. A `cumcount` filter is not a substitute.
- Length is ~220 lines, well outside the 80–160 band, without buying the missing APIs.
- Future **types** are dropped globally, then the first \(k\) remaining rows are taken. That rewrites ranks if a blacklisted name appears early and does not implement time-based prefixes at all. Events after \(T_{\text{pred}}\) that are not on the short blacklist stay in the log.
- `full_dataset` still contains `raw_default_label`, `is_activated`, `activation_time`, `is_mature`, and `competing_risk_target`. The audit only asserts the static blacklist. A routine `drop(target)` fit would leak the future.
- `default_90d` aggregated with `max` over the **entire** case is fine as a label factory and fatal if that column is treated as a feature.
- `label_available_at` is unused; maturity is a blunt 125-day rule.
- No `documents_total` repair, no experience repair, no calendar/policy features, no transfer linkage, no elapsed-time prefixes.
- Optional PM4Py utilities are unused; that is allowed, but then DFG “alignment” in §5 has no implementation and no as-of justification.

## Bottom line

The answer knows the rhetoric of a careful PPM study. It does not hold a single estimand from target through weighting, cohort rules, validation, and code. The two errors that dominate the grade are (i) selling IPW-for-selection as the primary analysis of a conditional-on-activation target, and (ii) a temporal backtest that cannot observe the migration it claims to test. Those, plus the missing required prefix API and several as-of leaks, keep it well below the “nearly flawless” band.
