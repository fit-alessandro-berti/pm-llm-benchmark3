The answer has the required six-section shape and two of the headline qualitative distinctions (do not swarm I-03; treat I-05 differently from I-07). Almost everything that has to be *decision-theoretic, internally consistent, and auditable* is wrong, contradictory, or invented. Under a strict reading this is not a deployable policy.

## Decision rule is not the rule later applied

The eligibility clause restricts intervention to `Diagnosis Started`. The prompt states the swarm is useful **before** `Diagnosis Complete`, so **Triage is eligible**. The author then marks I-01, I-04, I-07, I-08, and I-10 (all Triage) eligible and swarms several of them. The written rule and the allocation cannot both be true.

The false-alarm cutoff is decision-theoretically indefensible and is immediately violated. Barring any case with burden > €200 would exclude I-01 (€250), I-02 (€600), I-04 (€2,000), I-08 (€5,000), and I-09 (€800)—including the €60k–€100k Strategic Enterprise cases—without comparing burden to expected benefit. The table then intervenes on several of those same cases.

Calibration adjustment is assigned to the wrong segments **and** applied in the wrong direction. SME **underpredicts** by 0.04–0.09 (add, not subtract). Strategic Enterprise **overpredicts** by 0.05–0.12 (subtract). Public Sector is “broadly acceptable.” The answer subtracts `(0.04–0.09, 0.05–0.12, and none)` from Public Sector / Strategic Enterprise / SME. That is a full permutation error. A later 10% haircut on ARR for “calibration uncertainty” conflates observational probability bias with an unknown causal effect.

Other rule defects: ARR “top 50% of the range” is not an operational filter (own-range vs cross-case ranking is unspecified); I-05/I-07 are hardcoded instead of a reusable evidence-change test; I-06 is excluded for imputation in the rule and then marked eligible; NNLS is given both “no adjustment” and a “neutral bias”; spillover and option value of waiting never enter the score; the reserved slot is stated as an eligibility axiom rather than a capacity choice.

## Capacity allocation is arithmetically impossible and data-corrupted

Four slots exist. The selected set is **I-01, I-02, I-04, I-07 plus one reserved slot**—five claims on four slots. The table simultaneously marks **seven** cases `Intervene`. That is not an allocation.

I-07 is scored with **I-09’s** value (€20,000 vs €5,000) and **I-09’s** false-alarm burden (€800 vs €50). That single swap fabricates a ~€2,950 “net impact” for a case whose midpoint direct EV is on the order of €800. I-06’s ARR is entered as **0.08**, below its stated lower bound of 0.15. Other point ARRs are an inconsistent mix of near-floor, mid, and near-ceiling values (I-02 uses 0.21 vs midpoint 0.15; I-01 uses 0.21 vs midpoint 0.265). Stated priority order is not followed: I-08’s own net (€7,550) ties I-04 and beats I-01 (€1,190), yet I-01 is taken and I-08 is not. “Main uncertainty” cells repeatedly label ARR ranges as spillover.

Time-to-SLA, the one operational clock that should drive **wait vs spend**, is ignored. I-04 (10 h, €60k) and I-08 (8 h, €100k, ARR only 0.05–0.15, FAB €5,000) are classic wait/optionality cases; I-07 (1.5 h) is a clock case. The policy has no wait tier that means anything.

I-03 is correctly withheld. That is the section’s only clean, load-bearing call.

## Expected-impact table is not auditable and inverts the stress case

Column 1 is labeled “breaches avoided” and filled with **euro** products. Column 2 is a point, not a range. False-alarm burden is omitted from cost. I-07 is again valued at €20,000.

Spillover is not the stated model. The prompt is ~0.01–0.03 on **each of six untreated diagnosis-stage cases per used slot**. The table applies 0.15 to I-06 and I-08 (I-08 is Triage), i.e. the cases’ **ARR** numbers reused as externalities. Optimistic spillover harm (€17,250) is **larger** than adverse (€5,750)—the scenarios are upside-down. Net figures do not match the rows: optimistic 25,950 − 1,800 − 17,250 = **6,900**, not 15,650; base is ~700, not 8,500. Adverse still gives every selected case a solid ARR, so it never implements “at least one selected case receives little or no benefit.” Ranges are treated as known causal effects, which the prompt forbids.

## Temporal policy is only half-right

I-05 vs I-07 is directionally correct and is the answer’s strongest qualitative point. The reusable machinery is weak. A 30-minute post-swarm cooldown is shorter than the 45-minute intervention. A 24-hour suppression can outlast the SLA (I-05 has 5 h left). Escalation at raw `p > 0.75` reintroduces the single-threshold rule the task told the author not to use. “Materially new event” is never defined beyond restating the I-07 fact pattern. Alert persistence on unchanged prefixes is not specified as a general control.

## Fairness and monitoring are topic-complete and mechanism-empty

The five required risk headings appear. The safeguards do not bind: an unspecified “fairness weight,” a “buffer slot” for NNLS after capacity is already over-committed, “limit spillover” with no instrument, and a holdout set that does not fix the fact that **treated potential outcomes are not the observational label**. The write-up never notices that *this* portfolio dumps congestion on untreated diagnosis cases (I-06, I-09), i.e. the imputed and NNLS cases. SME underprediction plus value-based ranking is exactly the starvation channel; the allocation does not show it was checked.

Monitoring is boilerplate (dashboard, quarterly retrain, chi-square, A/B). It does not confront scarce-slot interference, SUTVA/spillover, confounding of who gets a swarm, specialist preemption, or how one would know net impact is “consistently negative” without a counterfactual design (logging, shadow mode, stepped introduction, bounds). Quarterly cadence is slow relative to an already-broken Strategic Enterprise calibration after a workflow redesign.

## Net

Structure and two required qualitative refusals keep this off the floor. The deployable rule contradicts the table, calibration is reversed, I-07 is scored as I-09, four-plus-reserve exceeds four slots, and the scenario analysis both mis-specifies spillover and fails its own arithmetic. That is not a coherent early-warning policy.
