**3.5**

The write-up looks complete and professionally structured, but it is not a reliable analysis. Several required deliverables are missing or only simulated in prose, and the quantitative claims are internally inconsistent with both the prompt and the attached code.

**Required evidence is not actually produced.** Section 4 is a base-case scorecard, not a policy-by-scenario table. U1–U5 never receive comparable `mean_delay`, tail delay, SLA, utilization, fairness, handover, or resilience values. The headline numbers (Spec 45 / Pool 28 / Hybrid 32 minutes; P1 SLA 84 / 96 / 95; max util 98 / 88 / 85) are asserted without method, seed, or interval. They cannot be recovered from the Python, which has no queues, no calendars, and almost no policy differentiation.

**The load analysis contradicts the performance claims.** Raw diagnostic-plus-gate work is already on the order of total paid minutes (~2,490). After multipliers, handovers, setup, coordination, and ME/ES double-counting of resource-minutes, the system is tight or overloaded. That cannot coexist with Hybrid/Pool max utilization of 85–88% and ~30-minute mean sojourn. The phrase “150%–165% of single-resource capacity” also misstates the comparison: 2,490 already *is* the multi-resource day. Parallel ME/ES does not create capacity; it consumes two people at once. That is a core operations error.

**Skill, scenario, and preemption facts are wrong in places.** Ada has no Software skill, yet Section 2 lists Soft and Section 3 treats Ada as a cross-skilled retainer. Deepa is parked on the E/S team despite being the only afternoon mechanical-capable person besides a slow Farah who has already left. U4 applies only to *diagnostic* tail probabilities; the claim that the bottleneck “shifts to Validation” is not derived. U5 cannot produce “endless restart loops” because a case may be preempted at most once. U1’s +8 pp P1 share is not renormalized against P2/P3. Hybrid reservation is 25% in one sentence and 20% in the next.

**Time-block × skill offered load is mostly qualitative.** The prompt asked for approximate load by skill and block, using shifts and speed. Missing: Farah’s 08:00–12:00 exit, Emil’s 12:00 start, morning Software (only Chen at 1.25 and Farah at 1.40), Deepa’s 10:00 start, and explicit U2 renormalization. Those are the actual bottleneck mechanics.

**Section 8 fails the specification while presenting itself as a run.** The “simulation” assigns Intake to Ada/Ben, Mechanical to Deepa, other diagnostics to Chen, Validation always to Ben. There is no contention, breaks, overtime, setup, team-switch +5, preemption, or parallel/sequential choice. Policy name barely changes routing. U2 and U4 are not executed; U5 omits the IT outage, Chen’s full absence as a capacity object, and the extra morning P1 share. Case IDs restart at `C001` in every run, so concatenated logs smash policies and scenarios into the same cases. Timestamps are minute-of-day integers. The code never calls `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` or `pm4py.get_service_time`. Omitted mechanisms are not listed, which is the only condition under which a scaffold was allowed.

**What still works, and why that is not enough.** Headings match. Two objectives include tail/SLA and fairness/robustness. Three policies have dispatch language, not just labels. Secondary effects, monitoring attributes, and a cautious pilot are directionally right. Recommending Hybrid for robustness rather than base-case mean delay is the correct *shape* of answer. Under a strict bar those are table-stakes, not a high score: the analysis invents precision, mishandles capacity, and ships non-auditable code that cannot distinguish the policies it recommends.
