**7.2**

The final six-section write-up is a genuinely strong forecast: it uses the parallel **max** rather than a sum, respects weekend and lab-close pauses, mixes FIFO vs risk-priority, treats the temperature flag as a dependence (not an independence) problem, and keeps uncertainty in ranges. That is the right skeleton. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless.

**What is solid.** Incubation arithmetic is correct (Fri 14:00 + 46–54 h → Sun 12:00–20:00). Chemistry almost certainly cannot finish Friday (min 1.5 h vs a ~16:42–17:30 start). Sunday on-call readout does not pull the QA join earlier because QA is closed Sunday — that is the key calendar insight, and it is used. Multi-server FIFO is handled correctly (B-417 can start once it is next in line, not after every predecessor has finished). Scenario overlap is stated instead of forced to 100%. Lab investigation is identified as the multi-day tail. Shortcuts (same service law for queued chemistry samples; coarse QA clearing) are disclosed.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Earliest completion is not an earliest-path calculation.** The write-up takes micro readout at ~09:30 (the upper end of 0.5–1.5 h after 08:00) and QA service at the **median** 1.5 h, then calls Monday 11:00–12:00 the earliest plausible terminal. A lower-tail construction is Monday ~08:30 join, optimistic residual QA queue, sub-median review, +0.25 h release → more like **~09:30–11:00**. Using typical stage times for an earliest envelope is a methodological error on a required output.

2. **“Central” is internally inconsistent.** Smooth-path central is Monday 12:00–20:00 (~68.5–76.5 h), and that path is given 30–50% probability. The unconditional P50-like window is then Monday 18:00–Tuesday 18:00 (75–100 h). Those cannot both be “central” without explanation: if a third to a half of mass finishes Monday afternoon, the mixture median sits near Monday evening, and the lower edge of a typical-completion band should not start at 18:00. The summary therefore reads later than the scenario table it rests on.

3. **Percentile bands bleed into each other.** P80 is run out to Thursday 18:00 (150 h). A Monday join plus a documentation query (4–12 QA hours) or a documentation-only investigation (8–20 QA hours) is usually Tuesday–Wednesday. The 3–7 **calendar-day** laboratory investigation is a few-percent event on the normal path (investigation 0.04–0.10 × lab share 0.20–0.40). Putting Thursday in P80 and starting the “P90/severe” band at the same Thursday clock time flattens the tail. The task allows one P90-or-severe row; it does not excuse overlapping, poorly separated bands.

**Lesser issues (each enough to keep this out of the 8s under the stated rubric).** Queued chemistry samples are given B-417’s triangular law with little knock-on sensitivity in the scenario table. QA overdispersion is named and then replaced by a deterministic clearing story. Smooth-path probability is a product of almost-independent events, then dependence is invoked — both cannot fully apply. Rerun and contamination rows share the same Monday 11:00 earliest as the smooth path, so the table’s “earliest” column does not separate the scenarios. Documentation completeness 0.82 is unused (the query rate was already given, so this is a small omission). Clock conversions are consistently ~0.5–1.5 h sloppy (68–70 h vs 67.5–68.5 h, etc.).

**Next-event section.** First microbiology readout is a defensible *highest-value* observation (contamination vs not is a day-scale update). It is not the soonest informative event; Friday chemistry start/pause and the temperature-flag resolution are earlier. Naming the flag as secondary is acceptable, not optimal.

**Net.** This is the strong class of answer the rubric describes — micro/staffing dominate the smooth path; chemistry spillover, QA congestion, and correlated rework tails are all examined. It is not a 9–10: the earliest envelope, the P50 vs smooth-path mismatch, and the mushy P80/P90 cut are real, not cosmetic. **7.2.**
