**6.0**

The operational analysis in sections 1–7 is substantially stronger than the score suggests, but two hard defects keep it far from “nearly flawless,” which is the only bar for a high score under a strict reading.

**What is done well.** Estimands, units, and opening-hours convention are defined carefully. Load is treated as a median/p90 **proxy**, not utilization, and median service is never substituted for mean flow time. The primary segmentation is defensible: baseline weekday, baseline weekend, outage, outage recovery, high-complex backlog weekday, intervention weekday, and high-complex weekend. Recovery days are explicitly not treated as sustainable throughput. Most primary and pooled figures check out (e.g. pooled arrivals 7.50/h, completions 7.17/h, net +3.93/day, WIP 29.9; high-complex weekday net +13.43/day; intervention net −6.00/day; cumulative net +110). The `peak_08_10_pct` vs hourly-vector mismatch is a real data-quality finding. Little’s law is handled with unusual care: completions as the non-steady-state throughput proxy, opening-hours WIP that **misses overnight sojourn**, and a verdict matrix that withholds `applicable` where 07:00 backlog is unknown. Pooled vs segmented comparisons (weekday/weekend mix, case mix, outage/intervention) and the “cut weekend staff from pooled WIP” policy are on-target.

**Defects that dominate the grade.**

1. **Section 8 is incomplete.** The required Python block is cut off inside `summarize()`. It does not finish pooled/regime summaries, does not compute a serial-dependence indicator, does not implement a change-point heuristic, does not compute Little’s-law windows, does not include the required PM4Py comments, and does not print comparison tables. That is a failure of an explicit deliverable, not a cosmetic omission.

2. **Alternative B is arithmetically wrong.** “Post-intervention (2026-04-29 to 2026-05-03)” is labeled as 5 days but the totals match **7 days**: intervention weekdays **plus all four high-complex weekends, including Apr 25–26**. That produces bogus rates (arrivals 8.95/h, completions 9.05/h, WIP 39.0, net −1.2). Correct Apr 29–May 3 figures are about 7.38/h, 7.68/h, WIP 30.4, net −3.6/day. Pre-period totals are the complement of the same 7-day lump divided by 23, so both rows of that table are internally inconsistent. The staffing story is therefore quantitatively distorted.

3. **Several precise claims are not produced by the delivered artifact.** Lag-1 autocorrelations (~0.40 arrivals, ~0.60 WIP) and the pooled staff–WIP correlation (~0.65) are asserted without working code or a shown calculation. In a reproducibility-required task that is a real gap.

4. **Smaller unclarities.** Regime “median 25 min” is a mean of daily medians. The peak-bin mismatch is flagged but the plausible off-by-one (e.g. 09:00–11:00 on 2026-04-07 ≈ 31%) is not checked. Within-regime lag-1 is described as “weaker or negative” without numbers.

The process-mining reasoning is often excellent. Under a hypercritical standard, an unfinished required code section plus a wrong alternative-segmentation table are enough to block anything in the high band.
