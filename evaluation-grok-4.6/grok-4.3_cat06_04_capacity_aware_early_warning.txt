**4.8**

The write-up has the right *shape* (stage gate, I-03 excluded, I-05 ≠ I-07, segment calibration, spillover language, six required sections). That is not enough. As a deployable decision policy it is internally inconsistent, arithmetically wrong, and not the rule that produced the portfolio.

**The stated rule does not generate the stated actions.** Section 1’s score formula is missing operators (`…breach  €450  false-alarm…`, `if score  €2,500`), so it is not deployable as written. Interpreting the gaps charitably as the suggested EV formula still fails. Midpoint EVs are roughly I-01 €1.7k, I-02 €2.7k, I-04 €9.0k, I-07 €0.8k, I-08 €4.6k, I-09 €3.6k, I-06 €2.6k. A €2,500 cutoff would take I-04/I-08/I-09 (and borderline I-02/I-06) and would **reject I-01 and I-07**. The table does the opposite: it swarms I-01 and I-07 and discards I-08 and I-09. The printed scores (€3.8k, €2.9k, €4.1k, €2.7k, €1.4k, €1.9k…) are not recoverable from midpoints, bounds, calibration shifts, or the `/ (1 + spillover cases)` term. A priority score that cannot be audited is not a decision rule.

**Slot math is operationally impossible.** Four slots exist. Section 1 hard-reserves one slot, then Section 2 marks four cases **Intervene** *and* reserves one more. That is five claims on four slots. The impact table prices **4 × €450 = €1.8k**, i.e. no reserve was actually applied. Two specialists running four 45-minute swarms is also three hours of senior time plus the stated 8–20 minutes of queue delay *per slot*; that capacity hit is never treated as a constraint.

**Section 4 contradicts Section 2.** “Escalation to swarm is allowed only on the second qualifying alert” would block first-alert I-01 and I-04, both of which are selected. Cool-down and the I-05/I-07 distinction are fine; the escalation clause makes the temporal policy incompatible with the portfolio.

**Calibration is described and then unused.** Adding 0.065 / subtracting 0.085 from `p_now` never enters a score built on supplied ARR × value. Observational `p` is not the intervention effect; adjusting `p` and then ignoring it is cargo-cult robustification. The 0.10 NNLS “discount” is ambiguous (on `p`, on ARR, or on the score?). I-05 is “Yes but suppressed” even though the eligibility rule requires a first alert or a new event—so it should be **No**.

**I-08 vs I-07 is a value-of-information error, not conservatism.** I-08’s base EV is several times I-07’s. Lower-bounding I-08 because the effect band is 0.05–0.15 is defensible only if the same robust lens is applied everywhere; it is not. I-07 is a €5k SME case still in Triage with 1.5 h left. A 45-minute swarm does not obviously leave enough residual time for resolution work, so even a large ARR may not be *breach-relevant*. Selecting I-07 looks like rubric-satisfaction (show you read the new-log hint) rather than maximization or maximin of the stated objective.

**Section 3 is not auditable and overstates benefits.** Midpoints (0.265, 0.15, 0.19, 0.26) × values are **€18.8k**, not the claimed **€23.5k**. Optimistic direct benefit of €34–52k exceeds even upper-bound ARR × value (~€27k). Base “breaches avoided” of 0.6–0.8 sits below the midpoint sum 0.865. False-alarm burden on the selected set is **€2,900** (including I-04’s €2,000) and is omitted from the impact table despite being in the prescribed EV identity. Spillover euros assume an undeclared untreated-case value and appear to use 0.01–0.02 rather than the stated 0.01–0.03; “€5.4k on six untreated cases” collapses *per-slot* spillovers into a single six-case pool. After correcting the €23.5k error, base net is comfortably positive, so “marginally positive” does not match the author’s own (already inflated) numbers. Allocation is a single midpoint ranking; Adverse (“at least one selected case gets little or no benefit”) is reported after the fact, not used to choose a portfolio that remains acceptable when I-04/I-02 are near their lower bounds.

**Waiting vs intervening is under-argued.** I-04 is first-alert Strategic, 10 h out, overpredicted, €2,000 false-alarm cost—the option value of waiting is large and ignored. I-06’s jump may be imputation; waiting is reasonable, but then I-01 (7 h, still Triage) is rushed in on a first alert. I-09’s midpoint EV is among the best on the board; “wait for sparse calibration” needed an explicit robustness calculation, not a discounted fiction of €1,900.

**Fairness and monitoring are generic and partly wrong.** A 25% non-Strategic floor is a real safeguard, but it is not what produced this mix, and it can still starve SME/public cases inside the remaining 75%. “Retrain only on cases whose intervention status is recorded” does **not** fix selective-label bias; it merely tags the confounder. Matched observational contrasts on stage / segment / `p_now` remain confounded by the value-, burden-, and score-based policy itself. The 0.025 spillover tripwire sits inside the *expected* 0.01–0.03 per slot, so it would fire continuously under this allocation. Non-monetary public-sector / regulatory duties are reduced to a reserve that the portfolio does not actually keep. Specialist interruption (ordinary diagnosis stops) is not monitored as a first-class harm pathway.

The answer avoids the dumb failure modes (I-03-by-`p_now`, treating ARR as `p`, identical handling of I-05 and I-07). It fails the actual grading axes: a rule you can run, numbers you can reproduce, a feasible slot plan, and a portfolio that is robust under the three required scenarios rather than decorated with them.
