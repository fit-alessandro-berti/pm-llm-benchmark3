**7.1**

The final JSON is a strong, schema-compliant process-monitoring writeup: it does not copy the DFG, does not treat missing events as absent work, and consistently uses severity, object/lot relations, queues, and feed quality. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-semantics and next-event calibration errors keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is solid.** C6-A vs C6-B is handled as the prompt requires: same prefix, different obligations. High/injury/lot/similar-complaint structure correctly blocks Fast Close and opens TI, RA, and Supplier Analysis on C6-A; low/no-injury/single-device/duplicate structure correctly points C6-B at review then non-investigation closure. C6-C correctly keeps RA mandatory under the country rule and refuses to treat different lots as an automatic Supplier Analysis trigger. C6-D is the best case: worklist RA-open at 12:40, source attachment, and a migration-affected flag are used as evidence of latent work, not as log facts. C6-E correctly keeps a second Evidence Review in front, treats Supplier Analysis as mandatory for a six-complaint lot, and uses completeness 0.72 for another evidence cycle. Cross-case notes on DFG limits, starts-not-logged, and interval calibration are accurate.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Next-event vs. later-in-the-path are mixed.** Rank-2/3 items are often downstream outcomes that can appear next only if one or two predecessors are missing. On **normal-feed** cases the stated delay rate is 8–15%, but C6-A still puts roughly 0.07–0.25 / 0.05–0.22 on TI/RA completions as the *next* logged event, and C6-C puts 0.12–0.32 on Disposition and 0.05–0.20 on Authority Notification. Authority Notification as next event requires RA completion, a reportable finding, Disposition Recorded, and readiness for the 16:00 batch. That is a chain, not a next activity. Those intervals overweight observation error.

2. **`process_enabled` is inconsistent and too rigid on C6-A.** S4 and the DFG both allow Technical Investigation after triage and concurrent TI/RA. High severity, injury, and R-T4 make concurrent TI plausible *now*, even with no ER completion. Marking TI/RA `false` while marking C6-D `Evidence Received` `true` (a delayed past receipt, not a currently enabled next action) and C6-E Supplier Analysis `true` uses three different meanings of the flag.

3. **C6-B Fast Close as rank 3 is a control-flow miss.** Fast Close is licensed at S1 and appears in the DFG only after Initial Triage. After an evidence cycle the licensed terminal is Close — No Safety Issue. The writeup admits this and still ranks Fast Close above a further evidence request.

4. **C6-D predicts Evidence Received without Customer Evidence Requested.** The normative cycle is Requested → Received. Both are missing; if delayed events surface in order, Requested is the likelier next log line. The unknown is noted, then ignored in the ranking.

5. **C6-A drops Supplier Analysis from the top three** after correctly calling it mandatory. RA completion as immediate next event is weaker than that object-level obligation, given a 12-deep RA queue and 2–14 h post-assignment times.

**Smaller issues.** RA intervals on C6-C/D sit a bit high relative to queue depth and time since assignment (C6-D: opened 12:40, prediction time 14:00, typical completion ≥2 h). Residual mass on C6-A/B/E is tight (~0.93–0.95 on three options) for “honest” uncertainty. Product Hold/Recall is never treated as a live unknown on the high-severity common-lot case.

The piece is process-aware and usable. Under a hypercritical bar it is a good expert draft with several ranking and semantics errors, not a near-flawless answer.
