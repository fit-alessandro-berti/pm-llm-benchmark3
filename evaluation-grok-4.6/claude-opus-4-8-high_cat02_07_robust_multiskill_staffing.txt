The write-up is a serious, well-structured operations analysis with a real dispatcher, explicit penalty constants, and a clear specialization-vs-pooling argument. It is not close to flawless. Several results are overclaimed, one shock is described as simulated but not coded, and the SLA clock is defined incorrectly relative to the prompt.

### What is actually strong
Offered-load arithmetic in §2 is auditable and correct on the mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.8, Software 38.5, Validation 15.7) and on block totals (444/587/455/651/391/192). The afternoon capacity cliff is the right structural finding: 14:00–16:00 already has ρ≈1.08 before multipliers and breaks; after 16:00 raw ρ≈1.63 with two people; after 18:00 Mechanical has no eligible resource. Recommending hybrid **plus** an evening-roster fix, rather than pretending routing manufactures capacity, is the correct decision logic.

Policies are specified as dispatch rules, not slogans. Two objectives are defined, survivorship bias on delay is labelled, U3 is used as the real discriminator, and §§6–7–9 cover gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, interpretability, event attributes, and a P1-protected pilot. The Python scaffold encodes the tables, three policies, handover/setup/parallel/preemption constants, a sweep, an interval log, and the required PM4Py calls with case-level vs stage-level labels.

### Material errors (these cap the score)

**1. U5 is not the scenario that was run.** `outage=(570,600)` is unpacked and never referenced. U5 in code is U1+U3 only. The narrative still asserts a 09:30–10:00 Intake stoppage, an Intake backlog cascade, and U5 metrics as if that mechanism fired. That is a results-integrity failure, not a documented simplification.

**2. SLA definitions are wrong for P2/P3.** Unfinished at end-of-day is scored as a breach. P2 is 8 elapsed hours and P3 is 24 elapsed hours; a 16:30 P3 is allowed to finish the next morning. Same-day truncation makes completion and P2/P3 SLA look catastrophic for every policy and then uses those artefacts as discriminators. Documenting “no rollover” does not make the SLA clock match the prompt.

**3. The recommendation contradicts its own table.** §5 says hybrid is “top or joint-top … in every scenario” on P1 SLA. Table 4b shows pooling better on P1 in U4 (0.66 vs 0.63) and U5 (0.59 vs 0.57). Under the stated lexicographic O1, pooling wins those two shocks. Calling 0.57 vs 0.59 a statistical tie is possible; claiming uniform dominance is not.

**4. Setup time is applied to the wrong object.** The prompt adds 5 minutes when a resource switches diagnostic skill family **between consecutive cases**. The code also charges setup on Intake→diagnostic and on sequential ME/ES stages of the **same** case. Sequential same-resource ME is supposed to avoid cross-team cost; this implementation can add an intra-case setup the spec does not authorize.

**5. Preemption does not match the spec.** The limit is “a case may be preempted at most once.” The code marks **resources** in `preempted_once`, so a resource is preemptable once per day while a case can be hit again on another resource. Preemption is also allowed on Intake/Validation, not only a “lower-priority diagnostic activity,” and the 10% penalty is deferred onto the next stage rather than restarting the interrupted activity.

**6. Hybrid reservation: prose ≠ code.** §3/§5 say reserved capacity is released when no P1 is waiting (or after 20 minutes with no P1 wait). The dispatcher holds the reserved person whenever **any other eligible resource is free**, even if no P1 exists. That is a different policy from the one recommended.

### Additional accuracy and clarity problems
- U1 “+8 percentage points” is implemented as P1 += 8 with P2/P3 unchanged, then renormalized (10/55/35 → 18/55/35 → effective P1 ≈ 16.7%, about +6.7pp).
- §2 ρ is total minutes over total on-shift minutes, not skill-feasible capacity. Software after 16:00 is only Emil; Mechanical after 16:00 is only Deepa; after 18:00 Mechanical is zero. The text notices thin pools but still leans on aggregate ρ.
- Realized utilization of completed work (max ≈ 0.77–0.83) is treated as satisfying the “planned utilization ≤ 90%” rule. Abandoned afternoon work never hits the clock; planned load is already above 1.0.
- U2 is described as pooling spreading electrical “across five resources”; after 12:00 Farah is gone, so four. Completion 0.50→0.51 is also unsurprising because U2 renormalizes mix and does not increase arrivals — over-explained as a pooling win.
- Strict eligibility excludes Ada from Mechanical (multiplier 1.00) and Deepa from Electrical (1.00). That is a legal policy, but it stacks the baseline toward collapse under U3 and is never justified as the natural specialist assignment.
- Table 4a parentheticals mix worst-shock, upper bounds, and Jain floors without a defined scale. Seed ±sd exists in the printer and is omitted from the required table, so uncertainty is not actually explicit there.
- Parallel ME/ES still pays +8 coordination if the second resource never appears, i.e. intended-parallel, executed-serial.
- Required output is “exactly these top-level headings.” The submission is wrapped in lab-note preamble and a reprise after §9.

### What this means for the grade
The core process-mining judgment is right: do not pick a policy on base-case mean delay; the roster is infeasible after 16:00; pooling beats brittle queues when Chen vanishes; delay among completers is a biased KPI. That is why this is not a 4–5. Hypercritical scoring still has to charge the unimplemented outage presented as evidence, the wrong SLA horizon, the O1 overclaim, and the setup/preemption/reservation mismatches. Those are not nits. A near-flawless answer would have matched spec mechanisms to code, reported U5 only as coded, and aligned the recommendation with the table.
