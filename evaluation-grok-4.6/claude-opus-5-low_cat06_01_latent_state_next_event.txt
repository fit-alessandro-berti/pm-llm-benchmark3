Under a strict reading of the required next-event task, several predictions are sequential forecasts mislabeled as competing next observations, and the required enabled-versus-observed distinction is applied inconsistently.

**Core logical flaw: “next” vs “next-after-next.”**  
Ranks are supposed to be alternative *next* log rows, not the second and third steps of a preferred path. The answer itself admits the opposite in places. C6-E rank 2 says a third evidence request “ranks second rather than first only because it must be preceded by a review completion.” C6-A rank 3 says a second evidence request “can only be logged after a review completion.” C6-B rank 2 is Close “directly after review.” C6-C rank 3 is a *second* technical completion justified by a DFG edge that fires *after* Regulatory Assessment Completed. Those are suffix events, not rival next events. Dressing them as a top-three next-event distribution inflates residual-looking mass onto outcomes that are almost definitionally blocked until rank 1 occurs.

**`process_enabled` is not applied as a single rule.**  
C6-D rank 3 correctly marks Disposition Recorded false because rule 4 still blocks it. C6-B rank 3 correctly marks Fast Close false as a control-flow deviation. The same logic is not used for C6-B rank 2 (Close before a visible review), C6-A rank 3, or C6-E rank 2. The calibration note even explains the flag well, then the case-level JSON contradicts that explanation. That is a direct miss on required item 6, not a cosmetic labeling issue.

**C6-C’s ranks 2–3 are the weakest block.**  
“Supplier Analysis Completed (or the first visible sign of a newly opened supplier obligation)” is not one logged activity. Completions are what this log records; openings are not. Rule 5 is also not cleanly triggered (shared failure mode across *different* lots). Giving that bundle 0.10–0.22 as the *next* row is poorly calibrated against a 6–36 h supplier cycle and an uncertain obligation. Rank 3 then leans on RA→Technical Investigation as if it supported a *prior* reopened technical completion. Two of three C6-C predictions are therefore underspecified or historically misread.

**Smaller factual and calibration slips (each would already block a high score).**  
“356 of the 412-ish evidence cycles” conflates Initial Triage→Customer Evidence Requested with Evidence Received→Review; the table gives no Received denominator. High-severity overtaking is stated for the *technical* queue, not Evidence Review; C6-A overclaims a review-queue pull-forward. C6-C is not “at the boundary” of the 48 h delay window; previous-day 15:10 is plainly inside it (as is C6-E). C6-D lists “probably” missing evidence events under `completed_obligations`, mixing confirmed and inferred work. Midpoints for C6-A (~0.89) and C6-B (~0.95) exceed the write-up’s own 0.7–0.85 coherence claim. C6-D’s 0.15–0.30 backfill range sits above the stated 8–15% delay rate without a tight enough mapping from “some event is late” to “the next analytical row is a backfill.”

C6-E’s low-severity label is correctly overridden by the six-complaint lot. C6-D is the best single case: worklist evidence, migration backfill as an *observation* target, and a clean enabled/observed split on disposition. Queue load, the 16:00 batch, and DFG limitations (concurrency flattening, unconditional counts, missing supplier obligation, load-blind medians, observation process vs process) are handled at a high level. Intervals are wide on purpose and usually avoid fake precision.

Those strengths are why this is clearly a strong attempt rather than a weak one. They do not cancel the next-event category error, the inconsistent `process_enabled` flags, or the C6-C specification/calibration problems. On a rubric that reserves high scores for nearly flawless work, that caps the grade in the mid-6s.
