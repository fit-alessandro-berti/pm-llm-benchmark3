**7.5**

The submission is a strong board-level review: it separates use cases, refuses to average incomparable metrics, reasons from exact process-tree languages, and proposes a genuinely better hybrid than either original. It is not nearly flawless. Several defects sit in the centerpiece (the third model and its checker), and a hypercritical reading has to treat those as more than nits.

**What is actually good.** Sections 1–6 and 10–11 are the best parts. Use-case trade-offs are stated clearly. Assumption A1 (exact acceptance ≠ nonzero-cost alignment) is the right interpretive rule and is applied consistently to B1–B8. Model S’s admitted traces are correct, and the intentional-vs-accidental split is right: optional Supplier is defensible; optional Credit/Technical and pre-check Expedite are not. Model C is correctly diagnosed as a frozen variant catalog; B2 and B5 are genuine counterexamples, not rhetorical ones. The pathology ranking leads with the two decision-reversing issues (fitness-by-optionalizing-controls; positive-log precision). The dissent is a real F7/F8 argument, not a token caveat, and it sits in the 150–250 band.

**Concrete errors and logical gaps.**

1. **Policy checker contradicts the audit table and its own dictionary.** `missing_attribute_verdict` is `data_incomplete_not_compliant_by_default`, but the function returns `conditional_needs_data_or_review` whenever `external_internal` is absent — including when Supplier Review already occurred. Presence of Supplier never violates P3. Section 8 still scores B5/B7 as accepted/valid. The implemented semantics and the claimed semantics are not the same artifact.

2. **The backbone overconstrains correction timing and does not say so where it matters.** Correct Dossier is allowed only after *both* initial checks. A routine path — Credit fails, Correct Dossier, Credit repeats, then Technical Review — is rejected. P2 only requires both checks before approval; P6 does not require both checks to finish before any correction. The limitations table mentions corrections after Supplier/Expedite, not this more plausible early-rework case. For a model sold as the policy candidate, that is a material omission.

3. **Post-approval leakage in the checker.** Exactly-one approval and last-activity = Release do not catch `…, Manager Approval, Supplier Review, Release Order`. Expedite-after-approval is flagged; other post-approval reviews are not. The tree would reject this, so the “explicit semantics” layer is strictly weaker than the tree it is supposed to police.

4. **Validation does not operationalize the paper’s own acceptance definition.** Section 5 insists on exact language membership; the skeleton runs `fitness_alignments` on the negative suite. A correct rejector can still post high partial fitness. There is no cost-zero / `check_is_fitting` / play-out membership helper, despite that being the decision rule in §10 (“accepts B3 or B4 exactly”).

5. **No pre-mortem of the third model’s own metric profile.** An unbounded `*(tau, Correct+recheck)` loop plus optional Supplier/Expedite will look worse than C on positive-log precision and worse than S on fitness if early corrections or missing-check interleavings exist. A skeptical reviewer should have predicted that and said which metrics they are willing to lose.

6. **Smaller slips.** Token-based generalization is treated a bit too much like “language size”; it is mostly a rare-transition replay statistic, which is why C’s duplicate labels crush it. S’s 0.992 training fitness is never reconciled with the complete absence of Correct Dossier (either corrections are rare, or alignments are hiding them). `case_features["check_order"]` uses first `.index()`, which is wrong on rework cases — the very cases they want to stratify. A4 does not distinguish the third model from S or C.

**What is not wrong.** Probe matrix S/C columns are accurate. A1/A2/A5 are good distinguishing traces. The process-tree loop `*(tau, redo)` is the standard “zero or more” encoding. Mandatory checks are not silently optionalized. No fabricated numbers. Format matches the required H1–H11.

**Net.** This would be useful in front of a governance board and is well above a generic metric-worship review. It is not a 9+: the third alternative is the assigned original work, and that is where the specification, the checker, and the evaluation story drift apart.
