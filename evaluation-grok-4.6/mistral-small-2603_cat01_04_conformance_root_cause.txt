The June–July fitness drop from 93% to 32% is treated as if it already supports logging hypotheses and weakens real change. The drop is the *explanandum*. By itself it supports every hypothesis.

### PM4Py section (largest single failure)
This was specified as nine concrete obligations. The code misses most of them and would not run.

1. It does **not** expand variants with `pm4py.parse_event_log_string(...)`. It invents `pm4py.objects.log.log.EventLog`, `pm4py.objects.log.trace.Trace`, and `pm4py.objects.log.util.event_generator.apply`.
2. The same `Trace` object is appended `count` times, so later mutation aliases every copy.
3. `normalize_log` does `trace.remove(event)` while iterating — a classic skip/corruption bug — and `log.copy()` is not a deep copy of events.
4. Unused, irrelevant imports (`dataframe_utils`, `generic_tree`, pandas).
5. Alignment “fitness” is a homemade `trace_is_fit` ratio, not alignment-cost fitness; mean alignment cost is never computed.
6. Source-sequence ordering is absent.
7. **Precision is never computed**, despite an explicit compare-fitness-and-precision requirement.
8. Caveats are a comment, not printed.
9. The candidate tree places `?('Supervisor Sign-off')` **after `Notify`**. The log puts sign-off *between* review/compliance and `Decision`. That model cannot produce the 66% → 86% movement in the table. It also still cannot encode the EUR 50,000 guard — which is the one caveat they were supposed to *print*.

A control-flow optional sign-off that is also in the wrong place is worse than an overpermissive model: it is the wrong language and the wrong location.
