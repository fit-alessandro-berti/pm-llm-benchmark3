**5.4**

The final write-up gets the policy call right (`conditional go`, do not copy blanket parallelization, keep statutory gates, start with digital/no-originals cases) and treats Site A’s 9-hour median drop as confounded. That is necessary, not sufficient. Under a strict reading of the spec, the answer has multiple material defects.

**Format and completeness.** Section 2’s prose is well under the required 180–280 words and is mostly a restatement of the adjacency list. Section 5 never supplies the required mapping from Site A metrics and Site B constraints to each numeric range; the table is assertion, not derivation. Several tables lost operators (`41h 32h`, `Identity  Document`), which makes the structural comparison harder to audit than it should be.

**Process structure and unused evidence.** The comparison table covers the mandated features and correctly flags originals gating, role split, locking, mix shift, and timestamp artefact. It does almost nothing with Site B’s DFG: 2,050 `Register → Identity Check`, 1,860 `Register → Originals Receipt`, 1,160 `Identity Check → Document Check`, 360 supplement loops. Those frequencies already show partial de-facto concurrency and a non-trivial legal loop; a transport argument that ignores them is analogically thin. `S_legal_requirement → legal_capacity` also conflates a statutory branch with a staffing node.

**Quantitative and pilot design.** Scenario ranges are directionally plausible, including one median-up / tail-down case, but they are not tied to the 18→12h wait, service-time moves, 62% originals, or 40% legal share. The pilot asks for 800 cases per arm per stratum in eight weeks. Site B completes 3,400 cases in six months (~130/week), so that design is infeasible. Case-level randomization is not reconciled with team-level staggered rollout.

**Model fidelity (section 8) is the largest failure.** Site A trees use underscored labels, not the given activity names. `core` and `legal_branch` (`Xor`/`Loop`) are built and discarded. The converted model is a flat `PartialOrder` that (i) forces `Assessment → Legal` for every case, (ii) orders `Legal` before `Approve`, `Reject`, *and* `Request Supplement` as if they were concurrent successors rather than exclusive paths, and (iii) introduces `RequestSupp → DocCheck` while `DocCheck → Assessment → Legal → RequestSupp` already exists, so the “strict partial order” is cyclic. Imports and conversion (`PartialOrder`/`Activity`, `powl.importer.apply`) do not match standard PM4Py POWL (`StrictPartialOrder`, `Transition`/`OperatorPOWL`, `convert_to_petri_net`). There is no `play_out` and no `check_is_workflow_net`. Comments on what the net cannot encode are fine; the executable model does not implement the required optional legal branch and supplement loop.

**What remains salvageable.** Co-interventions are listed and not treated as treatment. Eligibility, WIP caps, and logging in section 6 are the right intervention shape. Section 9’s transfer / do-not-copy / expand-modify-abandon logic is coherent. The assumption register is complete and testable.

Those strengths do not offset a non-compliant word budget, missing scenario reasoning, unused DFG, an impossible pilot N, and a POWL model that violates the partial-order and choice/loop requirements the question exists to test.
