**6.5**

The answer is a strong, well-structured process-level portfolio brief that hits the required seven sections, resists frequency-first ranking, defers the activation bot, and consistently treats controls, exceptions, and shifted demand. It is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading the factual slips, ranking inconsistencies, and the year-1 omission of the problem’s own low-frequency bottleneck keep it well below the top band.

**What works.** The matrix covers all twelve candidates with the required columns and qualitative ratings grounded in waits, DFG counts, pools, and control findings. A1 is correctly treated as a high-frequency trap (back-office slack after 15:00; accelerating intake into 9 analysts and 3 specialists). A11 is correctly last: upstream signals are unreliable (6.5% provisional signing, 8.5% contract remediation). Dependencies are real (A8 GIGO, A6 needs A5, A3 flooding a 3-FTE hub, two deep-integration slots assigned to A9/A10). P1/P2/P3 match the requested archetypes and state sacrifices. Effects cite specific loops (Request→Doc review 2,800; Resolve→KYC 1,160; Contract→Remediate 650). Risks cover audit trails, FN/FP, drift, exceptions, and four-eyes. The A1 argument in §7 is the best part of the piece.

**Inaccuracies.** A9 is not the “largest back-end labor pool”: credit analysis (8,900 × 110 min), document review (9,200 × 45), and normalization (5,200 × 75) all dominate 7,600 × 35. Calling the 52% doc loop and 8.5% contract loop the “two highest-volume rework loops” conflates rate with volume; Resolve→KYC (1,160) exceeds the 510–650 contract loop. Treating the back-office queue as purely an arrival-pattern artifact overreads “utilization <65% after 15:00”—morning congestion can still be real. Gate numbers (loop <25%, remediation 4%, signing p50 ≤24h, 95% catch, <2% extraction error, 70–85% utilization) are invented precision after the brief asked for qualitative ratings, not unsupported ROI-style targets.

**Ranking vs stated criteria.** The header weights critical-path/waiting impact, then ranks A4 sixth and A7 tenth. A4 is 31h wait, 38% rework, on the critical path in 82% of cases where present, and blocks KYC *and* signing in a 3-FTE specialist hub—the brief’s exhibit for “frequency is misleading.” A7 is the longest wait in the table (58h) and a hard synchronization point for 71% of equipment finance. First-year feasibility is allowed to swamp the stated process-level criterion; the table’s own “process-level benefit” column does not match rank order.

**Logical gap in the recommended set (A5+A6+A9+A10).** Speeding the document/normalization track and installing a hard KYC-before-sign gate *increases* the cost of leaving entity mismatch unaddressed: ~1,750 cases will fail the A10/A9 gates and sit on the 31h mismatch queue. That interaction is barely acknowledged (“watch KYC arrival rate,” wave-2 A3+A4). P4 therefore underplays the problem’s thesis and can shift, not remove, the bottleneck. P2 (A3/A4/A6/A7) is the better bottleneck design but fails the “at least one module strengthens controls, not only labor” constraint unless audit logging is stretched into a control module—the answer notes this but still offers P2 as a first-year alternative.

**Thinner than the artifacts support.** Micro / SME / high-exposure and product splits are mostly unused (collateral 71% vs 18%, renewal remediation 14%, weekly committee for high exposure). KYC ∥ document review concurrency is a Phase 0 logging caveat, not a design choice (early parallel start, early valuation trigger via A2). Four-eyes (5.8% at month-end) is waved through as “smoother flow reduces pressure” with enforcement parked in A8/A12. Committee packaging’s 26h wait and 15% artifact rework are correctly labeled GIGO and then dropped.

**Rollout.** Phase order A5 → A6 assist → A9 → A10 respects the two-slot rule in spirit; A10 at months 6–10 sits on the edge of “first six months.” Assist-mode A6 with “no core writes” is a convenient assumption, not evidence. Wave-2 sequencing (A4+A3, then A2/A7/A8/A12, A1 last, A11 only after clean control signals) is coherent.

**Net.** Portfolio reasoning, dependency talk, and the case against frequency-first are real. The recommended four-pack is a volume×wait hybrid that sidesteps the brief’s central low-frequency synchronization example, contains several overclaims, and substitutes made-up SLOs for qualitative judgment. That is a good answer with material defects, not a near-flawless one.
