# Assignment errors and internal contradictions

**C010 vs. the author’s own bottleneck rule.** Authorized Approval finalizers in this batch are Bruno (overtime-blocked), Eva (2), and Farid (3). C010 is normal, complexity 0.38, 20h remaining. Component **B** says not to spend scarce specialist capacity on work others can safely do. The table still gives C010 to Farid, who already has C002 (urgent, 2h) and C011 (urgent, 4h) plus C003 trainee work, at **4/4** commitments and 6.5h “remaining.” Section 4 then admits Approval is concentrated on Farid. That is not a disclosed trade-off with a reason; it is the policy refusing to apply itself. Eva at two high-priority cases is loaded, but C010 does not need same-day specialist attention. This is the clearest logical flaw.

**C001 vs. constraint 5.** High complexity, high handover, owner Celine at Security **1**, and Gita/Hugo supervision **is** feasible. Constraint 5 prefers continuity unless skill is 0/1 **and** supervision is not feasible. Constraint 6 also allows skill 1–2 under Gita/Hugo. The author overrides both with the case note “needs specialist,” while simultaneously citing the 2.7h median extra wait against a 3h SLA. The load argument that *would* justify the transfer—Celine must protect C007 (high, 6h, already 3 reassignments)—is not used as the main reason. The assignment may be operationally right; the constraint reasoning is not.

**Farid’s plan is not shown to be feasible.** C002 (2h) and C011 (4h) plus C010 plus C003 mentoring, on top of 5 open cases, is treated as a “joint scheduling” footnote rather than a hard capacity check. The author correctly says hours remaining are not uncommitted capacity, then still publishes the table as the recommendation. That undercuts Section 3.

**C003 is a strained 20% hack.** Moving a twice-reassigned diagnosis loop to a third owner, then parking a trainee on it, collides with the supplied 1.8×/1.4× evidence. The author flags this, which is honest, but still does it to hit 22.2%. Given C010/C012 were not used as safer development vehicles (C010 prepare-and-finalize; C012 is labeled trainee-suitable), the training story is narrower than the rhetoric.

Minor assignment nits: C013 (72h, low) as Jonas’s fourth commitment is unnecessary tightness; C012 is left unused for development with only a weak explanation once Bruno is excluded.

# Policy / scoring gaps

The math in Section 2 is not what produced Section 3. \(F^{90}\), slack, \(U_i\), and \(J_{ia}\) have no fitted or even stipulated weights, and slack is then declared unknown. That is intellectually honest, but it means the required “skill-match / SLA / continuity / fairness / training / resilience scoring” is a framework, not an algorithm that another dispatcher could rerun on this snapshot. Tie-break order is specified; dispatch order for C001–C016 is not.

Urgency \(U_i\) can let the SLA term (0–4) swamp encoded priority (1–4) depending on \(b\); that is a calibration landmine, not a derived property of the process.

A1’s 0.75 complexity cutoff is arbitrary (disclosed) and then used as if it were the definition of constraint 5’s “high-complexity” set. C003 at 0.66 is thereby excluded from continuity protection while being the case most likely to suffer a third reassignment.

# Comparison and explainability

Section 4 is conceptually right—raw first-available is illegal; specialist-only starves continuity and overuses Farid—but it never instantiates the two baselines on C001–C016. The dimensional table is hypothetical, which the author says, yet the question asked for a comparison on this queue, not only a methodology for a future simulation. Residual bottlenecks (Gita/Hugo, Farid, Celine) are identified; the C010 recommendation then aggravates one of them.

Section 5 is good and usable. It still omits the two facts workers would need: Farid is holding both urgent approvals/negotiations, and C001 is a high-cost owner change against a 3h clock.

# Audit and PM4Py

Section 6 is strong. A few unclarities remain: no numeric alert thresholds; “comparable adjacent-event pairs” is undefined; handover-network \(\beta=0\) is not justified. The insistence on an assignment ledger is correct and necessary.

Section 7 is the right shape: separate execution vs. decision vs. outcome views, no fake `"Amal+Celine"` resource, no invented log from the snapshot, whole-case filtering, overlap guard before `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, Quality batches detected then checked against policy rather than trusted. Strict issues:

- `discover_performance_dfg(..., perf_aggregation_key="median")` is very likely the wrong public parameter (aggregation lives on visualization / internal DFG stats; the public discovery helper is typically mean-oriented and does not take that kwarg).
- `get_service_time` defaulting to the completion key is directionally right as a gotcha, but stated more categorically than API versions warrant.
- Role discovery / similarity are correctly subordinated to the skill matrix—this is a real plus.
- The sketch audits after the fact; it does not “learn” the policy beyond a short closing paragraph on leakage-safe timing models.

# Net

This is a high-end, explainable, socio-technical design with unusual epistemic hygiene. Strictly, it loses a lot of points because the published queue plan violates the author’s bottleneck principle (C010), strains constraint 5 and the 3h SLA (C001), and never actually computes the scores it asks workers to trust. Those are not formatting nits; they are the decision the rest of the document exists to justify.
