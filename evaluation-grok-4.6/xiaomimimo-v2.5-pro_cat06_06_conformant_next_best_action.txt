**6.6**

The final six-section writeup is a competent, correctly oriented answer: it puts an independent second-officer path first, keeps A and B at the bottom as hard nonconformant shortcuts, treats rescreening as information rather than a substitute for review, and takes P-901 seriously. That is the core of what the rubric rewards. It is not close to flawless.

The most damaging error is repeated misuse of the prompt’s **0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval** range as **deadline-compliance** probability (table, traces, and cost arithmetic). Those quantities are different. Action C’s stated times are 35–75 minutes of review plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute, i.e. about 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. Starting S2 now is therefore a *strong* on-time path (most of that interval still finishes inside the SLA). Recycling 0.55–0.75 systematically understates Plan 1’s timeline reliability and makes the “expected deadline loss” figures in §3 unreliable. Calling a 75-minute review the “majority of the distribution” is also wrong: 75 minutes is the *upper* bound of the given range.

A second clear quantitative slip is in the dominance argument for Action A: operational benefit is written as **€0**. Immediate execution is the action that *captures* the €20k–€35k deadline value; the right claim is that regulatory tail risk still dominates that benefit, not that the benefit is zero. Treating regulatory realization as probability 1.0 in the same calculation is too strong (the later 5% sensitivity remark only partly repairs this).

Several design and labeling problems keep the answer well below an 8:

- Plan names are garbled (`Composite Plan **`, `Plan `), so the required table is harder to read than it should be.
- The T+40 fallback to S2 is presented as useful optionality, but after a 40-minute wait S2 still needs ~40–90 minutes. That fallback almost never saves P-884’s deadline; it is mainly a late, still-disruptive way to get a review.
- S4 is assigned the same 35–75 minute review duration as S2’s *expedited interrupt* without stating that this is an assumption. Even if S4 arrives at T+35, 35 + 35–75 + 5–15 = 75–125 minutes, so “swap to S4 as rank 1 if available at 35 minutes” overstates S4’s competitiveness on P-884’s clock.
- Action F is ranked above unambiguously conformant Action E. That can be argued, but the table does not weigh hard enough that F’s conformance is *unsettled*, that approval after proper review is the modal outcome (0.55–0.75), and that false-rejection harm is €15k–€80k.
- Continuations under-specify Rule 8 branches (hold / SAR). SAR non-disclosure (Rule 11) is ignored even while the fallback tells the customer about delay. The 12-minute-stale risk score is almost unused.

What remains good should still be counted: exact conformance labels; A/B correctly blocked from being “saved” by a favorable forecast; four-eyes and override preconditions stated accurately; parallel screening + S2 as a real composite with sequence and concurrency; API quota and S4-timing sensitivity treated as first-class; transportability of observational predictions flagged; primary recommendation still the right *family* of action.

Net: a strong process-monitoring answer with the right ranking skeleton and several material reasoning/clarity defects. Under a strict bar that is a mid-6, not an 8+.
