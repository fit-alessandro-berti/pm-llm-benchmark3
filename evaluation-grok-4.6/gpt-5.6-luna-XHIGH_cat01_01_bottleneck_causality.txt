**What it gets right.** Calculations I audited (group means, stratum gaps −2.14/−2.21/−3.43, wait 12.80 vs 8.79, service 7.04 vs 5.04, day/night 13.98 vs 21.74, load 20.03 vs 14.52, compliance 26.78 vs 11.95, rework cycle splits, appeal rates) match the case file. **Verdict is slightly misaligned with the analysis.** “Partly causal through mechanisms” at 58 sits awkwardly next to repeated statements that the contrast is not identified. That rubric label more naturally means a real group effect operating through mediators. The prose mostly supports a weaker claim: the crude gap is mainly case mix, night/load, and compliance composition, with a possible residual skill or queue-handling effect that cannot be isolated. “Not identifiable” or “mainly confounded” would have matched the evidence more tightly. The executive also never names total versus direct versus mediated estimands in one place, even though the DAG and tables imply the distinction.

**Section 4 column misuse.** Several “causal interpretation allowed” cells only restate associations (“the completed-case sample has longer Orion cycle times,” “night operations … are associated with longer cycles”). That blurs the column’s job. The “not allowed” cells are generally correct.

**DAG is serviceable but not clean.** Direct `complexity → cycle_time` is redundant if wait, service, and rework are the components. `unobserved_case_risk → resource_group` duplicates `risk → routing_rule → resource_group` without saying what residual assignment remains. There is no `resource_group → shift` despite Orion being a night-coverage bundle, and no `arrival_load → review_service`. Those are real structural ambiguities, not just omitted decoration.

**Hypothesis 4 has inverted evidence polarity.** Near-identical means among compliance-required cases (26.69 vs 27.00) and matched high-complexity rework rates (50% / 50%) *support* a composition or mediation story; they do not contradict it. Contradicting evidence would be residual group gaps after fixing complexity and branch, or group affecting `compliance_required` after complexity is held fixed. Ranking a “genuine Orion harm” hypothesis first is also poorly motivated once every stratum reverses.

**Overlap is underplayed in the decision-facing sections.** Almost no `complexity × shift` cell has both groups with \(n \ge 3\). The only two-sided cells are sparse (e.g., Low/Day 8 vs 1; High/Night 1 vs 6). The code warns about this; the executive still leans on stratum gaps of 2–3 hours as “strongest counter-evidence.” Those gaps are directionally interesting and statistically fragile.
