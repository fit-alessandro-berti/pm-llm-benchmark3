7.2

The answer is a strong, format-compliant advisory memo with real process-level reasoning, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has one material factual error at the center of the recommendation, several overprecise and internally tense effect claims, and a recommended year-one portfolio that underweights the problem’s own low-frequency bottleneck.

**What it does well.** The seven required sections are present and used as an argument, not a template dump. The matrix is ranked by leverage rather than frequency; A8 and A11 are correctly treated as downstream and unsafe until artifacts and controls are reliable; A5 is paired with A6 because the resource network warns that cleaner inflow will overload the 9-FTE analyst pool; A9/A10 are used to satisfy the “at least one control-strengthening module” constraint; the two-deep-integration cap is operationalized rather than mentioned. DFG facts are generally used accurately: the 3,300→2,800 missing-document loop, Resolve→KYC 1,160, Resolve→Contract 380, Wait-for-valuation→Packaging drop-off (~370), Classify→KYC 7,250 vs Classify→Doc review 3,940 concurrency, and population-weighted waits that correctly put signing (7,300 × 44h) ahead of valuation and credit analysis. Compensating controls (audit trail of suggestion/override/decision, hard gates, WIP caps, drift sampling, exception budgets) match the control findings. P1–P3 plus a recommended P4 is a legitimate portfolio structure, and the “frequency is a numerator” framing is the right conceptual move.

**Material inaccuracy: Receive is not Register.** A1 is auto-registration (11,880 cases, 8 min, 1.5h wait, back office). The 72% digital / 3 min / 0.2h figures belong to Receive application. The matrix already mixes them (“11,880 × 8 min ≈ 0.8 FTE but only 28% of cases are non-digital”). Section 7 then collapses the two activities and dismisses the “highest-frequency” target as “28% of 3-minute tasks” with a 0.2h wait. That is the climactic trade-off argument, and it is factually wrong. Registration’s actual weighted wait (11,880 × 1.5h) is not trivial; the *valid* reasons to defer A1 are back-office slack after 15:00 and downstream overload, not Receive’s metrics. The conclusion survives; the evidence offered for it does not.

**Recommended portfolio vs. the problem’s own thesis.** The prompt exists to punish frequency-only ranking and to force attention to low-frequency synchronization points. Resolve entity mismatch (1,750 cases, 31h wait, 38% exception rate, on the critical path in 82% of occurrences, 3 FTE with two-senior concentration) is the canonical example. P2 includes A4; P4 does not. Deferral is argued (coverage limits, need to co-design with A3, compliance modules crowding the four-slot budget), but year-one mitigations—“interim triage rules and cross-training”—are unspecified and do not substitute for a module. Leaving the scarcest internal pool untouched while automating portal nudges and e-sign is a defensible constraint-driven choice; it is not a choice the write-up interrogates hard enough given how heavily the artifacts spotlight that queue. Four-eyes (5.8% High-exposure, month-end) is likewise monitored, not designed against, with A12 only as a pull-forward.

**Overprecise effects and hidden tensions.** Section 4 assigns P4 point targets (signing 44h → high-20s, loop-back 85% → <60%, normalization 75 → 25–30 min, remediation 650 → <300, mismatch 8.5% → <4%) that are not derived from the table. A6’s labor claim is also light: 5,200 × 75 min is ~6,500 hours, closer to 3.5–4 FTE than “~3.” P3 correctly notes that a hard KYC-before-signing gate can *lengthen* measured signing waits for stuck cases; P4 claims both zero provisional signing and a large signing-wait cut without reconciling that tension. A10 is quietly rewritten from “e-sign + reminders” into the KYC hard-gate; that is good design, but it is a reinterpretation, not the candidate as specified.

**Thinner variant and dependency work than the artifacts support.** Micro vs SME vs High-exposure paths, committee skip (credit analysis 8,900 vs committee 3,900; decision→contract 3,650 vs contract 7,600), and equipment vs working-capital collateral rates are used as asides, not as portfolio design constraints. The dependency column sometimes lists downstream effects (A1 “feeds A2”) rather than prerequisites. A7’s year-two coupling to A2 is right; the ~19% late external valuations and partner-quality disputes are under-developed as residual risk in P4.

**Verdict.** This is competent process-mining portfolio advice with the right instincts: do not automate the visible queue first, do not stand up A11/A8 on dirty controls, bind A5 to A6, spend scarce core slots on A6 and a control gate, keep humans on judgment and four-eyes. Strictly graded, the Receive/Register conflation in the final argument, the optimistic numeric effects, and the year-one evasion of the specialist bottleneck keep it well short of excellent.
