**7.2**

This is a strong, format-compliant causal-policy writeup with real process literacy. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, quantitative presentation errors and a late overclaim are enough to keep it well below the top band.

### What is done well
The answer understands the actual design problem: endogenous selection of weak, high-volume branches; staggered availability that is not the same as use; rising `automated_fast_path_pct` on top of a pre-existing 4–6% fast path; and a rollout that is entangled with the fraud desk, routing, staffing, and the May complexity shock. Section 1 stays non-causal and correctly separates availability, assignment, completed fast-path, and work version. The estimand table is the best part of the memo: ITT availability, intensity, route assignment, rework, escalation, CSAT, dynamics, transport, and fraud-desk interference are all specified, and every row is honestly marked unidentifiable from the supplied panel. The identification discussion correctly rejects naïve TWFE as the target estimator, keeps already-treated units out of the control set, and names the small-cluster, short-pre, selected-adopter limits. The mechanism table is process-aware rather than generic econometrics. The rollout choice, `expand with an embedded evaluation`, is the right policy class.

### Material quantitative failures
Section 5 is where a strict grader should lose confidence.

The main DiD table does not use a consistent sign convention. Treated-minus-control is about **−4.5 h** (median), **−6.3 h** (p90), **−1.9 pp** (rework), **+0.9 pp** (escalation), and about **+0.08** (CSAT). The submitted row is `4.5 | 6.3 | 1.9 | +0.9 | +0.09`. Only escalation is signed. A reader can just as easily infer that adopters *worsened* by 4.5 median hours. The Delta/Epsilon CSAT change is also wrong as displayed: both never-treated branches declined by about **0.08**, not `0.08`. The DiD of `+0.09` is only coherent if the control change was treated as negative off-table. That is a real arithmetic/presentation error, not a rounding nit.

The same missing-minus pattern repeats: “roughly 8 to +4 h”, “roughly 12 to +12 h”, “About 8 to +4 hours”, “About 0.15 to +0.20 points”, and CSAT “roughly 0.02 to +0.09” (Beta is **−0.02**). After the answer itself insists on quantitative discipline and on not collapsing median and tail, this is a serious defect.

The underlying windowed means are mostly correct (Alpha/Beta/Gamma pre/post, workload, April-cohort DiD magnitudes, Gamma May–June vs July–August contrasts, April vs late-summer intensity stats). The arithmetic is better than the tables that report it.

### Identification and code mismatch
The stated primary design is a cohort-specific stacked ATT / event study. The code never estimates it. It builds relative-time dummies, then fits the naïve TWFE the prose warned against (`median_cycle_h ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)`) plus an endogenous intensity regression. Both are labeled descriptive and clustered, and the small-cluster / aggregated-median warnings are present, so section 9 formally meets the spec. It does not implement the design section 3 says is preferred. Listing a future saturation RCT as a fourth “identification strategy” for *these* data is also off-spec.

E1’s horizon is “launch through one month later,” but the quantitative work uses April–August and two-month Gamma windows. That is a small but real estimand/analysis inconsistency.

### Claim ladder overreaches
Section 7 says median is not identified (confidence 30), p90 is indeterminate (20), and a universal cycle-time claim is unsafe. Section 10 then puts, as the second-strongest claim:

> Conditionally causal: … consistent with roughly 4–5 fewer median-cycle hours and 6–8 fewer p90 hours.

That packages two different outcomes into one directional effect, treats relative *less deterioration* versus worsening controls as “fewer hours,” and ranks a heavily conditioned DiD above the more local Gamma observation. That ordering is hard to defend given the answer’s own threat list, the raw Alpha/Beta p90 increase of ~10 hours, and the two-month Gamma post window. The ladder should have been closer to: observed use rose and median/p90 diverged; suggestive Gamma improvement under stabler conditions; not a portable causal X%; the 4–5 / 6–8 hour figures are descriptive contrasts only.

### Policy design is good, not tight
Keeping high-risk cases manual, instrumenting routes and the shared fraud desk, and writing numeric guardrails are the right instincts. Sending **both** remaining branches into the experiment at once, with no 0% saturation arm, burns the last never-treated controls for calendar shocks and desk-level spillovers. A stricter design would phase Delta and Epsilon, keep a 0% cell, and pre-register the 5% / non-inferior p90 rule instead of adding a vague “or clearly favorable operational margin” escape clause.

### Net
This would be an 8.5–9.0 if the DiD/CSAT signs were clean, the ranges were readable, the code estimated the event-study it advocates, and the final ladder stayed as humble as sections 5–7. It is not that memo. The causal framing is sophisticated; the quantitative surface and the closing claim are not trustworthy enough for a high score under the stated rubric.
