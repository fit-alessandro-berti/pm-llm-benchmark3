**7.4**

The write-up is format-complete, decision-set based, and mostly careful about observational equivalence. It is not close to flawless. Several interpretive errors and section-level misses are material under a strict reading.

**Material analytical errors**

1. **QA’s “strongest contradicting episode” is not a contradiction.** A06’s 09:15 appointment starts at 09:20 because A05 occupies A1 until 09:20 and QA is non-preemptive—the answer itself treats QA as non-preemptive. The first free decision epoch after 09:15 selects A06. That is exactly what appointment-aware, non-preemptive dispatch predicts. Treating a resource-busy delay as evidence that “appointments are not exact hard starts” is a logic error, and it is the one contradiction featured in the diagnosis table. The real QA identifiability problem is the one they bury: 09:20 does not separate appointment-first from FIFO, and appointment *tolerance* is unidentified.

2. **Section 4 mostly fails as a discrimination exercise.** Two of the four chosen episodes (QA 09:20; QC 10:54) have *identical* next-job predictions under both “competing” explanations. An episode that both theories predict cannot discriminate them. The follow-on remarks about a better future case / earlier rankings are correct, but they are an admission that the selected episodes were the wrong tests. The QB 09:48 pair is also weak: “dispatcher reassignment named B03” is nearly tautological and is not a process rule.

3. **QC is labeled more cleanly than the decision table supports.** SPT matches 10:10, 10:18, and 11:34, and fails 10:32, 10:54, and 11:40. That is not a primary SEPT/SPT discipline with a couple of exceptions; it is the same grade of unidentified mixture they correctly refused to name for QB. Leading with “SEPT/SPT-influenced” while giving QB “undocumented hybrid” is an inconsistent labeling standard. Confidence 58 is appropriately low; the primary name is not.

4. **Idle/wait decisions are first-class dispatch decisions and are under-analyzed.** After A03 ends at 08:30, A1 is idle until 08:32; that gap is never reconstructed. D-X is available at 11:20 and idles until D06’s 11:30 appointment—that idle, not the 11:30 start, is the appointment evidence (FIFO would also pick D06 at 11:30). The 11:30 row therefore over-claims “strong support for appointment reservation.”

**Smaller factual / consistency issues**

- At QD 11:21 the eligible set includes D06, but the skill-based prediction is only “D03 or D05.” Skill eligibility also allows D06 unless an appointment-reservation rule is smuggled in.
- C05’s 10:50 deadline was already unachievable at the 10:11 arrival (`est_min` 40). “Reactive SLA once late” is therefore a weaker explanation of the 10:54 choice than the answer suggests; static priority also fails because C05 is ignored at 10:18 and 10:32. They note the confound but not the impossibility.
- `est_min` equals realized duration on every job in all four queues. They record this in §5 yet still leave “noisy estimates” on the table as a medium-plausibility excuse for QC/QB SPT misses. With perfect ex-post estimates, that excuse should be ranked lower.
- QA 82 / QD 84 are high relative to the residual ambiguities they themselves list (appointment window; D-F’s 11:36–12:00 non-work-conserving idle; within-pool order unidentified).
- Scan-delay wording is slightly inverted in places: a job recorded at 08:21 is system-visible at 08:21; delay makes it *physically* earlier, not automatically *eligible* at 08:20.

**What remains strong (and why this is not a mid-score answer)**

Eligible sets at service starts are generally correct, including remaining-time arithmetic on B01 (31 then 14), C05 visibility at 10:10, D-M vs D-X vs D-F skill partitions (D05 and D07 uniqueness), and the calibration / prior-job / meeting constraints. Competing-rule columns are mostly right, and the answer does not convert every FIFO violation into priority. §3 usually distinguishes constrained delays from true exceptions. §6 is well targeted. §7’s warning against inferring pure/preemptive priority is the right cross-queue conclusion and is evidenced on all four queues.

**Net.** Competent process-mining reconstruction with the right architecture (decision sets, competing predictors, uncertainty). The A06 contradiction, the non-discriminating “discrimination” tests, the QC/QB labeling inconsistency, and the neglected idle epochs are enough to keep it well below a high score.
