## Critical construction failures

The pipeline would not run. `events`, `relations`, and `o2o` are internally inconsistent, and later API calls are invented.

**Timestamps are wrong.** Event 13 must be Confirm Delivery D1 at `2026-04-06 13:00 UTC`. The code places that slot on `2026-04-07 09:00` and gives E13 and E14 the same timestamp. The narrative has no ties; the solution both drops a stated fact and introduces the exact failure mode it later warns about.

**Payment amounts are missing.** Amounts 100 and 150 belong on the payment events. They are never stored, so the later “M1+M2=250” check is unauditable.

**`relations` is not a legal table.** Column lengths do not match (`ocel:activity`, `ocel:oid`, and `ocel:qualifier` have 18 values; `ocel:eid` has 17). The timestamp list skips index 10 and then uses index 17, which is out of range. `index=range(1, 18)` does not fix that. Mandatory `ocel:type` is absent.

**Event-to-object linkage is misaligned with the 17 events.** E12/E13 are treated as payments and E14/E15 as delivery confirmations, but E13 is Confirm D1 and E15 is Record Payment M2. The activity list invents `"Confirm Payment"`. Qualifiers such as `contains` / `packs` are applied to events as if they were O2O facts (`Create Order` does not “contain” O100).

**The log is not object-centric.** There is essentially one relation per event. Reserve Stock is not related to both orders and both items. Pack P1 is not related to P1, I1, I3, and the two orders. Issue Invoice is not related to V1, O100, and O200. Payments never attach M1/M2. That erases the point of the example: P1 crossing orders, V1 billing both orders, shared reservation, partial settlement.

**`o2o` is also illegal and wrong.** Lengths are 12 / 15 / 12. O200→I3 is duplicated. P1’s two items are not aligned to P1. Extra V1→O100/O200 “closed” rows are not a required relationship. Both payments use undifferentiated `settles` instead of `partially_settles` / `completes_settlement`.

**Validation is incorrect.** `set(...).isin(...)` is not valid. `len(relations) == len(relations["ocel:eid"].unique())` treats multi-object events as duplicates; a correct OCEL would fail that check. Activity/timestamp checks only inspect the first row per event.

## Conceptual errors in the prose

E2O and O2O vocabularies are collapsed. Section 1 lists `contains`/`packs`/`carries`/`bills`/`settles` as event-to-object qualifiers; those are object-to-object roles. The claimed defense against mixing source facts with propagated relations is then contradicted by adding “analytical” settlement rows as E2O.

The audit table is inconsistent with both the narrative and the code. “Bills: Invoice → Order (1)” is false (V1 bills two orders). “Especially consequential” events are named as E12/E13 payments; the real convergence events are Reserve Stock, Pack P1, and Issue Invoice. E13 is not a payment.

Flattened-Order prediction omits reservation, packing, dispatch, and confirmation, then asserts that shared events are copied. Package flattening is assumed to include Confirm Delivery even though those relations attach to deliveries. OC-DFG is described as a path through object IDs (`O100 → Add Item I1 → Pack P1`); an OC-DFG is activity-to-activity with object-type annotations. The object-centric Petri net is given a single place/transition reading and an invariant (“cannot close until paid”) that discovery on this log does not uniquely support, against the task’s warning.

Alternative 2 says to “duplicate events for each Order.” In OCEL you add relations to one event; duplication is flattening, not a richer E2O policy.

Semantic check 1 is false: orders are not rows in `events`, and each order is involved in more than create+close. Check 6 cannot be performed on the constructed tables.
