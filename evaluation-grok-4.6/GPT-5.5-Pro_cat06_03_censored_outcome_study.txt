7.3

The design is conceptually strong and avoids the classic failure modes (training only on the 21,600 activated rows, coding rejects as non-default, leaking post-decision fields, random splits). The nine required sections are present, the 48,000-case flow reconciles exactly, and the estimand is honestly labeled as historical-policy joint risk rather than counterfactual default-if-approved. Under a strict methods review, however, several design and implementation defects are material.

**Target.** Flattening delayed labels and permanently missing feeds into the same outcome vector as reject/withdraw/transfer/activate-default is a category error. Observation mechanics are not application states. Section 3 later treats them as censoring/missingness, which is correct; Section 1 does not, so the primary \(Y\) is internally inconsistent. The “key risk quantity” \(P(\text{activated and default}_{90d}\mid\text{prefix})\) is well-defined, but it is not the same object as the seven-level multi-state target. The two-stage structure (pathway, then post-activation default with a clock reset) is the right model and should have been the primary definition.

**Cohort and time windows.** Inclusion rules never filter on *case start* in 2024–2025; the code filters *event timestamps* to 31 Dec 2025. That is not equivalent. Events and delayed feeds can legitimately exist through the 15 Feb 2026 extract (activations, collections, `default_90d`, `label_available_at`). Cutting the log at year-end can misclassify late terminals as open and drop the very labels the maturity rule is written to use. The snapshot numbers are treated as a clean partition; possible double paths (activate then transfer; terminate in Jan–Feb 2026 after a 2025-only event window) are not discussed.

**Censoring strategy.** Distinguishing administrative censoring, label delay, activation selection, transfer, and MNAR partner feeds is the best part of the write-up. The proposed estimator is not. IPCW, competing risks, and a post-activation 90-day binary live on different time scales and different risk sets. A single IPCW layer “for censoring and label delay” while also keeping transfer as a competing event is underspecified (no cause-specific vs Fine–Gray vs Aalen–Johansen vs discrete-time multinomial; no statement of the time origin after activation). Sensitivity analyses are directionally right but the 1.5×/2× partner bounds are arbitrary and not tied to an identified pattern-mixture model.

**Prefixes and features.** Fixed-\(k\) vs elapsed-time, short cases, and document loops are handled correctly in prose. Elapsed-time prefixes, `documents_total` as-of repair, and event-time resource tenure are not implemented. Historical default rates as features are dangerous even when lagged; that risk is under-discussed. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe(..., grouper_freq="W")` is log-level calendar aggregation, not a justified case-level prefix feature. `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` are computed on train prefixes and then unused, which reads as prompt-compliance rather than pipeline.

**Leakage audit.** The table is complete for the required fields and the keep/repair/drop calls are right. Two gaps: (i) joining `terminal_time` into prefix metadata before eligibility can leak if that field is later treated as a feature; (ii) `source_system` as a predictor will soak up partner missingness and look like “risk.”

**Validation.** Temporal splits and the Oct 2025 maturity cliff (activation + 125 days ≲ 15 Feb 2026) are correctly identified, and the random-split critique is accurate. The named “Test 1” window still has heavy right-censoring for late activations; it is not a clean default test set. The simple 2024 training slice does not implement the rolling as-of label rule the text itself requires (late-2024 activations are not mature at a 31 Dec 2024 cutoff).

**Code (largest concrete failures).** Far longer than the requested 80–160 lines. More importantly:

1. `label_observed_asof` requires a non-null `label_available_at`. A mature `default_90d` with a missing availability timestamp is routed to `activated_feed_missing_candidate` instead of the observed label. That can discard the labeled export.
2. Event filter through 31 Dec 2025 vs `AS_OF` 15 Feb 2026 is inconsistent, as above.
3. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is version-sensitive; there is no `convert_to_event_log` fallback, and the return is assumed to be a DataFrame with `.sort_values`.
4. Static attributes use `.first()`, which keeps a leading NaN rather than the first non-null as-of value.
5. Unmapped post-migration activity names are not quarantined, so terminals can vanish and look like open cases.

**Verdict.** This is a serious, process-aware design that would be a strong revise-and-resubmit, not a near-flawless methods package. The conceptual core is right; the target semantics, time-window logic, estimator, and skeleton all have defects that would bias cohort counts or the reported risk.
