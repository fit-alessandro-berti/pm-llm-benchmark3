**5.1**

The final write-up is a competent interval analysis that gets several hard cases right, then loses a large amount of credit to missing required rows, arithmetic mistakes, self-contradictory sensitivity tables, and a code section that does not do what was asked.

**What is actually good.** Clock-offset direction, resolution-before-correction, and the HUB/OPS-BER/SENSOR/VENDOR-NY constructions are correct. T-405 is the strongest part of the answer: the DST fold is resolved by ingestion order rather than collapsed into one giant interval, and the surviving UTC windows match the metadata. T-404’s inferred start correctly uses complete minus `[18, 25]` minutes and keeps the full range. The four-way CERTAIN_PASS / CERTAIN_VIOLATION / POSSIBLE_VIOLATION / INDETERMINATE vocabulary is used in the right spirit for the pairs that were actually scored. Section 7 hits the requested control list.

**Section 3 is incomplete against an explicit requirement.** The prompt asked for one row for every testable case-rule pair, including S1–S3 where events exist or can be inferred and every supplied temporal-profile pair. Close exists for T-401 and T-408, and Alert→Contain is testable without Investigation_Start, yet most profile rows and T-401 S3 are simply absent. That is not a formatting quibble; it omits several clean CERTAIN results (T-401 S3 and Contain→Close; T-402 Alert→Contain; T-408’s three failing profile pairs). ISO timestamps were required; most intervals are bare clock times without date or offset.

**Section 4 contains real errors, not just omissions.**
- T-402 S1 at +5 min is labeled CERTAIN_PASS. The feasible range is about `[958.5, 1231.5]` s against a 1200 s limit, so it remains POSSIBLE_VIOLATION.
- T-404 S2 is said to need only a >3 s buffer, then the +2 min cell is still POSSIBLE_VIOLATION. `[2277, 2703]` vs 2820 s is CERTAIN_PASS. The table contradicts the prose.
- T-406 S2 at +5 min is called still possible because “5 m buffer [is] insufficient.” Max 2911.5 s is under 3000 s, so it should flip to CERTAIN_PASS.
- T-408 S3 is written as `[138597, 138603]` s (~38.5 h). Containment is 16:05, not 16:00; the duration is ~38 h 25 m (~138297–138303 s). Classification happens to survive; the interval does not.
- T-408 Inv→Contain is CERTAIN_VIOLATION at ζ=2 in the matrix and “becomes PASS at Z=3” in the text, while the matrix still says CERTAIN_VIOLATION at ζ=3. At ζ=2 the band is `[900, 3300]` against `[3297, 3303]` (possible, not certain). At ζ=3 it is a pass.

Several other flips are never discussed (T-403/T-405/T-406/T-407/T-409 S1 under +5 min). The prompt required discussing every case whose classification changes.

**Weak evidence is treated too firmly.** T-407 has no Alert_Received. Bounding it by sent+latency and then hard-cutting it at Investigation_Start is one defensible scenario, not a fact; INDETERMINATE, or a two-row “with/without causal cut,” was the honest treatment. T-409’s NTP hole is exactly the “too weak” clause in the INDETERMINATE definition; using ±12 min as a crisp bound and calling S1 a possible violation hides that. T-406’s entry-delay semantics are stated, then never varied, even though dropping the extra five minutes from occurrence time would make S2 a certain pass. Midpoints are not abused as primary times, which is good; the required sensitivity to those semantic choices is still missing.

**Section 5 fails the spec.** The dataframe contains only T-401, T-402, and T-408. Interval bounds are pasted in, not computed with pandas/`zoneinfo`. DST alternatives are not represented. Classification logic is a stub for one pair. `pm4py.format_dataframe` and `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` for ζ = 1, 2, 3 are commented out and replaced with a string. Raw `timestamp` values mix local Berlin wall time with UTC bounds. This is not an executable reconstruction of the evidence.

**Net.** Core UTC-interval work on the well-specified clocks is clearly above a failing grade, and T-405 shows real interval reasoning. Against a rubric that asked for complete pair coverage, honest INDETERMINATE use, a working sensitivity matrix, and a specific PM4Py comparison, the published answer is too thin and too internally inconsistent to sit any higher.
