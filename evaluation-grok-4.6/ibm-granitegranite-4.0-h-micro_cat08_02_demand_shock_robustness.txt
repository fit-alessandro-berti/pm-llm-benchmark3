**3.5**

The submission has the required skeleton and some surface familiarity with the intervention list, but it is not a reliable process-mining redesign. Core facts are inverted, metrics are systematically mispaired, several interventions are applied to the wrong stations, and the “robust package” is just a restatement of I1–I10.

**Scenarios are the wrong shape and, in one case, the wrong direction.** Scenario 1 claims a surge in Urgent and Complex-panel work, then writes a mix of 70%/15%/12%/3% in the same Routine/Urgent/Complex/Repeat order as the baseline. That is a *Routine-heavier* mix, the opposite of both the stated story and the actual peak log (42/29/20/9). A mix-shift scenario that does not use the provided peak mix, and then invents numbers that contradict the narrative, fails the first required test. Scenario 3 collapses medical validators and specialist review into one role, then says Routine work is hit; the DFG shows most Routine cases take Technical validation → Release (11,700 of 15,890 normal TV exits). Scenario 4 treats “compression into 10:00–17:00” as if that were new, while the log already specifies discrete 10:00 and 17:00 spikes. None of the four scenarios is a compound winter-wave case (mix + courier bunching + Friday specialist gap), so they vary labels more than interacting stresses.

**Bottleneck migration is shallow and numerically sloppy.** Three of four “initial bottlenecks” pair *normal* utilization with *peak* queues (pre-analytical 74% with 9.5 h; medical validation 58% with 22 h; courier 60% with 7.5 h). That is not a small slip; it erases the utilization jump that is the evidence. For a mix shift toward Urgent/Complex, the first-order migration in the data is not pre-analytical clerks. Peak already shows medical validators 58%→93% (2 h→22 h), specialist review 43%→87% (5 h→36 h, then 18 h/48 h on the hold edge), technical validators 76%→101%, assays 71%→98% (4 h→18 h), plus Complex-panel 3.1× medical validation and 2.4× specialist hold. Identifying pre-analytical as the opening constraint, then “adding clerks,” violates both the evidence and the no-blanket-capacity rule. Relieving assay congestion by “redistributing to M2” ignores that M2 is the high-failure, night-shift repeat generator. Relieving specialist absence by “cross-training accession correction specialists to do medical validation” is operationally impossible and inverts I4.

**I4 is misunderstood everywhere it appears.** I4 is two *technical validators* doing accession correction. The answer treats it as (a) relief for medical validators, (b) a machine-outage policy, and (c) accession people covering medical sign-out. Peak technical validation is already at 101%. Using I4 as a general-purpose spare brain is exactly the bottleneck-migration failure the prompt warned about, and the answer never sees it.

**Section 3 is not a redesign package.** Listing every candidate intervention with a paragraph of recycled conflict-hint language is not selecting a coherent set that survives several worlds. I1, I2, I3, I4, and I9 are *anti-robust* in the prompt’s own hints; treating them as the robust core shows no filtering. There is no account of complementarity (for example I6 + I7 to protect intake, I5 + a narrow I10 for the medical/specialist tail, limited cross-training that *does not* drain the 101% TV pool). Downstream overload is mentioned as boilerplate, not traced with DFG frequencies or performance edges (recollection 42 h/60 h, batch wait 9 h/22 h, repeat 10 h/26 h, specialist hold 18 h/48 h are barely used).

**Scenario policies are mismatched and have on-triggers only.** Urgent-first (I1) under a joint Urgent *and* Complex surge is the starvation case the conflict hint names. I3 during machine degradation routes Routine onto the worse machine. I4 has no causal link to assay failure. I6 is a standing quality gate, not a mix-shift switch. The spec asked for on *and* off conditions; off is missing. There is no WIP, SLA, or Friday-coverage trigger, and no recognition that all case types cannot be prioritized at once.

**The trade-off table has multiple sign and coverage errors.** I5’s effect on Urgent is “Low” even though Urgent needs medical validation and has an 18 h penalty (peak Urgent p90 is already 33 h). I6’s rework impact is “High”; early 2-hour reject should *cut* the recollection loop that is already eating intake. I8’s next bottleneck is listed as registration clerks — that is the station being relieved; the prompt says I8 exposes pre-analytical and assays. I1’s Complex-panel effect is “Moderate” rather than harmful. I10’s rework is “High” (the real cost is 12 h transport and traceability, including Urgent SLA risk). Scenario-coverage cells contradict the policy section. Implementation burden is an undifferentiated Moderate/High. This is a checklist table, not an evaluated bundle.

**Residual risk is mostly pre-existing.** Assay reliability, specialist variability, and courier dependence were already in the brief. The required redesign-induced vulnerability is only weakly gestured at via I8. Missing: I1-driven Routine/Complex WIP explosion, I3-driven M2 repeat clusters, I4-induced TV collapse, I10 blowing the 18 h Urgent clock, and policy conflict if I1–I10 are all “robust.”

**The dashboard reverses leading and lagging.** Urgent cycle time and “>18 h” are SLA outcomes, not leading indicators. Warning at pre-analytical queue >9 h is warning at the *peak median*, i.e. after the process is already late. Absent as leading signals: inbound mix (Urgent/Complex share), M1 vs M2 repeat rate, specialist-Friday coverage, arrival bunching at 10:00/17:00, recollection rate, TV queue, specialist-hold queue, and WIP by case type. Rework >8% is barely above the normal 7.5% and well below peak 11.8%, so it is not a useful band.

**What is not all bad, and why that does not save the score.** The seven headings exist; four disruption classes are named; conflict hints are at least echoed; a table with the requested columns appears. That is a 3-range artifact, not a 6+. Under a hypercritical process-reasoning rubric — inverted mix, mixed-period metrics, wrong bottleneck for the mix-shift, I4 inverted, no selected package, cycle time sold as a leading indicator — this is far from nearly flawless.
