**Factual errors that should not have survived a pass over the timestamp table**

1. Delivery D1 is not a 1-hour Dispatch→Confirm span. `e15` is 11:00 and `e23` is 14:00 (**3 hours**). The 1-hour figure is written into the comparison matrix as a preserved performance fact. (11:00–12:00 is `e18` Issue Invoice, which is not a D1 event.)

2. Order flattening does not turn 3 Dispatch events into **5** rows. `e15` (O1) + `e16` (O1, O2, O3) + `e17` (O3, **O4**) = **6** rows. Section 3.3’s “5 rows for 3 truths” drops the second order on `e17` — the same event the surplus-row count elsewhere *does* include. The matrix’s Pack Package “6 vs 4” is correct; Dispatch is not.

3. There is no Confirm Delivery self-loop on O3 from `e24`→`e27`. O3’s directly-follows sequence is `e24` Confirm → **`e25` Record Payment** → `e27` Confirm. That is a cycle with Record Payment, not a self-loop. The actual Confirm→Confirm edge is on **O1** (`e23`→`e24`). Repeating this in both the matrix and §3.4 is a DFG mistake, not a wording slip: activity repetition with an intervening event is not a self-loop.

**Internal inconsistency on the two terms Section 1 was required to fix**

Section 1 defines convergence as *fusion into one case* (D2, V2, P3/P4) and divergence as *copying / within-case repetition*. The matrix then rates Delivery and Invoice **Low** convergence because those flattenings do not duplicate events. Under the paper’s own definition they are the high-fusion leads: D2 welds O1/O2/O3; V2 welds O2/O3. What the matrix actually scores is the textbook OCEL “convergence = event replicated across cases of the lead type” notion. The two uses are never reconciled. That is a direct hit on the evaluation criterion the prompt stated.

Section 3.4 then compounds this by parking the bogus O3 Confirm self-loop inside the *consolidated invoicing* item. O3’s two Confirm events are D2 vs D3 / P3 vs P4 (split fulfilment), not V2. V2’s instalment `e25` is exactly what *separates* those Confirms. Two distinct distortions are fused, and the causal story is backwards.
