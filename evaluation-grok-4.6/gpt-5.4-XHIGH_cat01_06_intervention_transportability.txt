**7.3**

The answer is structurally complete, format-compliant, and directionally correct: it refuses to treat Site A’s 9-hour median drop as an identified effect, rejects the blanket copy, keeps statutory ordering intact, and designs a segmented pilot that can separate mechanism failure from implementation failure. Under a normal rubric this would score much higher. Under the required hypercritical standard it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative, structural, and model-fidelity errors are material.

**Section 1.** The table hits the required features and the right design consequences. Two inaccuracies weaken it. The six-open-task cap is Site A *context*, not a listed co-intervention; attributing it to the after period quietly inflates how much of A’s gain is “queue management bundled with parallelization.” The DFG frequencies (e.g., Register→Identity 2,050/3,400, Originals→Document 2,108, Identity→Document 1,160, Legal→Supplement 360) are unused. Those counts already show that B is not a clean sequential twin of A-before and that some identity/document interleaving already occurs. Ignoring them makes the “process-structural” comparison more schematic than evidence-based.

**Section 2.** Required nodes and `S_` selection variables are present, and the prose correctly names the shared mechanism versus site-specific blockers. The graph is still thin where the rest of the answer is thick: nothing points into `rework` except `case_complexity`, so the legal/supplement and rushed-overlap quality pathways argued later are not in the DAG. `treatment_parallelization → waiting_before_assessment` bypasses `true_overlap`, blurring the distinction the answer otherwise insists on. `cycle_time → appeal` is asserted without a mechanism. The write-up is within the word band and conceptually sound; the graph is not a faithful compression of the causal story.

**Section 3.** This is the strongest section. Co-interventions are itemized, and headline metrics get appropriately low transport confidence. The column `transport confidence` is used inconsistently: 20–25 for effect sizes, 90–95 for “this is a useful warning.” That is a different estimand. A sharper table would have given low confidence that A’s *effects* transfer and separately scored confidence in the *confounding diagnosis*. A missed quantitative tell: completions rose 8.3% while verification FTE rose 11% (4,800/18 ≈ 267 vs 5,200/20 = 260 cases per FTE). Throughput per head fell. That strengthens the confounding argument and is not used.

**Section 4.** Coverage and signatures are good. The originals row is only half-right. Identity service is 3.4 h and originals delay is 18 h; Identity is almost never on the critical path for originals-required cases. Parallelizing Identity with Originals Receipt is legally safe and operationally tidy, but the expected cycle-time gain is near zero unless Identity is delayed far beyond its median. Calling that a first-class “beneficial signature” overstates the mechanism.

**Section 5.** The scenario set is the right set, and the required “mean up, tail down” case is present. The arithmetic is not. Overall median and p90 are not 38% × subgroup savings. The untreated 62% have an 18 h originals delay and dominate both the median and especially p90. Speeding the *shorter* digital cases need not move the overall median at all, and it almost cannot improve p90. Optimistic `p90 −1h to −5h` and digital-only `p90 0h to −3h` are therefore the wrong sign/magnitude for a tail owned by originals, legal review, and loops. Eligible-share figures are also loose: 38% is “no originals,” not “achieved overlap.” First-wave “20–25%” (no originals × no legal × not high-complexity) is optimistic if those flags are even weakly positively dependent; an independence sketch is already ~12%. Bounding savings by `min(3.4, 8.1)` is the right *service-time* cap and is then mixed with unspecified waiting reductions without a critical-path decomposition.

**Section 6.** The staged rules, statutory holds, WIP/logging, and supplement handling are the right intervention. The compact POWL is mostly faithful. Residual unclarity: Stage 2 is sold as a real concurrency treatment even though, as above, it is mainly administrative staging; the spec does not say that explicitly. WIP caps (4/4, assessor 9, legal 6) are invented without linking them to pool size, arrival rate, or Site A’s six-task rule.

**Section 7.** Design is serious: team-week assignment, strata, T1/T2, contemporaneous controls, interference, stop rules, ITT vs treatment-on-overlap. Two issues. Power math is tight and not shown: ~3,400/26 weeks ≈ 130 completions/week; a 12–25% first-wave stratum does not comfortably yield 150+150 plus ≥50 per key stratum in 10–12 weeks. The text says “extend duration rather than pool,” which is the right save, but the specification still reads as if 12 weeks is enough. Second, the mechanism-vs-implementation paragraph is clear but slightly overstates what overlap-up / cycle-flat identifies: that pattern is also consistent with a real mechanism whose savings are smaller than residual noise, not only with a downstream bottleneck.

**Section 8.** Conceptually the POWL is the right object: XOR document path, Identity ∥ DocPath, Assessment as a join, optional LOOP for legal/supplement. Implementation is not faithful to stock PM4Py. `Sequence` is not a class in `pm4py.objects.powl.obj`; sequence is a total `StrictPartialOrder`. `Operator` is normally `pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj.Operator`. `play_out(..., parameters={"no_traces": 5})` is not the usual parameter name. Duplicate `Transition("Document Check")` / `Transition("Assessment")` nodes are acceptable as relabeled copies, but a reader cannot tell whether that was intended. The comments on what the net cannot encode are correct and were required. A section whose job is model fidelity should not depend on a non-existent constructor.

**Section 9–10.** `conditional go` is the only defensible choice. The transfer/do-not-copy split and the first subgroup are right. Expansion/modify/abandon triggers are usable. The register’s eight assumptions are testable and load-bearing. Residual softness: assumption 1 treats “sizable no-originals subset” as necessary for transport, but the DFG already implies that subset exists; the binding uncertainty is critical-path materiality and lock-safe execution, not existence.

**Net.** The analogical spine is good. The grade is held down by unused DFG evidence, a wrong tail-aggregation in the scenario table, overstatement of Identity ∥ Originals as a cycle-time lever, inconsistent “transport confidence,” and PM4Py code that would not construct the claimed model on a standard API. Those are not polish nits; they sit on the exact axes the rubric weights.
