**6.2**

The audit is structurally complete and gets the main leakage story right, but it has several material errors and several required distinctions that are only half-done. Under a strict reading it is a competent draft, not a reliable audit.

**What is solid.** Section 1 correctly flags the obvious full-trace leaks (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`) and the subtler lineage bugs (`prior_claim_count_before_t0` vs `extraction_date`, nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, mutable `risk_score_current`, closure-time `last_resource_role`). It keeps the two signals that should survive: `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`. It does not treat SHAP rank as causality, and it does not throw out every correlated feature. Section 4’s narratives avoid restating leaked outcomes. Section 5 hits the requested validation topics.

**Section 1 is incomplete and sometimes sloppy.** The spec required every local-only feature for X/Y/Z. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` never appear in the table. Finding types for the two sequence patterns are labeled differently, but the write-up does not actually treat them differently: `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is process-legal after Partial/Deny and only unavailable at `t0`; `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is process-illegal and, here, a same-timestamp ordering artifact. Calling the appeal pattern “impossible to know” and giving both Critical / remove collapses that distinction. Remediation for appeals (“replace with `fraud_investigation_opened_at_t0`”) is a non sequitur. Calling the 65% `final_handler_team` overlap “coincidental” is wrong; it is a stale snapshot of an often-stable assignment. `num_document_requests_total` is leaked, but a prefix repair is also process-empty at this `t0` (requests start after coverage check), so “early warning” is not salvageable as stated. The fraud-sequence note is confused (“before t0 is rare”).

**Section 2 misses a requested check.** Prefix timestamps, alignments/footprints, same-timestamp index, complete-trace filters, and repeated settlement-after-appeal are present, and the DFG caveat is correct. The suite never operationalizes enabled vs impossible vs merely future-possible *at `t0`*. That is the check that separates “documents requested is the next legal step” from “appeal is not enabled yet” from “closed before assessment cannot occur.”

**Section 3 mixes classes and omits the worst features.** Region and week are dumped into both “defensible” and proxy/drift. The unusable bucket lists repairable lineage items but omits the features that are unusable even after repair (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`). Predictive vs causal vs actionable vs fairness is stated, but not cleanly applied.

**Section 4 has a serious status error.** Case X is dominated by leaked mass (`appeal_seen_anywhere` +0.26, `final_decision` +0.06 on a 0.82 score). Status must be **recompute**, not **retain**. Case Y correctly says recompute, then treats unrepaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` as a credible driver anyway. `initial_reserve` is used with no lineage caveat. Queue length is mitigating in the Y narrative but omitted from `credible_drivers`. Case Z’s invalidate call is defensible; the ambiguity list is the strongest part of the JSON.

**Section 5 is generic but complete.** Priorities are mostly reasonable; calling process/schema drift “Low” is the weak spot.

Net: the answer knows the difference between leakage and a real prefix signal, which is why it is not a 4–5. It still mis-labels a leaked prediction as retainable, skips required local features, and blurs the process-legal-but-future vs process-impossible distinction the rubric called out. That caps it well below a high score.
