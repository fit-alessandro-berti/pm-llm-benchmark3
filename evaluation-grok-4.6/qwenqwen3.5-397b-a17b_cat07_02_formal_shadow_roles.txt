6.5

The write-up is complete, format-compliant, and usually careful with the substitution and authority evidence. It still has enough factual slips and clustering shortcuts that it is not close to flawless.

The main structural miss is the Front Office role. Putting Nora, Kai, Lina, Amira, and Ben in one cluster is exactly the frequency grouping the benchmark is designed to defeat. The table *notes* quality and automation caveats, then proposes the naïve grouping anyway. A stronger structure would have split automation intake, seasonal low-complexity closure, and permanent case-owning agents. Combining Erik and Fatima as one “Specialized Review” role is a second forced cluster: they share rarity, not work, authority, or risk.

There are clear data errors. Kai and Lina do not have “0 high-complexity cases” (they have 2 and 4; they have 0 *critical* cases). Erik does not have the “highest critical case count” (Mateo 76, Hana 62, Erik 55). Hana is not shown to be the only person with final approval authority; Giorgio and Fatima also perform A9 regularly. Those are not rounding issues.

Section 3 is otherwise the strongest part: Giorgio as inefficient commercial cover, Mateo as broker without sign-off rights, Erik as low-volume critical specialist. Benefits and risks are treated correctly, and substitution is not mistaken for competence. Section 4 is also solid.

The PM4Py sketch uses the right function names at a high level, but it would not run as written. `format_dataframe` is given a nonstandard `resource_key`; `discover_organizational_roles` does not return a mergeable frame with `discovered_role` / `event_count` / `primary_activity`; handover-of-work is typically a graph, not a table with a `sink` column. Illustrative code is allowed, but inventing return types undercuts the “avoid over-interpreting frequency clusters” point the code is supposed to demonstrate.

Operational advice is mostly sound (do not staff Fatima’s seat with Giorgio; do not promote Mateo on preliminary A9; do not automate Erik from volume). It is weakened by the same FO over-aggregation and by underusing the security-surge tradeoff (filtered referrals vs. two critical cases waiting more than two days).
