**5.8**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and arithmetically careful on the daily table. The core operational story (weekday/weekend split, 15 April outage versus 16–17 recovery, 20 April mix/peak shift, 29 April staffing) is the right story. That does not make it nearly flawless. Several claims are over-precise, a few are unsupported, and two queueing arguments are simply wrong.

**Invented or overstated facts.** The Little’s-Law table asserts that R1/R2 “queue clears daily” with “negligible overnight carry-over.” The prompt says opening backlog is unobserved and that work carries overnight. Mean WIP of ~20 with 6 FTE is incompatible with a daily clear. \(K=48\) open-hours snapshots is stated as a definition, not an assumption; the prompt never says snapshots exclude the night. “Permanently” raising weekend staff to 3.0 FTE is one weekend. R1 is labeled a “stationary M/G/c queue in statistical control” despite unknown \(L(0)\), a split (non-contiguous) window, hourly Fano \(>3\), and no Poisson evidence. WIP time-averages (52→35) are treated as backlog headcounts.

**Little’s Law is both too strict and too loose.** The text says \(L=\lambda W\) “strictly requires” stationarity and \(\lambda=\mu\). The operational identity does not. Then R1/R2 are marked **applicable** on \(\sum\Delta N\approx 0\) over interrupted day-sets, while R5 (\(\rho>1\), \(+94\) in 7 days) is only “approximate.” For a supercritical integrator, \(L/\lambda\) is not an approximation to sojourn time. The 12-hour versus 24-hour snapshot convention is never treated as a first-class ambiguity, so every implied \(W\) inherits an unlabeled scale choice. Finite-window \(W=L/\lambda\) is reported to a tenth of an hour as if the boundary terms were known.

**Touch time is not offered load.** Median/p90 are active handling times on completed cases, and a large share of arrivals are multi-step. Using a lognormal map from those quantiles as \(\mathbb{E}[S]\) and then \(\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]/(cT)\) is an assumption, not a measurement. It is used as a fact (\(\mathbb{E}[S]\approx 43.9\), \(\rho=1.16\), capacity \(98.4\)/day, staff to 7.5 FTE). Completions in R5 (~100.6/day at 5.93 FTE) already strain that 43.9-minute mean (implied occupancy \(>1\)). Worse, R1’s own numbers do not cohere: daily \(\rho\approx 0.66\) with \(c\approx 6\) cannot produce \(W\approx 2.4\) h in a single-stage multi-server queue (M/M/6 wait is minutes, not ~2 hours). Intraday peaking and between-step idle could explain it; the answer never confronts the contradiction and still calls the extra 121 minutes “waiting.” That error flows into staffing policy.

**Kingman is misapplied.** Hourly-count CV (\(0.62\), \(1.03\)) is not the interarrival \(c_a\) in Kingman/Allen–Cunneen. A Poisson process has \(c_a=1\) and small count CV. The displayed formula is G/G/1, not G/G/c. “Expected queue delay triples independently of volume” does not follow: the factor is \((c_a^2+c_s^2)\), \(c_s\) is unknown, and \(\rho\) and \(\mathbb{E}[S]\) also changed. This is a definitional mistake, not a rounding issue.

**Pooling section overclaims Simpson.** The three contrasts (fictitious 90-arrival day, hidden \(\rho>1\), recovery mistaken for productivity) are real aggregation failures. None is a sign reversal. Within-weekday Fano \(0.66\) still mixes outage, mix-shift, and intervention; R1’s own Fano is ~0.26. Useful, not Simpson.

**Burstiness and dependence.** Hourly Fano/CV on the four vectors are computed correctly and the NHPP-versus-extra-Poisson point is noted. Alternative B then treats one day’s Fano as a regime parameter. Lag-7 “\(r_7=+0.992\)” and “WIP is an autoregressive integrated process” over-interpret 20–28 dependent points. Four exemplars cannot support a stochastic model — that caveat is right and should have constrained Alternative B and the Kingman paragraph.

**Segmentation.** The 8-way primary cut is defensible and recovery is not sold as a high-productivity regime. Confidence “Very High/High” on \(N=1,2,3\) windows is theater. Alternative A is a real alternative; it also dumps 16–17 and 29 May–1 into one “drain” state, which the primary text correctly refuses. The Python “change-point heuristic” is hardcoded dates. That misses the required transparent detector (CUSUM, mean-shift, threshold on \(\Delta N\) or `complex_pct`) presented as non-definitive.

**Other precision nits.** R3 \(\rho_{\text{active}}\approx 1.21\) is unexplained and does not match the later \(\rho\) recipe. Pooled \(\rho\sim 0.85\) is not reproducible from the same recipe as R1/R5. Peak-bin mismatch on 7 April is a good audit catch, then over-read as a one-hour offset. Survivorship/SJF speculation is unmarked. R8 \(N=2\) is not a demonstrated new equilibrium.

**What still works.** Estimands and six inference threats are mostly the right threats. Outage is isolated from recovery. Completions are not treated as same-day throughput. Median service is not used as flow time. Pooled versus segmented tables are numerically consistent with the daily file. The risky “5 FTE everywhere” policy is a fair illustration. The script rebuilds the tables, prints regime/pool/dispersion/LL summaries, and comments PM4Py without pretending the aggregates are an event log.

The narrative competence is well above average. Under a strict reading — no invented observations, labeled assumptions, correct queueing identities, honest uncertainty — the Kingman error, the unreconciled \(L\)–\(\rho\)–\(W\) gap, the daily-clear fiction, and the fake change-point keep this far from a high score.
