8.4

The final write-up is substantively excellent: all 12 cases are interpreted against the process tree and P1–P6, zero-cost policy failures (CL-609, CL-610) and fitting silent moves (CL-605) are called out explicitly, cost is never treated as risk, and the seven required headings and artifacts are present.

What keeps it well short of near-flawless:

- **Internal inconsistency.** Section 6 announces “six structural consequences” and then lists eight. That is a real logic/editing error in the section whose job is to be rigorous.
- **Imprecise quantitative claim.** “Inverted by roughly five orders of magnitude” mixes units and overstates the comparison (CL-602 vs CL-601 is closer to three orders on exposure-per-cost, not five).
- **A few severity calls are slightly soft or slightly hot** relative to the author’s own scale (CL-611 as 2 for a resolved synonym; CL-606 as 2 on a €75k HIGH evidence-location gap). None is a policy misread, but they are not tightly justified.
- **Code is complete on paper but not demonstrated.** `parse_process_tree` spacing, PM4Py alignment return shape, and default move costs are version-fragile. The fallback is a plus; an unrun reproduction still cannot be treated as verified.

Policy application itself is accurate: P1/P2 on CL-609 vs CL-610, P3 on CL-604, P4 evidence quality on CL-608, P5 telemetry/retries, P6 scope on CL-607/CL-612, and “missing event ≠ missing activity” on CL-602/CL-606. Remediation priority (containment → payment gate → data-aware routing → model/log hygiene) matches the cases. Under a hypercritical bar, that is a strong 8, not a 9+.
