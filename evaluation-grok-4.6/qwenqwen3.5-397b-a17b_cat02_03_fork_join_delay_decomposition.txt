6.3

The case table and the four worked examples are largely correct, and the write-up follows the required heading structure. The analysis is not close to flawless.

The most serious defect is in §3. The stated mean synchronization waits (A 7.1, B 0.0, C 26.5) do not match the data under either an all-case or a non-critical-only definition. Correct all-case means are about 11.0 / 4.3 / 30.4 minutes; non-critical-only means are about 13.2 / 13.0 / 36.5. Putting **0.0** on B is not a rounding issue. It treats B as if it never waits, which is false (F01, F04, F08, F11). The footnote is also internally inconsistent: it says the mean is taken only over non-critical cases *and* that critical-branch sync wait is zero. That error matters because the task explicitly asked for sync-wait comparison and warned not to equate service time with influence.

§4 is incomplete on the points the prompt required. CF1 is handled well (only F01 and F11 move; 5 and 10 minutes). CF2 is not. B-queue exists in every case, but **flow-time savings are zero** in F01, F04, F08, and F11 because another branch remains last. Saying “Cases Affected: All 12” contradicts the required distinction between direct benefit and zero-benefit cases. The text also never reports a full per-case or aggregate CF2 total, only “High.” Downstream masking is almost skipped: branch counterfactuals hold `downstream_queue` fixed, so an earlier `join_ready` is assumed to translate 1:1 into earlier `final_start`. The F1/F2 occupancy pattern (F03→F05, F07→F09→F11, F06→F08) shows that is not always true. CF3 is underspecified (“halves or eliminates”).

Smaller issues compound that. Formulas in §1 never use a case index `c` as requested; the double-counting argument is conceptually right but not shown as an identity. Tail contribution to `join_ready` is mentioned via two cases, not measured. Final service is 20 minutes in every case and is never remarked on. The Python block likely does not match PM4Py’s `format_dataframe` signature (`end_timestamp_key`), prints only five rows, and does not surface the full per-case counterfactual table the section promised.

§5 and §7 are the strongest parts: B-queue vs A-floor, the shift of criticality to A if B waiting is removed, and the limits on capacity, preemption, and queue-versus-policy are sound. Those sections do not offset the wrong aggregate metric or the incomplete counterfactuals under a strict reading of the rubric.
