**3.8**

The answer has the right skeleton and occasionally the right instincts, but it is not a reliable compliance assessment. It repeatedly misreads the case file, applies the wrong instrument at the wrong time, and then uses “emergency / justified exception” to paper over the gaps. Under a strict reading of the stated grading axes—consistency, process awareness, scope and time, evidential discipline, and argumentation—that is a serious failure, not a near-miss.

## What is actually solid

Section 1 states a usable method: evaluate at the time of the act, do not let later approval automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control, prefer contemporaneous evidence over ERP stamps, and treat the CFO email as interpretive rather than amendatory. Section 2 correctly refuses a single total ordering and flags that ECD-2 is stronger on timing and supplier onboarding than on segregation or Compliance. Section 7 is the best part of the paper: it forces explicit waivers, written time-limited exceptions, a definition of “critical lots,” a four-hour oral-to-written rule, and a check against emergency normalization.

Those are real strengths. They are also mostly announced and then abandoned.

## Fatal consistency break

The hierarchy says ECD-2 “probably cannot override” GPIP-4 A1 and A3 because C5 does not mention them. The rule catalogue and the case table then let C1/C2/C4 (and “critical nature”) erase Finance, Compliance, Plant approval, and segregation anyway. That is not a nuanced issue-specific hierarchy. It is two incompatible theories, with the looser one doing the work in Section 4.

The same break appears in the formalized rules. C1 is a *timing* rule (commitment may precede PO). The catalogue maps it onto A1 segregation and A2 Finance. C2 permits an unapproved supplier; the catalogue treats it as a waiver of A3 Compliance clearance. D1 is an uncontrolled email whose own D3/D4 deny authority to touch Compliance or SoD; the pseudo-logic still lets `amount <= 150 && CFO-D1` omit Finance, and then adds a second path that omits Finance whenever `ECD-C1 applies`. That is not defeasible logic. It is a permission generator.

A4 is the actual emergency baseline—tendering only; other controls remain unless A7. A7 is reserved to the CRO, who was unavailable and never ratified ECD-2. The method section mentions this and then never makes it do any work. “JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION” becomes a substitute for the missing A7 instrument.

## Treatment of time and scope is repeatedly wrong

**P-501.** Commitment is 07-14 08:50. ECD-2 starts at 09:00. The controlling interpretation is “ECDC4 overrides ordinary timing.” That is a temporal error, not a close call. The live instruments are GPIP-4 plus local B4 (four-hour stoppage). B4 can justify commit-before-PO. It cannot be rewritten as ECD-2. The row also invents a “supplier acknowledgment log” that is not in the file.

**P-505.** The live issue is whether commitment occurred *inside* the window. Supplier acknowledgment is 17:59:31; ERP is 18:06; the file says the ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast. The prompt forbids treating the late database stamp as conclusive. The answer instead calls the amount “>EUR100k” (it is 88k, so A3 is not triggered), fixates on Finance at 19:00, and speculates about an implicit post-expiry extension. It misses the one issue the evidence note was written to test.

**P-508.** The pump is “the only available pump capable of restoring the *affected* line.” That is core ECD-2 scope even on Internal Audit’s 24-hour reading. The answer puts it *outside* “critical sterilization-line lots,” then uses that invented scope failure to drive JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at “Critical” severity. Scope is inverted.

**P-506.** After expiry, no written extension, no joint authorization record. NONCOMPLIANT is defensible. The justification is not. The row imports BUY-8 from P-508, says A2 is satisfied because Finance is at 06:05, then says A2 is violated, and leaves a working note (“Wait:”) in the formal table. That is not an assessment. It is an unedited scratchpad.

## Evidential discipline fails on the cases that were designed to test it

**P-507** is the clearest collapse. The file opens with `Compliance_Clearance 07-17 08:50`, then IC authorization 08:55, commit 09:00. A3 is met. The real questions are missing Finance (A2/B2/D1) and whether the signed PDF at 12:40 is timely C3 evidence (3h45, inside four hours) while the ERP stamp at 14:30 is a logging defect. The answer states there is “**no Compliance clearance** recorded,” calls the case a justified exception for an implied Compliance waiver, and builds the Section 5 alternative on the same false premise. You cannot assess a control you have not read.

**P-502.** Amount is EUR 62,000. The answer says D1 “does not apply (>150k).” That is a misread of the amount, not a hard interpretive choice. It then treats Compliance at 18:30 as “permissible despite tardiness” after having endorsed A6. Same-day is not a cure. Supplier-onboarding language in C4 *might* be argued as a limited A3 overlay; the answer does not make that argument. It just waves the lateness through.

**P-504.** Classification NONCOMPLIANT is right; the reasons are not. B2 is “at least EUR 25,000.” EUR 24,000 does not trigger it. The supplier is stated to be unapproved; the answer wonders whether it is approved. The actual corporate hit is A3 (new supplier ⇒ Compliance before commitment). The local hits are B3 and B1. None of those appear as the controlling defects. Right label, wrong statute.

Invented or swapped evidence appears more than once (P-501 supplier log; P-506 BUY-8; P-507 missing Compliance). That is an evidential-discipline fail even before the legal theory is scored.

## Case table is flattened where the prompt demanded discrimination

Allowed labels include INDETERMINATE. The file is built around genuine indeterminacies: C6’s two readings of “critical lots,” whether COO+IC can count as A7, whether D1 can stand in for A2, whether IC members are budget owners. The answer never uses INDETERMINATE. It issues JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION five times (P-502, P-503, P-505, P-507, P-508). That is the move the prompt explicitly bars: do not treat emergency purchases as a class.

**P-503** is the showcase. Backup-line pumps, three days of stock, Audit’s 24-hour test fails, PROC-3 is requester *and* committer *and* one of two IC authorizers, no Finance, no Compliance, no Plant, no CRO exception. On the answer’s *own* hierarchy this is NONCOMPLIANT (A1 + A3 + doubtful C-scope), or at best INDETERMINATE on scope plus NONCOMPLIANT on SoD. Calling it JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION because the pumps were labeled “critical contingency” is circular: the contested label is treated as the waiver. Section 5 then admits the better reading. When the stronger alternative is clearly stronger, it should have been the primary.

Confidence is uncalibrated (P-501 “High” on a void instrument; P-508 “Low” / “Critical” on a mis-scoped file). Severity jumps from Low/Medium/High to “Critical” with no scale. Several “decisive evidence” cells cite documents the case does not contain.

## Rule catalogue and alternatives

The catalogue omits the clauses that actually decide hard cases: A4, A6, A7, B4, B5, B6, C5, C6, D3, D4. It therefore cannot represent “normally / unless / overrides_for(issue, scope, time).” The pseudo-logic uses an undefined `emergency_premium` and hard-codes the overbroad waivers already criticized.

Section 5 meets the four-case minimum, but quality is uneven. P-503→NONCOMPLIANT and P-505→NONCOMPLIANT are the right pressure tests—and they should have moved the primaries. P-506→COMPLIANT via oral extension is correctly marked weak. P-507→NONCOMPLIANT for missing Compliance is not an alternative interpretation; it is a restatement of a fact error. The alternatives that would actually flip a careful analyst—P-501 under B4 not ECD-2; P-502 on whether C4 reaches A3; P-505 on 17:59:31 as the commitment time; P-508 as the *best*-controlled emergency buy—are missing.

## Section 6–7

Section 6 copies the Section 4 mistakes, so the breach / logging / late-evidence / unprovable grid is largely unusable. The one distinction the evidence note exists to illustrate—P-505 and P-507 as late stamps of timely acts—is not drawn. The P-503 remedy (“formalize dual authorization as an implied A7 exception”) is exactly the post-hoc cure A6 and A7 refuse. You cannot invent CRO authority after the fact and call it remediation.

Section 7 would have been a 7–8 on its own. Even there, appointing “COO + Incident Commander” as fallback Emergency Exception Authority recreates the unresolved A7 problem instead of isolating it, and SoD is handled only in the exception clause, not in the overlay’s default.

## Bottom line

This is a complete template filled with an unstable theory and an unreliable reading of the record. A process-compliance memo that (i) applies ECD-2 before it exists, (ii) misses a Compliance clearance printed on the case row, (iii) gets two monetary thresholds wrong, (iv) inverts P-508’s scope, (v) invents evidence, and (vi) converts five messy emergencies into JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION cannot be scored as a strong answer. Credit for structure, a few sound methodological sentences, two roughly correct noncompliant labels, and a usable policy draft. That is not close to flawless, and under the required strictness it is well below the middle of the scale.
