**5.0**

The write-up follows the required heading contract and gets the hardest local reconstruction mostly right: missing-start bounds for P05/P07/P11/P15 are correctly pinned to the prior job on the same resource, queue entry, and completion; OPEN work is treated as elapsed rather than finished; waiting cases are kept; utilization is built from window busy time rather than completed cases only; and C2’s 10:55–11:10 gap is read off the surrounding C2 jobs. That is real process-mining work.

It is not close to flawless, and several defects are factual rather than stylistic.

**Counting errors that propagate.** S1 has **20** observed completions (P01–P20), one OPEN (P21), and one S1 waiter (P22). The station table’s “19 ends, 1 open, 2 waiting” is wrong; the thinking trail dropped P06 and the error survived. The same off-by-one appears as “19 entered S2” and “3 waiting for S2.” There are **four** S2 waiters at 14:00 (P16, P18–P20). Hourly S1 arrivals are also wrong: 10:00–10:59 is **4** (P14–P17), not 3. P17 at 10:45 was omitted, so the arrival series sums to 21 against a table that still says 22 arrivals. S2 mean service is computed from a sum of 490 min; the twelve fully observed services sum to **480**, so the 41 min figure is an arithmetic slip.

**Required quantities are missing.** Case-level total sojourn for completed cases is defined and never reported. Queue length and total WIP are not reconstructed over time; “peak ~9 / ~7” is asserted with no event calendar and no stated inclusion rule at a rounded timestamp (start-inclusive/end-exclusive, etc.). A1 mentions ties and ±2.5 min but does not actually apply a simultaneous-event convention to the real collision (C1 hands P07 → P09 at 11:00). Rounding is not propagated into any numeric range.

**Bottleneck reading overreaches the evidence.** Larger *historical* S1 waits after 10:00 are real, but end-of-window state is S2-heavy: 4 waiting + 2 in service at S2 versus 1 + 1 at S1. Late S2 waits (P14 65, P15 75, P16 already 85 min in queue) are S2 congestion after S1 has finished, not “inherited S1 backlog.” Calling S1 the continuing bottleneck through 14:00 is not supported. Little’s Law on 08:00–10:30 uses an unspecified W ≈ 0.7 h and λ ≈ 6.7/h (arrivals in that window are 16 / 2.5 h ≈ 6.4) on a transient, non-stationary interval; that is not a defensible check.

**Sensitivity is described, not computed.** Optimistic/central/pessimistic logic is sketched, but Section 6 does not recompute station metrics under those scenarios. Zero-service extremes are left as if equally plausible despite every fully observed S1 service sitting in 20–30 min.

**Section 7 fails its own spec.** `to_dt` maps `''`, `MISSING`, and `OPEN` to the same `None`, which the prompt forbids. The code never builds missing-start intervals, never computes WIP, and never implements the three imputation scenarios it mentions. The claimed “both stations fully observed” projection is `log_df` unfiltered, so P16-style S1-only cases still enter `insert_case_service_waiting_time`. Arrival/finish-rate columns are not printed. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is only a comment, which is allowed, but the pandas path does not actually carry the station-level uncertainty analysis.

**Clarity.** A1–A2 lose the inequality/λ symbols (`t_actual  [t-2.5…]`, `L =  W`, `Service 0`), so the formal layer is harder to trust than the case table.

The surviving core is a usable case-level bound table plus a directionally right story that S1 waits balloon through the arrival burst. The aggregation, WIP, sojourn, bottleneck-now vs bottleneck-then, Little’s Law, and code requirements are too sloppy for anything in the high band under a strict rubric.
