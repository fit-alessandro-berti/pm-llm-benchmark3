**6.0**

The write-up gets the central design stance right: it does not recode rejects, withdrawals, or open cases as non-default; it treats activation as a selection gate; it keeps an as-of view in the prose; and it uses a temporal split. Those are the mistakes the prompt treats as disqualifying, and they are avoided. The answer is still not close to a high score. Several specification errors would produce misleading estimates or a non-runnable pipeline.

**Target and estimand.**  
Conditional 90-day default given activation is a defensible primary target and is clearly distinguished from a competing-outcome formulation. The operational story is incomplete. Prediction is at 3 events / 5 days, when activation is still unknown, so the model is estimating a future-conditional quantity, not “default for every application.” The stated population (“all applications reaching the prediction point”) does not match the estimand (activated accounts only). Time origin is also underspecified: the 90-day clock starts at a later event than the prefix, which matters for survival versus binary-at-horizon estimation. That is left implicit.

**Cohort.**  
The 48,000-row identity is correct, and adding the 3,600 immature activated accounts back as censored is the right move. The still-open row is wrong: 5,100 open applications at 15 Feb 2026 are not “cases that never reached prediction.” Almost all of them have long prefixes and are undecided, i.e. a competing or not-yet-selected state. Partner-originated permanently missing feeds and the 60% transfer linkage are not broken out in the flow table even though the prompt flags both as distinct missingness mechanisms.

**Censoring.**  
The five-way distinction is present and the primary analysis does not impute undefined outcomes. Two technical claims are confused. Fine-Gray with “activation as the entry event” mixes competing risks with delayed entry / left truncation. IPAW is named as sensitivity without stating the unconfoundedness assumptions that would be required to interpret a pseudo-population in which rejected applicants are treated as if activated. Naive Brier / C-index are proposed later without IPCW or other censoring-adjusted scoring, which is inconsistent with keeping 3,600 right-censored labels.

**Prefixes.**  
The rule contradicts itself. The text says `min(3rd event, start+5d)` and then “the union of both constraints (whichever yields the earlier cutoff).” Union and min are not the same rule. Short cases, loops, and a rename map are mentioned, but cases that accept/activate inside the prefix window—and therefore can leak `Contract Signed` / `Account Activated` into an “early” prefix—are not handled.

**Features and leakage.**  
The feature list is directionally process-aware. Two leakage vectors are mishandled. Historical default rates “from cases ending before the current start” ignore label maturity (90+35 days), so recent “ended” cases can inject unfinished outcomes. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is recommended without restricting it to the truncated prefix; on a full case that function is a standard look-ahead. Resource experience is “repaired” by inventing historical employment dates the schema does not provide; the honest options are drop or a clearly weaker proxy. `default_90d` itself is missing from the audit table.

**Validation.**  
A random split is correctly rejected. The calendar cut is not. Test cases may start through 1 Nov 2025. A 1 Nov start cannot have a matured 90-day-after-activation label plus a 7–35 day feed by 15 Feb 2026, even if activation is immediate; any realistic time-to-activation makes this worse. That is exactly the immature-label problem the study is supposed to prevent. Validation is entirely pre-policy (through 30 Jun 2025) while test is entirely post-policy, so there is no legitimate slice on which to recalibrate for the 1 Jul 2025 regime. Post-migration test mass is at most two weeks of starts and is even less mature.

**Limitations.**  
This is the strongest section: association vs selection vs counterfactual is explicit, and the three deployment feedback loops are real. It does not rescue the maturity or implementation errors.

**Code.**  
The skeleton is about 45 lines against a requested 80–160, and it is not an implementable pipeline. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then ignored. No terminal-state table is built (reject / withdraw / transfer / open / activated / immature). `label_available_at` is unused. Elapsed-time prefixes are absent. The leakage “audit” is inverted: `assert not df[leak_cols].notna().any()` will fire on the real extract, because those columns are populated after the decision; the correct check is that they never enter the prefix feature table. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is run on the full `df_pm`, i.e. all events, all splits, including post-outcome timing. Train-only DFG/variants are exploratory only. Activity renaming, `documents_total` as-of repair, and `risk_score` as-of logic are not implemented. A reviewer could not execute this and obtain a leak-free, temporally split, fully accounted cohort.

Net: conceptually above the common failure modes, but not statistically tight and not implementable as specified. Material errors in label maturity, prefix semantics, and the PM4Py pipeline cap the grade at 6.0.
