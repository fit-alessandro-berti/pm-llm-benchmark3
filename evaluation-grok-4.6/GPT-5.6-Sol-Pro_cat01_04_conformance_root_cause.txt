This is a strong, well-structured investigation that correctly refuses a single-cause story and uses the staged preprocessing results as a quasi-experiment. Under a harsh reading it is not nearly flawless. The abductive core is right; several calibrations, completeness gaps, and small unclarities keep it well below a top score.

**What it gets right.** The ledger is broad and mostly well judged: UI rename, the 40-case audit, DFG substitution, gateway insertion, ETL ingestion time, the 11/70 screenshot check, the August policy/model mismatch, omitted high-value status, and the unaudited 55 missing-check cases are all used. The ranking correctly treats label change, technical events, and order defects as the main *measured* collapse; model obsolescence as the main August residual; real process change as genuine but not the June drop; and employee noncompliance as an unresolved local risk rather than the default explanation for extra log moves. The causal stack is the right shape. Unchanged approval/appeal rates are treated as weak outcome evidence, not as proof of data error or proof of compliance. The four-phase diagnostic program actually isolates provenance, then fixed-model attribution, then model comparison with attributes, then residual compliance—this is the right design. Governance rules map onto the six required controls. The code encodes the tree, expands variants, remaps `Risk Screen`, drops `Queue Routed`, compares a `tau`-optional sign-off candidate, and states the overgeneralization caveat.

**Material weaknesses.**

1. **Reproduction does not carry the argument it relies on.** The narrative’s third repair—source-sequence ordering—is the only June intervention that is *not* implemented on the supplied traces. A string-level `Notify`/`Decision` swap, or a documented proxy for the 70/45 reversed variants, was feasible on aggregates and would have made the 81%→89% and August comparisons inspectable. Instead the code prints a helper that never runs. That is a real gap against “logically coherent” reproduction of the staged claim, not a cosmetic omission.

2. **Posteriors are poorly calibrated.** Ranks 1–5 sit at 99/98/96/95/93. Those numbers do not discriminate “primary cause of the June collapse” from “real August policy change.” The prose distinguishes them; the table almost does not. Reliability scores of 97–99 are handed out freely, including for IT release notes and for a 93% vs 100% Apr–May inconsistency that still has several innocent explanations (cost convention, fitting definition, suppressed low-frequency detail). Primary-explanation confidence of 97 is too high given 11/70 order corroboration, no source audit of missing checks, and the unused high-value attribute.

3. **Arithmetic of the alignment table is only partly interrogated.** The answer is sharp on Apr–May (listed variants should be language-admissible, yet fitness is 93%). It does not apply the same skepticism later. After label map + queue drop + source order, Aug–Sep “66% fitting” is almost exactly \(1 - 210/620\), i.e. the complement of sign-off traces, which is hard to reconcile with 35 overrides and 30 missing checks remaining non-fitting. Jun–Jul 89% is also not the naive complement of the remaining listed deviations. Treating the staged percentages as clean incremental effects without flagging that second inconsistency is a provenance miss in an answer that otherwise advertises provenance discipline.

4. **PM4Py details are asserted, not shown.** `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is not a reliable high-level name; the block was not executed; `multi_processing` and diagnostics record shape are version-sensitive. Defensive parsing helps, but “checked against current official method signatures” plus an unused GitHub footnote overclaims. The optional model is also only compared on a *non-reordered* August log, so its printed fitness/precision cannot be read against the 66%/86% table the rest of the memo treats as evidence.

5. **Small unclarities and looseness.** “Roughly 560” of 650 queue cases is exact. “Signed semantic map” is unexplained. `Manual Override` is handled carefully in places and still slightly blurred in the causal graph (real action, possible routing correction, possible logging-patch discontinuity). Real process change and obsolete model are two sides of one August fact; the adjacency list would be cleaner if `hidden_high_value_status` constrained the model, not only `true_process`. Section 3 is within the word band but compressed on why missing `Compliance Check` and true bypass remain observationally equivalent after resource-completeness collapse.

**Why not lower.** It does not blame staff because log moves rose. It uses the 32→58→81→89 path. It takes the 55 missing checks and the EUR 50,000 rule seriously. It separates logging defects from model obsolescence. It warns that an optional sign-off tree can buy fitness by becoming overpermissive. Those are the axes the prompt said would be judged. The failures are calibration, completeness of the reproduction, and incomplete numeric interrogation—not a wrong diagnosis.

**Why not higher.** Under the required strictness, unverified code, bunched posteriors, an unimplemented key repair, and an unexamined 66% coincidence are enough to keep this out of the 9–10 band.
