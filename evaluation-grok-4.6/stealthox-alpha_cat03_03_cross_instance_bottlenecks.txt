**Serious reasoning errors**

The CAL-3 calculation treats TEST-1 as if J5 and J6 could occupy it together. J5 tests 16:10–16:35; J6 is ready at 16:05. Removing only the 16:35–17:20 recalibration still serializes J6 behind J5, so J6 starts about 16:35, not 16:05–16:10, and finishes about 17:00, not 16:35. The extra wait attributable to CAL-3 is the 50-minute TEST-1 idle (16:35–17:25), not 1 h 20 min and not a 75–80 min finish-time saving. The parenthetical “or a second unit” refers to CAL-3, which does not create a second tester. Section 2 then carries the inflated ~80 min into an S3 spillover on J5 even though J5/J6 pack times are not observed.

Narrative A asserts that a 09:00 FIX-A hold “ends up delaying J1’s dispatch at 19:25” via B2 and S1. That contradicts the same answer’s B2 arithmetic. J4 is ready at 13:10; B2 starts at 14:50 because of J5 (14:40) and RACK-7 (free 14:35). An earlier CNC start for J4 is absorbed as slack before the batch barrier; it does not move B2, J4’s 19:10 pack, or S1 at 19:25. Labeling this “speculation” and hedging on the 10:47–11:45 CNC-2 hole does not repair the missing slack argument. The prompt explicitly forbids equating succession with causation; this is the main place the answer does so.

Falsification item 6 (J1 dispatch has no SLA value) and item 7 (peak utilization >85%) do not weaken rank-1 batching; they weaken shipment and capacity. The header claims all six-plus observations target the top mechanism.
