**6.4**

The write-up hits the required six-section form, uses the mandated conformance labels, keeps A/B at the bottom for the right legal reasons, and treats re-screening as information rather than a substitute for second-officer review. That is the core of the task. It is not close to flawless.

The material error is the composite timeline. Rank 1, Section 5, and Section 6 treat S4 arrival plus “review/execution” as about 5–15 minutes and claim an earliest continuation of ~40–70 minutes, execution in ~55–65 minutes if S4 arrives at 35, and the “highest probability of meeting the deadline.” Action C’s own numbers make independent second review 35–75 minutes, with 5–15 minutes only to record the decision and execute. A serious second review of a €480k case with risk 72 and an unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match is not a rubber stamp after an API call. Waiting 35–65 minutes for S4 and then doing a real review is often 70–140 minutes and frequently blows the 90-minute SLA. The 50-minute switch to C is therefore too late: if review needs ~35 minutes plus recording/execution, the decision to interrupt P-901 must come much earlier. This error cascades into the benefit/harm cells, the 35-vs-65 sensitivity, and the primary recommendation’s operational claim.

Action D is never ranked as its own action. The prompt allows a composite, but it also requires ranking A–G. Subsuming D into Rank 1 hides the weaker standalone plan (screen, then still hunt for a reviewer) and makes the table look cleaner than the action set is.

F at Rank 3 is too high. Its label is correctly conditional, but Rule 6 gates Approve and Payment Executed, not Reject, and the prompt does not establish that the rejection threshold is met. Putting a possibly premature, possibly nonconformant reject above clearly conformant E (and treating F’s “benefit” as €0 regulatory avoidance) slides toward optimizing P-884’s clock rather than mandatory controls. E is slow, not unlawful.

The table’s euro ranges are ad hoc. Rank 1 lists the full €20k–€35k on-time prize despite approval only 0.55–0.75, residual rejection/hold risk, and a real chance of missing the deadline. Harm of €0–€15k does not include deadline loss or false-clear residual. Rank 2’s €10k–€25k / €15k–€35k is invented the same way. Observational probabilities are pasted onto intervened paths after the prompt warns they may not transport.

Counterfactuals for the top five are only partly specified. They sketch approve/reject/hold but barely treat SAR/hold-with-report (Rule 8) on a risk-72 unresolved-match case, and they do not systematically walk process-permit vs likely continuation vs P-884 benefit/harm vs spillover vs compliance vs intervention uncertainty. The stale 12-minute-old score is unused. P-901 interruption is fixed at 45 minutes even though C’s review can run to 75.

What remains good is still good: A/B cannot be rescued by a favorable forecast; G is an unenabled exception; four-eyes is enforced; P-901 and the API quota appear; 35 vs 65 minutes is discussed; primary / fallback / trigger are present. Those are necessary, not sufficient, under a strict bar. The recommended plan is directionally a conformant second-review strategy, but its SLA arithmetic and the F-over-E ranking are wrong enough that this is a competent compliance-aware memo, not a near-correct decision analysis.
