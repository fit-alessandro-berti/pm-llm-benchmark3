7.1

The writeup is a strong dialectical brief with a weak executable model. It follows the required headings, argues both sides at real strength, and mostly keeps authority, scope, and evidence separate. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, the PM4Py revision has objective construction errors, the revised model fails traces the architecture claims to accommodate, and a few classifications misuse the mandated taxonomy.

**What holds up.** Section 1 is the best part of the answer. Stipulated facts are mostly source-faithful; disputed items are actually disputed (scope of rejected leads, reactivation, post-expiry practice, CRM mutability, alias SoD, COI disclosure). The burden rule is coherent and is used later: written instrument plus predicates for `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`; informal emergency or “imminent amendment” does not carry that burden. Prosecution correctly treats sunset as snap-back and exception-normalization as the central systemic failure (ineligible Auto_Approve, SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-714, SUP-705). Defense correctly treats M0 as a bad sole oracle (PD-3/FD-7 lawful traces, missing loop, CRM missingness, label aliasing). Both sides make the required concessions. The matrix is complete, including M0-fitting resource cases. System judgment—core CS-9 sequencing is still binding; M0 is unfit as the only checker—is the right net.

**Classification and taxonomy errors.** SUP-711 is not `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`. The duplicate `Register_Supplier` is explained by a shared source transaction ID. The business path is known; the extra row is a migration artifact. That is compliant-with-dirty-log, not an open verdict. `INDETERMINATE` is for cases where the record cannot support a finding (SUP-706 is the actual example). SUP-713 as `COMPLIANT` is slightly too clean: the QA_Clearance mapping was approved a week after the case, so at event time the label was unofficial. `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` or a delayed-equivalence note would be tighter. SUP-715 as `MIXED` is explained, but it collides with the author’s own burden rule: COI policy required disclosure, none was recorded, and “what would reverse” is a disclosure/waiver. That is a layered `NONCOMPLIANT` (or `NONCOMPLIANT` on the resource overlay) more than two incommensurable verdicts. Confidence on SUP-702 is also a notch high while PD-3’s authority is still listed as disputed.

**Model semantics are the main failure.** The revised POWL reuses the same `Transition` objects (`sanctions`, `quality`, `activate`, `reject`) as descendants of multiple XOR children. In PM4Py POWL those nodes are object-identity graph nodes, not labels. Sharing them across exclusive branches is not a valid encoding of alternative routes; conversion or conformance can collapse exclusive paths into a single shared node. The defensible patterns are one pre-XOR `Sanctions_Check`, or distinct `Transition` instances with the same label. The code never states this limitation.

Related semantic gaps:

- `skip_quality_pre` is defined and unused. Section 6 allows quality waiver on reactivation; M1 cannot replay SUP-710 (sanctions → manual → activate, no quality). Every activation branch still requires `Quality_Review`.
- Auto and emergency routes *force* quality after activate. PD-3/FD-7 *permit* deferral; quality-before-activate remains valid and would be rejected.
- `Request_More_Info` sits only after register and before the whole decision. SUP-707 interleaves requests between sanctions and quality. The approximation is explained for the “no activate until evidence” constraint, not for this interleaving, so the advertised fix for the desirable loop still cannot accept the motivating case.
- Forced sequential quality-after on auto/emergency is not called out; that hides restrictiveness the prompt asked to surface.

The overlay table, residual constraints, and event contract are substantially better than the POWL. Frozen eligibility snapshots, immutable `notification_id`, `rule_version_id`, alias graph, and DRAFT marking of a permanent auto rule are the right data design. They do not rescue an ill-formed control-flow object.

**Smaller defects.** Special characters are stripped throughout (`requester  manual approver`, `2%29%`, `Quality_ReviewQA_Clearance`, `Trace deviation  business misconduct`), which blunts precise claims. “Monitored exception” vs later Compliance denial is listed as disputed, then eligible PD-3 cases are treated as clearly justified—consistent enough, but the tension is not resolved. Defense underplays that CS-9 itself already contemplates abbreviated rejected-lead pre-screen; that would have been a stronger scope argument than “model owner vs process owner.”

**Net.** This is a high-quality legal/process essay with a broken artifact in the one section that can be checked for formal correctness. Dialectical fairness, scope/authority handling, and evidence structure would sit in the high 8s. Model semantics and the executable revision pull the score down hard. Not a 9+ paper, and not a collapse: the judgment on SUP-703/708 vs SUP-702/704, and the refusal to treat sunset-breaking practice as policy, are sound.
