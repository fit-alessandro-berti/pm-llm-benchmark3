The analysis is structurally complete, process-aware, and usually careful about time, scope, and evidence. It is not close to flawless. The main failure is a classification that the author’s own method does not support, plus several smaller slips in consistency and evidential hygiene.

**What is strong.** The write-up follows the required headings and columns. It does not let the newest or loudest document automatically win. Commitment is treated as the legally relevant act. ON-44 and the P-505 clock note are used as evidential weights, not as hidden exceptions. P-501 is correctly kept outside ECD-2 even under a late ERP stamp. P-502 correctly refuses to apply the CFO email retroactively and treats missing pre-commitment Compliance on a new supplier as a real GPIP-4 A3/A6 failure. P-503 is the best case: scope is doubtful, A3 is unmet, and PROC-3 is requester, committer, and co-authorizer. P-504 is outside ECD-2. P-506 cannot rest on a verbal overnight extension. P-508 is rightly left open because D2’s “as well” does not clearly replace A2. The alternatives in §5 are premise-specific. §7 actually resolves authority, expiry, records, and emergency normalization.

**Material defect: P-507.** JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is not the strongest defensible reading under the author’s own rules. GPIP-4 A2 requires *both* an independent budget owner *and* a Finance Controller *before* commitment. A4 says other controls remain unless the CRO writes an A7 exception. The CRO did not. Incident Command’s charter does not make members budget owners. D3 lets the CFO interpret *Finance* controls; it does not authorize swallowing the budget-owner prong. D1’s “operational approvals” is not the same thing as A2. §1 says an unproven exception yields NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE. §2 says A2 probably cannot be removed by ordinary email. Pseudo-logic line 1 then lets D1 “satisfy” G-FIN-1, and P-507 is classified as justified. That is a method-to-application break. Medium confidence and a good §5 alternative do not repair a primary call that needed INDETERMINATE (or NONCOMPLIANT). The same A2/budget-owner gap is handled more cautiously in P-505 and P-508, which makes P-507 look result-driven.

**Smaller issues that still count.**
- SOP B2 never says “before commitment”; A2 does. The pre-commitment reading is disclosed, then applied as if it were text.
- P-501 mixes B4’s Shift Lead *Plant_Approve* substitute (absent manager + active delegation) with who may *commit*. Plant_Approve still occurred; the delegation fact does not do all the work assigned to it.
- P-504 confidence is too high. The SOP covers maintenance and *production-support*. A cafeteria freezer is arguably outside B, and “unapproved” is not automatically “new” for A3. The out-of-scope ECD-2 point is right; the rest is shakier than “High.”
- P-503 lists the authorization record as missing even though Emergency_Authorize is timestamped at 10:55, before commit.
- B6 is checked for P-501 and then mostly dropped. If the emergency form is ordinary local evidence, several ECD-2 cases are incomplete; if B7/C4 supersede it, that should be said once and applied.
- §5 for P-508 only argues toward JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. From INDETERMINATE, NONCOMPLIANT (A2 unmet, D1 cap exceeded, burden not carried) is the stronger opposing reading.
- A3’s “what it can override” cell is conceptually confused: A3 is a constraint, not an override engine.

**Net.** This is a serious, usable opinion with real command of timing and overlay logic. It would not survive a close consistency check on A2 versus the CFO email. Under a strict reading of “nearly flawless,” that plus the smaller evidential lapses caps the score well below the top band.
