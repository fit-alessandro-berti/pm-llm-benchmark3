**6.8**

The answer is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the event-time rules. It is not close to flawless. One core classification is wrong, the formalization overreaches in places, and several smaller judgment and encoding issues remain.

**What is solid.** The seven required headings are present and used as specified. Section 1 correctly treats blanks as non-updates, forbids backward fill, separates event / effective / record / evidence time, and uses `event_index` as the same-timestamp order. The rule table and pseudo-logic define temporal direction explicitly. Most case outcomes are right: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are clearly `NONCOMPLIANT`; TX-306 and TX-308 are `COMPLIANT` under a defensible reading of R4; TX-305 and TX-310 are correctly left `INDETERMINATE`; TX-311 is correctly `COMPLIANT` because the release-time jurisdiction is `DE` after event_index 4. The code reconstructs the log from `StringIO`, forward-fills only within case, does not leak the TX-305 settlement amount back onto release, tracks reviews and material changes, and computes flags instead of looking up narrative labels. Sections 6 and 7 hit the required failure modes and control improvements.

**TX-302 is misclassified.** At release the case already has an `Enhanced_Review` after the only prior data event and before release. R1 is therefore satisfied on the primary path. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` is the label for a waived missing control, not for a case that already performed the required review. A valid EX-77 is extra evidence, not the reason the case passes. The write-up even admits the `COMPLIANT` reading, then still chooses the weaker label. The code hard-wires the same error: any triggered R1 case with a valid exemption is labeled `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` even when `r1_review_met` is true.

**Formalization is slightly too broad and slightly sloppy.** Counting `Create_Transaction` as a “material data change,” and treating risk/customer updates as R1-invalidating material changes, is not licensed by the rule text. R1 is amount- and jurisdiction-dependent; R4 defines the amount threshold. `R3Satisfied` also leaves `u` unbound. The extra R2 constraint that Enhanced must follow the latest risk/customer change is reasonable as “relevant review,” but it is an interpolation, not the stated rule.

**Evidence handling is stronger in prose than in code.** Encoding EX-77 as `evidence_quality='strong'` and EX-88 as `weak` is close to baking the exemption verdict into the register. That is better than a case-id lookup table, but it is still a hidden classification knob. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called and then unused; converting timestamps to strings first is also a brittle extra step.

**Smaller judgment nits.** “Severity LOW” on compliant cases is undefined and unhelpful. Confidence on TX-306 is stated as HIGH while Section 4 simultaneously treats the 4.5% increase as a live alternative reading. Those are minor, but they add to the sense that labels are a bit loose.

The piece would be in the high 8s if TX-302 were `COMPLIANT`, material change were scoped to R4 plus jurisdiction (and only those attributes that actually affect the triggered rule), and the formal R3 clause were closed. As written, it is a strong compliance memo with a real taxonomy error and enough formal/encoding roughness that a strict grade cannot go much higher.
