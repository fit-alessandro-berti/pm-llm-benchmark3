**8.2**

The core diagnosis is correct and well evidenced: the net is a workflow net (unique source/sink; every node is on a source–sink path) but not sound, because `t_join_waived` is a one-input join and does not synchronize with the analysis branch. That is the right principal defect, and the business reading (authorization without completed analysis; waiver cases never reach the exact final marking) follows from it.

The marking argument is the strongest part of the write-up. Both sequences are legal, use transition names, start from `im`, show the AND-split tokens, follow the waiver route, and separate “a token is in `sink`” from “the marking equals `fm`.” CE1 shows `t_authorize` before `t_analyze`; CE2 shows it after; leftover `p_analysis_done` is correctly shown to be unconsumable because `t_join_normal` still needs `p_review_done`. Honesty that this is one root cause with two symptoms, not two independent defects, is appropriate. The 14 vs 10 reachable-marking counts are actually derived, not invented.

Repairs are genuinely distinct and match the required families. R1 (add `p_analysis_done → t_join_waived`) is the right minimal policy-preserving fix. R2 correctly avoids the invalid AND-wiring of `p_waived` into `t_join_normal`. R3/R4 change policy and say so. The cost model is explicit; the lower-bound argument that a zero-edit repair is impossible and that a missing consumer cannot be fixed by deletions alone is sound. Selected PM4Py code matches R1, is standalone, preserves labels, and uses the required variable names. Section 6 calls the required APIs on both nets; the helper checks enabledness. Section 7 correctly refuses to treat soundness as proof of business alignment.

It is not near-flawless under a strict reading.

- Structural notation is systematically unclear: arcs appear as `source  p_ready` without arrows, and compounds such as “oneinput,” “WFnet,” and “ANDsynchronization” are left unhyphenated. In a formal net diagnosis that is a real unclarity, not a cosmetic nit.
- “Every firing sequence that uses `t_waive_review` terminates in `{p_analysis_done=1, sink=1}`” is false as stated. Only *maximal* waiver sequences do; after `t_authorize` in CE1, `t_analyze` is still enabled. The verdict’s “every waiver-route execution deadlocks” has the same slip.
- The claim that *all* cost-1 arc additions were checked is an overclaim. The relevant candidates examined are the right ones, and R1/R3 do appear to be the only two sound single-arc repairs, but that was not an exhaustive place–transition enumeration.
- Cost accounting never says whether deleting a place/transition includes incident arcs, so R2’s cost of 10 is model-dependent rather than canonical.
- Reachability-graph terminal detection via `from_state.name` is an unverified API assumption; `fire_named` also ignores `arc.weight` (harmless here, still imprecise).

None of that overturns the diagnosis or the chosen repair. The marking derivation, the distinct repairs, and the soundness-vs-intent caveat are what the task asked for, and those are done well. The remaining defects are precision, notation, and overclaim, which keep it clearly below a top mark.
