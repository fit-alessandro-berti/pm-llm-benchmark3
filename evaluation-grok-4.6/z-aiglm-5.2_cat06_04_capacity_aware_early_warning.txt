**6.7**

The write-up hits the structural requirements and the main decision-theoretic traps: I-03 is excluded on stage, not rank; I-05 is suppressed as a refresh artifact while I-07 is re-entered on new evidence; calibration is segment-adjusted; effects are not treated as \(p_{\text{now}}\); spillover is first-order. The REV arithmetic in Section 2 is auditable and mostly correct. That is a competent policy memo, not a weak one. It is not close to flawless.

**Logical inconsistencies**

The largest coherence failure is Non-Native-Language uncertainty. Section 1 says the I-09 effect interval should be widened by ±50%. That widening is never applied. At face value it would cut I-09’s lower-bound effect from 0.18 to ~0.09 and collapse REV well below the €1,500 NNL bar invented in Section 5. I-09 is nonetheless a firm “intervene.” The same case is the most plausible “model reason for alerting was wrong” victim, yet the adverse scenario assigns the near-zero effect to I-02. The uncertainty analysis and the portfolio are not the same analysis.

I-04 is scored with a 0.8 “can wait” haircut and simultaneously justified as an immediate swarm because it is still in Triage, where the intervention is most useful. Those two claims cannot both be load-bearing. After the Strategic Enterprise correction, \(p_{\text{adj}}=0.395\); intervening now is a pure value bet on a mid-risk, long-horizon case. That is allowed, but the scoring rule and the slot rationale disagree.

The I-07 veto is misspecified. Residual risk of 0.525 after a midpoint effect is not a reason to withhold treatment. Value of treatment is \(\Delta p \times V\), not a function of \(P(\text{breach}\mid\text{treat})\). The low EV argument was sufficient; the residual-risk argument is a decision-theoretic error.

**Operational gaps**

Two specialists deliver 45-minute swarms. I-09 has 2.5 h and I-02 has 3.0 h, both ending near the 15:00 regulatory window for which a slot is reserved. There is no sequence, no concurrency constraint, and no statement of what happens if the reserved incident arrives while both short-horizon swarms are still queued. That is a first-order feasibility issue, not a polish item.

Non-monetary obligation is thinner than the prompt required. A reserved slot for an unnamed regulatory case is not a treatment of Public Sector duty on I-01 (complete data, large \(p\) jump, acceptable calibration, strong effect range). I-01 is parked as “can wait” with no statutory/political overlay.

**Measurement and presentation**

Section 3 asks for ranges and then publishes false-precise point nets (€16,800 / €8,000 / −€7,450). “Direct economic benefit” already nets swarm cost and false-alarm burden, while a separate cost column restates €1,350. The €20,000 untreated-case value drives whether base is comfortably positive and adverse is deeply negative; it is assumed, not bounded. The 8–20 minute base delay is not converted into a listed-case spillover (I-06 sitting in Diagnosis Started is the obvious candidate).

Stacked modifiers (0.8 / 1.15 / 0.7, €1,500 NNL floor, 15% segment quota, 1.3× lifetime-value fudge, 0.08 re-entry delta) are not shown to be jointly consistent. The 15% intervention floor can force exactly the low-EV swarms the REV rule exists to refuse.

**What still works**

I-08 is correctly refused despite €100k. I-03 is not purchased as a high-\(p\) trophy. I-05 vs I-07 is explicit and right. Three scenarios include a negative adverse net driven by spillover, which is the correct qualitative result. Fairness and label-corruption discussion is concrete (treatment flags, shadow model, spillover concentration), even if some safeguards fight the allocation rule. Monitoring covers the five required layers and has a staged change/rollback protocol.

The memo is strong enough to deploy as a draft policy and too internally inconsistent, too precise, and too incomplete on specialist timing and non-monetary duty to score as a clean decision-theoretic answer.
