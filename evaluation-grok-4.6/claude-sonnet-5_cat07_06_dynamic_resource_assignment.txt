It hits every required heading, treats the problem as multi-objective rather than a throughput contest, and is strongest in framing, worker communication, and qualitative comparison. ## Fatal inconsistencies in the assignments

**C012 violates the author’s own overtime rule.** Bruno is `overtime_risk = high`, already at 12 open cases with 4.0 hours left. C012 is *low* priority with 48h SLA, currently owned by Jonas (skill 3, 4 cases, 6.0h, low OT). Constraint 4 / S2, and the eligibility step that *removes* high-OT resources unless the case is urgent and no alternative exists, all forbid this. The table then admits the risk. That is not a trade-off; it is non-compliance with a rule stated two sections earlier.

**C006 cannot be “batched with C012”.** Batching is same-resource grouping of similar work under H5. Iris keeps C006; Bruno (supervised by Jonas) gets C012. Those are three people. H5 is computed in the pseudocode (`batch_eligible`) and then never used. The batch claim is ornamental.

**C001 breaks S1/constraint 5 without using the option the policy prefers.** Celine is owner, complexity 0.88, handover cost high, Security skill 1. Supervision by Gita/Hugo *is* feasible, which is exactly when continuity should be kept. Reassigning to Hugo may be defensible on a 3h enterprise SLA, but that SLA argument is barely made, and the written rule is not applied.

**C010 is mislabeled as training.** Amal’s documented priority is diagnosis, not approval. Skill-1 prepare / skill-2 finalize satisfies H2, but it does not satisfy S3’s “documented training priorities.” It also inserts an extra touch on a case Bruno already owns and can finalize.

**The 20% / 33% training claim is cooked.** Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, C015 (9). Only C009 is a clean training hit. C010 is the wrong skill; C012 buys the quota by violating overtime. Valid share is ~11%, below target. Saying S3 is satisfied is false.

**Counts are wrong.** Gita is listed at 2 new assignments; she only keeps C008. Continuity is said to be preserved on 11/16 and broken only on C001, C003, C014. C012 is also a reassignment (Jonas → Bruno). Owned cases are 15, not 16.

These are not nits. Section 3 is the decision the rest of the document exists to justify.

## Policy algorithm: incomplete and internally wrong

The high-priority eligibility line applies Gita/Hugo supervision to *all* urgent/high work, then comments “Security only.” For Diagnosis or Negotiation that would be the wrong supervisors. Skill ≥ 2 already includes skill 2, so the OR branch is only skill 1 — and it is attached to the wrong domain.

SLA score is applied *per resource* without saying why two eligible people would get different SLA scores. Hours remaining today are almost unused; open-case count does the fairness work. Weights are “illustrative” and do not form a coherent scale (positives sum to 0.95, then two separate subtraction terms). Functions are stubs (`f()`, `penalty()`, `normalize()`). An “explainable” policy that a worker cannot recompute is only narratively explainable.

Exception handling is theater: high-OT non-urgent work requires a “manager note,” then C012 is assigned to Bruno anyway.

## Comparison is asserted, not computed

The brief asks to compare with first-available and specialist-only on this queue. The answer never runs those policies on C001–C016. The table in §4 is plausible commentary, not evidence. Claims such as “routing supervised trainees into scarce-skill areas” are mostly not what the table does (C015 correctly blocks it; C010 is the wrong skill). Resilience talk sits poorly next to giving Hugo three cases including two Security Reviews.

## Process mining / PM4Py is only partly real

Required indicator names are present. Several operationalizations are wrong:

- `filter_event_attribute_values(..., [">0.7"])` does not accept comparison expressions; it matches literal values.
- `pm4py.view_sna(perf_dfg)` is the wrong visualizer; SNA views do not take a performance DFG.
- §6 ties rework detection to `filter_case_performance`, which filters by case duration, not activity name.
- SLA breach as `sla_deadline < event timestamp` flags late *events*, not case-level misses.

The sketch is an audit collage, not a learning loop. Historical 1.8× / 2.7h / 3.3h figures are never turned into fitted weights. `discover_batches` is mentioned usefully; that does not offset the API errors.

## Socio-technical layer

Section 5 is the one place the answer clearly exceeds a scoring-model dump. Tone, continuity, training-as-investment, and “fewer cases may mean you are already loaded” are right. It still never explains *this* batch, so workers could not reconcile C012 or the fake C006–C012 batch with the story they were told.

## Net

This is a strong outline of a policy and a weak execution of that policy on the only data that matters. Hypercritically: a near-flawless answer would apply its own filters, hit the training quota without cheating, compute both baselines on the 16 cases, keep PM4Py calls valid, and get the load/continuity arithmetic right. This one does not. **6.0** is generous relative to “nearly flawless = very high”; it reflects complete structure and several correct case calls, not correctness of the recommended roster.
