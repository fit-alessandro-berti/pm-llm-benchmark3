**4.6**

This is a polished, complete-looking writeup with the right section skeleton and some real operational reasoning, but the analytical core is unreliable. Under a strict reading it does not meet the quality bar.

**Format and evidence discipline.** The nine required headings are present, but an extra `# Conclusions` section violates the “exactly these top-level headings” rule. More serious is invented precision. Section 4 reports specific means, P90/P95s, SLAs, Ginis, and handover percentages as if they were model outputs. They are not produced by the code in §8, have no intervals, and are not labeled as speculative bounds. That breaks “do not invent observations,” “distinguish observations from estimates,” and “uncertainty must be explicit.”

**Section 2 is the decisive failure.** Several load and capacity numbers are simply wrong, and one modeling choice is conceptually incorrect.

- Case-type totals are mis-added: **M is 9.84, not 10.34; E is 11.06, not 10.66; S is 7.86, not 8.31.**
- Weighted service midpoints are wrong: Electrical is **32.82, not 32.4**; Software is **38.5, not 39.5**.
- Offered load for ME/ES uses `0.5×` branch counts. Parallelism changes *makespan*, not work content. An ME case still consumes a full Mechanical *and* a full Electrical service. This understates diagnostic load.
- Capacity arithmetic is inconsistent with the authors’ own table: five 450-minute shifts plus Farah’s 240 is **2490, not 2940**. Software equivalent capacity is **~981 min, not 714**. Electrical omits Farah (multiplier 1.40) and the written 1224 does not match `450/1.35+450+450+450/1.3≈1579`.
- **U1 arrivals are double-counted** (`42−16+11.9+15.3+(12+10+6+3)`). The correct total is about **53, not 70**; the 1.67× scale and “Software at 100%” claim are artifacts.
- U2 renormalization is not carried through (`+4 E, +2 ES` is too high). U4 doubles Intake/Validation tail probabilities even though the shock is diagnostic-only.
- The required **time-block × skill** view is mostly a daily aggregate. The binding structure is intra-day: Farah ends at 12:00, Emil starts at 12:00, Chen is the only all-day software person and also the fastest electrician, and lunch breaks stack at midday. That is the actual bottleneck-migration story, and it is largely missing.

Because U3/U5 “Software 120%/201%, infeasible/collapse” rest on these errors, later policy rankings are not earned. Directionally, software/electrical are tighter than intake/validation and U3/U5 are the nasty shocks — but magnitudes, and therefore “infeasible vs stressed,” are not trustworthy.

**Policies (§3) are implementable in tone, wrong in eligibility.** Alpha/Beta exclude **Chen from Electrical (1.00, best)** and **Ada from Mechanical (1.00, best)**. Beta is labeled “full pooling” but then restricts Mechanical to Ben/Deepa. That is not pooling to any qualified resource. Gamma’s 40/60% reservations are not shown to be jointly feasible on overlapping people. Parallel/sequential and preemption rules are clearer than average, which is why this section is only middling rather than poor.

**§4–5 overclaim.** Tables cover the requested columns, but they are a narrative consistent with the broken load model, not an audit trail. Beta often has better base and U1 P1 SLA in the authors’ own table, yet Gamma “dominates.” Mode-switch thresholds are operationally useful; the quantitative justification is not.

**§6–7 and §9 are the strongest parts.** Gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption thrashing, validation as a downstream bottleneck, and interpretability are handled at the right level. The event schema hits the required attributes. The pilot is staged and P1-protective. Even here there is leftover overconfidence (success criteria that assume the fictional SLAs).

**§8 does not satisfy the Python contract.** Tables are encoded, and an event log *schema* exists, but `run()` ignores policy, queues, shifts, contention, U2, the IT outage, ME/ES split, setup, and preemption (`pass`). Every case is forced through Ada → Ben → Ada on a single accumulating clock, so all policies would look alike. That is not a “scaffold with omissions identified”; it is a stub that cannot support §4. `pm4py.get_service_time` is never called. There are no repeated seeds. Case-level PM4Py service/wait fields are not labeled or separated from stage queues. Handover-network use assumes a dict API that may not match PM4Py.

**Smaller but real defects.** Uniform-within-block arrivals are called conservative; clustering is the conservative queue assumption. Handover is defined as non-resource time in §1 and then treated as blocking elapsed time everywhere else. Assumption 1.4 invents “only if P1 would miss SLA.” Objective weights are arbitrary with no sensitivity. Fairness-as-Gini is never computed.

**Net.** This reads like a strong consultant memo and a weak model. Process-mining monitoring design and the specialization-vs-pooling tension are competent; offered-load math, shift-level capacity, and the required executable comparison are not. A nearly flawless answer would get 9–10. After material arithmetic errors, a load-definition mistake, fabricated scenario metrics, and a non-comparing simulator, 4.6 is already generous.
