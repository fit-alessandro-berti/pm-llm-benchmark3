**4.0**

The write-up is well organized and the qualitative story (morning S1 surge, then S2 congestion, C2 outage as an aggravating interval) is directionally right. Case-level arithmetic on *fully observed* timestamps is mostly correct, missing vs OPEN vs waiting are conceptually distinguished, and the heading structure matches the spec. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading the answer is not close to reliable: core aggregates are wrong or irreproducible, the authors contradict their own assumptions, Little’s Law is staged rather than checked, and the required Python does not do what the prompt demanded.

**Case table vs later sections.** Observed waits/services for P01–P04, P06, P08–P10, P12–P14, P16–P22 match the grid. Missing-start windows are the right *kind* of bound (resource free time, queue entry, completion). Then the answer breaks its own rules. Section 2 / §6 force a 5-minute minimum service; the §3 ranges allow 0-minute service. Midpoints are not the midpoints of the ranges printed in §6. P05/P07/P11/P15 service intervals are therefore not internally defined.

**Station metrics are not recoverable from the data.**  
- Fully observed S1 waits (18 cases) average 63.3 min; with midpoint imputations for P07/P15 the 20 completed S1 waits average 65 min. **61.5 min is unexplained.**  
- Fully observed S2 waits average ~30.8 min; with the stated midpoints, 14 completed waits average ~33.6 min. **36.4 min is unexplained.**  
- Fully observed S1 service averages ~28.1 min (max 30, not 35). Adding the stated midpoints gives ~27.3 min, not 26.8. “Observed 20–35 min” mixes raw data with an imputation extreme.  
- Time-integral S1 occupancy is exactly determined by arrivals and S1 ends (missing starts do not affect station WIP): 2085 case-minutes / 360 = **5.79**, not ~5.3.  
- Under the authors’ own A1 (departures before arrivals at a tied timestamp), S1 peak WIP is **8**, not 9. Peak 9 only appears if arrivals are processed first — the opposite convention. “Queue peaks at 7” and “WIP peaks at 9 at 11:50–12:00” cannot both be true under A1 (7 queued + 2 busy = 9 would require P22 or a double count; 11:50 has P01–P21 arrived and P01–P13 finished → 8).  
- S1 busy time in-window is I2 = 290 plus I1 ∈ [245, 315] if 0-length service is allowed, or about [255, 315] with a 5-minute floor, plus P21’s 30 elapsed minutes: **about 535–605 min (74–84%)**, central ~575 min (~80%). Reported **560–630 min / 77.8–87.5% / 81.9%** overstates the upper bound (630 exceeds even max imputation). S2 busy time is 480 + P05 + P11 + 30 + 5 ∈ **[515, 570]** (or [525, 570] with a 5-minute floor), not 535–585. 83% of 705 is 585, which is 15 minutes above the physical maximum.

These are not rounding disagreements. They are incorrect occupancy accounting dressed as one-decimal utilization.

**False operational claims.** “S1 fully cleared its queue by 13:30” is false: P21 is still in service and **P22 is still waiting at 14:00**. Completions “0 during 10:50–11:00 and 1 case/hr during 11:00–11:40” depends on endpoint tricks; P07 ends 11:00 and P09 ends 11:40. Subperiod 08:00–10:00 at 7.0/h includes the 10:00 arrival after A1 said departures/period cuts come first. Capacity “reduced to 1.5” during the outage is rate language that collides with A3’s “one resource.”

**Little’s Law is not a check.** The 08:30–12:30 window does not contain 16 S1 arrivals (P06–P22 is 17; there is no clean 16-arrival cut that is also 4.00/h). W = 65 + 27.5 contradicts the 61.5 they just published. L̂ = 6.16 vs “observed 6.10” and “~1%” is spurious precision on a 5-minute grid with missing starts and an off-by-one arrival count. A real interval LL check is the case-time integral (which they never compute) and must treat boundary inventory; they do neither.

**Rounding is announced and then ignored.** A2 is fine. Duration uncertainty of ±5 minutes is never propagated into WIP, utilization, or rates. §6 is only a missing-start sweep, and even that sweep reports service-time means that fold P15/P17 *elapsed* service into “mean service,” which is the confusion the rubric forbids.

**Python fails the spec.** `parse_time` maps MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same `None` (requirement 2). Only hardcoded midpoints are used; there is no optimistic/central/pessimistic implementation (requirement 5). There is **no WIP/queue trajectory**. Cases still waiting never enter `df_events`, so open-queue work is dropped from the printed station table (quality bar: do not discard open cases). Utilization is a single midpoint print, not a range. Arrival-to-S1 wait is not an event, so `insert_case_service_waiting_time` cannot recover case waiting as defined in §2; the snippet never says that. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is called but not really interpreted. The convert-or-explain comment is the one requirement that is met. Printed output cannot reproduce §§3–4.

**What survives.** Tandem structure, C2’s 15-minute hole, longer S2 service, morning λ above S1 capacity, and an afternoon S2 queue of 4 + 2 in service at 14:00 are supported. Completed-case sojourns that depend only on arrival and S2 end are invariant to missing starts. That is a real but narrow result. It does not underwrite the precise tables, the LL “validation,” the 9-case peak, the 81.9%/79.4% utilizations, or the claim that S1 is empty after 13:30.

Polished narrative over incorrect and irreproducible measurement. Not a collapse, not close to flawless.
