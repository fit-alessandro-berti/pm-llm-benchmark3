**7.3**

The answer is a strong, well-structured transportability analysis that does the main intellectual work correctly: it refuses a literal copy of Site A, ties the transferable object to a partial-order join rather than a nine-hour before/after delta, keeps statutory originals and legal review as hard constraints, and designs a segmented pilot that can separate mechanism failure from implementation failure. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading the quantitative core, one process specification, and a few internal consistencies are weak enough to keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is genuinely good.** Section 1 covers the required structural features and, more importantly, draws design consequences from them instead of restating the prompt. The transportability graph includes the mandated nodes, uses selection nodes for site differences, and the 180–280 word commentary correctly says that a shared AND-join is not identifying. Section 3 is the best part of the memo: every major A metric and co-intervention is treated as confounded, and none of the confidence numbers treat 41→32 h as an effect. Sections 4, 6 (the eligibility rules), 7 (T1 vs T2), 9, and 10 are aligned with that causal story. The PM4Py block is not a sequential toy: Identity is unordered with the document path, Assessment has both predecessors, and the legal LOOP plus supplement partial order is a reasonable encoding. Limitations comments match the prompt.

**Material quantitative flaw (Section 5).** The optimistic portfolio median of 4–7 h is not supported by the same memo’s structure. For the 62% originals-required stratum, a 3.4 h identity service that can start at registration is typically off the critical path once an 18 h receipt delay and 8.1 h document service are admitted. Identity∥receipt is then mostly slack, not cycle-time reduction. If more than half the cases get ~0 structural gain, a 4–7 h movement in the *portfolio median* requires an extra, unstated assumption that the median case is digital or that originals cases also accelerate. The digital-only row repeats a softer version of the same error: “whole portfolio: 1 to 3 h.” The author notes that quantiles are not share-weighted averages, then still reports a portfolio-median range that looks like informal down-weighting of the eligible-group effect. That is the central quantitative-scenario failure. Confidence 25 on the optimistic row does not repair an ill-justified range.

Sign convention in the same table is sloppy. The footnote says negative changes are improvements, but most cells are unsigned magnitudes while p90 harm is written with `+`. A reader cannot parse “0 to +2 h” next to “4 to 7 h” without guessing. The bottleneck-shift write-up also says *mean* cycle falls while the column is median.

**Specification error (Section 6).** `Post-assessment` already contains `Decision`, then the closing line is `Post-assessment ; Decision ; Notify`. That is a duplicated decision in the compact POWL. The later edge list is cleaner and does not repeat the mistake, so this is a local inconsistency rather than a wrong intervention—but the prompt asked for a dependency-preserving process specification, and this one is internally contradictory.

**Pilot design is conceptually right and operationally under-solved.** Distinguishing C / T1 (legacy lock) / T2 (dual ownership), holding huddles and staffing fixed, and using overlap → join-ready → cycle as the mechanism chain is the correct learning design. Two problems remain. First, case-level 1:1:1 randomization across four identity, seven document, three assessor, and two legal specialists cannot be “ring-fenced” in any serious way; “no reviewer owns treatment and control tasks at once” with those pool sizes is close to implicit clustering, which the power paragraph never treats. Assumption 8 names the issue and then leaves the table’s interference control looking stronger than it is. Second, Section 6 keeps legal/high-complexity cases out of phase 1, while Section 7 wants ~250 legal cases and 60 supplement loops *per version*. Those two designs are not the same experiment.

**Smaller defects that a strict grader should still count.** WIP caps 16/21/9/4 are numerology (roughly 4×, 3×, 3×, 2× headcount) with little queueing justification beyond “tune later.” Repeat Document Check / Repeat Assessment as distinct labels will not match Site B’s DFG, where supplements return to the same activities. The IT-lock row’s claim of *worse* cycle time is only loosely motivated (workarounds, back-entry); a more natural forecast is a null on true time and a large bias on measured overlap. Section 3’s “transferable component” cells sometimes stretch to “this is a variable we should measure,” which is not the same as a transferable effect. The graph adds useful extra nodes (`verification_queue_load`, `measured_overlap`) but also leaves several theoretically important arrows unsigned and undiscussed (specialization → rework, legal capacity → rework).

**What does *not* fail.** The answer does not copy simultaneous Document Check creation for originals-required cases. It does not transport 9 h. It does anticipate bottleneck shift, including a scenario in which the center improves and the tail worsens. It does not treat recorded overlap as true overlap. The recommendation `conditional go` with a digital/nonlegal first stratum is the only defensible binary choice given the evidence.

**Net.** This is a high-quality analogical memo with the right causal objects and the right refusal to copy Site A. It is not a 9–10 because the quantitative transport ranges are not faithful to the author’s own critical-path logic, the compact process spec disagrees with itself on Decision, and the pilot’s identification strategy is stronger on paper than in the specialist-pool constraint set. Those are not cosmetic nits; they sit on the exact dimensions the prompt said would be scored.
