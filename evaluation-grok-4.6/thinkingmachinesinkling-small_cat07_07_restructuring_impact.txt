**7.2**

The answer is a strong, well-structured forecast that hits every required heading, stays conditional, treats East differently from North/South, and keeps fairness and learning in view. It is not nearly flawless. Technical looseness in the PM4Py sketch, a biased pilot, and a few internal inconsistencies keep it well below a high score.

**What works.** The mechanism map ties each restructuring action to a concrete log signal, which is the core of the task. The three scenarios are internally consistent and use the given handover and team tables rather than generic org-change language. The indicator table correctly splits leading queue/network signals from lagging cycle-time and SLA outcomes. Section 4 is the strongest part: tail latency, high-complexity paths, local-knowledge loss, fairness, skill atrophy, and accountability blur are all framed as effects that a stable mean cycle time can hide. The recommendation is appropriately “pilot first, do not trust early averages,” and it states what management should not infer.

**PM4Py and measurement errors (section 7, and leakage into 1–3).** This is the largest defect. `discover_handover_of_work_network` is a *resource* SNA, not a team/queue diagram. The write-up repeatedly treats “Technical Shared Service” as if it were a node you can read off the network without grouping `org:resource` into roles. That conflates activities, queues, and people. Subcontracting is described as “delegation / return of advice”; in PM4Py it is the A–B–A resource pattern, so the accountability claims built on it are only loosely grounded. The code presents invented or wrong API: `discover_performance_dfg(..., format="dict")` is not the high-level signature; `org:resource_shifted` is not a log column; `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is assumed to yield `case_duration`. `filter_event_attribute_values` is applied to case-level fields (`case_complexity`, `customer_segment`) without a case-level filter. `cross_team_rate` is a `pass`. `filter_variants` and `get_service_time` are named in the brief and then barely used. For a section that is supposed to show how to *compare before and after*, this is incomplete and partly incorrect.

**Pilot design.** Scoping the pilot to East only is a real design error. East is the team most likely to improve (E_Tech at 82%). A successful East pilot does not identify harm in North/South, which is the central distributional risk the rest of the memo describes. Rotating a North/South specialist into the shared pool also contaminates the controls. The memo later says learning and fairness take 3–6 months, then proposes 8+10+8 weeks. Those two claims do not fit together. Guardrails are otherwise specific and usable.

**Smaller analytical flaws.** Optimistic legal/risk wait of ~9h is hard to square with Legal/Risk already at 91–93% utilization and *higher* routed volume (12 vs 9 per 100). “Nominal handover reduction” is asserted in the prompt’s management story; the map is more honest that edges are renamed and centralized, but the code never actually computes handovers per 100 cases, which is the scenario’s main unit. Working-together is listed more than interpreted. Pre/post confounding (seasonality, mix shift) is not mentioned.

**Constraint adherence.** Good: not uniformly pro or con, not mean-cycle-time-only, East treated as a different regime, fairness and learning kept in the recommendation. The answer does not smuggle in a full rollout.

Net: a competent process-mining org-design memo with the right shape and the right caution, undermined by imprecise SNA semantics, a non-credible PM4Py comparison script, and a pilot that cannot falsify the main downside. That is clearly above mid-pack and clearly not near-flawless.
