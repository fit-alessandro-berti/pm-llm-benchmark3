**What holds up**

Process rules are handled carefully. Originals precede document check; identity may run earlier; no-originals concurrency is allowed; RMI is issued from assessment, not from document check; a new document check and assessment follow RMI; preliminary C1 does not replace final C1; S1 may overlap other work. The K-219 and K-411 derivations correctly show that M1’s signing window and the assessment queue can absorb most of the local speedup. Section 4 covers reservation of C1, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment peaks, weekend external delay, and timestamp vs true concurrency. The ranking (K-411 > K-219 > K-104 > K-337) is defensible if the criterion is system robustness rather than single-case speed. Word counts and table schemas match the spec.

**Material flaws**

1. **Best-case clocks fight the snapshots.** Tue 08:00 shows C1 = 16 h and A2 = 6 h; Wed 08:00 shows A2 = 5 h. Treating C1pre as Tue 10:00–12:00, A2 as Tue 10:00 (K-219), or A2 as Wed 08:00 (K-411) requires jumping those queues. “Reserve a provisional slot” is not the same as pre-empting C1 the next morning. A reserved slot after a 16–20 h C1 backlog is more likely Tuesday evening or Wednesday, especially after the two urgent compliance cases added Mon 16:00 — a snapshot detail the answer never uses.

2. **Internal inconsistency.** The K-104 table gives cycle-time change **−4 h to +1 h**; the derivation worst case is **+2 h** (Notify 20:40 vs 18:40). Same case, two numbers.

3. **Partial order is slightly wrong for high-risk rework.** `A0 < C_final` is stated as a high-risk law even when A0 is not the latest assessment. The RMI branch later adds `A_{k+1} < C_final`, so the model is recoverable, but the high-risk axiom as written is not the rule in the prompt.

4. **Path notation is sloppy.** K-104 lists I1 and D1 inside the concurrent block and again after it. That is not an explicit path; it is a draft of one.

5. **Best-case ranges are advertised as expected ranges.** Putting −10 h in the K-219 table depends on both an implausible A2 start and M1 signing Tuesday night, against a factual pattern of next-morning M1. The derivation is more careful than the table. The table is what the rubric asked to be calibrated.

6. **Missed snapshot constraints.** “One assessor in training” (Tue 08:00) is unused for K-411/K-219 assessment starts. “Two urgent compliance cases added” is unused for K-104 interference. Overnight D2 (Mon 21:00–Tue 03:00) is consistent with factual overnight D1, but never justified.

7. **Pilot power claim is wrong.** n = 30–50 per arm to detect a 2 h median shift with sd ≈ 6 h is underpowered (d ≈ 0.33 typically needs ~70/arm). Small, but it is a stated quantitative claim.

**Net.** Strong process-feasibility and interference reasoning; weaker temporal discipline and a few contradictions. That is a good expert draft, not a nearly flawless one.
