**Material flaws**

1. **I-09 is over-selected.** The prompt flags NNLS as the weakest cell for both calibration and effects. The rule claims a ×1.5 effect-interval inflation and scoring on the widened lower bound; the table still treats I-09 as T1 and dispatches it *first*. One live swarm does not identify a heterogeneous effect, and burning the earliest deadline on the least-identified case is the opposite of robustness. The adverse scenario the author themselves writes down (I-09 ≈ 0 benefit) is then realized on the slot that delays I-02.

2. **The €1,440 spillover charge is invented and then treated as policy.** Monetizing six unknown untreated cases at €12k converts a sensitivity assumption into a hard eligibility threshold. That single number is why I-07 is T4 and I-01/I-10 are “marginal.” The problem asked for explicit uncertainty about externalities, not a false precision shadow price. Labeling the assumption does not stop it from driving the allocation.

3. **Calibration is applied inconsistently.** Segment offsets hit `p` but not the effect ranges, except a stated-and-then-ignored NNLS widening. For I-04, Strategic overprediction puts adjusted `p` near 0.36–0.43 while the upper effect is 0.28 — barely feasible — and the midpoint 0.19 is used as if it were segment-neutral. 4. **Rule and numbers diverge.** NNLS widening, EV_low as a hard T1 test, and “do not treat ranges as causal effects” are announced, then the expected-impact table uses raw midpoints/upper-halves as if they were known reductions. Adverse net “−€3k to +€1k” and “worst draw −€8k” are rounded past the arithmetic the author just showed (~+€0.4k if all lower bounds bind; ~−€7.6k on the worst combination they define).

A €100k Strategic case (I-08) is deferred on a thin adverse EV, which is defensible as option value, but the same essay then invokes “strategic relationship” as a possible override — an escape hatch rather than a rule.

6. **“Deployable” is overstated.** Two EV statistics, a made-up externality charge, four tiers, a data gate, a cool-down, an escalation valve, a reserve-release clock, and dispatch-time stage re-checks are a design, not an SOP. Several thresholds (€1k, €500, 0.10, 2h, 15–20% floor, 40% spillover share) are arbitrary and untested against the same uncertainty the author emphasizes.

**Lesser issues**

I-06’s eligibility cell is not a yes/no. False-alarm burden is charged in full on every swarm (allowed by the prompt’s sketch, but it double-penalizes high-burden Strategic cases already hit by downward `p` adjustment). Value-of-information is asserted for I-09 without a VoI bound. Exploration-on-a-live-SLA-case sits awkwardly next to the more careful equipoise-band proposal.

The piece would be in the high 8s if I-09 were wait/caution rather than first dispatch, if spillover stayed in breach units with a sensitivity band, and if every announced robustness adjustment actually appeared in the scores. As written, the framework is better than the portfolio it produces.
