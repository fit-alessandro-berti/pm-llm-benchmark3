**8.1**

This is a strong, internally consistent OCEL construction and analysis write-up. The four tables match the narrative, referential integrity is real (not decorative), flattening arithmetic checks out, and the object-centric commentary is mostly precise. It is not near-flawless. Several design claims overreach the code, and a few runtime assumptions are brittle.

**What is actually right.** All 17 events, 12 objects, and the stated O2O facts are present. Timestamps are timezone-aware. Payment amounts stay on `events`. Source E2O participation for E06, E07, and E11 is correct, I2 is not given a fabricated reservation, P1 is the multi-order convergence point, and V1 is a single invoice billed to both orders. The derived-link census (34 source + 15 derived = 49) and the flattening sizes (Order 20, Package 6, Delivery 4, Invoice 3) match the relation policy. O100/O200 traces, the P1 vs P2 duration gap (3h vs 22h45m), and the Invoice `B→Y→Y` reading are correct. Required pipeline steps, validations, and section structure are all there.

**Provenance is documented, not enforced.** Section 1 claims derived links will not be confused with source facts via `relation_origin`, `context_*` qualifiers, and `source_relations`. Discovery is then run only on the mixed 49-row table. `source_relations` is never printed, compared, or wrapped in a second `OCEL`. Qualifiers and provenance are not constraints in PM4Py discovery, which the author admits—so the “prevention” is an audit comment, not a property of the analysis the script actually produces. That is a real gap against the required trade-off/control discussion.

**Non-standard columns are pushed into the constructor.** `relation_origin` on `relations` is useful for humans and risky for `OCEL(...)`. The compatibility note isolates only the constructor line, not extra relation attributes, flattening column names, or return shapes.

**API shape is assumed, then asserted.** Unpacking `graph, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat)`, sorting on `case:concept:name` / `time:timestamp`, and `assert len(flat) == ...` will crash on plausible PM4Py variants. `str(...dt.tz) == "UTC"` is also version-fragile. These are not isolated adapters; they are hard failure points in the happy path.

**Small semantic nits.**
- M2 jointly settles V1 in the narrative, but both payments are only `partially_settles`. Allowed, slightly weaker than the permitted `completes_settlement` for M2.
- Order DFG self-loops `P→P`, `S→S`, `D→D` are correctly called branch artifacts; O100’s `P→S` (Pack P2 then Dispatch P1) is the same artifact and is not called out.
- Logistics enrichment is coherent but procedural: confirmation hops Delivery→Package, dispatch hops Package→Item→Order, packing does not hop to Delivery. That asymmetry is under-explained relative to the care taken elsewhere.
- Section 2 adds prose after the single code block.

**Not deducted.** No hidden gold-standard layout was required. Choosing enriched logistics E2O is defensible. Not allocating M1/M2 to orders is correct. The OC Petri net is not over-interpreted. Unrestricted O2O closure to 204 rows is a good warning.

**Bottom line.** Semantically this is one of the better possible answers: the business story survives, the numbers reconcile, and the flattened/object-centric predictions are mostly exact. Strictly, the unused source/derived split, constructor impurity, and unshimmmed PM4Py shapes keep it well below a 9.
