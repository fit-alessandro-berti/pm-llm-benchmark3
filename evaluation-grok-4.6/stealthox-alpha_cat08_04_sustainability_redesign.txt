**Factual and logical errors.**

- **Rework impact is mis-attributed.** The 32 kWh and 1.8 kg figures are path averages for `MTO → CNC_B → inspection → rework → ground`, not the incremental cost of “each rework loop.” First-pass CNC, heat, inspection, and transport are inside that 32 kWh. Treating the whole path as the loop overstates the lever.
- **Obsolescence is over-interpreted.** “Not shipped within 14 days” is turned into “pure loss” of embodied energy and material. The evidence is delayed shipment under forecast error, not confirmed scrap or write-off. Inventory can still ship later. This inflates the batching penalty and is a lifecycle-allocation error.
- **Empty van returns are placed inside “measured via event attributes.”** They are qualitative notes, not logged events. The boundary section overclaims measurement.
- **S4’s “+~24h median” is the air–ground transit delta, not demonstrated cycle-time change.** Priority picking is supposed to offset warehouse time; SC-stock median is already 18h versus 31h for Regular stock. Using 24h as the cycle-time hit is an overstatement.
- **S3 cycle-time reasoning is incomplete.** The log says CNC_B is faster and dirtier. Diverting to CNC_A avoids some 160h rework paths, but first-pass cases can get slower, not faster. The write-up states cycle time “falls for diverted cases” too cleanly.
- **S2’s “up to ~50%” energy claim is the CNC_A per-item swing from batch 1–3 to 12–20, not the expected order-level effect.** Heat treat, inspection, and mixed batch sizes dilute that number. It reads as an intervention impact, which the prompt forbids inventing.
- **S9 loosely equates 760 emergency productions with precautionary volume.** Critical-downtime is 11% of cases; emergency production is a subset that also serves true stops. The split is the right idea; the mapping is too neat.
