**7.4**

The audit is structurally complete, process-aware, and mostly well judged. It keeps the hard distinctions the rubric cares about: full-trace SHAP is not treated as an early explanation; `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is not collapsed into the same-timestamp impossibility; congestion and prefix age are preserved; region/channel are retained as proxies rather than deleted; and the 65% `final_handler_team` coincidence is correctly rejected. The JSON explanations do not replay leaked outcomes. Those are real strengths.

It is not nearly flawless.

**Section 1 is the main weakness.** Four of five sequence patterns are typed as “Legitimate future signal” with Low/Medium severity. For a prediction whose admissible horizon is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`, those patterns are **t0-inadmissible temporal leakage**, even when they are later process-legal. The writeup does eventually say they are “not for t0 predictions,” but the finding type and severity understate the defect. A stricter audit would split: (a) unavailable at `t0` vs (b) process-impossible. `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` belongs in (a); `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` belongs in (b), possibly also (a). `Documents Requested → Documents Received` and `Damage Assessment Completed → Settlement Decision Recorded` are not low-severity curiosities if the explainer is attributing ±0.09–0.12 to them at `t0`.

A second gap: the team’s causal slogans (“Appeals cause breaches,” “The final team determines delay,” “Document rework is an early warning”) are never systematically tagged as **unsupported causal stories**, which the prompt explicitly required as a distinct category. Remediation tables imply the point; they do not audit it.

A third process miss: `num_document_requests_total → num_document_requests_up_to_t0` is the right repair, but at this `t0` that prefix count is typically 0, because evidence cycles start after coverage check. Calling repaired document-rework an “early warning” would still be process-incoherent. That subtlety is absent.

**Smaller consistency problems.** `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is “unusable until lineage is repaired” in §3 and then a Case Y “credible driver” on an “assuming SQL is fixed” hedge. If the stored value may include post-`t0` claims, the value `3` cannot be treated as known. `initial_reserve` is accepted as t0-valid with only a presumption; lineage was not given. Case Z’s uncertainty note omits `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, which Case Z actually uses and which can include same-day uploads after `t0`.

**Section 2 is competent but generic.** Prefix vs full-trace value change, footprint/alignment, missing sequence index, and “a frequent DFG edge is not a rule” are the right instruments. The suite is not instantiated tightly enough: at this `t0`, Appeal, Settlement Decision, and Claim Closed are not enabled; Documents Requested is; Fraud Investigation is only trigger-enabled and is not in the given prefixes. That enablement check is described in the abstract and underused on the actual patterns.

**Sections 3–5 are mostly sound.** The five-class taxonomy matches the prompt. Causal/actionable/fairness boundaries are stated. Case X/Y status `recompute` is better than a blanket invalidate. Case Z `invalidate` is defensible. The recommendation list hits every required heading. Remaining nits: the feature store “must reject any feature lacking a historical timestamp” overclaims (static attributes such as region do not need event timestamps); “adversarial debiasing” is an unearned default fairness fix; Case X’s causal boundary slightly slides from “associated” to “such claims require more processing time.”

Net: a credible, high-coverage audit with the central leakage cases right, weakened by mis-typed future sequence explanations, a missing causal-story pass, and a few lineage inconsistencies. That is clearly above competent and clearly short of excellent under a strict reading.
