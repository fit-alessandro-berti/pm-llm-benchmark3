## Defects that keep this well below a top score

**1. A better QC explanation is missed.**
The best account of C04 beating C06 is not a frozen initial batch. C06 arrives at 10:25 *during* C03 (10:18–10:32). If the next job is locked when the current job starts, then at 10:18 the SEPT remainder is C04 (22) vs C01 (36) vs C05 (40), so C04 is already committed before C06 exists. That is more parsimonious than “SEPT within a frozen C01–C04 batch,” and it does not require C06 to be ineligible. The 10:32 discrimination test therefore compares live SEPT to the wrong competitor. “Batch commitments” in the diagnosis is a vague gesture at this, not the mechanism.

**2. Section 1 overclaims support that Section 2 itself shows is confounded.**
- D06 is listed as FIFO support, but at 11:30 FIFO-among-D-X and the exact appointment coincide. Section 2 states that; the diagnosis table does not.
- D07 before D10 is listed as local FIFO support, but D07 is uniquely qualified for D-X while D10 is not. Unique-skill preference and FIFO are not separated at 11:48. The 11:36 test about D10 being locked to D-X is related, not a substitute.

**3. QA’s “strongest contradicting episode” does not contradict the stated rule.**
The diagnosis is already *soft*, non-preemptive appointments. A06 starting at 09:20, five minutes late and in FIFO order, is what that rule predicts. The cell is supposed to show the hardest evidence *against* the working diagnosis; instead it challenges a stricter rule the authors already rejected. Appointment structure is identified from essentially one discriminating start (A03). Confidence 85 is high for that.

**4. QC’s distinctive field is not in the decision table.**
The required columns are FIFO / priority / SEPT / appointment / skill. For QC the live competitors are also EDD and deadline-feasibility. Those appear in prose (C05, C01 vs C07) but not as predictions at 10:10, 10:18, 10:32, 11:34, 11:40. At 10:10 EDD is C03 (or C05), not C02; that should sit next to the SEPT hit so the reader sees the first start is *not* deadline-driven.

**5. Small unclarities and leftover gaps.**
- QA 08:30–08:32 is noted and then dropped; it is the same two-minute start lag as A01 and may be friction, not policy, but it is still an unexplained idle at a decision epoch.
- “Skill-based prediction: Any W” is not a prediction; the conventions paragraph has to carry it.
- QB 09:16 is consistent with both prio-1 preemption *and* SRPT if the reason code is ignored; the text leans on “urgent arrival” without stating that the duration comparison is equivalent. Using the supplied reason is fair; the equivalence should still be explicit.
- One confidence number for QD still compresses “skills ~high, ranking ~medium,” even though the following paragraph corrects it.

## What is not wrong

I did not find arithmetic errors in segment lengths, remaining-time calculations, deadline lateness, or calendar-fit checks (D05 at 11:36 overruns noon; D10 does not). The answer does not invent jobs, skills, or extra work. It does not treat every FIFO violation as priority. Preemptive vs non-preemptive is handled at busy vs free servers. Assumptions (FIFO tie-break, no lost progress on B01, conjunctive skills, supplied jobs = backlog) are labeled.

## Bottom line

This is a competent process-mining diagnosis with the right architecture and almost no factual slips. Under a strict bar it loses a substantial amount for (i) the wrong QC commitment model, (ii) FIFO credit for choices that appointments or unique skills also predict, and (iii) a contradicting-episode column that does not contradict. Those are identification errors, not writing nits. A nearly flawless answer would have put one-step SEPT lock-in on the QC table, split D07/D10 into FIFO vs specialist routing, and matched confidence and “contradiction” cells to the rule actually claimed.
