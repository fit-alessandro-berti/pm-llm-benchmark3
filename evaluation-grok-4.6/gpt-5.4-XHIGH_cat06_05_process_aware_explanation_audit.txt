**7.6**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that hits the main quality bar: it catches both obvious leakage and subtler lineage defects, keeps congestion and prefix-age signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as later-feasible but still inadmissible at `t0`, and keeps predictive / causal / actionable / fairness standards distinct. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-model and explanation-method errors are real, not cosmetic.

### What is actually good
Section 1 is essentially complete. All twelve globals, all five sequence patterns, and the local-only features (`claimant_channel`, `final_decision`, `initial_reserve`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`) are classified with the right failure mode. The subtle defects are seen: extraction-date SQL, nightly 23:00 refresh, missing historization on `final_handler_team`, mutable `risk_score_current`, and same-timestamp order without a sequence index. `queue_length_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are correctly preserved. Section 3’s five-way split is the right conceptual frame, and the Case Z note is the right posture (invalidate, do not invent a clean story). The JSON does not replay leaked outcomes.

### Material defects

**1. Section 2 bungles the requested enabledness taxonomy.**  
This is the most serious error because the prompt explicitly asks whether an activity is enabled, impossible, or merely future-possible at `t0`. The answer assigns the labels backwards. After `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`, `Documents Requested` and `Damage Assessment Completed` are *enabled* next (XOR fast-track vs evidence). They are labeled only “future-possible.” `Settlement Decision Recorded` and `Claim Closed` are *future-possible but not enabled*; they are labeled “impossible.” Appeal is not impossible as a later event; it is disabled until Partial/Deny. “Impossible at `t0`” is used as a synonym for “not yet observed,” which is exactly the confusion a process-aware audit is supposed to prevent. The following sentence about not treating future events as known is correct, so the practical conclusion is saved, but the formal check is wrong.

**2. Local SHAP values from a leaked model are recycled as if they were still locally valid.**  
Cases X and Y are correctly marked `recompute`, but `credible_drivers` still treat the leaked model’s directions/magnitudes as case-level facts (`queue_length = 18` increases risk, reserve increases risk, 1.2h decreases risk). Once `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, and `full_trace_variant_id` are in the model, attributions to the admissible features are not identified. The honest statement is: these features are *conceptually* admissible and process-plausible; their contribution to *this* score is unknown until prefix-only retraining. That distinction is blurred.

**3. `initial_reserve` is promoted too far.**  
No lineage is given. Section 1 correctly says “lineage proof needed,” then Section 3 parks it under “defensible predictive signal” and Case Y uses it as the main upward driver. That is inconsistent caution. It belongs in a conditional bucket, not as the spine of the revised narrative.

**4. `final_handler_team` is under-analyzed.**  
“High” plus “65% overlap does not prove availability” is directionally right and still too soft. The feature is the *final* team. The informative part is very likely the 35% of cases that were reassigned after `t0` (escalation, SIU, specialist closure). That is classic leakage through a field that is *usually* equal to a legitimate early value. This should be critical, with that mechanism named.

### Smaller but real issues
- `total_case_duration_hours` is not merely “downstream of the target.” Against a 10-day breach label it is near-label leakage. The answer understates that.
- A repaired `num_document_requests` prefix count at this `t0` is almost always 0, because evidence cycles start *after* coverage check. “Replace with a prefix count” is therefore not an early-warning feature at this prediction point; only backlog/state known at `t0` would be.
- `claimant_channel` is dropped from Case X rather than retained as an available-but-non-causal association pending fairness review. That sits slightly against “do not simply delete every correlated feature,” even though Section 3 handles the class correctly.
- Week 52 is the holiday alignment already given in the lineage notes; calling it only a generic “weak calendar proxy” is vaguer than the evidence.
- `last_resource_role = Special Investigation Unit` is caught as last-state leakage, but the extra process point is missed: that value also implies a fraud investigation occurred, i.e., a second leaked future activity.
- Employee-experience-as-of-extraction is a stated store-wide defect and never appears even as a monitoring/example item in Section 5.

### Why this is not lower
The answer is still clearly in the “strong” band of the rubric. It does not accept full-trace SHAP as an early explanation, does not declare every correlate causal, and does the Closed→Damage vs Appeal→Decision distinction correctly in Sections 1 and 3. Format compliance is exact. The failures are expert-level imprecision, not beginner leakage-blindness.

A near-flawless version would fix the enabled/future/impossible table, refuse to reuse contaminated local attributions, keep `initial_reserve` conditional, and treat final-team disagreement as the leaked signal. This version is a credible audit with several mistakes a strict reviewer should not let through.
