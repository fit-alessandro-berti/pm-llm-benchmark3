**7.2**

The memo chooses the right alternative (E), stays inside the stated constraints, and is structurally complete. It is not close to flawless.

**Executive memo.** Decision-oriented and correctly rejects A and pure B. Two problems sit in the highest-visibility section. First, the composition claim is backwards: “Pilot regions already contained more apartments and older stock … **so** aggregate negatives are not solely a composition artefact.” A harder mix makes the aggregate look *worse*; the reason the harm is not only mix is *within-segment* deterioration, which the memo does not use to justify the “so.” Section 3 later gets this right, which makes the memo slip sloppier, not better. Second, “driven by single-coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling” is a causal claim presented as if it were a result. The pilot confounds ownership, skip rule, and activation timing. Word count is at or over 700 once the header is included. Invented operating targets (85% utilisation) appear before they are labelled as judgment.

**Evidence table.** Separation is mostly clean and well above the eight-item floor. Still not strict enough. The incomplete full-cost estimate (EUR 198 → 207) is listed as a **fact** alongside measured visible cost; the prompt itself marks that figure as an incomplete estimate. Sparse start timestamps are assumed to distort “duration metrics,” but case-level cycle time from completion events does not require activity-start times—the assumption overreaches. Several “interpretation” rows are reasonable, but the table never flags that segment attributes are missing before week 3, so early-pilot segment effects are only partially identified.

**Metric conflicts.** The mixture-of-segments account of median vs tail, and the handover-network vs working-together-network account of resilience, are the strongest parts of the answer. The fitness/precision paragraph is the worst. Low precision means the *model* allows traces *not* seen in the log. Observed alignments (especially frequent **move-on-log**) and elevated DFG edges are log behaviour. Treating those as evidence that the discovered model is “overly permissive” conflates three different artefacts: discovered-model precision, normative-model alignments, and the DFG. High fitness (0.94) is also hard to reconcile with “frequent” move-on-log if both refer to the same model; the answer never distinguishes a discovered model from a normative one. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is tabulated and then ignored—exactly the statistic that should discipline a scaling discussion. Temporal-profile edges (install → activation on apartments; billing → complaint as a new long-tail relation) are named in the memo and then under-used in the conflict section.

**Alternatives.** A–E are evaluated with advantages, risks, and conditions. The ranking E > C ≈ D > B > A is defensible. Residual weakness: C is undersold as a fallback if real-time risk classification cannot be stood up (attributes are incomplete), and D is treated mainly as delay rather than as the only design that can unconfound the three bundled interventions. Idle survey-team capacity (78% → 52%) is never used as a reason that restoring surveys is operationally cheap.

**Value of information.** The M6 / M4 / M1 ordering is sensible. Decision-change logic is present. Ranking problems remain. M7 is cheap, immediate control of the two compliance-like alignments and should outrank diagnostic reason codes (M3) if the hybrid is already being deployed. M5 is ranked too low relative to the memo’s own claim that 93% utilisation and absence-stalls are first-order. Embedding M1 (randomised skipping) while “pausing” the skip rule is only barely reconciled; a reader can still see a contradiction. M2’s last-place rationale repeats the start-timestamp error.

**Final recommendation.** Immediate action / pause / scale / measure / threshold structure is clear and implementable. Thresholds are specific but partly arbitrary (discovered-model precision ≥ 0.70 is a weak operational gate; simple-house median ≤ 11 days is loose relative to the ~8-day implied pilot result). Utilisation targets drift between 80% and 85% across sections.

Net: strong strategy, incomplete PM literacy, one inverted causal sentence in the memo, and several fact/estimate merges. That is a good answer, not a near-flawless one.
