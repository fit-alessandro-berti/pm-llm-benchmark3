**7.4**

The design is strong on the intended axes (conservative defaults, provenance, partial-order-first linearization, and explicit trade-offs), but it is not close to flawless. Several internal contradictions, an unsafe “evidence-based” merge, and implementation defects are enough to keep it well below a high score.

**What is actually good.** The decision tree matches the required taxonomy and refuses the two forbidden shortcuts: merge-on-`(activity, timestamp)` and merge-on-`have_common_object`. The event table is complete for the mandated pairs, protects e05/e14, treats e08/e09 as a harmonization collision rather than a duplicate, and keeps e09/e10 as ordered distinct steps. The ordering policy is the best part of the answer: semantic constraints are separated from deterministic fallbacks, ingestion is demoted correctly, and the warning about stamping microseconds *before* fixing order is precise. Conservative retention of 15 (17 minus e04 and e16) is arithmetically right. The `have_common_object` discussion for e13/e14 is exactly the trap the prompt asked for. Sections 8–9 are complete and aligned with the constraints.

**Serious logical break: e15.** Section 5 says the ERP payment summary must *not* be folded into e13 or e14. Section 2 already flags that merging e15 into e13 orphans M2. The code then does `verified_pair_merge(..., keep_eid="e13", drop_eids=["e15"])` on the evidence-based path. That is not a documented residual risk; it is the pipeline implementing the failure mode it just forbade. The 13–14 retained-count range is mushy for the same reason: the author never actually chose a coherent e15 policy.

**Conservative track is slightly too aggressive.** Fact 6 says e11 and e16 are the same issuance *and* that each system is authoritative for different attributes. Merging them as the default “minimum-change” action is defensible as identity resolution, but it is not the most conservative option (dual-retain with qualifiers). The table admits medium confidence and an alternative of 16 events; the pipeline then merges anyway. In code, `authority_attrs={}` with a “fill in production” comment means the stated field-level authority map is not applied, so billing-authoritative attributes are silently lost—the exact failure mode listed in §9.7.

**PM4Py block is checklist-shaped, not trustworthy.** Required names are present, `ocel_raw` is not mutated, timedelta is sequenced after sort in the conservative helper, and the verified-pair helper is specified. But:

- `ev.loc[mask, list(merged_attrs.keys())] = pd.Series(merged_attrs)` aligns on the Series index, not column names, and will corrupt or null the canonical row.
- `prefer_columns_from` copies essentially every column, which can overwrite `source` / `source_event_key` on the survivor and leave multi-system identity only in JSON.
- `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` runs on a throwaway copy that never becomes a scenario output, so the “justified use” is theatrical.
- The aggressive path ends in `if False else ocel_agg`, so it does not actually apply the required sort/timedelta discipline.
- Evidence-based e15→e13 is labeled `WARNING` and still executed.

That is not a nearly executable conservative pipeline; it is a sketch with a live semantic bug.

**Sensitivity is directionally right, quantitatively thin.** All required artifacts appear, and the eight named edges are well chosen (false Pick rework, Pack/Execute→ERPStatus, warehouse-release→carrier-accept, parallel M1/M2 captures, hidden ERP lag). Exact E2O arithmetic was available from the table (raw 44 relations; conservative union of e03/e04 and e11/e16 yields 39) and was not done. Aggressive “~10–12” could have been pinned: activity+timestamp collapse without touching e10 (different label) leaves 10 events. OTG is waved through as “unchanged,” which is fine only if no object rows are deleted—an assumption, not a check.

**Smaller nits that still count under a strict rubric.** Cross-source `source_seq` is correctly labeled non-semantic in prose, then still used as a preference for e03* vs e05. Several precedence statements lost their `<` marks, which is sloppy in an ordering section. Provenance schema in §8 is richer than anything `merge_provenance` actually writes. Unused imports and repeated deep copies are harmless but add to the impression of untested code.

**Hard constraints are mostly honored.** The answer does not drop e05/e14, does not make ingestion the business order, does not erase `original_timestamp` by design, and does not claim a total order is physical truth. Those saves it from a much lower score. The remaining gap is between a careful design memo and a consistent, implementable pipeline: the memo is good; the pipeline disagrees with itself on the hardest ambiguous case and would not run cleanly as written.
