The S/C analysis is the strong part of the submission. The submission fails the bar for a high score because the third model — the required constructive core — is internally inconsistent and the “self-contained PM4Py” artifact would not run.

The Petri-net hub at `p_both_done` does **not** implement the stated at-most-once rule for Supplier Review or Expedite Review. `t_supplier` consumes `p_both_done`, the silent exit restores it, and `t_supplier` is enabled again. The same cycle exists for Expedite Review. The formal text, the external-check dictionary, and row A3 of the comparative audit all claim the third model rejects `RD, CC, TR, SR, SR, MA, RO`. Under the net as drawn, it accepts that trace, and it also accepts unbounded SR/ER interleaving after the mandatory block. That is not a nit. The author invented A3 specifically to distinguish the third model and then scored their own model incorrectly.

The executable requirement is also missed. `pm4py.PetriNet` with string `add_place` / `add_transition` / `add_arc` and dict markings is not the PM4Py Petri-net API (`PetriNet.Place` / `PetriNet.Transition` / `Marking` / `add_arc_from_to`). The try/except ladder does not salvage this. Validation skeletons compound the problem: `pm4py.from_dataframe(...)` is called with `case:conceptual` / `conceptual:activity` and without `case_id_key`/`activity_key`; `fitness_alignments(..., cost=cost)` is not the public cost interface; `pm4py.reachability` is invented. Process-tree loop syntax (`*`) would have expressed the intended backbone more tightly and avoided most of the net bugs; the final text does not justify why that was rejected.

What remains is a careful, well-structured review of S and C with a better *policy sketch* than either input, undermined by a broken formalization and a self-contradictory audit row. That is solid mid-tier work, not a near-flawless governance deliverable.
