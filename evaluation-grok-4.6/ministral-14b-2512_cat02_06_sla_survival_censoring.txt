3.8

The submission has the required skeleton and some correct high-level instincts (competing risks, do not drop opens, transfers are not automatically non-informative). It is not a competent analysis plan. Multiple required objects are defined incorrectly, several claims are false, and the code would not run and does not implement the specified estimands.

**Section 1 — material definition errors.**  
Time origin and horizon are fine. The event-time rule is not. `duration_or_age_h` is already age at cutoff for open rows; adding `last_event_gap_h` double-counts the inactivity interval and invents follow-up times (S09 becomes 123h, not 92h). The same error is restated for “censored cases,” which are not a separate observed status. Notation reuses `t` for origin and event time.

The two estimands are not cleanly identified and are not mapped to decisions. “CIF of resolution after 72h” is left ambiguous (late-resolution mass vs. \(1-F_{\text{resolve}}(72)\) vs. CIF at 72). “Still open at 72” is closer to a useful operational target, but the required case contributions are wrong: S03 resolved at **76h**, so it was still without a terminal disposition at 72h and **must** count as still open / not resolved by the deadline. Calling it “resolved before 72h” is a fact error on one of the four mandated examples. S09 is described as censored for a 72h snapshot even though it is observed past 72h. Already-observed breaches (late Resolve) are not distinguished from residual open breaches.

**Section 2 — incomplete and inconsistent encoding.**  
One table is forced onto two estimands. Transfer “assumption 3 = drop cases” is a biased deletion rule, not a serious identifying assumption; better thirds are competing exit, independent censoring, and bound/worst-case non-resolution. Notes then call assumption 2 both “external failure” and “external resolution.” Open cases are again censored at `duration + gap`. Sensitivity “treat open as competing” is conceptually empty. Withdrawal/rejection are labeled “internal failures” without separating SLA-irrelevant exits from unresolved work.

**Section 3 — right cases, sloppy reading.**  
Survivor bias and “do not condition on completed Resolve” are directionally correct. The gap calculation for S09 (last event at 61h) shows they understand `last_event_gap_h` here, which contradicts Section 1/8. S26 is used to illustrate a *small* gap; its gap is 72h, the largest in the extract. Opens below vs. above 72h are mentioned but not used as the central identification point (S15/S31 still eligible for on-time Resolve; S09/S11/S25/S26 already breached; S03/S13/S20/S28 are completed breaches). Cohort imbalance is anecdotal.

**Section 4 — several false method claims.**  
Aalen–Johansen is a reasonable primary *if* transfers are competing events, but it is **not** “robust to informative censoring.” Cause-specific hazards do **not** “ignore competing risks”; \(1-\mathrm{KM}\) applied to a cause-specific indicator overestimates CIF. KM is appropriately sidelined for incidence, then later used as if it answered the same estimand. RMST/landmarking/multi-state are listed, not designed for \(n=32\), few events, and missing activity clocks. The recommended “multi-state” later collapses to one transient state plus observation status.

**Section 5 — leakage and a non-process model.**  
Path length and “AutoCheck before Screen” are marked baseline / no leakage. Both are post-`Submit` path features; longer survivors accumulate longer prefixes (immortal time). Escalation-as-baseline is flagged, then time-to-escalation is waved through without a clock or landmark. The Mermaid diagram puts **Open** on the same footing as Resolve/Transfer; Open is a cutoff artifact, not a process state. Stage-hazard “findings” (rework helps; AutoCheck is faster) are two-case stories, not hypotheses.

**Section 6 — labels drift; reversals are thin.**  
Assumption numbers do not match Section 2 (excluded was 3, then becomes “Assumption 2”). Primary CIF is said to use “informative” censoring while coding transfers as competing events. No IPCW, tipping-point, or gap-based bounds. Policy non-randomization / incomplete compliance is not treated as a reversal threat for “B is better.”

**Section 7 — causal overreach.**  
The brief forbids treating scores as causal. The text then recommends replicating Cohort B AutoCheck in A and staffing rework paths because they “have higher resolution rates.” Calibration/fairness are named, not specified (no observed-vs-predicted CIF by priority, no transfer/reject disparity check).

**Section 8 — fails the specification.**  
- `observed_status` is `8+3+3+3+11 = 28` values for 32 cases and is in the wrong order (S05 is not resolved, etc.). The frame cannot be built.  
- Priorities for S29–S30 and escalated for S26 are wrong.  
- Open time is again `age + gap`.  
- Only one event indicator is encoded; three transfer scenarios are not.  
- `lifelines.competing_risks`, `CumulativeHazardRatioModel`, `KMCompetingRiskFitter`, and `predict_ci` are invented. `load_rossi` / `CoxPHFitter` are unused. No fallback.  
- `parse_event_log_string` is called with a broken `case:act\nact` string and a fake XES parameter. Variants are printed and never used as a covariate.  
- No priority stratification. Timestamps are not fabricated, which is the only complied constraint.

**Section 9** is the strongest part (clocks, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff/start dates, assignment, compliance, small \(n\)). It does not offset the identification and implementation failures.

Net: correct heading list and some competing-risk vocabulary, built on a wrong time variable, a wrong required example, false estimator properties, leakage-prone “baseline” features, causal operational advice, and non-executable, factually incorrect code. That is well below a usable analyst artifact.
