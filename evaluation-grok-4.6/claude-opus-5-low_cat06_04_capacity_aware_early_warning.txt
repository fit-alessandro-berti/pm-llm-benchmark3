**6.5**

The memo is unusually complete and gets the cases the rubric cares about right: I-03 is rejected on causal ineligibility, I-05 is suppressed as a refresh artifact, I-07 is treated as new evidence but denied a swarm on runway, and spillovers are priced rather than mentioned. Structure, arithmetic hygiene, and the monitoring/fairness sections are well above a typical answer. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict decision-theoretic standard the allocation does not implement the rule that §1 claims is deployable, and several numbers are used inconsistently across sections.

**The stated rule does not generate the stated portfolio.** That is the central failure. Gate B says cases with ≥10 h to deadline “are deferred.” I-04 is at 10.0 h and is the first case funded. The table itself admits the decision is reversible and that acting now burns a slot before the possible regulatory arrival. A coherent option-value policy would swarm the perishable in-stage cases (I-02, possibly I-09) and keep I-04 flexible until 14:00. Doing the opposite — locking I-04 now *and* holding a reserve — wastes the only real option in the book.

The scoring rule is also not executed. §1 funds a case only if `Δ̂·V − 450 − B_fa` stays positive at that case’s *marginal* slot price (€1,500 / €1,800 / €2,200 / €3,000). On the memo’s own conservative numbers only I-04 clearly clears a used slot; I-08 (€2,550) clears the first or second slot; I-02 is marginal; I-09 discounted (€1,342) is negative against a third-slot price of €2,200, and even against the first-slot price of €1,500. I-01 (€1,379) fails the same test. The funded set should have been some subset of {I-04, I-08, I-02} plus a reserve, not {I-04, I-02, I-09}. I-08 already has the weak effect *and* the €5,000 false-alarm burden inside its score, then is demoted again for those same facts, while lower-scoring I-09 is promoted to Tier 1. Learning value and runway can justify an override; the memo never says it is breaking its own funding constraint. A deployable rule that is not the rule used is not deployable.

**Calibration is theatre.** Segment offsets are computed to three decimals (I-02 0.575, I-04 0.395, I-08 0.345, I-07 0.785) and then barely enter the score. Allocation is driven by supplied effect ranges, not by `p*`. That would be acceptable if acknowledged: effect ≠ observational p. Instead the reader is given a parallel probability apparatus that does not bind, except when it is convenient to talk down I-08. SE p is haircut because of the workflow redesign; SE *effects* are not. If the redesign changed diagnosis, the swarm’s effect distribution is also stale. Treating p as biased and Δ as portable is an unjustified asymmetry.

**Effect discounts are applied where they help the narrative and dropped where they would shrink the victory lap.** I-09’s effect is cut ~40% in §1–2, then restored to 0.265 / 0.24 in the optimistic and base impact rows. If the 0.6 factor is a real belief about thin NNLS evidence, base benefit is overstated by ~€1.9k and “0.58 breaches avoided” is too high. If the 0.6 factor is only a ranking penalty, §1 should not have called it an effect adjustment. The I-02 line in §2 is simply wrong: the lower bound 0.08 × €25k − €450 − €600 − ~€1,800 externality is about −€850, not “barely covers cost + externality.”

**False-alarm cost is defined twice.** The decision formula subtracts the full listed burden. The impact table charges ~€1k / ~€2k / €3.4k by scenario and treats burden as something that “is not triggered” when effects are large. Customer disruption from a swarm is not a function of realized Δ. I-04 is the worst case for this muddle: calibrated p* ≈ 0.40, so a false alarm is the *base* outcome, and the €2,000 burden should be close to fully expected whenever that case is funded. Optimistic “FA largely not triggered” is conceptually false.

**Operational gaps that a live policy cannot leave implicit.** Two specialists run every swarm. Three 45-minute swarms is 2+ hours of interrupted diagnosis from the same people who generate the 8–20 minute queue externality; specialist utilization is never checked, only slot count. I-09 at 2.5 h still has diagnosis unfinished plus resolution and customer validation; Gate B’s 2.0 h Diagnosis Started floor is asserted, not defended, and is tight enough that “the swarm works here” is doing a lot of work. I-07’s “10-minute senior read” draws on the same scarce seniors and is not charged. The €15k untreated-case value is an invention (listed-book average is ~€29k; one untreated name is worth €100k). The memo flags this, then still uses it to justify both the slot prices and the three-vs-four stopping rule. No alternative book is scored. {I-04, I-08, I-02} is the obvious comparator and is not computed.

**§4 is the strongest section** and almost earns its keep: cool-down, rejection of pure time-decay as evidence, re-entry without stigma, second-swarm tax, and a clean I-05 vs I-07 contrast. Even here, collapsing I-07 into “wrong instrument, right information” is right, but the alternative instrument is hand-waved.

**§5–6 are good and still slightly decorative.** Value capping at €40k and a 25% SME weekly floor are proposed and then not applied to today’s book (0/3 SME slots; I-08 would flip negative under the cap). A 10–15% mid-band RCT on four slots/day is the right idea and will not identify segment-specific effects at the speed §6 pretends. “Stop funding a segment if the CI includes zero after 40 treated cases” is a reasonable tripwire and sits uneasily next to funding I-09 *because* the sample is small. Training on untreated rows plus logging treatment is correct; the memo does not discuss interference from the spillover itself, which contaminates the “untreated” labels it wants to treat as clean.

**§3’s scenario arithmetic is mostly auditable and the qualitative reading is fair:** three slots look positive in base, uncomfortable in adverse, and worse at four. That conclusion is more robust than the path used to reach {I-04, I-02, I-09}. The adverse row correctly zeros the thinnest case. Confidence labels are honest. None of that repairs the fact that the impact table evaluates a portfolio the decision rule would not have chosen.

Net: a strong operational essay with the right instincts on stage, repetition, and externalities, undermined by a score that is not the real allocator, a deferral rule that does not bind the most deferrable funded case, and discounts that appear and disappear. That is well short of deployable coherence.
