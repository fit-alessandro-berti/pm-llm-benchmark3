**6.6**

The final write-up is a strong process-mining counterfactual: paths stay feasible, four-eyes and the high-risk compliance gate are preserved, queue snapshots are treated as forecasts, K-337 is correctly treated as a **decision-policy** change rather than a time cut, and local gains are traced into congestion, displacement, and quality risk. That is well above a generic answer. It is not near-flawless.

**Serious flaws**

1. **Broken cycle-time arithmetic on K-337 (temporal consistency).** Assessment 2 ends Friday of **week 1**. A Friday-evening decision is then about **103 h** from Monday 11:00, not 163.5 h. The write-up assumes M1 can sign Friday 18:00–18:30 and still reports ~164 h — almost identical to the Monday-morning base case (~166 h). That is internally inconsistent and compresses the best-case range. The 164–170 h band in the case table inherits the error. The Monday-decision base case (~166 h vs ~214 h factual) is fine.

2. **Ranking contradicts the synthesis.** Section 5 ranks K-104 first while giving it only “moderate–high” robustness and K-411 “high.” Section 7 then says K-411 is the most robust. The rank table is therefore not ordered by the robustness column it displays, and the two required sections disagree on the headline conclusion.

**Material but narrower issues**

- **K-219 base path is too optimistic.** “Created at triage, blocked, then keeps its original D2 slot” plus “near the front of A2 at 08:00” is one possible discipline, not the default FIFO-among-ready-work reading. The reverse-evidence paragraph admits this, but the **base** 28–30 h path still prices the optimistic discipline as central rather than as a scenario.
- **K-104 is not strictly minimal-change.** The intervention is an early S1 packet and a provisional C1 slot. The derivation also shortens A1’s assessment because S1 “already flagged” sanctions. That is an extra causal hop, only partly marked as uncertain.
- **Formal partial order is damaged.** Concurrency / inequality symbols rendered as empty placeholders, so the fenced relation set is not a usable specification. Escalation is also written as if it were a general precedence rather than a K-104 add-on.
- **Hard word limits missed.** The 100-case discussion is roughly 800+ words vs 250–400; the synthesis is well above 220–320. Content is on-topic (C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment spike, weekends, timestamps vs true concurrency) but the required bounds are ignored.
- **Tight ranges vs own uncertainty.** K-104’s table band (52–54 h) is narrower than the derivation’s worst case (~55 h). Pilot “power” numbers are decorative.

**What still holds up**

Prerequisites are respected: originals before document check; identity free to run earlier; rework implies a new document check and assessment; final compliance still follows final assessment on high-risk work; preliminary review does not replace it. K-411 correctly separates earlier dual-check **readiness** from earlier **completion** (overnight assessment queue can eat the 1.5 h). K-219’s rework tail can beat the factual cycle time. The interference table’s signs and nonlinearities are directionally right: C1 reservation does not scale, D2 load plus handwriting error can reverse a local win, suppressing RMI migrates risk into compliance and appeals, parallel verification can spike assessment.

Net: rigorous structure and the right conceptual distinctions, undermined by a clear timing bug, a rank/synthesis clash, optimistic base queues, and ignored length constraints. Under a strict bar that is a mid-6, not an 8+.
