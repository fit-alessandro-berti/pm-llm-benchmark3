**7.2**

The answer is comprehensive, numerically careful, and aligned with the rubric’s intended story (March as semantic/provenance, later operational drift, mechanism-specific change points). It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several analytical, logical, and API issues are material.

**What it gets right.** Temporal synthesis is the strongest part: March is treated as a label/provenance break because mapped fitness and the normalized DFG stay near baseline, while May rework, June–August queueing, July priority, October seasonality, and December’s mixed recovery are separated rather than collapsed into one global break. Calculations for the Jan–Feb baseline, checkpoint deltas, and the 7-edge MAE distance check out. Causal language is mostly restrained. Sections 5–7 and 10 are usable operationally: Register-month cohorts, support rules, conditioning-bias notes, hierarchical alerting, and IF–THEN–BECAUSE rules that can distinguish schema incidents from recurrence.

**Quantitative and PM gaps.** The DFG distance includes the invariant `Register→Eligibility = 100` edge, which mechanically dilutes every month’s score; there is no sensitivity check on the six moving edges. Cycle-time decomposition correctly refuses false additivity, but then also refuses even rough ranges (Assess service +0.6h versus a 13h median / 36h p90 move). `fast_variant_pct` (60% → 35%) is validated and then almost unused. Most importantly, the fall in `Assess/SeniorAssessment→Decision` (83 → 72 per 100) is treated as routing drift. With rising cycle time and p90, that edge is equally consistent with right-censoring / WIP in immature monthly cohorts—an issue the segmentation plan later understands but the DFG analysis does not apply.

**Logical slack.** Overlapping June–August and July–August intervals blur onset. Rank-1 “evidence against” pure logging is mostly “this is not the whole year,” not a contradiction of the March hypothesis. Logging change points in June–July and December are thin (missingness ±0.5–0.7 pp) and look manufactured to meet the two-CP quota. The Part A screen is not faithful to the narrative: July is a nominated behavioral CP but fails both the rework and DFG thresholds; September recovery is flagged as behavioral change; the logging rule is too sensitive on a 5–6% missingness series.

**Section 8 is not manual-clean.** After `format_dataframe`, PM4Py typically emits XES column names; later calls still pass `activity` / `case_id`. `filter_time_range(..., timestamp_key=..., case_id_key=...)` is not a stable public signature. `discover_dfg` is stored as if it were a single object rather than `(dfg, start, end)`. Duplicate-handling comments are good; the executable contract is not.

**Net.** This is a strong, rubric-complete answer with accurate arithmetic and the right high-level attribution. It is not nearly flawless: it under-interprets censoring, under-uses a major variant signal, dilutes its own distance, and ships a PM4Py plan that would likely need rework before it ran on a real log. Those are enough, under the required strictness, to keep it well below the top band.
