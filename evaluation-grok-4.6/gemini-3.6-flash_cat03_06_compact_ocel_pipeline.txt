The submission is complete in outline and mostly executable-looking, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses substantial credit for internal contradictions, incomplete discovery predictions, and several precise mismatches with the stated OCEL facts.

**What is solid.** The four DataFrames, 17 events, 12 objects, timezone-aware timestamps, payment amounts on events, merge-based activity/timestamp mirroring, and the validation block are all in the right shape. E2O qualifiers are meaningful and mostly consistent with a declared “direct operational / no silent propagation” policy. O2O covers the required business links. The pipeline hits the required PM4Py calls, stores flattened DFGs/skeletons by object type, and keeps visualization commented. Sections 6–7 are the strongest prose: the three E2O policies and the eight failure modes are actually useful.

**Serious semantic defects.**

1. **Section 3 contradicts the model and Section 4.** After `Issue Invoice`, the audit claims payments “radiate across both order processes.” In the constructed relations, `e12`/`e15` touch only `Payment` and `Invoice`. Flattening on `Order` therefore *cannot* include those payments — which Section 4 later admits. That is a core consistency failure, not a wording slip.

2. **The most interesting Item lifecycle is omitted.** `I2` is never in `Reserve Stock`. Any Item OC-DFG / log skeleton must show `Add Item → Pack Package` as well as `Add Item → Reserve Stock → Pack Package`. The write-up gives only the I1/I3 path and therefore misstates discovery.

3. **Object-interaction claims underplay the hubs they were asked to discuss.** `e6` and `e7` put `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, and `I3` on the same events; `e7` also ties `P1` to both orders. The summary says `O100` interacts with `I1, I2, P1, P2, V1` and `O200` with `I3, P1, V1`, dropping `O100–O200`, `O100–I3`, `O200–I1`, and `P1–O100/O200`. Those are exactly the P1/V1 convergence facts the prompt asked to treat explicitly.

4. **O2O qualifier drift.** The spec asks for “Package *packs* Item.” The code uses `contains` for both Order–Item and Package–Item. Defensible, but it is not what was required and it collapses two different relations into one label.

5. **Flattened Order DFG is over-linearized.** `O100` has two consecutive `Add Item` events and two consecutive `Pack Package` events, so the DFG must include `Add Item → Add Item` and `Pack Package → Pack Package`. Those edges are missing.

6. **OTG is described as E2O co-occurrence.** An OCEL 2.0 / PM4Py object-type graph is primarily the O2O type skeleton (`contains`, `carries`, `bills`, settlement). There is no direct O2O `Order–Package` edge; that coupling is event-based. Mixing the two graphs blurs source structure and observed interaction.

7. **Propagation is promised but not performed.** Section 1 says downstream Order/Item links are “deferred to graph/O2O traversal during analysis.” Stock `ocel_flattening` does not walk O2O. The pipeline never implements that traversal, so Confirm Delivery and Dispatch stay invisible on Order traces with no actual analytical recovery.

**Smaller but real faults (each enough to block a high score).** Cardinality of Package–Item is instance-level 1-to-N, not N-to-M. Semantic check 6 demands a *strict* subset; every object appears in relations and O2O, so the sets are equal. The “invoice total is 250” is inferred, not given. “The OC Petri net correctly models…” is too unique for an artifact the prompt said is not uniquely determined. `view_oc_petri_net` is a guessed API name. Log skeletons are computed and then ignored.

**Net.** This is a competent homework-shaped solution with real modeling judgment, not a reference solution. The data construction would likely run and is referentially clean; the interpretation layer repeatedly misses or contradicts the consequences of its own E2O choices. That gap is exactly what this task is meant to test, so the score cannot sit in the high band.
