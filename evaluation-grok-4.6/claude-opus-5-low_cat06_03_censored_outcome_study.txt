**6.5**

This is a conceptually strong design that avoids the usual failure modes (naive 21,600-only training, rejected-as-non-default, random splits, post-outcome features). It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several design claims are internally inconsistent, the validation schedule contradicts its own maturity rule, and the implementation skeleton would not run as written.

## What is actually solid

The estimand work is the best part of the answer. Activation is treated as selection, not missingness; rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are never recoded as `default_90d = 0`; ordinary censoring is separated from undefined-under-policy outcomes; and the counterfactual “would they default if activated?” target is correctly parked as an untestable sensitivity. The leakage table is complete for the required fields, and §8 draws the right line between predictive association, selection, and counterfactual risk.

That is necessary. It is not sufficient.

## Major flaws

**1. The temporal backtest violates the embargo it treats as central.**

§7 insists on a 125-day gap (90 + 35) and then publishes four folds that all break it:

| Fold | Train end | Test start | Actual gap | Required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | 2024-09 | 2025-01 | ~92 days | 125 |
| F2 | 2024-12 | 2025-04 | ~91 days | 125 |
| F3 | 2025-03 | 2025-07 | ~92 days | 125 |
| F4 | 2025-06 | 2025-10 | ~92 days | 125 |

This is not a rounding slip. Every fold uses a one-quarter calendar hole and labels it “125d.” The skeleton then encodes the opposite rule (`assert test_start - train_end >= embargo`) and immediately calls `temporal_split(..., 2025-03-31, 2025-07-01, 2025-09-30)`, which fails that assert. The headline validation design is arithmetically false and the code cannot execute the example it prints.

Worse, training is filtered by *global* `label_mature` at 15 Feb 2026, not by labels that would have been available at the simulated deployment date. Even if the 125-day start-date gap had been computed correctly, later-fold training labels would still include outcomes that had not matured by test start. The text correctly diagnoses why random splits are optimistic, then rebuilds a large part of that optimism in the “correct” scheme.

**2. The primary target is not one target.**

§1 defines \(Y = 1\{A=1\}\times 1\{T\le 90\}\), then says the estimand is \(P(T\le 90\mid \text{prefix}, A=1)\), then proposes the product \(\hat P(A=1\mid x)\hat P(T\le 90\mid x,A=1)\) as the “population-level early-warning score.” That product is the joint / cumulative-incidence quantity of Alternative A, not the conditional risk sold as primary. Reporting both is fine; calling all three “the primary formulation” is not.

There is a second split on prediction time: §1 makes \(k=3\) primary; §4 says elapsed-time prefixes are “the headline early-warning claim.” An early-warning product also has a clock problem the answer never faces: the 90-day horizon starts at activation, which is still in the future at \(\tau\). At scoring time the operational wait is activation latency plus 90 days plus feed lag, not 90 days. That is a different decision horizon than the one advertised.

**3. The skeleton does not implement the study it describes, and some of it is cargo-cult PM4Py.**

Concrete defects:

- `pm4py.get_variants(pref).keys().__class__ and g["concept:name"].apply(...)` does not extract variants. It uses a type object as a boolean so the `apply` always runs. `get_variants` is invoked only to look used.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is already case-level in ordinary PM4Py usage; regrouping it by `pref["case:concept:name"]` and taking `.mean()` is confused and index-fragile.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` is called on the formatted *dataframe*. The API is log-oriented; there is no `convert_to_event_log`. This is exactly the sort of “appropriate API” miss the prompt warned about.
- `temporal_split` keeps `label_mature` cases only, i.e. activated-and-mature. That silently drops the 22,800 non-activated / open cases that §1–2 said remain in the propensity and multi-state populations. The code’s train set is the naive labeled cohort the prose refused to use.
- `right_censored = activated & ~label_observed` collapses administrative censoring and permanent feed loss after the text said those must not be collapsed.
- `feed_at_risk` is just `source_system == "partner"`. That is not a missing-feed indicator.
- Transfer collapse, as-of resource experience, and `documents_total` lag are specified in the design and left unimplemented (one of them as a comment that the join is “left as” future work).
- The required train/**validation**/test split is only train/test.
- The cohort-flow assert is tautological (`len(idx) == idx["cohort"].notna().sum()` after every row is assigned a bucket). It does not check the 48,000 accounting the section promises.
- The block overshoots the 80–160 line brief while still omitting the repairs it claims are mandatory.

A skeleton is allowed to be incomplete. It is not allowed to contradict the paper or advertise APIs it does not use.

## Smaller but real problems

The cohort table adds to 48,000, which is necessary, but it never isolates permanently missing outcome feeds. Those accounts are either inside the 3,600 or unaccounted for; §3 then treats them as a fifth mechanism. That is a hole in the only table that was supposed to classify every case.

`k=3` is equated with “through the first Credit Review.” That is true only if the first three events are always Started → KYC → Review. Document loops, missing KYC, and migration residue make that an assumption, not a fact. Short-case handling is better on the elapsed-time path than on the path §1 calls primary.

Inclusion rule 4 collapses linked transfers into the original id and never says how traces, timestamps, or conflicting terminals are merged. Unlinked re-entries can still sit in the 48,000 under a new id, so the denominator can be inflated in exactly the stratum already marked selectively unobservable.

Fine–Gray is listed as a peer of Aalen–Johansen for CIF. For the quantity they want (cause-specific incidence of activated-and-default), Aalen–Johansen / cause-specific hazards are the defensible default; Fine–Gray is a different estimand and needed a caveat, not a slash.

Policy-regime assignment by *start* date is reasonable, but F3 is not a clean “train regime ≠ test regime” fold: training through March 2025 already mixes pre- and post-1 July 2025 cases. The migration negative-control idea is good; discovering DFGs on the *full* log including post-prefix events is acceptable only because it is audit-only, and that is not stated at the call site.

Fast-feed selection is slightly overclaimed: the prompt says partner-originated accounts have higher *permanent* missingness, not that every present label before day 125 is a non-random fast feed. Discarding early labels is conservative; calling it required by the partner mechanism overstates identification.

The product score and the two-stage fit are never reconciled (separate models need not multiply to a calibrated joint probability). `activated`, `event_default`, and `label_observed` are joined onto the feature table; the audit only blacklists warehouse columns, so a later “fit on this frame” step can leak the thing being predicted. Footgun, not a feature.

## Strict scoring

A top-band answer needed a single primary estimand, a backtest whose dates match its own maturity math, a cohort table that separates every missingness class named in §3, and a skeleton that runs and does not undo the design. This answer has the right scientific posture and the wrong execution on the two artifacts that were supposed to prove the posture (the fold table and the code). That caps it well below “nearly flawless.”
