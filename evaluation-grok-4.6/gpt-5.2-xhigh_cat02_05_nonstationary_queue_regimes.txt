## Material flaws

**1. Little’s Law marked `applicable` where the author’s own conditions fail.**  
Baseline weekdays are a non-contiguous set (a weekend sits in the middle), 07:00 backlog is unknown, snapshots may or may not include closed hours, and the row itself lists medium carry-over plus intradaily nonstationarity. Time-average `L=λW` can be used descriptively over a long stable window; it is not “applicable” in the sense of a queueing-law claim from these aggregates. The quality bar asked the author to say when the data are insufficient. `Approximate only` was the honest cell; reporting `W≈2.44 h` as if the boundary problem were solved overstates what `mean_wip` and daily `completed` can support. Weekend `W` is labeled “open-hour time”; the weekday figure is not, so units are inconsistent.

**2. Data audit missed an internal contradiction.**  
For every representative day, hourly arrivals/completions *sum* to the daily totals, so they are presented as that day’s profile. They do *not* recover `peak_08_10_pct`:

| day | 08:00–09:59 from hourly vector | implied by `peak_08_10_pct` |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026-04-07 | 17/101 ≈ 17% | 31% |
| 2026-04-15 | 19/105 ≈ 18% | 32% |
| 2026-04-21 | 11/115 ≈ 10% | 47% |
| 2026-04-25 | 6/51 ≈ 12% | 22% |

§1 never flags this. §2 and §5 then treat `peak_08_10_pct` and the hourly vectors as mutually reinforcing evidence of a morning-peak/mix-shift story. That is using incompatible views of the same days as if they agreed.

**3. “Burstiness” Fano across 12 clock hours is the wrong null.**  
Variance/mean across bins of a unimodal diurnal curve (2026-04-07: 3,5,12,20,16,…,4) mostly measures *scheduled inhomogeneity*, not extra-Poisson burstiness. An inhomogeneous Poisson process can have Fano ≫ 1 across hours while remaining Poisson in each bin. The 2026-04-21 spikes (34, 24, 14) *are* bursty relative to 2026-04-07; the write-up never separates those two mechanisms, yet quotes Fano to three digits as a burstiness proxy.

**4. Reproducibility gap in §4.**  
The Simpson correlations and the 14/14 pre–post mix-shift medians are load-bearing numbers. The Python never computes `corr(staff_fte, mean_wip)`, never splits at 2026-04-20 for that table, and never prints those rows. Regime medians and IOD *are* in the script; the most rhetorically important pooling results are not. Several §2 cells promise `[p25,p75]` and then drop the IQR after the first two regimes.

**5. Pooled serial dependence is interpreted naively.**  
Lag-1 autocorrelation of net inflow ≈ 0.02 is offered as “build-up then burn-down rather than independent noise.” Within the high-complex weekday stretch, net inflow is persistently about +10 to +16; the near-zero pooled lag-1 is largely the outage spike (+47) followed by recovery (−17, −13) plus weekend mixing—the same pooling artifact the author diagnoses elsewhere. Within-regime lag-1 (or weekday-only / lag-7) is the indicator that was needed.

## Smaller issues

- **Service-time estimand for complex work.** `median_service_min` is active handling, but complex variants are multi-step. Whether the quantile is total active time or per-step is undefined; the offered-load index `A·E[S]/K` can be biased either way. A1 is labeled, but this identification problem is not.
- **WIP path around the outage.** WIP peaks on 2026-04-16 (52), not on the outage day (47). That is inventory inertia and should have been part of the recovery narrative.
- **Change-point theatre.** A four-feature standardized split is fine as a heuristic; asserting that it “selects 2026-04-20” without showing scores for competing dates (15/16 vs 20) is more confident than the method. Outage already moves WIP and service earlier, so the maximizer is not obvious from the prose.
- **Daily weekday IOD = 0.66** is *underdispersion* relative to Poisson, not merely “overdispersion explained away.” That actually strengthens a scheduled/capacity-constrained daily total, and it sits next to large *hourly* Fano—worth stating cleanly.
- **Intervention Little’s Law.** Computing `W≈3.67 h` on a three-day burn-down with unknown intra-day start time, then printing it as a number, still invites over-reading even with an “approximate only” tag.

Strictly graded because the author *claims* more than the aggregates allow on the one “applicable” Little’s-Law window, fails the data-audit job on peak share vs hourly profiles, mislabels diurnal Fano as burstiness, and leaves key §4 numbers outside the reproducibility block.
