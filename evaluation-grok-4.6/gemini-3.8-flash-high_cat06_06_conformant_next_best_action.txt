## Material quantitative fabrications

The prompt gives no probability that alternate screening clears the 0.46 match. §2 nonetheless states **D1 ~0.60–0.70 false positive**, **D2 ~0.20–0.30 true positive**, **D3 ~0.10 inconclusive**, and then treats clearance as dropping confidence below 0.20 **and** risk below 70. Those figures are invented and directionally convenient: a 0.46 unresolved hit, risk 72, sanctioned-border counterpart is not a 60–70% likely false match. The same pattern repeats: E1 customer-document success **0.65–0.80**, G1 director refusal **0.70–0.80**. Presenting made-up probabilities as branch weights is a serious error in a monitoring/counterfactual task.

Review duration is equally unstable. Action C is given as **35–75 minutes** plus 5–15 minutes to record. For D, S4’s review quietly becomes **15–25 minutes** once screening “clears,” then **~30–35 minutes** in §5 (65m arrival → 95–100m finish). That inconsistency is what makes the Rank-1 row look SLA-feasible (`t=55–85m` execution). If the prompt’s 35–75 minute review is used, S4 at 35–65 minutes plus record/execute is often **75–155 minutes**, i.e. a likely miss of the 90-minute deadline. The table still lists D’s benefit as **€20,000–€35,000** as if on-time execution were the central case.

The prompt already supplies **SLA-breach probability 0.48–0.64 under “continue normal processing.”** Waiting for S4 is that regime (plus extra screening work). The answer almost never uses this number. It therefore overstates D’s operational benefit and understates P-884 deadline risk. That is ignoring given evidence in favor of a homemade timeline.

Further numeric problems:
- P-901’s SLA penalty is **not** given as €20,000–€35,000. The €2,400–€9,800 spillover is `0.12–0.28 × 20k–35k` (min×min, max×max) on an assumed identical loss.
- False-rejection “expected harm” `0.55×15k` to `0.75×80k` is improper interval arithmetic, and 0.55–0.75 is called a **high** approval probability.
- Several cells claim **€0 regulatory risk** or E’s **maximum certainty / zero false-clear risk**. Residual false-clear after a human review is not zero.
- Confidence figures (0.85, 0.80, 0.95) are false precision.

## Process and counterfactual flaws

Rule 6 requires second-officer review before **Approve or Payment Executed**, not clearly before **Reject**. F2’s story that rejection without second-officer sign-off is a policy breach is not in the rules; the prompt only says F is conformant **if** a documented rejection threshold holds. Conversely, a confirmed screening hit should still go through the required review/SAR path, not an automatic jump of F to Rank 1. The §6 fallback (“unresolved or adverse finding → E or F”) can skip a still-mandatory second review while the match remains unresolved.

G2 treats even a **successfully enabled** Rule 10 override as “bypassing sanctions controls for commercial convenience.” If director authorization and a valid incident ID actually exist, that is the permitted exception, not an extra illicit bypass. The real objection to G is that those conditions are **not** met and are unlikely to be met for a queue/SLA problem—which §4 states, and §2 then blurs.

B is described as audit fraud / record-tampering, yet ranked **above** A. If B is worse institutionally than a naked skip, the order should reflect that, or the fraud language should be tempered. The Rank-1 plan is labeled `CONFORMANT NOW` while bundling a “dynamic fallback to S2”; the fallback is fine only if it does not interrupt P-901, which the table does not make crisp.

§2 trees mix outcomes with made-up probabilities and do not cleanly separate, as asked: what the process permits vs likely continuation vs P-884 benefit/harm vs spillover vs compliance/non-monetary risk vs intervention uncertainty. Observational-vs-interventional caution appears in §4 and is not applied to the 0.55–0.75 approval rate when it is reused as S2’s approval probability under interruption.
