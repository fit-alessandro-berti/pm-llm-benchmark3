**6.2**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and hits every required section. Conceptually it does the important things: it is not an independent-case replay, calendars and non-stationary demand are first-class, objections are treated as latent rather than observed, waiting and service are not silently collapsed, and it does not claim optimality. That is a competent simulation-design brief, not a near-flawless one.

**What holds up.** Scope, entities, queues, and cross-case state (architect bursts, related cases, district travel, latent objections, shared calendars) are specified at the right grain. The estimation plan is process-mining-aware: case-start NHPP, variants/DFG for routing, repeated activities for rework, HR calendars as structural assumptions, public-notice duration as a censored proxy. Assumptions are separated as required and mostly honest. The experiment matrix covers baseline, D1–D10, a true combined policy, and more than three stress cases, with factors, levels, and trade-offs. Warm-up, replications, CRN-in-name, CIs/bootstraps, sensitivity, and heavy tails are all present. Validation and decision criteria cover the requested metric families.

**What fails a strict reading.**

1. **PM4Py conventions are wrong in the one place the prompt said they must be right.** `pm4py.discovery.dfg.get_dfg`, `pm4py.discovery.performance_dfg.get_performance_dfg`, and `pm4py.discovery.variants.get_variants` are not the standard APIs. The usual surface is `pm4py.discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` / `get_variants`, or the `pm4py.algo.discovery.*` / `pm4py.statistics.variants` modules. `format_dataframe(..., resource=...)` is also not a normal signature (the TypeError fallback only half-saves this). Try/except around broken imports does not make the skeleton faithful.

2. **The routing skeleton omits the process it claims to twin.** `next_activity` never schedules fire, environment, or public notice, never joins those branches before decision drafting, and never sets `requires_legal`. D5–D9 have almost no executable hooks. For a “coherent” engine this is a structural hole, not a missing nicety.

3. **D3 is specified incorrectly.** `BundleManager` requires same architect, district, *and* case type. District-batched inspection (D3) is geographic; same-architect related-case bundling is a different rule. Collapsing them changes both the intervention and the fairness story.

4. **Arrival construction double-counts demand.** Daily Poisson volume is generated, then architect bursts are *added*, so mean load exceeds the calibrated rate. S1 then multiplies an arrival process that already embeds the 1.7× season and 2.2× Monday factors.

5. **Calendars are time windows, not resources.** `fte` and `part_time_fraction` (correctly 0.6 for environmental reviewers) are stored and unused. Capacity, queueing, and Monday clerk reservation are not represented. D4 becomes a permanent +1 FTE, not a peak-month inspector.

6. **Decision criteria misuse the variant table as SLAs.** Simple-clean-path 12d/24d is treated as the Simple renovation target, even though Simple-with-missing-docs is 22d/45d. Using observed p90 as the SLA makes baseline “compliance” nearly tautological. Several thresholds lost their comparison operators (“reduces  5%”), which is sloppy in the section that is supposed to be operational.

7. **This is a classical DES study, not a twin.** There is no WIP initialization from the live log, no periodic re-estimation, no shadow-mode sync. Warm-up advice is also internally loose (discard 3 months *or* a full year in a seasonal system).

8. **CRN is named, not designed.** One seed per replication is not synchronized streams for arrivals vs service vs objections, so paired intervention comparisons will not have the variance reduction the text promises.

The document would help a team start. Under the stated bar—minor defects count, only near-flawless work scores high—the PM4Py errors, broken routing, botched D3 logic, and SLA/target confusion keep it well out of the top band.
