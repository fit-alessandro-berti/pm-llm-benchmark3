**6.4**

The answer is well organized and causally literate, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses substantial credit for arithmetic errors that change interpretations, an unimplemented identification strategy, and several internal inconsistencies.

**What is strong.** The piece follows the required heading structure and generally hits the mandated tables and inclusions. It correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, distinguishes availability from `automated_fast_path_pct`, treats rollout as endogenous, separates median from p90, and flags fraud-desk interference / SUTVA, small-cluster inference, survey nonresponse, and treatment-version problems. The estimand list is mostly well specified. The recommended policy (`expand with an embedded evaluation`, Delta first, Epsilon as holdout, guardrails, scale-up rule) is the right class of answer for this task.

**Quantitative discipline is the main failure.** Several pre/post numbers are wrong in ways that are not rounding noise:

- Gamma rework pre is reported as 12.3 for a Jan–Jun window. May–Jun are 14.6, so the correct mean is about **13.1**. Post is 12.6, i.e. a small *decline*, not “12.3 → 12.6.” The “no rework improvement” claim is partly an artifact of dropping the high-complexity months from the pre mean.
- Gamma escalation pre is **8.7**, not 8.0.
- Delta/Epsilon escalation “pre” uses 6.8 and 8.0, which are Jan–Feb only. The stated window is Jan–Mar (8.8 / 10.0 in March). Correct Jan–Mar means are about **7.5** and **8.7**. This overstates the control-group escalation jump.
- Beta early p90 is **103.0**, not 102.8.
- The intensity contrast is the worst error. Alpha months at 39–48% include June (39%, median **53.5**). The reported high-intensity mean of 45.6 is just Jul–Aug, not the stated intensity bin. A consistent split is roughly **48.9 vs 48.2 hours** (≈0.7h), not 49.5 vs 45.6 (3.9h). The implied dose-response is overstated.
- “Worsened by +6–6 h” is garbled. Section 1 drops the minus signs on FTE changes (“Alpha 2 in May, Gamma 1 in June”).

**Identification vs. evidence are not aligned.** The primary design is a staggered event-study with relative time. The code builds `rel_time` and then **never uses it**. The fitted models are static TWFE-style OLS (`automation` or intensity + branch FE + month FE)—the design the prompt said not to treat as sufficient. `rel_time` is also implemented with `// np.timedelta64(1, 'M')`, which is a common runtime failure on pandas Timedeltas, so the “reproducible” block is not clearly runnable through step 4.

**Inconsistencies and overclaim.** Section 5 notes that never-treated branches worsened by similar (sometimes larger) amounts, which undercuts an adopter-specific tail effect. Section 7 still labels p90 “likely early harm” from automation. Control comparisons for Gamma’s Jul–Aug window are incomplete. Word counts drift over the 150–250 / 250–400 caps. Several required symbols (`−`, `≥`) are missing in the decision rule.

**Net.** This is a competent policy memo with the right qualitative instincts, not a near-flawless scientific answer. The arithmetic and the gap between the proposed estimator and the actual analysis are enough, on a hypercritical rubric, to keep it well below the top band.
