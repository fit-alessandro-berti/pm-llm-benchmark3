**6.6**

The memo’s strategic call is directionally right, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has presentation failures that obscure quantities, several overclaims, at least two internal contradictions, and a real process-mining conceptual error. Those are enough to keep it well below a high score.

**What it gets right.** The required six-section structure is present. The decision correctly refuses national scale (A) and blanket rollback (B), unbundles Redesign R, and treats segment heterogeneity as the central fact. The evidence table mostly separates facts, interpretations, and assumptions. The five metric-conflict explanations are the strongest part of the memo. The A–E appraisal is fair and does not pretend one option dominates. The M1-after-M6 sequencing is genuine value-of-information reasoning, not a flat list.

**Presentation is not executive-ready.** Core numbers are written as glued tokens: `14.210.8`, `EUR 184161`, `34%12%`, `4562 days`, `8276%`, `6.19.8%`, `0.790.51`, `819%`. Comparison operators are missing in the gates (`utilization 85%`, `cost per case  baseline`, `complaints  6.6%`). In the evidence table, simple-house complaints appear as `1pp` and old-building FTR as `11pp`—the signs that reverse the meaning are gone. An executive cannot reliably read the memo’s own evidence. That is a serious defect in a decision document, not a cosmetic one.

**Material overclaim.** “Simple low-risk houses captured nearly all” of the headline wins is false on the memo’s own segment table. Median cycle time also improved for apartments (−2 days), business customers (−3), and rural installs (−1). What is concentrated in simple houses is the *quality* and *tail* result, not median, visible cost, or handover reduction (the last of which improved globally, 34% → 12%). The closing line about a “winning third” / “failing two-thirds” repeats the same oversimplification.

**Internal contradictions.**
- “Pause… all further geographic or segment expansion of any R component” sits next to “extend coordinator ownership… to remaining regions” in weeks 2–6.
- M5 is said to *gate* any expansion of coordinator ownership beyond current volume, then ranked 6th and not run before that expansion. Named backups and an 85% cap are a workaround, not a substitute for the test the memo itself treats as a gate.
- Simple-house rollout is called “permanent” in the same memo that says M4 could still force full rollback if 60-day economics fail. Geographic extension in weeks 2–6 therefore precedes the measurement the memo ranks as decision-forking.

**Process-mining inaccuracies.** High fitness plus low precision is explained as “any model that fits well must allow many combinations.” That conflates observed variant diversity with precision. Precision falls when the model *permits unobserved* behavior; a process with many well-specified variants can still be precise. Low generalization is then read as “unstable week to week.” Generalization is not a temporal-stability statistic, and the joint drop in precision *and* generalization is not explained. Using “precision recovering toward 0.65” as a national-rollout KPI treats a model-quality metric as if it were a process-control metric.

**VOI ranking is not clean.** M7 is ranked #2 “by expected influence on the decision” while the memo says it “doesn’t change strategy.” That is not how decision value is defined. M4 (can flip the choice to B) and M5 (allegedly gates coordinator scale-up) are under-weighted relative to a monitoring control. M2 is correctly described as non-gating, then placed above items that the memo claims *do* gate the next move.

**Smaller but real issues.** “Skip rule often wrong” is filed as fact; in the source it is a note. “Compliance-type exposure” and “waiting-for-appointment complaints fell” are interpretations written in the executive voice as established mechanisms. The 85% / 90% / 0.65 / p95≤50 thresholds are invented with more precision than the data support (ratification language does not fully cure that). Right-censoring is flagged, but a reported p95 of 62 days in a 6-week pilot already implies follow-up beyond the window and is not reconciled. NPS, the SLA improvement for simple houses, and the survey-team underload / second-visit overload split are under-used relative to the instruction to use the process-mining artifacts, not merely list them.

The recommendation E-with-guardrails is still the defensible move. The memo would be a high-7 / low-8 if the operators were intact, the “nearly all” claim were withdrawn, and the pause/scale/M5 logic were consistent. As written, it is a strong strategy note damaged by unreadable quantities and avoidable contradictions.
