**8.2**

The two models are the right ones for this problem, and the write-up mostly does what the task asked: it states a policy, keeps the tree sound, puts the non-block-structured joins in the POWL, and uses witnesses instead of slogans.

The POWL is the stronger artifact. Partial-order edges encode the real cross-branch constraints (`Contain→Plan`, `Analyze→{Design, route, consult}`, `{Plan, Design}→trial`, `{trial, route, consult}→Release`) without forcing a phase join. The XOR/LOOP/SPO nesting is the natural control-flow reading of risk, optional consult, and repeat-until-pass. Unordered pairs are listed and justified, not hand-waved.

The tree is a genuine conservative under-approximation, not a disguised over-approximation. `SEQ(Reg, PAR(Contain, Analyze), PAR(work, route, consult), Release, Archive)` with `work = SEQ(PAR(Plan, Design), LOOP(SEQ(ET, IT), Adjust))` keeps the safety-critical pieces (trial after both work products, do/redo loop with no trailing Adjust, three-way release join, approval∥trial). What it loses is exactly the extra wait for the whole phase-1 pair, and those four exclusion families are named. The N-pattern argument is the right reason an exact tree is unreasonable. W4 (`Plan` before `Analyze`) is the correct way to exhibit the loss: intended and POWL accept it, the tree does not.

Witnesses are well chosen: low-risk, high-risk, consult, two trial cycles, the approximation row, and a real cross-branch negative (ET before Plan, not a start/end nit). F2/F3 hit incomplete route and trailing Adjust at the first bad event.

What keeps this off a high score is a set of small but real faults, not a bad model.

The required concurrency resolution is muddled. The task asked whether “may overlap” is true concurrency or merely missing order. The text calls it both, then defines it as either-order interleaving. In these formalisms that is mostly the same observationally, but it was a point to settle, not to fuse.

The compact tree string is treated as `parse_process_tree`-equivalent. The silent leaf is written as Unicode `τ`. PM4Py’s parser expects `tau`. Constructors are the real artifact, so this is not fatal, but the equivalence claim is false as stated.

The validation plan is concrete and aimed at the right checks (soundness, sampled playout, the eight witnesses, silent/duplicate scans, tree⊆POWL sampling), but it is brittle: `play_out(..., no_traces=...)` and `d['fitness'] == 1.0` are version-dependent, and Petri-net playouts from converted trees/POWL typically emit silent moves. `invariants_ok` does not filter them, so traces can fail `acts[0] == 'Register Deviation'` even when the visible language is fine. That is a plan bug, not a model bug.

A few smaller nits: the N-pattern proof is the right idea but informal about specialization; the optionality row in §2 is awkwardly typed; the validation script is sketched as if it were decisive evidence of inclusion.

None of that overturns the models. The POWL is faithful under the stated abstractions; the tree is a defensible sound subset; representational loss is not hidden. The remaining gaps are interpretation, parser honesty, and validation hygiene.
