**7.7**

The answer is a serious, process-aware design. It refuses the biased 21,600-row complete-case shortcut, does not recode reject/withdraw/open as non-default, keeps an as-of view, and separates undefined outcomes from ordinary censoring. Those are the load-bearing requirements. It is not nearly flawless. Several design, identifiability, and implementation faults are material.

### What is actually strong

The target taxonomy is the right one. Default is treated as defined only after activation; rejection, withdrawal, and transfer are competing observed paths; the counterfactual “would have defaulted if activated” is labeled as unidentified. The booked-portfolio secondary target is correctly described as conditioning on a future policy event. The cohort ledger sums to 48,000, the 12.5% rate is marked as selected, and the 5.625% figure is correctly rejected as an all-application default probability. The 13 October 2025 maturity cutoff (90 + 35 days before 15 February 2026) and the observation that there is no conservatively complete post-migration default cohort are exact and important. The leakage table covers every required field. The validation argument against a random split is right. The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points, keeps DFG/variants on training prefixes only, and does not invent a survival API.

### Target and estimand faults

The headline estimand is a single joint probability of activation within an invented 180-day window *and* 90-day post-activation default. That quantity is well-defined under the observed policy, but it is not the same object as “early warning of 90-day default,” and it is a poor primary reporting target.

- The 180-day application horizon is unmotivated by any SLA and is poorly aligned with the snapshot. Process observation ends 31 December 2025. Starts in August–December 2025 cannot realize a 180-day application horizon; many July 2025 starts barely can. Section 7’s out-of-time panel is exactly that window. IPCW/Aalen–Johansen can still produce a number, but the primary estimand is then identified mainly from earlier cohorts and heavily censored later ones. The text never states this limitation at the point where 180 days is chosen.
- The two-stage recipe in §3 is left informal. The joint event is a convolution: one must integrate activation time against default-from-activation, with competing exit and delayed ascertainment. Writing a probability and saying “combine the stages” is not an estimand. Cause-specific hazards, a discrete-time multi-state model, and a Fine–Gray CIF are not interchangeable here; the answer lists them as if they were.
- Landmarking is conceptually right and operationally underspecified. “Third event, normally first Credit Review” is a hope, not a rule, once document loops, unmapped migration labels, and duplicates exist. Eligibility is “still in process,” but the primary performance table then silently drops early terminals from the scored set. That is defensible only if early-exit calibration is reported with equal status; §1 still calls the joint activation-default probability the “principal reported risk.”

### Cohort, censoring, and missingness

The mechanism table is better than most answers. The implementation of those rules is not.

- The 3,600 unlabeled activations are never partitioned. The design says “subdivide” into 90-day censoring, feed delay, and missing-after-grace, then the flow table leaves them as one row. Without even a bounding exercise (how many activations can possibly be immature given a 13 October rule?), the reader cannot see whether missing-after-grace is a small remainder or a first-order bias.
- Administrative censoring is inconsistent. Prose says last reliable process time; code censors Stage A at `EVENT_CUTOFF` for every open case. That assumes complete observation through year-end, not through last event. If extraction simply stops emitting events, those are not the same.
- Transfer is treated as absorbing in the primary estimand, which is coherent, but 60% linkage is then only a sensitivity. If linked continuations can activate and default, the source-platform CIF is a platform-exit probability, not a credit-risk probability. That should have been the primary/secondary contrast, not a bullet in a sensitivity list.
- Partner missing feeds are correctly called potentially MNAR. Tipping-point bounds are appropriate. Inverse-probability-of-observation weights on pre-feed covariates cannot fix MNAR; presenting IPW as part of the *primary* strategy overstates what those weights identify.
- Simultaneous or contradictory terminals are quarantined in §2 and resolved by arbitrary name sort in the code. That is a broken as-of rule, not a skeleton simplification.

### Prefixes, features, leakage

§4–6 are the most careful parts, with two serious slips.

- `documents_total`, `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, extraction-time tenure, and `label_available_at` are handled correctly as drop/repair.
- The feature table then joins `cohort_status` onto `features`. That column *is* the outcome path. The leakage asserts only inspect prefix columns, not the analysis table. Anyone who fits a model on `features` gets near-perfect leakage. A skeleton that exists to demonstrate an audit cannot put the label in X.
- Labels are gated once at 15 February 2026 for every fold. §7’s own rule is that a model origin may use only labels with `label_available_at` at or before that origin. The code comment defers this. As written, 2024 training rows carry 2025–2026 outcome information that would not have been available at a 2024/early-2025 fit origin.
- Historical reference features are described correctly and then not built. Queue load, prior resource volume, and train-only variant rarity are the process-aware content; the code emits last activity, two risk values, and two loop counts.
- `time:timestamp` as live availability is flagged, then ignored. A one-line ingestion-lag stress test was the minimum honest implementation of that warning.

### Validation

The start-time split and policy/migration strata are right. A random split is correctly rejected. Several claims are still too clean.

- Development = all of 2024, validation = H1 2025 (old policy only), test = H2 2025 (new policy from day one). Hyperparameters never see the regime in which the model is tested. Rolling origin is mentioned, not specified (step, refit, which labels enter each origin, how the Oct 13 maturity wall is applied per origin).
- Post-migration default evaluation is correctly downgraded. The answer still lists IPCW Brier, time-dependent AUC, and 180-day CIF as if they were available on the same test panel. They are not, except as preliminary, high-censoring estimates.
- Metrics are mostly appropriate, then padded. Recall/PPV at capacity is the right operational number; “proper state-probability log score where censoring adjustment is valid” is hand-waving. PR-AUC on the 12.5% activated slice is fine for the *secondary* target and irrelevant to the primary multi-state estimand.
- Subgroup lists omit the one subgroup the prompt forces: partner-originated activated accounts with systematically missing feeds. Source system is not a substitute.

### Limitations

§8 is the right shape and still over-claims. “Censoring-aware estimates for similar policy, source-system, and observation regimes” is not something this snapshot can underwrite after a July 2025 policy break, an October 2025 migration, and an immature post-migration label. Deployment feedback modes are good; six of them do not replace a precise statement that the primary joint score must not be named “default risk” in a lender UI.

### Code skeleton

This is a usable draft, not a trustworthy pipeline.

- Real faults: tz-aware `case_start` compared to the naive string `"2025-01-01"`; empty `ACTIVITY_MAP` despite a named migration; conflicting terminals not quarantined; `elapsed_prefix` built and discarded; `cohort_status` on features; one global label vintage; no lineage-id fold constraint despite the prose; `handover_count` via `groupby.apply` is incidental.
- API use is mostly legitimate (`format_dataframe`, `get_prefixes_from_log`, `discover_dfg`, `get_variants`). Avoiding `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is correct. The unused elapsed landmark and the comment-only survival connection mean the skeleton does not actually implement the primary target, only a case-status ledger plus a k=3 feature stub.

### Net

Conceptually this is well above the usual failure modes for the prompt. Strictly, it still treats an arbitrary joint probability as the primary target, does not make that target evaluable on the stated test window, lets the outcome column back into the feature table, and leaves vintage-aware labeling unimplemented. Those are not polish issues. They are the difference between a strong memo and a study one could run without first redesigning the estimand, the test metrics, and the analysis table.
