**6.4**

The memo is structurally complete, operationally useful, and mostly causally disciplined, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict standard the quantitative core contains an error that is arithmetically impossible, methodologically mislabeled, and inconsistent with the rest of the write-up. That, plus a headline verdict that does not match the argument, keeps the score well below the top of the scale.

**Fatal quantitative error.** Section 4 claims that pooled-complexity direct standardization (weights 12/30, 10/30, 8/30) yields Orion 18.67h vs Nova 17.09h, difference **+1.58h**. Orion is faster in every stratum (7.50 vs 9.64, 17.17 vs 19.38, 31.57 vs 35.00). Any common-weight average of those three pairs **must** keep Orion faster. The correct figures are about **17.14 vs 19.65, contrast −2.51h**. The reported +1.58h is not a rounding slip; it looks like an ad hoc high-complexity-share adjustment with inconsistent implicit slopes, described as the method that was not used. The Section 7 code would print the right contrast. The committee-facing table therefore contradicts the strata, the executive narrative, and the accompanying diagnostic script. That is a serious failure on the exact aggregate-versus-stratified comparison the task asked to get right.

**Headline verdict is misaligned.** “Partly causal through mechanisms” (58) is a defensible *family* of answers, but it does not say *in which direction* or *relative to which estimand*. The body argues that the crude Orion delay is mix-driven and that any residual group effect is more likely beneficial via shorter waits. A reader of the banner line can easily hear “Orion partly causes longer cycle times,” which is the opposite of the analysis and is exactly the belief behind the 50% transfer. “Mainly confounded” or “not identifiable,” with a parenthetical on a possible beneficial bundle effect, would have matched the evidence. The last sentence of §1 is also confused: selection of *harder* cases cannot explain Orion’s *within-stratum speed*; if anything it biases Orion toward looking worse.

**Causal graph is good, not clean.** Supported vs assumed arrows and the queue-feedback remark are strong. Two graph problems matter. First, `quality_outcome → case_completed` is temporally backwards: the 30-day appeal is post-Close and cannot select the completed-case sample. Second, `unobserved_reviewer_skill → resource_group` treats skill as a cause of case assignment rather than as a bundled attribute of who sits in Orion. Missing or under-specified: `resource_group → case_completed`, and a clearer complexity → wait path not only via compliance.

**Smaller accuracy and clarity hits.** Orion compliance-required mean is given as “about 25.8h (9 cases)”; nine cases average **26.7h**. 25.8h is the eight-case mean after dropping C14. Orion mean review service is 7.04h, not 6.9h. In the decomposition table, overall “+6.72h” means Orion slower, while stratum “Orion 2.14h / 2.21h / 3.43h” means Orion faster—silent sign flip. Section 1 never names total vs direct vs mediated estimands as such (the idea appears later in the role table). Arrival-load positivity is thinner than the shift discussion implies (Orion is 13/14 High).

**What remains strong.** The Simpson reversal is seen and is *not* treated as identification. Group is correctly framed as a routing/staffing/queue bundle. Wait is separated from service. Completed-case selection, sparse appeals, and poor overlap are taken seriously. The role table covers confounder, mediator, collider/selection, and proxy. Seven hypotheses include the required harmful, beneficial-masked, shift/load, compliance/rework, and selection stories, each with a test and a predicted observation. The phased plan—instrument, small stratified random routing pilot, capacity/compliance intervention without relabeling, capability-equivalent transfer, scale-by-stratum—is the right operational answer, with real stop rules. The Python block embeds the CSV, warns on thin cells, states the weighting distribution, and uses frequency-only DFGs without invented timestamps.

The recommendation would still stop a reckless 50% move. The analysis is not safe to quote on the standardized gap, and the executive label invites the wrong causal reading. Those are not cosmetic defects.
