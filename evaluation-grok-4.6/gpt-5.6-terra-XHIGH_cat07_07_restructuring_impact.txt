8.3

The answer is unusually complete against the required structure and is substantively stronger than a typical “shared services will reduce handovers” write-up. It stays balanced, treats East differently from North/South, refuses to treat average cycle time as the decision metric, and conditions any rollout on a pilot. The remaining score loss is from analytical shortcuts, a few overclaims, and a sketch that does not fully do what it promises.

**What is actually strong.** The mechanism map is the right unit of analysis: destination, waits, loops, case mix, and distributional effects rather than a slogan about fewer handovers. The 343 → 359 listed-handover count and the ~588 → ~722 hour exposure proxy are correctly computed and correctly caveated. The utilization warning is directionally right: Legal/Risk are already near saturation, and a blanket “+15% utilization” target is a queueing error. Scenarios, indicators, hidden-average risks (including right-censoring), and the modify-and-pilot recommendation all match the brief. The PM4Py sketch covers the required families of methods and makes two important engineering points: one timestamp is only a wait proxy, and `home_team` must be frozen separately from the performing team.

**Real analytical flaws.**

The 343/359 comparison is treated as if it were a census. Both management tables omit Eligibility, Local Technical Assessment, Local Customer Contact, Implementation → Quality Check, Quality Check → Rework/Close, and the explicit centralization of “difficult Quality Check.” Those omitted arcs are exactly where the redesign adds a new hub. Calling the listed sum “not an aggregate reduction” is true of the table; presenting it as the restructuring’s handover effect overstates what the table contains.

The 15% utilization claim is applied too loosely. Management’s 15% target is attached to merging N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech, whose simple average utilization is about 74%, not to Legal at 93% and Risk at 91%. The Legal/Risk problem is the referral increase 9 → 12 per 100 into already-saturated queues, not a literal 15% lift past 100%. The conclusion (do not maximize utilization) survives; the arithmetic used to get there is sloppy.

East-masking is overstated. East is ~25% of volume (520 / 2100). A modest East improvement does not easily hide North/South deterioration in an aggregate median. The heterogeneity point is right; the “masked by East” mechanism is not quantified.

The pilot cannot observe the learning risk the rest of the memo treats as first-order. Twelve to sixteen weeks plus 30–60 days of follow-up can see queues, rework, and p90 cycle time. It cannot see skill atrophy. Guardrails stay verbal (“material,” “sustained”) with no precommitted numbers.

**Smaller issues.** Queue-exposure still multiplies frequencies by medians and then appears in the recommendation as a ~23% fact. Optimistic scenario quietly adds liaison capacity and RACI that are not in the proposal; that should have been labeled a design change, not a scenario of the proposal. `cohorts_before` / `cohorts_after` are built and never compared. Several PM4Py calls (`filter_variants(..., retain=True)`, `resource_key=` on social-network discoverers) are version-fragile even with the hedge. The go-live date is an arbitrary 2025 stamp.

**Verdict.** This is a high-quality, usable forecast with original quantitative work and the correct institutional recommendation. It is not near-flawless: the headline handover and utilization numbers are cleaner than the underlying tables, East’s weight is mishandled, and the pilot is too short for the learning effects the author otherwise takes seriously. Those are enough to keep it out of the 9s under a strict read.
