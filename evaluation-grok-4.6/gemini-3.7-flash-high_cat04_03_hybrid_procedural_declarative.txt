**4.2**

The write-up is well organized and hits every required heading, but it is not internally executable. The DECLARE and log-skeleton models contradict the happy-path traces, the verdict matrix, and in several places each other. That is a core failure on a modeling task whose point is constraint semantics.

**Critical model/verdict contradictions**

The dictionaries as written reject the ordinary accepted and rejected cases.

- `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` and `always_before: ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` are unconditional. They require a legal opinion before every activation. T1, T2, and T4 have no opinion. R5 is a *conditional* rule (after escalation). The right approximation is closer to `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` plus `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`, plus an explicit statement that “if escalation occurred, opinion before activate” is not a native unary/binary template.
- Both `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` are asserted. Together they demand *both* approvals before activation and violate R2. T1 and T2 cannot both pass.
- `precedence(Receive Clarification, Activate Vendor)`, `precedence(Receive Clarification, Reject Vendor)`, and `always_before: ("Receive Clarification", "Classify Risk")` require a clarification cycle on every case. T1 and T3 have none.

Section 8 still marks T1–T4 as DECLARE/log-skeleton `pass`. The matrix is therefore not an evaluation of the models in §§4–5. T1 is the cheapest sanity check and it already fails.

**Other formal errors**

- `absence2` is not in the allowed PM4Py template list (`existence`, `absence`, `exactly_one`, …). “At most one” for optional milestones belongs in `activ_freq` (`{0,1}`), and that limitation should have been stated.
- Log skeleton is inconsistent with itself: `equivalence` of `Receive Onboarding Request` with `Verify Bank Account` forces equal counts, while `activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1, 2}` allows a second verify for R11.
- Section 2 claims `response(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)`; the DECLARE dict does not contain it. R11’s “another verify before any later payment” is never encoded.
- `activ_freq` caps clarification at `{0,1,2,3}` with no defense, against the instruction to populate only defended constraints.
- T8 is marked resource/data `fail` with no SoD or emergency violation and no such check in the §7 code. That cell should be `pass`.
- Four-eyes handling is uneven: missing `org:resource` is fatal for bank/approval but silently ignored for capture/activate.

**Procedural backbone**

The ProcessTree is the strongest artifact: start, AND-split of the three checks, `*(τ, Request→Receive)`, XOR reject vs accept, low-risk vs high-risk, close after reject or payment, R11 kept out of the tree. That matches the stated backbone and correctly fails T5/T6 (Res 1)/T8 and accepts T1–T3/T7.

Still imperfect. Clarification is locked *before* `Classify Risk`, which is stricter than R3 (“before any final outcome”) and is not labeled as a block-structured approximation. Escalation is confined to a third accept branch after classification; R7 only requires it after screening. The compact tree formula is hard to parse and does not clearly match the n-ary XOR in the code.

**Policy and layering (relatively stronger)**

Two R5/R6 resolutions, an explicit choice, and a real compliance risk are present. Temporal conversions and `zeta = 2.0` are correct; treating R12 as warnings is right; T7’s 34h vs 24h bound is right. R9 is correctly pushed to a resource layer. Vacuity, support/confidence-as-policy, calendar-time distortion, and missing emergency attributes are discussed well.

Even here there is drift: Resolution 1 is “chosen,” then a hybrid with a Res-2 audit mode is smuggled in; R6’s hard 24h window is conflated with R12’s `(12h, 6h)` profile; T6’s overall verdict does not actually change across resolutions (only some layer cells do), because missing authorization always fails closed.

**Net**

This reads like a strong essay with plausible PM4Py-shaped objects. Under a strict reading it is not a coherent hybrid model: the declarative layers over-encode conditional rules as global order, the test matrix does not implement those layers, and several constraints fight R2, R5, R11, and the allowed API. Those are not cosmetic defects.
