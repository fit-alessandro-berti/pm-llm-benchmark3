**6.4**

This is a substantively capable committee memo that earns credit on the evaluation-emphasis items (Simpson reversal, no automatic identification from stratification, wait vs service, tests with predicted outcomes, safer learning-oriented intervention). It is not close to flawless. Several explicit requirements are missed or internally contradicted, and a few causal claims are sloppy enough that a strict grader should not treat this as a high-7/8 paper.

### What it gets right
The crude-versus-stratified reversal is the correct center of gravity, and the author does not pretend three-level stratification identifies a group effect. Orion’s mix (high-complexity, night, compliance) vs Nova’s is used as evidence of routing, not of reviewer slowness. Queue wait is distinguished from review service; completed-case selection is treated as a real threat; resource group is treated as a possibly non-manipulable bundle. The code actually does the required crude/stratified tables, pooled-weight standardization, overlap counts, variant listing, DFG attempt, and a diagnostic disclaimer. Phases 1 and 2 of the intervention plan match the requested shapes (restricted randomization; capacity rather than relabeling). Section 8’s five uncertainties are well chosen.

### Major flaws (these are why the score is not in the 8–10 band)

**1. Missing a required hypothesis.** Section 5 must include a hypothesis that assigns Orion a *genuine harmful* effect. The table has confounding, beneficial-Orion-masked-by-mix, shift/load, compliance/rework, and selection. There is no steelman of management’s theory (specialist bottleneck, slower certified review, night fatigue, group-caused rework). That is an omitted required element, not a nit.

**2. The “DAG” is not a DAG.** `complexity → unobserved_case_risk` and `unobserved_case_risk → complexity` is a cycle. A directed adjacency list offered as a causal graph cannot contain that. One direction (latent risk → recorded complexity, or complexity as a noisy proxy) was available; both is a methodology error.

**3. Compliance is called a mediator in contradiction to the author’s own graph.** The graph has no `resource_group → compliance_required/performed` path. Operational facts also make compliance a policy function of case type, mainly complexity, recorded as required/not. Yet §3, §4, and hypothesis 4 treat compliance as mediating a group effect and “inflating Orion crude wait.” If the graph is right, that share of the crude gap is *confounding/composition*, not mediation. This is the paper’s worst conceptual inconsistency, and it blurs the required total/direct/mediated distinction.

**4. Total vs direct vs mediated is not actually drawn in §1.** The executive section was required to distinguish those interpretations of an Orion effect. It contrasts crude association with within-stratum contrast (confounding), and later discusses mechanisms, but it never states a total-effect estimand (assign this case to Orion vs Nova), a direct-effect estimand (holding wait/service/rework/compliance fixed), and which paths would remain. “Mainly confounded, with a sliver of benefit” collapses those into a vibe.

### Additional inaccuracies and unclarities

- **`routing_rule` as “instrument-like” is wrong.** The rule is a function of complexity and shift, which affect outcomes directly. That is not an instrument; it is the assignment policy. Calling it instrument-like mis-teaches the estimand.
- **Day vs night and compliance vs not lack the demanded auditable arithmetic.** §4 is supposed to show enough numbers to check the comparison. Shift is waved at with “Night≈Orion, Day≈Nova almost 1:1,” which is false as a count (Orion has one day case; Nova has four night cases) and is not a cycle-time contrast. Compliance is percent-mix only; no mean cycle/wait split.
- **Pilot positivity region is misspecified.** Medium-day has *zero* Orion cases. Randomizing “medium complexity, both shifts” is not randomizing inside the observed overlap; it is a new operating regime. The actual tiny overlap is Low-Day (n_Orion=1), Low-Night (1 vs 2), Medium-Night (6 vs 1), High-Night (6 vs 1). A causal learning plan that names the wrong positivity set is not careful.
- **“The crude mean would fall only through relabeling” is false as stated.** Relabeling does not change case durations. Reassigning hard night work can change *which group’s mean* looks good and can *raise* system cycle time if Nova is slower in-stratum. The backfire story is directionally right; the mechanism sentence is not.
- **Within-stratum rework reversal is missed.** Crude rework is higher for Orion (28.6% vs 18.8%), but within Low/Medium Orion is *lower* (0% vs 10%, 16.7% vs 25%); High is 50–50. That is the same Simpson structure as cycle time. Treating crude rework as a group mark against Orion undercuts the author’s own method.
- **Stratified advantage is mostly wait, not service**, which the mediator table almost shows and the prose under-uses. Service is nearly identical by stratum; Orion’s point-estimate edge is queue. That matters for whether a “skill” story (hypothesis 2) is even the right mechanism.
- **n<3 warnings are too narrow.** Code flags complexity×group cells but not the required three-way `complexity × shift × group` cells with n=1. Positivity printing only flags zeros.
- **Front-door language is decorative.** Hypothesis 2 invokes a front-door via `review_service` without the identifying conditions (mediator captures all directed paths; no unblocked mediator–outcome confounding). Predicted observations for matching are fine; the method label is not.
- **`quality_outcome → case_completed` is weakly motivated.** Appeals are a 30-day post-process flag on completed cases; they do not explain why a case appears in the completed sample.
- **Confidence 68 sits awkwardly** next to n=2 cells, near-perfect group–shift aliasing, and an admission that a genuine effect in either direction is still open. `not identifiable` was available and better calibrated; 68 oversells “mainly confounded.”
- Minor: “equal or faster” / “High/Low/Med” order; graph explanation is at or over the 120-word cap; Phase 0 “halt if standardized gap ≈ 0” ignores that the same data already show an Orion-favorable standardized direction.

### Format and code
Headings match. The Python block is self-contained and framed as diagnostic, which is correct. The pm4py call is wrapped in a fallback, so it is not presented as proof. That section is one of the stronger parts; it does not offset the causal-graph and requirement failures.

### Net
The recommendation (do not move 50% of Orion work; learn in the overlap; add night/compliance capacity) is the right operational call and is grounded in the supplied tables. Hypercritical scoring still has to charge for a cyclic graph, a mediator/confounder self-contradiction, a missing required harmful-Orion hypothesis, an incorrect positivity region, and several comparisons that were required to be numeric and were not. This is a good draft with disqualifying technical holes, not a near-flawless analysis.
