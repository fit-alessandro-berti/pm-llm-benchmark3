**5.5**

The write-up is a competent audit memo with the right skeleton, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the rubric, several required moves are missing or wrong, and the model/data deliverable does not do the job it was asked to do.

**What holds up.** Headings match the spec. Section 1 separates dates, traces, and instrument identity from authority/scope/logging disputes, and the burden-of-proof rule (written instrument in force on the case date) is the right one. The two briefs are not a bland split: PD-3/FD-7 expiry, ineligible auto-approval, and SUP-708 are treated as real control failures; loops, rejected leads, reactivations, CRM mirroring, and label mapping are treated as model/data problems. The matrix is complete and mostly traceable. Most classifications are defensible: 701 compliant; 702/704 justified in-window overlays; 703/708/714 noncompliant; 706/711 data; 707/709/710/713 model or scope. The system finding—partial operational noncompliance plus an obsolete M0—is earned.

**Dialectical and authority gaps (required, not optional).** The prosecution never really concedes genuine model/data defects; CRM missingness is reframed as operations’ governance failure, and Request_More_Info, pre-screen rejects, reactivations, and QA_Clearance are left to the defense. The defense only glancingly admits risk (SUP-703 as “operator error”) and does not squarely accept that informal continuation of FD-7/PD-3 and SUP-708 are unacceptable even if M0 is wrong. PD-3’s master-data validation condition is ignored. Section 1 never tags propositions as supported / disputed / unproved in the form requested (e.g., whether committee minutes outrank the observer’s later disclaimer; whether 705’s flood still existed; whether 710’s prior cert is valid).

**Case-level errors.** SUP-702’s onboarding trace has no `Notify_Requester`; CRM evidence is the same N4 problem as 706, and the matrix treats 702 as a pure overlay case. SUP-715 is overcharged: the file says no rule bars the spousal relationship and only disclosure is required; calling it High/NONCOMPLIANT and bundling it with CS-9 SoD conflates requester–approver identity (712) with an unrecorded CoI disclosure. Confidence is almost uniformly “High.” Withdrawals are never scoped.

**Architecture.** Layering is conceptually right, but the spec asked which constraints belong in POWL vs DECLARE/log-skeleton vs pandas vs org controls. DECLARE/log-skeleton is absent. The “overlay table” is a CREATE TABLE stub, not a normalized table of CS-9 / PD-3 / FD-7 rows (windows, eligibility, spend cap, quality timing, authority). Eligibility snapshots, delegation, and immutable effective vs record timestamps are thin.

**Section 7 is the largest failure.** The block is not executable: it rebuilds `M1` three times and hits `activate_node = Activate` (`NameError`) before the last construction. The surviving graph is invalid POWL (the same transitions are both children of `OperatorPOWL` and top-level nodes; `activate` is edged from an approval XOR and also an XOR child). `Operator.LOOP` is given one child and then forced on every path, so `Request_More_Info` is mandatory and sits before both checks, which matches neither PM4Py loop semantics nor SUP-707. Worst: quality always precedes activation. That cannot replay the pilot or emergency order, and there is no provisional/emergency route at all. Timing is waved at an overlay that cannot relax a hard POWL precedence. That is hiding the limitation, not approximating it.

Strong casework and a usable matrix keep this well above a collapse. Broken, incomplete POWL plus missed required concessions and a few substantive case mistakes cap it well below a high score.
