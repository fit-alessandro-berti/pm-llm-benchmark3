## Critical error: microbiology ready window is off by a day

Incubation starts Friday 18 Sep 14:00 and is technically ready **46–54 h later**. That is **Sunday 12:00–Sunday 20:00**, not “Sun 12:00 – Mon 20:00”. Friday 14:00 + 54 h = Sunday 20:00. The upper bound is a 24-hour slip (off-by-one-day).

Consequences:

- There is **no** primary-incubation case with technical readiness Monday, let alone Mon 18:00–20:00.
- Routine readout is almost always **Monday 08:00–09:30** (0.5–1.5 h after microbiologist availability). Weekend on-call can only pull readout into **Sunday afternoon/evening**.
- **S3 is not a real path.** Assigning it 0.04–0.10 and using it as a required “delayed microbiology readout” scenario is a fabricated mechanism. The 4–10% band looks like 2/32 of a uniform clock spread from Sunday 12:00 to Monday 20:00 — i.e. uniform over a wrongly computed 32-hour window rather than over an 8-hour incubation interval.
- Assumption-ledger sensitivity (“if skewed late, shifts join/release toward Tue”) is false. Every point in [46, 54] h lands on Sunday; without on-call, staffing maps **all** of them to Monday 08:00.
- S1’s claim that microbiology is the typical limiting clock, with a “Mon morning→evening” spread, is therefore wrong.

## Wrong dominant clock on the smooth path

Chemistry **cannot** finish Friday. Earliest second-server free time is about 16:42 (the 1.2–2.0 h job); risk-priority still gives the first free analyst to the urgent sample. Friday remaining window is ≤1.3 h vs minimum chemistry service 1.5 h. Work pauses 18:00 Friday and resumes Monday 08:00. That part of the write-up is directionally right, but it was not used to identify the join.

Correct smooth-path join:

\[
t_{\text{join}}=\max(t_{\text{chem usable}},\ t_{\text{micro usable}})
\]

with \(t_{\text{micro usable}}\) typically Monday 08:30–09:30 (Sunday if on-call), and \(t_{\text{chem usable}}\) Monday morning under risk-priority (~45%) vs Monday afternoon — occasionally Tuesday spillover — under FIFO (~55%). The **maximum is often chemistry**, not microbiology. Micro staffing and the chemistry calendar share a Monday 08:00 floor; FIFO is the main reason the join slides into Monday afternoon. Weekend on-call has little effect on terminal time because chemistry is still closed until Monday. The answer overweights on-call and underweights FIFO as the join driver.

S1 central join “Mon ~14:00–18:30” can still occur, but mainly from **chemistry queueing**, not from micro dragging into Monday evening. The headline Monday–Tuesday envelope is partly luck: other delays paper over a wrong bottleneck story.

## Forecast summary

Clock arithmetic from Friday 15:30 (66 h → Mon 09:30, etc.) is correct **conditional on the stated clock times**.

- Earliest Mon ~09:30–13:00 is a stacked optimistic envelope (priority start Friday, short residual service, micro already waiting, empty QA, short review). Acceptable as an envelope, not as something with material probability.
- P50-like Mon 18:00–Tue 14:00 is not crazy as a **smooth-plus-mild-queue** band, but as an overall mixture median it is optimistic: query 0.15–0.30 even on clean tests, investigation 0.04–0.10 even if both branches are normal, plus FIFO and reruns. A mixture P50 more naturally sits **Tuesday**.
- P80 Tue–Thu and P90 into the following week are the right *shape* (queries / doc investigations vs lab investigations and the bad cluster). Those bands are the best part of Section 4 and do not depend on the false Monday micro tail.
- Terminal event ranking (release modal, reject a contamination/critical-defect minority) is sound.

Reporting ranges rather than a fake point forecast meets the task. The remaining-hour bands are still only as good as the scenario clocks feeding them.

## What is actually strong (and why this is not a 3–4)

The answer does not add unconditional stage means. It uses competition of clocks, calendar pause/resume, mixed queue discipline, path splits (rerun, contamination confirmation, query, doc vs lab investigation), and a qualitative common-cause story for the unknown excursion. Assumption ledger, overlap note, and six-section layout match the spec. Dependence is not swept under independence. Shortcuts are labeled.

That is the difference between a careful wrong-bottleneck forecast and a naive sum-of-means forecast. It is not enough for a high score when the microbiology clock — the scenario’s most calendar-sensitive quantity — is shifted by 24 h, an impossible Tuesday readout scenario is given its own probability band, and the ranked #1 uncertainty is therefore misplaced.

**Net:** methodologically competent, factually untrustworthy on the micro/join story, forecast bands only partly rescued by chemistry and QA. **6.0.**
