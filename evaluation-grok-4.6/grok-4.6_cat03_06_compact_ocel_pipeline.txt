## Material inaccuracies

**1. P2/D2 is attributed to the wrong order.**  
In §4 the Order omissions include “O200’s exclusive P2/D2 path.” P2 packs I2; I2 belongs to O100; D2 carries P2. There is no O200–P2/D2 path. The predicted traces immediately above that sentence are correct (O100 has two pack/dispatch/confirm pairs; O200 has one). The omission list therefore contradicts both the narrative and the author’s own model. That is not a wording quibble: it inverts the only asymmetric fulfillment fact in the process.

**2. Object-interactions are undercounted at the exact coupling points.**  
§5 says O100 interacts with I1, I2, P1, P2, D1, D2, V1 and O200 with I3, P1, D1, V1. If interactions mean co-occurrence on an event — and the author says “via events” — then at least O100↔O200, O100↔I3, and O200↔I1 are missing. Those are the shared e06/e07 (and later e09/e11/e13) facts that make this log object-centric rather than two parallel orders. P1 and V1 are described more carefully; the order-level lists are not.

**3. Stated non-propagation policy is not the policy implemented.**  
§1 says E2O rows are observed involvements and that transitive reachability such as `Confirm D1 ↝ I1` via `D1→P1→I1` is not materialized. Confirm Delivery is then related to Package with `confirmed_package`. That *is* one-hop O2O propagation (Delivery `carries` Package). Native objects for those events are the deliveries. The choice is defensible, but it is not the rule the author claimed to be following. Package flattening is then predicted as Pack → Dispatch → Confirm only because of that extra hop.

**4. “M1 and M2 are partial payments” vs `completes_settlement`.**  
The process text calls both payments partial. The task permits `completes_settlement`, and the author flags the missing invoice total. The disclosure is good; the qualifier still overclaims a business fact the log does not contain. Using `partially_settles` for both would have been more faithful. This is a smaller issue than (1)–(3) because it is labeled as an assumption.

## Unclarities and looser reasoning

- The §4 “Omitted” bullet mixes per-case omissions, global omissions, and the inverted P2/D2 clause into one list. Even without the swap it is hard to see which case is being described.
- OC-DFG language treats P1 and V1 as “places where orders meet.” An OC-DFG is typed activity directly-follows plus coupling at shared activities, not an instance graph. The intended insight is right; the structure named is not.
- The object-centric Petri net paragraph never mentions that `Pack Package` (and `Dispatch Package` / `Confirm Delivery`) fire with different object cardinalities: e07 binds two orders and two items, e08 binds one of each. That is the main reason the net is underdetermined, and it is more important than a generic “miners differ” caveat.
- Order flattening will show `Issue Invoice → Confirm Delivery` because payments were deliberately not attached to orders. The intervening M1 at 11:30 is omitted from that DFG. The author says payments are omitted, but not that the remaining edge is a flattened illusion of consecutive work.
- OTG is asserted to be “from O2O only.” If `discover_otg` is implemented from E2O co-occurrence — a common reading — the graph would also contain Order–Package, Order–Delivery, Order–Invoice, Package–Delivery, Payment–Invoice, etc. No caveat is given, unlike the constructor/API comments elsewhere.
- Qualifiers reuse `packs` and `bills` across E2O and O2O, and logistics orders are dumped into a generic `related_order` while Reserve/Invoice get dedicated names. Failure mode 9 notices the collision; the vocabulary is still sloppier than the surrounding prose suggests.

## Why not higher

A high score here requires the analysis to be as careful as the tables. This answer builds the right log and then, in the sections that explain what that log means, mislabels the O100-only parcel, drops the O100–O200 interaction, and violates its own E2O/O2O firewall. Those are exactly the facts a reader uses to judge whether the author understood convergence, divergence, and source vs. derived relations. The rest of the pipeline is good enough that these are not beginner mistakes; they are avoidable inconsistencies in an otherwise professional write-up.

A nearly flawless answer would have kept §4’s omitted line consistent with check 8, listed O100/O200/I1/I3 co-occurrence in the interactions summary, and either related Confirm only to Delivery or admitted Confirm→Package as a one-hop exception. This is clearly above a merely adequate solution and clearly below that bar.
