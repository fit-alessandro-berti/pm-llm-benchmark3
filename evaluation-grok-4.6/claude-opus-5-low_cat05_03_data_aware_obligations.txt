**7.8**

A strong, unusually complete answer: every required heading is present, all eleven case verdicts are defensible, event-time reconstruction is handled carefully, and the code would likely reproduce the dataset outcomes. It is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions, undefined formal predicates, and untested code branches keep it well below a top score.

**Temporal semantics (strong, not clean).** The four-clock distinction (event / effective / record / evidence), blank-as-no-update, lex order `(timestamp, event_index)`, and the refusal to backdate TX-305/TX-306/TX-311 are exactly what the task asks for. Two problems: (i) “material data change” is silently widened from R4’s amount test to an undefined `scope_change_between` that is not in the policy text; (ii) R3’s no-prior-screen case is treated as a worst-case trigger in prose and in the pandas path, but the Datalog `r3_trigger` requires `last_screen(R,S), before(S,U)` and would not fire. That is a formalization error, not a gloss.

**Formalization (ambitious, underspecified).** The rule table is the right shape. The pseudo-logic then leaves `review/1`, `completed/1`, `last_screen/2`, `scope_change_between/2`, `approves/2`, and `plain_sat/1` undefined. `r2_trigger_U` is wrong: if `risk_level=HIGH` and `customer_type` is unknown, R2 is established, not unknown. `verdict(..., indeterminate)` fires on `material_U(_,R)` even when no amount-dependent obligation is live. `verdict(..., compliant) :- not verdict(R,_other)` defaults to COMPLIANT, which is unsafe under R6. Directionality of `≺` / `⪯` is otherwise stated clearly.

Specific faults:

- **§6 vs §4 on TX-302/TX-310.** Section 6 claims the traces are “near-identical” and that opposite verdicts are “decided entirely by evidence timestamps.” That is false. Labels differ (`Enhanced_Review` vs `Standard_Review`). A control-flow precedence constraint on Enhanced_Review already splits them. Section 4 correctly says TX-310 “does not hinge on EX-88 at all.” The later section contradicts the author’s own better analysis.
- **TX-311 alternatives.** “The reading that *would* change the result” (jurisdiction change as material invalidation) does not change the result: release jurisdiction is DE, so R1 never activates. The author then says so. The lead sentence is simply wrong.
- **TX-305 “if amount were 150000.”** The same paragraph says the case becomes COMPLIANT under R1 and that R4 would keep it INDETERMINATE. The second clause is the one required by R4/R6; the first should not have been written.
- **TX-306 stricter-approval reading** is useful, but R2 as written requires approve after the *relevant Enhanced_Review*, not after every later sub-20% amount edit. Presenting that stricter reading as a near-miss overstates policy ambiguity.
- Severity/confidence are asserted, not derived. “High / High” on every clear breach is fine; it is not an argument.

Exemption handling is the best part of the paper: EX-77 is valid and redundant (so COMPLIANT, not JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION); EX-88 fails only on evidence time and interested-party testimony; late insert vs backdated display is applied consistently with R5.

**Code (useful, not trustworthy off this log).** It meets the mechanical checklist: `StringIO`, `format_dataframe`, re-sort with `event_index`, ffill-only, leak assert on TX-305, exemption join, diagnostics, coarse precedence contrast. It does **not** hard-code case labels. Problems that would matter on any nearby log:

1. `r1_met_on_merits`’s second disjunct is dead; it simplifies to “ER exists and increase is known ≤20%.”
2. `r2_approval_packet_unverified` is `r2_met and er is None`, which is impossible. TX-307 is caught only because ER is missing, not because the packet is standard.
3. Known R1 trigger + unknown review amount is pushed to **NONCOMPLIANT** (`violations` wins over `unknowns`). R4/R6 require INDETERMINATE / REVIEW_REQUIRED.
4. R2 trigger is `None` if *either* risk or customer type is unknown, even when HIGH or PEP is already known.
5. Requirement 5 asked to track the most recent material data change. The frame stores a boolean, not an event time.
6. `scope_change_between` from the formalization is not implemented. Formalism and checker diverge.
7. `independent_corroboration=True/False` is a pre-baked judgment, not derived from evidence fields. Allowed as structured evidence, but it is where the EX-77/EX-88 verdict is decided.
8. `include_groups=False` is pandas ≥2.2; the DECLARE block will crash on older pandas. No execution trace is shown, only a comment about expected output.

On *this* CSV the coarse verdicts line up with the narrative (TX-305 → `REVIEW_REQUIRED` is permitted). That is luck about the test set, not proof the checker implements R1–R7.

**§6 and §7.** Six required failure modes are covered and the extras (object-centric list updates, packet identity) are real. Point 2 is the overclaim above; point 3’s “identical activity sequence” for TX-308/TX-309 is fair, for TX-309/TX-311 is not. Section 7 is concrete and aligned with the failure modes (immutable release payload, four timestamps, sentinel unknowns, sequence numbers, review/approval foreign keys, exemption predicates, policy version, synchronous release gate). Little to cut there.

**Net.** This is a high-quality working paper with the right case answers and the right conceptual frame. Under a strict reading it still contains a section-level contradiction, an extra-legal invalidation predicate that is defined in one place and dropped in another, several three-valued-logic errors, and checker branches that were never true on any row. Those are not polish nits; they are exactly the class of defect this task is meant to expose.
