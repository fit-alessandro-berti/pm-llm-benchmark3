**8.5**

The memo is strategically strong and mostly disciplined: it refuses national scale of R, refuses a blanket rollback, separates the simple-house gain from apartment/old-building harm, and treats R as a bundled intervention rather than a single success. Facts, interpretations, and uncertainties are usually kept apart. The five metric conflicts are explained in process-mining terms, not slogans. VoI is ranked by decision impact, with operational urgency distinguished from information value. That is a high-quality answer. It is not nearly flawless.

**1. Executive memo**

The recommendation (narrow E, executed with D-style concurrent comparisons) is defensible. Immediate pauses on survey skipping and post-billing activation are the right containment moves. Two weaknesses keep this from being crisp enough for a 9+.

First, the memo treats simple-house improvement as a reason to scale combined validate/schedule, while also stating that the package is unidentified. Those two claims sit together uneasily. Simple houses improved under the *whole* of R, including skip rules and later activation. Pausing those pieces may erase the headline median gain; the memo does not say what happens if the residual workflow then fails to beat baseline.

Second, the 6-week pilot versus 62-day p95 is mentioned only later as a cohort-definition issue. In the memo it is used as a hard operational fact. A 42-day window cannot produce a completed-case p95 of 62 days without extended follow-up, pre-pilot cases, or an incomparable duration definition. That is not a small caveat; it is a threat to the tail comparison the decision rests on. The memo should have treated p95 as provisionally uninterpretable, not as proven harm of known magnitude.

SLA breach (13% → 16%, improved for simple houses) and the directional bias in incomplete cost data are also missing from the decision narrative. Cost coverage stops after week 4 while complaints rose; the EUR 207 figure is therefore likely *too optimistic* for R, not merely noisy. That should have been in the investment-case paragraph.

**2. Evidence table**

This is the strongest section: 16 items, arithmetic checks (3.4 / 7.5 / 17 days; 69% + 38% > 100%), and a real logging-versus-violation distinction. Still not clean.

- “Manual-activation billing disputes” for business customers is taken from the notes column and placed as fact. The measured facts are +4pp complaints and higher manual activation; disputes as the mechanism are interpretation.
- SLA is absent despite being a core metric and a stated simple-house benefit.
- “Equivalent follow-up is unconfirmed” is too soft for the p95/window inconsistency.
- Discovery-score rows correctly refuse to treat 0.94 fitness as policy compliance, but “may transfer poorly” slightly conflates generalization (held-out traces from the same process) with external validity across regions.

**3. Metric conflicts**

Explanations of fitness versus precision, median versus tail, handover count versus resilience, and visible versus downstream cost are accurate and unusually careful (harmonized labels, time-to-event, flower-model permissiveness). The cost decomposition (EUR 14 vs EUR 46 downstream; EUR 32 offset vs EUR 23 visible saving) is arithmetically correct and properly labeled incomplete.

The gap is the missing bias direction already noted: excluding late complaint cost when the complaint rate rose should push the reader to treat R’s economics as worse than the table, not symmetrically uncertain. The apartment story is also slightly flattened: apartment *median* improved by 2 days even as p90 and first-time-right collapsed. The text is not wrong, but it makes the segment look uniformly worse than the evidence.

**4. Alternatives**

A–E are evaluated on the right axes. A is correctly ruled out; B is not chosen just because some KPIs worsened; C is not smuggled in as a national mandate. The explicit narrowing of E (do not export skip/billing-before-activation even to simple houses) is a real decision, not a restatement of the prompt.

The hybrid “E supported by D” is coherent but slightly evasive: E already includes measurement before scale. The useful content is the operating rule (pause skip and post-billing activation everywhere; scale only merged scheduling plus provisional ownership). That rule should have been the named choice, with D as method, not a second letter that blurs accountability for a single alternative.

**5. Value of information**

The ranking logic is mostly sound: M6 before any skip trial because E is a routing policy; M1 only after M6 and never on known permit-sensitive stock; M7 as immediate control rather than a reason to delay restoring the rule. Ethical constraints on M5 are correctly drawn.

Deductions: M2 is ranked too low. Case durations are completion-to-completion with sparse starts; that is not a refinement of an already-trusted speed win, it is a threat to whether median/p90/p95 mean waiting, work, or recording. M4 is ranked as if the EUR 9 gap were two-sided noise; given week-4 cost truncation and higher complaints, the prior should be that more complete costing is more likely to *worsen* R’s case. M8 last is arguable, but the complaint-theme shift already does part of that job; the write-up should have said so more tightly. The section is also longer than its decision value: clustering, multiple comparisons, and “no sample size follows from the totals” are correct and then over-taught.

**6. Final recommendation**

Immediate / pause / scale / measure is aligned with the memo and with the constraints. Predeclared gates are required by the prompt and are labeled as proposed, not discovered.

The gates themselves overreach. One-sided 95% bounds that “rule out” +2 days p90, +3 days p95, −1pp first-time-right, +0.5pp complaints, and +1pp SLA, plus a EUR 5 full-cost cap, are presented with false precision. p95 non-inferiority at that tolerance is likely infeasible in any ethical, clustered, 60-day follow-up design; combined with “if p95 is immature, the gate is unmet,” the rule can freeze expansion indefinitely. The 85% utilization ceiling is likewise invented. Better to specify *which* harms must be excluded and *which* benefits must be shown, and to say that p95 is a monitoring statistic until follow-up exceeds the tail.

**Net**

No fatal strategic error. The decision is the one the evidence supports. Remaining defects are real: one fact/interpretation bleed, underplayed cost-data bias, underplayed impossibility of a 62-day p95 inside a 6-week window, unidentified-component scaling, SLA omitted from the core evidence set, and over-precise rollout gates. Under a nearly-flawless bar those are enough to keep the score well below 9.
