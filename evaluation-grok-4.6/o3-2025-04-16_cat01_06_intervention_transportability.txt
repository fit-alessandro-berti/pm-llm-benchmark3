5.3

The answer is strategically pointed in the right direction and complete in outline, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses a large amount of credit for invented APIs, invalid process semantics, broken arithmetic, format violations, and confident numbers that the same write-up earlier treats as unidentified.

**What is actually good.** The core analogical move is correct: Site A’s AND-join before Assessment is shared; Site B’s originals gate, legal loop, specialized pools, and case lock are not. Wholesale copying is rejected. The redesigned rule—create Identity immediately, create Document only when originals are not required or have already arrived—respects the statute. Co-interventions at A (API, +2 document FTE, mix shift, huddles, timestamp inflation) are named rather than swallowed into a treatment effect. The bottleneck-shift scenario does what was asked: mean can improve while the tail worsens. `conditional go` is the only defensible recommendation.

**Section 1.** The required twelve features and columns are present, but several cells are sloppy or wrong. Daily huddles are listed as Site A *before* and after; the prompt places them among co-occurring changes, so they are not a stable background feature. “Toward 94%” digital at A is invented. The complexity cell is a fragment (“48 % high  40 %”). These are not cosmetic: they blur the very confounders the rest of the memo depends on.

**Section 2.** This fails the specification. The prompt asked for a directed adjacency list and 180–280 words. What appears is informal graph slang (`-|->`, comments, `Identity_service_time`) and roughly 110 words, including the broken clause “digital_documents share.” Shared vs site-specific mechanisms are named, but too briefly to do the causal work. Selection nodes are incomplete relative to the required variable list, and `appeal <- cycle_time` is an underspecified afterthought.

**Section 3.** The refusal to treat A’s 9-hour median drop as identified is the strongest methodological sentence in the memo. Confidence scores are reasonably low. Then the table itself is ragged: deltas are labeled as if they were levels (“median cycle-time 9 h”), mix shift and the missing negative control are not first-class rows, and “API latency drop / transferable = non-portable” is confused. The DFG of 3,400 Site B cases is unused, so transfer claims are not anchored in B’s actual path frequencies (e.g. Originals→Document 2,108; Legal→Supplement 360; Supplement→Document 345).

**Section 4.** Coverage of the ten required modifiers is adequate. Several “signature” cells are empty or truncated (“wait-to-Assessment”, “tail CT”), so the beneficial/harmful contrast is not actually stated. “Identity reviewers starved” is a possible imbalance, but the more obvious Site B failure is document/originals delay plus a 2-person legal pool. Mitigation “keep legal WIP 2” just restates current headcount.

**Section 5.** Five labeled scenarios include the required tail-worsens case, but the quantitative mapping is asserted, not shown. Scenario 1 is structurally illicit: it parallelizes “digital & original cases” for 100% of volume even though Document Check cannot start before originals when they are legally required. Digital-only 7–11 h in-subgroup is an aggressive transport of a confounded 9 h movement at A, where wait-to-Assessment only fell 6 h and service times also fell for non-parallel reasons. Scenario 5’s “1 h” median change has no sign. The one-sentence “reasoning” (20 h wait × 0–80% overlap, plus A adjustments) does not reproduce the table.

**Section 6.** The policy design is the best part of the answer. The POWL sketch is not. Operators are missing, precedence symbols drop out, and eligibility “digital OR originals scanned” is then silently intersected with “not public-sector > €100 k”—a threshold the prompt never gives. Treating that intersection as 38% of volume is a hard error: 38% is 100%−62% originals, 40% is legal/high-value, and the joint share is unidentified. Later the recommendation repeats the 38% claim, so the error is load-bearing.

**Section 7.** The mechanism-vs-implementation idea is right; the design does not implement it cleanly. Case-level Bernoulli assignment is said to “eliminate team spill-over” in the same table that lets the same reviewers serve both arms—those statements cannot both be true. Huddles as V2 after four weeks are a team-level co-intervention and will contaminate controls. Power arithmetic is inconsistent: 550 pairs plus 1,200 eligible cases cannot occur in 6–8 weeks at ~215 digital completions per month. The stop rule “halt if p90 cycle >15 h” is absurd against a 110 h baseline; it would fire immediately. That single clause would make the pilot unusable as written.

**Section 8 (largest technical failure).** The prompt required `pm4py.parse_process_tree(...)` for Site A. The code never calls it. `Operator.SEQ` is not the PM4Py name (`SEQUENCE`). The POWL import (`POWL`, `POWLNode`, `AND`, `SEQ`, `add_node`, `add_order`) is not the library’s object model; `StrictPartialOrder` / `OperatorPOWL` / `Transition` and `order.add_edge` are. Worse than the fake API is the semantics: a strict partial order cannot contain the cycle Request Supplement → Document Check → Assessment → Legal Review → Request Supplement, and putting Legal Review, Approve, and Reject in the same node set means every case executes all three. `add_order(n_id, n_doc)` contradicts both the comment and the proposed intervention. `play_out` is called without a final marking. Workflow-net checks are incomplete; Woflan is imported and unused. The limitation comment is the only part that matches the brief. Model fidelity is therefore poor.

**Section 9.** `conditional go` is right, then the memo overclaims: 7–11 h median and 6–10 h p90 in phase 1, and huddles as “30%” of A’s gain. Both figures conflict with Section 3’s own warning. Expansion gates (wait-for-originals vs identity time; legal-loop stability) are sensible.

**Section 10.** The eight-row template is met. Several assumptions smuggle extra fiction (union objections) or tautology (“digital cases do not require originals”) while still conflating channel with the statutory originals gate.

**Net.** This is a competent transport memo with the right refusal to copy Site A, undermined by invalid POWL/Petri semantics, a pilot that cannot be fielded as specified, eligibility math that treats distinct strata as the same 38%, a short and non-compliant transport graph, unused Site B DFG evidence, and effect sizes recycled from a confounded before/after. Those are not polish problems. They are exactly the analogical, quantitative, and model-fidelity failures the rubric said to punish.
