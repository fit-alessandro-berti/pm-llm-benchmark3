5.5

The submission is complete in outline and conceptually aligned with the task, but it is not close to reliable. It systematically misstates the evidence it claims to rest on, invents quantities that are not in the log, mishandles several of the intended hard cases, and presents PM4Py usage that would not run as written. Under a strict reading those are not cosmetic defects; they break the central requirement to infer roles from the given matrices rather than from rounded storytelling.

**Evidence handling is the decisive failure.** Section 2 repeatedly converts raw counts into specialization percentages that are simply false, and the errors all run in the same direction: they make every role look cleaner than the matrix is. Erik’s A6 work is not 90% of his events (96/170 ≈ 56%). Hana’s A9 work is not 90% of hers (138/245 ≈ 56%). Fatima’s A7+A8 work is not 95% of hers (212/291 ≈ 73%). Giorgio’s A2+A4 work is not 60% of his (340/820 ≈ 41%). Mateo’s 42 reopens are not 20% of his events (≈10%), and the listed peripheral bundle is not 40% (≈22%). Ivan/Julia are not at 95% quality/KB; that figure is obtained by adding two people’s A11 counts and treating the sum as a single workload share (actual A11+A14 shares are ~75% and ~77%). Front-office “80%+” on A1/A2/A4 is only true if A12 Close is silently ignored; without it Amira is about 67%. “70%+ low-complexity” is also wrong (Amira 410/702 ≈ 58%, Ben ≈ 66%). Technical “60–80% high-complexity cases” is wrong (Chen high+critical ≈ 42%). These are not rounding slips. They manufacture the core/peripheral contrast the section is supposed to demonstrate.

**Several “facts” are not in the data at all.** Mateo is said to cut Erik’s load by 30%; the log only says Erik did fewer A6 events than expected after filtering. Chen’s 24 A6 events are placed “during high incident load”; that episode is about Erik/Mateo, not Chen. “55 critical cases (70% of their critical cases)” is circular. Giorgio’s 31 substitution A7 events are repeatedly conflated with 31% of Fatima’s work and with his 10-week total of 72. Once invented numbers enter the argument, later staffing and risk claims inherit them.

**A12 Close is missing from the front-office model.** Amira 260 and Ben 280 closes are among their largest columns, and the dominant happy path is Register → Validate → Standard Response → Close. Treating Close as invisible also produces the false 80%/98% core-activity claims for permanent and temporary agents. That is a process-structure error, not a table omission.

**The “why naïve clustering fails” column is often logically false.** Fatima would not collapse into front office on activity frequencies; her profile is already dominated by A7/A8 and near-zero A1/A4. Chen/Dina would not look like quality analysts on A10/A11 counts (215/12 vs 38/310). Hana and Mateo at cosine 0.54 are not a frequency-clustering collision. “Misclassified as shadow if we ignore title” is not a clustering failure mode. The one good contrast — permanent FO vs temps, Erik vs volume roles, Hana authority vs Mateo brokerage — is diluted by these unforced errors.

**Role assignment is mostly plausible but flattens the benchmark’s hardest distinctions.** Grouping Chen with Dina and Amira with Ben is defensible only if seniority/risk is then treated as a first-class split; the data are written to prevent that collapse (0.94/0.93 activity similarity, sharply different complexity and security-adjacent load). The writeup notes the caveat and then still emits a single role with no seniority tier. Mateo’s “core” of preliminary A9 plus A13 is also forced: his profile is flat (Close 52, Validate 50, A4 45, A9 44, Reopen 42). He is a broker because he touches everything, not because those two activities dominate. Giorgio is simultaneously a formal hybrid role (Section 1) and a substitution shadow (Section 3), without reconciling that 31 of 72 A7 events are an absence episode and therefore not baseline competence.

**Section 3 meets the prompt’s minimum but mislabels authority.** Giorgio, Mateo, and Erik are discussed with benefits and risks, which is what was asked. The extra “shadow” treatment of Hana is wrong: she is the formal approver; rechecking Mateo is ordinary hierarchical control, not a shadow role. “False escalations caused delays” compresses two different outcomes (complaints fell; two critical cases waited). Erik as a single point of failure is overstated given Chen 24 and Mateo 16 A6 events.

**Section 4 is structurally messy and aims at the weaker ambiguities.** Giorgio’s first “interpretation” is stated and refuted in the same bullet. Mateo as “temporary coordinator” contradicts his permanent Incident Coordinator title. Ivan/Julia as “pure quality vs quality plus 5% technical” is a trivial fork. The deciding evidence that actually matters — same activity mix, different complexity/authority/risk, substitution versus standing work — is only partly used. Amira vs Ben and Chen vs Dina are the obvious ambiguous pairs and are not framed as such.

**The PM4Py section is illustrative in intent and incorrect in API, data model, and method.** `discover_organizational_roles` does not take `variant_filtering`, `min_support`, or `max_depth`. Similarity and SNA calls are given the same invented kwargs. `format_dataframe` uses `activity=` rather than `activity_key`, and resource is not handled as `org:resource`. Role objects are treated as one-resource dicts (`role['resource']`), which is not what the miner returns. Post-format column `resource` will not match. Nora is dropped before discovery, so the automation role proposed in Section 1 cannot be recovered by the code. `case_complexity` is already a case attribute on the stated log, not a resource-level CSV to merge. The “avoid over-interpretation” helper only counts A9. There is no substitution-period split, no complexity-conditioned similarity, no comparison of profiles with and without temporary_substitution, and no use of the other named functions in a way that would stop frequency clustering from being treated as authority.

**Section 6 is the strongest part and still inherits the bad numbers.** The cautions about authority ≠ frequency, substitution ≠ competence, and complexity ≠ interchangeable staffing are the right cautions. Recommendations to formalize Mateo’s filter rules, train temps on entitlement notes, and avoid promoting on event counts are operationally sound. Then the action plan rests on the same overconfident quantification (“30%”, “17/190”, hire a commercial specialist, train Chen on a backup he already performs). Two required cautions are present; they do not offset the evidentiary problems upstream.

The piece reads like a competent template filled with un-checked arithmetic. The organizational story is directionally right: temps ≠ permanent FO, Fatima ≠ Giorgio, Hana ≠ Mateo, Erik is rare-critical, Nora is a boundary, shadow roles have benefits and risks. That is why this is not a low-end score. It is not a high score because a process-owner artifact that inflates every core-activity share into the 90s, invents a 30% load shift, forgets Close, and ships non-existent PM4Py parameters would mislead staffing decisions in exactly the way Section 6 warns against.
