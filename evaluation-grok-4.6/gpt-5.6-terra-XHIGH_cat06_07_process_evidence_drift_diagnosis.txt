**Conceptual softness on June.** Calling the V2 surge “covariate drift” is loose. Prediction is immediately after diagnosis, so V1/V2/V3 is usually a *downstream path*, not \(X\). A remote-routing policy can change \(P(Y\mid X)\) unless remotability is already in the features. The write-up follows the prompt’s preferred line (“need not imply concept drift”) but never states the condition under which that is true. It also asserts that mix “helps explain” June’s 0.15 breach rate without doing the obvious weighted check: \(0.30\cdot0.16+0.52\cdot0.09+0.18\cdot0.35\approx0.16\). That calculation would have turned a hypothesis into evidence. The same arithmetic for August (\(\approx0.31\) vs observed 0.28) would have shown the rise is almost entirely V3 rate × V3 share.

**Censoring is described, not bounded.** The answer correctly refuses to treat October as comparable, but it never gives the selection *direction* or simple bounds. With longer paths delayed, observed breach among available labels is likely *downward*-biased, especially for V3 and Partner Z. August 93% × 0.28 still implies a true rate of about 0.26–0.33; September 82% × 0.31 implies about 0.25–0.43. Two defects matter. First, timestamps are forced UTC, then `case_start.min().to_period("M")` is used; tz-aware `Timestamp.to_period` fails in common pandas 2.x builds, so the monthly loop may never start. Second, `raw.get("source", "unknown_source")` is a DataFrame footgun that only happens to work, and `perf_dfg.get((...), np.nan)` assumes a scalar edge value; in several PM4Py versions the performance DFG stores a statistic map. `consecutive_edge_median_hours` also drops the parts edge if an `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` sits between `Order Part` and `Part Received`, i.e., it is least reliable in the month you most need to audit.
