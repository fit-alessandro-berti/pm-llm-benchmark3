**7.5**

This is a strong, well-structured control-objective analysis that mostly does the hard parts right: it does not treat four-eyes form as the objective, it does not treat “a control fired” as sufficiency, and it does not grade from amount or outcome alone. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are instruction violations, a few factual/timing errors, and one real contradiction with the answer’s own sufficiency standard.

**What holds up.** The O1–O7 decomposition is the right grain: honest error, unilateral fraud, collusion, substance/receipt, payee redirection, recoverability, and attribution. Mapping AV-2 / MG-4 / RA-7 / ED-1 as prevent / detect / correct / fail-to-address is accurate, and treating ED-1 as a permission gate rather than a substitute control is correct. The seven-dimension equivalence test (timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance) is the right test, and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the sole standard is well argued. Case direction of travel is mostly right: AP-203/AP-207 can be MET despite same-user; AP-204 can FAIL despite distinct users; AP-205 is prevented-before-completion; AP-208 can FAIL despite a lucky legitimate outcome; AP-202 is a clean independence collapse. Section 6 correctly treats alerts as investigation triggers, not proof.

**Instruction and table defects (not cosmetic).** The required classification set is exclusive. AP-209 is given as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET / INDETERMINATE`, and AP-206 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET (lean MET)`. That is not “use one of these.” AP-209’s table row also has an extra `Medium` cell, so Severity / Confidence / Main rationale do not line up with the header. Those are failures on the specified output contract, not style nits.

**Logical tension with Section 2.** Section 2 correctly says governance, deterrence, and attribution can make a package unacceptable even when this instance’s expected loss is small. Section 3 then uses AP-201’s trivial EUR 1,800 exposure and “correct vendor” to avoid `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` for unjustified convenience same-user handling (no emergency token, so ED-1 is inapplicable). Low residual loss is a severity point; it does not mean the segregation objective was partially met. On the answer’s own rubric this is closer to “merely mitigating.” Calling m04’s review “substantive-adjacent” is also unsupported: the facts only say the manager reviewed after release and before settlement.

**Timing errors.** AP-201 audit is 25 hours after *release* but about 23 hours after *settlement* at 10:00, and it falls the next morning—i.e., within a normal “one business day” reading of RA-7. Treating it as outside both the recall window and one business day overstates the loss of corrective capacity. Relatedly, `Manager_Ratify` versus `Manager_Approve` is under-analyzed. The log uses Approve only before release (AP-203, AP-206) and Ratify only after release. Classifying post-release / pre-settlement MG-4 as “hybrid, weighted preventive” assumes the ratifier could still halt settlement. That is not in evidence; if Ratify is after-the-fact attestation, AP-201 and AP-207 are weaker than scored.

**Incomplete ED-1 application on AP-208.** The prose hangs the failure mainly on AV-2 being down and post-settlement ratification. ED-1 is independently dead on condition 2 (EUR 900,000 ≮ 100,000) and condition 4 (bank change three days earlier), plus condition 5. The code flags the amount cap; the case rationale does not. Outcome-independence is right; the condition coverage is not full.

**AP-206 wobble.** Section 3 is PARTIALLY_MET because the token is after Prepare. Section 5 then says the case “leans MET despite the ED-1 technicality.” Condition 1 is not a technicality; it is the pre-authorization artifact that stops a manufactured emergency. The substance of MG-4/AV-2 can still be strong without upgrading the classification.

**Smaller case issues.** AP-204’s fabricated receipt already fails O4 regardless of whether u12/u13 colluded; confidence should be high on `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`, not Medium-High. AP-202’s conflict is “sets performance rating,” not demonstrated “direct supervisor.” AP-209 is the one case that should stay single-label `INDETERMINATE` given missing org-design evidence; splitting the label is how the answer avoids committing.

**Code: usable evidence pipeline, not a clean join, and some invented facts.** The CSV reconstruction, UTC parse, defensive `format_dataframe` / `filter_four_eyes_principle` usage, manual four-eyes ground truth, blocked-release handling for AP-205, and “evidence not verdict” labeling all match the prompt. Version caveats on whether the filter keeps violators or conformers are warranted. Failures: there is no actual join of the log to the facts frame (only a separate `apply`); `manager_indep=True` is hardcoded for AP-204 and AP-209, which have no manager event; AP-202 `vendor_days=30` and AP-208 `token_before_prepare=True` are not given in the facts; AP-201 `audit_within_1bd=False` repeats the timing error. If `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps conforming cases—as current pm4py typically does—the printed “surfaced” list is easy to misread as violations even with the comment.

Net: high-quality control reasoning with the right non-mechanical case pattern, undermined by exclusive-label violations, a broken AP-209 row, a self-contradiction on expected-loss versus AP-201, sloppy recall-window arithmetic, and an incomplete ED-1 kill-chain on the largest exposure. That is well above a mediocre answer and clearly below a near-flawless one.
