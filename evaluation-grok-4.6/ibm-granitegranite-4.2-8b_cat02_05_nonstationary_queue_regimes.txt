## Fatal regime error

The primary segmentation is the core of the task and it fails.

- **NS** is 6–12 April and **RE** is 11–17 April. Those ranges overlap. 11–12 April cannot be both “normal steady” and “recovery.”
- **18–28 April is mostly unassigned** in the primary table, including the most important stretch in the data.
- On **20 April**, `complex_pct` jumps from the low 20s to **41**, `peak_08_10_pct` jumps into the 40s, median/p90 service jump, and completions fall persistently below arrivals through 28 April while WIP climbs into the 40s–50s. That is a distinct congested, mix-shifted regime at unchanged ~6.0 weekday FTE. The answer never makes it a primary regime.
- The alternative segmentation repeats the error in a worse form: it claims complex mix crosses 30% on **14 April (value 30)**. The table says 14 April is **24**, 15 April **25**, 16 April **27**. The break is **20 April (41)**. That is not a rounding issue; it is a misread of the only mix column.

Because of this, staffing intervention on 29 April is framed as a new high-WIP steady state. In the data, 29 April–3 May is **backlog burn-down** (completions ≥ arrivals, WIP 43 → 19) after nine days of net accumulation. Outage recovery on 16 April (129 completions, `staff_fte` 7.0, WIP 52) is then averaged into the same “RE” bucket as a normal weekend (WIP 14) and the outage day (58 completions). That is exactly the pooling the quality bar says not to do: recovery high-throughput is not a normal regime, and it is not the same object as the outage.

There are two staffing events, not one: extra FTE on 16–17 April, reversion to 6.0, then the 29 April intervention. The answer collapses this.

## Quantitative work is often wrong

Pooled arrivals are given as **105.1**. Sum of the 28 daily arrivals is 2619, mean **93.5**. Pooled completions are given as **107.0**; the mean is **86.0**. Those are the headline pooled numbers.

Hourly analysis confuses the **number of bins (12)** with the **mean count**. 7 April arrivals sum to 101, mean **≈8.4**, not 12. 15 April mean is **8.75**, not 11.5. Fano/ID/CV derived from those means are therefore invalid. “Lag-1 correlation” in the narrative is successive **ratios** (including values such as 1.8), not correlation. Later Python uses `np.corrcoef`, so the prose and the code do not implement the same quantity.

Little’s Law is written correctly as \(W = L / \lambda\) with \(\lambda =\) completions / 12 h, giving about **2.4 h** for a weekday with WIP 20 and 100 completions. The same answer calls NS “comfortable,” “pressure low,” and “queue typically <10” with WIP ~20 and 6 servers. That implies ~14 in queue and sojourn ≈ **six times** median service (~24 min). The implied wait and the capacity story are never reconciled. Using median service to compute a 180/day ceiling, then declaring slack, ignores the empirical \(L\) the author just computed.

The “reproducible” block prints **NS flow time 1.82 h**. Under the stated formula, NS weekdays are ~2.3–2.5 h and NS weekends ~3.8–4.1 h. 1.82 h matches neither the formula nor any coherent subset. Section 2 dates, Python masks, and printed tables are three different segmentations.

## Weaker but still material issues

- Simpson’s-paradox-like reversals are named in the audit and never shown. “Median service is not mean flow time” is not Simpson’s paradox.
- No cumulative arrivals−completions balance, which is the direct backlog accounting. The outage is a one-day inventory shock; 20–28 April accumulates a much larger net shortfall.
- Representative hourly profiles are treated as those calendar days, but they do not match `peak_08_10_pct` (21 April: 08:00–09:59 is ~10% of that vector vs 47% in the daily table). The prompt marks them as candidates; that mismatch is a data-audit item and is missed.
- Capacity math uses median service as if it were mean processing time, assumes 720 productive minutes per FTE, and still does not notice that open-hours snapshot \(L\) plus open-hours \(\lambda\) is **open-hours sojourn**, not calendar sojourn (overnight closure). NS is marked `applicable` with too little boundary caution.
- The straw-man policy “staff weekdays at the pooled 5.1 FTE” is weak: that pool is mostly weekend 2.5 mixed with weekday 6.0. A sharper pooled-vs-segment trap is “6.0 FTE is enough because theoretical capacity ≫ arrivals,” which fails in the mix-shift week.
