**3.5**

The answer has the right skeleton and a few sound case calls, but it is not a reliable controls assessment. Material fact errors, a broken classification taxonomy, an incomplete equivalence test, and non-executable checking code keep it far from a high score.

**Headings and form.** Required top-level headings were not used exactly (`### **1.**` rather than `## 1.`). That is minor next to the substance problems.

**1. Decomposition is shallow and partly misframed.** Five rows exist, but “late manager approval” is a compensating-control timing failure, not a FEP-1 threat. Several coverage claims are wrong: AV-2 does not prevent collusion or establish receipt; RA-7 is not “none” for account compromise; RA-7 can be partly corrective inside the recall window. ED-1 is treated as a generic override rather than a conditional permission that still serves the four-eyes objective.

**2. The sufficiency test is incomplete and internally inconsistent.** “Fully equivalent” requires AV-2 to *block release and* MG-4 to be pre-settlement—an illogical conjunction (a blocked payment needs no manager). “Mitigating” includes post-settlement MG-4, which is weak, not mitigating in any useful sense. Timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, and collusion resistance are listed but not turned into an operational test. The required explanation of why “same expected loss” is not the only standard is simply missing.

**3. Case findings contain taxonomy and fact errors.**

- **AP-201 as OBJECTIVE_FAILED** ignores the instruction not to map every same-user case to failure. Independent MG-4 occurred before 10:00 settlement; amount is EUR 1,800; payment was correct. Residual risk “High (no recall)” is false. This is technically nonconforming but tolerable / `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`.
- **AP-203 as LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION** is a category error. The payment completed and settled. ED-1 conditions largely hold. That is `OBJECTIVE_MET` (or at most partial), not a blocked loss.
- **AP-204** misses the fabricated service receipt, invents MG-4 (no manager event), and reports “RA-7 (no defect).” Form-satisfying four-eyes with a fabricated receipt and a repeated shared-workstation pair is `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`, not partial.
- **AP-206 as OBJECTIVE_FAILED** overreaches. The only clear ED-1 break is token-after-prepare, which the facts flag as possibly a logging delay. Other conditions hold. Bank-ownership “unclear” is invented (no bank change; AV-2 passed). `INDETERMINATE` or `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`.
- **AP-209** invents timely MG-4; the log has no manager activity.
- **AP-202, AP-205, AP-208** are the only solid classifications. **AP-207** as partial is defensible.

Corrective capacity is listed as “None” for almost every case, including those with pre-settlement manager action. Confidence is often inverted.

**4. Control-type semantics contradict themselves.** The table calls post-release / pre-settlement MG-4 a detective+preventive hybrid; the gloss then calls it detective+corrective. The chosen semantics are never stated cleanly. After release, the authorization decision has already occurred; pre-settlement review can still stop irrevocable settlement, so it is detective of release and potentially corrective of settlement—not preventive of FEP-1.

**5. Counterfactuals recycle the same errors.** AP-201 and AP-207 are said to have “no compensating controls” despite AV-2 and pre-settlement MG-4. AP-204’s loss mechanism is treated as “shared workstation,” not the fabricated receipt. The independent-second-releaser column often does not say what would change. Qualitative residual-risk language is crude and, for AP-201/206, factually wrong.

**6. Monitoring is a checklist, then mislabels proof.** Unresolved credential sharing in AP-202 is called “proof.” The facts say it is unresolved. Same-user “>5% (e.g., 3/60)” is an invented base rate that does not match this log.

**7. The code would not run and does not implement the spec.**

- CSV includes only AP-201/AP-202 and uses the wrong header (`case` vs `case:concept:name`).
- An XES importer is applied to CSV text.
- Imports are not `pm4py.format_dataframe` / `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`.
- UTC parsing and a proper `format_dataframe(...)` call are absent.
- `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps cases whose two activities have *different* resources; the code treats the result as violations and never takes the complement.
- AP-205 cannot be recovered from the truncated log.
- `!= "Warning" & != "Block"` has operator-precedence bugs; AP-206 is flagged for bank-change proximity though no bank change occurred.
- The version caveat is hand-waving over a wrong API.

**Net.** Some structure, some correct high-severity calls (202/205/208), and a gesture at form-versus-substance on u12/u13 are not enough. An assessor relying on this memo would mislabel a completed emergency payment as a prevented loss, miss a fabricated receipt, treat a low-value pre-settlement ratification as a hard failure, and could not reproduce the four-eyes check. That is well below a competent answer.
