The answer is substantively strong on transportability and correctly refuses a verbatim copy of Site A, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, broken model construction, inconsistent eligible-share arithmetic, and several causal-graph errors are enough to keep it well below the 8–10 band.

**What holds up.** The comparison table covers the required features and correctly treats statutory originals, legal review, role specialization, and the case lock as non-transportable structure rather than cosmetic site flavor. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, mix shift, huddles, timestamp recording, no control) are not laundered into a treatment effect; the −9 h median is explicitly unidentified. The bottleneck-shift scenario is the right qualitative prediction: median can improve while p90 worsens. `conditional go`, a digital/non-legal first stratum, and a pilot that separates ITT, true overlap, and lock/back-entry fidelity match the causal problem.

**Section 8 is a major miss on model fidelity.** The POWL is not a well-formed unique-node model. The same `t_document` object is a child of the digital XOR branch, of `Originals → Document`, and of the supplement redo; `t_assess` is both the top-level Assessment and a redo node. That is not a valid parent/child POWL. `OperatorPOWL(..., children=[legal_loop, None])` is invalid; wrapping `check_is_workflow_net(net, im, fm)` and `play_out(siteB_powl, no_traces=5)` in `try/except` conceals likely API mismatches (`check_is_workflow_net` is typically net-only; `play_out` expects a Petri net, not a POWL). The required soundness/workflow checks therefore may never actually run. Comments on what the model cannot encode are fine; the executable core is not.

**Quantitative transport is internally inconsistent.** Digital-only transfer uses 35–40% eligible while stating legal is excluded; later text uses 20–25%; the recommendation says “roughly one-third” for digital, non-legal, low/standard complexity. If originals-required (62%), legal (40%), and high-complexity (48%) are even weakly independent, the Phase-1 slice is closer to ~10–20%, not one-third. Population effects of −0.8 to −2.0 h are then too optimistic. The “ideal ~3.4 h” bound also confuses identity *service* time with the actual target, waiting before Assessment.

**Transportability graph errors.** `S_legal_strictness → legal_capacity` treats a demand/selection difference as a capacity node. `digital_documents → originals_required` is backwards. `S_case_lock → true_overlap` bypasses `IT_case_lock` while `S_IT_system → IT_case_lock` already exists. Capacity gaps (Site A 18–20 verification FTE vs Site B 4+7+3+2) lack selection nodes. The 180–280 word note is in range and conceptually right about shared waiting-time mechanism vs site-specific originals/legal/lock, but the graph does not fully support that prose.

**Smaller but real defects.** Queue discipline in the comparison table underplays Site A’s six-open-task WIP cap. The legal LOOP in the process spec (`LegalReview < XOR(Exit, SupplementPath)`) does not match the code. Pilot “400–600 cases per arm to detect 2–3 h” is asserted, not derived. Section 2 has a sentence fragment (“Identity-capacity… is also shared as generic queueing”).

**Not a 5.** The answer still does the hard parts: it does not parallelize a legally unstartable Document Check, does not ship Site A’s −9 h, names co-interventions, and designs a segmented pilot with stop rules. **Not an 8+.** A high score required a node-unique, actually checkable POWL, one coherent eligible-share, and a selection diagram that does not mis-wire demand as capacity.
