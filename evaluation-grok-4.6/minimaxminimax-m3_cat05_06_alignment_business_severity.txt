**6.4**

The write-up is structurally complete and, as business analysis, often strong: it separates cost from risk, treats silent `tau` as fitting, flags the zero-cost P1/P2 failures, and does not treat a missing event as proof of non-occurrence. Under a strict reading of the spec, that is not enough.

**Factual errors.** On CL-604 the alignment is `L(Pay Claim) → M(Fraud Screen) → S(Manual Approve) → M(Pay Claim)`. Fraud Screen is a model-only repair, not a logged activity. The table still says Fraud Screen and Manual Approve “are present but Pay Claim preceded them.” That misreads the most important control-failure case. On CL-610, P2 is not violated “in spirit”: recent beneficiary-bank change is an explicit P2 trigger. CL-602’s “both are present logically” is the same sloppiness on a EUR 480k missing-screen case.

**Section 7 does not meet the executable-code bar.** `supplied_alignments` mixes 2-tuples `(S, activity)` with 3-tuples `(L/M/T, activity, cost)`. Both `sum(cost for _, _, cost in moves)` and `for kind, act, cost in moves` raise `ValueError` on the first synchronous move. The required “one executable Python code block” is not executable.

**Aggregation logic is wrong even if the unpacking were fixed.** `P1_violated` / `P2_violated` are computed from amount, risk, and bank-change only. Any high-value or HIGH-risk case that correctly took Fraud Screen + Manual Approve (CL-606, CL-608, CL-611) is marked as a policy breach. P1 is an Auto Approve eligibility rule, not a general attribute flag. The table then sorts on `risk_signal = 3·P3 + 2·P2 + 1·P1`, which is exactly the formulaic ranking the prompt forbids, and there is no separate narrative Severity 1–5 column next to `raw_cost`.

**Smaller issues.** Pattern E is labeled “data-aware auto-approval failures” and then folds in CL-602/CL-604, which used the manual path. CL-606 at severity 4 sits awkwardly next to a logging-gap narrative with an external signed report. PM4Py alignments are computed and then ignored; that is allowed, but it makes the reproduction decorative.

Sections 1, 3, 4, and 6 are the best parts. The grade is held down by a broken required artifact and by misstating what the authoritative alignments actually say on the highest-severity cases.
