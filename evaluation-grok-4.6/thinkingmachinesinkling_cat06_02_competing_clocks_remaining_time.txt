**6.4**

The final six-section writeup is structurally complete and substantively above a weak PPM answer: it uses `max` rather than summed branch times, keeps Friday–Monday laboratory and QA closures explicit, separates queue wait from service, treats FIFO vs risk-priority as a real split, and discusses temperature-flag dependence instead of multiplying anomalies independently. Those are the main things the rubric rewards.

It is not close to flawless. Several issues are more than nits.

**Internal contradictions.** Scenario B is the clearest break. The decomposition’s “central” rerun finishes Monday ~17:18, but the scenario table’s central terminal window is Tuesday 09:00–16:00. That is not a rounding difference; it changes which day is treated as typical. Scenario A has a smaller version of the same problem: the table’s central terminal range starts Monday 15:00, while the prose has release Monday 17:30–20:00.

**Late-biased central forecast.** Clean-path probability is set at 0.20–0.30, which is low given baseline rerun (0.10–0.20), contamination (0.05–0.12), query (0.15–0.30), and investigation-if-normal (0.04–0.10). Even after a 0.20–0.40 temperature flag, a large mass should still finish Monday evening after a chemistry-dominated join plus same-day QA. A P50-like band of 90–110 h (Tue morning through Wed early) therefore sits about half a day to a day too late. The “most plausible” Tuesday/Wednesday claim inherits that shift.

**Arithmetic and clock mapping.** 90 h after Friday 15:30 is Tuesday ~09:30, not ~06:00; 110 h is Wednesday ~05:30, not ~08:00. The hour ranges and the printed clock ranges do not match.

**The answer disagrees with itself on the dominant smooth-path clock.** Scenario A correctly makes Monday chemistry the join bottleneck (microbiology without contamination is typically ready Monday ~08:30–09:30). Section 5 then ranks weekend microbiology authorization as the top driver of the join. On the answer’s own numbers, weekend call-out rarely moves the join; it only matters in a fast-chemistry left tail. Contamination confirmation *is* material; weekend staffing mostly is not. Scenario C’s title (“delayed readout or confirmation”) blurs that distinction.

**Incomplete queue/calendar mechanics.** Friday’s remaining analyst time (roughly 2 h + 1 h) is applied to B-417’s start, not to burning down the four samples already ahead. That systematically delays Monday chemistry. Scenario B’s “earliest” Monday 17:00 is also not earliest: a short primary plus short same-day rerun can finish Monday afternoon. The contamination path with weekend first readout (Sunday indication → Monday confirmation) is omitted.

**Uncertainty reporting is a bit loose.** P80 is written as 90–170 h, i.e. from the P50 floor through a laboratory-investigation horizon that is closer to a P90–P95 event (~0.05–0.10 after mixing investigation rates and the 20–40% lab share). Rejection is named but never even roughly sized.

What remains strong is the scaffolding: assumption table with sensitivities, overlapping scenario bands, join-as-max, weekend pause, future QA-queue uncertainty, and a well-chosen next observation (temperature flag) with signed updates. The answer is a competent approximate forecast with several consistency and calibration failures. Under a strict bar that reserves high scores for near-clean work, that is mid-6, not 8+.
