6.2

The write-up hits the required seven-section skeleton and is mostly grounded in queues, utilizations, variants, and the I1–I10 conflict hints. It does not just add capacity, does not optimize only the mean, and generally refuses to treat every case type as simultaneously first. That is the ceiling. The remaining defects are enough to keep it well short of a high score.

**Process-mining evidence is mishandled.** Normal and peak DFGs cover unequal windows (8 weeks vs 3 weeks). Raw arc counts are therefore not comparable, yet S1 treats Pre-analytical → Test preparation “dropping” from 16,860 to 8,530 as a fact that then has to be explained by longer edge times. Weekly, that arc *rises* (~2,108 → ~2,843). The same error understates peak loops: recollection, batch-wait, repeat-assay, and specialist-hold all roughly double or triple on a weekly basis. Utilization, queue, performance-edge, and mix figures are used more correctly and rescue some conclusions, but the frequency-DFG reasoning the prompt asked for is not reliable.

**P1 is internally inconsistent and operationally wrong.** When M2 degrades, the policy dumps all Routine work onto M1 and *suspends* M1 dedication for Urgent/Complex. That starves the 18-hour contractual class on the remaining scarce machine. The trade-off table then claims the opposite: “Protects Urgent/Complex on M1.” A core scenario policy contradicts its own scorecard.

**Clarity is repeatedly broken.** Bottleneck text is full of collapsed comparisons (`16,8608,530`, `22%54%`, `2.5h11h`, `760780+`). Those look like eaten arrows, but they make the evidence trail hard to audit and would fail an operations reader.

**Several numbers are invented or over-precise.** Recollection cut “~30%,” Complex p90 target `<110h` (tighter than historical normal p90 of 118h), and S1 mix shocks (`Urgent +30%`) are not derived from the log. I4 is also undersold: two tech validators who “remain primarily” in technical validation will not meaningfully clear a 14-hour accession queue staffed by only two specialists, while peak technical validation is already at 101% utilization—the exact I4 conflict the prompt flagged.

**The “robust” M2-for-Routine design is weakly defended.** Repeat-after-failure already clusters after M2 and night shifts. Making M2 the Routine workhorse is acknowledged as a failure mode, then still treated as the standing design rather than a contingent one.

What remains good: four scenarios that actually change mix *and* disruption type; bottleneck migration that names the next station; time-decay priority instead of naïve Urgent-first; I2’s batch-of-24 rejected; I6/I8/I7 used with downstream overload; on/off triggers; a redesign-caused residual (Complex-panel inversion); and a leading-vs-lagging dashboard with bands. Those keep it clearly above a mid/low answer. The DFG period error, the P1 contradiction, the garbled metrics, and the invented quantities are why it is nowhere near flawless.
