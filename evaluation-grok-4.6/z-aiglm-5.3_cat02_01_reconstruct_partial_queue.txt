**2.5**

The submission never leaves the planning scratchpad. The required artifact is a single Markdown document with exactly seven specified top-level headings, a case-level table, a station-level table, and one self-contained Python block that builds the log, calls PM4Py in the prescribed way, and prints results. None of that is produced. The text cuts off while still listing case values (`P08: 50; 30; 55; 45;`). Under a format-constrained process-mining task, that is a failed deliverable, not a near-miss.

What exists is internal reconstruction work. Some of it is competent and would have been usable if it had been finished and published as the report:

- Missing S1/S2 starts are treated as intervals constrained by resource non-overlap, queue entry, and nonnegative service, not as a single silent fill.
- OPEN activities are distinguished from completed service (elapsed vs final).
- Right-censored waits (P16, P18–P20, P22) are not dropped.
- Utilization is not inferred from completed cases alone.
- Little’s law is correctly split into a time-average identity (WIP integral = sojourn overlap) versus a non-stationary departure-rate version that is not defensible here.
- The bottleneck story is time-varying: early external overload, then S2 as the tighter station; the C2 outage is treated as a small capacity loss, not the main cause of delay.
- Several data-quality anomalies are real (I2 idle with P22 waiting; P08/P09 start-order swap around maintenance; P16 skipped at 13:55).

Those insights do not rescue the grade. They are unfinished, internally unstable, and never assembled into the required sections.

**Concrete defects even inside the scratch work**

1. **No required structure.** Assumptions are never labeled A1, A2, …. Formulas are never stated as definitions. Sections 1–7 do not appear. The Python contract (in-memory table, status-aware parsing, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` on a valid projection, `convert_log_to_time_intervals` or an explicit comment why pandas is safer) is entirely absent.

2. **The case table is incomplete.** P05, P07, P11, P15, P16, P17, P21, and P22 must appear explicitly. Only P05 and P07 are written in table form; P11/P15/P16/P17/P21/P22 remain scattered notes. Aggregation of unremarkable cases is allowed; omitting the mandatory rows is not.

3. **Central estimates never settle.** P05 S2 wait is 20 in one place and 25 in another (start 09:45 vs 09:50). P11 S2 service is 25, then 20–25, then 20. S2 busy time is 570, then 560. Those are not rounding envelopes; they are unresolved choices. A finished sensitivity section would have locked three named scenarios and reported one central table. That section does not exist.

4. **Arithmetic is repeatedly wrong, then patched, then wrong again.** S2 WIP integral is 1390, then 1340, then 1335, then 1340 after a missed P10 overlap. Subperiod means are divided by 240 minutes instead of 120, producing an impossible S2 12:00–14:00 mean WIP of 2.69 while the path is at level 4–7. The correction appears, but the “final” numbers are still a mix of pre- and post-correction figures. A judge cannot treat the reconstruction as internally consistent.

5. **Station metrics are not a table.** Arrivals, completions, wait/service summaries, mean/peak WIP, utilization ranges, and coverage are never placed in one S1/S2 table with a stated analysis interval. Several utilization percentages mix wall-clock 720 resource-minutes with availability-adjusted 705 and with “gap-as-artifact” readings without a single declared convention.

6. **Little’s-law check is only argued, not reported.** The identity 2085 / 1340 / 3425 is a good idea, but it is never shown as a compact check with interval, \(L\), \(\lambda\), implied \(W\), and a statement of which assumptions hold. Completer-mean vs arrival-average vs departure-rate \(W\) are discussed in overlapping, sometimes contradictory sentences.

7. **Rounding is asserted, not applied.** ±2.5 min per stamp and ±5 min per duration are mentioned, but almost all reported waits, services, and utilizations are treated as exact reported-scale integers. The required output was bounds or a clearly separated rounding envelope.

8. **Capacity claims overreach the evidence.** S1 “~4.3/h” vs S2 “~3/h” is the service-only reading. The same text notes 5-minute inter-task gaps that would pull S1 toward ~3.4/h and S2 toward ~2.7/h, and also notes S2 services of 20–25 min that overlap S1’s range. The bottleneck ordering is plausible and probably right, but it is not shown to survive the full uncertainty set in a finished Section 6.

9. **FCFS is used as both assumption and finding.** Imputed starts are justified by FCFS, then S2 is shown to violate FCFS (P08/P09, P16). That circularity is never resolved: either FCFS is a working hypothesis with exceptions, or the central imputations need a different rule.

**What would have been needed for a high score**

A closed seven-section document; locked interval bounds for every MISSING start; OPEN rows reporting elapsed service only; a station table with low/central/high columns; three named imputation scenarios with a stability statement; a Little’s-law check restricted to a justified interval; and working Python that does not treat MISSING, OPEN, and blank as the same token.

None of that is present. Credit is given only for the direction of the reconstruction and for not confusing service with sojourn in the notes. That is not enough to pass a strict format-and-consistency bar.
