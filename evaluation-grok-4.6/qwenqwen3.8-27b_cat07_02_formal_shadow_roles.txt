**7.2**

The final write-up is a strong, constraint-aware organizational-mining analysis, but it is not near-flawless. Data use and role logic are mostly excellent; the required code and some interpretive stretches are not.

**What holds up.** The six required headings are present and used as specified. Roles are not assigned by peak activity count: Nora is separated from front office despite huge A1/A2 volume; Erik is kept as a rare critical specialist; Mateo is a broker rather than a second supervisor; Chen and Dina stay together on activity mix but are split on complexity. Counts, similarities, medians, and the four substitution episodes are cited accurately. Giorgio is correctly treated as a hybrid (closer to Amira at 0.84 than to Fatima at 0.71) whose cover of A7/A8 is useful but slower (38→74 minutes). Mateo’s 13/21 rechecked A9 events and the two delayed critical security cases are used as authority limits, not as proof of competence. Section 6’s cautions match the problem constraints.

**Material defects.**

1. **The required join is broken.** `case_meta` is merged back onto `df`, which already has `case_complexity`, `temporary_substitution`, and the other case attributes. Pandas will emit `_x`/`_y` suffixes; later `agg(..., case_complexity=...)` and the resource/role merge (`events`, `cases`, `median_complexity` on both sides) would fail or silently analyze the wrong columns. That is the step the prompt asked for.

2. **PM4Py usage is invented past what “illustrative” covers.** Function *names* match the prompt, but many parameters do not (`event_attribute_keys`, `min_cluster_size` / `max_cluster_size` / `similarity_threshold` / `distance` on role discovery, `metric` / `min_similarity` on similarity, `clustering_algorithm` on `cluster_log`, `attribute_values=` on the filter). `filter_event_attribute_values` and service-time helpers are also called with nonstandard signatures. The code will not run against real PM4Py without substantial rewrite.

3. **Section 4 inflates ambiguity.** R08 (138 A9 vs 22 A8), R09/R10 (~300 A11 vs ~35 A10), and to a lesser extent R05 (96 A6 vs low teens on technical work) are not genuinely two-way assignments. Listing them as peer ambiguities weakens the section and treats residual activity as if it rivaled the core function.

4. **Unsupported numeric assumptions.** `escalation_level >= 2` and `case_complexity >= 0.7` are unexplained. The log field `case_complexity` is never specified as the 0–1 score vs a Low/Medium/High/Critical label; `pd.to_numeric(..., errors="coerce")` would zero out the latter. Activity strings in code (`"A9 Approval"`) are also not aligned with the process list (`A9. Supervisor Approval`), so the recheck/wait extracts are brittle.

5. **Smaller conceptual slips.** Quality analysts and temps are forced into “shadow/bridging” even though they are fairly clean formal roles. R07 is given a named proposed role *and* called a shadow role without a crisp statement of which status process owners should treat as official. Section 2 largely restates the table.

The organizational story a process owner would use is largely right. The score is held down by a non-working complexity/substitution join, non-faithful PM4Py parameters, and an ambiguity section that over-argues weak alternatives.
