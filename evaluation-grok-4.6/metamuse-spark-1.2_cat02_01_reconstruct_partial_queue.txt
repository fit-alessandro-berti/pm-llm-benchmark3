The write-up has the right skeleton and some real process-mining competence (resource-feasible imputation, OPEN vs elapsed service, case-level vs station-level PM4Py semantics, a three-scenario frame). It is not close to a high score. Under a strict reading it is numerically unreliable, internally inconsistent, and it leaves a required check unperformed.

## Format and framing

The seven required headings are present and the assumption list is genuinely useful. That is the high-water mark. Notation is then repeatedly broken: missing relation symbols (`W1 [ ... ]`, `s* max(...)`, `L = W` instead of \(L=\lambda W\)), truncated formulas (`Ub = s1e -`), and leftover debris such as “153% per server?”. A1’s 0.5-WIP convention is stated and then abandoned. Optimistic/pessimistic are defined as short-service / long-service, which **maximises** waits in the “optimistic” column — a naming choice that fights the rest of the paper.

## Case-level reconstruction — several easy totals are wrong

Fully observed waits and services for P01–P04, P06, P08–P10, P12–P14 are mostly correct. The required special cases are not safe.

- **P06 total sojourn is 135 min** (`10:50−08:35`), not 95. 95 is `s2_start − arrival`.
- **P05 total sojourn is 110 min** (`10:15−08:25`), not 170. The “(168–172)” band is invented.
- Completed sojourn is \(J=s2e-a\). For P05, P07, and P11 this is **fully observed**. Claiming that “imputed totals [are] highly sensitive to missing starts” is a conceptual error: missing starts only split \(J\) into wait vs service.
- **P15 S1 wait cannot be 155.** Arrival `10:10`, end `12:15`, previous I1 release `11:40` ⇒ \(W_1\in[90,125)\), \(S_1\in(0,35]\). Section 3 writes 90–155; section 6’s short-service case uses 125. The document disagrees with itself.
- P07 absolute wait is written 40–65; with \(S_1\to 0\) the upper end is **75**.
- Section 6’s “optimistic” P15 line (`W1=125`, `S1=10`, `s1s=12:05`) is arithmetically impossible: `12:05−10:10=115`, and \(W_1+S_1\) must remain 125. Same slip on P07 (`S1=10` ⇒ \(W_1=65\), not 60). Station sojourn is being confused with waiting time.

P11’s 25-minute C2 window vs a ~40-minute typical service is a good catch. That does not offset the sojourn errors.

## Station metrics — busy time and utilisation are not defensible

Known **completed** I1 intervals already sum to **215 min** (P01 20, P03 25, P05 25, P09 30, P11 25, P13 30, P17 30, P19 30) **before** P07, P15, or P21. Central I1 busy time is about **305 min** including those three; I2 is 290; system busy ≈ 595 / 720 ≈ **83%**. The table’s I1 band 172–187 (central 180) is impossible. I1 utilisation 58–65% is incompatible with the same section’s claim of ~5-minute gaps between I1 jobs.

C1 known completed service is already 265 min excluding P05; central C1 including P05 and P17 elapsed is ~300, not 262+5. Combined S2 utilisation ~73–77% is correspondingly low.

Other station-table problems:

- S1 waits, fully observed starts: median **75**, mean **~65** (19 values). The table swaps them (“median 65, mean ~70”).
- S2 observed waits: median **45**, not 35.
- S2 early arrivals via `s1e` are **6–7**, not 4–5; early S2 completions are **3**, not 4. Late S2 arrivals are **6**, not 8–9.
- Early-period S2 capacity is written `120+105` **after stating the C2 outage is not in E**. The 15 minutes were subtracted from the wrong interval. Late capacity `245` is unexplained (the window is 240).
- Peak S1 queue is around **12:10** (about 6), not “6–7 by 13:30” (by then only P22 is waiting). System WIP near `12:00–12:35` is about **11–13**, not 9–10. Mean system WIP “5–6” is then untrustworthy.
- Throughput “~3.5–4/h **per station**” overstates S2 (14 completions / 6 h ≈ 2.3/h).

## Bottleneck story — directionally partly right, outage reading is not

S1 waits trend up, S1 service is stable ~25–30 min, and the system is a **time-varying combination with S1 leading**. That qualitative claim can survive the bad utilisation numbers, but those numbers are what the text uses to say S1 “looks not saturated” at 70–75%. The 5-minute I1 gaps already refute that sentence.

The maintenance interpretation is the serious process error. P08 entered the S2 queue at **10:20**. C2 freed at **10:50**. The idle `10:50–11:15` is therefore **not** “S1 starvation.” A job was waiting. The outage (or a decision not to start a 45-minute job into a known outage) is the natural explanation of P08’s extra ~20–25 min. Calling the null effect “evidence-consistent” contradicts the event log the author already used to flag P08’s wait.

P09 starting on C1 at 11:00 while earlier P08 is still queued is an FCFS-at-station anomaly. A8 promised such cases would be noted; they were not.

## Uncertainty, Little’s Law, censoring

The three-scenario table is the right idea and P11’s infeasibility of a 40-minute service is well used. Execution is not: optimistic waits are miscomputed, section 3 vs 6 bounds disagree, and utilisation sensitivity (±5–8 points around a **wrong** centre) cannot be trusted.

Little’s Law is a required deliverable. A9 restricts it to stable, lightly censored intervals; section 2 defines \(L\) vs \(\lambda W\); section 6 cites a “mismatch over the whole window (see below).” **No numerical check appears.** Early S2 is the obvious defensible interval and is never tested.

Rounding treatment (±5 on a difference, not silent de-rounding) is one of the better parts. The “±7 min” sojourn remark is inconsistent with A2.

Structurally competent, numerically unsafe. That is a mid-fail against a near-flawless bar, not a high pass.
