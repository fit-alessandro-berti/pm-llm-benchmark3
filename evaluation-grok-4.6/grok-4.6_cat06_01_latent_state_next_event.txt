The answer is process-aware and far above a DFG-copy baseline, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has several real inaccuracies, a recurring probability-semantics slip, and some weak third-rank choices.

**Control-flow overclaim (C6-A).** Rule 5 requires two or more related *complaints* on the same lot or component. C6-A is one complaint spanning two devices on one lot, plus three “similar” prior complaints whose lot identity is not given. Treating Supplier Analysis as *mandatory* is stronger than the rule supports. The unknowns correctly hedge “confirmed common-cause versus coincidental multi-device capture,” which contradicts the mandatory wording in the state and supporting-evidence lists.

**Next-event probability is repeatedly mixed with time-to-event.** The target is the next logged activity, not whether it lands this afternoon. Queue delay and a 2–14 h RA service time do not, by themselves, widen P(event is next) unless a competitor can finish first. That confusion is explicit in C6-A (review queue “push[ing] completion past the remaining afternoon” as contradicting evidence), C6-D (interval kept off ~0.8 because RA is “often later today rather than immediately”), and C6-B (queue position making a “same-day close less likely”). C6-D’s width is actually justified by the competing migration flush; the written reason is not.

**C6-C rank 3 is a two-step skip.** Close — No Safety Issue is not a documented successor of Technical Investigation Completed. The table goes TI → Disposition (119) and Disposition → Close (132). Ranking an unenabled terminal at 0.05–0.22 as the next log line needed a tighter defense or a better third candidate (another evidence cycle after “plausible but not confirmed,” or a delayed RA already in the 6 h feed window).

**C6-E ranking and `process_enabled`.** Supplier Analysis is the distinctive mandatory obligation (lot now on six complaints; second pack already has a supplier certificate; first review already occurred, possibly yesterday). Duration 6–36 h is a fair reason it may not finish *soon*, but it is a better process-semantic next-completion candidate than Technical Investigation, which Low / no injury does not require. Marking TI `process_enabled: true` treats a suspected parallel task as currently enabled; that flag is used more carefully on C6-A/C/D.

**Smaller hygiene and calibration nits.** C6-B lists “No Technical Investigation…” inside `open_or_suspected_obligations` and folds Fast Close into an S5 alternative; Fast Close is an S1 gateway, which the predictions themselves argue. C6-D’s Evidence Received band (0.12–0.36) sits above the stated 8–15% delay rate; the migration flag plus source attachment justify some lift, not a 0.36 ceiling. C6-B’s Close-as-next high end (0.40) implies a frequent review skip/collapse that the control-flow story does not really allow.

**What is actually strong.** A vs B is not prefix-cloned; object/severity/lot rules, live queues, completion-only logging, and rule 7 on C6-D are used as first-class evidence; `process_enabled` is often doing the right job (gated evidence re-requests, late Evidence Received as an observation not an action); DFG counts are treated as unconditioned and non-normative; intervals are wide on purpose; the cross-case note on DFG failure modes is accurate.

Those strengths keep the score out of the middle band. The SA overclaim, the next-event vs elapsed-time mix-up, and the two weakest rank-3/enabled choices are enough that this is not a high-8 or 9 under the stated bar.
