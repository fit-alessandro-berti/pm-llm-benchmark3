**3.5**

The write-up is well organized and gets the high-level story right (A puts the check inside the loop; B lifts it outside; P1 and P3 distinguish the models; P2 can collapse them). That is not enough. On a formal-semantics task the core operators are misread, most witnesses are invalid, the complexity numbers are invented, and the proposed simplification is inconsistent with its own claims.

### Fatal semantic errors

**Loop redo cannot be last.** Binary `*(do, redo)` is: do once; then exit, or redo **then do again**. After `Rework Case` another analysis (and, in A, another check) is mandatory. T1 (`… Rework Case, Close Case`) and T3 (two analyses with **no** rework) are illegal in **both** models. Presenting them as A-only traces shows the loop rule was not applied.

**A is concurrent, not sequential.** The do-part is `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')`, then `X(Approve, tau)`. Check is **not** required after analysis; the two may interleave, and both must finish **before** approve or the implicit loop-exit. T2 (`Open, Check, Analyze, Approve, Close`) is therefore legal in **A and B**. The structural table’s “No concurrency” for A is false. P1 is also overstated as “immediately followed by,” which neither the prompt nor A requires.

**B’s check occurs exactly once.** `+('Check Revision', *(…))` is a single AND-branch, not a repeated check. Max checks in B is 1, not “unbounded.” Min is 1, not “one per revision if you want.” This error poisons the table, T3’s reason, and the B-side language.

**`tau` is skip-approve, not loop exit.** Exit is the silent choice of the loop operator after the do-part completes. The tau/approval discussion is therefore wrong and cannot support the verdicts.

### Equivalence notions and verdicts

P3 is defined with **exactly** 12 events and **exactly** 3 analyses; the prompt is **at most**. P2 is defined as “contain 1 Analyze and 1 Check”; the prompt is **at least** one of each. Duplicate-label treatment is only half-specified.

The verdict table is the strongest part: P1 not equivalent, P2 equivalent only under the dashboard abstraction, P3 not equivalent. Those conclusions are directionally right **despite** the broken traces. Confidence on P2 should be higher once the abstraction is written as the tuple (n_analyze, check_occurred, rework_occurred) with Open/Close fixed: both trees induce the same such tuples (`n≥1`, rework iff `n≥2`, check always true).

### Simplified model (section 8)

`*(Analyze, X(Approve, tau))` puts Approve in the **redo** slot. Approve then cannot follow the last analysis, and two analyses can occur with a silent redo and **no** Rework — a language neither original admits.

`X(Check, tau)` makes check optional, contradicting P2 and the claimed negative trace “missing check.” Rework sits **after** the whole analyze-loop, so `Analyze, Rework, Analyze` is rejected.

Of the three “regression” traces, only (1) is in the new language; (2) and (3) are not. Negative trace (2) is **accepted**. There is no structural comparison to A and B (node/depth/leaf counts), so “simpler” is unearned. The model preserves none of P1 and does not implement P2.

### Format and recommendation

An extra H1 and `##` headings violate “exactly the following top-level headings.” Section 9 is reasonable in preferring A for P1 and warning that a bound is not a proof; recommending the broken simplification as a replacement for B is not.

**Net:** competent survey prose around a process-tree reading that is wrong on loop, AND, tau, and check cardinality. For this task that is a fail on the stated quality bar, not a near-miss.
