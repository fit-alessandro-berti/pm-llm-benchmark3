**7.4**

This is a strong, process-aware causal write-up that meets the required structure and mostly keeps association, identification, and intervention separate. It is not close to flawless under a strict audit.

**What is actually good.** The verdict `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` is the disciplined one. The answer correctly treats Orion as a routing destination, a proxy for the assignment rule, and an operational bundle; distinguishes total versus controlled-direct versus mediated effects; and refuses to treat the crude-versus-stratified reversal as identification. Completed-case selection, positivity failure (especially Orion-day and Nova-high), interference under a 50% reallocation, and the wait-versus-service distinction are all handled with the right semantics. Sections 3, 5, and 6 are the best parts: roles are tied to estimands, every hypothesis has a predicted observation, and the proposed plan learns without moving half the book on a Simpson artifact.

**Material accuracy problem.** The required day/night decomposition is wrong. Night cycle-time sum is **369.6**, not 373.6; pooled night mean is **21.74 h**, not 21.98 h; Orion night mean is **23.05 h**, not 23.35 h. The stated night–day gap of +8.00 h should be about +7.76 h. The error is internally consistent (the pooled figure is just the bad Orion-night mean plus Nova night), which makes it look computed rather than typed, and it sits in a table the prompt asked to make auditable. Most other arithmetic (standardization, compliance, wait/service, rework) checks out.

**Presentation and causal hygiene nits.** Difference signs are sloppy: crude Orion–Nova is `+6.72 h`, but the Low row says `OrionNova = 2.14 h`, and several later “differences” omit direction even when the sign flips. That is not a formatting quibble in a committee table. `rework -> queue_wait` in the DAG is temporally misaligned with the supplied `wait_before_review_h` definition (pre-review / compliance wait), even though the prose later mentions Request-Info loops. Ranking a beneficial Orion-pool effect first sits in mild tension with an 88% non-identifiability verdict; the stratified pattern is evidence for a *hypothesis*, not a reason to put it on top after spending the rest of the memo explaining why those cells are not exchangeable. Confidence 88 is also more precise than the design can support.

**Code.** Section 7 is directionally right: embedded CSV, crude and stratified means, an explicit completed-case complexity standard, cell-level overlap, variant table, PM4Py DFGs without fake timestamps, and sparse-cell warnings. It is diagnostic rather than a causality theater. It would not have caught the night-mean error, and a few warnings are noisy (almost every variant/edge has n<3 in a 30-row log).

**Net.** Causal discipline and actionability are well above typical. Strictly graded, a wrong required contrast plus inconsistent contrast signs keep this out of the 8s. Fix the night arithmetic, impose one signed contrast convention, and this would be in the high 8s.
