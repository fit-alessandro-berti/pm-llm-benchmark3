**Substantive flaws.**

1. **Learning evidence is confounded.** The resource table’s “later events” service times (juniors 19.6→13.7, seniors 15.9→11.8) mix mid-shift stabilization with the late high-WIP speed-up the author elsewhere treats as pressure. Using those gaps as support for warm-up is circular. The cleaner window is positions 16–50 (queue ~31–33, errors at a minimum). The author knows the right test (within-resource, complexity- and WIP-adjusted residuals) but still leans on the uncontrolled contrast in both §1 and §2.

2. **QC timing is underplayed in the main narrative.** The log itself says Quality Check is deferred into the last 45 minutes. If `error_flag` is applied at discovery, late bins are mechanically inflated. Seven-day rework (8.2%→13.7%) still suggests real quality loss, so the speed–quality story survives, but §2 presents the late error jump as if measurement timing were not a competing mechanism. That belongs in the integrated explanation, not only in §5.

3. **Intervention 1 is mis-aimed.** Mean time in the 76+ bin is 459 minutes—still inside a standard 8-hour shift. A shift-length cap that does not cut below ~8 hours does not remove bins 51–75 or 76+. The write-up never says this; it talks about “extreme time-on-task” as if 76+ were overtime.

4. **The WIP×complexity table is treated as cleaner than the prompt allows.** The source table is labeled “controlling only coarsely.” Residual TOD, activity mix, experience, and assignment remain. The 9.4 min / 5.8% cell is strong directional evidence, not a well-identified tradeoff parameter.

5. **A cheap natural experiment is left on the table.** Early last-two-hour errors are 4.2% with end-queue 42; Day last-two-hour errors are 7.5% with end-queue 75. Same late-in-shift window, very different queues. That contrast does more work against pure fatigue/circadian stories than most of the proposed regressions. It is only gestured at.

6. **Overlapping shifts and the meaning of WIP are ignored.** Early/Day and Day/Late overlap. If `wip_at_start` is system-wide, “late Day” is partly “Late shift arriving.” Queue ownership, handoffs, and double-counting of pressure are first-order for this roster and appear only as dashboard items.

Those are not polish nits; they are identification mistakes in an answer that is otherwise about identification.
