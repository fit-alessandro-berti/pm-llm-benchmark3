**6.5**

The answer is structurally complete and clearly above a weak baseline: it rejects I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, treats effects as uncertain rather than as \(p\), and puts spillover and fairness on the page. Under a strict reading it is still not a high-quality operational policy. The rule is softer than it claims, the live allocation is internally inconsistent, and several numbers are not as auditable as advertised.

## Decision rule: not actually deployable

The rule is a well-phrased essay, not a procedure an on-call manager could execute without the author.

- Eligibility condition 5 says benefit should exceed cost “under at least the base scenario—and **preferably**” under a conservative effect. “Preferably” is not a gate.
- \(P_i\) is defined as the case’s share of spillover and then never computed. Because spillover depends on the selected set, this term is circular unless a default portfolio size and a default euro-per-spillover-breach (or a hard breach-cap) is specified. The text correctly refuses to invent untreated-case values, then leaves the ranking rule incomplete.
- The written formula weights false-alarm burden by the chance the swarm is unnecessary. The screening table subtracts the **full** listed burden. That is a different rule. Calling the table “conservative” does not reconcile it with the formula an operator is told to use.
- Calibration adjustments are described in ranges and then unused. SME is not actually lifted by 0.04–0.09, Strategic is not actually cut by 0.05–0.12, and those adjusted probabilities never enter remaining-risk, false-alarm weight, or a “material preventable risk” check. Segment uncertainty is narrated, not applied.
- Tiers overlap on purpose (“Tier A/B”, “B/C”). A deployable policy can have overrides; it cannot have ambiguous membership as the primary label.
- “Material new event” is only operationalized later, and the 0.10 re-entry jump is arbitrary relative to the rest of the rule.

The conceptual stance—stage gate first, do not treat \(p\) as a causal effect, keep spillover visible—is sound. The claim that this is a **deployable** rule is not.

## Allocation: several live decisions do not survive scrutiny

The I-03 / I-05 / I-07 calls are the right *kind* of calls. The portfolio around them is not tight.

**I-07 is the largest operational error.** It is still in **Triage** with **1.5 hours** to SLA. The swarm takes 45 minutes and only acts before `Diagnosis Complete`. After that the process still has Diagnosis Complete → Resolution Work → Customer Validation. Even an immediate, successful swarm leaves on the order of 45 minutes for everything after diagnosis. The supplied effect range does not license ignoring remaining-stage time; the answer’s own eligibility text requires that specialists can start “soon enough to **affect the deadline**.” Flagging timing as “main uncertainty” and still labeling I-07 **Tier A / Intervene** is not robust. This case is a candidate for rapid human review or a different escalation, not a committed scarce swarm.

**I-09 is treated more harshly than the fairness section allows.** It is already in Diagnosis Started, has 2.5 hours left, effect 0.18–0.30, no prior swarm, and midpoint direct net about €3,550. The answer waits because NNLS evidence is sparse—then later warns that “blanket discounting would itself create inequity” and that a human check should precede withholding. The live policy is closer to withholding than to that safeguard. Choosing I-07 (€800 midpoint net, worse stage/time) and a 10-hour I-04 hold over I-09 is neither clearly efficient nor consistent with the stated NNLS rule.

**The reserve is doing too many jobs at once.** The writeup “selects” I-01, I-02, I-04, and I-07; activates three; holds I-04 until 15:00; and lets a regulatory arrival, a validated I-06, or I-09 take that same slot. That is not “one slot reserved.” It is a pre-allocated fourth intervention with optional preemption. Management’s preference was a reserve for an unlisted regulatory incident, not a disguised hold for the highest-euro Strategic case.

**I-04 is over-credited as Tier A.** Ten hours left, still in Triage, Strategic overprediction of 0.05–0.12 on \(p=0.48\), and €2,000 burden. Lower-bound economics are still large, so waiting is reasonable; calling it an “approved slot” and using it as the default fourth treatment overweights a distant, miscalibrated, high-burden case.

**Four named recipients is aggressive relative to the answer’s own spillover math.** Base net breach reduction after 0.24–0.72 spillover is only about 0.15–0.63; Adverse can raise total breaches. A coherent robust rule would often stop at two live swarms plus a true reserve, or make the fourth slot conditional on a congestion check *before* naming I-04 as selected. A pause-if-delay-exceeds-20-minutes clause is added after the fact; it is not built into selection.

**Specialist capacity is almost ignored.** Two seniors run a 45-minute swarm. Three immediate swarms plus a possible fourth is several specialist-hours pulled off the same diagnosis queue that generates the 0.01–0.03 spillover. That displacement is the mechanism of the externality, not a footnote for the monitoring section.

**I-02 is defensible but under-robustified.** Diagnosis Started + 3 hours is the right stage, but Strategic overprediction plus a lower-bound effect of 0.08 is exactly the profile that should sit in a conservative / alternate tier, not “Tier A/B / Intervene.”

I-01 is the cleanest yes. I-03, I-05, I-08, and I-10 are handled well. I-06 as wait-for-a-fast-data-check is right; the check is not time-boxed against a 4-hour Diagnosis Started clock.

## Expected-impact table: partly unauditable, and it prices the wrong portfolio

The component sums for lower/mid effects (0.56 / 0.865) and the corresponding lower/mid gross euros (€10,620 / €18,835) are correct. That is the strongest part of Section 3. The rest is looser than a strict grader should accept.

- The table assumes **all four** of I-01, I-02, I-04, I-07 are treated. Section 2 implements **three now** and a contested reserve. Optimistic/Base/Adverse should have been shown for the actual policy (3-slot and 3+1), not for a 4-swarm world the allocation does not commit to.
- Optimistic “~0.9–1.2” breaches is a padded band around a 1.02 illustration.
- Optimistic net “€15k–€24k after cost/burden” does not follow from €20k–€26k gross minus €1,800 minus anything near the listed €2,900 burden.
- Base gross “€16k–€21k” is unexplained when the midpoint sum is €18,835 and the lower-bound sum is €10,620.
- Adverse “€0.3k–€7.4k after €1,800 and €2.9k burden” does not reconcile. Losing I-04 at lower bounds is about €4,620 − €1,800 − €2,900 ≈ −€80, not a clean €0.3k–€7.4k interval.
- Adverse spillover is “at least ~0.72” (the **base** upper end) plus vague “plausibly higher.” The 15–30 minute congestion path is never mapped into an incremental breach probability.
- Confidence is Low / Moderate-low / Low while the policy still pre-assigns four names. If the author believes the scenarios, slot count should shrink or become explicitly sequential.

Keeping spillover in breach-equivalents is honest. Mixing “probably positive in euros” with “net breaches may be small or negative” is the right qualitative caution; it is not a finished portfolio decision.

## Repeated-alert policy: the one clearly strong section

Section 4 is the best part of the memo. Episode-level alerts, post-review suppression, one swarm unless a documented new action space appears, and a material-change list are operationally realistic.

I-05 vs I-07 is drawn correctly: unchanged 30-minute refresh vs a new customer log that can re-enter even when \(p\) falls. That meets the stated quality bar.

Residual nits: the 2-hour cooldown and +0.10 / two-refresh rule are stipulated, not justified against the 30-minute scoring cycle or SME underprediction (I-05’s true risk may already be in the 0.61–0.66 band). Smaller, but this section is not where the answer fails.

## Fairness and monitoring: concrete, then unused

Section 5 names the right failure modes and proposes specific controls (two-part score, segment rate dashboards, NNLS human check, treatment logging, no naive retraining, spillover-exposure audit, net segment impact). Section 6 is similarly complete: congestion pause, hierarchical calibration, equipoise-band randomization with mandatory-treatment carve-outs, shadow evaluation on untreated outcomes, versioned rule changes.

Two problems keep this from scoring high:

1. **The live allocation does not use the safeguards it recommends.** Value still dominates (I-04 reserved by default). NNLS is parked. No capacity floor is applied to SME/I-10. Calibration offsets are not in the score. This reads as a checklist bolted onto a euro ranking.
2. **Causal and fairness plans are slightly too textbook** relative to four slots a day on critical incidents. Equipoise randomization is the right identification idea; the memo does not face how often a true equipoise band will exist once I-03-style ineligibles and I-04-style euro outliers are removed.

## What a stricter, coherent memo would have done

Keep I-03 out; suppress I-05; re-open I-07 only for **review**, not an automatic swarm; treat I-01 as the only high-confidence dispatch; put I-02 and I-09 behind a short evidence/language check; keep one **unallocated** slot until 15:00; apply Strategic down-shifts and SME up-shifts in the numbers; show conservative/base/optimistic NB for every row; compute 2-slot and 3-slot impact tables that match the rule; and refuse a fourth swarm unless congestion and remaining-stage time clear a pre-stated guardrail.

As written, this is a strong conceptual brief with a messy policy and a table that prices a portfolio the decision rule does not actually run. That is well short of near-flawless.
