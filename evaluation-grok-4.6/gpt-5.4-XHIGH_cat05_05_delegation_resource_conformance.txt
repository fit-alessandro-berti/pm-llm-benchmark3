**8.0**

The analysis is unusually strong on event-time authority, delegation scope, alias normalization, and the suspicion-versus-proof line. Most case calls are right, and the write-up consistently separates technical SoD from control-objective strength. It is not near-flawless. A required deliverable does not actually run, PM4Py usage is semantically sloppy, and a few judgments are sharper than the evidence.

### What is solid
Event-time reasoning is correct where it matters. Eva is not treated as QM because the current master says Quality Engineering. Leave does not strip Asha of QM authority. A later QMS row does not back-date D-QM-01; the 2026-08-09 PDF does. `u_bk` / `b.koch` are merged before RR1. `svc_qms` is never a human. D-IC-04 is void because D-IC-03 bars subdelegation. D-QM-01 is limited to production-equipment approval, so it cannot rescue cooling-system retrospective review. RR4 business-day counting is internally consistent (Tue 11 Aug → Thu 13 Aug EOD), so CHG-505’s Friday review is late.

Classifications that hold up under a strict reading: CHG-501 `VALID_SUBSTITUTION`; CHG-502 / CHG-508 `UNAUTHORIZED` after alias merge; CHG-507 `UNAUTHORIZED`; CHG-510 `UNAUTHORIZED` on both the void subdelegation and RR1; CHG-503 / CHG-506 `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM`. Section 4 gives a benign and an adverse story for each indicator and does not treat Clara–Ben concentration as proof. Section 6 hits every requested control theme. Section 7’s split among genuine authority failure, identity/data weakness, and pattern risk is the right systemic call.

### Material defects
**The Section 5 script is not executable as written.** The authority loop does `approvalish.itertuples()` and then `row._asdict()["case:concept:name"]`, `["concept:name"]`, and `["time:timestamp"]`. Those names are not valid Python identifiers; pandas renames them (`_0`, `_1`, …). `approval_requirement()` will `KeyError` before any interval logic runs. The task asked for one executable block. Conceptual completeness does not excuse a crash in the core check.

**PM4Py four-eyes is used as decoration.** `keep_violations=True` is not a standard `filter_four_eyes_principle` argument; the `inspect` shim will silently drop it. The default filter keeps *conforming* cases. If `resource_key` is also dropped, CHG-508 (`b.koch` vs `u_bk`) looks four-eyes-clean. Manual pandas flags are the real SoD engine — and they are correct — but the required PM4Py path is not a reliable checker and that is never stated plainly.

### Judgment and presentation nits
CHG-504 as `AUTHORIZED` is defensible only with the stated medium confidence. Security cannot exclude credential sharing; `INDETERMINATE` (or `AUTHORIZED` + `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM`) would have matched the brief’s “no single canonical answer” rule more cleanly. Severity “Medium” on an authorized case mixes residual identity risk with a conformance label.

CHG-509 as `UNAUTHORIZED` / High is a valid strict reading of the absence condition, but D-QM-02 exists *only* for that Saturday and that scope. High severity for an undocumented weekend-unavailability gap overstates the same facts the alternative-explanation cell already treats as potentially curative. A secondary `INDETERMINATE` would have been more honest.

CHG-505 is one row with two different theories: Farid’s IC approval may be a valid CI-44 substitution; Ben’s review is independently late, out of scope, and on sick leave. One primary label is allowed; not using a secondary label makes the case look cleaner than the analysis.

The CHG-501 “event-time authority” cell talks about Eva’s verifier role instead of Ben’s approval authority. Small, but it is the column that was supposed to carry the delegation claim.

### Why not higher
A hypercritical grade cannot sit in the 9s when the mandated code block would not run and the mandated PM4Py checks would, on a default API, report the opposite of the paper findings. The legal analysis is close to what this fact pattern deserves. The tooling and a few over-sharp labels are what keep it at 8.0 rather than 9+.
