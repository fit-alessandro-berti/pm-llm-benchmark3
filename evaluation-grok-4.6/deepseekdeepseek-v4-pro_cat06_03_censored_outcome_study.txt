**6.2**

The write-up is conceptually in the right family: it refuses to recode rejects/withdrawals/opens as non-default, conditions the primary estimand on activation, keeps an as-of prefix, and treats immature activated accounts as censored rather than complete cases. Section 8 is the strongest part. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading. The design contains several implementation-breaking contradictions.

**Target.** Time-to-default after activation with right censoring is a defensible primary target, and the multi-state alternative is correctly described as a different question. Two problems remain. First, the estimand is written as a hazard “among cases that *would* be activated,” which is counterfactual language for a selected-sample quantity. Second, Section 1 claims this “isolates borrower credit risk from underwriting policy.” That is false; Section 8 later says the opposite. Prediction at event 3 / day 7 for an outcome whose clock starts at a still-future `Account Activated` also leaves an operational hole: at scoring time activation is unknown, so the model is not actually a model for “every application.”

**Cohort.** The 48,000-row arithmetic works, and keeping the 3,600 immature activations in a 25,200-case survival cohort is the right instinct. The inclusion/exclusion rules do not match that table. The text requires a start date that allows a full 90+35-day maturity window, then includes immature cases anyway. The 15 November 2025 cutoff is the wrong date for a 90+35-day rule (that is about 13 October 2025 if the clock starts at application start; if the clock starts at activation, a start-date cutoff is the wrong instrument). The stated 3-event / 7-day prefix filter is never applied to the published counts. Linked transfers are treated as if linkage implied an observed 90-day outcome.

**Censoring.** The taxonomy (administrative horizon, delayed feed, activation selection, transfer, permanent partner missingness) is good, as is the testable / partially testable / untestable split. The mechanics are not. Surviving a fully observed 90-day window is an observed non-event for a 90-day default estimand, not “right-censored at 90d.” More seriously, delayed labels are censored with `min(extract − activation, 90)`. That ignores `label_available_at` and the stated 7–35-day feed lag: a case 100 days after activation can still have no label. Comparing covariate balance of early vs late activations is only a weak check of informative censoring.

**Prefixes and leakage.** Hybrid prefixes, migration aliasing, and a hard timestamp filter are sound. Then the design reintroduces leakage: “the time gap [prediction → activation] is captured as a feature.” That gap is unknown at the declared prediction time. The audit table covers the required fields and is mostly right, but comparison operators are missing in the `documents_total` / `risk_score` rows, and treating `Account Activated` / `Contract Signed` as always illegal is wrong. Those activities are leaky only when they occur after `prefix_end`.

**Validation.** A random split is correctly rejected. The actual split is invalid. Train is 1 Jan 2024–31 Mar 2025 and “Test 1” is 1 Jul 2024–30 Sep 2024, i.e. inside the training window. In the skeleton the `test1` branch is unreachable because `start_date <= TRAIN_END` fires first. A pre/post-1 Jul 2025 policy flag (and the migration flag) is constant on the training window, so it cannot be estimated. Test 2 is a reasonable regime-shift probe; the overlapping Test 1 is not.

**Code.** It hits the requested PM4Py surface (`format_dataframe`, `get_prefixes_from_log`, train-only DFG/variants/temporal features, temporal split as primary, `split_train_test` only as an anti-pattern, no invented survival API) and the leakage assert is the right kind of check. It is also well over the 80–160 line budget, imports `timestamp_filter` unused, no-op-renames the case id, never uses `label_available_at` for maturity, never implements the documents/experience repairs, and assigns `activation_time` with `np.where` on a differently indexed groupby Series. After already cutting events at the hybrid cutoff, `get_prefixes_from_log(..., length=3)` drops the elapsed-time prefixes the text promised to keep.

Net: honest target semantics and a usable outline, undermined by a non-identified validation split, delayed-label mishandling, a self-inflicted leak, and a skeleton that would not run as specified.
