The audit is directionally strong: it catches the obvious full-trace leaks, several subtler lineage bugs, keeps a predictive-vs-causal boundary, and mostly follows the five-section shape. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several errors in the revised explanations and a handful of mis-severed findings are enough to keep it well below the top band.

### What is actually good
The core leakage map is mostly right. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, and the nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot` are correctly treated as unavailable at `t0`. The SQL defect on `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (`<= extraction_date` rather than `< t0`) is a real lineage catch. `final_handler_team` is recognized as a closure-time warehouse field with no historical timestamp, not as a trustworthy `t0` team. Case Z’s same-timestamp `Claim Closed` / `Damage Assessment Completed` pair, missing sequence index, mutable `risk_score_current`, and complete-case `last_resource_role` are handled as uncertainty rather than as a confident local story.

The required distinction between the two sequence patterns is present in type, if not in severity: `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is labeled process-impossible; `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is labeled temporal leakage of a later but process-legal continuation. Section 3 does not declare every correlated feature causal, and `claimant_region` / `calendar_week` are kept as proxies. The JSON schema for section 4 is followed.

### Material errors (these drive the score down)

**Inverted slack story in Case X.** This is the worst flaw in the required deliverable. Local SHAP has `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0 = 1.2 h` at **−0.03**. A claim 1.2 hours after registration has *more* slack, which is why risk falls. The revised driver and the narrative both say the opposite: “already 1.2 hours old … leaving less slack.” That copies the *global* slogan for old claims onto a young prefix and contradicts the stated direction (`decrease`). The revised explanation is therefore not credible on a prefix-derived signal the rubric explicitly wants preserved.

**Residual-risk overclaim after removing leaked drivers.** Case X’s 0.82 is dominated by `appeal_seen_anywhere` (+0.26) and `final_decision = Partial` (+0.06). After those are stripped, asserting that “the prediction remains elevated” is unsupported. `recompute` is the right status; the narrative should not pre-judge the recomputed probability.

**Case Y treats a known-broken feature as a credible driver.** The lineage note says the prior-claim SQL is wrong. The JSON still lists `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` as a driver and the narrative asserts “all before t0.” That is an assumption the evidence contradicts. Until the query is repaired, this belongs in `removed_or_repaired_drivers` / unusable-until-repaired, not in the surviving story. `initial_reserve` is also flattened to “neutral” despite a local +0.08; if it is available at registration it is a defensible severity proxy, not a null.

**`claimant_channel = Broker` disappears.** It is a local-only feature the table is required to cover, it is almost certainly known at `t0`, and it is a channel proxy. Section 1 lumps it into a leftover “OK” row; section 4 neither keeps it nor lists it as removed.

### Severity and taxonomy problems
`queue_length_at_t0` is a timestamped congestion snapshot the rubric says to preserve. Calling it “temporal leakage (possible)” at **medium** over-flags a legitimate process-state signal. The right finding is “valid if snapshot time ≤ t0; verify the ±5 minute join,” severity low.

`final_handler_team` is under-flagged. A field populated at closure, used to support “the final team determines delay,” is not medium mutable lineage. The 65% coincidence with the `t0` team does not make the warehouse value admissible. This is high/critical leakage plus an unsupported causal story.

`Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is too lenient at medium. At `t0` immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`, evidence cycles are generally still future. Same for treating `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as usable “if Damage Completed ≤ t0”: settlement itself is still later in the normative order, so the pattern is not prefix-legal at this `t0` except in degenerate timestamp collisions.

Section 1 finding types are sloppy: `OK`, `Proxy`, and `Possible / Medium` are not leakage/impossibility/mutability labels. Proxies belong in section 3; stuffing `calendar_week` into the leakage table blurs the taxonomy the prompt asked to keep distinct.

### Section 5 gaps and format
The prompt requires a plan covering **calibration after feature removal** and **explanation stability**. Those two items are not actually specified. Language lint and fairness monitoring are not substitutes for “refit/calibrate once leaked features are dropped” or “check that prefix explanations do not flip under small, admissible perturbations.” Extra “Rationale” / “Credibility note” material after section 5 also violates “exactly the following five sections.”

### Net
This is a competent process-aware audit with the right instincts on leakage vs proxy vs congestion, and it does not make the weak-answer mistakes (trusting full-trace SHAP as an early explanation, or calling every correlate causal). It also ships a locally wrong elapsed-time story, overclaims post-removal risk, rehabilitates a feature whose SQL is known to leak, mis-severes both a legitimate congestion feature and a closure-time team field, and misses two explicitly required validation workstreams. That is clearly above a failing audit and clearly not near-flawless.
