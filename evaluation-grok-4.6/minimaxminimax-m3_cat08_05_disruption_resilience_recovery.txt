**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and conceptually aligned with the brief, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it contains material attribution errors, one core internal contradiction, and several numerical/logical loosenesses that should have been caught.

**What it gets right.** The seven required sections are present and usable. The answer correctly refuses to treat every T2 deviation as failure, correctly refuses to equate throughput with resilience, and correctly treats K/L as a hidden post-recovery workload rather than real-time compliance. Section 3 is the strongest part: B and a constrained A/F/I/J are resilience; C and G are shortcuts; K and L are concealed noncompliance; E/H/I are appetite-dependent. The regulator-reconstruction test is the right discriminating question. Metrics in T0/T2/T4 are mostly transcribed accurately, and keep/modify/retire judgments are generally defensible.

**Material errors.**

1. **T3/T4 process mix-up.** Partial recovery has no DFG. “Reconcile manual reservation (1,400)” and “Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200)” are stabilization (T4) frequencies. Placing them in T3, then reusing 1,400 again in T4, is a temporal-reasoning failure on a task that explicitly scores temporal reasoning.

2. **A is conflated with C.** The workaround DFG is three parallel exits from Emergency triage: Manual authorization 2,500 / Skip 1,600 / Fast lane 1,000. The 1,600 skip cases are not evidence that the fast lane “sometimes” bypassed authorization. The fast lane *itself* goes 1,000 → 920 inventory snapshot and never enters an authorization node. The conclusion (A can skip control) is roughly right; the cited evidence is wrong. That is a direct miss on abductive interpretation.

3. **Authorization policy contradicts itself.** Sections 2 and 6 retire C and require a time-boxed manual check for *every* order, including ambient. Section 4.3 then legalizes “sample-based (10%) authorization” for Ambient × Pharmacy-standard/Wholesale-bulk. That is not a nuance; it reintroduces the exact gap the rest of the memo forbids. For a regulated wholesaler, sampling authorization is also the wrong control type.

**Smaller but real defects.**

- T4 exceptions are called “2–4× baseline.” Traceability/cold-chain/dual-control are ~2.3–2.8×; rework is 1.66× and cancellation 1.43×. The band is inflated.
- Hospital p90 21h vs 15h is a 40% degradation; calling it “only” 21h understates the cost of A.
- P1 SLA of 4h is stricter than the *baseline* hospital median (8h). A recovery model that is tighter than peacetime is not operationally credible.
- “Shortcuts did not buy much extra throughput because Pharmacy fill fell to 61%” mixes causes. The fill collapse is more I/capacity rationing than C/G; C/G are still unjustified, but the causal claim is sloppy.
- POWL notation is announced and then the operators fail to render; the promised explanation is incomplete.
- Several segment claims (e.g., G “disproportionately Pharmacy-standard”) are asserted without DFG support.
- Fast-lane drop-off (1,000 → 920) and other volume leakage are ignored.

**Recovery redesign.** Triggers, a customer×product matrix, evidence gating of invoice close, and independent time-boxed remediation are the right bones. The model is undermined by the sample-authorization clause, an unrealistic P1 clock, and the garbled notation. It is a solid sketch, not a consistent target process.

**Bottom line.** Strong consultant-shaped memo with the right theory of the case, damaged by wrong timestamps, wrong evidence for A, and a self-contradiction on the central control. That combination bars anything in the 8–10 band.
