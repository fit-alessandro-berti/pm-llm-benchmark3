### Material reasoning flaws
**SP1’s 85% rule is treated as service-neutral.** Choosing the lowest-CO2 mode whenever \(P(\text{on-time})\ge 0.85\) can raise lateness toward 15% from current 5–7% on stock segments. SP1 is a *permission to use ground/truck when the existing promise is still likely to be met*, not a new service target. The table’s “late-delivery ↔ (guardrail \(P\ge 0.85\))” therefore overstates protection, and it sits in tension with Section 1’s own rule not to worsen Service-contract lateness.

**The effects table is internally inconsistent on “resilience risk.”** Late-delivery and rework columns treat ↑ as worse. The resilience column does not. Rows describing *improvements* (less turbulence, less remake reliance, clearer escalation, diversified carriers) are marked ↑, while capacity-concentration/brittleness concerns are marked ↓—i.e., the column is mostly filled as “resilience quality,” except S5, which treats ↑ as added risk. That is a systematic sign error, not a wording nit.

**S11 contradicts itself.** The table gives cycle-time ↓ and late-delivery ↓ for reducing partials; Section 5 correctly says hold-to-complete can increase waiting and cancellations. Both cannot be the expected directional effect without a segmented rule made explicit in the table (short expected backorder vs long). Energy ↓ for this stock path is also weakly supported: the evidence is extra shipments/contact (transport/packaging), not material process energy (1.4 vs 0.9 kWh).

### Overstatement and slight invention
S2 is tabled as if wait-capped batching still captures CNC_A’s full 2.8→1.3 kWh/item move to batch 12–20; caps that bind will leave smaller batches and smaller energy gains. S3 is written as if slack-conditional rerouting realizes the full 18%→9% rework gap and the 32 kWh / 1.8 kg variant as a plant-wide shift; CNC_B’s speed advantage and CNC_A congestion are noted in prose, then underweighted in the table (`cycle-time ↔/↓`). S8 adds reusable-crate backhaul, which is not in the log or candidate list—empty-return pooling is justified; crate loops are not. S9’s “air/ground/stock” fallback for precautionary urgent is too loose: substituting air for vans is still a high-CO2 exception and needed a slack-tiered mode ladder.

### Design-package gaps
Obsolescence is monitored and listed as rebound, but the recommended batching design is wait-caps only. Wait-caps do not by themselves stop forecast-driven overproduction (the 6% not shipped in 14 days). A WIP/release or firm-order batch rule is missing, so the package does not fully satisfy “do not ignore obsolescence” at the intervention layer. Recommending all twelve candidates, even sequenced, is a kitchen-sink package: it is organized, but not ruthlessly prioritized against share × intensity (e.g., stock air vs critical vans vs CNC_B rework). Cancellations after availability/backorder (~530 ends) are in the DFG and waste prior energy/material; they are indicators, not a designed control.

This is a capable multi-criteria process redesign, not a generic ESG memo. Strictly, the 85% service conflation, resilience-column sign errors, S11 table/rebound clash, and inflated S2/S3 magnitudes are enough to deny a high score. Remaining issues (crate invention, obsolescence control gap, all-12 bundling) are smaller but real.
