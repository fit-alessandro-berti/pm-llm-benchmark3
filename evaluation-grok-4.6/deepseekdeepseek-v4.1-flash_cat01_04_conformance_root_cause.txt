**7.3**

The answer is strong on the intended scoring axes—abductive structure, staged alignment evidence, provenance, and refusal to treat log-moves as guilt—but it is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading the defects below are enough to keep it well short of the top band.

**What is actually good.** The six-way ranking is the right causal order for the *measured* collapse: label change, technical `Queue Routed`, then model lag, then order/ETL, with real process change secondary and employee noncompliance last. The 32→58→81→89 and 66→86 ladders are used as discriminating tests, not as decoration. Optional sign-off is correctly flagged as overpermissive and not data-aware. Outcomes are not misread as proof of either “only data error” or “no compliance issue.” The ledger, counterfactuals, four-phase isolation, six governance rules, and required headings all match the spec. The PM4Py sketch uses the requested entry points and the right candidate tree shape (`X(tau, Supervisor Sign-off)` after the review xor).

**Logical and factual defects.**
- Causal graph: `policy_change -> high_value_case` is backwards. High-value cases pre-exist; the policy attaches a constraint to them. `system_of_record_audit` is an intervention, not a generating cause. `technical_events -> recorded_labels` conflates extra events with relabeling.
- Section 3 overclaims that “the ETL reorders `Decision` and `Notify`.” Ingestion time can scramble a subset of traces; it is not a uniform swap. Only 70/650 Jun–Jul traces show the reversal, and only 11 of those 70 were screenshot-confirmed.
- “Stable” business outcomes is too clean. Cycle time 26.0→27.4 and events/case 5.4→6.8 moved; the event-count rise is exactly the technical-event signal and should not be folded into a stability claim.
- Mixed-label evidence is unused. The 30 Jun–Jul traces that still say `Classify` plus `Queue Routed`, and the 90 with `Risk Screen` and no `Queue Routed`, are the cleanest support for incomplete rollout + optional technical event. Omitting them weakens discrimination among hypotheses 1, 2, and 5.
- Rank-table posteriors 95/92/90/88 do not numerically discriminate the top four even though the prose does.

**Unclarities.** `32%58%` (and similar) is unreadable; it looks like a compound percentage. Ledger row 14 “weakens severe outcome-visible behavioral decline,” which is not one of the six hypotheses. Diagnostic phase 1 lists source sequence as data, not as an analysis that *orders* the log; the required “ordering by source sequence” step is only half-present.

**Code.** Logically coherent and aligned with the asked APIs, but it does not summarize alignment *moves* (it prints two raw diagnostics), cannot and does not reconstruct source order, and depends on unquoted `tau` parsing. That is acceptable given variant aggregates, but it is not a full reproduction of the diagnostic program it recommends.

**Net.** Core story and process-mining interpretation are right; several causal, quantitative, and evidential slips, plus weaker operationalization of alignments/ordering, cap the score.
