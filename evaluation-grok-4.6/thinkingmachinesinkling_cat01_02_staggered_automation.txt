**6.8**

The answer is a strong, well-structured causal-policy memo that hits almost every required artifact and is conceptually much better than a typical “automation cut cycle time by X%” write-up. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict rubric the defects are large enough to keep it out of the high 7s–9s.

**What it does well.** Format compliance is mostly exact: the ten headings, estimand table, three identification strategies, mechanism table, threat table, effect-assessment table, one of the four rollout verbs, and a four-rung claim ladder. The estimands correctly separate availability (ITT) from intensity, median from tail, and static from dynamic effects, and they flag interference (shared fraud desk, routing) and treatment-version change as intensity ramps. Identification explicitly rejects naïve TWFE and proposes group-time ATT with never-treated and not-yet-treated controls; the IV exclusion-restriction critique is right. Process mechanisms are the best part of the memo: fast-path substitution vs. capacity creation, specialist/fraud congestion, learning vs. staffing/case-mix shocks, rework displacement, tail amplification, and survey selection. The rollout (Delta next, Epsilon staggered, intensity phase-in, event-level and queue-state collection, fraud-desk spillover watch, numeric harm guardrails, multi-criterion scale-up rule) actually buys both operations and identifiability. The claim ladder is disciplined: no single organizational X% statement.

**Quantitative discipline is the main failure.** The compact pre/post table—the one place leadership would read numbers—contains several wrong cell means, and they are not rounding noise. Alpha Apr–Jun `p90` is ~109.9, not 108.9; `automated_fast_path_pct` is 32, not 36; `high_complexity_pct` is ~32.3, not 34.3. Beta Apr–Jun `p90` is ~103.0, not 104.8; auto-fast share is 34, not 38. Gamma Jan–Jun escalation is 8.7, not 8.0 (Mar–Apr are 9.2, not 7.2); complexity is ~23.7, not 24.7. Several “early post” figures look like they were computed on Apr–Jul or May–Aug while labeled Apr–Jun. Late-window contrasts that drive the narrative (Alpha/Beta Jul–Aug, Gamma Jul–Aug, Delta/Epsilon) are mostly right, so the qualitative story survives, but a memo that advertises transparent arithmetic cannot miss this many labeled averages.

**Identification is oversold relative to the design and the code.** Branch-specific linear trends with three pre-months for Alpha/Beta are barely identified and will soak up the treatment path; that limitation is not faced. Group-time ATT / CS-style aggregation is named but never shown, even as a table of cohort-time means. Section 3’s claim that trends “absorb the selection rationale (poor historical performance)” confuses levels (branch FE) with slopes. The Python block then estimates the naïve TWFE specification the prose rejected, after building `months_since_adoption` and never using it in a model. Cluster-robust SEs on five branches are correctly warned about, but the code does not implement the stated primary design.

**Smaller format and inference nits.** Section 8 is well above the 250–400 word cap. Section 5’s “mitigation vs. controls” language slides from arithmetic into causal interpretation more than the instructions allow. Gamma’s ATT range of −3 to −8 hours is hard to reconcile with a raw −3.4 hour change plus an explicit mean-reversion warning. A natural Apr–Jun not-yet-treated contrast (Alpha/Beta vs. still-untreated Gamma) is discussed in principle and never computed. Confidence 40 on median cycle time is still a bit high given selection, the May product shock, staffing moves, and the fraud-desk spillover.

**Net.** This is a serious, process-aware answer with the right estimands, the right threats, and a rollout that would actually generate learning. It is not nearly flawless: the quantitative table is unreliable in several labeled cells, the primary estimator is not the one estimated, and a few identification claims are looser than the panel can bear. Those are enough, under a hypercritical standard, to land it in the high 6s rather than the 8–9 range the structure might suggest.
