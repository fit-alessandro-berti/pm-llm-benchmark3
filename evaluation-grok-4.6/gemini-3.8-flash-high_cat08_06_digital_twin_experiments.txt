**6.0**

This is a broad, well-structured design that hits every required section and gets several hard parts right (sojourn vs service, latent objections, calendars, cross-case dependence, CRN, hold-out validation). It is not close to flawless. Methodological errors, unidentified parameters presented as facts, and a simulation skeleton that does not implement the model it describes keep it in the middle of the scale.

## What is actually strong

The boundary is right: municipal workflow inside, applicant delay, objections, and council calendar outside. Entities, queues, fork-join sync for drafting, inspection staging, and architect bursts are the correct level of detail for this process. Resource calendars are not ignored. Missing start timestamps are not treated as service times. Objections are a mixture/proxy, not fake observed events. The experiment table includes baseline, D1–D10, and three stresses with trade-offs. Uncertainty treatment names warm-up, long horizon, replications, CRN, quantile intervals, and heavy tails. Decision rules include tails, SLA by type, district/architect fairness, utilization, rework, backlog, and stress robustness, and they stop short of claiming a calibrated optimum.

The PM4Py ingest path is the part of the code that matches the spec: `pandas.read_csv` (not a nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv`), `format_dataframe` on the required keys, `parse_process_tree` with a real limitation note, variants, DFG, performance DFG, inter-arrival, and a sojourn column.

## Serious design and estimation errors

**Arrival intensity is misspecified.** Taking \(\lambda_0 \approx 57\) cases/calendar day from 31,200 / 18 months, then multiplying by seasonal \(1.7\), Monday/Wednesday weights, *and* a diurnal factor without normalizing \(E[s\cdot d\cdot h]=1\), double-counts structure that is already in the mean. Off-peak \(\lambda_0\) should be backed out of the mixture of peak and off-peak months. In the skeleton the errors partly cancel (weekend weights \(<1\) vs seasonal inflation), which is luck, not a method.

**19% is not a completeness-rework probability.** “Simple with missing docs | 19%” is a *variant-group share of all cases*, not \(P(\text{missing docs})\). Simple renovation is 45% of volume; other types also loop. Using 19% (and then a geometric decay of \(0.20\cdot p\)) will understate clerk rework and overstate D1’s effect. Inspection “9%” is closer to a case share, but appeal/planning/legal rework is still folded into other buckets.

**Service-time fitting is treated as more identified than it is.** Matching lognormal \((\mu,\sigma)\) to provided p50/p90 is fine as a *provisional* generator, and \(\Phi^{-1}(0.90)\approx 1.28155\) is correct. Calling queue inversion an “upper bound” on service is wrong: for a busy server, inter-completion is approximately service, and with multi-FTE pools plus missing starts the interval is not a clean bound. Applicant p90 of 22 days is invented. The summary already splits (inferred) service and wait; those waits must be *calibration targets*, not second inputs. There is no Little’s-law / queueing-consistency loop given FTE, calendars, and NHPP load.

**Several “empirical” quantities are authorial.** Five districts and travel 25–45 vs 55–95 minutes; council freeze on calendar days 15–21 every month (a 25% legal-capacity hit if true); fire hours 08:00–16:00; utilization bands 75–80% clerks, 82–88% planners, etc.; SLAs 14/45/90/120 days; D2 covering “~70%” of simple cases; emergencies \(<0.5\%\); objection contagion \(+25\%\). Some of these belong in assumptions or sensitivity. Using the invented utilization numbers as a validation target with a 5% tolerance is circular. The 14-day simple SLA is tighter than the 22-day median of the missing-docs simple variant; the 120-day complex SLA is *below* the 140-day historical median, so the current system already “fails” by construction.

**D10’s “>35% median / >50% p90” is an unearned point forecast.** Expected direction is fine; those magnitudes are not. Combined with hard gates of 15%/25% reduction, the MCDA can reject smaller but robust policies (e.g. peak inspector only) before any calibrated run.

## Validation and uncertainty problems

Token-replay fitness \(>98\%\) against this *simplified* tree is the wrong target: redo labels `Completeness rework` / `Inspection rework` are not log activities, and planning/legal/appeal rework plus 7% rare variants are missing. Fitness that high would mean the engine is more block-structured than the log. Token-based replay also needs a Petri net (`convert_to_petri_net`); that step is omitted.

A two-sample KS test at \(\alpha=0.05\) on thousands of cases will reject any useful model. Wasserstein and Q-Q are the right tools and are mentioned, but the KS pass/fail rule is a textbook large-\(n\) error.

Warm-up seeding from “Day 90” can plant the system in a peak-season snapshot. Welch plus a representative (or multiple) start state would be safer. The uncertainty section piles Welch CIs, BCa, Beta-Binomial HPD, Sobol, GPD, and CVaR without saying which of those a municipality actually needs for D1–D10. Input uncertainty on the wait/service split and on \(p_{\text{obj}}\) should have been first-class, not generic GSA item 2.

Preempting *all* in-progress work at shift end is a bad default for site visits. No-shows are named in the brief and never modeled. D2’s “no prior violations” flag is not in the log and is not listed as a data gap.

## Code vs document (coherence)

The spec allows an incomplete engine. It does not allow an experiment harness that *looks* like it ran the twin.

`DigitalTwinEngine.run_simulation` does not execute the process tree, queues, calendars, rework, parallel sync, or batching. It hourly-increments time, Poisson-arrives cases, and completes each case when elapsed time exceeds a **fixed lead time by case type** (14d vs 8d if D2, 48d, 68d vs 52d if D5, 140d). `sample_service_time`, `sample_applicant_delay`, `sample_public_notice_duration`, `get_effective_capacity`, `busy_resources`, Monday clerk reservation, 40% environmental non-permit time, peak-only D4, bundle holding delay, and D1/D6/D7/D8/D9 are unused or only half-wired (D4 adds an inspector for the whole horizon; D3 only multiplies transit by 0.5 with no first-case wait). Printed median/p90 would be case-mix artifacts, not intervention effects. Architect “bursts” are a 31% Bernoulli label, not a cluster process. That is a simple independent-case delay model wearing DES clothing.

Minor API/design nits: possible fragility of `get_variants_as_tuples` vs `get_variants`; tree limitations note misses the redo-activity / missing-rework-gate issues; day-of-week weights \(\{1.76,1.10,0.80,0.80,0.54\}\) are an extra unstated model.

## Bottom line

As a *narrative* digital-twin design this is competent and aligned with the process-mining brief. As a *defensible* estimation, validation, and experiment plan it overfits invented numbers, misreads variant shares as routing probabilities, and misspecifies the arrival rate. As an implementation sketch it contradicts its own model. Those are not polish issues; they are the difference between a twin you could calibrate and a document that would ship the wrong queues.
