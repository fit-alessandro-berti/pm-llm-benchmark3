## Conceptual errors (costly, because they are the assignment)

**Convergence / divergence are defined as logistics join/split, not as flattening effects.** In this setting those terms are the reasons event copies and repeated activities appear. The answer’s glosses (P3/D2 as “convergence,” O1 splitting as “divergence”) are allowed as *an* interpretation, but they are never mapped onto the phenomenon the prompt itself states: a shared event is copied into every case of the chosen type. That choice then contaminates the matrix. Delivery and Invoice get **High** convergence because D2 and V2 *consolidate* other objects, even though **no event is related to two Deliveries or two Invoices**. Flattening on Delivery or Invoice does not copy events across cases. “Convergence risk” and “expected event duplication” therefore measure different things, and the table never says so.

**The DFG mechanism is wrong in the two most important distortions.** Flattening yields four order traces; a DFG has edges only *inside* a trace. e13/e16 being copied into O2 and O3 does **not** create “concurrency loops between O2 and O3” or a dependence of O2’s packing on O3’s items. The real effects are frequency inflation, O3’s `Pack Package → Pack Package` and `Dispatch → Dispatch` self-edges (e13→e14, e16→e17), and synchronized timestamps. Distortion 2 is backwards for the same reason: I32 and I41 share *one* event (e14/e17). An analyst would not infer “I32 cannot be packed until I41 is ready”; they would under-count physical packs and miss co-packing. Distortion 3 invents a causal story the log does not contain: O1’s second confirm is e24 because **I12 is on D2**, not because O1 “waits for O2/O3.” Full-order delivery at 16:00 is commercially meaningful; the bias is inseparability, not a fictitious queue behind P3.

These are not wording quibbles. The task is to say what flattening *duplicates, hides, or distorts*. If the causal channel is wrong, the ratings are unearned.
