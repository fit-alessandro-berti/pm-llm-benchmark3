## QA — wrong primary rule, impossible calibration story

The data are appointment insertion on an otherwise FIFO server, not “appointment + non-preemptive priority.”

At **08:30**, waiting jobs include A02 (P3, est 20) and A04 (P1, est 7). The server takes A02. That is a clean rejection of both priority and SPT. At **09:05** A05 (P3) is taken while A08 (P1, est 25) and A07 (P3, est 9) wait — again rejecting priority and SPT. After A01, every non-appointment choice is arrival order: A02, A04, A05, A06 (appointment, late), A07, A08.

The author’s strongest “priority” evidence is A04 starting at 08:58. That is just FIFO after A02 plus the recorded A1 calibration **08:52–08:58**. No special P1-hold is required.

The calibration excuse for skipping A04 at 08:30 is **impossible**. Calibration starts twenty minutes later. Treating “P1 jobs are reserved for post-calibration” as part of the primary discipline invents a mechanism that is not in the candidate list and is not needed.

Confidence 70 for a misidentified primary is miscalibrated. The secondary column (“FIFO for non-appointment jobs”) is actually the non-appointment rule; the table buries the right answer under the wrong one.

§4 asks for an **additional** observable that would discriminate two remaining theories. Both tests propose reading start times that are already in `service_segments`. For QC 10:54 the start times are already observed (C05 before C06). That is not a counterfactual test.

§5 ranks “calibration/meeting alignment” first and uses it to excuse the QA 08:30 choice. The ranking is therefore wrong: the highest-plausibility exception is applied to the episode it cannot explain. Dispatcher reassignment and SLA-endangered override are real and should have been ranked above a time-inconsistent calibration story. ## Process-mining competence

The vocabulary is there. The method is not executed. Eligible sets are the unit of analysis the prompt demanded; jobs are repeatedly included before arrival, two timestamps are treated as one decision, non-preemption is read as anti-priority, and a two-minute scan bound is stretched to seven minutes. Those are definitional failures, not rounding disagreements.

What remains after stripping errors: QA has appointments; QB preempts at least once for a P1 arrival; QC’s first pick is the shortest estimate; QD is skill-constrained and looks FIFO inside a resource. That is a start, not a diagnosis.
