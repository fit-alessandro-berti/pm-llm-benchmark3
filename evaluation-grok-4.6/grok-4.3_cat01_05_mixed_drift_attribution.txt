## Quantitative errors

The §3 change table is not a reliable artifact.

- The header says “Jan–Feb vs later,” but the deltas switch base month by cell. March cases `+35` is versus February (605), December cases `+70` is versus January (620), complexity/rework generally use January. That is not a decomposition; it is mixed arithmetic.
- March median cycle is **31.2 vs 31.5 in January** (a small *decline*). The table reports `0.3` as if it rose.
- July rework is `29 − 12 = +17` pp, not `+16`.
- Raw fitness is reported as unsigned positives (`0.38`, `0.43`…) while every other column is a signed level change. A reader would think fitness improved.
- DFG distance is specified as Euclidean-after-min-max on **six** relations. The supplied fragment has **seven**. `Register->Eligibility` is constant at 100, so min-max is undefined (divide-by-zero). The text says omitted edges are “assumed zero and excluded,” which is contradictory. The later code uses unscaled `np.linalg.norm` with no min-max. The printed distances (`0.04, 0.19, …`) are therefore unreproducible.
- Cycle-time shares (queueing 55–65%, rework 20–25%, mix 10–15%, service 5–8%) are asserted, not justified, and are not tied to a period. They cannot be right for both July and October: complexity is +7 pp by July and +20 pp by October. Service `+0.6 h` is the only component grounded in the prompt.
- The “change-point” rule `recovery > 0.3 → logging` is not a change-point detector. The raw–mapped gap is large in *every* post-migration month, so the rule labels the entire post-March series “logging” and never flags May, June, or October.

## Causal and temporal mistakes

The big picture is directionally correct; the local attributions are often sloppy.

- **Invented stratification.** §2 and §9 state that post-May DFG/rework patterns “are concentrated in Supplier Z cases.” No supplier-stratified DFG, variant, or waiting-time table is supplied. Supplier Z’s *share* jumps (18% → 43%); that is not the same as a within-Z rework effect. Treating the latter as observed is a causal-restraint failure on a prompt that warned the evidence is aggregated and non-unique.
- **March alternatives.** “None plausible” is overconfident. A concurrent real process change, partial migration of only some cases (`Register -> Eligibility Screen` 500 vs `Validate Eligibility` 140), and resource-missingness effects on downstream mining are all live alternatives.
- **June row is wrong.** `missing_resource_pct +0.7 pp` is not a staffing signature. The prompt says missingness is concentrated in *migrated activities*, not in the June headcount cut. The May cycle-time jump (32.0 → 36.5) *precedes* 15 June; staffing cannot explain it.
- **July priority is mis-signed as control-flow.** The priority rule is a queue-order intervention. The July rework rate (29) is the continuation of the May supplier break (12 → 24 → 27 → 29). Senior assessment only moves 7 → 9 in July; the real structural jump is October (22). Ranking July as priority-driven control-flow drift is backwards.
- **September is factually inverted.** Hypothesis 5 cites “some deterioration visible in Sep” as evidence against a purely seasonal story. September *improves*: rework 27 → 20, median 45.0 → 41.5, supplier Z 38 → 24, mapped fitness 0.87 → 0.89. That recovery after the 10 September supplier report is one of the best discriminating intervals in the file, and the timeline omits it entirely.
- **Open Case “does not alter control-flow semantics.”** In the raw DFG it does: `Open Case -> Register` is a new edge that wrecks raw fitness. The business meaning may be technical, but discovery/conformance care about the edge. That distinction is the whole point of the mapped series.
- Interaction is acknowledged once (supplier × staffing) and then undersold. Priority × October mix, supplier × non-random geography, and logging × senior-assessment visibility are the actual identification problems. December is correctly called entangled, then still scored 65 as if the overtime campaign were identified.

- Multiple testing is waved off with “two consecutive months.” Related monitors (raw fitness, mapped fitness, label count, duplicates) are highly dependent; there is no family structure and no monitor for the *gap* between raw and mapped fitness, which is the actual logging diagnostic.
- Event-ordering as a first-class monitor is missing. Outcome monitoring (`Approve/Reject` shift `> 5 pp`) has no baseline series in the packet.
