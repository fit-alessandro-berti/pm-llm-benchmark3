**7.2**

The write-up is strong on the dimensions the prompt actually grades: it follows the required seven-section structure, stays close to the DFGs and phase metrics, does not treat every deviation as failure, and is explicit that T2 volume was bought with control debt and that T4 cycle-time recovery is not compliance recovery. The A–L table is complete, the three-test resilience frame is useful, and most counts check out (1,600 skip-auth, 3,250 unscanned dispatch, 1,080 deferrals, 8.5% of closed invoices into audit remediation). That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading. The recovery redesign, which is a core required output, contains a scoring error that would undo the case’s main operational lesson, and several smaller classification and coverage problems compound it.

**Recovery model error (largest deduction).**  
Triage is \(S =\) criticality \(\times\) product risk \(+\) urgency, with \(S < 4\) as a deferral candidate. Hospital \(\times\) ambient \(= 3\), so a hospital-critical ambient order with no stockout flag is a deferral candidate. Even with a \(+2\) urgency flag it only reaches Standard, not Priority. The hard-rule patch (“no deferral of hospital-critical cold-chain or any controlled order”) does not save hospital ambient. That contradicts adaptation A and the T2 evidence that hospital protection was the one service outcome that held (91% fill, median \(8 \to 10\)h). Pharmacy-controlled scores \(8\) and also misses Priority. A redesign whose headline rule would deprioritize hospital ambient is not a small drafting nit.

**Keep / Modify is applied inconsistently.**  
A is correctly “Modify” (keep the lane, remove the bypass). B, F, I, J, and K are labeled “Keep” while the rationale adds time-boxes, 100% cold-chain coverage, published scores, or a restoration gate. F in particular should be Modify: the observed sample left ~1,950 of 3,600 packed cases with no release step and 4.8% missing temperature documentation. Calling that “Keep” blurs the same distinction the table is supposed to enforce.

**Required indicator coverage is incomplete.**  
Section 5 asks for throughput, service protection, traceability, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness. Fill rate and a p90 *ratio* appear; there is no leading or lagging throughput measure (cases/day, median cycle time vs baseline). That is a direct miss of a stated requirement, not a stylistic choice.

**Smaller factual and interpretive slips.**  
Recommendation 3’s “58% of T2 dispatches unscanned” confuses share of *cases* (3,250/5,600) with share of *dispatches* (~3,250/3,600 packed, ~90%). T4’s rise in compliance holds (1.7% \(\to\) 3.7%) is presented as a “good sign” of reactivated controls; it is equally consistent with dirty master data and re-verification of incident-period stock. The fairness cap (“deferral share \(> 2\times\) baseline cancellation rate”) mixes two different metrics. Several operating numbers (30 min trigger, 12h snapshot freeze, 15 pp fill-rate gap, 85% hospital-fill tripwire, 10–20% AQL) are asserted without a link back to the log.

**What remains creditworthy.**  
Temporal reasoning is mostly right: snapshot staleness is treated as time-dependent; T3 intake-before-sync is named as a sequencing error; the invoice-close \(\to\) audit-remediation tail is not equated with real-time control. Abduction on B vs C (capacity, not intent) and on dual-control gaps (documentation vs physical check) is disciplined. C and G are correctly retired; after-the-fact reconstruction is not treated as equivalent to a contemporaneous temperature or custody record. Section 7’s accept / refuse / what-would-change-our-mind paragraph is the right shape.

The piece reads like a high-quality draft with one design bug that matters and a handful of taxonomy and coverage misses. Under a hypercritical bar that is a mid-7, not an 8+.
