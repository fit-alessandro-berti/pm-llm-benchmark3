**8.1**

This is a strong, constraint-compliant process-redesign memo with almost no arithmetic errors, but it is not nearly flawless. It would be a high-8 under ordinary review; the deductions below are for omitted process-mining evidence, thresholds that are not derived from the logs, and a few operational unclarities.

**What is genuinely good.** The required seven-section structure is followed. Mix math is correct (≈2,300 → 3,300 orders/week, Routine roughly flat, Urgent +131%, Complex-panel +121%, combined ≈2.27×) and is used to reject volume-only thinking. Normal Urgent p90 already at 19h versus an 18h contract, and peak Urgent median 17h hiding a 33h p90, are the right success criteria. DFG imbalance (peak technical validation 7,240 in vs 8,080 out), 101% utilization as an alarm rather than a physical fraction, and the refusal to sum heterogeneous median edges are all sound. Repeat-assay *transition shares* (≈6.7% → 14.8%) are correctly separated from the 7.5%/11.8% overall rework rates. I1–I3 are rewritten instead of adopted; I9 is rejected with evidence; I4 is correctly shown to fail at observed peak technical-validation load unless backfilled; I10 is not allowed to treat “48h hold − 12h transport” as a saving. Every major lever names a next bottleneck. Case types are not all promised first place. Residual harm *caused by the redesign* (upstream aging from release gates, unused reserved slots, digital-complete bias) is explicit. Leading vs lagging indicators are actually distinguished, including the open-order censorship trap.

**Process-mining evidence is underused.** This is the main gap relative to the scoring rubric. The memo leans on utilization/queue tables and a few edges, but does not convert the DFGs into weekly workload shifts that would have sharpened bottleneck migration:

- Medical-bound TV outflows rise from roughly 534/week to ~1,383/week (≈2.6×), stronger than the 2.27× order-mix proxy the author refused to over-interpret.
- Batch-wait incidence rises from ~95 to ~393 events/week (about 4×), with Batch wait → Assay run 9h/22h.
- Pre-analytical recollection is ~95 vs ~260/week; recollection→cancel is already ~35–42%.
- Peak start 9,900 vs end 8,580 is left-censored WIP, not only a TV accounting quirk.

The stated 3.1× medical-validation and 2.4× specialist-hold propensities for Complex-panel cases are barely used numerically. Recollection, batching, and medical-share shifts are described qualitatively when the logs already quantify them.

**The standing control system is only weakly grounded in the log.** Least-slack dispatch, “latest safe start,” protected aging blocks, and 85/95% capacity bands are standard operations constructs. They are labeled provisional, but they are still the core of the redesign, and they require remaining-time, compatibility, setup, and stability data the flattened order log does not contain. Circularity (remaining time includes queues that depend on the same dispatcher) is not treated. Round thresholds (2h triage, 0–4h Urgent slack, 85/95%) are not back-solved from the 18h/72h clocks or from peak medians (assay queue 18h, medical 22h, specialist 36h).

**I5 is misplaced relative to the observed peak.** Peak medical-validation median queue (22h) already exceeds the entire Urgent contract; specialist hold-to-medical is 48h. For S1 as historically reported, an evening medical shift is not a nice-to-have overlay behind a 95% forecast trigger—it is the binding Urgent constraint. Keeping I5 strictly scenario-specific while putting courier smoothing (I7) in the *robust* package overweights an intervention that needs unproven route flexibility and underweights the station that already breaks the SLA on the given peak data. I6 as a standing extra inspection layer at 96% pre-analytical utilization is similarly optimistic; the author warns about duplication but still makes early triage a pillar without a named task substitution or who staffs it.

**Smaller unclarities.** S2 and S4 each bundle two tests (normal vs peak mix; degradation vs outage; compression vs delay). Repeat-after-failure as a *case type* is not always kept distinct from the Repeat assay *loop*. “Escalation” after repeated displacement is unspecified. Implementation burden in the table collapses to “medium–high” for almost every row, so it does not discriminate. Effects are appropriately directional, but that leaves the package as an untested candidate—which section 6 admits, and which a stricter reading of “redesign so that it remains acceptable” would have pushed toward a clearer infeasibility envelope for Urgent during S1+S2 (two machines at 98% with a 14.8% repeat share cannot be scheduled into 18h if one machine is lost).

**Constraints.** The answer does not add capacity everywhere, does not optimize the historical average, does not ignore downstream overload, and does not pretend every class can be first. Those bars are met. The remaining shortfall is incomplete extraction of the DFG, a control policy parameterized off-log, and a standing/contingent split that does not fully face the already-observed medical and assay queues versus the 18h Urgent clock.

Not a 9+: too much available log evidence left on the table, and the operational core is more generic OR than evidence-parameterized lab control. Not below ~7.5: no material calculation mistakes, no constraint violations, and the bottleneck-migration and anti-average reasoning are well above typical answers.
