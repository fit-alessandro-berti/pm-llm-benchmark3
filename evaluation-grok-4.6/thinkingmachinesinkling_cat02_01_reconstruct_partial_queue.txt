**4.8**

The write-up looks like a real process-mining analysis: labeled assumptions, interval logic for missing starts, elapsed vs final service on `OPEN` cases, and a coherent bottleneck story. Under a strict reading it still fails several required deliverables and contains calculation errors that a careful reconstruction would have caught.

**What is actually solid.** Assumptions A1–A10 are the right kind. Rounding is treated as ±2.5 min on stamps / ±5 min on durations. Queue entry, single-task resources, the C2 10:55–11:10 outage, and right-censoring are defined instead of being swept into point values. The eight mandatory cases are mostly reconstructed correctly: P05’s S2 sojourn is pinned by `10:15 − 09:25`; P07 is boxed by I1 free after P05 (~09:25) and complete at 10:00; P11 correctly cannot start on C2 until after P08 (~12:00) because of maintenance plus P08; P15–P22 keep elapsed service and residual queue time visible. Section 5’s qualitative claim — S2 is the tighter capacity (longer service, outage), S1’s long afternoon waits are partly a backlog symptom — is defensible. Section 6’s stable/unstable split is the right shape.

**Required numbers are wrong in the main tables.** The aggregated complete-case row is not computed. For P01–P04, P06, P08–P10, P12–P14 the S1 waits are 5, 5, 20, 20, 40, 50, 60, 70, 85, 80, 95 (mean ~48, range 5–95), not “mean 20 [0,40]”. S2 waits are 5, 5, 10, 15, 25, 55, 25, 50, 45, 50, 65 (mean ~32, range 5–65), not “mean 15 [0,30]”. That is not rounding noise; later complete cases were simply not included in the average.

S1 peak WIP is also too low. A case-by-case count of arrived-but-not-finished-S1 gives about 7 near 09:35–10:00 and about 8 around 11:00, not peaks of 5 and 6. Boundary handling drops completions: P14’s S1 end at 12:00 is excluded from every interval (6+7+6=19 instead of 20 S1 finishes); P04’s S2 end at 10:05 is omitted from 10:00–12:00 (3+4+6=13 instead of 14 S2 finishes). Case-level **total sojourn** for completed cases (`s2_end − arrival_s1`) is never reported, despite being an explicit required estimate.

**Little’s Law is not a real check.** A8 points at 12:00–13:00; the code uses 10:00–12:00. \(W\) is mean wait, not sojourn; \(L_{\text{obs}}\) is set to the case count. That is not Little’s Law, and the interval is not close to steady state.

**Bounds are tighter than the hard constraints.** Resource windows plus nonnegative service give P07 start \(\in\) [~09:25, 10:00] (wait roughly 40–75 min) and P05 S2 start \(\in\) [~09:45, 10:15] (wait roughly 20–50 min). The reported [35,60] and [15,35] ranges bake in an assumed minimum service (15–20 min) as if it were observed. That assumption belongs in the scenario section, not in the primary interval.

**Section 7 is not reproducible.** `Series.notin` does not exist (`~df[col].isin(...)` does); the script would crash before any PM4Py call. `parse_time` maps `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank to the same `NaT`, which violates the status-aware parsing requirement. Station WIP at 14:00 is not computed — the formula is incoherent and then replaced by hardcoded case lists. Window busy time is not actually allocated inside each interval. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are at least restricted to fully observed cases and labeled case-level, and the pandas-vs-`convert_log_to_time_intervals` comment satisfies the letter of item 7, but that does not offset an unrunnable notebook.

**Format nits.** Two station tables with interval rows invert the requested “one table, rows = S1/S2”. There is no queue-length/WIP path over time, only rough mean/peak figures, so the stated simultaneous-event convention is never applied.

The named-case reconstruction and bottleneck discussion are good enough that this is not a collapse. The headline tables, missing sojourns, fake Little’s Law, and non-running code keep it well below a competent complete answer.
