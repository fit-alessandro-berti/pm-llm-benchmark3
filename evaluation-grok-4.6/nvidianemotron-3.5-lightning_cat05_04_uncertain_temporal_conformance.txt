**3.5**

The write-up follows the required headings and states the right *framework* (offset direction, resolution before clock correction, interval difference bounds, CERTAIN vs POSSIBLE vs INDETERMINATE, no midpoint-as-truth). That framework is then applied incorrectly often enough that several material conclusions invert.

**Fatal quantitative / semantic errors**

- **Wrong temporal-profile band for `Alert_Received` → `Investigation_Start`.** The supplied pair is `(600, 180)`, so ζ=1 is `[420, 780]`. Tables repeatedly use `[1980, 3420]` (the Alert–Containment band). Prose sometimes uses `780` and sometimes `1980`. Every Alert–Invest profile row and most of the ζ-sensitivity matrix for that pair is therefore unreliable.
- **T-401 Close date.** Section 2 correctly places `Close_Incident` on **2026-06-02**, then Section 3 treats the gap as **518 s**. True duration is ~**86919–86947 s** (~24 h 9 min). S3 still passes, but the Contain–Close profile is a **CERTAIN_PASS** near the mean `86400`, not a CERTAIN_VIOLATION. The same “tiny duration” mistake is reused as if it were Contain–Close on cases that have **no close event**.
- **T-404 start inference uses the wrong activity.** Duration 18–25 min is from start to **`Investigation_Complete` (10:35)**, not `Containment_Complete` (10:55). Feasible start is about `[10:09:58.5, 10:17:01.5]`, so S1 is **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** (~597–1023 s vs 900), not CERTAIN_VIOLATION; S2 is **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION** (~2277–2703 s vs 2700), not CERTAIN_PASS.
- **T-407 inverts delivery latency.** Receipt is `Alert_Sent + [0, 20] min`, not minus. Their `[1017, 1143]` S1 CERTAIN_VIOLATION is physically a negative-latency model. With the stated bound, S1 is at best POSSIBLE_VIOLATION; given a missing receipt and an unlogged causal assumption, **INDETERMINATE** is the honest label. They claim no INDETERMINATE results exist.
- **T-409 ignores the case-specific offset.** NTP for that hour is missing; the weak bound is **[−12, +12] min**, not the catalog `[−90, +120] s`. That can put Investigation before Alert and flip S1 from CERTAIN_PASS to POSSIBLE / weak-evidence.

**Classification and pair hygiene**

- **S3 / Contain–Close assessed where `Close_Incident` does not exist** (T-405, T-406, T-407, T-409), often by recycling Alert–Contain seconds and calling S3 a certain pass. T-402–T-404 correctly omit S3 — inconsistent.
- Partial overlap is defined correctly, then sometimes treated as CERTAIN_VIOLATION in the ζ discussion (e.g. T-403 Alert–Invest at ζ=2: `[718.5, 1141.5]` vs `[240, 960]` overlaps).
- T-408 Alert–Invest: `[597, 603]` is **not** inside `[1980, 3420]`; the table still says “wholly inside” → CERTAIN_PASS. Internal contradiction even on their own (wrong) band.
- T-406: `13:14 − 630 s` is `13:03:30`, not `12:53:30`. The extra 10 min creates **spurious negative** Alert→Invest durations; Section 3 and Section 4 then disagree on whether S1 is POSSIBLE or CERTAIN violation.
- T-405 DST combo (Alert CEST, later events CET) is the right surviving fold; Alert–Invest **max is ~1080 s, not 1140 s** (17 min 59 s ≠ 1139 s). Class happens to survive.

**What is actually solid**

HUB ±1.5 s, OPS-BER floor + `[−90, +120]` on CEST dates, SENSOR 10 s rounding + ±5 s, VENDOR-NY EDT, and T-401/402/403/408 **S1/S2** (and T-408 S3) are mostly computed correctly. T-405 fold logic is careful. Governance hits the requested controls. Midpoints are not used as the primary clock.

**Section 5 fails the spec**

The block does not reconstruct all supplied events, does not run DST alternatives end-to-end, does not derive interval classes, does not attach the **given** profile `{(600,180), …}`, and is not executable (`Europe/Berlin` strings, DST tuple vs pair, stubbed cases). `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(df, zeta=…)` is not a faithful stand-in for discover + `check_temporal_profile` with the stated statistics. Severity and Confidence columns are left empty.

**Net:** a competent template with several correct easy SLA rows, undermined by systematic pair/band mix-ups, inverted or wrong-event inference on T-404/T-407, a dropped day on T-401’s 24 h close, phantom S3 tests, unused INDETERMINATE, and non-working PM4Py code. Under a strict bar this is far from a reliable incident-timing opinion.
