**5.6**

The write-up is well structured and shows real process-level instinct: it does not rank by frequency, argues correctly that registration is a low-leverage node, uses waiting time / rework / critical-path evidence, and treats analyst, compliance, and legal capacity as first-class constraints. That is not enough. Under a strict reading it has several material errors, hard-constraint violations, and internal contradictions.

**Hard constraint failures.** The brief allows at most four modules in year one. Section 6 then rolls out eight (A5, A4, A6, A9, A7, A10, A8, A12). The recommended set is four (A4, A6, A7, A9), so the rollout and the recommendation describe different programs. The quick-win portfolio includes A11 despite the explicit rule not to automate a downstream activation bot while upstream controls remain unreliable; the matrix even marks A11 as low suitability, then the portfolio ignores that finding. Compliance’s demand that the first wave include a control-strengthening module is satisfied only in the final pick, not in the named bottleneck portfolio.

**Control logic is wrong in places.** Four-eyes applies to credit analysis versus committee packaging, not to contract generation. Claiming A9 “hardcodes four-eyes segregation” maps the wrong control onto the wrong activity. A9 is the right lever for decision-to-contract matching (8.5% / 14% renewal loops); it is not the four-eyes control. KYC-before-signing (6.5% provisional, 1.1% remediation) is also weakly covered by the recommended four.

**Resource mechanism is inverted.** The social-network note says faster clean inflow can overload the nine analysts *unless normalization is improved*. A6 removes 75 minutes of analyst work and is the *mitigant*. The answer treats A6 as the thing that saturates analysts and then invents rebalancing/A5 as a necessary pairing. That misreads the bottleneck.

**Overclaiming and invented precision.** Cycle-time cuts of 5–10% / 25–35%, mismatch wait “31h to &lt;8h”, normalization “20h to &lt;5h”, and gate metrics (30%, 85%, 90%, 80%) are unsupported. A7 is rated as high process-level benefit as if triage deletes the 58h external wait; the wait is outside the bank, required mainly for equipment finance (71%) and rarely for working capital (18%). Treating A7 as a universal critical-path unlock overstates both reach and effect. Committee’s weekly cadence for high-exposure cases is barely used, so packaging automation is credited with delay it cannot remove.

**Dependency map is mixed.** Core-banking “deep integration” assignments are stated as fact. A4 is framed as needing clean KYC data; A4 *is* the dirty-data exception path. Concurrency of KYC and financial review is flagged in the brief and then largely ignored in effects and sequencing.

**Inconsistency across sections.** Bottleneck is defined as A4/A6/A7/A8, then recommended as A4/A6/A7/A9 under the same name. A8 is Medium suitability because of GIGO, dropped from the rec, then scheduled in year one anyway. A1 is correctly deprioritized on process grounds, but the political constraint is dismissed rather than managed.

What remains good is the anti-frequency argument, the matrix columns, the three-way portfolio contrast in spirit, and the use of DFG counts, FTE pools, and several control findings. Those strengths make this a competent draft, not a near-flawless one. The budget breach, A11 inclusion, four-eyes mis-mapping, and inverted A6 workload story keep it well below the top of the scale.
