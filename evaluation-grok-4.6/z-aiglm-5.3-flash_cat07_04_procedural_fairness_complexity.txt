7.5

The answer is a strong, well-structured fairness assessment that hits every required heading, respects the main constraints, and uses the tables with real care. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict reading, several analytical and technical defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is done well.** Raw gaps are never treated as discrimination, and case mix is never treated as a full excuse. `risk_score_band` is treated as a suspect control throughout. Rights-preserving steps are correctly framed as a possible source of burden rather than as optional delay to be cut. The eight fairness dimensions are all present and tied to the numbers. The strategy section is the high point: matching, path mediation, resource comparison, wait decomposition, leave-one-out/E-value sensitivity, negative-control transitions, region×channel slices, and within-group “exercise-of-rights” contrasts. Mitigations all include side effects and never propose removing support steps. The bottom line is calibrated: indeterminate on discrimination, unfavorable on burden and timeliness.

**Logical error on adjusted appeals.** Mechanism 3 attributes part of Group D’s **+5.7 pp adjusted** appeal gap to high prior-case count / “system navigation knowledge.” The preliminary model already controls for `prior_case_count`. That story can explain *raw* gaps; it cannot explain the residual. Section 3 later says the D appeal signal is *not* explained by complexity variables, so the write-up contradicts itself. That is a real inferential mistake, not a wording slip.

**Over-reading the given adjusted table.** The preliminary controls omit `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested`. Residuals for B (−5.5 pp / +2.6 days) and C (−2.2 pp / +4.1 days) therefore still include the mechanical effect of extra required steps. Calling those residuals the closest thing to “similar cases treated differently,” and calling B/C “comparable Group A cases” in the bottom line, overstates what the table identifies. The proposed matching set in §2.1 repeats the same omission (it copies the preliminary covariates and does not stratify on language or accommodation need). The within-group contrast partly repairs this, but the interpretation of the supplied adjusted numbers should have split: (i) extra-step duration vs (ii) residual disparity among people who do not need those steps. C’s +4.1 days is especially likely to be the 4.7-day accommodation queue the model never conditioned on.

**Weaker use of path evidence.** V6 (appeal path: 4/8/5/13%) is barely used despite being one of the six given variants. Submit→Verify waits (0.8 vs 1.4 days) are under-discussed relative to the in-person channel note. The “negative control” on Decision Draft→Notification uses *raw* medians (0.5 vs 0.8), then §8 treats that as evidence the late pipeline is even-handed. Relatively small, yes; evidence of even-handed *adjusted* delivery, no.

**PM4Py plan is conceptually right and execution-sloppy.** It covers variants, performance DFG, handover/working-together/roles/similarity, service/waiting insertion, duration filtering, log skeleton, and DECLARE. Required `discover_dfg` (frequency) never appears—only the performance DFG. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is assigned to `evdf` and then ignored. After a merge with `_A`/`_B` suffixes the code subtracts invented `wait_A`/`wait_B` columns that were never created. `conformance_diagnostics_log_skeleton` and `mode="traces"` on `filter_case_performance` are dubious API names. For a “code or pseudocode” plan this is still usable as a sketch; as written it would not run and it misses an explicitly listed function.

**Smaller issues.** Group C (n=820) is called “small” for main effects; it is small only in intersections. Sampling-error arithmetic is hand-wavy given clustering and covariates. “Unlawful discrimination” in §8 imports a legal standard the prompt did not ask for. Mechanism 1 likewise leans on already-controlled factors when discussing D, though it is more carefully limited to the raw pattern.

**Net.** Content coverage, constraint discipline, and the better parts of the identification strategy are well above average. The adjusted-gap misattribution, the omitted rights-need covariates in both matching and interpretation, and the broken/incomplete mining sketch are enough, under a hypercritical bar, to cap the score in the mid-7s rather than the 9–10 range reserved for near-flawless work.
