**6.5**

The write-up matches the required seven-section structure and is stronger than a generic scoring essay: it states hard vs soft constraints, gives a full candidate-filter + multi-objective score, assigns all 16 cases, and stays honest that no routing rule is a single optimum. Hard rules are mostly respected (skill 0 blocked, Security/Approval gates, no unsupervised high-priority work below skill 2, batch cap of 4, high-overtime staff kept off normal/low work, Quality batching only where allowed, 2/9 = 22% training on documented diagnosis priorities).

The central failure is that the policy, as specified and as applied, dumps too much critical work on **R6_Farid**. R6 already has 5 open cases and 6.5 hours left, then receives C002 (urgent approval, 2h), C011 (urgent VIP negotiation, 4h), C010 (another approval), and C003 as a **diagnosis trainee**. That contradicts the policy’s own SLA, capacity, and resilience goals. A global pass would have parked diagnosis training on R1 (C003 + C009 still hits 20%) and kept R6 for the two urgents. The greedy “sort by SLA, then assign, including training” loop is what produces this; the algorithm never reserves bottleneck specialists before handing them development work.

Other notable gaps, judged strictly:

- Baselines are compared only in the abstract. First-available and specialist-only are never instantiated on C001–C016, so the trade-off table is unearned.
- C001’s “R8 lead + R3 context” is conceptually fuzzy: either R3 is a supervised Security performer (skill 1, allowed) or a non-performing handoff. Counting it against R3’s cap of 4 is conservative, but it also makes R3 (C007 at 6h SLA plus two supervisions) look like a second hidden bottleneck that the text underplays.
- Continuity scoring says keep the owner when `reassignment_count >= 2`, then C003 (already 2, “repeated diagnosis loop”) is moved anyway. The trade-off is named, but it is not reconciled with the 1.8× rework evidence.
- High-overtime “only if urgent **and no safer alternative**” is implemented as a skip for normal/low plus a −2.5 penalty, not a last-resort test. Approval skill-1 “prepare but not finalize” is omitted.
- Additive scores are uncalibrated: SLA term `U` can dwarf skill, continuity, fairness, and training, so the “balanced” objective is not actually balanced.
- Hours remaining are never checked against SLA windows; several 6h-class jobs sit on people with ~6h left and existing load.
- C015’s note (Eva/Jonas + supervision) vs Security skill 0 is correctly blocked, but not flagged as a master-data / governance conflict.
- The PM4Py sketch is an audit dump with `...` placeholders. It does not learn handover cost, service/wait, roles, or similarity into weights. `filter_case_performance` is unused; `get_service_time` is not called with a real signature. Networks are discovered and then ignored by the policy.

Worker-facing text and the audit-indicator list are adequate and non-punitive. Those do not offset the R6 capacity bug, the missing baseline assignments, or the “learn from the log” gap. Under a hypercritical bar this is a complete, usable design with several material logical flaws, not a near-flawless one.
