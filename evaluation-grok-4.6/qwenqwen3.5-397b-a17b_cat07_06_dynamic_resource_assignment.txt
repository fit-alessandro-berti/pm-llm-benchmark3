The write-up has the right skeleton: all seven required headings, a multi-objective framing, a scored policy, a full C001–C016 table, a baseline comparison, a non-punitive worker note, audit metrics, and a PM4Py-shaped sketch. That is why this is not a low score. It is not a high score because the *assignments and constraint reasoning*—the actual test—contain material errors, one of them a hard-constraint violation the text then mis-justifies.

**Constraint failure on C012.** Constraint 4 allows overtime-risk `high` resources only when the case is *urgent* and no safer alternative exists. C012 is low priority, 48h remaining, and already owned by R10 (Quality 3, OT low, lightest load). Sending it to R2 (Quality 1, 12 open cases, 4.0h left, OT high) with R10 supervising is the opposite of the rule. Calling this “justified by low priority” inverts the exception. Constraint 3’s 20% training target is explicitly conditional on acceptable SLA/OT risk; it does not license breaking constraint 4. A legal second training case existed: C003 is normal-priority Diagnosis and R1’s documented training skill. Using the illegal C012 to hit 22.2% makes the quota claim cosmetic.

**Capacity and bottleneck.** R3 is given four cases (the batch cap) including C001 (urgent Security, 3h, complexity 0.88, skill 1 + supervision) and C007 (high Rework, 6h, complexity 0.81, reassignment 3). R3 has 6.0h left. Those two clocks alone do not fit, before C003 and C014. Historical evidence that a weak-skill owner adds ~3.3h of service time is ignored against a 3h SLA on an enterprise Security Review that “needs specialist.” Constraint 5 *prefers* continuity when supervision is feasible, so R3+R7 is arguable on paper; it is still a poor SLA/safety choice and is not stress-tested. C014 is worse: R8 is Rework 3, OT low, and not saturated, yet the case is dumped on already-capped R3. That is not resilience or bottleneck avoidance; it creates the specialist queue the essay warns about.

**Policy design is incomplete relative to its own checklist.** Eligibility, scores, and a 4-assignment cap are present, but hours remaining are treated as a zero-check rather than a feasibility constraint; SLA scoring is a coarse “<4h” bump; tie-breaks are “highest S”; supervision capacity (R7 also owns C008) is not modeled; Quality batching is allowed for both C006 and C012 and then split. Resilience is a 10-point OT penalty, not a load/skill-concentration rule—and the recommended batch violates the spirit of that term.

**Baselines are only qualitative.** First-available vs specialist-only is compared on the right axes and correctly notes skill-blind vs bottleneck failure modes. The answer never actually runs those two policies on C001–C016, so the comparison cannot be audited against the same queue. That is a missing deliverable, not a polish issue.

**PM4Py sketch is thinner and less accurate than required.** Useful calls appear (`format_dataframe`, handover SNA, roles, similarity, waiting-time insert, event-attribute filter, batches). The question also asked for working-together networks, `get_service_time`, performance DFGs, variants, and case-attribute filtering; those are absent. `filter_case_performance(..., 'sla_violation')` is not the PM4Py API (it is min/max throughput time). The object is treated as both a DataFrame and an event log (`log['org:resource']`, `len(log)`). The sketch audits a labeled `assignment_reason`; it does not show learning weights, handover-cost bands, or skill-mismatch from the log. Fine as a doodle; not a credible implementation sketch.

**What still works.** Hard/soft constraints are separated in §1. Security skill-0 candidates (Eva/Jonas on C015) are correctly rejected. R4/R9 are mostly protected from new OT. C002/C005/C008/C011/C016 are sensible. The worker explanation is the strongest section: concrete, non-algorithmic, and not punitive. Audit indicators map to the required list and to the log columns.

**Net.** This is a competent policy *template* with several correct local calls and a clear explainability story. Under a strict reading it fails the job it sets itself: implement the constraints on this queue, reason honestly about trade-offs, and show process-mining methods that would actually run. One inverted hard constraint, an infeasible R3 bundle, a missed legal training assignment, no concrete baselines, and a faulty PM4Py fragment keep it at the middle of the scale—not near-flawless.
