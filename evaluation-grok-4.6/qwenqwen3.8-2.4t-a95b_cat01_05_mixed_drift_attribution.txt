**7.1**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured drift diagnosis that hits the evaluation emphasis: March is treated as semantic/provenance drift without erasing later real change; rework is tied to Supplier Z; queueing is tied to staffing; priority redistribution and seasonality are kept distinct; monitors are segmented rather than one-threshold. Format compliance is mostly exact (headings, tables, word-count bands, seven IF/THEN/BECAUSE rules, Part A/B code). Causal language is generally restrained.

It is not near-flawless.

**Quantitative error.** In §3 the December Manhattan DFG distance is given as 27. The supplied rates vs January are \((0,-1,-2,+9,+9,+10,-5)\), so \(\sum|d_i|=36\). Euclidean 17.1 is correct; Manhattan is not. That is a required-deliverable arithmetic mistake, and it is inconsistent with the Part A code, which would print 36.

**PM4Py manual mismatch.** `pm4py.discover_dfg(log, variant=..., noise_threshold=...)` is the old algorithm API, not the current facade. The facade is `discover_dfg(...)` (frequency) and `discover_performance_dfg(...)`. Passing those kwargs would fail or be ignored. Defaulting monthly DFGs to a “performance” variant also mixes a temporal artifact into a structural comparison.

**Requirement miss.** §7 never fields a distinct temporal-profile monitor (pair-wise inter-activity mean/stdev), even though the spec lists it separately from queue waiting and service time. Queue/service are related but not the same artifact.

**Other defects.** The “data-driven” change-point rule hard-codes `2026-07-01`, so it imports the timeline instead of discovering it. Ranking a catch-all interaction first is weakly falsifiable. Cycle-time hour splits (e.g. “5–9h queueing”) are more precise than the aggregates support, even with range caveats. October-as-logging-change-point is under-identified: label count is flat from March, so the senior-assessment jump is at least as likely seasonal routing plus historically inconsistent recording.

Those are real flaws in quantitative reasoning, PM4Py fidelity, and monitor coverage. The rest of the analysis—timeline, raw vs mapped interpretation, competing hypotheses, discrimination table, and decision rules—is substantially better than those defects.
