**5.8**

The write-up has the right *shape* of a portfolio policy: stage gating, I-03 excluded, I-05 suppressed, I-07 treated as an evidence-changing re-alert, a reserved slot, explicit spillover costing, and coverage of feedback-loop and fairness risks. Under a strict decision-theoretic standard, that is not enough. The deployed rule and the live allocation do not implement each other, several numbers do not add up, and a few operational claims are unrealistic for hour-scale SLAs.

**Decision-rule vs allocation (largest defect).**  
Section 1 requires `Score > 0` after subtracting ~€2,400/slot of spillover, and it also requires the *lower-bound* ARR to yield non-negative net benefit. I-07 fails both if those words are taken seriously: midpoint net is about `0.26×€5,000 − €450 − €50 − €2,400 ≈ −€1,600`; lower-bound net is worse. I-09 is roughly break-even only at the midpoint and negative at the lower bound once spillover and the €800 false-alarm burden are included. Only I-04 clearly clears the robust bar. Selecting `{I-04, I-07, I-09}` therefore contradicts the rule that was just declared. The later “2× urgency weight” appears only in §4 and is not in the §1 score, so it cannot rescue the inconsistency.

**Inconsistent handling of second alerts.**  
I-02 and I-09 are structurally similar: Diagnosis Started, second alert, no prior swarm, no stated new event, `Δp` of 0.08 vs 0.07, 3.0 h vs 2.5 h to SLA. I-09 is swarmed; I-02 is waited. Section 4’s re-entry logic (`Δp > 0.05` and time-to-SLA `< 4 h`) would admit *both*. The allocation therefore is not an application of the temporal policy.

**Broken logical connective.**  
Re-entry is introduced as requiring “**both**” conditions and then specified as (new event) **or** (`Δp` and tight SLA). That is not a deployable rule.

**Arithmetic and scenario fidelity in §3.**  
Midpoint ARRs sum to `0.19+0.26+0.24 = 0.69` avoided breaches, but the Base row reports `0.4–0.6`. Optimistic “upper half” is treated as the range maxima in the worked value (`€24.4k`) while the breach count is a looser band. Adverse is supposed to zero *at least one* selected effect because the alert reason was wrong; the table compresses that into `0.1–0.3` without saying which case is assumed to be a null. False-alarm burden (`€2,850`) is used off-stage in the net narrative and is not auditable from the table columns. Spillover is flattened through an unexplained €20k average breach cost; congestion of 15–30 minutes in Adverse is not converted into additional harm beyond the 0.03 probability bump.

**Calibration is only half-applied.**  
Segment bias is added to `p_now` for a somewhat arbitrary `0.45` gate, but Strategic Enterprise *overprediction* is not used to shrink I-04/I-08 ARR, even though you cannot causally remove risk that is not there. SME underprediction is used rhetorically to help I-07, not symmetrically. The 0.45 threshold sits beside an effect-heterogeneous score without a derivation. Observational `p` is correctly *said* not to be a causal effect; the scoring still treats supplied ARR bands as if midpoint/lower-bound use were enough to make them decision-ready.

**Operational realism is thin.**  
A 24-hour cool-down on a book whose deadlines are 1.5–12 hours is almost a same-case lifetime ban, then immediately undone by exceptions. I-07 is still in *Triage* with 90 minutes left; a 45-minute swarm plus remaining diagnosis and resolution is not shown to be capable of converting ARR into an avoided breach. Two specialists delivering three sequential 45-minute swarms is a large withdrawal from ordinary diagnosis; sequencing, parallelism, and the fact that those specialists *are* the diagnosis queue are not analyzed. That undercuts the spillover discussion they otherwise take seriously.

**Fairness and monitoring are better, not tight.**  
§5 hits every required failure mode and proposes concrete controls (segment layers, shrinkage for NNL, holdout/DiD, untreated-rate cap). A 10–15% no-swarm holdout on contractual breaches is asserted without an ethical/operational constraint. Public-sector non-monetary duty is listed in §1 and then barely used (I-01 is waited with little discussion of obligation vs EV). In §6, “Brier degrades `> 0.05` → *lower* the intervention threshold” is the wrong direction if the model has become less trustworthy; the ARR-miss → raise-threshold trigger is more coherent. Utilization and PSI rules are plausible but generic.

**What still works.**  
I-03 is refused on stage, not on `p_now`. I-05 vs I-07 is the right qualitative distinction. I-06 is not spent on an imputed jump. One slot is reserved. The answer does not rank by `p_now` alone and does name queue externalities and label-feedback risk. That is why this is not a 3–4. It is a complete template whose live policy is not internally enforceable, which is disqualifying for a high score on a coherence-first rubric.
