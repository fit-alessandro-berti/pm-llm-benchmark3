**6.5**

The review of Models S and C is mostly strong: use cases are separated, metrics are not averaged, probes B1–B8 are reasoned from the expressions correctly, and the dissent is a real closed-world argument rather than a token objection. The grade is held down because the third alternative and its supporting code contain actual errors, undisclosed extra constraints, and overclaims. Under a strict reading, that is enough to keep the answer well below “nearly flawless.”

**Third-model language vs policy (real gaps, not style nits)**

The backbone `Register → AND(Credit, Technical) → optional correction loop → AND(optional Supplier, optional Expedite) → XOR(approvals) → Release` is a reasonable compact encoding of P1, P2, P4 (expedite after checks), P5, and “no recheck without correction.” It is safer than S and less enumerative than C. It is not the policy.

- Supplier Review is forced *after both checks*. P3/P4 do not require that. `Register, Supplier Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` is a plausible valid external case; S accepts it, the third model rejects it, and the audit never says so.
- Correction is pinned to a single slot between the initial checks and supplier/expedite. Traces such as correction after Expedite, or after Supplier Review, are excluded without discussion.
- B6 is **policy-dependent**, not valid. The tree treats it as ordinary accepted behavior. Section 8’s “flag as needs-attribute” is not implemented in `assess_case` or in the tree; that is an overclaim.

Those are unstated over- and under-approximations in a model offered for compliance.

**The post-hoc checker is incorrect**

`assess_case` is the only place P3/P6/P7 become attribute-aware, and it is not trustworthy.

- After one `Correct Dossier`, `seen_correction` is cleared on the first recheck. `…, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, …` is therefore marked P7-suspicious even though both rechecks are licensed by the same correction. That contradicts the tree, F6, and the stated P6 semantics.
- `labels.index(m)` raises `ValueError` if Expedite is present and a mandatory check is missing, so some of the clearest P2+P4 violations can crash instead of returning noncompliant.
- Later rules overwrite `status`. A P2 noncompliant case can be downgraded to `suspicious`.
- Expedite-before-checks is checked only against the *first* occurrence of each check, with no guard that the check exists.

A governance board cannot deploy this layer as written.

**`O(...)` and duplicate labels**

Using inclusive-OR for “repeat one or both checks” is the right idea, but:

- `parse_process_tree` / `O(...)` is version-dependent; the prompt’s POWL API was the robust way to encode this partial order, and it was ignored.
- Credit Check and Technical Review are duplicated by construction. Section 4 treats duplicate labels as a first-class defect of C; Section 7 does not apply that critique to `model_third` or discuss alignment tie-breaking on rework traces.

**Validation skeletons would not establish what they claim**

The evaluation *program* is conceptually right (seeds, negative suite, strata, no fabricated scores, reject-on-probe-failure). The code is not.

- `nt.append(dict(e))` is not a valid Event insertion.
- Sweeping `log_move_cost = model_move_cost ∈ {1,2,5,10}` does not test alignment-cost sensitivity; the ratio never changes, and those parameter keys are not the real PM4Py alignment API.
- Fitness on a four-trace negative log is not language membership and is not precision-against-negatives, which is the pathology Section 6 ranks first.

**Smaller inaccuracies**

- `Register, Supplier Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` is not “policy-ambiguous”; it is a P2 violation.
- Section 5 correctly notes alignment ≠ acceptance, then Section 9 still leans on alignment fitness as the negative-suite metric.
- Conservative extra orderings (supplier/correction placement) should have appeared in §7.5 limitations beside the missing attributes; they do not.

**What remains creditworthy**

Sections 1–6 and 10–11 are the high-quality part: S is unsafe because it optionalizes P2 and puts Expedite in the same AND as the checks; C’s 0.941 precision is consistent with variant memorization on a positive-only log; B2/B5/B8 are handled correctly; pathologies are ranked by decision impact; exploration vs enforcement is not collapsed; the dissent is within the word limit and would actually change the recommendation under named evidence.

Those strengths are why this is not a mid-fail. The third alternative is the core required deliverable, and it is not internally consistent enough to score higher.
