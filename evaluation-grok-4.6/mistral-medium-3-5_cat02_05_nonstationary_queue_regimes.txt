5.2

The answer has the right skeleton and avoids the worst process-mining failure modes, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it is a competent qualitative memo sitting on top of inconsistent arithmetic, a non-reproducing code appendix, and several definitional slips.

**What is actually strong.** The eight required headings are present. The data audit correctly refuses to treat median/p90 service as flow time, flags overnight carry-over, same-day arrival/completion mismatch, unobserved 07:00 backlog, and non-independent days. Outage and recovery are separated from ordinary high-throughput operation, which the rubric explicitly rewards. Several Little’s Law windows are marked `not defensible`. The operational section names a pooled policy that is dangerous once segmented. The Python block at least attempts all seven mandated ingredients and does not pretend the daily table is a PM4Py event log.

**Numerical work is not coherent.** Pooled completions are given as 89.1 versus a true mean of 86.0 (2409/28). That error is used to claim the system is roughly balanced (`λ ≈ μ`). It is not: arrivals total 2519, completions 2409, a net +110 case accumulation. Mean WIP 28.5 should be ~29.9. Pre-change weekday arrivals are 101.3 versus 100.1 (and the stated range 96–107 omits Friday’s 92). The weekend column in §4 is internally impossible: staff 2.6 looks like all eight weekends, complex 22% looks like pre-mix weekends only, WIP 16 matches neither subset. A pooled-versus-segmented section that cannot keep its own denominators straight cannot be trusted.

**Burstiness statistics are wrong, not merely rounded.** For the four hourly vectors, sample Fano factors are about 3.23, 4.14, 10.12, and 1.29 — not 1.87, 2.11, 4.23, and 1.02. The bursty day is understated by a factor of two. CV figures are likewise off. The claimed lag-1 autocorrelation of −0.15 on 2026-04-15 arrivals is implausible for a unimodal arrival profile; the write-up appears to blend the outage in *completions* into an arrival-dispersion claim. Section 5 therefore does not do what it says it does.

**Narrative and code disagree.** `pd.cut(..., right=False)` labels 16–18 April as Recovery and leaves the last edge fragile; weekends are then overwritten. In the memo, recovery is only 16 April, 17–19 April are orphaned, and weekend post-mix starts 25 April. Little’s Law in the memo is computed from *arrival* means; the function defaults to `completed`. Anyone running §8 will not recover the tables in §§2, 4, or 6. That is a reproducibility failure, not a style issue.

**Little’s Law is conceptually sloppy even where the verdict column is reasonable.** Throughput-proxy language reverses the post-mix imbalance (`λ < μ` when arrivals exceed completions and backlog is growing). Several rows say the proxy is completions and then divide by the arrival mean. Post-mix is called “stable (but high backlog)” and scored `approximate only`; with a structural deficit of ~14 cases/weekday, unknown overnight inventory, and extreme intraday burstiness, the honest verdict is closer to `not defensible`. The 12-hour conversion is used without a clean statement of whether \(W\) is open-hours sojourn or calendar sojourn. Snapshot WIP *is* a discrete time average; calling it “not time-averaged” is incorrect.

**Segmentation and paradox claims are thinner than they look.** Primary regimes miss the obvious continuation of recovery on 17 April (112 completions, extra 6.5 FTE, WIP still 35) and the 18–19 April bridge before the Monday mix jump. Extra staffing on 16–17 April is not distinguished from the 29 April intervention. The “alternative” mostly relabels the same date cut as peak versus mix rather than offering a materially different partition (backlog state, weekday/weekend only, or staffing-based). The Simpson example is ordinary confounding/aggregation bias, not a sign reversal. Daily-sequence dependence is described anecdotally; there is no lag-1 or cumulative inventory path for the 28-day series, even though the +78 net during the post-mix week is the central carry-over fact.

**Smaller cuts that still matter at this bar.** Greek letters are stripped to empty `()`, so intensity statements are hard to parse. Assumptions (open-hours clock, snapshot ≈ \(L\), mix shift vs coding change) are only partly labeled. Utilization is never bounded from service minutes × throughput / FTE-minutes, which would have shown post-mix capacity pressure more rigorously than WIP/FTE. The CUSUM heuristic is dominated by weekend seasonality and is not interpreted.

The qualitative operating story — weekday/weekend, outage ≠ recovery, mix/peak shift, intervention, pooling hides pressure — is the reason this is not a 3. The volume of incorrect figures, the false pooled-equilibrium claim, the Little’s Law proxy mix-up, and the non-reproducing appendix are why it cannot sit anywhere near the top of the scale.
