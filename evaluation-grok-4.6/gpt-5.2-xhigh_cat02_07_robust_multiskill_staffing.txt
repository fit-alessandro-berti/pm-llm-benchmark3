**5.4**

The write-up is well structured and hits the required nine headings, with a real offered-load calculation, an explicit three-policy comparison, and a robustness-first recommendation rather than “pooling wins on base-case mean delay.” That is the right shape for this task. It is not close to flawless. The quantitative core is internally inconsistent with the stated policies, mis-implements three of five shock scenarios, and then treats the resulting delays as if they measured policy quality.

## What is actually strong

Section 2’s mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74) and the 42-case day totals (Intake 291.5, Mechanical 475.8, Electrical 797.5, Software 494.7, Validation 661.1 → 2720.6) are auditable and correct under A1. Calendar capacity of 2670 minutes, including overtime as *maximum* availability, is also correct, and the conclusion that the system is already tight before multipliers/overheads is the right first-order fact.

Block staffing (480 / 600 / 540 / 570 / 300 / 150) matches shifts, breaks, Farah overtime into 12:00–12:30, and Ada/Ben/Chen overtime into 16:00–16:30. The late-day ratio >1 and the U1 morning-saturation story are the right bottleneck narrative. Objectives J1/J2, the specialization-vs-pooling tension, and the refusal to pick a policy solely on base mean delay all match the quality bar.

## Material errors that break the results

**1. Scenario transformations are wrong.** In `scenario_blocks`, operator precedence makes

`if scenario in ("U1","U5") and start in ("08:00","10:00") or (scenario in ("U1","U5") and start in ("10:00","12:00"))`

true for the **12:00–14:00** block as well. U1/U5 therefore inflate 08:00–14:00, not 08:00–12:00. The U2 condition likewise spills into **16:00–18:00**. Three of five uncertainty scenarios are not the scenarios in the prompt. Section 4’s U1/U2/U5 rows cannot be used as evidence.

**2. Policy text and code diverge on the thing the comparison is about.** SILO text gives Farah morning Mechanical/Software relief. `SpecializedFixed.HOME["Farah"] = set()` plus `allow_task` forbids her from every diagnostic. Hybrid text reserves Emil for Software, Deepa for Electrical, predicts P1 demand 30 minutes ahead, and caps family switches per hour. The code only implements “P1 anywhere, P2/P3 overflow if family backlog > 180.” The table is not a comparison of the policies in Section 3.

**3. Dispatch has an idle-capacity bug.** After a successful P1 start (or preemption), `dispatch` `return`s and never fills other idle resources at that timestamp. They wait for the next event. This systematically injects idle time, more so when several people are eligible, i.e. exactly when pooling/hybrid should help.

**4. Preemption corrupts the log and the remaining-time state.** The original interval is written with the full planned `end_min` and never closed. Remaining work is then logged again. Stale `done` events are skipped, so the engine does not double-advance the resource, but PM4Py service times *do* double-count. Remaining time is converted back through the current multiplier (`remaining / mult * 1.10`); if a later resource has a different speed, residual work is wrong. The “at most once” flag is on the victim case, which matches the prompt, but the 10% penalty is applied to a distorted remainder.

**5. Completions, utilization, and tails are dominated by calendar artifacts, not routing.** `can_start_now` refuses any start that cannot finish inside the current shift/break fragment. Long Software/Electrical draws (especially ×1.25–1.40) will not fit many windows, so resources idle beside a non-empty queue. Arrivals are day 0 only; the clock still runs overnight with zero night staff; incomplete cases at `days=2` are dropped from mean, p95, and SLA. Utilization uses two days of availability in the denominator. The reported means of ~530–830 minutes and p95s of ~1390–1816 minutes are mostly “wait until tomorrow / never finish,” which is why p95 barely moves across policies. That is not a useful tail comparison for 120-minute P1 targets.

**6. Parallel ME/ES vs PM4Py case waiting is ignored.** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is correctly described as a *case total*, but overlapping branches make summed service exceed sojourn, so `@@waiting_time` can be negative or meaningless. The requirement to compute stage-specific waits separately is stated in Section 7 and not done in Section 8 (no queue-entry timestamps are even emitted).

## Load analysis and table problems

The block ratios omit speed multipliers and skill-feasibility, so they are not skill-specific offered load. Electrical 797 minutes and Software 495 minutes cannot be spread across 2670 undifferentiated staff minutes; pre-12:00 Software is Farah (1.40) plus Chen (1.25), and post-16:00 Software is Emil only. That is discussed qualitatively and never turned into a bound, which is the actual bottleneck math the later scenarios need.

U5 is described as “>2× on base-minutes vs staff-minutes.” U1 scales morning load to about 1.6–1.7; a 30-minute Intake outage is not a doubling of all work. That sentence is false.

Section 4’s point estimates have no seed bands even though the code prints `mean±std`. Hybrid is said to “dominate” on worst-case P1, but the same table has SILO U1 P1 (0.83) above Hybrid U1 (0.80), and **no policy except SILO-base meets the 90% P1 target**. A robust recommendation that does not say “under this model the official P1 SLA is missed almost everywhere” is incomplete. J1 and J2 are never computed from the table.

Other modeling gaps that should have been labeled, not buried: handover/setup/coordination are all billed as *busy* time on the receiving resource; team-switch (+5) is only applied to the Mechanical–Electrical pair, not Intake-to-other-team; sequential ME/ES is forced onto the cross-skilled subset even when that subset is empty after Chen’s absence; `load_hint` is ready-queue minutes over `60 × n_resources`, unrelated to the block ratios in Section 2; within-priority scoring ignores positive slack, so urgency does not bite until the case is already late.

## What remains salvageable

Assumptions are labeled. Two genuine objectives exist, one tail/SLA and one max-min fair/robust. The three policies are specified in operational language, not slogans. Secondary effects (estimate gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, Validation migration, interpretability) are the right list. The monitoring schema is better than the simulator that is supposed to feed it. Section 9’s limits (correlated tails, non-Poisson bursts, overnight coverage, handover semantics) are honest, and the shadow-then-guardrailed pilot is a sensible deployment plan.

Those sections cannot rescue a comparison whose shocks are miscoded, whose SILO agent cannot use Farah, whose dispatcher parks idle staff, and whose delay/SLA/utilization statistics are horizon and calendar artifacts. Under a strict reading, this is a strong essay wrapped around an unreliable experiment.
