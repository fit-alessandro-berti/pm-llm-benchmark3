**7.1**

The final write-up has the right architecture and the right compliance spine. It uses the required six sections, applies the mandated conformance labels, puts A/B at the bottom, refuses to let a deadline forecast legalize an invalid approval path, keeps re-screening from substituting for second-officer review, and treats P-901, the API quota, and the S4 35-vs-65 split as first-class constraints. That is a strong answer on the dimensions the rubric actually cares about.

It is not close to flawless.

The largest analytical defect is false precision around Action C. C’s stated duration is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute, i.e. 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. The upper bound *is* the deadline. The table still prices C as if on-time execution were essentially assured (€10k–25k ≈ 0.55–0.75 × €20k–35k). That silently sets P(on-time | C) ≈ 1. The surrounding text never repairs the table. “Medium for timing” in the confidence column is a hedge, not a correction.

Related numeric sloppiness appears in the harm column. C is given “€0–30k direct customer harm,” which matches neither the prompt’s €15k–80k false-rejection/hold band nor a coherent expected-value derivation. The cell mixes expected values with ranges and treats some correct adverse outcomes as if they were customer harm. D’s “€5k–15k” is likewise invented without a transparent decomposition. The essay correctly says there is no single utility function, then fills the required table with pointy euro bands that the evidence does not support.

A few process claims are slightly too clever. The post-evidence Analyst Review prerequisite is a fair reading of Step 5, but once it is introduced it is not costed: if R17 still has to record that milestone, C’s 40–90 window shrinks and the already-optimistic deadline math gets worse. The suggestion that alternate screening plus a score drop below 40 could reopen Auto Clear is also too tidy. Auto Clear is specified as an early path after screening, not as a mid-cycle reset after Analyst Review has already been completed. S4’s review time is treated as the same 35–75-minute expedited band given only for S2, and the 65+40=105 ranking-change arithmetic depends on that unstated transfer.

E over F is defensible on reversibility and on F’s unproven rejection threshold, but the table’s own harm numbers do not support that order. E almost surely burns the €20k–35k deadline value *and* a prolonged-hold band; F’s false-rejection band is the same €15k–80k with no deadline-recovery option. Section 3 gestures at process risk rather than actually reconciling the ranks with the ranges.

Smaller presentation issues: the ranked artifact is C alone, while the real recommendation is a C+parallel-D composite with a cutoff fallback. The task asked for sequence, concurrency, and fallback to be explicit in the ranking, not only in §6. Section 2 covers the top five and is directionally right, but it does not systematically separate, for each row, what the process would permit, the likely continuation, P-884 benefit, P-884 harm, spillover, compliance/non-monetary risk, and intervention uncertainty.

None of this overturns the core judgment. A/B are correctly forbidden, G is correctly parked as an unenabled exception, second review remains mandatory, and the S2/S4/P-901 discussion is the right one. The answer is good expert work with several avoidable inaccuracies. Under a standard that treats minor defects as expensive, that is a mid-7, not an 8+.
