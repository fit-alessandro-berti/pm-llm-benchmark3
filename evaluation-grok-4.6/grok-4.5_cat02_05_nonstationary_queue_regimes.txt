6.4

The write-up is conceptually strong and format-compliant, but it is not close to flawless. Several quantitative claims are wrong, the primary table is partly unreadable, and the narrative is not reconciled with the code that was supposed to generate it.

**What is actually good.** Estimands and threats in §1 are the right ones: opening-hours clock, service ≠ sojourn, unmatched arrivals/completions, overnight carry-over, endogenous FTE, and mix-vs-coding ambiguity. Recovery (R3) is correctly treated as backlog burn-down, not a high-capacity operating point. Little’s Law is mostly handled with the right caveats (boundary terms, throughput proxy, opening-hours vs calendar sojourn, no substitution of median service for \(W\)). The alternative inventory/drift segmentation is a real alternative, not a relabeling. The pooled-vs-segmented story (weekday/weekend scale, mix shift, outage/recovery “false hero,” flat output-per-FTE with diverging WIP) is the correct operational moral. The Python block meets the mechanical spec.

**Material numerical errors.** These are not rounding nits.

- R1 is **7 weekdays** (6–10, 13–14 Apr), not 8. `mean_wip` median is **20**, not 20.5. The 20.5/8.4 → 146 min figure is therefore built on a miscounted sample. Apr 6–14 is 9 calendar days; subtracting only one weekend day is the likely source.
- Pooled means are wrong: arrivals **90.0** not 91.3; completed **86.0** not 88.0; `staff_fte` **5.14** not 5.3; `mean_wip` **29.9** not 30.4; `complex_pct` **32.9** not 33.4. Section 4 cites means while the code prints medians — the write-up was not generated from the script.
- R4 volume totals are substantially wrong: arrivals **798** not 733, completed **704** not 649. The ratio ~0.89 happens to be close, which conceals the error rather than excusing it.
- Smaller table errors: R3 \(\lambda\) median is ~8.8 not 9.0; R6 \(\lambda\) median ~3.71 not 3.6; R6 p90 median 68 not 69.5; R6 `complex_pct` median 31.5 not 32.5; R1 completion-rate median ~8.33 not 8.4.

A quantitative regime analysis that asks for reproducible code and then publishes hand-waved numbers the code would have contradicted is a serious defect.

**Internal inconsistencies.** The code’s `primary_regime` assigns 7 days to R1; the narrative table says 8. Alternative “S-drain” is said to include 29 Apr–1 May, but the stated rule / Python (`delta >= 10` and `mean_wip >= 35`) puts those three days in `S_high_wip_other` (deltas 5, 6, 7). The alternative is therefore not operationalized as described.

**Broken cells in the main segmentation table.** Lost arrows turn interpretations into fake numbers: “WIP falls 5235”, “WIP 433632”, “median 3329; p90 7260”. In a required comparison table that is not a cosmetic issue.

**Hourly analysis is slightly sloppy.** Bins are 07–08,…,18–19, so Apr 21 spikes are **10–11 (34), 13–14 (24), 16–17 (14)**, not “09–10 and 13–14.” Fano/CV for Apr 7 and Apr 21 are in the right ballpark (sample \(I \approx 3.23\) and \(\approx 10.1\)) but the reported 27.7 / 9.9 were not computed carefully. The four dated vectors **sum to the daily arrivals**, yet `peak_08_10_pct` does not match bins 08–09+09–10 (e.g. Apr 7: 16.8% vs 31%; Apr 21: 9.6% vs 47%). That discrepancy is in the prompt, but a careful audit should have flagged it instead of treating both sources as interchangeable facts.

**Softer theoretical nits.** Calling \(L/\mu \approx 5.2\) h in R4 a “lower-bound” on sojourn is not justified; finite-horizon Little’s Law has boundary corrections, not a signed bound. Lag-1 WIP dependence is asserted as “~0.7–0.9 qualitatively” rather than reported from the code. Causal credit for R5 (“+1 FTE flipped drift”) is stronger than the identification allows. `include_groups=False` is pandas-version-fragile.

**Net.** This is a competent process-mining essay with the right structure and the right qualitative conclusions, undermined by avoidable arithmetic, unreconciled code, a garbled primary table, and a non-matching alternative rule. Under a strict bar that reserves high scores for nearly exact work, that is mid-6, not 8+.
