6.5

The submission is a serious, well-structured compliance opinion. It follows the required headings, separates facts from assumptions, refuses a single total ordering, and generally respects pre-commitment timing, non-curative late approval, and contemporaneous evidence. It is not nearly flawless. Several process and temporal errors are material on this rubric.

**What is strong**

Section 1 is the best part of the paper. Authority is treated as issue-specific. ECD-2 is confined to what it actually displaces. The CRO/A7 gap is handled carefully, including the incomplete organizational fact that signed directives have been used as overlays except in new-supplier Compliance cases. A6 is applied as a real constraint rather than a slogan. The four-way classification definitions are usable.

The hierarchy table is dimension-specific in the way the prompt asked for. The rule catalogue and the `normally` / `unless` / `overrides_for` fragment are in the right logical register. Section 5 is genuinely useful: the alternatives name the premise that would have to change. Sections 6 and 7 are operationally concrete, and the 72-hour anti-normalization clause is a real safeguard rather than decoration.

**Material defects**

1. *Later-in-time is stated and then under-applied.* D1 is an email sent 15 July 07:20. P-502 has no calendar date, but it sits between a 14 July morning file and a 15 July 10:40 file, and its midday PO/recovery timestamps line up with ON-44. The opinion treats D1 as a live Finance substitute for P-502 without asking whether the email existed at commitment. That is exactly the error the time instruction was written to catch. A disciplined opinion would have branched: if 14 July, D1 is unavailable; if after 15 July 07:20, D1 is at most a Finance-dimension instruction.

2. *B4 is misread as a single bundled permission.* B4 grants two independent emergency allowances: commitment may precede the PO, **and** an absent Plant Manager may be replaced by an actively delegated Shift Lead. P-501’s four-hour stoppage can justify the first without the second. The assessment treats missing delegation as if it also defeated commitment-before-PO, then lists “Commit before PO absent an applicable ECD overlay” as a deviation even while conceding B4 can allow that sequence. ECD is irrelevant to an 08:50 act; B4 is the only candidate overlay. The Plant Manager being reachable still kills the substitution route, and post-commitment Finance still fails B5/B2. The classification can survive. The reasoning does not.

3. *Burden of proof and classification are not stable.* Assumption 9 puts the burden on the organization to prove exception, overlay, and pre-commitment approval. That should push unresolved budget-owner status toward NONCOMPLIANT, not toward open questions. P-507 is failed on the independent-budget-owner half of A2 even after D1 is allowed to carry Finance. P-505 has the same A2 gap on **both** timing forks: if commitment is after 18:00, D1 is dead and A2/B2 fail outright; if it is before 18:00, D1 still does not, on the paper’s own reading, create an independent budget owner. INDETERMINATE is defensible for the ECD-expiry fact. It is not defensible as the primary label once the opinion has already decided that A2’s budget-owner limb is non-waived and unproven. Secondary classifications were invited and would have solved this. None appear.

4. *P-504 gets the local threshold wrong.* EUR 24,000 is below B2’s EUR 25,000 trigger. “No Plant or Finance approval if B2 applies” is hedge language over a rule that does not fire. The file is still NONCOMPLIANT on B3, B1, failed B4, and out-of-scope ECD use. The threshold slip is small and unnecessary. The opinion also never decides whether “unapproved supplier” is GPIP A3’s “new supplier.” That distinction is load-bearing for P-504 and should have been an explicit interpretive choice.

**Smaller defects**

The defeasible fragment omits A1, B4, B6, C3’s four-hour record, and D2, so the formal layer is thinner than the prose. ON-44 is correctly treated as evidential rather than normative, but it is not applied systematically to the 14 July timestamps. P-507’s signed 12:40 PDF is, under the paper’s own evidence rule, better than the 14:30 ERP row; listing the ERP lateness as a live C3 deviation fights that rule. Seven of eight primaries are NONCOMPLIANT. That can be derived from the residual-controls definition of JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, but the prompt warned against flattening every deviation into one bucket. Mixed files (P-507, P-508) needed a secondary label.

**Bottom line**

The architecture is well above average. The case calls are mostly in the right direction. The paper still breaks its own method on time (D1), on local emergency mechanics (B4), and on how unproven A2 facts map to labels. Those are not nits on this assignment. A near-flawless opinion would have branched D1 from the moment of sending, split B4, and kept P-505/P-507 on one burden-of-proof line.
