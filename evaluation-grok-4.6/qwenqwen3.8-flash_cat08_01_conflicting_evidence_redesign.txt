**7.6**

The final write-up is strong on format, artifact coverage, segment-aware trade-offs, and experimental discipline, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several reasoning and grounding defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What it does well.** The diagnosis actually uses the artifacts instead of gesturing at them: frequency DFG volumes, performance-DFG means/medians/p90s, fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces, alignment deviations, low precision, rework rates (14.1% vs 3.3%, 26% repeat evidence, 12.5% vs 4.0%), the 31%→9% cost contradiction, segment medians/complaint rates, and the resource constraints (252 h/week, 82% queue threshold, fraud/escalation scarcity, underused QC, EUR 95 vs EUR 410). It does not treat the slow path as globally better, does not optimize only the mean, and does not fake ROI or Pareto dominance. A/B/C are different mechanisms (eligibility routing, evidence co-production, fulfillment/reopen control), each with targets, a causal story, and explicit sacrifices. The experiment plan is the best section: shadow mode, holdout, stop/go ranges, and an explicit test for improvement that is only a downstream shift (post-close complaints, cost-after-rework, displaced queues, re-run of conformance).

**Material reasoning flaw.** Redesign A repeatedly claims that running manual review in parallel with evidence collection will attack `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` (mean 34 h / p90 88 h) and make high-value cases faster than today’s manual/evidence loops. That edge is largely **customer wait plus re-queue**, not sequential analyst processing. If dispatch is held until evidence is complete (as R2 requires for non-Premium-low-risk), starting the reviewer earlier does not shrink calendar time to a compliant dispatch. That is a process-mining mistake, and it is load-bearing for A’s cycle-time story.

**Other defects.**
- **A bleeds into B.** A’s Lane A4 and “parallel evidence” are already B’s core. The three designs remain different in emphasis, but A is not a cleanly independent intervention.
- **Invented controls presented as if given.** Digital-literacy capture, firmware fields, a reopen root-code taxonomy, and an 80–85% utilization cap for quality inspectors (the 82% figure is only for manual eligibility) are not in the prompt.
- **C misses an obvious displacement.** Moving the two underused inspectors onto a pre-dispatch gate can starve post-repair quality check, which is exactly where high-value rework is 12.5%. Failure modes mention a gate queue, not loss of repair QC.
- **Selective “fast path is modest.”** Happy-path edge p90s are modest; `Fast approval/Dispatch → complaint` p90s are 150–180 h. The diagnosis later covers this, but the early claim is too clean.
- **60–90 day observation window** is far longer than the observed complaint tails (hours, not months). Conservative is fine; it is not derived from the performance DFG.

The piece is defensible, specific, and usable as a sponsor briefing. The parallelization error, A/B overlap, and a few ungrounded operational details are enough, under the required strictness, to block a high-8 or 9.
