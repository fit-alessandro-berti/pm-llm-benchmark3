**6.8**

The investigation is substantively strong on the hard parts—event-time roles, alias normalization, delegation conjuncts, RR5 attribution, and RR6 discipline—but it is not nearly flawless. Several factual slips and check-logic defects would change automated flags or misstate the evidence. Under a strict reading those are enough to keep the score well below the top band.

**What is done well.** Event-time reasoning is mostly careful. Eva is not treated as QM because the current master says Quality Engineering; CHG-507 is correctly unauthorized. `b.koch`/`u_bk` are merged before RR1, so CHG-508 is caught. D-IC-04 is void, so CHG-510 is unauthorized on both authority and SoD. D-QM-01 is dated from the signed PDF, not the later migration `created_at`. `svc_qms` is left unattributed on CHG-503 and only manually bound to Eva on CHG-509. Absence, VPN presence, and weekend-as-absence are treated as conditions at the timestamp, not as global role facts. Section 4 generally respects RR6: frequency is an indicator, and each signal has a benign and an adverse reading plus discriminating evidence. Controls in §6 match the actual failure modes.

**Material reasoning errors.**

- **Weekday calendar is internally inconsistent.** The log states 2026-08-15/16 are weekend, so 11 Aug is Tuesday. Section 2 still writes “approve Mon 2026-08-11 … day 1 = Tue 12, day 2 = Wed 13.” That cannot be true at the same time as Sat–Sun 15–16. The deadline date of 13 Aug happens to survive under exclusive +2 business days from a Tuesday, so CHG-505 is still late, but the worked example is wrong.
- **The “inclusive count could be on-time” claim is false.** Inclusive counting of the approval day *shortens* the window. Friday 14 Aug is late under both inclusive and exclusive readings. The only on-time path offered (“if 12th holiday”) contradicts the stated calendar (no holiday).
- **Section 4 misidentifies the Clara→Ben pair.** CHG-505 and CHG-510 were not Ben approvals (Farid / Clara); CHG-508 is Ben drafting a LOW training change. In this log the pair is CHG-501, 503, 506 only. That undercuts the network interpretation the judge is scoring.
- **CHG-505 retrospective authority is under-specified.** If D-QM-01 is only “production-equipment” QM *approval*, Ben’s cooling-system retro is out of scope, so RR4 fails on authority, not merely lateness. CHG-510’s retro has the same scope problem and is essentially skipped as “moot.”
- **Executive arithmetic is sloppy.** “Three” genuine authority failures then lists 507, 510, and 502/508 (four cases). 30/30/20 leaves an unexplained remainder and underweights the late/mis-scoped retro.

**Case-table defects (smaller but real).** CHG-506’s authority cell is contradictory/garbled (“if actor Ben → no valid authority” vs “delegation technically still valid”). Dual labels are used more often than the “one primary” rule implies. CHG-501’s AUTHORIZED/VALID_SUBSTITUTION blend is defensible; treating CHG-505 as a clean VALID_SUBSTITUTION plus a side “late” flag is less so.

**Section 5 would not implement the narrative.** This is the largest quality gap relative to the rubric.

1. `emergency` is never coerced. After `read_csv`, values are `'true'`/`'false'` strings, but `approval_authority` tests `row['emergency']==True`. Farid’s CHG-505 approval therefore becomes `Invalid_IC` in code while §3 calls it a valid IC substitution.
2. Unattributed `svc_qms` on CHG-503 is left as a distinct `human_for_sod`. `filter_four_eyes_principle` then treats Diego ≠ `svc_qms` as **compliant**. The write-up says RR2 cannot be proved; the check would print a pass.
3. `pm4py.filter_activity_done_different_resources` takes **one** activity (repeated resource use). Passing Draft and Approve is the wrong arity; the `try/except` hides the failure instead of performing the intended inspection.
4. `discover_handover_of_work_network` / `discover_working_together_network` typically return a graph/SNA object, not a dict. `handover.items()` will usually error into the except path, so the required network evidence is not actually produced.
5. `ben_sick` and most of `role_intervals` are unused. Sick-leave use, retro scope, and principal-absence edge cases remain comments, not interval checks. `Complaint cases` is a typo.

So the pandas/PM4Py artifact does reconstruct the CSV, `format_dataframe`, normalize aliases, and attempt four-eyes and interval flags—but it would emit **wrong authority and SoD results** on two of the most important cases and would often emit no network output at all.

**Net.** This is a competent forensic memo with the right structure and the right instincts on identity, stale master data, and collusion-versus-proof. It is not a 9–10: the calendar example is inconsistent, one alternative explanation is backwards, the pair list is factually wrong, and the required executable checks contradict the findings they are supposed to support. Those are not cosmetic issues on this rubric.
