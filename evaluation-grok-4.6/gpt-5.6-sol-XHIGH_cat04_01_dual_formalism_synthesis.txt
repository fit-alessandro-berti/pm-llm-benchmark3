## Local overclaims and fuzzy semantics

- Section 6 calls the two initial streams “exact” in the tree. Locally the two sequences are fine; globally they are not, because their AND-join is the very barrier that rejects A5. The next row admits the overconstraint, but the first row is still too clean.
- Assumption 8 says “may overlap” is true concurrency. For the process tree that is too strong: PM4Py trees give interleaving of completion events, not a true-concurrency semantics. Section 4 later retreats to “incomparability permits either order and does not require overlap,” which is the better statement and does not sit cleanly with assumption 8.
- A1 treats `ET, SR, IT` as proof that supervisor review occurred “during the trial interval.” That over-reads a completion sequence, and it contradicts the answer’s own warning that completion logs cannot establish interval overlap.
- D9 and D12 force XOR/optionality into a source/target precedence table. D12 has an empty source. The relations are understandable, but the specification table is sloppy exactly where choice and optionality needed to be crisp.

None of these break the models. All of them make the prose less precise than it pretends to be.

## The tree policy is defensible, not fully pressure-tested

Calling the tree a conservative under-approximation without label duplication is the right policy, and delaying reviews/supplier until after `{PV, DC}` while keeping review/trial overlap is a reasonable trade. What is missing is a proof, or even a careful language inclusion argument, that every tree trace is intended. The text asserts `L(tree) ⊆ L(intended)` and then lists the lost early-review/early-supplier behavior. That is plausible, but it is asserted, not shown. A hypercritical reading should not accept “we chose a subset” as a completed semantic argument.

A second unexamined point: the same barrier also blocks reviews and supplier from overlapping `Contain Material`. The write-up focuses on planning/design. That is the more important loss, but it is not the only one.
