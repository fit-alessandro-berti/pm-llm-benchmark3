7.8

The final write-up is a strong independent review: it uses the required headings, argues both sides with real concessions, traces claims to cases and sources, and draws a non-splitting line on authority and expiry. It is not nearly flawless. Several analytic slips and one structural POWL error keep it well below a top band under strict grading.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1’s CS-9 vs M0 point is the best insight in the piece: M0 forces `Quality_Review` before *any* decision, while CS-9 only requires it before *activation*. That correctly reframes SUP-709. The burden rules (temporal validity, exception claimant’s burden, known logging defects vs missing human records) are coherent and then used. The briefs are not straw men: prosecution’s normalization-of-deviance chain (704→705, 14 ineligible, 6 August cases) and defense’s 702/703 identical-trace argument are both load-bearing. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource/CoI failures. Verdicts are mostly earned: 701 compliant; 703/708 critical; 704 justified; 705 unauthorized copy of an expired overlay; 706 indeterminate on a mutable CRM link; 707 a model gap; 714 post-window auto-approve. Layers 2–5 plus the event schema (stable `notification_id`, frozen eligibility snapshots, canonical actors, overlay versions, dual timestamps) are the right place to put rules POWL cannot express.

**Section 1 pre-judges the dispute.** The ledger marks P3/P5 (“PD-3 is a valid time-boxed exception”; in-window eligible use is justified) as **Supported** before the briefs run. That collapses “stipulated vs disputed” into the Section 5 ruling and weakens dialectical sequence. PD-3’s force is the central contested authority question; it should stay disputed until judgment.

**M1’s XOR is too early (the main model-semantics miss).** M0 is `checks → XOR(approve, reject)`. M1 is `XOR(standard[checks→manual→activate], auto, emergency, exit[optional sanctions→reject/withdraw])`. There is no legal path that completes sanctions **and** quality and then rejects. CS-9 and M0 both allow that; a quality-fail after full review is inexpressible. Request-more-info then reject is likewise inexpressible because RMI lives only inside the path that must activate. The task required stating awkward approximations rather than hiding them; this limitation is not stated. That is a real regression from M0 on rejection, not a cosmetic extra.

Related control-flow nits: (1) auto-route forces `Quality_Review` *after* `Activate_Supplier`, but PD-3 only *permits* deferral (“may occur up to two business days after”), so auto-approve after a completed quality review does not fit standard (needs manual approval) or auto; (2) `exit_branch` can skip `Sanctions_Check` entirely via silent skip, which is looser than an abbreviated pre-screen; (3) `Master_Data_Validation`, `Reactivation_Assessment`, `Withdraw_Supplier`, and `Transaction_Monitoring` are new labels, only partly covered by the legacy-allowance notes; (4) the 12-month reactivation staleness rule is invented, not in CS-9.

**Classification and counting slips.** SUP-713 is conduct-equivalent with a late mapping; `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` is a strained bucket (label/data mapping is closer). SUP-715 as `NONCOMPLIANT` is internally consistent with the disclosure-duty rule, but the record only says no disclosure was *recorded* and no rule bars the relationship—confidence “medium” is right, the hard label is aggressive. Aggregate “~25 genuine noncompliance” / “six named cases + 14 + 6 + 9 unresolved” double-counts 703/714 and treats 9 missing-eligibility cases as if they were findings. Minor, but it overstates residual noncompliance, which is exactly the metric the essay otherwise warns against.

**Net.** Analysis of authority, expiry, scope, logging, and SoD is well above typical; the architecture and data proposal match the problem. Strictly, the undisclosed reject-after-checks hole, Section 1’s premature verdicts, and several classification/counting nits prevent a high score.
