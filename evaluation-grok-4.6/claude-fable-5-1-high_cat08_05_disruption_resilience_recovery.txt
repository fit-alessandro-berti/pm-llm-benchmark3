**7.6**

The answer is comprehensive, well structured, and clearly aligned with the required sections and judging criteria. It uses the DFGs, phase metrics, conformance findings, and A–L patterns as evidence rather than as decoration, and it consistently separates mission-preserving adaptation from control collapse. That is not enough for a high score under a strict standard: several quantitative claims are wrong or over-precise, a few interpretations fight the rest of the analysis, and some temporal points a process-mining answer should flag are missing.

**What it gets right**

The T0–T4 narrative is the strongest part. Phase volumes, fitness/fitting-trace figures, and the hospital vs pharmacy/bulk split are used to show that cycle-time recovery is not control recovery. The T3 “recovery relaxation” point (more absolute traceability exceptions than T2 because volume rose while controls were still weak) is a genuine temporal inference. Excess-gap arithmetic vs baseline rates on T2–T4 volume is directionally right. The table for A–L is complete, and keep/modify/retire decisions generally follow the evidence: B, I, J, K as resilience mechanisms; C and high-risk G as shortcuts; D/E/H as scope- and staleness-dependent. The four-question test in section 3 (purpose, contemporaneous evidence, declared bounds, reconcilability) is the right distinction, and the abductive claim that C and G appeared because B and F saturated is supported by the T2 frequencies (2,500 vs 1,600; 1,650 vs 1,800). After-the-fact audit remediation is correctly treated as not equivalent to real-time compliance. The recovery model, indicator set, and management paragraph match the constraints (do not punish every deviation; do not count throughput without controls; do not ignore the hidden tail).

**Material inaccuracies and logic problems**

1. **Authorization coverage is overstated in a way that contradicts the rest of the paper.** Section 5 says T2 authorization was “69% of triaged (target 100%).” That is (2,500 + 1,000) / 5,100: manual check plus the hospital fast lane. Section 2–3 already argue that A sometimes skipped authorization. Non-skip is not an authorization step. Coverage that is actually evidenced is 2,500 / 5,100 ≈ 49%, with another 1,000 in an ambiguous lane. The indicator therefore understates the gap the answer elsewhere treats as central.

2. **The dual-control counterfactual is invented, and 2.9% is treated as if it were F’s failure rate among controlled orders.** “Held to 2.9% rather than the ~30% it would have been if nothing were done” has no basis in the DFG or the metrics. Dual-control missing is an all-case exception rate (baseline 0.2% → T2 2.9%). If controlled items are a small share of volume, 2.9% of all cases can mean a large share of controlled orders lacked dual control; the opposite reading (97.1% fine) is the one the text implies. The ~390 excess count is a reasonable translation of the rate; the “held to 2.9% vs ~30%” sentence is not.

3. **T2 cycle times are reported as if they were observed inside T2.** Day 0 afternoon–Day 2 is ~60 hours; wholesale-bulk median 96h and p90 180h cannot occur inside that window. The answer correctly notes ~3,000 in-flight cases, then still treats those cycle times as T2 operating performance without right-censoring, completion-time spillover into T3/T4, or a definition of the metric. That is a temporal-reasoning miss on a temporal-reasoning task.

4. **T3 is called “arguably more dangerous than pure manual.”** Exception *rates* improved (traceability 9.5% → 5.4%, cold-chain 4.8% → 2.3%, controlled 2.9% → 1.4%, fitness 0.71 → 0.82). Higher absolute exception counts are a volume effect, not proof of a more dangerous mode. Unreliable sync creating false confidence is a fair hypothesis; it is not established by the data given.

5. **Excess exception totals are presented as “regulatory-grade” headcounts without mix or metric caveats.** ~1,500 / ~640 / ~390 assume constant case mix and that each percentage is a clean count of distinct affected shipments. T2 completed work is hospital-heavy; pharmacy/bulk were deferred. Mix shift can inflate or deflate those totals. Useful as order-of-magnitude illustration; oversold as counts a board should treat as exact.

**Smaller defects**

- T0 “~88% on the nine-step path” uses invoice-close / 42,000, which mixes WIP and cancellations with variant share; 91% fitting traces is the figure that actually measures conformance.
- Pack-rate “≈70% of normal” is closer to ~75% on the answer’s own 2.5-day assumption (3,600 packs / 2.5 vs ~1,910 baseline picks/day).
- T0 is given as 21 *business* days; T2–T4 daily rates treat calendar-style day counts as comparable without comment.
- DFG remainder is handled well in places (200 unresolved reconciliations, 400 PoD remediations, 200 open audits) and ignored in others (150 packs neither released nor dispatched; 220 snapshot checks with no outbound arc).
- “200 of 2,500 did not progress (held/lost)” over-interprets a missing arc as loss.
- L4 in 4.1 ends only when the remediation queue is empty; sections 5–6 then use a 15-day close target. Those are different policies.
- The process tree puts incident-level “Declare level” on the per-order path; `⟲(..., τ)` and the trailing `∧` of two queues plus Exit L4 are not clean POWL. The operators were defined, but the diagram is looser than the prose.
- Hospital stockpiling, skip “leaking” to non-ambient product, and several numeric thresholds in the redesign (8h TTL, 25% capacity share, 90-day whitelist) are design choices presented next to recovered facts without always marking the join.
- Calling the B-path a “controlled manual path” collides with “controlled-substance” terminology.

**Redesign and recommendations**

Sections 4–7 are usable management work: explicit triggers, hard product rules, whitelist as a *control* rather than a skip, contemporaneous evidence, split reconstruction vs unrecoverable-deviation queues, fairness scoring, and a decision paragraph that states what to accept, what not to accept, and what evidence would move the call. Residual-risk columns are honest. The quality of the redesign does not cancel the measurement errors above; it is why the score is not lower.

**Net**

This is a strong case write-up with a real analytical spine, not a generic resilience essay. Under a strict bar it is not near-flawless: it misstates T2 authorization coverage, fabricates a dual-control counterfactual, and skips the phase-window problem for cycle time. Those are not formatting nits; they affect the resilience vs noncompliance story. Score reflects complete structure and generally sound redesign, reduced substantially for those flaws and for over-precision in the excess-gap figures.
