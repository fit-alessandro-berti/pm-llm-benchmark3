**7.2**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the required seven-section format. It does the main intellectual work the prompt asks for: it separates mission-protecting adaptation from control erasure, keeps hidden T4 workload in view, and refuses to treat after-the-fact audit as real-time compliance. Those strengths are real. They are not enough for a high score under a strict reading, because the answer also makes avoidable factual and process-mining mistakes in the exact dimensions it is supposed to be judged on.

The most serious error is temporal. There is no partial-recovery (T3) frequency DFG in the source material—only baseline, T2, and T4. Section 1 nonetheless says the T3 DFG “shows” compliance holds at 1,300, manual-reservation reconciliation at 1,400, and repack/recheck at 900. Those frequencies belong to the **stabilization** DFG. That is not a rounding slip; it assigns later-period structure to an earlier window and then uses that invented structure as evidence of how recovery unfolded. For a temporal-reasoning task, that is a material defect.

A second process-mining misread sits in the A/C analysis. The T2 DFG does **not** merge the hospital-critical fast lane into “Skip system authorization check.” Emergency triage splits three ways: manual authorization (2,500), skip (1,600), and hospital-critical fast lane (1,000 → 920 into the snapshot check). The fast-lane path bypasses **both** authorization nodes. The answer replaces that cleaner reading with a looser claim that A “merged with skip.” It still reaches a defensible “modify A” conclusion, but it does so by misstating the graph.

Smaller accuracy and precision problems accumulate. Dual-control missing rose from 0.2% to 2.9%; calling F a reduction “relative to total volume” is softer than the aggregate evidence. “2,100 cases remain” in audit work slightly over-reads `Audit remediation → END: 2100`. Recall-sensitive product risk is named in the process context and then mostly dropped. Several recovery thresholds (30 minutes, 4-hour snapshot refresh, 48-hour/5-day reconciliation, 0.5% audit residual) are asserted as if they were implied by the log rather than designed. None of these sink the argument, but they keep it from being tight.

What the answer gets right should still be credited. T0–T4 metrics, T2 variant inventory, conformance scores, move-on-log/move-on-model deviations, and the T4 compliance tail are mostly used correctly. The A–L table is complete and, except for the A/C graph claim, judgmentally sound: B/I/K kept, C/G retired, L treated as debt. Section 3’s distinction between transformed controls and concealed noncompliance is the strongest part of the piece, and the fill-rate split (hospital 91% vs pharmacy 61%) is used abductively rather than as a vanity throughput win. The recovery model, indicator set, and management trade-off statement all hit the required elements and stay aligned with the prompt constraints.

The result is a capable advisory memo with one clear period-mix error and one clear DFG misread. That is well above a weak or generic answer, and well short of near-flawless.
