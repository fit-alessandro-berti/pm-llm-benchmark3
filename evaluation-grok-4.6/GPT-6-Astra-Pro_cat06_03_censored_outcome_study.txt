**7.4**

This is a strong, honest design that avoids the failure modes the prompt flags (training only on the 21,600 labeled rows, coding omitted cases as non-default, using post-decision fields, random splits). It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several target, timing, and validation problems are material, and the skeleton is less implementable than it appears.

## What is actually strong

The core statistical stance is correct. Activation is treated as selection, not as a missingness nuisance. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are never filled with `default_90d = 0`. The 48,000-row registry is the cohort, not the labeled export. Three clocks (prediction, label availability, event coverage) are kept distinct. The leakage table is the best section: `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, same-day `documents_total`, overwritten `risk_score`, extraction-time resource experience, and `label_available_at` are all handled with the right keep/repair/drop logic. The suffix-invariance idea is the right audit. The code’s `SAFE` column cut, `asof(cut)` rebuild, forbidden-column assert, and refusal to fit a six-class model on complete rows are aligned with the prose.

## Target definition: honest, but the estimand is not as clean as claimed

The primary target is a composite of two clocks, then presented as six mutually exclusive states resolved at \(H = S+90\). That is internally inconsistent. Rejection/withdrawal/transfer/unresolved can be determined by \(S+90\). Default among those who activated by \(H\) is determined at \(A+90\), which can be as late as \(S+180\), plus feed delay. The text later admits a 180-day window, so the authors know this, but the state table and the claim that the six probabilities sum to one still read as a single-time partition. They do not.

The 90-day application horizon is an extra design parameter, not implied by the warehouse target. It is labeled as an assumption, which helps, but it is not justified against the natural alternative: a semi-competing-risk model in which application exits compete continuously and default is defined only after activation, without snapping “eventually activated” into “activated by day 90.” Cases that activate on day 91 are forced into `unresolved90` even if their 90-day account outcome is later observed. That throws away labeled defaults/non-defaults for the sake of a hard cutoff.

The estimand \(p_j(h,\pi)\) is the right *kind* of object (policy-dependent, prefix-conditional, eligibility-conditional). It is underspecified on \(j\), on the time at which \(Z\) is fixed, and on how later landmarks change the population. “Eligible at \(\tau\)” is doing too much work. A \(k=3\) prediction is a selected subpopulation that has already survived to a third pre-decision event; that selection is stated, then not analyzed as selection.

The alternatives are well chosen. \(q\) is the honest activated-account target. The counterfactual “default if activated” is correctly labeled as non-identified. That part should remain.

## Cohort construction: good accounting, incomplete flow

The 18,900 / 2,700 / 9,100 / 5,700 / 2,900 / 5,100 / 3,600 split does sum to 48,000, and the table’s treatment column is conceptually right. Calling these snapshot categories rather than horizon states is an important correction.

It is still not a cohort-flow table. It does not start at 48,000 and pass through reconstructible starts, chronology/quarantine exclusions, landmark eligibility, maturity, and analysis subsets. The 3,600 unlabeled activations mix ordinary non-maturity with partner missing feeds; the design says the latter have no supplied count, then leaves them inside the same cell. Transfer linkage at 60% is handled cautiously in words (“do not stitch the linked subset into an enterprise cohort”) and then only as an empty `GROUP_MAP` in code.

Event coverage ending 31 December 2025 while extraction is 15 February 2026 is correctly described. That is one of the better as-of remarks in the answer.

## Censoring and missing labels: mostly right, with a factorization that is better than the 6-state story

The five-way distinction (right censoring vs delayed labels vs activation selection vs transfer vs permanent missing feeds) matches the prompt. Not treating competing exits as ordinary censoring is correct. Not treating “no feed by extraction” as survival-to-extraction is correct. The 10.7%–25.0% bound on the 25,200 activated accounts is a finite-population range, and they say so.

The factorized analysis (application-exit hazards × maturity-restricted default among activations) is more defensible than a six-class classifier, and it is the actual model they want. That means the six-state target in Section 1 is not the object Section 3 estimates. \(p_A(h)q(h)\) is a joint probability on a product space, not a cell in a single multinomial observed at \(H\).

Inverse-probability weighting for feed observation is proposed with the right untestable assumption (conditional independence of missingness and the unobserved default). Pattern-mixture odds multipliers (0.5/1/2/4) are scenarios, correctly labeled. What is missing is any diagnostic plan that could show those weights are unstable for partner-originated accounts, which the prompt says are systematically worse. “Report effective sample size” is named, not designed.

## Prefixes and features: sound rules, thin operationalization

Fixed-\(k\) vs elapsed-time landmarks, exact-\(k\) eligibility, not padding short traces into fake 3-event cases, keeping document loops, and treating event time as an availability approximation are all right. So is refusing `insert_case_service_waiting_time` when the log has no start/complete lifecycle.

The “canonical business-event vocabulary” is never specified. After a 15 October 2025 rename-and-duplicate migration, that vocabulary *is* the prefix definition. Leaving it as `ALIASES = {}` plus “print unique activities” defers the whole control-flow feature layer. Tied timestamps are quarantined; duplicates with offset timestamps after migration are not. Static attributes (`case:channel`, `case:region`, `case:requested_amount`) are discussed as needing provenance and then do not appear in the feature table. `risk_score` is a proposed prefix feature in Section 5 and a drop in the skeleton. That is conservative, but it means the implementation does not match the feature design.

WIP, queue load, and historical transition rates are correctly classified as as-of reference features. None are specified at a level that could be coded without new decisions (which population, which window, how to avoid same-day leakage).

## Validation: leakage-aware, then too austere to answer the default question

A random split is correctly rejected. The delayed-outcome backtest idea is right. The arithmetic that an October 2024 start needs until early June 2025 for \(90+90+35\) days is right. Reporting June / post-policy / post-migration slices is right. So is refusing to treat post-July 2025 as a fully mature default test.

The primary freeze date is still a poor choice for the stated target. Fitting on 1 October 2024 means the default *component* only has maturity support for starts through roughly late February 2024 — about two months, all pre-policy. The fully mature test slice is about 15 June–15 July 2025, i.e. one month, almost entirely still on the old policy. The frozen model therefore never learns the 1 July 2025 threshold change, while Section 5 correctly says a policy dummy cannot teach a regime that is absent from training. Rolling-origin refits are mentioned as a side analysis, not as a co-primary design. That leaves the lender’s actual early-warning model unevaluated under the policy that will be in force.

Validation is a single month of starts. Metrics mix factorized evaluation (good: CIF, weighted Brier, calibration of \(q\) and \(p_Aq\)) with “multiclass Brier/log loss adjusted for the remaining observation mechanism.” The adjustment is not defined. Partially observed activated cases (exit known, default unknown) are coarsened outcomes, not standard right-censoring. AUROC is acceptable for the activated default head; it should not be smuggled in as a headline for the pathway model.

## Limitations: adequate, not tight enough on decision use

The can/cannot list is directionally correct: historical-policy pathway probabilities, not counterfactual grant-time default, not enterprise follow-up from 60% linkage, not proven post-migration calibration. Deployment effects (threshold change, process intervention, transfer/workload, preventive servicing) meet the “at least three” bar.

The decision-use boundary is still soft. A six-state score that includes `unresolved90` and `rejected` will be gamed the moment it is used to reroute work; that Goodhart loop is only partly covered by “warnings trigger more reviews.” Shadow mode is recommended, but there is no rule for when a probability is allowed to affect approval, limit, or review intensity. Without that, Section 1’s claim of operational defensibility is overstated.

## Code skeleton: concrete, slightly overlong, and not as safe as the asserts imply

The skeleton does the required things: `read_csv` + timestamp parse, `format_dataframe`, as-of case table, temporal split by start time, `get_prefixes_from_log` with an explicit exact-\(k\) filter, no post-prefix columns in `X`, `discover_dfg` only as a training-prefix diagnostic, cohort prints, leakage asserts, and comments instead of a fake PM4Py survival API. That is well above a decorative import list.

Problems:

- Length is above the requested ~80–160 line band; the extra density is not extra coverage of time prefixes, static attributes, or mapping.
- The 24-hour path is implemented and never run.
- `assert len(master) == 48000` fights Section 2’s own quarantine rules.
- `pd.testing.assert_frame_equal` on the suffix test will likely fail on index names, NA vs 0 for resource changes, and simultaneous events even when the idea is right.
- `route_stop = min(exit_at, censor_at, app_horizon)` depends on `skipna` dropping `NaT`; that should be explicit.
- Empty `ALIASES` / `GROUP_MAP` make the migration and linkage story non-operational.
- Training uses `asof(FIT)` so prefixes cannot extend past the freeze, which is good; the default head still has almost no mature labels at that freeze, which the code flags (`supported_vintage`, `default_head_eligible`) but does not repair.

## Net

The answer would be in the high 8s if the grading brief rewarded “avoids the classic cheating design” and explicit limitations. The brief asked for strictness on inaccuracies, unclarities, and logical flaws. The two-clock target presented as a six-state partition, the primary freeze that starves the default model and cannot learn the policy change, the unspecified activity vocabulary, and a skeleton that drops the features the design itself called necessary are enough to keep this well below a high score.
