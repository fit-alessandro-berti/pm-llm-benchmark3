**7.0**

The final report is a strong, well-structured business reading of the alignments. It uses the required headings, treats the supplied alignments as authoritative, keeps technical cost separate from risk, and is right on the cases that matter most: CL-609/CL-610 as zero-cost policy failures, CL-605’s silent move as fitting, CL-604 as a cheap repair of an irreversible P3 breach, CL-602 as a high-exposure evidence/control failure, and CL-607/CL-612 as scope artifacts rather than missing payments.

It still falls well short of a high score under a strict reading.

**Section 7 is not executable.** The “highest-cost benign cases” print is a syntax error: `case_meta.loc[` is never closed, and `.to_string(...)` is chained to the column list. That alone fails the explicit “one executable Python code block” requirement.

**A second code defect would silently corrupt the model.** The fallback `PT_STRING.replace(" ", "")` strips spaces inside activity names (`Register Claim` → `RegisterClaim`). If the first parse failed and the second succeeded, every alignment would be wrong. That is not a safe fallback.

**A few overclaims sit next to a better principle.** The report correctly says a missing event is not proof the activity did not occur, then CL-604’s narrative states the claim was paid “with no fraud screen,” and CL-602 says no documented screen exists “in any system.” Those are stronger than the evidence. CL-608 is also slightly inconsistent: the narrative says the P4 link is not defensible, while the code sets `p4_ok=True`.

**Severity is generally defensible, not airtight.** Treating both CL-602 and CL-604 as 5 is reasonable given P3 and exposure. Rating CL-610 a 4 because the amount is small underweights P2’s explicit bank-change rule; that is a judgment call, but it is the one place the framework’s own “amount is not everything” rule is applied unevenly.

**What remains excellent.** The materiality framework (severity ≠ confidence), per-case table, pattern grouping, competing hypotheses with discriminating tests, and the cost-is-not-risk section (uniform costs, scope, tau, data conditions, labels, retries, external evidence, irreversibility) are thorough and mostly consistent. Remediation covers investigation, control redesign, data-aware monitoring, correlation repair, and model repair, and does not replace judgment with a formula.

The analysis would be in the high 8s to 9s. The broken required code, the label-mangling fallback, and the residual overclaims are why it is a 7.0, not higher.
