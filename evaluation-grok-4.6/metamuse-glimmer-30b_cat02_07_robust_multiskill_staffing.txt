**4.0**

The write-up has the required skeleton and a few real operational insights, but it is not close to a defensible analysis. Under a strict reading of the spec it fails the core evidence, uncertainty, and reproducibility requirements.

**What is actually solid**
Expected service times from range midpoints and tail mix are correct. The evening mechanical coverage hole after 18:00 and the tight 16:00–18:00 software pool (Emil only, 90 effective minutes) are the right bottlenecks to flag. Three policies are specified as dispatch rules rather than slogans, the recommendation is not “lowest base-case mean delay,” and the heading set matches the required document.

**Capacity analysis is incomplete and arithmetically wrong**
Section 2 never produces the skill-by-block offered-load matrix the task asked for. Intake, Validation, multipliers, breaks, handovers, setup, coordination, and preemption are mostly mentioned and then dropped, so load is treated as if it were unconstrained diagnostic minutes on a generic server. The later-block totals are simply incorrect: 14:00–16:00 diagnostic work is about 424 minutes, not ~470; 16:00–18:00 is about 256, not ~210; 18:00–20:00 is about 124, not ~80. “Intake and Validation are ample in all blocks” is asserted without showing late-day contention on Deepa/Emil.

U2 is a clear definitional error. The prompt says E and ES demand doubles **and proportions are renormalized**. After renormalization, electrical job counts rise on the order of ~20–30%, not “+~80% / +~90%.” That mistake invalidates the U2 bottleneck story. U1’s +8 pp P1 shift is never converted into P1 volume. U4 percentages are rough. Speed multipliers and time-varying shifts are not carried through the numbers even though the quality bar requires them.

**Section 4 invents results and calls them bounds**
The policy×scenario table is not computed from Section 2 or from the code. Large ranges for `mean_delay`, `p90_delay`, and SLA percents are presented as if they were estimates or bounds; they are hypotheses with no derivation, no seed variation, and no stated scale origin. Several required scenario cells are missing (P-A on U2/U4/U5, P-B on U4/U5, P-C on U2/U3/U4). A robustness claim cannot rest on an incomplete, fabricated grid.

**Policies have internal contradictions**
“Strict specialization with fixed queues” still parks Chen, Emil, and Farah on multiple diagnostic families, so it is not specialization. P-B’s “ECT + penalty × priority, P1 penalty negative” is underspecified. Parallel/sequential and reservation logic stay verbal; they never interact with the load math.

**Python fails most of the eight hard requirements**
Shifts, breaks, and the U1–U5 table are not encoded. `capacity_block` is a stub that returns arrival counts. Handover/setup/coordination exist only as unused constants, which is exactly what the prompt forbids. There is no preemption, no ME/ES branching, no three-policy model, and no base/U1–U5 metric table. The “event log” draws uniform arrivals, ignores the skill matrix, uses a generic `Diagnostic` activity, and keeps one resource for the whole case, so `discover_handover_of_work_network` cannot show handover burden. PM4Py case-level service/waiting outputs are not labeled, and stage-specific queues are not computed. A scaffold is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified; they are not.

**Smaller but material issues**
Objectives are lists, not formulations. Uncertainty is not separated from observation. Thresholds in Section 5 (45 minutes, 0.95 util, 85% SLA) are free parameters. Section 6 is a generic risk checklist with little coupling to this roster. Section 7 lists the right attributes but does not warn that PM4Py case-level waiting mixes stages.

The document looks complete and is not empty of process thinking. It is not auditable, not numerically reliable, and not reproducible. That is a low-to-mid score, not a high one.
