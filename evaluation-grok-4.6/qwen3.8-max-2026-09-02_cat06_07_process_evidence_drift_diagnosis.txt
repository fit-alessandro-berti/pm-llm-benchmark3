**8.0**

This is a strong audit: it correctly treats April as a schema/pipeline incident, June as a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September as overlapping process, concept, and population shifts, and October as dominated by immature, informatively censored labels. It triangulates AUROC/Brier/ECE with variant mix, DFG edge times, and Partner Z evidence, and the remediation is staged rather than a single “retrain everything” move. It is not near-flawless.

**What is done well**

The six required sections are present and used. Mix-adjusted expected breach rates are computed correctly (June ≈ 0.158 vs observed 0.15; August ≈ 0.31 vs 0.28; October ≈ 0.33 vs 0.16) and used to separate mix from underprediction and censoring. April’s stable 0.19 breach against a 0.28 mean score, 17% unknown activity, and 14% missing queue is not misread as concept drift. June’s stable variant-conditional rates are used to reject concept drift. October is not treated as comparable to mature months. The test list matches the prompt, each with competing expected results. Training-month safety is explicit. Monitoring includes alert gating so label delay does not auto-trigger retraining. The code sketch hits the required PM4Py surface and does not invent a drift detector.

**Flaws that keep it well below a top score**

1. **Concept vs process boundary is inconsistent with the prediction point.** The model scores immediately after diagnosis, so future `Order Part → Part Received` time is not in \(X\). A jump in V3 breach is then a change in \(P(Y \mid X)\), i.e. concept drift, even if it is causally due to the supplier. The table’s claim that explanation by “observed part delays” would make this covariate/process drift rather than concept drift blurs that distinction. Later text partly recovers (historical lead-time features), but the diagnosis table remains theoretically sloppy.

2. **Diagnostic-test table collapses distinct hypotheses.** Column 3 is “label-delay/censoring *or* pipeline failure.” Those are different mechanisms and should not share one expected-result cell. Several rows then mix April-pipeline and October-censoring predictions, which weakens the falsification design the prompt asked for.

3. **PM4Py event-calendar filtering is likely wrong.** `pm4py.filter_time_range` defaults to trace-level modes (`traces_contained` / `traces_intersecting`), not event-in-month filtering. Without an explicit event mode, the “event-calendar” cohort will not mean what the comments claim. Variant classification on those partial month-windows will also dump in-flight cases into Unmapped; that is noted only weakly.

4. **April metric denominator is not examined.** If prefixes were “not built at all,” mean predicted risk 0.28 and AUROC 0.67 may be computed on a selected subset. The write-up never asks which cases actually received a score.

5. **Code sketch quality is uneven.** Globals, `isinstance(perf, tuple)` hedging, and `len(v)` on `get_variants` values assume an API shape that varies by PM4Py version. Acceptable as a sketch, not tight.

6. **Minor over-precision.** Calling May “feature debt” and June “safe for calibration” is reasonable, but the May residual is inferred from a single audit sentence plus ECE 0.050; that is presented more firmly than the evidence warrants.

The core month-by-month story and the containment vs recalibration vs retrain staging are right. The grade is held down by the concept-drift definition slip, the conflated test hypotheses, and the event-filter/API inaccuracies—not by missing the main diagnosis.
