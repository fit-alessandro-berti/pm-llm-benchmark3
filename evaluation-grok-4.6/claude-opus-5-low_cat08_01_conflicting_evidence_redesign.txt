**7.6**

This is a strong, artifact-grounded redesign memo, not a generic Lean dump. It would be a high-8 or low-9 under a normal rubric. Under a strict standard it is not nearly flawless: several DFG readings are loose, one redesign quietly fails the conformance rules it claims to hard-encode, and a few mechanism claims contradict the same answer’s sacrifice sections.

### What is actually good

The diagnosis is the best part. It uses well more than five artifacts, and the three-way decomposition of the “fast vs good” contradiction (selection/misrouting, clock truncation at first close, cost migration from review into reshipment/credits/escalation) is real process-mining reasoning, not slogan-level trade-off talk. Segment disaggregation is done correctly: Premium low-risk accessories are treated as a case where the fast path is *right* (8h / 6% reopen vs 46h and 24h credit exposure), while Premium/Standard high-value are treated as the opposite (22%/18% vs 5%/6%). That satisfies the “do not assume the slow path is always better” constraint.

The three redesigns are materially different, not parameter tweaks: A is structural routing + a pre-dispatch control on the expensive tail; B is intake/evidence-loop collapse; C is ownership, measurement, and a learning loop. Sacrifices are mostly explicit. The table does not invent ROI. The experiment sequence is the other high point: Stage 0 instrumentation, reopen-inclusive resolution time, left-shifted abandonment, 82% utilisation as a hard constraint, and hold-out/control logic are all warranted by the artifacts.

### Material flaws

**1. Redesign A does not actually enforce R1.**  
R1 requires Manual eligibility review *or* Fraud/risk review before Fast approval above EUR 400. Lane 2 keeps Fast approval for high-value devices and inserts an invented “lightweight risk check,” then a part-match gate staffed by Quality check. That may catch wrong SKUs (the named driver of `Dispatch → complaint`). It is not the mandated review. The write-up still claims R1/R2 become “structurally unreachable” and that fitting traces should move “well above 68%.” That overclaims conformance. A and the table therefore treat a policy-shaped workaround as a control.

This is compounded by a second inconsistency: Stage 1 wants R1/R2 enforcement *and* a held-back rule rewrite so manual inflow does not spike. If R1 is enforced as written, high-value volume (35%) cannot stay on Fast approval without a real review token; inflow cannot stay near the prompt’s generic 15–35% band. A tries to keep replacement speed and claim structural compliance at the same time.

**2. Several frequency-DFG statements are sloppy.**  
The “46% highway” (2300/5000) treats `Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` as the auto lane. Fast approval inflows are 2550 (auto) + 240 (fraud) = 2790. A large share of the 2300 dispatches is auto-path, but not all of it; ~42% is the cleaner figure if the mix is proportional. Calling 2300 the “single four-step highway” overstates how cleanly the dominant path is isolated.

END accounting is ignored. `Close → END` 3015 + denied 220 + abandoned 390 = 3625, not 5000. About 27% of cases have no terminal edge in the given DFG. The memo treats 5,000 as a completed cohort when discussing 9.2% reopen, 12% no-service exits, and first-close speed. That is a basic log-completeness caveat.

Complaint edges also do not foot: inflows 140+80+260+30 = 510 vs outflows 310+120 = 430. Repeat reopens can explain some of this (and would support the 9.2% *case* vs event distinction), but it is never flagged. Mid-risk devices are named in the process context and then disappear; the five listed outcome rows sum to 100%, which should have been stated as a data limitation, not silently adopted as a partition.

**3. Internal overclaims.**  
B says front-loading “improves quality without slowing anything,” then admits heavier intake for the ~46% that never needed evidence and a real abandonment risk for low digital literacy. Those cannot both be true. C says cells “delete” the 7.5h and 22h hand-off edges, then admits two fraud specialists cannot be split and that the hand-off “partially returns.” “Only redesign that addresses selection-vs-causation at its root” is too strong: A’s segment-aware rules are also a selection fix; C is the *ongoing* fix.

**4. C is a programme, not one redesign.**  
Cells + clock redefinition + reopen attribution + escalation-board repurposing are four interventions. The memo later admits attribution will be hard because everything moves. That undercuts “materially different redesign” as a clean experimental object. Repurposing the escalation board also discards the one artifact that says the slot has a strong CSAT effect; weekly rule review does not replace case-level recovery for Premium.

**5. Resource fiction at the margin.**  
B staffs proactive contact from “spare” Quality check and repair-quote capacity. Quality check is underused *except after repair campaigns*; quote delay is wait-for-price, not idle technicians. A already assigns those same two inspectors to the gate. Calling A/B/C complementary in the table while they contend for the same two people is not resolved. The 6–18h gate is also talked into the low end via an undescribed “same-day batch”; the table’s “not dominated, but conditional on ~6h” depends on that hope.

**6. Smaller nits.**  
“32% non-fitting traces are structurally correlated with the cheap lane” is a reasonable inference from alignment diagnostics, stated as fact. The 9% residual “almost certainly” bimodal is plausible from the segment table, not shown. `Fast approval → Close` (350) is never interpreted. “Current process / Dominated? **Yes**” is stronger than the required caution against claiming Pareto dominance from this data; “conditional” would have matched the rest of the memo. Extra H1 title is a minor format deviation from “exactly the following top-level sections.”

### Bottom line

Evidence use and trade-off handling are well above typical. The answer is not a 9+ because it mis-states the dominant-path volume, skips DFG completeness, and lets Redesign A claim R1 compliance it does not implement. Those are not polish issues; they sit on the exact conformance and frequency artifacts the diagnosis otherwise uses well.
