**7.4**

The answer is unusually strong on temporal synthesis, causal restraint, and monitoring design. It still fails a strict reading of the spec in several places, and a few methodological claims are looser than they look.

### Format and discipline
Two hard word limits are missed by a wide margin. Section 2 is asked for 180–280 words and runs ~380–400. Section 9 is asked for 250–400 and runs ~550–600. That is not a rounding miss; it is failure to compress. Under a rubric that treats instructions as constraints, this alone blocks any top-band score.

### What it gets right
The core attribution is correct and well sequenced. March is treated as semantic/provenance drift (raw fitness 0.93→0.55, mapped ~0.90, labels 9→11, duplicates and missingness jump, normalized D1 = 3). Later movement is not folded into that story. May is tied to supplier-driven rework with the September reversal used as the best causal check. June–August is queueing under lost capacity, with the service-time observation (4.8h→5.4h) used properly. October is case-mix/seasonal, and December is left confounded. The argument that artifacts change on different dates — and therefore a single global change point is the wrong model — is the best part of the piece.

Quantitative work is mostly clean. Baseline construction, percentage-point vs percent changes, p90/median ratios, fitness deltas, and the seven-edge L1 table all check. Refusing to turn a partial DFG into a simplex is the right call. Cycle-time shares are given as overlapping ranges, and the text says outright that rework and queueing are mediated. The hypothesis table, discrimination matrix, segmentation plan, and the seven IF/THEN/BECAUSE rules are usable rather than decorative. Dual-track conformance (gap = logging, mapped level = behavior) is the right monitoring idea.

### Real flaws
The L1 distance double-counts the same loop. `Assess→RequestInfo` and `RequestInfo→CollectDocs` move almost in lockstep; summing absolute differences treats one phenomenon as two. July D1 = 59 and the “rework ridge vs senior spike” contrast are still directionally right, but the magnitudes are inflated and the write-up never says so.

Mapped-fitness decline after April is over-interpreted as residual behavior or a stale model. The intended model already allows `Request Info`. A more important monthly-window artifact is missing: as median cycle time goes from ~31h to ~49h, month-sliced traces are more often incomplete, which lowers fitness without any new deviation type. That is a process-mining explanation the answer does not consider. The stale-`Senior Assessment`-model story also cannot carry May–July, when mapped fitness is already at 0.86–0.88 and the senior edge has not yet jumped.

The March→April timeline row cites a March DFG (April is not in the supplied fragment) and says mapped fitness “stays 0.90.” Mapped fitness actually falls 0.93→0.90. Small, but it is the same over-smoothing the answer criticizes elsewhere.

Priority is internally inconsistent. The timeline gives the July rule evidence strength 55; later the text says there is “zero direct evidence” and declines to claim an effect. 55 is too high for an unidentified aggregate signature.

The primary monitoring population is described as “45–60% of cases every month, never below ~300.” That intersection is not identified. In October–November, with high-complexity at 41–45% and Supplier Z at 26–27%, the standard/non-Z cell can fall well below that band unless Z and complexity are strongly positively dependent. The quasi-control idea is good; the support claim is not computed.

Staffing “convex waiting” is asserted without a headcount denominator. Four leavers is a utilization shock only relative to the unknown assessor pool. Backlog carry-over from May is acknowledged, then the June change point is still treated as fairly sharp.

### Section 8
Part A is the better half: embedded tables, validation, baseline deltas, a transparent distance, fitness recovery, a MAD rule that is labeled as a candidate rule, and explicit caveats. Part B is mostly aligned with the PM4Py simplified API (`format_dataframe`, `filter_time_range`, `discover_dfg`, `get_variants`, `discover_temporal_profile`, `discover_log_skeleton`, `discover_declare`, `fitness_alignments`) and correctly keeps a copy for mapping.

It is not safe as written. `monthly_dfgs` stores the `(dfg, start, end)` triple; `normalized_dfg_rates` iterates `dfg.items()` as if it received the edge dict. `normalize_labels` drops every `Open Case` event, which is stronger than “remove duplicated `Open Case` events.” `monthly_variants` groups raw events by calendar month after the same function comments warned that event-sliced windows bias case-level measures. The `if False` branch in `prepare_log` is dead code.

### Net
This is a high-quality drift memo with the right mechanism map and unusual quantitative care. It is not nearly flawless. Hard length limits are broken twice, the DFG distance overstates unique change, a standard incompleteness bias is missed, a few evidence numbers disagree with the surrounding prose, and the PM4Py plan would not run end-to-end without fixes. Those are enough, under a strict bar, to keep it out of the 8.5–10 band.
