**7.4**

The answer is substantively strong: it catches the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, separates queueing from service, documents positivity failure, and proposes a safer learning intervention. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading the defects below are enough to keep it well short of the top of the scale.

**Verdict is internally inconsistent.** `mainly confounded` at 72 fits the claim that Orion does not *cause longer* times. It does not fit the rest of the memo. The author simultaneously treats the within-stratum Orion advantage as a *conservative* bound on a real benefit, ranks a genuine beneficial Orion mechanism second, and forecasts that moving work to Nova will add ~2.7 h per case. That package is `partly causal through mechanisms` (beneficial, masked by mix) or, given zero overlap in the strata that matter, `not identifiable`. Confidence 72 is too high for n=30, two n=2 arms, complete Medium/High daytime emptiness, and explicit selection on completion. “Mix fully accounts for the crude gap” in §1 overstates what §2–§5 later correctly unsay.

**Required causal distinction is only half-made.** Total vs direct vs mediated language appears in the role table and in warnings not to condition on `queue_wait`, but §1 never states a three-way decomposition of an Orion effect. That was an explicit minimum.

**Format limits are broken.** The graph note exceeds the 120-word cap (the congestion paragraph alone almost consumes it). The final recommendation is ~230 words against a 120–200 limit.

**Graph and “supported facts” do not match.** The adjacency list has `shift -> routing_rule -> resource_group`; the prose claims a supported `shift -> resource_group`. “Orion covers most night work” is at least as well read as group determining coverage (`resource_group -> shift`) as night arrivals determining routing. `arrival_load -> routing_rule` is drawn and never labeled an assumption. `resource_group -> unobserved_reviewer_skill` is a bundle encoding, not a causal process. Calling the residual identity “definitional” is false; it is an empirical regularity in what looks like a generated extract (they notice this later, which makes the earlier wording worse, not better).

**Several quantities are stated more cleanly than the data allow.** The residual is not an exact `0.8 + 1.4·compliance + 1.45·rework` identity: rework-only residuals are 1.5 h; C10 is 3.6; C12/C14/C30 are 3.7. Crude contrast is +6.72 h in the memo and +6.71 h in the “when run” blurb. H6’s hidden-severity gap of ~5.5 h is about half a complexity step, not “a full complexity level.” The recommendation attributes “86% of the group difference” to waiting; that share is of the *standardised* gap, not the crude +6.72 h (wait is ~60% of that).

**§7 is diagnostic and mostly correct, but not a clean PM4Py solution.** The try/except pandas fallback means the required `parse_event_log_string` / `discover_dfg` path is optional. The script never actually has to succeed as specified. Overlap, thin-cell warnings, and the two explicit weighting schemes are the best part of the code.

**What remains excellent, and why this is not a mid-scale score.** Arithmetic on the supplied tables is almost entirely auditable and right (pooled −2.51 h, Orion-mix −2.72 h, wait share of the standardised gap, compliance/rework crude-vs-within-cell splits, identical DFG edge sets). Positivity is diagnosed rather than waved at. H1–H6 cover the required hypothesis types and each test has a predicted observation. Phases 1 and 2 match the required intervention types. Selection, SUTVA/consistency, and the `review_service_h` semantics defect (rework cases do not show extra service time) are real process-mining points, not boilerplate.

The memo would be in the 9s if the verdict matched the evidence, the word caps were hit, the graph matched its own legend, and the residual/identity/86% claims were stated at the precision the author elsewhere shows they can achieve. They were not.
