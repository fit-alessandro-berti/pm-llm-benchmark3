**3.8**

The write-up has the right *skeleton* (staggered adoption, availability vs intensity, targeted rollout, co-shocks, refuse a single “X%” claim, expand-with-evaluation). It is not a competent analysis of *these* data. The quantitative core is wrong, several mandatory elements are missed or mis-specified, and the code does not do what the prompt required.

### Fatal quantitative error
Section 5’s “median” table is not medians. Alpha Jan–Mar median is **(50.4+46.9+49.4)/3 = 48.9 h**, not 98.5. 98.5 is the Jan–Mar **p90**. The same swap appears for Beta (~45.9 vs reported 93.4). Gamma’s “pre 93.4” is a copy-paste of Beta’s wrong number, not Gamma’s median (~40.4 for Jan–Jun) or p90 (~89). Controls are also in the wrong units and windows.

That is not approximation. It inverts the process fact the prompt told you not to collapse: **medians are roughly flat-to-slightly better late; tails blow out in May–June and only partly recover.** Alpha median goes 48.9 → 48.5 (Apr–Aug), with June 53.5 then August **43.3** at peak `automated_fast_path_pct`. Calling this “median rose +9.1 h” is simply false.

Naive DiD on the *actual* medians vs never-treated goes the other way from the write-up. Delta median Jan–Mar 34.5 → Apr–Aug 40.5 (+6.0); Alpha −0.4. DiD ≈ **−6 h**. Beta vs Delta ≈ **−4 h**. Controls also deteriorate after the May complexity shock. A careful analyst would not claim a clean benefit, but “no evidence of reduction; cycle times increased vs Delta” is the wrong descriptive claim.

Intensity is also misread. Highest-use months are not the worst medians; Alpha August is the best median. The “higher intensity, worse performance” line is an artifact of the p90-as-median swap plus ignoring dynamics.

### Identification and estimands
The estimand table is formulaic. Population is just “Branches.” E2 treats use as a post-only contrast vs 0, but `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6% pre-adoption and never zero. Identifiability is marked “Yes” for intensity and CSAT as if sequential ignorability and survey selection were free. Interference (shared fraud desk) and treatment-version (availability ≠ use ≠ fidelity; within-branch case routing) are not really clarified.

Primary design is named “staggered DID / event study,” which is the right family, then immediately undercut: (i) explanation is ~110 words (required 150–250); (ii) post-treatment `high_complexity_pct` is treated as a safe adjuster without mediation/collider discussion; (iii) SC with two donors and three pre periods is offered without saying it is unidentified in practice; (iv) the Python “FE panel model” is **WLS with no branch or time FE, no relative-time dummies, no clustered SEs**. That is the naïve regression the prompt told you not to treat as sufficient.

March escalation rises **exactly +2.0 pp in every branch** (fraud desk). That is the cleanest common shock in the file. The write-up calls escalation “stable (~10–11%)” and Alpha “stable.” Both are false (Alpha 7.8 → 11.6).

Bias direction on complexity is backwards: extra complexity in treated months makes unadjusted automation look *worse* for cycle time, not better.

### Format and role misses
- §1 never states the selection rule (Alpha/Beta chosen for high volume and poor history). That was an explicit distinguish-item.
- §6 omits named rows for **small number of branches** and **dynamic treatment effects**, both required.
- §5 never produces a real treated-vs-not-yet/never DiD, never separates median from p90 in the calculations, barely touches rework/CSAT, and the complexity sensitivity has the sign wrong.
- §7 then inherits the bad numbers: median “slightly negative,” “no claim of reduction,” confidence 50–70% in inverted assessments.
- §8’s option (`expand with an embedded evaluation`) is the least-wrong policy class, but it does not choose Delta vs Epsilon clearly, does not use remaining-branch randomization to buy identification, and is thin for a *process-mining* lead: no case-level event log, variant/rework-loop, WIP, or queue-length plan. Escalation guardrail at 15% never binds in-sample.
- §9 would not run as written (`pd.compat.StringIO`, `plt` never imported). `calc_contrast` sets pre = post = full-sample mean, so every contrast is **0**. `idxmax` on all-zero automation codes Delta/Epsilon as treated in January. Only one outcome is plotted, not five. No clustered covariance.

### Claim ladder
The ladder *shape* is right (Observed → Suggestive → Conditionally causal → Not supported), and rejecting “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is correct. Bullet 2 is not: it asserts increased medians and “no evidence of reduction” from a misread column. That is a strong-looking claim built on an arithmetic error.

**Net:** competent outline, failed measurement. For this task the median-vs-tail split, load/FTE, March/May common shocks, and honest DiD-vs-controls arithmetic *are* the analysis. Missing them, then stating the opposite, caps the score well below the midpoint.
