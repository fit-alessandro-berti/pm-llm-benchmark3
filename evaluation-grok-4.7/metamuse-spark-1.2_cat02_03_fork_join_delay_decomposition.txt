The core fork-join math is right and the required case table, identities, and CF1/CF2 per-case savings check out. The write-up is not nearly flawless: several counts contradict the same table, one counterfactual interpretation undoes the critical-path lesson, and the redesign section reports results the supplied simulation does not produce.

**What holds.** Definitions use `max` completion, not the sum of branch times. The no-double-count identity is correct, and the F01 illustration (sync sum 27 versus elapsed 50, with C’s wait overlapping A’s service) is the right argument. Every case row matches the timestamps, including queues, services, `join_ready`, critical branch, downstream wait, and flow time. Worked arithmetic for F02, F04, F07, and F11 is correct. Critical frequencies 2/8/2 and means for completion (50.0 / 56.7 / 30.6), sync, fork-join (61), downstream (14.7), and flow (115.25) are right. The Python builds the table, uses a deterministic A-B-C tie rule, runs the three counterfactuals, builds an interval log, and calls `format_dataframe` plus performance helpers, with a real comment that directly-follows graphs do not recover synchronization.

**Material errors.** “4 cases with fork-join ≥ 65” lists five cases (F03, F06, F07, F09, F12). “6 of 8 B-critical cases save” is false: all 8 save. “Full elimination would add ~9.6 min” treats leftover mean queue as leftover flow saving; after critical-path capping the extra mean flow saving is about 2.3 minutes, not 9.6. That mistake is the one the task specifically warns against. F11’s flow of 110 is called jointly shortest; F01 is 93. “Final service constant except F01” is false: final service is 20 for every case, and only upstream differs. After CF2, A is strictly critical in 4 cases, or 6 if A=B ties count — not 5. New mean A sync is about 3.3, not ~6. Section 4’s tail 130→115 and section 5’s 130→118 disagree; 115 is the one supported by the savings. Calendar utilization ~38% and traffic intensity 44% are both stated as what a third server “drops to,” and 57% and 66% are placed side by side without saying they are different denominators.
