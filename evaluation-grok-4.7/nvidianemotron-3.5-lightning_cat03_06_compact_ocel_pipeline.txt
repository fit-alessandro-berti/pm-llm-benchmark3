5.5

The pipeline covers the required sections, the 17 events, the object inventory, timezone-aware timestamps, O2O facts, and the main shared events (reserve, pack P1, invoice). Referential checks are real. The interpretation does not follow from that model, and several claims are factually wrong.

The coded E2O links orders only to create, reserve, pack P1, invoice, and close. Add-item, dispatch, and confirm-delivery do not touch orders, and packages only touch pack and dispatch. Section 4 still predicts order traces of 6–8 events and DFG edges `create_order → add_item → reserve_stock → pack_package → dispatch_package → close_order`, dropping `issue_invoice`, which is linked, and inserting activities that are not. Package traces are described as spanning e1–e10 plus invoice and payments; under this relation table P1 is only e7 and e9, and P2 only e8 and e10. Section 8 repeats that error (order traces ~6–8, package traces 7–9) and says pack P1 has six relation rows while listing five objects.

Qualifier semantics are uneven: `bills` is attached to the order and `carried_by` to the delivery. `dispatched_for` is used in the audit and interaction summary but never defined or stored. Order–delivery interaction is predicted even though those types never co-occur. The invoice amount 250 and “M2 truly completes settlement” are treated as narrative facts; the story only says both payments are partial and together settle V1.

OC-DFG is described as a graph of object-type nodes, and the object-centric Petri net as instance places (`O100_created`) with payment guards. Those are not what the named discovery routines return. `ocel_flattening(..., type=ot)` does not match the real `object_type` parameter, and `pm4py.objects.ocelex` is not a PM4Py module. Failure modes and the three modeling alternatives are the soundest part of the write-up.
