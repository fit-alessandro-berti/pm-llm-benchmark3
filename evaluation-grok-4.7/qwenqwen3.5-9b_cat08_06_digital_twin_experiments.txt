4.5

The write-up covers every required section and recognizes non-stationary demand, calendars, rework, architect/district coupling, and the objection-data gap. It is not a usable simulation design. Several methodological choices would bias intervention comparisons, and the code does not follow PM4Py or the stated computation requirements.

The largest design error is the treatment of time. Waiting is estimated from the log as if it were a fitted input, while queues and resources are also supposed to generate waiting. In a resource-constrained twin, municipal waiting must be an output. Only applicant and third-party delays, fixed public-notice minima, and travel should be exogenous. Sampling historical waits and then queueing again double-counts congestion, so D4, D6, and D8 cannot be compared fairly. Public notice is also the wrong shape for a generic heavy-tailed draw: it has a hard 14-day floor plus a separate, unobserved objection extension. The proposed probability \((p90 - median) / max\_duration\) is not a probability and has no defined maximum.

Warm-up is framed as a steady-state batch-means problem even though demand is seasonal and one intervention exists only in peak months. A one-month discard is far too short for cases whose median duration is 48–140 days and whose p90 reaches 260 days. An empty start would still have an unrealistic backlog. Initial WIP should be seeded from the log, and each replication should cover full seasonal cycles. Twenty runs, with no common random numbers and no tail-specific sample-size argument, are thin for p90 estimates on an 8–10% variant.

Parameter estimation stays at the level of “fit a distribution” and “build a co-occurrence matrix.” It never stratifies service, routing, or rework by case type, district, and requirement flags, despite the variant table showing order-of-magnitude duration differences. It does not separate completeness and inspection loops from planning, legal, and appeal rework, which the narrative includes and the process tree omits. It does not say how missing start timestamps will be bounded, or how fire and environmental branching will be imputed when area and sensitivity fields are incomplete. Intervention effects that are not in the log (D1, D2, D5, D7, D9) are binary switches with no effect-size range, so the experiment matrix cannot express the main uncertainty.

The experiment table lists baseline, D1–D10, the specified bundle, and three stresses, but levels are almost all on/off and several trade-offs are wrong. A peak-only inspector does not add January–February idle time. An aging-case priority rule does not inherently raise planner utilization; it swaps tail time against median or younger cases. Stresses are not crossed with policies.

Decision thresholds conflict with the supplied baselines. A 60-day simple-case median is loose against observed 12- and 22-day medians. A single 180-day p90 and an 80% within-90-days rule ignore that complex-variance median is already 140 days and p90 is 260. A district duration CV below 0.2 is unjustified given D3/D5 travel, and architect fairness is absent. Utilization of 70–85% as an average hides peak overload. The selection rule picks a minimum median without making the result conditional on calibration, effect-size ranges, and stress regret.

Validation names the right categories and then checks the wrong things. A KS test on fitted service times only rechecks the inputs. Utilization cannot be validated against HR calendars; those data specify capacity, not observed busyness. MAPE under 10% is arbitrary for heavy tails. There is no variant-frequency, seasonal-arrival, bundle-size, or conformance check, and no separation of calibration data from the holdout.

The skeleton misses required outputs and uses non-PM4Py calls. `format_dataframe` is invoked with `case_attributes` and `time_attributes` instead of `case_id`, `activity_key`, and `timestamp_key`. The excerpt is not loaded; `df['case']` is not a column in the given log. Variants and service/waiting columns are never computed. `groupby(...).size().mean()` is events per case, not case-arrival average (`pm4py.get_case_arrival_average`). `X(tau, ...)` is standard process-tree XOR with a silent skip, not custom notation, and the normalizer deletes parentheses so `parse_process_tree` cannot see the model. `get_dfg` and `get_performance_dfg` are not the PM4Py discovery API. The simulator omits clerks, fire, environment, calendars, routing, and cross-case batching; `case_handler` is called with the wrong binding; arrival helpers are undefined; and replication results overwrite each other.
