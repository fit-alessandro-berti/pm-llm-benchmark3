5.5

The case table and the main ranking (B ahead of A, despite A’s longer service) are the strongest parts of the answer. Every listed queue, service, join, downstream wait, final service, total, and critical branch matches the log, and the F02 walkthrough is correct. That is not enough to carry the rest.

The double-counting argument required in §1 is wrong. Summing branch sync waits gives \(3\cdot join\_ready - \sum_i end_i\), not \(3\cdot join\_ready - end_i\), and that sum is slack rather than a uniform doubling of elapsed time. The identity that avoids double counting is never written: total flow equals upstream plus \(\max_i(queue_i+service_i)\) plus downstream queue plus final service. Sync is defined and then dropped; it never appears per case.

§2 misses three of the four required worked cases. Pointing at §6 does not supply them: the code never prints those derivations.

§3 has no comparison table and the sync statistics are false. Mean sync is 11.0 min for A (132/12), 4.33 for B (52/12), and 30.4 for C (365/12). The answer assigns 7 to A and 11 to B, and calls B’s figure wait “imposed on others,” which is a different quantity. Tail contribution to `join_ready` is asserted, not measured. Frequency 2/12, 8/12, 2/12 is right, as is B’s mean service 37.5, but mean B queue is 19.17 and mean C queue is 10.42, not 19 and 10.

§4 gets CF-1 right: only F01 and F11 move, by 5 and 10 min, total 15. CF-2 does not. Halving B’s queue saves 92.5 min, not 93. With the stated A-before-B tie break, A becomes limiting on F02, F05, F06, and F10 — four cases, not three. The pass-through assumption (downstream minutes stay constant) is exactly the masking question the task requires, and it is not tested against F1/F2 occupancy. On the logged assignment, F05, F08, F09, and F11 are already released only when the same resource frees, so an earlier join is fully masked there. CF-3’s “no inter-case pre-emption” claim is false under that assignment: starting F09 and F11 at `join_ready` overlaps them on F1 (11:15–11:35 vs 11:30–11:50). “Frees 176 min of operator idle time” treats case wait as server idle time.

§5 invents “< 60% busy” for a second rig. Nothing in the log supports that utilization. Throughput and synchronization effects are thin, and the 75th percentile is named but not computed. The pilot advice is generic.

§6 would not run. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`. `pm4py.statistics.service_times.pandas.get_service_time` and `pm4py.algo.performance.dfg` are not the public APIs, so execution dies before the prints. Sync and fork-join elapsed are not calculated. Counterfactual output is aggregate only, so the required per-case savings are absent. The fork-join comment is one vague line. The pandas decomposition itself is largely the right calculation, which is why the hand table is correct, but the block does not meet the reproducibility spec.

§7 does cover capacity, preemption, causality, and release policy versus a real queue. It then claims that a shorter B queue would change arrivals to C. Within a case, C is released at `fork_release` and does not wait for B.
