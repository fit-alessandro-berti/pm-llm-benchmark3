7.4

The answer is a serious, well-structured causal review and it gets the main policy conclusion right: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not defensible. Availability is separated from intensity, rollout is treated as targeted, median and p90 are not collapsed, CSAT is correctly treated as unidentified, and the DiD arithmetic that is shown is mostly accurate. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The central identification problem is underweighted. For Alpha and Beta the routing increase begins in the adoption month, and for Gamma it begins at Gamma’s adoption month, so the volume shock is essentially collinear with availability. Shared shocks such as the March fraud desk and the May complexity jump are what never-treated branches can difference out. The adopter-only routing shock is not. Section 3 nevertheless names the May product change as the main failure mode of the preferred staggered design and still lists post-treatment arrivals and FTE as routine adjustment variables without saying that this changes the estimand. Those are bad-control and treatment-version problems, not minor covariates.

The quantitative section is less transparent than it looks. Alpha is paired only with Delta and Beta only with Epsilon, with no justification; the −2.5 to −6.4 hour spread is partly an artifact of that pairing. Gamma’s raw −3.4 hour median change depends on putting the May–June complexity spike inside the pre window, and that window sensitivity is not shown. The intensity result is stated as “roughly −0.15 to −0.25 hours per point” and then scaled to 7–12 hours, but no arithmetic is shown. Arrivals per FTE are never computed, even though Alpha’s load per FTE rises by about 30% while its median stays flat. That is the clearest descriptive evidence on capacity versus substitution, and the mechanism table misses it. The same table incorrectly says Alpha and Beta medians are “roughly flat in DID.”

Section 9 does not implement the strategy Section 3 recommends. Relative-time indicators are built and then unused. The fitted panel model is the single-coefficient two-way fixed-effects regression the prose rejects. The pre-trend check uses January–March for every branch, including Gamma, whose relevant pre-period runs through June. Clustered standard errors are appropriately caveated, but the code does not match the stated design.

The rollout choice is directionally sensible, yet the embedded trial randomizes only low-complexity cases inside one branch. That estimates a different contrast from the one leadership would scale, and shared specialist capacity means within-branch control cases are not insulated from treatment. Cross-branch fraud-desk interference is discussed carefully; this within-branch interference is not.
