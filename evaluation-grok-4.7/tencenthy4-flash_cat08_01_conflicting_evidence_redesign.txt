The diagnosis, redesigns, comparison, failure analysis, and experiment plan are complete, evidence-rich, and segment-sensitive. The three redesigns are genuinely different, trade-offs are explicit, and the answer avoids a generic Lean list, average-only optimization, and unsupported ROI claims.

Deductions come from precision and clarity, not missing structure. Alignment deviations are said to “map directly” to R1–R3, but they do not: R2 concerns dispatch before evidence completeness, not Fast approval; R1 allows either manual review or fraud/risk review before Fast approval for claims above EUR 400, not a risk review before every high-value dispatch; R3 requires manual review after reopen, which is not the same finding as reopen-after-close. In the comparison table, Redesign A’s compliance cell (“Lowest direct control improvement”) does not clearly state compliance risk and can be read as the weakest improvement. The dominance note also confuses whether an option is dominated with whether it dominates others, while still marking all three redesigns “No.” Mid-risk devices are named as a target of Redesign A without a routing rule, and parallelization is treated as if the Manual review → Fraud/risk edge time were mostly removable waiting time.
