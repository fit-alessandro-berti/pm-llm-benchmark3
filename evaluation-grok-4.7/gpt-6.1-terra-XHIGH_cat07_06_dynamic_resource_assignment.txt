7.2

The answer is complete, factually careful, and unusually honest about uncertainty, but it is not nearly flawless. Several design choices conflict with the rules it states, and the policy is not specified tightly enough to audit.

The skill levels, the nine normal/low cases, the 2/9 = 22.2% training share, the Security skill-0 block on Eva and Jonas, David’s Approval skill of 0, the approval rule that level 1 may prepare but not finalize, and the batch-cap arithmetic are correct. High- and urgent-priority work is kept at skill 2–3. High-overtime-risk workers receive no new work. Sections 1, 5, and 6 separate hard rules from preferences, avoid punitive language, and define usable audit measures with denominators, case-mix limits, and missing-data gaps. The refusal to treat the historical multipliers as causal effects is sound.

The recommended queue still breaks the answer’s own continuity rule. C001 is high-complexity with high handover cost, and Celine’s Security skill is 1 while supervision by Gita or Hugo is allowed. The stated exception applies only when that supervision is infeasible. The median 2.7-hour handover delay is also used as the reason to change performer, even though that delay is the cost of the change and is almost the entire 3-hour SLA. Keeping Celine as performer under immediate specialist supervision is the option that follows both the rule and the cited evidence. Naming her a coordinator does not repair that, and that coordination is omitted from a cap the answer otherwise applies strictly to supervision.

C003 and the Celine/Farid loads have a similar inconsistency. C003 already has two reassignments and is a repeated diagnosis loop. Moving it from David (Diagnosis 3) to Farid (Diagnosis 1) adds another reassignment and the weak-skill service penalty the answer itself cites. Safer Diagnosis 2–3 choices with acceptable overtime exist, including Celine, Eva, and Hugo. Farid is also given both urgent cases, C002 and C011, plus C010, reaching four commitments on 6.5 hours. Celine is placed at four commitments, including two supervisions and C007’s 6-hour SLA, before uncounted C001 coordination. The text says not to force the training target when the plan is unsafe, then publishes the forced plan as the recommendation. C010 could have used Eva’s Approval skill of 2, and C014 could have used Hugo’s Rework skill of 3, both of which would reduce that concentration. C013 to Eva is legal but weakly justified on fairness: lower-load Eligibility skill-2 capacity exists.

The scoring rule is not yet a reproducible policy. Skill, continuity, fairness, training, resilience, and SLA are named, but weights, scales, slack formula, and override thresholds are left open. Section 6 then proposes to audit deviations from that policy. Without a deterministic score, a deviation cannot be distinguished from a different weighting. The baseline comparison correctly says both baselines can break mandatory rules, including specialist-only routing putting Celine over the cap, but it never assigns C001–C016 under either baseline, so the seven-way comparison stays generic.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested families and correctly warns that synthetic assignment events, role discovery, and performance-DFG delays are not authorizations, service times, or causes. The snippet itself would fail as written: `cases` is undefined, `event_sequence` is not in the log, and the urgent log is unused. The indicator calculations from Section 6 are described afterward rather than implemented.
