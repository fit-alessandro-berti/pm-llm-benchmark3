The final write-up is a strong, evidence-bound redesign memo: all seven required sections are present, the fast-path contradiction is stated explicitly, and speed, cost, quality, compliance, and experience are separated by segment rather than collapsed into one average. Counts, hours, rules R1–R3, fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces, the 14.1% versus 3.3% complaint split, the 31% to 9% cost gap, and the resource constraints are used accurately. The three redesigns are genuinely different (routing guardrails, evidence co-production, fulfillment/reopen control), each names what it gives up, and the table correctly refuses exact dominance.

It is not nearly flawless. A few stop/go numbers are invented beyond the data: fraud p90 “no more than about 20%” and quality-inspector utilization “80–85%” extend the one stated 82% manual-review threshold to resources that were not given that limit. The experiment section treats fewer non-policy paths as fixing low precision; that mixes normative conformance with precision of a discovered model. The first pilot covers Premium low-risk and Standard high-value, but never explains why Premium high-value — the worst fast-path complaint gap (22% versus 5%) and the highest compliance exposure — is left out. Parallel review is also said to reduce the slow path before the capacity failure mode is admitted, and unbalanced DFG cuts are quoted as volumes without noting that several activities do not conserve flow. Those are limited slips, not structural failures.
