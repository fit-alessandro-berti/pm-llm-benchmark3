7.4

The answer is a strong regime analysis with the right central story, but it is not nearly flawless. Several reported figures contradict the supplied counts or the answer’s own definitions, and two of those conflicts are inside the reproducible script.

The main operating picture is sound. Baseline weekdays are nearly balanced near 100 arrivals, 6 FTE, short service, and WIP around 20. The 15 Apr outage is treated as a demand-continues freeze, and 16–17 Apr is correctly refused as a sustainable high-throughput regime. The mix-shift weekdays keep completion intensity near the baseline while arrivals, the service tail, morning concentration, and WIP jump, so inventory runs a deficit of about 13 cases per weekday. The 29 Apr intervention is a partial, still-transient drain, with 29 Apr itself contaminated. Little’s Law is not abused: median handling is never used as sojourn, \(L/\lambda\) is confined to locally balanced windows, and non-stationary, outage, recovery, and pooled windows are marked not defensible. Assumptions, alternative segmentations, and the pooled-versus-segmented reversals are substantive.

The numerical and consistency failures are concrete.

- In §4 the all-weekday mean FTE is 6.20. Weekday FTE sums to 123 over 20 days, so the mean is 6.15. Weekend FTE is correctly 2.62 and the pooled mean is correctly 5.14; those three table cells cannot hold together. Weekday \(L/c=5.6\) is the downstream effect of the wrong capacity.
- Weekend \(\rho_{\mathrm{med}}\) is given as 0.78. The ratio of slice means is about 0.76.
- §5 says the 07 Apr bins 12 and 20 are a 09:00–11:00 peak and are “consistent with” `peak_08_10_pct=31`. Under the stated bin clock, 08:00–09:59 is 5+12=17, or 16.8%, not 31%. The same 08:00–09:59 share also misses 15 Apr (18% vs 32%) and 25 Apr (12% vs 22%). Only the 21 Apr clash is acknowledged. The code computes `a[1]+a[2]`, so it would print those mismatches and contradict the prose.
- 15 Apr completion dispersion is wrong: population Fano is about 2.06 and CV about 0.65, not 1.54 and 0.56. Smaller CV slips appear for 21 Apr completions (about 0.33, not 0.31) and 25 Apr completions (about 0.45, not 0.41). Arrival Fano 3.85 on 15 Apr is slightly high versus about 3.79.
- The two-mean between-group sum of squares, as defined in the prose and implemented in the code, does not put `median_service_min` at 20 Apr. A split at 15 Apr scores higher (about 357 versus about 350). The claim that service “agrees” with the complex-mix break is false, and the script would show it.
- §7 places the zero-start gap of 110 at the end of the mix-shift regime. Through 28 Apr the cumulative completion-minus-arrival balance is −128; −110 is the balance only after the later +18 weekday surplus. Assumption A3 also calls −110 a lower bound on how much unfinished work “grew,” which muddles the sign: the conserved increase is +110.
- A9 is cited in §5 and never defined. R5 WIP is called rising despite the 46→44→43 dip. A “hard” completion cap of 10–12 per hour is too hard: the same 21 Apr vector has an hour with 13 completions. Hourly Fano is interpreted as overdispersion relative to Poisson without separating the deterministic diurnal shape; with one profile per regime, Fano above 1 is guaranteed for a non-homogeneous Poisson morning peak.

Those defects do not overturn the regime logic, the refusal to treat recovery completions as capacity, or the Little’s Law matrix. They do break internal consistency in the comparison table, the burstiness audit, and the change-point paragraph, which is exactly where this task demands checkable arithmetic.
