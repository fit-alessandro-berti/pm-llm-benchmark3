The required structure is complete, and several hard rules are handled carefully: skill 0 is excluded, urgent Security and Approval decisions have a legal finalizer, high-overtime resources receive no new work, the four-assignment cap holds, C007 and C016 stay with qualified owners, and C015 correctly rejects Eva and Jonas because supervision cannot cure skill 0. The training-share arithmetic is also honest: 1/9 documented normal/low assignments is below 20%, and the text refuses to invent a priority to close the gap. The audit indicators are specific and cautious about attribution.

The recommended queue does not reliably follow the policy or the matrix.

- C007 calls Celine Rework skill 2 and a “normal-skill” compromise. Her Rework score is 3, so the stated trade-off is false.
- C014’s main reason calls Jonas Rework skill 3. His score is 2. The assignment therefore drops a skill-3 owner to a skill-2 resource and never names that drop. Hugo, skill 3 with low overtime and spare hours, is not compared.
- C001 gives the urgent specialist decision to Gita (10 open cases, 4.5 hours, medium overtime) and then adds C010 preparation. Hugo is also Security skill 3, with 7 open cases, 5.5 hours, and low overtime, and he receives no new work. That contradicts the workload, overtime, and bottleneck rules.
- C010 uses Gita, whose training priority is “none,” as an Approval preparer. It consumes scarce Security capacity without counting toward the training target. Farid can finalize alone.
- C003 moves a repeated diagnosis loop from David, Diagnosis skill 3, and raises reassignments from 2 to 3. The policy allows that only if he is ineligible, unsafe, or unable to meet the deadline. None of those is established for an 18-hour SLA.
- C013 goes to Amal, who already has 9 open cases and the C009 training load. Jonas has Eligibility skill 2, 4 open cases, 6 hours, and low overtime. The stated reason does not identify a busier alternative that this choice avoids.
- C009 lists the trainee first after declaring that the first named person is the activity lead. The supervisor is the person who must release the diagnosis.

The scoring model is never applied. There are no urgency scores, service-time estimates, candidate scores, or running capacity totals, despite gates that depend on them. Combined load is unresolved for Gita, Farid (two urgent cases plus C010), Eva (two high-priority cases and 5 hours), and Celine.

Section 4 stays qualitative. It never assigns this queue under first-available or specialist-only routing, so the throughput and SLA claims are not checked against the snapshot. Section 5 misdescribes the moved work as low-handover: C003 and C014 are medium, and David’s loss of the diagnosis loop is omitted. In Section 7, `filter_case_performance(log, 0, 48 * 3600)` keeps cases lasting up to 48 hours, while the variable is named `late_or_slow_cases`. The sketch also never computes the Section 6 indicators from the roster, skill, handover, reassignment, or SLA columns.
