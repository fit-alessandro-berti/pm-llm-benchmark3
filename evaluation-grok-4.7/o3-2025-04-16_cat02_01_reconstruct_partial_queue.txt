3.5

The write-up follows the required seven-section structure and correctly separates MISSING, OPEN, and blank in principle, but its quantitative reconstruction is not reliable. Several spotlight cases are arithmetically wrong, station totals are physically infeasible, and the Python does not compute or reproduce the reported metrics.

# 1. Assumptions and notation
A1–A2, A4, A7, and A8 are usable. The tie-break (departure before a same-timestamp arrival) is stated. A5’s 5-minute minimum service is not supported by the observed services, which run about 20–30 minutes at S1 and about 25–50 minutes at S2, so the resulting ranges admit implausible services. A5’s earliest-start rule also omits the queue-entry constraint \(\max(\text{resource free}, \text{arrival or S1 completion})\), even though some later notes use it. A3’s “rounding noise 5 min” is too vague to audit overlaps. A6’s “35 minutes more (90th percentile)” does not match the fully observed S2 sample: the upper tail is about 45–50 minutes total, not 35 extra minutes. Rounding is defined per timestamp and then applied inconsistently: some durations move by \(\pm 2.5\) and others by \(\pm 5\). Two independent \(\pm 2.5\)-minute stamps move a difference by up to \(\pm 5\).

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas
Waiting, service, sojourn \(W+S\), queue length, system WIP, and the C2 availability of 345 minutes are defined in the right direction. What is missing is a workable rule for propagating rounding through durations, a time-average WIP definition, subperiod capacity (the 10:55–11:10 outage sits in 10:00–12:00), and a statement of whether reported means include right-censored waits. Case-level total sojourn is named and then never reported. The 10:00–12:00 Little’s Law window is still a ramp, not a steady regime, and \(L\), \(\lambda\), and \(W\) are not tied to one system boundary.

# 3. Case-level reconstruction
Fully observed S1 facts are: P05 wait 35 and service 25; P11 wait 75 and service 25; P16 wait 100 and service 30; P17 wait 95 and service 30; P21 wait 100 with 30 minutes elapsed by 14:00; P22 censored S1 wait 110. The table instead gives P05 and P11 S1 waits as copies of their S2 wait triples, P16 as 55, P17 as 35, P21 as 35, and P22 as a dash. Those are large errors on cases the task required to be explicit.

The S2 bounds for P05 (start in the C1 gap after 09:45, end 10:15), P07’s observed S2 (wait 20, service 40), and P11 (start only after P08 releases C2 at 12:00) are in the right region. The triples are not joint scenarios: earliest start is short wait and long service. P15’s S1 wait upper of 125 implies a zero-length service, while the service lower bound of 5 implies a wait upper of 120; the A5 midpoint is 105, not 107.5. A6 is not applied consistently: P15’s open S2 service is 30 / 40+ / 60+, P17 is stuck at 5+, and P21 is 45+, none of which is elapsed+5 versus elapsed+35.

The aggregate claim is also false. For the listed “unremarkable” cases, S1 waits include 5, 5, 20, 20, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 85, 95, 100, 100, and 105. The median is about 65 minutes, not 30. P18–P20 are not fully observed: at 14:00 they are still waiting for S2, with censored S2 waits of about 50, 35, and 15 minutes.

# 4. Station-level metrics
The table conflicts with the event list and with itself.

- S1 arrivals are not 14 / 8 / 0. P22 arrives at 12:10, so the 12:00–14:00 bin is not empty. 14+8+0 accounts for 22 cases only by mis-binning that arrival.
- S1 completions 7+6+6 = 19, but P01–P20 finish S1: 20 completions. S2 completions 2+7+4 = 13, but P01–P14 finish S2: 14. By 10:00, S2 has already finished P01, P02, and P03, so “2” is wrong. S2 “arrivals = 0” in 08:00–10:00 drops the morning S1 completions that enter the S2 queue.
- S1 busy time of 320 minutes in a two-hour block exceeds two resources × 120 minutes = 240. S2 busy time of 330 minutes in 10:00–12:00 exceeds capacity of about 225 resource-minutes after the 15-minute C2 outage. Observed S2 work before 10:00 is about 145 resource-minutes (P01 30, P02 40, P03 40, and 35 minutes of P04), not 70.
- The utilization figures do not equal busy time divided by subperiod capacity. Example: 320/240 > 1, while the reported S1 band is 0.67–0.71; 70/360 ≈ 0.19, which only appears if a short-interval numerator is divided by a longer-window denominator.
- S1 mean wait of 38 minutes in 10:00–12:00 is far below the waits of cases that arrive then (about 95–105 minutes). Data coverage is overstated: missing starts are P07 and P15 at S1 and P05 and P11 at S2, which is two S2 starts, not three, and well under a “>95%” fully timestamped share.

A direct count also rejects the later peak-queue claim. With the stated central starts, the S1 queue peaks around 6–7, not 8 at 09:30 (S1 station inventory at 09:30 is about 6). The S2 waiting line peaks around 5, and S2 station WIP around 7, not 9 at 12:30.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation
The directional story is the one part that survives a recount: early external arrivals exceed S1’s roughly four cases per hour, the S1 queue builds while the S2 queue is still small, S2 service is longer (median about 40 versus about 30), and by 14:00 the residue is mostly at S2 (P15 and P17 open, P16/P18/P19/P20 waiting) with P21 open and P22 waiting at S1. That is a time-varying congestion picture.

The evidence attached to it does not hold. “Eight” cases at S2 from 12:15 is high: S2 occupancy then is about 4, and at 14:00 it is 6 including service. “S1 has no new inflow” after 12:15 ignores P22 at 12:10 and continued S1 work through P21. The 10:30 switch time is sharper than the rounded data can support. The C2 outage is discussed in one sentence. In the log it sits in the 10:50–11:15 gap after P06; P08 has been eligible since 10:20 and does not start until 11:15. Whether those 15 minutes bind, and by how much relative to a multi-hour backlog, is not tested. Capacity bottleneck (S2’s lower service rate whenever both stations are fed) is not separated from the early queue, which sits at S1 because of the arrival surge.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis
Three named scenarios are the right design. The claimed stable results are not. Bottleneck order is plausibly stable. “Mean service 25–30 at S1 and 35–45 at S2” is a fair reading of the fully observed durations. The peaks of 8 and 9 are already wrong in the central scenario, so they cannot be scenario-robust. The S2 utilization swing 0.71–0.93 and the “about 20%” Little’s Law break are asserted without recomputed \(L\), \(\lambda\), and \(W\). Section 6’s \(W \approx 1.5\) h also disagrees with section 4’s S2 mean wait of 28 minutes in the same interval: \(3.5 \times 1.5 = 5.25\) matches 5.5 only as arithmetic, not as a measurement on one definition of WIP and sojourn. Rounding sensitivity and an explicit with/without-outage comparison are absent.

# 7. Reproducible Python
The 22-row table is complete, markers stay distinct in `mk`, fully observed intervals are filtered before `pm4py.format_dataframe`, and the three required PM4Py calls are present. The script still fails the analysis requirement. Pandas builds only a central S1 excerpt, skips OPEN and blank S1 ends, never builds an S2 imputation, and never calculates queue length, WIP, rates, busy time, or the three scenarios. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is called and its columns are not printed. The interval log mixes two-station cases with S1-only cases (S2 missing, open, or not started), so case-level service, waiting, and arrival/finish rate on that projection are not valid case sojourns; the print label does not say that. The imputation mask compares `s1_end` across datetimes and the strings `OPEN` and `""` inside one vectorized expression, which raises `TypeError` under pandas. The comments that defend pandas sit outside the code block. Nothing in the script prints the tables in sections 3–6, so those numbers are not reproducible from the supplied code.
