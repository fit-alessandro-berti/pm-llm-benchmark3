The answer covers every required section and reaches several defensible case labels, but it is not a reliable conformance judgment. Dates, authority, risk, and model semantics are repeatedly inverted, and the revised POWL cannot accept the routes it claims to add.

Stipulated facts already conflict with the record and with the answer’s later sections. About 70% of notifications were mirrored and missingness rose from 2% to 29%; the brief calls this “70% missing,” then later cites 29%. SUP-704 on 2026-07-11 is inside FD-7, while SUP-705 on 2026-07-13 is after its automatic expiry. The answer first calls both “FD-7 compliance,” then “expired,” then justified exceptions. SUP-713’s mapping was approved a week after the case; equivalence is evidenced, but it is not an unqualified stipulated fact. The burden section collapses CS-9, M0, and data defects into one test and never states that an expired written directive needs a fresh written authority, or that missing proof yields an indeterminate result rather than either violation or excuse.

The prosecution is not the strongest available case. It treats SUP-702 (2026-06-15) and SUP-704 as expired, so it misses the real authority argument: PD-3 was never incorporated into CS-9, and the compliance observer later denied approving any weakening. It says M0’s concurrency “allows” or hides SUP-708. M0 orders both checks before the decision, so that trace violates M0. SUP-708 contains manual approval after activation; it does not bypass approval. Notify_Requester is attributed to CS-9, which does not state it. SUP-707 and the missing loop are charged as noncompliance even though sanctions, quality, approval, and activation remain in the required order. SUP-705 is described as lacking retrospective approval although the trace contains that approval; the missing item is an extension. The 289 eligible-looking pilot cases are called compliant. Internal Audit’s severe rating and the EUR 180,000 ineligible purchase are barely used. Required concessions about genuine model and logging defects are mostly recast as violations.

The defense does not meet its duty to admit real risk. SUP-703 is a high-risk new foreign supplier; calling it “low-risk (EUR 180K)” with “no harm” is false on the record and unproved on harm. SUP-708 is a real sanctions-after-activation failure, not a data artifact or “no actual risk.” Absence of spend is mitigation, and proof of earlier spend would worsen it, yet that is listed as what would reverse the finding. “Ambiguity favors operations” is an unsupported presumption. The observer’s “monitored exception” is used without the same source’s later denial. SUP-710 is called de facto compliant despite a missing quality review and no reactivation rule.

The matrix repeats those errors. SUP-712 would be reversed by proof that the aliases are different people or by valid delegation; disclosure is the wrong cure because CS-9 requires different humans. SUP-715 is the disclosure case, and it is not the requester/approver rule. SUP-705’s missing evidence is not “none”: daily monitoring and a written extension are not established. SUP-706’s mutable, late link is the issue; CS-9 is the wrong charging standard.

Final labels are directionally sound for SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-708, SUP-711, SUP-712, SUP-713, and SUP-714. They are still under-reasoned. SUP-702 is justified by an effective, satisfied pilot, not by persisting “intent,” and confidence should be high on the given eligibility snapshot. SUP-705 is the clearest unauthorized continuation; “the emergency still factually existed” is an operations assertion, and classifying it as a justified exception adopts informal practice as authority. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are too low at medium severity. SUP-715 can be noncompliant with the conflict-of-interest disclosure duty, but high confidence that it violates CS-9 is unwarranted. The system conclusion that the operation is “partially compliant” and the revision is a “middle ground” splits the difference. Rejecting the prosecution because intent matters and because no harm was shown rejects the control standard itself. The aggregate—14 ineligible auto-approvals, 9 unproved eligibility snapshots, 6 post-expiry auto-approvals, and two audit-severe cases—supports a systemic authority failure alongside a separate finding that M0 is incomplete.

The architecture names the right layers and then assigns them poorly. Eligibility is written as if it were a DECLARE constraint; amount, risk, country, corporate-group, and time limits belong in data checks tied to a versioned overlay. The rejected-lead rule would exclude registration even though SUP-709 contains registration. Withdrawals are absent. Resource rules omit alias resolution, delegation, and the distinct spouse/disclosure rule.

The Python model misses the required behavior. Standard sanctions and quality are concurrent, but that block precedes every route, so deferred quality is impossible. The auto route contains only Auto_Approve, with no activation. The emergency route uses Provisional_Activate rather than Activate_Supplier and omits retrospective approval and deferred quality, so SUP-704 would still fail. Request_More_Info is a self-loop on a partial order, which is not a valid POWL loop, and that fragment is never connected; `Operator.LOOP` is the defensible approximation. `pd` is undefined, and the separation check applies `not` to a Series. The accompanying table is a field list, not a normalized overlay with rule IDs, windows, authority, eligibility, caps, and non-renewal on expiry. Delegation evidence and separate immutable record time versus effective time are missing.
