The final recommendation is directionally sound: a conformant second-officer path is ranked first, immediate execution and self-certification are last, and regulatory exposure is treated as a hard constraint rather than a number that can be traded away. The six-section structure, conformance labels, and A/B prohibition are in place. Several analytical errors still undermine the ranking.

The top plan’s timing is not grounded in the given review duration. Action C states a review of 35–75 minutes, but the S4 plan assumes about 30–45 minutes. That invented shortening produces “approval/execution ~65–110 minutes,” “best case meets the deadline,” and, in the sensitivity section, “near-certain deadline feasibility” if S4 is free at 35 minutes. Using the stated 35–75 minutes, S4 at 35 minutes yields roughly 75–125 minutes to execution, so the deadline is met only on the optimistic tail. S4 at 65 minutes misses it. The central C-versus-D comparison therefore rests on an unsupported clock.

The expected-value step is also wrong. Multiplying 0.55–0.75 approval by €20k–€35k to get €11k–€26k treats approval as if it were on-time execution. The stated SLA-breach range of 0.48–0.64 is never applied, and “effects may not transport, so the expected benefit is €11k–€26k” does not follow. The same section calls the evidence request dominated, then ranks it third, above a fast conditional rejection, without comparing false-rejection harm to a near-certain €35k–€115k deadline-and-customer loss.

The fallback misfires. If alternate screening shows elevated risk or a true match, interrupting P-901 to chase a faster approval-oriented review is the wrong response; rejection, hold, or a report becomes more appropriate. The claim that a failed override always leaves too little time for a conformant review is too strong: a 20-minute failure still leaves about 70 minutes. Rule mapping is incomplete for C, D, and E, and B is tied only to four-eyes, not also to the missing independent second review and the invalid execution path. “Second officer still required” when the match persists implies that clearing the name alone could drop the review, even though the score remains at least 70.
