The diagnosis is well grounded and the three redesigns, trade-offs, comparison table, failure modes, and staged experiment are all present and mostly segment-specific. Several concrete errors keep it well short of nearly flawless.

The frequency reading is wrong. Auto eligibility → Fast approval is 2,550, but Fast approval also receives 240 cases from fraud/risk review, and its outflows sum to 2,790 (2,300 dispatch + 350 close + 140 complaint). “Of those, 2,300 (46%) dispatch” mixes denominators: 46% is of all 5,000 cases, while about 82% of fast approvals dispatch.

Redesign B conflates “Premium” with premium high-value. The cited 14% + 21% + 22% excludes premium low-risk accessories (18%), yet the routing text sends “Premium” onto repair-first, then later keeps low-risk accessories on the fast path. The 24-hour service-credit threshold is stated only for premium low-risk accessories and is misapplied to high-value repair delays. The 3.3% vs 14.1% and 5% vs 22% gaps are also over-attributed to repair fulfillment; the better outcomes are on a reviewed/repair bundle, so review, evidence, and risk control are not separated from the repair activity.

Redesigns A and C are not fully distinct. Both update the auto-screen, add the evidence portal, and hard-route high-value cases to manual or fraud review. C is largely A with sampling and lighter triage rather than a different operating logic. The table’s “−30–50 h” and “net likely favorable” cost claims are sharper than the data support.

The experiment has a design contradiction: Phase 1 pilots the evidence portal on standard low-risk cases that already have complete evidence, then uses reduction of the repeated-evidence rate as a go metric. That cohort does not generate the loop being measured, so the pilot cannot show whether the portal improvement is real.
