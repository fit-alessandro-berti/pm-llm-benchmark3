6.5

The write-up gets the main design right: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72 h, estimands defined before estimators, open cases kept as right-censored, and withdrawal/rejection/transfer not treated as ordinary censoring. Concrete IDs and the cohort/priority counts check out (13 resolved, 9 open, 4 withdrawn, 3 rejected, 3 transferred; A 3/15 P1 vs B 5/17 P1; open below 72 h are S10, S15, S24, S29, S31). Immortal time for `Escalate`, the non-causal reading of cohort B, and the primary Aalen–Johansen choice are all defensible. The manual CIF update order is the standard one for the primary “transfer = competing event” coding.

Those strengths do not make the analysis tight.

The long-gap sensitivity is the wrong mechanism. For an `open` row, `observed_status` and `duration_or_age_h` already say there was no terminal event through the cutoff. `last_event_gap_h` is inactivity inside observed follow-up, not loss of follow-up. Re-censoring at `duration − gap` (S09 at 61, S11 at 26, S25 at 31, S26 at 32) throws away known event-free time past 72 h. That is not a valid informative-censoring analysis. The gap is a marker for residual hazard; transfer is the actual informative-censoring problem, and the direction of that bias is never stated (hard cases leaving follow-up makes the remaining resolution CIF look too high).

The state diagram drops observed edges: `AutoCheck → Resolve` (S01) and `Rework → Resolve` (S27). Section 4 claims cumulative incidences for other terminal states, but section 2 and the code collapse withdraw, reject, and transfer into a single cause code 2, so those cause-specific CIFs are not identified. Grouping transfer with non-resolve does not change \(F_R(72)\), \(1-F_R(72)\), or active survival, yet it is listed as if it could reverse them.

The code mislabels the sensitivity it is supposed to demonstrate. Under `censor`, S21 (26 h) and S08 (68 h) leave the risk set without an event, so the printed `active_72` is not P(still active). Under `success`, those same transfers are counted in `cif_resolve_72`, so the label is no longer resolution. S32 at 96 h correctly does not move the 72 h contrast; that point is right, but the printed columns are not comparable across scenarios. There are no standard errors, bounds, or stratum sizes, so three-decimal risks overstate precision at n=32. `Post-treatment` is never assigned, and S32 is grouped with observed late resolutions (S03, S13, S20, S28) even though at 72 h it was still active.
