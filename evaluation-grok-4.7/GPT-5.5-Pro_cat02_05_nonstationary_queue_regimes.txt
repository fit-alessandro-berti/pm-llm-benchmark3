The segmentation, conservation checks, and most regime arithmetic are strong, but a headline pooled statistic is wrong and a few queueing claims are softer than the tables imply.

The primary partition is coherent and complete: all 28 days are assigned once, outage is not treated as ordinary low productivity, and 2026-04-16/17 catch-up is separated from the 2026-04-29 staffing change. Regime arrival, completion, net, WIP/FTE, and hourly Fano/CV figures check out, including baseline net +4, high-complexity weekday net +94, full-sample net +110, and the 2026-04-21 Fano near 9.3. The peak-column mismatch (for example 17/101 versus 31 on 2026-04-07), the refusal to treat four illustrative days as a fitted process, and the code’s PM4Py boundary are all sound. Little’s Law is correctly kept in opening-hour units and is not replaced by median active service.

The pooled “median service around 32.5 minutes” is not the all-days median. The 28 daily medians have median 30.5 and mean about 31.0; 32.5 is the median of the 20 weekday medians only. That figure is placed beside all-days rates of about 90 arrivals and 86 completions, so the pooling comparison mixes universes. Baseline completions are also stated as “100/day” and “8.30/h,” which do not match each other (697/7 = 99.6).

Threat 10 is directionally right but loosely aimed. Late WIP falls while post-intervention net flow is negative, so that decline does not contradict accumulation. The sharper conflict is cumulative net +110 with ending mean WIP back near the start, and +94 net over the high-complexity weekdays while mean WIP only moves from the high 30s to about 50.

Implied sojourn times are not stress-tested. Baseline L/λ ≈ 2.44 opening hours against a 25-minute median service and nominal load near 3.5 Erlangs on 6 FTE; that gap may be real (internal waits, mean ≫ median, shrinkage), but it is left unexamined. It is also unstated whether 15-minute WIP snapshots cover only 07:00–19:00. Post-intervention windows are labeled transient and then graded “approximate only,” which is lenient relative to the same standard used to reject recovery days. Service times were already elevated on 2026-04-16/17, before the complex-share jump, and that sequence is underused when interpreting the 2026-04-20 change.
