**7.4**

The case outcomes are largely the right ones, and the time model is sound. The formalization and the checker are not. Several rules are stated in a way that would misclassify unseen cases, and one alternative reading contradicts the answer’s own R4/R6 principle.

**What holds.** Forward-only state, blanks as “no update,” `(timestamp, event_index)` order, and the split among event, effective, record, and evidence time are correct. All eleven classifications match a careful reading: TX-301/303/304/307/309 noncompliant, TX-302/306/308/311 compliant, TX-305 indeterminate, TX-310 noncompliant because EX-88’s certificate post-dates release and the only witness is the requester. The 20% tests are right (TX-306 ≈4.5% keeps the review; TX-309 118k→145k ≈22.9% kills it; TX-308’s decrease does not; 100,000 still triggers R1). TX-307 correctly refuses to treat a standard-packet approval as an enhanced review. TX-311 correctly evaluates R1 on jurisdiction DE at release, after the same-timestamp jurisdiction write. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and logging fixes with real case anchors.

**Formalization.** The pseudo-logic does not define the direction of the temporal relations it is required to define. What remains is gaps: `jurisdiction  DE`, `amount  100000`, `er  rel`, `effective_from  time(rel)`, and `HIGH  PEP` with no operator. R3 is not a usable formula (“latest Sanctions_Screen before rel is u rel”). R2’s missing-data rule is wrong: indeterminate only when risk and customer type are both unknown silently treats one unknown as not HIGH / not PEP, which is the low-risk default R6 forbids. R1 is the opposite mistake: either unknown forces indeterminate even when the other conjunct is already false (known DE, or known amount under 100,000), so a trigger that can be excluded is not excluded. “Material data change” never gets a definition beyond the amount test, then R1 adds the circular clause “no other material change … that policy treats as resetting.” The R2 row drags in amount-dependent invalidation even though R4 limits invalidation to amount-dependent obligations. “Pre-review amount” is the wrong baseline; the comparator is the amount in force at the review.

**Case reasoning.** TX-305’s main label is right, and Section 1 correctly says an increase from an unknown amount is not classifiable. Section 4 then says a release payload of at least 100,000 would make the case compliant because an enhanced review exists and no post-review increase is known. That skips the review document with no amount and the unknown baseline. A later amount, with no update event, still does not show that the review covered it or that the move was within 20%. TX-310 is well supported as noncompliant, but the contradictory oral claim is never left open as indeterminate, which this evidence pattern specifically invites. TX-302’s “ignore the enhanced review” alternative is a counterfactual, not a reading of the log.

**Code.** The script has the requested shape: `StringIO`, timestamps, `event_index`, `format_dataframe`, forward-fill, release snapshots, exemption join, diagnostics, and a trace-order check that does not pretend to be R1–R5. It would likely coarse-label these eleven traces the same way as the narrative. It is not a reliable checker. Unknown R2 attributes default to not triggered. An enhanced review with an unknown amount baseline is marked not invalidated (`er_invalid = False`). Exemption scope treats a missing amount as within the cap. Sanctions ordering ignores `event_index`. Classification is last-assignment-wins, so a later rule can overwrite an earlier indeterminate. R4’s first computation is discarded; max-after-review is computed and then unused, so an intermediate spike above 20% that later falls back would pass. Evidence quality is pre-labeled STRONG/WEAK, which hard-wires TX-310 to noncompliant instead of `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. There is no material-change timestamp in the diagnostics the spec asked to track. Dead variables and a duplicated `r2_ok` make the control flow hard to audit.

**Smaller defects.** Compliant severities are “—”. TX-301 is framed as R4 invalidating a standard review, although a standard review never satisfied R1. Several cells are garbled (`SRER`, `100000100k`) where a relation was lost. Currency is assumed to be EUR without saying so.

The narrative judgment on this log is strong. The reusable rule statement and the program are not strict enough for a higher score.
