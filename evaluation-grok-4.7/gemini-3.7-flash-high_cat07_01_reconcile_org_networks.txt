6.2

The write-up is complete and often directionally right, but it is not reliable as a process-mining interpretation. Several headline quantities are misread or inflated, and those errors are repeated in the risk section and the executive summary.

The clearest validity failures are these. Case-count percentile 0.91 is treated as “91% of non-standard routing topologies.” That metric is a volume rank, not a coverage rate. “Nearly all” medical and legal reviews are said to pass through Mira; 126 and 98 subcontracting loops against Omar’s 316 cases and Priya’s 251 cases are about 40%, not nearly all. Her 905 cases (22% of volume) are equated with “claims requiring exception intervention,” even though only 48% of her own cases carry `exception_flag=True`. Bot_Rules’ 0.31 exception share is described as the bot producing a 31% exception rate. Bot_Ingest sits on essentially every case at 0.19, so 0.31 is not a production rate and is not even a clean global base rate. Bruno is assigned Reopen Claim, which is not in his activity profile. The claim that Mira’s profile overlaps neither pod leads nor adjusters is false: she shares Reopen Claim with Alice and Request Missing Information with Elise, Farid, and Temp_1.

Time scales are collapsed. Median service times of 4.2, 37, and 51 minutes, and handover gaps of 16–19 minutes, cannot explain 8.4- to 13.9-day cycle times. Calling those minute-scale gaps “substantial” waiting, and attributing her “high variance” cycle-time contribution to specialist service durations, confuses service time with sojourn time. “Completely unaffected” ordinary claims overstates a stable median. The working-together account is also off: 960/905 ≈ 1.06 events per case does not mathematically suppress a case co-occurrence metric, and the Jaccard formula is asserted as if it were the PM4Py definition. A direct-succession reading of the handover edges is likewise unexamined: listed in- and out-edges sum well above her 960 events, and the subcontracting loops imply more repeat touches than 1.06 events per case allows. Those figures can be discussed only as ordinal, possibly multi-counted, or internally inconsistent aggregates.

Calibration slips in the same direction. “Proves,” “zero structural redundancy,” “must queue twice,” unilateral discretion, and off-system telephone or document work are stated as findings. The evidence supports a concern and a dependency on exception queues during absence, not those mechanisms. M/M/1, a ~25-case/day threshold, a 2-hour alert, and a linear drop after rule changes are invented precision. The fairness gap (350/330 versus 290/280 handover counts) is too small for the workload story built on it. Hypothesis 3’s “contradicting” evidence is a control risk, not evidence against brokerage. The proposed regression of cycle time on Mira’s touchpoints never mentions confounding by `exception_flag`.

What keeps the score from falling further is coverage and judgment. All six required sections are present; the executive summary is inside 120–180 words; bots, temps, and vendors are included; centrality is not treated as simply good or bad; four hypotheses each have a mechanism, support, limits, and predictions; and the recommendation does not remove the coordination function without a backup. The absence result is used appropriately. The strategic advice — cross-train a deputy, log routing reasons, monitor exception queues, and do not automate or delete the desk yet — follows the evidence better than the overprecise numbers do.
