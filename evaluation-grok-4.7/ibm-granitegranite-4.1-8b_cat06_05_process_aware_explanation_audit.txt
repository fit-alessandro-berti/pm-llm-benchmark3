The audit has the required five-part shape and correctly flags several blatant post-outcome fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, the nightly document snapshot, and the prior-claim SQL defect). Those hits are outweighed by systematic mistakes on the distinctions the task exists to test.

Section 1 mislabels admissible prefix age as leakage, calls region a process state, and treats `queue_length_at_t0` as both “acceptable” and medium-severity with no fix for the ±5-minute window. The prior-claim defect is described as claims that close after `t0`; the stated bug is `claim_created_at <= extraction_date`, so the count can include claims created after `t0`. `initial_reserve` is declared critical leakage from an unsupported assumption that reserves are set only after assessment. `final_handler_team` is given a forward-looking repair even though the warehouse has no as-of timestamp. Severity for the same feature changes between the global and local rows.

The sequence audit fails the central contrast. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is rightly impossible or order-ambiguous, but the proposed fix is to reverse the edge and flip the sign. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is a normatively legal later transition (appeal only after Partial or Deny, then a second decision) and is simply not enabled at this `t0`; “appeal opened before `t0`” is process-impossible on the stated prefix. The other three patterns are future complete-trace edges at a prefix that is only `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`, yet they are marked acceptable, including a causal reading that assessment-to-decision is “faster.” Employee-experience as-of extraction is never audited. Duration is not identified as nearly the breach label itself.

Section 2 builds “allowed” transitions from a DFG, despite a frequent DFG edge not being a normative rule, and offers an alphabetical timestamp tie-break that can manufacture the impossible order. Enabled, future-possible, and observed-at-`t0` remain blurred, which is why Section 1 accepts future patterns that Section 2 would call leakage.

Section 3 double-files region and week as both clean predictors and proxies, treats a repaired prefix variant as informative even though every case is still on the same two-event prefix, and then says repaired features are only post-closure. That erases repairable point-in-time signals such as a corrected prior-claim count. SHAP is treated as a robustness test.

Section 4 is not a credible revision. Case X invents region and a 0.78 probability, drops the stated Broker channel, and parks a decreasing elapsed-time effect in “removed” while calling it retained. Case Y is marked “retain” while its dominant driver is the full future variant; queue length 4 is given the wrong sign (it contributed −0.05); complexity is invented; the count of 3 is “verified” after a SQL fix that was not run; and the narrative restates the leaked evidence-loop path. Case Z’s recomputation list adds features that were not in the case and puts a future assessment-to-decision pattern back in.

Section 5 names the right workstreams, then inherits those errors: historical stand-ins for irredeemable outcome features, conformance tests based on the weak DFG design, and a fairness framing for a policy/holiday week confound. The revised explanations would still ship leaked futures and fabricated local drivers.
