The final answer is a strong, constraint-respecting design: it separates the six duplicate classes, keeps e05 and e14, merges only e03/e04 and e11/e16 in the conservative path, preserves abstraction and original timestamps, and does not treat a total order as physical truth. Counts 17 / 15 / 14 / 12 and E2O 44 → 39 are right, the `have_common_object` warning is correct, and the audit schema and failure modes cover every required item.

It is not nearly flawless.

Same-source `source_seq` is mishandled. e13 and e14 are both PSP (401 then 402), so within-source sequence is a semantic order, not a mere tie-break. The answer calls them concurrent and then sorts them with a deterministic fallback. That fallback also compares `source_seq` across sources, which the problem says is meaningless.

Ordering constraints are dropped after aggressive merges. Folding e09 into e08 deletes `e09 < e10`; folding e15 into e13 deletes “summary after both captures,” so the merged e13 can sit before e14. Source priority happens to keep e08 before e10, but the semantic edge is gone. e08 < e09 is optional in the prose and unconditional in the code.

The implementation contradicts its own safeguard. `exact_groups` is computed and then ignored, and `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` is called on the whole OCEL. That is the global-duplicate failure mode listed in section 9. Evidence-based code also omits the label refinements the prose relies on, and the audit rows do not store the schema from section 8.

Sensitivity analysis is broad but imprecise. An Item-flattened log excludes e07; it does not leave a disconnected pack node. Aggressive E2O count (32, if identical relations are unioned) is never stated, and 37 is hedged as “about 37.” The prose also runs long past the 1,900–2,900-word aim.

Those are design and PM4Py errors, not presentation nits, so the score stays below 8.
