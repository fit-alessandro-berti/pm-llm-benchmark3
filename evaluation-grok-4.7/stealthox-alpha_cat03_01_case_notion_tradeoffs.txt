6.4

The write-up is specific, complete, and often numerically careful, but it is not close to flawless. Several precise claims contradict the event table, and one of those errors is expanded into a distortion and a validation check.

The order-row accounting is largely right: 42 case-event rows from 31 events, 11 extra copies, pack counts of 4 physical versus 6 order-level occurrences, invoice copies 3→4 and payments 4→6, O3 in 13 events, and the O2–O3 intersection {e13, e16, e19, e22, e24, e25}. Dispatch really does cover 3 events, 17 distinct objects, and 19 object mentions. Item traces are uniformly Add→Pack→Dispatch→Confirm (24 rows over 16 item events). Package, delivery, invoice, and payment row counts (12, 6, 7, 4) are right, as are D2/D3 transit times (4h55m, 22h45m) and the V1/V3 cash cycles. Position B is argued with a real counterargument, and the required portfolio and sensitivity categories are all present.

The serious miss is e23. It is 2026-02-03 14:00, not 12:00. That single misread produces D1’s “60 min” transit (actual 11:00–14:00 = 3h), P1’s “10:00→12:00” doorstep time (actual 4h), and I11’s “09:15–12:00 = 2h45m.” I11’s last event is e23 at 14:00 (4h45m); 12:00 is e18, which is not even related to I11. D9’s same-timestamp tie, the claim that an OC-DFG would show those events as concurrent, the code comments, the residual risk, and S5 are all built on a collision that does not exist. No order case has an intra-trace timestamp tie. The bidirectional Record Payment↔Confirm Delivery edges are real, but only because O2/O3 run e22→e24→e25, not because of e18/e23.

Other inaccuracies stack up. Convergence for orders omits e27 in §1 and then “eight shared events” in §5, while S1 correctly weights e27: the shared-order set has nine events. Divergence is called the standard definition and then described as lifecycle fragmentation across another type. Under the usual object-centric meaning, divergence is highest on Order (repeated Add/Pack/Dispatch/Confirm forced into one sequence); the matrix rates it Low. S6 calls e13/e14/e16/e17/e27 the five multi-order events and then a “financial trio (e19, e22, e24, e25)”: that is four identifiers, e24 is a delivery confirmation, and O3’s multi-order set has nine events once e24 is included. The partial-payment window 12:40→09:00 is 20h20m, not 20h55m (20h55m is e19→e25). “20 objects” drops the four payments (24). The multi-object groupby does not isolate same-type convergence; it flags almost every event except e01–e04 and e28–e31, so the printed expectation is wrong. “Four variants purely from consolidation” overclaims: O2 versus O4 differs by the second instalment. “Spurious” self-loops also mix genuine repeated events (two Add Item events) with copied shared events.

The structure, identifier discipline, and object-centric portfolio would support a high score. The fabricated tie and the repeated wrong durations are material in a task whose point is not to invent structure, so the result stays well below that.
