8.7

The case outcomes are right, and the temporal model is strong. All eleven release states are reconstructed correctly, blanks are treated as “no update,” same-timestamp order follows `(timestamp, event_index)`, and settlement/final values are not copied backward. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are properly NONCOMPLIANT. TX-306 and TX-308 correctly keep reviews that a ≤20% increase or a decrease does not invalidate, including the inclusive EUR 100,000 trigger. TX-302 is properly COMPLIANT on the enhanced-review path, with EX-77’s signature time distinguished from the 11:00 insert. TX-305 and TX-310 are properly INDETERMINATE. TX-311 is properly COMPLIANT because release jurisdiction is DE. Sections 4, 6, and 7 cover the required alternatives, at least six control-flow failure modes, and the requested logging changes.

The formalization is a notch too wide. R4 defines amount invalidation as a strict increase of more than 20% from the reviewed baseline. Section 1 also treats a later change of risk or customer type as invalidating an R1 packet. Those attributes drive R2, and that extension is not required by R1’s jurisdiction-and-amount guard. It does not flip any given verdict, but the code implements it: any known change in those fields sets `material`, and `r1_review_ok` becomes false when that key is after the enhanced review. On TX-311 the jurisdiction move to DE therefore makes `r1_review_ok` false even though R4 leaves the packet unstale and the R1 guard itself is false. The verdict stays COMPLIANT only because implication short-circuits. That diagnostic is misleading.

Smaller defects keep it short of nearly flawless. The rule table’s R1 ordering reads as if a material change must exist; the pseudo-logic’s vacuous case is the accurate one. `r ≼ m ≼ t` also fails a change that shares the review’s key. R5’s exclusive expiry is an extra stipulation, harmless only because neither release falls on expiry. “Ordinary review” in the TX-302 evidence cell means the non-exemption path, which is easy to misread as Standard_Review. Severity is uniformly “High” or “None,” with no stated scale. The checker meets the main PM4Py/pandas obligations and its verdict rule matches the narrative, including JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only when the waiver is doing the work. It does not export a latest `Sanctions_List_Update` beside the other required activity markers, links approvals with a `"standard"` note search, and, although item 8 is optional, never shows a coarse trace-order check beside the section 6 explanation.
