7.5

The design is strong on the hard constraints: endogenous waiting versus service, calendar-aware capacity, non-stationary and cross-case effects, latent objections, mechanism-level policies, and no claim of optimality. It is not nearly flawless. Several statistical, conformance, and implementation inconsistencies are material.

The arrival rule is unclear and easy to apply backwards. “Use a non-homogeneous Poisson model only if overdispersion tests support it; otherwise use negative binomial or a Cox/cluster process” does not define what the test must support. Equidispersion favors a Poisson intensity; overdispersion favors negative binomial or a Cox process. The count model \(N_{d,a,k}\sim\mathrm{NegBin}(\lambda_{d,a,k},\phi)\) never defines \(d\), \(a\), \(k\), \(\lambda\), or \(\phi\).

Process-mining grounding misses a direct conflict in the supplied materials. Under PM4Py loop semantics, `*(A, 'Completeness rework')` makes `Completeness rework` an observed redo activity. P001 repeats `Completeness check` with no such event, so the literal tree does not fit the excerpt. The write-up discusses alignments only generically and never makes that diagnosis. Appeal re-entry is also named but not specified: which activities repeat, what the council calendar does, and when rejection occurs.

The skeleton is mostly faithful to PM4Py, and the completion-only gap is correctly left as missing service and waiting rather than treated as service. Routing is not coherent: `required_reviews` resamples residual fire and environmental branches on every call, so one case can change route between scheduling and the decision join. `build_resources` accepts a policy and ignores it, so D4 never changes inspector capacity. Monday’s clerk reduction, council-week legal closure, and the two simple-renovation-only planners are stated in prose and absent from the calendars. D3 is district batching, but `inspection_group_key` is district plus architect, which blocks cross-architect geographic trips. Common random numbers are oversold: shared global streams desynchronize once a policy changes the number of draws. Per-case streams are the design that would actually preserve latent difficulty.

Decision rules conflict. SLA compliance is a selection gate, but no case-type duration threshold is defined. Absolute “no growing backlog” can reject an intervention that improves an already unsustainable baseline. A confidence-interval half-width of 5% of the estimated effect is circular and collapses when the effect is small. Fire “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is implemented as two people on those days without stating that headcount interpretation.

Warm-up for a seasonal system, paired uncertainty, censored tails, snapshot transition runs, and the explicit objection proxy are all sound. Those strengths do not remove the conformance miss, the non-idempotent router, or the metric and calendar gaps.
