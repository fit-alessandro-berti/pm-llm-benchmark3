The four headline labels are defensible, and several decision rows are real tests rather than a retelling of start order. The write-up still fails the benchmark’s main standard: at the points where policies are observationally close, predictions are wrong, internally inconsistent, or treated as proof.

**QA.** Appointment-on-due-time plus non-preemptive FIFO is a fair reading. A03 at 08:20 is the only choice that appointment uniquely predicts (FIFO wants A02, priority and SPT want A04). That episode is buried. The table’s “strongest support” is 08:30, which supports the FIFO component only. A06 starting at 09:20 is listed as the strongest contradiction and then correctly explained by A05 still running, so it is not residual evidence against the stated rule. The rule is sharper than written: at 09:05 A06’s 09:15 appointment is not yet due, A05’s 15-minute estimate overruns it, and A07’s 9-minute estimate would finish before 09:15. The server starts A05 anyway. QA does not reserve capacity ahead of an appointment. The 08:58 row omits A08 (arrived 08:40, priority 1, estimate 25), who is eligible with A04–A07. A07 and A08 never appear. The clean anti-priority, anti-SPT decision at 09:05—A05 over A08 and over shorter A07—is missing, while 08:58 is non-diagnostic because FIFO, priority, and SPT all select A04. A1’s calibration from 08:52–08:58 is never named. Confidence 95 and the word “proving” are far too strong for one order-changing appointment. The identifiability cell restates “FIFO with appointment overrides,” which is the hypothesis itself. Every QA realized duration equals `est_min`, so a noisy-estimate escape for SPT is not available in these data.

**QB.** “Priority 1 can preempt; otherwise FIFO,” with 09:48 as a dispatcher exception, is one of the better compact hypotheses. The reconstruction does not actually establish it. The informative epochs are missing: 10:00 selects B05 over B01 and B04 (priority 1 beats FIFO and SPT once B03 completes), 10:14 resumes B01 over B06 (priority 2) and over shorter B04, and 10:36 starts B06 rather than overdue, shorter B07. The 10:28 row calls B07’s 10:30 appointment “late.” At 10:28 it is two minutes early. If B07 is ineligible until 10:30, SPT also selects B04, so that row does not separate FIFO from SPT. B02’s preemption is also what preemptive SPT would do; the “urgent arrival” reason is the evidence that breaks the tie, and it is not used that way. Both priority-1 stops occur two minutes after arrival (09:14→09:16 and 09:46→09:48). That parallel weakens a story in which B05 was still invisible at 09:48.

The serious error is the counterfactual. With B05 removed, the visible set is B01, B03, and B04. FIFO selects B01. Strict priority selects B03. Section 2 already says FIFO predicts B01 at 09:48. Section 4 then says a scan delayed to “09:48:05” would make B03 the FIFO choice. Section 5 says that same story would solidify “priority 1, else FIFO.” It would do the opposite: it would make 09:48 evidence for ordinary priority and against this hybrid. The interruption text already says “dispatcher reassignment,” so the exception is documented, not undocumented, and it should outrank a speculative scan delay. Second-level timestamps are also invented; the prompt’s resolution is one minute.

**QC.** FIFO with C01 held out is the right primary reading, and 10:32 is the right exhibit: C04 beats C05 (priority and earliest deadline) and C06 (shortest estimate). The first two starts do not show this. They match SPT as well, and earliest-deadline-first does not even predict C02 (deadline 14:00); it predicts C03. Later choices that would lock the diagnosis are absent. At 11:34 C06 is taken over C07 despite C07’s priority 2 and 12:00 deadline. At 11:40 C01 is finally taken over C07, which is exactly FIFO once C01 is eligible and which causes C07 to miss 12:00. The policy does not flip when the SLA is already lost. “Missing parts or customer not ready” is an unlabeled invention; the note only says C01 was oldest and last among the initial backlog. Realized service again equals every estimate, including C05’s 40 and C01’s 36, so C01 was not skipped for being the longest job: C05 is longer and runs earlier.

The QC discrimination test does not discriminate. A job arriving at 10:04 with estimate 50 still loses to C02 under both SPT and FIFO-excluding-C01. The claim that FIFO would then prefer that job to C03 is a later, different comparison, and it is not the next-job prediction at 10:10.

**QD.** Per-resource FIFO, hard skill filters, and D06’s exact 11:30 start are well supported. D-M’s 11:25 choice of D04 over D08 is a true rejection of both priority and SPT inside the eligible set. D05’s 30-minute estimate against a 24-minute gap from 11:36 to 12:00 is also real. Calling that bin-packing overclaims it. Bin-packing would fill the idle interval. D10 is electrical, estimate 12, and has been waiting since 11:25; D-F is qualified and free from 11:36 until 12:00 and does not take it. The same gap appears on D-X from 11:20 to 11:30: D08’s 8-minute estimate fits before D06, and D07 cannot go to D-M, but neither fact is in the decision table. “Schedules perfectly explain the sequence” and “priority and SPT are masked” contradict the answer’s own 11:25 and 11:48 rows, which falsify both. Skill is an eligibility filter here; once it is applied, sequencing is testable. Confidence 95 is not earned. QA’s appointment behavior and QD’s appointment hold are also different mechanisms, and the synthesis never compares them.

**Cross-cutting.** Format and coverage of the seven sections are met. Confidence is uniformly rhetorical. “Proving,” “perfectly,” and “strictly” are used where two rules still agree or where the listed contradiction has been explained away. Both required counterfactuals are invalid, and the QB mistake is repeated in the exception ranking. The dangerous-inference paragraph is the best synthesis point, with one false detail: C02’s deadline is 14:00, not an early deadline. The collection plan is relevant, but item 6 treats an SPT-to-FIFO switch as open after the same answer has already assigned QC confidence 90 and secondary component “none.”

The policies a manager would copy from the summary table are mostly the right ones. The reasoning that is supposed to justify those labels, rank exceptions, and say what evidence would change them is not reliable.
