The primary state calls are largely right, and C6-A versus C6-B is differentiated for the right reasons: high severity, injury, a shared lot, and a reportability-oriented triage path block Fast Close and keep Technical Investigation, Regulatory Assessment, and a possible Supplier Analysis open, while C6-B’s low severity, single device, no injury, and 0.93 duplicate similarity support a non-investigation closure. Obligations, unknowns, and the DFG caveat are mostly disciplined, and the intervals avoid fake precision.

The repeated failure is the distinction the task requires between a process-enabled action and the next logged event. `process_enabled` is true on all 15 predictions, including steps the current prefix still blocks. On C6-B, Fast Close (0.12–0.40) and Close — No Safety Issue are ranked as next events even though Evidence Received was only at 12:16, the feed is normal, and the normative next completion is Evidence Review Completed. Fast Close is also ranked above the post-review closure even though the case already passed the S1 fast-close point and entered an evidence cycle. On C6-E, Customer Evidence Requested at 0.12–0.32 is the successor of Evidence Review, not of an Evidence Received event 40 minutes old.

C6-C and C6-D compound that error. Authority Notification is not enabled until Regulatory Assessment concludes “reportable,” disposition is recorded, and the 16:00 batch runs. Both cases still give it roughly 0.08–0.32 as the next log line. C6-C’s feed is normal, so a story in which notification overtakes two missing predecessors is weak. C6-D’s migration flag is used to justify reordering of later events, but not the more specific trap: a delayed Customer Evidence Requested, Evidence Received, or Evidence Review Completed can be the next analytical event because an attachment exists in the source system and 8–15% of recent completions can lag by up to 6 hours. C6-D’s midpoint probabilities also sum to about 1.05, so the top three are not a coherent distribution.

Queue facts are underused. C6-A’s Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment ranges ignore the 7-item investigation queue, one engineer being out, the 12-item regulatory queue, and 2–14 hour regulatory service times. C6-C treats Regulatory Assessment as likely already in progress and prioritised, without a worklist opening and only 2.25 hours after Technical Investigation. C6-E correctly makes Supplier Analysis mandatory for a six-complaint lot, but leaves Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment as a later unknown despite that cluster being a possible reportability signal under rule 4.
