4.5

The audit correctly flags the obvious complete-trace and as-of defects (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, the extraction-date SQL for prior claims, the nightly document snapshot, mutable `risk_score_current`, and complete-case `last_resource_role`), and it correctly keeps queue length and registration-time complexity. That is not enough. Several conclusions invert the normative process or treat post-`t0` patterns as admissible early explanations.

The pattern audit is the central failure. At this `t0` the prefix is only registration then coverage check, so none of the five cited patterns has occurred yet. `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is the ordinary conforming path, not a “process improbability,” and nothing in the evidence says it is a “fast sequence.” `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is the allowed reconsideration edge after Partial or Deny; calling it backwards because “appeals are downstream of decisions” confuses that edge with Decision → Appeal. It is still future information and not a cause of breach. `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is a normal later cycle, not an early prefix signal, and a negative association is not “efficiency.” `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is future-possible and permitted, not a prefix deviation, so it cannot be retained as a `t0` explanation. The same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern is over-called as definite impossibility; the missing sequence index means the order itself is unresolved.

Coverage and consistency are incomplete. `final_decision` is absent from the required table despite being local outcome leakage. Severity `None` is outside the specified scale. `initial_reserve` is treated as known at registration although its lineage is not given. `claimant_channel` is minimized as low-risk, then dropped from the case write-up. Region is correctly kept for fairness review in the table, then removed in Case Y precisely because it is a proxy, which contradicts the instruction not to delete correlated features on that ground. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is diagnosed and then omitted from Case Y’s removed drivers. Case X drops the admissible negative `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` signal. Revised interpretations still say congestion is “leading to” delay and complexity “increases” breach risk, against the stated causal boundary. `total_case_duration_hours` is not identified as near-label leakage, and the 65% overlap that masks `final_handler_team` is missed.

Sections 2, 3, and 5 restate the requested checklist without making it operational. Enabled, future-possible, and impossible activities are not separated correctly; appeal is used as the example despite not being enabled at `t0`. DFG frequency is never distinguished from a normative rule. Claim limits are not stated per interpretation class, and queue length is filed as a generic predictor while post-`t0` edges are filed as process state. The validation plan is unprioritized and does not specify point-in-time joins, prefix-contained explanations, or how badly the published probabilities must move once leakage is removed.
