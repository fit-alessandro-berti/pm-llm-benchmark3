The final assessment is well calibrated and meets the required structure. It treats the log as a mixture, keeps Lena/Kim–V-77x as the only high-concern cluster, separates emergency rush channels and routine volume from that cluster, and does not accuse anyone of fraud. Sections 2, 3, 5, and 6 are concrete, and the report wording stays inside 200–300 words while distinguishing control exceptions from misconduct.

It is not nearly flawless. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` returns cases that respect four-eyes (different resources). The code labels that output as the violation set and then reuses it for the legacy-ERP comparison, so an implementer would review the complement of the true exceptions. The stated emergency exception allows same-day approval with later justification by someone else; it does not waive same-person Create PO + Approve PO. Treating that exception as a reason the 42 cases may be compliant overstates the rule. The 16% emergency rate belongs to the 44-case Lena/Kim subgroup, not demonstrably to the 9 same-person bank/payment cases. The quarter-close upload shows that 12 of 17 justification timestamps were batched; it does not show that the same-person resource attribution is false, yet section 1 leans that way. Late retrospective justification (about 18% and 21% on the two emergency pairs) is a real control break and is missing from the indicator table. Smaller slips: H4 calls the non-batch cases “genuine” self-justification, section 6 calls the Lena–Kim loops “correction loops,” and the 30-minute filter is equated with the given 16-case fast subgroup.
