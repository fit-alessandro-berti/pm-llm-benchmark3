4.5

The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape, avoids branding the four named unmodeled relations as violations merely for being off the edge list, and correctly treats cancel-then-dispatch (N3, 2 orders), cobirth (T6, 1), and the sensor–invoice link (E10, 1) as the sharpest signals. That is not enough to carry the analysis. Foundational semantics are inverted, several mandatory gaps are missed or attached to the wrong evidence, and the closing uncertainty statement swaps the two cases the prompt most clearly distinguishes.

Section 1 does name the seven deviation classes and the open- versus closed-world split, but the operative rule is wrong. It says that, for both forbidden and mandatory relations, absence implies noncompliance. For a forbidden relation, noncompliance is presence; absence is conformance. The example immediately after that is also backwards: an observed mandatory `Create Order -> Dispatch Package` edge would be conformance, not a violation, and that edge is not even in the norm. Typical-pattern absence is then flattened into “not a violation,” so the section never actually separates the classes it lists. N2’s “17 missing” also assumes a denominator of 100; the summary reports 83 orders with `Validate Order -> Close Order`, not 83 of 100.

The findings table has the requested columns and correctly ranks N3 first, keeps reopen (N6, 1), codeath (7), and `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) at low severity, and records a data-quality alternative for the sensor edge. It is still an incomplete inventory. Missing as rows are T6 itself, the N5 split (92 of 110 paid, 4 write-offs, 14 with no successor), `Validate Order -> Close Order` (83), T5 (3), `Manual Compliance Review`–Customer (3), and the E2 count gap (Add Item–Order 1448 versus Add Item–Item 1450). Deviation-class labels do not match the seven classes from section 1. `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3) is cited as N1, and `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` is cited as N2; neither norm governs that edge.

The carrier-reference fact is systematically misread. The prompt ties the carrier attribute to two `Dispatch Package` events that lack a Delivery object (E6: 198 of 200; T2 weight 200). The answer attaches that attribute to the separate N4 gap (Pack → Dispatch, 198 of 200) and describes those packages as dispatched without a packing record. A directly-follows gap does not establish a missing Pack event. That mistake is repeated in the table, the norm-gap list, and the remediation plan, and it produces a bad proposed change: relax mandatory packing because of evidence that concerns Delivery. The N1 lesson about “eventually” versus directly-follows is not applied to N4, which is the same abstraction risk.

Section 3 only partly does the required work. The N1 directly-follows warning is real and useful, and codeath is correctly left unregulated because the norm is silent. The subsections do not state, for each family, what the abstraction can establish, what it cannot, how counts and scope change the reading, or how one relation can conform in one family and look suspicious in another. The 200 Package–Delivery interactions versus 198 Dispatch–Delivery links, and the 1450 versus 1448 Add Item links, are exactly those cross-family cases and are never used. E8’s unlabeled payment is called a data-quality hypothesis; the prompt supports a labeling and scope gap (finance says unapplied cash is possible; the event is not typed that way), while the suspected join error is the sensor edge.

Section 4 places N3 under mandatory behavior even though it is forbidden. The mandatory list records the 98 conforming creations and calls the 198/200 pack-dispatch pattern “conforming (qualified)” instead of assigning the two gaps. It never states the condition that would move E10, E8, N1, or N4 between buckets. The claim that missing `Validate Order -> Close Order` is “due to” invoicing or write-off is unsupported: write-off is an invoice relation, and 72 invoice edges plus 83 close edges are not a partition.

The code sketch calls all six named functions, keeps the diagnostic objects, and adds a separate semantic layer with a comment that status metadata is not inside the bare graph. That meets the mechanical requirement. The layer only classifies two OC-DFG edges, invents a deviations schema, and never shows forbidden, typical, and unspecified metadata being maintained for OTG and ET–OT as well.

Five of the six norm gaps are legitimate: “standard” order, unapplied-cash typing, external-shipment intent, codeath silence, and identifier collision. The carrier-versus-pack gap is not; it rests on the misread evidence and recommends the wrong status change. The remediation sections are properly separated and rightly refuse to escalate reopen, codeath, and manual review just because they are rare. Priorities disagree with the table: the table’s critical pair is N3 and E10, while containment prioritizes N3 and T6, which the table omitted. Containment of N3 is justified; “pause any new orders” for a single cobirth edge is stronger than one observation supports.

Section 8 has the four required uncertainty slots and then fills them backwards. It calls E10 a robust forbidden conclusion even though the prompt marks that edge as a suspected join on a reused identifier. It assigns E8 to data quality even though the stated doubt is an unlabeled but possibly legitimate exception. N1’s dependence on an incomplete norm is right. What graph summaries still cannot decide is understated: object-level traces for the two N3 orders, whether a carrier attribute can satisfy E6, the 1448/1450 Add Item gap, and whether the 14 silent invoices are open terms or missing events.
