6.5

The case-level interval reconstruction is mostly right, and the required section structure is met, but several station-level claims contradict the same data, Little’s Law is misapplied, and the Python does not actually compute the queue/WIP results the prose reports.

The feasible windows for the four missing starts are correct under single-server, non-preemptive nominal timing: P07 S1 [09:25, 10:00], P15 S1 [11:40, 12:15], P05 S2 [09:45, 10:15], P11 S2 [12:00, 12:25]. The case table’s waits, services, elapsed OPEN times, and censored S2 waits match those bounds (P16’s elapsed wait is 85, P21’s elapsed service is 30, P22’s elapsed wait is 110). Sojourn-versus-service is defined properly, OPEN cases are kept as elapsed service and end-of-window WIP, and the 14:00 snapshot (S1 WIP 2, S2 WIP 6) matches the status column. Subperiod arrival and completion counts are internally consistent with bins [08:00, 10:00), [10:00, 12:00), [12:00, 14:00): S1 13/6, 8/7, 1/7 and S2 6/3, 7/5, 7/6. Full-window busy ranges [535, 605] and [515, 570] on 720 and 705 available minutes are arithmetically correct for the stated imputation extremes, including elapsed OPEN work.

Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors.

The “13 fully observed COMPLETE cases” are the 11 cases listed in parentheses. Their S1 waits sum to 530, so the mean is about 48 minutes, not about 52. The sequence is also not steadily increasing (P13’s wait is 80 after P12’s 85).

S2 completions by subperiod are 3, then 5, then 6. Throughput rises through the outage window. The statement that S2 throughput dips in the middle is false on the answer’s own counts.

For later completed cases, S1 waits stay larger than S2 waits (P12–P14: 85/45, 80/50, 95/65). The claim that later completed S2 waits are systematically longer than contemporaneous S1 waits is the opposite of the table and is then used as bottleneck evidence.

Total station WIP does not depend on the missing starts. With ends processed before arrivals at tied nominal times, S1 WIP peaks at 8 and averages about 5.8 over the 360 minutes; S2 peaks at 7 (13:45–13:50) and averages about 3.7. The reported bands of roughly 8–10 and 6–8, and time-averages of 4–6 and 3–5, invent uncertainty and overshoot the peaks. S1 remains the larger stock through late morning (about 7 versus S2’s 3–5 around 11:00). S2’s stock exceeds S1’s only after about 12:50. “S2 becomes dominant after approximately 10:00” is too early. “Mild” also undersells the early S1 buildup from 0 to 7.

The outage does not force P08 to start at 11:15. P08 has been waiting since 10:20, and C2 is free at 10:50–10:55 and again at 11:10; 11:15 is the usual 5-minute nominal gap after the outage. Fifteen lost resource-minutes cannot account for a residual S2 queue of 6. With service near 40 minutes, two calibrators are short of the work released by S1 over a six-hour horizon even without maintenance. That structural gap is missing.

The Little’s Law paragraph uses an early S1 wait of 0.4–0.5 h against an arrival rate of 6.5/h to get L ≈ 3–3.5, then calls that consistent with early WIP. Waiting time is the wrong W for total WIP, 0.4–0.5 h is too small for the 13 early arrivals, and early S1 WIP itself averages about 5.5 and ends near 7. The check fails the answer’s own trajectory. Startup, the outage, and right-censoring are fair reasons to refuse a later steady-state application; the one interval they keep is still computed incorrectly.

Calling the earliest-start scenario both “optimistic” and “preferred central” blurs the reporting. The dominant pattern is a 5-minute nominal gap, which would place those starts near 09:30, 11:45, 09:50, and 12:05, not on the zero-gap boundary. Full-window utilization of about 0.74–0.84 also folds in pre-load idle and those systematic gaps, so it reads as spare capacity while the resources are essentially busy whenever a queue exists. Per-resource figures would have shown that directly. Service times of 0 remain inside the headline utilization interval even though the text later calls them implausible.

The code parses MISSING, OPEN, and blank differently, builds an interval frame, and calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`. It does not compute queue length or WIP at all, despite requirement 5 and despite the WIP numbers in section 4. Known busy time is hardcoded as 535 and 515. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied to a start/complete lifecycle frame that has no `start_timestamp`, while the interval frame that has one is not used; that call is not a valid demonstration on the object the function expects. The COMPLETE-only projection also drops external arrival, so any PM4Py “waiting time” is only the S1-to-S2 gap. Censoring is turned into a fake completion at 14:00 inside the interval log. Subperiod rows leave utilization blank and describe WIP only qualitatively.

What survives is the case-level missingness algebra, the rise in nominal waits, high nominal occupancy, a real late accumulation at S2, and the fact that the four imputations do not move the 14:00 snapshot. What does not survive is the middle-period throughput dip, the S2-versus-S1 wait comparison, the stated WIP peaks and averages, the Little’s Law numbers, the timing of the bottleneck shift, and the attribution of the residual queue to the 15-minute outage.
