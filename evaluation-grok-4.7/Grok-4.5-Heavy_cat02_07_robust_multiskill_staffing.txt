4.5

The write-up covers every required section and much of the component arithmetic is auditable, but the decision-relevant synthesis is inconsistent with that arithmetic, and the code does not implement the policies or mechanisms it claims to test.

The mixture means are right (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.5, Validation 15.74), as are the doubled-tail U4 means. Block “capacities” match dedicated time divided by each multiplier, including breaks and shift edges, and the base offered-load table matches arrivals times mix times those means. U1 morning scaling, the U3 software-capacity vector, and software B5 at about 1.03 are real results of that accounting.

Those skill capacities are then treated as simultaneous. They are mutually exclusive upper bounds on the same minutes. Ada’s hour cannot be Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, and Validation at once. Summing the author’s own day loads gives about 2722 base-minutes against about 2490 staffed minutes (plus at most 180 minutes of overtime). Software load (495) already exceeds Emil’s 450 minutes at multiplier 1.0, so at least some software is done at 1.25 or 1.40. Minimum person-time therefore exceeds staffed time plus overtime before handover, setup, coordination, or preemption. A correct pool lower bound is already near saturation in the late morning and well above 1.0 after 16:00, not “everything except software 16:00–18:00 and mechanical after 18:00 is under 0.60.” The 8–18% penalty uplift is asserted with no calculation. The 0.90 planned-utilization constraint in Obj1 is infeasible under the author’s own totals, and that infeasibility is never stated. Missed consequences include an unbounded multi-day backlog and mechanical work after 18:00 that no on-shift resource can do.

Section 4 then contradicts Section 2. Hybrid is given base maximum utilization ≤ 0.92, and ≤ 0.95 under U3/U4, while the same answer has software block loads at or above the only qualified capacity. Routing cannot manufacture Emil-hours after Chen and Farah have left. SPEC is scored “High” fairness in scenarios whose utilization the problem statement itself defines as unfair (any individual above 0.90). The opening claim that P2/P3 SLAs are almost always safe is contradicted by later “Low” cells. U2’s “ρ ≈ 0.52 → 0.62” is a B4 electrical figure pasted onto B3/B4; B3 electrical is about 0.37 before the shock. U3’s “morning electrical capacity falls ≈ 40%” is true for 08:00–10:00 and about 29% for 10:00–12:00. Resilience is defined as P1 SLA ≥ 85% in four of five shocks and then not applied to the U5 row. The table is also not produced by the code; it is a separate qualitative artifact.

The urgency score is specified backwards: `max(0, target − elapsed − E[remaining]) / target` is remaining slack, and it is added, so looser cases outrank tighter ones. Assumptions A2 and A5 are not what the program does. Counts are rounded expectations, not Poisson. U5 delays a few arrival timestamps; it does not stop Intake during 09:30–10:00. Preemption adds 10% to the P1 activity itself instead of charging 10% of remaining time to an interrupted lower-priority diagnostic, and it fires even when nothing is interrupted. Cases are walked to completion in arrival order and lock resources into the future, so priority never reorders a queue. Parallel rules in code ignore the stated free-resource, 20-minute, and critical-path tests; both branches may be given to one person; if the pair is unavailable, `continue` drops the diagnostics and still validates. Setup is charged from Intake/Validation, not only between diagnostic families on consecutive cases. Service runs through breaks and past shift end. A mechanical case still open after 18:00 exhausts the empty-eligibility fallback and indexes an empty list. `hash(scenario)` is process-randomized and can be negative. Two seeds are averaged with no dispersion. PM4Py is called inside broad `try/except` blocks, so a failed `get_service_time` or handover discovery still looks like a completed analysis, and case-level service/waiting is not actually split from stage queue time.

Sections 3, 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest: eligibility, reservation, and overflow are concrete; gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, and validation bunching are discussed; the event attributes and the pilot’s P1 abort rule match the brief. Those sections cannot carry a recommendation whose dominance claim rests on utilizations the capacity section has already shown routing cannot achieve.
