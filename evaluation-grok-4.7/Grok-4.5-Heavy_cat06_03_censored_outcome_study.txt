6.5

The design correctly centers the estimand on matured activated accounts, refuses to code rejects and withdrawals as non-defaults, partitions all 48,000 cases, and prefers a start-time split over a random one. Those choices are the right core. The write-up then contradicts that core, blurs censoring with undefined outcomes, and ships a skeleton that does not implement the cohort or prefix rules it states.

**Target.** The primary estimand, \(P(\text{default_90d}=1 \mid \text{prefix}, \text{activated}, \text{mature})\) under the historical policy, is a defensible choice, and the multi-state alternative is rightly described as a different question. Two problems remain. \(\tau\) is left as \(k\in\{3,4,5\}\), first Credit Review, or day 3/7/14, so the conditioning set is not fixed and the estimand is not identified. The claim that the same score can be applied to every incoming application as “default risk if activated” is the counterfactual the section says it is not estimating. Section 8 later forbids that reading, so the operational sentence in Section 1 is the misuse the study exists to prevent. Positivity is never stated: prefixes that historically almost never activate have no support for that conditional probability.

**Cohort.** The flow table adds to 48,000 and labels the right roles for the six given blocks. The inclusion rule that drops unlinked transfers “from the activated set” does not match the table, which leaves all 21,600 in the labelled cohort and all 2,900 in the transfer row. Reappearance under a new id is not handled, so one economic application can sit in both the transfer count and the labelled activated count when the 60% linkage hits. Permanently missing partner feeds are not separated from the 3,600 immature activations, even though those two mechanisms justify different estimators.

**Censoring.** Delayed feeds, activation selection, transfer, and permanent missingness are distinguished, and the testable / partial / untestable split is mostly right. Still-open cases are then placed under “ordinary right censoring” beside activated accounts whose 90-day clock has not finished. For this target, default time has not started until activation; an open application is an undefined default, not a censored one. The multi-state outcome set puts “Right-censored/Open/Immature” in the same list as real terminals, and the code comment treats a six-class classifier as interchangeable with cause-specific hazards. Censoring is not a class. IPW is offered as a sensitivity without saying that it changes the estimand and fails when unmeasured underwriting information drives activation.

**Prefixes and features.** The prose rules for pre-decision \(k\)-prefixes, elapsed-time prefixes, short traces, rework loops, and rename/dedup are sound. The implemented population is only eventual activations, so every retained trace contains Accept, Contract Signed, and Account Activated later; nothing in the feature spec stops a \(k=4\) prefix from already containing Accept. `risk_score` is kept under the assumption that the value on an event row is the value known at that event. The export can just as easily repeat the latest score on every row, and that failure mode is not in the audit. `documents_total` is handled only in prose.

**Leakage table.** Required fields are present, and dropping `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, post-activation collection fields, and the label columns is right for a pre-decision \(\tau\). The `collection_status` availability cell says “After Account Activated,” which states when the field becomes informative about the outcome, not the rule that keeps it out of the feature table.

**Validation.** The anti-random-split argument (policy change, rename/duplication, maturity, drift) is accurate, and the metric set matches a 12.5% binary risk among matured activations (2,700/21,600). The maturity claim does not. “Train starts through 31 Mar 2025, fully mature” is false: maturity is a function of activation time plus 90 days plus feed delay, not start time. A March 2025 start that activates in late 2025 is immature at the 15 Feb 2026 extract. The Jul–Sep 2025 test window, after a hard maturity filter, over-represents fast activations. Validation ends 30 Jun 2025, so it is the same pre-policy regime as training and cannot show the policy shift the test is meant to expose. A post-migration hold-out is only marginally feasible: 15 Oct 2025 plus 90 days plus the 35-day buffer falls after 15 Feb 2026 except for immediate activations with a short feed delay.

**Limitations.** The claim boundary in Section 8 is the right one, and the three deployment feedback paths (tighter rejection, behavioral gaming, policy/process drift) are concrete. They do not repair the Section 1 instruction to read the score as default risk if activated for applications that the historical policy never activated.

**Skeleton.** The pipeline shape is the right one: `read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, as-of case attributes, a start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, an explicit leaky-column drop, and a later comment for survival and competing risks. Several steps do not do what the comments say.

- After sorting by time, `default_90d` and `label_available_at` are aggregated with `"first"`. Under the schema as written, those fields are missing until known, so the earliest row is the empty one, `is_mature` collapses, and the labelled cohort count is wrong. First non-null is the aggregation that matches the field definition.
- Still-open is `end_time >= cutoff - 1 day`. Open means no terminal event by the extract. Idle document-wait cases have an old last event. The event window ends 31 Dec 2025, so the threshold around 14 Feb 2026 never fires, and those 5,100 cases fall into `Other`, a bucket the cohort table does not have.
- The pre-decision prefix rule is never applied. `pred_time` is the max timestamp inside the prefix, so `time <= pred_time` drops nothing. A first-4-event prefix may contain Accept and any attributes attached to that row.
- Dedup is exact timestamp equality; the text says near-identical migration duplicates.
- Every start after 30 Jun 2025, including post-migration, is labelled test. The prose test window stops at 30 Sep 2025. `Timestamp("2025-06-30")` is midnight, so almost all of 30 Jun 2025, still pre-policy, is assigned to test.
- Prefixes and the feature matrix are built only for mature training cases. The temporal validation design has no validation or test feature table.
- `source_system` is loaded and then ignored, so the partner missing-feed sensitivity is not in the pipeline.
- `n_events` on a fixed-length prefix is constant and carries no information.

The skeleton is therefore not a reliable implementation of Sections 2, 4, and 7. The scientific framing is well above a naive 21,600-row classifier, but the contradictory “if activated” deployment claim, the censoring/competing-risk mix-up, the false “fully mature” window, and the cohort and prefix bugs are material.
