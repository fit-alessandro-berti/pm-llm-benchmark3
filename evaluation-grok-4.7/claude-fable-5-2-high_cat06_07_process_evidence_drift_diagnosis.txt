8.4

The diagnosis is the right one, and the month split is earned: April is a schema and prefix failure, June is a post-score routing mix shift, August is supplier-driven within-V3 outcome change before Partner Z exists, September adds population shift on top of that mechanism, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is a censored measurement. The reference decomposition, the refusal to split a model on a variant that is unknown at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, the alert ordering, and the failure modes are all operationally sound. The score is held down by evidence-tag slippage, one mis-aimed falsifier, an unstated mix assumption, and a few over-tight numerical claims.

The arithmetic that is shown is mostly right. Q1-rate versus own-rate mixes for June through October check out (about 0.16/0.16, 0.17/0.20, 0.19/0.31, 0.21/0.34, 0.20/0.34). The August split of roughly +0.02 composition and +0.12 within-variant, almost all from `0.35 × (0.64 − 0.31)`, is right. Reweighting August by dropping 7 points of V3 mass does land near 0.29 against the observed 0.28. A V1+V2-only October slice at September rates is about 0.15, next to the reported 0.16 and the 58% versus 55% maturity coincidence. Non-Z September V3 share really is about 36%, in line with August’s 35%, and `0.24 × 0.45 + 0.76 × 0.27` recovers the 0.31 aggregate.

Those checks are presented more cleanly than they were identified. The Partner Z path-mix gap of about 0.41 versus 0.31 is not identified from the data alone. Only V3 share by source is given; the 0.41/0.31 figures require assuming the residual mass splits in the overall V1:V2 ratio. That assumption is never stated, yet the result is tagged as arithmetic inference and then reused as “about 0.10 of the 0.18.” The residual “+0.04 / −0.04” is also not symmetric once weighted: it accounts for about 0.02 of the 0.34-versus-0.31 shortfall, not the whole gap. “About 85% of the August rise is within-V3” is about 82% if the baseline is the full 0.14 rise. June’s 0.158 mix-implied rate “equals” and “fully reproduces” 0.15 only loosely. September’s predicted 0.21 does match the Q1-rate mix; October’s 0.23 does not match 0.20, so “that rise matches (0.21, 0.20)” overclaims the covariate story for the month that is already the most confounded.

Evidence labels slip in the same direction. “Five of 31 May days were scored on broken inputs” is tagged observed. Only the 6 May patch date is observed; whether cases opened on 1–5 May were scored before the patch, and whether a linear blend of April’s 0.28 and a healthy 0.19 produces May’s 0.21, is a hypothesis. It is numerically suggestive and should have been marked as such. The un-recomputed “events before diagnosis” cache is an April training-feature defect; using it as a second cause of May’s prospective scores muddies the lineage. July’s “offset” of June-style V2 over-prediction and new V3 under-prediction is a good hypothesis and is not implied by the aggregates alone. Predicted 0.18 versus observed 0.19 versus a stratified expectation near 0.20 is compatible with cancellation, which is why the stratified test belongs in section 3 rather than in an inference tag.

The process-drift falsifier does not match the claim it is attached to. Rebuilt variant shares reverting to Q1 would falsify April’s unmapped pseudo-variant and would challenge the June and August mix shifts. It would not falsify the part that the row itself rates as high confidence: `Order Part -> Part Received` moving from about 35 hours to 65, then 130, 148, and 155. That component needs a falsifier on cleaned performance-DFG medians, case-start versus event-calendar, with the month boundary not cutting the wait. The other five falsifiers are aimed correctly, including the useful distinction between `P(breach | score)` changing and `P(breach | score, lead time)` not changing.

Smaller judgment issues, none of which overturn the actions: January–March part waits already rise 28 to 35 hours with no breach effect, which is noted, but the continued creep to 42 hours by June is left out of the “stable mechanism” claim. August’s matured V3 rate of 0.64 is treated as solid while the same selection logic is used to call September’s 0.61 a lower bound; at 93% maturity that is reasonable, but the second-order bias should have been named. Stage 1 still leans on August inside one to three weeks while Stage 3 correctly says August is unsafe without a censoring treatment. Those two instructions can be read as conflicting.

The code sketch meets the contract: pandas load, rename before any prefix logic, dedup contrasted with the post-20 April new-source rule, both cohorts with the right interpretations, fragments and cut waits called out, no invented drift detector, and the listed pm4py calls used to return summaries. Remaining defects are sketch-level but real. Median extraction assumes `discover_performance_dfg` values are statistic dicts; several releases return a scalar under the default aggregation and this would throw. Passing the same column as start and complete timestamp makes sojourn features degenerate. Event-calendar variant shares are still emitted after the comment says not to trust them. The 60-second same-activity collapse can delete genuine rework, and there is no explicit reporting-cutoff argument, so a full extract of recent case-start cohorts is only censored if the file itself is.

Section 6 is stronger than the required four rows. The immature-label retrain, the April calibrator that later under-predicts by about 0.09, the supplier effect dismissed as mix, and the Partner Z fairness reading without within-variant mature comparisons are the correct high-cost mistakes, and the mitigations match the monitors. Nothing in the weaker spots would justify a single drift label or treating October as comparable to a mature month. The deduction is for over-precise claims that the tables do not quite support and for a falsifier that misses the performance-drift half of its own row.
