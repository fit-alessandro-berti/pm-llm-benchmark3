6.8

The required structure is complete and the constraints are respected: fatigue is not treated as the sole cause, early complexity and late queue pressure are central, punishment is rejected, and quality checks are not dropped to raise throughput. The mechanism table, intervention risks, ethics section, and dashboard coverage are substantively strong. The score is held down by errors in the core causal reading and in several measurements.

The service-time story contradicts itself. Section 1 says the 18.8→10.8 minute decline “largely disappears” once complexity is held constant. Section 2 then uses the same tables to say that, within complexity, high versus low WIP still shortens service by about 3–6 minutes (12.7→9.4 and 22.4→16.8). Those within-band gaps are a large share of the 8-minute raw decline, so the “largely disappears” claim is unsupported. Case-mix is real, but its share is overstated.

The case-mix limitation is also mis-cited. The text says position effects remain inside the same complexity and WIP band, then offers high-WIP/low-complexity 5.8% versus low-WIP/low-complexity 2.1%. That contrast changes WIP, not position. No table identifies a residual position effect after both controls, yet Section 2 writes as if the mechanism were already identified. The proposed tests correctly say that comparison still needs to be done.

The experience comparison is inflated. Juniors’ own later low-WIP error rate is 4.4%, so 12.4% is about 2.8 times that baseline, not six times. Seniors move from about 2.2% to 4.9%. Absolute degradation is much larger for juniors; the “six times versus twice” wording is not.

The anti-fatigue contrast is weaker than the data allow. Early shift’s last two hours are 4.2% error with a falling queue (61→42); Day’s are 7.5% with a rising queue (38→75), at similar time on shift. Night versus Day is more confounded by staffing, complexity, and volume. Falling service time also does not rule out a joint account in which pressure raises speed and fatigue raises errors. “Pure fatigue must slow people down” is too narrow.

Smaller problems: the phase bins (1–10, 10–30) do not match the data; early queue levels of 46–61 are called high WIP against a >70 cutoff; capping shifts is said to remove seniors disproportionately without evidence; and “routine tasks” misdescribes the early workload. In the code, next-activity timing is unsorted, batch membership is compared on post-merge indexes, `start_position_bin` is never created, shift-boundary WIP uses unsorted `first`/`last`, and bot “corrections” are read from the bot event’s own rework flag rather than a later human correction. The fairness index would also absorb Bot_Risk and designed night-shift volume differences.
