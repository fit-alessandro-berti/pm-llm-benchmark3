7.4

The answer covers every required section, treats East differently from North and South, and correctly refuses an unmonitored full rollout. The legal/risk capacity warning, tail-risk discussion, and censoring caveat are the strongest parts. It is still not nearly flawless: several quantitative claims conflict with the given baseline or with the answer’s own queuing logic, and the PM4Py sketch would not run cleanly on this log.

The binding constraint is mishandled across scenarios. Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91%. Moving referrals from 9 to 12 per 100 cases is about +33% arrivals. At fixed capacity that implies utilization near 1.22, so there is no finite steady-state wait. Section 6 states that this breaks queuing laws, but the central scenario still offers a stable 14h wait, and the optimistic scenario accepts 12/100 referrals and management’s 10.5h figure without stating added capacity, shorter service time, or lower variability. Those scenarios cannot coexist with the recommendation’s math. Calling Kingman’s \(u/(1-u)\) term “exponential” is also the wrong shape.

Other figures are asserted rather than derived. “15% higher” utilization on a roughly 73% base is about 84%, not “~88%+,” unless new QC and triage load is being added on top; that extra load is assumed rather than sized. “64% of cases stay out of specialist queues” subtracts only the tech handover (36/100) and drops the separate legal/risk stream. “80% of the pooling benefit” has no basis. The combined North/South P90 target of 18.5 days would treat a rise in North’s current 16.4-day P90 as acceptable. East’s worse cycle time is pinned on E_Tech at 82% even though East has less volume than North, fewer intake resources, and a harder case mix, so a South–East pool does not obviously transfer slack. Part-time status of the legal/risk specialists is omitted from the capacity argument. Section 3 equates a resource handover rate with a DFG edge `Local Technical Assessment → Specialist Review`; those are different graphs.

The pilot is directionally sound (North as control, legal/risk routing held back, rollback rules), but it cannot see the learning loss the answer itself places on a 3–12 month horizon, and a two-week run-out does not fix right-censoring when P90 is about 16–23 days. “Pull completed advice” is also turned into reassignment to a different caseworker more firmly than the proposal states.

The sketch names the right PM4Py functions, then breaks in implementation. `temporal_features` is indented past the surrounding block, so `compute_case_kpis_and_fairness` raises `IndentationError`. The annotation says it returns a DataFrame while it returns a tuple. `get_service_time` and a wait-to-service ratio need a start timestamp; this log has only `time:timestamp`, so service time collapses and the ratio is undefined. `sla_days=15` contradicts the baseline: North’s P90 is 16.4 days while its SLA miss rate is only 6.5%, so the cutoff sits beyond P90. Customer-segment filters use invented labels. Variant “top 10” is an unsorted key slice, and the pre/post function stores two snapshots without differencing handover weights, waits, or SLA rates.
