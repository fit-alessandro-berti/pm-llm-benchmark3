6.5

The design covers the required boundary, calendars, latent objections, service-versus-wait split, experiment list, and PM4Py-versus-simulator separation, but several core quantitative choices are internally inconsistent or statistically wrong. Those defects sit in the decision rule, the non-stationary experiment machinery, validation, and the arrival implementation.

The decision table sets case-type median and p90 ceilings to 12/48/68/140 days and 24/95/130/260 days. Those figures are variant-group statistics, not case-type statistics. Simple renovation is a mixture of the 12-day clean path and the 22-day missing-document path, so a baseline that matches history will miss a “Simple ≤ 12 days” rule, while a rule that merely copies history does not select an improvement. D2’s missed-violation risk never becomes a hard constraint. “Best levels” for D10 are chosen before interactions are estimated, with no allowance for selection bias across ten policies.

Warm-up cites Welch’s method on weekly mean throughput. With March–May and September–November rate swings, that mean does not converge to a constant, so Welch’s steady-state diagnostic does not apply. The one-year warm-up is the appropriate idea and conflicts with the 180-day Welch recommendation. The 12-month/6-month holdout is not season-aligned.

Service times are assumed stationary given activity, case type, district, and resource, while D3’s benefit is a change in inspector travel, hence in site-inspection service time, once cases are batched. Cross-case objections are named in the state, then estimated as an independent per-case duration mixture. Predecessor waiting time is previous-event shift, which mis-assigns gaps under the AND of planning, fire, and environmental review. D5’s levels (“after planning” versus “after all reviews”) skip the tree’s actual legal position after decision drafting. “MAR given resource” understates a systematic, resource-specific absence of start timestamps. Overstating cross-case dependence is not automatically conservative for D3.

Replay validation is described as PM4Py conformance and variant frequencies. That does not replay historical arrivals and attributes through the resource model. Utilization is compared with workload counts even though many resources have no start timestamps, so busy time is not observed. Confirming the 40% non-permit reserve restates an HR input.

The arrival placeholder is not a Lewis–Shedler thinning: rejected candidates do not advance the clock. `base_daily_rate` is converted with `60 / max_rate`, which treats a daily rate as an hourly rate. The reported mean arrival rate uses `value_counts()`, dropping zero-arrival days. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a performance graph plus start and end activities; the skeleton stores that tuple as if it were the graph. `ResourcePool.request` never consults capacity or `next_available`. The engine ticks minutes and never calls arrivals, routing, calendars, architect bursts, or inspection bundles, so the code does not implement the dependencies the prose requires. Loop activities such as “Completeness rework” are not log activities, and the tree omits planning, legal, and appeal rework that the estimation section still tries to measure.
