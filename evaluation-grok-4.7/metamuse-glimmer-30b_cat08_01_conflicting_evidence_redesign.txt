The diagnosis is evidence-dense and correctly cites frequency, timing, fitness (0.91 / 68%), alignment deviations, the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split, the 31%→9% cost collapse, segment medians, and the manual-review 82% cliff. The fast-path contradiction is explicit, and the three redesigns are operationally different enough (guardrailed routing, a hard repair-first split, evidence-first plus post-dispatch monitoring), with sacrifices, failure modes, bottleneck shifts, and a staged pilot.

The central cost claim is wrong. After complaints, reshipments, and credits, fast approval is still 9% cheaper, so it does not “lose on true cost”; the advantage shrinks. The closing sentence also lumps Premium low-risk into that loss, despite an 8h vs 46h gap and service-credit exposure past 24h.

Redesign B treats the observed 5% reviewed-path complaint rate as the effect of forcing high-value cases onto that path, ignoring selection. “Cuts rework repair” is the wrong direction: shifting volume onto repair increases exposure to the 12.5% high-value rework rate. Track 1 still fast-approves low-risk accessories, including claims above EUR 400, so the claim that R1 is fixed is overstated. Redesign A’s part-selection gate is not the R2 evidence-completeness control for Standard accessories. A and B leave the reopen bypass of R3 largely open. Redesign C says pre-complaint outreach will shorten the post-complaint edges (40h and 54h); those edges start only after a reopen, so outreach would cut volume, not that wait. A and C also share the portal and pre-dispatch gate, so C is only partly a different design. Mid-risk is sent to repair-first with no outcome data.

The comparison table’s direction signs are missing, so several cells (“median for high-value, / for low-risk”, blank cost and compliance cells) do not state an effect. The first pilot is on Standard low-risk and excludes the high-value R1 change, while the stop line uses 85% utilization above the stated 82% queue cliff. Downstream checks are present, but pilot 1 has no concurrent control.
