The final memo is a strong, decision-ready synthesis: Alternative E is the right call, national scale and wholesale rollback are rejected for the right reasons, and the five metric conflicts are explained with the actual mechanisms (happy-path vs exceptions, fitness vs precision, handover reduction vs key-person fragility, visible vs downstream cost, segment heterogeneity). Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated in a usable table, process-mining evidence is used rather than name-dropped, and the VoI ranking is tied to how results would change the decision.

It is not nearly flawless. The gate criteria in the executive memo and the final recommendation are syntactically broken: comparison operators are missing, so the rollout thresholds (“complaints baseline +1pp,” “first-time-right baseline 2pp,” “permit-check conformance 99%”) do not state a testable rule. That is a material clarity failure in the decision itself. “These are design consequences, not noise” asserts causation more strongly than the incomplete attributes, regional case-mix, and bundled interventions allow; the evidence table correctly treats that causal story as interpretation. M6 is oversold: field-survey outcomes are missing exactly where the skip rule fired, so existing data cannot cleanly condemn or rehabilitate the rule on a 2–3 week observational pass, yet that is why it is ranked above the measurements that would actually identify false skips. The week-12 gate is not reconciled with a 60-day post-billing cost window. Smaller nits: 93% utilization is stated as general in the memo and as two regions in the table; “queueing delay grows non-linearly” is imported theory; the €14→€46 “tripled” add-on treats a truncated estimate as a precise ratio; and the 85% / 99% / ±pp thresholds are unargued policy choices.

Numbers that are stated are accurate, the constraints are respected, and the recommendation is hedged with pauses, fallbacks, and pre-registered gates. Those strengths keep it high; the unreadable decision thresholds and the M6 selection-bias gap keep it out of the top band.
