7.5

The recommendation is the right one: conditional go, with a guarded partial-order transfer rather than a blanket copy of Site A. Confounding is treated seriously, statutory originals and legal gates are preserved, the pilot separates implementation failure from mechanism failure, and the POWL model keeps Assessment dependent on both verification branches plus a supplement loop. That is the core of the task, and it is done well.

The score is held down by several concrete faults.

The transport graph mis-specifies mechanisms that the section is supposed to clarify. `appeal -> cycle_time` reverses a post-decision outcome. `case_complexity -> *_capacity` and `API_latency -> identity_capacity` conflate demand or latency with headcount. `role_cross_training` is disconnected. The accompanying explanation is also short of the required 180–280 words (about 160).

The quantitative scenarios do not follow from the critical path. Identity service is 3.4 h and originals delay is 18 h for 62% of Site B cases, so an optimistic median change of −7 to −11 h nearly reprints Site A’s confounded −9 h and even exceeds the answer’s own “9 h upper bound.” The digital-only row is internally inconsistent: a 20–30% treated share cannot produce a −2 to −4 h overall shift from a −5 to −8 h stratum effect without an unstated selection story. The mapping from Site A evidence and Site B constraints to the ranges is asserted more than shown.

The pilot’s “700 cases per arm in 12–16 weeks” does not fit Site B’s volume once the first wave drops legal and high-complexity cases. Several comparison-table cells also put before/after deltas in the Site A-before column, and some “transferable component” cells are monitoring advice rather than transportable causes.

Those are not cosmetic. Quantitative scenario analysis and graph fidelity were central requirements, and both are the weak parts of an otherwise strong design.
