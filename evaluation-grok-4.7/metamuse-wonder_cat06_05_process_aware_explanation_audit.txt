The audit is strong on the distinctions that matter. It correctly separates closure-time leakage (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) from repairable point-in-time defects (extraction-date prior-claim SQL, nightly document snapshot, closure-populated handler team, untimestamped `risk_score_current`, extraction-date employee experience). It keeps `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as normatively legal but unavailable at `t0`, and treats `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as an impossible ordering, worsened by the missing sequence index. Causal, predictive, actionable, and fairness standards are separated, and the five required sections, including the validation suite and the JSON shape, are covered.

The revised explanations are less rigorous than the audit. `claimant_channel` is admitted as assumed, and `initial_reserve` has no as-of proof, yet both are placed in credible drivers; `claimant_region`, which the notes say is known at registration, is removed. That inverts the evidence. Directions and the claims that Case X is a “higher-risk group” and Case Y is “materially weaker” are read off SHAP from a leaked model. Those attributions will not survive removal of the leaked features, and the large “base value and other features” residuals (+0.21 / +0.20 / +0.17) are never audited. “Target tautology” overstates the link between closure duration and a 10-day settlement breach. The suggestion that `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` selects the fast track is wrong: the fast track does not contain that pair. Severity is also soft: a purely future document-request total and the full-trace fraud-investigation pattern are not clearly less severe than the other post-`t0` patterns.

Those gaps keep the answer well short of nearly flawless, without undoing an otherwise credible leakage review.
