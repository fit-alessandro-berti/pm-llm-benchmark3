The write-up follows the required section and table structure, and it correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change, preserves four-eyes review, and names C1 displacement, D1/D2 substitution, assessment spillover, rework risk, and false concurrency. Those points do not rescue the counterfactuals.

The timings contradict themselves and the snapshots. K-104’s critical path is the long D1 queue and later A1 wait; identity and document work were already effectively decoupled, so “concurrency saves ~2 h” is not a real mechanism. Document completion stays around Tuesday morning and assessment around Tuesday afternoon, yet the table claims a 1.5–2.5 day reduction while the best case only moves the decision to Wed 15:30. Final compliance also cannot simply start at Tue 19:10 while C1 is carrying a multi-shift backlog. K-411 has the same defect: the case was already assessment-ready Tue 19:30, the scenarios move the decision by only a few hours, and the table still claims 0.5–1.5 days. D2’s longer Tuesday queue is ignored when both checks are started together.

K-337 is internally impossible. The intervention begins after Assessment 2, but the propagation both deletes Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 and places compliance on the following Tuesday afternoon—the factual end state—while claiming a 3–4 day saving. Compliance has to move to Friday or, with the weekend, Monday.

The scale analysis is directionally relevant but uses incoherent capacity math (100 reservations at “5%” each), mislabels queues that shrink, and misses the 250-word minimum. The synthesis is about 170 words, below the required range, and calls K-411 robust and predictable despite its own erased-gain case and the case-lock logging risk. K-104 is ranked too high for an intervention whose local gain is mostly preemption of the single compliance reviewer. Paths are concatenated activity lists without resources or times.
