The answer is substantively strong: staggered timing, endogenous selection, intensity versus availability, median-versus-tail divergence, fraud-desk interference, small-cluster limits, and a learning-oriented rollout are all handled seriously. It is not nearly flawless.

The arithmetic is mostly exact, including adopter pre/post windows, never-treated contrasts, and the structural finding that `p90 − 1.85×median` sits near 8 hours through April and near 16 hours from May onward, with Alpha near 21 after the FTE loss. One reported contrast is wrong: Beta versus not-yet-treated Gamma rework for April–June versus January–March is about −2.40 pp, not −2.29 pp. The dose narrative also overfits a smooth ramp. Alpha’s median gap versus Epsilon is about −2.4 h in April, −5.7 h in May, −4.5 h in June, and −6.1 h in August, so May breaks the claimed progressive intensity gradient.

Section 4 says that relative gap “widens” from −2.4 h to −6.1 h; section 5 says the same figures show that the gap “narrows.” Those are opposite claims. Section 1 also drops the sign on Alpha’s and Gamma’s FTE changes while marking Beta and Delta as gains. Dividing DiD shifts by average fast-path uptake is not an identified per-10-pp effect, and the routing/spillover intervals (about 5–8 h and ±3–5 h) are asserted rather than calculated.

The primary design and the code disagree. The strategy uses not-yet-treated Gamma in the April stack; the code drops Gamma from that stack. A positive `automated_fast_path_pct` while `automation=0` is noted but not treated as a measurement contradiction. The recommended within-branch RCT is the right policy direction, but it underplays interference through shared specialist and fraud-desk queues, which can make treated cases look faster by shifting congestion onto controls. E1 also mixes a “rollout package” contrast with an effect holding routing and staffing fixed.

Estimands, mechanism reading, uncertainty, and the refusal of a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim remain well above ordinary quality. The inconsistencies and the interference gap are what keep it out of the top band.
