The answer covers all seven required sections, separates hard rules from preferences, and most of the sixteen assignments are traceable to the skill matrix and the handover evidence. It is not nearly flawless. Several contradictions, an underspecified scorer, and at least one constraint break keep it well short of a high mark.

The comparison cell for first-available routing contradicts itself. That baseline assigns to the lowest open-case count, so Bruno (12), Iris (11), and David (14) are the last people it would load. The same cell says it would load those three. The rest of the baseline comparison stays impressionistic: neither baseline is actually run on C001–C016, so the throughput, SLA, fairness, and resilience claims are not demonstrated on this queue.

C001 breaks the answer’s own continuity rule. Rule 5 prefers the current owner on a high-complexity, high-handover case unless that owner is skill 0–1 and supervision is impossible. Celine is skill 1 and Hugo can supervise, so the exception does not fire. The table nevertheless has Hugo lead execution, while the reason text says an owner change must be avoided because of the 2.7 h penalty. Who `org:resource` is remains ambiguous on the tightest SLA in the batch (3 h), where both the 2.7 h handover penalty and the 3.3 h weak-skill penalty exceed the slack.

C003 breaks the overtime rule as written. David is overtime-risk high, the case is normal with 18 h left, and a safer Diagnosis-3 alternative exists (Celine, low overtime risk). The stated exception requires urgency and no safer alternative. Reclassifying the rule as a soft preference and overriding it for reassignment count is a policy change, not compliance. The batch check is also wrong: Hugo leads C001 and takes C014 and C015, so he is at 3 as well, not only Farid and Jonas.

The scorer cannot be executed or audited from the text. `sla_reference`, `est_effort`, the numeric coding of handover-cost bands, `RESIL`, and `SLARISK` are undefined. The claim that every term lies in [0, 1] is false: continuity points go negative, and fairness goes negative for high-overtime, high-load resources. SLA-risk weight is set to 0 for every normal and low case, including any normal case that is actually close to breach. Skill, handover, and waiting time are each counted in more than one term, so the weights are not the trade-off they appear to be. Final Approval eligibility lets a `preparer_only` skill-1 resource into the candidate set with no guard that they cannot win and finalize. Effort is never estimated, yet Eva (5.0 h, two high-priority cases plus a shadow), Farid (two urgents plus C010), and Hugo (urgent security plus two other cases and supervision) are treated as feasible.
