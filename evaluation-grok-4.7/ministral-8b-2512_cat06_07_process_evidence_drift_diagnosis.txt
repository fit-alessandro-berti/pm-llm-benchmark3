4.4

The write-up keeps the right broad arc — April as a pipeline break, June as a routing-mix change, late summer as several real shifts at once, October as immature labels — and it uses the required six sections. It is not a reliable audit. Several claims contradict the tables, required diagnoses and tests are missing or non-discriminating, and the process-mining sketch would not produce the evidence it describes.

The diagnosis table does not contain the six requested types. Covariate drift has no row. “Performance/process drift” is an extra category that collapses control-flow mix, edge duration, and outcome change. Concept drift is marked low confidence for August–September, while V3’s matured breach rate moves from about 0.33–0.35 to 0.64, overall mean predicted risk stays near 0.19 against an observed rate of 0.28, and AUROC falls from 0.76 to 0.68 before Partner Z exists. That is the period’s clearest change in the outcome given the diagnosis-time prefix. Label delay is confined to October. July is already 98% mature, August 93%, and September 82%, with median label delay rising from 36 to 42 days; September is material, and Partner Z matures more slowly. October’s observed breach rate of 0.16 is correctly treated as unusable, then over-read as “censoring, not drift,” despite the part-wait median still worsening to 155 hours and V3 still near 41%.

Month-level reading is off in ways that change the remediation. June’s V2 share is compared with April’s 29%. April is 18% unmapped because of the rename, so that baseline is corrupted; May is 34% and January–March are about 35%. The V2 breach move from 0.11 to 0.09 is tiny beside the mix shift that pulls the aggregate breach rate from 0.18 to 0.15 while predicted risk stays at 0.20. The falsification then says stratified V2 rates should be stable, which undercuts the earlier claim that the drop is evidence. July is called a mild, unclear wobble and given an unsupported technician-workload cause. The supplier miss starts on 10 July: the order-to-receipt median is already 65 hours, up from 42, and matured V3 breach is 0.46. The table’s claim that V3 was stable before August is false. Partner Z is 0% through August and cannot explain August’s 130-hour wait or 0.64 V3 breach; September adds the new population. The sign of the residual is never used: April and June over-predict, August–September under-predict, and October over-predicts again on a censored cohort. May’s falsification is also mis-aimed. The stale cached event-count feature is an April backfill gap; recomputing April does not by itself explain May.

The tests do not meet the two-hypothesis bar. Fixed-vintage backtesting is absent from the diagnostic section and appears only later as a mitigation line. Pipeline recomputation, DFG comparison, reweighting, and the lineage audit each state one expected result. The maturity-curve pair is vague: an immature October curve does not “plateau early,” and a concept-drift curve needs a higher asymptote at matched follow-up, which is not stated. The V3 logistic check conditions on part lead time even though the score is issued at diagnosis, before that wait is known, so a coefficient change leaks post-decision information. Reweighting is framed as whether Partner Z is “unfairly penalized,” using September’s 0.45 versus 0.27 without the slower Partner Z maturity the brief flags. September cannot be reweighted on rural distance and device age if those covariates were never stored historically.

The training plan is internally inconsistent and too aggressive. July is labeled safe and pooled with June for recalibration, although 10 July already changes the V3 outcome. January–March are clean and also the wrong concept for the post-supplier regime; that limit is not stated. August–September are unsafe, then used as the retraining window. “Full retraining (October)” means excluding October. Separate V2/V3 and Partner Z models are justified by base-rate gaps that already existed in January–March, when one model had AUROC about 0.81. June’s stable discrimination with a moderate ECE rise is a mix or threshold problem; August’s discrimination loss is not something recalibration can repair. Those two decisions are not separated cleanly.

The monitor list names the right families, including a label-delay stop on retraining, but intervention and deployment monitoring is only an alert slogan. There is no rule that an unknown-activity spike suppresses concept-drift retraining, that a variant-mix shift with stable within-path calibration stays a threshold action, or that a part-wait alert pages the supplier before it pages model training. The code sketch reads CSV with pandas and calls `format_dataframe`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, and it does not invent a drift detector. It still misses required `filter_time_range` and `get_variants`. Normalization uses an invented `diagnosis_activity` column and a nested `parameters` payload those functions do not take. Dates are never parsed, yet `.dt` is used. The month loop contains `...` and compares a period with a string. The case-start cohort keeps the first in-month event, which is neither cases that started in the month nor a trace from which a DFG can be built; both formatted cohorts are then discarded. DFGs are computed on the raw event slice, and storing the DFG back onto that dataframe does not yield an auditable monthly summary of variant shares, unknown-activity rate, or the order-to-receipt median. Cohort interpretations are one-line comments on the wrong objects.

The four risk rows match the requested examples and stay shallow. The October rule uses an arbitrary 90% cutoff and does not require maturity by variant and partner. The supplier mitigation is another model refit, not an operational split that preserves August as pre–Partner Z evidence. The Partner Z mitigation never blocks a fairness conclusion drawn from differentially censored rates.
