6.5

The write-up is complete, clearly structured, and strong on explainability, worker-facing language, and audit design. The recommended batch itself is not reliable: it breaks constraints the answer treats as hard, misstates training data, and then claims those breaches satisfy the development rule.

The overtime rule is applied inconsistently. Section 1 says high-overtime-risk resources get more work only for urgent SLA risk when no safer qualified alternative exists. C003, C006, and C014 are normal-priority cases with 18–36 hours of SLA remaining. David (14 open cases, 3.0 hours left, overtime risk high) still receives C003 and C014 even though Celine is Diagnosis 3 and Rework 3 with low overtime risk, and Hugo is Rework 3 and Quality 3 with low overtime risk. Iris, also high overtime risk, keeps C006 although Hugo and Jonas are Quality 3 with more remaining time and lower load. These are not documented exceptions under the answer’s own escalation rule.

Training assignments are factually wrong. Bruno’s training priority is quality, not approval, so C010 does not match a documented development priority; he is also Approval 2, so he may finalize, and the preparer split still adds non-urgent work to a high-overtime resource. C009 is the clean diagnosis-training case: Amal already owns it, has Diagnosis 1, and lists diagnosis as his training priority, with normal priority and 22 hours remaining. The answer moves it to Celine, who is already Diagnosis 3 and whose training priority is negotiation, while calling that a training assignment. C015 assigns Security Review to Jonas, whose Security score is 0. The rule allows skill 3 independently, or skill 1–2 only under R7 or R8. Eva is also Security 0, so the case note does not make either of them eligible. The table names Jonas as the assignee and the risk note then says the decision must remain with Hugo, which is not a determinate assignment. C010 and C015 are the two cases used to claim the 20% normal/low training share. Neither is a valid documented training assignment, so the quota claim is unsupported. Nine normal/low cases require at least two genuine matches.

Resilience is argued and then not used. C001 and C008 both go to Gita, who already has 10 open cases and 4.5 hours, while Hugo is Security 3, low overtime risk, and has a low handover cost on C008. The same pattern leaves Hugo largely idle as a Quality and Rework relief valve. Farid is given both urgent cases plus final accountability for C010 without a demonstrated capacity check against a 2-hour and a 4-hour deadline.

The algorithm does not implement the policy it describes. The 20% training quota and quality-check batching never appear in the pseudocode. The overtime exception consults `eligible` while that set is still being built, so the result depends on resource order. Score terms have no scales or weights, continuity is both a bonus and a handover penalty, and the batch-level cap is only a per-person filter. Constraint 5 is also restated more loosely than the source rule.

Sections 4–6 are the strongest. The baseline comparison covers every requested dimension and correctly refuses a single optimum, but it never shows what first-available or specialist-only routing would actually do with these 16 cases. The worker explanation is specific, non-punitive, and leaves room for correction. The indicator catalog is concrete and includes the required measures plus supervision, near-miss, and override views.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested discovery, timing, DFG, variant, SNA, and filter functions, but it would not catch this policy’s main failure: any reason text containing “supervis” clears a sub-threshold Security assignment, including skill 0, and the supervisor is never checked to be R7 or R8. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with parameter names that do not match the usual API, completion is inferred from the last event, the overtime aggregation is hard-coded to 0, and `discover_batches` is unused despite batching being part of the policy. The sketch audits more than it learns.

Several individual choices are sound: C002, C005, C007, C011, and the decision to keep Eva on C016 with specialist review. Those do not offset an assignment table that violates the answer’s safety, overtime, skill-0, and training rules while reporting that the rules were met.
