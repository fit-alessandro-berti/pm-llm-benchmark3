7.5

The answer is a strong conformant analysis, but it is not nearly flawless. Unlawful shortcuts are correctly excluded, screening and self-certification are not treated as substitutes for independent review, and the SAR, four-eyes, auto-clear, and dual second-review triggers are handled carefully. The grade is held down by a load-bearing timing error and several smaller rigor gaps.

The central flaw is the S4 feasibility claim. Using the answer’s own proxy—review 35–75 minutes and finalization 5–15—the expected completion is about 65 minutes after review starts. S4’s earliest availability is minute 35, so expected completion is about minute 100, past the 90-minute deadline. Even a confirmed minute-35 start leaves only 55 minutes for a task whose plausible length is 40–90 minutes. On-time execution is a left-tail outcome, not a credible deadline route. Section 6 nonetheless says a minute-35 start “supports the primary route,” and the spillover table says to retain D first in that scenario. That overstates possibility as a planning basis. The same answer calculates an optimistic latest start of minute 50 and warns that this limit is not a sensible decision point, then treats minute 35 as supportive anyway.

The duration proxy is also biased. The 35–75 minute range belongs to expedited S2 review, not to S4. Transferring it to S4 makes the preferred non-preemptive route look more deadline-feasible than the case supplies. Analyst reassessment is correctly identified as still required after Evidence Validated, then omitted from every numeric threshold. Sequential reassessment can push even immediate S2 review through the deadline, which can make preempting P-901 futile. That threshold is never stated.

Screening is bundled into D’s advantage over C, although one alternate-vendor call can run beside either reviewer. The rank-2 C plan therefore looks informationally weaker than the real fallback in section 6. The benefit column mostly restates \(qV\) inside €0–€35k and does not discriminate among the conformant plans. “Before unabsorbed overhead” is unclear, and “earliest plausible continuation” is filled with full windows rather than earliest continuations.

No case fact is materially misstated. A and B stay last, G is not treated as currently authorized, P-901’s earlier deadline and the non-linear 75-minute interruption risk are recognized, and observational breach and approval rates are not multiplied into an intervention effect. Those merits keep this well above a weak deadline-only answer, but the recommendation’s key 35-versus-65 split is not consistent with the arithmetic the answer itself presents.
