6.4

The design is careful in its conservative defaults, but it misreads the input and then builds quantitative claims on that misread. That is enough to keep it well below an excellent score.

The clearest error is e07. Its business timestamp is `2026-05-18 10:00:00`; `10:01:00` is `ingested_at`. Section 3 states that e07 “is actually timestamped 10:01, not 10:00.” Section 7 repeats the mistake as a one-minute detail-to-summary interval removed by merging. Those two columns are the distinction the problem is built around, and the answer both warns against using ingestion time and then treats ingestion lag as business duration.

That error changes the aggressive scenario. `ocel_merge_duplicates` grouped by harmonized activity and timestamp, with `have_common_object=True`, does not stop at the four groups listed. e06 and e07 share `Pack Package`, `10:00:00`, O1, and P1. e13, e14, and e15 share `Capture Payment`, `12:00:00`, O1, and V1. Connected components are therefore e03/e04/e05, e06/e07, e08/e09, e11/e16, and e13/e14/e15: 10 events and 28 E2O rows, not 12 and about 32. The hedge that “candidate-key details can vary” does not cover e15, which meets the criterion the answer itself uses. Section 7 then analyzes the wrong aggressive log: it never treats absorption of the ERP payment summary, or of the pack summary, as mechanical-merge damage.

The required e14/e15 decision is absent. e13/e15 is not a substitute: M2 is a different payment, and the summary must not be attached to it.

e11 → e12 is presented as domain or source-message semantics. The known facts require e12 before e13 for M1, and e15 after the payment messages. They do not order invoice issuance before authorization. Under the stated source-rank fallback, ERP would still precede PSP at 12:00, so the same serialization can appear for a non-semantic reason. Encoding e13 → e14 as a hard edge has the same problem: the prose correctly calls that PSP `source_seq` deterministic rather than causal, and the implementation then promotes it to a semantic constraint. e08 → e09 is handled more honestly.

The conservative counts are right: 17 → 15 events and 44 → 39 relations for e03/e04 and e11/e16; evidence-based inclusion of e06/e07 yields 14 and 37. The decision tree matches the required terminals, and e05 and e14 are retained. The `have_common_object` explanation for M1 versus M2, and for I1 versus I2, is correct. Ordering rules do separate semantic edges, within-source sequence, and mere tie-breaks, and they do not claim the total order is physical time.

The implementation meets the library checklist and does not mutate `ocel_raw`, but it diverges from the design. Refined labels are never written. Field-level authority is described for billing versus ERP and not implemented. The audit row stores the literals `"stored on source rows before merge"` instead of the dropped event’s activity and timestamp, so the reversibility procedure in section 8 is stronger than the code. After a library merge, semantic edges keyed by original eids are silently dropped when the survivor id changes. `ocel_drop_duplicates(..., parameters={"subset": ...})` is only a no-op here; if `subset` is ignored by the installed release, the same call is unsafe on any log that also contains legitimate same-time events.

One sensitivity sentence is causally wrong: evidence-based removal of e07 does not change the item-flattened DFG because e07 has no item relations, so it was already absent there. The item-level damage is the aggressive union of I1 and I2, which is described correctly.

Provenance and failure modes are otherwise specific and include the required eight modes. The framework is stronger than the event-level accounting. The timestamp mix-up, the wrong mechanical groups and counts, the missing e14/e15 row, and the overclaimed 12:00 edge are material, not cosmetic.
