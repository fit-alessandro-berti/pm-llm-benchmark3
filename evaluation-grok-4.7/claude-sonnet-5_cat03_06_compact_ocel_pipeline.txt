The score is held down by repeated semantic and structural mistakes in the analysis.

The data model contradicts its own policy and the business facts. Section 1 says only explicitly joint facts are source E2O links and that Dispatch, Confirm, and Payment are attributed to Orders only in separate derived frames. No such frames exist, and `e8` still links `P2`’s pack to `O100` even though the narrative names only `P2` and `I2`. `M2` is labeled `completes_settlement` although both payments are partial and no invoice total is given, so completion is invented. `order_fulfilled_by_package` also treats packing as fulfillment.

The audit misstates convergence and divergence. `e7` is a real convergence point for `O100` and `O200`, but `Pack Package` occurring twice on `O100` is `e7` plus `e8`, not an effect of `e7`. That repeated activity is the divergence; the write-up assigns both phenomena to one event.

Several expected-structure claims do not follow from the tables. Add Item already touches both orders (three order relations), while Reserve Stock and Issue Invoice have two, so those activities do not have elevated order counts relative to Add Item. Pack Package has three order relations, not weight 2 “because of P1.” An object-type graph is described as an instance graph of `P1`, `V1`, `M1`, and `M2`. A discovered object-centric net that merges both pack events cannot structurally demand two Order tokens, because `e8` involves one order; the non-uniqueness caveat does not repair that claim.

Section 4 also contradicts its own traces. Close Order is in both order traces, with timestamps about 23.5 hours after invoicing, so flattening does not hide when closure occurs. What the DFG hides is the missing delivery and payment steps on the direct edge Issue Invoice → Close Order. “~23-hour delay difference” for the deliveries is numerically loose: D1 takes 3 hours and D2 about 22.75 hours.
