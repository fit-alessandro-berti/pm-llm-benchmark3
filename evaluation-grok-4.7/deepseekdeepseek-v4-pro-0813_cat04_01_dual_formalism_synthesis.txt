The final model is a strong, explicit treatment of a genuinely non-block-structured workflow: the dependency table matches the narrative, the process tree’s loss is named as a conservative under-approximation rather than hidden, and the POWL partial order places the trial after both `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action` while leaving approvals and supplier consultation unordered with the trial and joined only at release. The eight witnesses are in the required order, the verdicts match those models, W5 actually exposes the tree’s delayed supplier/approval start, and F3 is a real cross-branch violation (`Plan Verification` before `Contain Material`) with the earliest failing event identified.

The score is held down by a concrete PM4Py error in both artifacts. `Operator.LOOP` in PM4Py process trees and in `OperatorPOWL` is binary: do-child, then redo-child, with exit implicit. The expression `*(->(ET, IT), AP, tau)` and `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [trial_do, ap, SilentTransition()])` add a third exit child. Conversion and tree semantics use only the first two children or reject the arity, so the silent “pass” child is not a faithful PM4Py exit, and the validation plan’s `convert_to_petri_net` / soundness steps are not reliable on this code. Visible-trace claims still happen to match a binary loop, but the submitted constructors are not correct API.

Lesser gaps: assumption 6 equates “true concurrency” with “no order constraint” instead of resolving that distinction; section 4.3 groups concurrent nodes and does not account for every intentionally unordered pair (for example `Plan Verification` with `ApprovalRoute`, `Design Corrective Action` with `SupplierChoice`, `Analyze Root Cause` with `Plan Verification`); the self-critique never names the loop-arity defect that would break the proposed checks.
