3.5

The write-up has the required shape and a few sound intuitions, but the executable models are incoherent, several verdicts contradict those models, and one test is misread. It is not a usable hybrid specification.

Policy choice is not actually made. Section 1 never states two different R5/R6 resolutions: the “alternative” is the same strict precedence rule already chosen. The stated compliance risk is backwards. Strict precedence detects early activation; it cannot see the missing emergency authorization, so the risk is false rejection of genuine emergencies. Undetected bypass is the risk of the lenient rule. Missing attributes are called violations in §1 and “not evaluated” in §7.

The process tree does not implement the backbone described above it. `Receive Onboarding Request` is a labeled leaf given children; `Classify Risk` is placed in parallel with the three checks and then XOR-ed with both the clarification loop and the terminal branches, so it is not a join and not a predecessor of approval. `LOOP(Request, Receive)` is `Request (Receive Request)*`, which always asks at least once and ends on a request. XOR-ing that loop with `Classify Risk` excludes the valid clarify-then-classify trace T2. Inserting `Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion → Activate Vendor` at the front of every high-risk branch forces escalation, forces a second activation, and rejects T2. The disclosed “approximation” is also wrong: classification without a clarification is allowed. T2 and T7 are marked procedural passes against a tree whose language does not contain them.

DECLARE encoding is not reliable. `exactly_one` is unary; a two-activity “exactly one of these” rule is ill-formed, and an unconditional version rejects T3, which the matrix passes. R3 needs alternate response (a reply before the next request and before a final outcome). The model uses plain response, and the receive/request precedence tuple is the reverse of the semantics table. R5, the chosen hard rule, is not in DECLARE. `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` still holds when the opinion arrives after activation, so T6 correctly passes this DECLARE model and is not controlled there. R8 is described as `never_together`, which is not a DECLARE template. Non-coexistence itself is the right idea for R1 and R8.

The log skeleton contradicts itself. `activ_freq` `{1}` means exactly once, not at most once, so `Reject Vendor` and `Activate Vendor` cannot both be required once and also be in `never_together`. That model accepts no trace. `always_before` / `always_after` are filled as reversed pairs and do not express response: T5 can pass them with an unanswered request, yet the text says they enforce R3, R4, and R11. R7 is claimed and not present. R11 is not conditional on a post-activation bank change; a global verify-before-payment pair is just the ordinary backbone. `directly_follows` is treated as mandatory immediate succession while listing three different successors of receipt. As an allow-list, which is how skeleton conformance treats it, every test fails. The matrix still marks the skeleton pass on T1–T8, including T8, where `never_together(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` is decisive.

Temporal numbers in the dictionary are the right seconds conversion, and treating R12 as warnings is right. The verdicts do not apply `zeta = 2.0`. The band is mean ± 2 standard deviations, both tails. T2’s clarification gap is 26 hours, inside [0, 96]; T6’s opinion gap is 17 hours, inside [0, 24], and 77 hours to payment is inside [0, 144]. Both are marked warnings by comparison with the mean, and T6 uses the raw timestamp 23 rather than 23−6. T7’s 34-hour opinion gap is a real warning, but the text says 40 versus 12. The checker in §7 is one-sided and only looks at the first pair.

The resource layer is not case-scoped: it intersects resources across the whole frame, so unrelated cases contaminate each other. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is imported and never used. The R6 code flags activation before escalation, which is the opposite of the emergency pattern, and it never checks 24 hours. That also reintroduces R6 after §1 adopted R5. T6’s opinion is 10 hours after activation and 17 hours after escalation, so the time window holds and only the absent authorization is open. The matrix’s resource “warning” does not follow from the code. T6’s alternative resolution never changes the overall verdict, because the broken procedural layer is left in place.

T4 is factually wrong. The trace has `Manager Approval` by Cora and no `Compliance Approval`. DECLARE non-coexistence does not fire. The real failure is four-eyes on `Verify Bank Account` and `Manager Approval`. T5’s explanation treats `Classify Risk` before a reply as the R3 breach; R3 binds the next request and the final outcome, not classification. §10 then names an accepting Petri net as canonical after delivering a process tree, and says both “stop at the first procedural failure” and “aggregate every layer.”

§9 notices some duplication and vacuity but misses the unsatisfiable frequency/exclusion pair, the reversed skeleton directions, post-activation re-verification, and the fact that support and confidence are discovery metadata. Setting them to 1.0 does not create a conditional rule or make a flat dictionary something `conformance_declare` can execute.
