The write-up has the required seven sections and a few defensible calls, but the decision reconstructions that the task centers on are incomplete and, in two queues, factually wrong. Confidence is not a substitute for those errors.

QA is the best-supported label: non-preemptive FIFO, with an appointment able to take a free server. Choosing A02 at 08:32 ahead of priority-1 A04 does favor FIFO. The contradicting-episode cell does not contradict that diagnosis; it restates the case against priority. The 08:58 eligible set drops A06, A07, and A08, all of whom had arrived well outside the two-minute scan lag. The later separations are missing: at 09:05 A05 is taken ahead of priority-1 A08 and priority-2 A06, and at 09:32 A07 is taken ahead of A08. A06’s late 09:15 appointment is never tested. Prediction cells are blank, and the SPT fragment at 08:20 is unsafe: A04’s estimate is 7, shorter than A03’s 10. Calibration only covers 08:52–08:58, so it does not explain why A04 was not chosen at 08:32.

QB’s primary label, preemptive priority with an SPT tie-break, does not survive the log. B01 is preempted at 09:16 for priority-1 B02 (“urgent arrival”). The second stop, at 09:48, starts priority-2 B03 under “dispatcher reassignment”; B05 starts only at 10:00, after B03 finishes, and does not preempt B03. The supporting claim that B01 is preempted twice for priority-1 arrivals is false and conflicts with the same answer’s 09:48 row. Remaining work at 09:48 is about 14 minutes (45 − 14 − 17), not ~26. B04 (arrived 09:35, estimate 8) is omitted, so pure SPT would not select B03. The same priority rule fails at 09:31 (B01 resumed over waiting B03), at 10:14 (B01 resumed over priority-2 B06), and at 10:28 (B04 over B06). B07’s missed appointment is ignored. Section 4’s “SPT predicts B03” counterfactual is therefore invalid, and a scan-lag fix on B05 would not flip the queue to SPT.

QC treats the start sequence 8, 14, 22 as SPT. At 10:32, C06 (arrived 10:25, estimate 6) is eligible and shorter than C04; the table omits C06, and also includes C05 at 10:10 although C05 arrives at 10:11. Earliest-deadline is already different at 10:10: C03’s 12:30 deadline is tighter than C02’s 14:00, so SPT and earliest-deadline are not observationally equivalent there. C05 was already impossible by 10:18 (estimate 40 versus deadline 10:50) and was skipped twice before the 10:54 start. At 11:40, C01 is chosen over C07, which is shorter, priority 2, and still near a 12:00 deadline. That episode cuts against both ongoing SPT and a general deadline rescue, and it is absent.

QD’s skill-eligibility plus per-resource FIFO reading is directionally right: D04 before priority-1 D08, and D07 before priority-1 D10, reject priority and SPT inside the capable set. The decision table then analyzes 12:16, when D07 is already finished and D10 is the only remaining D-X job, instead of the real fork at 11:48. The 44-minute D-F gap includes the documented 12:00–12:20 meeting. The open problem is the 11:36–12:00 idle period: D05 (30 minutes) does not fit before the meeting, while D10 does and is not taken. Appointment holding also leaves D-X idle from 11:20 to 11:30. Those constraints belong in the rule, not in an “undocumented meeting” contradiction.

Section 5 ranks scan lag above the explicit reassignment reason already printed on B01, and it does not say how each exception would change the four labels. Section 6 is a usable collection list but underweights routing assignment, candidate-resource sets, and updated remaining time. Section 7 correctly calls QD the most readable queue, then ties the QB risk to a priority-versus-SPT flip that these traces do not support.
