6.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure, rebuilds several real choice sets, and reaches a broadly defensible reading of QA (walk-in FIFO with soft appointments) and of the binding skill partitions in QD. Those strengths do not offset multiple factual, arithmetic, and logical failures. Confidence is also inflated relative to the ambiguities the same text documents.

**Hard errors**

- QD’s rationale says that starting D05 at 11:21 would collide with D-F’s 12:00 meeting. 11:21 plus the 30-minute estimate ends at 11:51, before the meeting. The meeting blocks D05 only at the later 11:36 decision. The stated reason for skipping D05 is false; FIFO and SPT already both select D03, so the error is doing explanatory work it cannot support.
- D-X’s 11:20–11:30 idle is treated as fully explained by protecting D06 from D07 (28 minutes). Under the answer’s own eligibility rules, D08 is a D-X-eligible mechanical job, priority 1, estimate 8, and would finish about 11:28. The idle is therefore not forced by the appointment. Section 7’s claim that QD idling is “completely explained,” and the 96 confidence, are not supported.
- At 11:30 the priority cell includes D06 (priority 2) together with D08 and D10 (priority 1). Strict priority does not predict D06.
- QB’s explanation that B05 may have been invisible at 09:48 because scans can lag by two minutes reverses the scan-delay definition. A scan recorded at 09:46 is already in the log at a 09:48 decision; delay means true arrival was earlier, not later. B05 was eligible. Selecting B03 over that priority-1 job is the sharpest contradiction of preemptive priority, and the table hedges it as “B05 (if visible) or B03.”
- The QB reconstruction never analyzes 10:14, when B01 is resumed over waiting B04, B06 (priority 2), and B07 (shorter, and the appointment job). That choice set is central to any resumption-versus-priority claim.
- The first discrimination test does not discriminate. After moving B04’s arrival to 10:22, both two-tier FIFO and aging select B06. The follow-up scenario then switches the rival from aging to static priority. The proposed observable is useful; the counterfactual itself is not.
- Section 1 says SPT and earliest-due-date stay entangled until C06. The same answer’s 10:10 table already separates them: SPT selects C02, while earliest due date and priority select C03. Processing time and deadline are not positively aligned in this log (C02 is short and loose; C05 is long and tight).
- Section 5 calls the initial QC backlog an indivisible SPT batch that only an SLA breach can interrupt. C06 is not an SLA breach and still runs before C01, which was in that supposed batch. The note that C01 was last among the initial backlog does not explain why C06 may pass C01 while the still-feasible, shorter, higher-priority, earlier-deadline C07 may not. At 11:40, C07 can still finish before 12:00; C01 causes that miss. “Pure SPT decisively proven” at 11:34 overclaims a single decision.

**Calibration and consistency**

QA is the cleanest queue: 08:32 and 09:05 reject priority and SPT, A03 is the appointment exception, and A05’s 15-minute estimate overruns A06’s 09:15 appointment, which cuts against hard lookahead. Listing that overrun as the strongest contradiction of a soft-appointment hybrid treats supporting evidence as contrary evidence. QD at 96 versus QA at 92, plus “by far the most identifiable,” disagrees with those scores and with D-F’s unexplained 11:36–12:00 idle. The resource table only documents a 12:00–12:20 meeting; extending unavailability back to 11:36 is a hypothesis in Section 5 and a “documented” fact in Section 3.

Every realized service duration in all four queues equals `est_min`. The analysis never uses that. It leaves estimate noise available as an escape hatch the outcomes do not support, and it leaves remaining-time arithmetic (B01’s 31 then 14 minutes) looking like an extra assumption rather than an exact implication.

The highest-ranked exception, 5–15 minutes of physical staging, is outside the prompt. The stated scan bound is two minutes, which cannot remove C06 from the 10:32 eligible set (recorded arrival 10:25). Staging also repairs only that one QC anomaly.

The closing risk argument mixes two mechanisms. Promising priority-1 service levels while the floor remains FIFO would breach those promises. Installing priority-weighted dispatch would change the floor sequence, so priority-1 jobs would not keep “unmitigated FIFO delays.”

**What holds up**

Choice sets at QA 08:20, 08:32, and 09:05, and at QC 10:10, 10:18, and 11:34, are correctly built and correctly scored against FIFO, priority, and SPT. B07 being shorter than B04 at 10:28 is correctly noted. Skill exclusivity for D05 (firmware plus electrical) and D07 (mechanical plus electrical) is real. The output format, job-level citations, and explicit hybrid language meet the task’s structural bar. The grade is held down by wrong arithmetic used as a reason, a non-discriminating counterfactual, a misapplied data-quality bound, a missing QB decision, and confidence that the text’s own contradictions do not earn.
