The answer is a strong, largely accurate causal-policy assessment: arithmetic I checked (pre/post means, never-treated contrasts, last-pre-month cohort contrasts, donor envelopes, workload ratios, and the p90–median gaps) matches the panel, median and p90 are kept separate, and absolute deterioration is not dressed up as a benefit. Estimands, interference, treatment versions, staggered cohort-time comparisons, and the refusal of a single “X%” claim are all at the right level. The rollout choice is operational and improves learning rather than merely naming an estimator.

The score is held down by real gaps in quantitative discipline. Section 7 assigns causal-directional confidence of 55 and 65 while the same analysis says the identifying assumptions are not established and the trend-stress intervals include substantial harm. The fast-path coefficients are reported to three decimals with no clustered standard errors, despite five clusters being the central inferential problem. The primary-strategy table lists pretreatment trends, risk, volume, and capacity as adjustments, but the stated estimator and code are unadjusted group-time differences. The 1.6–4.8 satisfaction figure bounds levels under a response-rate floor, not the customer-level effect. Averaged windows also bury the June tail spike, August recovery, the exact +2 point March escalation shift in every branch, and the later flat rework and escalation series.
