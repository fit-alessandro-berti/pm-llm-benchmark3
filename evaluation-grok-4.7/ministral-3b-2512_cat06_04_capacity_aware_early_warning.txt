**3.5**

The memo keeps the required six-part shape and gets three directional calls partly right: I-03 is left untreated after Diagnosis Complete, I-05 is not given a swarm on an unchanged refresh, and I-07 is treated as evidence-bearing. Those points are not enough to make the policy deployable. The allocation, the arithmetic, and the written rules contradict one another, and several factual adjustments reverse the problem statement.

**Capacity and selection.** The live book has four slots. The table marks I-02, I-04, I-07, and I-08 as Intervene, the portfolio line calls those four cases “3 slots,” and a fifth slot is reserved. That plan cannot be executed. The stated eligibility rule is also not the rule that produced the table. A 20% move from `p_prev` is undefined (absolute points versus relative change). Applied literally, it does not clear I-04 (first alert, +0.18, `p_prev` 0.30) or I-08 (+0.08), and I-07’s probability fell from 0.75 to 0.72. I-01 is marked ineligible for “Public Sector + low value” and “too low after adjustment,” yet its avoidable-breach value is €9,000 against a stated €5,000 floor, Public Sector is not an exclusion in the rule, and the rule’s own Public Sector adjustment raises probability. I-03 is “Wait” rather than “do not intervene,” which treats a stage that has already lost the intervention as if it were still pending. I-04 is described as a repeated alert; it is a first alert. I-06 is deferred for a “high” false-alarm burden of €300. I-08 is justified with Strategic Enterprise underprediction; the brief says that segment is overpredicted by about 0.05–0.12.

**Scoring.** There is no net-benefit score. Priority is a set of examples, and false-alarm burden, hours to deadline, imputation, and queue externality never enter the rank. On midpoint effects, labor, and the listed false-alarm burdens, I-07 is on the order of €800 expected direct net (€1,300 gross minus €500), while I-08’s lower-bound effect is about €5,000 gross against €5,450 of swarm-plus-false-alarm cost before any spillover. Both are still selected, and I-08 is selected with eight hours still on the clock. The €5,000 value floor is applied inconsistently (I-07 sits on the boundary) and would, if applied, be the starvation rule rather than a remedy for it.

**Impact table.** The three scenarios do not match the required definitions. Optimistic effects are not the upper half of each selected range, base effects are not the midpoints (those midpoints are about 0.15, 0.19, 0.26, and 0.10, summing to about 0.70, not 0.53), and adverse effects are not the lower bounds. Each euro line multiplies the sum of effects by I-02’s €25,000 and then adds other cases again. The displayed expressions do not equal the reported totals: the optimistic expression is about €33,950, not €19,700; base is about €28,250, not €13,500; adverse is about €20,050, not €7,200. False-alarm burdens (€7,650 on these four cases if each swarm incurs the listed burden) are omitted. Spillover is stated as 0.02 × 6 = 0.12 and then reused for a multi-slot portfolio, so slots are not multiplied through; optimistic spillover is not at the low end; adverse “0.18” is not derived from the 15–30 minute congestion case. “Break-even; spillover neutralized” and optimistic confidence “High” are unsupported. The table is not auditable.

**Calibration.** SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction are collapsed into ±0.05 point shifts, so the Strategic range 0.05–0.12 is under-corrected and never carried as an interval. Public Sector, described as broadly acceptable, is given the SME upward shift anyway. Non-Native-Language Support is given a floor of 0.60, which inflates a sparse segment rather than widening uncertainty on both the probability and the effect. Effect bounds are then tied to whether `p_now` is “near the high end,” which treats an observational score as if it selected the causal effect. The brief states that the score is not the probability under intervention.

**Repeated alerts.** Section 4 undoes the I-07 decision. I-07 is allowed to jump the queue only if probability rises 15% from the prior peak; it fell. Re-entry also requires value above €10,000; I-07 is €5,000. A 24-hour post-swarm blackout sits far outside SLA windows of 1.5–12 hours and a 30-minute refresh. Suppression is written as “if `p_now` is 0.7,” which is not an operational threshold. Probability jumps can reopen a case with no new event, which is the I-05 failure mode. “Materially new event” never becomes the primary re-entry test, and escalation “even if not in queue” can override the stage limit that correctly sidelined I-03.

**Fairness and monitoring.** The safeguard offered for value starvation is a higher value floor. The Non-Native safeguard (probability at least 0.60 and value above €15,000) would admit I-09, which the table tells to wait, and it piles a monetary hurdle on the segment whose estimates are already the weakest. Quarterly retraining on post-intervention labels, plus a holdout defined by swarms that failed, conditions on treatment and on outcome; it does not identify the effect or stop the model from learning the policy. The spillover response is an undefined “0.05 per slot” cut of 20%, and the later trigger says that if Public Sector absorbs the spillover, slots should be steered toward Strategic Enterprise. That concentrates treatment further on the segment already favored by value weights. I-03 is used as an example of a missed breach. “Ab-abs” before/after comparison does not separate selection from effect. Fairness is audited annually, policy triggers refer to an unobservable “adverse scenario,” and there is no monitor for missing live-queue fields, share of swarms still before Diagnosis Complete, or specialist minutes actually pulled from the queue.
