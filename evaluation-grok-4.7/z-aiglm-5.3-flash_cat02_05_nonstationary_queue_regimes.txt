7.4

The final write-up is a strong, format-complete queueing analysis: regimes are explicit, the outage is not treated as high throughput, Little’s law is applied with units, proxies, and closure limits, and pooling is shown to hide day type, mix, and the intervention. The `peak_08_10` contradiction, flow-versus-census gap, and labeled lognormal load estimates are real uses of the evidence.

It is not nearly flawless. Regime B2 is described as draining “+12 net” with a “+8 surplus” on 2026-04-25; those four weekend days have 192 arrivals and 180 completions, so the net is +12 accumulation (04-25 is +8, not a completion surplus). That reverses the backlog path, which the same answer correctly has still rising through 04-26 to a +128 peak. Regimes A and E are also called constant 6.0 FTE, but 04-10 and 04-24 are 5.5, so the flat-capacity claim and the Little’s-law matrix are wrong. The baseline arrival band 8.0–8.9 and Fano ≈ 0.16 match a set that drops 04-10; with 04-10 included the floor is about 7.7/hour and the Fano is about 0.26, so the “30–60×” dispersion contrast is overstated. Era I’s median is about 27.4, not 28.4. Smaller slips (outage shortfall ≈ 45, deficit “13 to 16,” clearance in “~3 weeks”) add up. The Python meets the required checks, but unused window code and prose that would not match its own prints weaken reproducibility.
