8.5

The analysis is numerically reliable, regime-aware, and careful about Little’s Law, but it is not nearly flawless. Definitions, one mixture window, and a few evidence choices are looser than the rest of the argument.

The core quantitative work holds. Totals are 2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions. Regime means, open-hour rates, nets, median-of-daily medians, p90 summaries, WIP, FTE, WIP/FTE, and completions per FTE-hour match the table, including low-mix weekdays (701 arrivals, 697 completions, WIP 20.3, 1.40 completions/FTE-hour), high-mix weekdays (798 and 704, net +94, identical 5.93 FTE), and the phase totals in the alternative segmentation. Hourly vector sums, the 08:00–10:00 reconciliation gaps, and sample Fano factors match, including pooled daily arrival and completion Fanos of 10.03 and 10.67 and the within-regime collapse to 0.26 and 0.29. April 6–10 really is 491/491, and 19.6/8.18 open hours is about 144 open minutes.

The operating argument is also sound. Outage, recovery, mix shift, weekday/weekend staffing, and the April 29 partial-exposure day are separated. Recovery is not treated as normal capacity. Median handling time is not used as mean flow time. Implied sojourn is correctly restricted to opening-hour time, with completions as the throughput proxy and an arrival sensitivity only on a balanced window. The weekday/weekend WIP reversal after FTE normalization, the outage/recovery cancellation, and the false demand drop from weekend mix in the phase means are real aggregation failures. Confidence is scaled to one- and two-day cells, and the code implements the stated summaries, dispersion, autocorrelation, a non-definitive split score, and Little’s Law only on the two named windows.

The deductions are specific.

The opening estimands are syntactically broken (`\hat{\lambda}*A`, `\sum*`), so the main rate definitions are harder to read than the later tables.

April 6–14 is given an implied flow time of about 156 open minutes even though that window mixes the weekday and weekend intensities the rest of the paper says must not be pooled. “Approximate only” limits the damage, but a single W for that block is still a weak estimand. April 6–10 is the defensible one.

The hourly outage vector is used for zeros and dispersion, then flattened. Pre-outage completions are about 7.5 per hour and post-outage hours about 5.6, against an ordinary weekday near 8.3. The day is not only a clean three-hour hole, and the 4.83 per open hour figure hides that.

The change-point prose states a strongest split (April 17|20), a near competitor (April 14–16), and a ranked pair inside the high-mix weekdays without showing the scores. Those ranks also give `peak_08_10_pct` equal weight after the audit shows that field disagrees with every representative hourly vector.

“Inventory-drain” and “supplied by inherited stock” are sometimes stated more strongly than the paper’s own finding that arrivals minus completions are not a closed stock-flow identity. The hedges and the WIP path mostly contain this, but the wording is uneven. The alternative-phase “service median / p90” column also drops the earlier warning that these are medians of daily quantiles, not case-level quantiles.
