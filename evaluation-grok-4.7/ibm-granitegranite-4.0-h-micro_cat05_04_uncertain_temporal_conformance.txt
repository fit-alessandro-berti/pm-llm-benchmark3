The write-up keeps the seven required headings and names several real issues (DST fold, entry delay, `Alert_Sent` ≠ `Alert_Received`, weak T-409 NTP, missing T-404 start). The quantitative core is wrong, and several results are the opposite of what the evidence supports.

Normalization contradicts the stated rule. Resolution is supposed to be applied before clock correction; the answer applies offset first, then does not apply either correction in the intervals. HUB ±0.5 s plus offset [−1 s, +1 s], OPS-BER [−90 s, +120 s], SENSOR nearest-10 s plus [−5 s, +5 s], VENDOR-NY [−180 s, +180 s], MOBILE-LON [−300 s, +300 s], and the T-409 weak [−12 min, +12 min] bound never enter the math. Uncertain timestamps are replaced by points.

Time-zone conversion is systematically off. On 2026-06-01, Europe/Berlin is CEST (UTC+2), so OPS-BER 10:14 is about 08:14 UTC, not 09:14. T-409 18:04 CEST is about 16:04 UTC and is left at 18:04. VENDOR-NY 04:00 EDT is about 08:00 UTC and is left at 04:00. MOBILE-LON 13:14 BST is about 12:14 UTC and is left at 13:14. T-405 does not retain the two fold images (CEST UTC+2 and CET UTC+1). Alert 02:50 is given as 01:05, Investigation_Start 02:05 is copied as that same 01:05 interval, and ingestion order is not used to discard impossible fold combinations.

Section 3 is not one row per case–rule pair. It pastes the profile parameters — (600, 180), (2100, 600), (2700, 720), (86400, 21600) — in as if they were measured durations, then labels every SLA and profile test CERTAIN_PASS. Point HUB times alone refute that: T-408 investigation-to-containment is 55 minutes against a 45-minute SLA, and containment-to-close is about 38.4 hours against 36 hours, so both are certain violations at every listed SLA tolerance. T-402 11:18 CEST versus 09:00 UTC is a certain S1 violation once the offset and minute floor are included. T-401, T-403, T-404, T-405, T-406, and T-409 produce possible violations or zeta-dependent flips. Acceptance regions mix the legal SLA with “± zeta,” severity is uniformly “Minor,” and confidence is “High” where the intervals were never computed. The (Alert_Received, Containment_Complete) profile is omitted, as are S3-absent cases.

Section 4 says nothing changes at +0/+2/+5 minutes or zeta 1/2/3. That is false. Examples that actually move: T-401 S1 becomes a certain pass only after a 2-minute tolerance; T-402 S2 likewise; T-403 S1 only after 5 minutes; T-402 alert-to-containment is a certain profile violation at zeta 1 and a pass at zeta 2; T-408 S2/S3 profile results change across zeta 1–3; T-409 investigation-to-containment changes between zeta 1 and 2. No uncertain case is recomputed as an interval.

The Python block is not executable and does not do the required work. `T_401` is an undefined name; case, activity, and timestamp lengths disagree; the normalization comprehension is invalid; DST is absent; classifications are absent. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile`. The API it does call is not current PM4Py, the “profile” is the same confused tuples, and the midpoint log is not built, labeled as lossy, or compared with interval conclusions.

Section 6 does not cover each indeterminate or possible result, and it treats T-409 as needing offset telemetry after Section 2 called that timestamp certain. It does not separate synchronization, missing-activity, duration, and semantic evidence, or say what bound would remove the violating tail. Section 7 lists the requested control topics, but the DST advice (“use the latest rules”) misses the fold, and the overlap policy never states whether possible overlap is enough to enforce.
