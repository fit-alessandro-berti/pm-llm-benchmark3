The answer matches the required structure and is strong on the central judgments: prerequisites and four-eyes are preserved, K-337 is treated as a decision-policy change, earlier readiness is separated from earlier completion, and local gains are allowed to displace other work. Those strengths do not offset several temporal and logical errors.

The clearest defect is K-337’s calendar arithmetic. A Monday decision against the factual Wednesday 08:30 decision saves about 36–48 hours, not the stated base of 60–95 hours. That 60–95-hour band is a weekend decision. The same mismatched pair is repeated in the case table and the derivation, so the largest claimed cycle-time benefit is not temporally consistent.

K-104 misstates the factual mechanism. Assessment did not wait for escalation; escalation ran after Assessment, from Tue 19:20 to 21:20. The real delay is the subsequent C1 wait and from-scratch reading. The later correction that Assessment still depends on the D1 completion and the Assessment queue contains the damage, but the causal sentence is wrong.

Section 4 contradicts itself. The table correctly says K-337 removes Document Check 3 and Assessment 3, while the prose says none of the four interventions removes mandatory work. It also says K-104 pushes the case into Assessment sooner, which the case derivation itself does not support.

Best cases ignore the supplied queues. D2 has about 5 hours of work at Mon 16:00, so a Mon 18:00 start for K-219 is not the unconstrained best case. D2 has about 4 hours at Tue 08:00, so K-411’s both-checks-start-Tuesday-morning case is similarly optimistic. K-411’s base final gain of 3–6 hours is also close to the entire verification gain even though both Tue 16:30 and Tue 19:30 readiness can miss the same Wednesday Assessment slot.

The 100-case discussion names the right bottlenecks but understates the scale. One C1 and one D2 cannot absorb 100 extra or substituted service intervals; the result is capacity collapse, not merely a refilled queue and a gain that shrinks toward zero. The synthesis then overclaims that K-411’s benefit does not depend on optimistic availability assumptions.

Ranking, pilot design, uncertainty language, and the distinction between recorded cycle time and exported loss or appeal risk are otherwise coherent and well calibrated.
