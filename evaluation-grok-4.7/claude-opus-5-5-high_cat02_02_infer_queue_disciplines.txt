The reconstruction is unusually strong: decision epochs are enumerated, eligibility sets are mostly right, competing rules are scored rather than asserted, in-sample hybrids are labeled as such, and the perfect `est_min` = realized match (all 32 jobs, including B01’s segments) is correctly treated as a leakage risk. The k-interval for QC aging (about 0.89 < k < 1.2 from the 10:32 and 11:34 comparisons), the QA/QB/QD tallies, slack arithmetic, and the C07 counterfactual (start at 11:40 meets both 12:00 and 13:00) all check out. Assumptions are separated from observations, and the data plan is tied to real ambiguities.

Those merits do not make the write-up nearly flawless. The clearest factual error is repeated: SPT, EDD, and priority do not coincide at 10:18 or 10:54. The section 2 table itself shows SPT selecting C03 while priority and EDD select C05 at 10:18, and SPT selecting C06 while priority and EDD select C05 at 10:54. Feasible-only variants still do not produce a three-way match at 10:54, so the parenthetical “EDD 2/6 if restricted to feasible jobs” double-counts. That false coincidence is then reused in section 7 as a reason it is dangerous to infer “QC follows SPT.” The danger conclusion can stand on estimate leakage and missed deadlines; that third pillar cannot.

Related summary problems: QC’s “strongest contradicting” episode, 11:40, is actually predicted by the stated primary (earliest arrival+est). QA’s 09:05 cell likewise challenges slot reservation, which the diagnosis already rejects, rather than the FIFO-plus-due-appointment rule. “Priority is used only in QB” conflicts with the QC P1-escalation component. At QB 10:28 an appointment rule is marked wrong even though 10:30 is not yet due, which is a stricter standard than the due-only rule used for QA. The QA SPT column also mixes non-preemptive SPT with preemptive SRPT.

Section 4’s claim that the aging parameter was fitted on 11:40 is imprecise: the binding constraints are 10:32 and 11:34, and any k in that band already picks C01 at 11:40. The caution is fair only as a model-selection point (aging was preferred to look-ahead because of 11:40), not as a parameter-fitting point. The k-band also ignores scan delay, which can move the lower bound across roughly 0.8–1.0.

QB and QD are carefully hedged, but both headline rules are built on the same episodes they then score 8/9 and 6/6. That is disclosed, yet confidence 75 for QA is still high when a single appointment decision separates the hybrid from plain FIFO and the tolerance is unidentified.
