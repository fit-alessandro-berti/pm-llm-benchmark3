7.5

The design is a serious, mostly coherent study. It keeps all 48,000 starts in view, refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, transfers, open cases, or immature activations as non-default, separates activation selection from ordinary censoring, and uses a case-start temporal split. The 90+90+35 maturity arithmetic is right: a 15 July 2025 landmark matures on 15 February 2026, and a 15 October 2025 landmark only on 18 May 2026. Sections 3, 6, and 8 are the strongest parts.

The score is held down by estimand, validation, and code errors.

**Target.** \(P(\texttt{DEFAULT\_90D}\mid H_l)\) is called an “unconditional” pathway probability while it still conditions on the prefix and on being in process at the landmark. The six-state \(Y_l\) in section 1 has no ascertainment state, so the displayed estimand is not the quantity section 3 identifies with inverse-probability weights. The business-facing score is \(P(\text{activate within 90 days}\mid H_l)\times P(\text{default}\mid\text{activation},H_l)\). Under the current policy that product assigns low scores to applicants the lender is likely to reject, so it forecasts on-book default pathways and is a weak underwriting ranking. Section 8 says it is not a universal-approval effect; it still offers that product as the early-warning number without stating this ranking inversion. Accepted-but-not-activated cases fall into `PENDING_90D`. The 7–35 day feed lag is treated as a hard maximum even though the brief says that range is what commonly occurs, so some slow feeds inside the “fully mature” cohort are still unlabeled.

**Cohort and validation.** The flow table is an extract-time disposition, which the text admits, and it never bounds how many starts survive the ~15 July 2025 landmark cutoff. Post-policy outcome support is about two weeks, which is stated. Policy regime and the “same-policy” May–June test are anchored on case start, so a slow application started in June 2025 can be decided after the 1 July threshold change and still sit in the pre-policy test. Extraction-time resource experience is repaired by rebuilding it from events in this log; if the field is tenure, the aligned value is extraction-time experience minus elapsed time, and pre-2024 tenure is not in the file. “Pre-prefix `risk_score`” in section 5 does not match the “last score at or before the landmark” rule in section 6.

**Code.** The skeleton is roughly 300 lines against an approximate 80–160 line budget. It scores only cases with three pre-decision events, so early terminals leave the modeling frame with no attrition count, while the prose primary score is the baseline for every valid start. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then unused. The leakage assert checks names and a timestamp inequality that is true by construction; `risk_score` is still taken from prefix rows even though section 6 says the column may be the latest score repeated onto earlier events. Any non-null `default_90d` with `label_available_at` at or before the extract becomes a final label, including a 0 stored before activation plus 90 days. `analysis_frame` drops `MISSING_OUTCOME_FEED` before the split, which is complete-case analysis; the IPCW primary strategy is only a trailing comment. The coded “test” pool merges the May–June window with the post-1 July stress window. `raw_status` uses a fixed status priority rather than earliest timestamp, so those counts can disagree with `classify_pathway`. Inclusion never requires `Application Started`. Timestamp ties are flagged at the landmark only, not when a kept prefix event ties the barrier event that row order removed.

The design would be a high-quality draft after those corrections. As written, the formal estimand, the decision use of the factored score, and the executable cohort are not the same study.
