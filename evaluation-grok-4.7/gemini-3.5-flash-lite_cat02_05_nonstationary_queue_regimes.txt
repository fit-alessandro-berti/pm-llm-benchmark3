6.2

The write-up has the right regime story and the required eight-part shape, but the central tables are numerically unreliable, internally inconsistent, and too confident for the evidence.

The primary segmentation is the right kind of cut: stable weekdays, weekends, a one-day outage, a transient recovery, a mix/service shift from 2026-04-20, and a staffing change from 2026-04-29. Recovery is correctly kept out of the steady high-throughput class, and median service time is not substituted for flow time. Those are the main conceptual successes. The date boundaries then contradict the estimates. Regime 4 is written as 2026-04-16 through 2026-04-19, and Regime 5 as 2026-04-20 through 2026-04-28, while those same weekends are also placed in Regime 2. The quoted rates only make sense for weekdays, and even then they are wrong: 16–17 April is about 105.5 arrivals and 120.5 completions, not 107 and 115; the three intervention weekdays complete about 121 per day, not 118.4; baseline completions average about 99.6, not 100.4. Weekend arrivals average about 44.9, not 44.4. All-weekday arrivals average 108.0, not 105.8. Pooled arrivals are 2519/28 ≈ 90.0 and mean WIP is 836/28 ≈ 29.9, not 89.6 and 31.5. Sample arrival variance is about 902, so the Fano factor is about 10.0, not 971.4/89.6 ≈ 10.8. Little’s Law inputs repeat the problem: baseline WIP is about 20.3, not 19.8; the surge weekday mean is about 44.3, and 2026-04-20 is already 37, so “41–50” and 45.4 are both off.

Those are not cosmetic. From 6 April through 3 May, arrivals exceed completions by 110 cases (2519 versus 2409) while snapshot WIP starts near 18 and ends near 19. Any backlog narrative that treats arrivals and completions as a closed inventory is unsupported until that gap is treated as a data-boundary problem (abandonment, definition mismatch, or unobserved stock). The answer never checks conservation. It also never reconciles `peak_08_10_pct` with the hourly vectors. On 2026-04-21 the 08:00–09:59 bins are 3 and 8 (about 10% of 115), while the daily column says 47, and the real spikes are 34 at 10:00–11:00 and 24 at 13:00–14:00. The hourly table is loaded in code and then unused.

The surge window is called approximately stationary and “steady under high load” while weekday arrivals exceed completions by about 13 cases per day and WIP drifts from 37 to 50. Arrivals are the wrong throughput proxy there. The 12-hour conversion of “days” into 2.36 and 4.79 opening hours is only implicit, with no statement of overnight freeze versus calendar sojourn. Weekend days are one regime across a jump from p90 service near 53–58 minutes and complexity near 21–24% to p90 near 78–85 and complexity near 39–42%, which is the pooling error the text elsewhere warns against. Complexity is treated as a real mix shift that “doubles” tail time; the stated coding-change alternative is never carried, and early-week p90 near 47 versus later weekday p90 near 83 is a large increase, not a clean doubling. Service times are already elevated on 15–17 April, before the 20 April complexity step.

The alternative segmentation is not fit to dates. Before 20 April no day has `peak_08_10_pct` above 34, so the “burst-loaded, moderate complexity” type has no clear members, and the scheme collapses back into the same calendar break. Assumptions are not labeled. Confidence is High or Medium-High on n = 2 recovery days and on an intervention whose start time inside 29 April is unknown. The service CV “> 0.6” has no distributional assumption. Lag-1 dependence is asserted from carry-over without separating day-of-week seasonality and level shifts. No worked Simpson reversal appears; aggregation bias is renamed as Simpson’s paradox.

The Python would not reproduce the prose. `var()` and the regime masks are a cleaner weekday-first partition than the overlapping table, but only one dispersion statistic is computed, the hourly frame is dead, and `rolling(5).mean().idxmax()` finds the peak of the high-complexity plateau (around 28 April), not the 20 April change. The PM4Py note correctly says these rows are not an event log, and it only lists function names. Capacity pressure stays verbal: there is no bound on offered load from FTE, open minutes, and median-to-p90 service, and `staff_fte` staying at 6.0 through a 180-minute outage is not used to show that FTE misses lost capacity.

Format compliance and the outage/recovery distinction keep this above a failed analysis. The arithmetic, the segment overlaps, the unused hourly evidence, and the unexamined +110 flow gap keep it well short of a strong one.
