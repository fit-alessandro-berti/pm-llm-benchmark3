6.2

The policy design is structured and often sound, but it breaks a hard safety rule, and the stated algorithm cannot produce the assignment table.

The clearest failure is C015: Jonas has Security skill 0 (“not allowed”), yet he is assigned to perform Security Review. The rule allows skill 3, or skill 1–2 only under Gita or Hugo. Eva is also skill 0, so neither named training candidate may perform it. A signed exception is added only to clear the case, and that illegal assignment is then counted so training share can be reported as 2/9 (22%). Without it, the only clean training match is C009, which is 1/9 and misses the 20% floor. The shortfall should have been reported, with Hugo performing C015 and others shadowing.

The eligibility filter (`open cases + new load ≤ 6`) excludes Hugo (7), Eva (8), Amal (9), and Gita (10), who nevertheless receive C001/C014/C015, C005/C016, C004/C009, and C008. The batch cap of 4 new assignments is never actually enforced. Section 1 and Section 3 therefore do not implement Section 2.

Other assignment errors: C013 is given to Celine as “lowest-loaded,” but Jonas is lighter (4 open, skill 2, 6h, low overtime). C016’s overflow to Farid is invalid (Diagnosis skill 1 on a high-priority case, unsupervised). C001 cites a 2.7h handover against a 3h SLA but monitors queue growth instead of breach risk, and does not test supervised continuity. The 2.7h high-complexity statistic is also misapplied to C003 (complexity 0.66, medium handover).

The PM4Py sketch names the right functions, but `format_dataframe` uses nonexistent parameters, the “SLA-pressure” filter keeps fast cases (under 3h), `skill < 2` misses a Security floor of 3, and replay compares the policy with the case’s last resource rather than the next activity.
