4.5

The write-up identifies activation selection, delayed labels, policy change, migration, and temporal validation, and it does not treat rejects as non-defaults in the narrative. Those merits are outweighed by accounting errors, an incoherent estimand, incorrect censoring mechanics, and a skeleton that would mislabel outcomes and call invalid APIs.

**Target.** The primary estimand is \(P(\text{default}_{90d}=1 \mid \text{eventually activated}, X)\) with prediction at the first Credit Review. Eventual activation is future selection, so that quantity is not an operational risk at the chosen decision time. The text never states a deployable population (score only at activation, or model selection explicitly). The horizon runs from activation, while features stop at the first review, before document loops and later reviews exist. Section 3 then replaces the binary target with a survival model and, in the same “primary” strategy, an IPCW classifier. The competing-outcome alternative is described as requiring counterfactual assumptions about default given rejection. An observed multi-state outcome does not require that; a policy counterfactual would. The two questions are collapsed.

**Cohort.** The snapshot already partitions all 48,000 cases: \(21{,}600 + 9{,}100 + 5{,}700 + 2{,}900 + 5{,}100 + 3{,}600\). The flow invents a 3,600 “no Credit Review” exclusion and still lists 3,600 immature activations. The indented rows sum to 48,000, not to the stated 44,400. Inclusion, exclusion, and the table disagree, and partner-originated missing feeds are not a row. Still-open cases are called censored for a default analysis even though they have not entered the activation risk set. Linked transfers are given false precision (1,740 / 1,160) without defining what linkage supplies as a label. Date cutoffs are approximate (15 Nov, 11 Oct) and then applied inconsistently: ordinary censoring is defined as activation after 11 Oct, which also drops the delayed-but-possibly-labeled band.

**Censoring.** Immature activations are “right-censored at prediction time.” For a post-activation clock, censoring belongs at extraction minus activation, not at the first review. IPCW is offered to fix activation selection; IPCW reweights censoring, not who is accepted. Multiple imputation of immature binary labels ignores time still at risk. The log-rank test is claimed as a test of independent censoring; it compares survival curves. Permanently missing partner feeds are named and then left inside the analysis, where missing `default_90d` becomes non-default.

**Prefixes and features.** Fixed prefixes of 3, 5, and 7 events, and 1-, 3-, and 7-day windows, are measured from Application Started and can contain Accept, Contract Signed, Account Activated, and collections. A later rule truncates at the first Credit Review. At that same instant, document-loop and repeat-review features are structurally empty because those loops occur after the first review. `risk_score` is the first-event value in one section and the value at prediction in another. “Default rate for similar cases” has no as-of or maturity restriction. Resource experience is marked “repair,” but the extract stores experience at extraction, so event-time experience is not recoverable. Short traces are padded rather than kept as shorter prefixes.

**Leakage table and validation.** Post-decision fields are correctly dropped, and `documents_total` is handled. `default_90d` is marked Keep. The split text says the test set is only post-15 Oct 2025; the dates put the test set at starts from 1 Jul 2025, mixing pre- and post-migration. Train is all 2024 starts; validation and test are further cut to mature activations, so the populations differ. One 15 Feb 2026 label cutoff is used for every fold, so validation sees labels that would have been unavailable at a historical decision date. No embargo is set for the 90-plus-feed delay. Metrics fit a binary classifier and a survival model at once, without IPCW-weighted scoring. The critique of random splits is sound; the backtest that replaces them is not a realistic as-of test.

**Limitations.** The section correctly blocks counterfactual default for non-activated cases and names feedback, policy, and process change. It still claims the model can flag high-risk rework patterns that the prediction time cannot observe, and it equates an external economic shift with a deployment effect.

**Skeleton.** The block is far beyond the requested length and would not implement the study. `label_available_at` is never parsed. Maturity uses last-event time plus the horizon, so cases with collections or default events look less mature. `end_time == cutoff` does not identify the 5,100 open cases. Activated rows with missing `default_90d` are coded non-default. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is called with `max_events` and column keys it does not take. `get_variants_sorted` is not a PM4Py function, and `.items()` is treated as case-to-variant. `discover_dfg` is global and unused. `create_features` reads `case_info` out of scope and indexes it by case id on a RangeIndex. Training IDs are unfiltered while validation and test IDs are filtered on a mis-specified maturity flag. The leakage audit only checks that forbidden columns are absent from a frame that never added them. Survival time origin, event indicator, and censoring indicator are never built.
