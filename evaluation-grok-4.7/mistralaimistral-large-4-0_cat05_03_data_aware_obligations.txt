7.2

The case outcomes are largely right, and the required sections, event-time reconstruction, and PM4Py/pandas pipeline are present and usable. The formal rules and several required counterfactuals are not reliable enough for a higher score.

Correct release-time results: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are NONCOMPLIANT; TX-302, TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are COMPLIANT; TX-305 is INDETERMINATE. TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT is a defensible burden-of-proof reading of R5. Blanks are forward-filled only, final state is not copied backward, `event_index` orders TX-311, decreases and the 4.5% increase do not invalidate reviews, and a standard packet does not satisfy R2. EX-77 is separated from EX-88 on signature versus certificate time. The code rebuilds the log, calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, forward-fills, and emits coarse R1–R4 flags without a case-label lookup. Sections 6 and 7 cover the requested failure modes and logging controls.

The TX-311 alternative reading contradicts itself. If `Update_Jurisdiction` had not occurred, or occurred only after release, the release jurisdiction would remain US, R1 would trigger, and the 16:20 enhanced review would still follow the 16:10 amount increase (90,000 to 115,000, about 27.8%). That review would satisfy R1. The write-up instead treats the jurisdiction change as both absent and as a later material change that defeats the review.

The TX-305 counterfactual is too strong. A release payload, or a settlement figure tied to release, at or above 100,000 would establish the R1 trigger only. The review document has no amount, and R4 says an increase from an unknown amount is not classifiable. Satisfaction would stay unresolved. The findings row is better than that counterfactual.

Missing-data logic does not match R6. R1 is marked indeterminate whenever amount or jurisdiction is unknown, including when jurisdiction is DE or amount is below 100,000, either of which excludes the trigger. R2 is marked indeterminate when either risk or customer type is unknown. A known HIGH risk or known PEP already triggers R2; the code can therefore miss a violation. The same over-broad test is in the rule table and the checker.

`material_change` folds every risk, jurisdiction, and customer-type change into R1’s “latest material data change” test. R4 defines amount invalidation as an increase of more than 20%, and a decrease does not invalidate. Section 1 limits those other changes to the relevant rules; the pseudo-logic and `r1_enh_ok` do not. R2’s “relevant” enhanced review is only “some enhanced review before approval.” The checker never requires that review to post-date the HIGH or PEP change. In R3, the rescreen quantifier uses `u` outside the scope of `trigger_R3`.

The exemption path hard-codes `evidence_supports_timely_approval` and never checks `waives`. Certificate-creation and qualified-signature times are prose, while the diagnostics expose displayed approval, effective, and expiry times. Both sample exemptions waive R1, so this dataset still gets a coarse R1 failure on TX-310 and a pass on TX-302 via the review. TX-310’s High confidence is firmer than the conflicting oral-approval evidence supports; INDETERMINATE remains a live alternative. The trace check counts create/review/release order and does not state a DECLARE constraint.
