8.5

The design is conservative in the right places and numerically careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Several ordering, scenario, and implementation gaps keep it below a very high score.

The duplicate decisions match the stated facts. e03/e04 and e11/e16 are merged, e05 and e14 are retained as distinct object-level occurrences, e06/e07 and e13/e15 stay separate in the minimum-change view, and e08/e09 are treated as a harmonization collision rather than a duplicate. The decision tree covers every required terminal and assigns a sensible action to each. Retained sizes are consistent: 17/44, 15/39, 14/37, and 13/34, and the unrestricted counterfactual of 10 events and 28 links with one capture tied to both M1 and M2 is correct. The `have_common_object` warning is also right: O1 and V1 do not distinguish M1 from M2.

Section 7 is similarly accurate where checked. All 17 events touch O1, so 16 directly-follows steps is correct, as are the cleaned lengths 14, 13, and 12. Pick→Pick falling from two to one on O1 and from one to zero on Item, dispatch being one event pair witnessed by O1, P1, and D1, and Order–Package weights 9, 8, 7, and 6 all follow from the table. Provenance, reversibility, and the failure-mode list are concrete and aligned with the pipeline.

The main logical shortfall is the partial order. The policy correctly separates domain edges, source sequence, and eid fallback, and it correctly warns that synthetic microseconds invent durations and destroy tie groups. The stated 10:00 sequence is only the lexical total order. Without a pick-before-pack contract, e05 is constrained solely by e03 and may sit after e06 or even after e07; those admissible orders are not given. At 10:15, e09, e10, e08 is also admissible, and at 12:00 e11 can occupy any slot before e15. Source sequence is described as non-causal recording order, yet it is inserted as a hard edge, so e13 before e14 is presented as required even though no payment lifecycle fact orders M2 relative to M1.

The implementation only partly does what it claims. `ocel_merge_duplicates` is a probe on an isolated block; its merged log is discarded and a custom union is what the aggressive scenario actually returns. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is never executed on this log. Sorting by `tie_rank` alone would scramble different timestamps, and the later timestamp sort is what actually saves the order. The library scenario also relabels e15 in code, while section 5 describes that scenario as scoped mechanical merging only, so the sensitivity table under-describes the library Order DFG. Conflicting payload hashes are nulled on the canonical row despite the e03/e04 policy saying both hashes are preserved.

Those are targeted defects, not a wrong cleaning strategy. The counts, identity decisions, and metric interpretations are strong enough that the answer remains clearly good, but the incomplete admissible orders and the gap between the PM4Py calls and the logs actually analyzed are material.
