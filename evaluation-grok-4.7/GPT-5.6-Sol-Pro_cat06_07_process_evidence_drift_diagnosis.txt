The audit correctly splits the year into distinct regimes and supports that split with the right evidence. April is treated as a schema and prefix-pipeline failure (unknown activity 17%, queue missingness 14%, unmapped variants 18%, mean score 0.28 against a stable 0.19 breach rate). June is treated as a routing-mix change: the January–March variant rates (about 15.7% / 10.7% / 31.0%) applied to June’s mix give about 15.8%, next to the observed 15%, with V1/V2 breach rates still stable. August–September are treated as supplier-driven path slowdown plus a change in breach risk given the deployed features, with Partner Z added only in September. October is refused as a recovery: 55% maturity, 52-day median delay, and a backlog that drops long paths. The composition arithmetic and the September source identity (0.24×0.45 + 0.76×0.27 ≈ 0.313) check out. Falsifiers, dual-hypothesis tests, month-level training gates, and the rule that a maturity alarm must not retrain are all in place. Realized-variant leakage, opposing June versus August intercepts, and unstratified Partner Z comparisons are handled carefully.

Those strengths do not make the answer tight enough for a top score.

The covariate row uses the wrong estimand in one place. A gap between mean score and prevalence is a calibration fact. It is not evidence that P(X) changed. June is also labeled high-confidence route-mix covariate drift, while the same answer later states that final variant is retrospective unless intended route was known at diagnosis. Post-diagnosis routing is process drift and can change P(Y|X) for the frozen feature set; calling it high-confidence covariate drift in the main table overstates what the shares identify. The hedge (“medium” for the deployed feature vector) is present, but the confidence cell still leads with “high.”

The parts edge is not flat before the named supplier shock: 28h, 31h, 35h, 34h, 38h, 42h from January through June, alongside a small V3 breach creep from about 0.31 to 0.35. The write-up treats January–March as a fully stable process baseline and June as mix only. The July 10 break is real; the earlier slope should have been stated so the baseline is not cleaner than the table.

Matured variant rates and cutoff headline rates are not reconciled. August’s matured within-variant mix is about 0.31 (0.18×0.25 + 0.11×0.40 + 0.64×0.35) against a 0.28 headline at 93% maturity; September’s analogous figure is about 0.34 against 0.31 at 82% maturity. The direction agrees with censoring of long paths, but the answer never closes that gap, even though it is careful everywhere else about maturity definitions.

June’s mean score rises from about 0.19 to 0.20 while volume shifts onto low-risk V2 and the observed breach rate falls to 0.15. The over-prediction claim is fair. Why the score distribution moved up under a lower-risk mix is left unexplained.

The code sketch follows the requested shape: label normalization before features, source-aware dedup, event-calendar versus case-start cohorts, frequency and performance DFGs, variants, temporal features, and summaries with no invented drift detector. It still has audit bugs. `duplicated(..., keep="first")` is applied before any timestamp or event-id sort, so which duplicate survives depends on file order. Case-start `part_edge_median_hours` is the median over completed edges only. The prose says open `Order Part` events must be counted because completed-edge medians are right-censored, and then the sketch does not emit that count. For October that is the bias the monitoring section is supposed to catch. `closed_case_share` only partly substitutes. The sketch also asserts “current” signatures for `extract_temporal_features_dataframe(..., grouper_freq="M")` and for variant keys as count-valued tuples, while citing source only for `filter_time_range`.

None of these points overturn the recommended containment sequence: repair April, do not train on October, do not globally recalibrate June and August–September together, and keep a supplier-state feature rather than baking August’s V3 rate into the model. They are enough to keep the answer short of nearly flawless.
