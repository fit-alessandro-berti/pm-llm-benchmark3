The write-up keeps the requested section and table skeleton, and it correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change with appeal and loss risk. That is not enough to carry the analysis. The counterfactual paths, times, and several intervention definitions are infeasible or factually mixed up, and two required prose blocks are far under length.

**Feasibility model.** The fenced “partial order” is not a partial order and mis-states the process. Originals Receipt is placed after the checks; rule 4 requires it before Document Check. Compliance is treated as interchangeable with originals and is not tied to the latest Assessment. Request More Info is written as if it always accompanies Decision. Decision → Notify is missing. The constraints are an unfenced copy of the rules. Intervention classes are wrong: K-104 is called timing-only even though it adds early S1/C1 work and a capacity hold; K-219 is resource routing, not a pure timing tweak; suppressing the second rework loop is primarily a decision-policy change.

**Case paths.** Minimal-change and mandatory controls fail on the two high-risk cases. K-104’s specified change is an early S1 packet and a provisional C1 slot, with final compliance still after final Assessment. The path instead assigns complex corporate documents to D2 (not the intervention; D1 is the faster complex-document resource) and replaces final compliance with a post-assessment “provisional” review. K-337’s intervention applies after Assessment 2. The path jumps from Assessment 1 to Decision, drops the first rework, Document Check 2, Assessment 2, and the required compliance review, and so breaks both the intervention point and rules 5 and 9. K-219’s happy path is only roughly ordered; the handwriting-clarification branch is not a path. K-411’s concurrency is the only structurally plausible path. Outcome cells are “±1 hour” or “±2 hour” on cases whose factual cycles run about 1.5 to 9 days. Section 3 then quotes 14–22 hour totals, which contradict the table and cannot survive the queues: K-104’s D1 backlog alone is about 13 hours, K-219 cannot finish before originals at Mon 17:30, and K-337’s prefix is fixed through Fri 10:30. Snapshots are unused. Earlier readiness is never separated from M1 or assessment-queue completion. Base cases are just the factual traces.

**Derivations.** K-219 is described as supplier-total rework; that is K-337, and the factual K-219 check was accurate with no rework. K-104 claims a C1 reservation speeds the document check, and calls D2 faster. K-337 says compliance is unchanged while the path deletes it, and treats Friday-to-next-Wednesday as a two-hour effect. Best/worst ranges ignore service bands, overnight work already visible in K-104, and the chance that an incomplete early packet creates a new information-request loop. “Evidence that would reverse the conclusion” mostly restates the hoped-for result.

**Capacity and rollout.** The interference table names an originals-receipt queue the snapshot does not have, marks D1/D2 and compliance shifts as “none,” and does not give a sign for the system effect. At scale, reserving the only certified reviewer is said to prevent compliance bottlenecks; with C1 already at roughly 20 hours on Monday and two urgent cases added, blanket provisional holds would grow that queue and delay other urgent files. The 100-case discussion is about 100 words against a required 250–400, and it does not analyze D1/D2 substitution, created versus avoided rework, assessment saturation, weekend external waits, or logged timestamps versus true concurrency.

**Ranking, pilots, synthesis, assumptions.** K-411 is ranked most robust with a “significant” time cut while its own text says the assessment queue can absorb the gain. K-219’s benefit is listed as less rework, although the change raises clarification risk. K-337 is rightly last on robustness, but reversibility is “high” after a notified approve/reject, which is hard to undo. The synthesis then calls K-219 the least robust, contradicting the table, and runs about 185 words against 220–320. The K-104 pilot again assigns D2. Quality and spillover are swapped, and the spillover queue is again originals receipt. The eight “assumptions” restate rules; items 7 and 8 are unlabeled, and none is an assumption that could be falsified by queue or event data.

Format recognition is real. The operational conclusions are not reliable enough to use.
