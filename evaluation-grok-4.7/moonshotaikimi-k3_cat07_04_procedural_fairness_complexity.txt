The assessment is careful, well-structured, and correctly refuses to treat raw approval gaps as discrimination or case mix as a full defense. All eight required sections are present, the reported figures match the scenario, rights-preserving steps are treated as a burden rather than a fix to delete, and the bottom line stays within what the evidence can support: observed inequity in time and burden, with discrimination left unresolved.

The score is held down by several concrete problems. The within-path check does not isolate V2. It keeps every variant that mentions a document request and omits language support, so later duration comparisons mix different paths. The comment calls that output a median in days, while the call prints every case duration in seconds. Service and waiting time are inserted from a log that has only one timestamp per event, then summarized with an event-level median; that split is exactly the measurement problem the limitations section flags, and the code does not handle it. `prior_case_count` is placed in the “clean pre-treatment” control set and, elsewhere, in the historical feedback loop that can launder disparity. The claim that the 3.8- and 4.7-day support queues account for most of the adjusted +2.6- and +4.1-day penalties is stronger than the exposure data allow: those waits apply only to cases that take the step, medians do not add, and other edges (intake, document request, supervisor review) also differ. Frequency DFGs are named in the task and not used, and dual-role staffing is escalated to something that “must be fixed” although the log does not show specialists reviewing their own cases.
