The incubation clock is wrong by a day, and that error is load-bearing. Primary readiness is 46–54 h after Friday 14:00, so Sunday 12:00–20:00. The answer places it at Saturday 12:00–Monday 04:00 (22 h and 62 h after Friday 14:00). Saturday QA is already closed before the batch can be technically ready, so a Saturday terminal event has probability essentially zero.

That mistake drives the headline forecast. S2’s 3–12% Saturday completion and the “plausible earliest” of Saturday 13:30–16:30 / 22–26 h are not available outcomes. Section 6 then says an early Saturday negative readout would pull the central forecast forward by 1.5–2 days. A Sunday on-call readout still meets a closed QA calendar and a chemistry branch that cannot finish before Monday, so the smooth-path join stays on Monday.

S2 is also inconsistent with the answer’s own rules. Readout or call-out is 0.5–3 h, and QA Saturday ends at 13:00. A result that first becomes ready at the answer’s own Saturday 12:00 is usable at or after that close. The table’s central Saturday range runs to 17:00, while the assumption ledger and the S2 write-up both refuse routine QA after 13:00.

Other material problems:

- Chemistry is under-queued. One urgent and three ordinary samples are waiting, and both in-service jobs finish before 18:00. The longer analyst is free at 16:42 at the earliest, and the minimum new service is 1.5 h, so that analyst cannot finish any new sample on Friday. Under priority, the first free analyst takes the urgent sample and B-417 meets the later analyst too late to finish. FIFO leaves B-417 behind all four waiting samples. “B-417 first,” “some ordinary samples,” and “one/two paused samples” are too shallow, so Monday 08:00–10:00 starts are treated as ordinary rather than as a favorable priority case.
- A Friday chemistry finish still cannot join on Friday, because microbiology is not ready. The “Friday-evening QA” aside skips the parallel join.
- S1’s earliest terminal time, Monday 09:30, is an earliest join. Minimum QA plus electronic release still has to run. The same column gives a rerun path a Monday 09:30–11:30 terminal time, which is earlier than a path that adds a second chemistry service.
- The displayed product \(0.30\text{–}0.55 \times 0.20\text{–}0.40 = 0.06\text{–}0.22\) is labeled a laboratory-investigation allowance. It omits the 20–40% laboratory share of investigations. The 1–9% figure in S6 matches a further multiplication the formula does not show.
- S6’s table puts the earliest laboratory-investigation terminal time on Friday 25, while the decomposition says three calendar days from Monday can finish Thursday morning.

What keeps this above a failing methodological answer is the weekday machinery. The no-callout readout really is Monday 08:30–09:30, because Sunday readiness still waits for Monday staffing, and the no-callout confirmation path really does land on Tuesday. The join is written as a maximum, scenario bands are allowed to overlap, temperature dependence is kept inside Fréchet bounds rather than multiplied through, contamination is not treated as a critical defect, QA arrivals are not forced into a fake queue distribution, and working hours are distinguished from the 3–7 calendar-day laboratory clock. The central Monday-afternoon to Tuesday-morning band is in a defensible region for the smooth path. The earliest case, S2, and the forecast update attached to the next event are not.
