The final answer is a strong, segment-conditional redesign memo with the required seven sections, real trade-offs, and three genuinely different levers (routing constraints, evidence/fulfillment integrity, and resource/commitment design). It is not nearly flawless.

The diagnosis uses the frequency DFG, performance edges, fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces, R1–R3 move-on-log deviations, the 31%→9% cost erosion, 14.1% vs 3.3% complaints, the evidence loop, and segment outcomes. The core contradiction is handled correctly: the fast highway is partly fast because it skips mandated work, and its cost advantage is not uniform across accessories and high-value devices. Sacrifices, bottleneck migration, and the experiment sequence (holdouts, adjacent edges, complaint-after-close, abandonment, fitness as a guardrail) are specific rather than generic Lean advice. Exact dominance and ROI are appropriately refused.

Defects that keep this below a high score:

- Complaint inflow is stated as 550. The logged edges are 140 + 80 + 260 + 30 = 510, against 430 outflows. The inequality survives, but the figure is wrong and is used to support the reopen-after-close claim.
- “About 10 hours end-to-end,” repeated as “~10h highway” in the comparison table, is the sum of four transition means. Those means are not a case cycle time. The prompt’s own fast-path medians are 8h, 13h, 20h, and 28h by segment, so the highway is not one ~10h process. The later caveat that edge times are not case-level does not correct the table.
- Redesign A’s mechanism over-attributes compression of the 96–120h reviewed path to parallelizing “14h + 34h + 22h + 30h.” Those edges are not one serial path, and the 34h evidence return and 30h customer approval are largely exogenous waits. Overlap can hide fraud review and price prefetch; it does not remove customer time. The rule-update inflow is also stated as “will” be +15–35%, while the source says “may.”
- A and B both rely on a pre-dispatch gate. The primary mechanisms still differ, but they are less independent than the write-up implies.

What remains well grounded: keeping premium accessories on the fast lane, forcing R1/R2 with enabling conditions, using idle quality-check capacity, the assisted-channel offset to portal abandonment, the 82% utilization cliff, and Monday/campaign fraud peaks. The conclusion is defensible; the arithmetic and cycle-time aggregation are not.
