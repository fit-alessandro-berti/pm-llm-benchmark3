5.9

The framework is real and several required calls are right, but the portfolio actually chosen contradicts the rule and the arithmetic that are supposed to justify it. That is a coherence failure in the core deliverable, not a cosmetic defect.

Section 1 correctly refuses to treat `p_now` as a causal effect, hard-gates on stage, separates I-05 from I-07, and writes spillover into the stopping rule. The rule is still not deployable. “Material” probability jump is never defined, so I-02’s move from 0.58 to 0.66 has no status. Calibration adjustments are midpoints of the bias ranges, then mislabeled a conservative posterior. The lower-bound net-value screen is not applied consistently later.

Section 2 is where the answer breaks. The table marks I-07 “Wait / backup,” then the portfolio says intervene now on I-04, I-01, and I-07. Using the answer’s own midpoint net figures, the base marginal spillover of one slot is about €1,800 (6 × 0.02 × €15k). Against that bar, I-04 (~€9,740) clears, I-08 (~€6,275) and I-02 (~€3,045) clear, I-01 (~€1,835) is a tie, and I-07 (~€839) fails. The selected third case is the one the stated gate rejects, while two higher-value cases are held back. I-01 is Tier A with a lower euro score than Tier B I-02. “Ample 7h runway” is cited as a reason to swarm I-01 now, which reverses option value: long-runway triage can wait, and the 3h diagnosis-started case is the one that cannot. I-07 has 1.5h left, is still in triage, and still needs resolution and customer validation after a 45-minute swarm, so the effect may not be realizable inside the contract window. Two specialists and no sequence are never reconciled with “three slots now.” I-02 is treated as having no new event; the brief only says that about I-05. I-08’s lower-bound expectation stays positive under the answer’s own `(1−p*)` burden, so “downside negative” is an extra, unstated objective.

Section 3 is auditable on the direct-benefit products, then mis-adds the adverse row. €7.6k − €1,350 − €7k–€9k is about −€0.8k to −€2.8k, not −€1k to +€2k. False-alarm burden, used in section 2, disappears here. The “wrong alert” is assigned to low-value SME I-07 rather than to Strategic Enterprise I-04, the segment the brief says is over-predicted, which flatters the portfolio. Calling €15k exposure conservative understates harm relative to the visible diagnosis-stage losses, including I-03 at €40k, which still sits in the queue the swarm delays. Optimistic effects are the top of each range, not the upper half.

Section 4 handles I-05 versus I-07 in the right direction. The 4h cool-down is longer than several remaining SLA windows, and suppression is applied to a never-swarmed case with the book’s largest effect range without stating that an earlier alert was reviewed and rejected.

Section 5 names the right mechanisms and gives concrete controls. It also collides with section 2: I-09 loses today’s consideration because its interval is wide, after the safeguard says sparse-segment cases should not lose slots for that reason. A 30% value-segment floor is not tied back to the negative-EV gate.

Section 6 covers the required monitoring types and is explicit that the supplied effects are assumptions. The hold-back is not restricted to cases near equipoise, so the plan would randomize away clearly positive interventions on high-value incidents. Concurrency of the two specialists is still absent.

I-03 is correctly left unswarmed, I-05 is not treated like I-07, and feedback loops and segment bias are real parts of the policy. Those strengths cannot carry an allocation that fails the answer’s own marginal test, an adverse scenario whose net is signed incorrectly, and a selected case the table itself places on backup.
