7.5

The POWL model is the right canonical object: the twelve-edge transitive reduction matches the stated completion-event dependencies, the risk XOR, the optional supplier, and `ET IT (AP ET IT)*` are exact, and the process tree is a genuine conservative under-approximation rather than a disguised exact model. The language counts 4,588 and 7,896 (difference 3,308) for traces of length at most 15 are combinatorially correct, and T5 is a real witness of the extra gate (ARC before PV, CM before DCA, CM before the approval route, CM before Consult Supplier).

The witness analysis contradicts the answer’s own loop semantics. Under assumption 4, `AP` is the redo after a failed inspection, and after `ET IT` it is enabled. In N1 (`… ET IT AP RM AC`) the passing inspection never occurs; `AP` is a legal redo and the first illegal event is `RM`, where `ET` is required (D14 / incomplete redo). Calling this “adjustment after the passing inspection” and predicting an alignment break at `AP` is wrong, including in the validation plan. The stated playout invariants also do not reject N1: `ET`/`IT` still alternate and end on `IT`, and `RM` is still after the last `IT`.

The dismissal of duplication is too strong. An XOR of total orders with repeated labels can realize a finite N-language; unbounded interleaving is harder, but “duplication cannot remove the N-shape” is not shown. Label uniqueness is a sufficient reason to refuse that approximation. The self-critique’s “containment is usually fast” rationale is not the structural argument given in Section 3 and is not in the problem.

The reported net census (tree 22/19 with 5 silent transitions, POWL 20/18 with 4), presented as the result of a run, does not match the usual process-tree expansion: four parallel operators contribute two silent transitions each, plus the supplier `tau` and a loop-exit `tau`, i.e. 10 silent transitions and 24 transitions before any reduction. Treat those structural counts as unverified.
