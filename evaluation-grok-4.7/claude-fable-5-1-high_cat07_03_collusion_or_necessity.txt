7.5

The assessment is well calibrated and complete: it separates the emergency channels from the V-77x bank-change cluster, treats routine co-working as a baseline, keeps data-quality artefacts in view, and does not accuse anyone. The required sections, priority tiers, and report constraints are all met, and the audit wording is about 260 words. The score is held down by repeated evidential overstatement and analysis code that would not support the claims as written.

The risk table attaches the 42% and 31% high-amount shares to the 42 Create/Approve violations. Those shares describe the full Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo subgroups, not that violation set, and the source only says many of those 42 cases are emergency. The subcontracting caveat compares raw loop counts (36 and 29 versus 33 and 30) and calls the shape normal. Against pair volume, the emergency loops are roughly four times as frequent; the later rate-normalization note does not correct the table. “Mostly emergency” is also stronger than the stated “many.”

Prioritization and the report carry the same kind of slip. H3’s count (~15 and ~22) is every non-emergency case in those channels, while the cluster is defined as non-emergency cases that were also fast or same-person. M2 and the report turn “late retrospective justification” (18% and 21% of the pair subgroups) into “late or missing” and “one in five emergency cases lacks a timely, independently entered” justification. Late, missing, and same-person are different facts; 17 same-person cases are not that rate. M6 is a medium-priority cluster for threshold clustering that the log summary does not show. The 16-case fast bank subgroup is described as all under one hour when only its median (17 minutes) is given.

Section 4 is the right analysis design, but the code breaks or undoes it in places. `vendor_family` is added to `df` after `fixed_log` is copied, so the network analysis and the V-77x timing check both reference a missing column. The `approved_by` overwrite then replaces the `entered_by` correction on the approval events the shared-account fix was meant to repair. Artefact detection compares case indexes only, so a case that violates different activity pairs in the two views is treated as the same finding. `discover_network_analysis` is also wired to consecutive events with vendor family as the source node, which does not show who touched each vendor. Pattern F is given only one benign explanation.

The priority order itself remains sound: Lena/Kim first, emergency-channel exceptions second, routine pairs as a control group.
