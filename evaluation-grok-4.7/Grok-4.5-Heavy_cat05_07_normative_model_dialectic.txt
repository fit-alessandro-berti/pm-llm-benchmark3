6.7

The dialectical write-up is unusually complete and mostly well calibrated, but the required formal model is semantically wrong in ways that would reject the compliant baseline, and the authority conclusion is asserted more strongly than it is reconciled.

Sections 1–5 cover every case, keep prosecution and defense in role, and use the aggregate counts without arithmetic error. The burden of proof is usable. SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714 are correctly treated as authority or eligibility failures. SUP-704 is correctly kept inside the FD-7 window, including a next-business-day quality review that may fall after 2026-07-12. SUP-706, SUP-709, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are assigned to data or scope for defensible dominant reasons. Both briefs make the concessions the task demanded.

The authority judgment is the main reasoning gap. The record contains both a contemporaneous “monitored exception” and a later statement that CS-9 was not weakened, plus the fact that PD-3 was never incorporated into the controlled standard. The prosecution brief cites that hierarchy and then narrows the violation to conduct outside the written windows, which gives away the strongest no-derogation case. The final judgment calls the overlays “valid” while rejecting prosecution, without weighing the retrospective denial against the minutes. That is the disputed proposition, and it is resolved by label rather than by an explicit authority rule.

Case-level precision slips in a few places that matter. SUP-708 is not only sanctions-after-activation: Manual_Procurement_Approval is also after Activate_Supplier, a second independent CS-9 breach, while Quality_Review before activation is what CS-9 requires. The matrix phrase “Quality early” obscures that. SUP-702 is called an exact, high-confidence match to PD-3 even though automated master-data validation is required and absent from the trace; the matrix itself lists that gap. SUP-712 is stipulated to be the same human under two aliases, which is a direct CS-9 resource breach. The answer’s own burden is about controlled rules, yet the primary label is MIXED while the prose says the dominant result is noncompliance. SUP-711 is described as a pure duplicate with high confidence and then labeled indeterminate; those claims pull apart. SUP-710’s prior certification is given as remaining valid, while the matrix calls it assumed.

Section 6 has the right layer split, and the data proposal covers notification identifiers, eligibility snapshots, resolved actors, delegation, authority references, and immutable timestamps. It still drops PD-3’s master-data validation from the enforceable rule set, does not separate effective time from record time, and does not keep a raw alias alongside the resolved person. The COI layer also adds a mitigation requirement the source does not state.

Section 7 is the decisive defect. In PM4Py, `Operator.LOOP` with children `[A, B]` executes A at least once and uses B as the redo child, not as an exit. `children=[rmi, skip]` therefore means one or more `Request_More_Info`. The comment and the limitation statement both say zero or more. As written, the model rejects SUP-701 and every other clean case with no information request. The loop also sits entirely before the route choice, so it cannot generate SUP-707, where both requests occur after sanctions and before quality. The same `Transition` objects are members of several XOR branches, so the value is not a tree-shaped POWL; sanctions, quality, manual approval, and activation need distinct nodes per route. On the auto route, the edge `activate → quality` obliges deferred quality. PD-3 only permits quality to lag; it still allows quality before activation. Partial-order edges are obligations. The overlay table and the attribute list are stronger than the model they are meant to accompany, but they cannot repair a POWL that mis-states loop6.5

The briefs, burden of proof, and case matrix are strong and mostly traceable. The required POWL is not. It is ill-formed, its loop semantics are stated falsely, and it rejects traces the judgment treats as conforming or desirable. That is a material failure on an explicit deliverable, so the answer cannot score as nearly flawless.

The loop is the clearest error. `Operator.LOOP` executes the first child, then either exits or executes the second child and repeats the first. `LOOP(Request_More_Info, skip)` is therefore one or more requests, not “0..n,” and the silent child is a redo step, not an exit. The prose asserts the opposite twice, including in the limitation statement. Because that loop sits on the path into every route, the model excludes SUP-701 and every other case with no `Request_More_Info`. SUP-707 still does not fit: its requests occur after `Sanctions_Check` and before `Quality_Review`, while the model places the loop only between registration and the whole route. The parenthetical that requests “may also be inserted inside routes” is not an implemented approximation.

The same `Transition` objects are reused across exclusive routes (`sanctions`, `quality`, `activate`, `manual`). A POWL is a tree; those leaves have several parents. Construction may not throw, but the object is not a valid POWL. Rejection is only `Sanctions_Check → Reject_Supplier`, so both-checks-then-reject, which M0 allowed, has no route. The auto route forces `Quality_Review` after activation, although PD-3 permits a lag and does not require one. Automated master-data validation is omitted. Those are semantic regressions, not acknowledged over-approximations. Sanctions-before-activation on every activation route, mandatory notification before closure, the overlay table, and the attribute list are the sound parts of section 7. The attribute list still has one timestamp, not separate immutable effective and record times, which is the distinction SUP-706 turns on.

Case reasoning is better. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are correctly high-severity noncompliance; expiry is correctly held against SUP-705 and SUP-714; SUP-704 is distinguished from SUP-705; CRM linkage is correctly treated as stable for SUP-702 and unreliable for SUP-706. The burden is explicit and usually applied.

It is not applied cleanly throughout. SUP-702 is a high-confidence justified exception while the matrix says required master-data validation is missing. SUP-711 is “indeterminate” at high confidence that the duplicate is artifactual; the supported conclusion is a compliant case with a logging duplicate. SUP-712 and SUP-715 are labeled MIXED after the text identifies a dominant non-control-flow breach, so the label softens the verdict the analysis already reached. SUP-710 calls validity “assumed” when the case states that the prior certification remained valid. “M0 predates both overlays” is not established. The defense conflates M0’s sanctions/quality concurrency with post-activation quality, which M0 does not allow. The prosecution names hidden resource defects but does not actually argue SUP-712 or SUP-715.

Dialectically, both sides are real arguments, and the system judgment correctly keeps CS-9 binding, accepts only in-window written overlays, and refuses to treat desirability or residual need as authority. The model meant to encode that judgment does not accept the cases that judgment relies on.
