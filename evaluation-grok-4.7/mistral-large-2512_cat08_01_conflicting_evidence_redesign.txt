The write-up follows the required section contract and offers three distinct bets (constrain-and-gate the replacement path, attack evidence loops, force high-value devices onto repair). That structure is not enough. Load-bearing figures are invented or reversed, so the diagnosis and the comparison table would mislead a sponsor.

**Evidence errors that change the story**
- The 240 `Fraud/risk review → Fast approval` cases are treated as R1 skips (“10% of fast approvals” that miss manual/fraud review). That edge is the subset that already had fraud review before fast approval, which is what R1 allows. Fast-approval volume is 2,790 (2,550 from auto-screen plus 240), so 240 is about 9%, and those cases are the compliant inflow.
- “Median 5.3 h” for `Intake → Auto eligibility → Fast approval → Dispatch` is not in the log. Edge medians do not add to a path median, and 0.5 + 0.4 + 1.8 + 3.2 is 5.9 anyway. Segment fast-path medians that are given are 8 h, 13 h, 20 h, and 28 h.
- “72 h median” complaint resolution swaps the mean. `Fast approval → Customer complaint/reopen` is mean 72 h, median 50 h, and that clock is time-to-complaint, not resolution time. `Customer complaint/reopen → Escalation board` is mean 54 h, median 42 h; the redesign then “cuts median 54 h to 30 h.”
- The “median 10 h wait” is attached to `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review`. That edge is mean 34 / median 26 / p90 88. Median 10 h is the opposite edge, `Manual eligibility review → Request missing evidence`.
- “60% of premium high-value are auto-approved,” “repair technicians idle 40%,” “EUR 410 × 130 cases/year = EUR 53k,” “eliminates 90% of violations,” “−50% dispatch complaints,” inspector/reviewer/tech utilizations of 80/85/90%, and table cost effects of −5% / −8% / +3% are not in the artifacts. The prompt bars exact figures the log does not support. The idle-40% claim also points at the wrong resource: quality inspectors are the underused pool; repair delay is part-price waiting.
- Fitness 0.91 and 68% fitting traces (32% non-fitting) are real, then pinned on R2 alone. Alignment deviations also include fast approval before evidence completeness, reopen after close, and dispatch without risk review on high-value devices. Calling the 120 escalation-board handoffs a “precision” failure mixes precision (model allows extra behavior) with a fitness/alignment claim.
- “26% of cases require 2 requests” drops the denominator. The 26% is of cases that already enter evidence request. The 320 abandonments are the evidence-request edge, not a measured count for the 22% unclear-defect segment. The residual fact that fast approval stays 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits is softened into “savings are eroded,” which overstates the cost case against the frequent path.

**Process-mining and trade-off logic**
- Redesign A sends complaints to a larger escalation board after the diagnosis treats `Customer complaint/reopen → Escalation board` (120) as an R3 bypass of manual review. The same design defaults standard high-value (21%, about 1,050 cases) toward fraud review staffed by 2 specialists who are already the Monday bottleneck, then names quality inspectors as the new bottleneck.
- Redesign C reads 3.3% vs 14.1% as the effect of repair. Those rates are observational and selected; the prompt’s own segment split is 22% vs 5% (premium high-value) and 18% vs 6% (standard high-value). Forcing both high-value segments (35% of 5,000) onto a quote path now measured in the hundreds (`Repair quote → Customer approval` 460, `Repair device` 390) with 3 technicians is not a “low complexity rule update,” and “90% utilization” understates the shock. The claimed move “from 28 h to 72 h” uses the standard high-value fast-path median as the repair baseline. Current reviewed/repair medians are already 96 h and 120 h. `Manual eligibility review → Repair quote` is already too slow for premium (mean 22 / median 15 / p90 60). Pre-approving “top 20” defects and cutting 30 h to 12 h uses the mean (median is 21 h) and a defect ranking the log does not contain. Quote-path abandonment (`Customer approval → Claim abandoned` 70) is ignored.
- Redesign B’s portal range (about 40% inside the stated 30–45%) and the low-literacy abandonment risk are grounded, and defaulting reopens to manual review matches R3. The “SLA from 10 h to 36 h” reuses the wrong 10 h edge. Adding 2 reviewers is a 29% capacity increase against a possible 15–35% inflow rise, and the table’s 85% utilization sits above the stated 82% queue-growth cliff. Moving 1 of 2 fraud specialists off Monday does not move Monday’s work.
- The Pareto column marks the current process “Dominated? Yes” and A and C “No.” On the table’s own cycle-time column every redesign is slower, so nothing shown dominates the current process. That is also the dominance claim the prompt says the data cannot support. Premium low-risk’s 24 h service-credit cliff is never used as a design constraint, even though A and C correctly leave complete-evidence low-risk on auto-approval (current fast median 8 h, complaint 6% vs 3% manual).

**Experiment**
Phase 1’s go/stop pair leaves premium high-value complaint rates between 10% and 15% undecided. Phase 2’s 6-week pilot ignores the answer’s own 3-month portal build. Sequencing says Phase 1 “validates manual capacity” for the portal, but Phase 1 pilots a different segment and a dispatch gate. Phase 3 reuses the same premium high-value slice and sets a repair-time stop at 96 h, which is today’s premium reviewed/repair median, without a washout. The control-group note and 30-day downstream watch are the right idea; they still do not test selection bias, right-censoring on complaint tails (dispatch→complaint p90 180 h), or whether cycle time improved because cases were denied or abandoned.
