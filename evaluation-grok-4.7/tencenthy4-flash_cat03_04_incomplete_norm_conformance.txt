The normative analysis is strong: statuses, open- versus closed-world scope, the directly-follows versus eventual-validation distinction, and the separation of forbidden behavior, mandatory gaps, typical absences, unregulated relations, and suspected data-quality problems are handled carefully and with the right counts and norm IDs. Benign absences from the edge set are not treated as violations, and the investigation plan correctly concentrates on N3 and T6.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 1 cites E1–E8 as examples of missing mandatory behavior, although several of those relations are fully observed; section 4 later classifies them as conforming, so the delivered answer is internally inconsistent. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` is “unregulated” in the table and “typical” in section 4. The semantic layer defaults a missing `present` flag to true and ignores partial counts, so a 98-of-100 mandatory shortfall would not be classified as missing. One norm clarification also hedges between unspecified and mandatory instead of choosing a status.
