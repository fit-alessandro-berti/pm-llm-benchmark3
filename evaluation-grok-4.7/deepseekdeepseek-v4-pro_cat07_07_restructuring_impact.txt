The structured forecast covers every required heading and lands on a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout, with East treated differently from North and South and with tails, fairness, and learning kept in view. It is not reliable enough to score higher.

The mechanism map misstates the management table. Listed handovers rise from about 343 to 359 per 100 cases: technical routing 36/31 to 41/36, approvals 42 to 49, legal/risk 9 to 12. Only cross-team transfers and the intake-to-caseworker step fall. Saying the handover count “may drop slightly” is the wrong direction; the central scenario later says specialist handovers increase, so the report contradicts itself. Utilization is written as “~7082%” to “~8595%.” Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91%, so a further 15% utilization increase, plus difficult quality checks, is infeasible, and that capacity break is never calculated. Removing one of three supervisors while approval volume rises from 42 to 49 per 100 cases is likewise left out of the queueing story.

The optimistic case assumes legal/risk waits fall to 8.5 hours because triage gets stricter. The stated design does the opposite: detection broadens from 9 to 12 per 100 cases and the management wait rises from 9.6 hours to 10.5 hours. Exception handling is only a clause inside accountability, while tail delay and complex-case rework largely repeat the same point.

The pilot does not test the proposal. North and South keep embedded specialists, so N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech are never pooled, and legal/risk plus the shared approval queue stay unchanged. A clean East result therefore cannot support the later claim that a successful pilot could justify full rollout. The rework guardrail of 12% after the central specialist is weaker than the current 8–9% rework on the local technical loop.

The PM4Py sketch names the right families of techniques, but the comparison code would not produce the claimed results. `get_all_case_durations` is treated as a frame that can be grouped by team and segment, `SLA` is undefined, event-level `reassignment_count` is averaged as if it were per case, and `get_service_time` is used where waiting time is required. Resource filters invent pool names that are not in the log.
