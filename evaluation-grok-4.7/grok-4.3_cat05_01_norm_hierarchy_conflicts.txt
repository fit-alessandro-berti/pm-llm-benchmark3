4.0

The memo has the required seven-part shape and a few sound local judgments, but it does not apply its own hierarchy to the cases. Several classifications contradict the method in §§1–3, two case rows misstate the record, and one row treats a fact that is not in the evidence as decisive. Under a strict reading, that is a failed consistency and evidential-discipline performance, not a near-miss.

The method is already unstable before the cases. Section 1 says an ordinary CFO email cannot waive GPIP-4, while §2 and the third pseudo-logic line let that email override Finance approval up to €150,000. A2 is itself a GPIP-4 pre-commitment control, so those statements cannot both govern. Rule R2 then says the €50,000 finance-and-budget-owner requirement has no exception, and R7 says finance is not waived. The first pseudo-logic line is broader still: it makes ECD-2 displace R1–R3, and the fifth line says ECD-2 does not displace R3. R1 is unusable as written: the behavior cell omits the prohibition and reads as if the requester or committer is the final approver. R4 incorrectly attaches Incident Command joint authorization to a B4 emergency; B4 does not require that authorization. The hierarchy table also puts the wrong content in “What it probably cannot override” for GPIP-4 and the Aachen SOP. Severity is never defined. “Critical sterilization-line lot” is never chosen as an assumption, even though C6 is an express dispute.

Case application is where the method breaks.

- P-501 is the clearest factual miss. Stoppage predicted in four hours meets B4’s six-hour definition, so “B4 emergency definition not met” is false, and commit-before-PO is permitted in that emergency. The emergency form at 07:50 on 15 July is inside 24 hours of 08:50 on 14 July, so “Satisfied rules: None” is false. The row’s decisive evidence, a supplier acknowledgment at 08:50, is not in the case. The known facts are different: ECD-2 was not yet in force, the Plant Manager was reachable and had not delegated, and Plant and Finance approvals came after commitment. The ERP outage covering 08:42 and 08:50 is ignored.
- P-502 is classified JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION while the same memo says late Compliance_Clearance does not cure an A3 miss and that ECD-2 cannot override that clearance. This is a new supplier, and clearance is at 18:30 after a 10:15 commitment. Finance at 13:05 is also after commitment. The date is not in the evidence, so a 14 July reading would also predate the CFO email. Section 6 then calls this late evidence of a timely act. The late clearance is a late control performance; the 12:55 joint record is within four hours of 10:10 and is timely.
- P-503 is rightly NONCOMPLIANT, and the PROC-3 requester/committer/authorizer overlap is real. The controlling text nevertheless treats the audit’s 24-hour reading as settled. The label can stand without that reading, because missing A3 clearance, missing A2 finance and budget-owner approval, and the segregation overlap survive either scope reading. “Decisive evidence: None” discards the execution record that actually decides the row. The §5 alternative does not change the result unless A1 and A3 are also waived, which the stated premise does not do.
- P-504 is the cleanest noncompliance: cafeteria repair, no production or sterilization effect, unapproved supplier, one caller, no Incident Command authorization. High confidence is still a little hard, because the row never separates “unapproved” from GPIP “new supplier,” and it never asks whether a cafeteria freezer is inside the SOP’s maintenance and production-support scope.
- P-505 handles the expiry clock well. The 17:59:31 acknowledgment is better evidence than the 18:06 ERP stamp, and Medium confidence fits that inference. COMPLIANT does not follow. Finance_Approve is at 19:00, after both the inferred commitment and expiry; Create_PO is absent; the committer is unnamed; the four-hour recording is unshown. The row does not explain how A2 is met.
- P-506 correctly rejects an unrecorded verbal extension after 18:00 on 18 July. It then invents a deviation. Finance_Approve at 06:05 is before Commit_to_Supplier at 06:20. The live timing defects are Plant_Approve at 09:10 and PO after commitment without a B4 emergency or a written extension.
- P-507 correctly prefers the 12:40 signed PDF to the 14:30 ERP entry: 12:40 is inside four hours of 08:55, and Compliance_Clearance at 08:50 is before commitment. The row still omits the absent Finance event on a €110,000 purchase and does not list R3 as satisfied. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is reachable only if the CFO email substitutes for A2, which §1 and R2 reject.
- P-508 is the largest unsupported compliance call. €155,000 is above the email’s €150,000 operational-approval cap; D2’s Legal_Clearance is additive and is present, but there is no Finance event and no Plant event. A2 and B2 are unmet on the answer’s own catalogue. Segregation against BUY-8 and pre-commitment Compliance_Clearance are satisfied, and the affected-line pump is the strongest scope fit in the set. Those points support a partial pass, not “deviations: None,” COMPLIANT, and High confidence. The four-hour recording artifact that the memo demanded for P-507 is not demanded here.

Section 5 meets the four-case count and misses the point of the exercise. The P-501 “broad” B4 reading is the ordinary reading, and even that reading leaves the delegation failure and the late local approvals. P-503’s alternative cannot flip the label. P-504 and P-506 do not say why the alternative is weak. The classifications that most need a flip path are P-502, P-505, and P-508.

Section 6 names the right categories and then misfiles P-502. Retroactive Chief Risk Officer ratification is recommended for substantive breaches without stating that A6 mitigates consequences and does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval, and without showing that A7 authorizes an after-the-fact exception. The useful budget-owner warning in the monitoring paragraph is not fed back into P-505, P-507, or P-508.

Section 7 preserves segregation, new-supplier or €100,000 compliance clearance, a four-hour recording duty, and a real anti-extension rule. It does not resolve the dispute that drove the case errors: the €50,000 budget-owner and Finance Controller control, the €25,000 local dual approval, the €150,000 email cap, CFO authority, or the meaning of “critical.” “Explicitly defined” delegates the scope question instead of closing it.

What keeps the paper above a lower failing mark is mechanical completeness, the P-504 scope exclusion, the P-505 preference for the acknowledgment over the ERP clock, the P-506 refusal of an oral extension, the P-503 segregation catch, and the P-507 PDF-versus-ERP distinction. Those are real process skills. They are not applied evenly, and they do not control the final labels.
