The audit is strong and well aligned with the task. It correctly separates closure-time and full-trace leakage from repairable lineage defects, keeps queue length and elapsed time as admissible signals, and treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legal but unavailable at `t0`, while treating `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as both future and process-impossible. Proxy, drift, causal, and actionability standards are distinguished, and the Case Y narrative does not restate the leaked variant. Coverage of the top-12 features, sequence patterns, and local-only fields is complete, and the validation plan is concrete.

The revised explanations are less reliable than the audit. Signed “credible drivers” are still taken from a model dominated by leaked features. For Case X, appeal, final decision, and the residual account for most of 0.82, so the surviving signs are not defensible until retraining. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is known to use the wrong time window, and `initial_reserve` and `claimant_channel` are unverified, yet all three appear as credible drivers.

The process narrative is also too loose. For high complexity, the normative next activity is a document request; damage assessment belongs to the low-complexity fast track or follows evidence. The Case X narrative still lists both as enabled. Section 2 then exempts a Withdrawn decision from the damage-assessment ordering rule, which the stated process does not support. A same-day document count of 6 is also called hard to reconcile with the process, although documents can exist at registration without a `Documents Requested` event. `total_case_duration_hours` is described as encoding the breach label, without distinguishing closure time from time to settlement. These are local inaccuracies, not a failure of the overall leakage judgment.
