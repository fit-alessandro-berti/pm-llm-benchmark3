7.2

The answer is structurally complete and often careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Quantitative sign discipline breaks down, the preferred design is not the design that produces the reported numbers, and the final claim ladder reintroduces a quasi-precise causal hour estimate that the rest of the answer has already judged unidentified.

Section 1 stays descriptive and correctly separates availability, the pre-existing 4–6% fast-path share, endogenous selection, co-interventions, and aggregate measurement. The estimand table is the strongest part: ITT versus intensity versus case-level assignment, median versus tail, rework versus escalation, CSAT under nonresponse, dynamics, transport, and fraud-desk interference are distinguished, and “not identifiable from the supplied panel” is the right default. Two weaknesses remain. E1’s horizon is only the launch month plus one month, which does not match the April–August contrasts later treated as evidence. Several “No” cells also describe conditional estimability, which makes the later “conditionally causal” hours look like a retreat from the table.

The primary strategy is the right family for staggered adoption: cohort-specific comparisons to not-yet-treated branches, no already-treated controls, and an explicit rejection of naïve two-way fixed effects under heterogeneous timing. Interrupted time series, synthetic control, and a prospective saturation design are credible alternatives, with failure modes that fit five branches and three-to-six pre-months. The strategy is not executed. The reported contrast uses Delta and Epsilon for all of April–August and never uses not-yet-treated Gamma in April–June. No baseline-slope or complexity-adjusted cohort estimate appears. The code builds relative-time indicators and then ignores them, fitting the very two-way fixed-effects specification the prose warns against, plus an intensity regression that conditions on arrivals per FTE and complexity after those variables may be affected by rollout. The caveats keep this from being a causal claim, but they do not make the code a check of the stated design.

Most pre/post arithmetic checks: Alpha and Beta post means, Gamma’s windows, the roughly +0.7 / +9.7 / 0 / +2.7 adopter changes, the +5.2 / +16.0 / +1.9 / +1.8 never-treated changes, workload gaps near +7.5 versus +3.0, and Gamma’s local −8.5 versus −4.3 median comparison. The results table still fails auditability. Pre and post figures are concatenated (`28.033.8`, `98.5108.1`, `8.511.4`), so the central display is ambiguous. The difference-in-change row has no single sign convention: cycle time and rework are written as unsigned improvements, while escalation stays treated-minus-control. Delta/Epsilon CSAT changed by about −0.08, not `0.08`; the `+0.09` contrast is only coherent if that minus sign is restored. The January–March baseline also mixes in the March fraud-desk jump, which attenuates escalation changes, and no sensitivity drops March. The April versus July–August intensity comparison misses the August arrival decline in every branch, including never-treated ones, so late median improvement is not cleanly a dose or learning signature. Alpha’s own post median is 48.9 to 48.5, so “Alpha and Beta did not show raw average median improvement” is true only as a pooled worsening.

Mechanisms and threats are substantively strong: substitution versus congestion, displaced rework, tail amplification, survey selection, SUTVA through the shared desk, treatment version, aggregation, and five clusters are all real. Direction is sometimes “ambiguous” where a likely sign under a stated true effect could still be given. Effect-assessment ranges are honest but informal; confidence 20–30 and an indeterminate p90 assessment are appropriate.

The recommendation, embedded saturation in Delta and Epsilon with case-level offers, queue and fraud-desk instrumentation, and harm guardrails, matches the evidence better than immediate expansion. The scale-up rule is weaker than the design: “5% median improvement or a clearly favorable operational margin” leaves an undefined second path to 100%.

The claim ladder is the largest logical break. It treats “about 4–5 fewer median hours and 6–8 fewer p90 hours” as conditionally causal if trends are parallel after measured adjustment. Those hours are unadjusted relative contrasts, not adjusted estimates. The same answer says the effects are not identified, gives p90 confidence 20 and a −12 to +12 hour span, and shows Alpha/Beta raw p90 worsening by about 10 hours. That bullet is stronger than anything section 7 permits and is ordered above the narrower Gamma suggestion. The closing rejection of a universal “reduces cycle time by X%” is correct, but it does not undo the overprecise conditional claim above it.
