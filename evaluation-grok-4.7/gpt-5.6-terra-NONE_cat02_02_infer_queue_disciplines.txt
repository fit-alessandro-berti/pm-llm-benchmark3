The write-up is a real decision-set analysis with the required structure, labeled assumptions, and several correct rule comparisons, but load-bearing facts and the headline diagnoses are unreliable. Under a strict reading it cannot score higher.

The required diagnosis table conflicts with the later argument. QA is labeled “appointment-aware non-preemptive priority,” with priority among ordinary jobs as the secondary rule and confidence 72. Sections 2, 3, and 7 then correctly show that ordinary selections are FIFO-like and that strict priority fails at 08:32 (A02 over A04), 09:05 (A05 over A08), and 09:32 (A07 over A08). The table’s own “contradicting” cell is that 08:32 choice. QD’s secondary component likewise asserts resource-local priority, while D04 over D08 at 11:25 and D07 over D10 at 11:48 reject local priority and SPT; FIFO fits both. Section 3 still says D08 at 11:35 “supports priority,” even though that row shows FIFO, priority, and SPT all predict D08.

QB is the most serious factual failure, and it is the queue called most identifiable at confidence 88. The service order B01–B02–B01–B03–B05–B01–B04 is stated correctly, but the second stop is then moved. B01’s segments end at 09:16 and 09:48; 10:14 is the start of B01’s third segment after B05 completes, and 10:14–10:28 is service, not an idle gap before B04. The summary says B01 is interrupted at 10:14 for B05 and that each interruption is followed by a higher-priority job. The contradicting-episode cell even admits that selecting B05 at 10:00 supports priority. The actual priority violations are underweighted or missed: resume B01 over B03 at 09:31, start B03 over already-arrived P1 B05 at 09:48, resume B01 over waiting P2 B06 at 10:14, and start B04 over B06 at 10:28.

The scan-delay argument is reversed against the answer’s own assumption. A recorded arrival at 09:46 means physical arrival in [09:44, 09:46], so B05 was already visible at 09:48. Delay cannot place B05 after that decision. Section 5 then claims a 09:48 decision could precede visibility of a 09:46 scan.

QC omits C05 at 10:18. C05 arrived at 10:11, is priority 1, and has the longest estimate. Priority therefore predicts C05 and SPT predicts C03; they do not agree. The observed C03 is stronger evidence against priority than the write-up allows. The diagnosis table still cites C04 at 10:32 as SPT support, while the body notes that SPT would select C06. The later C05-versus-C06 and C01-versus-C07 discussions are sound, so QC is the best-calibrated queue, but the early eligible sets are not.

Other material misses: A06 at 09:20 is not served ahead of A05, because A05 occupies the server through 09:20 and finishes as A06 starts; FIFO also selects A06, so that episode does not identify an appointment rule. A03’s 08:20 start coincides with A01’s completion, so the clock time itself does not show that dispatch waited for the appointment; the real evidence is the choice of A03 over A02 and A04. “A03’s early service” misstates the note: arrival was early, service was at 08:20. Every realized duration equals `est_min` (B01 only after summing segments), which cuts against rank-5 “noisy estimates,” and B01’s segments sum exactly to 45 with no visible preemption overhead. On QD, D-F is free from 11:36 to 12:00 while D05 and electrical D10 are waiting; D10’s 12-minute estimate fits that window, and D05’s 30 minutes would collide with the 12:00 meeting. The answer treats 12:20 as simple return-from-meeting and repeats “D-F unavailable until 12:20” without reconciling the idle interval. D-X’s idle 11:20–11:30 is the real appointment evidence for D06; at 11:30 FIFO selects D06 as well.

Sections 4 and 6 are the strongest. The three discrimination tests name the right extra observables (appointment state, SLA/escalation flag, assignment timestamp), and the collection plan is specific to these ambiguities. Those sections do not repair the mis-timed QB log, the omitted QC eligible job, or headline policies that the narrative itself abandons. QB is not the most identifiable discipline; one labeled urgent preemption shows that preemption can occur, not that stable preemptive priority governs later choices.
