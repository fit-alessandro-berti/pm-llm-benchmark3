The final answer meets the required seven-section structure, keeps three genuinely different redesigns, and uses many correct mining figures (2,550/2,300 fast-path volume, 31% then 9% cost advantage, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen, 96h/p90 180h dispatch-to-complaint, 22% vs 5% premium high-value, 26% repeated evidence, 890/34h evidence return, 68% fitting traces). The comparison table breaks its own scale. “Compliance risk” is High for the current process because of violations, Low for Redesign A, then High again for Redesign C with the parenthetical “avoids dispatch violations.” High cannot mean both bad risk and good compliance. Redesign B’s cell is not a risk rating at all. Redesign C also routes high-value repairs from the auto-screen to an auto-quote while bypassing manual or fraud review, which can breach R1, so the favorable compliance label is not earned.

Redesign A equates a gated replacement flow with the existing reviewed/repair outcome. The 5% complaint rate and 96–120h medians belong to the repair path, not to risk review plus a 6–18h pre-dispatch gate followed by replacement. The table then states a “96h+” cycle-time effect, while the experiment stops A if cycle time exceeds 48h. That is an unsupported magnitude and an internal contradiction. The diagnosis also assigns both R1 and R2 to “dispatch without risk review”; R2 is the evidence-completeness rule, and R3 is omitted.

The experiment misapplies evidence. The 82% utilization cliff is given for manual eligibility, not fraud/risk. The Stage 3 quality stop treats 45 rework cases as a rate; that is an absolute count and will rise if repair volume grows even when quality improves. “21-hour manual quote” mixes the fraud-to-quote edge with the manual edge. “Drastically reducing” the 140h unclear-defect median overstates a portal expected only to cut repeat evidence requests by 30–45%. Sequencing is ordered but not justified, and the portal pilot’s downstream check mostly watches the same loop rather than complaints, cost, or compliance after close.
