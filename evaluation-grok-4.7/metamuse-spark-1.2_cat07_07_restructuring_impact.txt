6.4

The memo covers every required section and is rightly conditional: it separates East from North/South, treats handover counts, subcontracting, tails, fairness, and learning as different phenomena, and refuses a full rollout on early averages. That structure is not enough to carry the grade. Several quantitative claims contradict each other or the given volumes, the pilot contaminates its own control, and the PM4Py sketch misstates APIs and rework.

The central scenario says East’s 90th percentile improves from 22.8d to 19–20d and North/South move from 16.4d/18.2d to about 19–22d, then summarizes the same result as East “+1d” and North/South “−1 to −2d.” Those cannot both be true. Optimistic SLA movement “6.5% → 5.5%” uses North’s miss rate as if it were the organization baseline; the volume-weighted baseline is about 7.6%. Utilization is given as 85–95%+ in the diagram and 83–86% in the table. “Utilization >100%” is not a utilization; a queue that never drains is offered load above capacity with utilization at 100%. The local tech loop is 36 per 100 one way and 31 back, but the map treats “36→0” as the loop.

East is the smallest team (520 of 2,100). A volume-weighted average cannot stay flattering mainly because East improves while North and South worsen by similar amounts. The later claim that the average over-represents North is the consistent reading; the “East hides the damage” reading is not. “60% low-complexity” is not in the evidence. The optimistic case also invents a language/region routing attribute the proposal does not have. Collapsing Technical Shared Service, the legal/risk/security queue, and shared approval into “one pooled queue for four functions” fights the management table, which keeps those handovers separate. The single-server “knee at ~85%” is the wrong queue model for a multi-person pool, which is the pooling benefit this redesign is betting on. A drop in completed cases from 2,100 to 1,800 is a WIP/transient claim, not a new steady-state throughput.

The pilot borrows 0.5 FTE from North/South tech and still calls those teams concurrent controls for difference-in-differences. That borrow is the spillover the guardrail says must not happen. Moving East’s only embedded tech into the pool also leaves East’s non-high work without a local specialist, so the East low-complexity contrast is confounded. Immediate disappearance of Local Technical Assessment is the routing rule itself; calling that drop “deskilling within days” mixes a policy change with a learning effect the same memo places at 2–6 months.

The sketch is not reliable pseudocode. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` has no `activity=` argument and counts repeated activities, not the activity named Rework and not `rework_flag`. `__service_waiting_time` is not a column `insert_case_service_waiting_time` creates. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a tuple, as with `discover_dfg`, and with one timestamp it mixes service and waiting. Event-level means of `rework_flag` and `outcome` overweight long traces. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` does not yield queue depth; role discovery does not return a silhouette; a subcontracting graph is not “edges per case.” `get_service_time` is never called. Stratified DFGs are announced and then computed on the unfiltered log.

Clarity is also damaged where the direction of change is missing (“nominal count , subcontracting count”, “service_waiting_time 30–50%”) and by the stray `h_vs`. The recommendation and the leading/lagging split are the soundest parts; the numbers, the experiment, and the code do not meet the same standard.
