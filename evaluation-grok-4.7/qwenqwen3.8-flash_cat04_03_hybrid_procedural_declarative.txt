6.1

The policy analysis is strong, but the executable models do not implement the behavior the verdicts assume. That is a central failure for a PM4Py modeling task.

The R5/R6 treatment is the best part. The two resolutions are separated, the implemented choice is conservative legal precedence, the compliance risk is stated, and T6 is shown under both policies. The R1–R12 mapping uses the right formalisms for most rules, especially by refusing to encode “legal opinion before every activation” as a global DECLARE precedence. Temporal conversions and z-scores are right, including T7 (34 h, z ≈ 3.67, warning; 2 h, z ≈ 1.94, inside ζ = 2). T4 is correctly an activity-level pass and a four-eyes fail. The R10/R11 clash — at most one `Release First Payment`, yet a later release after a bank change — is a real contradiction and is identified.

The procedural model does not match that narrative. Standard PM4Py process trees use `ProcessTree` plus `Operator` (`SEQUENCE`, `XOR`, `PARALLEL`, `LOOP`) and a string `label`. There is no `ProcessTreeModelActivity`, `ProcessTreeModelOperator`, or `OPERATOR_EVENT`. A PM4Py loop is do/redo: the first child runs at least once, and the second child is the redo, not an exit. `loop(Request → Receive, Classify Risk)` therefore requires a clarification and treats classification as the redo. Under that semantics T1–T8 do not have the procedural results in the matrix; traces with no clarification cannot pass, and T2 does not place `Classify Risk` after the loop. The prose says “zero or more cycles, then classify,” which is a different operator. The matrix follows the prose, not the code.

The log skeleton has the same split. The stated rule is that `always_before` is vacuous if either activity is missing, but T5 is marked `fail` because `Receive Clarification` never occurs. Under that rule T5’s skeleton should pass. T8’s “activation before release” failure has the same problem; only `never_together` actually fails T8. `directly_follows: set()` is also wrong for `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`: that set is an allow-list, so an empty set flags every consecutive pair. The reported skeleton passes would not survive execution.

DECLARE, the temporal profile, and the resource checks are largely coherent, and the layered T4/T6/T7 readings are careful. Those strengths do not repair a backbone and a skeleton whose executed behavior contradicts the verdict matrix.
