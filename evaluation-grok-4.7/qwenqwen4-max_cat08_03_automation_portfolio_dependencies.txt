The final recommendation is a strong, constraint-aware portfolio: A5, A4, and A6 before A9, with A1 and A11 deferred, Phase-0 control gates, and explicit workload shifts. It uses the DFG, waits, rework, pools, and control findings rather than frequency, and it satisfies the four-module and two-deep-integration limits in substance.

It is not nearly flawless. Calling A4 the Wave-1 control module stretches the compliance requirement; the real control module, A9, is Wave 2, while A5 and A6 are labor and flow tools. “Roughly half” of contracts are asserted to skip committee via delegated authority, but known inflows (committee 3,650, mismatch 380, remediation 510) leave about 40% unexplained, and remediation cases may already have had a decision. The “45% / 55%” analyst sentence is unclear, and “A6 is the only candidate that materially expands” the analyst pool ignores A5’s effect on the 24% document-review rework. About 1,265 KYC hits are treated as specialist escalations even though KYC sits in Compliance ops, not the 3-FTE specialist pool. P2 credits A7 with pulling valuation onto the critical path early, while P4 treats that same early start as a non-module rule and still calls the 58h wait a gap. Weekly-cadence loss of about seven days is applied too broadly for a high-exposure-only pattern, and A6’s “downstream bottleneck” cell describes same-step verification, not a downstream queue.
