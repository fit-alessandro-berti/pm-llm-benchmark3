6.2

The conceptual frame is strong, but the timestamp table—the empirical core—is unreliable, and the required code is unlikely to run.

The flag-versus-unflagged contrast is right: triage start about 14.4 vs 148.8 minutes, triage service 36 vs 57, manual review 10/10 vs 4/10, manual wait about 390 vs 112.5 among reviewed cases, escalation 6/10 vs 2/10, and total cycle about 990.6 vs 478.2. Absent stages are correctly kept as missing rather than zero. Interference, exposure-induced confounding, and the distinction between review occurrence and review waiting are handled carefully, and the P2 pilot is concrete.

Those strengths do not carry the stage table. R06 is Medium, not High, so the complexity rows are wrong: Medium is n=8 with an escalation, not n=7 with escalation `NA`; High is n=8, not 9, and its means are off. R10’s manual-done-to-escalation wait is 600 minutes (23:42 to 09:42), not 480, so Flag 1 and Flagged Escalated escalation waits are 420, not 400, and Flagged Normal-load is 450, not 390. Flagged High-load total cycle sums to 6486 minutes, mean 1081, not 914.3. Section 4 then cites the false figures 1164.7 and 914.3 as evidence. Conditional stage means are also printed without the support that produced them, so a 2-case escalation mean looks like a group mean.

The estimands conflate “well-defined” with “identified” for E3–E5, and those interventional indirect effects never state which treatment regime the outcome is under. The opening promises controlled direct effects that the table does not define. P1 is display suppression, but several contrasts treat it as if it also removed automatic routing. Off-hours inside the long waits are not examined. The script uses `case_id_key` / `case_id_col` rather than PM4Py’s documented arguments and calls `.items()` on `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg`, which return a tuple; every event’s `start_timestamp` is arrival, not the stage start.
