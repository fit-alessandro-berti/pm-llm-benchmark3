The answer is a strong, well-structured analysis with mostly exact DFG arithmetic, a real resilience-versus-noncompliance split, and a usable recovery redesign. It is not nearly flawless. Several precise claims are wrong or stronger than the log allows, and those claims are used as evidence that controls “worked.”

The clearest factual error is in T2: “only 2,100 cases (37.5%) reached an end.” Cancel → END is 430, so 2,530 cases reach an end. Section 5 then says 37.5% “reached spreadsheet hold”; 2,200 cases enter that activity (39.3%), and 2,100 leave it to END. In G, “half” of 3,250 unscanned dispatches also skipped release; the bypass is 1,800/3,250 (55%). “Recovered by Day 7” treats a T4 period median as a recovery date. “New customers in T4 paid for T2’s stale snapshot” is not in the log: the 300 cancels are Reconcile manual reservation → Cancel, with no customer identity.

The repeated logic flaw is right-censoring. The answer correctly notes that T2 p90 cycle times outlast the phase, then reads missing outflows as control performance: 200/2,500 manual authorizations “did not pass” and prove B was discriminating; 200/1,650 release cases are a 12% catch rate proving F worked; 740 pick lists “never packed” are a demonstrated cost of the stale snapshot. Those residuals are equally consistent with work still open at the phase cut. Baseline hold and repack rates are not valid comparators for in-window DFG leakage. That weakens the quantitative basis for calling B and F proven resilience, even though the qualitative case for both still stands.

Smaller overclaims remain. “74% released” is a return from compliance hold to validation, not a release. The 17% “deferred” figure is the backlog-entry share (960/5,600), not deferral edges (1,080/5,600). The T4 hold-rate rise is treated in the table as evidence that skipped authorizations were wrong, then only later as a hypothesis. Dual-control missing at 2.9% does not show that a pharmacist check failed to be dual control. Unexplained DFG leaks (80 fast-lane, 100 skips, 150 packs, 220 snapshot checks) are ignored while nearby edges are interpreted causally.

What keeps the score from falling further is the substance that is right. Multipliers, absolute exception counts (about 770 in T4 versus about 530 in T2), the 33% backlog-cancel rate matching baseline backorder cancels, all 750 T4 cancels coming from backlog or reconciliation, the 8.3% post-invoice audit tail, and the 1,800 release bypasses all check out. The answer separates priority from authorization, refuses to equate throughput or later remediation with compliance, and specifies triggers, triage, control floors, contemporaneous evidence, backlog rules, and an evidence gate before invoice close. The hypothesis table is doing work the assertions in the main table should have done first.
