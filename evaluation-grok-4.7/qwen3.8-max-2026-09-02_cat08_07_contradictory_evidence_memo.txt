7.2

The memo meets the required structure and reaches a defensible recommendation: do not scale nationally, do not fully roll back, and keep the simple-house gains while containing tail, rework, and billing/permit risk. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated, the five metric conflicts are explained, process-mining evidence is used, and M1–M8 are tied to decision changes.

It is not nearly flawless. The operating decision is ambiguous: survey skipping is paused only for complex segments, but it is also not included in what is deployed for simple houses, so the treatment of the skip rule in the segment being scaled is unresolved. Post-billing activation is narrowed to complex segments in the memo and paused more broadly in the final recommendation. The memo’s claim that the visible cost improvement is incomplete because downstream data are missing conflates a real cost-accounting gap with the visible-cost result. Precision is defined with an extra notion of “controlled” behavior, generalization is recorded but not reconciled with low precision, and several rollout gates stay qualitative (“materially,” “tight,” “sustainable”). Ranking conformance monitoring first also overstates its information value, because those deviations are already described as frequent.
