5.5

The write-up follows the required sectioning and correctly keeps the selected repair small: scanner/carrier completions for ev08 and ev10, with ev11, ORD-B billing, and PAY-2 left open, and with arithmetic explicitly treated as defeasible. That core abductive choice is sound. It is not reliable enough beyond that. Several claims are factually unsupported or contradict how object-centric discovery actually works, and those errors sit in the sections that are supposed to audit lifecycle consistency and downstream effects.

The evidence model overstates defeasible cues as hard law. Temporal order is classified as a hard invariant even though the domain only makes timestamps informative. Customer C17 and route NORTH are repeatedly counted as positive evidence although every customer-bearing object is C17 and both deliveries and both packages are NORTH, so neither feature discriminates. The combination “framework” names score components and then never uses them. Confidence values are intuitive point estimates, and P-05 (0.50) and P-06 (0.45) are not stated as compatible or mutually exclusive.

The table invents physical evidence. “Package weight budget fits” and the later claim that “container weight budgets independently support” the repair cannot be true: no item weights are in the data. PK-8’s gross weight of 2.9 therefore supports nothing. P-02 and P-04 are projections of P-01 and P-03, yet they receive the same confidence and are described as corroborated. The circularity audit flags this fallacy for the invoice and then commits it for packing and carriage. E2O qualifiers such as packs, bills, and settles also copy O2O verbs onto events, so event-to-package “packs” does not mean what PK-8 packs IT-B1 means. Sequential bank references are treated as evidence that both payments hit the same invoice; they only show recording order. The stronger competing reading—that 100 aligns with ORD-B and 130 with ORD-A, while the invoice is attached only to ORD-A—is barely developed.

Bundle A’s physical choices are the best simple reading: PK7-1/PK8-1 already establish the scanner pattern, and N-421? points at DEL-Y rather than DEL-X. Calling the result a complete physical lifecycle, and calling ORD-B “unbilled,” still over-reads absence. The domain’s scanner-outage story, which would leave ev08 item-only, is not carried as a live bundle at all. Bundle B closes every object, including a 0.40 unresolved ORD-B confirmation, and narrates the payments as installments rather than order-sized amounts. Bundle C directly contradicts P-12: the table rejects ev10→DEL-X at 0.15, then the bundle adopts it by inventing an “erroneous route inquiry” and a $100 freight residual. It also assigns ev11 uniquely to DEL-X without showing why the wildcard, portal-loss, and DEL-Y readings are weaker.

The lifecycle write-up mixes direct participation with multi-hop propagation. ORD-A’s direct events are create, two adds, confirm, and invoice; pack and dispatch are not on the order. Unrepaired ORD-B is create and add only. ev08 is on IT-B1, not on ORD-B. Calling ev08 and ev10 orphan events is wrong; they already have an object and are merely cardinality-incomplete. DEL-Y is a true orphan object before repair. The composed order–package relation can be described as many-to-many only after projection; there is still no direct order–package O2O edge.

Section 6 is the largest failure. In the unrepaired log, IT-B1 already has Add Item → Pack Item, and PK-8 already has Pack Item → Dispatch Package. Adding PK-8 to ev08 inserts a second pack on that package, so the new directly-follows fragment is Pack → Pack, while Pack → Dispatch remains a single traversal. It is not “restored,” and it is not traversed twice. Adding DEL-Y to ev10 does not add any event to PK-8. The object-type graph claims are instance edges mislabeled as type edges: Package–Item, Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, and Payment–Invoice already exist, so neither bundle adds those type pairs. Standard flattening keeps events that name an object of the chosen type; it does not pull dispatch onto ORD-B through item and package links. The unrepaired counts “ORD-A has 8 events” and “ORD-B has 3 (Create, Add, Pack)” are therefore wrong, and Bundle B does not give the two orders identical traces. ORD-B would directly gain confirm and invoice only; ev13 and ev14 stay on the invoice and payments unless a further hop is assumed. The activity-link arithmetic (pack 5→6, dispatch 3→4) is one of the few precise counts.

The code meets the requested checklist: copies, appends Bundle A, checks referential integrity and duplicates, builds an OCEL, calls the three discovery functions, and flattens Order and Delivery without treating O2O enrichment as semantic proof. The version note is not trustworthy: `OCEL` has long lived at `pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL`, and the claimed 2.7.8 top-level constructor split is unsupported. The over-repair list is specific on forced invoicing, unapplied cash, and wildcard SLA closure. The Inductive Miner “AND-fork” warning is not. Flattening copies a shared event into separate order cases and tends to yield a sequence, not an AND-split; object-centric synchronization is a different mechanism.

Calibration is good on the invoice and on not forcing ev11 in the selected bundle. It is undermined by fabricated weight support, non-discriminative context features, dependent edges scored as independent, an alternative bundle that revives a rejected link, and downstream graph claims that describe changes the repaired relations do not produce.
