4.5

The paper keeps the required structure, covers all eight cases, and reaches several defensible headline results: P-501 falls before the directive, P-502’s late compliance is not cured, P-503 breaks segregation, P-504 is outside scope, P-505’s ERP stamp is not the commitment time, P-506’s verbal extension fails, P-507’s clearance and signed PDF are timely, and P-508 sits above EUR 150,000 with finance still missing. Those merits do not hold the reasoning together.

The finance theory splits three ways. Section 2 says the CFO email can interpret finance controls and cannot repeal GPIP-4 A2. The pseudo-logic then deletes the pre-commitment finance requirement up to EUR 150,000, which would make the purchase compliant. The case table instead records a deviation and labels P-505 and P-507 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. P-502, also under EUR 150,000 and covered by joint Incident Command authorization, is treated as a finance breach because the directive cannot waive finance, without deciding whether the email even existed at commitment. The date of P-502 is never established.

The rule apparatus is unreliable. Operators are missing, so several formal rules are ambiguous between conjunction and disjunction. R7 invents a 24-hour PO deadline by merging it with the emergency-form rule. B4 is recorded as a waiver of Plant and Finance approval, although it only changes commitment-versus-PO timing and allows a delegated Shift Lead to stand in for an absent Plant Manager. “New supplier” and “unapproved supplier” are collapsed, so P-504 is assigned a compliance-clearance breach the amount and supplier wording do not establish. A1 is applied as universal two-person control, including to the EUR 24,000 purchase that has no approval requirement. The approved-supplier ordering says the local register outranks ECD-2 and, in the same entry, that ECD-2 wins inside its scope. GPIP-4 is also treated as if it contained the register rule.

A2’s two approvers are not applied consistently. Budget-owner approval and Finance Controller approval are misnumbered in P-502, omitted as deviations for P-506 and P-507, and left only as “missing evidence” for P-508. The method defines an evidence gap as INDETERMINATE, then never uses that class. P-505 lists an uncertain budget owner as a satisfied rule and calls Plant approval delayed when no Plant event is recorded. P-507 says segregation is satisfied while the committer’s role is unknown. P-501 cites a phone log that is not in the facts and treats the stated absence of delegation as an open proof gap. P-502 claims no missing evidence. Section 6 proposes retrospective countersignatures that the paper’s own non-cure rule cannot treat as repair.

Alternative 5 is invalid: finding a budget owner for P-503 would leave the missing compliance clearance and the requester-committer-authorizer overlap intact, so the result would not become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Alternative 3 changes severity only and leaves the stated classification in place. The closing clause is the most coherent section, but it still does not settle new-supplier clearance, budget-owner authority, or the status of contemporaneous records versus later system stamps.
