The write-up keeps the right broad story — March is mostly semantic, later cycle-time and rework changes are operational, and the mechanisms do not share one change point — but it misses required counts, overstates weak claims, and presents quantitative results the aggregates do not support.

## 1. Drift timeline
Six rows and the required columns are present, and March, May, and October are aimed at the right families of cause. The rows are dates rather than intervals, and several timings are misclassified. June treats a further rise in missing resources as a staffing effect; missingness steps up in March (about 1% to about 5%) and is concentrated in migrated activities. July is labeled control-flow drift at strength 80, but `Assess -> Senior Assessment` only moves from 7 to 9 per 100 cases then, while the large move is October (22). December gives the overtime and quality campaign strength 85 and leaves the simultaneous seasonal easing as a mere alternative, despite the brief stating that the two coincide. September’s partial reversal (Supplier Z share, rework, and cycle time all fall while staffing is still unrepaired and before renewal season) is absent, so the timeline cannot separate supplier recovery from capacity or seasonality. Partial migration is also flattened: in March, 140 of 640 cases still use `Validate Eligibility`.

## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
This is about 175 words, short of the required 180–280. The useful core is present: most of the raw-fitness collapse closes after mapping, duplicates and missing resources harm different analyses, and post-May rework plus longer queues with nearly stable assess service time are not a labeling artifact. The opening claim that the gap implies semantic shifts rather than structural violations is too strong. Mapped fitness still drifts from 0.92–0.93 to 0.86 by July, so it does not “stabilize.” Duplicated `Open Case` is about 4–6% of cases; calling it a material inflator of variant complexity overstates a small technical duplicate. “Gap between mapped fitness and cycle time” compares unlike quantities.

## 3. Quantitative drift decomposition
The baseline deltas that are shown for raw fitness, median cycle time, rework, and complexity are arithmetically correct. The section still fails its own specification. The table has four metrics, not six, and drops February, April, and August–November. Logging drift has one candidate change point; two were required. The normalized-DFG “Jaccard” values 0.95, 0.85, and 0.80 are not computed from the seven supplied rates. Every listed relation is already positive in January, so presence Jaccard is 1 all year. March’s normalized fragment is almost identical to January (L1 of about 3 rate-points across seven edges); May and October are the real moves (L1 roughly 40 and 64). “Senior Assessment edge appears” is false: the January rate is 4 per 100 cases. The October cycle-time split (+5 mix, +4 rework, +6 queue, +1.3 service) sums exactly to +16.3 hours. Assess service is given as about 4.8 to 5.4 hours, so +1.3 hours contradicts the evidence, and no within-segment durations exist to identify the other shares. That is manufactured precision. P90’s much larger rise (69 to 116 hours) is unused, as is the fast-variant collapse from 58% to 35%.

## 4. Competing mechanism hypotheses
All eight required hypotheses and the required columns appear. Rank is undefined. “Evidence against” is “None” or “None significant” for nearly every row, which the non-unique evidence does not allow. Supplier rework does not return to the 11–12% baseline when Supplier Z share falls in September and December. Staffing cannot explain the May cycle-time rise, which precedes 15 June. The priority rule has no measured signature in these aggregates; “variant order change” is the wrong predicted object for a queue policy. Complexity is already 29% in August, before the 1 October season. Resource missingness is not a separate smooth drift; it is a March step inside the migration. The interaction row covers only staffing plus season and omits Supplier Z’s pre-existing complexity, priority crossed with mix, and migration crossed with missing identifiers.

## 5. Behavioral, population, and logging discrimination
The six requested segments are present. The cell logic is often inverted or empty. Missing-resource movement is placed under behavioral drift for teams. “High cycle stable” does not indicate behavioral drift. The complexity row says logging has no pattern, missing the stated historical inconsistency of `Senior Assessment`, which is exactly the logging-versus-behavior contrast on that edge. October’s senior-assessment rate (22 per 100 cases at 41% high complexity) is far above a mix-only rescaling of January (4 per 100 at 21%). Alignments, variant distributions, log skeletons, and DECLARE constraints are not actually specified.

## 6. Segmentation plan
Support rules mix counts (“50 cases/month”) with shares (“10% of cases,” “20% of cases”), and priority is a post-July assignment tied to complexity, so conditioning on it biases the comparison. The stated primary population is non-Z, low-complexity cases. That stratum is a useful reference for supplier and mix effects, and it still absorbs staffing loss and priority spillover onto standard work. Making it the only primary population removes the strata where rework and seasonal risk actually concentrate.

## 7. Monitoring architecture
There are ten monitors and the header includes owner, but every row leaves owner empty. Event ordering, variant mix, and temporal profiles are thin or absent; the raw-minus-mapped fitness gap, Supplier Z share, duplicate `Open Case`, and priority-stratified wait are not monitors. A rolling two- or three-month reference will adopt a degraded regime as normal. Mapped fitness below 0.85 would not have fired on the observed mapped low of 0.86. FDR is mentioned once; correlated monitors and a season-versus-residual comparison are not specified, so recurrence is not separated from new drift beyond a generic seasonal-adjustment sentence.

## 8. Reproducible Python and PM4Py plan
Part A embeds six months and five fields, not the monthly summary or the normalized DFG-rate table. It does not validate ranges or schema, does not flag change points with a rule, and computes fitness recovery without printing it. After `to_datetime`, `month == '2026-01'` does not select the baseline, so the script fails before a result. The coded Jaccard uses three shared edge names and ignores rates, so it returns 1 and contradicts the 0.85 figure in the prose. Part B uses the requested function names, but the calls do not match the PM4Py manual: `format_dataframe` is given `event_id` and `timestamp` instead of `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`; `filter_time_range` is called with `start_time` and `end_time`; month 12 builds an invalid end date; `discover_skeleton` is not the log-skeleton API. The mapping copy assumes an `event_type` column that formatting would already have renamed.

## 9. Provisional attribution
About 227 words, short of the required 250–400. March-as-semantic and the staffing uncertainty are directionally right, then the section conflicts with itself: Supplier Z is first “plausible” and later “high confidence,” and “the drift was semantic rather than behavioral with near-certainty” erases the residual mapped-fitness decline. “Historical patterns” are not in the table. Priority effects are said to be inferred from queue-time shifts; those shifts are not observed. A later drop in rework is treated as a refutation of the capacity account, which is a category error. September’s partial recovery and December’s two-cause improvement are not assigned. The revision tests do not include the comparisons that would actually separate the mechanisms: Z versus non-Z rework at fixed complexity, and wait time by priority class inside the same mix and supplier.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules
Seven rules use the required IF / THEN / BECAUSE shape. Rules 1, 3, and 5 match the evidence structure. Rule 6 says a priority policy shifts wait times rather than cycle times, even though wait is inside cycle time; the observable “variants move up” is also ill-defined. Rule 7 sends a new activity label to a duplication check. Distinct labels and duplicated `Open Case` are different faults. None of the seven rules says how to tell a seasonal recurrence from a new residual.

Overall, the skeleton would brief a stakeholder on the main calendar, but the distance, the cycle-time shares, the July control-flow claim, the priority “queue shifts,” and the non-running analysis are concrete reliability failures, and several explicit output constraints are unmet.
