7.5

The design is strong on the hard parts of this problem: waiting is an output of calendars and finite pools, objections stay latent, the simplified tree is correctly rejected as an executable model, and interventions are judged by paired differences under both capacity stories. It is not nearly flawless. Several methodological claims are stronger than the model that is actually specified, and the simulation skeleton does not implement the engine it describes.

The capacity audit is real and correctly computed. With the stated volumes and lognormal means implied by the median/p90 pairs, offered load versus HR calendars is on the order of 2 for clerks, about 13 for planners, 14 for fire, 11 for environment, 9 for inspectors, and 20 for legal. Keeping H1 (more effective staff) and H2 (inflated touch time) as structural scenarios, because they change what “add one inspector” means, is the right call. The weekend timestamp check on the excerpt is also accurate (2026-01-03 is a Saturday; clerk in-calendar share on the excerpt is 1/4). PM4Py usage is largely conventional: pandas instead of a nonexistent `read_csv`, `format_dataframe` with the three standard keys, `parse_process_tree` after mapping unlogged redo labels to `tau`, then variants, DFG, performance DFG, and case-arrival average kept outside the engine. Not treating performance-DFG sojourns as service times is correct.

Those strengths sit next to direct contradictions.

Censoring is mishandled in the measurement design. A 24-month cohort plus a 12-month drain still right-censors complex cases that arrive late in the cohort (the published complex p90 is already 260 days). The write-up says those cases are followed to completion so tail estimates are unbiased. The KPI code then takes p50/p90 on completed cases only (`dropna`), while SLA counts unfinished cases as misses. A policy that stalls long cases can improve p90 and worsen SLA in the same run. That is not a coding footnote; it breaks the decision metrics in section 7.

Common random numbers are overclaimed. Section 5 says policy switches do not move later draws, and cites unconditional D1 adoption as proof. Adoption is drawn unconditionally, but the preparation delay, D7’s Monday shift, D8’s triage coin-flips, D9’s early-warning draw, D2’s skipped inspection draws, and D3’s different number of service draws all consume the per-case stream only on some policies. Later service times, rework, and reject decisions are not synchronized. Paired t intervals and the indifference-zone ranking therefore rest on a variance reduction the skeleton does not provide. Named streams per purpose (routing, service, policy, exogenous delay) were the actual fix, and they are not specified.

The arrival process does not conserve the statistics it is built from. Daily negative-binomial counts are aimed at about 31,200 cases and a 31% top-architect share, then burst children are added on top (`burst_prob` 0.25, mean extra size 2). That inflates volume by roughly 15% and pushes the top-architect share well above 31%, because children are also top-architect cases. Section 2.3 never says the backbone rate must be fit net of bursts. Attribute sampling is independent given case type, while the same section correctly warns that independent marginals destroy the commercial/value/environment dependence, and the class docstring calls the sampling joint.

Default routing probabilities are not reconciled with the variant table. Independent fire and notice rates of 0.70 on new residential produce about 14% of cases with both, against the stated 18% (feasible only with positive dependence that is never imposed). Complex variance is 10% of the log and 8% are the notice-plus-appeal/rework group, but the appeal and rework probabilities generate substantially less than that. Inspection-rework mass and the commercial fire-environment-legal cell happen to land near the table, which makes the misses look like unchecked defaults rather than placeholders.

Council meetings are only a legal-capacity shutdown. The problem states that council weeks also affect the public-notice period. Notice in the model is a pure calendar timeout of 14 days plus a latent extension, with no hearing calendar, no pause, and no council-gated end. D9 mediation does not repair that. The baseline is also specified two ways: section 4’s B1 includes current informal geographic batching, which section 2.10 correctly says must be reproduced before D3 is credited, but the coded baseline turns batching off, so the D3 contrast absorbs the status quo.

Policy logic in the engine contradicts the assumption register. A11 makes unknown violation status ineligible for D2; every synthetic case has a known violation bit, and the “60% data coverage” level from the experiment matrix is not a parameter. D8’s fast-share factor is dead: the environmental branch runs only when `requires_env` is already true, so there is no correctly fast-tracked easy case, only a shorter review of cases that needed the full one. Inspection rework is a constant-probability geometric loop after section 2.8 says not to use a geometric loop. A failed applicant response on re-inspection does not withdraw the case; it falls through into decision drafting and can still be issued. Bundled inspections drop no-shows, so D3 looks better by deleting a delay. The reserve planner’s age filter closes over the request-time clock, so a case that crosses the aging threshold while queued is not reclassified until some other store event, and affinity to that planner can stall a non-aged case.

Resource lifecycle in the skeleton is not plausible SimPy. `Store.put` / `FilterStore.put` are not yielded in `serve`, after a bundled inspection, when absentees return, or when the peak-season inspector is added. Those puts are discarded; the worker is not returned. Direct `store.items.remove` on the seasonal inspector desynchronizes the store from its queues and can duplicate the temp worker in peak months because “not in `items`” is also true while that worker is busy. As written, each worker can complete about one activity, then every later request waits until the horizon. Utilization divides the busy minutes of whoever happens to be idle in the store by wall-clock minutes, which is neither working-time utilization nor the 65–85% band in the decision table.

Smaller but real cuts: `reconcile_capacity` under H2 scales every role by the worst rho, which erases relative bottlenecks; under H1 it inflates headcount but leaves “two simple-only planners” fixed, so the skill constraint vanishes exactly in the scenario where staffing was wrong. The offered-load check ignores burst inflation. Scheduler overload is omitted from the prose ratios. An intermediate `Reject application` is written before an appeal that may later issue, so replay variants are polluted by a lifecycle the tree does not have. Rework in the KPI counts only two applicant-delay labels, not completeness or legal loops.

Section 7’s refusal to declare an optimum, the intended-versus-municipal clock for D1, latent objection truth kept out of the simulated log, and the stress matrix (bundle thinning, objection wave, staff shock, slow applicants) are the right decision frame. They do not offset a measurement design that censors the tail it claims to protect, a CRN scheme that does not survive the policy list, or an engine that never returns its resources.
