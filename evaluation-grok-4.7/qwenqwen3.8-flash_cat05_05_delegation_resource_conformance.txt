The required structure is complete, the rule formalization is precise, and most case judgments are well supported. Alias normalization, service-account treatment, D-IC-04 as prohibited subdelegation, CHG-507 as unauthorized, CHG-502 and CHG-508 as same-person draft/approve breaches, CHG-503 as unattributed verification, and CHG-510 as a clear authority failure are all sound. Business-day counting, the distinction between a pre-event signed PDF and a later migration record, and the refusal to treat repeated collaboration as collusion are also correct. The code covers every requested PM4Py/pandas operation and keeps collusion as a manual judgment.

The main error is CHG-509. D-QM-02 is valid only if Asha is absent, and the record shows her leave ended on 2026-08-14. A weekend is not evidence of formal unavailability. The matrix and the code correctly leave that condition unresolved, but the case table still assigns VALID_SUBSTITUTION. That contradicts the answer’s own delegation rule. The matching primary classification is INDETERMINATE.

Two further gaps remain. Asha’s 2026-08-13 VPN session and self-reported approval are not tested against D-QM-01’s requirement that she be absent, so CHG-506 is analyzed only as a Ben identity problem. Secondary labels are also misused: CHG-501 is an evidence-timing gap, and CHG-508 is a resolved alias plus an SoD breach, yet both are labeled data/identity problems. In the checker, every cooling-system case is treated as CI-44, and retrospective authority depends on the word “review” appearing in a note. Those shortcuts happen not to misclassify the present emergency reviews, but they are not reliable interval logic.
