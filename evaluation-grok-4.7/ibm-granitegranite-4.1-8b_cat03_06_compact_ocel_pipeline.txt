2.5

The pipeline is not executable and does not encode the stated process. Unequal column lengths make both `relations` and `o2o` fail to construct; `events.loc[..., 17]` is out of range; `set(...).isin(...)` is invalid; and the discovery calls do not use the required PM4Py functions. Mandatory `ocel:type` is absent, payment amounts 100 and 150 are never stored, and D1’s confirmation is moved from 2026-04-06 13:00 to 2026-04-07 09:00, creating a timestamp tie while section 7 claims the timestamps are unique.

E2O rows are misaligned: activities include a nonexistent “Confirm Payment,” shared events such as Reserve Stock and Pack P1 are not related to all participating objects, M1 and M2 never appear in E2O, and qualifiers such as `contains` and `packs` are used as event qualifiers. The uniqueness check `len(relations) == len(unique eids)` forbids the multiple relations one event must have. O2O lists do not match, duplicate O200–I3, and use undifferentiated `settles` instead of partial versus completing settlement. The import and call surface (`pm4py.v2`, `FlatteningTechnique`, `variants.apply`, `ocel.get_activity_frequencies`) is invented, and DFG/skeleton results are not stored by object type.

The prose contradicts the code and the algorithms. Assumptions list O2O qualifiers as E2O qualifiers and never decide whether logistics and finance link directly to orders and items. The audit’s cardinalities are wrong (V1 is not 1:1 with orders), and it treats E13 as a payment. Order flattening both omits and includes Reserve Stock, adds payments that the audit does not attach to orders, and collapses O100 with O200. The OC-DFG is described as one instance path, O200 is falsely called the same pattern, and the Petri-net write-up asserts a unique invariant interpretation. Alternative 2 proposes cloning shared events. Several “semantic checks” are not inspectable or are false, notably that each order appears twice in `events` and that the payments equal a stated invoice total.
