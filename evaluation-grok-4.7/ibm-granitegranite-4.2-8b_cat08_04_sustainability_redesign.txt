5.5

The required seven-part structure is present, and some grounding is real: segment differences, SP1 air on standard-stock service-contract orders, CNC_A versus CNC_B rework (9% vs 18%), air at 4–6×, consolidation at 35–55%, heat-treatment lateness at 1.7×, the 6% obsolescence note, reinforced packaging’s return benefit, empty van returns, and not banning true critical express. That is not enough to carry the answer.

The control logic is wrong at the center. Slack is defined as promised lead time minus estimated processing time, then processing time is set to median cycle time plus a 25% buffer. Median cycle time already includes queue, batch wait, and transport, and promised-lead-time values are never given, so the 12h/24h gates are not identified from the log. SP1’s “probability above 85%” test is never operationalized. The same section states “MTO Regular median 31h”; 31h and 18h are standard-stock medians. Made-to-order medians are 94h and 78h. Those figures are then used to justify MTO batch caps.

DFG frequencies are misread as impacts: the 260 and 360 rework-packaging arcs are repeatedly called “260+360 kg” or “kgCO2e,” and 420 → 130 is treated like a change rather than two outgoing branches. Unsupported parameters are stated as if evidenced: a 5 kg air cutoff, 2% and 1% defect gates, a 25% buffer, a 30% van replacement, a 4–6h courier guarantee, a 5–10% Q1 baseline, and 10–30% reduction targets.

The effects table’s energy claims are shaky. Mode shift barely changes `energy_kwh` (stock ground 0.9 vs air 1.0). Wait caps that stop batches short of 12–20 raise CNC_A energy per item relative to the efficient batch; the write-up also says efficiency falls “because energy per item drops at size 12,” which reverses the evidence. CNC and heat-treatment batching are collapsed despite different mechanisms.

The package A+B+D+F+H does not match the emission and service hotspots. Critical-downtime is about 11% of orders at 38 kgCO2e (dedicated-van path 52) and is left to an exception log, without the true-versus-precautionary split. Consolidation, the best-supported large-volume transport lever, is dropped. The partial-shipment path (about 7%, 24% late, 7.2 vs 3.6 kgCO2e, second shipment) is absent. CNC_A routing is excluded in the recommendation and then included in the implementation steps.

Rebound coverage is incomplete. Large-batch obsolescence, doubled reinforced-pack mass, and second-shipment or expedite displacement are not clearly treated as rebounds. Several listed risks concern options the package rejects. Governance names SP1/SP2/Q1, segments, and an exception log, but it monitors the invented thresholds rather than path shares, batch-size distributions, empty returns, 14-day unshipped stock, or an 85% on-time probability by route.
