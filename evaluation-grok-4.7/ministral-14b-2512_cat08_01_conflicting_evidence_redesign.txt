4.5

The write-up has the required skeleton and a directionally right story: the frequent auto-approval path is fast and cheap up front, then loses much of that advantage to complaints, wrong replacements, and rule breaks, and the remedy should differ by segment. That is not enough. Several load-bearing readings of the log are wrong, the redesigns invent effects the data do not support, and the experiment rules would accept a non-improvement.

**Diagnosis errors.** Log fitness is 0.91; 68% is the share of fully fitting traces. Calling fitness “low (68%)” collapses two different conformance results. The 5,000-case graph is not a weekly volume: Fraud/risk inflow is not “400 cases/week,” and 400 is only the auto-screen edge (manual review adds 360 and evidence requests add 220). “26% of cases” repeat evidence requests; the 26% applies only to cases that enter that activity. The “median 4.5 hours” path adds Intake→Auto median 0.5 to the means 1.0 and 3.0, skips Dispatch→Close (median 3.2), and treats a sum of edge statistics as a case median. “32% Fast-path errors” does not appear in the evidence. R3 and “complaint/reopen after close” are unused. Manual review is not shown to be underused on low-risk work.

**Redesign logic.** A’s “Express Review” bypasses Fraud/risk and is not clearly a Manual eligibility review, so it can reproduce both R1 and the known high-value dispatch-without-risk deviation while claiming a large R2 fix. The EUR 0.50–1.50 gate cost is invented; 6–18 hours is delay, not a priced touch-time. The stated +15–35% manual inflow from a rule update is ignored. B’s portal range is real, but “loops −50%,” p90 60→40, “high-value −20h,” EUR 0.30, and a 20% fraud-volume cut are not. B also never forces high-value claims off the fast path, so the 22%/18% complaint problem can remain. C compares Fraud/risk→Repair quote mean 21 hours, mislabeled a median, with the 20-hour full fast-path median, then elsewhere says ~30 hours and +10 hours. The reviewed/repair segment medians are 96 and 120 hours. Mandating repair-quote treatment also throws away the legitimate Fraud/risk→Fast approval→replacement path. C reuses A’s pre-dispatch gate, so the three options are less distinct than claimed. “−100%” compliance and “+50%” fraud utilization are false precision.

**Table and experiments.** Marking the current process dominated by every redesign contradicts the instruction that dominance cannot be established here, and it conflicts with C’s own cell that low-risk speed is unchanged while the dominance note says C sacrifices it. Phase 1’s “32% of cases” is unexplained. A Fast-path complaint go-line of <15% is weaker than the observed 14.1%. A stop at a gate above 8 hours rejects the stated 6–18 hour design. Escalation “cases/week” repeats the weekly-volume mistake. The first pilot changes rules, routing, and the gate together, and the long complaint tail (p90 150–180 hours, including complaints after close) is never made an observation window. “Shifting complaints upstream” reverses the direction of the risk.

The segment split and the 31%-vs-9% cost point are sound. The numerical and conformance mistakes are large enough that an executive could not safely use the recommendation.
