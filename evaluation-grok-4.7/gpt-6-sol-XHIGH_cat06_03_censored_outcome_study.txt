8.0

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activation as non-default, keeps a landmark-specific estimand, accounts for all 48,000 cases, and separates administrative censoring, feed delay, activation selection, transfer, and permanently missing feeds. Temporal validation is anchored to case start, with an honest admission that July 2025 policy and October 2025 migration cannot support a fully matured test under the February 2026 extract. The leakage rules and the decision-use boundary are sound.

The remaining defects are real. Section 1 lists rejection, withdrawal, and transfer beside “no activation by day 180” without putting those exits on the same 180-day horizon, so the reported probabilities are not an explicit partition; Section 2’s “prior” exit also blurs cases already out of the risk set with future competing events. Section 3 says “cause-specific” probabilities and then cumulative incidence, and stacks risk-set censoring, IPCW, and a feed-observation model without a single identified estimator. The 180-day activation window is unexplained and is not in the sensitivity plan. Multiclass log loss is named for a state variable that also contains right-censored, pending, and feed-missing rows.

The skeleton matches the design more closely than the prose, but it has concrete errors. Activation and terminal timestamps that tie fail both `a.lt(e)` and `e.lt(a)` and fall through to no-activation or censored. `label_ok` accepts a label that arrives before activation plus 90 days. Channel, region, and requested amount are taken as the last prefix value after the audit required the first recorded value. The test cut is `start_at <= 2025-04-15 00:00:00 UTC`, so almost all of 15 April is excluded. `K = 3` is what actually runs, while the stated primary population is the event-1 risk set, and an Accept or Contract Signed event inside those three events can still enter `X`.
