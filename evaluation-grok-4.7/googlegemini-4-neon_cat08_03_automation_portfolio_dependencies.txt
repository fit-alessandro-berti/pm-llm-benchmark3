The answer covers every required section, respects the four-module, control, and two-deep-integration constraints, and makes a coherent case for A4, A5, A6, and A9 over frequency-led automation. The anti-A1 and anti-A11 arguments, concurrency at the KYC/document join, specialist and analyst capacity, and the H1/H2 integration split are well grounded.

It is not nearly flawless. Several process-mining readings are wrong and are reused in the ranking and portfolio comparison. The 7,250 / 3,940 directly-follows counts are treated as the volumes of the concurrent KYC and document branches, although the activity frequencies are 11,500 and 9,200; those edges show which activity was logged first. The 380 Resolve-entity-mismatch → Contract-preparation edges are called “late blocks,” but that edge is the continuation after resolution, not a defect count. Median waits are added into a “48h” normalization-plus-credit wait, and that invented figure is then called one of the three longest critical-path waits, even though customer signing is 44h and longer than the 31h mismatch wait. KYC’s 11% rework/exception rate is relabeled a “hit rate.” The matrix says A9 “eliminates” the 8.5% / 14% contract-remediation loops; later text only claims a reduction. The 52% repeated missing-document rate is set beside the 2,800 / 3,300 return loop without noting that those are different ratios.

Four-eyes relief is also softer than claimed: A6 reducing month-end rush is an indirect hope, and an A9 check detects the violation after credit analysis and committee packaging, which is where the 5.8% breach occurs. Portfolio 2’s sacrifice discussion never clearly tests whether A4, A6, and A9 would consume three deep core slots.
