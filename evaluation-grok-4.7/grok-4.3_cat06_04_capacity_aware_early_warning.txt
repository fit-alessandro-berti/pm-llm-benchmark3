The answer covers the required six sections and the main qualitative traps, but it is not a deployable, auditable policy. The allocation contradicts the slot constraint and the written rules, and the euro figures do not follow from the inputs.

The capacity decision is infeasible. Four slots exist. The rule reserves one, which leaves three. The selected portfolio still assigns swarms to I-01, I-02, I-04, and I-07 and also keeps a reserve. Intervention cost is computed as \(4 \times €450 = €1{,}800\), so the impact table spends the reserve rather than holding it. A controller cannot execute both claims.

The decision rule is not one rule. Eligibility requires no prior alert or a new event, which excludes I-02 and I-09, yet both are marked eligible and I-02 is swarmed. Section 4 then allows a swarm only on the second qualifying alert and imposes a 90-minute cool-down. That blocks immediate action on first alerts, while Section 2 swarms first-alert cases I-01 and I-04. The priority formula is missing its operators, so “value of avoiding breach €450 false-alarm burden” and “score €2,500” are not computable. “Adjusted risk reduction” never states whether the SME/Strategic offsets adjust \(p\) or the causal effect range. The Non-Native-Language 0.10 adjustment is a one-sided penalty; the source note describes two-sided uncertainty, so the adjustment systematically lowers I-09 without a bias estimate.

The scores are not reproducible. Using midpoint absolute risk reduction \(\times\) breach value:
- I-01: \(0.265 \times €9{,}000 = €2{,}385\) gross, about €1{,}685 after €450 and €250.
- I-04: \(0.19 \times €60{,}000 = €11{,}400\) gross.
- I-07: \(0.26 \times €5{,}000 = €1{,}300\) gross; the upper bound is \(0.32 \times €5{,}000 = €1{,}600\).
- I-08: \(0.10 \times €100{,}000 = €10{,}000\) gross, about €4{,}550 net of €450 and €5{,}000, and about −€450 at the lower bound.

I-07’s stated €2,700 score exceeds every effect in its range. I-08 is the highest-value eligible case at €100,000; the claim that I-04 is the highest-value eligible case is false. I-08’s low score does not match either its midpoint or its lower bound. Skipping I-08 can be argued from the €5,000 false-alarm burden at a 0.05 effect, but that is a different number from €1,400. I-01 is called “strong calibration”; the note only says Public Sector calibration is acceptable with a moderate sample.

The impact table fails its own audit. The cited base effects \(0.265, 0.15, 0.19, 0.26\) times €9k, €25k, €60k, and €5k sum to about €18.8k, not the stated €23.5k. Those same midpoints sum to about 0.87 breaches avoided, not the base row’s 0.6–0.8. Upper-bound gross benefit is about €27.1k. The optimistic direct-benefit range of €34k–€52k is above that maximum. Base spillover of €3.6k–€7.2k is consistent with a hidden €15k case value and a 0.01–0.02 probability shift, not the stated 0.01–0.03 band, and the assumed values are not disclosed. On the table’s own base numbers, €19k minus €1.8k minus €7.2k is still about €10k. “Marginally positive” does not describe that result.

I-03 is correctly left untreated. I-05 is correctly suppressed for an unchanged refresh, and I-07 is correctly distinguished because of the new log. Spending a slot on I-07 is still a weak economic act: gross benefit is at most about €1,600, the deadline is 1.5 hours, and the swarm consumes 45 minutes of specialist time. I-06’s wait for imputation risk is reasonable, with no re-entry test. Timing, two-specialist sequencing, and stacked interruptions are not analyzed.

The fairness section names the right mechanisms. The 25% non-Strategic quota and the 0.02 untreated-breach audit are concrete. Recording intervention status does not correct training labels; intervened and unintervened outcomes remain different regimes, and the safeguard does not call for an intervention indicator, inverse-propensity weighting, or a held-out randomized slice. The quota can also force a negative-value swarm when Strategic cases dominate the eligible set. Spillover incidence by segment is stated, then left unmeasured.

Monitoring names utilization, segment mix, queue delay, stratified calibration, a monthly matched comparison, and a spare-capacity experiment. Breach “within 2 hours of alert” is the wrong endpoint for deadlines of 1.5–12 hours. Matching only on stage, segment, and \(p_{now}\) leaves value, effect band, and deadline unbalanced, so the contrast does not identify the swarm effect. Experiments only when spare capacity exists are run in a different congestion regime from the spillover the policy is meant to control. The 0.025 spillover trigger sits inside the planned 0.01–0.03 band, so the base model itself would trip a rule change. The €1,500 revision trigger is not tied to the €2,500 allocation threshold.

The write-up recognizes stage limits, alert fatigue, calibration direction, and externalities. The portfolio, the formula, and the scenario arithmetic do not form one coherent decision.
