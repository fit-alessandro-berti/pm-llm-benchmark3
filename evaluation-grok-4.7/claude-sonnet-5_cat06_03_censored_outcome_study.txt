6.5

The design correctly refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, and open cases as non-defaults, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and separates a joint multi-state question from a conditional-on-activation question and an untestable counterfactual. That core judgment is sound. The estimand, censoring scheme, validation window, and skeleton are not coherent enough to implement without re-deciding the study.

The primary target never fixes a time origin. Section 1 sets an administrative horizon of about 150 days from an unspecified clock. Section 3 then reports the cause-specific CIF at H = 90 days. Default is defined as 90 days after Account Activated, while activation itself occurs at a random time after Application Started, and the feed lags another 7–35 days. Those are three different clocks. A CIF at 90 days from start cannot contain a full post-activation default window; a CIF at 150 days from the prediction landmark is a different estimand. The event time for the compound state “activated and defaulted within 90 days of activation” is never defined, so Aalen–Johansen or Fine–Gray cannot be applied as specified.

Competing risks and censoring are mixed. Section 1 treats Activated-unresolved and Still open as absorbing states. Sections 2 and 3 treat the same rows as ordinary right censoring. Transfers are simultaneously an absorbing competing event and an informative loss-to-follow-up inside the censoring hazard. Those choices change the CIF: coding unresolved activation as competing removes those cases from the default risk set and pushes the default incidence down; censoring them redistributes their mass. The worked example “41% default-if-activated-path incidence” is a conditional probability, not a CIF of the joint event the estimand actually names.

The IPCW description is reversed. Weights belong on cases whose outcomes are observed, inflated when their covariates resemble censored cases. The text says cases likely to have unresolved outcomes are themselves up-weighted. Cause-specific hazards, Fine–Gray, and IPCW are also offered as interchangeable primary estimators.

The cohort table is arithmetically closed, but the 3,600 immature activated accounts remain one bin, so permanent partner-feed missingness is not distinguished from not-yet-mature labels. The stated 1,740/1,160 transfer split treats an approximate 60% linkage rate as an exact observed partition. The at-risk set at the prediction landmark is missing: a case already rejected or accepted before t is not a prediction case, and an elapsed-time prefix can still contain Accept, Reject, or Transferred. That is direct outcome information, and the leakage table does not blacklist those activities.

Temporal splitting by case start is the right objection to a random split, and the policy and migration reasons are real. Test A still runs through 14 October 2025 while maturity is defined as activation + 90 + 35 days. Many of those cases cannot be mature by 15 February 2026, which is the same defect the design claims to have isolated in Test B. The prose also drops 15 October; the code assigns that day to Test B.

The skeleton follows the requested PM4Py calls and does not invent a survival API, but it does not implement the design. `label_available_at` is compared with a timestamp without being parsed. The elapsed-time prefix merges a Series on `case:concept:name` and will not build the per-case cutoff. The leakage assert checks the global extraction date, so it passes even if later events enter a prefix. Transfers are not split, variants are taken from full training traces rather than prefixes, and `main` labels every fold as of the final extract instead of the fold cutoff. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is recommended without a start/complete lifecycle, which this log does not have. Canonicalization is global in §4 and fold-specific in §7.

Section 8 is the most reliable part: association, activation selection, and counterfactual risk stay separated, and the four deployment feedback loops are concrete. The study as specified would still not yield one defensible number for early default incidence.
