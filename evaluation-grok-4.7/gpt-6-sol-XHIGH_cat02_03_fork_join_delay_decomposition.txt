7.5

The fork–join arithmetic is accurate and the main conclusions follow from it. Upstream, queue, service, join, downstream gap, final service, and total flow reconcile on all 12 cases; B is last 8/12, A and C 2/12 each, with no completion ties. Means, ranges, sync waits (11.00 / 4.33 / 30.42), lead sums (A 15, B 120, C 12), and both service-cut counterfactuals match the max-join rule, including caps when another branch remains last (A −20% saves only 15 minutes total; B queue −50% saves 92.5, not the uncapped half-queue). The identity \(T_c=U_c+H_c+D_c+V_c\) and the warning that \(Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci}=H_c\) on every branch are the right anti-double-counting argument. The final-station discussion is also right: observed gaps are not a work-conserving two-server queue (F1 is free while F03 is already join-ready), and a two-server immediate FCFS policy would leave only F11 waiting 5 minutes (171 of 176), so a third server is not identified as necessary.

The reproducible script does not run. `pm4py.get_service_time` requires an activity and returns one float; the call omits the activity and then uses `.items()` as if it returned a dict of seconds. That line is before any `print`, so the required tables are never produced. The pandas decomposition itself is sound.

“If F responds immediately, mean flow becomes 114” is the wrong description of that 114. It is 115.25 minus the 1.25-minute join saving with downstream delay held constant. Immediate final dispatch would also remove the downstream gaps and would not leave the maximum at 130. The assumption paragraph states the constant-delay rule, but the sentence that reports the result contradicts it.

Summing synchronization waits is treated as overlap rather than shown as double counting inside \(H_c\). For F02 the waits are 10, 0, and 35; those 45 minutes already lie inside the 60-minute stage, so \(H+\sum W\) counts them twice. The text shows non-additivity more clearly than that double count. Per-case sync waits are also only written out for four cases, though they are recoverable as fork–join minus queue minus service. The final-resource column supports a stronger policy claim than the single F03 idle example: assignment alternates F1/F2 by case order and often leaves the other server unused. The conclusion that capacity and release policy are confounded is still correct.
