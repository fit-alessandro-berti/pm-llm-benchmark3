5.5

The review has the right shape and several sound judgments, but the centerpiece is technically unreliable and internally inconsistent.

The metric table, use-case split, Model C overfitting diagnosis, and most probe calls (B1–B5, B7, and B6 as a non-acceptance) are defensible. Pathologies are named and ranked, no new scores are invented, and the governance split between exploration and enforcement is clear.

The language of Model S is misread. `X(a, tau)` is at most one execution, and `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` is exclusive. Model S does not accept B8, a repeat without `Correct Dossier`, or both approvals. Those are marked accepted or “ambiguous,” so P5, P7, T_a, and the adversarial case against S are wrong. The uncertainty note does not match the expression: there is no loop.

`model_third` is not runnable PM4Py. `OperatorPOWL` has no `AND`, `XOR`, or `Sequence` operators; sequence and concurrency belong on `StrictPartialOrder`. The partial order is given edges from `checks`, which is not one of its nodes, and one `SilentTransition` is reused in two operators. `Correct Dossier` is absent from the code, yet sections 7 and 8 claim a correction loop and acceptance of B6 and T_c. The DECLARE strings are not DECLARE. The audit therefore scores a model the code does not define.

Validation is incomplete against its own spec: `generalization_tbr` is called with two logs, duplicate-event perturbation and a real comprehension protocol are missing, and “negative suite recall > 0” is the wrong rejection rule. The dissent is a real objection, but it is about 100 words, short of the required 150–250.
