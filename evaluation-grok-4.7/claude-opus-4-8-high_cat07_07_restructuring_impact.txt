6.8

The forecast is complete, well structured, and right to refuse a full rollout, but several quantitative and measurement claims are wrong or internally inconsistent. Under a strict reading those are material, not cosmetic.

The central mechanism error is the claim that nominal handovers fall. Summing the given frequencies gives about 343 handover occurrences per 100 cases now and about 359 after the change. The local specialist loop does not collapse: caseworker→tech and tech→caseworker move from 36+31 to 41+36. Approval handovers rise from 42+39 to 49+43 while one supervisor post is removed, so approval capacity falls as volume rises. The summary table’s “nominal handovers ↓” and the “loop collapses into a single node” wording contradict the management table the forecast is built on. What actually falls is cross-team and intake→caseworker volume. Edge-count decline is also not what `discover_handover_of_work_network` would show if `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, and `E_Tech` remain distinct resources: cross-links would multiply. Edge collapse appears only if analysts first relabel those people as one node. That caveat is mentioned, then treated as the expected measurement.

The subcontracting interpretation is also wrong. A subcontracting tie is a logged A→B→A pattern. Informal early advice that never becomes an event is invisible to that network, and the logged return loop is forecast to become more common (31→36), not to disappear. Section 7 then tells the reader to read a subcontracting drop as expertise loss, so the conceptual error is operationalized.

The optimistic wait line misreads the table. 3.2h and 4.0h are one-way waits into technical review, not round trips. Current technical round-trip wait is 3.2h+1.6h; the management estimate is 4.0h+2.2h. Utilization targets are similarly unanchored. Case-weighted embedded-tech utilization is about 73%. “+15%” is never resolved as relative versus absolute, yet the central scenario asserts 88–92% and the optimistic one stays under 80%, which cannot both sit with that benefit. The 85% “queueing knee” is stated as general; with three pooled servers the knee is higher than for the single-server Legal/Risk resources already at 91–93%. Those two regimes are blurred.

The pilot and the recommendation disagree with each other. Section 5 starts with a combined technical-plus-approval pilot and treats splitting queues as a later adjustment. Section 6 states separated queues as an upfront design change. “A single region” is then East plus North. Lagging outcomes are said to need 12–16 weeks, while expansion is gated on roughly four weeks of lagging metrics. Approval headcount loss is never tied to which supervisor is removed or to the East versus North/South asymmetry.

The sketch would not reliably compare before and after. `drop_duplicates` keeps the first event, so an outcome known at close is often missed, and `outcome == "SLA_miss"` is not a value the log is stated to contain. `rework_by_complexity` averages `rework_flag` over events, so long cases dominate. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` and resource similarity are computed and then ignored. Specialty and customer-segment slices are printed only after the change. `filter_variants` is assigned and never measured. Waiting-time code assumes a dataframe column `@@waiting_time` from `insert_case_service_waiting_time(..., service_time="mean")` without showing that this is the object the call returns.

The required topics are all present, East is treated differently from North/South, tails and fairness are not collapsed into the mean, and the recommendation is correctly conditional. Learning is only folded into local-knowledge loss rather than developed as the separate future-skill effect staff raised. Those strengths keep the answer well above a failed response; the handover arithmetic, the subcontracting story, the wait misread, and the broken comparison code keep it well short of a high score.
