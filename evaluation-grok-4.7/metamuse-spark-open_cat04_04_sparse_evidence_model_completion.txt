The final write-up is complete and the three candidates are genuinely different, replay the 30 observed cases, and separate high-confidence constraints from weaker stakeholder claims. It is not nearly flawless.

Evidence arithmetic is wrong and internally inconsistent. S1 is contradicted by 11/30 traces (V2, V4, V7), not 13/30. V5 is CC before ER and does not contradict S1. A strict CC-before-ER rule therefore rejects 11 traces (about 37%), not the stated 14 traces (47%). Those figures are also used to justify concurrency.

Shared assumptions overclaim. Not every model encodes SC/EX optionality as XOR-with-silent: the conservative tree enumerates concrete variants and has no silent skip. That assumption is stated as common to all three models.

Underfitting is misapplied. The permissive “underfitting” list is over-generalization (SC before both checks, low precision, masked drift). The balanced unobserved language is 7 traces, not about 8–12; the permissive language is finite and has 18 traces total (11 unobserved), not “~18+” unobserved interleavings. These models have no loops, so the claim that exhaustive comparison is impossible because the languages are infinite is incorrect.

The validation suite labels traces such as EX-without-SC and SC-before-both as globally positive even though conservative, and in the latter case balanced, correctly exclude them. It prints alignment scores and does not state an expected pass/fail oracle per model. One audit cell still contains a garbled self-correction, and “false positives must be zero, so use the conservative model” mixes up false alarms on novel legitimate cases with failure to flag extra behavior.

The recommendation, D5/D6 partial-order choices, DFG-versus-concurrency discussion, and the mandatory-SC alternative that would change the recommendation are sound. The numerical and terminology errors keep this below a high score.
