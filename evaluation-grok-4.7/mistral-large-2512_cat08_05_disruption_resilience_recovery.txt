5.5

The write-up covers every required section and reaches the right headline calls on C and G, but it is not a reliable incident analysis. Phase-level rates are repeatedly treated as if they measured single adaptations, the fast lane is both condemned and later praised for keeping controls, and the recovery design never closes the failure modes the logs actually show.

The timeline names T0–T4 and quotes the acute exception rates and the 91% / 61% fill split, yet it skips the comparison that defines the incident. Hospital median cycle time barely moved (8h → 10h → 9h → 8h) while pharmacy and wholesale medians and, more sharply, p90s stayed broken through Day 21 (pharmacy p90 49h → 74h, wholesale 72h → 120h). Conformance is unused: fitness 0.96 → 0.71 → 0.82 → 0.90 and fitting traces 91% → 44% → 59% → 76%. Absolute burden is also missed. Stabilization traceability exceptions are about 770 cases versus about 530 in the manual phase, and the audit queue is 2,300, so the “recovered” window carries more compliance debt than the outage. T1’s “no visibility” claim sits badly beside the morning snapshot used that afternoon. T3 is given variants the log does not support; partial recovery is described only as intake back with unreliable inventory sync and scanning. “High throughput” for a 91% fill rate confuses service level with volume. Case intake in the manual window was roughly baseline daily volume.

The adaptation table has the right columns and several defensible dispositions (retire uncontrolled C and G; keep B; qualify J). The evidence cells are not. B is an eligibility check on 2,500 triaged orders, not a controlled-substance control; that role is F. Fast-lane volume is 1,000, of which 920 go straight to the snapshot and none through an authorization activity, so the bypass is structural. D’s “stockouts” are not in the data. F and G are scored with the phase rates 2.9% and 9.5%, which mix every path; about 1,800 packed orders never entered temporary release, and dispatch-without-scan is fed by two edges (1,800 and 1,450) before 2,600 phone proofs. I is charged with the full 7.8% cancellation rate, while the graph shows 320 backlog cancels inside 430 total cancels. J “reduced cycle times” is the opposite of the pharmacy and wholesale stabilization results. L is credited with cutting exceptions to 2.2%, which treats after-the-fact remediation as real-time control and ignores that 2.2% is the residual rate on 35,000 cases, not the yield of the 2,300-case audit path. Spreadsheet invoice hold (2,200 cases), a named move-on-log deviation, never appears.

Section 3 states a useful slogan and then blurs it. Service protection on the fast lane is offered as true resilience even though that path dropped authorization. K and L, which are explicit reconstruction debt, are folded into concealed noncompliance. E, F, and J are left out of the three-way classification. The move-on-model gaps — missing quality release, scan, proof of delivery, and temperature record — are not tied back to paths.

The recovery model contains every requested block, and the control table is the strongest part of the answer. It still has operational holes. A hospital “must ship within 12h” rule is tighter than the baseline p90 of 15h and does not say what happens when the control bundle cannot be finished in time, which is how speed overran controls in this event. The matrix has no row for wholesale controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive product. Snapshot age is not a gate. Partial recovery is absent: there is no rule for when intake is up but sync and scanning are untrustworthy, which is exactly T3. Invoice close is not blocked on a minimum evidence set, so the spreadsheet-invoice and audit-tail pattern can recur. The BPMN sketch then collapses pharmacy and wholesale into “manual workflow.”

Indicators cover most requested families and the “within 20% of baseline median” idea is sound. Rework is missing despite moving 3.5% → 12.5% → 9.0% → 5.8%. Cross-segment cycle-time variance under 15% is already false in normal operations (8h versus 36h). Fill-rate targets of 95% and 85% have no baseline fill rate behind them. Complaint rate is invented. p90 and daily completed volume are absent, so the indicator set would have flashed green on the hospital median while wholesale tails and the audit queue were still deteriorating.

Recommendations are prioritized and state residual risk, but effort is not credible: an offline, later-syncing scan application is grouped as immediate and low effort in a validated wholesale operation. “Read-only replica with basic order processing” contradicts itself. Nothing hard-stops dispatch of controlled or cold-chain goods, bounds snapshot age, staffs the compliance bottleneck in B, or refuses to call stabilization complete while exception rates and the audit queue remain multiples of baseline.

The closing trade-off is decision-shaped, then undoes itself. It says the fast lane maintained reasonable controls after the table had already recorded an authorization bypass, and it treats a 61% pharmacy fill rate as a mild cycle-time concession. The conditions that would revise the decision are generic regulatory or technology changes, not a measured control result from this log.
