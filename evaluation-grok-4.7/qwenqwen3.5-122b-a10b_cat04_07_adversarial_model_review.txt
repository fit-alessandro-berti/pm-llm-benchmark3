The review is structurally complete and correctly flags Model S’s optional mandatory checks, Expedite-before-check interleaving, Model C’s eight-trace memorization, the holdout-fitness collapse, and positive-log-only evaluation. Those points do not survive contact with the model expressions.

Model S is misread. `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` makes approval mandatory, and there is no loop and no Correct Dossier. Traces that skip approval, repeat Credit Check, or contain Correct Dossier are not in its language, yet they are treated as accepted. One listed “undesirable” trace is policy-valid B2. The required five admitted bad behaviors are therefore not established.

Model C is also misclassified on probes. B2 (Technical Review before Credit Check, then Auto Approval) and B6 (recheck of Technical Review only) are absent from the eight branches; the matrix marks both accepted. That B6 claim contradicts the earlier, correct statement that Model C hard-codes both rechecks.

The highest-ranked pathology inverts precision: a model that allows everything has low precision, not 100%. Silent-transition effects and alignment-cost sensitivity are missing or replaced by runtime. Missing-event bias is recast as missing attributes.

`model_third` does not run (`Activity`, `SilentTransition`, and `Operator.PLUS` are not a valid PM4Py process-tree construction). Its XOR makes Supplier Review and Expedite Review mutually exclusive, so B5 is rejected, while the audit says accepted. `tau` on the loop redo allows a repeated check with no Correct Dossier, violating P7, while the audit says the opposite. Supplier Review is simply made optional despite the instruction not to handle the missing external/internal attribute that way. Section 8 omits B1, B2, B4, B6, and B7 and mixes “satisfies policy” with “accepts trace.”

The validation sketch omits a stakeholder protocol, an explicit reject-despite-good-metrics rule, alignment-cost sweeps, duplicate-event tests, and unseen-combination tests. The dissent invents a 5–10% exception rate and argues from unstated policy instead of these defects. The board would be misled on acceptance, compliance, and deployability.
