The final recommendation (A3, A4, A5, A6) is a real portfolio argument, not a frequency ranking, and it stays inside the four-module cap, keeps a human control role, and correctly refuses to lead with registration or an activation bot. It is not reliable enough to implement as written.

The decisive error is treating median wait as time the module removes. Entity-mismatch service is 55 minutes; the 31.0h is wait before the activity. An assistant can cut the 38% rework and the 1160 returns to KYC, but Section 7 still sells A4 as removing a 31h critical-path block. The same slip makes Portfolio A’s cycle-time effect “minimal” even though signing is 44h on about 7300 cases. Volume-weighted, signing, credit-analysis wait (28h), contract preparation (22h), and external valuation (58h) dominate a 31h delay on roughly 1750 cases.

Section 7 corrects an earlier reversal on A6: normalization absorbs analyst work, while cleaner inflow is what overloads the 9 FTE. The rollout then recreates that overload. Phase 1 deploys missing-document nudging before normalization, and the KYC pre-classifier before the specialist assistant. That is the resource pattern the case warns against: compliance escalation hits 3 FTE, two of whom already concentrate the handover network, and analysts receive cleaner files with the 75-minute normalization step still manual.

A3 is also the wrong “control” module for the measured failures. False negatives are severe, and a pre-classifier does not fix four-eyes breaches (5.8% of high-exposure cases at month-end), contract-to-decision mismatch (8.5%, 14% on renewal), or provisional signing (6.5%). Those point to A12, rule checks in A9, and an enforced KYC gate before e-sign. Four-eyes is discussed only as a design constraint on A8, which is not in the portfolio. Audit sampling is stated, but Year-1 monitoring never tracks those three violation rates.

The required comparison is missing. Portfolio B (A4, A5, A6, A7) is the actual flow alternative; the recommendation silently swaps out valuation triage, the longest synchronization point and the one tied to 71% of equipment finance, then argues only against A1. Concurrency is ignored: KYC and financial-document review run in parallel, and the document branch is the longer pole, so a KYC pre-classifier is a weak cycle-time lever. Weekly committee cadence, credit-line renewal rework, and judgment on high-exposure credit analysis never change the portfolio. A4-depends-on-A3 and A6-depends-on-A5 are overstated, and they drive the phase order. Section 4 never applies the effects table, variants, or directly-follows edges to the set that is actually recommended.
