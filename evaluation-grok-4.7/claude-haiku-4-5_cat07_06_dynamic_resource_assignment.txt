**4.5**

The write-up covers every required section and is specific enough to audit, but the policy cannot be executed as written, several assignments break the stated rules and the scenario data, and the baseline comparison misstates both reference policies. Those are core failures, not polish issues.

The scoring function does not rank resources in the direction the text claims. Workload, overtime, and non-owner “continuity” are built as penalties and then added into an `argmax`. Busier, higher-overtime resources score better. For non-owners, a higher reassignment count and a high handover cost also raise the score. SLA urgency is the same for every candidate, so it never changes the choice. Composite weights do not sum to 1, and the comment ranges (“1 .. 1”, “0.5 .. 0.5”) are not real ranges. Training adds at most about 0.02, so the 20% training rule is not enforced. Phase 2 never checks it.

Eligibility does not implement the supervision rules it describes. Urgent or high-priority skill below 2 is removed before scoring, including security skill 1–2, so the later supervision branch is dead. `estimated_duration` is used as a hard filter and is not in the data. High overtime is waived for any “high” priority case, not only when the case is urgent and no safer resource exists. Final Approval “prepare but do not finalize” never appears. String sort on priority is not a severity order.

Section 3 does not follow that algorithm, and several rows contradict the matrix or the answer’s own arithmetic.

- R1_Amal’s Eligibility score is 3, not 2.
- C003 goes to R4_David (overtime risk high, 14 open cases, 3.0 hours, normal priority, 18h remaining) even though the filter would drop him and safer diagnosis coverage exists (R3 skill 3, low overtime).
- C010 and C013 go to R2_Bruno, also high overtime, on normal and low work. The C013 row calls this his 4th new assignment and says he has the lowest open-case load. He receives two cases and starts with 12 open cases, near the top of the load range. R4 is listed as an approval alternative with Approval skill 0.
- C014 moves rework onto R9_Iris, another high-overtime resource, and treats normal priority as a reason that overtime risk is acceptable. That reverses constraint 4. R8_Hugo is skill 3 on rework with low overtime.
- C011 is taken off R6_Farid, the owner, with Negotiation skill 3, 5 open cases, and 6.5 hours. The justification says C002 leaves him 4.0 hours; the summary says about 0.5 hours. No task durations are given, and the invented ones are not stable across rows.
- C001’s note says the handover cost was already paid. The field is the cost if the owner changes. Keeping R3_Celine (Security skill 1) as performer on a 3h SLA also ignores the stated median +3.3h service time for a weak-skill owner. Supervision does not remove that time. R7 or R8 should perform it.
- R3’s open-case total is reported as 7 after two new assignments from a base of 6. R5 is described as near a 15-case cap while the same summary shows 11. R6, R7, and R8 do not share one hours-remaining model.
- C006 and C012 are deferred as a batch. That is allowed, but R10_Jonas is the quality specialist, the owner of C012, and the lightest resource (4 open, 6.0 hours). The text treats that light load as a reason to give him nothing. C012 is marked as a trainee case and is not used for training.
- Only C009 supports a documented training priority. Normal/low work is 9 of 16 cases, so 20% means at least two. Slack exists (C003/C009 diagnosis for R1 or R6; C010 can be prepared by a skill-1 resource and finalized by skill 2–3). The claim that urgency made the quota impossible is not true of this queue. R5 and R10 cannot be given C015: Security skill 0 is outside the skill 1–2 supervision exception. Shadowing is mentioned, then left unresolved.

The comparison reverses first-available routing. That rule fills the lowest open-case counts and would not pile work onto R4, R2, and R9. Specialist-only, with ties broken by open cases, also does not match the narrative: only three security cases exist, not five; the only Approval skill 3 is R6, and R4’s Approval skill is 0; Diagnosis skill 3 with the lighter load is R3, not R4. The 7-day rates, Gini values, and handover counts are presented as simulation output without a simulation, and the rework formula is not the given 1.8× / 1.4× evidence. Section 4’s “12 direct assignments” disagrees with the 14 direct assignments in section 3.

The worker note is the strongest part, but it quotes skill weight as 25–35% (the pseudocode uses 0.15 or 0.25) and promises that someone at 14 open cases will not receive another except in an emergency. C003 does exactly that.

The indicator list hits the requested categories. The measurements often do not. Handover-network edges are distinct resource pairs, not reassignment events. The Gini weights are reversed and can go negative. Training alignment tests whether an activity name is a substring of a priority such as `"diagnosis"`, which misses these activity names. The unsupervised-skill filter still counts supervised rows. The sample audit report mixes this queue with made-up compliance percentages.

The PM4Py sketch omits role discovery, activity-based resource similarity, batch discovery, and filtering by event or case performance, which the question explicitly points at. `format_dataframe` is called with `case_id_key` and `resource_key`. `get_service_time(..., case=...)` is not that API. Variant values are treated as lists of event dictionaries. Service and waiting columns are assumed without the lifecycle/start timestamps this log does not have. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is then divided as if those fields were timedeltas named `waiting_time` and `service_time`.

There is also an extra top-level `# Summary`, so the answer does not keep exactly the seven required headings. The trade-off discussion and the plain-language section show the right intent; the decision rule, the queue plan, and the baseline numbers do not agree with each other or with the case data.
