4.8

The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape and the right qualitative skeleton: baseline weekdays, a 15 April outage, a recovery discharge, a case-mix break near 20 April, a late staffing change, and thinner weekends. It also correctly refuses to treat median handling time as mean flow time, and it marks the outage window as unfit for Little’s law. The quantitative layer does not support that skeleton.

Regime boundaries are not a partition. “Outage recovery” (16–19 April) and “staffing intervention” (29 April–3 May) both contain weekends that are also placed in “all weekends,” while the printed rates describe only the weekdays inside those spans. Recovery “completions exceed arrivals” is false for Saturday 18 April (46 vs 48). Pre-mix `complex_pct` is 18–24, not a stable 24. Intervention service is median 32–35 and p90 72–80, not median up to 36 and p90 up to 88. “p9050” and “WIP falls from 5019” are broken figures. The 16–17 April capacity bump (7.0 and 6.5 FTE) is folded into recovery instead of separated from the logged 29 April intervention, whose start time is unknown, so 29 April is itself a mixed day.

Pooled arithmetic is wrong. Weekday arrivals average 108 per day, or 9.0 per open hour, not 8.7. Mean `staff_fte` is about 5.14, not 5.3. Dropping 15 April changes weekday mean WIP by roughly 0.6 case, not 8. Daily medians are treated as a poolable service median. No numeric mix comparison is given, and no Simpson-style reversal is actually shown.

Dispersion is misstated. On 7 April the hourly arrival Fano factor is already about 3 (CV about 0.6 is not mild relative to Poisson). On 21 April it is about 9.3, so “Fano > 3” erases the contrast. Completions on that day are much smoother than arrivals, and the 15 April completion vector has three exact zero hours; neither fact is used. Completions above arrivals show inventory imbalance, not lag autocorrelation. Weekend days are called one stationary regime even though mix, service, and WIP shift with the weekday break.

Little’s law is under-specified and partly mislabeled. Post-mix weekdays have completions below arrivals and WIP rising from 37 to 50, so the “stationary” cell is untenable. Intervention carry-over is still large (WIP 43 to 32 on weekdays), not low. Implied times (about 2.3 h, 4.4 h, 3.3 h, 4.8 h) are rough and low versus L/λ on the stated windows, and the cells never state the open-hour versus calendar-time choice, snapshot coverage, or why arrivals are the proxy while the balance is off. The recovery regime, where a law claim is least defensible, has no matrix row. Implied sojourn is never set next to handling time or against a 6-server capacity check.

The 08:00–10:00 smoothing recommendation follows `peak_08_10_pct` and ignores the representative profiles. Those profiles put the large 21 April mass near 10:00–11:00 and 13:00–14:00, and they do not reproduce the published 08:00–09:59 shares (the same mismatch appears on 7 April, 15 April, and 25 April). A 0.5 FTE post-outage buffer is far smaller than a three-hour stoppage that still admitted arrivals. “Uniform 6.0 FTE” is a fair pooled trap; the rest of the staffing advice is not tied to a stated capacity calculation.

The Python block omits p90, mix, outage, peak, and completion vectors; prints scalars rather than comparison tables; and detects no change point (it hard-codes “around 2026-04-20”). Its “pre-mix” filter is `date < 2026-04-20` on weekdays, which includes the outage and 16–17 April, so the coded flow time is about 3.3 h and does not match the 2.3 h in the essay. The PM4Py note only names the three functions. Assumptions are unlabeled, and several high-confidence cells sit on ranges that exclude days inside the stated bounds.
