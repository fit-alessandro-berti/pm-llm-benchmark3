The write-up covers every required section and sees the real modeling problem: R5 and R6 cannot be merged, temporal bounds are warnings, and resource rules need attributes the activity log does not carry. The concrete models and the verdict matrix do not survive that analysis.

The procedural layer contradicts itself. The compact tree puts the three checks in an exclusive choice and places clarification as an alternative to the whole case. The code uses a parallel block, which matches the backbone, then appends a one-child loop after the terminal path. PM4Py loops need a body and a redo child, and `ProcessTreeNode` is not the library API, so `procedural_model` would not execute. Sanctions escalation and the legal opinion never appear, yet traces that contain them are later marked as procedural passes.

The DECLARE dictionary inverts the required shape (template, then activity or pair). `succession` from both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` to `Close Onboarding` cannot hold in the same model: every closed case fails one of the two. `responded_existence` is weaker than R4 and R3; R3 needs alternate response, because each request must be answered before the next request and before a final outcome. `chain_response` (also the wrong template name) demands that the opinion be the next event. That fails T7, where the opinion correctly precedes activation but enhanced due diligence sits in between, and it fails T6 for adjacency rather than because of the 24-hour rule. Lowering confidence to 0.8 does not encode an exception.

The log skeleton repeats that closure error: `always_before` pairs both activation and rejection with close, so every test trace violates one pair. The stated reading of `always_after` is the semantics of `always_before`. `directly_follows` is only a partial whitelist, so ordinary low-risk order (capture then screening, verify then classify) is forbidden. `Release First Payment` is missing from `activ_freq` despite R10. The skeleton therefore does not encode R3, even though T5 is marked as a skeleton failure for R3.

The emergency check is reversed. The branch `opinion_time < activate_time` is the R5-compliant order, but it is treated as the emergency; an opinion after activation falls through as success. It never reads the joint authorization the chosen policy still requires. `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps cases that satisfy segregation; the text treats its result as the violation list. T4 is the same activity sequence as T1 and can fail only in the resource layer, but the matrix marks the procedural layer failed.

T7’s lateness arithmetic is right, and the seconds conversion and `zeta = 2.0` match R12. Those local successes do not repair the matrix: under the dictionaries as written, T1–T3 and T7 do not pass DECLARE or the skeleton, and T6’s DECLARE result is not policy-dependent. Deployment then recommends an accepting Petri net that was never given and returns on the first skeleton hit, which hides the cross-layer contradictions the design says should be shown.
