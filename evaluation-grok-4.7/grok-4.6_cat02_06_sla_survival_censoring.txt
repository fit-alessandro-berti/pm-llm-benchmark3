6.5

The estimand framework is strong: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72, competing exits distinguished from right censoring and from already-observed non-resolution, and the four illustrative cases are handled correctly under A/B/C. The finite-population bounds \(\theta_A,\theta_B\in[9/32,14/32]\), the 9/18/5 split, the 4/13 completed-case contrast, transfer counts, and the refusal to treat `Escalate`/`Rework` as baseline covariates are all right, as is the judgment that a cause-specific Cox model on \(n=32\) is not primary inference. The Aalen–Johansen update order keeps open cases and is a real estimator, not a sketch.

Those strengths are undercut by contradictions the answer itself creates.

The claim that “P1 is the worst SLA stratum” holds under T-compete is not supported by the estimand used to justify it. P3 has no case open before 72, so on-time internal resolution is exactly 2/10 (S02, S17). P1 is only 2/8 known on-time (S04, S18), with upper bound 3/8 if S29 still resolves. P3 is worse on \(\theta_A\) precisely because fast rejects and withdrawals count as non-resolution — a point Section 7 makes and Section 6 ignores. The stratified code would print that reversal. P1 is the heavy transfer/stall group (S08, S11, S21, S25, S32), which is a different statement.

T-fail does not change \(\theta_A(72)\) or \(\theta_B(72)\) relative to T-compete: late resolves sit after 72, and transfers are already non-resolve events, so relabeling them as cause 5 leaves the product-limit to 72 unchanged. The scenario that would actually move P1, timely hand-off counted as success, is discussed and then not encoded. T-censor is also applied to the all-cause survival that the text defines as \(\theta_B\), even though Estimand B treats transfer as terminal in every scenario. The printed bounds still count S08 and S21 as known non-resolutions under T-censor. S2’s stall recode and S3’s AutoCheck split are not in the code.

The landmark block is labeled “history only; no future leakage” and then sets `usable_escalated` from the full prefix and prints final `current_stage` for every case with \(t>48\). That is unsafe whenever the last event is after 48 (S09: age 92, gap 31, so `Review` is at hour 61; S24: last event at 63) and for every terminal case, whose intermediate times are unidentified. The prose states the correct restriction and the code does not apply it.

The state diagram drops observed transitions needed for paths the text highlights: `Screen→Resolve` (S16) and `AutoCheck→Review` (S15, S17). `Screen→AutoCheck` is annotated as cohort A, but S17 is cohort B. `Stalled` is labeled as hypothesized; those missing logged edges are not.

Dummy `1970-01-01` timestamps violate the no-fabricated-times rule. Disclosure that they are ordinal ranks limits the damage, and survival times do not use them, but `get_variants` is not what builds `variant_family`, and PM4Py’s variant map is not a count. “Complete-follow-up 67%” is the known-status subset 18/27, not follow-up of all 32.

Section 9 and the censoring discussion are otherwise at the right level of caution. The score is held down by the false stratum conclusion, the null sensitivity, the estimand/code mismatches, and the landmark leakage.
