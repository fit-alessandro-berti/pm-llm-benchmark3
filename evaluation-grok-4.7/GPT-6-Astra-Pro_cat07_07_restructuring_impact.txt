7.5

The forecast is unusually careful: the arithmetic checks, the recommendation is conditional rather than a full rollout, East is treated differently from North and South, and fairness, learning, tails, and accountability are all carried through the scenarios, indicators, and pilot. It still has enough analytical and methodological holes that it is not nearly flawless.

The listed handover totals (343 vs 359), the +4.7%, +14.9%, +13.6%, and +33.3% component changes, and the frequency-weighted wait index (5.883h to 7.222h, +22.8%) are computed correctly, and the text rightly refuses to treat that index as expected waiting time. What it never does is put 1.3 hours back into cycle times of 6.8–8.6 days. The central scenario’s “median roughly stable” and section 1’s prominent +22.8% therefore sit unresolved. A reader can easily take a coordination-edge movement of well under one percent of sojourn time as a large cycle-time effect.

The capacity stress tests are directionally useful but over-precise. Scaling Legal/Risk utilization by 4/3 to 121–124% assumes those referrals are the whole of current load. The approval figure \((49/42)\times(3/2)=1.75\) assumes one removed supervisor cuts approval capacity by one third and that all approval work, not only medium/high-value work, moves onto the shared queue. Both are labeled as sensitivities, then still presented as sharp ratios. “Not achievable” also sits awkwardly beside the later overtime point.

Section 7 is the clearest technical weakness. Follow-up truncation is handled well for cycle time and SLA, including open cases and unreached percentiles. The same truncated traces are then passed to handover, DFG, variant, and rework mining. A slower arm has fewer events by day 56, so handover and late rework rates are biased downward exactly when delay is worse. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting results are also stored as raw or internally normalized networks, while only the DFG and a custom edge count are scaled per 100 cases, so volume and pilot share confound the comparison the sketch is for. `local_expertise_flag`, `handover_reason`, and `reassignment_count` never enter the code despite being the log’s direct evidence for expertise, ownership, and learning. Role discovery is left as an unaligned clustering. Several API details (`perf_aggregation_key="all"`, variant key type versus `filter_variants`, service-time column names) are fragile, and the documentation URLs are internally inconsistent (`api/api/...` versus versioned `_modules` paths).

Smaller gaps: the optimistic “early warning” cell does not say what would confirm that scenario; in-flight cases have no rollback path; and 5.883 / 7.222 overstates the precision of one-decimal inputs. The pilot design, guardrails, and “do not read early averages as success” recommendation are otherwise strong and internally consistent.
