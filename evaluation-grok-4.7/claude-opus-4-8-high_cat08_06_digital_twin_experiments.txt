6.7

The design is a serious discrete-event twin: shared calendars, held-out waits, latent objections, seasonal arrivals, architect bursts, and intervention-level trade-offs are all present, and PM4Py is kept on the log/model side. It is not close to flawless. Several choices would bias the comparison the municipality would actually use.

The warm-up procedure contradicts the process it is applied to. Complex-variance and rework cases run 140–260 days, so resource occupancy and backlog keep the imprint of the initial WIP for many months. Deleting the first 4–6 weeks of statistics does not remove that. Welch and MSER-5 assume a stationary series with a transient into a steady mean; here the mean is seasonal (1.7× in two peak windows, Monday arrivals 2.2× Wednesday). A correct setup is a calendar-anchored start date, a WIP snapshot taken from that same season, statistics reported inside season windows, and a transient check measured against the seasonal trajectory. The skeleton then passes `horizon_days` as an int while `ArrivalProcess.rate` calls `t.month` and `t.weekday()`, so the non-stationary clock is not actually wired.

Decision metrics mix two clocks. Section 7 defines median lead time on a municipal clock that excludes applicant waiting, then sets targets of 12 days and 48 days. Those figures are end-to-end variant medians, and the 12-day figure is the clean simple path (26% of cases), not Simple renovation as a case type (45%, often the missing-document path at 22 days). Stop-the-clock rules are never defined, even though statutory permit SLAs usually turn on them. That undercuts D1: the dominant delay on the missing-document path is the applicant tail (median about 5 days, heavy), so a metric that strips applicant wait and a target taken from total duration answer different questions. D2 and D8 are allowed to trade inspection and environmental review for speed, but the selection rule has no guardrail for missed violations, false-negative environmental triage, or appeal reversals. Utilization “60–85% healthy, under 40% waste” is also the wrong denominator for fire (Tuesday–Thursday only) and environment (40% time already reserved).

The experiment section says factors are crossed, then lists S1–S3 as separate baseline shocks. S3 is the only stress tied to an intervention (D9). Rank stability across surges, legal/fire shocks, and objection prevalence is therefore a criterion without a matrix. D2’s eligible volume depends on latent `prior_violations`, which is not swept. The arrival write-up adds a background Poisson stream plus architect batches without stating that the top-20 architects’ 31% is partitioned out of the base rate, so volume can be double-counted. Planning-review rework is in the process description and absent from the loop state (completeness, inspection, legal/appeal only). Appeal return point, whether a 14-day public-notice floor is hard calendar time, how council weeks move that clock, whether multi-day service is preempted at shift end, and whether one inspector is seized for a whole district tour are all unspecified. The two Simple-only planners are a sub-pool without saying whether the other six can still take Simple renovation; with 45% of volume, that choice dominates planner queues.

PM4Py usage is largely the right API (`format_dataframe`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `parse_process_tree`, `convert_to_petri_net`, `get_case_arrival_average`), and the tree’s missing guards are stated. The skeleton still does not load the given excerpt (`rows=[]`), `ObjectionModel.sample` compares against 0 so the swept objection rate never fires, and one RNG is described as per-stream CRN. Outer Monte Carlo (about 50 parameter draws) times 40 replications times Sobol, on 14-plus runs of 24 months, is treated as routine with no budget or surrogate. Replication effort stops on the median CI while the stated primary outcome is the tail.

No optimality is claimed, waits are not injected as service, and objections stay latent. Those are the right calls. The remaining gaps sit on the quantities that would decide D1–D10: clock definition, warm-up versus cycle time, and whether stress and quality risk can change the ranking.
