2.5

The write-up has the required eight sections and a few directionally valid cautions (outage days are special, medians are not flow times, four hourly profiles cannot identify a stochastic model, opening backlog is missing). Those do not offset systematic misuse of the supplied table.

Regime boundaries contradict the series. “Baseline weekday” runs through 11–12 April, which are weekend days. “Post-outage burst” (16–20 April) mixes a true recovery day (completions above arrivals, 7.0 FTE), a near-balance Friday, a normal weekend, and the 20 April mix break. Complex share in that window is 23–41%, not 27–47%. “Weekend/recovery” (25 April–3 May) then swallows the worst deficit weekdays (27–28 April, WIP 49–50, about 10 arrivals per open hour) and the actual extra-staff period that starts 29 April. Arrival, completion, and WIP ranges in those rows do not match the dates they label. Outage capacity is called ~25% underutilized; the hourly profile shows three hours of zero completions while arrivals continue, which is lost capacity, not slack. Baseline utilization of ~30–40% is not derived and is implausible beside WIP around 20 and service medians near 25 minutes.

The alternative segmentation claims to follow the 29 April intervention, then cuts on 15 and 21 April. That is an internal contradiction, and it still fails to separate the outage from the later mix shift.

Pooled comparisons are numerically wrong and inconsistent with each other: day share is 20/8, not 88/12; weekend arrivals are about 14%, not 12% and not 20%; unweighted mean complex share is about 33%, not 28%; unweighted mean FTE is about 5.1, not 5.8; unweighted mean WIP is about 30, not 27.5; the mean of daily service medians is about 31 minutes, not 29. Segmented complex share described as “weekdays 20–24%” erases the post-20 April jump the section is supposed to reveal. No association is shown that actually reverses within regimes.

Dispersion figures do not come from the data. Early weekday daily arrivals are under-dispersed (Fano well below 1, CV near 0.05), not Fano 1.2–1.3 and CV 0.35. Lag-1 autocorrelations are stated as narrow ranges with no series definition. The 21 April spike of 34 is hour bin 10:00–11:00, not 08:00–09:00, and a second spike of 24 is ignored. The same vectors sum to the daily totals but disagree with `peak_08_10_pct`; that data conflict is never flagged. Staffing is also treated as something that changes arrivals.

Little’s Law is misstated as requiring mean service time. The only defensible use of these aggregates is an implied sojourn \(L/\lambda\), with proxy, window, and net-inventory caveats. The matrix instead marks an hourly 7 April row “Defensible” while pairing WIP 18 (6 April) with an unexplained 7.8/h rate, and treats a single unbalanced Saturday as stationary. Allowed verdict labels are not used. Implied sojourn is never set against median service, so the waiting-time gap is missed. Recovery throughput is still blended into a high-throughput regime.

Operations advice inherits those bins. It calls WIP 43–50 “acceptable,” and it recommends placing work into the 11:00–14:00 outage gap. The pooled-versus-segmented staffing warning is the right kind of point, but it is attached to the wrong WIP normal and the wrong dates.

The code block is not reproducible: values are ellipses, completions-by-hour are omitted, SciPy is unused, only one dispersion statistic is computed, there is no serial-dependence statistic and no change-point heuristic, and a second block violates the one-block rule. The named PM4Py helpers are not the ones required, and nothing prints the comparison tables the prose depends on. Assumptions are not labeled, and many precise figures are presented as calculations they are not.
