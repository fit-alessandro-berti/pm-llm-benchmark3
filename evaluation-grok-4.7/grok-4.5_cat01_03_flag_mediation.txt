7.5

The memo is a serious interventional analysis: the flag is treated as a policy bundle, natural direct/indirect effects are rejected for interference and cross-world reasons, review occurrence is separated from review waiting, and P0–P4, the pilot, and the reversal list are concrete. The format requirements are met. It is not nearly flawless.

**Timestamp table.** Most flag contrasts check out (flag 0 vs 1 triage wait about 149 vs 14 minutes, triage service 57 vs 36, manual-review wait 113 on n=4 vs 390 on n=10, cycles about 490 vs 991; escalated vs not about 1285 vs 549). Absent stages are correctly left as missing rather than zero. These cells are wrong if the stated arithmetic means are taken seriously: medium complexity arrival-to-triage is 71, not 84; medium residual-to-close is 74, not 72; high-complexity manual-review wait is 341, not 338; high-complexity escalation wait is 437, not 447. The published code would not reproduce the medium arrival figure, so the table and the script disagree. Conditional means also cannot be added to total cycle time; that non-additivity is never stated.

**Identification facts the file actually forces.** Flag is perfectly separated by `risk_score` (flag 0 ≤ 0.61, flag 1 ≥ 0.64). E2’s population of “overlapping scores” is empty; a within-band contrast is only a tiny regression discontinuity. Triage staff are likewise perfectly separated (flag 1 is only T1/T2, flag 0 only T3/T4), so lane and resource identity are the same observed treatment. Both facts appear only as side remarks. Every reject is review resource M2; “often” understates that.

**Graph and estimands.** `risk_score` never points at manual review, escalation, or reviewer behavior, so the P1 path “hide the bit, leave the score visible” is missing from the graph the policies rely on. `complexity → flag` is an extra direct edge the threshold does not support. `triage_wait → triage_service` is unexplained. E6 labels a P2-versus-P0 contrast as waiting-time mediation even though P2 shuts off automatic review occurrence. E8’s “controlled direct effect” outcome is only triage time.

**Pilot.** Case-level Bernoulli assignment is the primary design after the memo itself argues that shared queues break unit-level effects. Spillovers are secondary outcomes, not part of the estimand. The safety valve restores automatic review for high complexity; it is not a P4 escalation-criteria change, despite the “P2 + light P4” label. Eligibility and the futility rule are partly unreadable, and section 4 invents a “P8”.

Qualitatively, faster triage plus downstream review and escalation is still the right description, and P2-as-learning versus P3-as-capacity is a defensible split. The score is held down by wrong published stage means, missed deterministic overlap failures, and a pilot that does not match the interference claim.
