7.5

The decomposition, case table, aggregates, worked examples, and replay totals are arithmetically reliable, and the fork-join identity is handled correctly. The answer is not nearly flawless because the redesign narrative repeatedly misstates what the counterfactuals imply.

The core accounting is sound. `T(c) = upstream + max_i(queue_i + service_i) + downstream + final` is the right additive split, synchronization slack is correctly kept non-additive, and the F07 75-versus-225 illustration shows why. Every case row checked out, including critical branches (A: F01, F11; C: F04, F08; B: the other eight), sync waits, the 73/103 blocking-versus-residual split, and totals (flow 93–130, fork-join mean 61, downstream 176). F02, F04, F07, and F11 are worked correctly. B is rightly ranked above A despite shorter mean service, because critical frequency (8/12), queue-plus-service (mean 56.7, max 75), and both 75-minute stages sit on B. C is rightly treated as a queue-spike problem. Assumptions about the residual gap are labeled, and §7 covers the real identification limits (derived join, invisible branch capacity, preemption, policy versus queue, n=12).

The counterfactual engine is also numerically consistent under its own rule `start = max(join, resource free) + fixed residual`. A −20% saves 15 minutes of join time and only 5 minutes of flow time, because F11’s 10 minutes is absorbed by F1 until 11:50. Halving B’s queue saves 92.5 minutes of join time and 122 minutes of flow time after cascade. A third F resource saves 73; deleting the residual gap saves 171. Those figures are the strongest part of §4.

The synthesis then overreaches those figures. After the 50% B-queue cut, sole criticality is A in four cases, B in four (F03, F07, F09, F12), C in two, plus two A/B ties. That is not “A-dominant,” and B is not removed from the tail: F07 and F12 remain worst, at fork-join 60 rather than 75. Further B improvement is still worth about 27.5 minutes of join time before A binds. C’s untouched spikes (F04 at 57, F08 at 55) become part of the new tail, so “the next lever is A” and “further B improvement is nearly worthless” are not supported. The same overclaim is repeated in §3, §4, and §5. A second concrete error is grouping F10 with F04, F08, and F11 as gaining flow time “without any change to join_ready”: F10’s join does move (11:20 to 11:15); only 3 of its 8 flow minutes are cascade. §3 also says resource busyness explains waiting in four cases (F05, F08, F09, F11); F04 and F10 are partial blocking as well, so the count is six. §7’s “15-minute spacing” and “constant upstream of 20” omit F01 (10-minute arrival gap, 15-minute upstream). The overall mean A-sync of 11 minutes is the wrong statistic for “runner-up when B is critical”; that conditional mean is 15. The DFG remark is internally inconsistent: completion-ordered directly-follows times are nonnegative, while A→B ≈ −43 matches successor-start minus A-end on the three cases where B is the next start, not “time between completions.”

What keeps the score from falling further is that the primary influence ranking, the zero-benefit logic for speeding a noncritical branch, the masking-versus-cascade distinction, and the preference for instrumenting the join-to-F gap before buying F3 are all defensible. The miss is the post-intervention critical path, which is exactly the claim used to choose the second redesign.
