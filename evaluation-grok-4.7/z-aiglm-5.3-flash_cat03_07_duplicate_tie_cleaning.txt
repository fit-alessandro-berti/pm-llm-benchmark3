7.4

The policy analysis is strong and mostly conservative: the decision tree covers every required case, the event table keeps e05 and e14, abstraction levels and the e08/e09 label collision are not collapsed, original timestamps are retained, and ingestion time is not treated as business order. Counts for the stated policies are internally consistent (15/39, 14/37, 14/36, and about 10/28), and the `have_common_object` warning correctly explains why sharing O1 and V1 does not make e13 and e14 duplicates.

The ordering implementation contradicts the stated order. `DOMAIN_PRECEDES` gives e14 no predecessor, so Kahn puts it in layer 0 with e11 and e12 and layer 1 holds e13. The comparator then emits e11, e12, e14, e13, e15, while section 3 requires e11, e12, an arbitrary e13/e14 tie, then e15. That manufactured layer gap presents Capture M2 before Capture M1 as semantic precedence. A3 (issue before money movement) is also described but never encoded; ingestion time silently separates those layer-0 events. Same-source `source_seq` additionally forces e13 before e14 even though the prose calls that pair interchangeable, and the tie tag cannot repair a cross-layer order.

Secondary gaps: the aggressive path never validates referential integrity; one global audit log mixes scenarios; VR-03 records refined activities rather than originals; section 8’s `relation_delta` is not written; and the drop-duplicates comment wrongly credits relabeling for protecting e03/e05, which actually depends on object-set inequality. “activity+tijd” is a leftover slip. The result is a careful design with a material defect in the required deterministic order.
