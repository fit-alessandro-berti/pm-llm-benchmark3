5.5

The reconstruction is competent on fully observed flows, but several reported system metrics are wrong, one required check is only sketched, and the imputation scenarios contradict the answer’s own queueing assumptions.

The case arithmetic that can be checked directly is mostly right. S1/S2 entry definitions, the four same-resource feasibility windows, the special-case table for P05, P07, P11, P15, P16, P17, P21, and P22, subperiod counts (13/8/1 arrivals, 6/7/7 S1 completions, 3/5/6 S2 completions), completed S2 sojourns (35–110 min, mean 70), in-window busy time (S1 535–605 of 720; S2 515–570 of 705), and the 14:00 stock (2 at S1, 6 at S2) all follow the log. Sojourn invariance when a start is missing is correctly identified, and open cases are kept in the narrative.

Those strengths do not carry the station-level picture. Post-event S1 WIP peaks at 8 from 10:10 onward, and the stated arrival-before-completion tie break would spike it to 9; the claimed peak of about 7 at 12:30 is a non-peak snapshot. Time-average S1 WIP is about 5.8 and S2 about 3.7, not about 5 and about 3. S2 itself peaks at 7 just before 13:50, which is never stated. In 08:00–09:00, S1 WIP is already 4–5, not 1–3, so the “mildly loaded, headroom” reading starts too late. The closing claim that late cases have 65–100+ minutes of S2 wait, naming P08, P10, and P14–P20, is false: P08 is 55, P10 is 50, and P18/P19/P20 are censored at about 50/35/15. “130+ minutes of S1 queueing” is also unsupported; observed waits top out near 105 and the pessimistic P15 bound is 125. Sojourn is being slid into the wait column.

Assumption A2 (FCFS, multi-server) is not applied to the missing starts. Work-conserving FCFS pins P07 at 09:25, P15 at 11:40, P05 at 09:45, and P11 at 12:00. The central imputations leave a server idle with that case waiting, and every pessimistic start lets a later arrival begin on the other resource first. The 0.73–0.85 utilization band is therefore mostly the width of infeasible idle-with-queue schedules. Under the answer’s own discipline, busy time sits at the top of that band. The P11 note that the C2 outage forces a start at or after 12:00 is the wrong constraint; P08 occupies C2 until 12:00, and the outage ended at 11:10. The queue already waiting at 10:55 (P08, P09, and P10) is undercounted.

Little’s Law is required as a numerical consistency check on an interval where its assumptions are defensible. Section 2.4 only writes \(L \approx \lambda W\), then points at the full 08:00–14:00 window, which the text itself says is nonstationary. No \(L\), \(\lambda\), and \(W\) are computed or compared. The “mean WIP consistent with Little’s Law” sentence is circular: the WIP figures look backed out of completed-case \(\lambda W\) (about 5.1 and 2.7) rather than from a trajectory, and censored end-of-window cases make those two quantities disagree.

Rounding is named and then set aside. Many handoffs are exactly one 5-minute grain apart, and C1 has a logged 11:00 end/start pair; \(\pm 2.5\) minutes can create or remove overlaps and move a missing start. That is not turned into bounds. Queue length excluding service is never separated from WIP, despite the event-order convention being introduced for that purpose.

The sensitivity section is partly right that completed sojourns and the 14:00 stock do not depend on how a missing start is split. It overstates what was tested. Optimistic versus pessimistic open-case tails do not change in-window busy time, because P15, P17, and P21 already have known starts. Zero-length service is treated as an operational scenario after being called unrealistic.

The Python block does not reproduce the station results. It never builds WIP, busy time, rates, or a Little’s Law comparison; optimistic and pessimistic frames are computed and discarded; open elapsed service is set to null; the central midpoint is unrounded (09:42:30, 12:12:30), while the prose uses 09:45 and 12:10. `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time(..., end_timestamp_key=...)` do not match current PM4Py signatures, so the script as written is likely to raise before printing. The case-level PM4Py calls are also on the wrong projection: activities with a missing or open endpoint are dropped, so case service and inter-activity wait are not valid case metrics, and initial S1 wait is absent because arrival is not an event.
