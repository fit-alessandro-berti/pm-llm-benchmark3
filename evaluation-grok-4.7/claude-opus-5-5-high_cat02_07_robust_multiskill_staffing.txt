The capacity arithmetic, scenario scaling, policy contrast, and process-mining caveats are strong and largely auditable. The score stays well below a near-flawless mark because the headline delay results are horizon-censored, several dispatch rules in the write-up do not match the simulator, and a few bottleneck claims are stronger than the evidence.

The service means, bounds, and base load check out. Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, and Validation 15.74 are the correct uniform-mixture midpoints, and the daily totals 2,065 / 2,721 / 3,376 follow from the task counts. Scheduled supply of 2,490 minutes, the block staffing (including the 12:00–16:00 breaks), and the block loads and ρ values (0.93, 0.98, 0.89, 1.14, 1.87, 1.60, day 1.09) are right. U1 at 3,443, U2 at 3,188 with Electrical 12:00–16:00 equal to 571 against a 420 home ceiling, U3 supply 2,040, U4 at 2,962, and U5 at ρ 1.69 are coherent. The 90% fairness bound (about 3,023 scheduled minutes) is a valid whole-day bound under multiplier 1. The zero-queue path means that can be checked in closed form match the table (M 56, E 63, Chen software 73, Chen ES sequential 106). Block-level P1 and P2 attainment reweights to the reported day SLAs (HYB_RF base P1 0.860 versus 0.86; P2 0.813 versus 0.82). Assumptions A1–A13 are labeled, and the two objective families plus minimax regret of J_fair meet the required decision frame.

The specialization-versus-pooling tension is real in the reported results: SPEC leaves Farah near half utilized and cannot cover morning software; POOL buys P1 and P3 with slow multipliers, setups, and 7–11 preemptions a day; the hybrids keep most of the P1 gain with less churn. Recommending HYB_RF only under an explicit cost, with HYB_R and HYB as zero-hour fallbacks, and stating that neither the SLA targets nor the 90% cap are feasible at the central service level, is the right structure. The PM4Py section is accurate on the points that usually get misread: `start_timestamp_key` must be passed, case “service” sums overlapping branches so the residual is not queue time and can be negative, and stage waits have to come from `queue_entry`. The pilot power figure of about 680 P1 cases per arm is correct.

The main metric problem is in Section 4. Unfinished cases are scored as finishing at the simulation horizon, and the unfinished fraction is never shown. With a structural deficit, mean delay of 440–1,500 minutes and p90 delay of roughly 1,300–3,200 minutes are horizon-dependent lower bounds dominated by overnight backlog. Section 9 admits that, but the comparison table still presents them as policy performance. SLA, 20:30 backlog, and the empty-start sensitivity are the sounder evidence; the delay columns are not interpretable on their own.

The hybrid that was simulated is not the hybrid that was specified. The rule card says a guard, when free, takes P1 first, and that ties go to home skill and earliest finish. The score tuple places `guard_bonus` above those keys, so an idle Farah (×1.40) or Deepa takes a P1 away from an idle faster specialist. That is a different rule, and it is active in every recommended variant. Section 3.1 also says every non-home assignment, including P1, requires an empty home queue; the rule card and the code exempt P1. Farah has no home diagnostic skill, so her guard-release test can never fire, contradicting the stated 120-minute release. A5 says Farah never triggers the extra 5 minutes; `team_of` still assigns MECH or ES from the diagnostic activity, so a handoff into her electrical or software work does trigger it.

Smaller overstatements accumulate. “Nobody qualified” for Mechanical at 18:00–20:00 ignores Deepa at ×1.25 until 18:00 and her overtime to 18:30. Chen’s “210 minutes in a 120-minute block” assigns all 08:00–10:00 electrical and software to him even though Ada and Farah are qualified. “Cannot be completed” for a P1 after 18:30 is too hard: a simple path has mean near 56–73 minutes, and the mode trigger at 18:30 disagrees with the 19:30 statement in Section 2. The U2 sentence that the milder reading drops about 460 minutes “to about base volume” skips the mix shift from renormalizing M, S, and ME. The SLA intervals treat cases as independent, so the “about 4 standard errors” claim is too sharp; common random numbers would narrow the difference, but the printed half-widths do not show that calculation. J_fair cannot be tied exactly to the rounded table. The surge, absence, long-tail, and outage modes in Section 5 are unsimulated operating rules, while only the standing hybrid plus roster was scored. Handover burden sums both branches, not the critical path. The parallel-versus-sequential estimator charges handover on a branch the intake resource might have kept.

None of these break the capacity conclusion or reverse the qualitative ordering by themselves. The guard-score bug biases results against the hybrids, so preferring them to POOL and SPEC is conservative rather than optimistic. The answer still does not earn a top mark: the central results table and the recommended dispatch rule are not fully the objects the prose describes.
