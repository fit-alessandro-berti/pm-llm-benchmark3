The answer meets the required structure and is unusually well grounded: the fast-path contradiction is explicit, segment differences are respected, and the three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms (routing control, evidence acquisition, and fulfillment/repair control) rather than Lean slogans or parameter tweaks. Counts, conformance results, rework rates, segment outcomes, and resource limits are used accurately, and each redesign states a real sacrifice. The experiment plan also checks whether gains merely move downstream.

It is not nearly flawless. Redesign A classifies mid-risk devices but never gives them an operating lane. It also loosens R2 by floating a “low-value exception” for Standard accessories that the rule does not allow. More importantly, the Stage 1 go threshold treats the 5–6% reviewed/repair complaint rate as the expected result of adding eligibility review, even though the answer itself says wrong-part selection is a dispatch problem that Redesign A does not fix. The 150–180 hour stop line borrows complaint-lag p90s and applies them to cycle time. The dominance column is uniformly “Conditional,” so it does not distinguish the current process from the redesigns. Frequency imbalances (about 3,625 recorded ends versus 5,000 starts, and dispatch inflows that do not match outflows) are also ignored.
