The final answer is a strong, mostly well-calibrated causal and process analysis: the crude Orion disadvantage (6.72 h) is correctly reversed within every complexity stratum and under pooled-weight standardization (Orion about 2.51 h faster), positivity failures are identified, queue time is separated from review service, completed-case selection is treated as selection, and the proposed 50% reassignment is rejected in favor of a medium-complexity randomized pilot plus a compliance-capacity change. Arithmetic for the main contrasts, night/day splits, rework, and appeals checks out.

It is not nearly flawless. The compliance-required comparison (26.69 vs 27.00 h) is repeatedly treated as evidence that the group gap shrinks once process path is held fixed. That contrast is still mixed by complexity: Orion’s compliance cases are heavier (6 high / 3 medium) than Nova’s (2 high / 2 medium). Within complexity, compliance-required Orion cases remain faster, so this is not evidence against an Orion advantage.

That mistake carries into the mediation hypothesis. Compliance required is correctly described as policy-driven by complexity and not caused by group, but the same row then calls for a natural indirect effect of resource group through compliance. That estimand requires the mediator to be downstream of treatment; the appropriate analysis is confounding control, not front-door/mediation of the group effect.

The H2 “predicted observation” states the result that would weaken H2 (Orion still faster after risk adjustment), while other rows predict the result that would support the hypothesis. Section 1 also says reassignment is “likely to increase” cycle time after explicitly denying that within-stratum differences identify a causal Orion benefit.

The DAG treats `resource_group → shift` as fact-supported. Coverage is a fact; direction is not. If arrival shift drives routing, shift is a confounder, and the mediator role is wrong. `rework → cycle_time` is drawn parallel to `review_service → cycle_time` even though review service already includes the repeated review. Case-level interference in a shared queue is only weakly handled by a daily cap.

The diagnostic script would fail immediately: `pd.StringIO` is not a pandas attribute (`io.StringIO` is required). Synthetic timestamps are used, though not for performance durations. The required structure, role coverage, falsification logic, and learning plan are otherwise met.
