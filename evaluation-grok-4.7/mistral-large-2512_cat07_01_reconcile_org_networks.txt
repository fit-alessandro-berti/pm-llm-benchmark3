The write-up covers every required heading, keeps several hypotheses open, and correctly treats handover centrality and working-together co-occurrence as different phenomena. It is not reliable enough on the measurements that actually support those claims.

The central reconciliation is only partly right. Short, episodic routing can produce high handover and subcontracting centrality with low joint-case rates, and the absence result is real evidence of dependency on the exception path. That point is then undone. Low co-occurrence is used as evidence against an exception desk and against brokerage, even though both roles predict exactly that pattern. A 3.5-minute Bot_Rules → Mira handoff, comparable to the team-lead handoffs, is later called queueing. “High variance” in cycle-time contribution is restated as disproportionate delay. Mira → Omar in 42 minutes is treated as specialists waiting on her, rather than time after her event, likely at the specialist.

The subcontracting direction is reversed. Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules, with a later Fraud Screen update, means the bot returns; it does not show Mira updating or overriding rules. Vendor_Verify → Mira → adjusters is not in the handover table. “Assign Specialist” is outside the stated activity list and is never flagged. “Main activities” are treated as complete profiles. “No audit trail” and “undocumented rules” are asserted from missing fields in a summary, and a naive cycle-time regression on Mira’s involvement ignores selection into hard cases.

The absence numbers are mishandled. The 90th percentile moved from 8.4 to 13.9 days, a rise of about two-thirds, not “nearly doubled,” and queue buildup does not prove she accelerates exceptions. Inbound speed while she is present and failure when the role is vacant are collapsed into one bottleneck. Workload fairness compares her 920 exception handoffs with Alice and Bruno’s ordinary routing counts. Data-quality risk is rated low despite top-edge truncation, no lifecycle semantics, and the activity-label mismatch. Bots and vendors stay peripheral: routine Alice/Bruno → Vendor_Pay bypasses her, and the Temp_1–Vendor_Verify loop is barely used.

The executive summary runs past the 180-word limit and states discretion, lack of rules, and acceleration as facts. Immediate actions assign high-complexity exceptions to team leads, while “do not merge her into a team” assumes the boundary-spanner hypothesis. Several “event-log predictions” are training records, emails, and policy documents.
