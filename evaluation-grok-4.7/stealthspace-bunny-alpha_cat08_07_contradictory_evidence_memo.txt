7.2

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and respects the main constraints: no national scale, no blanket rollback, no claim that one design wins every metric, and an explicit split among facts, interpretations, and assumptions. Sections 3 and 6 are the strongest. Fitness versus precision, median versus tail, handover counts versus resilience, visible versus downstream cost, and segment heterogeneity are explained without treating the pilot as a clean causal test. Rollout gates (pre-specified noninferiority, loaded cost, permit and activation controls, resilience) are concrete.

The evidence table, which is supposed to carry the facts, is not reliable. Simple low-risk houses are stated as having a median of 6 days and a p90 of 8 days, with complaints at “1 point.” Those figures are changes (−6 days, −8 days, −1 pp), and the memo itself later calls them improvements. That is an internal contradiction on the subgroup that justifies the recommendation. Most other baseline-to-pilot pairs are concatenated without a readable “from–to” marker (for example “p90 worsened 3138.5 days,” “82%76%,” “€184€161,” “+21+17”), so several “direct facts” cannot be checked as written. Interpretive lines such as “may have created concentration risk” also sit inside fact rows.

The reasoning overreaches in a few decision-critical places. Simple-house gains were observed under the full bundle; retaining only merged scheduling and coordinator ownership will not automatically “preserve” them, and alternative E’s risks omit that interaction. “Pause expansion” of survey skipping is softer and less clear than stopping a rule already associated with apartment harm outside a controlled test. Ranking M6 first is only partly right: validating the skip rule against survey outcomes mainly governs whether skipping can resume, not whether the simple-house path should expand. “Simpson’s paradox” is the wrong label for a mix of within-segment tail harm plus a different building mix. Business and rural segments, the shift in complaint themes from waiting to “installed but not working” and billing before activation, and the simple-house SLA improvement are thinly used. Coordinator load at 93% is noted, but the immediate plan keeps that ownership model without a capacity action.

Strategic shape and metric literacy are well above a generic summary. The pivotal fact error, unreadable table, and unbundling overclaim keep it well short of nearly flawless.
