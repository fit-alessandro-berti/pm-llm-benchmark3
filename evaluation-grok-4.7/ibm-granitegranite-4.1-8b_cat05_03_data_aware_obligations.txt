The write-up has the required sections and reaches a defensible label on several plain cases (TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-306, TX-307, and the TX-309 outcome). It does not reliably implement event-time state, exemption evidence, or the requested checker. The failures sit on the points the task isolates: late evidence, same-timestamp order, unknown amounts, and materiality.

Section 1 names event, effective, record, and evidence time, blanks-as-no-update, and `event_index`. The exemption example is dated 2004-04-06. “Material change” is left as anything that affects a trigger, so decreases and sub-20% edits are not separated from R4 invalidation. Final-state non-retroactivity is stated, then not held in the later casework.

Section 2’s table drops comparison operators (“effective_from  obligation trigger”, “evidence  INDETERMINATE”), so several cells do not state a rule. The pseudo-logic has mechanical defects: R1 treats every `Update_*` as material and has no event-index or >20% test; R2 uses `er` outside the quantifier that binds it and never requires the review to post-date the HIGH/PEP change; R3 does not test that the list update concerns the current jurisdiction; R4 compares an increase with the latest review without requiring the increase to be later, and it numeric-compares an unknown base; R6 sets the whole case INDETERMINATE whenever any of the four attributes is missing, including when that attribute cannot change the trigger; R7 rebuilds state from events of the same type only. A standard-packet `Compliance_Approve` is not excluded.

Case table: “Triggered rules” lists R1 or R2 on cases the narrative then says do not trigger. TX-308’s release state is given as 140,000 after a decrease to 100,000, and a Standard_Review that is not in the log is cited. TX-309 places the 95,000→118,000 increase after the Enhanced_Review and calls it +24.7%; that increase is at 15:20, before the 15:25 review, and is about +24.2%. The later 118,000→145,000 step is what invalidates the enhanced review; NONCOMPLIANT is still the right label. TX-310 is the serious misjudgment: EX-88 is called a justified exception at 90% confidence, the requester’s oral statement is treated as the strongest evidence, and the decisive-evidence cell claims a qualified signature. The qualified certificate was created at 10:30, after the 10:00 release, and R5 says a displayed backdated field is not conclusive. That record supports INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT, not a waiver. TX-311 reverses the stated order: Enhanced_Review is event_index 3 and the DE update is event_index 4, so jurisdiction becomes DE after the review. The release state is DE, so R1 is off and COMPLIANT can stand, but the stated mechanism is the opposite of the log. Confidence is 100% on almost every row, including the indeterminate amount case.

Section 4 covers the four named cases and then argues the wrong counterfactuals. Invalidating EX-77 would leave TX-302 compliant because an Enhanced_Review at 09:20 still sits after creation and before release. TX-305’s settlement figure is not an immutable release amount; using 150,000 would turn R1 on against a review that recorded no amount, not establish COMPLIANT. TX-311’s alternative says a US jurisdiction at review makes R1 fail for lack of an Enhanced_Review, even though that review exists and R1 is tested at release.

The code does not meet the specification. Obsolete `pm4py` factory imports are unused, and `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called. The embedded log rewrites CORPORATE as CONTRACT, shortens notes, and drops “approved standard review packet.” Forward-fill direction is right only while row order holds. `latest_material_change` compares misaligned series and is not computed per case. `Risk_Reclassify` is not a tracked material event. R3 and R4 are absent. The exemption join treats scope label `R1` as validity and ignores signature time, witness independence, jurisdiction, customer type, cap, and expiry. A missing amount falls through R1 as `N/A`, which is the silent low-risk default R6 forbids. The diagnostics frame has no evidence timestamps and no unknown-value flags. There is no DECLARE or trace-order check.

Section 6 lists six modes, but “final-state leakage” is described as failing to see an earlier change. Leakage is copying the final attribute vector backward. Section 7 does hit immutable release payloads, effective versus record time, rule version, exemption provenance, change provenance, and stable ordering; signature time is only indirect, and review events still would not store the amount and jurisdiction actually reviewed.
