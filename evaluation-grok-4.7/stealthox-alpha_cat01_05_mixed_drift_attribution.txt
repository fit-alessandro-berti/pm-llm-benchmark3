7.0

The final write-up hits every required section, uses the right mechanism split (March semantic break versus later rework, queueing, priority, and seasonality), and most of the baseline deltas and the 7-edge L1/L2 fragment distances check out. It is not nearly flawless: several load-bearing numbers are wrong, and one decomposition invents precision the aggregates do not identify.

The performance timing claim is miscalculated. April to May median cycle time is +4.5 h (32.0 to 36.5), not +0.8 h. The +0.8 h step is March to April. May to June is +4.3 h, so the slope does not “steepen sharply” at the 15 June staffing cut; the large step is already present with the May supplier shock, and June continues it. That error props up a June performance change point the monthly medians do not isolate. The October interaction claim mixes baselines: September to October is +20 h at p90 but +6.3 h at the median (41.5 to 47.8), not +2.8 h. The +2.8 h figure is August to October, over which p90 rises only +6 h. The “tail explodes while the median barely moves” contrast is therefore not the October contrast.

The cycle-time split treats an unidentified class gap as data. Baseline mix does not imply standard 29 h and high-complexity 39.5 h; any gap is compatible with one aggregate median, and a different gap moves the mix share outside the stated 15–20%. Reweighting class medians is also not a mixture median. The residual “queueing is about half or more” reading is still directionally consistent with flat assessor service time, but the component table is tighter than the evidence.

Section 9 then over-attributes. Resource-field loss does not explain the raw fitness collapse; activity-model fitness moves because of the rename and duplicated `Open Case`, not missing `org:resource`. “Median rose ~14 h” in June–August is the cumulative rise from the January–February baseline through August, not the post-staffing increment (May 36.5 to August 45.0 is +8.5 h; June to August is +4.2 h). Calling the later mapped-fitness drop unambiguous behavioral drift is slightly strong: it is also what an old model does when `Senior Assessment` or incomplete paths become more common. The control-flow point in section 2 is similarly loose: a screen that checks less does not lower fitness unless the trace changes.

Smaller factual slips remain in the final text. February versus March high-complexity share is 20% versus 22%, not 22% versus 23%. Rework ties November with July at 29%, so “peaks in July” is incomplete. October-to-December L1 is not a consecutive-month distance. The non-Z × standard cell is not stably 55–65%. Rule 3’s “service within 10%” band excludes the observed 4.8 h to 5.4 h move (+12.5%) that the attribution calls flat. In the code, `import pm4py` sits on the execution path of Part A, and a full-series MAD with k = 3.5 does not flag the median series at all (the largest deviation from the median difference stays inside 3.5 × MAD), so the advertised detector misses the cycle-time shifts the prose relies on. Event-mode DFGs divided by trace-contained case counts also bias the Part B rates.

Causal restraint, supplier versus mix discrimination, seasonality versus December interventions, and the multi-monitor design are substantively good. The arithmetic and identification errors above are large enough that this should not score in the high 8s.
