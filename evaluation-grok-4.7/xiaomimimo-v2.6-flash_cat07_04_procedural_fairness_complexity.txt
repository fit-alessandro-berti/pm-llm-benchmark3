7.4

The assessment meets the required structure, covers every mandated fairness dimension and mitigation, and reaches a properly calibrated conclusion: raw approval gaps are not discrimination, case mix does not erase residual burden, `risk_score_band` is not treated as neutral, and support steps are kept while their queues are criticized.

Several errors keep it well short of nearly flawless. Within-group variant shares are summed across groups (about 101–113%) and treated as proof that the cohorts overlap and must not be added; those percentages have different denominators, so the sum does not identify overlap. Group B’s V4 share is 24% versus 1% for Group A (24×), but the write-up and bottom line repeatedly call this 10–15×. The 1.5× specialist-load comparison treats “3600 Group A cases / 52 caseworkers” as comparable to specialist-step volume over 7 or 5 people, which the assignment text does not support, yet the bottom line states it as a finding. The V5 median-reweighting check is not a valid decomposition of medians and is still described as very close to the +4.1-day adjusted residual. Adding `language_preference` in M2 is presented as isolating legitimate routing, without flagging near-collinearity with how Group B is defined.

The PM4Py section is not reliable code. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a 3-tuple whose edge values are statistic dicts, not scalar means; log-skeleton keys `allowed` / `not_allowed` / `activities` are wrong; `level="cases"`, `SPECS_M1`, `cases_duration_series`, and `g` are broken or undefined; and `get_service_time` is used as an aligned per-case series. The fairness judgment itself is directionally sound, but these logical, numeric, and API mistakes are material under a strict reading.
