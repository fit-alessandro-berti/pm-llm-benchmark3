The answer follows the required schema and is strongest where case attributes change obligations: C6-A versus C6-B, the migration/worklist treatment in C6-D, and the lot-level block on closure in C6-E. Those parts still sit on several material reasoning errors, so the forecasts are not reliable as next-event distributions.

C6-A treats Supplier Analysis as already mandatory. One complaint linked to two devices from the same lot does not meet the two-complaint trigger, and the three similar complaints are not shown to share that lot. Technical Investigation Completed is given 0.15–0.32 as the next log event although no investigation opening is visible, the relevant medians are 8.5–9 h, and the engineering queue is short-staffed. A second evidence request is not enabled until Evidence Review completes; with a normal feed, 0.05–0.15 is too high for it to be the next logged activity.

C6-B’s primary state is right, but the alternative “S1” is not available after a completed evidence request and receipt. Close — No Safety Issue (0.15–0.35) and Disposition Recorded (0.08–0.20) are downstream of review, and both are marked not process-enabled. Their midpoints plus the review midpoint exceed 1, so this is not a coherent next-event distribution. Assignment to R-T4, who usually handles potentially reportable cases, is unused as contrary evidence. The claim that duplicate closures often skip disposition is unsupported.

C6-C is the weakest case. Medium severity, no injury, and a country rule that repeated malfunction “can be” reportable leave S5 DISPOSITION_READY as a live alternative; it is never considered. Disposition Recorded is the most frequent logged successor of Technical Investigation (119 versus 74 for Regulatory Assessment) and is omitted. The 74/51 counts show either-order execution, which the scenario already states; they are read instead as a second technical-investigation cycle. Supplier Analysis remains a weak trigger across different lots, yet its completion still receives 0.10–0.25.

C6-D correctly refuses to treat missing events as proof the work did not occur, and the worklist supports an open regulatory assessment. The alternative “S2/S3” is a side issue about evidence logging, not a competing case state; the real alternative is a delayed regulatory completion moving the case toward disposition. Disposition Recorded at 0.12–0.28 is poorly timed: the assessment was opened at 12:40, typical completion is 2–14 h, and high severity plus injury blocks disposition until that assessment finishes. Rank 3 also fuses two different activities into one prediction. A known source-system attachment under an explicit migration delay is underweighted relative to that disposition jump.

C6-E correctly keeps the second cycle in evidence review and correctly withholds a low-severity closure while six complaints share the lot. It then invents a completeness “drop”; only a post-receipt score of 0.72 is given. A third evidence request is again a post-review action, not an enabled next event, at 0.15–0.30. Supplier Analysis is described as likely already in progress but flagged `process_enabled: false`, and 0.08–0.20 ignores that the case is already about a day old against a 6–36 h supplier cycle.

Across A, B, and E, gated later outcomes are repeatedly entered as next logged activities. Contradiction lists often restate missing events that are already the premise, while stronger contrary facts—the disposition edge, queue and duration mismatch, and R-T4 on the low-severity case—are left out. The cross-case note and DFG limitations are substantively good, but they describe a more disciplined method than the case rankings use.
