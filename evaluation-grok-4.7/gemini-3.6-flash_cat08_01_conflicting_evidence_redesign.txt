The write-up follows the required structure and cites a wide set of real artifacts (volumes, edge times, fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, 14.1% vs 3.3% complaints, 31%→9% cost gap, segment shares, portal and gate options). The three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms, and each names a sacrifice. That is not enough to carry the analysis, because several load-bearing claims contradict the evidence or each other.

The fast path is treated as a 1–4 hour process (“median execution under 4 hours”; table baseline “Fast path 1–4h”). Those figures are sums of selected directly-follows medians. Segment cycle times on the fast path are 8, 13, 20, and 28 hours. The same table then uses the real manual-path band (46–120h), so the baseline mixes two incompatible clocks. Activity accounting is also off: Fast approval is 2,790 cases (2,550 from the auto screen plus 240 from fraud/risk), yet 2,300 dispatches and 140 direct complaints are turned into 90.2% and 5.5% of the 2,550 only.

Rule diagnosis overreaches. The stated alignment deviations are fast approval before evidence completeness, complaint/reopen after close, and dispatch without risk review on high-value devices. The report converts the second of those into an R3 breach (close or denial with no manual review). R3 is then dropped from every redesign. “Waiting times spike exponentially” above 82% utilization is stronger than the stated “queue grows sharply.”

Redesign A’s effect sizes cannot sit together. High-value complaints are projected from 18–22% down to the 5–6% reviewed/repair rate, while cycle time is only +12 to 24 hours. The reviewed/repair medians are 96h and 120h against fast-path medians of 20h and 28h. Hitting the repair-path quality rate by inserting a short review is not supported. The 15–35% manual-inflow range is the prompt’s caveat for an auto-rule update, not the result of moving the whole 35% high-value book onto seven reviewers. The flow also adds a “dynamic evidence” path for the 22% unclear segment, then says evidence friction is left unresolved. The table’s “Zero Risk” on compliance conflicts with the redesign’s own gaming failure mode, and the two-person fraud desk is barely treated as the scarce R1 resource.

Redesign B invents a “2-minute” physical check while the only given gate effect is +6 to 18 hours. “Eliminates” repeated evidence and wrong shipments overstates a 30–45% repeat reduction and a gate that “could catch” mismatches. The −40 to −60h evidence saving converts a repeat-count estimate into hours of the 140h segment cycle time. R1 is largely untouched, yet compliance is scored “Low Risk.” Putting every claim, including Premium low-risk (median 8h, service-credit exposure past 24h), through that gate is the central unpriced trade-off. Resource pressure is “Low” in the table and a new warehouse bottleneck two sections later.

Redesign C’s split is Premium 32% vs Standard 46% and never places the 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment. “Under 24 hours” is the Premium low-risk credit cliff, not a feasible SLA for Premium high-value once fraud review and evidence completeness are real. Quality check already exists and inspectors are underused; cutting high-value repair rework from 12.5% to under 5% by adding another audit is unsupported. Catalog pricing is credited with removing the entire Manual review → Repair quote mean of 22h, which the text itself does not establish as pure price-wait. Failure magnitudes are made up: intake→manual mean 7.5h becomes “over 48 hours,” and starved Standard high-value cases “exceed 140 hours,” a number taken from a different segment.

The dominance row states the current process is dominated. On cycle time, resource pressure, and implementation it is not, and the prompt forbids treating Pareto dominance as knowable here. “Non-Dominated” for all three redesigns is likewise too clean; several cells are conditional. The experiment then sets success as a fast-path net-cost advantage above 25% versus reviewed paths, up from 9%. That threshold pulls the pilot back toward the cheap path the diagnosis just showed is inflated. Phase 1 also turns on A and B together, so a complaint drop cannot be attributed, and later phases stack C on top. Stop/go cuts such as &lt;1% wrong-part rate, &gt;90% mismatch catch, and &lt;8% complaints have no baseline in the log. The 22h “quote generation” metric mislabels a broader edge.

Correct local counts (72/28 split, 14.1% vs 3.3%, EUR 95/410, 2.4% escalation inflow, 320 of 390 abandonments, R2’s Premium low-risk exception) show the log was read. They do not repair contradictory cycle times, misapplied capacity ranges, absolute compliance and elimination claims, a missing segment, or a success rule that undoes the trade-off.
