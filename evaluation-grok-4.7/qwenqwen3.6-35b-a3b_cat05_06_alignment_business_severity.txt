The final answer covers all seven required headings and gets the central point right: raw alignment cost is not business risk. Zero-cost auto-approvals (CL-609, CL-610), the silent `tau` fit (CL-605), pay-before-approval (CL-604), and inflated cost on denials/withdrawals are interpreted in the right direction. The rest has enough concrete errors that it is not a reliable control assessment.

The per-case table does not match its own header. Every row has nine fields under a ten-column schema, so “Immediate action” is empty and the cost commentary sits in the wrong column. No case states an immediate action. CL-601 is typed as an event-label mismatch; those log moves are harmless UI telemetry under P5. CL-603’s duplicate notify is a transport retry, not telemetry. CL-610 is scored only as a P1 breach. A beneficiary-bank change one hour before payment is also a P2 trigger requiring Fraud Screen and Manual Approve, and Auto Approve was used. CL-604 asserts a P2 breach without saying which predicate fires: the claim is MEDIUM with no bank change, so the trigger is the EUR 90,000 amount. CL-608 marks P4 satisfied even though the only linkage is a mutable text field, which is too weak to treat secure-portal/CRM notification as retained linked evidence. CL-607 is given high confidence as out of scope, although P6 says that scope is disputed between the process owner and the model owner. Withdrawal (CL-612) is repeatedly placed “out of scope per P6”; P6 does not mention withdrawals. Severity 4 is given both to the EUR 480,000 missing-fraud case and to the EUR 4,500 / EUR 18,000 auto-approvals, with no application of the amount/exposure factor the framework itself lists. Levels 1–5 are never defined.

Pattern frequency is mishandled. The 74 same-message-ID retries are grouped with UI telemetry. The nine external-adjuster `M(Assess Loss)` cases, including two without a stable document-to-case identifier, never appear. Label mapping, external assessment, and CRM notification are collapsed into one pattern, and later “Pattern B/D/E” labels do not match that table. Section 4’s six hypothesis pairs are real, but CL-609 and CL-610 are given one shared pair that does not test bank-change data, and the two “mapping table” hypotheses for CL-611 are the same hypothesis. The supplied dispute on CL-607 is unused. Section 6 uses more than five cases and covers hidden moves, data conditions, scope, irreversibility, and weak external evidence. It does not address duplicated telemetry or activity granularity, and uniform cost-function design is only named. Remediation has the requested columns and includes model repair, correlation, and control redesign. It does not add data-aware conformance as a detective overlay, and the already-accepted CL-604 payment has no corrective or investigative action. One validation metric is “alignment cost drops to 0,” which reuses the measure Section 6 rejects.

The Python block is not executable. `case_id` has 73 entries and `activity`/`time` have 74 because CL-603 is short one case id, so the dataframe constructor fails. The log is not formatted to PM4Py’s case/activity/timestamp columns. `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` is called with the net first and the log last. The CL-611 trace includes `Assess Loss`, which is a model move, not a log event. The aggregation groups deviation labels only; it does not aggregate move patterns or risk levels.
