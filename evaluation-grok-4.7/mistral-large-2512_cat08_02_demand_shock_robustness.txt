The write-up has the required seven sections, scenario types, triggers, and trade-off columns, but it is not a reliable redesign. It invents loads, reverses several causal chains, and barely uses the logs it was given.

**Arithmetic and evidence.** Normal volume is 18,400 cases over 8 weeks, about 2,300 per week. Peak is 9,900 over 3 weeks, about 3,300 per week, roughly 1.43×, not “2×.” Scenario multipliers do not match the stated weekly counts: 2.2× is not 4,000; 1.8× is not 3,300; 1.5× is not 2,750; 2.0× is not 3,650. Queue jumps to 8h, 15h, 25h, 48h, 72h, and a 40% rework cut are not derived from the tables. Observed peaks already show pre-analytical recollection about 4% to 8%, assay repeats about 6.7% to 14.8%, batch-wait share about 4% to 13%, direct technical release about 74% to 50%, and specialist holds about 1.6% to 6.2%. Almost none of these frequencies or the timed edges (batch 9h to 22h, repeat return 10h to 26h, medical 8h to 24h, specialist 18h to 48h, recollection 42h to 60h) appear in the reasoning.

**Bottleneck physics.** Migration is mostly a repeated list, not a relieve-X-overloads-Y argument. In an M2 outage, completed assays fall, so technical validation does not see “increased volume from M1.” M2 is the higher-failure machine and repeats cluster after M2 and night shifts; assigning M1 a 30% higher failure rate contradicts that. Specialist absence does not make assay the final bottleneck via “fragmented priority rules.” Pre-analytical does not become the final bottleneck because an analyzer is down. Registration at 68% to 85% and a 0.7h to 2.8h queue is treated as a primary break point even though assay is at 98%, technical validation at 101%, medical queue at 22h, and specialist queue at 36h.

**Policy contradictions.** The core rule caps urgent work at 30% of assay capacity, then Scenario A still claims the 18h target is met while urgent mix is 35% and historical urgent p90 is already 19h normal and 33h peak. Section 3 sends most routine work to M2 and overflows only after 24h. Section 4, during an M2 failure, sends all routine work to M1 and raises batch size to 24, which removes the last flexibility on the only machine. Outsourcing is 30% above a 48h specialist queue in one place and 50% above 36h in another. Holding routine releases during specialist absence does not clear specialist review, and routine p90 is already 120h against a 72h target. Cross-training pulls technical validators toward accession while technical validation is the pool already over 100%, and the stated next risk (accession shortage) points the wrong way. A medical validator retrained into technical validation spends the scarce peak skill on the wrong station. Image scoring, a physician portal, a recollection coordinator, and courier bonuses are new capacity and technology, not evidence-based uses of the candidate list.

**Coverage and monitoring.** Repeat-after-failure is in every mix and consumes assay time twice, yet it has no policy. Urgent bypass falling from 74% to 58% is ignored, so urgent-first at preparation and assay is treated as the main lever while peak upstream edges already rival the 18h limit. Dashboard bands are mis-set: recollection red above 15% and correction red above 8% would stay green through the historical peak, and technical-validation queue is missing despite 101% utilization. Residual risks are the strongest part of the answer, but they do not repair the package.
