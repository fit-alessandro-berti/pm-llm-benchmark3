7.2

The analysis is specific, numerically careful, and well structured, but it is not nearly flawless. Several claims that bear on DFG interpretation and on where information actually lives are wrong or internally inconsistent.

The Order row counts check out: 42 rows, 31 events, 11 copied rows, and the shared set is exactly e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, and e27. Dispatch, Confirm Delivery, Pack Package, and Record Payment inflate to 6, 6, 6, and 6 rows against 3, 3, 4, and 4 events. Item (24/16), Package (12/10), Delivery (6/6), Invoice (7/7), and Payment (4/4) are right, as are the cited durations (I11 4h45m, I32 24h37m, P4 23h40m, D1 3h, D2 4h55m, D3 22h45m, V2 20h55m, Create→Close about 26h, Create→last confirmation 7h00m / 6h58m / 24h56m / 24h54m). Trace reconstructions for the payment–delivery interleaving on O2 (e22→e24→e25) and O3 (e24→e25→e27) are right, and the partial-settlement trap on V2 is the strongest part of the answer. Coverage gaps (Item 15, Invoice 24, Package 21, Delivery 25, Payment 27) and the order–order pairs (O1–O2, O1–O3, O2–O3, O3–O4) are also right.

These errors keep the score down.

The matrix says O1 and O3 both produce a Confirm Delivery→Confirm Delivery self-loop. O1 does (e23→e24). O3 does not: e25 sits between e24 and e27, which the same cell also states. That is a directly-follows mistake on the distortion the answer is trying to name. Section 3.8 then lists only e08→e09, e13→e14, and e16→e17 for O3, so the two sections disagree.

Section 7.4 says Create Order, Add Item, and Close Order are the activities where OC-DFG events, unique objects, and total objects coincide. Create Order and Close Order are 4/4/4. Add Item is 6/4/6. Unique objects are 4 because O1 and O3 each contribute two Add Item events. That is the divergence case the answer already uses, so the sensitivity criterion contradicts the earlier analysis.

Cross-order structure is repeatedly described as visible only in the object–object graph. It is already in the event–object table: e13 lists O2, O3, I21, I31, P3; e16 and e24 list O1, O2, O3, P2, P3, D2; e19 lists O2, O3, V2. Flattening drops that context when it keeps only case, activity, and time. The object–object qualifiers are necessary for `partially_settles` versus `completes_settlement`, which the answer handles correctly, but not for noticing consolidation. The same misattribution appears for Package, Delivery, Invoice, and distortion 4.

Distortion 2 says I41 waited to be packed with another order’s item. Add-to-pack for all six items is 45–57 minutes, and I41 is 55 minutes (09:25→10:20). Packing does not show a consolidation delay. The long delay is D3’s confirmation at 10:00 the next day.

The lexicographic removal test treats a vanished edge as non-behaviour. Under that rule e16 stays on O1 and e19 stays on O2, so O2 loses Dispatch→Issue Invoice even though both events really involve O2. Down-weighting by 1/k is the sound check; the removal interpretation overreaches.

`cluster_equivalent_ocel` is justified as a connected-component test that should return one Order cluster. The answer also records four distinct order variants. Behavioural equivalence and graph connectivity are different tests, and a single expected cluster contradicts the four-variant result the answer itself relies on.

Smaller faults: “three of four packages (P3, P4, and O1’s split)” does not count to three; I32 and I41 are activity-equivalent, not identical traces (e09 versus e10); the object–object component claim assumes undirected traversal without saying so; and Record Payment→Confirm Delivery is also direct on O1 (e21→e23) and O4 (e26→e27), so the bidirectional pattern is narrower than the edge in general.

Position B is still a defensible decision, the required portfolio and sensitivity checks are present, and the code names the requested artefacts and refuses to treat a picture as the semantics. The grade is for repeated, checkable mistakes in an otherwise unusually precise argument, not for a weak one.
