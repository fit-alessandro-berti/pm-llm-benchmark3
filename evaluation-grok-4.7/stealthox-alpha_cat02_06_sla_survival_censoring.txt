4.5

The delivered analysis is strong through the estimand definitions, case-level contributions, and competing-risk arithmetic, but it is an unfinished document and is not nearly flawless.

What holds up: time origin at `Submit`, the 72-hour horizon, and the split among already-observed non-resolution, competing exits, and administrative censoring are defined before estimation. E1–E4 are tied to different decisions. The four illustrative cases are mostly handled correctly, including S03 still in process at 72, S06 as a competing withdrawal rather than a censoring, S09 as a known E1 failure but censored at 92 for later resolution, and S08 as out of the internal process before 72. The T2 Aalen–Johansen figures are internally consistent and match the data: CIF of resolution by 72 hours is about 0.304, occupancy about 0.380, withdrawal about 0.140, rejection about 0.101, transfer about 0.075, and E1 about 0.696. The Kaplan–Meier contrast of about 0.361, the identification bounds E1 ∈ [0.500, 0.719] and E3 ∈ [0.281, 0.438], the resolved-only means (A ≈ 54.7 hours, n=6; B ≈ 45.6 hours, n=7), and the priority mix are also correct. Open cases are retained, and escalation/rework are correctly flagged as non-baseline.

Material defects in that same final text: `AutoCheck → Resolve` is not present in any prefix. “AutoCheck-second paths: 2/15 vs 13/17” is the count of paths that contain `AutoCheck`, not paths whose second activity is `AutoCheck` (cohort A has none). “13 events per cause” is false; only resolution has 13 events, while withdrawal, rejection, and transfer have 4, 3, and 3. Under T1, transferred cases leave the risk set; they do not “stay at risk.” The reported RMST of about 58.8 hours is the Kaplan–Meier integral that censors competing events, which sits badly beside the claim that this Kaplan–Meier overstates resolution, and the integral notation is garbled. “Lifetime CIFs (E2 ≈ 0.30)” collides with the section 1 definition: the late-resolution increment is about 0.30, while the resolution CIF through the last observed jump is about 0.61. E5 is defined but the four required cases are never scored on it. “9/32 competing and 14/32 censored” is easy to read as disjoint counts even though the 14 contains the 9.

The required document then stops mid-sentence in section 5. There is no finished immortal-time argument, no sensitivity section stating which conclusions can reverse, no operational translation with calibration or priority/cohort fairness checks, no limitations section covering the requested missing data, and no code block. The mandatory encoding of two estimands and three transfer scenarios, retention of open cases, stratified comparison, and a PM4Py prefix/variant construction are therefore absent. Those omissions are required deliverables, not optional extension.
