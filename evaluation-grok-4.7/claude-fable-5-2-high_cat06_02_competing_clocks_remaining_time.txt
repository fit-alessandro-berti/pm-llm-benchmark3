7.5

The forecast’s skeleton is sound: calendar anchors check out, the join is handled as a maximum, weekend incubation is separated from staffing, the temperature excursion is treated as a common cause, and the Monday chemistry-versus-QA offset is a real effect. The write-up still has enough internal inconsistencies and unsupported numerics that it is not nearly flawless.

The chemistry envelopes do not match the spill claims. Under the answer’s own priority order, B-417’s earliest Friday start is about 16:42, against a 1.5 h minimum service and a 18:00 close, so Friday completion probability is effectively zero. The “below 2%, and only if it jumped the urgent sample” line mixes in an out-of-model jump. On the FIFO path, the stated Monday start band of about 10:00–13:30 cannot produce the claimed Tuesday spill, while a slow draw (late Friday starts, services near 4.5 h) pushes B-417’s start toward about 15:30 and a Tuesday morning finish. The fast side is also tight: if the first queued sample finishes Friday, B-417 can start nearer 09:00 than 10:00. The “under 5%” spill and the 11:30–18:00 result band are therefore not the same distribution.

S5 is anchored too late. The decomposition starts the query clock at Monday 16:30 and gives Tuesday 10:00 as the earliest terminal time. The same answer’s prioritised review can end around 12:30–15:00. A 4 h query from that earlier end, plus a short re-review, can still release on Monday evening. S6’s earliest column allows an early Monday start; S5’s does not. That missing Monday slice also makes the cumulative “0.38–0.55 by Monday 20:45” hard to reconcile with S1 at 0.32–0.55 and a 15–20% Tuesday roll of S1.

The 15–20% smooth-path roll past 20:00 is high relative to the lognormal the answer itself fits (median 1.5 h, P80 3.5 h, mean about 2.5 h). A prioritised start near 13:00 needs an extreme service to miss 20:00; a FIFO start near 15:00–16:00 spills more often. A blend is closer to roughly 10% than to 15–20%. QA waits are in a plausible numeric range, but the text cites a 0.45/h net drain while FIFO wait depends on gross service capacity. With the stated mean service, 3–4 batches ahead is closer to 4–5 h than to the low end of 2.5–4.5 h. The central Monday 14:30–18:30 band is therefore a little early, though the “about half the chemistry gap is absorbed” conclusion still holds under a consistent mean-service wait.

Scenario probabilities are only loosely tied to the cumulative table. The conditional excursion split is a reasonable mixture, but the mixture is not shown, and the 30–60% “confirmed critical” rate is an extra assumption sitting under the 0.03–0.08 rejection share. Section 6’s “0.30–0.40 still finish with no query and no investigation” after a confirmed flag is the pessimistic edge of those bands; the centre is nearer 0.40–0.50, so the confirmed-flag P50 is pulled toward Tuesday a bit aggressively. S2’s “30–40% of FIFO reruns spill to Tuesday” has the same problem as the FIFO envelope: it is plausible only in the slow tail the central start times do not reach. Several “central” intervals, especially S2 and S4, run from a typical case out into the spill tail.

The driver ranking and the choice of the temperature-excursion flag as the main update are defensible, and the on-call readout is correctly treated as almost inert on the smooth path and material only on the confirmation path. Those are the right mechanisms. The grade is held down by the query-clock inconsistency, the FIFO envelope, the spill percentages, and the gap between scenario probabilities and the Monday cumulative band.
