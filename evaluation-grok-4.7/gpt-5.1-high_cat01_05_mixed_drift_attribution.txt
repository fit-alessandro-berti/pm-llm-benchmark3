7.3

The answer is complete, numerically careful on the published aggregates, and right about the main sequence: a March semantic break, a May rework shock aligned with Supplier Z, later queue growth with little Assess service change, an October volume and complexity surge, and a confounded December improvement. It is not nearly flawless. Several required judgments are over-precise, mechanistically blurred, or inconsistent with the monitoring and PM4Py claims.

The January–October cycle-time split assigns roughly 60–70% to queueing, 15–25% to rework, 10–20% to case mix, and under 10–15% to service. Those bands are not an additive partition of the +16.3 median hours. Rework and case mix operate partly by creating the extra queue time, so the shares can be counted twice. The only measured service delta is about 0.6 hours against 16.3, near 4%, and it is a July–August Assess figure applied to an October endpoint that also mixes supplier, staffing, priority, and renewal. The prompt allows ranges only when the aggregates support them; these percentages are finer than the data.

Senior Assessment is repeatedly treated as an effect of the 2026-07-01 priority rule. That rule is a queue-ordering change. The normalized senior edge only moves from 7 to 9 per 100 cases by July, then to 22 in October as high-complexity share moves from 26% to 41%. Label cardinality stays at 11, and missing-resource and duplicate rates do not jump, so October is a weak second logging change point. The prompt’s statement that senior assessment was recorded inconsistently is the better rival account, and the answer never normalizes senior edges by the high-complexity base (about 19% of complex cases in January versus about 54% in October). The second performance “change point,” July–October, is a span; the sharper step is May to June (p90 84 to 95), which is the staffing window. June is clearer in the code than in the prose.

Evidence scores are too high for co-movement. The March rename is directly visible (500 Eligibility Screen versus 140 Validate Eligibility; duplicate counts match the 4% rate) and is stronger identification than the supplier story, yet supplier is scored 93 and migration 92. April “continued tuning” is not in the timeline. Supplier Z’s share is already falling in August, before the 2026-09-10 recovery date used to explain July–September. High-complexity share rises from 21 to 29 before renewal season and is left mostly uninterpreted. Mapped fitness is described as a residual 2–5 point decline; January 0.92 to July 0.86 is 6 points.

The partial L1 is reproducible: January’s seven edges sum to 403, and the reported distances (0.01, 0.10, 0.15, 0.07, 0.16, 0.09) match absolute deviations over that sum. Calling the result an underestimate is only half right. Omitted edges are absent from the numerator, but the denominator is only the fragment, and the rework pair moves together, so the same loop is counted twice. March–April is persistence of the logging state more than a new drift interval.

The monitor set covers the requested families and avoids one global threshold, but the reference design fights the diagnosis. Rolling 3-, 6-, and 12-month baselines will absorb a level shift and then go quiet. A frozen pre-March and pre-May reference, plus a separate seasonal band, is what can separate recurrence from a new regime. The DFG alert at 0.12 for two consecutive months sits above the May distance the answer itself calls a clear structural shift. The ordering monitor uses a single canonical path and would treat intended Request Info loops as violations. The primary population drops renewal, Supplier Z, and high complexity, which is reasonable for detecting novel core drift, but rule 7 then looks for multi-metric failure in the population shielded from the mechanisms that actually moved the year.

Part A transcribes the tables correctly and implements the stated distance, gap, and month-to-month flags. Part B does not match the PM4Py manual closely enough for the required plan. `filter_time_range` is called with `start_time`, `end_time`, `include_start`, and `include_end`; the documented parameters are `dt1`, `dt2`, and `mode`. `format_dataframe` is given `resource_key`, which is not part of that function. The DECLARE import path under `declare.discovery` is wrong and is swallowed so the function returns `None`. Variant and temporal-profile helpers accept column names they never pass through. Monthly DFGs are cut by event timestamp, so cases are split across months. Duplicate `Open Case` removal, which the fitness discussion depends on, is not implemented in the normalized comparison.

Section 9 and the seven rules are otherwise the strongest control on confidence: they separate direct logging evidence from mediated queueing, name the falsifier on standard non-Z non-renewal cases, and keep supplier, capacity, priority, and season on different triggers. Those parts cannot offset the non-additive decomposition, the priority-versus-control-flow conflation, the self-absorbing baselines, and the API mismatches.
