The analysis is structured, covers all seven required sections, and reaches the right core judgments: authorization bypass and unscanned dispatch are control failures, manual authorization is a genuine substitute, throughput is not resilience when controls vanish, and post-close audit work is not real-time compliance. Most DFG counts and baseline multipliers check out, and the recovery design includes triggers, triage, product-tier controls, contemporaneous evidence, backlog rules, and a parallel reconciliation path.

It is not nearly flawless. Under partial recovery, non-fitting traces are 41% (59% fit), not 56%; that figure belongs to the manual phase. The hospital fast lane and “skip authorization” are mutually exclusive DFG branches, so “overlap with pattern C” is a misread, even though the fast lane itself has no authorization arc. Adding 1,400 reconciliations, 1,200 proof-of-delivery fixes, and 2,300 audit cases into “4,900 cases = 14%” treats possibly overlapping activities as unique orders, and the 200/400 “limbo” counts are missing arcs stated as facts. Pattern K is labeled true resilience although it is after-the-fact correction, and pattern L is left out of that classification entirely.

The indicator section invents a baseline pharmacy-to-hospital fill ratio of ~0.85 (baseline fill rates are not given) and sets “110% / 130% / 150% of baseline” targets under a fill-rate heading, which confuses fill rate with cycle time. Operational backlog age and a true throughput measure are thin. In the recovery model, Tiers 4–6 are mislabeled “Wholesale-bulk, Ambient,” which would allow phone proof of delivery for wholesale controlled and cold-chain orders despite the model’s own stricter rule. Unsupported specifics (30–60 minutes, 5–10% throughput) and a daily-volume comparison that ignores how few manual-phase cases actually reach END further reduce precision.
