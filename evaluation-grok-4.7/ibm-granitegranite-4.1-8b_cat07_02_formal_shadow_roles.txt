The write-up follows the required six-part outline and roughly names the right families (front office, technical, security, commercial, quality, approval, automation). It is not a reliable role analysis. Several core assignments contradict the counts, the shadow logic is inverted, the episode evidence that was meant to break naïve clustering is mostly unused, and the PM4Py code invents an API that hides the exact mistake the scenario warns against.

**Role table.** Technical work is mis-specified. Chen and Dina’s dominant activities are diagnose (260/255), configure (240/235), and implement (215/220). Quality check is 12 and 10. The table makes A11 core and drops A10. That swaps a main execution activity for a peripheral one. Erik’s stated peripherals, register and standard response, are both zero; his real secondary counts are implement (18), diagnose (16), configure (10), and KB update (9). Nora has no A3–A14 events at all, so “rare manual exceptions” is false, and frequency clustering would not paint her as a full-service agent. Quality work is not “>90%”: A11+A14 are about 75–78% of Ivan and Julia. Calling Ivan “nuanced” and Julia “uniform” fights the given 0.97 similarity and similar complexity. “10decimal events” is not an intelligible claim.

The clusters also repeat the naïve grouping the misclassification column describes. Temps (median complexity 0.18–0.19, no certifications, no implement/configure/quality, and the entitlement-note reopens) sit in the same role as Amira and Ben. Giorgio is listed as a commercial member even though about 68% of his events are front-office intake and closure (A1+A2+A4+A12 ≈ 560 of 820) and only about 20% are pricing plus negotiation. **Core versus peripheral.** The same errors are repeated and quantified badly. Front-office diagnosis and negotiation are attributed to temporary substitution in weeks 3–4 and 7–10. **Shadow and bridging.** Giorgio’s shadow direction is reversed. His formal title, home team, and volume are front office; the informal work is commercial, backed by a pricing certification and the week 3–4 cover. The answer says he steps into front office without authority. It never uses the decisive substitution result: pricing-exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes. Erik is a formal security specialist, not a shadow role; labeling him one misses that scarcity and Mateo’s filtering are the operational issue. Benefits and risks of shadow coverage are not actually weighed.

**Ambiguity.** The most ambiguous people, Giorgio and Mateo, are absent from the ambiguity table. Ben is floated as a validation lead on 415 versus 390 validations, despite lower complexity than Amira. Dina’s “config-assist” alternative is not supported by nearly equal configure counts (235 vs 240). Hana’s approval role is clear; the ambiguity belongs to Mateo’s temporary preliminary approvals. Deciding evidence mostly asks for future monitoring instead of tenure, complexity mix, security adjacency, rechecks, duration, and the 17 temp-related reopens already in the log.

**Code.** The pipeline shape matches the requested steps, but the parameters are not PM4Py’s. `format_dataframe` has no `variant='event_log'`. `discover_organizational_roles` does not accept `use_case_complexity` or `use_temporary_substitution`; it returns correlated activity groups, not a resource-to-role dict. `variant='cosine_normalized'` and `pm4py.save_network` are not real. The row loop cannot join that return value, and the commentary claims those fake flags make clustering respect complexity and substitution. That is the over-interpretation the section was supposed to avoid. Real work would be a separate resource-level join of median `case_complexity`, substitution flags, and formal title, then a deliberate refusal to read frequency clusters as authority.

That equates activity profile with risk exposure. Formalizing Giorgio and codifying Mateo to cut reapproval cycles pushes the other way from the duration doubling and Hana’s rechecks. The two cautions are directionally right and then contradicted by those recommendations. Shift cover (late Chen, Ivan, Lina), the bot inflating intake counts, and Erik/Fatima/Hana as single points of failure are not used.
