8.5

The diagnosis is accurate and well sequenced: April is treated as a landmark and feature-pipeline failure, June as a routing-mix change, August–September as overlapping parts delay, within-V3 risk change, and Partner Z intake, and October as label censoring. The August bound (at least \(0.93 \times 0.28 \approx 26\%\) versus a July ceiling near 21%) and the October range (about 0.088–0.538) are arithmetically correct and stop the immature 0.16 rate from being read as improvement. Falsifiers, stratified tests, training quarantines, and the label-delay gate on retraining are all present and proportionate. The code normalizes the renamed diagnosis, deduplicates with an identity check, and separates calendar-event from case-start cohorts.

Points come off for taxonomy and unfinished measurement. June and the September route shift are placed under covariate and process drift even though the text itself says diagnosis-time inputs may be stable and that Partner Z accounts for much of the later V3 mix. The standardization stops at a four-point mix effect (15.8% to 19.9%) without the within-route remainder, and without stating that those weights already miss June’s published 15% rate. September’s 82% maturity never receives the same bound as August, so the 0.31 versus 0.21 gap is left more ambiguous than the arithmetic allows. In the sketch, temporal features are taken from month-sliced events, `filter_time_range` is given timezone-naive strings against UTC timestamps, and variant output is not reduced to counts with an open-case denominator.
