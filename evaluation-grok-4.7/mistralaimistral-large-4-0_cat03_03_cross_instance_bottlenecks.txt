6.4

The write-up covers every required section, uses concrete object IDs and timestamps, keeps machine capacity off the top of the ranking, and repeatedly labels counterfactuals as non-causal. The lot-to-job linkage problem and the J2 override are handled carefully. Those strengths do not offset several quantitative and structural errors.

The mandated waits for J1 (3 h 55 min) and J2 (3 h 40 min) match the evidence, and 3 h 55 min + 3 h 40 min + 10 min = 7 h 45 min is arithmetically right as a sum of job-time. That sum is concurrent waiting presented as one total, and the zero-wait counterfactual assumes all three jobs can be cured at their own ready times on one oven. The FIX-A figure is weaker. J4’s arrival-to-start gap is 11:45 − 09:50 = 1 h 55 min, but CNC-2 is documented idle only from 10:05 to 10:47 (42 min), and the machine summary’s longest CNC-2 gap is 48 min. The interval 10:47–11:40 is therefore not established as tool wait. Treating 1 h 55 min as the delay associated with FIX-A overstates what the idle evidence can support. The 5 min from release at 11:40 to start at 11:45 is the only fully observed post-release lag.

The S1 arithmetic 19:25 − 13:30 = 5 h 55 min is right and is the appropriate consolidation wait. The further “excess” of 4 h 49 min is not. It subtracts the shipment-flattened 0.8 h + 0.3 h median, which the case itself defines as `Create Shipment → Load → Dispatch`, a path that does not measure how long an already packed job waits for partners. A baseline anchored to S1’s own last-pack lag (19:25 − 19:10 = 15 min), or to the 25/35 min S2/S3 post-pack lags, would be the consistent counterfactual.

The CAL-3 partition does not add up. 16:05 to 17:25 is 1 h 20 min, while 25 + 45 + 5 = 75 min. The missing slice is 16:05–16:10, before J5’s test. Folding 17:20–17:25 into the CAL-3 delay also assigns a post-recalibration gap to the tool. The 45 min recalibration is the clean figure; 50 min is only defensible as the TEST-1 idle window 16:35–17:25.

Two machine facts are left in conflict. OVEN-1 is idle from 12:55 to 14:50 (1 h 55 min) while its longest recorded event gap is 73 min. TEST-1 is idle from 16:35 to 17:25 (50 min) while its longest gap is 39 min. Those pairs cannot both be uninterrupted event gaps. The same tension sits behind “no job is more than 1.8 SD above the mean” and J1’s 5 h 55 min `Pack → Dispatch` against a 1.1 h median: with n = 6, that edge should move total duration unless the SD is inflated by shared delay or the duration statistic excludes the wait. The text states both facts and does not reconcile them.

The ranking table has the right candidate set and a justified low rank for machine capacity. It is less clean as a partition. RACK-7 is a concurrent constraint inside batch synchronization, a shared-tool case, and its own rank-5 mechanism. For B2 the binding constraint is J5’s 14:40 readiness: RACK-7 is free at 14:35, so the rack does not set the 14:50 start. Intervention 1 still claims that batch splitting would remove J4’s full 1 h 40 min. A singleton J4 batch still waits on RACK-7 until 14:35, so the avoidable slice is on the order of 15 min, not 1 h 40 min. L8, C1, C3, J1, and J3 are listed as affected by lot-release coupling even though the only quarantine/release pair is L7 at 10:20 and 13:50.

The coupling graph is missing its main cascade. The misaligned cliques are batches {J1, J2, J3} and {J4, J5}, shipments {J1, J4}, {J2, J3}, and {J5, J6}, lot L7 on {J2, J5}, and FIX-A on {J3, J4}. The operational chain is J3 holding FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40, J4 starting CNC-2 at 11:45 and becoming batch-ready at 13:10, B2 starting at 14:50, J4 packing at 19:10, and S1 holding J1 from 13:30 until 19:25. The narratives keep those links in separate stories. Shipment consolidation is therefore ranked as if it were independent, while the evidence-against cell only says J4’s late pack is upstream. Motif 4 is a two-type edge; the other motifs do meet the three-type bar. Narrative 1 files “B1 waits for all three jobs” as policy when that rule is inferred.

Section 6 fills the required fields and includes synchronization, tools, shipment policy, and logging. The spillovers are local harms. They omit the cross-mechanism effects that matter here: releasing J4 earlier at CNC-2 or at B2 shortens J1’s S1 hold; shortening B1 does little for J1’s customer wait once J1 is already packed at 13:30; a logging change can redirect the intervention toward a recorded wait and away from the binding one.

The Python block names every required function and correctly refuses to read waiting from machine gaps alone. The frequency comparison does not work. `ocel_objects_interactions_summary` returns nested type-to-type counts, not a frame with `source_type` and `target_type`. `discover_objects_graph` is not something `.items()` turns into `(edge, {frequency})` pairs. Concatenating those series aligns nothing, so the two metrics are not actually compared. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a three-tuple, so the two-way unpack fails. The ready-to-batch calculation is a comment, and a `concept:name` filter for “Ready” has no such activity in the evidence.

Falsifiers 1, 2, 4, and 7 can genuinely weaken batch synchronization. Falsifier 5 cannot: a later inspection wait does not explain the pre-cure gap. The extra data list is specific enough to separate tools, lots, batch rules, and shipment rules.

Checklist compliance is high. Time-account consistency, feasible counterfactuals, and the cross-clique cascade are not.
