7.5

The primary estimand is coherent: 90-day default only after activation, on a mature official feed, with rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases left unlabeled. The 48,000-case partition, the refusal to code non-activation as \(D=0\), the 125-day gate against early-default verification bias, the leakage table, and the three deployment feedback loops are all sound. The score is held down by errors in the sensitivity, the validation link to the code, and several concrete slips.

The survival sensitivity is not a valid counterpart of \(\eta\). Official `default_90d` arrives 7–35 days after day 90, so it is not a continuously observed failure time. Setting censoring to \(\min(90, T_{\mathrm{asof}}-T^{\mathrm{act}})\) for in-grace cases with no feed yet treats them as survived through day 90. That is the same early-negative bias the complete-case rule is written to avoid, and the code comment repeats it by putting `feed_delayed` on the as-of censoring clock. Administrative censoring before day 90 has the same problem if the event is the official flag rather than a timely `Default Recorded` event. \(P(T\le 90\mid A=1,x)\) is also a wider population than \(\eta\), which further conditions on the feed having arrived.

Section 1 puts “label mature at \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\)” inside the estimand, then describes that quantity as the default risk of accounts the policy booked. Maturity at a fixed extract date selects on activation time and on feed arrival. Calendar-mature complete cases are an estimator for \(P(D=1\mid X_\tau=x,A=1)\) only under a missingness assumption the answer elsewhere rejects for partner feeds. The Manski bounds are the right tool, but they repair a different estimand than the one written in section 1.

The expanding as-of scheme in section 7 is the right backtest. The skeleton is not that scheme. Folds are cut on case start, while `label_mature` is computed at 15 February 2026, so a training case can contribute a label that was unknowable at the start of validation. A long case can start before 1 February 2025, reach first Credit Review after 1 July 2025, and still enter the training fold. That puts post-policy prefixes into the pre-policy fit, which is the leakage this split was meant to stop. The late “evaluable” slice is also the short cycle-time slice (activation early enough to finish the grace), and that selection is never stated. The queue/random-split paragraph confuses dependence among same-week cases with lookahead; a \(\tau\)-measurable queue load does not become leaked merely because the case split is random.

Two narrower mistakes matter because they are stated as facts. The pre-activation prefix does not make \(A=1\) known: contract signing can still fail, so training on eventual activation while scoring before activation is still a selected association. In the feature code, resource experience is taken from the full log at the prefix end timestamp via a max across all events at that timestamp, so a same-timestamp Accept/Activate event that was removed from the prefix can still supply the resource count. Status flags also have no state for “feed already in, grace not closed,” so those held-out activations disappear from the cohort tally exactly in the backtests where the grace rule binds.

Smaller gaps: still-open applications are called ordinary right censoring of default even though the default clock has not started; policy era is pinned to first Credit Review, so a pre-July review decided after the threshold change is mis-tagged; the activity crosswalk and unmapped-name audit described in the text are not in the leakage checks. Date arithmetic, the Brier benchmark \(0.125\times 0.875=0.109\), PM4Py usage on truncated prefixes, and the decision-use boundary are accurate.
