6.5

The diagnoses for G1, G3, G4, G6, G7, and the logging-versus-physical distinction are largely sound, and the G6 wait/service mechanism is right (mean recorded wait is about 103 minutes, with every duration at 0). The write-up still has factual, logical, and specification failures that a strict reading cannot treat as polish.

**Diagnosis and consistency.** G5 puts “concurrent work or multitasking” ahead of the shared account even though `analyst_pool` is given as a shared login for up to three people. The 13:12–13:20 four-way overlap is a real conflict with that cap, so “mild” understates it. G6’s second label, queue accumulation after capacity loss, fits a recovery-and-service pattern like G8; G6 is a multi-hour arrival spread collapsed to one zero-duration bulk write. Several `strongest_counterevidence` cells argue against a rival (G1, G2) rather than against the chosen top label. G2 is scored 88 and left out of the validation set while §3 still leaves parallel service (8/14/21/27 min) and sequential touch time (about 8, 6, 7, 6 min) both open. G4 at 95 treats contiguous single-server FIFO, with every arrival already in before 12:05, as settled “batching.” G8’s sequential chain is clear; calling the safety-check gap “genuine” capacity loss outruns the different-clocks caveat.

**Signatures.** The matrix is usable, but the coarsening row is wrong on its own terms. Displayed times are already identical, so a smaller `merge_distance` still sees distance 0. What weakens G7 is interval censoring at 15-minute precision, which is a different operation. “Artificial” overlap for a shared account also blurs real multi-person work with a false overlap.

**§5 error.** The second “same pattern” example says G3 and G6 are identical completions with staggered starts. G6 starts are all 17:00, the same instant as completion, so PM4Py would class G6 with simultaneous/zero-duration batches and G3 with end batching. The claim that G6’s duration “remains zero even under censoring” is also wrong: 1-minute granularity makes true service an interval inside the bin, and the bulk form is a typed equality, not proof that work lasted 0 seconds. Assumptions such as ±30 s rounding are not labeled.

**Code versus prose.** The sweep sorts starts before ends at the same timestamp, so exact adjacency (G4, G8) is counted as `max_conc=2`, and G6’s identical instants become `max_conc=7` and `adj_rate=1`. The hardcoded summary then asserts `max_conc=1`. That summary is not computed from `discover_batches`. The documented call takes a `parameters` dict, so `merge_distance=` / `min_batch_size=` as keywords is likely a `TypeError`. The 0-vs-60 s and 2-vs-3 sweeps would not split these clusters anyway, because every intra-cluster gap is already ≤0 and every cluster has at least four cases. Overlap rate and timestamp-granularity indicators are required and absent; `precision` is never used. Conformance bias and throughput, required in the analytical task, are essentially only the G6 throughput remark.

**What holds.** Fixture size plus 1-second PLC equality for G1, the begin-group control for G2, folder submit for G3, scanner adjacency for G4, the rounding rule and negative waits for G7 (four of five arrivals fall after 14:00; finer `recorded_at` steps by one minute), and exclusion of G6 from ordinary queue math are the right conclusions. The redesign covers the requested instrumentation and is prioritized coherently. Those strengths are why this stays in the 6s rather than failing; the G6 pattern mix-up, the censoring mistake, and a batch script that contradicts its own printed comparison keep it well short of a high score.
