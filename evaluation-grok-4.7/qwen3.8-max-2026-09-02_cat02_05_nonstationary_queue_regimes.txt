6.5

The final write-up is strong on structure, queueing judgment, and most regime arithmetic, but it is not close to flawless. A required pooled-versus-segmented comparison contains a large, repeated p90 error, and two other consistency problems keep the score well below the top of the scale.

The p90 error is the main defect. Primary-regime p90s are right, and the low-complex window (through 2026-04-19) is right at 53.6 minutes. The later aggregates are not. The correct unweighted means are about 67.1 minutes pooled (not 58.6), 66.6 on weekdays (not 54.7), and 80.5 in the high-complex period (not 63.5). Section 3 repeats 63.5, and section 4 turns it into “p90 rises … about +18%.” The actual increase is about +50%. That changes the service-tail story and makes weekends look much heavier-tailed than weekdays; weekday and weekend p90 means are in fact almost the same (66.6 vs 68.3).

Little’s Law is otherwise handled carefully: completions are the throughput proxy, medians are not used as flow time, units and carry-over are stated, and outage/backlog-building windows are rejected. The verdicts are not consistent with that standard. Pre-intervention high-complex weekdays are “not defensible” at about +13 cases/day, while post-outage recovery is only “approximate only” at about −15 cases/day on a similar WIP. The larger boundary break gets the more lenient label.

The code matches the primary regimes, hourly Fano/CV, and the main Little’s Law windows, and it correctly refuses to treat the daily table as an event log. It does not reproduce the section 3 alternative: the prose averages 2026-04-06–19 including the outage, while the code splits 2026-04-15 out, so the printed low-complex means will not match the text. Commented PM4Py calls are illustrative rather than reliable API usage; that is a smaller issue because they are not executed.

What remains is genuinely good: labeled assumptions, weekday/weekend and mix separation, outage not treated as high throughput, burstiness versus compositional overdispersion, lag dependence, and a pooled staffing rule that is unsafe once regimes are split. Those strengths do not offset the wrong tail comparison.
