8.4

The design is strong on the hard constraints: non-stationary clustered arrivals, shared queues, architect bursts, latent objection episodes, inspection bundles, calendars, and a hard split between work content and waiting. It refuses to treat objections as observed, refuses to stack historical waits on top of simulated queues, and does not claim an optimum. PM4Py use is mostly conventional and correctly separated from the DES.

Points come off for concrete gaps. “Two clocks” is followed by three. S4’s “D3/D5” collides with intervention IDs, and S2’s “fire capacity 20%” does not say whether that is remaining capacity or a cut. “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is implemented as headcount 2 on that calendar, without stating whether FTE means weekly labor or people present; the same ambiguity hits the four inspectors. Environmental capacity is argued as reserved blocks, but the skeleton stores `available_fraction=0.60` on a calendar whose `is_open` ignores it, so the template can be read as the multiplier the text rejects. Scheduler contention is asserted from a 3-day wait that may be join or notice delay, while no scheduler pool is actually templated. Public-notice events are single timestamps against a 14-day minimum, and that point-versus-interval problem is never named. The annual completion/arrival ≥ 0.99 rule is phase-biased for long cases. Simple-versus-general planner dispatch, case-owner stickiness (visible in the excerpt), and the urgent-legal exception to council blackouts stay unspecified. Shift hours for fire and inspectors are invented in code and not listed as assumptions.
