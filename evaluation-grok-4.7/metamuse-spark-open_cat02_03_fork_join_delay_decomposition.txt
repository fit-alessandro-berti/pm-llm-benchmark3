7.5

The core fork-join analysis is correct and well structured. Case timings, critical branches (B 8/12, A 2/12, C 2/12), service/queue/sync means, and all three counterfactuals (15, 92.5, and 176 minutes) check out, including zero-benefit cases and the max-based warning against summing synchronization waits. Masking by downstream delay is treated with explicit optimistic and pessimistic bounds, and the limits section uses real idle gaps rather than generic caveats.

The score is held down by a material process error in the redesign: B is described as a single overloaded server, but the same timestamps show up to four overlapping B intervals, which falsifies that claim and conflicts with the later statement that a 37.5-minute B service is not the throughput bottleneck at a 15-minute arrival spacing. Secondary figures are also unsupported or wrong: mean fork-join time is 61.0 minutes, not 60.6, so the post-change mean is about 53.3 rather than 52.9; the claimed flow-time 90th percentile of ~112, the “realistic” 3–7 minute saving, and the jump to ~80% final utilization are not derived. Required formulas are weakened by missing operators, and the 30-minute F1 idle gap overstates policy-caused idle by about 10 minutes before the next join-ready time.
