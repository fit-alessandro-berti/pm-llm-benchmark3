3.5

The write-up keeps the required seven headings, labels assumptions, and gets a few early completed cases right (P01–P04, P06, and parts of P10, P14, P20, and P21). Those strengths do not carry the reconstruction. The mandatory cases and the station totals are numerically unreliable, the uncertainty scenarios point the wrong way, and the required code does not implement the analysis.

**Case table vs the log.** P05’s S1 start is observed at 09:00 (service 25 min, wait 35 min); only the S2 start is missing. The answer treats the S1 start as missing and places it at 08:55. P07’s S2 start is observed at 10:20 (wait 20 min, service 40 min); the note sets that start at 10:40 while the table still shows a 40 min service, which cannot both be true. P11’s S1 start is observed at 10:40 (wait 75 min, service 25 min). The imputed S2 start of 11:55 on C2 overlaps P08 (C2, 11:15–12:00) and ends at the observed 12:25 only if service is 30 min through that overlap. A feasible C2 window is after 12:00 and before P13 at 12:30.

Fully observed values are also off: P08 S2 service is 45 min, not 40; P09 S2 wait is 25 min, not 65; P10 S2 wait is 50 min, not 60; P12 and P13 S2 services are 45 and 50 min, not 30. Later S1 waits are short: P16 is 100 min, not 85; P17 is 95, not 85; P18 is 100, not 80; P19 is 100, not 95. P17’s S2 wait is 13:55−12:50 = 65 min; the 5 min figure is elapsed open service, placed in the wait column. P16, P18–P20, and P22 are left as “–” although each has a right-censored wait through 14:00 (for example P16 has already waited 85 min for S2, P22 110 min for S1). Completed-case total sojourn is defined and then never computed.

**Station metrics and Little’s law.** The 08:00–10:00 block does not match the log or the case table. Arrivals in that window are not “6 (P01–P06)”; S1 completions by 10:00 are P01–P06 (P07 ends at 10:00), and S2 completions include P03 at 09:45. A mean S1 wait of 7 min is not the mean of the waits in the case table. Later blocks assign cases such as P07 (arrived 08:45) to 10:00–12:00 and mix S1 completions into S2 rows. Utilization percentages and WIP peaks are not tied to a busy-time or slot calculation. The reported Little’s-law gaps of about 4 min and 6 min are not computed anywhere, and the difference \(L-\lambda W\) is in cases, not minutes.

**Scenarios.** Taking starts later and ends earlier lengthens waiting; the write-up defines that choice as the optimistic, minimum-wait scenario and the opposite choice as maximum-wait. Section 6 then asserts large drops in waiting and WIP under that “optimistic” rule without recomputing the affected cases. Bounds are mostly a flat ±5 min around the central point, including where the central point is outside the feasible resource window.

**Code.** The block collapses MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same null. The embedded P05 row changes the observed 09:00 S1 start into MISSING. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, or `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, and it neither calls `convert_log_to_time_intervals` nor explains a safer pandas substitute. The interval builder contains invalid syntax, WIP is explicitly skipped, and the script would not run (`StringIO` import/path, broken conditional, unbound variables).

The maintenance remark is directionally useful: C2 is down only from 10:55 to 11:10, and P08’s 11:15 C2 start is the clearest footprint. The load story is still incomplete. Arrivals are on the order of five cases per hour against two servers with roughly half-hour services, so both stations are over capacity for much of the morning; the 15-minute outage is a short extra constraint, not a substitute for that capacity comparison.
