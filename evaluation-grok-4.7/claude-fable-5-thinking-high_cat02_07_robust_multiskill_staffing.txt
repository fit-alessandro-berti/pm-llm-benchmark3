6.5

The write-up is a real capacity-and-policy analysis: labeled assumptions, auditable block loads, three operational dispatch rules, scenario-wise tradeoffs, a monitoring design, and a guarded pilot. It is not reliable enough to carry the recommendation it makes. Several mechanisms the argument depends on are specified one way and implemented another, and the policy that is finally recommended is not the policy whose numbers are shown.

**Capacity arithmetic is mostly right, with a few concrete slips.** Mixture means (6.9 / 26.3 / 32.8 / 38.5 / 15.7) and the base block loads check out, including day totals of about 2,720 base-minutes against 2,490 net rostered minutes. The evening cliff, the post-18:00 mechanical coverage hole (Emil has no Mechanical skill), morning software limited to Chen (1.25) and Farah (1.40), and electrical concentration on Chen and Deepa are the right structural facts. The 16:00–18:00 parenthetical is wrong: that block is Deepa for 120 minutes and Emil for 90 (break 16:00–16:30), not “Deepa 90 + Emil 120”; the 210-minute total is accidentally correct. U4’s mechanical mixture mean is 29.6, not 30.6, so the “~13–16%” diagnostic inflation and the 14:00–16:00 rho moving to “~1.3” (recomputed diagnostic uplift lands near 1.24) are loose. The claim that realistic utilization is 1.15–1.25 is asserted from “every multiplier is ≥ 1” without a skill-weighted multiplier. Treating U2’s alternative reading (E/ES counts double, so afternoon volume rises) as covered by U1 is not valid: U1 is a morning all-skill surge, and a doubled afternoon electrical volume is a different bottleneck.

**The simulation does not implement the rules in Sections 1 and 3.** Victim choice sorts to the lowest-priority diagnostic with the least remaining time; A8 says the most remaining. Remaining work is `(finish − now) / multiplier`, which still contains leftover handover and any scheduled break pause, then multiplied by 1.10, so the restart penalty is not “10% of remaining base service.” Resume charges handover and setup again because `last_res` / `last_family` update only on completion. `choose()` ranks on the drawn base time; A12 says the dispatcher uses expected cost. HYBRID’s text parallelizes P1 whenever two resources can be found; the code uses the same idle-now test as P2. Parallel mode is latched if two qualified people are idle at intake completion, including the reserved anchor, and the 8-minute coordination is still charged if those branches never overlap. The extra 5-minute mechanical↔electrical/software team move is not applied to parallel branches, which biases the parallel/sequential comparison. Preemption can interrupt a victim and then fail the overtime check, leaving the victim penalized with no P1 started. Duplicate `concept:name` segments after preemption will distort `get_service_time` and the handover network. The handover-network parser assumes `.connections` or `dict(graph)` edge weights; a PM4Py 2.7 NetworkX graph will not yield the printed “top pairs.”

**Objectives and the recommendation are internally inconsistent.** O1’s P2 constraint has no tolerance, and O2’s lambdas are never set, so neither objective is evaluated. The >0.90 fragility indicator fires for HYBRID in every reported scenario (max utilization 0.94–0.99); the text then treats “≤ 0.99” as success. “Matches POOL’s worst P1 of 0.69” is false on the table’s own figures (0.75 vs 0.69). Differences under ~20 minutes are called noise, and the stated HYBRID mean-delay premium is ~15 minutes. Mean delay drops incompletes, and HYBRID leaves more incompletes in every scenario, so its delay is differentially censored. Raw handover minutes are not per completed case, which favors the policy that finishes less work. U3’s P1 gap (0.86 vs 0.85) is counted as a win.

The fallback is the operational heart of Section 5 and is absent from the code. “On-shift headcount ≤ 4” is the ordinary roster outside 10:00–16:00 (four people at 08:00–10:00 even with Chen present; two after 16:00). Applied as written, it turns reservation off for most of the base day, so Section 4 is not evidence about the recommended policy. The pilot sentence that P1 handling is identical across arms is incompatible with a reservation that exists to change P1 waiting.

What still holds without the decimals: the day is over-offered, 16:00–20:00 is not a routing problem, strict home queues cannot see bottleneck migration, and P1 protection has a real P2/throughput price. Those conclusions are stronger than the SLA table and the untested mode switch attached to them.
