The design covers the required eight sections and is directionally aware of seasonality, calendars, bundling, applicant delay, and objection proxies. It is not a sound, internally consistent digital-twin specification.

The arrival model asserts a Gamma renewal process, a non-homogeneous Poisson process, a base Poisson rate, and 24-hour architect clusters at once. Those are incompatible, and the log supports same-week bursts, not a 24-hour window. Routing is treated as known at submission from case type, district, and value, despite missing building-area and environmental-sensitivity data. Rework is set from variant share; that is a case mix, not a transition probability. Section 2 infers activity starts from the previous completion, which folds queue wait into service, while other passages sample a separate service distribution. Public-notice duration is both an independent mixture and a cross-case objection contagion. The statutory 14-day floor is never enforced.

Resource assumptions omit the Monday portal clerk, simple-renovation-only planners, the 40% non-permit environmental reservation, inspector field days, and council-week legal rules. The 5% sick-leave rate is invented.

The experiment table is not operational. D1, D5, D8, and D9 have no effect-size levels, so they cannot be simulated from the log. D9 has no mechanism once objections are not events. D6 is coded as removing one planner rather than reserving capacity. D1’s extra applicant effort is omitted. Interaction effects in D10, especially inspection policies that shrink D3 and D4, are not designed. Stress cases are generic and underspecified.

Warm-up seeks steady state in a seasonal system, then mixes an 18-month calibration sample with the simulation horizon. Normal mean intervals do not cover the p90 and SLA metrics under heavy tails, and the wait/service split is not a sensitivity factor. Min/max across 30 runs is not an interval.

Validation confuses token replay with trace-driven simulation, sets an arbitrary 90% variant target, and checks planners against an invented 85% utilization. Counterfactual policies are not distinguished from a validatable baseline. Holdout of the last three months ignores seasonality.

Decision rules invent statutory 60- and 120-day SLAs, apply one 70–85% utilization band to every pool, and omit rework, backlog, and architect fairness. The final selection rule drops tail and robustness tests and asks combination policies to be additive.

The skeleton avoids `pm4py.read_csv` and names the right columns, but `format_dataframe` is called with `activity_id` and `timestamp` instead of `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`. It never builds a performance DFG or service/waiting columns, and the arrival rate walks the log incorrectly. Cited helpers such as `pm4py.log_statistics.get_case_timestamps`, `pm4py.statistics.transition_occurrence`, and `pm4py.performance.analysis.get_performance` are not PM4Py’s API. The process-tree string is not parsed, and its real limits—rework labels absent from the log, no data guards, no appeal or planning rework, no resources or cross-case state—are not explained.
