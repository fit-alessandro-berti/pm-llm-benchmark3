The answer meets every required section and stays unusually close to the log: segments, route modes, CNC_A versus CNC_B, heat-treatment waits, partial shipments, SP1/SP2/Q1, and the stated batch, air, consolidation, packaging, and obsolescence figures are used in the right places. The boundary is explicit, air and dedicated vans are not treated as uniformly unjustified, and the recommendation protects Service-contract and true downtime service while keeping an alternate route. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The effects table is logically unreliable. The legend defines ↑ as an increase, and the late-delivery and rework columns correctly use ↓ for lower risk. The resilience-risk column does not. Scheduler brittleness and CNC_A concentration are marked ↓, while clearer escalation, fewer emergency remakes, and carrier diversification are marked ↑. Those arrows cannot be repaired by flipping the column, because other cells use ↑ for a genuine risk increase. A required decision table that reverses its own scale on the resilience column is a material flaw.

Several central estimates also fight the same answer’s rebound analysis. Capped batching is credited with the full uncapped CNC_A drop from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh/item. Partial-shipment reduction is given an unqualified drop in cycle time, even though the later hold-to-complete risk increases waiting and cancellations. Q1 enforcement is leaned toward lower material use even though reinforced packaging doubles material mass. CNC_B is described in the source as faster, but the machine-shift row shows cycle time flat to down and does not price in lost speed outside the rework path. Shared emergency lanes are said to keep speed, with reusable-crate backhaul added without log evidence.

Other gaps are smaller but real. R1 is availability of an alternate route during disruption; historical mode variety does not measure that. Rushed inspection is tied to expedite shortening the check, while the stated mechanism is inspection-cell queues. Priority picking can displace Regular orders, and avoiding partials by expedited replenishment can move emissions upstream; neither displacement is in the risk set. The package also implements all twelve candidates. Phasing helps, but interactions among batch caps, the CNC_A shift, inspection load, and promise changes are not estimated.

The prose recommendation is more careful than the table, and the validation plan is concrete. The grade is held down by the broken risk scale and the repeated one-sided effect directions.
