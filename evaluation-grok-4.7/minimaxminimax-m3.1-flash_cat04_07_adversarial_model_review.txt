7.5

The review is complete, well structured, and strong on the actual decision: use-case separation, probe-level behavior of both models, refusal to collapse metrics into one score, a third design that keeps mandatory checks mandatory and missing attributes `UNDETERMINED`, and a real dissent. It is not nearly flawless.

The required `model_third` code does not implement the stated semantics. `OperatorPOWL.LOOP` is given one child; PM4Py’s loop operator requires exactly two, so this construction fails. `credit_2` and `tech_2` are reused across XOR branches, which is not a valid POWL tree. The formal description says correction is `0..n`, but a loop body is not optional, so the net would not accept the legal no-correction traces the audit table marks as accepted. `policy_check` calls `_affected_after`, which is never defined.

Alignment precision is misread. `precision_alignments` is an escaping-edges measure. Model S at 0.584 is largely the metric detecting real optionality, not “alignment determinism,” and it is relevant to compliance even though a positive-only log still cannot prove policy soundness.

The validation suite contradicts the design. `N_expedite_after_appr` is a legal expedite-before-approval trace, and `N_over_recheck` is the both-checks recheck shape the third model itself allows. The hard gate then rejects the proposed model for accepting traces that were mislabeled. Several APIs are not real PM4Py (`fitness_guava`, `pm4py.alignment(..., return_cost=True)`, `OperatorPOYL`). Supplier Review is also forced after the checks without a policy basis. The dissent’s “25%” is not in the evidence.
