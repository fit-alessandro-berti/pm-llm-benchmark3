The main verdicts are right: A and B are not visibly trace-equivalent, not P1-equivalent, and not P3-equivalent, and they match only under the P2 abstraction. Loop scope, `#CR = #AR` in A, `#CR = 1` in B, the four separating traces, and the reading of `*(P, Q)` as `P(QP)*` are correct, and T1–T5 really do witness both inclusions inside the P3 bound. The simplification preserves `α₂` and is honestly marked as losing P1 and P3.

The write-up is not consistent with its own shuffle semantics. Lemma B puts the single Check Revision in shuffle with `AR(ε|AP)(RW AR(ε|AP))*`, so Check can sit between Analyze and Approve (`Open, AR, CR, AP, Close` is in B only). Invariant I-B2 and the structural table nevertheless say Approve is directly after its Analyze. That claim is false and contradicts the lemma.

The footprint prediction is also wrong. In A, both `CR` directly followed by `RW` (`AR, CR, RW`) and `RW` directly followed by `CR` (the next segment may start with Check) occur, so the relation is parallel, not `CR → RW`. B has the same pair of direct successions. Equality of footprints would still not give trace equivalence, but the expected difference used to motivate the check is not there.

The stochastic code does not implement the distribution it declares. `random_playout` returns a set, so `empirical` assigns equal mass to distinct sampled words and drops the uniform-enabled frequencies. Joining traces into comma-separated strings is also the wrong key type for `compute_emd`, which expects tuples. A low EMD from that code would be hard to interpret.

`S₂` is smaller than both originals (8 nodes versus 12, depth 3 versus 4 and 5), but it is not the smallest P2 model. `->(Open, *(AR, RW), CR, Close)` has 7 nodes, depth 2, and the same `α₂` image. “Smallest” and “replace neither original, retire B” overreach: B is the right description of one concurrent check, just not of P1.

Lesser problems: several proof lines lose the membership and inequality symbols, so the invariant arguments are not readable as written; “the code confirms” is stated for a program that is not executed; Petri-net simplicity is left uncompared; and strict P1 builds “any order of AR and CR, AP last, exactly one AR per segment” into compliance without separating what the business sentence requires from what Model A happens to allow. The P1/P3 separations survive those choices because T1, T3, and T5 do not depend on the AP-last rule.
