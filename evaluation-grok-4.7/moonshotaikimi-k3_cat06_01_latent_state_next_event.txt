The answer is process-aware and well structured: states match the normative model, C6-A and C6-B are split by severity, injury, lot linkage, and duplicate evidence rather than by the shared prefix, fast close and early closure are blocked where the rules require it, and missing completions are not treated as proof that work did not happen. Queue load, the 16:00 notification batch, and migration delay are used concretely, and `process_enabled: false` is applied to outcomes that still need a predecessor.

The probabilities are less disciplined than the narrative. For C6-D, regulatory assessment was opened at 12:40 and typical completion is 2–14 h after assignment, so at 14:00 that completion is a weak candidate for the immediate next log event, yet it is given 0.35–0.60 and ranked ahead of a backfill that can arrive at once. The backfill itself is named Evidence Received, although a delayed Customer Evidence Requested would ordinarily surface first. The same pattern appears elsewhere: downstream events (Close — No Safety Issue, Disposition Recorded, a new evidence request, Supplier Analysis Completed) are given roughly 0.10–0.25 as the next logged event on normal feeds, which only works if a predecessor completion is hidden or out of order—more often than the stated 8–15% delay rate supports. C6-A’s midpoint mass is about 0.95, which crowds out the supplier and regulatory paths the case text itself treats as mandatory, and the calibration note’s claim of 0.8–0.9 midpoint totals does not match case A. The triage→technical-investigation DFG count is also used as if it showed concurrency after an evidence cycle, which that relation does not show. Supplier Analysis Completed is marked enabled for C6-C even though the complaints are on different lots and a shared component is unconfirmed.

These are calibration and enablement errors, not a failure to read the control flow. They are repeated enough that the result is not nearly flawless.
