The answer meets the required seven-section format and is grounded in the logs: mix shift, weekly critical-case and medical-validation loads, recollection and repeat loops, queue times, and the candidate-intervention conflicts are calculated and used correctly. It avoids a blanket capacity increase, does not optimize the historical average, separates case types, and pairs each main lever with a downstream overload, a trigger, and a switch-off rule. The redesign-induced risk (smoother intake exposing pre-analytical and assay work) is explicit, and leading indicators are separated from lagging outcomes.

It is not nearly flawless. The medical-shift discussion is causally muddy: adding evening medical validation clears a downstream queue and can reveal technical validation or specialist review as the binding constraint, but it does not itself create more blocked technically validated work. Specialist review is also compressed into the same final step as medical validation in S1, although the log path is hold, then medical validation. “Peak normal p90” is a labeling slip, the Complex “green” band (under 150h) accepts a worse tail than the normal-period p90 of 118h without justifying that as the acceptability standard, and several trade-off cells stay vague (“may help,” “neutral to positive”) rather than stating the mechanism as sharply as the narrative does.
