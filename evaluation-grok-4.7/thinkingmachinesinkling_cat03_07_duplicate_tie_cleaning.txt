**6.9**

The design covers every required section and gets the main duplicate calls right: merge only the reconciled e03/e04 pair, keep e05 and e14, keep e06/e07 and e15 as separate abstraction levels, split the harmonized e08/e09 labels, and explain why a shared O1/V1 link cannot justify merging M1 with M2. Provenance fields, a non-mutating business timestamp, and ingestion time as a last-resort key are all present. That is not enough for a high score under strict checking.

The ordering policy overstates the evidence. Known facts fix e09 before e10 and e12 before e13, and place the ERP payment summary after the payment messages. They do not fix warehouse release before carrier acceptance, detailed pack before the ERP pack status, or invoice issuance before authorization; those pairs share a timestamp and should stay concurrent, with any serialization marked as deterministic. The write-up instead treats e08 → e09 → e10 as the only admissible order and treats e06 → e07 the same way. It also calls e13 and e14 concurrent, then says reversing them is inadmissible because of PSP `source_seq`. Those two claims cannot both stand. A deterministic total order is therefore presented as if it were a domain order.

The decision tree is not the policy actually applied. Confirmed same-physical-event pairs are defined as merge terminals, yet conservative handling retains e11 and e16. Suspected summary/detail pairs are defined as quarantine terminals, yet e06/e07 stay in the log with renamed activities. e11/e16 is as explicitly supported as e03/e04 (“same invoice issuance” on V1); moving that merge into the “aggressive” scenario needs a sharper rule than the tree gives.

The implementation misses required library use. `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` never appears. `pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates` is only commented out, so the aggressive scenario is not executed. The e11/e16 merge is “omitted for brevity.” The relation table is not rebuilt; integrity checking treats `objects.index` as the object-id set and assumes `related_objects` is already a list. `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` is correctly placed after sorting and on a copy, but the sensitivity section never measures how index deltas erase the real gap from 10:15 to next-day delivery.

Sensitivity coverage is broad, but several claims are wrong or too coarse. e04 contributes 3 E2O links, not a reduction of 4. P1 is the package, and the OTG sentence calls it a person as well as a package. The ingestion failure example says 10:01 would place e07 after 10:15 events; those clock values do the opposite. The real ingestion distortion is e16 (12:00:04) falling after e12–e14. The order-flattened DFG never separates legitimate Pick→Pick concurrency (I1 vs I2) from false rework caused by dual observations (e03/e04, e11/e16, e08/e09). The aggressive retained count stays a range of about 10–12 instead of a count under stated merge rules.

The audit schema and the eight required failure modes are complete and usable. The prose is also well past the 1,900–2,900 word aim once the code block is excluded, largely through repeated decisions. The result is a careful pipeline with the right instincts, undermined by unsupported precedence, an inconsistent tree, factual slips, and an incomplete PM4Py block.
