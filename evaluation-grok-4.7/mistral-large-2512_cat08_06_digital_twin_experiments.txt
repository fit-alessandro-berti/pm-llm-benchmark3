4.5

The write-up covers every required section and recognizes seasonality, calendars, case mix, and shared resources, but it is not a coherent digital twin. Several design choices contradict the process, the data limits, or each other, and the skeleton does not implement the model it describes.

The largest simulation-design failure is identification of delay. Section 2 proposes to recover waiting time with steady-state M/G/1 or M/G/c formulas and to set service time to `min(observed duration, a p95)`. With only completion timestamps, the gap between events is not service, and a stationary single-queue formula is not identified under calendars, peaks, batching, and parallel branches. Those waits must be outputs of contention. Plugging historical waits in as inputs would make queue-changing interventions (D3, D4, D6, D8) unevaluable. The code then ignores that plan and tries to generate waits, so the estimation plan and the engine describe different twins.

Missing objections are treated as observed. “Public-notice duration >30d implies objection occurred” turns an unobserved mixture into a label. The 30-day cut is arbitrary relative to the stated 14-day floor and 60-day objection tail. D9 and the objection-spike stress have no identified latent variable, only that proxy.

Assumptions cancel the interventions or contradict the resource model. “Architects don’t change submission behavior” and “case mix stays stable” leave D1 and D7 with nothing to change. “No dependencies between unrelated cases” is false once clerks, planners, inspectors, and legal officers are shared. “Rework probabilities are constant across case types” contradicts the variant table. District-wide shared objection status overstates the stated neighborhood effect. Inspectors batching “without setup time” is smuggled into the baseline. Prior violations, required for D2, are never given a missing-data treatment.

The seasonal arrival model misstates the given fact: peaks are 1.7 times January–February, but the code uses 1.7/0.8. Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, summer, and December factors are invented. Weekend keys are missing and will throw. Architect “bursts” are an independent 31% coin flip, not same-week clusters. Day-of-week multipliers are applied on top of weekly demand without normalizing, so mean volume drifts. `int(daily_demand)` makes arrivals nearly deterministic.

Process structure does not match the tree or the excerpt. The parallel block is implemented as a random serial choice. The environmental continuation looks up `requires_environmental`, not `requires_environment`. P001 is New residential and has Legal check; the router skips legal unless the type is Commercial or Complex variance. Decision drafting has no join state. Completeness, inspection, and appeal rework are collapsed into wrong return edges. Public notice is either a 14-day occupation or an unstaffed “system” activity, so the 60-day tail cannot appear. Cases are processed before their submission time.

Calendars are named and then not enforced. Availability is checked only at assignment; service is added in continuous minutes across nights, weekends, fire reviewers’ off days, council blackout weeks, and the 40% environmental reserve. The Monday portal clerk and the two simple-renovation-only planners never affect routing. Utilization divides cumulative minutes by minutes since midnight.

Experiment and decision rules are not operational. The matrix is On/Off with no effect sizes, no common random numbers, and no crossing of D1–D10 with the stresses. D10’s “synergy” is asserted; D2 removes the inspections D3 and D4 are meant to relieve, so antagonism is the more plausible interaction. Adopt thresholds conflict with the earlier targets (15% vs 20% median, 30% tail) and ignore sampling error, multiple comparisons, defect risk from skipped inspections, and cost of the extra inspector. SLA “within X days” leaves X undefined. A hard “p90 must not increase” would reject a fairness policy such as D6 on noise alone.

Warm-up and validation do not fit a seasonal twin. Welch’s method looks for steady state; this demand does not have one. Deleting 12 months can be conservative, but that is not what the pilot procedure estimates. One hundred unpaired replications do not give a CI for a policy difference. Gamma(shape=2) and Weibull(shape=5) are light-tailed and calibrated so the gamma mean equals the reported median, which is not the gamma median. Kolmogorov–Smirnov against distributions fit on the same log is not a validation test. Last-three-months holdout ignores season. Historical utilization cannot be the benchmark when starts are missing and calendars came from HR. Variant median and p90 targets in the brief are the natural acceptance criteria and are never used. Fitness and precision of a discovered tree are not simulation validation. A flattened completion log will make the AND-split look sequential in a DFG; that bias is never mentioned.

The PM4Py layer misses the requested API. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` exists and this string is the usual process-tree syntax; the skeleton claims no parser exists and returns `None`. `discover_variants` and `pm4py.create_resource_calendar` are not the library’s functions. A plain DFG is never built. Pandas sojourn and attribute helpers are called on an `EventLog`; sojourn time is a dict of inter-completion gaps and is stored as service time. `get_case_arrival_average` is an inter-arrival time, then printed and used as cases per day. Parameters from two illustrative cases are not distinguished from the 18-month log. `interventions` is stored and never read, so D1 is the baseline. `history[-1]` on a new case, calendar methods that are called but not defined, `self.calendar` used before assignment, and cycle time filtered on a status that is never set to completed make `main()` fail before any comparison. The closing notes say architect bundles, district bundles, and neighborhood effects are in the engine. They are not.

Score reflects full section coverage and a modular outline, against incorrect PM4Py claims, a non-identified delay model, objection data treated as observed, assumptions that nullify the policies, and a skeleton that neither runs nor matches its own design.
