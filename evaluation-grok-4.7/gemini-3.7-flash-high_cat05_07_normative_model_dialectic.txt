The answer is complete and often well judged, but it is not close to exact. Several errors sit in the places this task weights most: model semantics, authority and evidence discipline, and consistency between the stated burden and the classifications.

The case outcomes are mostly defensible. SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-705, SUP-707, SUP-708, SUP-713, and SUP-714 are classified on the right dominant ground. SUP-712 is rightly noncompliant on segregation of duties, and SUP-706 is rightly unresolved. The judgment does reject both normalization after expiry and the idea that every M0 mismatch is misconduct. The layer split is the right architecture, the standard route keeps sanctions and quality concurrent, every activation route puts sanctions before activation, and notification remains before closure.

Those strengths are undercut by hard inaccuracies. SUP-708 is called a foreign-supplier breach of international sanctions and AML law; the record only supports a high-risk activation 40 minutes before screening, with no transactions. SUP-712 is called willful concealment and fraud even though the matrix itself says intent is unproved. PD-3 is given a EUR 50,000 cap and other registry rows add a 12-month reactivation rule and a EUR 100,000 cap that no source states. Automated master-data validation is a PD-3 precondition and is never mentioned, yet SUP-702 is a high-confidence justified exception under a five-part test that required every element to be proved. SUP-709 is called a model defect with very high confidence while the same answer says the scope charter is missing. SUP-711 is labeled indeterminate in the same breath as “fully compliant.”

The proposed model has semantic faults, not just omissions. DECLARE `Response(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` is defined backward and would forbid the early-reject path the POWL adds. The information loop finishes during intake, so it cannot generate the actual SUP-707 order, sanctions then two information requests then quality review, and that limitation is not disclosed. The silent intake bypass is not tied to reactivation, so registration can disappear from routes that still require it. Expired pilot and emergency behavior is written into the base XOR as if codified. Withdrawals, activity-label mapping, and source-transaction deduplication are missing. The prosecution brief does not concede genuine logging and scope defects, and the defense still treats post-expiry continuation as legitimate.
