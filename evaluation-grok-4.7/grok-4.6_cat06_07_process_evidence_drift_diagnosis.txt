7.4

The audit narrative is strong and mostly well triangulated: April is treated as a prefix/lineage failure, June as routing mix with stable within-variant rates, July–September as stacked supplier performance, V3-specific \(P(Y\mid X)\), and (from September) Partner Z, and October as right-censored. Weighted checks line up (June ≈0.16, July ≈0.20). Unsafe training windows, maturity gates, and the “label delay must not open a retrain” rule are proportionate. It is not nearly flawless.

Covariate drift is over-claimed. June’s cited support is V2 share and volume, which are post-diagnosis path mix and throughput, not a shown shift in diagnosis-time \(X\). “More V3 prefixes” has the same problem: V3 is not in the prefix. That blurs the covariate/process split the task requires. The “August–September V3 22%→35%→42%” series starts at July’s 22%, not August’s 35%.

The label-delay falsifier is the wrong experiment. August starters scored on 1 Dec should be mature and should not reproduce October’s 0.16 breach and 0.64 AUROC. T1 later states the right vintage test; the table does not. Section 4 then treats overall AUROC 0.66–0.68 as proof that ranking failed inside V3. Mix plus a higher V3 base rate can cut pooled AUROC while within-slice rank is intact, which is exactly the recalibrate-versus-retrain fork. T3 is proposed and then preempted. A “V3 head” is also under-specified: the score is issued before the path exists, so the head is valid only if intended route is known at diagnosis. The supplier feature is the instrument that actually matches the prediction point.

The sketch calls the right libraries and correctly separates case-start from event-calendar cohorts, but several reported metrics do not measure what they name. Unmapped share is \(1 - \sum counts / n_{cases}\), which is ~0 whenever variants partition the cohort. Diagnosis-alias share is computed after aliases are rewritten to the canonical name, so it cannot see the April rename. `discover_performance_dfg` defaults to mean, while the column and the evidence are medians, and it is run on events clipped to the month. Pairs that cross the boundary—the long `Order Part → Part Received` waits this audit exists to catch—are dropped or rewired, so the calendar DFG will not recover the 130–155 h medians the prose attributes to it. Temporal output is only a static column-name list.

Sections 3 and 6 are the cleanest: competing expectations are actually different, and the failure modes (immature retrain, calibrating the April bug, ignoring V3 concept change, fairness claims on Z) have matching gates. Those strengths hold the score. The wrong falsifier, the \(X\)-versus-path conflation, the pre-empted discrimination claim, and audit code that would mis-measure the incident keep it well short of excellent.
