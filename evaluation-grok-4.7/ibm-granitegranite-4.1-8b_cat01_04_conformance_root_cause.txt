The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape and correctly treats the June fitness collapse as mostly a recording problem, with a residual compliance question left open. The quantitative and process-mining reasoning is not reliable enough to carry that conclusion.

The evidence ledger inverts several inferences. Stable approval and appeal rates are listed as support for real process change and employee noncompliance; they are evidence that the measured drop did not show up in those outcomes. The 1 August policy row both supports and weakens an obsolete model. `Queue Routed` is treated as a timestamp defect and as something that weakens the label hypothesis, although the alignment path shows those mechanisms stacking. The 40-case audit is only a label check, but later text treats it as a hint of bypass. The 11-of-70 screenshot finding is written as if screenshots covered all 70 inversions. `Manual Override` is dated 12 September and classed as a technical event, against the 12 August logging change and IT’s statement that it is a user action. The July memo is scored as support for noncompliance even though it reports no rise in violations. High-value attributes, the rise in events per case, the rise in missing resources, and the directly-follows counts are unused. “Variant count drop/rise” is applied inconsistently to the same direction of change: fitting traces move 32% → 58% → 81% → 89%.

The ranking does not follow that decomposition. Timestamp/order is placed last at 38, below noncompliance, despite the source-sequence step and the screenshots. Real behavioral change at 62 is far too high as an account of the collapse: after label mapping, dropping `Queue Routed`, and source ordering, June–July is already at 89% fit and cost 0.12, with no model edit. The table never separates “cause of the fitness drop” from “later operational concern.” The order test tells the analyst to confirm that `Notify` precedes `Decision`. The model and the screenshots both put `Decision` first. Discriminating tests also bundle label, gateway, and ETL fixes, so they do not discriminate. The 40-case UI audit is again cited as evidence of bypass.

The causal list omits hidden variables (true order, missing compliance events, claim value) and links `true_process` directly to `measured_fitness`. `policy_change` never meets `normative_model`, which is the actual obsolescence path. `Queue Routed` (gateway, 3 June) is folded into the ETL change (15 June). The prose then skips source ordering when it names the parsimonious account, and it calls the EUR 50,000 sign-off a minor tweak.

The counterfactual table has seven rows, not eight, and no reviewer-level test. Label mapping alone is predicted to restore about 95% fitness; the supplied result of that exact repair is 58%. An optional sign-off is predicted to add about 5 points; the supplied August–September comparison is 66% → 86%. Those predictions are already falsified by the brief. The noncompliance “falsifier” is a later rise in appeals, which would support the hypothesis. Three rows repeat the sign-off edit with incompatible effect sizes. There is no statement that a globally optional activity can raise fitness while losing precision.

The four-phase program does not isolate causes. Phase 4 sorts by source sequence, deletes `Queue Routed`, stratifies by value, and edits the model in one step. Precision is named and never given a method. No phase is a manual or provenance audit of the 25 and 30 enhanced reviews with no `Compliance Check`, which is the main unresolved compliance question. The cited calls (`discover_dfk_dfg`, `compute_alignment_fitness` with `trial-2`, `apply_transform`, `remove_events`, `extend_process_tree`) are not PM4Py operations.

The code block misses the required construction. It does not expand the frequency tables with `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`, does not call `pm4py.discover_dfg`, does not compute alignment diagnostics or precision, and does not compare August–September separately. `convert_to_petri_net` returns a net plus markings, which the fitness call does not unpack. The candidate tree places `* ( 'Supervisor Sign-off' )` after `Notify`. `*` is a loop, not an optional skip, and the observed sign-off sits before `Decision` on both the standard and enhanced paths (60 and 150 cases). That net does not accept the traces it is meant to explain, and it cannot express the EUR 50,000 guard. The printed caveat states the data-condition limit, but the comparison the caveat refers to is not actually run.

The conclusion’s headline is the strongest part: artifacts and labels first, a separate and weaker compliance residual, and a warning that flat appeal rates do not clear the process. It still attributes `Queue Routed` to the ETL switch, treats the memo as corroboration of bypass, and gives about 65% confidence that isolated bypasses exist where the brief says no audit has separated bypass from missing events.
