6.4

The case-level fork-join arithmetic is sound, but the aggregate statistics, synchronization summary, and counterfactual accounting are not reliable enough for a high score.

The definitions correctly use max-based `join_ready`, critical-path stage time, and the identity upstream + stage + downstream queue + final service. The explanation that summed synchronization waits sit inside the stage interval and must not be added again is right. All 12 case rows and the F02, F04, F07, and F11 walkthroughs match the timestamps, including critical labels A/B/C = 2/8/2. The main conclusion is also right: A’s constant 50-minute service is not control of completion; B’s queue plus service is.

Section 3 then reports numbers that the same table does not support. B’s mean queue is 230/12 = 19.17 minutes, not 19.6, so mean B duration is 56.7, not 57.1. Synchronization totals are A 132, B 52, and C 365 minutes. Means are about 11.0 / 4.3 / 30.4 over all cases, or 13.2 / 13.0 / 36.5 over noncritical cases only. The published 7.1 / 0.0 / 26.5 match neither rule, and 0.0 for B contradicts the footnote: B waits in F01, F04, F08, and F11. That is an unsupported result in the comparison used for the ranking.

Counterfactuals are only partly correct. Cutting A’s service by 20% saves 5 and 10 minutes on F01 and F11 and nothing elsewhere, under a constant downstream-duration model. The B-queue cut does not benefit “all 12 cases”; F01, F04, F08, and F11 gain nothing, and the prose never gives the aggregate (92.5 minutes under that model). Downstream masking is required and missed: those CF1 gains are smaller than the observed gaps to `final_start`, so a fixed final start would erase them. `final_resource` is unused. F05, F08, F09, and F11 start exactly when the assigned server frees, while several other waits occur with the server already idle, so halving every downstream gap is not what extra F capacity would do. Recommendation 2 treats the lag mainly as a release handshake.

The Python covers parsing, the critical-path split, tie priority, and three counterfactuals, but it never calculates synchronization or stage duration, prints per-case savings for only five rows, and its PM4Py service-time call is fragile. Section 7 correctly flags capacity, preemption, and queue-versus-policy limits without repairing the earlier point estimates.
