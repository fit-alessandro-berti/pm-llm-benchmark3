The design is strong on discrete-event scope, calendars, cross-case coupling, finite-horizon non-stationarity, and the refusal to treat objection flags or service times as fully observed. It is not nearly flawless: the PM4Py path is broken, a few decision rules are mutually inconsistent, and several mechanisms stay underspecified.

The planner load bound is right. With a non-negative distribution, median 3.5 h and p90 14 h imply a mean of at least \(0.4 \times 3.5 + 0.1 \times 14 = 2.8\) h. About 400 cases per week over 18 months times 2.8 h is about 1,120 h, against \(8 \times 8 \times 5 = 320\) scheduled planner hours. The weekend and off-calendar timestamps in the excerpt are a real reconciliation issue. Variant shares are correctly rejected as branch probabilities. Notice length is correctly treated as unable to separate objection incidence from objection delay. Performance-DFG times are correctly treated as elapsed gaps, including across the parallel reviews. Common random numbers, replicate-level dependence, parameter uncertainty separate from Monte Carlo error, censoring, and restricted means are the right uncertainty tools. Initializing from open work, and refusing a steady state when backlog drifts, matches the capacity gap.

`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns one performance-DFG dictionary. Unpacking it as `perf_dfg, _, _` fails, so the required performance DFG is not actually computed. `discover_dfg`, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, and the arrival arithmetic are in line with current PM4Py use. The excerpt mean inter-submit gap is exactly 33 h 9 min, and the service and wait columns correctly stay missing when only one timestamp exists.

The 5-day half-width target on the overall p90 difference cannot support the 10% p90 rule for short case types. The go/no-go SLA table does not lock the clock to calendar time versus authority-controlled time, even though that choice changes the ranking of D1 against D4. D2, D5, and D8 lack numeric levels. S2 stacks an inspector outage and a legal blackout, so those two causes are not separable. The workload-infeasibility result never becomes its own experiment arm or a gate before the 5% improvement rules. Statutory notice is a hard floor under several medians, and the selection rules do not say when a 5% cut is infeasible for that reason.

Rework is described both as an unlogged loop label and as a return to a named task, without a rule for whether the redo consumes a scarce resource. Scheduler headcount is left open. Same-planner continuity from review to drafting is visible in the excerpt and absent from the state. D6’s reserved planner is not tied to the two Simple-only planners, so the reserve can be ineligible for the aged commercial and variance work it is meant to protect. Queue discipline is named only as “observed priority.”

Provisional thresholds are appropriately conditional, and the write-up does not claim an optimal policy from an uncalibrated run.
