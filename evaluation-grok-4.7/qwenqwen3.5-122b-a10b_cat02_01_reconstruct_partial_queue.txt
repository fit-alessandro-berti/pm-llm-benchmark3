3.5

The write-up has the required seven sections, labels assumptions, and gets a minority of fully observed rows right (P01–P04, P08–P10, P12–P14). Those points do not carry the reconstruction. Case arithmetic, missingness, WIP, utilization, the bottleneck call, and the code disagree with the supplied log and with each other.

**Missingness is misread.** P05’s S1 start is 09:00 and P11’s S1 start is 10:40; only their S2 starts are `MISSING`. P15’s S2 start is 13:30, not missing. The case table still treats those starts as unknown (`MISSING_START_S1_AND_S2` for P05 and P11; S2 wait `[0, 90]` for P15). The later P05 note then gives a different window, `[30, 35]` with a central start of 08:50, which is a third value and still ignores the recorded 09:00. P15’s table (wait `[140, 180]`, service 135) also contradicts the later note (`[90, 115]`).

**Several observed durations are simply wrong.** P06’s S2 wait is 25 minutes (09:45→10:10), not 5. P16’s S1 wait is 100, not 75; P17’s is 95, not 70; P20’s is 105, not 95; P21’s is 100, not 90. P21’s elapsed S1 service to 14:00 is 30 minutes, not 15. P15’s elapsed S2 service is 30 minutes, not 45. Right-censored S2 queue time is reported as 5 minutes for P16, P18, P19, and P20; the elapsed lower bounds are 85, 50, 35, and 15. P22 has already waited 110 minutes for S1, not 5. Section 6 then says P16–P20 never started S2; P17 started at 13:55.

**Imputed service and wait are not jointly feasible.** With a fixed end, a longer wait is a shorter service. P07 is given wait `[15, 75]` and service 60. A 60-minute service ending at 10:00 starts at 09:00, which overlaps P05 on I1 (09:00–09:25). After P05, P07’s start is bounded by about `[09:25, 10:00]`, so wait is about `[40, 75]` and service about `[0, 35]`. The same break appears for P05/P11 S2: wait ranges include starts while C1 or C2 is still busy or, for C2, inside 10:55–11:10. P11 on C2 cannot start before P08 ends at 12:00.

**Station aggregates do not match the log.** S1 arrivals in 08:00–10:00 are 13 (P01–P13), not 10, and S1 completions by 10:00 are 7, not 10. Only P22 arrives after 12:00, not 5 cases. S2 completions are 14 (P01–P14), not 15. Early S2 completions by 10:00 are 4, not 6. S1 WIP peaks near 8 and S2 near 7; the stated peaks of 4 and 3 miss the queues. Known S1 service sums to about 535 minutes, and the two missing S1 services can add at most about 70, so busy time is about 535–605 against 720 available minutes (roughly 74–84%). The reported 660 minutes and 91–97% utilization are above that bound. S2’s 570 busy minutes is essentially the maximum imputation, presented as the estimate.

**The bottleneck claim does not follow the evidence.** S2 service on known starts averages about 44 minutes versus about 28 at S1, with the same two servers, so S2 has less capacity. By 14:00, S1 WIP is 2 (P21 in process, P22 waiting) and S2 WIP is 6 (P15 and P17 in process, P16/P18/P19/P20 waiting). That is a shift toward S2, not a persistent S1 bottleneck with idle S2 capacity. “Idle but cannot keep up” is internally inconsistent. The 15-minute C2 outage is one resource: about half of S2 capacity during that window, about 6% of resource-time over 10:00–12:00, not ~25%. I2 is free after P20 ends at 13:45 while P22 is still waiting; that conflict is never examined.

**Uncertainty and Little’s Law are asserted, not calculated.** Only P07 and P15 have missing S1 starts, and their feasible ranges move the S1 mean by only a few minutes. The optimistic/central/pessimistic means of 55/65/80 are wider than those constraints allow. The 08:00–10:00 Little’s Law check mixes an ill-defined cohort, total WIP, and a 58-minute sojourn that is not shown, then treats the gap as handoff time.

**The code does not implement the prose or the required PM4Py steps.** P05’s S1 start is entered as `None`. `OPEN`, `MISSING`, and blank are all stored as missing. The `scenario` argument is unused; the three scenarios are a hardcoded print. S2 WIP counts only in-service time when the start is known, so the queue is dropped. Events are split into point rows with `start_timestamp == time:timestamp`, not interval events. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is absent, the waiting-time call is wrapped so failure is swallowed and is not restricted to the filtered projection, and there is no `convert_log_to_time_intervals` and no comment justifying the omission. Running this script would not reproduce the tables above.
