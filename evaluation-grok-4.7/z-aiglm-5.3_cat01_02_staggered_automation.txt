The later conclusions are a serious staggered-adoption analysis: availability is separated from use, the single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is rejected, never-treated DiDs are computed on transparent windows, median and p90 are not collapsed, escalation is isolated from the common March +2.0pp fraud-desk step, and the rollout choice (embedded evaluation, Delta next, Epsilon held out, case-level randomization, desk capacity, guardrails) matches the design problem. Main unadjusted contrasts check out (Alpha early median DiD about −13.9pp; placebo about +3.9pp median and +4.6pp p90; March escalation exactly +2.0pp in every branch).

The grade stays well below excellent because the required deliverable is not finished—the write-up stops inside the threats table, so sections 6–10 never appear as the mandated artifact—and because several final quantitative claims are not identified as tightly as they are stated.

The complexity “adjustment” applies a five-point January between-branch slope (~1.0h median and ~1.9h p90 per complexity point) to within-branch changes. That slope mixes scale, staffing, and process with case mix. It is what drives the headline heterogeneity (Beta’s median DiD from about −11pp to roughly 0). Those adjusted figures are then placed beside percent-change DiDs even though one is a difference of percent changes and the other is a residual-hour gap divided only by the treated baseline.

The “system-alone” band of about −10% to −25% stacks a volume elasticity taken from Delta/Epsilon onto the DiD. Those branches’ cycle-time changes also contain the complexity shift, the fraud desk, and FTE changes, so the elasticity is not a pure volume effect and the doubling is not a clean bound.

The p90 interval of roughly −8% to +2% is not entailed by the reported DiDs, which are negative; the positive end is an unquantified spillover haircut. Calling one Delta-versus-Epsilon pseudo-adoption a ±4–5pp noise floor overstates a single contrast. In the drafted code, escalation is run through the same percent-change function as cycle time and then labeled “pp.” Clustered event-study standard errors with five clusters are still printed after the small-cluster warning. Confidence values such as 55 and 70 read as calibrated precision on ranges that still depend on those judgment calls.
