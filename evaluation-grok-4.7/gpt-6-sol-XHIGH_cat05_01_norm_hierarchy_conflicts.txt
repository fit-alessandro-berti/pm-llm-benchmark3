7.5

The assessment is rigorous and mostly reliable, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and classification choices are material under a strict reading.

The method is well built. Board policy, the local SOP, the incident directive, and the CFO email are separated by issue rather than ranked by age or force of wording. A7 is not invented from COO and Incident Commander signatures. New-supplier status is kept distinct from the approved-supplier register. Rules are applied at commitment. Later approval is not treated as a cure. ON-44 is confined to 14 July, the 17:59:31 acknowledgment is preferred to the 18:06 ERP stamp, and the 12:40 PDF is preferred to the 14:30 ingestion. P-501 is correctly placed before ECD-2. P-504 is correctly kept outside the directive. P-506’s verbal extension is correctly rejected. Arithmetic on the 23-hour, 4-hour, and 3-hour-45-minute deadlines is accurate. Section 5 identifies the premise that would have to move, and section 7 covers authority, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, records, expiry, and emergency normalization.

The rule application is not consistent. The hierarchy limits ECD-2 to PO sequence and supplier registration, and R6 still requires Plant approval. P-505, P-507, and P-508 treat missing Plant approval as a deviation, but P-502 omits it entirely despite no Plant event. P-503 counts the missing Plant approval only if ECD scope fails, which silently gives ECD-2 a Plant waiver the hierarchy denies. P-502 then lists the late Finance and Compliance acts under both “Satisfied rules” and “Apparent deviations,” contradicting A6 and the author’s own timing rules.

The formalization has the same slip. The rule table makes C3 a condition of the ECD override, but the pseudo-logic allows commitment before the PO whenever the purchase is merely ECD-covered, without the two-person authorization and four-hour record.

Classification discipline is weaker than the evidence work. All eight primaries are NONCOMPLIANT, so the required distinction among compliance, justified exception, indeterminacy, and breach is never used in the primary column, and COMPLIANT is never distinguished from JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. That matters for P-501: if B2 has no pre-commitment timing, following B4 is compliance, not a justified exception. It also matters for P-504, where SOP coverage is admitted to be an interpretation rather than a fact; under the stated burden, an unestablished trigger supports INDETERMINATE more cleanly than NONCOMPLIANT. Unproved budget-owner authority, the missing P-505 PO, and the unknown P-508 recording time are placed in “Apparent deviations” even though section 6 correctly says inability to prove is not a breach. Section 5 also wrongly suggests ON-44 could explain P-501’s 15:10 and 15:12 approvals; those stamps are hours after the outage, and the body text already says a 12-minute lag cannot move them before 08:50. The CFO email of 15 July 07:20 is likewise not time-barred for the 14 July 10:15 commitment in P-502; it is only described as non-curative. B6 is checked for P-501 and then dropped.

The outcome on the clear cases, especially P-502, P-503, P-505, and P-506, would survive these defects. The defects still show unresolved conflict between the hierarchy, the rule logic, and the case table, which is enough to keep the answer well below a near-flawless score.
