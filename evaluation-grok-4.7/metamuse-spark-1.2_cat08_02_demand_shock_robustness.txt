6.5

The redesign is structurally complete and directionally serious, but its process-mining evidence is too often the wrong ratio or the wrong time base, and two operating rules contradict themselves.

**Evidence errors**
- Opening weekly mix math is right (Urgent 414→957/week, Complex-panel 299→660/week). Later DFG comparisons drop that normalization. Hold for specialist review is given as 290→610, “+110%.” Weekly rates are about 36→203, roughly +460%. Batch-wait 760→1,180 is the same mistake: weekly rate rises about fourfold (95→393), not by half.
- Repeat rates “6.9%” and “15.5%” divide Assay run→Repeat assay by Test preparation→Assay run (1,140/16,550 and 1,260/8,140). On assay exits the shares are about 6.7% (1,140/17,030) and 14.8% (1,260/8,500). Direction (roughly doubles) survives; the percentages do not.
- Technical routing is cited as 3,420/7,240 = 47% to medical and 4,050/7,240 = 56% to release. 7,240 is Assay run→Technical validation. Technical outflow is 3,420+4,050+610 = 8,080, so those shares are about 42% and 50%. The normal-period percentages are right only because inflow and outflow both equal 15,890.
- “Accession queue at 14h blocks 420 cases” treats a 3-week flow count (Pre-analytical check→Manual accession correction) as a queue stock.
- The dashboard sets releases 15,870 beside 8,040 “per period” without a common window.
- Repeat-after-failure at 9% of cases is said to take ~18% of assay slots because those cases run twice. Complex-panel orders also need multiple assays, so 18% overstates this type’s machine share.
- “30–40% of Routine,” “capacity −25–40%,” “technical utilization stays above 95%,” “WIP ~450 / red >600,” and a “600-case” Friday backlog are stated as facts. Headcount, snapshot WIP, and metadata completeness are not in the logs. An M1 outage is also not a 25–40% capacity cut on a two-machine pool.

**Logic errors**
- Urgent batch-wait bypass falls 74%→58%. That removes the urgent speed advantage. The same sentence says the fall causes more schedule fragmentation. Less bypass produces less fragmentation from bypass.
- The robust rule caps urgent bypass at 65%. Peak bypass is already 58%, so the cap does not bind in the period it is meant to stabilize.
- R1 is said to work in S2 and, in the same subsection, to fail when the defect is analytical (S2).
- An evening medical-validation shift is treated as relief for specialist review (queue 36h, edge 18h→48h). Specialist review is a separate one-person pool.
- P-S2 says both “stop M2 for Routine” and “reduce M2 to low-complexity assays only.” Those conflict if routine work is the low-complexity stream. A second pre-analytical check for repeat-after-failure also fights the answer’s own claim that M2 repeats are analytical and cluster after night shifts, and it loads a station already at 96%.
- The table equates a 48h specialist wait with a 12h outcome. The 12h is transport only; partner processing sits on top.
- In one impact column, “−−” means a strong rework reduction and “−” means harm. “5→8h edge faster” and “68→120h p90 reduced” are not readable.

**What still holds**
Scenario types match the brief, and the peak problem is correctly a mix shift rather than pure volume. Migration from assay relief into technical validation (101%) and then medical/specialist queues follows the utilization and edge data. Always-on urgent-first dispatch, batch size 24, and holding routine releases are rejected for reasons that match the conflict hints. Policies have off-triggers, and simultaneous S1/S3/S4 triggers are correctly left as an explicit choice to protect the 18h urgent contract. Leading queues are separated from lagging cycle time, and registration automation is correctly flagged as creating the next pre-analytical flood.

The package would be usable after the rates are rebuilt. As written, the quantitative backbone is not tight enough to score higher.
