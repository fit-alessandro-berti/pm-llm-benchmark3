The write-up has the required seven sections and some fully observed service durations, but the queue reconstruction that the task asks for is not reliable, and several mandatory checks are missing or internally contradictory.

Case-level waiting times are frequently wrong against the stated definitions. S1 wait is start minus arrival, yet P01 and P02 are given as 0 rather than 5, and later observed waits are off (P13 95 vs 80, P14 105 vs 95, P16 115 vs 100, P18 135 vs 100). Many S2 waits match the gap between consecutive jobs on the same resource, or some other quantity, rather than start minus S1 completion: P06 is 5 vs 25, P09 is 0 vs 25, P10 is 10 vs 50, P12 is 5 vs 45, P14 is 10 vs 65, and P17 is 5 vs 65. Right-censored S2 waits are point values and far too large (P16 245 vs elapsed 85, P18 230 vs 50, P19 195 vs 35, P20 175 vs 15). P19 and P20 are also described as still in the inspection queues after S1 has finished.

Missing-start bounds do not obey the resource constraints in A2–A3. P07 cannot start at 08:55 on I1 while P05 occupies I1 from 09:00–09:25; the feasible start is about 09:25–10:00, not [08:55, 10:00], and the reported wait [5, 100] does not match that interval. Service is then fixed at 25 while wait remains a range. P11’s S2 start [11:10, 12:25] runs through P08 on C2 (11:15–12:00); after the outage and P08, the feasible window is roughly 12:00–12:25. P15’s S1 start from 10:35 overlaps later I1 work; service is again a point inside a wait range. OPEN service (P15, P17, P21) is shown as a completed duration, not elapsed time with an unbounded final duration.

Station totals do not follow from the log or from section 3. S1 completions are 20, not 21; P21 is still open. S2 arrivals are the 20 S1 completions, not 22. S2 completions are 14, not 13. Averages such as S1 wait 68, service 27.4, S2 wait 82, and service 39.6 are not recoverable from the case table. Utilization is a point, not a range, and section 2 divides busy time by 360 minutes while two resources are available. The WIP note says a peak of 7 S1 cases plus 2 S2 cases, while the S2 row lists a peak of 9. No sojourn times or subperiod arrival and departure rates are actually computed. The same-timestamp WIP rule is reversed: counting the arrival before the departure raises WIP.

The bottleneck story treats a real completion gap as a full diagnosis and then contradicts the waits. S1 queue time is already climbing through the morning, so “S1 lightly utilized” from 08:00–10:30 is not supported. The 15-minute C2 outage is blamed for delaying P11 at 10:55, but P11 is still in S1 until 11:05 and cannot take C2 until P08 finishes at 12:00. The case the outage can delay is P08, queued since 10:20.

Section 6 names optimistic, central, and pessimistic imputations but does not compute them. “±5% utilization” is unsupported. The Little’s Law check uses λ ≈ 0.2 cases per minute (about 72 cases in six hours) against a system whose S2 throughput is 14 completions and whose two-server capacity at ~40 minutes of service is near 0.05 per minute. It then says the law holds in the central scenario while L ≈ 14 is set next to WIP 3.8. Censoring is said to underestimate waits that the table has already overstated, and completed throughput is confused with cases still open.

The Python block stores the raw table, then erases the required status distinction by mapping MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same NaN. Interval rows are duplicated pseudo-events with numeric minutes rather than one datetime interval per activity, so `format_dataframe` is not applied to a usable log. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are never called. There is no pandas queue, service, WIP, or three-scenario calculation; utilization is a count of end timestamps times 25 minutes. Printed output is not the analysis.
