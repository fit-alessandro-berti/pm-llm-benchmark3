The answer is numerically careful, regime-aware, and well matched to the requested structure, but it is not nearly flawless. Several second-order analytical and reproducibility gaps keep it below a very high score.

What holds up. The audit correctly fixes a 12-hour scheduled day, separates active-service percentiles from flow time, and treats the 2,519 versus 2,409 counts (net +110) as an inventory identity with unknown opening and closing WIP. The primary cut — low-mix through 14 Apr, outage on 15 Apr, drawdown on 16–17 Apr, a thin weekend interlude, high-mix through 28 Apr, then an uncertain staffing transition — is the right mechanism split, and recovery is not treated as a sustainable high-throughput regime. Checked figures match the table: weekday 108.0 arrivals and 103.2 completions versus weekend 44.9 and 43.2; high-mix weekdays 114.0 versus 100.6 (shortfall 94, about 13.4 per day); transition weekdays 115 versus 121 with WIP falling 43 → 36 → 32; complex means about 21.1%, 43.8%, and 42.4%, with arrival-weighted values about 21.0%, 44.3%, and 43.2%. Hourly vector totals match the daily counts. Little’s law is applied only to the two near-balanced low-mix windows, in open-hour units, with completions as the proxy, and is refused for the outage, drawdown, high-mix deficit, transition, and the 28-day pool. Assumptions and confidence are labeled, and the risky policy (withdraw the added FTE because the five-day pool shows 92.2 completions against 88.6 arrivals) is the right segmented counterexample.

The main analytical miss is the peak-share contradiction. Labeled 08:00–09:59 shares of 16.8%, 18.1%, 9.6%, and 11.8% versus table values 31, 32, 47, and 22 are real. Suggesting time-bin alignment as a resolution is incomplete: on 21 Apr no contiguous two-hour window reaches 47% (the maximum pair is 42/115 ≈ 36.5%). Three of the other days are nearly consistent with a one-bin shift to 09:00–10:59, so the conflict is not one global labeling error. That check was available from the vectors they already used.

The change-point heuristic is weaker than the prose implies. A complex jump of at least 10 points flags 20 Apr, but a staffing increase of at least 0.75 FTE also flags ordinary Sunday-to-Monday jumps (13, 20, and 27 Apr) plus 16 Apr. Three of five flags are the weekend cycle. The narrative only says the screen can flag 20 Apr and 29 Apr, so the code’s actual behavior is not disclosed. The 0.75 FTE rule is not a serious within-weekday intervention detector.

Smaller gaps. The pooled weekday completion rate of 103.2 is still a mixture of the outage (58), the drawdown (129 and 112), and later regimes, yet it is offered as the segmented contrast to 86. The “middle” WIP band under the alternative cut is essentially one day (1 May, WIP 32), not a real state. The early-window sojourn of about 2.45 open hours (roughly 147 minutes) is never set next to the 24–26 minute weekday service medians, which is the clearest reading of that approximate Little’s-law result. “Congestion” is a loose explanation of higher active handling time on the outage day when the metric already excludes waiting. Within-regime daily dispersion is not quantified, so the hourly Fano factors (diurnal heterogeneity, correctly caveated) are never contrasted with the relatively tight early-weekday daily totals. The 2.45 open-hour figure is also a slightly high rounding of 20.29/8.30.

No material arithmetic failure, invented observation, or steady-state misuse showed up in the regime balances, weighted mix, Fano inputs, or Little’s-law refusals. The deduction is for an unresolved data contradiction, a confounded heuristic, and a few places where a pooled or threshold summary is cleaner in the prose than in the evidence.
