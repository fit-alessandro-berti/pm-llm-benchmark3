6.5

The assessment is well calibrated and meets the required structure: it separates routine specialization, emergency channels, and data-quality artifacts from the tighter `VendorMaster_Lena` / `AP_Kim` / `V-77x` exposure, avoids naming fraud as fact, and keeps the audit wording inside the requested scope and length. Statistics that are copied from the scenario are generally accurate.

The prioritization math is not. The 9 bank-change/payment four-eyes cases are same-resource violations; the 44-case figure is a two-resource co-case count. Treating the 9 as a subset and reviewing a “remaining 35,” then repeating that nesting in the report, is unsupported and can mis-frame the testing population. The 11 subcontracting loops and 16 fast paths are also overlapping subsets, not a clean queue inside those 35. The 84 and 62 fast PO approvals are flagged as indicators but never given an explicit review cluster unless they also happen to fall into the 42 PO violations.

The PM4Py section has material API errors. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called without `keep_violations=True`, so under the current default it returns compliant cases while the code labels them as violations. `discover_network_analysis` sets both the grouping columns and the node columns to `effective_actor`, which does not build the intended resource network, and the comment claims a `source_system` split that the call does not perform. `manual_upload` and `legacy_erp` frames are created and then unused. The DECLARE and log-skeleton models do not encode “different person within 3 working days”; the separate business-day check is the real test, and that check is fragile on time zones, missing timestamps, and duplicate events. Later sections also harden “post-approval” loops and “documented” emergencies beyond what the log shows.
