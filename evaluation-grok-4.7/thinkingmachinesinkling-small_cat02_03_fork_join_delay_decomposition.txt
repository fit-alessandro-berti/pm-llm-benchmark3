6.5

The fork-join decomposition itself is largely right: upstream, branch queue, service, max-based `join_ready`, critical-branch identity, downstream wait, final service, and total flow time match the log for all 12 cases, and the additive identity holds. The double-counting argument is the correct one (each branch’s queue + service + sync spans the same parallel interval, so summing sync waits triple-counts clock time). Ranking B ahead of C and A, despite A’s longer service, is supported by critical frequency (8/12 vs 2/12 vs 2/12), mean queue-plus-service, and the observation that speeding a noncritical branch does not move `join_ready`.

That core is undercut by concrete errors and missed evidence.

The definitional formulas are not usable as written. Subtraction operators are absent (`U_c = fork_c arrival_c`, `W_i,c = max(0, J_c end_i,c)`, `(startfork) + (endstart) + (Jend)`), and the F01 check is an invalid chain of equalities (`15 + 50` is not equal to `50 + 8`). The intended arithmetic is recoverable from the table, but section 1 was required to state the formulas.

Counterfactual 2 is internally inconsistent. Halving B’s queue reduces join time by half the queue only while B stays strictly critical. It does not on F05 (A finishes at 10:00, so the drop is 10 minutes, not 12.5) or F10 (A finishes at 11:15, so the drop is 5, not 10). The claim that faster B could become critical on F01 and F11 reverses the direction: an earlier B end cannot overtake A. The “about 8–9 minutes across B-critical cases” figure matches neither the naive half-queue mean among those eight cases (12.5) nor the actual join reduction (about 11.6). The code’s `max` recomputation would get the join deltas right, but the printed prose states the wrong rule.

Downstream delay is treated as a capacity queue. The `final_resource` column is never used. F1 and F2 do not overlap themselves, and a large share of `final_start − join_ready` occurs while the assigned server is already idle (F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F12 entirely; F04 and F10 in part). Only about 73 of 176 downstream minutes line up with the assigned server still being busy. Recommending added final capacity as a universal saving of every downstream minute, and calling A and C underutilized, goes beyond the log. Section 7 then says queue versus release policy cannot be identified, which is too strong: the named servers make a partial split possible. The right hedge appears only as a pilot suggestion.

The Python block builds the table and the critical-path fields correctly, but the masked-savings formula `max(0, delta − downstream)` is not a coherent flow-time effect under either a fixed `final_start` or a resource-free-time model. The PM4Py calls are wrapped so that failures disappear, and the keyword names (`case_id_key` on `format_dataframe`, `.edges` on a performance DFG) are unlikely to return the tables the prompt asked to print. Tail contribution to `join_ready` is only ranges and two worst cases, not a margin or percentile.
