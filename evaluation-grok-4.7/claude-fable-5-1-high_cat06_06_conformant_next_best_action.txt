The recommendation is directionally right: a conformant independent second review, with re-screening in parallel, unlawful shortcuts last, and P-901 treated as a real constraint. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and logical claims are unreliable, and one opening claim contradicts the later analysis.

The intro states that no action removes the rule 6 precondition. That is false on the answer’s own branch: a refreshed score below 70 plus a resolved match would lift both triggers. The main plan also never clearly records the mandatory post-validation Analyst Review. The prefix stops at Evidence Validated, and rule 5 returns that cycle to Analyst Review. “R17 prepares a package” is not the same control, even though a second officer is still required before execution.

The benefit and harm figures do not hold together. The table treats on-time execution probability as about 0.35–0.55 and as the complement of breach probability, then multiplies by the full €20k–€35k. The prompt’s deadline value is the value of executing on time, and the stated approval probability is only 0.55–0.75, so those execution probabilities are too high. Section 3 admits the approval condition and then does not use it. The claimed €4k–€9k gain of C over D+, “after discounting by approval probability,” matches an undiscounted increment, not a discounted one. P-901’s €2k–€10k spillover assumes a deadline value the case never gives. Endpoint products such as €8k–€60k overstate ranges. Short second-officer delay is also priced toward the €15k–€80k prolonged-hold band.

The dominance claim overreaches. A best-case €35k benefit sitting below a plausible €100k harm figure is not strict dominance “before weighting by any probability.” Unlawful execution is certain; harm of at least €100k is not. A and B are still correctly disqualified on deontic grounds, and the non-transportability point is sound. “No realistic chance of getting away with it” is asserted rather than shown.

Other unsupported precision: clearance probabilities 0.4–0.6, 0.3–0.45, and 0.1–0.2, conditional approval rates of about 0.8 and 0.2, review time shortened from the given 35–75 minutes to 25–60, and a “high” probability that the Compliance Director is unreachable. E is ranked above F, but the monetized comparison never actually shows that a certain deadline miss plus prolonged hold beats an immediate rejection.

The P-901 timing trap is the strongest piece: a T+30 interruption can run past P-901’s deadline, so the stated +0.12–0.28 does not apply to a late fallback. That insight, the four-eyes and SAR handling, and the refusal to let re-screening or self-certification replace independent review are what keep this above a merely adequate answer.
