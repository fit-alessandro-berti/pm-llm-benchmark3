The write-up covers every required section and sometimes states the right mechanisms, but the central empirical claim is wrong and several cited figures are misassigned. That makes the explanation unreliable.

Service time in the sequence table falls in every bin: 18.8, 15.2, 13.6, 12.9, 11.4, 10.8. It does not rise late. Section 2 opens by saying service time falls and then rises again, and the fatigue row says service time rises under high WIP. Both contradict the same table and the later claim that times “plummet” to 10.8. High WIP is faster within complexity bands (12.7 to 9.4; 22.4 to 16.8), so the fatigue signature of slowing is absent. The question asks why service time falls while errors rise only in some conditions; the thesis answers a different pattern.

Error bins are swapped. 2.5% is positions 16–30, 2.7% is 31–50, and 4.6% is 51–75. The fatigue row treats 2.7% as 16–30 and 4.6% as 31–50. Section 2 then calls 4.6% the 31–50 rate. Queue pressure is also timed too early: mean queue at 31–50 is still 31, and the jump starts at 51–75 (queue 62) and 76+ (queue 88). The 10.8 min / 7.9% pair is the 76+ sequence bin, not the high-WIP stratum (errors 5.8% and 10.9%). Queue length 88 is labeled as WIP. The 48% high-complexity share is the Early shift’s first hour, not sequence positions 1–5. Night staff’s 8.2% is later events under high WIP, not the night shift’s last two hours (5.1%). The Day-shift “13.4 to 12.7” decline is not in the data; 12.7 is the Early shift’s last two hours.

The best discriminating evidence is barely used. Later under low WIP, errors stay flat for seniors (2.2%), mid-level (2.9%), and night staff (3.7%), and juniors improve (5.1% to 4.4%). Errors jump under high WIP, worst for juniors (12.4%) and high-complexity work (10.9%). Day shift builds the queue (38 to 75) and speeds up; night’s last two hours stay slow (15.2). That pattern separates pressure and mix from a general late-shift fatigue effect. Minutes since start, sequence position, and WIP move together, so a rise in error after minute 450 does not identify fatigue. Complexity falling while errors rise is noted, then still narrated as learning plus familiarity.

The proposed code does not implement that separation. `discover_batches` is imported from the wrong path and called with `similarity_threshold` and `activity_attribute='resource_experience_months'`, which are not the real arguments. Group-bys use an `error_rate` column the log does not have. Treating `case_complexity_score` as a random effect is the wrong specification. Rework analysis is a raw count. Arrival rate, dotted charts, performance DFGs, rework-by-activity, time filters, and same-clock-hour contrasts across shifts are named or omitted rather than used. `pd.cut` on sequence position drops the left edge and misaligns the published bins.

Intervention 8 still lists punishment as a quality incentive, and intervention 6 still lists throughput as the benefit of deferring quality checks. Shifts are already eight hours, and the 76+ mean of 459 minutes sits inside that window, so a blanket cap does not address the observed spike. Moving complex work out of hour one risks the worst cell in the tables: high WIP and high complexity. The dashboard includes the requested indicators, but waiting time is sourced from WIP and queue length, and the fairness metric is unweighted event volume, which penalizes seniors on longer complex cases.
