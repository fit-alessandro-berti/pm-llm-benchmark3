The construction is semantically strong and largely faithful: all 17 events and 12 objects are present, O2O directions match the narrative, derived logistics respect the P1/I1–I3/O100–O200 versus P2/I2/O100 split, and the Order, Package, Delivery, and Invoice trace predictions match that E2O policy. Validation, amount-as-attribute handling, and the shared-event double-count warnings are real, not decorative.

It is not nearly flawless. `completes_settlement` for M2 treats an unknown invoice total as fully settled; the narrative only says both payments are partial payments that settle V1. Section 1 says analytical context adds orders and items, but Confirm Delivery also materializes packages, so the stated policy is narrower than the code. Qualifiers are mostly type names (`package`, `delivery`) rather than event roles, so Create, Close, and derived payment context all share `affected_order`.

Section 5 slides between instance and type level. An OC-DFG and an object-centric Petri net aggregate activity and object types; they do not represent E07 or P1 “once” as an event, and E07 is merged with E08 under Pack Package. The OTG paragraph hedges enough to avoid a clear predicted structure. Section 4’s abbreviated labels (Create, Payment, Confirm) do not match the activity strings the code would actually emit, and the aggregated Order DFG cycle Record Payment ↔ Confirm Delivery is left unremarked even though no case loops. Log skeletons are computed and then ignored. The audit table also mixes described and derived touches without marking which object types are source facts.
