The final answer is a strong, case-traceable conformance review with real but non-fatal defects in the revised model and a few overprecise claims.

The dialectic is the best part. Stipulated facts are separated from authority, scope, identity, and logging disputes, and the burden for both a noncompliance finding and an exception is explicit. Both briefs are genuinely strong: the prosecution concedes the missing loop, label drift, CRM cutover, and M0’s resource blindness, while the defense concedes SUP-703, SUP-708, post-expiry reliance, alias segregation-of-duties evasion, and missing eligibility snapshots. The judgment does not split the difference. SUP-702 versus SUP-714 and SUP-704 versus SUP-705 turn on documented authority windows; SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, and SUP-713 are treated as model or scope defects; SUP-708 remains noncompliant despite no spend; SUP-712 is noncompliant despite a perfect M0 replay. Dates, amounts, risk classes, and the 312/289/14/9 and 47-case aggregates match the record.

The main weaknesses are in the POWL revision. The auto-approval branch forces `Activate_Supplier -> Quality_Review`, but PD-3 only permits quality to follow activation; an eligible auto-approval with quality first fits neither the standard nor the auto branch. Rejection exists only as sanctions-then-reject, so M0’s ordinary reject-after-both-checks path disappears, and that limit is not clearly stated. The reactivation overlay says to reuse the standard branch with quality waived, yet that branch cannot waive quality. The `Request_More_Info` star is a disclosed approximation, but it cannot accept SUP-707, the trace used to justify the loop, because the loop finishes before sanctions. Lesser issues: SUP-711 is determinate duplication yet labeled `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`; SUP-708’s manual approval after activation is underplayed; “a third of deviations” and the Europe/Berlin sunset are unsupported; SUP-712’s severity is Medium-High in the matrix and High in the judgment.
