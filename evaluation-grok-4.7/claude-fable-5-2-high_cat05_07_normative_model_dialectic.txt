7.4

The brief is strong on authority, scope, and case-level judgment, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and a non-runnable model block keep it well below a top score.

The control-flow reading of M0 is accurate: four interleavings, three raw fits (SUP-701, SUP-712, SUP-715), and the observation that an optimal alignment can explain SUP-703 and SUP-714 as a rejection plus a spurious activation. The burden split among stipulated facts, disputed authority, and unproved conditions is careful. Calendar checks hold (2026-06-20 Saturday, FD-7 Friday–Sunday, 2026-07-13 Monday, 2026-08-02 Sunday). Counts hold (289/312, 14+9, six post-expiry cases). Both briefs concede real points, the matrix covers all fifteen cases including resource-only failures, and MIXED is avoided with a stated dominant reason. The layering in section 6 correctly keeps money, identity, and authority out of POWL.

The defects are material.

The Python block is not executable as written. `PartialOrder.add_transitive_edges` and `is_strict_partial_order` are not part of the PM4Py API used to define M0. `po()` calls both on every submodel, including at `M1 = build_m1()`, so construction raises `AttributeError` before any net is built. Section 7.1 nonetheless reports a table under “What the self-check shows.” Those rows are unverified predictions. SUP-710 is especially unsafe: `build_m1(())` always offers the draft reactivation branch beside the standard branch, both missing two activities, so the cheapest alignment is not determined by the code.

That reactivation branch also contradicts the text. Section 7.2 says the route has no normative force until CS-9 adopts it, and section 6 warns that placing a route in the model without a gate legitimizes it. `M1_BASE` includes it unconditionally.

The emergency route adds an edge from `Quality_Review` to `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. FD-7 allows both to be retrospective; it does not order them. The comment states this as if it were a requirement. A valid emergency trace with approval before quality review would fail the model. Request_More_Info exists only on the standard and reactivation routes, so a pilot or emergency case with an information cycle cannot fit, and that limit is not disclosed.

SUP-702 is classified `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` with “notification proven by stable CRM ID,” while the same answer says the exception proponent must show authority over the derogated clause and every compensating condition. D1 leaves PD-3’s authority unproved, U1 leaves master-data validation unevidenced, and U9 notes that the CRM hit predates the migration that is supposed to explain it. Medium confidence records the doubt without applying the stated burden. The defense brief says SUP-705 met every substantive FD-7 safeguard, while U7 and the matrix correctly treat daily monitoring, critical-supplier designation, and review timing as unproved. The matrix then says a disproved SUP-706 message would be a “minor breach,” although section 1 correctly says CS-9 does not require notification, so a missing notice is an M0 extra.

SUP-715 as `NONCOMPLIANT` is a stretch in the other direction: the answer itself says CS-9 and M0 are satisfied, the relationship is not prohibited, and only an external disclosure duty was missed. The Monday-staff argument against FD-7 assumes the directive exists because approvers were absent; the text grounds it in flood supply continuity.

Under the answer’s own standard, the cleaner holdings are SUP-701 compliant, SUP-703 and SUP-708 noncompliant at high severity, SUP-705 and SUP-714 noncompliant because the written windows expired, SUP-707 and SUP-709 model or scope defects, and SUP-711 a resolved duplicate. Those calls are well supported. The grade is held down by the broken executable artifact, the invented self-check output, the emergency ordering error, and the failure to apply the announced burden to SUP-702.
