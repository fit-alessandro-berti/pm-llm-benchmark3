7.6

The audit is unusually complete and gets the central distinctions right: post-closure and full-trace fields are separated from repairable as-of defects, `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is treated as normatively valid but inadmissible at `t0`, and `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is treated as both future and order-invalid. Queue length and elapsed time are preserved, region and channel are kept as proxies rather than causes, and the SQL `extraction_date` bug, nightly snapshot, mutable risk score, and “last role” definition are all caught. Sections 2 and 5 are concrete enough to implement. The score is held down by contradictions and overclaims that a strict reading cannot ignore.

The revised Case X narrative says the case is 1.2 hours old and therefore that no slack has been consumed. Those claims conflict. The same narrative says risk is raised by two admissible factors and then discusses complexity, queue, and channel, and it says the wait occurs before any case-specific work, after registration and coverage have already occurred. Case Y puts `initial_reserve` in `credible_drivers` and narrates €25,000 as a booked fact, after Sections 1 and 3 correctly mark it unverified and unusable until an as-of history exists. That violates the requirement to explain X and Y using only information defensibly available at `t0`.

Case Z’s summary says all five cited drivers are post-`t0`, order-ambiguous, or of unknown vintage, and that 0.64 has no admissible support. `calendar_week = 52` is known at `t0`; it is a confounded regime proxy, which the detail list itself later admits. The status line over-quarantines a legitimate signal. Section 1c also says `final_decision = Partial` invalidates X’s score as a pipeline failure, while Section 4 marks X `recompute` and Y `invalidate` without a stable rule. Both published scores are leakage-dominated and need prefix-only rescoring.

The +0.21 / +0.20 / +0.17 bundles are repeatedly called unexplained leakage. They include the base value. The base rate is not a leaked driver; the unnamed remainder is simply unauditable until it is split out. Smaller overclaims accumulate: total duration is called a tautology of the 10-day settlement label, although closure time and time-to-settlement are not shown to be the same quantity; the 35% team mismatch is called “exactly” the escalated hard cases; appeal is first “not even future-possible” and later conditionally future-possible; the global document-count row cites a negative sign that appears only locally; and “requesting documents lowers risk” misstates the request-then-received pattern.

Process checks are strong, but footprint is conflated with eventually-follows, `response(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded)` is stronger than the stated “can lead to,” and `end(Claim Closed | Withdrawn)` treats a decision outcome as an end activity. The CI rule that a small prefix/full-trace disagreement is more dangerous than a large one is a useful warning about `final_handler_team`, not a general law.
