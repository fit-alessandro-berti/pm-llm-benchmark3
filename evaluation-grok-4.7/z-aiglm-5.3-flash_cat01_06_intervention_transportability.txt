The answer meets the required ten-part structure and is substantively strong on transport: it refuses to treat Site A’s nine-hour drop as an identified effect, keeps originals as a hard gate, segments eligibility, and separates a true-parallel arm from a trigger-only arm. The comparison table, selection-node graph, modifier table, conditional-go recommendation, and eight-assumption register are aligned with the process constraints.

It is not nearly flawless. The quantitative scenarios do not follow their own formulas. S2’s stated population effect of −0.6 to −1.3 hours is not what eligible share × in-stratum saving × 0.5 produces (about −0.25 to −0.8). S1’s −1.5 to −3.0 hours likewise exceeds the component sum printed beside it. The claim that a six-hour wait reduction “proves” confounding because it exceeds a 3.5-hour service-overlap ceiling is too strong: that ceiling bounds critical-path service time, not queue wait, which parallelism can change by more than the overlapped service time. The conclusion that the nine-hour figure is confounded remains right for other reasons.

The POWL program is not reliably executable. `check_soundness` is subscripted as if it returned a tuple, and the legal-loop partial order calls `add_edge` toward a decision node that was never added to that order, so construction can fail before the Petri-net checks and playout. The textual specification also places Legal Review both before and inside the loop. The pilot asks for 250 cases per arm in eight weeks, which the stated digital volume and three-arm split do not support, and it incorrectly invokes “clinician-independence” in a claims process.
