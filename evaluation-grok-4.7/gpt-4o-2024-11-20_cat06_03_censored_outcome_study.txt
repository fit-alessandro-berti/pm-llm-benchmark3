The write-up keeps the nine-section shape, reconciles the 48,000 cases, and does not recode rejects as non-defaults. It is not a usable study design: the estimand, censoring model, validation window, and code contradict each other.

**Target.** The primary estimand is stated as P(default within 90 days | activation) while the population is “all applications” and the same paragraph folds withdrawal, rejection, and transfer into that conditional probability as competing risks. Those are different functionals. Default cannot compete with rejection on the same hazard until activation has occurred; the coherent object is a multi-state process, then a 90-day risk among the activated. Prediction time is also unset: fixed length, elapsed time, and “first Credit Review” are offered together, so availability of the decision is undefined. The alternative lumps rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open into one non-activation state and calls that counterfactual. Observed terminal states are not counterfactuals; still-open is censoring. The counterfactual target (default had a rejected case been activated) is named incorrectly.

**Cohort and missingness.** The flow totals are right, but the mechanisms are not. All 3,600 immature activations are called delayed labels; many have not finished the 90-day window by 15 February 2026, which is ordinary right censoring, distinct from a 7–35 day feed lag. All 2,900 transfers are called right censoring, despite a 60% linkage table and a competing-exit interpretation. Permanently missing partner feeds never appear in the flow. Open cases are both excluded and retained as censored. No maturity rule of the form activation + 90 days + feed buffer ≤ as-of date is stated. The primary analysis then abandons the conditional target for a competing-risk model and proposes imputing immature labels. Assumptions are not sorted into testable, partially testable, and untestable (partner MNAR feeds, unlinked transfers, and any activation counterfactual are untestable).

**Prefixes, features, leakage.** Short traces, loop collapsing, and renaming are one-liners. Collapsing document loops before the cut uses future rework; migration duplicates are ignored. Extraction-time resource experience cannot be “adjusted” to event time without a historical HR table; it leaks later tenure. The leakage table drops the right fields in direction, but the availability-rule column is missing, rules are not conditioned on the landmark, and `default_90d` on event rows is never audited.

**Validation.** Train through June 2025 / validate July–October / test November–December does not match the claim of pre, post, and mixed splits. November–December 2025 starts are mostly label-immature by the extraction date, so that test set cannot score 90-day default. There is no walk-forward as-of backtest, no IPCW or cumulative-incidence metric matched to one estimand, no subgroup plan, and no explanation of why a random case split leaks policy, migration, and shared calendar effects. C-index is attached to a target introduced as a binary conditional probability.

**Code.** About 40 lines, not the requested pipeline. `pm4py` is never imported; `get_prefixes_from_log` is replaced by a nonexistent factory; events are filtered at the cutoff before `case_end > cutoff` is computed, so the censor flag is identically zero. There is no activation time, terminal state, or label-maturity table, no case-start split (the cut uses event timestamps), no validation fold, no stripping of post-prefix and label columns, and no leakage or cohort-flow check. Survival is a trailing comment, not a hook.

The draft shows awareness of selection and of the obvious post-outcome columns. It does not define one estimand, one risk set, or one as-of label rule a lender could implement.
