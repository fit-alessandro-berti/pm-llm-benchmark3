6.5

The audit correctly rejects the main future-derived features, keeps queue length, elapsed time, and frozen complexity, and separates the impossible close-before-assessment pattern from ordinary future leakage. Those calls are directionally right, but several conclusions are overconfident, underspecified, or unsupported.

The largest accuracy problems are in the case write-ups. Case Y’s “true” probability of about 0.55–0.60 is invented; SHAP values cannot be subtracted to produce a new probability, and 0.71 is not still “plausible” when a future variant containing appeal and a second decision is the dominant driver. Case X’s claim that the recomputed probability “would be lower” has the same defect. Broker “service agreements” and the claim that region R4 “has shown higher breach rates” are not established. `initial_reserve` is treated as known at registration even though no lineage says so.

Process distinctions stay too coarse. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is inadmissible at `t0` but normatively legitimate after Partial or Deny; it is not the same kind of defect as the same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` artifact, and the other future edges are normal transitions rather than generic leakage to delete. `total_case_duration_hours` is near-label leakage, not merely a future duration. Immediately after coverage check, all three prefixes are identical, so a repaired prefix variant and a document-request count do not discriminate. Elapsed time is remaining slack, not congestion, and a repaired prior-claim count is history, not congestion.

Section 2 names useful checks but is not a concrete suite: no append-future invariance test, no explicit treatment of fraud running in parallel, and no conformance result for the actual two-event prefixes. The four standards—predictive validity, causation, actionability, and fairness—are only gestured at, while week 52’s holiday and policy confounding is underweighted. The recommendations cover the requested topics, but they stay generic and blur retraining with recalibration.
