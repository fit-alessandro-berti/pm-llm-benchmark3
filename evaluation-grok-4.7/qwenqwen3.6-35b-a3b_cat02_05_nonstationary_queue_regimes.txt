5.5

The final write-up has the right qualitative skeleton: separate weekday, weekend, outage/recovery, mix-shift, and post-29 April staffing regimes; refuse a single pooled queue; keep outage recovery out of steady state; use completions rather than arrivals as the Little’s-law throughput proxy; and do not treat median handling time as mean flow time. Format and section coverage match the request, and the PM4Py note correctly says these rows are not an event log.

The quantitative layer is not reliable, and several claims contradict the same table or each other.

- The panel is 28 days, not “all 30.” Pooled means are wrong (arrivals about 90.0, not 88.2; mean WIP about 29.9, not 31.2; complex share about 32.9%, not 30.5%). Weekday/weekend levels (about 108 and 44.9) are not 105.3 and 43.6.
- Primary windows overlap and leave a hole. “Baseline weekdays” through 14 April contains a weekend; “high-mix” through 28 April contains another; 17 April (112 completions, WIP 35, 6.5 FTE), the real continuation of drain-down, is in no regime. Averaging 15–16 April completions to 93.5 makes the outage/recovery block look like an ordinary day.
- Point estimates do not come from the windows named beside them. Post-29 April arrivals/completions (115 and 121) match, but the WIP used for that same window is stated as about 33–33.3 versus the three-day mean of 37, and 29 April itself is still 43. Baseline 99.6 / 98.4 / 19.6 and strain 113.1 / 40.5 are not the means of the stated dates. The 88.1% strain completion ratio only matches if weekends are included, which then cannot produce an arrival mean near 113.
- The code does not implement the prose segmentation. Inclusive slices put baseline on 6–12 April, “outage” on 14–15 April, strain through 27 April, and “post-intervention” from 28 April. It cannot reproduce the reported regime table.
- Dispersion figures are off and internally inconsistent. 7 April’s hourly mean is 101/12 ≈ 8.42, and the stated Fano factor of about 4.4 does not follow from the stated mean and standard deviation. 21 April’s Fano factor is about 9–10, not 11.4. Lag-1 correlation of daily WIP is about 0.61, not 0.75–0.85, and that correlation is inflated by the level shift, not identifiable as carry-over.
- The alternative WIP bins contradict their own examples: 16 April (WIP 52) and 29 April (WIP 43) are cited as moderate pressure under a ≥35 high-pressure rule.
- The hourly vectors sum to the daily arrival and completion totals but do not match `peak_08_10_pct` (21 April’s 08:00–09:59 share in the vector is about 10%, not 47%). That conflict is never flagged. The coding-change explanation allowed by the prompt is also dropped, while complex share is treated as a real service-time mix shift.
- “Simpson’s paradox” here is only mixture masking, not a reversal. Utilization figures (ρ ≈ 0.85 baseline, ≈ 0.75 weekend) are underived and not reproducible from one service-time convention. Weekend days are called steady-state even though they cross the mix shift and the 2.5-to-3.0 FTE change.

Conceptually this is a competent regime sketch. As a numerical queueing argument it is not coherent enough to trust.
