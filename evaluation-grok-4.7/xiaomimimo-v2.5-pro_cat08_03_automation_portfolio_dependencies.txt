5.5

The strategic conclusion is directionally sound, but the deliverable is not executable and contradicts itself on binding constraints. That keeps it well below a strong score.

The matrix covers all twelve candidates with the required qualitative columns and correctly treats A8 and A11 as unsafe until upstream artifacts and controls are reliable. The dependency section hits the requested categories, and the three portfolios are distinct, with a real optimize-versus-sacrifice contrast. The final trade-off—that registration is a local, high-frequency win that can flood KYC, document review, and analyst work—is the right process-level judgment and is grounded in waiting time, rework, and the specialist bottleneck.

The recommendation then fails its own constraints. The budget allows at most four modules in year one. The rollout deploys about ten (A5, A12, A1, A4, A6, A2, A9, A7, A10, and a pilot of A3) across months 1–12 and never mentions the cap. It also breaks the conclusion it claims to implement: Portfolio B is A4, A5, A6, and A7, and the text argues registration should not go first, yet Phase 1 spends one of the two scarce core-banking integrations on A1. Portfolio B itself has no clear control-strengthening module, despite the requirement that the first wave include one. “B with elements of C” patches that only by abandoning the four-module limit. A10 is treated as a quick win and, later, as unsafe until KYC clearance improves.

Section 4 does not meet the demand to use activities, variants, and directly-follows relations. The 1160 entity-mismatch returns to KYC, the 2800 missing-document loop, the 650/510 control-remediation loop, credit-line renewal’s 14% contract rework, and the weekly high-exposure committee cadence are unused. Cycle-time claims treat the 31h and 20h pre-activity waits as if the assistants remove them, and they overstate relief for complex cases that still sit on the committee calendar. Concurrency of KYC and financial-document review is ignored, including the risk that the 7250/3940 split is partly a sequential log of parallel work rather than pure misclassification, which weakens the case for spending a deep integration on A2. Four-eyes (credit analysis versus committee packaging, 5.8% of high-exposure cases at month-end) is wrongly assigned to contract generation. Identity and consent risk on e-sign is answered with softer customer-friction controls. Resource timing—back office under 65% after 15:00, legal ops month-end overload, dependence on two senior compliance specialists, and RM-triggered document loops—is only lightly used. The higher-loan-value claim is not in the evidence. Gate percentages are unsupported.

The answer shows real portfolio judgment, then issues a plan a bank could not follow without violating budget, integration limits, or the stated priority.
