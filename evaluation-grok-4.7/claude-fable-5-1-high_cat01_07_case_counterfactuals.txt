The K-219 path is the clearest temporal failure. Tuesday 08:00 already shows about 6 h of assessment work queued, and that snapshot is from a world in which this case is not yet ready. A document check ending around 04:30–06:30 therefore sits behind that stock; it does not arrive “before the 6 h backlog builds.” A start near 11:00 is not supported. The same row then allows a decision window beginning at 14:00 against an assessment that may end at 16:30. The no-rework cycle of 30–36 h depends on that optimistic pairing. K-337 has a similar range-pairing problem. A review that starts at 18:30 and lasts 4–6 h cannot finish on Friday evening, and a Friday 18:30–19:00 decision cannot follow every compliance start listed in that cell. The derivation then says that applying the same cap after Assessment 1 would save roughly one day. Stopping there would also delete Document Check 2, Assessment 2, the weekend loop, Document Check 3, and Assessment 3 — on the order of six days, not one. The weekend point is real; that sentence is not.

In the 100-case analysis, 350–600 h of C1 demand adds the full final review to the preliminary screen. Final review is baseline load for these high-risk cases. The marginal effect is about −1 to +2 h per case, which the local table already states. The conclusion that 100 reservations are impossible is still directionally right, because baseline C1 load already exceeds one reviewer-week, but the headline arithmetic attributes baseline work to the intervention. Assumption 7 then says the only intervention-induced service-time changes are the C1 and A1 adjustments, which omits the D1-to-D2 change on which K-219 rests.

Smaller inconsistencies accumulate. K-104’s base cycle of 52–53 h is not the same claim as −5 to −8 h. K-411 says readiness improves in every scenario and also gives a worst case of 0 to +2.5 h. The robustness table says K-411’s benefit is never negative except through an IT failure, which is stronger than its own worst case and spillover discussion. The pilot’s claim that 100–150 cases per arm will detect a 2–3 h mean shift against an 8–10 h standard deviation overstates the power. D5 is also narrower than rule 7: a high-risk rework needs a new or reaffirmed compliance review after the renewed assessment regardless of whether an earlier review had already occurred.
