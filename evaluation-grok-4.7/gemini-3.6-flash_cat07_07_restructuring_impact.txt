6.6

The write-up covers every required heading and stays appropriately conditional: East is treated differently from North and South, fairness and learning appear, and the recommendation is a monitored pilot rather than a full rollout. The forecast is still not reliable enough to score higher. Several quantitative claims conflict with the supplied baselines, queueing is applied one-sidedly, the pilot is not identifiable, and the PM4Py sketch misses required comparisons and would mis-measure rework.

The mechanism map names the required channels, but it treats net handover reduction as given. Summing the management table raises handover events from about 343 to about 359 per 100 cases; only cross-team and some local loops fall. “Fewer unique resource-to-resource handovers” is therefore unsupported. Routing is also collapsed: high-complexity technical work and legal/risk are described as one queue, while the routing rules separate Technical Shared Service from the specialist queue. Utilization “leveling out across units at ~80–85%” does not match a single pooled desk. Current volume-weighted tech utilization is about 73%; Legal and Risk are already at 91–93% with volume moving from 9 to 12 per 100 cases. Pooling’s variability reduction is never set against the utilization penalty, so the queueing argument only supports the downside.

The scenarios are internally uneven. The optimistic case cuts Legal/Risk wait from 9.6h to 8.5–9.0h while increasing arrivals and adding no staff, which is implausible near 90% utilization. The central case calls ~7.2d a flat median and 18.5d an enterprise 90th percentile; the volume-weighted median is about 7.5d, and averaging team percentiles is not an enterprise percentile. North’s 8.2% rework baseline is also applied to South, whose baseline is 9.0%. In the adverse case, Technical Shared Service returns above 50 per 100 cases against about 52 outbound referrals are read as multiplied ping-pong; that ratio is a normal return path, close to today’s 31 returns on 36 referrals. A falling `local_expertise_flag` is an effect of the redesign itself, so it cannot serve as an early warning that the adverse case has occurred. The “~15–20%” high-complexity share is invented and sits poorly beside a 41/100 specialist stream. Hour-scale queue shifts are then turned into multi-day median moves without a time-scale bridge.

The indicator table has a real leading/lagging split, but several definitions would mislead. One specialist-wait target of median under 3.0h mixes a 3.2h local-tech baseline with a 9.6h legal baseline and with management’s own 4.0h tech estimate. A drop in subcontracting-network density is labeled loss of collaboration; subcontracting measures delegation and return, so an increase is the more natural signature of shared-service ping-pong. The 0.65 silhouette cutoff is not a PM4Py output, and a sharper activity clustering can coexist with worse accountability. Learning and absence resilience have no log indicator.

Section 4 covers tail delay, complex-case stagnation, tacit triage, regional fairness, skill atrophy, and diffused ownership. Exception handling is not given its own risk, despite absence cover already showing an 8.9h wait and an 18% rework rate. Cherry-picking and the minority-share story are stated as mechanisms the log does not establish.

The pilot has the requested parts, but the comparison is contaminated. A shared queue fed only by East plus half of North will run under-loaded relative to full rollout, and the untreated half of North keeps a lighter local specialist. East is wholly treated and South wholly withheld, so team differences stay confounded. Scope never says which of the six changes are live, including which single supervisor is removed. The 6.0h Specialist Review stop rule is already below the legal baseline of 9.6h, so it either trips immediately or silently ignores the scarce queue. Ten weeks is short relative to 16–23 day tails and cannot show learning loss. Guardrails in the pilot and in the recommendation flowchart do not match.

The recommendation itself is the strongest part: pilot only after separating legal/risk capacity, keeping medium-value approval local, and refusing to read early medians, raw handover counts, or utilization above 85% as success. It still states too flatly that utilization above 85% will degrade throughput, and it does not say that a short pilot cannot clear learning, fairness, or resilience.

The sketch calls the right family of functions, then does not complete the comparison. Working-together, subcontracting, both DFGs, service time, and temporal features are computed and unused. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is absent. There is no filter on `required_specialty` or `customer_segment`, and no SLA check on `outcome`. Segmentation is post-only. `len` on a handover graph counts nodes, not transitions. Rework is `rework_flag` summed over events, so the rate is not a case rate, and the `get_rework_cases_per_activity` result is ignored. Reading the flag from CSV with `astype(bool)` turns the string `"False"` into true. `filter_variants_list` is not the PM4Py argument, and a `phase` column is assumed though it is not in the log.
