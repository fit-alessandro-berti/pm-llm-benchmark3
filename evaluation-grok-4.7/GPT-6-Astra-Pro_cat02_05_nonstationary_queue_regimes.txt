The analysis is numerically reliable, regime-aware, and unusually careful about what these aggregates cannot identify. Arrival and completion totals (2,519 and 2,409), regime rates, the weekday/weekend staffing composition reversal (pooled completions about −2.7% versus about +20% within day type; standardized post rate 105/day), the April 15–17 pooling trap (8.31 completions/hour versus 4.83 then 10.04), Fano factors, the April 20 complexity split (about 95.1% SSE reduction), and the pathwise WIP bounds (45 vs 23, 57 vs 20, 59 vs 22, 65 vs 19) all check out. Little’s Law is not misapplied: median service is never treated as mean flow time, recovery is not treated as normal capacity, and the only L/λ figures are confined to short, nearly balanced early windows with an `approximate only` verdict.

Deductions are for smaller but real defects. The sandbox download link points to a file that is not supplied. “Break-even mean labor budget” of 37.4 staff-minutes per arrival is stronger than assumption A2 supports: that ratio ignores shrinkage, non-additive labor, and carry-in work sharing the same staff. The primary table gives WIP as a single mean even where the path is wide (high-mix weekdays run from 37 to 50) and several regimes have only one or two days. “Net 0” in the Little’s Law matrix is recorded flow balance, while the same answer shows global arrival–completion–WIP conservation is untenable; the early-window hedge is present but the column still reads like inventory stability. The April 7 hourly path implies that day’s opening inventory was at least 3, which is not connected back to the B₀=0 bound used later. The code is substantively correct, but `sep=r"\s+"` and English `day_name()` are fragile, and Fano and count CV are one indicator displayed twice (this dependence is at least disclosed).
