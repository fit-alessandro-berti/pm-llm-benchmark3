6.3

The final write-up keeps the right multi-mechanism story: March is mostly semantic/provenance drift, later rework and queue growth are real, and priority, seasonality, and the December confound are not collapsed into one cause. Structure, required coverage, and the directly-follows distances are largely in place. It is not numerically or methodologically reliable enough to score higher.

The central fitness claim is wrong and self-contradictory. Raw fitness falls from 0.92 to about 0.55 while mapped fitness returns to about 0.90, so mapping accounts for roughly 95% of that drop. Calling this “about 40% of the conformance loss” undercuts the later claim that March is overwhelmingly logging drift. The code’s recovery formula, `(mapped-raw)/(1-raw)`, does not match that 40% figure either.

Several published figures are corrupted or unsupported: rework “2427%,” Supplier Z “4346%,” and “12%21%+” are unreadable; the March cycle-time change is not −0.6%; and an 8–10 hour rework loop is invented. That loop also fails the answer’s own share arithmetic: a roughly 15-point rework increase at 8–10 hours is closer to a tenth of the cycle-time increase, not the stated 20–25%. Senior-assessment intensity rises much faster than high-complexity share (about 19% of complex cases in January versus about 54% in October), so attributing that spike primarily to seasonal mix is too strong. September’s partial reversal is missing from the timeline.

Logging drift is given only one change point, not the required two. Several discrimination cells list the absence of drift in columns that ask for a positive signature. “Direct-frequent-graph” is the wrong expansion of DFG. The Python is split into two blocks, embeds an incomplete monthly table, uses an invalid `resource_key` and a commented `filter_time_range` with the wrong argument names, and its threshold rule marks whole post-shock regimes rather than onsets.

Two operating rules would not have detected this episode: p90/median peaks near 2.5, below the 2.8 trigger, and mapped fitness never falls below 0.85. The monitoring design is broad and the seasonality/false-discovery discussion is sound, but those thresholds and the permanent missing-resource alert weaken it.
