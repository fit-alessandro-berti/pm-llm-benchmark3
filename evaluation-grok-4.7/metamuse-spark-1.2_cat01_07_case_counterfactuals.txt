The write-up is structurally complete and gets the main qualitative distinctions right: K-337 changes decision policy rather than merely compressing time, preliminary compliance does not replace a final review, four-eyes is kept on the compliance cases, and earlier readiness is not treated as earlier completion. The derivations repeatedly fail their own arithmetic. For K-219, a Mon 20:30–23:00 start plus 6–10 h of work does not finish Tue 04:30–08:00, and that window is not “5–8 h” before D1’s Tue 13:30 completion. Incomplete evidence on K-337 is also likely to lengthen, not merely advance, compliance and the manager’s decision, so the “one-to-one” deletion of the weekend block overstates the saving.

The 100-case analysis has the right sign and the wrong magnitude. One certified reviewer cannot absorb 100 similar high-risk reviews by growing a queue from 5–8 h to 30–40 h; the added work is on the order of hundreds of reviewer-hours. Several other figures are presented as facts without evidence: ±30–50% queue error, 40% incomplete packets, 25–40% versus 5–10% rework, a 20–30% reservation threshold, 30–40% assessment growth, and 80% power.
