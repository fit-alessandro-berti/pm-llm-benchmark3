The write-up has the required 10-part shape and a roughly right informal reading of several traces, but the formal models are not executable, contradict those verdicts, and mishandle the distinctions the task explicitly requires.

Policy choice is incomplete. Only one R5/R6 resolution is given, while two are required, and T6 is not scored under both policies. R5 is then encoded as an unconditional “opinion before every activation,” so ordinary accepted cases such as T1 are illegal in the procedural, DECLARE, and log-skeleton layers, yet the matrix marks them pass.

The procedural artifact is not a PM4Py process tree, POWL model, or accepting Petri net. It invents activities (`LowRiskPath`, `HighRiskPath`), makes clarification, legal opinion, and bank-detail change look mandatory, and cannot represent the stated AND-split of the three checks.

The DECLARE dictionary is not the template-keyed PM4Py shape. `exactly_one` is used as a binary template, `activ_freq` is not a DECLARE template, and R4 is stored as precedence while the prose describes response. Alternate response is aimed at `Classify Risk` instead of the clarification cycle. Unconditional precedence from opinion to activation also rejects every non-escalated acceptance. Chain response versus alternate response, and noncoexistence versus nonsuccession, are not actually distinguished.

The log skeleton has the six keys, but the constraints are not defensible as stated. `activ_freq` of `{1}` rejects legal zero counts (at most once allows `{0,1}`). `directly_follows` is only a low-risk happy path, so even T1 fails it. `always_after` pairs such as classify-then-manager approval are false on the high-risk and reject routes. Pair orientation is not explained. R11 is not captured: an initial bank verification satisfies “verify before release” even when a later bank change is never re-verified.

Temporal numbers and the T7 lateness arithmetic (34 h versus a 24 h bound) are right, and zeta equals 2 because each standard deviation is half the mean, so the lower bound is 0. The matrix still marks that deviation `fail` and fails the case overall, against R12 and against the claim that time deviations are warnings.

The resource code does not group by case, checks only manager approval rather than the applicable approval, and treats every activation without a prior opinion as an emergency breach, including T1. It never checks the 24-hour opinion deadline. The T4 explanation is factually wrong: Cora verified the bank account and approved; Ana captured master data.
