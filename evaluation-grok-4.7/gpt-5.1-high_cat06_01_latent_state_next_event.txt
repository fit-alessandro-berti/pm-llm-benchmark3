The write-up is structured, case-ordered, and often genuinely process-aware: primaries for C6-A through C6-E are mostly the right abstractions, C6-A is separated from C6-B for the right contextual reasons, disposition is correctly marked blocked for C6-C and C6-D, and the DFG limitation note is sound. It is not a next-event forecast that holds together under the control rules, clocks, and observation model.

The repeated defect is that ranks 2 and 3 are usually the successor of rank 1, while the probabilities are written as if they were competing next log records. C6-A says Technical Investigation Completed is expected later than Evidence Review Completed, then assigns it 0.15–0.40 as the next event about two hours after receipt, against an 8.5–9 h median, a short-staffed investigation queue, and no Evidence Received → investigation edge. C6-B calls Close — No Safety Issue a follow-up after review, then gives it 0.20–0.50; that upper bound crosses the review interval’s floor (0.45), so the ranking is not identified. Review → close has a 3.1 h median, so closure is a poor immediate-next candidate on a normal feed. C6-E does the same with another Customer Evidence Requested “if the second review finds gaps,” 40 minutes after receipt. Those activities are not yet enabled. `process_enabled: true` is right for the blocked-disposition cases and wrong for pre-review re-request and closure.

C6-E is the sharpest control-flow miss. Six complaints on one lot make Supplier Analysis mandatory and disposition wait on it; the new package contains a supplier certificate; the case has been open since yesterday, inside the 6–36 h supplier window. The state lists that obligation, then the top three omit Supplier Analysis Completed and substitute Technical Investigation Completed, which low severity and no injury do not require. C6-A overstates the same rule: two devices inside one complaint are not “two or more complaints,” and the three similar complaints are not said to share the lot.

C6-D’s alternative says Regulatory Assessment may never have been required despite high severity and injury. Rule 4 requires it, and R-R2 already opened it at 12:40. With an 80-minute elapsed time and a 2–14 h regulatory service time, completion is a fair eventual next milestone, but the migration-affected hole is mishandled. A source-system attachment with no evidence events is not “almost certain” proof that a full evidence cycle occurred, and the likely next log lines include delayed Evidence Received or Evidence Review Completed catching up. Rank 2 instead treats Customer Evidence Requested as a new forward task. Lack of a lot cluster does not make disposition more available before regulatory completion.

C6-C is the best prediction set, including `process_enabled: false`. It still cites the DFG as support for Technical Investigation → Regulatory Assessment while ignoring the larger Technical Investigation → Disposition count (119 versus 74). The case context can override that base rate; the write-up should have said so. Queue facts are used selectively: the down engineer, reportability-priority regulatory queue, supplier backlog, and 16:00 notification batch barely constrain the numbers. Several “contradicting” bullets are only caveats (normal feed, no supplier link). Intervals are honestly coarse, but the top-three midpoints are coherent only if ordered successors are treated as alternatives.
