The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and is strong on temporal reasoning, the resilience-versus-noncompliance distinction, and a concrete recovery redesign. It uses the DFGs, conformance drop (0.96/91% to 0.71/44% and the slower recovery of fitting traces), segment cycle times, and the post-close audit tail rather than treating every deviation or preserved throughput as success. The substitution/omission, recoverable/irrecoverable, and declared/silent tests are applied consistently, and the redesign covers triggers, triage, non-waivable controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, and reconciliation gates.

Several concrete defects keep it short of nearly flawless. Hospital-critical fast lane to snapshot is 920, not 1,000, and the 1,500 skip-authorization cases reach the snapshot check, not picking. Cold-chain ~269 and controlled ~162 are stated as case counts with no denominator caveat, although those rates may be subset rates. The triage score multiplies by product risk and then says risk does not change queue position; age-boost is also described as both a factor and an additive +1. The fairness index is defined as non-critical degradation divided by critical degradation, but “~2.4× p90” is pharmacy p90 versus its own baseline (118/49), not that ratio (about 1.7× in T2). The claim that only 1,500 backlog cases were tagged against ~3,000 open T2 cases overreads a T4 receive-order branch, and overlapping authorization, release, and POD gaps are added as if they were distinct exposure.
