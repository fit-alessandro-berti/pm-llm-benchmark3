The estimands, censoring rules, and case-level contributions are coherent, and the headline calculations check out. Aalen–Johansen survival and resolution incidence are about 33.9% and 29.2% in A (miss 70.8%) and 41.6% and 31.6% in B (miss 68.4%); restricted means are about 49.6 h and 51.1 h; the (72, 96] resolution increments are about 8.5% and 19.4%. Priority counts, direct standardization (68.8% vs 67.5%), transfer-as-censoring (70.8% vs 66.8%), net Kaplan–Meier survival (63.9% vs 63.8%), and the internal, end-to-end, active, and late-resolution bounds all match the table. Competing events are not treated as ordinary censoring, open cases are retained, overdue open cases are separated from cases younger than 72 h, and escalation is correctly treated as immortal time and leakage.

The score is capped by presentation and robustness gaps. Headline rates are given to 0.1 percentage point with risk sets of 4–6 and no sampling interval, even as a width. “B is worse on timely disposition” is true only for the primary Aalen–Johansen fit; the reported active-at-72 bounds overlap, so that ranking can reverse, unlike the resolution-miss ranking, which is explicitly stress-tested. “Its complement is about 63.9%” is easy to read as the net resolution probability rather than Kaplan–Meier survival. The end-to-end estimand is not given the same random-variable definition as the internal ones, and continuity of the SLA clock after transfer is assumed quietly. In the code, `active_72` and `one_minus_resolve_cif_72` change meaning across scenarios while keeping those names. The state model is a complete directly-follows list, not a diagram, and terminal gaps of 0 are not flagged as possibly definitional.
