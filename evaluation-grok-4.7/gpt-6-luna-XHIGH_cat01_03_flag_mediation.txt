The stage table is numerically exact (even-n medians, supports, and NA for stages that never start), and the estimands correctly reject natural direct/indirect effects under post-treatment review, shared queues, and cross-world incompatibility. Policy contrasts, interference, and the P2 block pilot are substantively well specified.

Deductions are for concrete faults, not taste. Section 4 lists rejects as R03, R13, and R20 and omits R09, so the 2/2 flagged–unflagged reject split is wrong. Triage resource use is perfectly separated (flag 1 is only T1/T2; flag 0 is only T3/T4); “mostly” and “M1–M3” understate that and add nothing. No flagged low-complexity case exists, and escalation given review is about 6/10 versus 2/4, but neither positivity failure nor the occurrence-versus-conditional-escalation distinction is stated. The graph reverses the definitional link (`cycle_time -> close`), gives `underlying_risk` a direct arrow into a score-generated flag, and leaves unobserved `Q_*` and `priority_lane` unmarked. E4 holds queue history fixed while claiming the indirect effect of waiting, which blocks the congestion path P3 would change. The negative-control example, pre-triage processing time, is itself the priority-lane outcome. The pilot’s primary “review wait among eligible cases” is undefined when P2 prevents review. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with `resource_key`, which is not a parameter of that function, so the required script fails before either DFG call. Separable effects are never explicitly accepted or rejected, despite the display-versus-routing distinction being the point of P1.
