7.5

The audit is thorough and mostly reliable: every global feature, sequence pattern, and local driver is covered; obvious post-closure leakage and the subtler SQL, snapshot, mutable-score, and closure-time team defects are identified; queue length, prefix age, and registration-time complexity are kept; and prediction, causation, actionability, and fairness are separated. The validation suite and remediation plan hit the required checks.

It is not nearly flawless. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is labeled process-impossible in the same way as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, although only the latter is normatively anomalous and timestamp-ambiguous; an appeal followed by another settlement decision is allowed later and is simply unavailable at `t0`. `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is the ordinary path, not a process violation. The Case Y narrative repeats the leaked future path and calls the first decision Partial, while an appeal only implies Partial or Deny. Value agreement on `final_handler_team` is treated as if those rows were clean, even though “no later reassignment” is itself future information. The prior-claim bug is minimized as roughly 0–1 claims, `total_case_duration_hours` is overstated as the settlement label itself, week 52 is asserted to be the holiday week, and local SHAP directions from the leaked model are presented as credible drivers without noting that those attributions can move once the leaked features are removed.
