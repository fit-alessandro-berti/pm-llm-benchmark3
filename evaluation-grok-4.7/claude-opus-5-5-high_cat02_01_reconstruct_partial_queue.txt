7.5

The reconstruction is unusually careful and mostly numerically reliable: station sojourn is correctly separated from the wait/service split, open and blank cases are retained, missing starts are bounded rather than silently filled, and the main busy-time, cohort-wait, Little’s-law identity, and capacity figures check out against the event table. The structural conclusion that S2 is the capacity bottleneck while the visible queue sits at S1 until about 12:55 is supported and is stable across the reported imputations. Format compliance, including the PM4Py projection labels, is complete.

Those strengths do not make the write-up nearly flawless.

- Assumption A2 reverses the arrival-clock test. A reported 5-minute wait constrains how early the arrival clock can run (a more-early clock would make true wait negative). A late clock inflates true wait and is not bounded by those two cases. The later ±5-minute sensitivity is still usable, but the detectability claim is the wrong way around.
- At 09:25, when I1 is released, the S1 queue holds P07, P08, P09, and P10, and P11 arrives on the same stamp. “3 cases waiting” is false. P07 is still the oldest, so the imputation preference survives, but the stated queue size does not.
- The S2 flow check for 10:00–14:00 does not close. Entries minus completions are 14 − 11 = 3, while WIP2 moves from 4 at 10:00 to 6 at 14:00. The snapshot at 10:00 already contains the boundary entry P07, so the comparable increase is +2. The same boundary slip does not appear in the S1 check.
- “Q1 > Q2 until 12:55” skips the tie Q1 = Q2 = 4 on [12:50, 12:55). “WIP2 > WIP1 from 12:50” is not continuous either: at 13:00 both station WIP levels equal 5. The strict Q2 > Q1 result from 12:55 onward does hold.
- The 0-minute C1 handoff at 11:00 is not known to be conflict-free. With ±2.5-minute rounding, that reported gap is compatible with a true overlap of up to about 5 minutes. “Rounding cannot produce” the systematic 5-minute gaps is also too strong; it can, if true gaps are small and the fractional parts line up. The modal-5-minute interpretation remains reasonable, and the h = 0 versus h = 5 capacity comparison is the right sensitivity.
- “Never idle when work existed” after 08:35 overstates the logged record. The 5-minute gaps, the 10-minute C2 gap before P15, and the 10:50–11:15 C2 hole are idle time with work waiting. A13 is stronger than the data require: 10:50–10:55 is exactly the usual hand-over, and it lands on the outage. Lost work relative to a 10:55 start is about 20 minutes, not 25. The later +8-minute translation is too precise for a non-steady, two-server shock.

Smaller reservations: the 28-gap count is right only if the outage-straddling C2 transition is dropped, which is not stated; “≈100% occupied” still leaves the extra 5 minutes inside the 13:20–13:30 gap; and the Kaplan–Meier slice `[:20]` is correct only because of row order. Completed-service means also omit the two open S2 jobs, which is disclosed but not carried into μ.

What remains intact is the part that matters. Logged utilization near 80% versus hand-over-inclusive occupancy near 100%, μ_S1 ≈ 3.6/h against μ_S2 ≈ 2.8/h, the 12:55 queue crossover, saturation after start-up, and the outage as a persistent shock rather than the source of the overload all survive the early/central/late bounds and the logical extremes. The deducted points are for concrete false statements, not for the overall process-mining frame.
