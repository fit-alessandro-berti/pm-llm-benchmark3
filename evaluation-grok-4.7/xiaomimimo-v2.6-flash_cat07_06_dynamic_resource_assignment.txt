The write-up is complete and readable, and most case-level choices respect the hard skill, supervision, approval, and overtime rules. It does not, however, hold together as one auditable policy.

The scoring rule cannot produce the published queue. Continuity is weighted so heavily, and workload so lightly, that C006 stays with Iris and C010 with Bruno. C013 goes to Amal (eligibility 3), not Jonas. SLA urgency is identical for every candidate, so it never affects who is chosen. The table nevertheless reassigns those cases and presents the formula as the reason. A worker or auditor replaying the stated rule would not get the stated outcome. The security exception also returns a hardcoded `R3` rather than the selected resource.

The 20% training claim is not earned. Only C009 matches the assignee’s documented training priority. C015 is performed by Hugo, whose priority is approval; Eva and Jonas have security priority but skill 0, so they cannot perform the review. Indicator I9, as defined later, would not count that case. The result is 1 of 9 normal/low assignments, about 11%, while the text claims 22%. The notes on C010 and C012 are not reconciled with that gap, including the fact that nobody with an approval training priority has approval skill above 0.

Capacity is not actually controlled. Hours remaining never enter eligibility or scoring. Farid is given both tight urgent cases plus C010. Jonas is given three queue items and a shadowing role on 6.0 hours. Eva keeps two high-priority cases and is also asked to shadow. David, with 3.0 hours, keeps two cases. The comparison then says David is “the only specialist,” which is false: Celine is also diagnosis 3, and Celine, Hugo, and Iris are rework 3. It also suggests first-available would further load David, Bruno, and Iris, who are already the most loaded, so that rule would pick them last.

The audit design would falsely fail the policy’s own C001: skill-mismatch counts every security assignment below skill 3 and does not exempt documented supervision. `filter_case_performance` is given a percentile API it does not have, and assignment state is never updated as the queue is processed. The prose comparison does discuss trade-offs and avoids crowning a single optimum, but several of those comparisons rest on the errors above.
