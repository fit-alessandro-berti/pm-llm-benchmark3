7.4

The pipeline is conceptually strong: the decision tree separates the six required duplicate classes, the event rulings match the source facts, and the ordering policy correctly treats domain, within-source sequence, and object lifecycle as semantic while keeping ingestion and stable keys as tie-breaks only. Preserving `original_timestamp`, refusing to drop e05 or e14, splitting e08 from e09, and explaining why `have_common_object=True` still merges M1 with M2 are the right calls. The audit schema and failure-mode list are specific enough to implement.

The sensitivity numbers are wrong. The relation rows sum to 44, not 47: O1 on all 17 events, plus 5 item, 9 package, 4 delivery, 6 invoice, and 3 payment links. Verified merges remove e04’s three links and e16’s two, so the conservative log has 39 relations, not 42. Absorbing e07 into e06 then deletes e07’s duplicate O1 and P1 links, leaving 37, not an unchanged 42. The claim that the e06/e07 merge removes no E2O row confuses “no new object appears” with “the relation count stays the same.”

Section 7 also treats one linear extension as the DFG story. e05 versus e06, and e11 versus the PSP payments, stay incomparable under the stated constraints, so Pick→Pack and Issue Invoice→Authorize Payment reverse in other admissible orders. That instability is only partly acknowledged in section 3 and is missing from the metric comparison. Fact 8 is stronger than the “may add an edge” wording: the ERP summary is described as generated after the payment messages, which puts those messages before e15 when that fact is accepted.

The implementation does not carry the stated merge policy. `merge_verified_pairs` deletes the absorbed row and stores values in an in-memory list; it never namespaces scanner fields onto e03 or applies the billing-versus-ERP authority map. Cloning the evidence-based log and calling the helper again replaces `merge_audit`, so the e03/e04 and e11/e16 provenance is dropped. `call_supported` silently discards any argument that is not a named signature parameter. `ocel_drop_duplicates(..., have_common_object=True)` is applied to the whole log while the comment calls that scenario exact-duplicate removal. That flag is an overlap test, which is the failure mode the text itself warns against. The topological order is a hardcoded list, so cycle detection and the published precedence rules are not actually executed.

Event counts of 15 and 14 are arithmetically consistent with the chosen merges, and the aggressive 10–13 range is at least bounded; a same-activity, same-timestamp, shared-object component collapse of this table is 10 events. Those correct structural choices do not offset the relation-count error or the gap between the provenance rules and the code.
