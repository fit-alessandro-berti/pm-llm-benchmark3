F08 and F09 are wrong even under that arrival-to-join reading (70 and 80 versus 75 and 85). The counterfactuals miss the slack logic the task asks for. Cutting A’s service by 10 minutes saves only 5 minutes of join time on F01, because B finishes at 08:55, not 10 minutes on both A-critical cases. A 50% cut in B’s queue is 15 minutes on F12, not 17.5, and the gain is capped by the runner-up on F05 (10 minutes) and F10 (5 minutes). F01 and F11 cannot become B-critical by speeding B. `final_resource` is never used, so capacity masking is not tested: several downstream waits occur while an F resource is idle (F01, much of F02 and F03), while others line up with a busy server (F05, F09, F11). Adding capacity therefore does not remove every downstream minute.

The Python block does not meet the spec. It never computes synchronization waits, implements only the first counterfactual, and leaves the other two as a comment, with no per-case savings.
