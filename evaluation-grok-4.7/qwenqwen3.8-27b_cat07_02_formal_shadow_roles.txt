The role analysis is strong and largely faithful to the evidence: it separates automation, permanent front office, temporary staff, technical work, security, commercial work, supervision, quality, and coordination; it uses complexity, substitution episodes, and authority rather than raw frequency; and it treats Giorgio, Mateo, and Erik as shadow or bridging roles with both benefits and risks. The required sections, table, alternatives, and operational cautions are all present and mostly precise.

The score is held down by defects in the required code and a few overstatements. Merging `case_meta` back onto `df` duplicates `case_complexity` and the other case attributes, so later aggregations on `case_complexity` would fail. The “security wait” is time from case start to A6, not wait for the security review. `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with `attribute_values` rather than PM4Py’s `values`, and activity labels such as `"A9 Approval"` do not match the names used in the prose. Thresholds for complexity and escalation are hardcoded as if they were known. “Very few medium” understates Kai and Lina’s medium-complexity volume, and Mateo’s filtering is said to have caused the two multi-day critical waits when the evidence only places those waits alongside that filtering.
