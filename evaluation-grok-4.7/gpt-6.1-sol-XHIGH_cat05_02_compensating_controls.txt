The analysis is unusually careful: it separates nominal FEP-1 from the authorization objective, treats ED-1 as a conjunctive permission, refuses to treat a clean outcome or a logged activity as sufficiency, and gets the case arithmetic (24 minutes, 35/80 minutes, ~19/22/25 hours, ~50 hours, settlement-versus-release clocks) right. Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6 are strong, and the code is a real evidence engine rather than a decorative filter call.

The score is held down by internal inconsistencies and a few evidence overstatements.

AP-207 is labeled OBJECTIVE_MET even though section 4 says a post-release ratification is preventive only if a settlement veto actually exists, and that mechanism is listed as missing. The chronology is compatible with a hold; it does not establish one. That comes close to treating Manager_Ratify plus a good review as sufficient.

AP-206 is classified INDETERMINATE mainly because the recorded token is two minutes after Prepare_Payment. That is a real ED-1 permission defect, which the answer itself says is not the same thing as failure of the control objective. m11 approved before release, which is a stronger authorization boundary than AP-207’s post-release ratification, yet AP-207 is MET and AP-206 is indeterminate. Missing review contents justify uncertainty; using the token log as the objective verdict blurs the distinction the earlier sections draw.

AP-204’s OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET is a permitted judgment, but it sits awkwardly against the answer’s own “ineffective for the material threat” test: a fabricated receipt on a EUR 220,000 payment passed the second person, and detection landed near four days later, after ordinary recall was likely gone. High severity and the upgrade path to OBJECTIVE_FAILED keep this from being a clear error.

Smaller faults: calling the eight-month AP-208 vendor “longstanding” is inaccurate, even though the ED-1 failure list correctly omits vendor age. In the code, unstated shared-workstation and bank-change facts are stored as False rather than unknown, and “new vendor” is stored as a definite failure of the 180-day test. m09’s performance-rating authority over u23 is left as an open independence question, softer than MG-4’s unresolved-conflict rule, though the case still fails for many independent reasons. AP-206’s approval at 13:04 before validation at 13:05 is not discussed. L3 is unknown for every manager, so AP-203’s OBJECTIVE_MET is slightly ahead of the MG-4 definition; the answer does disclose that and does not pretend to certify ED-1.

AP-202, AP-205, AP-208, and AP-209 are well judged, including the refusal to treat u12/u13 repetition as proof of collusion. The PM4Py block meets the specified structure, labels its output as non-verdict evidence, and defensively handles filter polarity. No canonical label was required; the deductions are for contradictions with the answer’s own timing and sufficiency rules, plus a handful of evidence-coding and wording inaccuracies.
