The causal framework is serious and often well aimed: availability is separated from fast-path intensity, median and p90 are kept apart, rollout is treated as selected and confounded, interference through the fraud desk is explicit, and the rejected headline “reduces cycle time by X%” is the right bottom line. The required tables, estimands, threat list, and Delta-only randomized phase-in are substantively in the right family. The grade is held down because the quantitative core does not audit, and several of those bad figures are reused as evidence.

Section 5’s comparison table is internally inconsistent and, for Gamma, uses the wrong calendar controls. Alpha’s March-to-April/May median change is about −1.0%, not 1.0%; Alpha rework is about −4.5%, not 4.5; control CSAT falls by about 0.0675, and that minus sign is omitted. With the printed signs, Alpha median 1.0 versus +11.2 does not produce 12.2, and Alpha CSAT +0.035 versus 0.0675 does not produce +0.103. Beta’s CSAT contrast is printed as +0.014; adopter minus the same control change is about +0.058. Escalation mixes level changes with percent changes: Alpha’s post-window change is about +1.25 pp, or about +13% relatively, not +2.0, yet the third entry behaves like a gap in percent changes. Those gaps are then called 8.4–13.6 pp excesses. The actual rate gaps versus Delta and Epsilon are on the order of 1 pp. Gamma’s July–August row repeats the April–May control changes (+11.2%, +15.1%, +8.9, +2.7, 0.0675). July–August controls are a different path, including a large never-treated p90 and rework increase after May and a partial summer median recovery. Gamma CSAT is also off: July–August versus March is about +0.06 and August versus March is +0.08, not +0.07 and +0.09.

Those figures are not confined to a scratch table. Section 7 cites 12.2, 7.4, and 17.3 as the strongest median support, and 8.4–13.6 pp as the strongest escalation support. The escalation scenario upper end of +12 pp has the same unit problem: observed post-March moves are roughly +1 to +2 pp, on baselines near 9–11. The initial-window FTE line says −5.9% for Alpha over the same window as the +17.1% arrival mean; April–May FTE averages 33 versus March’s 34, about −2.9%. −5.9% is the August staffing level. March is called the immediate pre-month for every adopter; for Gamma the immediate pre-month is June. Decreases in the adopter table are often unsigned while increases carry a plus, so Beta’s early median worsening is marked and Alpha’s small median decline is not.

The claim ladder repeats the median mistake. Beta’s April–May median is higher than March (about 48.2 versus 46.4). “Alpha and Beta’s early median improvements” is false for Beta. “By August … 25–50%” also blends April’s 25% floor with August levels of 48%, 50%, and 32%. Section 3’s “raw median improvement in Alpha and Beta” has the same early-window problem. The threat table’s “only two adopting branches” is wrong; three branches adopt.

The code is more careful than the prose and would not reproduce Section 5. It validates the panel, separates the five outcomes, builds relative time, clusters by branch, and labels the regressions as non-causal. The event-study comment is still wrong: only relative month −1 is described as the reference, but Gamma’s relative months −6 and −5 are also coded as zero, so January and February are forced into that reference. The fitted model is a pooled branch-and-calendar event study, not the cohort-specific matched design argued as primary. With five clusters that specification is a descriptive diagnostic at best; the answer says so, then does not show that the printed contrasts came from the script.

Estimands, mechanisms, and the rollout design are the parts that hold up. E1 through E8 distinguish policy availability, selected use, tails, rework, escalation, CSAT, dynamics, and case-mix heterogeneity, and the interference paragraph is real. The mechanism table correctly treats fast-path share as substitution until capacity, queue time, and loop location are observed. Selection, nonparallel trends, staffing, anticipation, nonresponse, aggregation, and dynamic treatment versions are all present with usable consequences. `expand with an embedded evaluation`, Delta first, concealed within-branch randomization, case-level queues, fraud-desk spillover checks, and pre-registered harm stops is a coherent learning policy. It still underweights transport: Delta is the easiest untreated branch, so a clean Delta result would say little about Epsilon’s higher complexity and longer cycles. The guardrail of “p90 no more than 10% above baseline” is also an operational threshold, not an identified effect.

Net: the essay knows what cannot be claimed and designs a better next experiment. Its reported contrasts, escalation magnitude, Gamma benchmark, and one ladder bullet are not reliable enough for the quantitative standard this task sets.
