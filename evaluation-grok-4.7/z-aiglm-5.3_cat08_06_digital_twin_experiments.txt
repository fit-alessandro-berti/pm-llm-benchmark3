7.5

The design is complete and unusually rigorous: terminating seasonal runs, latent objections, service/wait kept apart, cross-case coupling, common random numbers, and an explicit non-optimality rule. It is not nearly flawless. Several concrete errors keep it well below a top score.

The feasibility table is the right calibration idea, but the hours are medians times volume. Planning, fire, environmental, and legal are strongly right-skewed (p90 is about 4–6× the median), so mean touch time is the workload measure and the stated multipliers understate load. “480 checks/week” and “~180 fire reviews” are unexplained, and “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is treated as 48 h/week without resolving whether FTE is headcount or weekly labor. Clerks are called capacity-feasible at 200 h while Monday portal support is only a parenthetical.

`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)` with float aggregates, same shape as `discover_dfg`. The skeleton calls `.items()` and then `stats.get("mean")`, so the demo path does not match PM4Py. Variants use `.agg(" > ".join")`, which is not a pandas reducer and fails before that. Council-week dates are merged into every pool’s exception calendar, so clerks, planners, fire, and inspectors also stop; the problem and §1 restrict that blackout to legal, with urgent exceptions. `seed_offset` is commented as common random numbers, but an offset desynchronizes streams.

Bidirectional DFG edges are treated as proof of concurrency. That footprint also arises from length-2 loops and rework, so it does not by itself “correct” the tree. The screening design is written `2^(94)` rather than a \(2^{9-4}\) fraction. Welch plots are prescribed after the text correctly says there is no steady state; seasonality and initialization bias are confounded in that diagnostic. The adoption rule drops any pool whose mean utilization leaves 50–85%, so a policy that removes inspection load (D2, D4) can be rejected for creating slack. Holidays and council meetings are stated to change the public-notice period itself; the model only blocks resources and keeps a fixed 14-day calendar minimum.

SLA gates (21/60/90/150) and a case-level correlation of 0.6 are policy choices, not log results, and 90% versus 95% intervals are mixed. The experiment matrix, parameter-set propagation, holdout plan, and refusal to treat objections as data are otherwise sound.
