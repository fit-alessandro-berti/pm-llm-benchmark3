The assessment is substantively strong and constraint-compliant: raw gaps are not treated as discrimination, case mix is not treated as a full excuse, `risk_score_band` is treated as suspect, support steps are analyzed as burden, and no remedy removes those steps. All eight required sections are present, the scenario figures are mostly used accurately, and the bottom line is properly split between structural burden (B/C), audit-dependent risk routing (D), and indeterminate residual approval differences.

The score is held down by accumulated defects.

Clarity breaks in the decision rules. In §2 the inferential bullets are ungrammatical fragments (“controls  legitimate routing”, “paths  structural procedural unfairness”, “sensitivity  concerning”, “bias  indeterminate”). The same missing connectives appear in the waits and handovers (“Language Support  Eligibility”, “general  bilingual  general”). Those sentences are the classification rules for disparity versus legitimate routing, so the ambiguity is material.

Loose quantitative claims. The +2.6 and +4.1 day adjusted cycle-time gaps are said to “align” with the 3.8- and 4.7-day support waits. Those waits apply only to the minority of cases on V4/V5, so they cannot by themselves account for the average residuals. “Especially for B and D” is attached to supervisor review, document requests, and appeals together, but document requests are elevated for B and C (+9.5 and +4.2 pp), not D (+1.5). V2 is described as a B/C funnel even though it is common in every group (A 24%, D 26%).

Methodological overreach in the PM4Py plan. `discover_log_skeleton` on the pooled log does not test attribute-conditional rules such as “accommodation requested ⇒ Accommodation Review” or “language need ⇒ Language Support Contact.” Those checks require filtering to the flagged cases, then an eventually/response check. As written, group-level skeleton fitness mostly restates different activity mixes. Several calls are also non-executable rather than clearly marked pseudocode: `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time` with `case_id_column` / `activity_column` / `resource_column`, `filter_paths_performance` without thresholds, and `pm4py.conformance.fitness_log_skeleton`. The log has only `time:timestamp`, and the plan never says that service-versus-wait insertion is unreliable without start timestamps.

Smaller evidence gaps. Group B’s assignment to three overloaded regional officers is dropped from the resource narrative. The plan’s example stratum (high risk, incomplete, online) is sparse, with small-cell risk mentioned only later as a dashboard side effect.

§4–§6 and the closing judgment are the best parts: capacity scarcity, endogenous completeness, historical risk, handover friction, and appeal feedback are separated cleanly, and each mitigation has a real trade-off. The defects are local, not a wrong overall conclusion, but they are enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
