7.5

The audit is well structured and gets most of the central calls right: full-trace and post-closure fields are removed, `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is treated as a later-legitimate path rather than an impossible order, queue length and elapsed time are kept with their signs, region is retained as a proxy rather than deleted or called causal, and Case Z is invalidated. The five required sections, the table coverage, and the standards split among prediction, causation, actionability, and fairness are all present. Under a strict reading it is still not nearly flawless, because several of its own findings are contradicted in the revised explanations and one required process distinction is mis-assigned.

The most serious credibility failure is Case Y. `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` is listed as a credible driver while the write-up itself says the extraction-date SQL makes that value untrustworthy. A count that may include claims opened after `t0` cannot support a revised at-`t0` explanation. The Case Y narrative also restates the leaked future path — document cycles, an appeal, and a second settlement decision — after being told not to reproduce future outcomes. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` are promoted to credible drivers even though their own lineage notes only say to confirm availability. Directions inherited from the contaminated local SHAP explanation are presented as the repaired account, without stating that those attributions are not valid once leaked covariates are removed.

Section 2 blurs a distinction the audit was required to make. At `t0`, the first `Settlement Decision Recorded` and `Claim Closed` are future-possible, not impossible; appeal is conditionally future-possible only after a later Partial or Deny. The check instead puts all three in an “Impossible” bucket, while still correctly treating the backwards closure-before-assessment pattern as a hard violation. That conflates “not yet observed” with “normatively forbidden.”

Smaller inaccuracies accumulate. `total_case_duration_hours` is described as post-closure leakage, but not as near-label leakage relative to a 10-day breach target, which is why its importance is circular. The admissible document-request count at this prefix is structurally zero on conforming traces, yet the remediation still reads as if a prefix count remains an early-warning feature, and the text calls that count “unknown.” Section 1 says appeal follows a settlement decision, omitting the Partial/Deny restriction that Section 2 later states. “Fraud propensity” is attached to prior claims without a fairness caveat. For Case X, 1.2 hours of elapsed time is large remaining slack; calling it “modest slack” confuses a small SHAP contribution with the feature’s meaning. The residual “other features” mass (+0.21 / +0.20 / +0.17) is never flagged as a place where unlisted leaked globals can still sit. The validation plan omits a time-based split despite week-level policy and holiday confounding, and it treats importance collapse as confirmation of leakage.

What keeps the score in this range is the quality of the rest: the ±5-minute queue tolerance, the 65% team coincidence, the nightly document snapshot, the mutable risk score, the missing sequence index, employee-experience as-of extraction date, DFG-versus-normative limits, repeated-decision semantics, and the expectation that calibration will fall once leakage is removed.
