6.5

The write-up is well structured, hits the required headings, and gets the main regime story right: weekday versus weekend, a discrete outage, recovery as backlog clearance rather than a high-capacity steady state, a 20 April mix break, and a 29 April staffing change. Most regime means that were checked (baseline ~100/100, high-mix pre-intervention ~114/101 and about −13 net, post-intervention ~115/121 and about +6, pooled arrivals ~90, WIP ~30, staff ~5.1, completion ratio ~0.96, hourly Fano/CV on the four vectors) are accurate. Little’s Law is not fed median service time, open-hour units are stated, and the best windows are marked only approximate. That is real queueing competence.

It is not nearly flawless. The serious failures are internal contradictions and unused conflicts in the supplied evidence.

The backlog account does not close. From 20–28 April, arrivals exceed completions by about 106 cases (~94 on the seven weekdays), yet mean WIP only moves from 37 to 50. Over the full series the arrival–completion gap is about +110 while WIP ends near where it started (~18 versus ~19). Section 7 still says the high-mix regime lets backlog grow by “more than 90 cases.” That equates net flow with system-content change after the WIP series has already falsified a closed inventory identity. Other exits, a narrower WIP definition, or bad aggregates are all possible; none is raised. The same gap also weakens the Little’s Law rows, because \(L\) and \(\lambda\) are not shown to describe one conserved process.

The day count in that sentence is wrong: 20–28 April is seven weekdays, or nine calendar days, not nine weekdays.

The peak claim is unchecked against the hourly vectors the answer itself analyzes. `peak_08_10_pct` is defined as 08:00–09:59, and high-mix days are described as putting 45–50% of arrivals there. On 2026-04-21 that column is 47, but bins 08–09 and 09–10 are 3+8 = 11 of 115 (~10%). The ordinary day 2026-04-07 is 31 in the column and 5+12 = 17 of 101 (~17%) in those bins. The burst the answer correctly flags (34, then 24) is not in 08:00–09:59. Section 7’s morning-smoothing recommendation therefore targets a window the only high-mix hourly trace does not support. That conflict should have been a named threat to inference.

The change-point paragraph is false as written. A rule that flags \(|\Delta\text{staff}|>0.5\) also flags every weekend drop from ~6 to 2.5 and the Monday return. It does not “recover these three dates.” The code implements that broader rule, so the script contradicts section 5.

Smaller consistency and inference problems add up. Lag-1 autocorrelation of `complex_pct` ≈ 0.93 is mostly the level shift, not within-regime dependence, but it is reported as serial dependence. The prose Fano ≈ 0.66 matches a sample variance; `np.var` in the code is the population variance (~0.63). Service “medians” are means of daily medians, not pooled quantiles. High-mix weekends are pooled in the section 2 table and split in the code. The post-intervention weekday cell is labeled high confidence on three days whose WIP falls from 43 to 32, with the intervention time unknown inside 29 April. Section 6 correctly calls 4.7–5.3 h not defensible, then section 7 uses “sojourns well above four hours” as if it were evidence. “Demonstrates that the extra FTE was necessary” is stronger than a three-day, confounded before/after contrast. The outage-day service jump (median 31, p90 68 at only 25% complex) is reported and barely interpreted, so mix is over-credited for the earlier service shift. Nominal load is never bounded: if mean service were near the 25-minute median, baseline utilization at 6 FTE would be only moderate, and a ~2.4 h sojourn would need a heavy tail, multi-step wait, or a failed steady-state assumption. The answer treats the gap as straightforward queue wait.

Format, outage handling, and the refusal to treat recovery or the pooled mean as a staffing standard are the parts that hold the score up. The unreconciled inventory identity, the missed hourly-versus-peak contradiction, and the false heuristic claim are why it stays well below an excellent mark.
