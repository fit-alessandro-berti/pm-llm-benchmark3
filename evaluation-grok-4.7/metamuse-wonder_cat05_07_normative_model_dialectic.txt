7.4

The brief is complete, well-structured, and dialectically serious: both sides concede what they must, every case is classified with a stated reason, and the layered revision matches the requested headings. It is not nearly flawless. Several evidence and model-semantics errors are internal, not merely matters of taste.

SUP-702 is mishandled against the answer’s own burden. PD-3 requires sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation. That validation is absent from the trace; the matrix lists it as missing evidence. Section 3 and the judgment nevertheless say every PD-3 condition was proved and classify the case as a high-confidence justified exception. “Eligibility fields complete” does not establish the validation activity. Under the stated burden, an exception must be proved contemporaneously; this verdict does not meet it.

SUP-710 treats prior-certification validity as an open hinge. The case statement says the certification remained valid. That is stipulated, so it cannot be the fact that would reverse the conclusion.

FD-7’s two-business-day deadline is attached to retrospective manual approval in the architecture and overlay table. The directive puts that deadline on quality review (with daily monitoring as a separate duty). The spend cap runs until retrospective approval, with no two-day approval clock. The error is repeated, so it is a mis-stated norm, not a slip of phrasing.

The revised POWL regresses relative to M0. Standard, auto, and emergency routes all end in activation; `Reject_Supplier` exists only after sanctions. A quality failure after both checks has no terminal reject path, which M0 could express and a real onboarding model needs. The `Request_More_Info` loop is `checks (Request_More_Info, checks)*`. That language excludes SUP-707 (sanctions, two info requests, then quality, with no repeated checks), the very desirable trace used to justify the loop. A partial order of the two checks concurrent with `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)` would admit that trace; the write-up calls the stricter loop the approximation and does not say that M1 still rejects the motivating case. The auto route also forces quality after activation, while PD-3’s “may” allows deferral up to two business days and does not forbid earlier quality. `SilentTransition` is constructed and never placed in the model. Reactivation is promised as a defined route in section 6 and then omitted from the executable model without a section 7 limitation.

SUP-715 is the split the task warned against. The preamble says neither rule system dominates; the table’s “dominant element” is control-flow compliance, while also calling the missing disclosure a breach. “None was recorded” is given; suggesting an off-log disclosure as the live uncertainty softens a stipulated fact. SUP-704 is cited as an example of disciplined pilot use even though it is an FD-7 case. SUP-708’s manual approval also follows activation, an independent CS-9 breach that the classification line collapses into the sanctions point.

The prosecution/defense split, the matrix, expiry handling for SUP-705 and SUP-714, SUP-703 and SUP-712, the CRM missingness argument, and the separation of POWL from data, identity, and authority are strong. Those merits do not cancel the traceability contradictions or the control-flow holes in the proposed norm.
