The memo is a strong, usable decision document: it chooses a tightened hybrid, refuses both national rollout and a blanket rollback, and keeps the segment split, conformance failures, and incomplete cost boundary in view. The evidence table really does separate facts, interpretations, and assumptions across more than eight items, and the five metric conflicts are explained with the right process-mining objects (fitness versus precision, alignments, the directly-follows shifts, temporal profile, and the handover versus working-together networks).

The score is held down by concrete slips and a few places where the reasoning is tighter than the evidence. Complaints went from 6.1% to 9.8%, a rise of about three-fifths; “nearly rose by half” is the wrong magnitude. The median-versus-tail account relies on mix and segment effects and never tests the measurement threat built into the redesign itself: moving activation after billing start can shorten time-to-billing for ordinary cases while pushing failure into the post-billing tail. In the cost conflict, manual activation is grouped with work outside the EUR 161 boundary, although that boundary is defined as “before complaint handling”; only complaint and revisit handling is clearly on the far side, and visible cost still fell while manual activation and second visits rose. “Restore the permit check” treats a move-on-model (expected in the model, absent in the log) as a removed control. The memo also states the resilience story as an effect, while the source only says fewer handovers may have reduced resilience.

The value-of-information ranking is detailed but misaligned with the decision it is supposed to serve. M1 and M6 are placed first even though survey skipping is already paused and may not be what produced the simple-house gain. M4 is described as the result most likely to reverse the “keep simple houses” leg, yet it is ranked third, and M5, which decides whether coordinator ownership is safe to keep, is fifth. The operating recommendation is also slightly incomplete on the control path: non-simple cases lose the single coordinator, but the ownership model that replaces that role is not specified.
