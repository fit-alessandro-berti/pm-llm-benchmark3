6.5

The fork-join definitions are sound, and most of the arithmetic holds. `join_ready` is the latest branch completion, elapsed time is \(T_c=U_c+FJ_c+D_c+F_c\), and summing each branch’s queue, service, and synchronization wait replicates \(FJ_c\) three times. Worked cases F02, F04, F07, and F11 match the clocks. Critical counts are A 2, B 8, C 2. The three direct counterfactuals are right: 15, 92.5, and 88 minutes in total, including the zero-benefit cases and the handoffs where A becomes limiting. That is not enough for a high score.

F01 is wrong in the main table. Upstream time is \(08{:}10-07{:}55=15\) minutes, and total flow time is \(09{:}28-07{:}55=93\). The table gives 35 and 113. The claimed upstream mean of about 21 minutes is just that 35 averaged with eleven correct 20s; the timestamp mean is 19.6. The Python would print 15 and 93, so the prose contradicts the script. Inside the bad row, \(35+50+8+20=113\), but those two inputs disagree with the log.

Section 3 groups F10, F11, and F12 as 20-minute downstream waits. F10 and F11 are 20; F12 is 10. F12’s 125-minute flow time is a 75-minute B fork-join plus a short final wait, so the example attributes that tail to the wrong stage.

`final_resource` is loaded and then ignored. F1/F2 alternate by case. A large part of `final_start-join_ready` occurs while the assigned server is already free: all of the wait on F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, and F12, and part of it on F04 and F10. Only some waits equal the previous same-resource completion (F05, F08, F09, F11, and slices of F04 and F10). Section 4.3 and redesign 2 still price a 50% cut of every downstream interval (about 7.3 minutes per case) as comparable to halving B’s queue. Section 7 later says the interval might be a release rule. The log already supports splitting blocking from unexplained delay, and that split is missing.

Overlaps also bound capacity from below. Four B intervals are open together around 10:52, and C overlaps itself during the long C queues. Section 7 says branch capacity cannot be identified. A lower bound can. Redesign 1’s “add B equipment” story is therefore ahead of the intervals, even though it is labeled as a hypothesis.

B is the right first branch: highest critical frequency, highest queue-plus-service (about 56.7 versus A’s flat 50), and the 75-minute tails. Ranking C strictly above A is weaker. They tie at 2/12 critical, and A’s mean synchronization wait is about 11 minutes against C’s 30.4, so A sits closer to `join_ready`. The text itself says C mainly creates synchronization waste, then still ranks C second for control of completion time.

Smaller faults: the case table and printed columns omit per-case synchronization waits; the triple-count algebra drops the \(\max\{0,\cdot\}\) used in the queue definition; under the B-queue cut, F06 ends in a tie with A but is described as B remaining critical; “or becomes critical” misnames the cases where A takes over.
