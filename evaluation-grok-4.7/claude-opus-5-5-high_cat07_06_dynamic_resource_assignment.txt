The design is complete, constraint-aware, and often sharp, but several quantitative claims and the audit code do not hold up.

The recommended plan respects the hard safety rules. Urgent and high cases go to skill 2–3, Security Reviews go to skill 3, Final Approvals are finalized by skill 3, high overtime-risk workers receive no new work today, and the skill-0 training priorities (Eva and Jonas on Security, David and Hugo on Approval) are correctly treated as certification gaps rather than assignable work. The worker-facing explanation, reason codes, contestable close calls, and Goodhart cautions are substantive. The first-available trace for C001–C016 matches a lowest-open-case, skill-above-0, lower-id tie-break.

The comparison numbers are not reliable. On the specialist-only table as written, performer changes are C001, C003, C005, C006, C008, C009, C010, C011, C014, and C016: 10 of 15 owned cases, not 8. Continuity is also counted more generously for the proposed policy than for the baselines. C006, C010, and C014 change owner, and the performer also changes on C001 and C012, yet the comparison reports “3 owner changes.” Section 1 says an already-owned case is not a “new assignment”; Section 4 then counts Celine’s owned C007 toward the specialist-only cap of 4 and calls 5 cases a breach. The first-available simulation claims an hours check, but Celine is given C001, C007, C011, and C015 (about 7.25 h against 6.0 h remaining) with no one skipped. “Skill mismatch on 8 cases” is unexplained: that trace gives skill 1 on ten cases. The fairness range “about 33% to 80%” omits Bruno and Iris, who are planned at about 0%.

Training compliance is overstated. The 22% figure is 2 of 9 normal/low cases, and C012 is conditional on Bruno’s overtime flag being clear next shift. If that fallback fires, only C009 remains (11%), and no other normal/low case can legally serve a documented training priority. The repair loop cannot restore the quota. Eva’s dispatch order (C005 before C016) also cuts against the policy’s own slack ordering: C016 has 7 h left and C005 has 8 h.

C001’s feasibility depends on shrinking the stated 2.7 h high-complexity handover delay to a 15-minute start. That is flagged operationally, but it is doing most of the work in a 3 h SLA. Utilization and “Security reserve” are computed only from this queue’s estimates, while Gita already has 10 open cases; the memo never states whether “hours remaining” is spare capacity or shift time still occupied by WIP.

The PM4Py sketch would not run as written. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with `timestamp_key` and `start_timestamp_key`; the public arguments are `timestamp_column` and `start_timestamp_column`. Those inserted columns are never used. `discover_batches` yields `(activity, resource, batches)`, but the loop unpacks `((act, res), n_batches, detail)`. Approval “hard violations” flag every Final Approval event whose resource skill is below 2, so a legal skill-1 preparer is counted as a breach. Workload Gini is computed on assignment counts, while the indicator definition is hours over available hours. `rework_after` plus `groupby.apply` is index-fragile and will mark earlier events in a case that already contains Rework Correction.
