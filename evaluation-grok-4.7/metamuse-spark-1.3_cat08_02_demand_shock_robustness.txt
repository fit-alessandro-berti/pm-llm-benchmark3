6.4

The redesign is structurally complete and often directionally sound, but it is not reliable as process-mining advice. Several evidence and mechanism errors are large enough to mis-set controls.

The required seven sections, four disruption types, case-type separation, conditional triggers, downstream-overload discussion, and a redesign-caused vulnerability are all present. Conflict hints are used, p90 and SLA tails are prioritized over average cycle time, and blanket capacity is avoided. Many proportions are right: recollection 760/18,120 (4.2%) versus 780/9,730 (8.0%), technical-exit shares, and weekly volume 2,300 versus 3,300.

The analysis then mishandles the same logs. Raw 8-week counts are compared with 3-week counts. “760 to 1,180 batch waits despite fewer total cases” ignores that peak weekly volume is higher; Hold 260 to 560 “more than doubles,” while the weekly rate rises about 5.7×. Medians are added as if they were path times: assay-repeat 8h plus 26h becomes a “34h median,” and registration, courier, and pre-analytical queues are stacked even though Register to Receive is already 8h. Peak technical exits are treated as 8,080 while assay-to-technical inflow is 7,240; that non-conservation is never discussed.

Mechanism claims conflict with the resource table. Technical validators at 101% utilization cannot be “holders of WIP” blocked by medical review; blockage would lower utilization, whereas the 16h queue shows scarcity before technical validation. An evening medical shift is said to overload release immediately, but Medical to Release only moves from 2h to 5h. “M2 loss removes 40–50% of capacity and doubles re-entry” is invented. M2 is the higher-failure machine, so an outage and routing all Routine work onto M2 are opposite repeat effects; they are blurred. Observed peak deltas are also cited as if they were effects of I4, I6, and I8.

The package contradicts itself: R3 allows a pure-Routine batch of 24, then closes the Routine batch at 12 units or 4h. Dashboard bands are mis-calibrated against the cited baselines. Routine p90 red at >65h is already true in the normal period (68h). Batch-entry red at >15% of prep exits would not fire in the peak (about 12.7%). Normal Urgent p90 is already 19h against an 18h penalty and is never clearly treated as baseline noncompliance.
