3.5

The write-up has the right section structure and many correct branch queue and service cells, but the fork-join results that drive the conclusion are wrong, internally contradictory, and resolved in the wrong direction.

**Definitions.** Upstream, queue, service, `join_ready = max(ends)`, downstream queue, and final service are defined correctly. The double-counting argument is not. Synchronization wait is `join_ready − branch_end`, which ends at `join_ready`. It is not the interval from `join_ready` to `final_start` (that interval is downstream queue). For every branch, `queue + service + sync = fork_join`, so those three branch sums are the same calendar span. Adding the three sync waits stacks overlapping slack on top of time already counted in the critical branch. The additive identity that avoids this is `total_flow = upstream + (join_ready − fork_release) + downstream_queue + final_service`. “Total flow is the sum of all components” never states that identity. The critical branch’s own sync is zero; summing sync waits does not double-count downstream delay.

**Case table.** Upstream (15, then 20), all `A_queue = 0`, all `A_service = 50`, all `final_service = 20`, and almost all B/C queue and service values match the timestamps. The controlling columns do not. `join_ready` is the true latest branch end only for F01. For F02–F12 it is copied from `final_start`, so downstream queue is reported as 0 everywhere except F01’s 8 minutes. Actual downstream queues are 8, 15, 15, 18, 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 20, 20, and 10 minutes (mean 14.7). Actual critical branches are A, B, B, C, B, B, B, C, B, B, A, B: A 2/12, B 8/12, C 2/12. The table marks F06 and F10 as A. F07’s `B_queue` is 35 in the table and 30 in the worked arithmetic (`10:10 − 09:40`). Every `total_flow_time` is wrong. F02 is `10:00 − 08:05 = 115`, not 75; the twelve totals are 93, 115, 120, 115, 110, 120, 130, 110, 120, 115, 110, and 125. The worked examples for F02, F04, F07, and F11 recompute the correct joins (09:25 B, 09:52 C, 10:55 B, 11:30 A) and the correct downstream gaps (15, 18, 15, 20), then discard them because they disagree with the summary table. That table is derived, not source data. The timestamps and the problem’s definition of `join_ready` are the authority.

**Aggregates.** Stated critical frequencies, 4/12, 5/12, and 3/12, do not match this table either (that table is 4 A, 6 B, 2 C) or the data (2, 8, 2). B is a clear majority, not a near-tie with A. Mean B queue is 19.2, not 17.5; mean C queue is 10.4, not 12.5; mean C service is 20.2, not 20.5. Mean sync waits are about 11, 4.3, and 30.4, not 12.5, 15, and 17.5. “Mean total contribution” (queue + service + sync) must average to the same fork-join length on every branch; reporting 62.5, 67.5, and 50.5 breaks that identity and then uses it as an influence score. No margin or tail is reported. Actual control margins when critical are often 10–25 minutes for B and only 5–10 for A and 5–7 for C.

**Counterfactuals.** Reducing A service by 10 minutes saves join time only while A remains strictly last, and only up to the gap to the runner-up: 5 minutes on F01 and 10 on F11. F06 and F10 are B-critical, so the claimed 10-minute saves there are zero. The other eight cases also stay zero. Halving B queue moves `B_end` earlier by half of B’s queue, then the join falls only until the next branch. Rough join savings are 10, 10, 10, 15, 15, 12.5, 5, and 15 on F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, and F12. The narrative gives F03 no queue, gives F04 a benefit although C remains last by a wide margin, and uses the wrong half-queue shifts (13, 8, and 8 minutes). Both branch counterfactuals also ignore masking. Several true downstream gaps already exceed those join reductions, and F1/F2 are partly serialized (F05 starts when F03 ends, F08 when F06 ends, F09 when F07 ends, F11 when F09 ends). If `final_start` is held by that resource, earlier `join_ready` becomes more downstream wait and flow time does not move. Other gaps occur while the assigned resource is idle (F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F12), so those are not obviously a capacity queue. Counterfactual 3 treats the zero column as real and concludes that extra final capacity has nothing to remove. The removable part is the busy-resource portion, with chain effects, not 12 independent zeros and not the full 176 minutes either.

**Redesigns and code.** Prioritizing B is directionally defensible because B is last on 8 of 12 cases and has the larger margins, but the stated 42% frequency and the dismissal of downstream delay make the sizing unreliable. Parallelizing A is a weak second choice: A is last twice, and both margins sit inside an existing downstream gap. The Python path recomputes joins from the timestamps, so a successful run would disagree with the printed table. It also takes `idxmax().str[1]`, which yields `"_"` from `"A_end"` rather than the branch letter; subtracts the join delta with the wrong sign, so savings flip; calls `format_dataframe` on a performance DFG rather than the event frame; and calls `.apply(lambda x: x.mean())` on a timedelta series. The fork-join comment only says the quantities are unavailable in PM4Py. It does not say why directly-follows performance cannot see synchronization slack. Section 7 correctly names capacity, preemption, causality, and queue-versus-release-policy, but it presents the downstream contradiction as a property of the log rather than of this calculation.
