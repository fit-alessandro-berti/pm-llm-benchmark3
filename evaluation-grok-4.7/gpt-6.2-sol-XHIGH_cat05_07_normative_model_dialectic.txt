The answer is rigorous, traceable, and unusually careful with dates, authority, and control-flow semantics, but it is not nearly flawless. The briefs and the burden rule do not govern the dispositions, and the revised POWL leaves a real language hole.

Section 1 correctly states M0: both checks are mandatory and unordered, both precede the XOR, manual approval precedes activation, and notify precedes close. It also keeps CS-9’s activation gates and the human-separation rule distinct from M0, and it treats PD-3’s power to derogate as unproved while treating FD-7 as time-bounded authority. The burden rule is the right one: the exception claimant must prove authority, eligibility, and conditions; otherwise CS-9 remains the baseline, and absence from a known-incomplete source stays open.

That rule is then applied unevenly. SUP-702 has an unproved derogation and an unlogged master-data validation. Under the stated burden, the primary result is noncompliance with CS-9, reversible only by proof of authority and conditions. Calling it MIXED, while the reverse test still points both ways, splits the very case the burden was written to decide. SUP-704 is the mirror error: FD-7 does authorize the sequencing, but the answer also says cap and full-period monitoring are not certified, after declaring that unverified safeguards block full exception certification. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION can be the dominant sequencing result, yet the write-up never reconciles that with Section 1. SUP-705 as High and SUP-714 as Moderate is defensible, but the severity rationale stays thinner than the factual distinction.

The case analysis itself is strong. Sanctions-before-activation is not confused with quality-before-activation. SUP-708 is correctly limited to the sanctions and manual-approval breaches. SUP-702 is not blamed on the July CRM cutover. SUP-704’s follow-up may fall after 12 July without defeating an in-window activation. SUP-712 is a resource breach invisible to M0. SUP-715 is disclosure, not a phantom spouse ban. SUP-706, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are not forced into policy violations. Counts are not invented, and no denominator is fabricated. Prosecution and defense both concede what the prompt requires.

Section 5 then partly argues with briefs the answer did not write. Its own defense already refuses to excuse SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-714, and SUP-708, and already denies that model defects explain most deviations. Its own prosecution already warns against scoring every unmatched row as a breach. The system call that high-severity, evidenced gate failures make the operation materially noncompliant, while M0 is only a standard-route reference, is nevertheless coherent and evidence-based.

The POWL is largely real PM4Py: partial-order concurrency, n-ary XOR, `LOOP(τ, Request) = Request*`, notify before close, and route conditions left outside the net. Two limitations are understated. Provisional routes, once chosen, cannot reject or abort after a failed sanctions check, and an admitted sanctions failure without quality fits neither branch. Mandatory `Master_Data_Validation` also puts every observed pilot trace outside the language, which sits poorly with using that structure to judge historical behavior. The overlay tables and collection proposal are concrete and cover identity, snapshots, delegation, spend, notification identity, and immutable timestamps.
