2.6

The design is not a usable early-warning study. Core definitions contradict the business question, the cohort does not partition the 48,000 applications, censoring is misstated, and the code would not implement the stated design.

**Target.** The prediction time is set at 90 days after Account Activated, which is the end of the outcome window (and still before the usual 7–35 day feed lag). At that time the 90-day default status has already elapsed, so the setup is not an early forecast from the first events. The population is “all applications,” while the outcome exists only after activation, and the estimand never states whether it is conditional on eventual activation, on activation given the prefix, or on a future policy. The alternative (“default for every application”) leaves the outcome undefined for rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases and does not specify a multi-state, censored-time, or explicitly counterfactual estimand.

**Cohort.** Inclusion and exclusion contradict each other: withdrawn and transferred cases are both “included as censored” and “excluded because they were omitted from the labeled export.” One inclusion rule keeps cases with a Default Recorded event, which selects on the outcome. The flow table labels 18,900 non-defaults as defaults, places 2,700 defaults on a separate “Active” row, omits all 9,100 rejections, double-counts transfers and immature cases, and does not sum to 48,000. There is no as-of rule tied to 15 February 2026, the 90-day horizon, or label delay. The net rule is the biased export of 21,600 matured activated accounts.

**Censoring.** Completing 90 days without default is an observed non-default, not right censoring. Right censoring is follow-up that ends before the horizon (immature activations, extraction cutoff, lost transfer follow-up). Delayed labels are described as “use the label if it is present,” which conditions on arrival. The sensitivity analysis assigns rejected cases a default risk of 0, which treats a never-exposed outcome as a negative. Transfer linkage at about 60%, reappearance under a new identifier, and permanently missing partner feeds are not given separate mechanisms. The untestable claim about never-activated cases is the one clear distinction in this section.

**Prefixes.** Fixed-count prefixes are defined as the last n events in a time window before activation, which is neither a first-k prefix nor an elapsed-time prefix and is anchored at a late process state. Short traces are dropped rather than retained as short prefixes. Document loops are collapsed into one cumulative event, removing the rework signal. Renaming is acknowledged; duplicate events after 15 October 2025 are not given a deduplication rule. Nothing enforces that the prefix end timestamp cuts off later events.

**Features.** `time_to_default` is a function of the outcome. `time_to_decision` and `time_to_activation` are future relative to an early prefix. `documents_total` and extraction-time resource experience are proposed as keepers despite the stated snapshot and tenure problems. Control-flow, queueing, and historical reference features are named only loosely. `risk_score` is listed as both a static attribute and a time-varying field.

**Leakage table.** `decision_reason` is kept even though it is filled at or after Accept/Reject. `documents_total` is rated low risk. Resource experience is taken “as of activation” although it is stored at extraction. `label_available_at` is kept as if it were a predictor. Availability rules often say “use only before activation” on rows marked Drop. Future events have no row. `collection_status` and `final_limit` are at least marked Drop.

**Validation.** A split by case start time, separate policy regimes, and a warning about random splits are directionally right. The design has no embargo of 90 days plus 7–35 days of label delay, so train labels and test prefixes can overlap in calendar time. Label maturity is described as requiring labels inside the prediction window, which drops or selects recent cases. Cohen’s kappa and ordinary AUC do not match a 2,700/21,600 base rate or a censored outcome; no proper scoring rule or censoring-aware metric is specified. The later code uses a random three-way split, so the validation section is not the operational plan.

**Limitations.** The text does not separate association under the observed activation policy, selection into activation, and a counterfactual default risk. The three deployment effects are external policy change, system migration, and thinner labels near extraction. Those are data shifts, not changes caused by acting on the score (who is activated, when collections start, how resources and traces move).

**Code.** The skeleton is about half the requested length and does not implement the pipeline. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`. Imports and calls for DFG discovery, variants, temporal features, prefixes, and `split_train_test` do not match PM4Py signatures; variants are computed from a DFG, temporal features are asked to emit outcome timings, and the split returns three folds with `random_state`. Features are aggregated on the full trace, including post-activation events, `default_90d`, and a max of `documents_total`. There is no configurable cutoff, no start/activation/terminal/censoring/maturity table, no temporal split by case start, no removal of post-prefix columns, and no competing-risk or survival hook beyond a closing note. Cohort counts and the leakage table are hardcoded, not computed from the log.
