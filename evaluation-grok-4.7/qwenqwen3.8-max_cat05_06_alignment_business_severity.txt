The final analysis is strong, complete, and mostly accurate: all twelve cases are interpreted in business terms, raw cost is kept separate from severity, zero-cost policy failures and the silent transition are handled correctly, and the six deviation types, uncertainty, pattern evidence, and remediation portfolio are generally well distinguished.

The main miss is in section 6. It was explicitly required to address activity granularity as well as cost design, scope, hidden moves, data conditions, duplicate telemetry, external evidence, and irreversibility. Label/granularity mismatch (CL-611, Assess Damage versus Assess Loss) is never used there.

CL-602 also overreaches. Missing fraud-screen evidence is treated carefully in the hypotheses, but the case action and remediation still move to “seek recovery” and “recovers funds,” which the evidence does not establish. Severity 4 is applied equally to the medium-risk threshold breach (CL-609) and the recent bank-change auto-approval (CL-610), despite the framework treating fraud indicators as distinct from amount. The evidence table’s summed “exposure” also includes a withdrawn, unpaid claim. These are judgment and completeness flaws, not a failure of the overall diagnosis.
