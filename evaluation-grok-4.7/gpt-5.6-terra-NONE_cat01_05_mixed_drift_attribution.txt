7.2

The account is substantively strong: March is correctly treated as a semantic/provenance break, later rework and delay are not collapsed into logging drift, and the mechanism-specific change points, confounding, and monitoring design are careful. It is not nearly flawless. Several required quantitative and implementation claims are wrong or unusable as written.

The normalized DFG distances do not match the stated formula. With \(D=\frac12\sum|r_m-r_b|/100\) on the seven supplied rates against January, the distances are March 0.015, May 0.200, July 0.295, September 0.140, October 0.320, and December 0.180. The published figures (0.025, 0.105, 0.180, 0.090, 0.185, 0.095) are not that distance, not half of it, and not total variation after renormalizing the rows. Rank order happens to survive, but the scale is invented. Part A implements the stated formula, so the code would contradict section 3.2. The March narrative also drops the Collect Documents→Assess change of −1.

In the baseline table, July–August raw-fitness loss is about 0.395 and 0.425, not “0.40 to 0.40.” Fitness “changes” are unsigned magnitudes in a table whose other rows are signed, so the same column both rises and falls without a sign convention. March–April median cycle time is about +0.05 h and +0.85 h, not “about +0.9 h.”

Arrow-stripped figures make required cells false or unreadable: “labels rise 911,” “1%5%,” “7195h,” “79 per 100,” “2921%,” “0.930.55.” Restoring a missing arrow repairs some of them, but May–June p90 and median are still anchored on April (71 h, 32.0 h) rather than on May. Senior Assessment 4→5→7→9 is a pre-July gradient; tying “7→9” to the 1 July priority rule overstates a sudden behavioral break. The sharp senior-routing jump is October (to 22 per 100), and it is more than proportional to complexity (about 4/21 in January versus 22/41 in October). That ratio is never computed. Fast-variant share, from about 59% to 35%, is barely used.

Logging “change points” on 1 April and 1 December are level persistence and small wobble inside the post-migration plateau, not new breaks. June mixes pre- and post-departure days and is not treated as a contaminated month. The primary population of completed cases omits the backlog censoring that would understate cycle time precisely when queues are the claim.

Part B does not match the PM4Py manual. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key` argument, so the helper throws if a resource column exists. `filter_time_range` takes `dt1` and `dt2`, not `start_timestamp` and `end_timestamp`. After formatting, the timestamp column is the standard `time:timestamp`, while later calls still default to `timestamp`. Monthly DFGs are built by splitting on event time, which cuts cases and manufactures starts and ends; the caveats do not say so. The two-point, 3-SD flag is not a noise estimate: January–February dispersion flags trivial moves through the OR with the practical thresholds.

Sections 2, 5, 7, 9, and 10 are the best of the answer: word counts fit, raw-versus-mapped limits are stated correctly, discrimination segments are actionable, thresholds are not universal, and confidence is properly lower for priority, December, and clean staffing attribution. Those strengths do not offset a wrong central distance table and a required PM4Py path that does not run.
