5.0

The write-up has the right checklist and the right hard calls on stage and alert identity: I-03 is ineligible once Diagnosis Complete has passed, I-05 is suppressed because the repeat is only a 30-minute rescore, and I-07 is allowed back because a new customer log is new evidence. Spillovers, segment bias, label contamination, and a reserve slot are all treated as design constraints. Those parts are real. The grade stops there because the stated rule, the allocation, and the impact table are three different policies, and the numeric core does not audit.

The decision rule is not the rule that produces the portfolio. CANEV is defined as midpoint absolute risk reduction times breach value, minus €450, minus false-alarm burden, minus a €2,000 queue penalty. On that arithmetic the direct nets, before the penalty, are about €8,950 (I-04), €4,550 (I-08), €3,550 (I-09), €2,700 (I-02), €2,625 (I-06), €1,725 (I-05), €1,685 (I-01), €1,490 (I-10), and €800 (I-07). After €2,000, only I-04, I-08, I-09, and I-02 stay positive. I-07 is about −€1,200, the worst eligible net in the book. The table still labels I-07 Tier 1 “High CANEV” and spends Slot 3 on it, labels I-08 “Highest CANEV” even though I-04 is about double, and parks I-09 as a moderate wait. I-01’s upper net (about €2,450) and I-09’s lower net (about €2,350) both clear I-07’s upper net (about €1,100). Selecting I-07 over them is not a stated tradeoff for the 1.5-hour clock; the table gives a false reason.

Calibration never enters the score. The −0.08 / +0.06 shifts, the ±50% Non-Native band, and the 20% imputation discount are declared and then dropped. Section 5 later replaces the ±50% band with a different 20% penalty. Strategic cases, the over-predicted segment, still take two or three of the slots. A point shift would not have been robustness in any case: the bias ranges are 0.05–0.12 and 0.04–0.09, and the effect intervals are not causal estimates. No case is stress-tested at the bad end of its own band before a slot is locked.

Slot order ignores the resource constraint that makes order the decision. Two specialists deliver one swarm, so four slots are sequential 45-minute blocks, not four parallel tickets. I-07’s deadline is 13:30. As Slot 3 it would start at the deadline. It had to be first if it was going to be done at all. I-04, with 10 hours still in Triage, is Slot 2. The reserve rule is also dead on arrival: I-08 has 8.0 hours at 12:00 and 6.5 hours at 13:30, so a “≤ 6.0 hours before 13:30” release never fires. I-06 is “wait” with 4 hours left in Diagnosis Started and a possibly imputed jump from 0.20 to 0.52, with no time-boxed check to restore the live queue field.

The impact table does not describe this portfolio. “1.8” optimistic breaches avoided is impossible: the selected upper bounds sum to 0.97 (I-02 0.22, I-04 0.28, I-07 0.32, I-08 0.15), and the upper-half midpoints sum to about 0.84. Base midpoints sum to 0.70, not 1.1. The audit line applies one ~0.15 effect to €190k and gets €28.5k; case-level midpoint gross benefit is about €26.5k, which only happens to sit inside €18–31k. The optimistic €54k top is not recoverable (upper-bound gross is about €39k). Optimistic spillover of €1,200–€2,400 does not match the table’s own identity, 4 × 6 × 0.01 × €15,000 = €3,600. “24 untreated cases” counts slot-exposures as if they were 24 people. Every scenario costs four slots (€1,800) while Section 2 claims a three-slot cap with a conditional fourth. False-alarm burden on those four cases is about €7,650 and is omitted from the netting, while the optimistic gloss claims no false-alarm backfire. The €15k “average untreated stake” is asserted, not derived. Adverse “net negative” is directionally plausible once a failed case, €5,000 of I-08 false-alarm cost, and congestion are stacked, but that conclusion sits on unauditable inputs.

Sections 4–6 name the right mechanisms and then leave them unusable. The 60-minute cool-down and the I-05 versus I-07 contrast are sound, and the new log is correctly treated as re-entry evidence rather than as proof that risk rose (p moved from 0.75 to 0.72). “SLA compression exceeding 50% of the remaining window” is not an operable trigger. The monthly 25% low-value quota does not bind today and can force negative-CANEV swarms; there is no regulatory or public-sector duty term beside euro value. IPW or dropping intervened cases is the right family of fix for training on treated outcomes. The 10% case holdout ignores interference: controls sit on the same diagnosis queue the swarm delays, so a case-level A/B estimate mixes benefit with the spillover the policy is supposed to measure. “Tighten the CANEV hurdle by 15%” refers to a cutoff the decision rule never set. Freezing only Tier 3 when the queue blows up does nothing, because Tier 3 is already defined as do-not-intervene.

The result is a readable policy memo whose portfolio is not the optimum, or even a robust feasible set, under the objective it wrote down.
