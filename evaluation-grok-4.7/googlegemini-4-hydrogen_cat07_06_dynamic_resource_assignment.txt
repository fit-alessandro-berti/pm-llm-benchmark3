The answer covers every required section, all 16 cases, the hard skill rules, and the named PM4Py functions, but it is not internally consistent and several recommendations fight the policy it just defined.

The urgent Security case is mishandled. Constraint 5 and soft rule S2 say to keep the owner when supervision is feasible. R3_Celine has Security skill 1, and R7/R8 can supervise, so she should execute C001 under specialist supervision. The assignment instead makes R8_Hugo the lead. Section 5 then tells workers the opposite: Hugo joins Celine’s case. That is two different accountable owners for a 3-hour SLA. The cited 2.7-hour handover penalty and 3.3-hour weak-skill penalty are not reconciled with a same-day hour budget.

Capacity is not actually enforced. The scorer never reduces `hours_remaining` as the batch is filled, so a resource looks feasible for every case independently. R3_Celine has 6.0 hours and is given C001 (urgent, 3h), C007 (high, 6h, complexity 0.81), C003, and C009 supervision. C001 and C007 cannot both fit. R6_Farid’s 6.5 hours are similarly filled by C002, C011, and C010 supervision. The “max is 3” summary is also wrong once co-work, supervision, and shadowing are counted: R3 and R10_Jonas sit on the cap of 4.

The algorithm does not produce the recommended queue. Skill 0 is dropped before pairing, so the C015 Jonas shadow cannot occur, yet that shadow is one of the two items used to clear the 20% training rule. C014 assigns Rework to scarce security specialist R8 even though the resilience term penalizes exactly that. Weights do not stay normalized (urgent, complex, high-handover cases sum to 1.15), and the high-overtime branch does not do what the comment claims: a score penalty is not a disqualification.

The baseline comparison asserts results it never computes. Specialist-only with lowest-open tie-breaks does not overload R9_Iris; R10_Jonas and R8 also have Quality skill 3 and fewer open cases, so R9 loses those ties. The 3.3-hour weak-skill statistic is then applied to first-available generally, although the evidence is about keeping a weak owner. C003 is a lateral move from R4_David (Diagnosis 3) to R3 (Diagnosis 3) that pushes `reassignment_count` past the stated harm threshold of 2; “fresh eyes” does not offset that evidence.

The audit sketch lists the right indicators, but the code would not recover this policy. It searches `assignment_reason` for “train,” while the C015 reason text never uses that word, and it never measures the handover-delay or rework priors the policy claims to learn. Service time is treated as one average, and SLA misses are flagged from any event rather than case completion.

What keeps this above a failing design is real: skill 0 is not allowed to sign Security or Approval, high-overtime resources get no new non-urgent work, Quality batching respects the 24-hour rule, high-priority work is not given to an unsupervised trainee, and the worker explanation is plain and non-punitive. Those are necessary corrections, not a finished, auditable policy.
