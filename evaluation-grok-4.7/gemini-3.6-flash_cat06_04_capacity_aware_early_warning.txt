6.0

The write-up has the required six sections, excludes I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, and puts queue spillover into the scenario table. Those are the right headline calls. The policy still does not hold together as one rule: the published score, the persistence rule, the scenario definitions, and several rationales contradict each other.

**Score identity.** Section 1 defines \(EV_{net}\) as midpoint effect times breach value, minus €450, minus \((1-p_{adj})\) times false-alarm burden, minus €1,800 spillover. Every Section 2 score is a different identity: midpoint \(\Delta p \times V - 450 -\) the full false-alarm burden, with spillover left out. That hidden identity matches the table exactly: I-04 \(0.19\times 60{,}000-450-2{,}000=8{,}950\); I-08 \(0.10\times 100{,}000-450-5{,}000=4{,}550\); I-09 \(0.24\times 20{,}000-450-800=3{,}550\); likewise I-01 \(1{,}685\), I-02 \(2{,}700\), I-07 \(800\), I-10 \(1{,}490\), and I-06 \(\approx 1{,}781\) after the 25% haircut. A constant spillover would not reorder the top three, so the portfolio can survive the omission. The formula as written still does not produce the scores as written.

**Persistence rule applied only to the highlighted pair.** The rule suppresses a repeat, unacted alert unless there is a new event or \(\Delta p_{adj}\ge 0.15\). I-05 (\(+0.02\), no new event) is suppressed. I-02 is a second alert with \(\Delta=+0.08\) and no stated new event, yet it stays eligible. I-09 is a second alert with \(\Delta=+0.07\) and no stated new event, yet it receives a slot. Under the written rule both are suppressed, so the selected portfolio contains a case the decision rule rejects. The Section 4 flowchart also drops the new-event exception that §1.3 allows after a prior swarm.

**Adjustments that never enter the decision.** The SME \(+0.065\) and Strategic Enterprise \(-0.085\) shifts are the correct medians of the stated bias ranges, and they never enter \(\Delta p\) or the Section 2 scores. The \(\pm 20\%\) widening for Non-Native-Language Support never enters I-09’s score or the scenario bounds. The 25% I-06 haircut is an arbitrary constant, and the promised verification hold is not a gate; I-06 is simply outranked. I-05, the largest effect range in the book (\(0.25\)–\(0.40\)) and an underpredicted SME case, is removed rather than kept at an unboosted score.

**Rationale errors and operational gaps.** The answer’s own order is I-04, I-08, I-09, I-02, I-06, I-01, I-10, I-07. The I-01 note calls that case rank 4. The I-02 note says its effect is below the selected cases; \(0.15\) is above I-08’s \(0.10\). I-07 has \(1.5\) h left and is marked “wait,” which on that clock closes the intervention window. I-04 (\(10\) h) and I-08 (\(8\) h) are taken at once, while I-10 is described as deferrable because the horizon is long. Deadline pressure and proximity to Diagnosis Complete are outside the score, so I-02 (diagnosis already started, \(3\) h, lower-bound effect still covering cost and full false-alarm burden) loses to I-08. At I-08’s lower bound, \(0.05\times 100{,}000-450-5{,}000=-450\) before spillover.

**Scenario table.** The euro totals add up, and \(0.03/0.06/0.09\) matches three slots at \(0.01/0.02/0.03\). The €4,733 false-alarm term is not derived. It equals \((1-p_{adj})\times\) burden only with the Section 1 weights: \(0.605\times 2{,}000+0.655\times 5{,}000+0.31\times 800\). The adverse row then switches to the full €7,800 while the prose attributes the loss to an I-08 misfire alone. Optimistic effects are the range maxima; the stated optimistic case is the upper half of each range. Spillover cash harm rests on an assumed €15,000 value per untreated diagnosis case that the case list does not provide, with no sensitivity. Base confidence is marked high while the same table runs from about \(+€29{,}000\) to about \(-€7{,}650\), and that swing is not allowed to drop I-08.

**Safeguards and monitoring.** Section 5 names starvation by value, Non-Native-Language uncertainty, segment miscalibration, label confounding, and spillover incidence, and it offers concrete controls: a non-enterprise floor, intervention flags, inverse-propensity or S-learner retraining, and a 5–10% exploration share. That floor is not reconciled with two Strategic Enterprise selections plus a regulatory reserve, and the spillover penalty it requires is the term Section 2 does not compute. Section 6’s 25% hike to the minimum \(EV_{net}\) refers to a cutoff Section 1 never sets. A regression discontinuity at “the” eligibility threshold fits a single cutoff better than a four-slot expected-value ranking. The ECE, delay-per-slot, and reserve-burn triggers are specific enough to run.

The result is a serious sketch with the right exclusions and the right monitoring topics. Scoring, persistence, scenarios, and rationales do not describe one policy. That is the basis for 6.0.
