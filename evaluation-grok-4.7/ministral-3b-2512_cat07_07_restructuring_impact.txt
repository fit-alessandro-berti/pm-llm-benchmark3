4.5

The forecast covers every requested section and correctly chooses a monitored pilot over an immediate full rollout, but quantitative reasoning, internal consistency, and the PM4Py sketch are not reliable enough to use.

**Mechanism map.** The required themes are named, yet several links point the wrong way. Caseworker-to-specialist volume is described as a reduced local loop while the cited rate moves from 36 to 41 per 100 cases; the return path also rises, from 31 to 36. A 15% utilization increase is treated as relief from the central queue. At the stated loads that is the pressure that creates the queue: embedded tech is already at 68–82%, and Legal/Risk are at 91–93%. Routing legal/risk work from 9 to 12 per 100 cases is about a one-third demand increase. Applied to a resource near 92% utilized, implied load exceeds 1.0, so the management figure of a 10.5-hour median wait is not a feasible steady state. Pooling is only gestured at. North and South have slack that might help East; the same pool also absorbs triage and difficult quality checks. Rework, learning, and role clarity are slogans in the linkage lines rather than mechanisms tied to handover edges, sojourn time, or resource–activity profiles. “Remove one local supervisor” is also widened into wholesale replacement of local approval.

**Scenarios.** All three scenarios are filled in, and the numbers are more precise than the evidence.
- Optimistic: specialist loops are “eliminated” while the same scenario still routes large volume to the shared service. A 6-hour legal/risk wait and a 2.0-hour blended handover wait require capacity the proposal does not add. The 22.8-day 90th percentile is East’s current tail, not an organization baseline; North is 16.4 days and South is 18.2. Organization median cycle time of about 7.5 days falling to 5.5 days is not reconciled with handover waits measured in hours (4 hours is about 0.17 day). “E_Tech specialization is preserved” contradicts merging N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech. Utilization of 95% and “caseworker satisfaction” of 4.5/5 are presented as success signals; 95% utilization is a saturation warning, and the 4.x figures in the data are customer satisfaction.
- Central: rework both “stays at 8%” and “increases to 9%.” East both “benefits” and has “no improvement.” “E_Tech’s overcapacity” reverses the evidence: East is the overloaded team at 82% utilization and an 8.6-day median. The 41-per-100 figure is an increase on today’s 36, not a reduced loop. The single 3.0-hour “median wait” is only the management estimate for the approval queue.
- Adverse: waits lengthen while specialist utilization falls to 85%, with no capacity-loss mechanism. Cross-team handovers “increase” to 5 per 100, which is still below today’s 6. Across scenarios, higher utilization is treated as healthier (95%, then 90%, then 85%). Queueing risk runs in the opposite direction.

**Indicators.** The leading/lagging split exists and names some log fields (`get_variants`, `get_service_time`, `local_expertise_flag`). Handover waits and service times, which are observable within days, are placed as lagging. Cross-team handover frequency is listed as both. Skill development “weekly” and case-note accountability are not measures in this log. Learning is not operationalized as a shrinking local activity repertoire, falling activity-based similarity between caseworkers and techs, or fewer complex cases touched locally. `reassignment_count`, `handover_reason`, `priority`, `outcome`, and performance-DFG sojourn are unused.

**Risks hidden by the average.** Tail delay, complex-case skew, local expertise, fairness, accountability, and exceptions are present. Learning—the distinct, slower loss of skill because hard cases are routed away—is not, despite being required. “20% of cases with 30%+ rework” mixes a case-level rework flag with a rate. Detection methods do not match the APIs: temporal feature extraction is not a percentile analysis, role discovery does not measure `local_expertise_flag`, and subcontracting is not exception handling. There is no composition effect: a better average can be a mix shift toward simple local cases while complex and regional cases slow down.

**Pilot.** Scope, a comparison group, windows, segments, guardrails, feedback, and rollback are all mentioned. The design conflicts with itself. Phase 1 is both “30% of cases” and “high-complexity only,” with no base rate for high complexity. The control is another 30%, so 40% of volume is undescribed, and assignment is unspecified. Moving the embedded techs contaminates local control cases; that interference is ignored. The pre window is 1 month and the post window is 3 months, against phases of 2 and 4 months. Specialty is collapsed into complexity instead of `required_specialty` in {legal, risk, security}. East is only a “high-risk” cell, so the testable claim that East should improve while North and South lose informal advice is not a registered comparison. Rollback when utilization falls below 85–90% would stop a pool that still has slack and would not stop a pool approaching saturation. Customer satisfaction below 4.0/5 is already true for East today (3.8). Rework above 8% on complex cases is tighter than current team rework of 8.2–11.5% and current post-handover rework of 9–18%, so the trigger is not baseline-calibrated. Legal/Risk phase 2 adds demand with no extra FTE and no wait cap.

**Recommendation.** “Phased pilot, conditional on monitoring” matches the decision asked for, and the warning against reading success from average cycle time, raw handover counts, or utilization alone is the right caution. The conditions repeat the inverted utilization rule and the uncalibrated 8% and 4.0/5 cutoffs. Early East improvement, mix shift, and the management routing table are not listed among the things averages can falsely certify. The proposal is not modified before the pilot: no protected local-advice channel, no Legal/Risk capacity, no RACI for the shared approval queue.

**PM4Py sketch.** This section does not implement the requested comparison. The log is introduced as a column set (`case:concept:name`, `org:resource`, `team`, `case_complexity`, and the others); the sketch opens XES and never calls `format_dataframe`. Working-together is drawn with the handover routine filtered by `required_specialty`. Subcontracting, performance DFG, waiting time, and rework use module paths and an `attribute_filter` argument that are not these APIs. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, `get_variants`, `filter_variants`, and `get_rework_cases_per_activity` are absent. `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with the wrong parameter names, then `.groupby` is applied to the result of an XES import. SLA misses are named and not computed. Role discovery and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are the only calls aligned with the specified library. The sketch would not run and would not distinguish handover, collaboration, subcontracting, and role similarity.

The outline is complete enough to recognize the decision problem. The operating conclusion—utilization up is good, 36→41 is a reduction, East’s 22.8 days is the corporate tail, Legal/Risk can take more work at a 10.5-hour wait—is not a sound forecast.
