The headline choice (hybrid, no national scale, no full rollback) matches the evidence, and the six required sections are present. The memo is not reliable enough for the decision it argues.

Factual errors are material. Apartment median duration improved by 2.0 days; the evidence table reports +2 days. The −8 and +12 day segment figures are p90, not p95. “Missed permits in 18% of old buildings” treats the 18% segment share as a miss rate. Generalization (0.70 to 0.56) is omitted.

Process-mining concepts are inverted. Low precision means the model allows too many unobserved paths; it is not overfitting, and precision is not generalization. Section 3 states both the wrong definition and “Handovers = resilience,” which contradicts the resilience finding and the same section’s own prose.

Value of information is mis-ranked. M2 (start timestamps) cannot validate the skip rule or show that tails are “not systemic.” M1, the randomized stratum test, is called higher-bias and ranked below observational M6. Case-mix bias, incomplete segment attributes before week 3, cost truncation after week 4, idle survey capacity, and the second-visit team at 92% are barely used, so the pilot is treated as cleaner than it is.

Alternative analysis fails its own test. B is “never” reasonable, on the unsupported claim that baseline has no resilience or benefits. C’s risks cite skipped surveys and post-billing activation, which C suspends. The recommendation both deploys Redesign R for simple houses and pauses the skip rule that may explain their gain, and the “<5% cost” rollout bar is invented. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are column-separated, but several cells are mislabeled or false.
