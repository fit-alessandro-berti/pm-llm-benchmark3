6.2

The write-up keeps the right population and refuses to code rejects as non-defaults, but the estimand, maturity clock, and backtest do not fit together, and the skeleton would not implement the study it describes.

The primary target mixes a fixed-horizon multinomial outcome with a cause-specific hazard model. Activated-non-default is the absence of default through day 90 after activation, not a competing event with its own hazard. Cause-specific hazards also do not equal cumulative incidence unless every cause is modeled and combined; that step is never specified. Time zero is undefined, while the horizon is variously “after the final credit decision” and “after activation.” Reject, withdrawal, and transfer usually occur at the decision, so they are not 90-day post-decision events. Prediction time is left as both the k-th event and elapsed time t. The conditional-on-activation alternative is the right contrast, and the selection warning is sound.

The 48,000-row flow adds up and correctly keeps 21,600 as the only fully labeled activated book. Maturity is then anchored to the wrong clock. 15 February 2026 minus 90+35 days is about 13 October 2025 only if that date is the activation (or label-horizon start). The validation section and the code apply it to case start. `label_available_at` is the actual maturity field and is never used as one. Still-open cases from 2024 would be marked mature. Rejected cases are called both a fully observed competing outcome and “selectively unobserved default.” Transfers are both an absorbing outcome and missing follow-up; the 60% linkage is not a cohort split, and permanently missing partner feeds are not located inside the 48,000.

Delayed labels are structural given activation time, extraction time, and feed lag, not MCAR. Open cases, immature activations, and permanent partner missingness do not share one censoring mechanism. The independent-censoring assumption is not stated, though open cases and partner feeds are plausibly informative. The Heckman sensitivity changes the question to a counterfactual reject-inference estimand and omits the exclusion restriction; it does not stress-test the primary CIF. No rule is given for whether linked transfers stay “transferred” or receive an outcome.

Short traces are “excluded or padded with a Terminal token.” Padding with termination leaks the result. Excluding them without saying the risk set is “still open at prefix end” either drops early rejects or scores cases after the outcome is known. Migration duplicates are ignored. Document loops are both preserved and collapsed, with no order of operations. The code keeps traces shorter than k, so it does not match this section.

The leakage table has the required columns and the right drop decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-cutoff events, extraction-time tenure, and `label_available_at` as a feature. `final_limit` leaks the decision, not the default label. `documents_total` cannot be rebuilt from the stated schema unless upload events exist; the safe rule is the last snapshot strictly before prediction time. The code drops it instead of repairing it. Forward-fill of `risk_score` is valid only if each row still holds the score as of that event and the frame is time-sorted; the code never sorts before `ffill`. A latest-value overwrite is not fixed by forward-fill.

The random-split warning, case-level split, policy-era validation, Brier score, time-dependent AUC, and calibration are the right family. The test window is not. Cases starting 1–13 October 2025 are before the 15 October 2025 migration, not after it, and thirteen days is not a migration backtest. Post-migration activations generally cannot have a mature 90-day label plus feed by 15 February 2026; the design claims the opposite. Train is entirely pre-policy, validation is where hyperparameters are tuned on the new policy, and the test slice does not carry mature post-migration defaults. Metrics omit inverse-probability-of-censoring weights, the at-risk condition, and `source_system`, which is the informative-missingness slice.

Section 8 then describes the model as learning who defaults among historically approved accounts. That is the alternative estimand, not this multi-state target. The deployable claim is association with terminal states under the historical acceptance policy. The three feedback loops (auto-reject removing future defaults, gaming, and policy or partner drift) are concrete and usable.

The code reads and formats the log, renames activities, splits on start time, drops obvious post-outcome columns, and asserts. It does not call `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`. It never builds activation time, a competing-event code, a censoring time, or maturity from `label_available_at`. `is_mature` is `case_start_time <= extraction − 125 days`. `default_90d` is taken only from the last event. Timezone-aware start times are compared with bare date strings. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is handed an event log. Cohort output is only split sizes, and the single closing comment does not state the censoring assumptions a survival model would require.
