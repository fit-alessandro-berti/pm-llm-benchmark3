7.3

The answer is structurally complete and directionally right: conditional go, no illegal Document Check start, co-interventions not treated as the treatment effect, a real partial order with a legal/supplement loop, and a pilot that separates delivery failure from mechanism failure. It is not nearly flawless. The quantitative layer, which the task weights heavily, contains internal contradictions and one clear transport mistake.

The most serious problem is the eligible-share figure. Section 1 correctly says Site B’s digital share is unknown and must be measured. Section 5 then multiplies the non-originals, non-legal share by Site A’s 90% digital rate to get “~20%,” and that number drives Wave 1 size, the ~110–120 cases/month, the power timeline, and the recommendation. That is exactly the kind of parameter transport the exercise is about not doing. Assumption 1 admits the 20% is untested, but the planning numbers are still written as if it were known, and the highest-confidence scenario (S2, 55) rests on it.

The Little’s Law WIP figures are not a reliable basis for the control rule built on them. Medians are used where the law needs means, on a process the answer itself calls heavy-tailed. The implied arrival rates behind “~13” open identity tasks and “~33” open document tasks do not match (8.1/3.4 = 2.38, but 33/13 = 2.54). The Site A “before” cell uses the after-state cap of 20×6 = 120. Worse, the design then creates a parallel Document Check only when document open tasks are ≤30, which is below the answer’s own ~33 in-service estimate, so the rule would often block the intervention it is meant to enable. No sensitivity is shown.

The “structural bound” of ~3.5 h × 71% ≈ 2.5 h misreads the overlap metric. Overlap rose from 8% to 71%, so the incremental share is 63 pp, and “any overlapping interval” is not full concealment of the 3.5 h identity service. Calling 2.5 h a bound overstates how much of the 9 h has been ruled out. The 1,160/1,292 “~90% of non-originals” claim is likewise too strong: the answer elsewhere shows the DFG is not a case partition (Register’s outgoing counts are 3,910 against 3,400 cases; originals-predecessor counts are 2,880 against 2,108 required cases), so Identity→Document cannot be allocated entirely to the non-originals stratum.

S3 is labeled a bottleneck shift but raises assessor utilization via extra re-assessments, which is the legal/rework mechanism of S5. The median effects in S1 are essentially weighted means after the text has already said order statistics make the median move less than that. The log-cycle SD of 0.6 used for power is optimistic relative to the overall p90/median ratio (110/44 implies σ near 0.7 if lognormal). Expansion and stop thresholds on p90 (10% vs 15%) leave an unnamed middle zone.

The causal graph, co-intervention inventory, statutory guards, z-scores, Kingman arithmetic, appeal censoring, and FTE-productivity decline are handled carefully. The POWL code matches the usual constructors (`StrictPartialOrder.order.add_edge`, `Operator.LOOP` as do-then-redo, XOR skip, `convert_to_petri_net`, soundness, playout) and correctly refuses to encode data-dependent guards, capacity, locks, and durations. The redo `Assessment` does not itself have explicit edges from identity and document verification; only the first assessment does. Case-level randomization under shared pools remains the primary design after the text has shown why that estimator is contaminated; the switchback is only a sensitivity check.

Under a strict reading, those quantitative and design contradictions are enough to keep this well below a near-flawless score, despite the strong process-structural reasoning.
