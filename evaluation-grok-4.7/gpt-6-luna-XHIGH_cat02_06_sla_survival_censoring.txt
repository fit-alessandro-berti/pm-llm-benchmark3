7.8

The write-up is strong on estimand definition, competing risks, and leakage, and the case counts check out. It is not nearly flawless: the code undoes part of that care, and a few claims are looser than the data.

Sections 1–4 correctly set time zero at `Submit` and τ = 72, separate an internal resolution cumulative incidence from active occupancy at 72, and show that those are not complements. S03 (76), S09 (92), S06 (27), and S08 (68) are handled in line with that split, including the point that an open age past 72 is already a known miss while follow-up after that age is still censored. Withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are not waved through as ordinary independent censoring. The resolved-only contrast is computed correctly (A 4/6: S01, S02, S04, S07 vs S03, S13; B 5/7: S16–S19, S27 vs S20, S28), as are the open-case split (known past 72: S09, S11, S25, S26; censored earlier: S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) and the P1 imbalance (3/15 vs 5/17). Aalen–Johansen is a justified primary estimator here; Kaplan–Meier, Fine–Gray, restricted mean, landmarking, and a timed multi-state model are distinguished for this sample rather than listed generically. Immortal time on `escalated` is identified, and the data gaps in section 9 match the identification problems.

The main break is internal. Section 1 and section 4 define occupancy at 72 as overall survival in the transient states, explicitly different from 1 − F_R(72). The code never returns that survival probability. It labels 1 − F_R as the second estimand, which is the quantity the prose says is a different decision. The hand-rolled Aalen–Johansen update itself is the right discrete formula and keeps open cases in the risk set.

The three transfer scenarios are not three analyses of the printed endpoint. `competing` and `failure` both leave transfer as a non-null competing code, so the resolve CIF and its complement are identical; only `censor` moves the estimate. The exclusion and external-success interpretations discussed in sections 2 and 6 are not what that third code does. The crude bound is a valid best/worst range for on-time resolution if early transfers and early-censored opens are the unknowns (9 known on-time resolves out of 32; upper end adds S08, S21 and the five pre-72 opens), but the print calls it an external-outcome bound while also swinging the five internal early-censored cases.

`pm4py.get_variants` in current PM4Py returns variant → count. `len` on those counts raises `TypeError`, and the `ImportError` fallback does not catch it, so the required control-flow step fails when the library is present. The manual `EventLog` build is a fair substitute for `parse_event_log_string`, and it does not invent timestamps. `last_event_gap_h = 0` on every terminal row is a fair reading of the field, and using it as a baseline predictor would separate open from terminal rows.

Smaller slips: the state list asserts a direct `Review → Review` loop, which does not occur (repeats are `Review>Rework>Review` or `Review>Escalate>Review`; S27 goes `Rework>Resolve`). “Net survival” is the wrong name for a Kaplan–Meier curve whose event is resolution. Section 4 asks for intervals and risk-set counts; the code prints point CIFs only, with no variance. Cohort-by-priority CIFs on cells of size about 3–5 are shown, with a small-sample caveat that should have been the headline next to those numbers.
