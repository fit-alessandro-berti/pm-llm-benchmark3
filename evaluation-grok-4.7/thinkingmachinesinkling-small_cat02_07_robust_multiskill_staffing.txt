4.6

The write-up has the required nine-part structure, labeled assumptions, two objectives (SLA/tail and fairness/robustness), three operational policies, a shock-aware recommendation, and a usable monitoring/pilot sketch. Those strengths do not offset a capacity analysis and code artifact that contradict each other and the given data.

Offered load is not a time-block analysis. Branch counts are inflated to about 5/6/4 mechanical/electrical/software cases per block against roughly 3.0/4.1/2.1 from the demand table, so the 130/198/156 minute loads are not auditable. Shifts are collapsed into a single “everyone has ~112 minutes every block” picture: Farah is counted after 12:00, Emil before 12:00, and Chen/Ada after 16:00. The real coverage holes—an Emil-only 18:00–20:00 window and no in-shift mechanical skill after 18:00—are missed. Ada’s 2-hour electrical and validation capacities (~60 and ~75) do not follow from 112 minutes and multipliers 1.35 and 1.15 (~83 and ~97).

U2 is misread. “E and ES demand doubles; proportions are renormalized” does not double electrical minutes or push a ~400-minute block, and it does not materially raise software. The comparison table’s delays and SLA rates are not derived from the load math, and they disagree with the code: strict U5 delay is >250 in the prose, 220 in the summary frame, and 170 in `approx_metrics`. Hybrid is said to give up a little base mean delay versus full pooling, but the table has hybrid better on mean delay, P1 SLA, and resilience in nearly every cell, so the claimed trade-off is not in the evidence. Switching ME/ES to sequential when utilization exceeds 90% does not cut resource-minutes.

The Python block encodes skills and service ranges, emits an interval log, and calls the requested PM4Py functions, but it does not encode the demand or scenario tables. U1–U3 are identical factors, resource calendars and contention are absent, setup and preemption are constants that are not applied, and a `None` multiplier can break ME/ES duration math. Case-level `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` are not separated from stage or queue time. Printed “metrics” are hardcoded heuristics, not repeated-seed outputs of the log.
