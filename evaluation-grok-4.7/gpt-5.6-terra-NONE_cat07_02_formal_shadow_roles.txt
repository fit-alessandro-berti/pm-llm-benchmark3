The role model is largely faithful to the counts, complexity mix, similarities, and substitution episodes, and it correctly refuses a clean frequency clustering: Nora stays an automation boundary, temps stay separate from R01/R02, Chen is not treated as interchangeable with Dina, Giorgio is a hybrid shadow, Mateo is a broker rather than a second supervisor, and Erik is kept as a low-volume, high-criticality specialist. Benefits and risks of shadowing are both stated, and sections 4 and 6 do not treat substitution or activity overlap as authority.

Deductions are for evidence that gets rewritten and for code that would not support the claims.

Several performed events are relabeled without attributes that would justify it. Giorgio’s 20 and Fatima’s 16 A9 events are called “approval preparation,” and Mateo’s A9 work is generalized as preliminary review, although the log only says that 21 of his 44 A9 events during Hana’s absence were preliminary and that 13 were later rechecked. “Rechecked” is also upgraded to “required.” Chen’s 24 A6 events, and Mateo’s 16, are actual security-review executions; “A6-adjacent,” “security-adjacent diagnosis,” and “A6-related contacts” soften performer semantics. Amira’s 42 negotiations, third-highest in the matrix, are only “occasional,” so the front-office row understates a possible second commercial bridge that section 4 only partly recovers. The technical happy path is described as validate–diagnose–configure–quality–close while A10 is simultaneously called core for Chen and Dina. “Nearly exclusive” for Nora’s A1/A2 is ambiguous at best: those activities dominate her profile, but humans account for most validation volume.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested functions and joins complexity and substitution onto roles, but it is not coherent against ordinary PM4Py parameters. Role discovery, similarity, handover, working-together, and subcontracting are called with direct `resource_key` / `activity_key` / `case_id_key` arguments; the usual API takes a `parameters` dict (and `beta` on handover). The role adapter looks for `resources` / `members` / `name` and never reads `originator_importance`, so on a real `Role` list the merge would be empty. Complexity is aggregated at event level, so it will not reproduce the case-level medians used in the prose. `astype(bool)` is unsafe if `temporary_substitution` is a string. Activity literals drop the scenario’s “A6.”-style names, and the Fatima window is an invented 2024 fortnight rather than the six-day absence. Service-time results are computed and then unused, so the 38-to-74-minute finding is never actually measured.

Shift is barely used, and the control section does not state the segregation issue already in the matrix: Chen both implements and performs a material share of A6. Those gaps do not overturn the structure, but they keep the answer short of a near-flawless reading of the log.
