The simulation does not implement the dispatch rule claimed in Section 3. Pool and Hybrid rank a resource–task pair by smaller slack, and slack is `deadline − now − resource-specific duration`. A slower qualified resource produces a smaller slack and therefore wins the `min` over a faster one for the same task. The third key would prefer a shorter duration only after priority and slack already tie, which different speeds prevent. Section 3’s “earliest completion using speed” and Section 3’s “speed-aware choices” describe the opposite of this sort. The priority weights inside the slack term are inert, because priority is already the first lexicographic key. This contaminates every Pool and Hybrid delay, SLA, utilization, and fairness figure. The Strict-versus-flexible contrast is still directionally informative, because the bug makes the flexible rules worse than specified and they still show lower mean delay. The Hybrid-versus-Pool edge is not informative.

Hybrid, as operated in the code, is a small variant of Pool: parallel threshold 20 versus 12, preemption slack 35 versus 15, and an extra overhead term on an already-overheaded duration (overhead is effectively counted three times on that key, not twice). Primary-family eligibility, the 45-minute P2 and 15-minute coverage overflow triggers, 15-minute re-evaluation, and P3 aging are absent. Section 3 discloses part of this, then Section 4 still labels that variant “Hybrid,” and Section 5 recommends Hybrid because it preserves named specialist primaries. Those primaries were never enforced in the runs behind the table. The recommendation is therefore ahead of the evidence the answer itself generated. The prose hedge that five seeds cannot separate Hybrid from Pool is appropriate; naming Hybrid as the operating rule anyway is not.

Several other metric choices weaken Section 4. Unfinished cases are SLA misses but are dropped from mean and p90 delay, so the tail statistics omit the worst outcomes in the scenarios where completion differs. Slack for preemption sums original base work across all incomplete activities, including both branches of an ME/ES case, and ignores speed, so the 15- and 35-minute triggers are not clock-time slack. Electrical and Software are coded as one setup family, so an Electrical-to-Software switch incurs neither the 5-minute setup nor, on a resource change within that family, the team-transfer penalty. That is a debatable reading of “skill family” and should have been stated as an assumption. Handover and setup, later estimated near 11–14 minutes per case and therefore on the order of 500 minutes per day, are left outside the Section 2 staffing bound. “At least two” added shifts is a true lower bound only under the zero-overhead, multiplier-1 ideal; with those penalties it is already a weak staffing plan. The answer says two may be insufficient, without closing the arithmetic.

The required event-log fields and the PM4Py calls are present, and the warning that `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is case-level, can go negative when parallel branches overlap, and is not stage-queue wait is correct and important. `discover_handover_of_work_network` is then read through `.connections`. Sections 1, 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest part of the answer: assumptions are separated from observations, both U2 readings are kept alive, the 90% utilization constraint is recognized as infeasible on this roster, P1 sample size and one-day cohort optimism are stated plainly, and the monitoring design distinguishes recorded segment duration from active service. Those sections are why the result is still a serious analysis. They do not repair an invalid horse-race or a recommendation whose distinguishing mechanism was never simulated.
