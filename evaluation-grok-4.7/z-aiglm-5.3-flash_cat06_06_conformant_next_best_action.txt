The final recommendation is directionally right: a conformant independent second review is primary, alternate screening is only a parallel input, P-901 spillover is explicit, and immediate execution and self-certification are correctly left at the bottom as currently nonconformant. The six-section structure, labels, and F/G validity conditions are also largely right. It is not nearly flawless.

The timing model is wrong in a load-bearing way. Action C is a 35–75 minute review followed by 5–15 minutes to record and, if approved, execute, so the stated window is 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. The table extends execution to t+105, then treats the deadline as met only in the lower half and assigns a residual breach probability of about 0.25–0.5. That double-counts the post-review interval and makes the S2-versus-S4 comparison look tighter than the given ranges support.

The primary plan also skips a mandatory process step. The prefix ends at Evidence Validated, and the evidence cycle returns to Analyst Review before approval. Section 4 instead says analyst review is already satisfied and sends the file straight to S2. Second-officer review is additional while the score stays at 72 or the fuzzy match stays unresolved; it does not replace that return review. The later trigger that a lower score would “revert to the ordinary analyst path” makes the omission sharper, because the analyst step is required regardless of score.

Several quantities are stipulated rather than derived. The re-screen branches, roughly 0.3–0.5 and 0.5–0.7, overlap and do not partition the outcomes. S4’s review is assumed at 25–45 minutes, faster than S2’s expedited 35–75, and that unsupported assumption drives both Rank 3 and the claim that availability at 35 minutes makes the no-interruption plan best. The €20–35k deadline value is multiplied by an on-time probability without a clear joint probability of approval and timely execution. The P-901 spillover, given for a 45-minute interruption, is applied to a review that may occupy S2 for 35–75 minutes. Section 3 also calls false rejection reversible in principle, while Section 2 treats irreversibility as F’s defining feature.

The ordinal judgment survives these errors, which is why the answer remains clearly competent. The clock error, the skipped post-evidence analyst review, and the incoherent invented ranges keep it well below a near-flawless score.
