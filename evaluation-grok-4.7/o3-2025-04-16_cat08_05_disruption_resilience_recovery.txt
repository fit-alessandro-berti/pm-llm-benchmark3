4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and reaches several defensible judgments, but it is not a reliable reading of this event. Invented figures, misread directly-follows graphs, and a resilience classification that treats after-the-fact cleanup as control compliance undermine the analysis a manager would act on.

The timeline is directionally ordered and repeats several real phase rates (hospital median 8 h to 10 h, traceability 9.5 %, cold-chain documentation missing 4.8 %, controlled-substance dual-control missing 2.9 %, fitting traces 44 % / 59 % / 76 %, hospital fill 91 %). It then adds states the log does not support. Day 0 morning is described as a scanning and temperature-IoT blackout with throughput near zero. The record only says the warehouse system became unavailable; the manual phase still processed 5,600 cases. Partial-recovery gaps are called 3–5× baseline; traceability 5.4 / 0.8, cold-chain 2.3 / 0.4, and dual-control 1.4 / 0.2 are about 6–7×. Stabilization is called almost back to baseline. Hospital median cycle time did return to 8 h, but pharmacy median was 28 h versus 23 h, wholesale 58 h versus 36 h, wholesale p90 120 h versus 72 h, traceability 2.2 % versus 0.8 %, and rework 5.8 % versus 3.5 %. Fitness values 0.96, 0.71, 0.82, and 0.90 are never separated from the share of fully fitting traces. Pharmacy fill of 61 %, the rework path, and wholesale delay are left out of the narrative, so the distributional cost of protection is missing.

The adaptation table states precise facts that are not in the DFGs or the phase summaries. Fast-lane and skip-authorization are sibling branches of emergency triage (1,000 versus 1,600 versus 2,500 manual checks), yet the table says 16 % of fast-lane orders skipped electronic authorization. Other unsupported claims include +8 % ambient throughput, a 6-hour snapshot limit that “caused” the 1,400 later reconciliations, zero controlled-substance diversion, 98 % sample QC, 1.3 % missed temperature records (the phase rate is 4.8 %), a 40 % cut in dock dwell, a 5,200-item scan gap, 99.7 % book-stock accuracy, 1,400 reconciliation lines per day, and no regulator finding. Dispatch-without-scan inflows are 1,800 + 1,450 = 3,250, with 2,600 continuing to phone proof of delivery. The 1,400 reconciliations are the stabilization total, not a daily rate. Audit volume is inconsistent inside the answer: 2,330 cases in the timeline versus 2,300 dossiers in the table. The log shows 2,300 entries and 2,100 completions. Phase-level results are also pinned on single activities: the 91 % hospital fill on the fast lane, the 7.8 % cancellation rate on deferral (320 of the cancels leave the backlog; about 430 cases cancel overall), and the p90 improvement on backlog prioritization, although system restoration was already underway in the partial-recovery window.

Backlog prioritization (1,500 of 35,000) and manual-reservation reconciliation (1,400) are called the dominant stabilization variants. The dominant path is ordinary receive, authorize, reserve, pick, pack, release, dispatch, and invoice. More manual packs bypassed temporary release (1,800) than entered it (1,650). That split, the move-on-log set (skip authorization, dispatch without scan, phone proof of delivery, spreadsheet invoice hold), and the move-on-model set (missing release, scan, proof of delivery, and temperature record) are not interpreted. Spreadsheet invoice hold, about 2,200 cases, is never discussed. Recall-sensitive product is never mentioned.

Section 3 correctly treats skip-authorization and scanless dispatch as noncompliant shortcuts and treats the snapshot and phone proof as time- or product-conditional. It then labels reconciliation and audit remediation as true resilience. Those activities restored files after operational close and left a compliance tail; they are not evidence that minimum controls held while product moved. Manual pick lists are placed with resilience only by assuming later reconstruction, which is the traceability failure the workaround created. The stated test — a documented, time-bounded compensating control — would also have excluded post-hoc dossier repair and would have flagged that controlled-substance dual-control misses still rose from 0.2 % to 2.9 % while the temporary release was in use.

The future model contains the requested blocks: a trigger, hospital triage, a product matrix, offline evidence, backlog ordering, and a reconciliation and audit path. The 6-hour rule then suspends new picks, and absence of an offline scanner holds the shipment. Both rules, applied to an outage that lasted days, would stop hospital supply unless another path is defined. Deferral of noncritical orders, which the table says to keep, is absent from intake. Partial recovery, when order intake was back and inventory synchronization was untrustworthy, is not an operating mode. Lot or serial capture, recall-sensitive handling, and invoice-hold control are absent. Cold-chain control is reduced to a label and an infrared spot check, which does not document the temperature history the incident lost. The 10-minute, 20 %, and 5-day parameters are design choices, but they are not tied to product risk: a controlled-substance gap and an ambient paperwork gap share one SLA.

The indicator list covers fill rate, temperature-log completion, scan completion, backlog age, rework, dual-control misses, and audit age. Throughput is required and missing. The fairness ratio of pharmacy median cycle time to hospital median cycle time treats the intended priority gap as unfairness and ignores wholesale and within-segment consistency. No indicator separates real-time evidence from reconstructed evidence.

The recommendations are practical and carry effort, benefit, and residual risk. Their order does not follow risk: retiring authorization bypass is high benefit and low effort, yet it ranks below laminated checklists. Offline scanning, codified triage, a surge compliance roster, and pre-agreed deferral terms match the observed bottlenecks. The list still misses a partial-recovery rule, recall-sensitive handling, and a regulatory escalation trigger when dual-control or temperature evidence is missing.

The closing trade-off is the strongest passage: protect patient supply, refuse undocumented authorization and custody bypasses, and demand contemporaneous compensating evidence. It conflicts with the model that holds every unscanned shipment, and with the earlier decision to call audit repair resilience. The five-day reconciliation clause again makes later paperwork the test of whether the adaptation was acceptable.
