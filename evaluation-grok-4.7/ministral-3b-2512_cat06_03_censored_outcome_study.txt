3.5

The design is not usable as specified. It names a conditional-on-activation estimand, then analyzes a different one, mis-dates both structural breaks, and supplies a skeleton that does not implement the required cohort, split, or leakage controls.

**Target semantics.** The primary estimand is only loosely the allowed “default within 90 days given activation.” Calling that quantity a conditional survival probability swaps incidence for survival. Prediction time is “the first event that meets the prefix criteria,” which is not a fixed landmark and disagrees with the later 3-event rule. At that early time, activation is still in the future, so restricting the unit of analysis to eventual activations conditions on a post-prediction event. That selection is never separated from the quantity an early-warning score could actually emit for every open application. The alternative is mis-specified: rejected, withdrawn, and transferred cases are called competing outcomes and, in the same sentence, non-default. Default is nested after activation, so rejection is not a competing event inside an activated-account model, and a cumulative incidence function is not a causal pathway and does not undo selection. The counterfactual “default had this rejected case been activated” is correctly called untestable, then blurred back into the competing-risk target.

**Cohort.** The six omitted-and-labeled buckets do sum to 48,000, but the flow is not an as-of accounting. Inclusion requires a start before 1 January 2024, which is outside the observation window. Immature activated cases are both excluded and described as censored. Transfers are excluded in the rules and censored in the table. Still-open cases are treated as known non-activations rather than unresolved at extraction. The 3,600 not-yet-mature activations are not separated from permanently missing partner feeds. Total activated volume is 25,200, not the 21,600 labeled slice shown as “activated.” Shares for still-open and immature cases are rounded incorrectly. Statuses required for the flow — labeled, censored, competing, selectively unobserved, not yet mature — are collapsed to a yes/no label flag.

**Censoring.** Ordinary right censoring, delayed label arrival, missingness at prediction time, and administrative immaturity at the 15 February 2026 extract are treated as the same 3,600 cases. A prospective prefix never has `default_90d` yet; that is the outcome, not a censoring type. The stated primary method is Fine–Gray “for activated cases,” with rejection and transfer as competing events and still-open cases censored. Those events occur in cases that are not in an activated cohort, so the likelihood does not match Section 1. For a fixed 90-day horizon measured from activation, label-mature accounts support a binary probability; accounts with follow-up shorter than 90 days plus the 7–35 day feed lag are administratively censored. Permanently missing partner feeds are a different mechanism and can bias either a complete-case mean or a censoring model if they are dropped or coded as event-free. The sensitivity analysis only toggles among Fine–Gray, a generic survival model, and an unspecified binary model. It does not bound missing feeds, compare linked versus unlinked transfers, or vary the activation filter. Linkage completeness is not fully testable from a 60% link table; “partially testable (cannot verify)” is not a defined assumption class.

**Prefixes.** A count prefix of length 3 is arbitrary but coherent. The elapsed-time rule is not a prefix: “the first event after 24 hours” is one event, and it is applied only when the count prefix is ambiguous. Dropping cases with fewer than 3 events or less than 24 hours removes fast decisions and is not connected to bias. `pm4py.discover_dfg` does not build a rename map for the 15 October 2025 migration. Duplicate events from that migration are unhandled. The snippet calls `get_prefixes_from_log` and then indexes a `"length"` column; that function returns a log of truncated traces, not a frame.

**Features and leakage.** Control-flow, timing, and static channel, region, and amount are directionally appropriate. Resource, queue, rework, and calendar features are mostly absent or mislabeled: `risk_score` and `documents_total` are not queue features, and an in-prefix aggregate is not historical reference data. Policy era and migration era, which are the known shift markers, are not features. The audit correctly drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-prefix events, and `label_available_at` as predictors. The availability rules contradict those actions. `final_limit` can exist after the credit decision and before activation. `collection_status` is post-activation and can encode default, so “before activation” is not a valid window. `documents_total` repaired “at the prefix timestamp” still admits a nightly snapshot that includes later same-day uploads. Keeping the latest `risk_score` “before the prefix” both drops scores on prefix events and keeps a case-level field if that field was overwritten by later updates. Resource experience is stored at extraction, so it cannot be repaired by reading it off the event row; tenure has to be rebuilt from events at or before the prediction time. `label_available_at` is invalid as a feature and still required for maturity logic. `default_90d` repeated on event rows is not audited.

**Validation.** The split places the policy break in the second half of 2024 and calls January 2024–June 2024 “pre-migration.” The policy change is 1 July 2025 and the migration is 15 October 2025. Validation on late 2024 does not stress either break, and the test window from January 2025 through 14 February 2026 welds the policy change, the migration, post-window start dates, and label-immature cases into one score. There is no as-of rule that a training label is usable only when activation plus 90 days plus feed lag is on or before the simulated decision date. Calibration is not specified. “AUC for cumulative incidence” is not a defined metric; the log-rank test is not a predictive score; prevalence is about 12.5% among mature activated accounts, and no precision-recall, Brier, or calibration slope is given. The requirement to explain why a random case split misleads across policy and migration is omitted. Subgroup checks mention partners and case length only.

**Decision use.** Observed association for historical activations, selection into activation, and counterfactual default for people never activated are not held apart. Under a header of claims the study cannot make, the text states that the model estimates observed default risk. Two of the three “deployment” effects are the already-occurred migration and already-missing partner feeds. Deployment would change the data-generating process if scores changed who is activated, how much rework a case receives, or which risky cases remain in future labeled extracts. Those feedback paths are absent, so the use boundary does not say that the score is undefined for rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open applications.

**Skeleton.** The code is about 35 lines against the requested 80–160. It reads the CSV and parses one timestamp, and it names `get_prefixes_from_log`. It does not call `pm4py.format_dataframe`. The CSV importer path used is not the appropriate PM4Py entry point, and the log-converter import is unused. If `log` is an event log, column indexing fails. If it were a frame, start time, activation time, and label time are global minima and maxima, not case-level values, and activation is tested by comparing case identifiers with activity names, then collapsed with `.any()`. The merge key does not match the metadata column. There is no configurable cutoff, no terminal activity, no censoring flag, and no maturity rule of activation plus 90 days plus feed lag. There is no train, validation, and test split by case start, temporal or otherwise. No feature table is built, so post-prefix events and columns are not blocked. The leakage check is a hardcoded three-row frame. Comments do not mark the activation-conditioning assumption or a later survival or competing-risk attachment point. Cohort counts printed from this frame would not reconcile to 48,000.

Together, the right high-level family of target and a few correct drop decisions are outweighed by an internally contradictory estimand, a cohort that cannot be rebuilt from the rules, leakage repairs that still leak, validation eras that miss both known breaks, and code that would not produce the study it describes.
