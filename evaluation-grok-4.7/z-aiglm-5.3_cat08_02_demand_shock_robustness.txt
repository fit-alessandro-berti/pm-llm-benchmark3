The redesign is complete, well-structured, and mostly faithful to the logs: class-level volumes, defect split (6.9% to 12.3%), accession recovery (86% to 74%), batch-wait share (4.4% to 12.7%), bypass failure (26% to 42%), repeat share (6.2% to 12.3%), medical-validation coverage (~23% to ~41%), direct-release share (74% to 50%), and weekly specialist holds (36 to 203) all check out. The score is held down by several claims stated as evidence that the logs do not support. Open traces are treated as proof that backlog grew from 10.7% to 13.3%, and that figure then drives S6 and a dashboard band. Absolute unfinished cases fell from 1,960 to 1,320, and the shorter peak window plus longer cycle times can raise the share without any extra accumulation. Migration magnitudes are invented with false precision: batch wait to 40h+, medical queue to 60h+, holds to 70h+, prep to 20h+, assay queue to 30h+, and registration above 95% in spikes. “Every Urgent case” needs medical validation, which conflicts with the answer’s own ~85% Urgent-plus-Complex calculation and is not reconciled with in-flight or direct-release cases. M2 “about 2×” assumes equal machine capacity even though M2 is faster; “well past 100%” assumes an unknown medical-validator headcount; repeat reduction is said to free 4–6% of capacity when 12.3% to 8% is about 4.3 points. I9 “guarantees” a Routine breach, and the Urgent mix shift is attributed to penalty-driven over-flagging, although the prompt frames it as a winter-wave case mix. Samples aging in downstream buffers are also described as failing pre-analytical checks, which is the wrong station.
