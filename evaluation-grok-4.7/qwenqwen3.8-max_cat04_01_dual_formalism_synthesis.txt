8.3

The POWL model is the right canonical choice and is largely faithful: partial-order edges capture the cross-branch join (plan and design before the trial loop; trial, approval route, and optional supplier before release), the high-risk reviews are unordered, supplier consultation is an XOR with a silent transition, and the loop encodes repeat-until without a trailing adjustment. The eight witnesses match those models, include the required low-risk, high-risk, supplier, and two-iteration cases, and F2 is a genuine cross-branch violation. Assumptions, loss, and validation limits are stated rather than disguised.

The process tree is a valid conservative under-approximation, but it is not as faithful as a still block-structured alternative. Forcing the approval route and supplier consultation to finish before the trial deletes the pattern the narrative centers on: approvals and supplier work overlapping the trial, and release synchronizing those independent obligations. An equally simple tree—join containment/planning with analysis/design, then run the trial loop in parallel with the approval XOR and the optional supplier—would keep that overlap and the release join, while only delaying when approvals and supplier work may start. The chosen tree makes release wait only on the trial. That loss is acknowledged, but the approximation policy is coarser than necessary.

Smaller gaps: the concurrency argument does not really account for every incomparable pair (plan versus approvals/supplier is left to “all other pairs”); allowing supplier or approvals to overlap containment is looser than the overlaps the narrative actually lists; and the playout snippet depends on a `max_events` argument the API is not shown to support. Relative to the models as defined, the trace verdicts and loop reading are consistent.
