6.5

The role model is directionally sound: temps are separated from experienced front-office agents, Nora is treated as automation, Giorgio is a commercial hybrid, Erik is a low-volume critical specialist, and Mateo is a broker rather than Hana’s peer. Complexity, substitutions, and authority are used, and shadow roles are given both benefits and risks. That is not enough for a high score. Several claims contradict the matrix, one operational conclusion reverses the evidence, section 2 does not cover every proposed role, and the code does not do the join it describes.

Factual and logical problems:
- Amira and Ben have A9 = 0, but A9 is listed as a peripheral activity. Amira also has 18 A7 events, so “they do not approve pricing” is false.
- Hana’s 62 critical cases are not the most; Mateo has 76. “Most critical cases” is wrong.
- Kai and Lina are not low-only and not “zero A5–A10”: they have substantial medium-complexity volume and A8 counts of 3 and 4. Their median scores are not the lowest in the organization; Nora is 0.11.
- Mateo’s core set includes A7 (10 events), one of his smallest activities, while higher-volume A2, A4, and A12 are omitted.
- Section 1 calls A6 “Chen only”; the later 24 vs 6 comparison is the one that matches the data. Dina has 6 A6 events.
- Erik’s total is 170, not ~160.
- “Similarity to Fatima is 0.71, significantly higher than Amira/Ben” is unsupported. 0.71 is below the given Amira–Ben similarity of 0.93, and Fatima’s similarity to Amira or Ben is not in the evidence.
- A pricing “threshold” and Giorgio’s “lack of tool access” are invented. The log only shows duration rising from 38 to 74 minutes.
- Section 3 correctly says Mateo covered approvals while Hana was away and that 13 of 21 were rechecked. Section 6 then says the opposite: that Hana had to step in because Mateo was absent. That reversal is used as evidence for a staffing risk.

Coverage gaps:
- Section 2 was required for each proposed role, including frequency, complexity, substitution, and authority. Only tier-1 agents, technical engineers, Fatima, and Mateo are discussed. Temps, Erik, quality, Hana, Giorgio, and Nora are missing there.
- The quality “naïve clustering would call them junior technicians” rationale is implausible: A11 (about 300) dominates A3 (about 20), so a frequency cluster would still see a quality profile.
- Mateo’s 16 A6 events are conflated with filtering referrals away from Erik. Those are different mechanisms in the scenario.

Code:
- The pipeline names the requested PM4Py calls, but it is not coherent. `format_dataframe` and `filter_event_attribute_values` are called with the wrong signatures. An event log is iterated as if it were a flat list of events. `sub_log` used in the authority check is only the last resource from the previous loop, so the frequency-versus-authority test does not run per resource. Role output is never actually joined to `case_complexity` or `temporary_substitution`; the comment admits the join is mocked. `KMeans`, `numpy`, and `EventLog` are unused.

Section 6 does include two real cautions (do not promote Ben on volume; do not merge Chen and Dina on a 0.94 profile). Those cautions fit the constraints, but the Mateo absence claim does not.
