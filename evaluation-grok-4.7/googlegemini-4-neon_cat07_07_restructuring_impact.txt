7.7

The forecast is strong, evidence-based, and complete: all seven required sections are present, East is treated differently from North and South, fairness and learning are carried through the scenarios and the pilot, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The load arithmetic is right (approvals about 294 to 515 per remaining supervisor, roughly +75%; Legal/Risk referrals 9 to 12 into a 91–93% queue; tech referrals 36 to 41).

It is not nearly flawless. The analysis sketch hard-codes a 14-day SLA, which contradicts the given data: North’s P90 is already 16.4 days while the SLA miss rate is only 6.5%, and East’s P90 is 22.8 days with a 9.8% miss rate. That threshold would invent a much higher baseline miss rate and corrupt the pre/post comparison. Specialist utilization is also inconsistent across sections: +15% on a 73.7% base is about 85%, the central case uses 84–88%, and the recommendation warns about 88–92% as if that were the same target. The mechanism map says listed handovers “may not decrease,” but management’s own table sums to about 343 versus 359 handover events per 100 cases. Scenario P90 shifts of several days are stated with false precision and are not bridged from the hour-scale waits in the routing table. The pilot’s North difference-in-differences control ignores interference through the already shared Legal/Risk queue, and the 20% local office-hours reservation is not reconciled with the utilization and queue effects being tested. The PM4Py sketch is broad and mostly faithful, but it does not note that a single `time:timestamp` makes service-versus-waiting splits approximate, and `get_variants` is sorted as if values were counts.
