8.2

The forecast is complete, balanced, and unusually quantitative: handover totals (343 to 359), the frequency×median wait index (588.3 h to 722.2 h per 100 cases), volume-weighted baselines, the legal/risk ρ≈1.24 instability, the approval-load +75% case, and the Erlang-C pooling comparison are arithmetically right and correctly used. All seven required sections are present and actually answer the question. East is treated differently from North/South, fairness and learning are operationalized, average cycle time is not the decision metric, and the recommendation is a conditional, modified pilot rather than a full rollout. The censoring trap, utilization-as-constraint point, and non-inferiority pilot are strong.

It is not nearly flawless.

The headline break-even, “pooled ρ must stay below ~0.88–0.90,” is an M/M/c result under equal, exponential service. The proposal mixes complex work, legal/risk triage, and quality checks into one queue, which raises service-time variability and would move that threshold down. That caveat is missing from the claim that later drives the recommendation. The same section’s “+1.34 h/case” figure is a sum of medians, then section 6 calls it “average queue wait.” The central scenario still has median cycle time falling while that index rises, without a quantified offset.

Capacity advice is internally inconsistent. “+45% legal/risk capacity” is the figure implied by 0.93×1.33 at a 0.85 target, but the optimistic scenario and the pilot add “+0.5 FTE.” These people are part-time, so 0.5 FTE is not known to be +45%; if they are near 2 FTE combined, +0.5 leaves ρ near 0.98 and does not produce the optimistic queue.

The PM4Py sketch would mis-measure several things it claims to measure. `max(nx.shortest_path_length(...))` compares dictionaries and does not return depth. Betweenness with `weight="weight"` treats frequency as distance, so busy edges look far. `wt_modularity_by_team` is modularity of detected communities, not of the team partition. `absence_stress` does not recompute pool ρ after removing one server. SLA day limits are invented and unlabeled, so the code cannot reproduce the stated miss rates. The log has one timestamp per event, but utilization is computed as if `@@service_time` were observed resource busy time. Role similarity, role discovery, and temporal features are defined and then left out of `compare`.
