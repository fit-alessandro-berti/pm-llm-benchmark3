The case-level table is the strongest part: observed waits and services are arithmetically correct, open cases are kept as elapsed service rather than dropped, and the missing-start intervals for P05, P07, P11, and P15 follow resource precedence. That does not carry the station-level argument.

Counts do not add up. P01–P20 all have an S1 end, so S1 completions are 20, not 19, and only P22 is still waiting for S1. S2 therefore has four waiters (P16, P18, P19, P20), not three. Arrivals in 10:00–10:59 are 4, not 3, because P17 arrives at 10:45; the subperiod counts sum to 21 rather than 22. Fully observed S2 service averages 40 minutes, not 41.

WIP is not reconstructed. The stated definition omits the S1 queue, and the reported peaks (about 4–9 overall, about 7 at S2 around 12:00–13:00) do not match a census of the log: system WIP peaks near 13 around 11:30, and S2 occupancy peaks at 7 only at 13:45. No simultaneous-event convention is actually applied.

The Little’s Law check is not defensible on the numbers given. For 08:00–10:30, λ is not 6.7/h, W is not 0.7 h if W means sojourn, and in-system WIP is already about 7–11, not 4–6. Section 2 also uses W for queue wait, so the identity is ambiguous. The claim that S1 waiting “grows monotonically” is false (P13, P17, and P21 dip). The “±15 min” shift in mean S1 wait from the missing starts is far too large; those two ranges move the mean by only a couple of minutes. “S2 tighter early” conflicts with early utilization: S1 is busier, while S2’s longer service (about 40 vs 28 minutes) is the capacity constraint and is not reconciled with the queue story. The C2 outage sits inside P08’s 55-minute wait and is treated too lightly. The P07/P09 boundary at 11:00 on C1 is never examined. Rounding is assumed and then not propagated, and completed-case total sojourn is defined but never reported.

The code collapses MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same null, does not compute WIP or the three imputation scenarios, and the comment promising a both-stations projection is not what the filter does.
