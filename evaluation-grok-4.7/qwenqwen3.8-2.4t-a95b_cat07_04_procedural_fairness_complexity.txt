7.5

The final assessment is complete, constraint-compliant, and numerically faithful to the scenario. It covers every required fairness dimension, separates disparity from legitimate routing and from discrimination, and reaches a calibrated conclusion: procedural-burden problems are real, while discriminatory intent is not established. Mitigations keep language support and accommodation review and pair each option with a trade-off.

The score is held down by a few real flaws.

The largest analytical slip is in the path comparison. The +9.5 percentage-point gap in Request Missing Documents is already adjusted for documentation completeness, yet the “likely legitimate” explanation offered for that residual is Group B’s higher incomplete-documentation share. That covariate cannot explain a gap estimated after controlling for it. The same looseness appears when Group D’s fraud-review routing, which is largely consistent with the higher risk mix, is bundled with the +5.7 point appeal residual, which remains after risk is controlled.

The PM4Py plan would not measure what it claims. Case durations and path/case performance filters are treated as days; PM4Py uses seconds, and there is no conversion. Waiting-time aggregation assumes `service_time` and `waiting_time` columns and groups event rows, so cases with more events are overweighted. The slow-case statistic is a group composition share, not a within-group rate, so Group A’s larger base would dominate it even with a lower slow-case rate. `discover_declare` parameter names are also off.

A smaller conceptual tension: discrimination is defined as a gap that resource constraints cannot explain, while the rest of the answer correctly treats understaffed bilingual and accommodation queues as unfair. Residual gaps such as −2.2 percentage points and +0.9 days are called meaningful without any uncertainty, standard error, or practical-significance check.

Those issues are localized. They do not overturn the bottom line, but they keep the answer short of nearly flawless.
