The answer covers every required section, reaches the right decision (`conditional go`), and treats Site A’s nine-hour median drop as confounded. Those strengths do not offset material errors in the causal graph, the quantities, the pilot’s feasibility, and the POWL model.

**Process comparison.** Several rows collapse Site A’s before and after into the “before” cell and leave “after” blank (case mix, tail, rework, timestamp reliability). Site B’s mix is described as static from a single baseline. The feature list itself is otherwise the right one: originals gate, legal loop, role split, case lock, and timestamp artifact are all identified, and blanket copying of the six-task cap is rejected in this section.

**Transport graph.** Every required node appears, and the prose correctly says a shared `+` is not a shared effect. Several edges are mechanistically wrong. `API_latency -> identity_capacity` treats a latency change as a staffing change. `originals_required -> document_capacity` treats a start-gate as a change in headcount. `S_legal_requirement -> legal_capacity` sends a case-mix selector into a resource stock instead of into a legal-review indicator. The 18-hour originals delay, the dominant Site B cycle-time driver, never reaches `cycle_time`. The write-up then calls the effect modifiers themselves selection nodes. Selection nodes point at mechanisms that differ; they are not those mechanisms.

**Evidence table.** Confounding is handled well: mix shift, two added reviewers, API upgrade, huddles, absent negative control, and logging artifact are separated from the parallelization claim. Confidence values of `n/a` break the required 0–100 scale. Overlap is scored as less transportable than the waiting-time change that is supposed to be mediated by overlap.

**Modifiers.** Coverage is complete. The supplement rate is wrong: 360 supplement requests out of 1,360 legal-review cases is 26.5%, while 10.6% is the share of all 3,400 cases. Identity work is already allowed to start at registration, so concurrent task creation does not surge the four-person identity pool; it pulls document checking forward and synchronizes arrivals at assessment. The claim that waiting falls only in the 38% no-originals subset drops the partial gain from running identity in parallel with originals receipt.

**Scenarios.** The required pattern — median improves, p90 worsens — is present in the legal-congestion row. The magnitudes are not derived. `38% × (1 − 40%)` is 23%, and the stated 23–30% band has no upper-bound calculation. Scenario 1’s 15–25% median change is described as Site A’s waiting-time drop “scaled” by eligible share, which double-counts eligibility already stored in its own column and treats a subgroup effect as if it were a blended median. Medians of mixtures are not weighted averages of medians, yet Scenario 2 converts a 10–20% subgroup change on 15–20% of volume into a 3–6% blended median. Sign convention flips: unsigned percentages mean improvement in Scenario 1 and `+` means worsening later. An overall median gain of 8–15% (about 3.5–6.6 hours on a 44-hour median) is too large once 62% of cases carry an 18-hour originals delay that front-end overlap cannot remove. The median case is likely inside that majority, so the overall median can stay nearly still while a minority stratum improves.

**Candidate design.** Tiering is the right modification: no document check before originals, identity allowed beside originals receipt, legal review and the supplement loop left intact, system-generated timestamps required. The design then hard-codes identity WIP at 6, the Site A number just rejected, and invents document and assessment caps with no arrival or service calculation. Holding the assessment-to-legal handoff to “the two-reviewer service rate” misstates steady-state flow: the same demand does not raise legal arrivals permanently; it creates a transient bolus and tighter synchronization. The textual order also puts `Document Check` and `Assessment` both outside and inside a loop, which is not a strict partial order.

**Pilot.** The mechanism-versus-implementation split is clear: instrumented overlap first, then waiting time, with stratum-specific queue and rework signatures. Assignment is not credible against the stated pools. Two legal reviewers and three assessors cannot be partitioned into two treated and two control clusters without splitting people or sharing them, which reintroduces the interference the cluster unit was meant to block. Three treatment versions times four strata times two arms is far beyond a four-week Phase 1 of roughly 500 completions. “300 cases per cell” from `p90/median ≈ 2.5` is not a power calculation; that ratio is not a standard error. A 20% relative rise on a 5% appeal rate can trip the stop rule on noise.

**Code.** The script hits the requested calls: process-tree parse, Petri conversion, `StrictPartialOrder` edges into assessment, a loop, soundness checks, and playout. The model is not a valid POWL of the proposed candidate. `document_check` and `assessment` are the same objects in the top-level order and in the loop redo, so the graph is cyclic through containment. The comment says both originals branches are represented; the net always requires `Originals Receipt` before `Document Check`, so the Tier 1 identity–document parallel path — the actual analogue of Site A — is absent. Limitations listed afterward (data guards, capacity, locks, durations) are accurate and do not repair the model.

**Recommendation and assumptions.** `conditional go`, Tier 1 first, and expansion only after audited overlap, a waiting-time drop, and stable downstream queues, is the right policy. The “what transfers” paragraph then limits identity–document concurrency to the non-legal 23–30%, which contradicts Tier 3, where front-end concurrency is still allowed and legal review stays downstream. Eight assumptions are present and mostly testable. Assumption 6’s label is malformed, and Assumption 1’s test depends on a Site A audit that this engagement does not have.

The strategic judgment is sound and the confounding analysis is strong. The score stays here because the quantitative mapping, the selection diagram, the resource-feasible pilot, and a sound POWL of the candidate intervention are central requirements and are not delivered cleanly.
