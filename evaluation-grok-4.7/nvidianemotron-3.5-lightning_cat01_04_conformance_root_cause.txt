5.5

The delivered analysis correctly treats the June collapse as a stack of logging, technical-event, and ordering artifacts, keeps the August sign-off change separate from that collapse, and refuses to infer employee misconduct from alignment moves alone. That core judgment is the strongest part of the answer. It is not enough to carry the submission, because several required artifacts are factually wrong, internally inconsistent, or not executable.

The evidence ledger has the required shape and twelve rows, but it misses the decisive staged alignment path (32% → 58% → 81% → 89%, then August 66% → 86%). It also mis-assigns support: the UI rename is said to support a timestamp defect, and stable appeal and approval rates are said to weaken every hypothesis, including the data defects those rates actually leave standing. `Queue Routed` is repeatedly attributed to the ETL. The chronology puts that event on the gateway on 3 June and the timestamp change on the ETL on 15 June. That provenance error is repeated in the causal narrative and in the provisional conclusion.

The ranking overweights ordering. Source-sequence sorting is the smallest measured gain (81% to 89%, cost 0.21 to 0.12), yet it is ranked above technical-event removal. The same label-change row expects label enforcement to restore roughly 90% fitness while its own contradicting-evidence cell reports 58% after mapping. `Manual Override` is treated as a removable technical event even though IT classifies it as a user action and it remains after `Queue Routed` removal. Model obsolescence is not clearly separated from “cause of the June drop” versus “later operational gap.”

The causal list is not a usable directed graph. `measured_fitness → business_outcomes` reverses the relationship, and `policy_change → normative_model` states the opposite of the record: the model was not updated. Required links among true process, recorded labels, reviewer behavior, and alignment moves are missing. The prose explanation itself is within the word limit and makes the right joint-cause point about stable outcomes.

The counterfactual table has six rows, not the required eight, and mostly restates repairs already measured. One falsifier bundles label mapping with technical-event removal, so it cannot discriminate. “80% misalignment” is ambiguous, and the ordering row calls cost 0.12 the pre-July level when that level was 0.08.

The four-phase program covers DFGs, variants, alignments, remapping, source ordering, stratification, and audit, and its risk column is useful. It never isolates model comparison or the precision cost of an optional sign-off. Several named PM4Py calls are not real methods.

The code fails the explicit requirement that it be coherent and executable. It never calls `parse_process_tree` or `parse_event_log_string`, builds the tree with nonexistent modules, and expands variants incorrectly: labels are keys such as `"Apr-May 1"` while counts are activity tuples, so the supplied frequencies are never encoded. `discover_dfg` is treated as a single edge dictionary, alignment APIs are invented, and the candidate sign-off tree does not express an optional activity. The EUR 50,000 caveat is printed and is one of the better technical remarks, but it sits on a script that cannot run.

The conclusion and six governance rules are the cleanest sections: confidence levels, stakeholder actions, residual missing-check uncertainty, and bans on overclaiming are all present. They still inherit the gateway/ETL mix-up and do not state that the 86% optional-sign-off fitness can be bought with an overgeneral model. Under a strict reading, those strengths do not offset the broken reproduction, the short counterfactual table, and the causal and provenance errors.
