The document is complete and often precise about the POWL partial order, but it is not reliable as an audit: a required completion test is implemented incorrectly, and one table states a conversion outcome that the text itself treats as unrun.

The bounded explorer records a completed trace only when a visible transition’s post-marking equals the final marking. Silent successors never update `completed`. BPMN-derived nets commonly reach the sink through an unlabeled end/routing transition after Archive, so a behaviorally correct round trip would fail every positive witness and the language comparison. That misses the required exact-final-marking rule. The tau budget can also drop valid traces without reporting truncation, and extra silent routing in a round trip makes false mismatches likely. Footprints are compared by `repr`, the reachability graph is only summarized, and playout is not inspected.

Section 4 marks every Petri-to-tree cell `conversion failed` while saying the table is pre-execution and that success is still possible. `conversion failed` is an observed outcome; the consistent pre-run term is `not tested`. Several Petri-to-BPMN cells are marked `approximated` where the stated concern is layout or gateway reading, which the later visual review correctly treats as separate from behavior.

The invariant table, pipeline choice, try/except boundary, rejection list, POWL-versus-BPMN authority split, and residual limits are substantively strong. Those parts do not offset a validation procedure that can reject a faithful net or a preservation table that asserts an unrun failure.
