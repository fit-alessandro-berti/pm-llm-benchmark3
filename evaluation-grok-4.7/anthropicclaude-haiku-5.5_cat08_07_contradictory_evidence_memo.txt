6.5

The memo reaches the right strategic posture: hybrid containment (E), no national scale-up, and no wholesale rollback. Counts, segment shares, cost figures, and discovery metrics are mostly transcribed correctly, the five conflicts are all addressed, and the evidence table does keep facts, interpretations, and assumptions in separate rows. Under a strict reading it is still not a reliable decision instrument, because the process-mining explanation is conceptually wrong, the median/tail account is too coarse, and the rollout rule contradicts itself.

The fitness/precision section misstates the mechanism. High fitness means observed pilot behavior can be replayed; it is not evidence that the model is flexible. Low precision means the model allows combinations the log did not show. Frequent move-on-log deviations are the opposite alignment: events in the log that the model does not accept. The memo treats those log moves as support for low precision and then reads the pair as proof that Redesign R “created multiple unstable paths.” That skips the mining team’s own account (a dominant simplified coordinator path, plus an overly permissive model) and never reconciles low precision (0.51) with low generalization (0.56). Those two metrics pull in different directions; listing both as one point is a miss on a criterion this memo is specifically meant to handle.

The median-versus-tail explanation is only the between-segment story. Simple houses improve on both median and p90. Old buildings worsen on both. Apartments (−2 days median, +12 days p90), business (−3 / +5), and rural (−1 / +9) show the split inside the segment. Row 13’s claim that the median gain is “driven by simple houses” overstates a 34% slice and understates median gains in three other slices. The tail is therefore not only a mix effect.

The decision rule is not operable. Section 6 requires every threshold to pass before a segment expands, then rolls a segment back only after two or more failures. A single severe miss, including billing before activation, neither expands nor rolls back. “Baseline tolerance” for coordinator absence is undefined. Several gates (85% utilization, +2 days p90, +5 days p95, manual activation ≤8%, second visits ≤ the aggregate 11%) are unanchored, and the 11% second-visit bar is an aggregate baseline applied as if it were each segment’s baseline. The 90-day decision date also cannot observe M4’s full 60-day post-billing cost for cases billed late in the window.

Value-of-information ranking is mostly tied to decisions, then breaks on the highest-ranked item. If survey skipping is harmless for medium-risk apartments, the next move is a broader skip rule. Mapping that result to “C with a broader skip rule” contradicts C, which suspends skipping. M5 is ranked near the bottom even though coordinator ownership is what the memo would scale immediately, including the absence failure the pilot already observed. Segment and building-risk attributes are incomplete before pilot week 3, and the risk rule is unvalidated, yet simple-house routing still uses those attributes now. The table records the incompleteness; the scale decision barely uses it.

Smaller faults add up. Fact rows carry interpretive “implications,” while the memo says the harms “trace to” skip, permit, and post-billing activation more strongly than the table’s own hedges. Visible-cost savings are attributed to skipped surveys and deferred activation without remaining room for shorter cycle time or fewer handovers. Permit-check model moves are specified for older apartment buildings, not only the 18% old-building segment. Utilization of 93% is limited to two regions in the evidence and generalized in the narrative. SLA (13% to 16%, better for simple houses) never enters the decision. A 62-day p95 inside a 6-week pilot raises censoring and open-case issues that the memo does not flag.

The recommendation is directionally defensible and the format is complete. The errors sit on the exact points the judgment turns on: metric conflict, process-mining artifacts, and a rule that could actually be executed.
