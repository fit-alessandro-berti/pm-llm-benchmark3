7.6

The write-up is a serious competing-risks analysis with the right time origin, a real split between factual non-resolution, late-resolution incidence, and no terminal disposition, and a clear refusal to treat withdrawal, rejection, or transfer as harmless censoring. It also handles open cases on either side of 72 hours, immortal time for escalation, non-random cohort B, and leakage from full prefixes. Those are the parts the task most needed. It is not nearly flawless: several concrete errors sit on points the task made central.

The resolved-case count in §3 is wrong. There are 13 observed resolutions (S01–S04, S07, S13, S16–S20, S27, S28), not 14, and 19 other cases, not 18. That is the denominator of the survivor-bias argument.

The §2 encoding table misstates cause-specific hazards. For the cause-specific hazard of resolution, withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are censored in the partial likelihood; they are competing events in the Aalen–Johansen probability, not “censoring only for a hypothetical Kaplan–Meier.” The column that was supposed to separate those rules conflates them. §4’s risk-set description is better than the table, but the table is the specification.

Estimand D never says how the required quartet contributes: resolved at 76 (S03), open at 92 (S09), withdrawn at 27 (S06), and transferred at 68 (S08). A, B, and C do. The late-resolution horizon “120 hours” is past the last observed time (110). On the age scale there is also no single “observation cutoff”; the freeze induces different censoring ages.

The state diagram omits the observed `Rework → Resolve` transition (S27) and adds an unobserved `Review → Review` self-loop. Repeated review in these prefixes returns through Rework or Escalate. That is a false control-flow claim, not only a sketch omission. S32 (transfer at 96) is also left implicit: at 72 hours it was still internal and already unresolved, unlike S08 and S21, whose transfers occur before the horizon. Transfer bounds do not move S32’s status at 72.

The code keeps every case and builds a PM4Py log without invented timestamps, but it does not estimate the Aalen–Johansen curves recommended as primary. The fitted Kaplan–Meier censors every non-resolution and then prints \(S(72)\) as “P(not internally resolved by 72),” which is the factual estimand the prose correctly says this curve does not estimate. `transfer_scenario_2_transfer_censored` is just that same net-resolution indicator, so it also censors withdrawal and rejection; the three scenarios are not contrasted as numbers. Open cases use `>= 72` and terminal events use `> 72` in `known_active_at_72`, and the prose elsewhere says “at/after 72.” No row lands on 72, so the counts do not move, but the boundary rule is not stable. Long `last_event_gap_h` values are also treated a bit loosely as informative administrative censoring; the gap is structurally 0 on every terminal row and is better read as a stall or missing-event marker among open cases than as the censoring time.

§1, §4, §6, §7, and §9 are otherwise coherent: decision-to-estimand mapping, small-sample caution, landmarking, calibration by priority and cohort, and the missing timestamp, external-outcome, workload, and rollout data are all in the right place. The grade is held down by the wrong event count, the cause-specific censoring slip, the diagram error, and code that reports the secondary hypothetical survival curve under the primary estimand’s name.
