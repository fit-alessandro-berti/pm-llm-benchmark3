The completed diagnosis is serious decision-set work, but it is not a finished answer and it is not internally consistent enough to score higher.

Sections 1–2 do the right kind of analysis. QA is reconstructed from eligible sets, and FIFO is earned by A02 beating A04 (priority 1, estimate 7) at 08:30 and A05 beating both A08 and A07 at 09:05, while the 08:20 choice of A03 is correctly left as appointment/SPT equivalence. QD’s within-resource FIFO evidence is real, especially D04 before D08 and D07 before D10, and the appointment idle on D-X is used properly. The final document then fails its own standard. The D-X 11:20 table says SPT predicts D07; D06’s estimate is 18 and D07’s is 28, so SPT predicts D06. That is a direct calculation error in the submitted table. QC is still labeled SPT in the diagnosis even after the same write-up shows SPT loses at 11:40 (C01 over C07) and is only saved at 10:32 by an implausible claim that C06, arrived at 10:25, was invisible at 10:32. The arrival-plus-estimate ordering, which fits those episodes better, is noted and then not allowed to revise the primary label. The 09:48 QB stop is the decisive anomaly for the P1-only model, yet it is not a reconstructed decision row; the “B05 scan race caused B03 to be started” story does not actually predict selecting B03.

Required sections 4–7 are absent, and section 3 stops mid-sentence. There is therefore no counterfactual test, no ranked exception list, no collection plan, and no cross-queue conclusion on identifiability or the most dangerous misinference. Noisy estimates, scan delay, and SLA-triggered policy change are only partly handled. Strong local reconstructions cannot carry an unfinished, self-contradictory report.
