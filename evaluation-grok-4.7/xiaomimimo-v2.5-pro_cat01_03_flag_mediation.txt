5.5

The write-up has the right skeleton—interventional rather than natural effects, interference, a P0–P4 contrast, and a pilot—but the empirical core and several required estimands are wrong, so it cannot score higher.

Mediation estimands are mis-specified. E3–E5 take manual-review occurrence, manual wait, and escalation occurrence as the outcomes. The requested estimands are effects on cycle time (or decision quality) mediated by those variables. Occurrence mediation and waiting-time mediation are therefore never actually defined, despite the label “indirect.” P1 is also treated as if hiding the flag automatically removes priority routing; display suppression and “set the routing flag to zero” stay conflated.

The stage table is not a defensible approximation. Flagged high-load vs normal-load is 6 vs 4, not 5 vs 5. Flagged escalation is 6 vs 4 not escalated, and those counts are swapped. Flag 0 escalation is marked NA even though R09 and R20 escalate; under the answer’s own rule those rows should be conditional on occurrence, as manual review was. Flagged total cycle time is given as 1380 minutes; the mean and median are both about 990. Flag 0 central cycle time is about 345 (median) or 490 (mean), not 520 vs 1380. Complexity strata, which were required, are absent. R14 is later called flagged; it is flag 0. “Unflagged cases rarely” enter review is false (4/10).

Those errors change the mechanism. Risk score perfectly separates the flag (flag 0 max 0.61, flag 1 min 0.64), so there is no overlap for a within-score or local-threshold contrast in this sample; complexity does overlap. Triage resources are perfectly split (flagged T1–T2, unflagged T3–T4). Both facts are more important than the M2 story and are missed. Unflagged high-complexity escalations have cycle times around 1100–1150, so “unflagged cases bypass the queue” is case-mix, not a pure flag contrast. The graph omits `cycle_time`, `triage_service`, and `senior_service`, and treats arrival-time `arrival_load` as if the case’s own flag could feed back into it.

The identification section names useful designs, but the RDD and “flagged cases that bypass review” tests are positivity-empty here, and there is no actual sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk distinct from the negative control. The P3 pilot ignores overnight carryover visible in the timestamps: odd/even days do not isolate a queue that runs past midnight. The synthesis then treats a drop in cycle time as confirmation of interference and a null as proof that cases are intrinsically slow, which does not follow—especially because escalation waits are as large as manual waits, and P3 does not touch senior capacity. The synthesis also runs past the 260-word limit.

The code parses and avoids inventing absent stages, but it does not discover an unflagged DFG, does not report case durations, and builds point events so the start timestamp never encodes service. `calc_waits` subtracts the previous stage’s start, so the printed “wait” includes prior service. That fails the timestamp decomposition the task is graded on.
