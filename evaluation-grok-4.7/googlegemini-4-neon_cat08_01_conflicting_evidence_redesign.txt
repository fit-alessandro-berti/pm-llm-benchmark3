The answer meets the required structure and is substantively strong: segment-specific trade-offs, three genuinely different redesigns, non-exact dominance language, failure/bottleneck shifts, and lagged cohort measurement are all handled well. It is not nearly flawless.

The central process-mining error is treating all 890 `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` transitions as rework. That edge is largely the normal evidence-completion return, including first manual touches after `Auto eligibility screen -> Request missing evidence` (650). Only the given 26% repeat rate, plus the 310 reopen returns, supports a rework reading. The headline “46% of 2,600 manual entries are rework” is therefore several times too high, and listed manual inflows (2,600) do not match listed outflows (1,400). The same 890 figure is then reused as “loopbacks” in Redesign B, the comparison table, and the Stage 2 capacity story.

Other concrete problems: the global 26% repeat-evidence rate is assigned to the unclear-defect segment; low precision is conflated with alignment deviations; the overall 3.3% repair-path complaint rate is applied to standard high-value repair-first, where the segment figure is 6% reviewed/repair versus 18% fast; summed fast-path medians (~6h) are not reconciled with segment fast-path medians of 8–28h; the 22h manual-to-quote sojourn is equated too cleanly with part-price waiting; “zero service credits” and “<8h” on the premium accessory lane overstate the evidence.
