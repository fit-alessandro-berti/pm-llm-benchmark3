7.5

The role analysis is factually careful and matches the evidence: counts, similarities, complexity medians, and the substitution episodes are used correctly, and the structure does separate volume from authority, complexity, contract type, and rare control points. Shadow roles are treated as useful but risky, temporary coverage is not treated as permanent competence, and the ambiguities come with evidence that would resolve them. The required sections and cautions are all present.

The score is held down by problems in a required section and by thin use of the non-frequency evidence. The PM4Py sample would not run as written. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key` argument. `discover_organizational_roles` returns a list of resource groups, so `roles.items()` is invalid. `similarity_threshold` is not a direct keyword of `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`; it belongs in `parameters`. The substitution filter looks for `["Yes", True]` on `temporary_substitution` after the code created a different boolean column, `is_substitution`. Complexity is aggregated per event with a 0–3 code median, which is not the case-level median complexity score in the scenario and will inflate resources who touch long cases.

Section 2 mostly repeats the first table instead of adding a real discussion of frequency, complexity, substitution, and authority for each role. The six sequence fragments, which are the main evidence that handover structure is not the same as an activity profile, are barely used. Fatima’s 16 supervisor-approval events are not treated as a parallel ambiguity to Giorgio’s 20. Giorgio’s pricing certification, and the Chen/Dina tenure and shift split (5.1 years, late shift versus 1.2 years, day shift), are left out of the deciding evidence even though they are in the resource table.
