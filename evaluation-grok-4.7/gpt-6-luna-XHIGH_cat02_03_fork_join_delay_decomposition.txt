7.6

The case arithmetic, fork-join identities, and critical-path ranking are accurate, but several interpretations of synchronization and slack are wrong or internally inconsistent. That keeps the answer well short of a near-flawless score.

The additive decomposition is correctly defined. \(U_c+T_c+D_c+G_c\) recovers flow time, \(T_c=\max_i(Q_{ci}+S_{ci})\), and \(W_{ci}=T_c-(Q_{ci}+S_{ci})\). The warning that parallel branch paths each span \(T_c\) and must not be summed is the right double-counting argument. The F07 illustration is weaker than that argument: sync waits of \(25+0+52=77\) versus a 75-minute stage are not two measures of the same elapsed quantity. On F01 the sync sum is 27 against a stage of 50, so “sum of waits exceeds the stage” does not demonstrate the overlap problem. The overlap claim itself is still right.

Every required case figure checks out, including F01’s 15-minute upstream interval, the four worked cases, critical labels (A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, B on the other eight), sync waits, downstream gaps, and flow times. Means match the table: upstream 19.58, fork-join 61, downstream 14.67, final service 20, flow 115.25. Service, queue, and queue-plus-service summaries match, including the interpolated P90 values 74 for B and 53 for C. Conditional critical-stage means of 65, 56, and 50 are right. Ranking B by critical frequency and queue-plus-service tail, while refusing to treat A’s flat 50-minute service as dominance, is the correct influence argument. A and C are honestly left frequency-tied and then distinguished.

The three counterfactual tables are numerically right: A’s 20% service cut saves 15 case-minutes (5 on F01, 10 on F11); halving B queue saves 92.5; halving downstream wait saves 88; maxima move 130→130, 130→115, and 130→122.5. Zero-benefit counts 10, 4, and 0 are right. The code implements the same definitions, breaks ties deterministically, rebuilds the join under each scenario, and separates custom max-join logic from PM4Py interval service time.

The explanation of the B-queue result is not. Total B queue cut is 115 minutes and flow falls by 92.5, a gap of 22.5. The four noncritical cases account for only 15 of that gap (2.5+5+2.5+5). The other 7.5 is slack absorption on originally B-critical cases: F05’s offset falls from 60 to 47.5 but A at 50 limits the saving to 10; F10 falls from 55 to 45 but A at 50 limits the saving to 5. Attributing the whole 115-versus-92.5 difference to F01, F04, F08, and F11 overstates those cases and misses the partial critical-path handoff the task asks for.

The downstream-masking paragraph contradicts itself. Absorption occurs when an earlier join does not move `final_start`. That is a hold, batch, or a still-busy resource. It is not what follows from “final work can start as soon as a resource is available,” and the appointment example is that other regime. The later statement that the fixed-wait arithmetic does not model join feedback is sound; the preceding condition is not.

The B-pilot recommendation reverses synchronization. If B is critical and its completion moves earlier while A and C completion stay fixed, \(W\) for A and C shrinks because \(J\) shrinks. The accelerated branch can pick up sync wait only if it stops being strictly last, as on F05 where new B sync is 2.5. “Longer synchronization waits for other branches” is the wrong direction in the case the paragraph describes, the case where the join advances. The second redesign never says what happens to synchronization. A downstream-only change leaves branch sync unchanged; that point is simply absent. The 56.6% final-station figure is arithmetically right (240 over \(2\times212\)) and is labeled as a window assumption.

The resource column is unused. Several downstream gaps occur while the assigned final resource has no overlapping work (F01 waits 8 minutes from 09:00 with no prior F1/F2 occupancy; F02 waits until 09:40 while F2 is idle). That evidence bears directly on whether \(f_c-J_c\) is congestion. Section 7 is otherwise careful about causality, unlogged branch capacity, preemption, and release policy versus queueing.

Score reflects exact primary calculations and a correct max-based ranking, reduced for a reversed sync effect, an incomplete slack account of a headline counterfactual, a contradictory masking rule, and a thin use of the final-resource evidence.
