6.5

The final model is structurally complete and semantically strong, but it is not reliable as a deliverable. The POWL design, witness verdicts, and under-approximation policy are largely right; the construction code and several precise claims are not.

The dependency reading is sound. Low versus high risk is an XOR, high risk is unordered QR and RR, supplier consultation is an optional silent skip, and the trial is a do/redo loop whose exit stands in for a passing inspection. Forcing approvals and supplier consultation to finish before the trial is a genuine conservative under-approximation: it excludes legal overlap and does not admit an unsafe trial before planning and design. W5 correctly exposes that gap, and W6 is a real cross-branch violation rather than a start/end error. The partial-order edges encode the intended joins: PV and DCA before the trial, approvals and optional consultation only before release.

The required POWL code does not construct that model. `OperatorPOWL` takes `children`, not `operands`, so all three operator nodes raise `TypeError`. The validation snippets are also not PM4Py: `play_out(model, 1000, ...)`, `maxLoopIterations`, `pm4py.conformance.check_trace`, and `tree._get_leaves` are invented. The plan’s checks are the right kinds of checks, but the concrete calls would not run.

Two claims overreach. The tree still allows PV to overlap design, approvals, and supplier work; what it forbids is overlap with the trial. Saying `PV || RiskChoice` is “partly lost” is incorrect. The “30–50% of intended interleavings” figure is unsupported. The compact tree expression also depends on operator glyphs that are not readable, although the ASCII parse string is.
