The final recommendation is normatively sound: A and B stay nonconformant and last, G stays disabled until both override conditions exist, second-officer review is not replaced by rescreening, self-certification, or a SAR, and P-901 spillover plus the 35- versus 65-minute S4 sensitivity are explicit. The six-section format and conformance labels are correct.

The top plan is not internally consistent. Action C already consumes 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. Waiting until a ~40-minute cutoff and only then starting that same review finishes around 80–130 minutes, so the fallback usually misses the deadline and still interrupts P-901. The table calls that path “review by ~80–100 min” and “near the 90-minute boundary,” which is the fast edge, not the central case. The S4 branch is also too fast: “review by ~50 min, execute by ~60–75” only works if review takes about 15 minutes, while the only stated review time is 35–75 minutes. Section 5 itself says a 65-minute S4 arrival plus review plus execution exceeds 90 minutes, which contradicts that compressed S4 timing.

The benefit figures do not match each other. Rank 2’s €11k–€26k is €20k–€35k times 0.55–0.75. Rank 1 claims the same weighting but prints €18k–€32k. Deadline loss and false-rejection/hold harm are also stacked as if they were separate. Alternate screening is mapped to the customer-evidence cycle, and the close says not to reject without second review while still offering conditional F and an “S2 unavailable” fallback even though S2 is available at the cost of P-901.
