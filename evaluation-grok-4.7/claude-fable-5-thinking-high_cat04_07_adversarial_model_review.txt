7.4

The review is a serious governance memo: use cases are separated, metrics are not averaged, probe verdicts are mostly derived from the actual tree languages, the third model correctly keeps Credit Check and Technical Review mandatory, and the dissent is a real counterargument. It is not nearly flawless. Several claims a board would rely on are false or over-precise.

The most decision-relevant error is in the missing-event pathology. Deleting Supplier Review from Model C’s third branch yields Register, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order, which is branch 1 and still fits at cost zero. The same holds for the other Supplier Review branches (4→2, 6→5). Missing Supplier Review therefore does not punish C’s fitness, and the claim that the two scores are biased in opposite directions by that defect is wrong. Missing Credit Check would have supported the point; Supplier Review does not.

Other concrete inaccuracies:

- B6 on Model S is not one log-move. Correct Dossier is absent from the alphabet and the second Technical Review cannot fire, so a cost-zero failure involves two log-moves. B8 is the one-log-move case. Grouping them overstates how “near-conformant” B6 looks.
- The third tree does not duplicate Credit Check and Technical Review “once.” Each label is a leaf three times (initial parallel, recheck singleton, and recheck parallel). That understates a duplicate-label effect the review itself treats as analytically important.
- The correction-free census is wrong. 2×2×2×2 = 16 ignores Expedite/Supplier order, which the review elsewhere treats as distinct, and C contributes 7 correction-free branches plus one correction branch, not “8 of those 16 cells.”
- Training fitness is not shown to be “generated by” the tau-skips. Those skips make illegal traces perfectly fit; they do not, by themselves, explain a 0.992 on an unlabeled log. Section 2’s “partly” is the defensible wording; Section 6 overclaims.
- “Pathologically flat” under deletion is too broad. Model S absorbs loss of the four parallel activities only. Deleting Register, the sole approval, or Release still costs.
- Supplier Review is forced after both checks and before approval, but P3 states no such order (only P4 orders Expedite Review, including relative to Supplier Review). That extra constraint is not listed with the other design choices.
- Of the added traces, only X1 separates the third language from both originals. X3 matches S; X2 and X4 match all three on accept/reject. The required three distinguishing traces are not actually there.
- `not_coexistence` / `pm4py.conformance_declare` are asserted as API rather than hedged, the P3 helper mixes audit notes with violation flags, and the unseen-combination rule never says how policy-ambiguous cells are labeled, so the 5% rejection rule is not operational.

What keeps the score from falling further is the main technical spine. Model S’s language (skip, reorder, no Correct Dossier, at most one of each parallel activity) and Model C’s eight sequences are read correctly on B1–B8. The loop idiom `*(tau, body)` is the right zero-or-more pattern, the recheck XOR really does encode only-Credit / only-Technical / both, and B3 and B4 are rejected structurally rather than by optionalizing every activity. Validation is specified without invented scores, and the dissenting view stays inside the length bound and attacks the recommendation’s actual weak points (B5’s “plausible” status, silent Supplier Review gaps, unverifiable rechecks, untested two-layer comprehension).
