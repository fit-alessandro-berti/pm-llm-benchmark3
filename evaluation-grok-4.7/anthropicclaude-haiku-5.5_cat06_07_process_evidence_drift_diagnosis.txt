7.5

The diagnosis is right in its main shape: April is a pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change, August–September stack supplier performance, a V3-conditional outcome shift, and (from September) Partner Z, and October is not a recovery. The month split, falsification tests, alert gates, and risk section are concrete and mostly consistent with the tables. It is not nearly flawless. Several claims are stronger than the evidence, and the remediation and code would mis-measure or mis-fix the problem.

The label mechanism is overstated. The brief says metrics use labels available at cutoff. The write-up repeatedly treats immature cases as coded non-breach. That is one possible production bug, not what the table defines. Under either selection or zero-fill, October’s 0.16 is not comparable, so the conclusion survives, but the mechanism is asserted as fact. The more useful trap is missed: mean predicted risk in October is 0.23, above the censored 0.16 and below a September-rate mix projection near 0.34. A naive calibration gap flips sign. The code then contradicts the prose: maturity is `label_available_ts <= CUTOFF`, and immature cases are dropped, not filled with 0.

May is pinned on the cached “events before diagnosis” feature that was not backfilled for April. These rows are case-start cohorts. That cache defect is an April-case problem. May still has its own residue (patch on 6 May, unknown activity 2%, missing queue 3%, ECE 0.05), which is the better explanation. “Unsafe until backfilled” therefore cites the wrong defect.

September’s V3 share is treated as continued supplier-driven process drift (22% to 41%). August’s rise to 35% with no Partner Z is the supplier mix shift. September non-Partner-Z V3 is 36%, and Partner Z is 24% of volume at 58% V3, which accounts for essentially all of the further step to 42%. Edge time keeps worsening through October; the share path does not have the same cause. The Jan–Jun edge creep (28 h to 42 h) while matured V3 breach stays near 0.30–0.35 is also blurred by calling 42 h an early supplier signal. The breach break coincides with 65 h and then 130 h, which is threshold evidence, not a smooth continuation.

Stage 1 fits one V3 calibration map on Jan–Mar and June (V3 breach about 0.30–0.35) plus transitional July and censored August (0.64). Pre-shift V3 volume outweighs August, so the map under-corrects the regime it is meant to patch. V1/V2 pooling is fine. V3 needs a recent, maturity-weighted window and a short expiry. Stage 2 then adds “Order Part → Part Received elapsed time” beside real model features. The score is issued immediately after diagnosis, before that edge exists. Realized duration is leakage. A supplier-level lead time known at prediction time is the non-leaking feature; the write-up does not separate them.

Smaller slips: March matured V3 is 0.30, not 0.31–0.32; September mix-implied breach is about 0.34, not 0.35; April variant rates are a bit high (V2 0.13) on a mapped subset while 18% are unmapped, so “outcomes intact” is slightly strong; Partner Z distance and device age are omitted variables and a population shift, and only loosely “covariate drift” of the deployed feature set.

The code sketch does the important structural things: normalize the renamed diagnosis before formatting, dedupe, separate event-calendar from case-start cohorts, and avoid a fictional drift detector. It still misses the requested `discover_dfg` call, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is only a comment with unverified arguments. `discover_performance_dfg` values are statistic dicts in current PM4Py, so dividing the edge value by 3600 breaks. An events-mode monthly filter also invents directly-follows edges. The 60-second same-activity rule chains across long runs and does not catch an `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` twin of a real event, which is the April failure mode. The cutoff `2026-10-31` cannot sit with a 52-day October median delay and 55% maturity; half of October starts are not even 52 days old on that date.

The core triangulation would still steer an audit correctly. The score is held down by an unsupported label mechanism, a calibration set that averages away the V3 break, a leaked feature, a mis-attributed September mix shift, and process-mining code that would not run cleanly on the incident it is auditing.
