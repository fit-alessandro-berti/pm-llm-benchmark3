5.8

The capacity arithmetic in §2 is largely right and is the answer’s only fully audit-able quantitative core. Mixture means (6.94 / 26.3 / 32.82 / 38.5 / 15.74 and the U4 diagnostics) are correct, and the worked block loads match the demand table. Effective capacities at 10:00–12:00, 16:00–18:00, and 18:00–20:00 match on-shift minutes divided by speed, with breaks respected and overtime excluded. The binding ratios I checked (base 14:00–16:00 E/S, 16:00–18:00 S, U1 morning E/S, U2 renormalized E, U3 without Chen, U4 tail means, U5 U1×U3) are reproducible. Shift structure is read correctly: no mechanical coverage after 18:00, Emil as the only late software specialist, Chen as the morning E/S anchor, Farah at 1.40× too slow to be a steady owner.

That core is then undermined by the comparison the recommendation depends on.

§4’s policy metrics are not outputs of the §8 model, despite the claim that they come from a block-capacity Monte Carlo. Fairness gaps (about 48 / 40 / 28 pp) and handover minutes are policy constants. Utilization is a capped linear function of max ρ plus `resource_bias`, not resource-level planned load. `p1_wait_factor` (0.90 / 0.72 / 0.68) forces hybrid to win P1 SLA. Reporting those as scenario results makes hybrid’s dominance circular. The same spec utilization, 88–90%, is repeated for base, U1, U2, and U3 while offered load changes sharply.

The code also contradicts §2. `capacity_minutes` silently extends every shift by 30 minutes, so 16:00–18:00 software is no longer Emil-only 90 minutes (Chen’s overtime adds software capacity and the 1.03 ratio falls), and 18:00–20:00 mechanical capacity is not zero. The prose says overtime is emergency slack, not planned capacity. Case sojourn uses unmultiplied draws, so Farah’s 1.40× and Chen’s 1.25× never lengthen the path the customer experiences. The 10% restart penalty is not applied; preemption is a flat 14–16 minute credit with no victim cost. Handover, setup, team transfer, and the 8-minute coordination delay are collapsed into constants, which §8.3 of the task forbids unless the scaffold identifies every omission. It does not.

The synthetic log does not implement the three policies. Resource choice is a fixed owner list. Parallel ES both prefer Chen, then serialize on `resource_next_free`, while still adding the parallel coordination delay. Breaks are absent. `pause_resume_count` is always 0. One hundred sixty replications are pooled into point estimates, so the required seed-level uncertainty is never shown. Base rows with mean elapsed time near 140–145 and p90 only about 10–15 minutes higher are not a credible queueing-plus-mixture distribution; a ~2% next-morning mechanical tail would lift the mean and still miss p90, and that artifact is never discussed. P2 SLA stuck at 95–97% in every cell, including U5, is likewise uninterpreted: the 8-hour target barely discriminates routing.

Interpretive overreach follows. A 1.03 ratio in 16:00–18:00 is only a few minutes of excess software work before a much lighter 18:00–20:00 block; it is a real hole, not a collapse. U5’s dominant fact in the author’s own ratios is E/S overload (about 1.23–1.72), not a 09:30–10:00 intake pileup of a few cases. Reserving “one E/S slot” before noon, when Emil is absent, can mean locking up most genuine software capacity; that opportunity cost is not counted. The pilot window, 08:00–16:00 and only P1/ME/ES, excludes the evening coverage failure identified as structural, and the 95% pilot cap disagrees with the 90% fairness constraint.

§3, §5, §6, and §7 are operationally serious: owner lanes, slack thresholds, stress-mode triggers, P3 aging, estimate gaming, and the case-level versus stage-level PM4Py distinction are the right objects. `format_dataframe`, case service/waiting insertion, `get_service_time`, and handover-network discovery are used with a correct warning that `@@service_time` / `@@waiting_time` are not stage queues. Assumptions A1–A8 are labeled, and the recommendation correctly refuses to treat U5 as a routing problem. The document is complete against the nine headings. It is not a trustworthy quantitative comparison of the policies it recommends.
