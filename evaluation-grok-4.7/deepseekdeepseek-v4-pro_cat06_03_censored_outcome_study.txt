5.5

The design correctly refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, and open cases as non-defaults, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and treats activation as selection rather than ordinary missingness. The leakage table drops the worst post-decision fields, and the limitations section correctly narrows use to association within the activated subpopulation. Those choices are outweighed by errors in the estimand, censoring, validation, and skeleton.

The primary estimand stays ambiguous. “Time to 90-day default” is not identified from `default_90d` alone, and no rule defines the event time from `Default Recorded` versus the delayed feed. Conditioning on observed activation is then restated as the probability “if activated,” which is a different, counterfactual claim, including in the final decision-use statement. The population is also not the one implied by “eventually activated”: still-open cases are dropped, so slow-to-decision applications are truncated, while the stated inclusion rule requires a fully mature label and the primary cohort of 25,200 simultaneously keeps all 3,600 immature activations.

Delayed labels are named and then treated as ordinary right censoring at `min(time since activation, 90)`. That is invalid when the 90-day window has already elapsed and only the feed is late: absence of a label is not survival to day 90. The proposed censoring check (early versus late activations) does not test that assumption. Permanently missing partner feeds never appear in the cohort flow. The 0.5×–3× bounds are arbitrary and answer a different estimand. The 15 November 2025 maturity cutoff does not match a 90-day horizon plus up to 35 days of feed delay.

Prefixing at the earlier of three events or seven days is reasonable, but the time from that prefix to activation is then used as a feature even though activation is still in the future. Known non-defaults are also described as unlabeled censoring rather than observed non-events at the horizon.

Validation does not hold out the claimed pre-policy test. Cases started 1 July–30 September 2024 sit inside training (through 31 March 2025), and the split function assigns them to train before the test branch can run. Post-policy test performance through 15 November 2025 mixes immature labels with the 15 October migration, so it is not an estimate of policy-shift performance. Backtests do not freeze labels at the information available on each backtest date.

The skeleton is far beyond the requested length and would not build the cohort: activation time is assigned from a case-indexed series inside `np.where`, so it does not align to the case table. `default_90d` is aggregated into the analysis table and is not in the prohibited-column check, post-prefix columns are warned about rather than removed, and temporal features are fit only on training cases. Survival duration and event indicators are never constructed.
