6.3

The policy write-up is structured, covers every required heading, and is often careful. It is not reliable on the queue itself. Several recommended assignments break the stated rules or the author's own algorithm, and the training-quota claim is wrong.

The clearest error is C010. R8_Hugo's Approval score is 0, which the matrix defines as not allowed. Rule 7 allows only skill 1 to prepare and skill 2–3 to finalize. The Stage 1 filter also drops skill 0. Assigning Hugo to prepare, then counting that pair as documented training, is illegal under the policy being proposed. The legal skill-1 preparers are Amal, Gita, and Jonas, and none of them has Approval as a training priority. R5_Eva (skill 2) or the owner R2_Bruno (skill 2) could finalize. Stacking finalization on R6_Farid, who already keeps urgent C002 and urgent C011, also cuts against the resilience term in the scorer.

The 33% training claim depends on that illegal pair plus C015. C015 is a real insight: Eva and Jonas are Security skill 0, so they cannot perform the review even under R7/R8. Shadowing is not an assignment of the activity, and the pseudocode has no shadow path. The only clean documented training assignment is C009 (Amal, Diagnosis skill 1, normal priority, supervised by Celine). That is 1 of 9 normal/low cases, about 11%, under the 20% target. The "if SLA risk remains acceptable" clause would have allowed an honest shortfall. The answer manufactures compliance instead.

C003 contradicts both constraint 4 and the pseudocode. The case is normal, not urgent, handover cost is medium, and R3_Celine is a Diagnosis skill-3, low-overtime alternative. Stage 1 would remove David (high overtime, non-urgent) before scoring. Keeping him to avoid a third reassignment is a real trade-off, but it is not the exception the hard rule allows, and 14 open cases with 3.0 hours left is never checked against the hours filter.

C001 is under-argued. Owner R3_Celine is Security skill 1 on an urgent, complexity-0.88 case with a 3h SLA and a high handover band. Constraint 5 prefers continuity unless supervision is infeasible, and rule 6 explicitly allows skill 1–2 under R7 or R8. The cited median handover wait on high-complexity cases is 2.7h, which consumes almost the whole SLA. The note that a specialist is needed is relevant, and weak-skill retention costs about 3.3h of service time, so both options are bad. The answer picks a handover and tells the reader to "start immediately" without facing that arithmetic.

The comparison section has factual slips. First-available selects the lowest open-case count, so David (14) is last, not someone who "looks available." Specialist-only would load high-overtime people who are skill 3: Bruno on Eligibility, David on Diagnosis and Rework, Iris on Quality and Rework. They are not spared by luck. The seven comparison criteria are discussed only generically. Neither baseline is actually run on C001–C016.

Smaller defects: weights stay symbolic, so the audit "deviation from score" indicator is not reproducible; urgency sort order is undefined; Celine's day (C007, C014, C009 supervision, C016 consult, 6.0 hours) is only lightly stress-tested; `filter_case_performance(log, 0, 7 days)` keeps shorter cases but is labeled long-running; `discover_performance_dfg` is unpacked into three values although that call returns the performance DFG itself.

What holds up is substantial. Hard rules are separated from preferences. The pseudocode has eligibility, SLA fit, skill, continuity, fairness, training, resilience, tie-breaks, and escalation. C002, C004, C005, C006, C007, C008, C009, C011, C012, C014, and C016 are defensible, batching respects the 24h/normal-low rule, the batch cap of 4 is met, and no high-priority case is given to an unsupervised trainee. The worker text is specific and non-punitive. The audit table defines all ten requested indicators. The PM4Py sketch names the requested resource, role, batch, service-time, DFG, variant, and filter functions and ties them to a review loop. Those strengths are why this is not a failing answer. The illegal assignment, the false training percentage, and the algorithm/table contradictions are why it is not a strong one.
