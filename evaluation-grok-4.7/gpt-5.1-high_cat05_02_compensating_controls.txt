The case reasoning is substantial and mostly non-mechanical, but the sufficiency test contradicts itself, several timing judgments are over-precise or wrong, and the required evidence code misses or mislabels flags it claims to emit. That is well short of nearly flawless.

The control-objective split is the strongest part. Seven threat scenarios are each mapped to what AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 can prevent, detect, correct, or leave open, including collusion, bank-change and new-vendor risk, emergency-loophole abuse, and structural pairing. ED-1 is correctly treated as a conditional permission, not as the disappearance of four-eyes. The expected-loss discussion properly adds tail risk, governance, attribution, and accountability.

The equivalence test does not hold together. “Fully equivalent” treats independent review before settlement as enough. “Partially equivalent” then uses that same post-release, pre-settlement pattern as a typical partial package. Those categories cannot be applied consistently. Section 1 calls that manager action hybrid preventive/corrective; section 4 calls it hybrid detective–corrective and only partly preventive. Section 4 also gives RA-7 real corrective force inside the recall window, then treats post-settlement MG-4 as essentially non-corrective, with no separate row for manager review after settlement but still inside 24 hours.

Case classifications are mostly defensible and avoid a mechanical same-user/different-user rule. AP-202 as failed, AP-205 as stopped before completion, AP-206 as indeterminate, AP-208 as failed despite a later legitimate outcome, and AP-204 as failed despite formal four-eyes are the right shape of judgment. AP-203 and AP-207 can reasonably be met under ED-1. The write-up still overclaims. It says all ED-1 conditions are satisfied for AP-203, while manager level L3 is never evidenced and the source only says the conditions appear mostly satisfied. The 8-month vendor in AP-208 is called relatively new or newish even though about eight months clears the 180-day tenure gate; the real ED-1 breaks there are amount, the three-day bank change, AV-2 being down, and late approval and audit. For AP-201, audit at 09:00 on May 5 is about 25 hours after release but about 23 hours after the stated 10:00 settlement. The table treats that audit as already outside the recall window and mainly non-corrective. That clock is not established. AP-209’s partial rating also slides from this payment, where no defect was found and audit was timely, to a system-level judgment about the u12/u13 pair.

Counterfactuals cover more than five cases and stay qualitative. They correctly separate a nominal second person from a genuinely independent one, and they treat AP-205’s block as already breaking the chain. The AP-206 residual-risk claim of an uncontrolled window before manager approval is muddled: approval at 13:04 preceded release at 13:08, so the open question is token timing, not an unsupervised release. Monitoring recommendations cover every requested signal and keep alerts distinct from proof of misconduct.

The code reconstructs the log, parses UTC timestamps, calls the requested PM4Py helpers, separates AP-205, joins case facts, and labels the printout as evidence rather than a verdict. The evidence layer is not reliable. `audit_after_recall_window_flag` searches for the substring `>>24h`, so AP-201 (`post_settlement_>24h`) and AP-208 (`four_days_later`) are not flagged even though the prose treats both audits as late. Emergency eligibility is not computed: there is no test of token-before-prepare, amount under EUR 100,000, vendor tenure, override, manager independence, or audit within one business day. AP-206 therefore looks like the other emergency rows. The unused `emergency_high_risk` flag is only token AND recent bank change, and it is not even printed. The blocked-case rule is any block or attempt event, not an attempt with no completed release. The four-eyes result is assumed to be the violating set, with no `positive` parameter and no check against the script’s own same-user column, despite known version differences in whether that filter keeps violators or compliant cases.
