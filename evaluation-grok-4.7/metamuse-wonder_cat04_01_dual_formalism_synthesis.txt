The control-flow reasoning is strong, but the required process-tree implementation is not executable, and one forbidden witness misstates the earliest violation.

The POWL model is the right canonical choice and is largely faithful. Cross-branch edges are only `Contain → Plan` and `Analyze → Design/approval/supplier`; trial waits for both plan and design; approval and supplier join only at release; the high-risk reviews are unordered; supplier consultation is an at-most-once `XOR` with a silent skip; and `LOOP(Execute→Inspect, Adjust)` matches do-then-optional-redo semantics. W1–W5 and W6–W7 are classified correctly, and W4 really does separate the tree’s extra join from the intended partial order. The approximation policy is also the right one: forcing the initial `Contain || Analyze` join before planning, design, approval, and supplier work is a conservative under-approximation that still preserves approval/trial overlap.

The process-tree code does not construct that model. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` recognizes parallel composition as `+`. The submitted string uses `/\`, so parsing raises an unrecognized-operator error. The compact expression and the mapping paragraph use the same token and never give the PM4Py spelling, so the required `tree` object is not produced.

W8 is not pinned to the earliest failure. The prefix through `Adjust Parameters` is still a valid prefix of a two-cycle trace. The trace first becomes impossible at `Release Material`, where the redo has not been closed by another `Execute Trial, Inspect Trial`. Citing D17–D20 is too broad: D17 is satisfied if that inspection is treated as the failure that justifies adjustment. “Related only transitively” is also loose: approval and the trial stay incomparable in the transitive closure and meet only as co-predecessors of release.
