7.5

The final assessment is complete, case-specific, and strong on time-of-act and evidence, but it is not nearly flawless. Several stated rules contradict the classifications and the draft clause that is supposed to settle them.

What holds up: all eight cases are judged at the moment of commitment; ECD-2 is not treated as automatically superior to GPIP-4; late ERP times are not treated as conclusive (P-505’s 17:59:31 acknowledgment versus 18:06, P-507’s 12:40 PDF versus 14:30); post-commitment approval is not treated as a cure; P-501 is correctly kept outside the 09:00 window, with ON-44 pushing the real time earlier rather than later; P-502’s new-supplier clearance and P-506’s expired, unrecorded “extension” are handled tightly; P-504 remains noncompliant even if food spoilage were an emergency, because the register rule is not waived. Alternatives name the premise that would flip the result, and the four evidential categories are actually used.

What costs the score:

- The framework says A7 is the only relief from GPIP-4, then lets an uncontrolled CFO email displace A2’s Finance Controller limb. That same contradiction is copied into the recommended clause: §2 makes both ≥50k approvals reserved, and §5 substitutes Incident Command for the Finance approval.
- The burden rule says a missing mandatory pre-commitment approval is presumed unmet. The budget-owner half of A2 is then carved out as “not a breach,” and P-507 is labeled COMPLIANT (P-505 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION) even though that limb is expressly not covered by D1 and no such approval is evidenced. Under the answer’s own catalogue those primaries should be NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE.
- The pseudo-logic `unless` on the Finance substitution excuses the whole A2 conjunction, including the budget owner, while the comment says it does not. It also runs to 16 lines against the required 5–12.
- In the hierarchy table, “what it cannot override” is filled, for the apex rules, with sources that cannot override them. The columns are used backwards.
- Plant approval is waived via C3/B7 after the answer says waivers are construed narrowly and C4 covers only timing and onboarding.
- P-503’s C3 finding (“PROC-3 is not Incident Command”) overrides the case text calling PROC-3 one of the two Incident Command authorizers. A1/A2/A3 still support NONCOMPLIANT, so the label survives, but the confidence and the B1 “independent of scope” claim are overstated.
- SOP B’s stated scope (maintenance and production-support) is never tested against the cafeteria freezer, which is the main local-rule basis for P-504.

The factual traps are mostly caught and the reasoning is explicit. The primary labels and the closing policy text do not consistently follow the hierarchy the answer itself adopts.
