The three candidates are genuine competing hypotheses, the observed language is handled correctly, and the uncertainty analysis is careful. The score is held down by a formal-language typo, one overstated boundary, and a few places where the prose is less exact than the code.

The evidence ledger covers all seven variants, D1–D7, and S1–S7, and it correctly treats bidirectional directly-follows edges as compatible with separate sequences, overlap, ties, or ingestion order. Shared assumptions are sound: completion-only logging, at-most-once labels, frequencies kept out of the language, and the silent Supplier branch disclosed as “no recorded event,” which mixes stocked skips with possible integration loss. Both a process tree and POWL are used, and the conservative tree is exactly the seven observed traces. Duplicate labels and poor scaling are stated plainly.

The balanced partial order is the right shape. Credit and Engineering stay unordered; Expedite is optional and waits for both; Supplier is either absent or placed after Credit, or after Engineering, by an XOR of partial orders. That union accepts every observed variant, including `Register, Credit, Supplier, Engineering, …`, and it rejects Supplier before both checks. Duplicate derivations when both checks precede Supplier are real and are correctly described as harmless to the language. The permissive model is also consistent: Supplier is unordered with the checks, while Expedite still waits for both and approval remains a gate. Calling the balanced choice a completion-level strengthening of a start/readiness rule is accurate.

The compact conservative expression is not clean. `REC SAPZ` inserts a space, so the displayed set is not literally the seven traces; only the code is exact. In the permissive boundary, “either Supplier–Expedite order” is too broad. Expedite can precede Supplier only after both checks have already completed. If Supplier is first, or sits between the checks, Expedite cannot precede it. The formal expression and the later examples do not make that mistake. The S1 fallback, `Register ≺ Credit ≺ Engineering ≺ … ≺ Approve ≺ Release ≺ Archive`, leaves Supplier and Expedite inside the ellipsis, so it does not say whether the observed `Credit, Supplier, Engineering` order would remain legal. One balanced bullet filed under underfitting is a case the text itself calls too permissive. Several inline precedence markers are blank (`A  P  Z`, `C  X`, `XOR(, activity)`), which makes those sentences harder to read than the LaTeX.

The audit’s admitted and excluded traces match the models, and the earliest-violation claims hold. The matrix stays qualitative and separates fitness from precision. The validation program is self-contained and does the required work: log rebuild, conversion, soundness, the four metrics, sampled playout with an explicit non-exhaustiveness warning, and an exact positive/negative replay. The soundness guard `is False` is brittle if the call returns a tuple or diagnostics object. The recommendation of balanced for use and permissive for sensitivity analysis follows from the evidence, and the collection plan (start/complete timestamps, component source, reconciliation of missing Supplier events, and traces that separate sequence, choice, overlap, and optionality) would actually resolve the open questions.
