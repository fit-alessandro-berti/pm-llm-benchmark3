3.5

The fork-join identities in §1 are the right ones, and the four worked cases are numerically right, including F02 (join at 09:25, critical branch B, fork-join 60, downstream 15). Almost everything load-bearing after that is inconsistent with those identities, with the worked F02, or with the log.

The case table mis-states the critical path on 3 of 12 cases and breaks its own row checks. F02 and F03 are marked critical A at the A completion, but B finishes later (09:25 and 09:45). Correct joins are 09:25 and 09:45, fork-join 60 and 65, downstream 15 and 15. F08’s sync columns already imply C (C sync 0, join 10:50), yet the row is labeled critical B and total flow 120; 11:25−09:35 is 110, and 20+55+15+20 is 110. F12’s B queue is 30 (11:25−10:55), not 50; 50+45 cannot reconcile with B ending at 12:10. F05/F06 sync waits do not match join minus branch end (F05 B/C should be 0/35, not 35/55; F06 B should be 0, not 60).

Correct last-completer counts are A 2 (F01, F11), B 8, C 2 (F04, F08). The stated 4 / 7 / 1 set includes F02 and F03 as A and omits F08 as C, which directly contradicts the F02 write-up. Slack is often 5–10 minutes (F01, F04, F08, F10, and several at 10). The claim of few near-ties, and the “recheck” that B is critical on F08 when C ends at 10:50 and A at 10:45, are false. “Top 2” frequencies are also false: C is last or second on F04 and F08; B is third on both.

Aggregate figures do not match the data or the answer’s own table. B service mean is 37.5, not 38.1; B queue mean is 19.2, not 20.8; mean fork-join is 61, not 59; mean flow is 115.25, not 117.5; mean downstream is 14.7, not 17.5. Sync-wait means are given as 4.25 / 7.86 and, elsewhere, 20.17 for B and 32.42 for C; neither pair is the mean of the printed case rows. High sync wait is treated as evidence that B is influential, while the same quantity is treated as evidence that C is not. Sync wait is slack: zero means the branch sets `join_ready`.

§4 does not recompute `join_ready = max` of the perturbed ends, so the intervention ranking is reversed. With final start moving by the join improvement (no extra masking): cutting A service 20% (end −10) saves only on F01 (5 min, until B) and F11 (10 min, tie with B) — about 1.25 min/case, not 10+10 on F01 and F03 with F11 at 0. Halving B queue moves the join on 8 cases and saves about 92.5 minutes in total (~7.7 min/case), not 10 minutes on F03 alone. Several CF2 rows state a new B end earlier than A and still keep the old join (F05 new B end 09:57:30 vs join left at 10:10; F06, F07, F09, F10, F12 similarly). CF3 savings are not half the queues printed in the same rows (F06–F09 list a 7.5 minute cut and a 5 minute saving; F10/F11 list a 10 minute cut and a 5 minute saving). The stated 68 minute total does not equal the column sum. Downstream is therefore not shown to be ~3.4× the B-queue lever.

The §6 counterfactual algebra holds `final_start` fixed, so `fork_join_new + downstream_new = final_start − fork_release` and case savings are identically 0. That run cannot reproduce §4. It also never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `get_service_time` / `discover_performance_dfg` / `convert_log_to_time_intervals` (those names are only imported), and `Series.round` on `critical_branch` will throw before the tables print. The fork-join comment is the one part of the code requirement that is actually met. Masking is the right question and is not computed: F1/F2 are each about two-thirds busy (service 20 min, arrivals every 15 min, two servers ⇒ 20/(2×15) ≈ 67%), with real idle gaps, not 20/15 ≈ 130% over-utilization. The third-server ROI and the “queue because over capacity” claim depend on that error. The 70% B-utilization figure and the 5+10 split of the 20 minute release decision are not in the log.

§7 does cover non-identifiability of capacity, preemption, and release policy versus queue, but it repeats the bad means, and overlap already rules out a finish-to-start dependence of B on A (B starts while A is still open). Definitions and the “longest service is not the critical branch” moral are sound; the case counts, tails, counterfactual savings, code, and capacity recommendation are not reliable enough to act on.
