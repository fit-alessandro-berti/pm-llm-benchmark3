4.5

The write-up follows the required section order and shows some real queueing judgment: service medians are not flow times, the outage day and the next high-completion day are not steady regimes, weekday/weekend pooling is misleading, and the April 20 mix and peak shift is visible in the table. Those merits do not survive the arithmetic, the partition, or the code.

The primary segmentation is not a partition. April 17–19 never appear. April 17 still has completions above arrivals (112 vs 99), staff at 6.5, WIP 35, and complex share 25, so it is recovery, not “post-mix.” April 18–19 match the earlier weekends (complex share about 23–24, staff 2.5), but “weekend pre-change” is only April 11–12, while “weekend post-mix” is dated April 25–May 3 and therefore swallows weekdays. Recovery is also misread: WIP rises from 47 on the outage day to 52 on April 16, so that level is not “backlog clearance”; only the flow gap (129 completions vs 112 arrivals) says partial drain.

Service timing is wrong. Median handling steps from the mid-20s to about 29–33 on April 15 and stays there through April 19, then steps again to 34–36 on April 20. Tying the whole service shift to the complex-share jump collapses two changes into one and drops the intermediate regime.

Section 4’s pooled figures do not match the table. Over all 28 days, arrivals total 2519 (mean about 90.0) and completions total 2409 (mean about 86.0), a net accumulation of about 110 cases. Reported means of 89.5 and 89.1 invent a near balance that the series does not have. Pooled mean WIP is about 29.9, not 28.5. All-weekend arrivals average about 44.9, not 44.3; weekend WIP averages about 17.4, not 16.0; weekend complex share averages about 32%, not 22%. Pre-change weekday arrivals average about 100.1, not 101.3; post-mix weekday arrivals average 114.0, not 113.3. The Simpson example is internally false: 101.3 is not a higher arrival rate than 113.3, and a level shift in both series is not a reversal.

That false balance hides the main operating fact. Almost all of the +110 is the post-mix weekday deficit (798 arrivals vs 704 completions over April 20–24 and 27–28). A global stock check also fails: cumulative net input is about +110 while snapshot WIP ends near where it started (18 on April 6, 19 on May 3). Mean snapshot WIP cannot be used as a conserved backlog. The audit never flags this, even though it is the central threat to Little’s Law and to any recovery narrative.

The hourly dispersion table is not computed from the given vectors. For arrivals, sample Fano factors (variance/mean, \(n-1\)) are about 3.23, 4.14, 10.1, and 1.29 for April 7, 15, 21, and 25, not 1.87, 2.11, 4.23, and 1.02. April 25 is not the sole Fano-below-1 day. April 15 arrivals are a smooth unimodal hump, so lag-1 autocorrelation should be positive; a reported −0.15 blamed on the outage confuses the arrival vector with the completion cliff (zeros from about 11:00–14:00). Fano across hours also mixes the diurnal profile with stochastic overdispersion; that split is missing, as is any lag statistic on the daily sequence.

The Little’s Law matrix contradicts itself. The throughput column says completions, but the arithmetic uses the (already wrong) arrival means: 20.3/101.3, 44.0/113.3, 37/115, 16/44.3. Post-mix is not approximately stationary: completions run near 101 while arrivals run near 114 and WIP trends from 37 to 50. Intervention WIP falls from 43 to 32 because completions exceed arrivals, so “stable / approximately stationary” is not defensible; “approximate only” is already generous. One pooled weekend flow time mixes a pre-mix regime near 4 operating hours with a post-mix regime near 5. The ×12 conversion from “days” to hours is used without stating that this is open-hour time and that overnight carry is outside the snapshot clock. Outage and one-day recovery are correctly marked not defensible.

The risky pooled policy (staff near 5 FTE because the grand mean is about 5.1) is the right kind of example, because that mean averages weekend 2.5 FTE with weekday coverage. The follow-through overclaims: nothing shows that 5.0 FTE was sufficient before the mix change, or that post-mix weekends need 3.5–4.0 FTE merely because the complex share matches weekdays. Volume is still about 45 cases, not about 114. Training is recommended as if the complex-share jump were known to be real mix rather than a possible coding change.

The single code block does not reproduce the prose. Duplicate `pd.cut` labels are invalid in pandas, so the script should raise before printing. Even if it ran, recovery would be April 16–17, all weekends would be one regime, and May 3 falls outside the last bin. CUSUM on raw arrivals, standardized by the full-sample mean, is dominated by the weekend cycle, so it cannot locate the mix or staffing changes. In code, Little’s Law uses completions and would not yield the section 6 hours (post-mix would be about 5.3 open hours, not 4.7). The PM4Py note correctly says these rows are not an event log and names the requested helpers; that is the only cleanly satisfied part of the code requirement.

Assumptions (full-day intervention on April 29 despite an unknown start time, snapshot means as \(L\), complex share as a real mix change) are not labeled as assumptions. Observations, estimates, and recommendations are mixed in the same cells. Coverage of the eight headings is real; the calculations, the missing days, the conservation failure, and the non-reproducible regime code are not small defects.
