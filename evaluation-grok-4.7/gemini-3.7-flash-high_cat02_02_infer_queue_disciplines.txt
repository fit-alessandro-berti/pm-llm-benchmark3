The write-up is complete, decision-oriented, and right in broad outline on QA (FIFO plus exact-time appointment insertion, no lookahead). It is not reliable on the episodes that actually separate the competing rules. Several errors are internal contradictions, not close calls, and they prop up the QB, QC, and QD diagnoses.

**Scan delay is defined correctly and then applied backwards.** Section 5 states that a scan is recorded up to two minutes after physical arrival, so the logged time is late. B05’s logged arrival is 09:46, so by 09:48 B05 had already been visible for about two minutes and may have been physically present even earlier. The claim that B05 might have been scanned at 09:48:01, and that this removes the priority violation, contradicts both the definition and the table. That mistake is then ranked as the most plausible exception and is used to defend preemptive priority. The 09:48 choice of B03 (priority 2) over an already logged B05 (priority 1) remains a real counterexample.

**A decisive QB decision is missing.** At 10:14 B05 ends and B01 (priority 3, about 14 minutes left) is resumed while B06 (priority 2), B04 (priority 3, shorter), and B07 (appointment 10:30, shortest) are waiting. That is the same bench-resumption pattern as 09:31, now against a higher-priority arrival, and it is absent from the decision table. Together with 10:28, where B04 (priority 3) is taken over B06 (priority 2), the trace fits “priority-1 preemption, otherwise resume or FIFO, plus one manual reassignment” better than the stated primary rule. Confidence 75 is too high. The 10:28 SPT cell (“B07 or B04”) is also unargued: on the given estimates SPT selects B07 alone.

**QC’s main identifiability claim is rejected by the answer’s own table.** At 10:10 and again at 10:18, SPT selects C02 then C03 while earliest-deadline and minimum-slack select C03 then C05. C05 already had negative slack at 10:18 (deadline 10:50, estimate 40) and was still deferred. EDF/MST are not observationally equivalent to SPT. The two decisions that do threaten dynamic SPT are omitted: at 10:32 C04 (22) starts although C06 (6) has been waiting since 10:25, and at 11:40 C01 (36, priority 3) starts over C07 (18, priority 2, deadline 12:00). The second case also breaks the secondary “SLA escalation” story, which is fit to C05 and not checked on C07, whose slack at 11:34 was about 8 minutes. Section 4 then runs a counterfactual of SPT versus EDF on the 10:10 episode, which the log has already settled, instead of dynamic SPT versus a frozen 10:10 batch. Citing C02→C03→C04 as clean SPT support, and “C01 last,” leaves out C06 and C07.

**QD’s eligible sets are wrong, so confidence 90 is not earned.** D-F is qualified for electrical work and is idle from 11:36 until the 12:00 meeting. D10 (electrical, priority 1, estimate 12, arrived 11:25) is eligible throughout that window and is not “only D05.” At 11:48 the meeting has not started; “D-F in meeting” is false. The note that D-F is unavailable until 12:20 applies at D10’s 12:16 start, which falls inside 12:00–12:20, not at 11:36 or 11:48. At 11:20 D-X can also do D03, which is omitted. Idle time is also stricter than appointment protection: D08’s estimate of 8 fits inside 11:20–11:30 with about two minutes to spare, and D04 meets 11:30 exactly, yet neither is packed. Skill constraints and D06’s exact 11:30 start are real; they do not make the queue the most identifiable one, nor do they justify treating D04-before-D08 and D07-before-D10 as fully explained.

**Other consistency problems.** For QA, A06 starting at 09:20 is what non-preemptive FIFO without lookahead predicts, so it is not a contradicting episode. Priority and SPT are already separated by 08:32 (A02 over A04) and 09:05 (A05 over A07 and A08); the stated identifiability problem says the opposite. Every recorded duration in all four queues equals `est_min` exactly, so the “noisy estimates” exception is not calibrated to these data. Appointment windows versus exact instants are barely examined. QA is the cleanest queue; the synthesis names QD.

Section 6 and the QA decision rows are the strongest parts. The grade is held down by eligible-set mistakes, one inverted data-quality argument used as the top exception, and omitted decisions that change QB and QC.
