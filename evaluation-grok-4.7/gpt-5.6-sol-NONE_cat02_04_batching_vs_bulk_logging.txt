7.5

The diagnoses are process-mining literate and usually well matched to the evidence. Equal timestamps are not treated as proof of simultaneous work, PM4Py categories are treated as temporal patterns, G6’s paired failure mode is explained correctly, and G7/G8 uncertainty is handled carefully. The required sections, table, and code skeleton are present. The score is held down by concrete errors and a few reasoning and diagnostic gaps.

Waiting-time arithmetic is wrong in three places, against the answer’s own definition \(W=\text{start}-\text{arrival}\). G3 is 18–22 minutes, not 5–22. G5 is 15–16 minutes, not 13–15. G8 rises from 38 to 58 minutes; 65 minutes is Q05’s arrival-to-completion sojourn, so one service interval was folded into wait. Those figures would also disagree with the code’s `wait_sec` column.

G1’s runner-up, start batching, is inconsistent with identical ends. A copied fixture-cycle interval fits the counterevidence better. G4’s rising waits are a finite FIFO backlog (\(\Delta W = 4-1\) minutes), not ongoing arrival pressure, and capacity loss is unevidenced. G7’s `recorded_at` values, 14:16 through 14:20 in sequence order, are unused; that serial insertion is independent evidence against reading the shared 14:00–14:15 window as one physical measurement.

The code keeps suspect rows and calls the requested APIs, but the comparison is weak. `flatten_keys` collects dictionary keys and drops batch-type strings stored as values. The crosswalk is hardcoded. `min_batch_size` 2 versus 4 and `merge_distance` 0 versus 60 do not separate these clusters. Zero-duration ties are counted as adjacency, so G6 yields \(\binom{7}{2}=21\) “adjacent” pairs. `second == 0` is 1 for every cluster, so it is not a granularity indicator. Queue build-up saturates at “all arrived before first start” for most groups.

Section 6 and the adjudication are strong, and confidence is usefully defined as ranking confidence. The numerical contradictions and the non-diagnostic batch comparison are still material.
