3.5

The deliverable is not a usable model of the stated process. The cross-branch release skeleton is recognizable, but the trial cycle, both implementations, and the witness table contradict the narrative and each other.

The repeat-until rule is wrong in both models. A cycle is Execute Trial, then Inspect Trial, then either stop or Adjust Parameters and repeat, i.e. `(ET, IT)(AP, ET, IT)*`. The tree instead builds `LOOP(Execute Trial, SEQ(Adjust Parameters, Execute Trial))`, so its language is `ET ((AP, ET), ET)*`. Inspect Trial never appears. The POWL loop is `ET (AP, ET, IT, τ, ET)*`: the first iteration is never inspected, Inspect Trial occurs only on the redo, the silent “pass” does not exit the loop, and every exit ends on an uninspected Execute Trial. Release can therefore happen without a passing inspection. Duplicating Execute Trial is not required; `LOOP(SEQ(Execute Trial, Inspect Trial), Adjust Parameters)` uses one Execute Trial label. The claim that a trace contains Execute Trial at most twice is also false for an unbounded loop.

The tree code does not implement the described “Encoding B.” It is a sequence of blocks: parallel containment and root-cause analysis, then parallel planning and design, then the same loop object placed twice in one parallel, then approval, supplier, and release. That over-orders Plan Verification on Analyze Root Cause and Design Corrective Action on Contain Material, forbids approval and supplier from overlapping the trial, and does not duplicate a subtree (one node cannot be two children). `leaf("")` is an empty visible label, not a silent skip. Section 3.3 and section 6 then say the opposite: that the tree under-constrains those dependencies and admits W5 and W6.

The POWL script does not construct a model. `OperatorPOWL` accepts only XOR and LOOP; the `PARALLEL` construction raises, and the earlier `operator=None` call raises first. The self-critique names the fix and still leaves the broken code as the model assigned to `powl`. The edges among register, the two streams, planning, design, the approval XOR, supplier, trial, release, and archive are the right partial-order idea. The archive-concurrency paragraph ignores transitivity: Archive Case is after everything that precedes Release Material.

The XOR-plus-skip for supplier is 0-or-1, not an over-approximation that allows two consultations. Calling it under-constrained multiplicity inverts the operator.

Section 5 does not meet the required 5 allowed and 3 forbidden rows (it is 4 and 4). W3 and W4, the two-iteration witnesses, are `ET, AP, ET, IT`, which skips the failed inspection the narrative requires; the intended pattern is `ET, IT, AP, ET, IT`. W1–W4 are not in either model’s language, yet they are marked allowed. For W5 and W6 the POWL column says allowed while the reason says POWL forbids them. Those rows therefore do not expose the tree approximation.

Section 6 marks the loop “exact” in both formalisms. It is not. The validation plan would not confirm fidelity: conversion of the given POWL fails, the positive witnesses do not fit, and “as many transitions as POWL nodes” is the wrong Petri-net expectation because XOR and LOOP expand. The choice of POWL as the canonical representation is still the right conclusion for this dependency shape, but it is supported with witness claims the table itself contradicts.
