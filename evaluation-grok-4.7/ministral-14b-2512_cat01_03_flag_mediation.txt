The write-up follows the required section order and shows some real causal vocabulary, but it is not a reliable analysis of this log. Sample sizes, stage times, and several mechanism claims are wrong, estimands are mislabeled, and the code cannot be run.

The empirical core fails. There are 10 flagged and 10 unflagged cases, not 8 and 12. Flag is a hard split on `risk_score` (every flagged case is at least 0.64; every unflagged case is at most 0.61), so the [0.7, 0.8] contrast in E7 has no unflagged cases. Unflagged cases are not escalation-free: R09 and R20 are escalated, so escalation is 6/10 flagged versus 2/10 unflagged, not 42% versus 0%. Rejection is 2/10 in both groups, not 60% versus 12%. Manual review is 10/10 versus 4/10, not 100% versus 25%. High complexity has 8 cases, and all 3 unflagged high-complexity cases are reviewed. The stage table repeats the same figures across subgroups, puts triage wait into the service column (flagged service median is about 36 minutes, not 12), treats unflagged review and escalation as absent, and calls R13’s manual service 12 hours when that interval is 3.5 hours. “10× cycle time” does not match either the reported 1200 versus 372 or the actual medians (about 987 versus 345 minutes). Low and medium complexity and flagged normal-load rows are missing.

The causal labels do not match the contrasts. P0 versus P2 is not a natural direct effect of flag through priority. P0 versus P3 is a capacity change, not an interventional indirect effect of priority. P2 versus P3 changes both the review mandate and capacity, so it is not a controlled direct effect. E6 is not a separable effect, and E7 contrasts P0 with itself. The text says natural effects are avoided, then specifies a natural-effects model fit on flagged cases only, where the flag does not vary. A drop in review under P1 would be evidence that displaying the flag changes routing, not evidence of exposure-induced confounding. Resource assignment cannot be a negative control here: flagged cases use only T1/T2 and unflagged cases only T3/T4.

The graph omits `manual_service` and `cycle_time`, leaves `U_reviewer_perception` disconnected, and orders review before its own queue wait and escalation wait before the escalation decision. Policy numbers are invented. P0’s “120-minute triage” is an unflagged figure, while P4 is assigned that same slow triage even though it keeps priority routing. The P3 pilot restricts eligibility to off-peak arrivals, which avoids the congestion the policy is supposed to test, and its primary comparison with unflagged cases is outside the randomized population.

The Python block truncates the CSV, calls removed or nonexistent APIs (`pd.compat.StringIO`, `filter_log`, `calculate_case_durations`), never attaches `flag` to the event table, and does not compute stage wait and service times. Reversal conditions and the policy-bundle language are the strongest parts, but they sit on top of incorrect observations stated as facts.
