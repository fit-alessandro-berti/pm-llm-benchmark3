6.2

The write-up meets the required seven-part structure and is aimed at peak mix, tails, and disruption rather than average cycle time. WIP caps, the rejection of pulling technical validators into accession correction, and the point that registration automation exposes pre-analytical check are sound. Those merits do not offset several process-logic errors.

Specialist review is treated as downstream of medical validation. The log shows the opposite: technical validation branches to release, medical validation, or hold, and hold then flows into medical validation. Adding an evening medical shift therefore does not push load onto the specialist; the 36 h specialist queue is an upstream, parallel constraint. The same reversal appears in the mix-shift migration sequence and in the table’s next-bottleneck cell for that shift.

The courier scenario concludes that compression starves pre-analytical check. Peak evidence shows the reverse: pre-analytical utilization is 96% and the median queue is 9.5 h, with receive-to-check time rising from 2 h to 9 h. Recollection callbacks do consume clerks, but that loop does not invert a flood into starvation. Registration overtime errors are also conflated with pre-analytical sample failures.

The dual-lane rule prioritizes urgent work and releases routine work when routine WIP is high, yet the table marks complex-panel cases as highly prioritized. Complex panels are the variant that drives medical validation (3.1×) and specialist hold (2.4×), and they are unprotected. Larger routine batches are scored as having little effect on urgent cases, despite the stated risk that batching to 24 delays them. The assay-queue trigger of 8 h sits below the observed peak median of 18 h, so that “scenario-specific” policy would be on through the whole peak. Off triggers, which were required, are absent.

Process-mining use stays at headline utilizations. Frequency counts, performance edges (technical validation to medical validation 8 h to 24 h, hold to medical validation 18 h to 48 h, repeat re-entry 10 h to 26 h), and the peak completion gap (about 9,900 started versus about 8,580 ended) are not used. Accession correction, with two specialists and a 14 h peak queue, is left unresolved after the cross-training option is rejected. Technical validation at 101% is never reconciled with the medical-validation shift it would have to feed. Repeat-after-failure, which consumes assay capacity twice and clusters on M2 and night shifts, has no policy.

Dashboard bands are miscalibrated. An assay warning at 4 h equals the normal median, so it alarms in stable operation, while a 10% recollection warning sits above the roughly 8% peak rate. Outcomes track urgent p90 only, not routine or complex-panel tails. Assumptions and failure conditions for the robust package are thin relative to what was asked.
