6.5

The answer has the right compliance skeleton and the wrong decision arithmetic, and the arithmetic errors sit inside the primary recommendation.

The six required sections, the four conformance labels, and the treatment of Actions A and B are in good shape. Rules 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11 are applied correctly: both second-officer triggers stay live while the fuzzy match is unresolved, self-certification is ranked as a falsified control, a SAR does not authorize execution, and the override stays disabled until a real director authorization and a real incident identifier exist. Parallel screening, an explicit S4-versus-S2 gate, and a hold/evidence fallback are the right kind of plan. That is why the score stays above the middle of the scale.

The operational core does not hold together.

The printed timeline for Action C is review at T+35–75 and record/execute at T+40–90, against a deadline at T+90. Every point in that interval meets the deadline. The claim that the slow half misses, and that the breach probability is about 0.40–0.50, is false on the answer’s own interval. The later claim that P1 weakly dominates C depends on that false miss rate. Immediate C is the conformant path whose stated duration still fits; P1 is a spillover-saving tradeoff, and the answer describes it as dominance.

The T+20–25 gate is internally inconsistent. S4’s arrival window is 35–65 minutes. Under a uniform reading, P(ETA ≤ T+40) is about 0.17, so the interrupt-P-901 fallback fires about four times in five. The table states a fallback probability of about 0.3–0.5 and describes P-901 as untouched unless that fallback fires. Waiting until T+20–25 to start a 40–90 minute S2 path also pushes much of that path past T+90, which removes the timing property the fallback was supposed to preserve. An ETA request is available immediately, so the 20–25 minute wait is unexplained.

The benefit cell for P1 states €8k–€26k as the product of 0.35–0.60, 0.55–0.75, and €20k–€35k. That product is about €3.9k–€15.8k. Section 5 then uses €5k–€11k for the same quantity. Action C’s €10k–€28k is inflated the same way. The slow-S4 paragraph then says a certain €20k–€35k loss is preferable to a “comparable-or-greater expected” spillover when costs are comparable. The same section earlier computes that comparable case as a €2k–€10k expected spillover and calls interruption roughly break-even. Those two conclusions cannot both be used. On the stated C interval, learning at T+0 that S4 arrives at T+65 makes immediate S2 likely to finish by T+90, so “interrupt and still probably breach” is the wrong branch description.

The screening tree assigns approximately 0.30–0.45, 0.40–0.50, and 0.10–0.20 to a three-way partition. Those bounds sum to 0.80 at the low end and 1.15 at the high end, so they are not probabilities. The opening framing says no re-screen can remove the rule 6 gate. Later the answer correctly says an authorized resolution plus a score below 70 would deactivate it, but the continuation tree never opens that branch or its shorter approval path. The rank-2 spillover of €12k–€28k embeds an unsupported €100k breach cost for P-901. The confidence column means “confidence this rank is right” for A and B and “confidence the action succeeds” for G.

Smaller gaps add noise rather than changing the constraint set: S4’s review is shortened to 25–50 minutes with no basis, F is ranked fourth while the prose says a rejection at T+0 is premature, “formally waived” has no basis in the rules, and pre-briefing S2 is not checked against independent judgment.

The conformance ordering would score much higher alone. The recommendation’s timing, dominance, expected-value, and probability claims contradict the ranges the answer itself prints, so this is not a near-flawless response.
