The write-up follows the requested seven-part structure and correctly rejects a pure frequency play and an unsafe activation bot. Those merits are outweighed by systematic misreads of the event data, invented effects, and a recommendation that contradicts its own rollout and constraints.

The candidate matrix is an unranked A1–A12 list, and volumes are repeatedly treated as cases per day. A1 is tied to 12k rather than registration volume. A8 is sized at 2.7k, which is the credit-analysis→packaging edge, not the 3,900 packaging cases. A3’s false negatives are described as blocking progression; a missed KYC hit is a control failure, while delay comes from true and false positives. A4’s “38% of KYC delays” is the mismatch activity’s own exception rate. “82% of critical paths” drops the qualifier that this is 82% of mismatch cases only.

The bottleneck portfolio is not a synchronization portfolio. KYC’s median wait is 4 hours. The long waits and loops are external valuation (58h), signing (44h, 21% reminders), entity mismatch (31h, and the return edge to KYC), missing-document recirculation (52% repeated; 2,800 returns), and normalization/credit queues. A7, A5, and A10 are omitted, then valuation wait is still shown falling from 58h to 20h. A3 is later called low-frequency. The “75%+” analyst-load cut and the 31h→0h mismatch wait are unsupported; the assistant is specified as limited-coverage. The portfolio also contains no module that clearly strengthens controls, yet that same gap is used to discard the quick-win set.

Compliance-first is rejected because four modules “exceed” a cap of four. Effect tables assign signing, financial-review rework, and committee-calendar cuts to modules that do not touch those activities. Provisional-signing outcomes are “reduced to 1.1%,” which is the current remediation baseline, and A3 cannot cause that result. Four-eyes is assigned to contract generation. Almost none of the cited variant or directly-follows structure is actually used.

The rollout then abandons A3+A4+A6. It schedules seven modules, puts packaging and contract generation ahead of normalization and mismatch resolution, measures mismatch time in a phase that does not deploy the mismatch assistant, and never consistently applies the two-deep-integration limit in the first six months. Section 2’s claim that only A1 and A9 can be deeply integrated is not what the constraint says, and A1 never appears in the plan. Gate targets such as 90% untouched normalization ignore stated PDF and exception limits.

The directional warning against automating registration first is sound. The portfolio a bank would implement from this answer is not: the numbers, the control logic, the phase order, and the final choice do not agree with each other or with the log.
