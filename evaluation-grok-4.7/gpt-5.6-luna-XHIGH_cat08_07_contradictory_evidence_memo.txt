8.0

The memo is decision-ready and largely faithful to the evidence: it refuses national scale and blanket rollback, keeps the simple-house gain, and ties rollout to tail, rework, cost, and activation controls. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly kept apart, and the fitness/precision, median/tail, handover/resilience, and cost conflicts are explained correctly.

It is not nearly flawless. The fact column says apartments “worsened,” but their median improved by 2 days. “Risk-blind” misstates a predictive skip rule that was often wrong on medium-risk addresses. Failed-visit “13% to 19%” is a DFG frequency share, not a defined case rate. Calling the action “E implemented as C” blurs two different scopes: C would extend coordinator ownership while suspending the risky parts; the recommendation withholds that ownership outside low-risk houses and never states whether that subset is national or still regional. M7 is treated as a rollout veto yet ranked fourth, and M5 is ranked too low for a decision that still scales single-owner coordinators. The p95 of 62 days is not reconciled with a six-week pilot, and the survey-skip experiment is not limited to strata where re-exposure is still defensible.
