The case outcomes are largely the right ones, and the temporal model is sound: blanks are non-updates, state is forward-filled only, release is judged on values known then, `(timestamp, event_index)` orders TX-311, and TX-305’s later settlement amount is not copied back. The findings table still misstates TX-311’s release state as RETAIL. The create event sets CORPORATE, and nothing updates customer type. The classification happens to survive, but that column is the reconstructed state and it is wrong.

The TX-311 counterfactual is also wrong. If the DE update were ineffective until after release, release would be US at EUR 115,000. The enhanced review already saw that amount and US, with no later effective material change, so R1 would still be satisfied and the result would stay COMPLIANT. The other branch (a post-review move to a different non-DE jurisdiction) is the scenario that would actually make the review stale.

The R1/R2 formulas do not compose with R6. `R1_satisfied` is false when the trigger is unknown, no fresh enhanced review exists, and no valid exemption exists. That is a violation, while R6 requires INDETERMINATE. The same hole exists for R2. The code’s three-valued triggers are better than the formulas, but the formalization never states that R6 overrides those predicates.

The checker is serious and, on these eleven traces, lines up with the narrative (`REVIEW_REQUIRED` for TX-305 and TX-310 rather than a case-id lookup). Two design gaps remain. A sanctions-list update with no earlier screen becomes `NO_PRIOR_SCREEN_R3_NOT_APPLICABLE`, which the coarse scorer treats as compliant. `Record_Exemption` is sought only before release, and the register frame has no insertion time, so the 11:00 post-release records that distinguish TX-302 from TX-310 never appear in the diagnostics. TX-304’s “satisfied obligations” cell also credits the 08:10 screen, which does not satisfy R3.

TX-310 as INDETERMINATE is a permitted reading of contradictory evidence, and the flip conditions are stated.
