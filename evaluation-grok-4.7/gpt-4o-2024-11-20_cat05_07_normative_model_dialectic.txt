The answer keeps the seven required headings and correctly treats SUP-701 as clean, SUP-703 as a severe ineligible auto-approval, and SUP-704 as inside FD-7. Those points do not carry the paper. Required case-level work, dialectical duties, and the revised model are incomplete or wrong, and several claims contradict the record or each other.

Section 1 states conclusions as stipulated facts and misattributes concurrency to CS-9; concurrency is only an M0 partial order. CS-9 requires both checks before activation and does not specify that they are unordered. The corporate-group condition, master-data validation, business-day deferral, the compliance observer’s later denial that she weakened CS-9, and the main aggregates are missing. SUP-708 is grouped with provisional activation even though no emergency or pilot applied. The burden of proof equates the model with the standard and accepts “implied authority,” which is too loose for an exception to a controlled standard.

Section 2 calls SUP-702 a post-pilot auto-approval. SUP-702 is dated 2026-06-15, inside PD-3. It also treats the 289 apparently eligible pilot cases as normalization. The 14 ineligible and 9 attribute-missing cases, which are the actual aggregate abuse signal, are never used. The brief does not acknowledge genuine model or data defects, although that concession is required. Alias separation-of-duties, mutable CRM links, duplicate registration, and retrospective label mapping stay undeveloped. Desirable behavior is not shown to be incapable of amending CS-9.

Section 3 asserts that a continuing factual emergency justifies SUP-705 and that imminent-policy belief justifies post-expiry auto-approval, without accepting the limit on informal practice. It never acknowledges SUP-703 or SUP-708. The concurrency claim is backwards: M0 already leaves sanctions and quality unordered; its defect is that it forces both before every decision, so authorized deferral looks like a fitness failure. Trace deviation versus misconduct is not developed for the duplicate register event, the unlogged-but-linked notification, or the information-request cases.

Section 4 supplies four of fifteen required rows and then an ellipsis. The SUP-704 “missing evidence” cell discusses post-FD-7 documentation, which is a SUP-705 issue. SUP-702 is marked as having no missing evidence even though PD-3’s master-data validation is absent from the trace. The prosecution column also conflicts with Section 2 on SUP-702.

Section 5 classifies only SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-704, and SUP-705. SUP-705 is labeled MIXED with a fragment of a reason, which is the substitution the prompt forbids. Confidence is promised and not given. The system finding says the model should follow operational convergence and does not judge operational compliance. Rejecting the prosecution on SUP-715 “because M0 is narrow” is a category error: a missing conflict disclosure can be a policy breach precisely because M0 cannot see it.

Section 6 is four vague bullets. Resource rules are replaced with scope topics, withdrawals are absent, and there is no assignment of controls to POWL, declarative constraints, tabular data checks, and organizational authority.

Section 7 does not implement the required model. The partial-order edge into the checks does not create a repeatable `Request_More_Info`; a strict partial order executes that activity once, and here it is mandatory and before the checks, so the clean SUP-701 trace would fail. There is no emergency or provisional route, quality review cannot be deferred on the auto route, and one `Activate_Supplier` object is shared by both approval branches. No limitation is stated. The required policy-overlay table and the data proposal—stable notification IDs, rule versions, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, authority, and immutable timestamps—are absent.

The result is a partial sketch with a few right case intuitions, material factual errors, an unfinished matrix and judgment, and a POWL revision that misses the behaviors it was required to represent.
