The answer covers every required section, keeps the four protected relations unregulated, and separates forbidden behavior, mandatory gaps, typical absences, and open-world noise with mostly sound severity. The clearest error is treating equal aggregate weights as per-object coverage. Pack–Package 200 and Dispatch–Package 200 do not imply that each of the 200 externally shipped packages has both events; duplicate events on some objects can offset missing events on others. Finding 4 and the OC-DFG discussion state the opposite as a consequence (“both events exist for all 200 packages, so the gaps are inversions or intervening events”). The same move appears when Order–Customer weight 100 is read as “every order has a customer.” That mistake changes the N4 hypothesis set and sits in tension with §8, which correctly says per-object identity and event order cannot be decided from these summaries.

The OTG reading has a related category error. `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` weight 200 and the seven `object_codeath` edges are different relations.
