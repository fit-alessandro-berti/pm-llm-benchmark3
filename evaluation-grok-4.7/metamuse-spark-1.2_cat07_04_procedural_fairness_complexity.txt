7.2

The answer meets every required heading, honors the constraints, and uses the supplied figures correctly in most places. The residual-gap versus case-mix distinction is the right frame, and the capacity story for language support and accommodation review is well supported. It is not nearly flawless: several factual slips, one internal contradiction on bias, and a few overclaims keep it well below a top score.

The clearest logical break is in §8. Adjusted gaps are called a lower bound if `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` are biased. §2 and mechanism 5 correctly say omitting those covariates can move the gap either way. Biased controls do not have a fixed sign, so “lower bound” is stronger than the answer’s own sensitivity design.

`prior_case_count` is treated inconsistently. §2 puts it in the primary, “defensible” adjustment set. §4–§5 then describe it as a possible feedback loop from earlier barriers. That variable needs the same omit-and-compare treatment as risk and documentation completeness; the plan never gives it one.

A few claims are not in the tables. “Fraud→Eligibility” waits of 2.8 versus 2.2 days are the Risk Screening → Fraud Review waits, mislabeled. “Automated” identity verification is not in the log, and those waits already differ (0.8–1.4 days) and are tied to in-person filing, so it is a poor negative control. The claim that complete-documentation, low-risk Group B cases leave the fast path is an extrapolation; the adjusted model is not a V1 share within that cell. “Gini-like” concentration and “lower quality, less explanation, more errors” are inferred from assignment to 7 and 5 workers, not measured. Raw approval “15–20 percentage points” drops Group C’s 9-point gap; median “5–8 days” drops Group D’s 4.0-day gap. The Decision Draft → Notification “0.5–0.8d gap” is the level of the waits; the between-group gap is at most 0.3 days.

§8 also splits the verdict: “not procedurally fair” and then “indeterminate to moderately unfair.” The evidence supports a structural-burden finding with indeterminate discriminatory intent. The first sentence is firmer than that calibration.

The PM4Py sketch names the right family of techniques, but two calls would not do the stated job on this log. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `get_service_time` need a start timestamp; the log has only `time:timestamp`, so service versus wait cannot be split as written. `filter_paths_performance` filters on a performance range; it does not return median waits. Within-language comparison of Group A versus Group B is also badly underpowered (about 2% of Group A), and the plan flags small C and D samples without flagging that cell. Top variants leave about 18% of Group A and 10% of Group D unexplained, which the path discussion ignores.

Coverage, constraint compliance, and the main substantive judgment are strong. The grade is held down by the lower-bound contradiction, the contaminated control, the mislabeled edge, and the unsupported cell-level and resource-quality claims.
