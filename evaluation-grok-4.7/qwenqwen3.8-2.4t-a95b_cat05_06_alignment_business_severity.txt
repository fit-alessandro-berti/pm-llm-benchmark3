The analysis is strong and hits the central point: raw alignment cost is not business risk. Zero-cost policy failures (CL-609, CL-610), the fitting silent transition (CL-605), the cheap but severe fraud and pay-before-approval cases (CL-602, CL-604), label mismatch versus a real control miss (CL-611), external evidence versus a missing log event (CL-606), and high-cost scope mismatches (CL-607, CL-612) are all interpreted in the right direction. Severity is separated from confidence, patterns and owners are grounded in the monthly evidence, and the remediation order is mostly sensible.

Deductions are for concrete defects, not tone. In the required code, CL-612 is given amount 0 even though the case is EUR 2,000, which contradicts the narrative and corrupts the amount aggregation. CL-607 is assigned risk LOW and amount 0 although neither is supplied. P3 is stretched onto CL-602, where manual approval is before payment and the defect is a missing fraud screen (P2), and P6 is stretched onto withdrawal, which the policy text does not name. The portfolio also leaves the already-paid auto-approvals, especially the one-hour bank change in CL-610, out of incident containment and treats them mainly as a future rules fix.
