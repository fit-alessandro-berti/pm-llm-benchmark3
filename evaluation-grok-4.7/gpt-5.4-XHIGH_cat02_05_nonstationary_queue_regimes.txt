7.2

The analysis is competent and largely reproducible: opening-hour rates, the outage-versus-recovery distinction, the weekday/weekend scale split, the Little’s Law refusals, the peak-share conflict, and the Simpson composition example are all real and mostly correctly computed. It is not nearly flawless. Several interpretations are loose enough to change the operational story.

The service-time contrast in section 4 is inaccurate. “Before 2026-04-20” is not a 25–29 minute median and 47–56 minute p90 regime: that window includes the outage and recovery (medians 31 and 33, p90s 68, 72, and 60). “From 2026-04-20 onward” is not a 34–36 / 78–88 regime either: intervention days fall to medians 32–33 and p90s 72–76. Those ranges erase both the pre-mix service spike and the later easing that the regime table itself records, so the pooled-versus-segmented contrast overstates a single step change.

The backlog path is unlabeled and then misapplied. The sequence “about −51, −21, −115” matches weekday-only cumulative completions minus arrivals exactly, while the all-day path is about −52, −22, and −128. Section 5 presents those figures as relative backlog with an arbitrary zero, without saying weekends were dropped. By 2026-04-28 the omitted weekend net is −13. The same −115 is then cited as evidence inside the high-complex weekday row, although that regime’s own weekday net is −94 and about −21 was inherited from the outage and incomplete recovery.

The staffing conclusion repeats, in milder form, the mistake the answer correctly avoids for 2026-04-16 to 2026-04-17. Post-intervention weekday completions of 121 per day sit above arrivals of about 114.5, and WIP is still falling (43 to 36 to 32). That 121 rate includes backlog liquidation. Once WIP stabilizes, completions should move back toward the arrival rate. Section 6 withholds Little’s Law from this window, but section 7 still quotes 100.6 to 121 as the capacity gain.

The alternative segmentation is not a clean causal partition. The written dates redundantly restate 2026-04-25 to 2026-04-26 inside 2026-04-20 to 2026-04-28 and only vaguely gesture at later weekends, while the numbers (90.0 arrivals, 80.4 completions, net −106) require folding in 2026-05-02 and 2026-05-03. Those weekends did receive a staff increase, from 2.5 to 3.0 FTE, but the staff≥6.5 rule labels them unassisted. Assisted versus unassisted therefore mixes the intervention with weekday/weekend composition, and the −106 versus +18 contrast is the same kind of composition effect diagnosed elsewhere.

Smaller defects add up. Pooled daily Fano near 10 falls to about 0.66 on weekdays alone, so the overdispersion is day-type composition; calling it regime mixture overstates the within-weekday shift from about 100 to about 114 arrivals per day. Pooled `complex_pct` and `peak_08_10_pct` are unweighted means of daily percentages, not arrival-weighted shares. The change-point score reuses the same series that define the split and full-sample standardization, so it is not independent confirmation. Weekday lag-1 autocorrelation joins Friday to Monday. The alternative date wording cannot be executed literally.

The required format, code structure, PM4Py caveat, dispersion measures, and refusal to treat the outage flush or the full sample as a steady queue are in place. The grade is held down by the inconsistent service ranges, the unlabeled backlog total, the unsustainable 121-per-day reading, and the confounded alternative partition.
