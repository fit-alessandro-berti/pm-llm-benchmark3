7.5

The memo is decision-ready, constraint-compliant, and unusually careful about confounding, but several analytical slips keep it short of a near-flawless strategic product.

The recommendation (targeted E, with C’s safeguards and D’s experimental discipline) is defensible. It keeps the simple-house gains, refuses national scale, refuses a blanket rollback, and ties rollout to segment results, capacity, and downstream cost. The evidence table actually separates observation, causal reading, and open questions, and all five metric conflicts are addressed with the right directional story. Numbers in the table match the brief.

The deductions are for reasoning, not structure.

The executive memo treats the survey team’s utilization drop as support that work was pushed into exceptions. That drop is the mechanical result of skipping surveys; the second-visit rise, first-time-right decline, and complaint shift are the evidence of downstream displacement. The same paragraph warns that no single component is identified, then still scales the merged validate-and-schedule path as the carrier of the simple-house gain.

The fitness/precision explanation is loose. Fitness 0.94 is not “94% of traces replayed.” Low precision means the model permits many unobserved combinations; calling skipped survey, manual activation, repeat visits, and early billing things that “rarely or should never occur” mixes a precision claim with a compliance claim. Those behaviors are frequent in the log. The conflict section also misses a direct measurement mechanism: moving activation past billing start can shorten recorded cycle time while exporting failure into complaints and the new billing-to-complaint tail. Simple-house p90 improvement shows this is not the whole story, but it belongs in the median-versus-tail account. Unequal 10-week versus 6-week windows and completion-only timestamps, which especially threaten p90/p95, are not flagged. Generalization is cited as “weak” and not interpreted. Business (8%) and rural (11%) outcomes never receive a disposition, and SLA appears only in the final gate.

Value of information is complete but not strictly ranked by decision value. The stated criterion adds speed and ability to act. M7 is placed first even though frequent billing-before-activation and missing permit checks are already treated as grounds to stop; monitoring mostly polices a decision already taken. M1 is ranked above M6 even though a miscalibrated risk rule contaminates the strata the experiment would use. The concurrent-run caveat limits the damage.

The rollout gate is softer than the brief’s request for a threshold, then too absolute in one place. “Material” and “operationally meaningful” are undefined, while “no billing before successful activation” and “no missing permit checks” demand zero deviation against a baseline that already had a 3% billing-to-complaint edge. Using 93% and 92% utilization as the capacity standard recycles the pilot’s observed overload rather than a capacity rule. Alternative D’s “reasonable if” cell collapses into E.

Fact and interpretation are cleanly split in the table and mostly signaled in prose. The remaining problem is a few decision sentences that are sharper than the identification the memo itself claims.
