7.2

The main verdicts are right: A and B are not visibly trace-equivalent, not P3-equivalent, and not P1-equivalent, while they match only after the stated P2 abstraction. Loop scope, `count(C)=count(A)` versus exactly one `C`, the two-sided counterexamples T1/T2 and T4/T5, and the warning that a 12-event bound is not a proof are handled correctly. The simplified tree is a real structural reduction (11 nodes versus 12) and the recommendation correctly refuses to treat one simplification as valid for both check-scopes.

Those results sit next to several definite mistakes.

The approval analysis is wrong. Optional `Ap` is not “orthogonal,” and it is not “not a distinguishing feature.” In A, `->(+(A,C), X(Ap,tau))` forces both `A` and `C` to finish before `Ap`. In B, `C` is concurrent with `->(A, X(Ap,tau))`, so it can sit after `Ap`. Thus `O A Ap C X` is complete for B and impossible for A, with one analysis and one check. Hiding `tau` does not remove that difference. Calling approval and silence red herrings, and saying check-scope is “the whole story,” is false.

The required counterexample table contains a false row. T3 lists `O C A X` as A=no, B=yes. A admits it, because the first iteration’s `+(A,C)` can fire `C` first. The note admits this and then tells the reader to ignore the row. T1, T2, T4, and T5 are valid, so the separation still goes through, but the table as printed is incorrect. Section 2 repeats the same slip by treating “`C` before the first `A`” as B-only.

Section 6’s concrete footprint witness is false. T1 (`O A C R A C X`) and T4 (`O A R A C X`) do not have the same directly-follows pairs: T1 has `C→R` and not `A→R`; T4 has `A→R` and not `C→R`. The broader claim that equal footprints or a small EMD would not prove language equivalence is sound, and refusing to invent probabilities is right, but “footprints are likely equal” is an unsupported guess. `Ap→C` is possible only in B, so a language footprint should differ.

Section 1 and Section 8 disagree. P1-equivalence is defined to include equality of the full `tau`-hidden languages, which would make it imply visible-trace equivalence by stipulation. The simplified model then drops every `C`-before-`A` trace and is still called behaviorally P1-equivalent to A. The preservation table says approval positions are “identical to A”; they are not (`O C A Ap X` is in A only). The loss of intra-iteration order is disclosed elsewhere, and the final recommendation is more careful than those sentences.

Label treatment is muddled. The working notion is word equality, so repeated labels are distinguished by order and count. The text also says they are observed “by label only” and that two `C`s are indistinguishable. Identity versus position is never stated cleanly.

The bounded BFS is the right idea: silent steps, `(marking, visible word)` deduplication, length and `A`-count caps, final-marking acceptance, and an explicit non-exhaustive reading of `play_out`. It was not run, place-name keys and `ClassicSemantics.execute` are fragile, and no bounded trace sets or `simplicity_petri_net` values are produced. Tree counts themselves (12/12 nodes, depths 5 and 6, 6 visible leaves, 1 silent leaf, 0 duplicate labels) are correct, and not fabricating simplicity is appropriate. The remark that B’s net is “slightly cleaner” is not.

Score is held down by the false T3 row, the missed `Ap`/`C` separator, the false directly-follows claim, and the P1 preservation overclaim. The central equivalence decisions and the usable multi-attempt witnesses are what keep it above a failing analysis.
