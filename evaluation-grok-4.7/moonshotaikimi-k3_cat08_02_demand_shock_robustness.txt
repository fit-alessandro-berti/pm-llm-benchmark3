The redesign is strong and complete: five scenarios vary mix and disruption type, bottleneck migration is traced station by station with downstream overload called out, the package refuses blanket capacity and static priority, and the trade-off table, redesign-caused vulnerabilities, and leading-versus-lagging dashboard all match the required format.

Deductions are for quantitative and causal slips in the final claims. Peak technical-validation shares are computed on assay inflow (7,240) even though recorded exits sum to 8,080, so the stated 56% direct-release and 47% medical-validation shares are high versus about 50% and 42%. The line that peak repeats burn “roughly 3–5%” of assay capacity does not match the log: repeat re-entries are about 10.5% of peak assay starts (1,080/10,250), and the gap versus the normal start share is about 5 points, not a 3–5% absolute burn. Expanded routine auto-release is treated as the main way to free medical validation under the mix shift, but peak urgent plus complex-panel volume (29% + 20%) already lines up with the ~50% of releases that pass through medical validation, so that lever cannot absorb the surge the package assigns to it. P2 also calls the resulting cleaner flow “absorbable” at technical validation while citing that pool at 101%. Several control bands (open-recollection counts, exact flex floors) are stated more precisely than the DFG can support.

The architecture, scenario triggers, and refusal of I1/I2/I3/I9 as written still hold.
