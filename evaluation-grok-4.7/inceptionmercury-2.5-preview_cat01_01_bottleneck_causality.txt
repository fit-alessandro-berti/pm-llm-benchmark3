The answer catches the central sign reversal and correctly rejects a blanket 50% reassignment, but it is not a disciplined causal or process-mining assessment. Factual mistakes, an incoherent graph, non-identified “allowed” causal claims, and a diagnostic script that does not meet its own specification keep it well below a passable committee note.

Section 1 is about 130 words against a required 180–300, and it never separates a total effect of assignment, a direct effect holding mechanisms fixed, and a mediated effect. “Mainly confounded” is a reasonable label for the crude “Orion is slower” claim, but confidence 75 is too high: two of three strata have a cell of size 2. The cited “means” High 31.6 and Medium 17.2 are the published medians (means are 31.57 and 17.17). “Nova is 87.5% low-complexity” is false: low is 10/16 = 62.5%; 87.5% is the non-high share. A random half of Orion’s book is about 14% low, 43% medium, and 43% high, so the note overstates that the transfer “likely targets” high-complexity work.

The edge list omits any arrow from `resource_group` to `queue_wait`, `review_service`, `rework`, or `cycle_time`, so the treatment reaches cycle time only through `resource_group -> shift`. That direction is the weak one: shift is largely when the case arrives, and night coverage is a routing rule, so `shift -> routing_rule -> resource_group` is the fact-supported path. `compliance_required -> review_service` is contradicted inside Medium Orion, where service is about 6.2 hours with or without compliance. The prose then calls `cycle_time -> case_completed` a feedback “not in this DAG” even though that edge is in the list. Supported structure that is missing includes `complexity -> review_service`, `complexity -> queue_wait`, `compliance_required -> compliance_performed -> queue_wait`, and `unobserved_case_risk -> routing_rule`.

The role table names a proxy, a confounder, and a selection node, which is useful, but it conflicts with the graph: shift is an adjustment confounder in the table and a downstream consequence of group in the graph. Compliance is not a mediator of group. Policy ties it to case type, and group does not assign it. Conditioning on it does not block a group mechanism; conditioning on shift would block or adjust a different estimand depending on which graph is believed. Queue, service, rework, load, routing rule, and the two unobserved factors never get a role relative to a named estimand.

The decomposition table misses the Medium stratum, which is the least thin contrast (Orion 17.17 hours, n=6, all night/high load; Nova 19.38 hours, n=4, only one night case). Several “allowed” cells state causal conclusions the data do not allow. Appeals are 1/14 versus 1/16, not 1/14 versus 2/16. Night does not have higher wait regardless of group: among Low cases, night waits are 3.3, 3.8, and 5.1 hours, while Nova’s Low day waits average about 5.2 hours. The night-cycle gap is mostly complexity mix. Compliance is not a clean “~2× queue” effect. Within Medium, the only stratum with both levels, Orion compliance cases average about 16.9 hours and 8.6 hours of wait, versus about 17.4 and 9.9 hours without compliance. The real process split is missed: within complexity, review service is nearly identical (Low 3.25 vs 3.64, Medium 6.17 vs 6.40, High 9.17 vs 9.30), and the stratified cycle gap is almost all wait. Putting “service differences are negligible” in the not-allowed column inverts that description. Crude rework (28.6% vs 18.8%) also reverses or ties inside strata (High 50% both; Medium 16.7% vs 25%; Low 0% vs 10%), and `wait + service` is less than cycle time, so residual register/classify/compliance/close time is unaccounted for.

The five hypotheses cover harm, masked benefit, shift/load, compliance, and selection, but the tests do not discriminate. Rank 1 proposes “within-group” routing, which cannot compare groups, and treats a disappearing gap as the prediction without stating what would support harm. Rank 3 says that a slow night Nova would prove shift is the bottleneck, which recycles the complexity confound. Rank 4 predicts the effect will either shrink or persist. Rank 2’s complexity-only matching would still leave shift and load imbalanced, and the note never states the positivity failure: Medium-day and High-day Orion have zero cases, and every night Nova cell in Medium and High has n=1. Sections 4 and 5 also contradict each other on whether night wait is generally higher.

The pilot-plus-capacity shape is the right safer alternative, and the recommendation is about 85 words against a required 120–200. Phase 1 does not restrict randomization to cases both groups may legally handle, despite Orion holding the certified reviewers for difficult work. A two-hour cycle cap is not a real-time safeguard. The 30-day appeal outcome is too rare and too lagged to be the stop rule (two appeals in thirty cases). Phase 2 targets compliance capacity even though, within complexity, compliance does not explain the wait gap; night queue capacity for high-complexity review is the better mechanism bet. Phase 3’s “full redistribution” can recreate the original proposal.

The Python block embeds the CSV and prints stratified means, but it does not print the crude contrast, does not warn on n<3 or empty support, and its comment says Nova weights while the code uses the pooled mix (12/30, 10/30, 8/30). Merging that Series on `complexity` is not reliable Pandas, and `.filter` on trace strings after parsing the combined log cannot split groups: the same variant string appears in both. `discover_dfg` returns a DFG triple, so `len` is not a variant count, and no directly-follows edges are compared. The script is not a reproducible diagnostic.

Section 8 is the cleanest part: five real gaps, each with one data field or design change. That does not repair the body of the note.
