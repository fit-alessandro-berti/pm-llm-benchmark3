The audit covers the required items, catches the main future-trace leaks, and keeps causal language appropriately weak. It is not reliable as a process-aware revision.

The largest error is the process clock. At t0, immediately after Coverage Check Completed, damage assessment and document cycles are later activities. Fast track does not place Damage Assessment Completed before t0, and Documents Requested → Documents Received is normally still future. Treating those patterns as includable whenever a timestamp happens to fall before t0 misstates what is enabled, impossible, or only future-possible.

The revised Case X explanation is internally inconsistent. Elapsed time of 1.2 hours contributes −0.03, but the narrative says that age leaves less slack, which would raise breach risk. Against a 10-day target, 1.2 hours is a young prefix with ample slack. `initial_reserve` is given direction “neutral” despite a +0.08 contribution, and `claimant_channel` disappears without being kept or removed. `prior_claim_count` is both used as a driver and flagged as broken, while the narrative asserts that all three claims predate t0.

`total_case_duration_hours` is identified as post-closure leakage but not as near-label leakage: breaching 10 days is a function of duration. `final_handler_team` and the `<= extraction_date` prior-claim query are under-severed. Appeal → second Settlement Decision is correctly separated from the same-timestamp closure pattern, but the suggested at-t0 appeal repair is not something the process can support. The recommendation list misses calibration after feature removal and explanation stability.
