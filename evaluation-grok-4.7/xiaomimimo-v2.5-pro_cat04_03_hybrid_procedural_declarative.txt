The write-up never settles into the required 10-section model. Its later conclusions still contradict the checks it assigns.

The log skeleton treats `directly_follows` as two mandatory endings, `(Release First Payment, Close Onboarding)` and `(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding)`, then marks T1–T3 and T6–T7 as passing. In PM4Py that set is the allowed direct-succession footprint, so every normal step (`Receive Onboarding Request` → `Capture Master Data`, `Manager Approval` → `Activate Vendor`, and so on) is a violation. The verdict column is therefore inconsistent with the skeleton.

`Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` are left out of the process tree, yet T6 and T7 are scored as procedural passes. Those labels are log moves against this tree, and in T6 `Obtain Legal Opinion` also sits between `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment`, so it breaks that sequence. “Flexible insertion” is not what an accepting tree/net checks.

`Verify Bank Account` is given frequency `{1}` and equivalence with the other two checks. R11 explicitly allows another verification after a post-activation bank change, so that encoding rejects a case the policy requires. Clarification frequency is also capped at 5 with no such limit in R3.

The DECLARE object is nested by template name. The required dictionary uses activity keys and `(source, target)` tuple keys. `absence` with `n: 2` is left ambiguous against PM4Py’s count parameter, so R10 is not reliably “at most once.” The R5/R6 checker calls `.total_seconds()` on hour offsets.

R5 versus R6 is identified, unconditional legal-opinion precedence is correctly kept out of DECLARE, T4’s same-resource approval, T5’s open clarification, T8’s double terminal, and T7’s 34 h versus 12 h ± 6 h warning (`|34−12|=22 > 12`) are the parts that hold. Those do not repair the skeleton semantics, the fitness verdicts, or the missing final specification.
