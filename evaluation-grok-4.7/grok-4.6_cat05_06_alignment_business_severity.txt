The business reading is strong and largely faithful to the overlay: zero-cost P1/P2 breaches are separated from fitting tau, pay-before-approve is not excused by a cheap repair, missing events are not treated as proof of non-performance, and raw cost is kept distinct from severity. The required sections, case columns, pattern set, hypothesis tests, and remediation types are all present.

It is not nearly flawless. Population counts contradict each other: CL-603 is treated as one of the 74 retry cases in the case narrative, then as “CL-603 plus 74” in the pattern section, and CL-606 is added on top of the nine external-adjuster cases while the auto-approve pattern correctly treats four as the inclusive total. “P2’s hard gates are not all present” uses the wrong quantifier for an OR rule; the CL-609 conclusion happens to be right only because none of the triggers are present. CL-610 is called the sharper fraud vector and given an immediate recall action, but the remediation portfolio does not investigate that already-paid diversion and the code then ranks CL-609 above it by exposure. The code’s comment says confidence is a sort key, yet `confidence_rank` is unused. `p4_notify_evidenced=False` and `p6_scope_dispute=True` on CL-612 turn an invented model completion and a withdrawal (P6 names denied leads and re-opened claims) into false overlay hits. Risk is displayed per case and is not aggregated. Those are concrete consistency and evidence-table errors, not stylistic nits.
