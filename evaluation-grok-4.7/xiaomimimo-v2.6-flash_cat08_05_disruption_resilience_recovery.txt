7.4

The required seven sections are complete, the A–L classifications are mostly well grounded, and the redesign correctly separates sequencing from control level and operational close from compliance close. The score is held down by repeated quantitative overclaims. Period totals are treated as proof that T3’s lower exception rates were mainly a denominator illusion, without normalizing for the longer window or acknowledging the real per-case improvement. Compliance holds are cited as “+86%” across unequal volumes and durations, and that rise is asserted as the reappearance of skipped authorization. Cancellation “14×” and reconciliation “10×” compare conditional rates on already-failed subsets with the overall 2.1% baseline; baseline backorders themselves cancel at about 33%. T4’s 1,400 reconciliations and 2,300 audit cases are identified with T2 snapshot checks and spreadsheet invoices rather than offered as hypotheses. Missing DFG edges are sometimes treated as proof that evidence never existed, and the open-item tally is internally inconsistent (about 200+400+200 versus “~600”).
