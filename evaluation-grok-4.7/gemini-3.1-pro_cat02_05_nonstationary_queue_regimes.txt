The regime story is recognizable and the required eight-part structure is complete, but several headline figures are wrong, some of them contradict the code that is supposed to reproduce them, and a few inferences cross lines the data and the task both flag.

**What holds up.** The primary split (baseline weekdays, outage plus recovery, weekends, post–20 Apr complexity/burst regime, 29 Apr staffing window) matches the visible breaks in mix, WIP, throughput gap, and FTE. The accumulation-versus-depletion alternative is genuinely different, and its weakness—collapsing a supply shock with a mix shock—is stated clearly. Little’s Law is not fed median handling time; the full sample, the outage day, the accumulating mix regime, and the burn-down window are correctly marked not defensible. The risky pooled-staffing policy is the right kind of Simpson warning. The Python block builds both tables, assigns the same regimes, and names the three PM4Py routines while saying these rows are not an event log.

**Numerical errors.** Pooled mean arrivals are 2519/28 = 90.0, not 91.5. Pooled mean completions are 2409/28 = 86.0, not 89.6. Baseline weekday arrivals (6–10 and 13–14 Apr) are 701/7 = 100.1, not ~102, so λ ≈ 8.35/h and L/λ ≈ 2.43 h, not 8.5 and 2.35. For the 21 Apr arrival vector, population index of dispersion is about 9.28 and CV about 0.98, not ~7.4 and ~0.88. Lag-1 autocorrelation of `mean_wip` is about 0.61, not ~0.88. Those last two, and the flow-time figure, are what the code would print; the prose reports different values. Weekend p90s average about 68, not ~65, and that average is itself a mixture.

**Inferential problems.** The 21 Apr bins do not support the claim that hourly burstiness “aligns with” `peak_08_10_pct` of 47: 08:00–09:59 is 3+8 = 11 of 115 (about 10%). No two-hour block equals 47%. That contradiction is a data-audit finding, and it is missed. Comparing the hourly index of dispersion with 1 does not show extra-Poisson noise, because a time-varying rate alone inflates variance across hours. Lag-1 WIP correlation is not identified as overnight carry-over; day-of-week pairs and multi-day regimes produce the same signature, and 07:00 backlog is unobserved. “Capacity matches demand” on the baseline sits unresolved next to an implied sojourn of roughly 2.4 operating hours versus a 25-minute median handling time. Section 4 treats 121–129 completions, including 16 Apr, as the system’s maximum throughput / capacity limit, and section 7 repeats “>120 completions/day” as the effect of 7.0 FTE. That is the outage-recovery mistake the task warns against: 16 Apr is backlog flushing under distorted service times, and mean WIP rises from 47 to 52 on that day, so the snapshot mean is not end-of-day inventory. The coding-change alternative for `complex_pct` is never carried; the text says the 20 Apr jump “proves” mix-linked staffing. The weekend bin is labeled high confidence while mixing early p90s near 55 with later p90s near 80. “Unrecoverable” overstates a backlog that the same series shows falling after 29 Apr.

**Smaller gaps.** Assumptions (open-hours-only snapshots, no overnight arrivals, arrivals as λ) are often stated as facts. Regimes are point estimates without ranges despite n as small as 3. The Little’s matrix omits weekends. `~21%` before 20 Apr is about 22.4% through 19 Apr. The PM4Py note is a discarded string, not a comment.

The operational skeleton would survive a revision. The reported arithmetic and several causal sentences would not.
