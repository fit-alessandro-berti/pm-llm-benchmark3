7.0

The memo is a serious, issue-specific compliance analysis with the required structure, a disclosed hierarchy, threshold discipline, and several hard evidentiary calls made correctly. It is not nearly flawless. Two case rows contradict the memo’s own method, and several conspicuous timing and authority facts are left unresolved while confidence is stated too high.

The hierarchy in Sections 1–3 is defensible and mostly well controlled. Board-level integrity rules are kept above the incident directive and the CFO email; ECD-2 is confined to timing and supplier-onboarding inside its window; later approval is treated as non-curative; the EUR 25,000, 50,000, 100,000, and 150,000 lines are applied in the right direction. The defeasible rules match that reading. The catalogue is damaged in presentation: inequality signs are missing from R2 and R9, and R1’s actor cell does not state the prohibition. R20 also writes the Internal Audit reading of “critical” into the rule itself.

Case outcomes are mostly stable under the stated hierarchy, and the two evidence traps are handled well. On P-505, the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment is correctly preferred to the 18:06 ERP stamp, and the A2 failure survives either commitment time. On P-507, the 12:40 signed PDF is correctly treated as a timely authorization record despite the 14:30 ERP entry. P-502 correctly separates an ECD-2 register waiver from a still-binding pre-commitment Compliance clearance. P-503 correctly holds the EUR 140,000 approved-supplier purchase to A3. P-504 correctly stays outside both B4 and ECD-2. P-508 correctly refuses to stretch the CFO’s EUR 150,000 sentence to EUR 155,000. P-501’s B4 timing reading is a disclosed choice, and the B2 alternative in Section 5 is the right one.

The classification and justification errors are these.

P-501 is marked COMPLIANT with A1 “satisfied,” while the same row lists the Plant and Finance actors as unknown. That directly breaks the memo’s burden rule that the party asserting compliance must prove segregation. Medium confidence is attached to the B2 timing debate, so it does not repair the A1 overclaim. The row also ignores two facts the scenario isolates: the 08:42 and 08:50 acts fall inside outage ON-44, and the Plant Manager was reachable and had not delegated SHIFT-1. R11 makes that delegation failure a defeater of substitution; the memo never says whether SHIFT-1’s commitment even needed that substitution.

P-506 treats late Plant_Approve as an A2 breach cured only in consequence under A6. Assumptions 2 and 5 say the opposite: Plant_Approve is not assumed to be the independent budget-owner approval, and B2 has no express pre-commitment deadline. Finance_Approve at 06:05 was before commitment. The durable defects are B1, because ECD-2 had expired and no B4 emergency is shown, and an unproven budget-owner approval. The written justification conflates those defects. High confidence is not earned while the row still lists budget-owner identity as missing.

Smaller evidential misses accumulate. P-502 has no calendar date, yet ECD-2 is treated as satisfied at high confidence. If that purchase fell outside the window, the register and timing findings would change, even though A2 and A3 would still support NONCOMPLIANT. On P-503, the controlling view is that three-day backup stock is outside ECD-2, but the deviations cell does not then record B1. P-505 lists C3 as satisfied while the authorization record is still missing. B6 is checked only for P-501; every other emergency-path purchase is silent on the 24-hour form, and that silence is neither a deviation nor missing evidence. Section 6 defines “inability to prove compliance,” then largely converts proof gaps into policy breaches without saying when the burden rule stops.

Section 5 is real alternative analysis, not a gesture. The P-502 and P-503 flips correctly require an A7 premise, because a CFO-finance reading cannot remove new-supplier or above-EUR-100,000 clearance. The P-505 package adds the clock issue, which is unnecessary to the classification flip once A2 remains in force. Section 7 resolves authority, thresholds, scope, records, expiry, and normalization, although the proposed segregation clause quietly expands A1 by separating requester from committer and barring both from Incident Command authorization.

The grade is held here because the analytical frame, the threshold map, and the P-505/P-507 evidence rulings are strong, while P-501’s primary label and P-506’s A2 theory do not follow the memo’s own assumptions.
