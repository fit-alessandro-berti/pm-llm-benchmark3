The final recommendation is a conformant second-officer review, with alternate screening run in parallel, A and B ranked as currently nonconformant, and an explicit P-901 spillover analysis. All six required sections, the conformance labels, and the reasons A and B cannot be rescued by a favorable forecast are present and largely precise.

The score stays well short of the top because the case for interrupting P-901 is weaker than the ranking states. The €7–18k “expected deadline value” is the complement of the stated SLA-breach band times €20–35k, so on-time completion is priced as on-time execution. Capturing that value also requires approval, given as 0.55–0.75. Applying that factor to the §5 incremental gain (€3–12k) puts it in the same range as the P-901 spillover (€2–10k), so the claimed slight edge for S2-now is not established. The primary plan also caps the interruption at about 45 minutes while S2’s review is 35–75 minutes and S4 may arrive only at 65. In that late-S4 case — where the answer says S2-now is clearly best — relief cannot hold the interruption to 45 minutes, and the given +0.12–0.28 spillover (defined for 45 minutes) is then too low. Benefit and harm cells also mix probability-weighted deadline value, its complement, and unweighted regulatory tails, so the table itself does not support the near-wash claim in §5.

Smaller gaps remain. Action E is never tied to the evidence-cycle rule in §4, although every action was supposed to be mapped. A decision as late as t80 does not yield execution by t90 if recording takes the stated 5–15 minutes. Section 6 names two fallbacks. None of this overturns the bottom ranking of A and B, or the refusal to treat screening, self-certification, or a suspicious-activity report as a substitute for independent review.
