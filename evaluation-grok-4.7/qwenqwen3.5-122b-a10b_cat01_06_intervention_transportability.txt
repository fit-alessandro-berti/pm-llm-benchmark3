4.6

The final answer is structurally complete and lands on the right decision class, but it is not reliable as a transportability analysis. Under a strict reading, factual mistakes, an invalid causal graph, underived numbers, a non-executable Site B model, and internal contradictions keep it well below competent.

The prescribed skeleton is present: ten headings, the required table columns, twelve process features, five scenarios including one where the median improves and the p90 worsens, eight assumptions in the requested dash format, and a `conditional go`. The main design choice is also right: do not open Document Check for every case at registration, and pilot only the stratum that is not legally waiting on originals.

That decision is not supported with the required rigor.

**Process structure.** Site A does not have a separate legal review for high-value cases; the source says high-value cases do not. Site B is not a sequential process: Identity Check and Originals Receipt may both start after Register, and the directly-follows counts show both. “38% digital” is not in the evidence. Originals are legally required for 62%; that is not a measured digital-channel share. Several “before” cells also paste the before→after change into the before column.

**Transport graph.** `originals_required -> NOT true_overlap` is not an adjacency over the required nodes. Only one selection node is used, so site differences in role pools, the case lock, complexity, capacity, and co-interventions are not represented as selection. `API_latency -> identity_capacity` and `legal_capacity -> assessment_capacity` are the wrong mechanisms: latency is not headcount, and legal review sits after assessment and can loop back. The explanation is about 127 words against a required 180–280, and it never states edge signs or the critical-path distinction between Identity overlapping the originals wait (already lawful) and Document Check starting before originals (not lawful).

**Evidence and scenarios.** Co-interventions are named, which is necessary, but they are not disentangled. Completions rose about 8% while verification FTE rose about 11%, so labor productivity fell; that count is never made. Rework and appeals worsened slightly while the high-complexity share fell from 48% to 40%, yet the interpretation says quality was maintained and assigns transport confidence 90. The nine-hour median drop is still treated as something parallelism “did.” Scenario deltas (−15%, −6h, −10%, +5h, +16h) are not derived from identity service 3.4h, document service 8.1h, originals delay 18h, wait-before-assessment 20h, or the pool sizes 4/7/3/2. “Eligible share” means subgroup size in one row and implementation success in the IT-lock row. The directly-follows table is unused. The reasoning paragraph does not map Site A’s confounded components onto those ranges.

**Intervention, pilot, and model.** The textual “POWL” uses set notation that reads as concurrency for Approve, Reject, and Legal Review, and it adds a supplement back-edge that destroys a strict partial order instead of using a loop operator. Originals Receipt is absent from the relation set. Section 6 installs a WIP cap of six; section 9 says not to copy that cap. The pilot randomizes cases that share four identity reviewers, seven document reviewers, three assessors, and two lawyers, then offers “separate queues” without a design. A randomized contrast does not need propensity scores. The process-mining column is often not a process-mining measure, and the write-up never operationalizes the mediator that would separate true overlap from task creation, lock conflicts, and back-entry. The code quotes neither multi-word labels, invents `POWLNode` / `POWLEdge` / `RelationType`, and then sets the Site B net equal to Site A’s after-tree. Soundness and playout therefore do not run on the candidate model. The limitation comment is the only solid part of that section.

**Recommendation and assumptions.** `conditional go`, digital-or-non-originals first, is the right call, but the section is about 195 words against a required 250–400. A 10–15% median gain is asserted without a critical-path bound: hiding a 3.4h identity activity beside an 8.1h document activity cannot by itself produce that gain. A null result is blamed on downstream bottlenecks, which erases the implementation-failure contrast drawn in the pilot. The rework expansion trigger, 18%, sits on top of a 17% baseline. Several assumptions are mis-aimed: legal review does not block verification start, and “huddles won’t be needed” inverts the actual threat, which is that Site A’s result is bundled with huddles, the API change, extra FTE, and an easier mix.

The memo would point a manager away from a blind copy. It would not identify a transportable effect, a sound partial-order model, or a pilot that can tell mechanism failure from implementation failure.
