3.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and correctly handles several fully observed early cases (P01–P04) and many observed S2 service durations. Those parts do not carry the analysis. Core waiting-time arithmetic, resource constraints, station summaries, the bottleneck claim, and the Python reconstruction are inconsistent with the supplied log.

S1 waits are wrong for most of the log. Fully observed examples include P06 (40 min reported as 20), P09 (60 as 10), P11 (75 as 15), P12 (85 as 10), P14 (95 as 15), and P16–P21 (known starts turned into ranges beginning at 0, with incorrect widths). P05’s observed S1 start at 09:00 is treated as a 0 min wait. P08 is only 5 min off, which shows the errors are not a single rounding convention. Because those waits are the main evidence of S1 congestion, later conclusions do not survive.

Missing-start bounds contradict the notes in the same rows. For P05 the note correctly limits the C1 start to [09:45, 10:15], so service is at most 30 min and S2 wait is about [20, 50]; the columns say wait 50 and service 0–50. P07’s note gives an I1 start in [09:25, 10:00], hence S1 wait about [40, 75], but the column says 0–110. P11’s “55–25 min” is not a range; the note’s [12:00, 12:25] start implies wait about [55, 80]. P15 assigns the predecessor to P14, who is on I2; the previous I1 completion is P13 at 11:40, so service is not 0–15 min from 12:00. P17 is flagged as S1-start missing even though 12:20 is observed. P22 pairs a 110 min wait with the interval 12:10–13:20.

Censoring is mishandled. Blank S2 fields mean the case is still waiting at 14:00, not that service starts at 14:00 or that service time is 0. OPEN S2 on P17 is given as 5 min while P15 is given as 30+; elapsed service and final service are not kept distinct. Total sojourn is defined as S1 end minus arrival, which stops at the first station.

Station metrics do not follow from the cases. S1 completions are 20, not 14; S2 queue arrivals by 14:00 are 20, not 22. “Avg ~25 min” S1 wait and “mostly <20 min” disagree with both the true log and this table (P08 is already 55). I1 and I2 are both given as 5 h 15 min / 87.5% without a sum; I2’s fully observed busy time is 290 min. C1’s 4 h 50 min is not 81.7% of six hours. Coverage is called “Full” despite missing starts, OPEN cases, and right censoring. Rounding of ±2.5 min is assumed and then never applied. No tie convention is stated for simultaneous rounded events.

The bottleneck section therefore overclaims. Growing S1 waits of roughly 5 min early to about 100 min later show S1 itself is congested; S2 waits also grow and S2 service is longer. The evidence fits a time-varying combination, which the prompt asked to consider. The C2 outage account is factually off: at 10:25 C2 is still in P06 until 10:50, so P08 could not have started at 10:25 absent maintenance. Section 6 names optimistic, central, and pessimistic imputations but never computes them, then calls the S2-only bottleneck “stable.”

The code does not implement the stated method. P05’s S1 start is entered as MISSING. MISSING, OPEN, and blank all become None. Interval timestamps are strings such as `"08:00 08:05"`. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are never called. WIP drops post-S1 cases with no S2 end and counts cases before arrival. Little’s Law multiplies an S1-only sojourn by end-to-end completions over a nonstationary six-hour window. No imputation scenarios are calculated.
