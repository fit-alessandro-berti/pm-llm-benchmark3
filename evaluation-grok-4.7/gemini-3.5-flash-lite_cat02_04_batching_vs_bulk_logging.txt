The write-up follows the seven-heading format and gets the coarse story right for G1 (fixture cycle), G6 (bulk stamp with zero duration), G7 (quarter-hour censoring, including a negative wait), and the G8 serial drain after a blocked interval. Those parts do not carry the rest. Several diagnoses, bias directions, and the required PM4Py comparison are wrong or missing.

**Diagnoses.** G3 is ranked backward. Staggered starts, one shared `11:30` complete, one shared `11:31` insert, consecutive `sequence_no`, and the folder-submission note are an end-batch / bulk-complete signature. Overlap is produced by that shared end. Putting concurrent multitasking first, at 75, treats the artifact as the execution. G2’s second place (genuine simultaneous batch) fights the table’s own `physical_concurrency_plausible = No` and the ~6–7 minute completion cadence, which is serial work under a group start. G4’s second place (simultaneous batch) is incompatible with a zero-overlap chain. G7’s second place (“legacy system rounding”) restates the first explanation. G8’s second place (start batching) does not match unequal, chained starts. G6’s second place (capacity-loss queue) would look like G8, with positive service, not `start = complete`.

**Evidence column.** Counterevidence is inverted on three rows. For G5, the shared id is support; the unused counterevidence is four-way overlap against a stated cap of three analysts, plus 1-minute censoring. For G7, finer queue arrivals and starts before later arrivals support coarsening; serial `recorded_at` values one minute apart (14:16 through 14:20) further separate it from G6’s single insert. For G8, Q01 at 15:38 near a 15:35 clearance is support; the supplied counterevidence is that the safety clock and the scanner clock differ, so 85 confidence overclaims the cause.

**Performance bias.** Directions are not reliable. G1 arrival-to-start waits are 6, 5, 4, and 2 minutes; that does not underestimate wait, and summing four concurrent 3-minute services overstates load (about 400% in a 3-minute window) rather than understating it. G2’s naive service is mildly long for S01 (8 minutes) and badly long for S04 (27 minutes); the text says earlier cases are the inflated ones. G3’s overlapping intervals do not “look like sequential single-tasking.” G4’s resource gap is zero, but case waiting time is not low: about 7 minutes for P01 and about 19 minutes for P05. G6 is the one clean bias account: multi-hour arrival-to-stamp waits plus a zero service. The arrival window is misstated as ending at 16:02; C07 arrives at 16:25, so the short wait is about 35 minutes, not “1 to 3 hours.”

**Signatures.** The matrix is generic and slips. Pure end-batching is coded as unequal starts, so it misses the G6 point-stamp where start and complete are both the submit time. The coarsening row says a smaller tolerance makes the artifact disappear; equal coarse timestamps stay equal under a tighter merge distance. That cell confuses detection tolerance with a finer source clock.

**Code versus the spec.** The frame keeps every cluster, calls `format_dataframe` and `discover_batches` on a 2×2 grid, and prints start/complete cardinality, mean record lag, and max queue wait. It does not compute overlap rate, observed granularity versus stated precision, or minimum wait (so G7’s negative waits disappear behind the max). It prints only a batch count inside a blanket `try/except`, never the PM4Py category. There is no comparison. That misses the main methodological result: identical start and complete would label G1, G6, and G7 all as simultaneous batches even though only G1 has a physical multi-item cycle. G5’s partial overlap is likely invisible to those four batch types. `iloc0` is not a pandas attribute; the fallback hides it. `io` is unused.

**Robustness and adjudication.** Resource-id sensitivity is only G5, and timestamp censoring is only G7. The “three cases of one pattern, several mechanisms” request is not met: G1, G2, and G6 are different signatures, and G4 is a single ambiguity. Missing same-pattern splits include G1 (true fixture cycle versus one PLC cycle time copied onto four positions), G7 (rounding versus a real quarter-hour batch), and G5 (three workers versus one multitasker, which still cannot honestly cover a four-way overlap). Section 7 is more cautious on G3 than the table, but it never interval-censors G7, never states G6 as a high-confidence logging batch that must also be dropped from queue KPIs, and treats “interval-censored” as the remedy for a shared login.

Section 6 does list every requested collection control, but the order is an unranked catalog. Coverage of the outline is real; the classifications, bias signs, and batch-discovery comparison are not stable enough to use.
