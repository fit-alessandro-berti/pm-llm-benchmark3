# Capacity and bottlenecks

Offered load is treated as case counts. A 2-hour block never becomes resource-minutes, so the utilization figures (95%, 110%, 120%) have no auditable path from service ranges, multipliers, shifts, or breaks.

The only fully written blocks are arithmetically wrong. For 08:00–10:00, ES is 10% of 7 = 0.7, while the table uses 0.84. Electrical load is written as E + ES only. ME also requires an electrical branch, so that block is about 3.85 electrical activities, not 2.59. The 10:00–12:00 electrical figure, 3.375, has the same omission (correct count is about 5.4) and an unexplained 1.125. The remaining blocks are ellipses. Assumption A3’s “5–10% handover uplift” is unlabeled guesswork.

Shift calendars are misread. Chen ends at 16:00, yet the base write-up makes Chen a 16:00–18:00 software bottleneck. After 18:00 the only on-shift person is Emil, who has no mechanical skill; Deepa’s overtime ends at 18:30. M/ME work in the last block has no qualified resource, which is the sharp P1 exposure, and it is absent from the bottleneck list. Intake is the smallest work pool (~7 minutes before multipliers), yet Ben/Chen intake at ~95% is called the morning bottleneck. Electrical, software, and validation dominate person-minutes; Farah’s 08:00–12:00 cross-skill capacity is ignored. U1–U5 “peaks” are asserted, with no renormalized U2 mix, no doubled diagnostic means for U4, and no 09:30–10:00 intake blackout for U5.

A day-level bound makes the table incoherent: base expected work at multiplier 1.0 is already on the order of the staffed minutes after breaks (~2,500). Multipliers above 1, handover, setup, and the 8-minute coordination add more. Sustained utilization above 100% with mean delays of 110–180 minutes and P90 delays of 200–400 minutes is not reconciled with a finite-day backlog or an unstable queue.

# Assumptions and objectives

The triangular specification is impossible as written: mode at the minimum and mean at the midpoint cannot hold together. The code then sets the mode at the midpoint, so the prose and the generator disagree. Multiplier direction is unstable. The problem defines 1.25 as 25% slower, but Policy 1 calls Ben (1.10) a fastest intake resource ahead of Ada/Deepa/Farah at 1.00, and the SERT score divides by the multiplier, which ranks slower resources as better.

Assumption A1 does not match the control flow. Sequential ME/ES execution needs one person who holds both skills; parallelism needs two qualified people and costs 8 minutes. “Sequential unless a cross-skilled resource is available” inverts that. Parallel versus sequential cycle time is never quantified (coordination of 8 minutes versus adding a whole second branch to the critical path).
