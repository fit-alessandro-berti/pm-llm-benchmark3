The write-up matches the required section skeleton and gets many fully observed service durations right, but the reconstruction is not usable. Waiting times, missing-start bounds, station counts, the bottleneck story, and the Python are wrong in ways the prompt specifically warned against.

Case arithmetic fails on ordinary, fully observed rows. S1 wait is arrival to start, and S2 wait is S1 completion to S2 start. The table instead reports 0 for P04, P05, P08, P09, P10, P13, and P14, whose observed S1 waits are about 20, 35, 50, 60, 70, 80, and 95 minutes. The same error hits S2: P03 is 10 not 5, P04 is 15 not 5, P06 is 25 not 15, P08 is 55 not 65, P09 is 25 not 5, P10 is 50 not 15, P13 is 50 not 15, and P14 is 65 not 5. P13’s S2 service is 50, not 45. Several notes treat a short resource gap as a zero customer wait (“I1/I2 available immediately”), which is the wrong clock. P17’s S1 “wait” of 125 equals end minus arrival, so service has been folded into waiting. P18’s note gives P17’s arrival.

Missing-start bounds are infeasible and internally inconsistent. A contiguous single-resource interval cannot overlap other work on that resource. P07 on I1 cannot start in [08:45, 09:00]: I1 is in P03 until 08:55 and P05 from 09:00 to 09:25, while P07 is recorded as ending at 10:00. The feasible start is after 09:25, so the wait is roughly [40, 75) minutes and the service is at most about 35 minutes, not a 60-minute service with a 0–15 minute wait. Section 1’s interval [08:45, 10:00] also disagrees with the table. P05’s S2 start is placed at [10:15, 10:35] even though S2 ends at 10:15, the service is given as 60 minutes, and the note frees C1 “after P04,” which was on C2. P11’s S1 is fully observed (10:40–11:05, wait 75), yet it is described as a missing S1 start; the S2 window [11:45, 12:05] overlaps P08 on C2. P15’s observed S2 start is 13:30, so S2 wait is 75 minutes, not [0, 30], and the S1 wait, service, and stated start window do not add up. Section 2 swaps the service bounds: an earlier feasible start lengthens service.

Censoring is mishandled. P21 is in service from 13:30, with elapsed service 30 minutes and final service only bounded below; the note calls that waiting. P16 and P18–P20 are waiting for S2, so S2 wait is right-censored (for P16, at least 85 minutes), not a dash. P22’s S1 wait is at least 110 minutes. The 10-minute residual that caps P15 at 40 minutes is not justified by the observed S2 durations. Rounding of ±2.5 minutes is named and then never applied.

Station metrics do not match the log. In 08:00–10:00, S1 has on the order of 13 arrivals and about 6–7 completions, not 8 and 8; S2 has about 3 completions by 10:00, not 8. The 12:00–14:00 row (3 S1 arrivals, 2 completions, WIP about 1.5) inverts the backlog: many S1 completions fall in that window, and both S2 servers are busy at 14:00 while P16, P18, P19, and P20 are still queued. Utilization “falling because of OPEN cases” treats censoring as idleness. Busy-time, WIP “±” bands, and the sensitivity deltas (WIP 1.5 vs 3.5, utilization 95% vs 60%) are not derived. The claim that S1 utilization is stably above 75% contradicts the table’s own 38–50% last interval. The outage is 10:55–11:10; it does not make P06 wait, and it is not a 15-minute delay for P06 and P08 or a one-case WIP increase shown from the data. A real anomaly is missed: after 13:50, C1 takes P17 while earlier S2 arrival P16 is still waiting.

The code does not implement the method. `s2_start`, `s2_end`, and `s2_resource` have 23 entries for 22 cases, so the frame cannot be built; P17’s S2 stamp is shifted onto P18, and P21’s S1 start is coded as `MISSING` instead of 13:30. `to_datetime(..., errors='coerce')` maps MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same missing time before any status logic, and the later `== 'MISSING'` checks are dead. Per-station `diff()` on one row per case does not measure queue time. The Little’s Law routine counts starts, calls that count WIP, compares 2026-05-12 with datetimes that have no date, and the printed “error 0.1” contradicts 0.8×25=20 versus WIP 8. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are not called, and `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is neither used nor excused in a comment. The “key outputs” were not produced by this script.

Format compliance and a subset of observed service lengths are the only solid parts. The quantitative picture, uncertainty ranges, bottleneck timing, and reproducibility claims do not hold.
