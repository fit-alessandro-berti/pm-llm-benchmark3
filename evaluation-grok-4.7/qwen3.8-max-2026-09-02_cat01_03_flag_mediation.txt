7.1

The final write-up is complete, numerically careful, and appropriately cautious: stage medians match the timestamps, absent stages are not zero-filled, the flag is treated as a policy bundle, and occurrence, waiting, escalation, and interference are kept apart. Natural effects are correctly set aside.

It is not nearly flawless. `risk_score` reaches review, escalation, and the decision only through `flag`, so P1 is missing from the graph, and `U_underlying_risk → complexity` is an unjustified direction. Flag and triage resource are perfectly separated (flagged cases are only T1/T2; unflagged only T3/T4); calling that a mild clustering understates the main obstacle to a priority-lane effect. E3 treats P0 versus P2 as an indirect effect through review occurrence even though that policy also changes queue waits, and E4 is marked well-defined while targeting an “unflagged-like” wait that is selected and interference-dependent. “Rejects concentrated in high-risk escalated cases” is wrong: all four rejects are high-complexity escalations, but R09 and R20 have moderate scores and are unflagged. The pilot excludes a pre-review “senior-escalation hold” that this process does not have, and it drops the two highest-score flagged cases that drive the longest cycles. The code is substantively aligned, but `timedelta` is unused and sorting performance-DFG values will break if PM4Py returns metric dictionaries.
