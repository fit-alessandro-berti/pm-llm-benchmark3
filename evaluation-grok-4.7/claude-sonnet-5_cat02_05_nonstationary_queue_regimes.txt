6.2

The write-up follows the required eight-part structure and gets the main operating story directionally right: baseline, a one-day outage, a transitional recovery, a mix-shift backlog build, and an incomplete staffing drawdown. Several headline totals check out (2519 arrivals, 2409 completions, gap 110, complex-percent sum 920, mix-shift 892 vs 786 and deficit 106, outage deficit 47, recovery surplus 30, intervention-weekday surplus 18). Outage recovery is correctly kept out of any steady high-throughput regime, and the pooled “about 4% short” staffing-cut policy is a real aggregation trap. Those strengths do not offset the calculation and interpretation failures below.

Baseline numbers are not one cohort. Weekdays from 2026-04-06 through 2026-04-14 are seven days, arrivals 92–107, mean 100.1, completions mean 99.6. The stated 96–107 and mean 101.5, and completions mean 100.3, are exactly the six-day subset that drops Friday 2026-04-10 (92 arrivals, 5.5 FTE). That exclusion is never declared, while the same table labels the regime as all those weekdays at “6.0 FTE.” Weekends inside that boundary are two days (means 40 and 39.5), not “n=4,” mean 41.8 and 41.5. Those four-day figures appear only if 2026-04-18 and 2026-04-19 are folded in — the same days given a separate bridge regime. Section 6 then uses a third, undocumented variant: Friday dropped, and arrival and completion means averaged to λ ≈ 100.9/day and L ≈ 20.3. The code defines yet another cohort (all days on or before 2026-04-14; weekend n=2; throughput = completions only) and would not print the narrative figures. The same split manufactures the Section 5 dispersion contrast: all seven baseline weekdays have Fano ≈ 0.26 and CV ≈ 5%, essentially the same as mix-shift weekdays (Fano ≈ 0.29, CV ≈ 5%). The claimed baseline Fano 0.15–0.20 and CV ≈ 4% is the Friday-deleted sample.

Other factual misses are material. Mix-shift weekday WIP is 37, 41, 46, 44, 43, 49, 50 — an upward drift with a mid-window dip, not a monotonic climb. Median handling goes from about 24–26 to 34–36 minutes (~40%, as Section 4 later says); “service time and p90 nearly double” overstates the median. Pooled weekday arrivals are not “102–121”; several weekdays are in the low-to-mid 90s. The intervention surplus is +5, +6, and +7 cases on the three weekdays, not “+6 to +10/day.” The Section 4 pieces 106 + 47 − 30 − 18 = 105, not the pooled gap of 110; the missing baseline deficit is small but the reconciliation is incomplete. Mean WIP is treated like a closing stock: on 2026-04-16, completions exceed arrivals by 17 while mean WIP rises from 47 to 52, which is informative about intraday averaging and is never discussed.

The outage service reading contradicts the audit. Section 1 correctly says median and p90 are active handling, excluding wait. Section 2 then calls the outage-day move to 31 / 68 “queue inflation, not true handling change.” Given the stated definition, that move is a change in measured handling or in which cases were completed. Selection during a three-hour zero-completion window is the live hypothesis; queue wait is not in those two columns.

The hourly evidence is misused. The four vectors sum to the daily totals, so they are partitions of those days. For 2026-04-21, arrivals in 08:00–09:59 are 3+8=11 (about 10%), against `peak_08_10_pct` of 47. The mass is at 10–11 (34), 13–14 (24), and also 16–17 (14). Calling that profile bimodal and “consistent with” the 08:00–10:00 share rising to ~45–50% is not supported. The same mismatch exists on the ordinary weekday (5+12=17 of 101, against 31%). That conflict belongs in the data audit. Separately, “96/day” snapshots are stated as fact and imply 24-hour sampling; threat 2 then says overnight coverage is unknown.

Little’s Law judgments are mostly the right verdicts (outage, recovery, growing mix-shift, and the full pool are not steady-state applications). The required window, units, and boundary story is still thin: W ≈ 2.4 h is not tied to a reproducible cohort, and it is unclear whether that figure is open-hours time or clock time when WIP may sit idle overnight. Capacity pressure stays qualitative. Load per FTE is nearly the same on baseline weekdays and weekends (~16 arrivals per FTE-day) while weekend handling medians are higher, so absolute WIP makes weekends look quieter than the per-server load does. No bound is placed on mean handling given only a median and a p90, and multi-step occupancy (a case in WIP between steps without consuming an FTE) is named and then left unused. The alternative “continuous” mix tiers ignore a hard gap: after 27% the next values are 39–47%, so the 25–35% bin is essentially the outage and recovery days, not a middle operating regime.

The code block does meet the mechanical checklist (in-memory tables, pooled and regime summaries, Fano and CV, lag-1 and lag-7, a clearly non-definitive z-score rule, Little’s Law only on named windows, and a PM4Py comment that these rows are not an event log). Because its labels disagree with the prose statistics, it does not make the document reproducible. Arrow and relation characters are also missing throughout (`04-0604-14`, `5235`, `3750`, `324336`), so several table cells are not readable as written.
