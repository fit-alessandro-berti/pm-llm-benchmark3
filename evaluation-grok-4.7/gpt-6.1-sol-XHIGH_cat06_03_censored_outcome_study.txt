8.3

The design is statistically serious and gets the central point right: `default_90d` is an account outcome under observed activation, rejection is not a non-default, and the 21,600 labeled rows are a selected, maturity-filtered subset. The six-way pathway estimand, the refusal to inverse-weight rejections into counterfactual defaults, the Manski bounds \([(1-r)p,(1-r)p+r]\), the 215-day maturity arithmetic (15 July 2025, 2 October 2025, 18 May 2026), and the leakage table are all sound. The main fixed-prefix feature path is built from a whitelist and checked against post-decision activities.

The score stops there because the skeleton and a few claims are less careful than the prose.

The target column admits fast feeds. `outcomes_at` writes `activated_default_90d` / `activated_nondefault_90d` as soon as `label_available_at <= cut` and the 90-day horizon has ended. The 35-day allowance is only a side flag (`mark_window_complete`). That contradicts the stated rule that early labels stay out of the conservative mark-training risk set. Fitting on `target` and dropping pending rows recreates the fast-feed selection the text forbids. `labels_at` also hard-asserts that every official label timestamp is at or after activation+90, so a real early default feed aborts the pipeline instead of being handled.

`elapsed_prefix` is not as-of safe. It keeps events through `start + horizon` whenever that instant is before 31 December 2025, with no split knowledge cutoff. A December 2024 training case therefore contributes January 2025 events. The frame is unused, but the comment tells the reader to reuse it. Eligibility also depends on full-log `bad_path`, so a future contradictory exit drops the case from earlier landmarks.

Other defects are smaller but real. “Cause-specific hazard” is used for transient states (pending review, acceptance, signing); those are multi-state intensities, and the displayed integral marginalizes post-landmark path covariates only in prose. A random split does not itself over-represent activated cases; complete-case labeling does. `review_returns = count(Credit Review) - 1` is the proxy section 5 says is not a completed loop. `discover_dfg` runs on length-1 prefixes, which have no directly-follows relations. `start_month` drops year. Exact-row duplicate handling, pandas-versus-PM4Py tie order, and `assert` on bad paths (the bad-path count is then unreachable) are weaker than the audit the text promises. `policy_new` is constant on every training and validation start landmark, so it does not identify the July 2025 shift the validation section is built around.

Section-level design is strong enough that these are implementation and wording failures, not a wrong estimand. They are still too many for a near-flawless score.
