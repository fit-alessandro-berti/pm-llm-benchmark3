The benefit argument contradicts itself. A 10% enforcement probability is said to produce €10k–€50k and to already eclipse the €20k–€35k operational gain, while the same section’s break-even band is about 0.04–0.35. At the low end, €10k does not eclipse €20k–€35k. The 0.05×€300k “regulatory” credit for screening also misstates risk that a proper second review is already meant to control.

Time bounds do not agree. Rank 1 is given as 75–95 minutes on the fast path and 55–120 with the S2 fallback; the continuation trace says about 75–115; section 5 then prints impossible totals, \(35+35+1080\) minutes and \(65+75+15155\) minutes. The 55-minute fallback floor is also too low if interruption waits until 25–30 minutes and review still takes at least 35.

Both forfeit the deadline value; rejection is only conditionally conformant, and the claim that it limits customer harm relative to a hold is not supported by the shared €15k–€80k range.
