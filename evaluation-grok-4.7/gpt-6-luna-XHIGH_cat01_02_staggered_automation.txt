7.5

The answer is careful, well structured, and right on the central conclusion: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not defensible. Availability is separated from uptake, rollout is treated as endogenous, median and p90 are not collapsed, and the recommendation (phased expansion with a real comparison group, spillover checks, and stop rules) fits the decision problem. Most of the headline arithmetic checks out.

The deductions are for concrete slips and for quantitative choices that soften the result.

Section 1 says FTE changed in three branches. Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta all changed; Delta’s June gain is a control-group confounder. Section 4 repeats that omission, while section 6 includes Delta. The pre-trend sentence says Gamma improves before rollout. It does not: Gamma’s median rises through May–June and falls only after July activation.

The main pre/post table is arithmetically accurate for the windows chosen (Alpha median +1.53 h / +3.1%, p90 +11.47 h; Beta +2.53 h / +5.5% and +10.03 h; Gamma −6.47 h / −14.8% and −9.27 h; early median DiD about −4.2 h; Gamma median DiD about −3.8 h). Those windows still distort the story. Jan–Mar mixes pre- and post-fraud-desk months, and Apr–Jun stops before Alpha/Beta’s highest use and before Alpha’s August median of 43.3 h. Over Apr–Aug, Alpha’s average median is roughly flat versus Jan–Mar, not +1.5 h. Jul–Aug improvement is largely shared with Delta and Epsilon; that parallel rebound is mentioned only qualitatively. Arrivals per FTE are never computed, even though Alpha’s early-window load rises on the order of 30% versus roughly 13% in the controls, which changes how a small median increase should be read. The rework “decline” is also window-dependent: Beta’s May–August rate is about 12.7 versus a 12.8 baseline, and Gamma’s 12.6 is still above its Jan–April 12.3. The sensitivity span labeled “trend-adjusted” is a raw change versus a two-period difference-in-differences, not a trend model, and the stated rework comparison of about −1.7 to −2.2 pp is not reproducible from the same contrasts (they are nearer −2.0 and −2.1).

The code validates the panel, prints separate outcome paths, builds relative-time indicators, and labels the regressions as descriptive. It then features the pooled two-way fixed-effects specification the prose warns against, bins Gamma’s early leads (−6, −5, −4) into the reference, and reports cluster-robust standard errors for models with more parameters than the five clusters, so the covariance is rank-deficient. The printed “support” counts are restricted to `automation==1`. `warnings` is imported and unused.

Estimands, mechanisms, and the threat list are substantively strong. The “conditionally causal” rung is a claim about a future design, not a conditional effect in these data.
