7.1

The decision-set reconstructions are largely accurate and the right way to do this problem, but the headline summaries contradict those tables often enough that the diagnosis is not reliable as written.

The per-decision comparisons for QA–QD are the strongest part. Eligible sets, remainders (B01 at 31 then 14), the est = realized identity on all 32 jobs, C05’s 10:54 start after 10:50, the 34-minute C07 miss, D-F’s 11:36–12:00 idle while D10 fits, and the rule hit rates (QB two-class 6/7, QC SPT 3/6, QD FIFO 3/5) all check out. Observational equivalence is handled seriously (SRPT vs p1 at 09:16, SPT vs feasible-EDD at 10:18, FIFO vs appointment at 11:30, hold vs late availability of D-X). Confidence is appropriately lowest on QC, PM4Py is correctly declined, and the data-collection list is tied to real ambiguities. Hypotheses are usually labeled.

Those merits are undercut by summary claims that the same document falsifies.

The synthesis says five of six QA decisions are predicted only by FIFO. That is false. Unique FIFO hits are 08:32 and 09:05. 09:20 is also appointment, 09:32 is also SPT, and 08:58 is every rule. “FIFO 5/6” counts consistency, then section 7 upgrades it to uniqueness. The QA identifiability cell is also wrong: dropping A04 and A08 does not turn those wins into ties. Without them, 08:32 is still A02 against shorter A06, and 09:05 is still A05 against shorter A07.

The QC cell says priority and deadline are perfectly correlated and that SPT, EDD, slack, and priority therefore make the same or unfalsifiable predictions on most decisions. Neither claim is true. Priority is only a coarsening (shared p2 and p3 classes hide different deadlines), and the section 2 table shows the rules diverging on most of the six decisions. The real QC problem is that each rule fails different rows and that est = realized collapses SPT into SEPT, which section 5 states correctly and the diagnosis table does not.

Smaller faults accumulate. “Six discriminating decisions” in QA includes non-discriminating 08:58. A06’s five-minute lateness is treated as appointment tolerance even though A1 was busy. A07 at 09:05 ends 09:14, one minute before 09:15, not “exactly.” C05 is called infeasible from arrival using the scan time 10:11+40, while the stated two-minute scan lag can make a 10:09 arrival feasible, and the 10:10 row already marks C05 as possibly present. The “14” p1 bypasses do not match the episode list: D08 is “×2” while both D08 and D10 are passed over at 11:30, and 14 requires counting uncertain C05-at-10:10 as certain. The cross-queue scorecard gives B05 at 10:00 an “alternative reading,” but section 2 correctly says that choice rules SRPT out; among the stated rules, priority is unique there. The 10-minute appointment window is an unlabeled parameter. The script does not reproduce the idle rows, feasible-EDD, or “possibly present” conditionals, despite the claim that it reproduces every row. “Not before” is asserted for QA even though A1 was never idle before an appointment; that behavior is observed on D-X, not on A1.

QD’s skill-filter-plus-FIFO reading is directionally right, but D05 at 11:21 would have finished at 11:51, before the meeting, so the scarce-skill failure is sharper than “non-discriminating” suggests, and the FIFO cell at 11:36 silently drops D05. Ranking QD as more identifiable than QB, at 65 vs 60, overweights constraint compliance and two confounded choices relative to QB’s repeated two-class pattern.

The underlying scheduler reconstruction would support a high score. The grade is held down because the diagnosis table and final synthesis misstate uniqueness, correlation, and what survives the readiness caveat.
