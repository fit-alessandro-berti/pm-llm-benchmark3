8.5

The analysis is rigorous, arithmetically careful, and properly separates a measurement collapse from a residual compliance question. It still over-discounts its own best evidence and stops short of one impossibility result that its method already implies.

The variant arithmetic is right: April–May is an exact fit of the tree (600/600), raw June–July is 0/650, label mapping alone is 90/650, and mapping plus removal of `Queue Routed` is 535/650. Queue counts (560; 530 directly after `Risk Screen`), the 25/170 and 30/270 missing-check rates, 210 sign-offs, and 3,975 events (6.12 per June–July case versus the reported 6.3) all check out. Alignment semantics are handled correctly: a missing `Compliance Check` is a model move, a `Notify`/`Decision` swap has alternative optimal repairs, and a `Decision` model move does not mean no decision occurred. Employee blame is properly resisted. The August policy is not treated as misconduct, and the optional sign-off model is correctly flagged as unable to enforce the EUR 50,000 rule. The staged program isolates provenance, label/queue/order, policy versus permissive replay, and residual audit. The governance rules cover the six required controls. The code uses the required PM4Py entry points, does not invent source sequences, and the reported 300/620 = 48.39% and 510/620 = 82.26% language-fit rates are correct. Section word counts and the required nodes are satisfied.

The main analytical miss is August. After the same label map and queue removal, normative language fit is 48.4%. Repairing all 45 reversed `Notify`/`Decision` pairs can raise that only to 345/620 = 55.6%, because 210 sign-offs, 35 overrides, and 30 missing checks remain illegal. The reported 66% is therefore impossible as an exact-fit rate on this table, not merely “unreconciled.” That ceiling also changes the interpretation of 66 → 86: sign-off, not leftover June artifacts, dominates the cleaned August gap. By contrast, June’s 81% versus a calculated 82.3% is nearly confirmatory, and 89% is still below the 93.1% ceiling if every reversal were repaired. Rating those staged results uniformly at reliability 65, and calling the percentage-point changes unusable, flattens a result the variant table actually supports.

Smaller faults: the jump in events missing a resource, from 1.2% to 5.9%, is not what 560 resourceless `Queue Routed` events would produce (that would be on the order of 15%), nor what fully resourced technical events would produce (dilution below 1.2%). The evidence row does not stress-test that. Timestamp provenance did change on 15 June, but order reversal is corroborated for only 11 of 70 cases, so folding “unreliable ordering” into the primary cause is a step stronger than the order evidence. The counterfactual says optional sign-off precision may fall; the later reconstruction says it rose, with a plausible explanation left outside the table. The same 35% is used both for contribution to the measured drop and for the chance that the 55 missing checks are real bypasses. `ETL_change → event_completeness` is not established by the chronology given. `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` may not be a public name, and the parse-then-`convert_to_event_log` round trip can reorder events if timestamps tie, with no assertion that reconstructed variants match the input strings.

No hard arithmetic error, no employee-blaming, and no overpermissive deletion of `Compliance Check` turned up. The score is held down by the missed August ceiling and the overly global rejection of the staged percentages.
