6.2

The framework is serious and mostly well aimed, but the case holdings do not follow the interpretation the answer itself adopts. That break sits in the part of the task that has to be reliable.

The stated rule is that, inside the ECD-2 window and at or under EUR 150,000, joint Incident Command authorization can stand in for Finance and Plant operational approval, while it does not make Incident Command members independent budget owners, does not waive segregation of duties, and does not waive pre-commitment Compliance clearance. Later approval mitigates and does not cure, and missing proof of an exception counts against compliance. Applied evenly, every in-window purchase of at least EUR 50,000 still needs a pre-commitment independent budget owner, and P-505 cannot be COMPLIANT.

The case table then splits that rule. P-502 and P-505 treat the Incident Command authorization as satisfying the finance side. P-507, with the same joint authorization, the same window, and EUR 110,000, treats the finance side as unmet and says the authorized proxy is still required. P-508 correctly keeps the EUR 150,000 ceiling, then lists missing Plant approval as a deviation, while P-507 omits Plant approval on the same facts. P-505 both lists post-commitment Finance as a deviation and classifies the case COMPLIANT because “A6 mitigation” is available. That uses A6 as a cure after the answer has defined A6 as non-curative. P-505 also counts R-A3 as satisfied for an EUR 88,000 purchase from an approved supplier, although A3 is triggered only above EUR 100,000 or for a new supplier. The committer is unnamed, so segregation of duties is unverified, and the missing budget-owner record is noted and then ignored. Under the answer’s own burden rule, that row is NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE.

Two evidential points are mishandled. P-502 has no calendar date. The CFO email exists only from 2026-07-15 07:20, so applying D1 to P-502 with high confidence, and omitting that uncertainty from missing evidence, skips a time-of-act question the method promised to ask. P-505 is 18 July. The nine-minute-fast ERP clock is a fact of that case; the procurement outage was 14 July. The timing conclusion can rest on the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgement, but the answer credits the wrong evidential mechanism.

Section 5 does not consistently do what it claims. The P-503 alternative changes only the reading of “critical,” then concedes that segregation of duties, Finance, and Compliance still fail. That premise does not change the primary classification. The P-507 “alternative” — that D1 covers Finance approval up to EUR 150,000 — is already assumption 4, so it is not an alternative to the stated method.

The hierarchy, the refusal to treat the COO/Incident Commander directive as a Chief Risk Officer exception, the narrow reading of criticality, the cafeteria exclusion, the rejection of an unwritten overnight extension, the preference for the signed PDF and supplier acknowledgement over late ERP stamps, and the draft clause in section 7 are defensible. Most NONCOMPLIANT labels would survive a corrected rationale. The catalogue and pseudo-logic already contain the same ambiguity: line 2 waives both halves of the EUR 50,000 rule under D1 and, in the same line, says Incident Command members are not budget owners. P-506’s “R-D residual” citation and the compressed “Commit after Finance but Plant after” cell are additional failures of precision, as is the missed post-expiry purchase-order sequence.

The required apparatus is present and often acute. The operative classifications and their justifications are not stable enough for a higher score.
