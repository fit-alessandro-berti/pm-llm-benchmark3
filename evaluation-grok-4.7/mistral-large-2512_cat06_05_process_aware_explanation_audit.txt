5.5

The audit correctly catches the obvious complete-trace leakages (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`) and several real lineage bugs (extraction-date prior-claim SQL, nightly document snapshot, untimestamped `risk_score_current`, end-of-case role). Case X’s narrative avoids appeal and decision outcomes, region is kept as a fairness-sensitive proxy rather than deleted, and all five required sections exist. That is not enough to carry the audit. Several findings reverse the process clock, contradict other sections, or invent case evidence.

The sequence audit is the largest failure. `t0` is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Evidence cycles start after that, so `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is future and unobserved in these prefixes. Marking it Valid, “before `t0`,” and safe to retain is false, and it contradicts the correct leakage call on `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`. `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is process-feasible and future-possible, but it is not an observed prefix signal; retaining it as valid collapses that distinction. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is only separated by a finding-type label. The required point is that the edge is normatively legitimate after Partial or Deny and is inadmissible solely because both activities are after `t0`, whereas `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is post-`t0` and order-infeasible or same-timestamp corruption. Both are simply “remove.”

Coverage is incomplete. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` never appear in the leakage table. `initial_reserve` has no lineage at all, yet Case Y treats €25,000 as a confirmed complexity driver. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is correctly called unusable until the SQL uses `< t0`, then reused in Case Y as a credible driver. Case Y also drops `queue_length_at_t0 = 4`, a legitimate negative congestion signal. Case X drops broker channel without listing it as removed or repaired. Case Z’s recompute list adds `queue_length_at_t0`, which is not in that case, and the ambiguity list omits the same-day document snapshot that is in the case.

Other defects compound this. `total_case_duration_hours` is not merely unavailable early; it is nearly the 10-day breach label. At this `t0`, a repaired prefix variant and a repaired document-request count are almost constant, so they are not early-warning replacements. Original SHAP directions are reused as if they survive feature deletion. The same-timestamp test counts events timestamped at `t0`, which would miss two later events sharing a timestamp with a missing sequence index. Queue length’s ±5-minute join and the non-retrospective gap for handler team are smoothed over. Narratives restate mechanisms the audit itself says cannot be claimed: repair-network coverage, socioeconomic status, and regional processing differences. Drift monitoring is ranked low even though week and policy/holiday confounding is one of the stated problems. The recommendations list the right topics, but they do not correct these reasoning errors.
