4.5

The write-up keeps the required sectioning, preserves final compliance after assessment and the four-eyes split, and correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a pure time cut. Those strengths do not offset broken temporal claims.

Every case’s cycle-time range contradicts its own derivation. K-104 is given as −10 to −24 h in the table, while best/base/worst is only 0–3 h of shorter review; −24 h is also infeasible because assessment ends Tue 19:10 and the factual close is Wed 18:40, so residual compliance and decision time cannot be removed entirely. K-219 is ±2 to +8 h in the table, about 19 h earlier in the derivation, and +24 h in the worst case. K-337 is −48 to −72 h in the table, but a Tue 12:00 close versus Wed 09:10 is only about 21 h. K-411 is −4 to −8 h in the table and in the synthesis (“clear cycle-time gains”), yet the derivation’s base case leaves assessment at Wed 09:30 and saves no cycle time. A reader cannot tell which prediction is intended.

Queue and gate constraints are missed where they change the result. D2 still had about 5 h of queue at Mon 16:00, so K-219 does not start at Mon 17:30. M1’s Wed morning decision can bind K-219 even if documents finish earlier; earlier readiness is not earlier completion. That distinction is noticed for K-411 and then abandoned. K-104’s “minimal” intervention places preparation on Wednesday morning, after identity and documents, despite the intervention allowing preparation during those checks, and the reserved-slot story is never reconciled with C1’s multi-day backlog.

The feasibility classification is inconsistent: K-219 is labeled both “timing only” and a routing change, and K-411 is called timing-only although it changes the control-flow from a case lock to true concurrency. The partial order collapses all four cases toward one generic sequence and omits originals, rework, escalation, and four-eyes. The K-337 path is an ellipsis, not an explicit path. Rejection is stated as the base outcome without a reason to prefer it to approval with conditions.

The 100-case discussion is short of the required 250–400 words (about 220) and the synthesis is short of 220–320 (about 190). Weekend and external-response effects, and timestamp integrity versus true concurrency, are required and largely absent. The capacity table calls the D2 swap system-positive while the prose says rework can erase the gain, and it marks K-337 as affecting no other cases even though skipped loops free D1, A1, and C1. Assumption 8 (idle assessment capacity) contradicts the congestion claim; assumption 5 (D2 accuracy comparable to D1) contradicts the rework warning. The K-219 pilot assigns treatment when D1 wait exceeds 4 h, so congestion is confounded with the intervention.
