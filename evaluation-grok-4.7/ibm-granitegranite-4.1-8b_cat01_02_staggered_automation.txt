3.5

The write-up has the required ten sections and names the main design problems, but its numbers, signs, and code are not reliable enough to support the claims built on them.

**Observed facts are misstated.** Gamma was piloted in July after readiness work; it was not selected with Alpha and Beta for poor historical cycle time, and its extra routing starts in July, not April. The fraud desk opens for every branch in March; extra borderline referral is a later automation feature. Delta has the shortest cycles and highest satisfaction, so calling it the next “historically weak” branch is false. Pre-period fast-path shares of about 4–6% while `automation=0` are never discussed.

**Section 5 does not compute the windows it defines.** Alpha’s “pre-average” mixes March median (49.4) and p90 (99.4) with January–February escalation (7.8) and January CSAT (3.83). The post figures are mostly June peaks or August CSAT, not two-month averages. Gamma’s escalation change is reported as +0.0 pp; 10.7 − 9.7 = +1.0 pp. The never-treated table labels single months as averages (Epsilon median 41.4 is January; Delta p90 88.2 is August) and compares post levels. Adopters were already slower, so that gap is mostly selection. A change-versus-change contrast is absent. Alpha and Beta medians are roughly flat to worse in absolute terms; both they and the controls worsen in the spring, which this comparison cannot show.

**Signs and mechanisms contradict the panel.** Escalation rises; it does not “modestly decline.” Alpha p90 goes from the high 90s to 119.9 in June, not from 101 to 98. Section 7 then calls Beta’s p90 change of +12.9 h an improvement. Rework’s May jump appears in Delta and Epsilon as well. “Stable staffing” is false. The fast-path row’s claim that medians fall 3–10 h does not hold for Alpha or Beta.

**Inference is overclaimed and internally inconsistent.** Estimands are marked “Partial” while interference through the shared fraud desk and routing is set aside in a footnote. Event time is described as if differencing controlled staffing, mix, and the fraud desk. Lagged rework and escalation are proposed as covariates even though they are outcomes. The synthetic-control donor pool does not exist beyond two or three branches. The intensity results (0.9 h per 10 points, p = 0.07, and the other p-values) are not produced by the code and conflict with the later claim of a 5–10 h reduction at 30% fast-path use. “Nondifferential” is the wrong word for non-significance. Regression to the mean is tied to upward pre-trends; Alpha’s January–March median is not an upward trend, and Gamma’s July drop coincides with a decline at Delta and Epsilon too.

**Required pieces are missing or broken.** The identification narrative is well under 150 words. Measurement aggregation and dynamic effects are absent from the threat table. CSAT nonresponse is given a negative bias and, in the same row, said to hide dissatisfaction. The Python does not validate the panel, uses subplots despite the instruction not to, builds event time from the first month rather than adoption, calls `get_level_names` and `C(quarter)` on objects that do not exist, clusters a regression it never reaches, warns of four branches rather than five, and never warns that these are aggregated medians. Assumptions are not stated as code comments.

**The decision does not secure learning.** “Expand with an embedded evaluation” is an allowed choice, but the 20% volume pilot has no random assignment, so easy cases can be selected. Spillover monitoring still treats Delta as never-treated after Delta is chosen for rollout. A 0.02 CSAT tripwire sits inside ordinary month-to-month noise, and the scale-up rule demands a 5 h median cut without a concurrent control or a p90 constraint. The claim ladder’s “Observed” rework reduction is not true for all adopters, and the conditional 5–10 h effect is not earned. The only fully defensible conclusion is the last bullet: a single “reduces cycle time by X%” statement is not supported.
