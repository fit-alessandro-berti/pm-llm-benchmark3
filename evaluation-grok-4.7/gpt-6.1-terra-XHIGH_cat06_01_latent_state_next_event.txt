The answer is process-aware and structurally complete: states, obligations, object semantics, queue conditions, and the completion-only log are handled carefully, and C6-A versus C6-B is genuinely differentiated. It still has calibration and next-event logic problems that a strict reading should not overlook.

C6-A is the strongest case. S3, with S4 only if investigations are already open, fits the prefix. High severity plus injury correctly forces technical and regulatory work; the two devices in one complaint are correctly kept distinct from the two-complaint supplier trigger; and the three next-event candidates can actually occur without a missing prerequisite. The 0.45–0.65 / 0.15–0.35 / 0.10–0.25 split is a bit schematic, and regulatory versus technical order is only weakly identified, but the overlap between those two intervals admits that.

C6-B has the clearest reasoning error. Evidence Review Completed is the right first prediction. Customer Evidence Requested and Close — No Safety Issue are then given 0.10–0.30 and 0.05–0.25 even though both are marked not process-enabled and, on the answer’s own account, appear next only if a review completion is missing or delayed. The feed flag is normal, and completeness is 0.98, so that conjunction is much smaller than a floor of 0.10. Those intervals describe “what review might do later,” not the next logged event. Naming S5 as the alternative is also loose: a duplicate non-investigation closure need never pass through disposition-ready. Fast Close is correctly kept out of the ranked list once the evidence cycle has started.

C6-C is substantively right to treat the country rule and repeated malfunction as a reportability signal, to keep supplier analysis unproven across different lots, and to mark disposition as not enabled while regulatory completion is unseen. The low end of 0.45 for Regulatory Assessment Completed is still too firm: assignment is unknown, 12 items are waiting, and the fresh “root cause not confirmed” result can produce a faster evidence request. The prose states that uncertainty; the interval does not.

C6-D is under-discriminated from C6-C. It reuses the same 0.45–0.65, 0.15–0.35, 0.05–0.25 pattern despite a worklist open only since 12:40, a migration-affected feed, and a source-system attachment with no analytical evidence cycle. The migration caveat is careful and correctly refuses to turn 8–15% into a case-level probability, but the ranked triple does not use that observation model.

C6-E gets the primary state and the conditional supplier duty right, and it correctly separates the 40-minute wait from the question of which completion is next. Supplier Analysis Completed at 0.15–0.35 still sits high for an activity with a 6–36 hour typical duration, three items waiting, and no evidence it was opened early enough to finish first. A suspicious six-complaint lot can also support a reportability signal, yet regulatory completion never enters the top three.

Across cases, several intervals are reused rather than re-estimated, and some midpoint triples sum to about 1 while the note says other events remain plausible. The directly-follows discussion, the A/B contrast, the enabled-versus-observed distinction, and the refusal to treat a missing event as proof of no work are all at a high level. The numerical layer is less disciplined than the narrative.
