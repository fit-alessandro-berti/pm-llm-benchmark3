4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure, cites many identifiers, and lands on a defensible portfolio (order as the primary case notion, invoice and package as secondary views, object-centric graphs for interactions). That compliance does not rescue the analysis. The central constructs are defined one way and applied another, several event–object claims are false, and the directly-follows distortions are often mechanistically wrong.

Convergence is defined as merging distinct entities into one trace, and divergence as fragmentation or truncation. The matrix then rates item convergence High because items “share” events. Sharing, under flattening, copies an event into multiple item traces; it does not merge those items. That is the opposite of the stated definition, and it is also factually wrong for the cited events: e11, e15, and e23 relate to I11 only, not I12. I12 is on e12, e16, and e24. The same split appears for orders: convergence is rated Low while duplication is rated Moderate–High, even though e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, and e27 are exactly the multi-order replications that convergence is. Divergence is also applied inconsistently. Truncation is part of the definition, yet delivery traces of two activities are Low divergence and payment singletons are High.

The distortion section repeats a directly-follows error. Copying one event into several cases inflates frequency; it does not create a self-loop. A self-loop needs two successive occurrences inside one trace. O3’s Pack→Pack and Dispatch→Dispatch come from distinct events (e13 then e14, e16 then e17), not from O3 and O4 sharing a timestamp. Separate package cases likewise do not produce a P2→P3 activity edge. P2 and P3 are objects. Each package trace is Pack Package → Dispatch → Confirm Delivery. P3 and P4 also do not share pack or dispatch events, so the claim that they have identical pack/dispatch times is false (e13 at 10:12 versus e14 at 10:20; e16 versus e17).

Delivery is over-merged. D2’s direct relations are only e16 and e24. e15 belongs to D1. Flattening on delivery does not pull Create, Add Item, invoice, or payment into that trace. Saying the D2 trace runs e15–e24 and merges three order lifecycles silently propagates relations after Section 1 forbids that. e13 and e14 are similarly grouped with D2 and D3, which those pack events do not reference.

The invoice/payment reading overstates harm. No event relates to more than one invoice, so invoice convergence is not Moderate. V2’s trace Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment is two real events, M2 then M3, matching partially_settles and completes_settlement. Treating that order as a false causal variant ignores the qualifiers. The activity log still labels both “Record Payment”; the matrix’s Partially_settle / Completes_settle labels are not activities. Payment singletons, and the loss of the M2–M3 link once the case notion is Payment, are correctly identified. Item truncation is real, but four-event item traces are not near-singletons, and “e05–e23 only” fits I11, not I12, I32, or I41.

The code names the requested routines, but it would not run and does not implement the claimed check. `discover_dfg` returns a frequency graph plus start and end activities, not a performance graph, and it returns three values. `event_type_frequency` / `object_type_frequency` and `filter_events` are not part of the OCEL API used here. `ocel.events` is a dataframe, not a list of dicts with `related_objects`. Even under that dict model, `'Order' in [dicts]` is always false, so the shared-event filter removes nothing. The propagation sensitivity example is also empty: Add Item already relates the order and the item, and Issue Invoice already relates the order and the invoice.

Lead-object choice B is structured and not absurd, but its “strongest evidence” that orders have a full e01–e31 lifecycle and low convergence is the same overclaim the matrix makes. The answer is specific enough to check, and many of those checks fail.
