4.5

The paper keeps the required seven-part shape and reaches a few defensible holdings, but it is not a reliable case assessment. Time-of-act, threshold, and record facts are repeatedly inverted, and the case outcomes contradict the hierarchy the paper itself adopts.

The method treats several conclusions as facts: that Incident Command cannot touch GPIP-4, and that the CFO email may defer Finance approval to a later review. A6 says a later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment control. “Deferred” is then used for approvals that never occurred. The burden line is also one-sided. Some deviations are clear on the documents; others stay unproved because actors, recording, or the meaning of “critical” are missing. Those are different findings.

The hierarchy table has the right columns and correctly parks ON-44 as evidence only. It then collapses. Approved-supplier onboarding is ranked as if it were GPIP-4, and GPIP-4 is placed below the local SOP on that issue. A3 (new-supplier or above-EUR-100,000 Compliance before commitment) is not B3 (approved register). ECD-2 is said not to override segregation, Finance, or Compliance, and the CFO instrument is given only a narrow Finance-timing role. Section 4 then treats those same gaps as justified exceptions. That is an internal defeat of the hierarchy.

The rule catalogue is not operational. Comparison operators drop out, so thresholds become “EUR 50k” and “EUR 100k” rather than at-least-50,000 and above-100,000. R-004 ties the local emergency regime to the ECD-2 window, so the pre-09:00 regime disappears. R-005 states “before commitment” although B2 does not. B6’s 24-hour emergency form, A4’s tender-only waiver, A6’s non-cure rule, C3’s four-hour record, and D2’s above-150,000 Legal add-on are missing. The pseudo-logic is worse: segregation and the register are required only when there is no emergency, and an in-window “critical lot” plus R-004/R-007 is treated as sufficient for compliance. That logic cannot decide these cases.

Case results:

- P-501 is not an ECD-2 exception. Commitment is 08:50; ECD-2 starts at 09:00. EUR 38,000 never triggers A2, and the supplier is already approved, so R-002 and an R-006 waiver are inapplicable. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate; the paper invents a telephone recording and an implied delegation. The emergency form at 07:50 the next day is inside 24 hours and is never assessed. Local B4 may allow commitment before the PO; that is not an ECD-2 justification.
- P-502 fits the sterilization scope, and the 12:55 record is inside four hours of the 10:10 verbal authorization. C2 can cover a new supplier. Compliance at 18:30 and Finance at 13:05 are after the 10:15 commitment. Calling that a high-confidence justified exception, while Section 5 admits a non-waivable A3 breach is credible, hides the controlling conflict.
- P-503 is properly noncompliant on segregation and on wholly missing Compliance and Finance for EUR 140,000. It is still overstated: the supplier is approved, so R-006 is not a deviation, and “no rule satisfied” ignores two-person timing and an in-window PO sequence. The three-day backup-line scope dispute is underused.
- P-504 is properly outside “critical sterilization-line lots,” and a unilateral commitment to an unapproved supplier is noncompliant. The cited rule is the wrong one, and the cafeteria alternative is not credible on the given facts.
- P-505 does not lack Compliance. Clearance at 17:40 was not required for an EUR 88,000 approved-supplier purchase, and it preceded commitment. The 17:59:31 acknowledgment is the right kind of evidence against the 18:06 ERP stamp, but the paper then says ECD-2 also covers post-18:00 acts. Finance at 19:00 remains an open A2 problem, not a satisfied exception. No four-hour authorization record is shown.
- P-506 correctly rejects an unwritten overnight extension. Finance approval at 06:05 is before the 06:20 commitment, and the supplier is approved. “No rule satisfied,” an R-006 breach, and low confidence do not follow from those facts. The live gaps are the post-commitment PO and Plant approval, plus the independent budget owner.
- P-507 reverses the record. Compliance at 08:50 is before commitment at 09:00, not a retroactive clearance. The 12:40 PDF is within four hours and is better than the 14:30 ERP entry; that point is sound and then misapplied. There is no Finance event to “defer.” Section 6 later says this case has no breach, after Section 4 and the remediation list treat it as one.
- P-508 gets Legal, pre-commitment Compliance, affected-line scope, and segregation directionally right. EUR 155,000 is above the CFO email’s EUR 150,000 substitution sentence, and Plant and Finance are absent, not delayed. High-confidence justified exception is the wrong confidence and likely the wrong primary class.

Section 5 covers more than four cases, but several “premises that would change the class” are not the premises that matter. P-501’s alternative still assumes ECD-2 and a EUR 50,000 rule. P-507’s alternative attacks ERP timing of a clearance that was already timely. P-505 is omitted. Where the alternative is actually stronger, as with P-502 and P-508, the paper leaves the weaker primary in place at high confidence.

Section 6 does not separate policy breach, logging defect, late proof of a timely act, and failure of proof. P-501’s stamps fall inside the ERP outage and are not tested. Retroactive collection of approvals is recommended as if it cured A6. Monitoring items are generic.

The draft clause does include a post-emergency audit safeguard, a scope boundary, a record rule, and a split between waivable workflow steps and integrity controls. It still permits Finance to be cured after the fact, barely specifies thresholds and expiry, and makes segregation immune even to the Chief Risk Officer without justifying that change. It restates the paper’s main error in policy form.

The usable core is the structural skeleton, the P-503 and P-504 noncompliance calls, the refusal to extend ECD-2 by an overnight verbal claim, and the recognition that a CFO email and an unsigned-by-CRO directive do not automatically erase GPIP-4. The case table does not apply that recognition to the acts, amounts, and documents actually given.
