The write-up follows the required section order and gets many fully observed early cases right (P01–P04, P06, P08–P14, and P16’s S1 times). Those strengths are outweighed by broken constraint reasoning, reversed censoring, internal contradictions, and code that cannot reproduce the claimed results.

The required hard cases are not reconstructed defensibly. For P05, C1 is occupied by P03 until 09:45 and P05 completes at 10:15, so the S2 start lies in [09:45, 10:15) and S2 sojourn is fixed at 50 minutes, with wait and service trading off one-for-one. The table instead reports wait [5, 15] and service 45, which implies a 09:30 start inside P03’s C1 interval. The note then argues with that table and still ends on 09:30. For P07, I1 is busy with the fully observed P05 interval 09:00–09:25, so the earliest S1 start is 09:25 and S1 sojourn is fixed at 75 minutes (wait about [40, 75], service about (0, 35]). The table’s [15, 45] / [15, 45] and the note’s start near 08:55 both overlap P05. For P11, C2 runs P08 from 11:15–12:00 after the 10:55–11:10 outage, so the earliest S2 start is 12:00, not 11:10; the reported service of 80 minutes is the S1-end-to-S2-end sojourn. For P15, I1 is still occupied by P11 and P13 until 11:40, so S1 wait is about [90, 125] and service about (0, 35], while elapsed S2 service at 14:00 is 30 minutes. The table’s S2 service of [105, OPEN] is the elapsed S2 sojourn, placed in the service column. The same sojourn/service mix-up appears in the P05 and P11 rows.

Censoring is inverted for every case still waiting at 14:00. P16, P18, P19, P20, and P22 have already waited 85, 50, 35, 15, and 110 minutes, so those figures are lower bounds. The table reports them as ranges from 0 up to a shorter or unrelated number, and several notes disagree with their own row (P16 [0, 60] vs [0, 85], P18 [0, 120] vs [0, 50], P21 wait 140 in the table vs 100 in the note). P21’s observed S1 wait is 100 minutes and elapsed service is already 30 minutes, so service is not [0, OPEN]. Simple observed waits are also wrong for P17 (95, not 100), P18 (100, not 115), P19 (100, not 120), and P20 (105, not 125).

Station metrics are not a trace of the log. Under the answer’s own definition, S2 arrivals are S1 completions, but section 5 says afternoon S1 completions (4) are less than S2 arrivals (6). S1 external arrivals in [08:00, 10:00) are P01–P13 (13), not 8, and 6+6+6 S2 arrivals cannot cover the 20 observed S1 completions. Waiting-time “summaries” are unlabeled ranges, WIP is asserted at unnamed instants, and utilizations such as afternoon S2 at 50–60% are not derived from resource occupancy. With the open intervals included, both S2 resources are busy for most of 12:00–14:00. Little’s Law is defined and then never computed. The three sensitivity scenarios stay qualitative: no imputed numbers, no rounding propagation (a difference of two rounded timestamps spans about ±5 minutes), and no shown change in WIP, throughput, or utilization. The claim that S1 becomes the post-12:00 bottleneck is therefore not a stable result. At 14:00 the log shows S1 WIP of 2 (P21 in service, P22 waiting) and S2 WIP of 6 (P15 and P17 in service, P16 and P18–P20 waiting).

The Python block collapses `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank to `NaT` before any status is kept, drops missing starts “for simplicity,” and never builds WIP or the three uncertainty scenarios. `s2_start`, `s2_end`, and `s2_resource` each have 21 entries against 22 cases, so the DataFrame constructor fails. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied to single-activity station projections, where case waiting time has no prior event and is not station queue time, and the printed column names are not established as the columns that function returns.
