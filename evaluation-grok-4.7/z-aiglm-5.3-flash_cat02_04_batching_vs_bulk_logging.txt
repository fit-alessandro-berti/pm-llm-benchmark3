7.2

The final write-up is a strong process-mining diagnosis, but it is not close to flawless. Several rendered figures are simply wrong, and the mandatory code can contradict the prose.

What holds up is the core reasoning. Identical timestamps are not treated as physical simultaneity. Operational batches are separated from logging batches. G1 is tied to the four-slot fixture and cycle-boundary PLC logging, with fast serial work inside one cycle left open. G2 and G3 are read as opposite fusion artifacts (shared onset versus consolidated submit), using the begin-group button and T-C’s submission practice without over-trusting them. G5’s peak of four open intervals against a pool of three is a real contradiction. G6 correctly produces both huge waits (35–175 min, mean about 103) and zero service because one keystroke fills both lifecycle fields. G7’s negative waits (1, 4, 7, and 11 min on four of five cases) internally prove that the quarter-hour stamps cannot be points. G8’s drain is chained at 7 min after a 15:38 restart, with the 15:00–15:35 hold kept uncertain because the clocks differ. The bias directions, hypothesis matrix, identity-versus-censoring sensitivity, redesign priorities, and three-tier adjudication are coherent and match the required headings.

The numerical presentation is not reliable. In the finished text, ranges are collapsed into false magnitudes: G3’s 28-to-6 min services become “286 min,” G2’s 8-to-27 becomes “827 min,” G2’s 18-to-6 waits become “186 min,” and G4’s 7-to-19 waits become “719 min.” Those are not the quantities calculated elsewhere in the same document. G8’s recording lags are stated as 3–6 s; the stamps are 6, 5, 3, 4, and 2 s, so the range is 2–6 s. “Peak 3.8” for the censored G5 overlap is undefined.

The code meets the structural checklist (in-memory rows, interval log, `format_dataframe`, a `merge_distance` × `min_batch_size` grid, no row drops, cluster flags, pandas overlap/equality/lag/grid/backlog diagnostics). It does not safely implement them. `pd.to_timedelta` on bare `HH:MM` is the minutes:seconds parse, while `HH:MM:SS` is hours:minutes:seconds. Every two-part lifecycle stamp is compressed by 60, and `recorded_at` lags on PLC/scanner rows become multi-hour nonsense. Under that scaling, the 61 s chain rule false-chains G5 (true gaps of 10–20 min become 10–20 s) into a sequential drain, and G7’s quarter-hour test fails, so G7 falls through to fused walls instead of coarsened time. Those are the opposite of the prose classifications. Category comparison by scraping `repr` is also too brittle to count as a real PM4Py comparison.

Taken together, the judgment and uncertainty handling are at an expert level, but the false figures and a timestamp bug that breaks the required computational section keep this well below a near-flawless score.
