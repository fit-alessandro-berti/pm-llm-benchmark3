The write-up has the requested nine-section shape and names the right problem family (72-hour origin at `Submit`, competing exits, transfers, leakage). The definitions, case arithmetic, method choice, and code are unreliable enough that the document should not be used as an analysis plan.

# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions
The two estimands are not operationalized coherently, and the four required case contributions are wrong or self-contradictory.

- Resolved at 76 hours (S03) is an observed late resolution: unresolved at 72, event at 76. Calling that “100% risk” turns a realized outcome into a probability.
- Open at 92 hours (S09) is already known to be unresolved at 72 and is censored only at age 92. The assigned risk of 0.5 has no basis in the table.
- Withdrawn at 27 hours (S06) is described as both a breach and “0% risk of not resolved,” which cannot both be true. Under a resolution estimand this case is either a competing event that precludes resolution or a censoring at 27; it is not a resolution.
- Transferred at 68 hours (S08) is assigned risk 0.96 with no calculation. If transfer censors follow-up, the status at 72 is unknown; if transfer is competing, internal resolution is precluded at 68.

`Transfer` is placed in both the censoring list and the competing-risk list without tying either treatment to an estimand. “Cumulative incidence of resolution after 72 hours” is then illustrated as “1 case out of 1 total,” which is not a cumulative incidence. Right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches are not separated. No third estimand, such as state occupancy “still with no terminal disposition at 72 hours,” is defined.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding
The encoding cannot be used. Resolved, rejected, and withdrawn all receive event indicator 1, so a rejection would be analyzed as the resolution event. Transferred is coded as event 0 and as competing cause `Transfer` at the same time. Open cases are described as censored at a “72h cutoff”; the censoring time in the extract is `duration_or_age_h` (for example 23, 47, 81, 92, 104), not 72.

The three transfer alternatives are not three plausible assumptions. Non-informative censoring, censoring everyone at 72, and censoring at `last_event_gap_h + 72` are variants of censoring, and the third is undefined: transferred rows have `last_event_gap_h = 0`, so it collapses to 72, and it invents follow-up after observation stopped. Missing are the standard alternatives: competing event, administrative SLA failure, and best/worst bounds on the unobserved external outcome. The table does not give separate indicators for two estimands.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased
The survivor-bias claim is reversed. A completed-resolved analysis includes S03 (76h), S13 (110h), S20 (74h), and S28 (88h). It drops unresolved cases, including open cases already past 72 hours (S09, S11, S25, S26). That omission makes breach risk look better, not “toward early breaches.”

S26 is cited as open at 92 hours. S26 is open at 104 hours with a 72-hour gap; S09 is the open case at 92. S32, transferred at 96 hours, is grouped with “early” pre-72 transfers (S08 at 68, S21 at 26) even though S32 had already passed 72 before transfer. Open cases still under 72 (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31), whose breach status is unknown, are never contrasted with open cases already over 72. Cohort imbalance is asserted with no priority or escalation counts and no case identifiers.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan
Cause-specific Cox regression is recommended as primary on 32 cases, but cause-specific hazard ratios are not the SLA probabilities the decisions need. The justification that cause-specific hazards avoid immortal-time bias is false; that bias is fixed by timing of covariates, not by the hazard type.

Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence, subdistribution hazards, and restricted mean time are not compared, despite being required. Kaplan–Meier is dismissed outright, including for composite or explicitly cause-specific estimands where it has a defined meaning. Landmarking is described as assuming “stability post-72h”; a landmark fixes immortal time by conditioning on the history at a landmark, and 72 hours is the horizon, not automatically the landmark. The multi-state secondary analysis is not identified here: only one duration per case is supplied, so stage sojourn times cannot be estimated. Small-sample sparsity (13 resolutions, 3 rejections, 4 withdrawals, 3 transfers, 9 open) is not discussed.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards
`escalated` is listed as baseline, then later called unsafe as a baseline. It is a deterministic function of `Escalate` appearing after `Submit`, so the baseline classification is leakage and immortal time. Final `rework_count` and `last_event_gap_h` are end-of-follow-up summaries; entering them as ordinary predictors of the same duration leaks the future. Full prefixes that end in `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, or `Transfer` contain the outcome.

The diagram misses the main post-change path, `Submit → AutoCheck`, and observed edges such as `Screen → Reject`, `Screen → Withdraw`, `Screen → Escalate`, `Review → Resolve`, `Escalate → Review`, and `Rework`. `Rework` is absent. The `||` syntax is not valid Mermaid. Cohort B compliance is visible in the prefixes (many B cases are AutoCheck-first; S17, S20, S23, S26, and S30 are not) and is not used.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis
The three “analyses” do not vary withdrawal and rejection, and they do not state a qualitative conclusion that could reverse. Directional claims (“underestimate resolution risk,” “inflate survival”) are not derived. “Cohort B has higher escalation rates (e.g., S18 vs S04)” compares two escalated P1 resolutions and does not measure a rate. S26 is again placed “at 72h,” mixing the inactivity gap with age. The informative-censoring alternative that matters here—censor an open case at the last observed event (`age − last_event_gap_h`) versus at cutoff—is not specified. For S26 that moves the censoring time from 104 hours to 32 hours and changes whether non-resolution at 72 hours is treated as known.

# 7. Operational interpretation
The alert rule is a threshold on observed age, not a survival or cumulative-incidence output, and the example is again S26 “at 92h.” Path rules use the full prefix, including activities that have already happened on completed cases such as S13. Calibration and fairness are one sentence each: no predicted-versus-observed check, no priority-specific cumulative incidence, no small-sample caveat. The scenario’s non-random, incomplete rollout is not treated as a bar to causal use of cohort.
