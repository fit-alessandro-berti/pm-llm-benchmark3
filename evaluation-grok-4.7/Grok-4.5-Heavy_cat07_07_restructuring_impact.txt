The answer covers every required section, uses the supplied handover and team figures accurately, keeps East distinct from North and South, and refuses a full rollout on average cycle time alone. Those strengths are real. Under a strict reading, several decision-relevant errors keep it well short of nearly flawless.

The pilot in §5–§6 answers a different question from the one the recommendation treats as settled. Moving only East onto a shared model, with North and South left embedded, estimates an East-versus-control contrast. It leaves North/South expertise loss unmeasured, and it never loads the shared queue with pooled North+South+East volume. Pooling spare North/South capacity (about 68–71% utilization) into East’s overloaded tech role (82%) is the main stated benefit; a one-team pilot does not create that pool, so it cannot show the bottleneck that appears only when all three streams share one queue. Legal/Risk are already at 91–93%, and the extra referrals are specified per 100 cases across the whole organization; an East-only cut of that increment will not reproduce the capacity squeeze. The claim that North and South “provide the necessary contrast to measure expertise-loss effects” misstates what an untreated control identifies. The 8–12 week window is also too short for the learning and skill-atrophy effects the same answer treats as material to a go/no-go.

The optimistic cycle-time claim is not reconciled with the wait data. Management’s own routing table raises median wait on the specialist return path (3.2h to 4.0h), the approval path (2.4h to 3.0h), and Legal/Risk (9.6h to 10.5h), while referral counts stay similar or rise. A 10–15% median cycle-time gain (on the order of a day, against baselines of 6.8–8.6 days) therefore needs a separate mechanism, such as large rework reduction or multi-day queue collapse. The scenario states the gain without that mechanism. Hour-scale handover waits and day-scale cycle times are left unconnected, including in the adverse case, where 8–15h waits become multi-day cycle-time damage only if utilization pushes queues into backlog. The central scenario’s “+≈5%” is also easy to read in the wrong direction beside East’s “−1 to −1.5 d.”

`local_expertise_flag` is used as a weeks-scale leading indicator of skill erosion. Once hard cases are routed to the center, that flag falls because the routing rule changed, even if nobody’s skill changed. The same conflation reappears in the guardrails. The fairness trigger fires when the East versus North/South gap widens in either direction, so an East improvement with flat controls can force a rollback. Utilization is operationalized as activity counts and, in the table, as the working-together network; those measure volume and co-handling, not busy time over available time.

The PM4Py sketch hits the requested artifacts, but it would not run as written. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key` argument. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` need a start timestamp, and the log has only `time:timestamp`, so service versus wait is not identified; the performance DFG is the measure that actually fits this log, and the sketch does not say so. Working-together and subcontracting are built for the pre period only, `filter_variants` is unused, SLA misses are a comment, and `dataframe_utils` is imported and unused. The optimistic “early warning signs” are success markers, while the same heading means failure markers in the other two scenarios.

The mechanism map, the invisible-average risks, and the indicator split are the strongest parts and are specific enough to use. The grade is held down by the mis-identified pilot, the unsupported day-scale cycle-time magnitudes, and the measurement and API errors above.
