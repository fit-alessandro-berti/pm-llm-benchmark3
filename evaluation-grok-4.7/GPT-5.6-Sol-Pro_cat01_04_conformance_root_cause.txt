7.5

The write-up is complete, well structured, and substantively strong: all eight required sections are present, the six hypotheses are separated rather than collapsed into employee blame, staged repairs are used as a quasi-experiment, sign-off is treated as model obsolescence plus a real policy change, and the optional control-flow model is correctly rejected as a final EUR 50,000 rule. The ledger recounts (530 `Risk Screen → Queue Routed`, about 560 gateway cases, 210 sign-offs, 55 missing checks, 70 reversals) and the governance rules are precise. That is not enough for a high score, because the central numerical claim is not reconciled with the variant census the answer itself knows how to audit.

Under ordinary alignment semantics, a trace fits only at cost zero. Every June–July variant contains `Risk Screen` or `Queue Routed`, so raw perfect-fit should be about 0%, not 32%. After mapping `Risk Screen` to `Classify` alone, only the 90 queue-free standard traces fit: 90/650 = 13.8%, not 58%. Those two reported jumps are therefore impossible if the variant table is exhaustive. Label map plus `Queue Routed` removal is coherent (calculated about 82% versus reported 81%), and source ordering can plausibly reach about 89%. August–September is likewise inconsistent: after the same preprocessing, sign-off (210), overrides (35), missing checks (30), and reversals (45) are disjoint, so perfect-fit cannot be 66% (the non-sign-off share is 66%, but the same row lists the other deviations as dominant moves; the variant-implied ceiling is about 56% even if every reversal is repaired). The answer flags April–May, where all 600 listed traces are language-admissible yet fitness is 93%, and then assigns reliability 98 to the irreconcilable 32→58 step and builds the conclusion on “+26 / +23 / +8 points.” The same audit would have shown that only the later June increments are usable and that the split between rename and technical-event effects is not identified by those two percentages.

A second missed cross-check: April and August variant lengths match the reported means (about 5.43 and 6.78), but June’s variant-implied mean is 6.12 against a reported 6.3. The claim that +0.9 events is “quantitatively consistent” with the gateway uses the business summary and skips the tighter variant arithmetic, which fails only in June. The 30 residual `Classify` traces and 90 `Risk Screen` traces without `Queue Routed` are also underused; they are the direct evidence of partial rollout.

Smaller defects: `hidden_source_sequence → recorded_order` points the wrong way—the source sequence was an unused witness, while ingestion time produced the recorded order. Posteriors 99/98/96/95/93 barely discriminate contribution size. The unused ordering helper sorts rows but does not rewrite `time:timestamp`, so typical PM4Py replay would reorder by ingestion time and undo it. Reliability 100 on an acknowledged evidence gap is category confusion. The code otherwise follows the requested pipeline, preserves `Manual Override`, compares fitness and precision, and states the overgeneralization caveat; it was not executed.

The qualitative ranking can survive: rename, technical `Queue Routed`, ordering, and an unversioned sign-off rule jointly dominate the measured drop, and missing checks remain an unaudited residual. The answer does not earn a higher mark because it treats inconsistent fitness percentages as a controlled experiment after demonstrating the check that falsifies the headline increments.
