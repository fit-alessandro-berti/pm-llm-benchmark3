The answer is a complete seven-part memo with a full case matrix and a final label for every case, but it is not reliable as an audit judgment or as a POWL revision. Factual reversals, missed authority limits, and a combined model that cannot accept the canonical compliant trace keep it well below a competent conformance review.

The final labels are the strongest part. SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are directionally right, and SUP-702 and SUP-704 are rightly treated as in-window overlays. Those calls are then undercut by the briefs. Section 2 puts eligible, in-window SUP-702 under “Unauthorized Auto_Approve.” It says the EUR 20,000 FD-7 cap was exceeded by SUP-704 (EUR 18,000) and SUP-705 (EUR 12,000). It applies that same FD-7 cap to SUP-703, a June PD-3 case whose EUR 180,000 purchase is harm evidence, not a pilot spend-cap breach. It denies retrospective-approval evidence for SUP-704 even though the trace and context place manual approval on the next business day. Section 4 then contradicts the brief on the cap arithmetic.

Authority and rule identity are mishandled throughout. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation is never mentioned, so every Auto_Approve trace is assessed with a missing control. The compliance observer’s later denial that she had approved weakening CS-9 is never used. Notification is treated as a CS-9 duty although it exists only in M0. SUP-715 is charged to CS-9; the actual defect is an undisclosed conflict under a separate policy, while the CS-9 segregation rule is requester versus approver and is the SUP-712 issue. CS-9’s abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads, and the “before activation” qualifier, are largely ignored when SUP-709 is argued. The six August Auto_Approve cases are not carried into the system judgment.

The burden of proof equates noncompliance with proved harm and treats acceptance of an exception as proof that the model is defective. Exceptional authority, eligibility, dates, and conditions should be proved by the party relying on them. SUP-711 is labeled indeterminate despite an established same-source duplicate. SUP-713 is marked COMPLIANT at high confidence although the mapping was approved a week after the event. Withdrawals, required in the architecture section, are absent.

Dialectical fairness is mostly formal. The prosecution concludes that M0 is fit for purpose and does not concede the missing loop, the unmodeled resource rule, the CRM break, or overlay scope. The defense claims M0 cannot express concurrency even though `checks` is already an unordered partial order, treats compliance inaction as acceptance, and ends by blaming CS-9’s rigidity. SUP-708 is conceded only in a matrix cell.

The section 7 model fails the minimum control-flow brief. `standard_approved` and `standard_decision` are never connected to `combined_model`; the standard XOR child is only concurrent sanctions and quality. A normal SUP-701 trace therefore fits no branch. The emergency branch is an XOR between quality and manual approval, whereas FD-7 requires sanctions first and then both retrospective approval and quality review, plus monitoring and the cap. `Request_More_Info` is explained as repeatable, but the partial order executes it once, and `Operator.LOOP` is unused. Because every node in that partial order occurs, the path also contains both activation and rejection. The same transition objects are reused across parents, and the emergency decision is ordered despite not being a node of `emergency_route`. `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` does not match the observed `Activate_Supplier` label. The overlay table misfiles the conflict rule as CS-9, omits delegation evidence, country, master-data result, bitemporal effective/record timestamps, and authority identifiers, and lists aliases and conflict disclosure as mutable.
