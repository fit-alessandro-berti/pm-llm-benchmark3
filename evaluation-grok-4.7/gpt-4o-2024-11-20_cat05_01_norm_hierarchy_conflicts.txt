## Method and hierarchy
Section 1 says a more specific, later directive can override GPIP-4. Section 2 says ECD-2 probably cannot override GPIP-4 finance or compliance controls. Section 4 then treats ECD-2 as justifying a post-commitment compliance clearance. Those three positions cannot be held together.

The GPIP-4 row reverses A5. Stricter local controls are what the local SOP is allowed to add. GPIP-4 is the floor that blocks silent weakening. The table instead lists “local SOPs if stricter” as something GPIP-4 can override.

ECD-2 is given medium-high weight and a presumption that it yields to core GPIP-4 controls, while section 1 has already assumed it is a legitimate overlay because of past practice. The facts limit that past practice: signed incident directives have not been treated this way for new-supplier compliance clearance, and the CRO did not ratify ECD-2. A7 is left as an ambiguity rather than as the burden the exception-claimer must carry.

The CFO email is described as able to override “timing of Finance-related approvals.” D1 claims that joint Incident Command authorization is sufficient for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000. That is a substitution claim, not a timing rule. D4’s integrity caveat is noted in section 1 and then dropped from the hierarchy’s operative effect.

Putting ERP timestamps in the norm hierarchy mixes an evidential-weight note with a source of obligation. ON-44 is expressly not a normative exception.

The table is one rough ranking. There is no issue-specific ordering for segregation of duties, pre-commitment finance, compliance clearance, approved-supplier status, PO-before-commit, the 24-hour emergency form, or the four thresholds (EUR 25,000, 50,000, 100,000, and 150,000).

The row then overclaims. “Timing” is not independently failed if B4 applies, because B4 allows commitment before the PO. The emergency form at 07:50 the next day is inside 24 hours and is never checked. Plant and Finance the same afternoon sit under B2, whose text does not clearly require pre-commitment approval, and A2 does not apply below EUR 50,000. R3 is listed as partly satisfied when the substitution condition failed. ERP logs are called decisive even though 08:42, 08:50, and 12:20 fall inside the ON-44 outage. A stamp up to 12 minutes later than the act would place the real commitment even earlier, still outside ECD-2. High confidence is not earned once those distinctions are made. Severity has no stated scale.

The filters are a strong scope fit, the supplier recording supports 10:15, and the verbal authorization at 10:10 was recorded at 12:55, inside C3’s four hours. The row misses that last point and calls the problem a “post-commit authorization.” Compliance_Clearance at 18:30 is a different act. For a new supplier, A3 required it before commitment. A6 says a later clearance does not cure the miss. ECD-2 does not mention compliance, and prior practice specifically withholds this kind of overlay from new-supplier clearance. EUR 62,000 also triggers A2, and Finance_Approve at 13:05 is after commitment. No emergency form is in the record, so B6 is unproved. “Telephone & PDF proof” imports the PDF from P-507. “Missing evidence: scope-critical” points at one of the clearer facts and ignores the actual gaps: committer identity versus the two authorizers, budget-owner status, the form, and whether compliance is onboarding. Low severity plus a justified-exception label, while section 2 says ECD-2 cannot override compliance, is an inconsistent result.

P-503 as NONCOMPLIANT is the right primary label, and high confidence is fair, but the row does not state the grounds that make it so. EUR 140,000 requires pre-commitment compliance under A3 and pre-commitment finance under A2. Neither event exists, so A6 has nothing to cure. Backup-line pumps with three days of stock fail both the Internal Audit reading of “critical” and B4’s six-hour test, so ECD-2 and the local emergency path are both weak.
