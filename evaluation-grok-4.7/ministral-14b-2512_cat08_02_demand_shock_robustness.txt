The write-up has the required seven sections, four mixed scenarios, a full trade-off table, a redesign-caused failure (M1 as a single point of failure), and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard. That structure does not survive the evidence. Several causal claims are reversed, counts from an 8-week window are compared with a 3-week window as if they were rates, and medical validation is repeatedly treated as specialist review.

**Evidence errors.** Complex-panel is 13% of normal mix, not a 13% specialist-review rate, and the 3.1× factor is medical validation, not hold-for-specialist (that factor is 2.4×). Peak “Repeat assay → Medical validation: 120” is not 120 complex-panel holds; holds are 610 peak and 290 normal, and they sit after technical validation, so they do not waste assay capacity. Weekly repeats roughly triple (about 143 to 420), but 1140 versus 1260 is presented as a small rise. “Batch waits: 14h → 22h” stitches two different peak edges (preparation → batch wait 14h, batch wait → assay 22h), not a before/after. Queue moves such as 14h → 20h, validation 3.5h → 8h, recollection 760 → 1200, and repeats → 1500 are not in the log. Registration “68% → 50%,” a 60% digital-order share, a 5% or 8% M2 failure rate, 20% outsourcing, and “medical-validation WIP > 500” are invented.

**Bottleneck logic.** In Scenario 1 the pre-analytical station is “relieved” by I5, an evening medical-validation shift, which does not touch that station and cannot move the bottleneck onto medical validation. I4 trains technical validators into accession correction, so it relieves accession and starves technical validation; Scenario 2 and the table say the opposite (validation is relieved and the accession queue grows). I8 exposes pre-analytical and assay only if automation succeeds; the stated risk is low adoption, which is the case in which downstream stations are not flooded. Registration is not the peak constraint (queue 2.8h versus pre-analytical 9.5h, assay 18h, technical validation 16h at 101% utilization, medical validation 22h, specialist 36h).

**Scenario and policy problems.** Scenario 1 stacks mix shift, courier compression, a medical-validator absence, and an M2 outage, so migration cannot be attributed. A medical-validator absence is then said to double the specialist-review queue. Scenario 2 routes work to M2 and also queues Routine on M1 while M1 is down. I10 outsources complex-panel overflow; the 12h transport hit is not an Urgent 18h penalty, because those classes are separate in the mix. Section 3 makes I3+I2 and I5+I10 always on; Section 4 and the table turn them off outside narrow cases. Triggers are inside ordinary peak baselines: assay utilization is already 98%, batch-wait → assay is already 22h, courier median queue is already 7.5h, and specialist median queue is already 36h, so “>95% and batch wait >10h,” “courier >6h,” and “specialist >12h” would stay on through a normal peak. Urgent-first is treated as robust despite starving Routine and complex-panel and raising WIP. I1’s harm is softened to a “minor” Routine delay and blamed on triage.

**What is left out.** Performance edges that actually blow up are barely used (recollection return 42h/60h, hold → medical 18h/48h, technical → medical 8h/24h, repeat → assay 10h/26h). Open cases (about 11% normal, 13% peak), technical validation already over 100%, clerk overtime raising errors, and courier smoothing (I7) are not part of the design. The dashboard’s 6h technical-validation and 12h specialist limits sit below observed peak medians, so they do not warn of further deterioration. Urgent “>95% within 18h” ignores a peak median of 17h and p90 of 33h.
