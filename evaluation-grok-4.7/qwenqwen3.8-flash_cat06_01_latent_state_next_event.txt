The final JSON is a strong, process-aware forecast: states, obligations, queues, migration, and the C6-A versus C6-B split are handled carefully, and it does not copy raw directly-follows frequencies or treat a missing event as proof that the task never happened. It is not nearly flawless.

The clearest overclaim is C6-A. Two devices in one complaint plus three merely “similar” complaints do not establish rule 5’s same-lot or same-component condition, yet Supplier Analysis is called mandatory. C6-E then swings the other way: a lot tied to six complaints is correctly used for Supplier Analysis, but is never tested as a possible reportability signal that would also require technical and regulatory work under rule 4.

C6-D’s second prediction is a delayed Evidence Received event, while the named alternative state is disposition-ready. That mismatches the hypothesis, and Customer Evidence Requested—the normative predecessor that migration could backfill first—is left out of the three outcomes. C6-B calls the alternative S5 even though a no-investigation duplicate often closes directly from review, and a Close probability as high as 0.25 is hard to square with a normal feed when review should be logged first. Midpoints on C6-B and C6-E leave almost no residual. C6-C correctly ranks regulatory assessment above disposition, but never faces the opposing base rate (technical investigation proceeds to disposition more often than to regulatory assessment in the supplied frequencies). `process_enabled` is at least applied consistently to the primary state and explained, including the useful C6-B contrast between an enabled fast close and the event more likely to appear next.
