7.6

The final answer is a strong, format-complete diagnosis: March is correctly isolated as logging/semantic drift, later rework and delay are not collapsed into that migration, and supplier, staffing, priority, and seasonality are kept as separate, partly confounded mechanisms. The baseline deltas and the seven-edge L1 distances are arithmetically exact, the fitness gap is interpreted with appropriate restraint, and the monitoring rules can separate recurrence from a new break.

It is not nearly flawless. The September row treats July as the pre-recovery peak: supplier 44% to 24%, rework 29% to 20%, median 44.2h to 41.5h, and p90 104h to 96h. August is omitted, although median and p90 were higher then (45.0h and 110h), so the path was not a monotonic fall from July. The “Jul–Aug” median increase of +13.1h matches July only (44.2 − 31.15); August is about +13.85h. Calling mapped fitness “stable” after March also overstates a series that moves from 0.91 to 0.86 and back to 0.90.

In the discrimination table, the pre/post-migration row lists a stable mapped DFG and stable case mix as patterns indicating behavioral and population drift. Those are patterns indicating the absence of those drifts. The L1 also adds nearly collinear rework edges (`Assess → Request Info` and `Request Info → Collect Documents`) plus the falling decision edge without saying that one loop is being counted several times, so May–October distances are inflated.

Part A of the code is transparent and usable. Part B does not match the PM4Py manual: `filter_time_range` is called with `start_date`, `end_date`, and `parameter_timestamp_key` instead of `dt1`, `dt2`, and `timestamp_key`; `discover_dfg` and `discover_temporal_profile` are given unsupported `parameters` (`dfg:minimum`, `tp:variant: median`); and the DECLARE call is `discover_declare_model` rather than `discover_declare`. After `format_dataframe`, timestamps are `time:timestamp`, while later helpers still default to `timestamp`. Those functions would not run as written.
