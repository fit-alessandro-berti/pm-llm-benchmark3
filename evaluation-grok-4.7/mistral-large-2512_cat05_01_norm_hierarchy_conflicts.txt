4.8

The memo has the required seven-part form and a real grasp of segregation of duties, the CRO reservation, the outage as an evidence problem, and the cafeteria purchase sitting outside the directive. Those merits do not carry the assessment. Outcome-determinative errors, internal contradictions, and misread facts recur across the hierarchy, the rule logic, and the case table.

Authority is unstable. Section 1 ranks GPIP-4 above the SOP, ECD-2, and the CFO email, and section 2 says ECD-2 probably cannot displace Finance approval. The rule catalogue and the case holdings then let ECD-2 and the email displace Finance approval, including the board-level pre-commitment rule in A2. A4 keeps every control other than competitive tendering in force unless the CRO issues a written A7 exception. The CRO was absent and never ratified ECD-2. The email is outside the controlled repository, D3 limits the CFO to finance interpretation, and D4 keeps essential integrity controls. Treating D1 as a clean waiver of A2 is the “strongest wording wins” move the task forbids. The hierarchy table also fails the demand for an issue-specific ordering: timing, supplier register, finance, compliance, and segregation are different conflicts, and one presumptive stack does not state them.

The formalization contains concrete rule errors. The emergency pseudo-logic lets the SOP emergency rule override the approved-supplier rule, which B5 expressly refuses to waive. D2’s Legal clearance “as well” above EUR 150,000 is encoded as overriding Finance approval; the email adds Legal clearance and, by its own “up to EUR 150,000” cap, does not relax finance at EUR 155,000. The 24-hour emergency-form duty is mis-scoped to ECD-2 critical lots, while the separate four-hour authorization-record duty is never a rule of its own. The approved-supplier rule is widened to all purchases. R8 is given a pre-commitment deadline the SOP does not state, then waived wholesale by ECD-2.

Case results follow those errors. P-508 is called COMPLIANT at high confidence with no missing evidence. At EUR 155,000 the email’s substitution cap is already exceeded, Legal clearance is additional, and there is still no Finance Controller or independent budget-owner approval before commitment. A6 does not let the later narrative cure that gap. P-502 is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION while the same row admits pre-commitment compliance was required and failed; on that premise the primary label is NONCOMPLIANT, with only supplier register and PO timing capable of a narrower exception. The row also marks the emergency form satisfied although no form is in the file, and it never fixes the calendar: the commitment evidence belongs to 14 July, before the 15 July email. P-501 is COMPLIANT at high confidence in a row that simultaneously cites A6 for the proposition that a later approval does not cure. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate; approver identity is unknown; “missing evidence: none” is untenable. P-506 correctly rejects the verbal overnight extension, then reverses the record: Finance approval at 06:05 preceded the 06:20 commitment, so “Finance after commitment” is false. P-505’s INDETERMINATE label attaches only to the 18:00 boundary and ignores facts that are adverse either way: Finance approval at 19:00, no Plant approval, and no shown budget owner. Its “strongest” alternative, full COMPLIANT, is not the strongest alternative. Assumption 5 says a contemporaneous supplier acknowledgment outweighs a late ERP stamp; section 5 then refuses to use the 17:59:31 acknowledgment.

Section 5 tests the wrong flank on P-502 and rejects, for P-507, an alternative it has just called highly credible because the email is explicit. It never stress-tests P-501 or P-508. Section 6 sorts P-501’s later approvals and P-502’s later compliance into “late evidence of a timely act.” Those are late acts. P-507’s signed PDF versus the 14:30 ERP stamp is the one place that distinction is handled well. Personal cost recovery from SHIFT-2 is an unsupported remedy. Section 7 is the most usable part: it preserves segregation and compliance, confines a CFO finance waiver to a written instrument at or under EUR 150,000, requires CRO ratification, and adds a post-incident review. That draft would condemn P-507 and P-508 as the memo decided them, which shows the case table was not applying the memo’s own best reading.
