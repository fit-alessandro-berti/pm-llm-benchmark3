3.5

The write-up has the required eight sections and names the right phenomena (seasonality, calendars, batching, applicant delay, objections as a proxy). It is not a usable twin design: core quantities are wrong, queueing mechanisms contradict the interventions, and the skeleton misses the required PM4Py contract and would not run.

**Scope.** Decision drafting is placed after a wait for legal check. In the given tree, legal check is optional and comes after drafting; drafting must wait for the applicable planning, fire, environmental, public-notice, and inspection results. The planner calendar is fused with the fire/environmental Tuesday–Thursday calendar. Parallel `+` / loop `*` semantics are not a state machine: there is no AND-join state, no loop exit, no appeal path, and no split between timer delays (applicant response, statutory public notice) and resource-seizing work. D4 and D7 are written into the baseline boundary.

**Estimation.** 31,200 cases over 18 months is about 1,733 cases per month, not 173. That 10× error feeds the backlog target later. Waiting time is defined as end minus start (that is service, when both timestamps exist), then as `queue_length * mean_service_time`, then in code as a timestamp diff within an activity. Historical queue length times mean service cannot respond to staffing or policy changes, so it cannot compare D1–D10. The 9% “inspection rework variants” share and the 8% complex-variance share are treated as activity rework probabilities. Fire review is given the D2 €50,000 value cut, and D3/D5 travel districts are treated as the environmentally sensitive set. `Receive missing documents` is a zero-duration portal event; the heavy tail is the applicant clock (median about 5 days), which is then given a Gamma with median 0 and p90 0. Gamma(α=2, β=median/p90) is not a fit to median and p90, and α=2 is not a heavy tail. PM4Py is named, but estimation is ad-hoc dataframe aggregation, not variants, a directly-follows graph, a performance DFG, or case-arrival statistics.

**Assumptions.** “Poisson with gamma inter-arrivals” is not Poisson. Weekend portal arrivals appear in the excerpt and are omitted. A fixed +46 days for a latent objection turns the 14-versus-long public-notice mixture into a point mass and treats a missing event as a binary covariate. Council weeks are called log-identified even though calendars come from HR. Effect sizes (30% Monday smoothing, rework cut to 5%, and so on) are stated as facts. Service-time independence plus an algebraic wait formula leaves resource contention, calendars, and cross-case coupling outside the generative model.

**Experiments.** D1–D10, a baseline, and three stresses are listed, but levels are single invented points, not factors. Several “trade-offs” change the wrong variable: an extra inspector is said to cut inspection service from 1h to 30min; district batching is said to cut travel from 8d to 3d, but 8d is the median wait before inspection, not travel. D10 is D1+D2+D3+D4, yet the stated synergy includes parallel legal checks (D5). Stress 1 adds inspectors and stress 3 turns on D2, so they are confounded. Cycle-time boundaries for D1 and D7 (clock starts at pre-submission effort versus at portal submit) are undefined. No common random numbers.

**Uncertainty.** Warm-up is “100 cases or 10% of the arrival rate,” which mixes a count with a rate and is a few days against cycle times of 12–260 days. Run length is never set. Seasonal load does not have a flat steady state to warm up into, and an empty system is the wrong initial WIP. One `random.seed(42)` makes replications identical. “90% credible intervals” are claimed from simulation replicates, while MCMC and inverse-probability weighting are named without a likelihood, priors, or a selection model. Start-time missingness is not a propensity-weighting problem.

**Validation.** Face validity is defined as matching historical medians. Replay is “simulate 10%,” not conformance of the tree to the log or a controlled generative comparison. An 80% planner load is a validation target with no source. Log gaps are treated as queue lengths, although gaps mix wait, closed calendars, and parallel branches. A random 20% case holdout is not out-of-sample under shared resources and seasonality; a temporal holdout is required. No acceptance margins are set before intervention ranking. A KS test at this sample size will reject trivial gaps.

**Decision criteria.** Weights sum to 120%. The required tail metric is absent, and the prose prefers the median to the p90 in a process whose pain is the tail. SLA is one 90-day bar, not by case type. Architect fairness is missing. D2’s inspection-skip risk (safety, appeals, later rework) is not a criterion. A backlog cap of 500 cases is inconsistent with Little’s law at the stated volume: on the order of a couple of thousand cases of WIP, not 500. The 500 figure only looks plausible if the 173/month rate is kept. A weighted score is presented as a selection rule for an uncalibrated model.

**Code.** The skeleton does not build a dataframe from the excerpt, does not call `pm4py.format_dataframe`, and does not call `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. Imports include non-APIs (`ConceptData`, `process_tree.convert_from_string`, `pm4py.algo.conversion.process_tree.process_tree`). Renaming `concept:name` to `case:concept:name` destroys the activity column. “Variants” are case-type counts; the “DFG” is activity frequencies; a performance DFG and case-arrival average are absent. Service time is case span; waiting time is a within-activity diff. `generate_arrivals` walks events, computes a weight it never uses, and assigns no timestamps. There is no routing placeholder. Clerk Mondays are marked unavailable. `run_experiment` reads `total_completion_time` and calls `.values()` on a list. Cross-case batching and calendars are not on the execution path. The log path and the engine are not actually separated.

The design would send the municipality the wrong volume, the wrong levers (service time instead of waiting and timers), and a ranking score that does not add to 100%. Structure alone does not offset that.
