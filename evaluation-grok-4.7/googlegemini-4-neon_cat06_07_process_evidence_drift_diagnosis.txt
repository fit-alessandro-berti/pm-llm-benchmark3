The audit correctly separates the period into four mechanisms: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September combine a parts-edge performance shift with concept drift, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable because maturity is 55% and censoring hits long paths. Deductions are for specific overclaims and sketch defects, not for the overall diagnosis. May’s 2.0% unknown activity and 3% missing queue are full-month figures, not measurements of 1–5 May only. July’s ECE drop is asserted as proven cancellation of June’s V2 over-prediction by V3 under-prediction; aggregate calibration only shows predicted 0.18 versus observed 0.19, and no stratum-level evidence is given. August is listed as covariate drift even though mean predicted risk stays flat at 0.19, which cuts against a shift in observed \(X\). April’s within-variant moves are not a uniform +0.02 (V1 0.15→0.18, V3 0.30→0.34). Concept drift is called severe for all of July–October, which overstates July and treats October’s labels as stronger than the process proxy they are. The event-calendar performance DFG drops any `Order Part → Part Received` pair that crosses a month boundary, so the monitor under-samples the exact latency it is meant to catch; `tz_localize(None)` on UTC-aware timestamps and string bounds passed to `filter_time_range` are fragile. Those are localized faults in an otherwise well-triangulated answer.
