8.4

The comparison is unusually careful and mostly right: the visible languages, the one-revision overlap, the check-count split for two or more analyses, the four witnesses, the P3 cardinalities (76 vs 85, intersection 4), and the standard net sizes are all correctly derived. The verdicts themselves are the right ones. The remaining defects are local but real, and they sit in the equivalence argument and in code that is presented as operational.

The main logical slip is the claim that erasing Check and Approve is a sharper projection than P2, and that P2 equality “follows” because P2 is coarser. Those maps are incomparable. The skeleton projection deletes every check, while P2 keeps the Boolean “a check occurred” and throws away Analyze/Rework order. Equality of skeletons does not imply equality of dashboard records; a language with no Check at all would have the same skeleton image and a different record. The conclusion still holds here, but only because both models separately force at least one Check and set the rework count to \(n_A-1\). Section 3’s “hence” makes the same jump. That is an invalid refinement argument in the section whose job is to define the observation boundary.

P1-equivalence is also not cleanly defined. Requiring language equality and compliance with the four rules makes the relation fail reflexivity on any model that breaks the rules, including Model B. The “exactly \(L_A\)” characterization depends on an extra intra-attempt grammar that the four bullets do not state. The later caveat admits this, but the definition itself overclaims.

The bounded search is a genuine BFS over `(marking, visible word)` with silent steps hidden, bounds applied monotonically, and final-marking equality required, so the exhaustive-vs-playout distinction is sound and the expected counts match the tree language. Two code claims are weaker. `play_out` is called with `no_traces` and `max_trace_length`; current PM4Py playout keys are `noTraces` and `maxTraceLength`, so those arguments are ignored. The declared uniform-policy walker drops the probability share of every skipped or self-loop transition instead of renormalizing at the marking, and it never aggregates paths by state, so it is not a correct implementation of the policy it describes. The prose correctly says a small EMD need not yield trace equivalence; the sketch does not reliably compute that EMD.

Smaller issues: the structural cell that says Check may sit “anywhere” omits the no-approval slots between Analyze and Rework and between a completed iteration and the next Rework, and “identifies the two languages” is looser than the projection result. The simplified P1-Seq tree, its 11-node / 7-place / 15/17-simplicity comparison, and the preservation and regression tables are accurate and appropriately weaker than full \(L_A\).
