7.8

The transport argument is strong: Site A’s before/after contrast is treated as a confounded bundle, statutory originals and legal gates are preserved, and the pilot separates execution fidelity from effect. The required sections, columns, node set, word bounds, and “conditional go” choice are met. Arithmetic that is shown (FTE completions, Fréchet bound on the no-originals/no-legal intersection, critical-path identities, sampling SEs, 13-week support) is correct.

The quantitative headline is looser than that standard. For a typical originals case, the answer’s own fixed-duration skeleton shows a saving of at most the 3.4 h identity median, and 0 h if identity already runs during the 18 h receipt wait — which Site B’s rules already allow and which a 62% originals share makes median-relevant. S1 nevertheless puts the all-case median at −7 to −3 h by adding up to 4 h of “queue improvement,” motivated by Site A’s confounded 6 h wait drop. That pads a transport scenario with evidence the rest of the answer refuses to transport. S1’s p90 band of −15 to −4 h has the same problem: originals-delay variance is the obvious tail driver and is not removed by parallel release. S3 and the mean/p90 numerical example are coherent; S1 is not.

Two data facts that should have disciplined those ranges are unused. Originals Receipt → Document Check is 2,108, equal to 62% of 3,400, and Assessment → Legal Review is 1,360, equal to 40%. Those edges are the cleanest structural anchors in the extract. Separately, 3.4 h and 8.1 h cannot be pure touch labor: 3,400 × 3.4 h already exceeds four identity reviewers’ six-month capacity by several times, so the median “service” figures are elapsed times. The scenarios never stress that distinction.

The POWL specification itself is the right partial order: identity is incomparable with originals and document checking, document checking waits for originals, assessment joins both branches, and the legal supplement loop can repeat document checking and assessment. The Python does not implement that specification on PM4Py 2.7’s actual API. `StrictPartialOrder.order` is a NetworkX digraph; `add_transitive_edges`, `is_strict_partial_order`, and `is_edge` are not methods, so `build_site_b` raises before any net is built. `check_soundness` is called as if it returned `(bool, diagnostics)` without `return_diagnostics=True`; the documented default is a bool, so the unpack fails even after the order calls are removed. Playout is present but switched off. The script was not run, and the cited utils/analysis pages do not document the invented order methods.

Smaller design limits: week-level saturation inside one shared pool is too entangled by carryover to identify the interference the table asks for, and the quality margins (±2 and ±1 percentage points) are already shown to be out of reach at ~300 per arm. Those limits are mostly disclosed. The broken runnable model and the optimistic scenario’s reuse of non-transportable Site A magnitudes are not.
