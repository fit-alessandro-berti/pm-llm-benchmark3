7.4

The final design is methodologically serious and avoids the main failure modes: it does not restrict training to the 21,600 labeled activations, does not code rejects or withdrawals as non-defaults, keeps an as-of prefix, and uses a temporal split across the policy and migration breaks. The nine required sections are present, the cohort table sums to 48,000, and the leakage table correctly drops post-decision and post-outcome fields.

The estimand is still not tight enough. \(E\) is defined as activation plus a 90-day default, while the reported quantity is “\(E\) by \(t_p+H\)” without defining the event time or requiring the 90-day window and the 7–35 day feed delay to finish inside \(H\). That gap becomes a validation error. With extraction on 2026-02-15, a 365-day horizon is not observable for starts on or after 2025-07-01, yet that post-policy window is the primary test set. Censoring-aware metrics are named, but the design never states that \(F(365)\) is unidentified there. Cause-specific hazards are also mixed with IPCW without separating a cause-specific/Aalen–Johansen model from a Fine–Gray weighting scheme.

The 3,600 not-yet-mature activations are merged with permanently missing partner feeds, which the brief treats as different mechanisms. In the skeleton, `cohort_state` is merged into the saved feature table and is missed by the leakage assert; cases whose prediction time falls before every event can disappear; and no censoring or event time is built. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and unused, and the single “earliest of 4 events or 7 days” rule never actually estimates the two prefix policies separately.
