7.3

The forecast is complete, balanced, and well matched to the required headings. It treats nominal handover cuts as potentially illusory, separates East from North/South, and refuses a full rollout on early averages. The score is held down by a contradictory pilot, a misleading PM4Py sketch, and a few local logic slips.

The pilot cannot identify the effect it claims to test. An East-only “Technical Shared Service” with one embedded specialist is a relabeling, not pooling. Moving North/South specialists into that pool changes South, so South is no longer an untreated difference-in-differences control. The design states both “pool technical capacity” and “South does not change,” and never resolves the conflict. Case-volume “600+” / “900+” over 12 weeks is also far below the stated monthly volumes.

The PM4Py sketch would not run and misstates several measures. `format_dataframe` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are called with `resource_key`, which those functions do not take. `discover_performance_dfg(..., variant="case")` is not a valid aggregation. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` has no `activity=` argument and counts repeated activities, not visits to an activity named Rework. `get_variants` returns a dict, so `top_variants[0][0]` is wrong, and passing one variant tuple into `filter_variants` does not select that variant. The log has only one timestamp, so `get_service_time` is undefined, and that limit is not stated. Team filters also ignore cross-team cases, which the scenario says exist.

Smaller faults: the optimistic “early warning signs” list healthy states under a double negative, so the reader cannot tell what would falsify that scenario. “Pink-type” role clustering is not a PM4Py method. “Utilisation >100%” is loose; the real claim is that arrivals above capacity make backlog grow without bound. The 14-day SLA is unexplained relative to the reported cycle times.
