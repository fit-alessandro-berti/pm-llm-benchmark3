6.7

The conservative decisions are mostly right, and the required sections, counts for the recommended path, and the partial-payment warning are in place. The write-up is not reliable as a pipeline: several ordering rules contradict each other, the Python does not implement the order it describes, and the account of `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` is specific where it is wrong.

The decision tree correctly separates technical duplicates, multi-system observations, summaries, harmonization collisions, true concurrency, and unresolved pairs. Keeping e05 and e14, refusing to fold e15 into one capture, and relabeling e08/e09 instead of merging them are the right conservative calls. Merging only e03/e04 and e11/e16 yields 15 events and 39 E2O relations (44 − 3 − 2). The `ocel_merge_duplicates` count of 10 events and 28 relations is also arithmetically right, and the explanation that `have_common_object=True` still merges e13 with e14 because they share O1 and V1 is correct.

Ordering is not stable under the answer’s own rules. R3 is defined as semantic inside one source, then the 12:00 alternatives put e14 before e12 or between e12 and e13, which breaks PSP `source_seq` 400 ≺ 401 ≺ 402. R6 is defined as lexicographic `(source, source_seq, eid)`, which places SCANNER/e05 before WMS/e03; the stated 10:00 order and `TIE_RANK` do the opposite and attribute that direction to R2/R3, which cannot compare those two sources. The code then labels both picks `R6-fallback`. `Issue Invoice ≺ Authorize` is treated as an asserted lifecycle constraint; the known facts only force e12 ≺ e13 and place e15 after the payment messages. e11 is chosen as canonical partly because it was ingested earlier, after the text itself says ingestion is not business order.

`establish_order` sorts by timestamp, tie rank, and fallback, then calls `ocel_sort_by_additional_column` on `tie_rank`. That rank restarts at 0 in every timestamp cluster, so a sort keyed by `tie_rank` interleaves 09:00, 10:00, 10:15, and 12:00 before `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` runs. Scenario B is copied from that already-adjusted log, so e13 and e15 no longer share a timestamp and the later library drop cannot see them as duplicates. `TIE_RANK` / `SEMANTIC_RULE` are hardcoded; the cascade is not computed.

`ocel_drop_duplicates` keeps one event per equal activity, timestamp, and related-object set. It does not deduplicate at `(activity, timestamp, object_id)` and then delete events that lost every relation. Globally that removes e04, e09, and e16 only: 14 events and 36 relations, with e05 and e14 intact. It does not remove e07 or e15, and it does not leave e05 holding only I2 or e14 holding only M2. The real loss is e09, because harmonized `Dispatch Package` plus an identical object set looks like a duplicate of e08. Scenario D, the scoped `{e13, e15}` drop, the “13 events, 35 relations” comment, failure mode 9, and the sensitivity paragraph that depends on stripped survivors are therefore wrong. Section 7 also widens the evidence-based result to 13–14 events and 35–39 relations after section 5 fixed it at 13 and 35. Handover at 10:15 to confirmation at 09:00 the next day is 22 h 45 min, not 17 h 45 min. Raw same-activity self-loops are asserted as if tied events were already adjacent; within a tie they exist only under a chosen serialization.

Provenance in prose is thorough, and `ocel_raw` is left untouched. The code’s global `PROVENANCE` list is shared across scenarios, omits hashes, ingestion times, and adjusted timestamps promised in section 8, and `ocel_merge_duplicates` is not shown to retain the extra columns it was supposed to protect. `pm4py.filter_ocel_events` is not the supported filter API.
