The write-up has the required nine sections, correct midpoint service expectations (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.5, Validation 15.74), and a real structural reading of the day: Emil is the only software resource in 16:00–18:00, and no mechanical resource remains after 18:00. The hybrid recommendation is also aimed at tail risk and fairness, which is the right objective family. Those merits do not carry the quantitative core, the scenario matrix, or the required code.

Offered load is not auditable and is wrong for half the day. Diagnostic work from the stated mix and those expectations is about 424 minutes in 14:00–16:00, 256 in 16:00–18:00, and 124 in 18:00–20:00. The answer gives 470, 210, and 80. Full-shift minutes (450/240) are stated, but breaks are not placed in blocks, and speed multipliers are not applied to the load. “Intake and Validation are ample in every block” ignores 18:00–20:00, when Emil is the only person on duty. Assumption A6 subtracts the 30-minute overtime cap from capacity; overtime is extra capacity. Deepa’s 30-minute overtime at a 1.25 mechanical multiplier covers less than one expected mechanical job (about 33 minutes).

Shock arithmetic does not follow the scenario definitions. Doubling E and ES and renormalizing does not raise electrical jobs by ~80% and ~90%; with fixed arrivals the increase is on the order of 20%, and even if those case counts double while other types stay fixed it is roughly two-thirds, not the figures used. U4’s service increases are about +12.5%, +13%, and +16%, not +11%, +12%, and +15%, and equating that service increase to a 10–15% delay increase ignores queueing near saturation. Chen’s absence is described as losing 450 minutes of electrical and 450 minutes of software; those minutes are one shared shift. The “~30% software” loss is also too small for 12:00–16:00, when Chen is one of only two software resources.

Policy A is not strict specialization: Ada, Chen, and Farah remain eligible for more than one diagnostic family. Policy C’s score adds a utilization fraction to `0.5 × handover minutes`, so a 7-minute handover swamps utilization and the rule is not an implementable workload balance. The comparison table omits U4 entirely and omits U2 and U3 for the recommended policy, while presenting SLA and delay bands as bounds from a simulation the code never runs. The U3 rationale says Farah can absorb Chen’s software work; Farah leaves at 12:00, and after 16:00 Chen’s absence does not change software coverage at all.

The Python block misses most of its specification. Shifts, breaks, and U1–U5 are not encoded. `capacity_block` is an empty stub and is never called. Handover, setup, and coordination constants are unused; preemption and parallel-versus-sequential choice do not appear. There is no policy-by-scenario table and no repeated-seed summary. The event log draws random durations, labels every diagnostic activity “Diagnostic,” and keeps one random resource for the whole case, including unqualified resources, so the handover network is vacuous. `get_service_time` is treated as a frame with `.head()`. Case-level PM4Py service and waiting time are neither labeled nor split into stage or queue metrics. The omitted mechanisms are not identified, so this does not qualify as a specified scaffold.

Monitoring omits queue entry, the assignment decision, the eligible-resource set, and the SLA clock. Section 9 then calls the same scaffold auditable for drift and bottleneck migration. The pilot also does not handle interference from routing 20% of cases differently on a shared resource pool.
