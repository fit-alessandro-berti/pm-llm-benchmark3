The core fork-join accounting is right and unusually complete: the max-based identities, the double-count argument, the 12-case table, the four worked cases, critical-branch counts (A 2, B 8, C 2), and the case-level join savings for the A −20% and B-queue −50% counterfactuals all check out. The answer also correctly refuses to treat A’s 50-minute mean service as control of completion, and the two-server pooled FIFO totals (171 minutes, mean flow 115.25 → 101.0) are reproducible.

It is not nearly flawless. Several published statistics are wrong. Sample standard deviation of sojourn is about 13.71 for B and 12.34 for C, not 12.85 and 11.39. A linear P90 of sojourn is 74 and 53, not 73.0 and 49.3. “D_B ≥ 60 in 5 cases” is false; seven cases are at or above 60. Downstream-queue standard deviation is about 3.78, not 3.82. The near-tie rule is stated as ≤5 minutes and then includes F04 at 7.

Counterfactual interpretation overreaches. Only F11’s A-service gain is clearly absorbed by F1 still being busy until 11:50. F01 has no prior F1 job, so “0 net flow-time reduction” and “completely absorbed” are a hypothesis stated as a result. Recommendation 1 says both “queue elimination” and “F07 drops by 15 minutes”; full removal of B’s 30-minute queue on F07 saves 25 minutes until A’s 10:30 finish, and C’s sojourns of 57 and 55 remain, so “worst-case stage time falls to 50–55” is not true. The ≈30% capacity gain and ≈15% throughput gain are not derived. Arrivals are essentially every 15 minutes against two 20-minute servers, so the line is arrival-limited; those figures conflate flow time with throughput. SMED and drum-buffer-rope are prescribed before the cause of B’s queue is identified, which section 7 itself says the timestamps cannot show.

Assumptions are often explained but not labeled. The script implements the three simple counterfactuals and the required PM4Py calls, but it does not reproduce the pooling result or the faulty tail statistics in the prose.
