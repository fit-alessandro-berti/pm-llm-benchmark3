7.5

The design is unusually complete: hard rules are separated from preferences, the skill-0 training plans are caught correctly, high- and urgent-priority work stays at skill 2–3, and the audit section treats reassignment, confounding, and worker override as real measurement problems. The score is held down by factual and internal inconsistencies that a strict reading cannot treat as polish.

The continuity claim is wrong. Ten cases keep their owner (C002, C004, C005, C007, C008, C009, C011, C014, C015, C016), five move (C001, C003, C006, C010, C012), and C013 is new. Section 4.2 and the worker note both say eleven stayed, and the comparison text names only three moves, dropping C006 and C012. That batch also misses the answer’s own continuity watch of at least 70 percent, with no comment.

The hour model does not add up, yet the riskiest decision rests on it. Farid is given C002 at 0.7h, C011 at 1.0h, and C010 at about 0.5h, which sums to about 2.2h, while the load table charges him 2.7h and omits the C003 shadow. Hugo’s C001 is described as 1.4h of work, which is consistent with Gita’s 1.2h on C008, but not with Hugo’s 2.9h once C015 is included. C012 is “roughly 1 hour” in the narrative and closer to 1.5h under the stated skill factor. Base hours are never published, so the table’s precise utilizations cannot be reproduced.

Those utilizations also count only this batch. Hugo already has 7 open cases and 5.5 hours; Gita has 10 open and 4.5 hours; Iris has 11 open. Labeling Gita a 26 percent idle reserve and Iris 0 percent utilization treats the backlog as free capacity. The same open-case counts are used elsewhere as evidence of overload, so the capacity check and the narrative use two different models.

The 20 percent training result is fragile. It is 2 of 9 only because Bruno, a high-overtime resource, executes C012. That case is low priority with 48 hours of slack, and safer quality executors exist, so the scenario’s exception (urgent, and no safer alternative) is not met. Farid, whose documented priority is diagnosis and whose overtime risk is low, only shadows C003 instead of executing a normal diagnosis under supervision. C010 is the case marked for supervised approval practice, and the exception ladder’s first step is prepare-plus-finalize, but the case is fully transferred to Farid, who already holds both urgent C002 and urgent C011. C015 is the slack security case that could move David from Security 2 to 3, which section 3.4 itself calls the main bottleneck fix; the batch instead keeps Hugo on it and gives Jonas, who is Security 0, observation only.

The scoring function cannot be audited against the queue. The seven weights are never given, Wave B’s assignment costs are never shown, and Phase 5’s required runner-up and top-three factors are absent. The recommendations read as careful judgment attached to an algorithm after the fact. The first-available story is similarly over-specific: C011 to Jonas, four-plus cases dumped on Jonas, C007 forced to a fourth owner, and C016 pulled off Eva all depend on processing order and an unstated tie-break.

The worker text is clear and non-punitive, and it also states that a repeated override “has already happened once,” which is not in the evidence. In the PM4Py sketch, setting the start timestamp equal to `time:timestamp` collapses service time on a log that has only one timestamp, so `empirical_competence` cannot calibrate the skill factor the policy says it learns. Rework is attributed without temporal order, the dumping index does not match its definition, the near-miss code uses a one-hour cutoff against a 10 percent-of-window definition, and training share is an event-level mean rather than a share of normal/low assignments.
