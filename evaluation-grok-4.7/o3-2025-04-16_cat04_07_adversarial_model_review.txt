4.0

The review is complete in outline and sometimes directionally right, but it is not reliable enough for a governance board. Several central claims about what the models accept are false, the proposed model does not implement the semantics asserted for it, and the validation program misuses a core metric.

**Operator and language errors.** In this process-tree notation, `+` is parallel composition, not inclusive OR, and `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` is exactly one approval. Model S therefore satisfies P1 and P5, allows each of the four middle activities at most once, and does not contain `Correct Dossier` at all. The review asserts the opposite on P5, attributes permissiveness to an “OR,” and fails the required distinction between deliberate skip-noise (`X(..., tau)`) and accidental loss of order. Repeated checks and correction traces are not in S’s language, yet §3, B6, B8, ADV-1, and ADV-3 mark them accepted. ADV-1 is also impossible in S because approval sits after the parallel block, so Expedite cannot follow it.

**Model C probes.** C is an exclusive choice of eight concrete sequences, not a loop. B2 (`Technical Review` then `Credit Check` then `Auto Approval`) matches no branch; the cited “2nd branch” ends in `Manager Approval`. B6 rechecks only `Technical Review`; branch 7 rechecks both and is not the same trace. Both are marked accepted. Branch 6 already contains Supplier then Expedite, so “Expedite-after-Supplier is missing” is false. What is missing is Expedite before Supplier, plus the Auto Approval combinations. Calling the single correction sequence a loop overstates the model.

**Third model does not mean what §7 and §8 say.** The PM4Py fragment is not runnable: `ProcessTreeOperator` is undefined, `ProcessTree` has no `node_type`, a leaf labeled `"tau"` is visible, a loop is given three children, and parents are never linked. Even if repaired to a two-child loop, approval is inside the do-body, so a correction can occur only after an approval and every correction adds another approval. That violates P5 and rejects the ordinary pattern correct-then-recheck-then-one-approval, including B6, which the audit scores as accepted. Expedite is neither “at most once” nor free relative to Supplier; the sequence forces Supplier before Expedite, and ADV-2 treats that policy-valid order as a defect. P4 explicitly allows both orders. The P7 “flag” is not implemented; those traces are simply outside the language. The supplier guard is a no-op when `source` is absent, which is the actual data situation, while the net still makes Supplier optional. That is the silent-optionalization move the task forbids, and §8 still reports P3 as solved.

**Metrics and validation.** The metric table and the ranking of fitness-by-optionality, positive-only precision, and split risk are the strongest part, but missing-event bias and runtime confounding by model size are only glancingly covered. §9 never specifies unseen-combination tests or alignment-cost sensitivity. Stratification keys on an attribute the log does not have. `simplicity_petri_net` is called without the initial and final markings. The decision rule’s “precision on the negative suite” inverts the metric: alignment precision against forbidden traces does not measure rejection of those traces.

**What still holds.** Use-case separation, refusal to average the scores, the memorization reading of C’s precision and holdout drop, S’s real failures on mandatory checks and Expedite-before-checks, and the dissent’s length and substance are sound. The dissent is aimed at the right risks, but it inherits the false “flags rather than rejects” account of rework. The governance split (S for exploration only; do not enforce with C) is a defensible conclusion reached through an incorrect behavioral audit and attached to a compliance candidate that fails P4 and P5.
