The conservative case split is mostly right, but the write-up repeatedly gets the effects of merging backwards, miscounts relations, and ships code that cannot produce the pipelines it describes.

The decision tree covers every required terminal class, and the pair table makes the right calls on the traps that matter: merge e03/e04 and e11/e16, keep e05 and e14, treat e08/e09 as a label collision, keep e06/e07 and the payment summaries as abstraction-level events, and put e09 before e10 and e12 before e13. Those strengths do not survive the later sections. Evidence-based merging claims three further deletions and 12 events, but e13/e15 and e14/e15 share one summary event, so e15 can be removed only once (15 − 2 = 13). The same scenario says merging inflates rework; merging reduces a repeated activity to one occurrence.
