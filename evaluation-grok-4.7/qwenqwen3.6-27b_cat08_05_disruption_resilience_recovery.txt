6.5

The final answer covers all seven required sections, uses the phase metrics and fitness scores accurately in most places, and reaches a sound management conclusion: protect hospital supply with explicit triage, keep a manual authorization path, and refuse authorization bypass, unscanned dispatch, and after-the-fact remediation as a stand-in for live control.

The adaptation table is the strongest operational product. C is correctly retired, D is correctly time-limited, G and H are correctly tightened for chain of custody, and K/L are correctly treated as residual workload. Section 7 matches that judgment.

Several final claims are still inaccurate or over-specific. Dispatch without scan is cited as 1,800 cases; that is only the Manual pack arc, while Temporary release control adds another 1,450, about 3,250 unscanned handoffs, of which 2,600 later have a phone proof. The 4.8% cold-chain gap and 7.8% cancellation rate are phase totals, but the table presents them as the result of F and I. Section 3 then says C, G, and H “directly caused” the 9.5%, 4.8%, and 2.9% gaps. Those are joint outcomes.

T4 is described as operationally near baseline because the hospital median returned to 8h. Pharmacy median/p90 are still 28h/74h versus 23h/49h, and wholesale 58h/120h versus 36h/72h; traceability, cold-chain, dual-control, rework, and cancellation all remain about double baseline or higher. The audit tail is recognized, including 2,300 cases, but the 200 remediation cases still open and the variable phone-proof delay are not.

Section 3 calls A, B, I, and J true resilience that preserved compliance fundamentals. The fast-lane path itself skips the manual authorization check (1,000 in, 920 straight to the snapshot). The table and closing paragraph later require a control on that lane, so the decision is repaired, but the classification section still equates a control-skipping priority path with resilience.

The recovery model includes every requested element, yet several design choices fight the evidence. Dual signature on every order ignores the compliance-capacity bottleneck that produced 1,600 skipped checks. Customer priority and product risk are merged, so a wholesale controlled order can outrank pharmacy-standard demand and repeat the 61% pharmacy fill collapse. A 48-hour backlog cap for hospital-critical work is loose against an 8h/15h baseline. Triggers, the 500-case daily cap, and the 5% remediation cap are unanchored. T3’s distinct failure mode—systems up, sync and scans unreliable—is not a separate control state. The fairness target of 1.0 : 0.85 : 0.70 builds in unequal service. Recommendation benefits of 9.5% to under 2% exceptions and 61% to about 80% pharmacy fill are presented as expected results without a basis.
