4.5

The write-up has the required seven sections and a complete A–L table, and it correctly treats C and G as unacceptable and notices the post-recovery compliance tail. Those merits do not offset repeated numeric, temporal, and classification errors.

The timeline misstates baseline conformance: fitness 0.96 is not “96% of traces fitting”; the fitting-trace rate is 91%. T0 rates are rounded (0.8%, 3.5%, 2.1% become 1%, 4%, 2%), and cycle times are omitted. T1 is described as already running emergency triage, which belongs to T2. Phase-level T2 rates are pinned on single adaptations: 9.5% on C, 4.8% temperature gaps on E, and 2.9% dual-control gaps on F. G, not C or E, is the traceability problem, and F is the control that was meant to limit controlled-substance failures. T3 places backlog prioritization, manual reconciliation, and audit remediation in the partial-recovery window; those activities are evidenced in the stabilization directly-follows graph. Calling T3 cycle times “close to baseline” is false: hospital median 9h versus 8h is close, but pharmacy 38h versus 23h and wholesale 84h versus 36h are not, and the p90 gaps are larger. T4 is called operationally stable while pharmacy and wholesale medians and p90s, traceability (2.2% versus 0.8%), rework (5.8% versus 3.5%), and fitting traces (76% versus 91%) remain materially worse.

The table repeats that attribution error and adds others. C is given “evidence of resilience” for preserving throughput and revenue, which is the prohibited reading. F is said to have reduced controlled-substance failures because missing dual control was 2.9% versus a 0.2% baseline; that is a large worsening, not a reduction. D invents a two-hour staleness limit for a snapshot that covered an outage lasting into Day 2, and an hourly refresh is recommended without explaining how a snapshot is refreshed while the warehouse system is down. B is scoped to pharmacy and wholesale and marked Retire, although manual authorization is the compliant substitute for the skipped system check. G is scoped mainly to urgent hospital and controlled orders even though dispatch-without-scan is the dominant pack path, and it is marked Retire while the rationale describes a modified evidence path. K’s “bury reconciliation” wording conceals the hidden workload the case requires to be made visible. L is marked Keep as resilience despite the explicit point that after-the-fact remediation is not real-time compliance. “HL7-tiered risk” is not a prioritization scheme supported by this case.

Section 3 then contradicts the table. B is Retire in the table and ambiguous later; I is Modify in the table and a risky shortcut later; F, K, and L are labeled true resilience. Modify appears in every bucket, so the taxonomy does not separate resilience, shortcuts, and appetite-dependent choices. The closing distinction is conceptually sound and is not applied to the classifications above it.

The recovery model names a trigger, tiers, evidence, backlog, and remediation, but the tiering collapses customer criticality and product type into one axis. Recall-sensitive goods never appear. Cold-chain temperature evidence is absent from both minimum controls and evidence capture, while the prose claims temperature documentation never disappears. Medium-priority paths have no release, temperature, or dual-control gate, and there is no stop-ship rule when minimum evidence cannot be produced. The Day 3–6 hazard—intake restored while inventory sync and scanning are unreliable—is missing. Section 4 also reinstates B after Section 2 retires it, and the fast lane omits the manual authorization checklist Section 2 required.

Indicators fabricate a 96% baseline hospital fill rate, segment cancellation and rework targets, and a “Day27” window. A target of 90% of hospital orders inside 8 hours is incoherent with a baseline whose median is 8 hours and whose p90 is 15 hours. Dual-control performance is tagged to C and G. Cold-chain documentation and the age of the audit-remediation queue are absent. Leading indicators are drills and quarterly surveys, then described as real-time visibility. Fairness is a satisfaction score instead of the observed 91% versus 61% fill split and the cycle-time divergence.

Recommendations 1–3 do not match the claim that all three attack authorization and traceability gaps; the snapshot item is inventory freshness. Standing authorization and hourly snapshots are treated as available during a warehouse-system outage. The tradeoff paragraph does not state the service disparity management would be accepting, nor that an open remediation queue means recovery is incomplete. “The current set” is ambiguous between the incident workarounds and the redesign, and the two-hour restoration, zero false positives, and 0.2% alert false-positive tests are ungrounded decision thresholds.
