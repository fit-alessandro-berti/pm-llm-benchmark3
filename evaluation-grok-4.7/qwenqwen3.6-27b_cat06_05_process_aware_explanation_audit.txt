The audit correctly catches the main future-information failures: closed-case duration, any-time appeal, closure team, full-trace document counts and variant IDs, post-t0 sequence patterns, mutable risk score, last role, extraction-time prior-claim SQL, and the nightly document snapshot. It also keeps queue length, registration complexity, and elapsed time, separates appeal-then-decision as a legitimate later transition from the reversed close/assessment pattern, and labels Case Z invalid. The required checks, signal classes, JSON shape, and validation plan are present.

Those strengths do not make the result reliable. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` never appear in the mandatory finding table, then both are promoted to credible drivers without a lineage decision. `total_case_duration_hours` is treated as ordinary post-closure leakage rather than a near-transform of the 10-day label. The revised Case X and Case Y narratives still explain the original high probabilities as driven by the surviving features, even though appeal, final decision, full variant, and the mixed base/other term dominate the supplied attributions and the status is “recompute.” All three prefixes are identical, so a prefix variant cannot discriminate them; that point is missed. “Document requests are always 0,” fraud is “not yet enabled/triggered,” and week 52 is not tied to the stated holiday/policy confounding are smaller but real inaccuracies. Employee-experience as-of-extraction leakage is omitted, and several interpretations assert mechanisms while the causal boundary tries to withdraw them.
