The diagnosis is right in its main lines: April is a schema and pipeline failure with intact outcomes, June is a post-decision routing mix shift, August–September stack supplier-driven path deterioration with a smaller Partner Z shift, and October is not comparable because labels are immature. The six required sections are all present, the O/D/H split is mostly disciplined, falsifiers are concrete, and the retrain gates correctly refuse September–October binary labels. The score is held down by overclaims and a few quantitative or code defects, not by the overall story.

The clearest analytical overclaim is July. Aggregate calibration (0.18 vs 0.19, ECE 0.042) is treated as a proven cancellation between over-predicted V2 and under-predicted V3. That cancellation is not in the data. No variant-level scores are given, and ECE bins on predicted risk, so opposite errors in separated score ranges would raise ECE rather than hide it. What is shown is weaker and still useful: the V2-heavy mix masks the V3 rate jump (0.35 to 0.46) in the aggregate breach rate. Stating the calibration mechanism as fact is too strong.

Several derived figures are a bit loose. The July-to-August Kitagawa split is directionally right, but the within-V3 rate piece is about +0.04 to +0.06, not +0.05 to +0.07. Non-Z volume is called flat (4,180 to about 3,876 and 3,833), which is a decline of roughly 7%. June’s mix-implied 0.158 vs observed 0.15 is called a match inside a ±0.01 band, but baseline months sit about 0.01 the other way, so the residual flips sign by nearly 0.02. The Partner Z back-of-envelope (0.58·0.61 + 0.42·0.15 ≈ 0.42) uses the overall September V3 rate, which already includes Partner Z (about a third of V3). It is marked as a hypothesis, but it is partly circular. April’s narrative also states leakage, imputation, and duplicate-inflated counts more firmly than the audit; those are plausible mechanisms, not observations.

The decision section has one internal gap. Stage 1 recalibrates a Z vs non-Z segment on June–August, yet Partner Z is 0% through August, and September is elsewhere barred from binary-label fitting. The path-decomposition argument is sound, including the point that a model on the realized variant is not deployable.

The code meets the requested API list and the two-cohort distinction is the right one, but it is not clean. The event-calendar branch never builds a DFG, so the comment that month-boundary edges are cut describes code that is not there; part-wait and variant shares are case-start only. `discover_performance_dfg` on open case-start cohorts drops unfinished `Order Part → Part Received` edges, which biases recent medians down exactly when delay is worst, and that bias is not flagged. `isinstance(n, int)` misses `numpy` integer counts and can throw. Forcing `promised_resolution` to UTC against possibly naive event timestamps is a latent comparison bug. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is only a side CSV, not part of the monthly audit table.

None of this overturns the month-by-month conclusion or the containment order (freeze retrains, do not calibrate toward October’s 0.16, repair features before a full retrain, escalate the supplier issue). It is a strong audit with enough local overconfidence and sketch defects that it is not near-flawless.
