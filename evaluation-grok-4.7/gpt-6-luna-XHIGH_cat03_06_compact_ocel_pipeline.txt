7.5

The submission is a coherent, largely faithful OCEL construction: all 17 events and 12 objects are present, timestamps are timezone-aware, payment amounts stay on events, both payments remain partial, shared participation on reserve, P1, and V1 matches the narrative, and the required checks, flattenings, and discussion sections are all there. The order, package, delivery, and invoice trace shapes and DFG edges follow from the chosen E2O table, and the failure modes and review assertions are concrete and mostly checkable.

The pipeline is not reliable as written. `ocpn, ocpn_diagnostics = pm4py.discover_oc_petri_net(ocel)` treats a standard single-return discovery result as a pair, so step I crashes on mainstream PM4Py, and the commented `view_ocpn(ocpn, ocpn_diagnostics)` repeats that assumption. Section 1 mentions varying return signatures, but this call is not isolated or adapted. `primary_order` is worse than unused: it is filled for create, add, and close, and left empty for reserve, pack P1, and invoice, so the events table contradicts the relations on the convergence events.

`settled_invoice` clashes with `partially_settles` and with the decision not to infer completion. Qualifiers such as `order`, `delivery`, and `payment` are much vaguer than the rest of an otherwise consistent vocabulary. Section 5 calls the OTG an object-type graph of Order→Item-style edges and then describes P1, I1, O100, M1, and M2 as if they were nodes in that same graph. OC-DFG and the Petri-net discussion similarly slide between type-level structure and instance IDs.

The order-trace prediction is factually right and still under-reads the main modeling consequence: O100’s flat trace contains one pack, P1, while I2 is never reserved and P2/D2 never appear, so the trace both copies shared work and drops half of O100’s fulfillment. That asymmetry is only implicit in the listed trace.
