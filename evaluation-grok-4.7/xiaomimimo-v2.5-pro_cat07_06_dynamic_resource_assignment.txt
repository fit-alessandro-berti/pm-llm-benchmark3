4.5

The write-up covers every required section and gets several easy cases right, but the policy is not internally consistent, breaks its own hard rules on the queue, and misstates both baselines. That is a core failure for an explainable, auditable assignment policy.

The assignment table and the algorithm do not implement the same policy. Section 2 filters out high overtime-risk resources unless the case is urgent and no safer alternative exists, and it overrides continuity only when complexity is above 0.7 and the owner’s skill is 1. The table then gives non-urgent C003 to R4_David, C006 to R9_Iris, and C010 to R2_Bruno, all high overtime-risk, with safer skill-2/3 alternatives available (Celine or Hugo on diagnosis, Hugo or Jonas on quality, Farid or Eva on approval). C016 moves a high-complexity, high-handover, high-priority diagnosis off R5_Eva (skill 2, 7h SLA) onto Celine. Constraint 5 keeps that case with Eva: her skill is 2, not 0 or 1, and the stated historical penalty is about 2.7h of waiting on a 7h SLA. The algorithm’s own skill-1 exception does not cover her.

Training claims are wrong against the documented training-priority column. Only C009 (Amal, diagnosis, skill 1) matches a documented priority. C010 is Bruno on approval, but his priority is quality and his approval skill is already 2. C013 is Jonas on eligibility, but his priority is security and his eligibility skill is already 2. C015 is Celine on security, but her priority is negotiation. Marking those three as training, then saying the plan meets the 20% normal/low training share (Amal, Bruno, Jonas, Celine), is false. Of nine normal/low cases, one real training assignment is about 11%. C012, explicitly suitable for a quality trainee, stays with specialist Jonas; Bruno is the person whose priority is quality and whose quality skill is 1. C015 is also the wrong vehicle for the Eva/Jonas note: their security skill is 0, so they cannot perform the review even supervised, but Hugo already owns it at skill 3. The legal move is Hugo performs and they shadow. Giving the review to Celine (skill 1) instead stacks C007, C015, and C016 on someone with 6.0h left. Supervision is left as “Gita or Hugo” / “Celine or David,” not a named supervised pair with capacity checked.

Section 4 misdescribes the baselines. First-available is lowest open cases plus enough hours, excluding skill 0; it is not a random policy and it does not ignore capacity. Specialist-only, tie-broken by open cases, sends diagnosis to Celine (6 open), not David (14), and both approvals to Farid, the only approval skill 3. The claimed David bottleneck does not follow. The resilience claim that Eva and Jonas shadow security is not in the assignment at all.

Smaller design holes compound this. The SLA score is identical for every candidate on a case, so it never changes the choice, and cases are not ordered by urgency before capacity is consumed. The four-assignment cap, batching rule, reassignment-count penalty, medium handover band, and hours-remaining feasibility never enter the selection rule. David has 3.0h left for a repeated diagnosis. Audit thresholds such as reassignment &lt; 1.5 and SLA misses &lt; 2% are unanchored to the 1.8× rework, 1.4× appeal, 2.7h, and 3.3h evidence. The worker note is clear in tone, but it tells Celine that C015 matches her training goal.

The PM4Py sketch names the right family of calls and would not run as written. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key` argument. `get_service_time` requires an activity and does not take `format="seconds"`. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a graph plus start and end activities, which `view_performance_dfg` needs separately. `discover_batches` and `filter_case_performance` are absent, and the snippet never computes the section 6 indicators or estimates handover, rework, or training weights from the log.

What holds up is the shell: hard versus soft constraints, skill 0 blocked, urgent security and approval kept on skill 3, C007 kept because it already has three reassignments, the indicator list hits every requested metric, and the comparison at least refuses a throughput-only optimum. Those pieces do not offset a queue plan an auditor could not reproduce from the stated rule.
