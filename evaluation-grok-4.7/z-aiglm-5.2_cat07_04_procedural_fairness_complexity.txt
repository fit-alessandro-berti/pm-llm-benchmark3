6.5

The final assessment is well structured, constraint-compliant, and mostly faithful to the supplied figures, but it is not nearly flawless.

The eight required sections are present and cover the requested dimensions, controls, variants, mechanisms, limits, mitigations, and PM4Py techniques. Raw rates are not treated as proof of discrimination, case mix is not used to dismiss residual gaps, the risk band is not treated as neutral, and support steps are kept rather than removed. Variant shares, waits (3.8 and 4.7 days), staffing (7 and 5), and the main adjusted approval and appeal figures are accurate.

The conclusion misreads the adjusted results. Group D’s residual gaps are estimated after controlling for `risk_score_band`, so those residuals cannot indicate that the risk band is what drives the remaining friction. That reverses the role of a controlled covariate and overstates what the preliminary model shows. The same section’s global verdict of procedural unfairness is stronger than a split conclusion: B and C have a supported timeliness and handover burden, while outcome discrimination and Group D remain more uncertain.

Section 2 lists the requested techniques but never explicitly separates disparity, discrimination, legitimate routing, and uncertainty. Section 1 infers reduced support quality, which the log does not measure and which section 5 correctly treats as unobserved. Section 5 also overclaims that the risk band was “likely trained” and illustrates it with wrongful denials not present in the evidence.

The PM4Py plan would not reliably support the assessment. `format_dataframe` is given a nonexistent `resource_key`; `filter_variants` is passed one comma-separated string rather than an activity sequence; the performance-path filter targets Language Support directly followed by Eligibility, which V4 does not do; and `len` on the resulting dataframe counts events while the text calls them cases. The log-skeleton call is not turned into an actual constraint check.
