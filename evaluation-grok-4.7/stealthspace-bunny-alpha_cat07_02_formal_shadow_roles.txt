6.8

The qualitative role model is careful and mostly faithful to the counts, complexity, substitutions, and constraints, but it is not nearly flawless. Several analytical slips and a code section that does not follow PM4Py’s actual interfaces keep it well below an excellent mark.

The central table is numerically accurate and correctly refuses to equate frequency with authority. Security stays rare-but-core, Nora stays non-human, temps stay low-complexity, and Giorgio’s pricing work is treated as regular boundary work (72 A7 and 88 A8), with the absence episode (31 and 18) kept separate from permanent competence. Hana’s 13 rechecks of Mateo’s 21 preliminary approvals are used properly. The Coordinate-versus-A13 caveat is a real data problem, not an invention.

The Chen/Dina misclassification cell is logically backwards. Their activity volumes are nearly the same (diagnose 260/255, configure 240/235, implement 215/220). A naïve frequency cluster would merge them. The sentence that says such a method might split them because of different volumes describes the opposite failure. The real miss is that 0.94 similarity hides Chen’s critical load (42 versus 9) and security load (24 versus 6).

Front-office peripherals are incomplete. Amira’s A8 count is 42 and Ben’s is 25, with Amira also at 18 A7. Those are larger than the A5/A10 work the table calls occasional, and together they are a substantial share of all negotiation. Leaving them out makes commercial negotiation look more exclusive to Fatima and Giorgio than the matrix shows. Amira has no pricing certification, which makes that omission analytically useful, not cosmetic. Giorgio’s given pricing certification is also underused: section 4 treats commercial authorization as still-missing evidence when the roster already lists it.

Section 3 defines shadow work as secondary or delegated, then applies that label to Erik, the quality pair, Nora, and Hana, whose roles in this log are primary. Erik belongs in the section as a rare critical specialist and a single point of failure; he does not fit the definition the answer just gave. The Hana wording that all 21 preliminary approvals “were subsequently rechecked in 13 instances” can be read as a full recheck. The source supports only 13 of 21.

The code is the largest constraint miss. `format_dataframe` takes `activity_key`, not `activity_id`, and does not take `resource_key`. It renames columns to `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, and `time:timestamp`. The answer sends it the opposite direction and then uses `caseid` / `activity` / `resource`. `discover_organizational_roles` is threshold-based and does not take `num_roles`, so the k-sweep and the hard-coded `SELECTED_K = 5` are not a PM4Py-style sensitivity test. That choice also fights the ten-role narrative: k=5 would collapse the distinctions the essay argues for. Activity-based similarity is handled as a list of triples with `method="cosine"`, while handover and working-together are handled as graphs. In PM4Py those SNA calls return graphs, and cosine is not that parameter. The substitution join is substantively wrong even as illustration: `case_any_substitution` is true if anyone in the case substituted, then it is summed onto every resource who touched the case. That attributes Fatima’s absence, or Hana’s, to unrelated participants. Subcontracting, the SNA view most relevant to Giorgio covering Fatima and Mateo preparing Hana’s approvals, is never computed.

Sections 2 and 6 otherwise respect the brief: complexity is kept distinct from craft, substitution is not treated as certification, shadow work is given both continuity value and control risk, and the staffing cautions correctly separate Nora’s volume, temp low-complexity closure, and the non-causal reading of the security-filter episode. Shift coverage (Chen/Ivan/Lina versus day-shift peers) is barely used, and Fatima’s 16 A9 events never become their own ambiguity row.

The result is a strong operating hypothesis with correct arithmetic, weakened by one inverted clustering claim, incomplete cross-functional counts, an inconsistent shadow definition, and illustrative code that would not run and would mis-measure substitution.
