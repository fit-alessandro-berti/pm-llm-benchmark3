5.5

The case-level service and wait reconstruction is mostly careful, but several station-level results contradict that same reconstruction or the stated scenario definitions. Under a strict reading, those breaks keep the answer out of the high band.

The fully observed waits and services in Section 3 check out, and the missing-start intervals are the right resource-feasible ranges: P05 S2 start in [09:45, 10:10], P07 S1 in [09:25, 09:55], P11 S2 in [12:00, 12:20], and P15 S1 in [11:40, 12:10], with station sojourn held fixed. Open and still-waiting cases are retained as elapsed or wait-so-far lower bounds. Central busy time on the reported grid is also right: I1 315, I2 290, C1 300, C2 270 on 345 available minutes. Definitions correctly separate queue, active service, and sojourn, and the bottleneck narrative is directionally supported by the growing waits, the 15-minute C2 outage, and the 14:00 residuals.

The aggregate quantities do not follow from those case figures.

- S1 waiting time in the table sums to about 1480 case-minutes, so mean S1 queue length is about 4.1 and mean S1 WIP is about 5.8 once the 605 busy minutes are included. Section 4’s mean WIP of roughly 3.0–3.5 and peak of 4–5 are far below that. Around 11:00–12:00, S1 still holds about 7–8 cases. S2’s claimed peak of 4–5 is already below the Section 5 end state of 4 waiting plus 2 in service.
- P07’s completed sojourn is 135 minutes (11:00 minus 08:45), not 155. The reported mean of about 148 is the mean of that incorrect list; the 14 completed sojourns average about 146.
- Section 6’s “85 (10:25)” for P07 is not 85 minutes after 08:00. That clock time is 09:25, and 10:25 is after P07’s recorded end.
- Optimistic and central are defined as the same imputation, so the three-scenario comparison is only two scenarios. The four missing-start slacks are about 30, 30, 25, and 20 minutes, about 105 minutes in total, not 30–40. Pessimistic I1 busy time falls from 315 to 255, and utilization from about 0.88 to about 0.71. The claim that every resource stays at or above 0.75 therefore fails inside the answer’s own pessimistic corner. The Section 4 bands of 0.80–0.92 and C1 busy time of 300–310+ are wider than those scenarios allow. I2’s grid busy time is fixed at 290.
- With cuts at 10:00 and 12:00, external arrivals are 14, 7, and 1, not 14, 6, and 2: P21 arrives at 11:50. S2 completions by 10:00 are 3, not 4 or 4–5. The code’s half-open bins (13, 8, 1 arrivals and 3, 5, 6 completions) match neither the prose nor a single stated boundary rule.
- Early-window time-average system WIP is about 6.8, not 4.5–5.5, so the implied Little’s-Law wait is also low. Assumption A9 treats 08:00–10:00 as defensible, while Section 6 correctly says the steady-state assumption fails there.
- Two queue facts are missed. I2 is idle from 13:45 to 14:00 while P22 waits, so dispatch is not work-conserving at the end. P17 enters C1 at 13:55 while earlier S2 arrival P16 is still waiting at 14:00. Both conflict with the FCFS rationale used for central imputation. Most of P08’s 55-minute S2 wait is time behind P06 before the outage, not post-outage congestion; the outage itself accounts for 15 of those minutes.

The Python has the right status split, the correct hardcoded bounds, and the required PM4Py calls on a completed-case projection. It would not reproduce the prose rates or WIP summaries, and it never derives the bounds from the resource chains. PM4Py case waiting time on that projection also omits an arrival event, so it cannot recover S1 waits measured from `arrival_s1`. Format compliance is otherwise good.
