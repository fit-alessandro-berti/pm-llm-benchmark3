7.0

The memo is decision-ready and directionally sound: it rejects national scale and full rollback, keeps the simple-house gains, pauses survey-skipping and post-billing activation, and ties rollout to further evidence. Sections 3–5 are mostly coherent, and the required process-mining artifacts are used. It is not nearly flawless.

The evidence table misstates core facts. Segment figures are changes, but simple houses are written as “median 6.0 days, p90 8.0 days” and “complaints 1pp,” with several first-time-right changes left unsigned. That contradicts the memo and can reverse the complaint result. “Cases stalled when a coordinator was absent” is placed as fact, although the source only says reduced handovers may have reduced resilience. The assumption that a “60-day” cost estimate already exists conflates today’s incomplete cost with proposed measurement M4.

Process-mining language conflicts with itself. Section 3 correctly treats high fitness and precision 0.51 as a permissive model, then the memo calls that model “overfit,” which is the opposite diagnosis. Generalization is reported and never interpreted. SLA and NPS are largely unused.

The recommendation is also looser than it looks. “High-confidence” overclaims an observational, mixed-region pilot with incomplete risk attributes. “What to scale” is ambiguous against the instruction not to scale until the thresholds are met. Those thresholds—95% confidence, a 0.5-point complaint cap, zero violations, 85% utilization—are false precision, and zero violations is brittle. M6/M1 are ranked first even though skipping is already paused, while M5, which tests the coordinator model they intend to keep, is ranked last.
