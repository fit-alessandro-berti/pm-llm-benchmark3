The final answer is complete, numerically careful, and appropriately refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. Availability versus use, median versus p90, absolute change versus control-adjusted change, and selection plus concurrent shocks are kept distinct, and the checked pre/post and never-treated contrasts match the data.

Points come off for quantitative and design gaps. The within-branch use slopes are linear summaries of a non-monotonic path: Alpha and Beta tails worsen through June and then improve while use keeps rising, alongside a similar late improvement in the controls. March fraud-desk onset is left inside the pre window, so escalation contrasts are partly baseline-contaminated. The stated primary design is a staggered event study, but the code builds relative-time indicators and then estimates the two-way fixed-effects specification it warns against. “Excluding May–August” is not a coherent sensitivity, escalation confidence of 60 is high given the shared desk shock, and the scale-up rule invokes confidence bounds that two branches cannot support.
