The seven required sections are present, case types are kept distinct, and the package is conditional rather than an average-cycle-time or blanket-capacity plan. The score stays here because several numerical and causal claims in the core analysis are wrong, and one control rule contradicts its own coverage claim.

The peak assay repeat share is 1260/(7240+1260) = 14.8%. The answer’s ≈17% equals 1260/7240, repeats divided only by the technical-validation branch. Normal’s ≈7% is the all-exits ratio, so the two periods are computed differently. That inflated baseline feeds S2 and the dashboard’s critical band above 15%, which sits on the observed peak rather than beyond it. S1 is also described as matching the peak summary while the mix is moved from 42/29/20/9 to 40/30/22/8.

Accession correction follows pre-analytical check in the DFG. The S5 claim that an unblocked accession case later fails pre-check reverses that order. The return path the log supports is pre-check → accession → recollection (70/500 normal, 110/420 peak) → receive sample → pre-check. The variant text says urgent bypass fragments machine schedules. The write-up treats the drop from 74% to 58% as the cause of greater fragmentation, then says priority lanes will cut that fragmentation while keeping bypass. Batch-wait evidence is cited as raw counts, 760 versus 1180, across an 8-week window and a 3-week window. Per week the flow is about 95 versus 393, and the share of preparation exits rises from about 4.4% to about 12.7%. The quoted counts soften the shift the DFG shows.

Modified I4 is allowed only when technical-validation utilization is under 90%, while the S1/peak state given in the data is 101%. The trade-off table still marks S1 coverage as high, so the lever is credited for a state in which its own rule keeps it off. I10 is set at 24 h in the package, 18 h in P1, and 12 h on the dashboard, with no link among those levels. P1’s off-rule refers to “all three metrics” after an on-rule with four clauses. Section 2 places I6 and I7 with actions that raise downstream throughput. Early rejection removes work; courier smoothing holds total volume fixed and dampens the 10:00 and 17:00 spikes. The volume push is I8’s. P2 then says to accelerate technical validation of repeats after the same section has identified technical validation as the station repeats overload, and it prefers M1 for urgent and complex work, the I3 pattern section 3 rejects, without placing routine volume on the higher-failure machine. P4 adds registration overtime without using the stated rise in error rate after 9-hour shifts.

Migration sequencing, case-type floors, trigger hysteresis, a redesign-caused traceability risk, and the leading-versus-lagging split remain coherent. The reversed accession path, the bypass-mechanism inversion, the unnormalized frequency comparison, and the I4 contradiction are enough to keep this well short of a near-flawless mark.
