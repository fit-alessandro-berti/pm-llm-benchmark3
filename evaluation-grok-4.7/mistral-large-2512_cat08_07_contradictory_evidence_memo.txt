The memo reaches a defensible hybrid and covers every required section, but it is not analytically reliable enough to score higher. Several errors sit on the exact points the task asks to get right: metric meaning, fact versus inference, and a single clear decision.

The fitness/precision account is wrong in substance. Precision is how much behavior the model allows beyond the log, not “unsafe paths that did not occur in the baseline.” Billing before activation is observed in the pilot (move-on-log; the billing-to-complaint edge rises from 3% to 8%). Missing permit checks are the opposite alignment move (expected on the model, absent in the log). Generalization also falls from 0.70 to 0.56 and is never used. DFG shares, temporal-profile deviations, and the handover-network versus working-together-network split are mostly ignored despite being part of the evidence the memo was required to use.

The recommendation contradicts itself. The memo scales Redesign R only for simple low-risk houses and pauses survey-skipping and post-billing activation for everyone else. Section 6 then scales coordinator ownership to all cases and calls the handover drop “proven.” The evidence table correctly lists that causal claim as an assumption. Those cannot both stand. Pilot mix makes the scope error sharper: simple houses are 34% of the pilot, and the pilot is already heavier in apartments and older buildings than the baseline, so 34% is not the national opportunity.

Fact separation slips in the same place. EUR 207 versus EUR 198 is a reported estimate, not an assumption that true cost exceeds the estimate. Visible-cost savings are also attributed to simple houses without segment cost data. “Demoralizes teams” and “regulatory exposure” are added without evidence. The p90 “15%” threshold has no base, and an 80% first-time-right bar is weaker than the 82% baseline. M5 is ranked too low for a decision that would spread single-coordinator ownership while two regions are already at 93% utilization, and M7 is treated as decision value when it is mainly a control.

The hybrid direction itself matches the constraints: no national scale, no full rollback, segment benefits kept. The write-up does not hold that decision still.
