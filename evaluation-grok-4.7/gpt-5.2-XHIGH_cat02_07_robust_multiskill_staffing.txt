The write-up matches the required nine-section structure and contains a real offered-load calculation, but the scenario comparison and the recommendation that rests on it are not reliable. Several mechanisms in the simulation contradict the stated policies, and small SLA gaps are treated as if they were stable.

Section 2’s base arithmetic checks out. Mixture-of-uniforms means are right (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74). Day totals of about 291.5 / 475.8 / 797.5 / 494.7 / 661.1 minutes, 42 expected arrivals, and 2,670 staff-minutes with full overtime are right. The block ratios 0.93, 0.98, 0.84, 1.14, 1.31, and 1.28 match arrivals × type-weighted work against shift, break, and overtime calendars. U4 diagnostic means 29.60, 37.14, and 44.50 are right. Assumptions A1–A6 are labeled, and the two objectives do cover tail/SLA risk and worst-case fairness.

That load section still misstates its own bound and misses the skill analysis it needs. Charging each arrival block with the whole case (intake, both branches, validation) pushes work forward in time, while ignoring multipliers and qualification pulls the other way. Calling those ratios an optimistic lower bound is the wrong direction for the within-block comparison. “Diagnostic mean increases ~9%” under U4 is the increase in total work (about 242 / 2,721 ≈ 9%), not in diagnostic work. Diagnostic minutes rise from about 1,768 to 2,010, roughly 14%. There is no skill-by-block account of base minutes versus qualified, speed-adjusted hours. That matters: before noon, Software is only Chen (1.25) and Farah (1.40); after 16:30 it is only Emil; under U3/U5 the morning Software resource is Farah alone. Parallel versus sequential is also never sized. On the means, ME parallel critical path is about max(26.3, 32.8)+8 ≈ 41 versus sequential ≈ 59; ES is about 46.5 versus 71. Those gaps are large enough to change the policy comparison.

The reported table cannot support the ranking. The code’s scenario filters use `and`/`or` precedence, so U1/U5 also scale the 12:00–14:00 block by 1.7 and add 8 P1 points, and U2 also doubles E and ES in 16:00–18:00. The prompt’s windows are 08:00–12:00 and 12:00–16:00. U4 doubles every tail probability, including Intake and Validation, despite the text limiting U4 to diagnostics. Within a priority, the score is `priority − 10·lateness − 0.5·expected time − switching`. On-time slack is ignored, and a more overdue job scores worse, so the rule is priority plus shortest processing time, not the slack rule in Section 3. `dispatch` returns as soon as one P1 diagnostic starts, so other idle resources are not assigned on that event. Preemption adds the full planned duration to `busy_min`, leaves the original interval in the log through its original end, and rebuilds remaining work as `remaining_clock / multiplier × 1.10`, which still includes setup and handover and then adds those overheads again. Utilization, service time, and the handover network are biased wherever preemption is common. Unfinished cases are dropped from mean, p95, and SLA. Under the overload Section 2 argues for, that censors the tail and inflates SLA. Utilization is busy time over two full days of calendars, with arrivals only on day 0, so it is not the planned same-day utilization behind the 90% rule. With about 5–8 P1 cases per replication, a 0.05 SLA gap is within sampling noise; the code computes seed standard deviations and the narrative never shows them.

Policy text and code diverge. SILO in prose uses Farah before noon, lets Emil take Electrical only when Software is empty, and serves FIFO within priority. In code Farah’s diagnostic set is empty, Emil never takes Electrical, and the shared score replaces FIFO. Victim choice is resource-dict order, not largest remaining time. Hybrid’s hourly switch cap, “P1 expected within 30 minutes,” and “preempt only if slack < 30 or wait > 10” are absent. `load_hint` is queue base-minutes over 60 × headcount, not the next block’s work/staff ratio used to justify the 0.85 and 0.75 thresholds.

Section 4 then over-reads that table. Hybrid’s worst P1 SLA in the printed numbers is about 0.72, versus about 0.67 and 0.64, but on U1 its P1 SLA is the worst of the three (0.80 versus 0.83 and 0.81) while the failure-mode cell says reservation reduces P1 misses. On U5, Hybrid has the worst mean and p95. J1 and J2 are never evaluated at stated weights, so “dominates” on the robust-fair objective is not shown. POOL is more balanced on the printed Gini in every row. A fair reading of these figures is a trade-off under an infeasible fairness cap: 0.9 × 2,670 = 2,403 staff-minutes, already below 2,721 base minutes before multipliers, handover, setup, and coordination. The text never makes that infeasibility explicit. Delays of roughly 530–830 minutes on average, with p95 near 1,400–1,800, almost certainly mix same-day sojourn with the 20:30–08:00 closure. P2’s 8-hour target is largely a calendar fact for anything still open at the end of Emil’s overtime. That split is not reported, so policy effects are not separated from the night gap.

Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the most usable parts. Gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption churn, validation migration, and auditability are concrete. The attribute list covers queue entry, eligible set, score components, handover, setup, preemption, policy version, and shock flags. The pilot is shadowed before a guarded alternation, with SILO as rollback. PM4Py use is partly right: `format_dataframe`, case-level `insert_case_service_waiting_time` labeled as case totals, `get_service_time` by activity, and a handover network. The demo log still omits arrival, so those waiting times exclude pre-intake delay and are not the SLA clock; stage waits are not computed; service intervals include handover, setup, and coordination; preempted intervals overlap. Section 7 names the attributes that would fix this, and the executable log does not emit them.

The capacity identities and the specialization-versus-pooling discussion are real. The numerical claim that Hybrid is the robust policy is not established by a simulation that implements different scenarios, a different dispatch rule, and censored delay.
