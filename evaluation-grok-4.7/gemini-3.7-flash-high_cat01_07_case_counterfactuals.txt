The write-up is complete and often qualitatively right, but it is not temporally reliable. The largest failure is K-337: factual cycle time is about 214.2 h (Mon 11:00 to the following Wed 09:10), not 190.2 h. The base case then drops the weekend entirely, calling a Monday 13:30 finish 122.5 h when it is about 170.5 h. Those errors are reused in the savings (84–92 h), the ranking, and the synthesis. The table’s 98–106 h band also excludes the derivation’s own base and worst cases.

Section 2 and Section 3 contradict each other on three cases. K-104’s table base is ~45 h (range 39.5–51.5); the derivation’s base is 50.3 h, best 37 h, worst 57 h. K-411’s table base is ~15 h; the derivation’s base is 26 h and its best is 9 h. K-219’s best case, 26.5 h, falls outside its own table. Uncertainty is asserted twice and does not agree.

Several “base” schedules are infeasible against the snapshots. Tue 08:00 D2 already has about 4 h of other work, so K-219 cannot start then without preempting the queue. K-411’s D2 start at Tue 10:00 has the same conflict with a 4 h morning queue. K-219’s best case also puts A2 on the case at Tue 08:30 despite A2’s 6 h Tuesday queue. K-104 moves A1’s start earlier because the file is better prepared; queue position does not work that way. Final C1 at 1.5–2.0 h is the extreme edge of the stated 1–3 h savings, not a base rate.

The partial order is not a partial order: the rework clause is cyclic, and “Assessment precedes escalation” conflicts with the concurrency clause. Concurrency is allowed when originals are absent, not required. K-411 is not timing-only; it changes control flow. K-104 is not resource rerouting; it inserts preparatory work and a capacity claim. K-337 is correctly treated as a decision-policy change, and four-eyes, originals-before-check, and final compliance after the latest assessment are preserved.

Interference reasoning is the strongest part: C1 reservation is zero-sum, D2 substitution can create rework, avoided loops move risk to appeals, and parallel release can bunch assessment. That is undercut by “strongly positive,” “zero quality risk,” and “extremely high” robustness, by ranking C1 preemption above D2 substitution, and by invented 70–80% and 20–30% probabilities plus an unpowered “80% power” sample size. Odd/even assignment is alternation, not randomization. Assumption 3 says a delay is “multiplied by” hours; assumptions 7 and 8 are mislabeled.
