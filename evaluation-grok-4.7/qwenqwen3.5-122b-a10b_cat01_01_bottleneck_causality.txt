The memo detects the crude-versus-stratified reversal and correctly warns against a blanket 50% transfer, but it treats that reversal as causal identification, misstates mechanisms, and ships a required calculation that cannot recover the contrast.

Section 1 chooses an allowed verdict, yet confidence 85 and “demonstrably slower” overclaim what cells of size 2 can show. It never separates total, direct, and mediated Orion effects. The closing “selection effect” label conflates completed-case selection with complexity confounding. “Handover delays” are not in the evidence.

The graph points `resource_group → shift` and `shift → arrival_load`. Coverage patterns do not establish that assignment causes shift, and load is an arrival condition. Those arrows contradict the later “confounder” label for shift. The note states one feedback loop but does not separate supported arrows from assumptions. `unobserved_reviewer_skill` is unused, and complexity is not linked to service or queue time.

The role table names a confounder, mediator, and collider, but estimands stay generic (“group effect”). Shift cannot be both a descendant of treatment and a confounder without a different estimand.

The decomposition misses a Medium row and any 30-day outcome comparison. Day/night and compliance claims are not auditable. “Compliance adds ~10–12h” and the allowed reading “compliance adds time” are confounded by complexity; within Medium, compliance-required cases are not longer. Rework’s crude Orion excess reverses or disappears inside strata, which is not stated. Within-stratum cycle gaps are mostly `wait_before_review_h`, while review service is nearly the same; the memo still later credits certification. Night is perfectly nested with high load, and within Low complexity night is not slower, so “shift might cause delay” is not an allowed causal reading. Every night case in the file is high load.

No hypothesis assigns Orion a genuine harmful effect. Rank 1 predicts a null matched gap even though the stratum means already differ. The compliance row is confounding by policy, not mediation. The shift test compares Nova-day with Orion-night and would not isolate shift. Predictions are not tied to results that would favor versus weaken each claim. Appeal counts (one case each) are ignored.

The pilot and night-capacity phase meet the intervention checklist, but the capacity stop rule is backwards: wait above 24h would halt the staffing meant to cut wait. The write-up calls Orion operationally superior because of certification, which neither the service times nor the design identify. A Medium-only pilot does not justify migrating high-complexity night work, and night coverage constraints are unaddressed.

The Python block loads the cases and prints crude and stratum means, but the standardized contrast assigns both groups the same complexity means, so the printed contrast is identically zero. DFGs are not compared or printed; variant output is only the top three trace counts. Overlap warnings exist, yet the night–high-load nesting is never called out.

Section 8’s five data gaps are real. They do not include the design change this sample most needs: a shift-and-load-aware randomization with enough overlap to separate queueing from service.
