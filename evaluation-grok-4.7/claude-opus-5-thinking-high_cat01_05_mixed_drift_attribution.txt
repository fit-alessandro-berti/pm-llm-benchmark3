7.4

The diagnosis is strong where it matters: March is correctly isolated as a semantic/provenance break, later drift is not collapsed into that break, and the main quantitative identities check out. It is not nearly flawless. Several claims are sharper than the aggregates, one monitoring premise is arithmetically impossible, and the reproducible detector does not implement the change-point story told in the prose.

Verified and correctly used: March rollout 500+140=640 (78%), `Open Case` 26 versus 4% of 640, request-info counts 163/680, 207/715, and 167/760, and the L1 distances 3, 40, 59, 28, 64, and 36 with the stated rework and senior shares. Total variation for March (0.004), May (0.063), and December (0.063) matches the stated definition. Baseline-relative percentages are almost all correct. The service-versus-cycle contrast, the one-request-per-reworked-case reading, right-censoring on the decision edge, mediation of supplier rework through utilization, and the refusal to treat July’s priority rule as identified are the right restraints. Section 2 is inside the word limit and does not treat a successful label map as proof the business process was unchanged. The seven decision rules have the required shape, and the monitor set actually separates schema, ordering, rework depth versus prevalence, queueing, mix, and conformance.

The material problems:

The cycle-time story invents a staffing model the table cannot support. “If the pool was ~25–30” becomes a demand/capacity factor of about 1.7 and a move from utilization 0.75 to 0.90. Neither the headcount nor the baseline utilization is observed. That is manufactured precision around an otherwise fair qualitative claim that waiting, not the 4.8-to-5.4-hour service move, dominates.

The primary monitoring population is mis-sized. In November the standard share is 55% and the non-Z share is 73%, so their intersection is at most 55% and at least 28%. “55–70% even in the worst months” is above that maximum. Weekly cells can fall through the stated 100-case floor, and restricting to complete traces in the most censored months makes the control stratum smaller exactly when queueing is worst.

Section 3.5 contradicts the earlier table. Volume and complexity were already up by summer (about +17% cases and +8.5 percentage points complexity before October); “nothing until October” is false. December also reverses rework, mapped fitness, volume, and complexity. “Only the performance dimension” reversed is false. October–November supplier attribution is then called “near zero” while Z is still about 12 percentage points above baseline and rework is 26–29%.

The change-point code cannot see the break it is supposed to find. `min_history=3` skips January–March, so the March fitness step is ineligible. The expanding median then swallows the post-step months, so persistence is unlikely to mark the logging break at all. Part B’s default conformance path discovers the Petri net on the same normalized window it scores, and its recovery ratio divides by `1 - raw` rather than by the Part A baseline gap. A high normalized fitness is then partly by construction. `b9` claims a waiting-versus-service split and only returns the pre-Assess gap.

Smaller faults that still count. Hand-rounded recovery ratios for August and September (0.87 and 0.91) do not match the formula used in the code (0.861 and 0.904). The 17–18% September rework “prediction” is never derived, then used as hysteresis evidence. L1 double-counts one rework loop and still leads with a 60% share. Monitor M12 treats recovery above 0.85 as “do not page operations,” which is true of May, when mapped fitness has already fallen; rule 1 is stricter than the monitor. The claim that two-of-two persistence cuts false alarms roughly quadratically ignores weekly autocorrelation. “Priority is recorded only after July” is not in the evidence. Complexity “nearly doubled”; from 20.5% to 45% it more than doubled. April’s phased-rollout row is scored at 80 without an April label split. The widening gap between flows into `Collect Documents` and `CollectDocs->Assess` (about 14 per 100 cases in January versus about 34 in July) is never checked, so the “omitted edges unchanged” assumption is weaker than the distance section admits.

The core mechanism split would survive these corrections. The score is held down because the over-precise capacity arithmetic, the impossible stratum size, the contradictory change-point summary, and the non-equivalent code are concrete failures of the standard the answer itself sets.
