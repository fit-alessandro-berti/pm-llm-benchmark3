The month-by-month story is the right one: April is a schema and prefix failure, June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September combine a parts-edge shock with (from September) Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The six-way table, training quarantine, and the four failure modes follow that story. The score is held down by overclaiming, a missing monitor, and a process-mining sketch that does not meet its own specification.

Evidence is repeatedly stated as established mechanism. June is called pure covariate drift with invariant \(P(Y\mid X)\) and “preserved” within-variant performance, but the only stable figures are variant base rates, not stratified discrimination or calibration. June ECE is 0.060 and mean risk is 0.20 against an observed 0.15; that calibration gap is omitted. July is grouped with concept drift at very high confidence even though aggregate risk (0.18 vs 0.19) and ECE (0.042) do not show the August collapse. October is given the same very high concept-drift confidence although variant breach rates are explicitly too immature to estimate; the parts edge at 155 h supports ongoing performance drift, not a confirmed conditional-risk estimate. September’s cutoff rate of 0.31 is called the rate “on mature cases” at 82% maturity. Partner Z’s 0.45 vs 0.27 is used as population evidence without the slower Partner Z maturation the brief flags, including in the bias failure mode, where that gap is treated as already explained by rural distance, device age, and V3 mix. August’s V3 share jump from 22% to 35% is left out of the control-flow timing (assigned to September–October). “Q1” V3 breach of 0.30–0.34 folds in April’s 0.34. Pipeline recovery is described as unknown activity under 1% and missing queue features under 3% from June onward; June unknown activity is 1.0%, and September–October queue missingness is 3%.

Falsifiers are not stated per explanation. Several tests rediscover numbers already in the tables. The transport check treats 0.45 as the target and does not align follow-up or maturity, so differential censoring can imitate a Partner Z residual. Variant-by-source strata are empty until September. What would still be ambiguous after the published matured-by-variant table—within-V3 case mix versus a true path-conditional break—is not the contrast drawn.

Containment before recalibration, April only after prefix repair, and October held out until high maturity are sound. May is still marked safe beside June despite residual unknown-activity share, queue missingness, and ECE. The V3 hard flag and the later V3 recalibration step sit before supplier lead time is a feature, which is the static bake-in warned about in the failure modes. Variant-specific sub-models are argued as necessary without treating path as future to the diagnosis-time decision, so a lead-time and partner-feature model is not really tested as the smaller fix.

Monitoring covers prediction, calibration, maturity, data quality, variants, performance DFGs, and population, and label delay suppresses retraining. An intervention and deployment monitor is absent, so the April rename, May patch, June policy, and September onboarding are not first-class alarms. The calendar-month cohort is computed and then unused; summaries are case-start only. `filter_time_range`, `discover_dfg`, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are not used. `get_variants` on a dataframe is treated as if every value were a list (`len` on an int count crashes), and the performance DFG is read with `.get("median")` without forcing `perf_aggregation_key`, so the “median hours” column is either a crash or a mean mislabeled as a median.
