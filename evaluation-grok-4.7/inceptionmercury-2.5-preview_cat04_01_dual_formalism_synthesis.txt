The write-up has the required sections and a few right instincts (risk as a control-flow choice, supplier as an optional branch, trial repetition as a loop, POWL as the better fit for cross-branch joins). The models themselves are not faithful, the two artifacts disagree, and the verdicts are not derived from the code.

The process tree is not a usable approximation. `Operator.LEAF` is not a PM4Py operator, so the constructor code fails before it can build a tree. The fenced expression is a different model from the Python: the expression always runs `Execute Trial`, `Inspect Trial`, `Adjust Parameters` once, while the code uses `LOOP(Execute→Inspect, Adjust→Execute)`. That redo inserts a second `Execute Trial`, so a retry is Execute, Inspect, Adjust, Execute, Execute, Inspect. High-risk review is a sequence, so Quality and Regulatory cannot overlap or finish in either order. Supplier sits after the approval choice, so it cannot overlap approvals or the trial. The trial is sequenced after the whole preparation block, so approvals must finish before the trial. That is an under-approximation of allowed concurrency. Section 1 calls the policy a permissive over-approximation, and Section 3 says release is not synchronized, but the sequence after the parallel block does join, and it joins too early. Assumption 4 forbids duplicate labels, yet the loop duplicates `Execute Trial`. The parallel with a silent sibling adds no behavior. A correct control-flow reading of “pass” is loop exit after `Execute→Inspect` with redo equal to `Adjust Parameters` only; “Inspect immediately followed by Release” is already too strong once approvals may still be open.

The POWL model misses the same obligations in a more serious way. Every activity is one node of a single `StrictPartialOrder`, so Supervisor, Quality, Regulatory, and Consult Supplier are all mandatory and can run together. Absence of an edge means concurrency, not choice or skipping. `OperatorPOWL` and `SilentTransition` are imported and then unused, with a comment that treats that omission as flexibility. The edges `Execute→Inspect→Adjust→Execute` are a cycle, so this is not a partial order and cannot mean “repeat until pass.” Adjust is a required successor of Inspect, so a one-pass case is impossible, and one `Execute Trial` node cannot yield two iterations. Lookup uses `.name`; POWL transitions expose `.label`. The acyclic edges for register, contain→plan, analyze→design, and release→archive are real, but they do not rescue optionality, exclusivity, or the loop.

The witness table does not test these models. T1 omits Design Corrective Action. T2 omits Contain and Design and still plans verification. T3 omits containment, design, planning, and the approval route. T4 omits the approval route. T5 omits inspection and every review and still marks the case allowed. None of the five “allowed” rows is an intended accepting trace, so the low-risk, high-risk, supplier, and two-iteration obligations are unmet. T7’s claim that the tree allows release without approval is false for this tree, because an approval branch is inside the block that must finish first. T8’s earliest violation, as written, is the trial starting with no root-cause analysis and no design; the missing final inspection is later. Section 6 then reverses several directions: the tree makes the trial too late, not too early, and the POWL risk and supplier rows are not “no distortion.”

The validation plan names conversion, soundness, and playout, but its positive tests are the invalid traces above, and it never checks the duplicate `Execute Trial` or the illegal cycle. The self-critique calls T5 the bad-sync witness; that is T7. The preferred-representation claim that this POWL encodes the cross-branch pattern without approximation is not true of the model that was written.
