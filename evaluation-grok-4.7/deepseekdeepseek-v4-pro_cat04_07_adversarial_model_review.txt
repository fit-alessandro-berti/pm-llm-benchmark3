7.4

The review is technically strong: probe judgments for Models S and C are correct, the third model is a real control-flow design, pathologies are ranked rather than averaged, and the dissent is substantive. It is not nearly flawless.

Model S is described as having “five activities visible.” It has eight labeled activities. Section 3 promises at least five undesirable admitted traces, but only four are actually admitted traces (items 5–7 are a non-admission, an untraced blind spot, and a behavior the text itself calls acceptable).

“Overapproximates” is used for a language that rejects valid single-check rework. That is under-approximation. The same slip shows up in the claim that duplicate labels boost alignment fitness; they enlarge the net and confuse precision, but one log event still synchronizes once.

The compact tree omits `tau` in the Supplier/Expedite choices even though the code includes it. Section 8 covers P1–P7 and three new traces, but it does not put B1–B8 in the required four-column comparison; Section 5 has no third-model column. The validation skeleton calls `.to_csv()` on a split event log and shows a non-PM4Py alignment-cost dict.

The governance conclusion itself holds: S is exploratory only, C is an overfit catalogue, and the loop-based third model is the only candidate for compliance, with the missing external and affected-check attributes left explicit.
