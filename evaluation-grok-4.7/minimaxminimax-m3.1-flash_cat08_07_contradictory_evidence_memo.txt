8.4

The memo is a strong, decision-ready synthesis: E is the right call, national scale and full rollback are correctly rejected, and the bundle is split rather than treated as one treatment. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly kept apart. All five metric conflicts, alternatives A–E, and M1–M8 are covered with usable decision consequences. Process-mining evidence is used properly, including the move-on-log versus move-on-model distinction, fitness versus precision, and handover versus working-together networks. The rollout gates are explicitly illustrative and mix-adjusted.

The evidence table still has a real defect. In the segment row, negative changes lose their signs (“median 6 days,” “complaints 1pp,” “2/+12,” “8pp/+5pp”). That row is supposed to state facts, and those signs are the basis for targeting simple houses. The executive memo states the same changes correctly, so the conclusion survives, but the table itself is not reliable as written.

Calling the action “E implemented as C plus D” is also imprecise. C scales coordinator ownership generally; the stated action limits it to simple low-risk houses. Permit restoration is extended to business and rural cases without segment evidence for missing permits. The immediate plan leaves 93% coordinator utilization behind an unnamed guardrail, and the numeric gates mix margins with hard baseline caps without explaining the difference.
