The memo is strong on the things this task rewards: it separates availability from intensity, refuses a single “X% cycle-time” claim, keeps median and p90 apart, treats rollout as targeted and staggered, and recommends a design that buys both operations and identification (order randomization plus case-level assignment, queue and fraud-desk data, tripwires, a pre-registered scale rule). Estimands, mechanisms, threats, and the claim ladder are the right objects and are mostly well argued. The main DiD arithmetic (Alpha/Beta/Gamma vs Delta/Epsilon on the primary windows, the April escalation step, the intensity endpoint slopes, utilization) checks out.

It is not nearly flawless, and the defects are in quantitative discipline and reproducibility.

- Gamma’s Jan–Apr p90 contrast is wrong: the pre mean is 78.4h, so the change is about +6.5h (+8.3%), not +5.2h (+6.6%). That cell is an addition error, not a window choice.
- The strongest “Observed” bullet says never-adopters were still ~3–5h above their Jan–Mar medians by August. Delta is about +4.5h; Epsilon is about +0.5h (Jul–Aug mean about +2.5h). The same bullet also says adopters ramped to ~50% fast-path share; Gamma only reaches 32%.
- Section 5.3 states that the intensity regressions “yield small negative coefficients” without having run them. That sign is not an arithmetic fact from the table.
- The relative-time line will fail on current pandas: same-frequency Period subtraction already returns an integer, so `.map(lambda x: x.n)` raises before the models run. The required analysis is therefore not actually reproducible as written.
- The preferred estimator is misdescribed: under not-yet-treated comparisons, Alpha/Beta’s pre-periods do not form Gamma’s July–August counterfactual; only Delta and Epsilon do.
- Smaller slips: Delta and Epsilon sit at 4%, not 4–6%; post-shock p90/median ratios top out near 2.34, not 2.5; “scale to remaining branches” is empty once both non-adopters are the evaluation sample.

Those are enough to keep this out of the top band. The identification story and the decision are still the right ones, and the primary contrasts are careful, which is why the score stays high rather than collapsing.
