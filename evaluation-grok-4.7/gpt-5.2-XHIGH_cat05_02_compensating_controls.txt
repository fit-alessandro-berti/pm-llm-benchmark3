7.5

The assessment is substantively strong and mostly faithful to the case facts, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal inconsistencies, one clear control-type error, and a few evidence gaps keep it well below a top score.

The core judgments are defensible. Same-user execution is not automatically treated as failure, and distinct users are not automatically treated as success. AP-202 and AP-208 are failed for timing, independence, override, outage, and recoverability reasons. AP-204 is failed because a fabricated receipt passed a formally separate pair. AP-205 is correctly stopped before completion. AP-203 is a credible objective-met emergency. AP-201, AP-206, and AP-209 are graded as partial rather than by amount or by the raw four-eyes result. AP-208 correctly separates a legitimate payment from an inadequate control package. Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6 cover the required objectives, sufficiency dimensions, hybrid timing question, counterfactuals, and alert-versus-proof distinction.

The main logical flaw is that Section 2’s own rule is not applied. It says missing stop-settlement authority, ambiguous token time, or uncertain review quality should produce `INDETERMINATE`. Those are exactly the open facts in AP-201, AP-206, and AP-207, yet `INDETERMINATE` is never used. That also creates a ranking problem: AP-206 has independent approval before release, while AP-207 has only post-release ratification, but the ambiguous token pushes AP-206 down to partial and AP-207 up to met. The relative weighting of a logging defect over authorization timing is not reconciled with the control objective.

The findings table has concrete defects. AP-209 lists timely audit as a preventive control and omits the distinct releaser, contradicting Section 4. Post-release, pre-settlement manager action is defined as hybrid and then entered only as preventive for AP-201 and AP-207. Severity is undefined and inconsistent: objective-met, low-residual cases are scored Medium or Low–Medium. AP-204’s corrective capacity is softened to low–moderate even though the audit is about four days after release, outside the recall window. Manager level L3, an explicit MG-4 condition, is never tested or listed as missing.

The code meets the requested structure and correctly refuses to treat its flags as a verdict. It is still more fragile than the write-up admits. The strict emergency flag uses 24 hours rather than one business day, the unclear PM4Py branch can mislabel violators, and trace-attribute extraction plus `np.where` datetime handling can break across PM4Py and pandas versions.
