The write-up covers every required section and names real constraints (18 h urgent penalty, M2’s higher failure rate, specialist-review delay, courier spikes). The operational reasoning is still unsound in ways that would misdirect a peak response.

Scenario A’s mix is 45% + 25% + 20% and never reaches 100%, and it is not “similar” to the observed peak mix (Urgent 29%, Routine 42%, Complex-panel 20%). “One of two” specialist-review physicians is invented; the only headcount of two is the accession-correction pool. Urgent cases are said to keep the 18 h target in a worse-than-peak wave, while the observed peak urgent p90 is already 33 h and the normal p90 is already 19 h.

Scenario B applies the combined peak utilization of 98% to a normal-mix outage and says M1 “remains” there. Routing repeats onto M1 is said to raise the failure rate; the log says M2 is the less reliable machine, and repeat-after-failure cases already cluster on M2. Assay failures are then treated as pre-analytical recollection. Those are different loops: recollection returns to Receive sample or Cancel order; Repeat assay returns to Assay run or Medical validation.

Scenario C makes courier bunching increase the count of accession corrections and then backs the queue up into registration. Registration is upstream of intake. Arrival compression lengthens queues; it does not create accession defects. The 110% utilization and 20 h queue are unsupported.

Scenario D largely repeats Scenario A. The 36 h specialist queue is the observed peak median, not the result of a five-day blackout. The 240 h figure is the peak p90 for repeat-after-failure, not complex-panel (210 h). Flow counts over 8 weeks and 3 weeks (290 holds, 1,140 repeat edges) are treated as queue populations.

The migration table ranks stations but does not use transition rates or the performance edges. The important peak routing shift is unused: after technical validation, direct release falls from about 74% of exits to about 50%, while medical validation rises from about 25% to about 42% and specialist hold from about 2% to about 8%. Assay repeat edges rise from about 7% to about 15% of assay outcomes. Batch-wait incidence rises from about 4% to about 13%, and urgent batch bypass falls from 74% to 58%. Downstream medical or specialist blockage is also said to create assay repeats and pre-analytical failures, which reverses the flow.

The always-on package conflicts with those measurements. Technical validation is already at 101% with a 16 h peak queue, yet cross-training two technical validators into accession correction is permanent. The stated rationale, that this stops accession work from starving technical validation, inverts the transfer. Registration automation is justified by an invented 40% digital-complete share, and its failure mode is bad metadata. The stated downstream effect is idle clerks, while the overload lands on pre-analytical check and the analyzers. Courier smoothing is “always on” in one section and limited to scenarios C and D in the table.

The M2-outage policy is internally inconsistent: dedicate M1 to urgent and complex-panel work, which sends routine volume to M2, during an M2 outage. Urgent-first dispatch is described as not starving complex-panel, then the trade-off table says it may delay complex-panel. Several triggers use released p90, which is known only after the case finishes, and separate M1 versus M2 utilization, which the data do not split. Claimed effects such as a 30% cut in recollection and urgent p90 under 18 h are not derived from the log.

The residual list does include one self-inflicted flaw, extra M2 repeats under M1 dedication, then mitigates it by capping batch size at 12. Batch size does not change M2’s failure rate, and those repeats consume assay capacity twice. The permanent technical-validation drain and urgent-first WIP growth are not treated as redesign failures. Repeat-after-failure, the slowest type, is almost absent.

The dashboard omits technical-validation queue and utilization, the station already over capacity, and omits repeat rate, urgent bypass rate, and the technical-to-medical share. Overall rework is labeled leading; normal rework is already 7.5% and peak is 11.8%, so an 8% warning is on for the whole peak.
