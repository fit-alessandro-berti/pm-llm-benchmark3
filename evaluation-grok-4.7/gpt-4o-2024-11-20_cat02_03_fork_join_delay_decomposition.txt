3.5

The fork-join skeleton is right, and most branch durations and critical-branch labels match the timestamps, but the write-up breaks required identities, reverses a core synchronization result, and leaves several mandatory analyses unfinished.

Section 1 defines upstream time, branch queue, service, `join_ready = max(end_i)`, downstream queue, final service, and flow time correctly. The double-counting argument does not. `W_A + W_B + W_C ≥ fork_join_elapsed` is false on this data (F01: 27 versus 50). Synchronization waits overlap concurrently inside one fork-join interval; they do not “overlap sequentially.” The missing identity is `total_flow = upstream + max_i(Q_i+S_i) + downstream + final_service`. Summing `Q_i+S_i+W_i` over branches equals `3 × fork_join_elapsed`.

Section 2’s queue, service, `join_ready`, and critical-branch cells check out, and there are no exact ties. The flow-time column does not: F09 is 120 not 130, F10 is 115 not 135, F11 is 110 not 120, and F12 is 125 not 135. F05’s downstream queue is 10, not 20, so that row does not add up (`20+60+20+20 ≠ 110`). Units are mixed without labels. F07 and F11 are omitted despite an explicit request for worked calculations. Synchronization waits and fork-join elapsed time never appear case by case.

Section 3 gets the critical frequencies (A 2/12, B 8/12, C 2/12) but little else. Mean B service is 37.5, not 40. Mean C queue is 10.4, not 15. Mean synchronization wait is about 11.0 (A), 4.3 (B), and 30.4 (C). Calling B “High” and C “Low” reverses the evidence and contradicts B being critical on 8 cases, where its sync wait is 0. There is no tail contribution to `join_ready`, no queue-plus-service comparison (means are about 50 / 56.7 / 30.6), and no near-tie discussion. F01’s slack is only 5 minutes. The rank order B > A > C is plausible and not shown.

Section 4 names the three scenarios and notes that a non-critical branch can have zero case-level effect, then stops. A 20% service cut is 10 minutes, but F01 only has 5 minutes of slack to B, so the join moves by 5; F11’s slack is 10. Whether an earlier `join_ready` is absorbed by `final_start` is never tested, which is the masking question that was required. B’s 50% queue cut and extra final capacity are not computed per case. Downstream queues sum to 176 minutes (mean about 14.7), yet several F1/F2 handoffs have gaps, so that time is not all resource blocking.

Section 5 names two redesigns and does not cover throughput, flow-time tail, utilization, a new bottleneck, synchronization side effects, or what to pilot. “Large flow reduction” and “smaller but distributed gain” are unsupported. B is the better first lever only after slack-truncated, downstream-masked savings are compared with the final-station gap.

Section 6 is a stub. The table is incomplete, timestamps are not parsed, nothing is calculated, ties are not handled, counterfactuals are not run, and there are no printed results. `pm4py` is used without being imported; `performance_dfg` is imported and never called. There is no interval log with one row per branch or final activity, no `start_timestamp` / `time:timestamp` pair, and no comment that directly-follows performance cannot recover synchronization wait. The block would not run.

Section 7 briefly flags unknown branch capacity, preemption inside start–end, and that a final gap may be a policy rather than a queue. It never addresses causality: being last does not identify why the queue exists or what an intervention would save. Also unidentified are server pools, the constant 20-minute upstream step after F01, one-minute censoring, the single day of n=12, and C’s two long queues (35 and 25).
