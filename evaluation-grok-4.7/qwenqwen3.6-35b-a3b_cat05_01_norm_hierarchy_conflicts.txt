5.5

The final assessment is structured and often pointed in the right direction, but it is not reliable. It contradicts its own hierarchy on a central rule, misstates a source, and overstates confidence where the materials are expressly uncertain.

The clearest failure is P-505. The stated method says a later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment finance approval, and the hierarchy says the incident directive cannot displace that requirement. P-502 is treated as noncompliant on that basis. P-505 then receives JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION on the ground that the same finance rule was “waived by continuity,” with high confidence. That does not follow. The €88,000 approved-supplier purchase also does not trigger the compliance rule marked as satisfied. “May have been nine minutes fast” is treated as a corrected clock, and the wholly absent plant approval is never addressed. If the ERP time is real, the directive had already expired.

The hierarchy misreads the CFO email. The source gives the CFO authority to interpret finance controls and withholds any stated power to waive compliance or segregation of duties. The table treats finance and compliance as equally beyond the email. That error affects every in-window case above €50,000. Combined with a burden rule that resolves ambiguity against the actor, it produces seven noncompliant results and no indeterminate result, despite undefined directive scope, an unresolved question of whether the directive is a formal exception, and the €150,000 finance-interpretation issue.

Other applications are incomplete or uneven. Plant approval is required locally from €25,000 but is missed for several cases, including P-502 and P-507. P-501 treats same-day local approvals as pre-commitment breaches even though the global finance threshold is not met and the local rule does not state that timing; the commitment was also during the ERP outage and before the directive, which the case row does not confront. The emergency-form deadline, the difference between a new supplier and an unapproved supplier, and later-in-time priority are largely undeveloped. The authorization-recording rule is marked satisfied for P-508 without a recording time.

Section 5 does not repair this. The P-503 alternative claims compliance if the CFO email is elevated, but that premise still would not cure segregation of duties or the missing compliance clearance. Section 6 conflates the 14 July ERP outage, the 18 July clock issue, and the separate document-system outage, and it calls proved out-of-scope spending an inability to prove compliance. The closing clause is the strongest part: it separates authority, thresholds, segregation, expiry, and emergency normalization, although it still does not define critical scope.
