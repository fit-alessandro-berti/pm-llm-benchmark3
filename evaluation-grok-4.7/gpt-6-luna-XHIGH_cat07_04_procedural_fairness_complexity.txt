The answer is careful, numerically accurate, and well calibrated: raw gaps are not treated as discrimination, case-mix differences are not treated as a full defense, risk and documentation measures are treated as possibly contaminated, and support steps are treated as possible burdens rather than as steps to remove. All eight required sections are present, the constraints are respected, and the bottom line correctly stays at “indeterminate, with procedural concerns.”

It is not nearly flawless. Consistency of routing is folded into procedural burden instead of assessed as its own dimension. The resource evidence is incompletely used: Group A’s spread across 52 caseworkers, the three overloaded regional officers, and the two accommodation specialists who also perform supervisor review never appear, so concentration and dual-role effects are understated. “An appeal flag does not reveal whether an appeal was filed” is unclear, because that flag is the filing indicator; the real limits are outcome, accessibility, timeliness, and awareness.

The PM4Py sketch covers the requested families, but as written it only splits by group. Stratified filtering, median or tail performance, slow-path filters, organizational roles, and resource similarity are left to comments. `get_variants` is described as mapping variants to traces and then passed to `len`, which does not match the usual dataframe return of counts; the adaptation disclaimer only partly repairs that. Discovered Declare and log-skeleton models are not checks against pre-specified policy rules. The bottom line also underweights Group D’s residual appeal gap relative to the approval and delay findings it emphasizes for B and C.
