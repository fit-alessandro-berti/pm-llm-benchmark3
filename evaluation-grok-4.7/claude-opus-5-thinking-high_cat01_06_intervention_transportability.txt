The answer meets the required structure and reaches the right decision class (`conditional go`): Site A’s before/after contrast is confounded, unconditional identity∥document creation is unlawful for the 62% originals cohort, and any pilot has to separate fidelity from effect. The transport graph, modifier table, and mechanism-versus-implementation 2×2 are substantively strong. The score is held down by quantitative and design contradictions that the same write-up does not reconcile.

The headline cohort arithmetic does not close. `Register → Identity Check` (2,050) plus `Register → Originals Receipt` (1,860) is 3,910 directly-follows outcomes against 3,400 cases, and `Register → Originals Receipt` (1,860) plus `Identity Check → Originals Receipt` (1,020) is 2,880 receipt starts against 2,108 statutory originals cases. Section 1 flags imbalance, then Sections 5–6 and 9 still treat 1,020 and 2,108 as clean, nested cohort sizes. If those arcs are alternative predecessors, front-loading is already the directly-follows behavior for a large share of receipt events, so applying a −3 to −7 hour segment effect to the whole 62% overstates the lever.

Assessment load is double-counted. Assessment outflows already sum to 3,300 (1,360 + 1,220 + 720), which includes repeat executions. Adding the 345 supplement returns again produces the “≈47 per assessor-week” figure used to justify the bottleneck. The binding-constraint story may still be true, but that denominator does not support it. Selected arcs are also treated as a partition of all 3,400 cases: known approve/reject arcs cover 2,920 decisions, so “~30% rejected” is only the share of listed reject arcs.

Scenario magnitudes conflict. S2 says a −4 to −8 hour gain on the only mechanically eligible 38%, who sit on the fast side of the distribution, moves the overall median by −0.5 to −2 hours. S1 assumes the same ~38% can actually overlap and still claims an overall median move of −3 to −6 hours (44 → 38–41), with extra WIP harm on the other 62%. Under the answer’s own median logic, S1 cannot beat S2. S7’s −4 to −8 hours, repeated in Section 9 as the expected package result, sits above the sum of its own component scenarios.

The rollout contradicts itself. Wave 1b starts all 62% of originals cases immediately. Wave 3 and the recommendation then hold high-complexity legal originals until a later safety read. Those cases are inside the 62%. Stratum shares of ~23% and ~15% also require originals-required and legal-review to be independent, which is nowhere in the evidence and not in the assumption register.

The parallelization “ceiling” is over-tight. Sum-minus-max of the service medians is about 3.4–3.5 hours of activity span. The same paragraph concedes handoff and queue removal, which is part of deleting a serial edge, and Site A’s waiting-before-assessment fell by 6 hours. The 9-hour drop is still confounded; it is not direct evidence that a parallelization-only residual is 3–5 hours, and the “~2–4 hours from the mix shift” credit is not derived. The per-FTE drop (4,800/18 = 266.7 to 5,200/20 = 260) is real arithmetic on a denominator that is not productivity.

The pilot’s first-stage sample-size arithmetic is internally correct for an iid log-ratio test (~93 per arm), and the cycle-time log-sigma implied by median 44 and p90 110 is about 0.71. The cycle-time calculation uses 0.55 instead, and the design is a stream×period stepped wedge with shared pools (2 legal reviewers). Both choices make end-to-end power look better than the design and the baseline distribution support. Ring-fencing two legal reviewers across arms is asserted more than shown.

The POWL candidate does the required thing: `Identity Check` and `Document Check` are unordered, both precede `Assessment`, and `Originals Receipt` remains a predecessor of `Document Check`, with an optional legal loop. The formal redo path does not re-apply the originals gate that the prose requires, and legal pre-screen is absent from the node set the design depends on. The free XOR for statutory branches is correctly disclosed as not encoding the data guard.

Assumption coverage is serious, especially lock exemption, elapsed-versus-touch time, and evidence dependence. The eight tests would not catch the DFG non-partition, the legal×originals dependence, or the wave collision.
