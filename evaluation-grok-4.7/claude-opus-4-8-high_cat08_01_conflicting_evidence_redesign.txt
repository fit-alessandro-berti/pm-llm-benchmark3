The diagnosis is evidence-dense and numerically careful, and the three redesigns are genuinely different, segment-aware, and explicit about sacrifices. The score stays well below the top of the scale because several mechanism claims are stronger than the artifacts, and a few are wrong.

The fast-path contradiction is handled correctly: modal speed and the 31% pre-rework cost gap are set against the 14.1% versus 3.3% reopen split, the collapse to a 9% residual advantage, the 96h/180h complaint tail, fitness 0.91 with only 68% fitting traces, and the segment split in which accessories tolerate the fast lane while high-value devices do not. Failure modes, bottleneck migration, and the demand to judge net cost over the complaint horizon are all specific.

The mechanism section then overreaches. Amber routing forces a review before approval. That implements R1. It does not hard-wire R2, which is an evidence-completeness control before dispatch, and it does not remove “complaint/reopen after close.” The claim that A clears all three alignment deviations is false. The 22% to 5–6% reopen shift and “recovers most of the lost margin” treat path outcomes as treatment effects. The log does not give path mix by segment, and after rework the fast path is still 9% cheaper in aggregate, so “net cost down” is an unsupported decomposition. Redesign C ties the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate to a pre-dispatch replacement check; those are different activities, and the 96h edge is the duration of cases that already complained, not hours saved on every gated case. In the comparison table the compliance column is risk, and A/C use downward arrows for lower risk, but B uses an upward arrow while describing better R2 conformance. The pilot sequence says A needs C’s data “to calibrate gate rules”; A has no gate. Mid-risk devices, named in the process scope, never receive a lane. The Stage 1 stop rule benchmarks high-value reopen against accessory rates.

Numbers and required sections are reliable. The causal and control-flow claims are not.
