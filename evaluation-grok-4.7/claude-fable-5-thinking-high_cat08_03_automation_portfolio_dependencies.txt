7.5

The portfolio is coherent and unusually well grounded in the event log, but it is not nearly flawless. Several process-mechanism claims are overstated, one required effects section is incomplete, and the closing trade-off misstates what is actually given up.

The matrix, constraint handling, and control design are the strong parts. All twelve candidates are ranked with the requested columns and qualitative ratings. A1 is correctly treated as a visible, non-binding queue (1.5 h wait, 4% rework, back-office utilization below 65% after 15:00). A11 and A8 are correctly deferred as fail-open automation on unreliable upstream artifacts. A9 is a sound compliance anchor: it can fail closed on the contract-decision match (8.5%, 14% on credit-line renewal) and on KYC-before-signing (6.5% provisional signing). Pairing inflow cleanup (A5) with analyst-time relief (A6) matches the resource note that cleaner inflow overloads the 9 credit analysts unless normalization improves. Section 5 covers audit trails, false negatives, drift, four-eyes, provisional signing, and redeployment rather than assuming automation removes compliance responsibility. The four-module cap and the two-deep-integration limit are respected.

The central analytical error is equating an activity’s preceding wait with time the automation can remove. Resolve-entity-mismatch service time is 55 minutes; the median wait is 31 h. A4 is a medium-feasibility assistant for 3 specialists, not a removal of that wait. With rework at 38% and 1,160 cases looping to KYC, the defensible benefits are fewer loops, faster specialist touch time, and an audit trail. Section 4 nonetheless says A4 “cuts most of the 31 h,” and the matrix rates that process benefit “very high,” on par with the document loop. The same looseness appears when A5 is said to “remove” the 8 h request wait plus a repeated 18 h review wait, and when A4 is described as covering “the KYC branch.” A4 touches about 1,750 of 11,500 KYC cases. For the rest, the 4 h KYC wait and 11% exception rate stay in place because A3 is deferred. KYC and financial-document review are concurrent, so the critical path moves only where the treated branch was the longer one.

Related misses weaken the dependency story. The directly-follows relation Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation (380) is never discussed. That path can reach contracting without the 1,160-case return to KYC, which matters precisely because signing is illegal before KYC clearance. Section 2 says any wave that accelerates flow should include classification hardening, because an 8% classify exception rate misroutes the control path. Portfolio R accelerates the document branch without A2 and never reconciles that claim. A6’s matrix warning that successful extraction will push more cases onto credit analysis is also backwards in mechanism: normalization and credit analysis share the same 9 analysts, so A6 mainly converts 75-minute normalization work into capacity. The residual 110-minute judgment task and 28 h analysis wait can still bind, but that is a different statement.

Deep-integration labels are asserted rather than argued. A1, A9, A11, and part of A3 are treated as the deep core-banking set, while A4 is treated as a shallow workbench. Entity resolution often writes the customer master, so A4 may consume a scarce slot the plan thinks it saved. Using the second first-half slot to pre-build A3, which is outside the year-1 portfolio, is a choice rather than a constraint, and A4 is then scheduled in months 7–12 even though it does not depend on A5 or A6.

Section 4 does not meet the requested effects list for the portfolio being recommended. Q, B, and C are scored on cycle time, waiting, rework, handovers, resource demand, and control violations, with directly-follows evidence. R is one synthesis sentence. It therefore never states R’s handover or resource-demand effects, and it imports B’s cycle-time story even though R drops A7 and only partially covers KYC.

The final trade-off is pointed at the wrong sacrifice. The write-up says year 1 forgoes the registration headline and external valuation. Customer signing is the largest recorded wait pool in the log (7,300 cases at 44 h, plus a 21% reminder loop), ahead of the 58 h valuation tail on roughly 2,350 cases. A10 is feasible, ranked sixth, and only blocked until A9’s KYC gate exists; the text never weighs it against A4’s much smaller case count. Committee weekly cadence for high-exposure cases is also left out as a residual cycle-time floor. Four-eyes violations (5.8% of high-exposure cases at month end) are monitored and described as a future workflow block, but no year-1 module actually implements that segregation. Duplicate detection inside A1 is ignored, so A1 is dismissed solely as a frequency trap. Gate numbers such as “repeat rate below ~30%” and “remediation halved” are unanchored.

Portfolio R remains a defensible four-module set because A5, A6, and A9 sit on real loops, the constrained analyst pool, and a control finding. The grade is held down because the argument for the fourth module, the KYC-branch effect, the bypass path, and the stated sacrifice do not hold at the level of precision the rest of the answer aims for.
