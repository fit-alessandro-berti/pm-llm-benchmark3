The conclusions are technically strong: Model S and Model C are interpreted under standard process-tree semantics, the B1–B8 acceptance judgments are correct, and the metric results are treated as non-comparable diagnostics rather than a leaderboard. The third design is a real alternative, not a restatement of either submission. Its visible language enforces both initial checks, exactly one approval, bounded Supplier/Expedite behavior, and repeatable selective rechecks, while refusing to turn missing externality or affected-check data into a pass. The validation plan and dissent are substantive, and no new metric values are invented.

It is not nearly flawless. The structural model imposes contiguous checks and forbids Supplier Review between them, but the assumption inventory and adversarial audit mainly surface Supplier-before-checks and the correction window. Those are different restrictions, and the literal policy does not settle the between-checks case. The external checker also trusts a caller-supplied `correction_cycles` annotation instead of deriving and reconciling the repeated-check sets from the accepted trace, so an inconsistent annotation can yield a false pass or failure. The alignment-precision mechanism is described a little too definitely for an unspecified PM4Py version. These are decision-relevant holes in an otherwise careful governance argument.
