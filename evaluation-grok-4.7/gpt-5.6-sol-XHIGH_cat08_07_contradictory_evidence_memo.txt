8.0

The memo is a strong, constraint-respecting decision document: it selects a segmented hybrid, refuses both national scale-up and a full rollback, keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions apart, and uses the mining evidence rather than only the KPI table. The numbers that are cited match the brief, the five metric conflicts are explained in the right causal shape, and the measurement ranking is tied to whether rollout scope should change.

It is not nearly flawless. The opening memo allows “explicitly authorized” post-billing activation, which is the same exception class that produced billing-before-activation and complaint migration, while the final section bans that practice unless it is separately tested. Section 4 then calls coordinator ownership a strong default for non-simple segments; section 6 allows it outside simple houses only inside an absence test. Those are different decisions.

Low generalization is treated as evidence that the six-week model will not travel nationally. In process mining that score is about overfitting to the log, and pairing it with very low precision (a permissive model) is left unresolved. The rollout gates look quantitative but are not: “1-point” covers first-time-right, complaints, and SLA without units; the “or” list does not say whether every bound is required; 95% sensitivity ignores negative predictive value and whether anyone remains eligible to skip; and a zero unauthorized-billing rule sits beside the memo’s own warning that event order may be a logging artifact. Business and rural harm are also paused mainly as survey-skip problems, despite notes pointing to manual activation and rescheduling. The complaint-theme shift and the simple-house SLA improvement are barely used.
