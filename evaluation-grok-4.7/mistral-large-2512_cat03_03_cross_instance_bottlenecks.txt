5.5

The write-up hits the required eight-part skeleton, keeps machine capacity off the top of the ranking, and gets the plain B1 arithmetic right (J1 3 h 55 min, J2 3 h 40 min, J3 10 min). It is not a reliable account of the coupling. Several quantities are wrong or over-claimed, one narrative links objects that do not form a path, and the cascade the timestamps actually support is never assembled.

**Quantitative errors and over-claims.** OVEN-1’s idle window is 12:55–14:50, which is 1 h 55 min, not the “2 h 50 min” used as rank-1 evidence. The L7 hold is 10:20–13:50 (3 h 30 min), but that is an upper bound only if J5 was otherwise ready at quarantine start, which is not shown. Calling it a “minimum delay,” then counterfactually placing J5 ready at an invented 11:00, reverses the bound and contradicts itself (14:40 − 11:00 is not 3 h 30 min). The FIX-A figure of 1 h 40 min (11:45 − 10:05) treats the whole span as tool delay. Documented CNC-2 idleness is only 10:05–10:47; 10:47–11:40 is unexplained, and the tight evidence is the 5 min from FIX-A release at 11:40 to process start at 11:45. “After setup” invents a duration. S1’s 19:25 − 13:30 = 5 h 55 min is arithmetically right, but S2/S3 and the shipment DFG (Load → Dispatch median 0.3 h) show a normal post-pack lag. J4 is packed at 19:10, so partner wait is about 5 h 40 min and the last 15 min is ordinary dispatch. Labeling the full gaps “avoidable” and, in the ranking table, “due to FIX-A” / “delays J5 by 3 h 30 m” drops the counterfactual standard the calculations themselves announce.

**Coupling structure.** The graph records `(J2, waits_for, L7)` even though J2 entered B1 on a supervisor override before `Release Lot` at 13:50, and `(J3, waits_for, FIX-A)` even though J3 is the holder. Batch–machine and batch–tool links are event co-occurrence, not given O2O qualifiers, yet are marked direct. The strongest cross-batch fact is almost unused in the numbers: RACK-7 is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35 while the oven is idle and J4 has been ready since 13:10. J3 holds FIX-A until 11:40 and is batch-ready only at 11:55, so B1’s long wait may be J3’s tool hold amplified by the batch barrier, not an independent “batch bottleneck.” J4 then shares B2 with lot-gated J5, packs at 19:10, and holds already-packed J1 in S1. That Job–Tool–Machine–Batch–Tool–Lot–Shipment cascade is the coupling story. It is never traced. Narrative 2 spans only Job, Tool, and Machine. Narrative 3 staples L7 to S1, although S1 is J1+J4 and L7 is C2+C5 (J2, J5). A noon cure slot that J3 merely caught is the obvious rival to “wait for J3,” and it is not taken seriously. L8 (C1, C3) is listed and then ignored. The 1.8 SD job-duration fact is unused.

**Flattening, interventions, code, falsification.** Section 4 names all six views and correctly says idle machines can coexist with job waits, but it never says what flattening duplicates (one `Batch Cure` copied onto J1–J3) and its “misleading conclusions” are generic slogans. Interventions cover batching, tools, shipment policy, and logging, yet harms are thin or wrong (dynamic batching’s real spillover is extra OVEN-1/RACK-7 cycles pushed onto B2, J4, J5, and S1, not “J3”). “Perverse incentive” is usually a behavior, and logging is given “None.” The Python block names most required APIs and does not derive waits from machine gaps, but it never calls `discover_otg`, does not compare two object-centric frequency metrics, and would not run (`view_ocdfg`, invented event columns, `extract_ocel_features` arguments, batch-wait loop not filtered to the current batch). Several “falsifiers” do not touch batch synchronization (in-cure utilization, within-batch service-time variance).

Checklist coverage is real; the arithmetic, the graph, and the causal story are not trustworthy enough to go higher.
