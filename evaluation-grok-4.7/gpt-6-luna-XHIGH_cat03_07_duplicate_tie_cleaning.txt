7.2

The conservative design is coherent: the decision tree separates the six required cases, the recommended log keeps distinct picks and partial payments, and abstraction-level events stay unless a separate scenario says otherwise. Provenance, reversibility, and the semantic-versus-deterministic ordering split are also largely sound. The score is held down by a direct contradiction in the aggressive scenario and by several smaller accuracy gaps.

The mechanical-merge count is wrong on the answer’s own rule. `have_common_object` with equal activity and timestamp groups every connected event that shares an object. e15 is `Capture Payment` at 12:00:00 and shares O1 and V1 with both e13 and e14, so the cluster is e13/e14/e15, not e13/e14. That yields 10 retained events, not 11. Union of typed links gives 28 E2O relations: picks 9→4 (−5), packs 6→4 (−2), the two dispatch rows 6→3 (−3), invoices 4→2 (−2), and captures 8→4 (−4). The stated 11 events and about 30 links omit that cluster. Section 7 then uses 11/30 as a dataset baseline, so the aggressive OC-DFG, payment flattening, and ET–OT comparison understate the loss of the ERP balance summary and the union of M1 and M2 onto that summary. The version hedge does not repair this, because the omission already follows from the stated any-common-object rule.

Section 4 also misstates the conservative relation arithmetic before correcting it. It says the two merges remove three redundant links, while the parenthesis describes e04’s three links and e16’s two. The later formula 44−5=39 is the consistent one: raw relations are 44, and e03/e04 and e11/e16 contribute −3 and −2. Evidence-based 14 events and 37 links follow correctly from also dropping e07’s two redundant links.

Ordering is careful in prose and looser in the implementation. Invoice-before-authorization is correctly treated as unconfirmed, and e09 before e10 is grounded in the known carrier sequence. The code nevertheless always ranks e11 before e12 and e08 before e09. Release-before-acceptance is a plausible lifecycle reading, not a supplied constraint, and no alternative admissible tie is kept. Ingestion time is still listed as a last-resort ordering input.

The verified-pair helper is not runnable as written. `merge_pair` adds `_source_records_json` and related fields to the Series, then assigns `a.index` back with `.loc` before those columns exist, which raises `KeyError`. The exact-duplicate signature also includes `ingested_at` and every other attribute, so it does not implement the decision tree’s “transport identity only” test. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is constrained by a temporary activity rewrite whose effect depends on an untested library interpretation, and relations of a dropped row are not repaired inside that helper.

The required PM4Py calls, deep copies, original timestamp and activity columns, referential checks, and the e13/e14 common-object warning are present. Those strengths do not offset the incorrect aggressive partition or the broken custom merge.
