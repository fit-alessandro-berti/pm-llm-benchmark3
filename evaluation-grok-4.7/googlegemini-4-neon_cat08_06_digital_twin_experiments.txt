7.7

The design is strong, complete, and mostly faithful to the constraints, but it is not nearly flawless. Several concrete inconsistencies would bias intervention rankings or validation.

The scope is the right kind of model: a calendar-aware discrete-event twin with case and resource state, latent objections, architect bursts, neighborhood coupling, and inspection batching, with PM4Py kept outside the engine. Service time is not collapsed into sojourn time, missing objections are treated as latent, and the text correctly refuses an “optimal” policy from an unvalidated model. The experiment set covers B0, D1–D10, a combined portfolio, and more than three stresses, with credible trade-offs (especially bottleneck shift and possible redundancy of D4 once D2 and D3 exist). Warm-up over a full seasonal cycle, common random numbers, nested aleatory/epistemic uncertainty, and heavy-tail quantiles are appropriate.

The most serious problems:

- Baseline is misspecified as “no batching.” The brief lists architect/district bundling as a current routing rule, not only as D3. Calibrating travel and inspection waits with zero baseline batching, then crediting D3 for introducing it, double-counts the intervention and will fail replay against the log.
- The coded batch trigger uses case submission time, not time of arrival to the inspection buffer. Long-running cases would flush batches immediately, which breaks the stated wait-versus-travel trade-off.
- Default `parcel_cluster_id=""` puts every unset case in one objection cluster, so the cross-case shock couples unrelated applications.
- The objection duration draw, `14 + LogNormal(3.2, 0.65)` with probabilities 0.18/0.08, is not labeled as uncalibrated and does not reproduce the given 14-day floor and 60-day objection tail. NumPy’s lognormal parameters are the underlying normal, so the implied objection quantiles are far from the summary the mixture is supposed to match.
- SLA gates are not reconcilable with the supplied durations. Simple ≤ 20 days at ≥ 90% is already incompatible with a 22-day median on the missing-docs path and a 24-day p90 on the clean path. Those thresholds would reject almost every policy, including ones that remove missing-document rework.
- “Replay validation” mixes fixed historical paths with resimulated routing. Alignments and variant shares then confound control-flow error with stochastic rework, so timing and resource checks are not identified.
- Section 2 correctly wants sojourn from the enabling predecessor, but the code uses timestamp `shift(1)`. Under the parallel planning/fire/environment block, that attributes one branch’s clock to the other and will bias service/wait deconvolution. The write-up never warns that a performance DFG also linearizes concurrency.
- Assumptions say background arrivals are inhomogeneous Poisson; the estimation plan says a negative-binomial GLM. Those are different variance models.
- `effective_capacity` omits public holidays, the D6 planner reserve, and urgent legal work during council weeks, despite the prose and the problem statement treating holidays and council calendars as first-class. Appeal re-entry is named but has no path. Planner dispatch among review, drafting, and rework is unspecified except for D6. Utilization is a 75–85% band with no denominator (shift hours versus paid FTE), which is not comparable across a Tuesday–Thursday fire pool and a Monday-reduced clerk pool.
- Welch’s moving average is a steady-state warm-up tool. Using it as the transient check for a system whose mean is supposed to keep moving with the season is the wrong diagnostic, even though a 12-month warm-up is the right idea.

PM4Py usage itself is largely conventional: pandas load, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, and pandas `case_arrival.get_case_arrival_average`, plus an explicit process-tree caveat. The skeleton is modular, but the parts that are implemented are not always consistent with the design, and D1/D7 applicant cost never appears in the returned KPIs. This is a serious, usable design with errors that a strict review should not waive.
