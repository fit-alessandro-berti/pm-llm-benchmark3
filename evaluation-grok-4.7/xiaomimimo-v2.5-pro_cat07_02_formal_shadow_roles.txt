6.5

The final write-up is organized and mostly faithful to the counts, similarities, complexity medians, and substitution episodes, and it respects the main constraints: complexity is used, substitution is not treated as permanent competence, similar profiles are not treated as equal authority, and shadow work is given both benefits and risks. It is not a reliable role design.

The role table omits R05_Erik and R07_Giorgio. Erik is the clearest rare specialist in the log (96 security reviews, median complexity 0.86) and never appears as a proposed role. Giorgio is only a footnote, so the structure a process owner would adopt has no commercial-shadow member and no security function. Mateo’s “core” set incorrectly includes A6 (16 events) and A8, while his heavier A2/A4/A12 work is brushed aside; that conflicts with the later, better claim that he filters security work rather than owning it.

Section 2 does not cover each proposed role. Automation, quality, and supervision are skipped, and substitutions and authority are thin. The temp-rework explanation is wrong: the 17 reopenings are tied to incomplete Validate Entitlement notes, a failed core activity, not to a lack of peripheral authority. Chen is asserted to have backed up security “during high loads”; the high-load episode is Mateo filtering Erik’s referrals. Dina’s knowledge-base volume (11 versus Chen’s 14) is grouped into “much less,” which overstates the gap. Amira’s negotiation is assigned to low-complexity edge cases without evidence.

Several “naïve clustering” cells are implausible. Fatima’s mass is on pricing and negotiation, so she is unlikely to be pulled into front office. Quality is dominated by quality check and knowledge-base update, so clustering them with technical staff on small configure/implement counts is not credible. The real traps—the Chen/Dina split, the temp/permanent split, Giorgio’s hybrid profile, and Mateo looking like a generalist—are identified, but they sit next to these weaker claims.

Section 4’s decision rule for Dina does not separate the interpretations: equal performance on hard cases would not falsify intentional tiering. For Mateo, 13 of 21 preliminary approvals being rechecked is evidence against deputy authority, yet the text says that episode strongly supports a deputy-supervisor reading. That contradicts the more careful rework point made earlier.

The code names the requested functions and correctly baselines roles after removing substitution events, but it is not coherent PM4Py. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key`. `discover_organizational_roles` returns role objects, not a dict, so `roles.items()` fails. Median complexity is computed on events, so cases with more events are overweighted against the case-level complexity table. Handover and working-together networks are created and then unused.

The staffing cautions about Dina versus Chen and about removing Giorgio’s cover without replacement are sound. “Service times doubled” overgeneralizes a pricing-exception duration change from 38 to 74 minutes, and “the commercial process would halt entirely” ignores other resources who already touch pricing.
