The writeup is a serious causal analysis with the right headline conclusion, but it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative, logical, and reproducibility failures keep it well below an excellent score.

What holds up: availability is separated from use, selection, and concurrent shocks; median and p90 are not collapsed; the single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is rejected; mechanisms and the threat list match the process (fraud desk, routing, staffing, case mix, learning, survey nonresponse, SUTVA, small clusters). Hand-checked pre/post levels and the reported DiDs are arithmetically right (Alpha median about −0.4 h, p90 about +9.7 h, Alpha−Delta median DiD about −6.4 h, Gamma pre/post median about −3.4 h). Low confidence and “expand with an embedded evaluation” are defensible.

Material problems:

- Signs are missing on quantities that are negative, next to explicit “+” values (Alpha median “0.4”, DiDs “6.4” and “7.9”, Gamma “3.4”, rework “13.211.2”). As written, the section 5 tables can be read with the wrong sign. The underlying arithmetic matches the correct signed contrasts, so this is a severe clarity failure in the delivered text.
- The sensitivity row “Gamma’s own pre-trend extrapolation | about 3 h | about 1 h” is not an extrapolation. A linear fit to Gamma’s January–June median (about +2.1 h per month) predicts a July–August mean near 49 h versus an actual 37.2 h, an implied gap near −12 h, not −3 h. Those figures are just the simple pre/post deltas (−3.4 h and −0.9 h), so one required sensitivity is mislabeled and duplicates another contrast.
- Section 7 says Alpha/Beta p90 “rose by about 10 h immediately after adoption.” That is false. Alpha p90 went from 99.4 in March to 97.3 in April; Beta from 93.8 to 95.6. The tail jump is in May, with the complexity shift and Alpha’s loss of two FTEs. Averaging April–August into “immediate” hides the timing that matters for mechanism.
- The claim ladder’s “Conditionally causal: … roughly 4 hours” overstates a crude, selectively paired DiD (Alpha−Delta and Beta−Epsilon only; Alpha−Epsilon and Beta−Delta are omitted). The same paragraph says those assumptions identify median but not p90, rework, escalation, or CSAT. Identification would not switch on by outcome; only the estimates differ. That conflates design with result, and it conflicts with the earlier statement that the design is only a disciplined description.
- Section 9 does not run. `rel_-3` and `rel_-2` are parsed by Patsy as subtraction, not column names. `adopt_month.str.slice(...).astype(int)` is evaluated on Delta/Epsilon missing adoption dates and will hit a NaN integer cast. Even if fixed, leads −6/−5/−4 for Gamma are coded as the omitted baseline, and the fitted model is the TWFE event study the identification section says not to treat as primary. No event-time contrasts appear in the narrative despite that being the stated design.
- Rollout text both randomizes Delta versus Epsilon and pre-assigns Delta first. Workload is never computed, despite a large arrivals/FTE rise (Alpha roughly 31 to about 40 cases per FTE) with a flat median — the sharpest descriptive fact in the panel. The section 3 rationale is only about 120 words against a 150-word floor. “48–50% by August” ignores Gamma at 32%. “Partly reverts” understates Alpha rework, which falls to 11.2 in April and then sits at 14.5, above the 13.2 baseline. E1’s population is “eligible branches or the treated population,” which mixes ATT with a different target.

The judgment that a single percentage claim is not identified, and that any expansion needs a holdout, guardrails, and case-level paths, is the right decision frame. The timing error, the false extrapolation, the over-precise conditional effect, and non-runnable code are large enough that this should not score in the high band.
