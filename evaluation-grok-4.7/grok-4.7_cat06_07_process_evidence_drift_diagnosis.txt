The diagnosis is strong and correctly refuses a single drift label. April is treated as a schema and pipeline failure with mature labels, June as a routing-mix change whose base-rate drop is reproduced by stable path-conditional breach, August as supplier-driven performance and \(P(\text{breach}\mid V3)\) change before Partner Z exists, September as that regime plus population shift plus thinning labels, and October as non-comparable censoring. The score is not higher because the required code does not run as written and a few claims are looser than the evidence.

The process-evidence function calls `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_starting")`. The supported modes are `events`, `traces_contained`, and `traces_intersecting`, so the month loop dies on the first cross-check. `discover_performance_dfg` is then forced through `float(value) / 3600`. That holds only if the installed build returns mean seconds; a stats dict or timedelta raises, and the independent median never gets returned. `median_events_before_diagnosis` is computed only on cases with at least one prior event, so zeros drop out and the median is biased high exactly on the cached prefix feature the April audit needs. In-progress case-start traces with no `Order Part`, `Remote Fix`, or `Dispatch Technician` yet are labeled `unmapped_other`, so October would look like April’s mapping failure. Exact `(case, activity, timestamp)` duplicates are discarded before sorting and are absent from `near_duplicate_rows_removed`; the one-second consecutive rule also misses non-adjacent migration duplicates and can drop real repeats.

In the prose, 17% unknown-activity share and 18% unmapped variants are called the same mass, but one is an event rate and the other a case rate. “AUROC stayed near 0.79” is attached to a sentence that includes July, whose AUROC is 0.76. Concept drift is marked high for August–September even though the same answer’s falsifier says a stable wait–breach curve would reclassify it as process change plus a missing delay covariate; those are different claims and should not share one confidence. Section 1 says not to add partner identity, while the covariate-drift action and the retrain feature list both add `partner`. “Live” parts delay is also left ambiguous between an exogenous supplier lag, which is a valid diagnosis-time feature, and the case’s own future `Order Part → Part Received` wait, which would leak. The safe-month list does not say that pooling January–March with June without an intended-route or policy indicator mixes two base rates.
