8.4

The capacity arithmetic, scenario mechanics, objective arithmetic, and simulation design are unusually careful and internally consistent. The answer still falls short of a near-flawless policy evaluation because the headline ranking rests on one strained baseline, a thin deciding contrast, and a non-steady-state experiment.

The offered-load work checks out. Midpoint means (6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50, 15.74), occurrence counts (42, 18.09, 24.30, 12.85, 42), daily base load 2,720.6, net supply 2,490, the 90% budget of 2,241, the endpoint band 2,065–3,376, block capacities, and the U1–U5 load and supply shifts all follow from the stated definitions. Because every multiplier is at least 1, mean base work already exceeds even full-overtime clock time (2,670). That infeasibility result is the right foundation for the staffing conclusion. Unit-speed minutes and worker-minutes are easy to mix in the block table, but the text does identify the 14:00–16:00 and 16:00–18:00 comparisons as unit-speed tests, so the overload direction is valid.

The robust score is also auditable. With shortfalls as fractions, U5 is the binding scenario for all three policies, and the reported losses 2.44, 2.61, and 4.49 match the table. The stated P2-weight sensitivity is real: raising that weight from 1 to 3 makes Pool’s U5 loss smaller than Hybrid’s. Assumptions that the prompt leaves open — Poisson counts, uniform draws, team labels, staff-free handovers and joins, zero overtime, empty start, and a quiet drain — are named.

The three dispatchers are concrete enough to implement: fixed time-of-day owners, earliest predicted finish with single-worker preemption, and primary-skill gates, a one-person P1 reserve, P3 aging to a 600-minute virtual due date, and an explicit four-term route cost. Parallel-versus-sequential examples (Deepa at 65.7 versus a 40.8 parallel path; Chen at 80.9 versus 46.5) use the right means and multipliers. The code’s aging offset of 840, U1 reweighting, U2 renormalization, U4 diagnostic tails, once-only diagnostic preemption, and PM4Py caveats match the prose. Treating `@@waiting_time` as sojourn minus summed interval service, and refusing to use it as queue delay when branches overlap, is the correct process-mining reading.

The fixed arm is a weak basis for the specialization-versus-pooling claim. From 12:00–16:00 the fastest electrical resource is locked to Validation while electrical load is 138 and then 204 minutes; after 16:00 one person owns Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, and Validation. The failure modes admit this, but there is no second fixed roster, so the large SLA gap is not identified as pooling itself rather than that roster. Hybrid versus Pool is similarly soft. Their P1 intervals overlap in every scenario, including U5 (about 55–56%). The 0.17 loss gap is carried by P3, p90, and an arbitrary weight vector, yet those deciding metrics have no intervals, and the composite loss has no paired uncertainty. Fairness and peak utilization enter only from Base, where the 90% screen has already failed for every policy.

The simulated SLAs are one empty day plus a drain with no new arrivals. That design is disclosed, and it is not a steady-state ranking. Handover time is “7 minutes per edge” but implemented as a maximum; slack ignores multipliers; the 600-minute P3 age is aggressive relative to the 480-minute P2 deadline; team labels drive the extra 5 minutes and are not varied. Section 9 states these limits and the pilot is appropriately protective. Those disclosures keep the answer coherent, but the point estimates in §4–§5 are still sharper than the experimental design supports.
