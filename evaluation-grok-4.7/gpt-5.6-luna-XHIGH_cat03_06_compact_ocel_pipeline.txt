7.5

The construction is internally consistent and largely faithful to the narrative, but several interpretive claims misstate what the requested views actually contain. That is a central failure for this task, so the score stays well below excellent.

The data model itself is strong. All 17 events, all 12 objects, timezone-aware UTC timestamps, event-level amounts of 100 and 150, and the required O2O edges are present and match the story. Relations are derived from the event table, so activity and timestamp cannot drift, and the asserts cover unique IDs, orphans, duplicate rows, and event–relation consistency. Linking e006 to O100, O200, I1, and I3, and e007 to both orders plus P1, I1, and I3, follows the narrative. Leaving e008, dispatch, confirmation, and payments off the orders is a coherent source-fact policy, and keeping both M1 and M2 as `partially_settles` is right: no invoice total is given, so `completes_settlement` would invent a fact. The Order, Package, Delivery, and Invoice trace shapes, copied shared events, omitted steps, and likely DFG edges follow from those E2O choices. The Payment-length argument is correct.

Section 5 blurs type-level discovery with instance-level facts. An OC-DFG aggregates activities and object types; it does not retain e006, e007, V1, M1, M2, or distinct close events for O100 versus O200 as nodes. The OTG paragraph starts at type level (“Orders connect to Items”) and then treats V1, O100, O200, P1, I1, and I3 as graph nodes in the same structure. The P1 bridge through I1 and I3 is a real O2O insight, but it is an instance multi-hop, and the write-up never fixes the granularity. The same section is more careful about ET–OT, which makes the OC-DFG and OTG over-specification stand out. “P1 contains I1 and I3” also collides with `contains`, which this model reserves for Order→Item; the package qualifier is `packs`.

Section 6 then overreaches. Calling the chosen policy source-minimal is fair under their own definition, but “object-centric structures make the paths available through O2O relationships” is not. Flattening, the OC-DFG, and the usual interactions summary follow direct E2O co-occurrence. They will not place dispatch, confirmation, or payment on an Order merely because O2O can walk Order–Item–Package–Delivery or Invoice–Payment. That sentence contradicts the provenance split argued in sections 1 and 7.

Section 3 names convergence well (e006, e007, e011, and the two payments on V1) and correctly flags e008 as sparse. It does not identify divergence in the object-centric sense: one object with several events of the same activity. O100 has two Add Item events, and V1 has two Record Payment events. The Pack Package incidence table also reports Order for the activity as a whole even though only e007, not e008, touches an order; the prose repairs this, the table does not. “Interchangeable payments” is slightly too strong, because 100 and 150 remain event attributes after invoice flattening even though M1 and M2 disappear as objects.

The qualifier inventory is applied consistently but is morphologically uneven (`dispatched` beside `dispatch_delivery`, `issued` beside `billed_order` and `settlement_target`). Validation never checks that a relation’s `ocel:type` matches `objects` for the same `ocel:oid`. Failure modes and the manual checks are specific and mostly accurate; the Petri-net caveat correctly refuses a unique net.

The executable-looking pipeline and the flattened predictions are the part that holds up. The grade drops because the required account of what OC-DFG, OTG, and O2O each reveal is partly the wrong granularity and partly a stronger claim than those structures support.
