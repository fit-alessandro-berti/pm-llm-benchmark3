**6.8**

The twin design is substantive: shared pools, business versus calendar clocks, latent objections, seasonal burn-in, paired common random numbers, separate aleatory and epistemic uncertainty, pre-registered checks, and a non-inferiority rule. Those parts are usable. The score stays well below excellent because the PM4Py grounding is reversed, one headline arrival figure is inconsistent, and the skeleton does not implement the design it states.

**Process tree.** In PM4Py, `X` is exclusive choice, `+` is parallel, `->` is sequence, and `*` is loop. The supplied string already follows that: optional fire and environment reviews, a parallel review block, and an exclusive issue/reject choice. The write-up states the opposite (`+` as XOR, `&` as parallel). The fallback `TREE_PM4PY` then rewrites a valid tree into a bad one: `+(tau, 'Fire-safety review')` is a parallel of skip and fire, so fire always runs, and `+('Issue permit','Reject application')` is a parallel of both terminals. A sequence after that parallel block already joins before decision drafting. The real gap is attribute-conditioned routing and cross-case coupling, which the prose does cover elsewhere.

**Arrival numbers.** `16.8a = 31,200` applies a 12-month seasonal weight to an 18-month caseload, so the stated low-season rate of about 1,857 cases per month (about 60 per day) sits above the overall mean of about 1,733 per month. With six months at 1.7×, the low-season month has to sit below that mean. The default seasonal index also averages about 1.4 while `base_rate_per_day` is the overall mean, so the generator inflates volume.

**Capacity and objections.** The planning, fire, and environment load ratios, using 31,200/18 and a lognormal mean `exp(μ+σ²/2)`, support the conclusion that the published “service times” cannot be pure occupancy. The same test also makes the clerk pool infeasible, and that pool is left out of the audit. The long public-notice component is treated as an objection mixture, while council meetings and holidays are also said to stretch public notice, so the latent objection rate that D9 depends on is confounded.

**Skeleton.** `from sklearn...` is a syntax error. `heapq` is used in `_try_schedule` and imported only inside `run`. Completions are stored on `case.trace`, while `run` returns `self.traces`, which stays empty. `release` never starts the next queued case, so contention does not propagate. `busy_fraction` is ignored, leaving environment at 3 FTE rather than 1.8 permit FTE. `make_experiment_matrix` sets `d1`–`d9` without the levels that change behavior (`missing_doc_reduction` stays 0, `batch_window_days` stays 0, `weekday_target` stays empty). `get_event_attribute_values` weights case mix by event count. The district standardized p90 drops a missing case type and does not renormalize the weights. The performance DFG call uses `Algorithm.DFG_PERFORMANCE`; the maintained name is `Variants.PERFORMANCE`. The manual fallback is the part that works. `format_dataframe` is used correctly, and there is no `pm4py.read_csv`.

**Rules.** A KS p-value cutoff of 0.05 on sojourns with tens of thousands of cases rejects trivial differences. The utilization band [0.65, 0.85] on every pool rejects both a real reduction from overload and the unlimited system pool. `prior_violations`, which D2 requires, is absent from the log and is not treated as missing data.

The experiment logic still carries the score: D1 false-complete rework, D2 bottleneck shift, D3 median-versus-tail trade-off, D4/D2 subadditivity, D9 marked as unidentified, stresses S1–S5, replication-level intervals, and an explicit refusal to treat a simulation ranking as an optimum.
