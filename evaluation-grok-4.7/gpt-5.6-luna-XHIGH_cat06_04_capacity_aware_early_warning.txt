The allocation then breaks the rule that produces those numbers. The acceptance test requires the lower net to stay positive after the queue shadow price, and it forbids treating spillover as zero when untreated values are unknown. I-07’s pre-spillover floor is only €500 and its central direct net is about €800. At the answer’s own €15,000 orientation, one slot’s shadow price is about €900–€2,700, so I-07’s marginal net is negative across that range and dropping it improves the base portfolio. Section 5 still cites I-07 as meeting a “non-negative conservative net after queue harm.” That claim is false under the answer’s own spillover model, and it is the case that makes the three-slot book fragile.

Smaller inaccuracies compound that. Strategic adjustment of I-08’s 0.43 is 0.31–0.38, not 0.34–0.41. I-01’s €920 floor is not below every selected Tier A case, because I-07 is €500. I-09’s rise is +0.07, short of the stated 0.08 gate, while I-02’s +0.08 meets it and still waits. I-04 is dispatched immediately with about 10 hours left, which sits poorly with the rule’s urgency weight and with the instruction that an opportunity which will not disappear should not consume a slot early. “High-risk/action band,” the urgency multiplier, and the fairness capacity floor never become numbers an on-call lead could apply. The base row folds in the full 0.48–0.90 effect range, and the adverse net of −€7,700 to +€3,700 pairs failed effects with low spillover, so the positive end is outside an adverse scenario defined by congestion. The 0.05 calibration tripwire is already inside the known SME and Strategic biases, so it does not detect deterioration beyond those bands.
