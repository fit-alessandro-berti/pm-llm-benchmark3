7.0

The assessment is complete, numerically faithful to the tables, and properly calibrated: raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination, residual gaps are not waved away as case mix, risk scores are treated as suspect, and rights-preserving steps are kept while their waiting time and handovers are counted as burden. Section 8’s “indeterminate overall, unfair in specific procedural dimensions” conclusion matches the evidence.

The score is held down by real methodological errors, not polish.

Matching is described as creating “case-mix twins within each group.” Matching has to pair cases across groups. That sentence gets the design wrong.

The sensitivity analysis has the causal direction confused. If `risk_score_band` is a biased mediator, dropping it should typically widen the group gap; a gap that shrinks when the control is removed does not show that the score “transmits bias.” The documentation-completeness claim likewise mislabels ordinary confounding as suppression. That undermines the required distinction among disparity, legitimate routing, and bad controls.

The PM4Py plan misuses several functions it was required to use. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` does not take start and end activities. `filter_paths_performance` filters a log by path-duration bounds; it does not return a median cycle time. `filter_case_performance` filters on case duration, not on high-risk or in-person status. `format_dataframe` is given nonexistent `resource_key` and `other_attributes` arguments, `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with `attribute_values` instead of `values`, and the variant-filter line is not executable. The surrounding prose states the right intent, but the code would not perform the analysis claimed.

Smaller slips add up. V1 is more than double for Group A versus B and D, not “nearly double,” and that raw share gap is largely what the case mix predicts, so it is weak evidence of unexplained routing. Rework is highest for Group C (24%), but later text assigns elevated rework to B and D. Section 1 calls the residual approval gap an “unjustified disparity,” which is stronger than the indeterminacy argued in Sections 5 and 8.
