6.5

The stage table, occurrence counts, flag–score separation, and T1/T2 versus T3/T4 split check out, and the rejection of natural direct/indirect effects is well motivated by positivity failure, exposure-induced queue confounding, and shared-queue interference. That is not enough to carry the answer. Several claims that drive the causal conclusion and the pilot are wrong or internally inconsistent.

The sensitivity result is miscalculated. Flagged high-complexity cycle times are 16.4, 19.7, 24.1, 25.2, and 26.6 hours, so the median is 24.1, not about 21. R17 (high complexity, unflagged, reviewed, not escalated, 8.6 hours) is omitted while the text says high complexity is slow regardless of flag. The remaining within-path gap is several hours, so “complexity largely closes the gap” is not supported. The complexity benchmark is also circular: low versus high complexity is entangled with the flag and with who enters review.

The local-threshold design is already dead on these rows, and the answer does not say so. In the stated band 0.55–0.67, every unflagged case is high complexity and every flagged case is medium complexity. After stratifying on complexity there is no overlap anywhere: high flagged scores start at 0.82, high unflagged scores stop at 0.61; medium unflagged scores stop at 0.46. E3’s reference distribution given score and complexity is empty, not merely thin away from the threshold. The proposed balance check would reject the design immediately.

Other concrete faults: M2 does not review every high-complexity case (R17 is M1). “Unflagged waits are longest exactly where flagged density is high” is asserted, not shown, and is confounded with `arrival_load` and time of day. Triage service is treated as evidence for the priority lane and, separately, as a negative control that should be flag-insensitive. P3’s “−4 to −5 hours” cannot leave escalated flagged cases at about 22 hours. Section 5 says to randomize clusters, not cases; the pilot then randomizes cases inside shared queues. The audit covers only skipped discretion-arm reviews, so the “exceeds control by 2 percentage points” stopping rule has no parallel control measurement. The 0.64–0.80 pilot band is entirely medium complexity in these data. Reversal 1 is backwards: extra hidden severity among unflagged cases would not explain why flagged cases are slower. The PM4Py service/waiting helper is described as an overlap-safe union; the usual implementation sums interval lengths and can double-count. The main descriptive decomposition and the policy-bundle framing remain strong, but the identification and decision sections overclaim what these 20 cases can support.
