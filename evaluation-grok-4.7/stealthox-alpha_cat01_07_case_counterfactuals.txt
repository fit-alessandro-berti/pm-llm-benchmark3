The write-up is structurally complete and often sharp: precedence and four-eyes are preserved, K-337 is correctly treated as a decision-policy change, C1’s ability to reopen a suppressed loop is recognized, and earlier readiness is distinguished from earlier completion. Those strengths do not offset the quantitative and consistency failures below.

The headline ranking is not supported by the answer’s own scenarios. K-104 is credited with an expected saving of 10–20 h, yet the same row assigns a 15–25% chance of a 90–140 h outcome against a 58.5 h factual cycle. Probability-weighting that tail drives expected savings to about zero. K-219 is condemned by exactly this weighting, and is then described both as negative expected value and as “0 in expectation.” The final synthesis still calls K-104 the robust 10–20 h intervention. That double standard is the central flaw.

Several timings contradict the answer’s own constraints. The primary K-104 path has M1 deciding at Tue 22:15, while the constraint set and assumption audit say M1 does not sign outside business hours. K-219’s assessment “start ~12:00–14:30” plus a Tue 15:30 decision is impossible at the late end of that range, and a 12:00 start also ignores the stated 6 h A2 queue. K-411’s best case puts A2 on Tue 19:00–21:30 even though, with other cases held fixed, A2 is already on K-219 from 20:00–22:30. The Tue 08:00 D2 queue of 4 h is applied as if it began at 09:35.

The capacity claim that one C1 can complete 35–50 reviews of 3–6 h in a week is arithmetically impossible; weekly capacity is closer to roughly 8–15 reviews. The qualitative scarcity point survives, but the figure is off by about a factor of four. “Nearly free slack” and “every intervention is locally attractive” also conflict with the stated 18–20 h C1 backlog and the negative K-219 result.

The preliminary screen is specified as concurrent with the checks and then scheduled after both have finished. Handwriting clarification is equated to an 8–36 h external loop without showing why that scale applies, and that choice alone flips K-219’s sign. Pilot “80% power” claims have no variance basis.
