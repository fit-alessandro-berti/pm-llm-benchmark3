The business analysis is strong and internally consistent: raw alignment cost is kept separate from severity, silent `tau` moves are treated as fitting, and the zero-cost P1/P2 failures on CL-609 and CL-610 are identified correctly. Case narratives, uncertainty (a missing event is not proof the activity did not occur), pattern evidence, and the cost-versus-risk discussion are mostly precise.

It is not nearly flawless.

The required Python block is not executable against the long-standing PM4Py API. `pm4py.format_dataframe` takes `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`, not `activity` and `timestamp`. That `TypeError` fires before the log is built, so `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` never runs and requirements 3–7 of the code section fail together.

P3 is stretched onto CL-602. In that alignment, `Manual Approve` is synchronous and precedes `Pay Claim`. The failure is a missing Fraud Screen (P2), not payment before approval. P3 is the CL-604 fact pattern (bank acceptance at 10:03, approval recorded at 10:20). The same conflation appears in the CL-602 policy flag.

CL-606 states that linkage to the core case is weak. The supplied fact is a signed external report at 10:05. The “two of nine lack a stable identifier” statistic is population evidence, not established for this case.

The remediation portfolio labels CL-609 and CL-610 as preventive only. Both payments already happened; CL-610 in particular needs corrective fraud review of a completed auto-approval after a same-day bank change. That investigation is omitted from the corrective row, which covers only CL-602 and CL-604.

CL-612 is grouped with the P6 scope dispute. P6 names denied pre-screen leads and re-opened claims, not withdrawals. The withdrawal is a real model gap, but it is a different scope question.
