6.5

The case analysis is serious and mostly well traced, but the revised model misstates POWL semantics on points the task made central, and one M0 reading is wrong.

The briefs, burden of proof, and matrix are the strong part. Authority versus expiry is handled on both sides; PD-3 and FD-7 are not treated as permanent amendments; SUP-703 and SUP-708 stay severe; SUP-712 and SUP-715 are correctly pulled out of a pure control-flow reading; aggregates (312 / 289 / 14 / 9, 47 information requests, 2% to 29% notification loss, six early-August auto-approvals) are used rather than ignored. Classifications are generally defensible, and neither brief is allowed to win by default.

The model section does not do what it claims. In a `StrictPartialOrder`, every node executes once. Putting `Request_More_Info` on the only path makes it mandatory, so a clean case such as SUP-701 would fail the revised model. That is not a loop, nor an optional approximation; PM4Py `Operator.LOOP` plus a silent skip was available and unused. `checks` (sanctions and quality, unordered) is ordered before the XOR, so quality still precedes activation on the auto and emergency branches. Those branches therefore reject the very pilot and flood traces the text calls justified. The comment admits emergency deferral lives in an overlay, but it does not admit that auto-approval has the same defect, or that the POWL still forbids both. One shared `activate` object is reused in three XOR children, which is not a valid single-activity POWL. New labels (`Retrospective_Manual_Approval`, `Retrospective_Quality_Review`) also fail to replay the actual justified logs.

M0 itself is misread for SUP-709. Quality sits in `checks`, before the XOR, not only on the approval branch. A rejection trace with no quality review is a control-flow deviation if the case is in scope. CS-9’s “before activation” wording and the intake-scope dispute can still support `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT`, but the matrix’s “compliant if the reject branch needs no quality” is false. The defense never addresses concurrency. DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints are never assigned, despite being required. “Far above any cap” for SUP-703 invents a pilot spend cap PD-3 does not state. SUP-702’s judgment line reduces a real control-flow exception to a notification logging gap.
