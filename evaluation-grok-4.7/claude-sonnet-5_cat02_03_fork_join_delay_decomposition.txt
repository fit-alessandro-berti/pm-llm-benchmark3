The case table, critical-branch labels, aggregate means, and counterfactual savings are numerically right and internally consistent, and the max-based fork-join identity is the right model. The write-up is not nearly flawless: the required double-counting argument overclaims, a few influence claims are misstated, and supplied resource timestamps are left unused where they would change the queue interpretation.

The telescoping identity is correct: `upstream + fork_join_stage + downstream_queue + final_service` reconstructs flow time, and for every branch `queue + service + sync = fork_join_stage`, so adding branch totals counts one wall-clock interval three times. The parenthetical that summing the three synchronization waits alone also yields three times the fork-join stage is false. On F01 those waits are 0+5+22=27 against a stage of 50, not 150. Sync waits overlap the critical branch; they are not a second copy of the stage.

Calling A “already-fast” in CF1 reverses the evidence. A has the longest service (50, zero variance) and is rarely critical because B’s queue plus service usually exceeds that floor. The low payoff is non-criticality, which the rest of the ranking states correctly. “One outlier” for C’s queue omits F08’s 25-minute queue, the other case where C is critical. A and C are tied at 2/12, not near-tied. F07’s 25-minute lead over the second branch is shared with F12.

CF1 (15 minutes total, mean 1.25; only F01 and F11) and CF2 (92.5 minutes, mean about 7.7; zero on F01, F04, F08, F11) match a recomputation, including F05’s cut being capped at 10 rather than 12.5 and F10’s shift to A. The prose flags the F06 tie and the F10 shift, but not the F02 tie, the F11 tie under CF1, or that F05’s raw queue cut does not fully reach flow time. CF3’s 14.7 and 7.3 minute means match the downstream column. Holding `final_start − join_ready` fixed is labeled, but “masking” is treated mainly as a KPI confound. The operational version—an earlier join lengthening a real final-station wait—is not quantified.
