7.5

The answer is complete, well structured, and right in its main judgment: conditional go, partial-order transfer only, no universal simultaneous Document Check, and no use of Site A’s −9 h median as an identified effect. The comparison, DAG, modifiers, segmented pilot logic, and AND-join before Assessment are substantively strong. It is not nearly flawless. The numeric and design machinery has several real errors.

The transport-confidence column is not one scale. Outcome rows use low scores to mean “this delta should not be copied” (median cycle 30, p90 20). Confounder and co-intervention rows use high scores for the opposite conclusion (two added reviewers 95, marketing mix 95, no negative control 95, API upgrade 90, universal rollout 95). High-complexity share is scored 85 even though the text says Site A’s 48%→40% shift makes the after period non-comparable. A reader cannot rank those numbers.

Scenario arithmetic is looser than the prose. S1 pairs “overlap approaches 71%” with “only ~38% immediately dual-executable,” and a 4–7 h median change is not scoped to subgroup versus portfolio. If only the dual-executable share can earn that gain, a portfolio median cannot move 4–7 h. S2’s “portfolio 1 to 2 h” treats a median as if it scaled with case share. The 4–6 h subgroup target is 40–70% of Site A’s confounded −9 h, not a critical-path bound. Pure synchronization is on the order of the shorter check (identity service ≈ 3.4 h) plus only the queueing component that survives after removing A’s extra FTE, API change, huddles, and mix shift. Signs flip without a definition: unsigned positives mean improvement in S1–S4, “+” means worsening in S5 and in S4’s p90, and S6’s “2 to +3 h” is unreadable. S3 is directionally right but still credits 1–3 h for originals cases even though Site B already allows Identity Check to run during receipt, and an 18 h median receipt delay will often leave a 3.4 h identity check off the critical path.

The pilot cannot do what it claims. About 131 completions a week and a ~23% first-wave share yield roughly 90–120 cases per arm per complexity band in 12–16 weeks, short of 250 per arm per stratum, before T2/T3 split the sample again. The appeal stop rule is not observable on a weekly review: 90-day appeals for late enrollees fall outside the enrollment window. A +2 pp rework stop and a +1 pp appeal stop sit inside ordinary sampling noise at those sizes. Case-level randomization is chosen for lock-safety, while the larger cycle-time threat is shared-pool interference; the cap helps but does not fix that mismatch. Assumption 8 then asserts that rework and appeals are sensitive enough to detect material harm.

The section 6 partial order does not close the legal loop. After Request Supplement → repeated Document Check → repeated Assessment there is no return to Legal Review, so a mandatory-legal case can finish the repeat without a legal Approve, Reject, or further supplement. The code’s loop is closer to sound but does not match that specification. `SilentTransition(label=None)` is also a likely constructor error and would stop the script before the Petri-net checks.

WIP caps (16/28/12/8) are presented as design parameters with no arrival, effort, or utilization basis. The DFG is used for the supplement count and the 62% figure, but not to test how often identity already precedes or overlaps receipt. Those gaps do not overturn the conditional-go recommendation; they do keep the quantitative and pilot sections from being reliable as specified.
