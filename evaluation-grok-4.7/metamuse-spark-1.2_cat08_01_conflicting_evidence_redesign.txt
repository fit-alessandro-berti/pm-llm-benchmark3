The diagnosis is specific and largely faithful to the artifacts: the auto-to-fast-to-dispatch path is correctly identified as dominant, the 14.1% versus 3.3% complaint split and the 31% versus 9% cost reversal are used properly, and fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, the evidence loop, segment medians, and the 82% review threshold all appear as operating constraints rather than decoration. The three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms — compliance routing, evidence and part-selection control, and a repair-default fulfillment policy — each with an explicit sacrifice, and the pilot sequence is segment-stratified, staged, and alert to downstream shifting.

The quantitative reasoning is not reliable enough for a higher score. The dominant path is chained as fast approval 2550 to dispatch 2300 to close 2320, which cannot be one continuing flow: dispatch also receives escalation cases, 260 dispatches complain, and 2320 closes exceed the 2300 fast-path dispatches. Edge counts are repeatedly called cases, including a complaint total of 510 set beside the 9.2% case rate (about 460) with no reconciliation. Summed transition means are labeled a theoretical median. High-value devices are treated as equivalent to claims above EUR 400, and “dispatch without risk review” is mapped directly onto R1 even though R1 is a pre-approval rule satisfied by either manual or fraud review.

Those slips change the trade-offs. Redesign A still permits fast fulfillment after a gate, but its sacrifice and table price that change at the full 96–120 hour reviewed/repair median. Redesign C then adds repair, quality-check, and rework hours on top of that same median, although those times are already inside it and rework hits 12.5% of high-value repairs rather than the typical case. Redesign B samples the gate at 20% for low-risk work, then moves the premium low-risk median by the full 6–18 hours. The claim that EUR 410 dwarfs repair labor is unsupported: repair parts and hours are absent. The fast path is also described as avoiding bottlenecks only by violating rules, which erases the legitimate premium low-risk exception.

Redesign C contradicts itself. Its flow defaults high-value claims into fraud triage, while its bottleneck section says fraud load falls because fraud is no longer a dispatch gate. The failure threshold “explodes to p90 60h” matches the current manual-to-quote p90 rather than a worsening. The experiment plan is the strongest section, but two decision rules are mis-specified: a 15–20 percentage-point absolute drop in repeat evidence exceeds the portal’s stated 30–45% relative effect, and “narrow the 31% to 9% gap” misreads a before-versus-after-rework comparison as a pilot target. The conditional time from dispatch to complaint is also treated as a duration the gate should shrink, rather than a lag observed only on cases that already failed. Selection into the repair path is noted only lightly, so the 5–6% complaint rates are too readily treated as transferable.

The comparison table retains the required columns and correctly refuses global dominance, but many effect cells lose their direction markers and read as unsigned fragments. The strategic structure holds; the arithmetic and several internal mechanisms do not.
