The answer is structurally complete and normatively careful: open-world versus closed-world scope is applied correctly, the banned non-violations stay non-violations, N1’s eventually-follows versus directly-follows trap and N3’s directly-follows under-count are handled well, and the remediation plan does not inflate rare but permitted behavior. It is not nearly flawless. Several repeated inferences are stronger than the summary supports.

The clearest error is treating Add Item weights 1450 (Item) versus 1448 (Order) as exactly two Add Item events with no Order. That claim is stated as fact in the findings table, the OTG discussion, the mandatory list, and the uncertainty statement. Those weights are aggregate pair or event counts. An Add Item event can carry multiple items, and the OTG edge (Order, object_interaction, Item) is also 1450, which fits extra item links on events that still have an order. The problem states the two-event reading explicitly for Dispatch–Delivery and the one-event reading for Record Payment–Invoice; it does not do so for Add Item. Citing T1 alongside E2 as the violated norm blurs the cross-abstraction point made later, because the type-level T1 weight is present.

The OTG explanation then mis-assigns relationship types. A Package–Delivery object_interaction weight of 200 cannot be explained by seven object_codeath edges. Those are a different qualifier. The valid observation—that a healthy type-level edge can hide a per-event shortfall—is undercut by that account of the surplus.

N4 is internally uneven. The class column allows an abstraction mismatch, but the business reading is “dispatched unpacked, or never dispatched.” The denominator is 200 externally shipped packages, so “never dispatched” fights the given scope. The directly-follows gap is equally compatible with an intervening event or a missing Pack record. Section 4’s “not truly external” escape also sits loosely against a denominator already labeled externally shipped; intended shipment versus shipped status is the sharper scope question, and it is left implicit.

E8 drifts across sections. The norm withholds the unapplied-cash exception unless the event is explicitly typed, and none are. Section 3 says the case is strictly in scope. The table calls it insufficient evidence, and Section 4 says it conforms if finance certifies it. Certification without the required label does not meet E8. Section 6 later repairs this; the earlier buckets do not.

Smaller defects accumulate. Gap 5 is given the status “documented meta-policy,” outside the required mandatory / forbidden / typical / unspecified choice. E10 is ranked high, then denied operational containment; the dual severity phrase does not make that ranking stable. “Legitimate named exception” and “unregulated / typical, conforming” sit outside the seven-class scheme defined in Section 1. E3, E4, E5, and E7 are called fully satisfied without event denominators that would prove every event of those types carries the mandatory object. The code sketch calls the required discovery and conformance functions, keeps raw diagnostics, and correctly parks status metadata outside the bare graphs, but the classifiers `_iter_*_diffs` are undefined and the semantic layer never emits abstraction mismatch, data-quality error, or insufficient evidence as classes. Create→Validate 98/100 and Create→Manual Compliance Review on 3 orders are not checked against a 100-order directly-follows partition.

The highest-severity judgments on N3 and on leaving write-offs, reopen, codeath, customer links, and risk review unpunished are sound. The grade is held down by false precision on counts, one relationship-type conflation, and inconsistent classification of the same mandatory gaps.
