4.5

The write-up has the required seven-part shape and names the right objectives, but the concrete policy is not internally consistent and several assignments contradict the scenario data and the stated rules. That is a failure of the main deliverable, not a presentation nit.

The scoring procedure does not do what it claims. SLA urgency is added as the same constant to every candidate, so it never changes who is chosen, and nothing orders cases by urgency before scarce people are consumed. “High” priority is enforced only for the label “urgent.” The quality-check rule is implemented as “assign nobody,” rather than “do not batch.” Security supervision is coded as “the assignee is already on the Risk team,” which is not supervision by R7 or R8. The 20% training floor is a +4 bonus that specialists’ skill points overwhelm, so the quota is not enforced. Case complexity never enters the continuity score. The routine returns one person, yet the queue table emits supervised pairs. High-overtime exclusion depends on an undefined `no_alternative` check over a list that is still being built.

The queue table does not follow that procedure, and several rows are factually wrong. R6 has 5 open cases, not 14. C008’s owner is R7, not R8. R3’s negotiation skill is 1, not 2, so C011’s “R5 supervised by R3” pairing reverses the skill relationship and drops R6, the skill-3 owner, on a low-handover urgent VIP case. C015 gives Security Review to R5, whose security skill is 0. Rule 6 allows skill 1–2 only under R7 or R8, and the answer’s own hard constraints ban skill 0; Eva and Jonas are both skill 0, and that conflict is never stated. C003, C010, C012, and C014 all add work to high-overtime resources when the SLA is not urgent and safer skill-qualified people exist. C012 is the clearest miss: a 48-hour, low-priority, trainee-suitable case is taken from lightly loaded R10 and given to high-overtime R9, whose training priority is negotiation. C006 is justified as “batchable” and then not batched with C012. Counting only valid normal/low training moves leaves the plan short of the 20% training rule.

Section 4 then credits this plan with controlled overtime, low error risk, and a 20% training share. First-available routing is marked poor on workload fairness even though it assigns to the lowest open-case count. Specialist-only routing is marked low SLA risk despite the stated Security and Approval queues.

The worker note is readable, but it says R7 or R8 can supervise approval. R8’s approval skill is 0 and R7’s is 1, so they cannot finalize or supervise final approval. It also describes a strict priority order the additive score does not implement.

The audit list covers every requested indicator, then operationalizes several incorrectly: handover frequency is “reassigned 1 time,” skill level is compared with the skill-name field, `discover_organizational_roles` is treated as a Gini workload measure, and `filter_case_performance` is treated as an SLA-deadline test. The sketch never uses working-together networks, role discovery, `get_service_time`, `get_variants`, or batch discovery in code; event counts are labeled as case counts; and there is no real procedure for learning weights or handover effects from the log.
