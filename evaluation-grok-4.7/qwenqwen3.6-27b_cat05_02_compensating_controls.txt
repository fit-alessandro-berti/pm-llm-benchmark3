6.2

The case dispositions are mostly well differentiated, but the answer is not close to flawless: several classifications rest on inconsistent control-type semantics, Section 5 violates an explicit instruction, and the required code would mis-state the evidence it claims to produce.

The required seven headings and the classification vocabulary are present, and the analysis does not merely count activities or map same-user to failure. AP-202 and AP-208 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`, AP-203 and AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET`, AP-205 as `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`, and AP-204 as failed despite distinct users are defensible readings of objective, timing, independence, and outcome. AP-201 as only partially met, given convenience use and no emergency token, is also reasonable.

Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors. AP-205 is marked a nominal four-eyes violation even though FEP-1 applies only to completed payments and `Release_Payment` never occurred. Pre-release manager approval on AP-203 and AP-206 is placed only under detective controls, which contradicts the answer’s own Section 4 statement that pre-release MG-4 is preventive. AP-201 lists a missing emergency-token justification when the record affirmatively says no token existed. Corrective capacity is scored from release time and inconsistently: AP-201 at about 23 hours after settlement is “Low,” while AP-207 at about 20 hours is “Medium.”

Section 2 names the right dimensions but never gives an aggregation rule for when a package, rather than a single dimension, is fully equivalent, partial, mitigating, or ineffective. It also calls both pre-release and pre-settlement timing “fully equivalent,” then treats those timings differently in the cases. Section 4’s hybrid reading of post-release, pre-settlement manager action is clear and usable.

Section 5 is materially weaker. It invents probabilities (~85%, ~70%, ~20% to ~60%) without labeling them illustrative, despite an explicit instruction to use qualitative ranges. For AP-204 it treats a late audit as breaking the loss chain and says an independent second releaser would be unlikely to break collusion, which misses the counterfactual of replacing the paired releaser. For AP-208 it leaves €900k “critically exposed” after stating the payment was legitimate, conflating closed ex ante exposure with remaining loss.

Section 6 covers every requested monitoring topic and separates alert from proof, but the rule to alert on every post-release manager action would flag the very pre-settlement pattern the answer elsewhere accepts. Thresholds such as 5% and 2% are unexplained.

The code reconstructs the log, calls the requested formatter, and labels its output as evidence rather than a verdict. It still has functional defects. Standard `filter_four_eyes_principle` returns a filtered log and defaults to `keep_violations=False`, so the unmodified call returns compliant cases, not violations; the result is then consumed as if it were a list of case IDs, and only `AttributeError` is caught. `emergency_eligible` ignores token-before-prepare, so AP-206 is treated as eligible. AP-208’s audit interval is coded as 96 hours rather than about 115. Missing manager action falls through to `post_settlement`, and the repeated-pair flag is computed only on nominal violators, so the u12/u13 pattern cannot appear.
