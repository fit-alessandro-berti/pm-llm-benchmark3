4.5

The memo covers every required section and cites many real path shares, policy rates, and batch or mode facts, but its decision logic is unreliable in the places a spare-parts redesign has to be exact.

**Boundary and indicators.** The boundary stops at delivery confirmation and names energy, transport, scrap, and packaging, yet obsolescence of unshipped batch output and damage-driven reshipments stay outside it even though both are in the evidence. Returns are excluded and then used as a quality indicator. The indicator table replaces measures with unsupported targets. A single 10% late-delivery target would allow Critical-downtime lateness to rise from 4% and Standard-stock Service-contract lateness to rise from 5%. Scrap and package mass are added together. “Carrier disruption events” are not an attribute in the log. Obsolescence, slack versus `promised_lead_time_hours`, expedites, cancellations, and SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance are missing as indicators.

**Intervention errors that change the recommendation.**
- S1 says moving Standard-stock Service-contract orders from air to ground reduces lateness. Stock–air is 3% late at 17 h; stock–ground is 8% late at 34 h. The cited 5% is the segment rate, not ground. The +12–24 h cycle-time figure is the consolidation window, not the air–ground gap.
- S4 treats the 18 h Standard-stock Service-contract median cycle time as median slack and claims no service loss. The 5% stock–air path is also treated as if it were entirely that segment.
- S2 caps batch wait at 12 h for contract work and 36 h for regular work while still claiming the energy gain of batches of 12–20, which the log associates with about 38 h of wait. The cap and the saving work against each other. The 5–10 point lateness cut does not follow from the 1.7× heat-treatment risk.
- S3 equates the 5% path that already contains rework with all CNC_B volume, then treats an 18% to 9% rate gap as a 50% reduction and a 10–20% longer cycle. Leaving that rework loop should shorten the 160 h path, unless CNC_A congestion is shown.
- S6 calls reinforced packaging “13% of MTO.” Inspection sends about 3,470 cases to reinforced packaging, roughly 29% of orders. It also flags reinforced packaging with parcel ground as incompatible, which cuts against Q1 for heavy made-to-order parts.
- S7 describes inspection queues as feeding CNC_B. Flow runs the other way: production, then inspection, then 470 rework releases back to CNC.
- S8 invents a shared-courier factor of about 35 kgCO2e and a 30–40% saving. No such factor is in the log. The grounded mechanism is empty dedicated-van returns.
- S9 assigns precautionary orders a 4 h need and then puts them on ground. The critical stock–ground exception already has a 23 h median and 9% late rate. A 50% van-emission cut assumes an unobserved true-versus-precautionary split.
- S10 reads a longer negotiated promise as a higher late rate. Lateness is measured against the promise. The 40–60% transport cut is not derived, and the regular made-to-order energy and batching benefit is left out.
- S11 applies a full-order rule without separating service-contract and downtime lines, where a partial ship can be the resilient action. The effect table then uses question marks.

**Effects table.** Several cells are blank where the path table already gives a direction, notably S4 cycle time and lateness. Rework reductions are written into the material column while the rework column is empty. S12 places policy compliance under transport and lateness. Precise percentages are used where the requested output was direction or a broad range tied to given evidence.

**Rebounds.** Batching correctly uses the 6% of batch output not shipped within 14 days, then obsolescence repeats that point. Packaging mitigation shifts to recycled or reusable materials, detached from route, geometry, Q1, and the 40% damage-return effect. Customer behavior is described as perception of longer lead times, not as reclassifying orders into Critical-downtime or expediting after a green promise. Missing displacements include wait caps raising kWh per item, suppressed air creating later expedites, CNC_A congestion pushing overflow back to CNC_B, and fewer partials becoming cancellations that re-enter as emergency van orders.

**Package and governance.** S1 and S4 are the same lever counted twice. Twice-daily consolidation is stacked onto Service-contract stock orders whose median cycle is already 18 h, without an explicit SP1 test that on-time probability stays above 85%. The rationale cites shared emergency couriers and regular customers accepting longer promises, but S8 and S10 are outside the package. R1 is asserted rather than reserved as a disruption route. Obsolescence is not controlled. Validation then sets “<150 packaging-rework events per month” and “>3 violations per week” on a 12,000-order log with no time base, and repeats the uniform 10% lateness target.

The skeleton is usable: enforce SP1 where slack is real, prefer CNC_A when due-date slack exists, check packaging before shipment, justify vans, and keep true downtime fast. The memo as written mis-signs service effects, fabricates baselines, and bundles waits in a way that can miss contract promises.
