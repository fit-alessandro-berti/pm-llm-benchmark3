The answer has the right compliance skeleton: A and B are ranked last and correctly treated as hard control breaches that no forecast can repair, G is an unenabled exception, F is conditional, and the preferred family is an independent second review with screening and an S2/S4 choice. Asymmetric regulatory harm is not traded away for the €20k–€35k deadline value, and the six required sections, labels, and A–G coverage are present.

The ranking is not reliable, because the clock arithmetic that justifies putting D first is wrong. S4 “available in 35–65 minutes” means the review starts then. Action C’s review is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute. S4 at 35 minutes finishes around 75–125 minutes; S4 at 65 minutes finishes around 105–155 minutes. Against a 90-minute deadline, the late case always misses, and the early case misses on most of the review range. Section 5’s claim that 65 minutes plus 5–15 minutes of decision time is still about 70–80 minutes, and the claim of an “ample buffer” if S4 is free at 35 minutes, both drop the review itself. “Most paths” under D therefore do not preserve the deadline benefit, and the low P-901 spillover is not the base case: a deadline-protecting fallback to S2 would trigger for most of the 35–65 minute window, so D often collapses into screening plus C.

That error also warps the sensitivity analysis. At 65 minutes, C does not merely “approach” D; a pure S4 plan cannot both finish a real second review and execute inside the window. The primary recommendation’s benefit cell (“largely preserves” €20k–€35k) is overstated, and medium-high confidence in D over C is not earned.

Smaller problems accumulate. A 0.46 fuzzy score does not make a clearing result from the alternate vendor “likely.” After a genuine S2/S4 approval, residual sanctions false-clear risk is not the same thing as unlawful execution. If rescreening actually resolves the match and drops the score below 70, rule 6 no longer requires a second officer, and the already completed analyst review can permit a faster conformant approval; the write-up notices the trigger can fall away and then voluntarily reimposes the delay. The 0.55–0.75 approval range is observational and is still used as a branch probability under intervention. The 12-minute-old score is barely used. Rule 11 (do not disclose a suspicious-activity report to the customer) is never mapped, including onto the customer evidence request. P-901’s loss is treated as comparable to €20k–€35k without a stated loss function.

The conformance direction is sound. The operational comparison that decides rank 1 is not.
