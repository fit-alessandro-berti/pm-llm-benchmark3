The answer is strong, process-aware, and unusually careful about completion-only logging, but it is not nearly flawless. Several judgment and coherence problems keep it well below a top score.

What holds up: C6-A and C6-B are correctly kept in S3 while split by obligations. High severity plus injury blocks fast close and forces technical and regulatory work; the duplicate, low-severity, single-device case does not inherit that fork from R-T4. Rule 5 is read properly: two devices on one complaint are not two complaints, different lots are not automatically a supplier case, and six same-lot complaints are only a supplier trigger if they are apparently related. Absent regulatory and evidence events are treated as unconfirmed, not as proof the tasks never occurred. Migration is applied mainly to C6-D and is not confused with the 8–15% event-level delay rate. Queue facts, the 16:00 notification batch, and DFG limits (no outgoing denominators, cycle double-counting, bidirectional investigation edges, medians that are not residual service times) are used rather than copied. Interval midpoints are ordered, internally arithmetically consistent, and explicitly not calibrated probabilities.

What should cost substantial credit:

- `process_enabled: false` on C6-A’s technical and regulatory completions fights the required distinction. Those activities are allowed concurrently and may already be in progress; the flag instead means “not enabled in the author’s primary reconstruction.” The caveat admits this, but the boolean itself is still misleading. Ranking technical completion above regulatory assessment is also weakly motivated: injury, R-T4, and reportability-based priority cut the other way, and the intervals overlap heavily.
- C6-C overstates the country rule. “Can be reportable” becomes “a required regulatory assessment” and then a 0.55–0.80 next-event interval, despite a 12-case non-FIFO queue and no assignment evidence. Rank 3, another evidence request, is a transition the control flow does not define; a range whose floor is 0.00 is not a plausible top-three outcome.
- C6-D’s named alternative is disposition-ready, while its second prediction is a backfilled Evidence Received. Those are different latent worlds. With a migration flag, a missing evidence cycle is at least as serious an alternative as hidden disposition readiness. The 0.45 lower bound on regulatory completion is optimistic: technical completion is only 55 minutes old, regulatory service is 2–14 hours after assignment, and more than one event type could be delayed.
- C6-E packs two alternatives into one field (supplier analysis already open, and the second review already finished 40 minutes after receipt). The six-complaint lot is flagged as a possible reportability signal, then dropped from the prediction set in favor of a further evidence request that itself requires an unobserved review.
- Enablement, horizon, and “lower bounds are not simultaneous” are explained globally and densely, but individual case fields remain easy to misread. Negative statements are also filed under completed obligations.

The reasoning is clearly expert-level. The deductions are for inconsistent state alternatives, one invented transition, an over-hardened reportability obligation, and enablement flags that do not match the in-progress observation model the scenario requires.
