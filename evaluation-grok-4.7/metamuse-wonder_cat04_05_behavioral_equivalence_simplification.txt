The verdicts are right: A and B are not visibly trace-equivalent, not P3-equivalent, and not P1-equivalent, while their P2 images coincide. A1, A2, B1, B2, and B3 are genuine complete witnesses inside the P3 bounds, the loop reading is correct (`#C = #A` in A, exactly one `C` in B, approval after both `A` and `C` only in A), and the node, leaf, silent-leaf, and edge-depth counts are correct. The P2 simplification is smaller (7 nodes, depth 2, no `tau`) and its abstract image matches both originals.

The P1 definition is not precise. “At least `k` checks” is not equivalent to “one check in each analysis segment”; two checks in one segment and none in another meet the count and fail the rule. As written, P1-equivalence also collapses to ordinary visible-trace equivalence, because it keeps every visible label and hides only `tau`, so the compliance clause adds no separate observation.

The structural table’s phrase “after hiding interleaving” muddies a comparison that is supposed to retain interleavings. The claim that no simpler model can preserve P1 exactly is asserted, not shown. Section 6’s snippet is not runnable (`tree_a`, `net_a`, `im_a`, `fm_a` are undefined), and `simplicity_petri_net` is never actually compared. The two negative traces are rejected by A, B, and the simplification alike, so they do not test any behavior the simplification drops, such as `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` or a two-check trace from A.
