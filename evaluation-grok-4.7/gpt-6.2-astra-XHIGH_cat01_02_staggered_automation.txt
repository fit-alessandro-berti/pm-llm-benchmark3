8.4

The answer is rigorous, arithmetically reliable, and well matched to the decision, but it is not nearly flawless. Several presentation and identification choices still overstate precision or leave the main quantitative story incomplete.

What holds up: availability, completed fast-path use, targeted rollout, and co-interventions are kept apart, and section 1 makes no causal claim. The estimands separate an availability contrast from actual fast-path receipt, median from p90, rework from escalation, respondent CSAT from all-customer satisfaction, and a short-run branch effect from a network expansion effect. Cohort-specific calendar matching is the right primary design; the Goodman-Bacon problem with pooled two-way fixed effects is stated correctly, and post-treatment path shares are kept out of the adjustment set. Checked pre/post contrasts, control changes, donor ranges, one-month contrasts, pre-trend gaps, and the median intensity slope of about −1.30 hours per +10 percentage points all reproduce. Absolute April worsening versus a smaller rise than the controls is not collapsed into one “cycle-time effect.” The claim ladder and the embedded within-branch trial are appropriately modest.

Weaknesses that keep this below a near-perfect score:

- Section 7’s rework and escalation intervals are only single-donor spreads under zero extra drift, about −2.2 to −2.0 and +0.85 to +1.00 percentage points. That is not the uncertainty standard used for cycle time. The same section notes that a 2.05 or 0.90 point differential would wipe out the April contrasts, so those tight ranges read like identified effects.
- The written intensity results are given to two or three decimals with no standard errors, despite five clusters. The small-cluster warning appears only in the code.
- The written analysis stops at a two-month window. It never reports the later calendar-adjusted path, including June’s tail blowout and the partial August recovery, even though the code builds that series. The relative-time dummies are constructed and then unused.
- Section 1 illustrates outcomes with Alpha’s flattering March-to-August median drop. The early-window fact that both April adopters’ medians and p90s rose is postponed.
- Differential routing and the uneven May complexity shock are named, then bounded only by a generic ±5/±10 hour scenario, not by a mix- or load-based bias calculation the panel could support.
- Confidence of 60 and 65 for rework and escalation is high relative to confounding of the same size as the contrasts. The rollout thresholds (2 hours, +1 percentage point, −0.10 CSAT) are arbitrary, and treating Delta and Epsilon together spends the last never-treated branches at once.
