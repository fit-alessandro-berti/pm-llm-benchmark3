7.4

The selected conservative repair is substantively sound: `ev08→PK-8`, `ev10→DEL-Y`, and `ev14→INV-44`, with matching directional O2O edges, while `ev11` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B` stay open. Cardinality defaults, identifier matches, and anti-circularity are handled carefully, and the directly-follows, flattening, and frequency claims for Bundle A are mostly right. The answer is still not reliable as a finished reasoning artifact.

The relation table contradicts the repair it claims to record. The header says those statuses are the decisions of the selected bundle, yet P10 and P11 are `provisional` while Bundle A and the JSON leave `ev12→ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B` unmaterialized and call them unresolved. `provisional` and `unresolved` are distinct required statuses, so a reader cannot tell whether consolidated billing is in the repair.

Evidence is mis-aimed on the payment. `bank_ref=B-772` only reconfirms the already observed `ev14→PAY-2` link. It is cited in the evidence model, in P12, and in the JSON justification as support for `ev14→INV-44`. The real case for that edge is the missing Invoice endpoint, the sole invoice, and the normal payment cardinality. The written justification instead leans on a non-identifying timestamp, a weak sum, and an irrelevant bank-reference match, while still marking the edge `accept` at 0.87 and the derivative `settles` edge `accept` at 0.84.

Bundle B is overstated as internally coherent. Under the shared packing repair, ORD-A is split: IT-A1 travels PK-7/DEL-X and IT-A2 travels PK-8/DEL-Y, while ORD-B’s only item is IT-B1 on PK-8/DEL-Y. Attaching both deliveries and ORD-B to one `ev11` confirmation therefore confirms ORD-B against DEL-X, which carries nothing of ORD-B. That asymmetry is never used to rank “ORD-A only,” “both deliveries,” and “ORD-B too.” Bundle C then makes the weaker move of assigning `ev11` uniquely to DEL-X after rewriting `N-421?` as an error, which largely presupposes the delivery choice the wildcard does not settle.

The OTG paragraph overreaches. Bundle A repeats type pairs that already occur on ev06/ev07, ev09, and ev13, so an unweighted type graph does not gain edges; weights and ET–OT counts do. “Delivery–Delivery” is an object-level co-occurrence, and only if both deliveries are placed on `ev11`. The new type-level edge in B is Order–Delivery. Attributing the default OTG to derived lifecycle graphs is stronger than the usual event co-occurrence construction, and the citations do not separate those mechanisms.

Smaller calibration problems remain. Equal 0.54 scores for `ev11→DEL-X` and `ev11→DEL-Y` do not say whether they are marginal link strengths or mutually exclusive alternatives, despite the warning not to add them. P09 uses the repaired mixed package as support without labeling that dependence. Similar gross weights are unused as even weak counterevidence to putting two items in PK-8. The code is careful and meets the required calls, but it never checks that the version-dependent constructor actually retained `o2o`, and only that constructor is compatibility-gated.
