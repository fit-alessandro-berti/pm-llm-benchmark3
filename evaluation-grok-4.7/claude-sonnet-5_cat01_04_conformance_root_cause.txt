The write-up is complete, well structured, and right in its broad conclusion: the June fitness collapse is mostly label drift, `Queue Routed`, and ingestion-time reordering; the August sign-off rule makes the control-flow tree stale; stable approval and appeal rates neither prove a data artifact nor clear a bypass; and an optional sign-off branch can buy fitness by overgeneralizing. Reliability notes, the EUR 50,000 guard, and the six governance rules are specific. That is not enough to carry the ranking, the causal graph, or the tests.

The ranking answers the wrong estimand. It says the scores explain the measured collapse, then puts obsolete model at 85 and timestamp/order defect at 62. Supervisor sign-off begins 2026-08-01. It cannot move April–May 93% to June–July 32%. On the June–July staged repair, label remap and dropping `Queue Routed` do almost all of the work (32% to 81%); source-sequence ordering is the next in-period gain (81% to 89%). Model obsolescence explains only part of why August–September, after the same cleaning, is still 66% rather than ~89%. Treating “real behavioral process change” as 38 and supporting it only with Manual Override repeats the split: the 2026-08-01 rule is a real process change and a stale model at once. Section 7 then says it must not yet be claimed that the process changed. The mandated sign-off already changed it. What is unproved is informal drift and reviewer bypass.

The same collapse is mis-attributed again in the adjacency list and the 180–280-word explanation. `policy_change -> normative_model` is a non-event: the tree was not updated. `ETL_change -> missing_resource_field` is unsupported; the missing-resource jump fits `Queue Routed` and the new logging better than the Decision/Notify timestamp switch. “All four” mechanisms are said to produce the fitness drop, including a policy that postdates it.

The source-sequence falsifier is reversed. For the 70 Notify-before-Decision traces, the log order is the reversed order. Screenshots that match that recorded order mean the source system agrees with Notify-before-Decision, which supports a real reorder and weakens the ETL story. The 11 screenshots that show Decision first are what support the defect. The cell states the opposite and places a supposed confirmation in the falsify column. The label-change “contradicting evidence” cell is not a contradiction. The business-outcome row puts weakening language in the supports column.

Two quantitative checks are missed. After a label-only remap, `Queue Routed` is still in 560 of 650 June–July cases, so perfectly fitting traces cannot be 58% if that column means perfect fits; only the 90 traces with no extra or reordered activity qualify (~14%). The published variant lists also exhaust the case counts, and every April–May variant is in the tree language, which sits badly beside 93% fitness. Those figures needed to be flagged as metric or census conflicts. The 30 June–July traces that still say `Classify`, and the fact that Manual Override is already present before the 2026-08-12 logging patch, are underused.

The diagnostic sequence isolates the right factors in the right order, but Phase 3 names `filter_event_attribute_values` for a case attribute. The code uses the required constructors and compares fitness with precision, and the EUR 50,000 caveat is accurate. It never emits alignment diagnostics, and it only runs the combined remap-plus-deletion, so the staged contrast the argument depends on is not reproduced.
