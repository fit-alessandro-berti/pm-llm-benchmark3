The diagnosis is right in outline and most of the arithmetic checks: March is a semantic/provenance step, May is a real rework shift, queueing dominates the cycle-time rise, July does not show an aggregate priority step, October changes the DFG’s composition, and December is confounded. Structure, restraint, and the monitoring design are strong. The score stays well below the top because several precise claims are false or overstated.

The raw-to-mapped gap is not 0.35–0.40 in every month from March. It is 0.35–0.40 only through July; August–December are 0.32–0.34. March mapped fitness versus the stated January–February baseline of 0.925 is −0.025, so “residual 0.01–0.02” and “within 0.02” are too tight. `Eligibility->CollectDocs` falls 2–3 per 100, not 4–6, and 9–11% of cases not reaching `Assess` includes a baseline gap of about 5 per 100. “Only the rework edges move” contradicts the same answer’s May/July main-path L1 of 13 and 20. Those two rework edges move in lockstep, so a 58–60% rework share double-counts one loop. The median decomposition assigns rework a direct share of the median while also arguing that the median case is non-rework; the stated ranges do not partition the deterioration.

Request Info is inside the normative model, so a mapped-fitness dip should not be attributed to that loop. The decision-coverage drop and the post-May Assess inflow imbalance are the better fitness clues, and they are underused. `noise_threshold=0.2` on an inductive miner can filter the baseline loop of about 12%. “Nine weeks each side” does not match May–14 June versus 15 June–August, and nothing shows that December is a short month. Benjamini–Hochberg is named without a way to get p-values, and a closed-case primary population sits awkwardly beside queue monitors that need open cases.
