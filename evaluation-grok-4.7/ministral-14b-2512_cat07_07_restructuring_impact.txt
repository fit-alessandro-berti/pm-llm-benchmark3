The write-up covers every required section and does not treat the change as automatically good or bad, but it is not a reliable forecast. Several core claims contradict the supplied numbers, and the PM4Py sketch would not measure what it claims.

**Mechanism errors.** Outbound technical handovers move from 36 to 41 per 100 cases, and the return leg from 31 to 36, so that path is replaced and slightly busier, not reduced from 36 to 0. Adding the other rows, counted handovers rise (about 343 to 359 per 100), so “nominal handover reduction” is not established. Cross-team wait is said to worsen “from 8.9h to 6.0h,” which is arithmetically backwards, and the map’s “36h” figure is not in the data. Utilization is also inverted: management’s +15% and pooling of 68/71/82% resources cannot become “local specialists drop to ~50%,” and Legal/Risk at 91–93% cannot absorb 9→12 referrals plus difficult quality-check work with only a small wait increase. East’s benefit is attached to standardized approval rather than to pooling an overloaded E_Tech. Keeping intake local is drawn as “local expertise retention,” which conflicts with removing N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech.

**Scenarios.** The three bands restate management waits with round offsets and false precision (CSAT 4.2/4.0/3.5, “20% of cases re-approved,” rework “doubles”). The optimistic case has Legal/Risk waiting 9.0h, better than today’s 9.6h, despite higher volume and no added capacity. It also says North/South “maintain local expertise” after those specialists are removed. East versus North/South is only a fairness aside; handover, queue, and cycle-time results stay aggregated. Resilience and learning barely appear. Tails are quoted as single p90s, without mix shift or censoring.

**Indicators and hidden risks.** The risk list hits the required themes, but several “checks” are mis-specified. Resource similarity is not utilization. `get_service_time` is service time, not queue wait. Fewer local referrals measure the new routing rule, not skill decay. The eight risks recycle the adverse scenario’s 25.0d / 30% figures instead of separating a composition effect (fast simple local cases masking slower complex, regional, or East-versus-North/South cases).

**Pilot and recommendation.** East, the team the prompt says may benefit differently, is excluded, so E_Tech never enters the pool and the pilot is not the proposal. `case_complexity > 5` is invented. About 10% of an already rare legal/risk stream is underpowered. A 4-week post window cannot observe tails when p90 is 16–23 days; completed-case averages will favor short cases. Utilization triggers at 90–95% are already nearly true for Legal/Risk before any change. “Full proceed” after two quiet weeks is weaker than, and inconsistent with, the later rework/+15% tail conditions. The recommendation is still the right class—pilot, with explicit warnings against reading early medians—but it does not condition on a capacity plan for the saturated legal/risk queue.

**PM4Py sketch.** Argument order for `filter_event_attribute_values` is reversed. `filter_variants` is given activity names rather than variant traces. `discover_performance_dfg(..., variant=dfg)` is not the API. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` returns a dict, not a frame with a `rework` column. `compare_working_together_networks`, `visualize_roles`, and the handover import path are not real. Grouping `time:timestamp` by `team` and taking max−min measures the log’s span, not case cycle time. `df_post_pilot` and `org:resource == "absent_staff"` are undefined. Before/after comparison is therefore not actually specified.
