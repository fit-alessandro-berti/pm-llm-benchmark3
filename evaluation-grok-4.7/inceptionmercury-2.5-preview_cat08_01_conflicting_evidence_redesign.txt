5.5

The write-up follows the required section structure and gets the central tension directionally right: the high-volume auto-approval path is fast, while complaint and conformance outcomes are worse, especially on high-value devices. It is not a reliable process-mining recommendation. Several counts are wrong, segment logic contradicts itself, the comparison table and dominance call fight the evidence, and the experiment plan would misread its own results.

**Evidence errors.** Auto eligibility → Fast approval is 2,550. Fast approval → Dispatch replacement is 2,300, and Fast approval’s total inflow is 2,790 once the 240 fraud-review approvals are included (outflows 2,300 + 350 + 140). The diagnosis and Redesign B both treat 2,550 as the dispatch path, and B adds an unsupported “per year.” “Median ~1 hour post-intake” does not match the fast-path segment cycle times (8–28 hours) or the summed medians through dispatch and close. Log fitness 0.91 is described as if it were the weak conformance result; the sharper figure, 68% fitting traces, is omitted. The cost fact is misstated as a “9% gap.” The actual comparison is a 31% pre-rework cost advantage that falls to 9% after complaints, reshipments, and credits. “124h for Premium” and a high-value pilot at “20% of volume” are not in the data. High-value share is 14% + 21% = 35%, which the same answer already calculated.

**Reasoning gaps.** Manual eligibility is treated as if it were the reviewed/repair path. The 22% → 5% and 18% → 6% complaint gaps are for reviewed/repair versus fast approval, not for inserting an eligibility or fraud check and then still dispatching. R1 can be satisfied and the wrong-part failure (Dispatch → complaint, mean 96 hours; EUR 410 on high-value devices) can remain. R2’s explicit exception for Premium low-risk accessories, R3’s reopen-before-close rule, the reopen/escalation loop (310 + 120), 12.5% versus 4% repair rework, underused quality inspectors, and the stated +15–35% manual inflow from a rule update are not designed into the flows. Redesign A’s prose routes all Premium customers to manual or fraud review, which would push Premium low-risk accessories from an 8-hour median toward 46 hours and into the >24-hour service-credit zone, while the target line quietly limits the change to 35% high-value volume. Those are different designs. Redesign B staffs the gate from the three repair-quote technicians, who are already delayed on part prices, and skips the two underused inspectors. The gate is also applied to all fast approvals, including low-risk accessories where the expected wrong-part cost is EUR 95 and a 6–18 hour delay is a poor trade.

**Trade-offs and the table.** Sacrifice paragraphs exist, and low-risk versus high-value outcomes appear in the diagnosis. The redesigns still do not keep the fast path where the complaint delta is small (Premium low-risk 6% vs 3%, Standard low-risk 8% vs 4%) and move high-value work toward repair where the delta is large. As rendered, the Pareto cells lose their direction signs, so several effects cannot be read. Marking the current process “dominated? Yes” while every redesign is “Conditional” is inconsistent, and it asserts dominance the case data cannot support. The three proposals are also the three interventions already listed in the resource notes (rule change, pre-dispatch gate, evidence portal), each left as a single lever rather than a segment-specific operating model.

**Experiments.** The first pilot’s joint rule is incoherent: complaint rate below 10% is a soft bar against baselines of 18–22% fast and 5–6% reviewed, while a 72-hour median is stricter than the observed reviewed/repair medians of 96 and 120 hours, so a successful quality shift would fail the speed rule. Utilization “stop above 90%” sits past the stated 82% queue break. The second pilot runs the evidence portal on Standard low-risk accessories (25%), not on the unclear-defect / repeated-evidence population the redesign names (22% share, 140-hour median, 26% repeat requests). Running B on Premium high-value after A has diverted that segment off fast dispatch removes most of the cases B is built to catch. Downstream displacement is only partly instrumented: denial, abandonment, service credits, reshipment, and censored queue time can all lower the measured complaint rate without a real quality gain. Pilot 2’s abandonment check is the one clear control for that bias.
