The answer covers every required section, uses the requested table schemas, and gets the main story right: March is mostly a label and provenance break, while rework, cycle time, and queueing move later and for different reasons. That multi-mechanism reading is the strongest part of the response. The quantitative core and several diagnostic claims do not hold up.

The baseline-relative percentage table is arithmetically sound, and January versus March control flow is treated as nearly stable, which the normalized rates support. The reported DFG distances are not. Under the stated weighted Jaccard formula, January versus May is about 0.04, not 0.14; July is about 0.07, not 0.22; October is well below 0.26. January versus March is also not distance 0.00: Collect Documents to Assess, Senior Assessment, and the decision edge all move slightly. The cycle-time split is internally impossible. Median cycle time rises by 12.7 hours from January to July, yet queue wait is said to rise by about 20 hours, from 26 to 46. Residual time after Assess service is about 26.7 hours in January and 38.8 hours in July, and that residual is not queue-before-Assess. A 0.6-hour service increase is about 5% of the 12.7-hour rise, not 10–15%. Seventeen extra rework cases per 100, at the stated 8–12 hours each, add roughly 1.4–2.0 hours, not 30–40% of the deterioration. The shares overlap and do not add up.

Timing is partly off. Cycle time and rework break in May with the supplier shock; June 15 amplifies that break rather than starting performance drift. September’s partial reversal, before the renewal spike, is missing from the timeline, so supplier recovery is never isolated. December is labeled sudden and then narrated as intervention relief, although volume, complexity, and supplier mix all fell together. The October Assess to Senior Assessment jump, from 4 to 22 per 100 cases, is used in the distance note and then dropped from the attribution, despite the known inconsistent recording of that activity.

Mechanism tables are complete but some evidence is misfiled. Modest Assess service time supports a queueing account; it is listed as evidence against it. Missing resources are a migration artifact, not support for staffing cuts. The priority rule is described as having changed delay patterns when the aggregates cannot show waits by priority class.
