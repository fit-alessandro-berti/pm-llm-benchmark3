The final answer reaches the right high-level decision — conditional adoption, with originals and legal ordering left intact — and it does recognize confounding, capacity shift, and timestamp artifacts. It is not reliable enough on the criteria that carry the task.

The comparison table has 11 rows and omits loops. The tail and rework rows are column-shifted: Site A’s after state is given Site B’s p90 of 110 h and rework of 17%, while “p90 9678 h” and “11.812.4%” are garbled. That table states false process facts.

The 38% of cases that do not require originals are repeatedly treated as Site A-style digital claims. Nothing in the evidence establishes that equivalence, yet it defines eligibility, scenarios, the pilot, and the recommendation.

The transport graph does not connect capacities or site constraints to cycle time, puts prose inside an edge, and adds nodes outside the requested graph. The prose overstates a shared “independent data” mechanism at Site B.

Scenario ranges are not derived from Site A’s 9 h confounded change, the 6 h wait change, service times of 3.4 h and 8.1 h, the 18 h originals delay, or the DFG counts. “Optimistic” transfer is given a smaller effect than the narrower digital-only scenario despite the same 38% share. Both use the overall 44 h median as the subgroup baseline. The bottleneck scenario’s ranges include improvement and worsening for both median and p90, so it does not actually show a better average with a worse tail. Site A throughput per verification FTE is slightly down (about 267 to 260 cases); that is never used.

The pilot cannot support its own claim. A week-scale phased rollout conflicts with the six-month duration and the 200-cases-per-arm rule. Assignment is by team, but identity, document, assessment, and legal staff are shared pools of 4, 7, 3, and 2, so interference is unresolved. There is no concurrent randomized control. “Support” is used as if it were statistical power. The expansion rule in the recommendation lists a rework increase as a trigger.

The PM4Py block would not run: `import add_ enabling` is a syntax error, and `formatadores`, `create_powl_from_edge_list`, bare `POWL()`, and `get_traces_as_list` are invented. The edge list makes legal review, approval, and rejection all mandatory and contains a supplement cycle, so it is neither a strict partial order nor an optional legal branch. The limitations comment is the only sound part of that section.

Assumption 5 tests the statutory 40% legal-review rate rather than the supplement loop it names (360 of 1,360 legal reviews). The format is otherwise largely present, which keeps this from a lower score, but model fidelity and quantitative transport are not established.
