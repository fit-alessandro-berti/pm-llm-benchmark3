3.5

The write-up names the right problem (72-hour resolution SLA, open cases, competing terminals, transfers, leakage) and picks a defensible primary estimator family, but the estimands, censoring clock, case contributions, and code are wrong in ways that would reverse the analysis.

**Estimands.** Time origin at `Submit` is right. The event definition is not. “Any terminal status” and “not resolved (or otherwise terminated)” are different random variables, yet both are treated as one \(P(T>72)\) that also “treats withdraw, reject, and transfer as competing events.” If those outcomes are events, they are not competitors; if they are competitors, \(T\) is not time to any termination. The second estimand is mislabeled: cumulative incidence is \(P(T>72,\text{cause}=\text{resolution})\), not \(P(\text{resolved after 72}\mid\text{event is resolution})\). Conditioning on eventual resolution is the completed-case estimand the task rules out. The worked examples do not actually split by estimand. Withdrawal at 27h and transfer at 68h are called “on-time.” For a resolve-by-72 SLA those are failures or unknowns, not successes. S03 is an observed late resolution, not “not observed.” S09 (open at 92) is an already-observed non-resolution at 72, still in the risk set until 92. The text censors it at 72 and stops the risk set there, so the required distinction among right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches is missed.

**Encoding.** The status table contradicts itself: rejection is `competing = 0` while the prose calls rejection competing, and the code later codes it as competitor 1. Open cases are collapsed to “Open (72h)” and censored at 72. Ages are 23, 35, 47, 51, 67, 79, 81, 92, and 104; censoring time is that age. Transfer option A (competing event) is coherent. Option B censors at 72 rather than at the transfer time, which invents follow-up after S21 (26h) and S08 (68h) and throws away S32’s observed internal breach at 96h. Option C puts a transfer flag on the transferred cases and, in §6, weights them by an invented 48-hour external delay. That flag is known only when observation stops; it is not an IPCW covariate, and 48h is not in the extract.

**Bias section.** Dropping unresolved cases can understate tail risk, and immortal time is real, but the concrete claims are off. S09 is not censored at 72. Open cases still under 72 (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31), whose breach indicator is unknown, are never contrasted with open cases already past 72 (S09, S11, S25, S26). “Many transfers” in B is two cases (S21, S32) against one in A (S08). Long gaps are not B-specific (S11 has gap 55). No risk-set count or bound is computed from the 32 rows.

**Methods.** Aalen–Johansen is a reasonable primary choice for cause-specific incidence, and KM is the wrong summary for a resolution CIF. The rest of the plan is loose. Cause-specific hazards are not biased by competing events; they are biased only if read as effects on the CIF, and stratification is not the repair. Fine–Gray does target the CIF; the real constraints here are the unnatural risk set, time-varying covariates, and sparsity (about 13 resolutions, 3 rejections, 4 withdrawals, 3 transfers, 9 open). Restricted mean time is \(E[\min(T,\tau)]\), not average time-to-event among those who finish. A multi-state survival model is appropriate; fitting it “via PM4Py” is not, because PM4Py does not estimate transition hazards. Stratified curves by cohort and priority are recommended with no note that six strata on \(n=32\) are mostly empty. Nothing is tied back to one cleanly defined estimand.

**Process features.** The feature table calls `HasEscalate` time-varying, then §5 calls it a safe baseline “set at Submit.” Escalation is not known at submit; that is the leakage the task flags. The same error covers end-of-trace `escalated`, `rework_count`, `TransferFlag`, and final `last_event_gap_h` if used at \(t=0\). Cohort is a case-level label, not a within-case time offset. The state diagram omits `AutoCheck`, the activity that marks the policy change, has no edge into `Rework`, and routes `Transfer` through a fictional Review2. Observed paths such as `Screen>Reject`, `Screen>Withdraw`, `Escalate>Transfer`, and `Rework>Resolve` do not fit the diagram.

**Sensitivity and operations.** Direction claims are not estimand-specific. Treating pre-72 transfers as competing events does not count them as SLA successes; relative to censoring them and reading \(1-\widehat S\), it raises, rather than lowers, estimated probability of non-resolution. Withdrawal and rejection are never re-coded. “Policy-compliance” cannot be toggled: compliance is not a column. The 15% alert cut and tighter P1 SLA are unlabeled inventions. Priority/cohort stratification is mentioned, but the C-index is discrimination, not calibration, and cohort contrasts are not fenced off as non-causal under non-random, incomplete rollout.

**Code.** The single code block does not implement the plan. `DataFrame` construction is invalid: 32 case ids, cohort length 24 (`["A"]*12+["B"]*12` instead of 15 and 17), 33 statuses, 30 prefixes, no `escalated` or `rework_count`. Prefixes omit or scramble S21, S22, S27, S28, S30, and S31; S25’s gap is 0 instead of 48. Open-case time is `72 - age`, so S09, S11, S25, and S26 get negative times. `assign(**apply(...))` will not expand dicts. The PM4Py call uses a nonexistent `pm4py.objects.log.import_from.df`, does not explode prefixes, passes `tag_separator` to `get_variants`, and never uses the variant result. `HasRework` is a string test; `time_to_first_activity` returns another rework bit. Lifelines is called with a Cox-style `duration_col` / `show_progress` interface and `cumulative_hazard_` at exact time 72; a binary `event_resolve` column codes competitors as censored, so this is not a CIF. The KM subset uses bitwise `~` on 0/1 codes, drops open cases, and sets the event indicator to `censored`, which is then all zeros. The transfer “sensitivity” rewrites competitor 3 to 3. There is no cohort or priority stratification. The fallback is one sentence, not a formula.

**Limitations.** Intermediate timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, calendar time, workload, and incomplete rollout are rightly listed. The wording then says missing calendar dates block time-since-submit; that duration is already in `duration_or_age_h`. Assignment mechanism is only implied. “Causal inference” overstates what those fields would identify.

The skeleton shows familiarity with the vocabulary. The definitions required before any estimator, the handling of the prompt’s own example cases, and the executable encoding are not reliable.
