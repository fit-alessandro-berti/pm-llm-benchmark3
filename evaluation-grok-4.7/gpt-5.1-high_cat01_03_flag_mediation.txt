6.4

The stage arithmetic is careful and largely correct, absent stages are not zero-filled, natural direct/indirect effects are rightly rejected because of interference and exposure-induced confounding, and the required sections, contrasts, and cautions are present. Those strengths do not offset several central reasoning failures.

The mediation estimands are mislabeled. E3 and E4 take manual-review occurrence and manual waiting as the outcomes, so they are effects on mediators, not effects mediated by them. E5 contrasts P2 with P4 while describing that contrast as holding manual review fixed; P2 removes mandatory review and P4 retains it. E2 calls a policy contrast a controlled direct effect while “holding downstream handling as under each policy,” which is not a controlled direct effect. E7 simultaneously calls one contrast a total effect and an interventional indirect effect. E8 attributes cross-world assumptions to separable effects, which are defined to avoid that cross-world construction. The opening discussion never cleanly separates “set the recorded flag to zero” from “stop displaying it” from “change automated routing.”

The data patterns that dominate identification are understated or missed. Flag is perfectly separated by `risk_score` (highest unflagged 0.61, lowest flagged 0.64), so “enriched for high risk” is the wrong description and no within-score adjustment is possible in this sample. Triage resources are likewise perfectly separated (flagged T1/T2, unflagged T3/T4). Escalation is almost collinear with high complexity and reviewer M2, including unflagged cases, yet flag→escalation is still treated as a main pathway. The shared-queue claim is contradicted by the same table: unflagged manual waits are about 1–3 hours, not long. Reject rates are 2/10 in both flag groups.

The mechanism graph puts `cycle_time` before `decision_outcome`, reversing time. Reversal conditions 1 and 3 would count against removing review (P2), not against P3, which keeps review and adds capacity. The pilot then randomizes cases into “added capacity,” although capacity is a system-level intervention and case-level assignment leaves shared-queue interference inside the contrast. The code does compute explicit waits correctly and warns against causal DFG readings, but it omits the observed arrival event, invents a SeniorTeam resource, and its performance DFG therefore cannot show the triage-wait contrast and mixes waiting with service on the edges it does show.
