6.3

The essay covers every required section and reaches the right general concern: residual gaps remain after the reported controls, and language-support and accommodation queues are the clearest procedural burden. It also avoids the prohibited moves of equating raw approval gaps with discrimination and of dropping rights-preserving steps to shorten cycle time. Those strengths do not offset several concrete errors.

Raw and adjusted figures are repeatedly mixed. The 13.7- and 15.9-day medians are raw, yet they are described as persisting after adjustment; the adjusted cycle-time gaps are +2.6, +4.1, and +0.9 days. Supervisor waits of 1.9 and 1.6 days are treated as gaps versus Group A, whose wait is 1.0 day, so the actual gaps are 0.9 and 0.6 days. Group D’s 20% reopen rate is called the highest; Group C is 24% and Group B is 22%.

The adjusted +9.5 percentage-point gap in Request Missing Documents is interpreted as Group B having more incomplete files. That estimate is already controlled for documentation completeness, so it is evidence of different routing at similar recorded completeness. The same mistake appears when that gap is later called a legitimate documentation-complexity mechanism.

The adjustment set itself is over-read. It does not control for language need or an accommodation request, which are exactly what separates much of Groups B and C from A. Residual cycle time and extra supervisor review therefore still mix mandatory support steps, excess queue time, and possible differential treatment. The write-up treats those residuals as if documented complexity had already been held fixed. It also calls controlling for risk_score_band a way to “control away legitimate disparities,” which reverses the problem: a historically biased score can hide a discriminatory pathway.

“Legitimate mechanisms” include understaffed bilingual and accommodation teams. Those are plausible causes, but they are not fairness justifications. The same capacity story is then repeated as a concerning mechanism, so the classification does not separate justified complexity from unequal burden. The bottom line then calls the process systemically unfair for B, C, and D together. Group D’s adjusted cycle-time gap is small, Group C’s adjusted approval gap is only −2.2 points, and no interval or sensitivity bound is given. That verdict is stronger and more uniform than the evidence.

The PM4Py plan would not support the assessment. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied to a log rather than a dataframe; `get_service_time` is an activity aggregate and cannot be subtracted from `get_all_case_durations`; `filter_case_performance` does not accept a waiting-time vector; `np` is never imported. The language-support check filters traces down to Submit Application and then looks for Language Support Contact in those reduced traces. `filter_variants` is passed one flat activity list, approval is inferred from a final Benefit Activation event instead of `case_outcome`, and the V2 summary used as the filter does not contain activation. Log-skeleton output is read from a nonexistent `constraints` key, resource similarity is treated as activity-to-resource assignment, and `language_preference == 'non-majority'` is invented. Performance DFGs are not compared by group.

Formatting also misses the required heading level, an extra title is added, and several sentences lose the words needed to state causal links. The capacity and missing-SLA findings are sound; the numeric claims, causal reading of the adjusted model, and analysis code are not reliable enough for a higher mark.
