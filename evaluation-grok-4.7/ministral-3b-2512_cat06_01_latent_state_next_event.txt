3.6

The write-up keeps the required JSON shape and gets several coarse states into the right neighborhood (C6-A, C6-C, C6-D, and C6-E are at least placed after the last logged milestone). The forecasts, obligation reading, and evidence separation are not reliable.

C6-B is structurally wrong. The prefix already contains Customer Evidence Requested and Evidence Received at 12:16, so the case is in evidence review, not S1. Completed obligations drop those events. Fast Close at 0.70–0.95 treats a path the case left at 09:30, when evidence was requested instead of the 1.1 h triage-to-fast-close edge, as if it were still open. Evidence Review Completed, the direct historical successor of Evidence Received (356 cases, median 2.8 h; about 1.7 h elapsed; 4 items and 2 reviewers), is ranked last at 0.00–0.10. The low bound of 0.00 also conflicts with the text saying the chance is not zero.

C6-A has the right primary state and correctly keeps Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment mandatory, then predicts the wrong next log event. Evidence Review Completed is still outstanding; investigation completions sit after that review (median 8.5 h) and behind a technical queue of 7 with one of three engineers out. Rank 1 claims there is no investigation backlog and credits triage resource R-T4 for investigation speed. The S2 alternative denies an Evidence Received event that is already in the prefix. Two devices from the same lot plus three similar recent complaints make Supplier Analysis a material suspicion; it is dismissed. Rank 3 fuses Evidence Review Completed with a later request for more evidence. “74/125” is not a base rate in the directly-follows summary.

C6-C inverts rule 5. Four complaints share a failure mode across different lots, which is not a same-lot or same-component trigger, yet Supplier Analysis Completed is ranked first at 0.60–0.90 and called mandatory “lot overlap.” The 6–36 h figure is an operating-condition range, not a directly-follows median. The real open gate is Regulatory Assessment: medium severity, no injury, but a country rule that repeated malfunction can be reportable, with technical work finished at 11:45 and no assessment completion visible. Disposition is marked process-enabled while those gates are still treated as open. “No immediate backlog” is also false for a 3-item supplier queue whose typical completion is 6–36 h.

C6-D is the best case. High severity plus injury, technical completion at 13:05, migration flag, source-system attachment without evidence events, and R-R2’s 12:40 worklist open are all used. The ranking is still inflated: Regulatory Assessment Completed at 0.50–0.85 sits only about 1.3 h into a 2–14 h range with 12 items waiting, while the text claims no regulatory backlog. A delayed evidence event flushing into the feed is a serious rival for the next log line and is only loosely separated from work still to be done. Supplier Analysis has no stated lot or component trigger.

C6-E is in the right state and correctly expects another review after the 13:20 receipt, then misses the clearest supplier obligation in the set: one lot linked to six complaints, plus a supplier certificate in the second package. Rule 5 makes Supplier Analysis mandatory and blocks disposition; both are omitted, and Disposition Recorded is marked process-enabled. “Dropped to 0.72” invents a prior score. Another evidence request at 0.60–0.90 is a minority historical branch (101 versus 173 technical investigations and 68 non-safety closures) and is a poor fit once a cluster and a supplier certificate are already in hand. Technical Investigation Completed cannot be the next log event 40 minutes after receipt.

Across cases, process_enabled is true on activities the rules still forbid, so the required split between an enabled task and the next visible completion is mostly missed. Contradicting-evidence lists restate missing events or restate the conclusion. Midpoints are not a coherent top-three distribution: C6-A is about 0.55/0.38/0.23 and C6-C about 0.75/0.30/0.15. The cross-case note says C6-A and C6-B share a prefix only through triage. They share the full path through Evidence Received and should diverge inside S3: C6-A toward technical, regulatory, and possible supplier work; C6-B toward review then a non-investigation or duplicate closure. Queue delay, migration delay, and directly-follows limits are named, but the directly-follows discussion does not cover concurrency, invisible starts, or mandatory gates, and the calibration note claims the intervals are calibrated while they overlap across most of the scale.
