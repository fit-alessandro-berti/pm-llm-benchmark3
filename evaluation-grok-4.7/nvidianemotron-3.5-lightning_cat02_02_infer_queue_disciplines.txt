The write-up has the required seven-part structure and a few locally correct observations (A03’s appointment start, B02 interrupting B01, D06 starting at 11:30, C1 free only at 10:10, D05 needing D-F). Those do not rescue the decision reconstructions, which are the core of the task and are factually wrong on every queue.

QA omits A04 at 08:20, even though A04 arrived at 08:13 and is priority 1 with the shortest estimate. A03 is therefore not merely chosen over older A02; it also beats a waiting priority-1 short job. The later claim that calibration “fully explains” A04 is false: calibration is only 08:52–08:58. At 08:32, with A1 free, A02 (priority 3, 20 min) is started over A04. At 09:20 the FIFO prediction is also reversed: A06 arrived at 08:29, before A07, so FIFO selects A06, not A07. Priority is repeatedly falsified while the resource is free, yet it is still listed as a real secondary rule.

QB misstates the timeline. At 09:31 B01 resumes; B03 does not start until 09:48. That resumption, ahead of waiting B03, is the central test and is missing. At 09:48 B05 (priority 1) is already waiting and is still not chosen. After B01 completes, B04 (priority 3) is chosen over waiting B06 (priority 2). “Preemptive priority” at confidence 80 cannot survive those eligible sets. Section 3 also miscounts B01’s two stops as three interruptions and ties B07’s missed 10:30 appointment to a reassignment reason that belongs to B01.

QC drops C01 from every eligible set. C01 arrived first (10:00) and is explicitly the oldest job, executed last among the initial backlog. FIFO would select C01 at 10:10, not C02. The reported “arrival-order” support is an artifact of ignoring that job. The short-before-long pattern (C02, C03, C04, with C01 deferred) is the obvious SPT/SEPT comparison, and it is never taken seriously. Later choices are also mis-specified: at 10:32 FIFO is C01, not C04; at 11:40 C01 is chosen over higher-priority, shorter, and tighter-deadline C07.

QD is the least wrong diagnosis, but the mechanism is still misstated. At 11:25 D-M is free and selects D04 over already waiting priority-1 D08; “D-M was busy” does not explain that choice. D-F is idle from 11:36 to 12:00, not busy until 12:20; D05 does not fit before the meeting, while D10 would. At 11:48 D08 and D09 are not waiting, so the priority prediction naming D08 is invalid. The real D-X choice is D07 over priority-1 D10.

The counterfactual tests mostly restate fields already in the log, rather than naming a new observable, and several of the paired predictions use the same bad eligible sets. The exception ranking puts a ≤2-minute scan delay first, which cannot explain these reorderings, and it never applies the SLA-triggered policy change to C05. The collection plan and the QD-versus-QA synthesis are usable, but the synthesis still treats QA priority and QC FIFO as live findings. Because the eligible-set errors flip the main diagnoses, the document is not a reliable process-mining argument.
