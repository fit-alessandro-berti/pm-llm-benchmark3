7.5

The analysis is unusually specific and mostly numerically reliable. Trace membership, the 42/31, 24/16, 12/10, 6/6, 7/7, and 4/4 occurrence counts, the order DFG frequencies (Pack→Pack 2, Dispatch→Dispatch 2, Record Payment→Confirm Delivery 5, Confirm Delivery→Record Payment 2), the absence of a direct Dispatch→Confirm Delivery edge in the order log, the delivery lags (3h, 4h55m, 22h45m), the invoice lags, and O3’s 9 shared events all check out against the given E2O relations. The required matrix, distortion categories, position B, and sensitivity themes are present and internally argued rather than generic. Treating copied events as both synchronization evidence and counting duplication is consistent, and the warning that deleting shared events would erase O2’s only dispatch and confirmation is right.

The score is held down by a material false claim and one contradiction. Sections 6 and 7 assert that direct E2O is already the transitive closure of the O2O graph and that a propagated-relation delta must be empty, citing e13, e19, and e22. That is not true. One-hop expansion adds relations that are absent from E2O: e13 does not include D2, although D2 carries P3; e19 does not include M2 or M3, although both settle V2; Create Order, Add Item, and Close Order likewise do not include the items, packages, invoices, or payments reachable through contains, packs, bills, or settles. The code then stores an empty `propagated_delta` instead of computing the diff, so a required check is both unimplemented and pre-declared with the wrong result.

That error collides with an otherwise correct finding. The order DFG audit correctly expects Dispatch→Confirm Delivery = 0, but the OC-DFG comment and sensitivity check 4 assign that same edge “Order 6” object occurrences. Those 6 are dispatch event–object participations, not directly-follows edge counts. The parenthetical justification “e16 ×3, e17 ×2” sums to 5, not 6, and does not equal the 6−3 gap. Section 4 states the gap correctly as two extra copies of e16 plus one of e17, so the later arithmetic is inconsistent with the answer’s own earlier count. The package lag “55m–1h” also drops P3’s actual 53 minutes.

Those defects sit inside an otherwise strong argument for Order as the primary lifecycle and Invoice plus Delivery as secondary views. They are large enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
