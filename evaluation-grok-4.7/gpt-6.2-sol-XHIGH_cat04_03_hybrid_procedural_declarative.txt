7.5

The policy split is coherent: strict legal precedence is the implemented rule, the emergency exception stays external, and T6 is correctly `fail` versus `policy-dependent`. R5 is rightly kept out of binary DECLARE, the Petri-net route structure matches the backbone, and the T7 arithmetic is right (34 h gives z ≈ 3.67; 2 h gives z ≈ 1.94, so only the opinion pair warns). Label use, seconds conversion, template directions, and the R1/R8/R10 split are sound.

The log skeleton is not executable as written. In PM4Py, `directly_follows` is an allowlist. `set()` plus `check_log_skeleton`’s call to `conformance_log_skeleton` rejects every trace with two or more events, so the Section 8 cells that say `pass` contradict the code. The frequency adapter is also log-relative: allowed counts for unrestricted labels are copied from the log under test, so that wrapper cannot state a stable norm. No `consider_directly_follows=False` escape is given.

Smaller defects remain. R9 both abstains when the approver is ambiguous and fails on a known equal pair, without a defined precedence. The R6 checks are sequential `if`s that can emit overlapping statuses. Section 10 calls the net the canonical allowable language even though accepting runs can still escalate before screening; only DECLARE forbids that. The net’s open-case loops also leave opinion, suspension, and bank-change order entirely to later layers, which is disclosed but makes “procedural pass” a weak claim.
