7.5

The role model is largely coherent and uses complexity, substitutions, and authority rather than raw frequency alone. It still contains factual slips, internal contradictions, and code that would not run as written, so it is not nearly flawless.

The weeks 7–8 episode is misread in §3. Erik was not absent. Mateo filtered low-risk security referrals, Erik therefore did fewer A6 events, false escalations fell, and two critical cases waited more than two days. §2 describes that episode more accurately, so the write-up contradicts itself. Calling Erik’s formal Risk/security role a “shadow” role because a clustering algorithm might miss him also mixes algorithmic invisibility with informal authority. §6 then adds Chen to the shadow set without having established that role.

Temp agents are over-restricted. Kai and Lina have medians of 0.18 and 0.19 and zero critical cases, but they also have 76 and 70 medium-complexity cases (about one fifth of their load) plus a few high-complexity cases. “Very low to low only” and “almost no medium/high/critical” are stronger than the table. The parenthetical similarity 0.98 is Kai–Lina, not their similarity to Amira/Ben, yet it is used to explain a merge with R01/R02.

Core versus peripheral is inconsistent. The QA row treats A10 as core; §2 correctly treats it as peripheral (about 35–38 events against about 300 quality checks). The technical row puts “some A2” in core; §2 moves A1/A2/A4 to peripheral (Chen/Dina A2 is only 18 and 24). A3/A5/A10 are summarized as about 240–260 each, but A10 is 215 and 220.

The PM4Py sketch names the right functions and the caution about not reading clusters as formal authority is directionally right. The join is not coherent: `df` already contains `case_complexity` and `escalation_level`, so the second merge creates `_x`/`_y` columns and the later `agg` on `case_complexity` fails. `share_true` is unused. Complexity is event-weighted inside each role, so busy cases dominate the median. `escalation_level == "critical"` invents an encoding and conflates escalation with the numeric complexity score the question asked to join. `case_id` on the social-network calls is not part of those APIs. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting graphs are computed and then not actually used to test the broker claim.

§4 is the strongest part: Giorgio, Mateo, Erik, Amira, Chen, and the QA pair each get two readings and a concrete deciding test, and substitution is not treated as proof that Giorgio or Mateo permanently holds the absent person’s authority. §6 covers staffing, temp documentation, specialist backup, and more than two cautions about short-window logs and spike-driven clusters. Those strengths do not cancel the misread episode, the complexity overclaim, the core/peripheral contradictions, or the broken join.
