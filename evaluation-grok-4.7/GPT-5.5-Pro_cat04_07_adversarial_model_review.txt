7.4

The analytical core is strong: use cases are separated, metrics stay disaggregated, Model S and Model C are read with the right process-tree semantics, and B1–B8 are classified correctly under exact language acceptance. The governance split (S exploratory only, C not an enforcement model, third model conditional on validation) follows from that evidence. The score is held down by concrete defects in the proposed alternative and in the validation/dissent logic, not by the review of S and C.

The process tree is a coherent backbone: both checks are mandatory and unordered, expedite cannot precede them, approval is exclusive, and a correction loop can recheck one or both checks. That is safer than S and less brittle than C. The checker does not implement those semantics. `approval_pos > len(trace) - 2` only catches an approval that is the final event, so an internal trace such as Register, Credit, Technical, Manager, Supplier, Release is returned as valid. Supplier before the checks is likewise returned as valid when the attribute is external or internal, even though the tree rejects it. Expedite between the initial checks and a later correction is accepted by the checker and rejected by the tree. The conjunction rule is never stated, so the two artifacts disagree on the language being governed.

The missing-attribute rule is also internally inconsistent. The checker emits an unknown whenever `external_internal` is absent, including when Supplier Review is already present. Under the stated reading (required if external, not forbidden if internal), that attribute cannot change the verdict once Supplier is present. Section 8 still treats B5 and B7 as accepted. The unused `policy_constraints` dict can drift from the function, and already does.

Section 8 only partly meets the distinguishing-trace requirement. A2 separates the third model from both originals. A1 and A5 separate it from C only. A3 and A4 are rejected by all three, so they do not distinguish the proposal. Supplier-before-checks is an extra constraint beyond P3 and is not labeled as one. The correction-before-supplier/expedite limitation is disclosed; the supplier-before-checks overconstraint is not. Forbidding a correction with zero rechecks is a reasonable design choice, but it is stronger than P6 and is written as if it were given.

The evaluation program names the right tests, yet the skeleton does not carry the decision rule. Aggregate `fitness_alignments` on a negative log is not exact acceptance, which Section 5 itself warns. There is no per-trace cost-zero check and no alignment-cost sweep, despite that being a required experiment. The split code is an unstratified shuffle with stratification left as a comment. Training fitness of 0.973 for C is reported but not used: C does not even cover its own training log, which weakens the “frequent-variant mirror” claim more than the write-up says.

Section 11’s rebuttal is real and within length, but the first item offered as decision-changing evidence (a policy that S is exploratory only) confirms the recommendation rather than challenging it. The later items, especially a negative suite plus proof that unseen recombinations are actually unauthorized, are the ones that could rehabilitate C.
