4.5

The assessment is structurally complete and directionally alert, but it is not a stable compliance opinion. Its hierarchy, rule table, pseudo-logic, and case holdings contradict one another, and several primaries are reached by misstating the rule that was applied at the moment of commitment.

The method says norms are applied at the earliest controlling act, then the cases do not do that. P-501’s commitment is 08:50 on 14 July, before ECD-2’s 09:00 start; recovery timestamps can only make the real act earlier. The row still treats commit-before-PO as allowed by ECD-2. P-502 is also on 14 July, before the CFO email of 15 July 07:20, yet the finance analysis never asks whether that email existed. P-505’s finance approval is after both commitment and the 18:00 expiry.

P-501 is held COMPLIANT at high confidence on a justification the facts reject. B4’s shift-lead substitution requires an absent manager and an active delegation; the record states the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. That is affirmative noncompliance with the substitution condition, and the write-up files it as a logging defect and as a missing record. The same row marks R3 and R6 satisfied even though R3 requires formal delegation and R6, as catalogued, requires Plant and Finance before commitment. Decisive evidence cites a supplier acknowledgment at 10:15, which is P-502’s evidence.

P-502 is the clearest breach of the stated anti-cure rule. It is a new supplier at EUR 62,000, so GPIP-4 A3 and A2 both attach before commitment. Compliance is at 18:30 and Finance at 13:05, after a 10:15 commitment, and there is no Plant approval and no identified budget owner. The row lists post-commitment Compliance as a satisfied R2, while R2 itself requires clearance before commitment, A6 denies automatic cure, and the hierarchy says ECD-2 does not waive A3. “Same day” is used as cure. Missing evidence is marked “none critical.”

P-505 is held COMPLIANT under GPIP-4 A3. A3 does not trigger: EUR 88,000 and an approved supplier. The sensor is in scope and the 17:59:31 acknowledgment is better evidence of commitment inside the window than the 18:06 ERP stamp, including the nine-minute-fast clock. That timing point is then treated as the whole case. Finance is later, Plant is absent, the committer is unnamed, Create_PO is unstated, and C3’s four-hour recording is unproven. The strongest contrary reading is never the one tested in section 5.

P-508’s JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at high confidence does not follow from D1–D2. D1 makes joint Incident Command authorization sufficient only up to EUR 150,000. EUR 155,000 is above that cap. “Legal clearance as well” adds Legal; it does not erase A2’s pre-commitment Finance Controller and independent budget owner, or local Plant approval. The row lists those absences as deviations and still calls the exception justified, with no critical evidence missing. C3 recording is in fact unshown. The section 5 alternative also fails logically: BUY-8 was neither authorizer, so recasting Incident Command as the approver does not place BUY-8 in the approver role prohibited by A1.

P-507 is internally split. Compliance at 08:50 does precede the 09:00 commitment, and the 12:40 PDF falls inside four hours of the 08:55 authorization, with 14:30 explained as a queued system entry. Section 4 treats that PDF as justifying the exception. Section 6 then calls the same authorization a missed four-hour recording. The finance theory is similarly split: R1 is an “apparent deviation,” and the justification says D1 makes Finance unnecessary. The section 1 six-hour definition of “critical” would also push an eight-hour need outside ECD-2, which the case outcome ignores. D4 and the budget-owner half of A2 are not weighed.

P-503 and P-504 are the holdings that survive. P-503’s segregation failure, three-day backup stock, missing Finance, and missing Compliance support NONCOMPLIANT even on Operations’ reading of “critical,” because A3 still applies above EUR 100,000 and the answer’s own hierarchy does not let ECD-2 waive it. The row nevertheless credits R4 as satisfied while denying scope, and omits A3 from the deviations. P-504 is outside sterilization scope and outside the approved register, with one shift lead and no Incident Command authorization. The cited rule IDs do not match that theory: R5 is an allowance, not the breached prohibition, and the maintenance/production-support limit of the local SOP is not used. P-506 is rightly outside the expired directive, and an undocumented verbal extension fails, but R6 is marked satisfied when Plant approval is at 09:10, after the 06:20 commitment, and commit-before-PO is left unassessed.

The catalogue cannot generate these outcomes. R2’s exception column says ECD-2 C2 displaces Compliance for a new supplier; the pseudo-logic comment says it does not; the formal `unless overrides_for(Compliance_Clearance, …)` waives it anyway. Finance is not overridable by ECD-2 in the hierarchy and is overridable by ECD-2 in the pseudo-logic. A2 has no hierarchy row. A7, the actual route around A1 and A3, is absent from the override columns. A4, the rule that emergency status waives tendering only, is weighted “Low.” A1 is folded into an at-least-EUR-50,000 rule, so segregation disappears below that threshold. The CFO exception to R6 is used to displace Plant approval, although the CFO’s stated authority is interpretation of Finance controls. The method line also ranks local procedure and the incident directive ahead of the board policy as a general order, which A5 does not say.

Section 5 does not supply the strongest classification-changing premises. P-505’s alternative is the weak choice to treat the ERP clock as conclusive. P-507’s alternative requires Compliance before authorization, but Compliance already precedes authorization, so the outcome does not change. P-503’s backup-line reading would still leave A3, A2, and segregation in place. The six-hour scope gloss is itself an assumption: ECD-2 leaves “critical” undefined, and the live institutional readings are backup equipment versus 24 hours, not B4’s six-hour emergency test.

Section 6 does not keep the required categories apart. Late system evidence of a timely act is the right description of P-507’s PDF and of P-505’s acknowledgment; both are handled as something else. P-502’s late Compliance and Finance are not treated as policy breaches. Recommended retroactive approval for P-506 is left available despite A6.

The draft clause in section 7 restates the ambiguity it is meant to close. It forbids waiver of Finance approval above EUR 50,000 without the Chief Risk Officer, then allows Incident Command plus a CFO writing to replace Finance up to EUR 150,000. It also narrows A3 to new suppliers above EUR 100,000. A3 is above EUR 100,000 or any new supplier, and A2 attaches at EUR 50,000, not above it. The independent budget owner is omitted. The sunset, written-extension, and audit safeguards are the soundest part of the answer.
