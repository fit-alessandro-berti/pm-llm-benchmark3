6.5

The design covers the required sections and treats non-stationary demand, calendars, latent objections, shared resources, and cross-case effects as first-class concerns. It does not collapse waiting into service without a caveat, and it does not claim an optimal policy. Those parts are sound.

Several design choices are not reliable enough for the comparison being asked for. A 30-day warm-up from an empty system is far too short for sojourns whose medians are 48–140 days and whose tails reach about 260 days, and it would mis-state the backlog entering the March peak. Right-censoring of cases still open at the end of the 18-month log is never addressed, so duration and rework estimates would be biased. The given process tree is not reconciled with the log: redo labels such as “Completeness rework” are not events, and the tree’s full parallelism conflicts with the sequential example and the note that reviews are only partly parallel. Resource pools are treated as homogeneous, omitting the Monday portal clerk and the two planners restricted to simple renovations. D2 depends on prior violations, which are not in the log, and that gap is not flagged. Policy effect sizes (for example a 0.4× missing-document loop rate) are single points rather than ranges, and those counterfactual effects are missing from the sensitivity list.

Decision rules are loose. SLA caps include the historical p90, so the baseline can “pass,” and rework ceilings sit next to variant shares, which are not conditional rework probabilities.

The skeleton is modular and avoids `pm4py.read_csv`, but `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with `activity_id` and `timestamp` instead of `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`, so that path fails under current PM4Py. `WorkingCalendar.is_working` stores hour windows and then ignores them. Arrival, routing, queues, and resources are never connected, even as a minimal event loop.
