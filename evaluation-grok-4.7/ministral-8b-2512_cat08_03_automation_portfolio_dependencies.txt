The deliverable keeps the seven required sections and reaches a defensible directional conclusion: do not lead with the highest-frequency registration task, and treat entity mismatch, KYC quality, classification routing, and statement normalization as higher-leverage than raw volume. That thesis is not executed reliably enough to advise a bank. Candidate identity, dependencies, the portfolio, and the rollout contradict each other, and several headline numbers are attached to the wrong activities.

The candidate matrix is an ID-ordered listing, not a ranking. Entity-mismatch resolution is labeled A3, duplicating the KYC pre-classifier and dropping A4. A1 is said to depend on A2, while Section 2 reverses that arrow. A2 is described as violating four-eyes; four-eyes is a separation between credit analysis and committee packaging on High-exposure cases, which classification does not enforce. A7 is given limited cycle-time impact even though the external-valuation wait is 58 hours. A4 and A10 are rated low downstream-bottleneck risk in the same cells that cite 31-hour and 44-hour waits. A8 is marked as needing A6 first, then placed in the compliance portfolio without A6. A6 is treated as a source of analyst overload; the resource note says the overload comes from cleaner inflow when normalization is not improved. Column meaning shifts between “risk this automation creates a bottleneck” and “this activity is a bottleneck.”

Section 2 names the required dependency types, then misstates several of them. Four-eyes is assigned to A2 and A8. Analysts are described as already overloaded; the table only supports a conditional future load, while back office already falls below 65% utilization after 15:00 and compliance work concentrates on two seniors within a 3-FTE pool. The deep core-banking pair is A1 and A9 here and A1 and A2 in Section 6. Concurrency of KYC and financial-document review is never used. The missing-document loop (3,300 forward, 2,800 back, 52% repeated) is not treated as a prerequisite to normalization, so A6 is scheduled on documents the process often does not yet have. Most entity-mismatch cases return to KYC (1,160) rather than proceed to contract preparation (380); that loop is unused.

The three portfolios are recognizable, and the quick-win set correctly notes that it fails the requirement for a control-strengthening module. The bottleneck set still omits both dominant synchronization waits (58-hour valuation, 44-hour signing) and the largest rework loop. An assistant for 1,750 mismatch cases cannot turn a 31-hour specialist queue into an 8-hour queue without reducing arrivals or expanding the two-person escalation path. The compliance set advances committee auto-assembly while its own matrix says that module is garbage-in without normalization, collateral, and credit notes. None of the portfolios differentiates Micro, SME, and High-exposure, or Working-capital, Equipment finance, and Credit-line renewal, despite 71% versus 18% collateral incidence, weekly High-exposure committee cadence, judgment-heavy credit analysis, and 14% renewal contract remediation.

Section 4 covers two portfolios, not three. It converts median waits into unsupported targets: 31h to 8h, 20h to 12h, packaging 26h to 18h, four-eyes 5.8% to 3% or 1%, provisional signing 6.5% to 2% or 1%, and an end-to-end move from 60h to 36h. The 20-hour wait and 5,200 frequency belong to normalization, not financial-document review. The 24% document-review rework is driven by missing documents, which A6 does not fix. Provisional signing is a KYC-clearance breach; a hit pre-classifier is a different control. Entity-mismatch delay is applied as if it sat on every case, although it is a 1,750-case variant and is on the critical path in 82% of those cases, not 82% of the book. DFG edges, the 98% registration-position variant, and recorded-versus-actual concurrency are largely absent.

The central trade-off math is wrong. Receive-application exception rate is 1%; registration rework is 4%; classification exception is 8%. “1% misclassification in A1 produces 120 rework cases per day” uses the wrong activity, the wrong rate, and a daily unit the frequency column does not state. The rollout then implements A1, A2, A3, and A6, while the recommendation is A2, A3, A4, and A6. A4 never appears in the plan; A1 does, first. The union is five modules against a four-module budget. Phase 1, the actual first wave, has no clear control-strengthening module. The gate “compliance utilization > 70%” pushes a scarce specialist pool toward higher load. The “resolution time < 2 hours” gate mixes 55-minute service time with a 31-hour wait and assigns it to A3, not the resolution assistant.

Risk coverage names false negatives, A6 drift, and logging, but the audit requirement is suggestion, human override, and final decision for every module. False positives into a two-specialist network, month-end four-eyes failure, policy ambiguity from A12, and residual compliance accountability are thin or attached to modules the recommended portfolio does not fund. The answer also asserts a hidden ground truth after the brief states there is none.

The directional portfolio instinct is real. The artifacts, resource network, variant structure, and control findings are not handled carefully enough for the plan to be followed without creating the downstream and control problems the brief warns about.
