5.4

The write-up covers every required section and keeps several live hypotheses, but it is not reliable on the numbers or on the metric the question turns on.

The handover reading misstates Mira_Coordinator’s betweenness. At 0.77 she is first, ahead of Bot_Rules at 0.41, yet the text calls 0.77 second-highest “after” Bot_Rules. It also has her receiving work from the team leads and sending work back to them. Those edges are not in the reported handover table. Incoming edges that are listed run from Bot_Rules, the temps, and the two specialists.

The central statistic is read backwards more than once. “Share of cases with exception_flag=True” is the flag rate among cases a resource already touches. Mira_Coordinator’s 0.48 is therefore not the share of exception cases that reach her, and it is not higher than Omar_Medical (0.62) or Priya_Legal (0.71). The sentence that calls 0.48 the highest rate and “even higher than” those specialist rates contradicts the figures in its own parentheses. That error is reused to claim exception cases “touch her at 48%.” The only coverage figures actually given are 22% of all claims, 64% of reopens, and 71% of complaints.

The event arithmetic is inverted. 960 events on 905 cases is about 1.06 events per case, slightly lower than the team leads and adjusters, not evidence of many events per case. The subcontracting claim that she returns within two events in “>80%” of cycles has no denominator. Those rows are already defined as returns, while 126 and 98 return patterns cannot be reconciled with only about 55 events above one per case. That inconsistency is never flagged. Bot_Rules’ 0.31 flag rate is also treated as if 4,750 events were exception routings and “the other ~3,000 go elsewhere.”

The handover versus working-together reconciliation is the weakest process-mining step. A handover already implies co-presence in that case, so a large edge and a low co-case rate are not explained by short event duration. The 27% Alice_TeamLead–Carla_Adjuster rate is also incompatible with their case counts if the denominator is the full log, and that definitional problem is missed. Hypothesis 3 then says brokers should have high working-together scores, while the executive summary says the low score confirms that she is a broker.

Hypothesis 6 says missed quality checks made cases move faster. The absence evidence says the opposite: the 90th percentile for flagged cases rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days. The informal-lead hypothesis still contains the draft correction that her caseload is “36–40% lower” than the team leads, then “high depth,” after 905 is in fact higher than 665 and 620 and her touch depth is shallow.

The recommendations are directionally cautious and do not remove her without a replacement, but the targets do not match the actions: two shadow days a week is paired with a 15–20% volume drop, and specialist waiting is described as “currently ~3.5 min,” which is the Bot_Rules-to-Mira elapsed time, not the 42- and 51-minute specialist edges. Section 6 is roughly 270 words against a 120–180 limit and is labeled 172. It also collapses the brief to one label, “essential coordinator,” and dismisses the queue-cleaner account the body had left open.
