The answer meets the required structure and reaches the right decision: conditional transfer, no blanket copy, originals as a hard gate, the nine-hour median treated as confounded, and a pilot that separates “overlap was never delivered” from “overlap did not move cycle time.” Those are the core judgments. It is not nearly flawless. Several internal inconsistencies and one process-structure error are large enough that the quantitative and model sections cannot be trusted as specified.

The eligibility arithmetic contradicts itself. E1 is defined as no-originals and not public-sector/high-value, then repeatedly sized at about 38 percent. Thirty-eight percent is the no-originals share only. With a 40 percent legal/high-value share, the joint stratum is smaller unless those flags are mutually exclusive, which is never shown. Section 5 then uses “~60 percent” once for the originals group and again for the non-legal group. That makes eligible share unusable.

The supplement loop is wrong in both the textual partial order and the code. Rule 7 says a supplement can force document checking and assessment to be repeated. The model loops Legal Review with Request Supplement → Document Check and returns to legal review, skipping reassessment. Capping loop iterations is also proposed without showing that a cap is legal. The code is otherwise aligned with the requested constructors: Site A trees, both Petri conversions, a real strict partial order, Assessment joined to both checks, an optional legal XOR, soundness checks, and an honest list of what an untimed net cannot encode (data guards, capacity, locks, durations).

Scenario ranges are not derived from the critical path. Site B’s median originals delay is 18 hours against a 20-hour pre-assessment wait and a 3.4-hour identity service. For the 62 percent, identity parallel to receipt can save on the order of identity’s own service, not most of the wait. Site A’s six-hour wait drop and nine-hour cycle drop are already confounded by mix, two added reviewers, the API, and huddles. Scenario S1’s −5 to −8 hours therefore sits on top of the confounded Site A headline rather than below it. The mean-improves / p90-worsens case is present, but the hour ranges are stipulated. The directly-follows counts are barely used, even though Originals Receipt → Document Check is 2,108 / 3,400 = 62 percent and Assessment → Legal Review is 1,360 / 3,400 = 40 percent.

Smaller causal errors accumulate. Document service time falling from 7.2 to 6.5 hours is attributed to two extra reviewers; added headcount mainly cuts queue time, not touched service time. Completed volume rose about 8 percent while verification FTE rose about 11 percent, so throughput per FTE did not improve, yet volume is still read as supportive. In the graph, `legal_capacity → rework` conflates pool size with the existence of supplement loops, and identity capacity is omitted from `true_overlap` while document capacity is included. Current Site B rules already allow identity and originals receipt to start after registration; the 6 percent overlap is largely the case lock and back-entry, so the “partial parallel” arm is partly an implementation fix, not a new partial order.

The pilot’s fidelity split is the strongest design idea, but team-week blocks do not separate four site-wide specialist pools, the “~6 hour” power target imports Site A’s confounded wait drop, and a 3–4 month window cannot observe a 90-day appeal for later entrants.
