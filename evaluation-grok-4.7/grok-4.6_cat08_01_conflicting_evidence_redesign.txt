7.4

The diagnosis, three redesigns, comparison table, failure analysis, and experiment plan are complete, segment-specific, and tied to the frequency DFG, performance DFG, fitness gap (0.91 vs 68% fitting traces), rework rates, segment outcomes, and resource limits. A, B, and C are genuinely different (who may enter the fast path, how dispatch is controlled, and whether high-value work is repaired under a different operating model). Sacrifices, the 82% utilization cliff, the 15–35% manual-inflow risk, the idle inspectors, and the 6–18h gate are handled without a fake ROI.

The core cost claim is internally inconsistent. The text correctly says fast approval remains about 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits, then says that path “is dominated on fully loaded cost.” A shrinking advantage is not cost dominance; the table later states the 9% figure correctly, which makes the diagnosis sentence a real contradiction on the decision the sponsor has to make.

Conformance mapping is wrong in the same section. “Fast approval before evidence completeness” is tagged as R2, but R2 constrains dispatch and already exempts Premium low-risk accessories. “Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices” is tagged as R1, but R1 is a pre-fast-approval rule for claims above EUR 400 and allows manual review or fraud review. “Reopen after close” is only loosely R3. The 120 reopen-to-escalation edges, which can reach denial or dispatch without manual eligibility, are the clearer R3 exposure and are not used.

Two process-mining readings are also too strong. Chaining Fast approval 2300 into Dispatch close 2320 treats edge counts as a conserved funnel; known dispatch outflows are 2580 and escalation also enters dispatch. The 3.3% repair-path reopen rate is a selected full sequence, while the high-value reviewed/repair rates given in the prompt are 5% and 6%; Redesign C and its table cell use 3.3% as the high-value benchmark anyway. The 22% vs 5% gap is treated as what rerouting “should” deliver, with selection bias postponed to the pilot rather than stated as a limit on the mechanism. “120 cases” and “390 abandonments are friction” (70 of those follow customer approval) add smaller edge-versus-case overclaims.

The experiment sequence is the strongest part: one causal question at a time, shadow scoring before enforcement, lagged complaint tails, and explicit downstream-shift checks. Those strengths do not erase the cost contradiction or the mislabeled controls.
