The analysis is strong: mechanisms are separated, recovery is not treated as a steady high-throughput regime, Little’s Law is refused where the aggregates cannot support it, and most regime arithmetic checks out (totals 2519/2409, baseline 701/697, high-mix 798/704, \(I_{\mathrm{med}}\) 17530/29880 and 27951/29880, hourly partitions, TV distance ≈ 0.30). Assumptions, hypotheses, and verdicts are labeled, and the Python block covers the required summaries, dispersion, a non-definitive change-point rule, restricted \(L/\lambda\), and PM4Py-as-comment.

It is not nearly flawless because a few quantitative statements contradict the answer’s own definitions.

The net-drift sign is inconsistent on the main pooling contrast. Drift is defined as arrivals − completions, and the high-mix and intervention pieces are +13.4 and −6.0. Their blend is \((7 \times 13.4 - 3 \times 6)/10 = +7.6\) (1143 − 1067)/10. Section 3’s table and section 4 both call that blend **−7.6/day**. The parenthetical “still short” and the “loses about 8 cases a day” prose match +7.6, so the operating story survives, but the headline comparison the task asks for is self-contradictory. A reader who copies the table gets the wrong direction.

Section 6 says the baseline-weekend net is −1 on 167 arrivals. Those four days are 167 arrivals and 166 completions, so the net is **+1**, the same sign convention as the weekday “net +4” row.

The all-days completion range is written “38–129, or 58 if the outage is kept.” 38 and 129 are the true endpoints; 58 lies inside that range. The clause does not describe a real alternative bound.

Lag-1 Pearson autocorrelation of the 28 daily arrivals is about **0.41** under the overlapping-sample correlation the code uses (`Series.autocorr`), not “about 0.39.” WIP lag-1 ≈ 0.61 is fairly stated as about 0.60. The baseline-weekday lag-1 near −0.3 is also computed on a filtered series that treats Friday 2026-04-10 and Monday 2026-04-13 as adjacent, so it is not a clean day-to-day dependence.

Smaller nits: pooled p90 is left as “the 60s–70s” (the mean of the daily p90s is 67) while nearby quantities are given to one decimal; \(c=6\) is used in the baseline wait check when mean weekday FTE is 5.93. None of these overturn the segmentation.

The regime story, the refusal to read 129 completions or pooled \(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.75\) as steady capacity, and the within-regime underdispersion versus pooled IDC ≈ 10 are the right conclusions. The grade is held down by repeated sign and bound slips in the statistics that are supposed to carry those conclusions.
