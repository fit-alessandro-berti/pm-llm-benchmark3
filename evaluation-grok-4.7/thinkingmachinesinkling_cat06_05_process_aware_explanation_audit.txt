7.5

The audit correctly separates critical future-trace leakage from repairable lineage defects and from signals that are actually known at `t0`. It keeps queue length, registered complexity, and prefix age; treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as a normatively valid but post-`t0` pattern; and treats `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as an impossible or unordered artifact, including the missing sequence index. Region, channel, and calendar week are kept as proxies rather than causes, and Case Z is properly invalidated. The five required sections, the validation suite, and the remediation plan are all present and largely precise.

The revised Case Y explanation violates the availability rule. `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` is produced by `claim_created_at <= extraction_date`, so the value 3 is not known to be a pre-`t0` count, yet it is listed as a credible driver and narrated as fact. `initial_reserve = €25,000` has no stated timestamp and is likewise promoted to a verified credible driver. Those two items belong only in the repair list until recomputed.

Other gaps keep it short of a near-flawless audit. `total_case_duration_hours` is removed as post-closure leakage, but not identified as near-label leakage for a 10-day time target. The claimed prefix-alignment fitness of 1.0 is wrong for ordinary alignment, which penalizes a mandatory suffix that has not happened yet; only a prefix-aware conformance check would do that. The normative partial order is incomplete (re-request loops, parallel fraud, withdrawal) and its ordering operators are missing. Directions for the surviving Case X/Y drivers are taken from a model trained with leakage, without stating that those signs are provisional until refit. The ±5-minute queue join and `claimant_channel` are treated as clean despite an unproven lineage.
