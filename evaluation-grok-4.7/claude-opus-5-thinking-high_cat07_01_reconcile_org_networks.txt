6.5

The write-up meets the required structure and stays multi-hypothesis: it refuses to treat centrality as good or bad, keeps bots and vendors in view, separates allocative from adjudicative discretion, and will not remove the role unless routing rules, a pull queue, a deputy pool, and escalation SLAs replace it. The score is pulled down because the quantitative spine used to reconcile the networks does not hold.

The working-together reconciliation is invalid on the denominator it assumes. Co-case rates are treated as shares of all claims, so Mira’s 8/7/6/5/4 are divided by her 22% coverage to get about 36/32/27/23/18, then compared with Alice–Carla at 27%. Under that same denominator Alice is in only 665/4100 ≈ 16% of claims, so Alice–Carla at 27% is impossible, as are Bruno–Diego 26%, Alice–Elise 24%, Carla–Elise 20%, and Diego–Farid 19%. The cap stated for Mira (no pair can exceed her own coverage) applies to every resource, and the largest edges in the same table break it. The rescaled table, the claim that the contradiction is only apparent, the “mathematically guaranteed” one-touch result, the later prediction that conditional weight rises to team-lead levels, and the executive summary’s “in fact tightly connected” all rest on that unchecked reading.

The subcontracting bound is also wrong. Of the 286 patterns, the 62 `Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules` rows have Mira as B and need one Mira event; the text later says this and still counts them in “every such pattern requires at least two Mira events.” The other 224 patterns do not imply ≥572 Mira events or ≥286 multi-event cases. Those bounds assume exclusive, one-pattern-per-case consumption. What does hold is the handover check: listed out-edges 1,640 and listed in-edges 1,580 both exceed 960 events, so those figures cannot be immediate single-count successions.

“Only ~4% of cases reach a specialist at all” misreads the Omar–Priya co-case cell. Specialist coverage is the union of 316 and 251 cases: at least 7.7% of 4,100 claims, and higher after overlap is taken out of the sum. That figure is then used to narrow H4.

Smaller misses: the absence contrast is an unchanged median for ordinary low-value claims, called “no effect on the mean”; “direct evidence” is attached to role clustering that was not run; a beta=0 recomputation is predicted to cut edge counts by “roughly half to two-thirds” with rank preserved, with no derivation; “sole predecessor” of the specialists is stronger than a top-edge list; one touch does not mathematically guarantee top handover centrality. The executive summary (153 words) also collapses the body to “the organization’s exception router” and says “almost every hard case,” while the counts given are 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints.

The surviving substance is real: handover as succession rather than authority, fragile betweenness on a 15-node hub, Bot_Rules and the vendors as the larger aggregate exposures, subcontracting as a possible bookkeeping signature, one uncontrolled absence window, and a PM4Py plan covering parameter sensitivity, conditional co-occurrence, n-sensitivity, role discovery, exception/variant/absence filters, and service versus waiting time. That does not repair the reconciliation the question asks for.
