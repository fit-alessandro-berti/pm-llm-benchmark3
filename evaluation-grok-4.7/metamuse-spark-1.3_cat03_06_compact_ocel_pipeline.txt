6.5

The in-memory OCEL is largely coherent: 17 timezone-aware events, 12 objects, E2O rows that copy activity and timestamp, the required O2O links, structural validation, and the requested PM4Py calls are all present and referentially consistent. Order trace membership (14 events for O100, 10 for O200), the P1 cross-order bridge, payment double-counting (250 counted as 500), and the weakness of Payment flattening are right.

The expected-analysis sections are not reliable. D1 is confirmed at 13:00 on 6 April, after D2 is dispatched at 10:15; the claim that D1 confirmation precedes D2 dispatch by about 20 hours is false. The long gap is D2’s own dispatch-to-confirm interval. Flattened DFGs use activity labels, so O100’s two packs, two dispatches, two confirms, and two adds are self-loops, not distinct P1/P2 branches. The same collapse means an object-centric Petri net has one Record Payment transition, not separate M1 and M2 transitions. The OC-DFG description assigns Pack Package to Delivery, which this relation set never does. The ET–OT discussion treats Pack P1 and Dispatch D1 as event types; at type level E7 and E8 are the same Pack Package–Package/Item/Order pattern, so that graph cannot show P1’s special role. “P2 carries only O100” drops I2 and P2. Calling M2 `completes_settlement` because 100+150 “settles V1” invents an invoice total the narrative does not give.

Section 2 also pastes the assignment rubric before the code. `pd.Timestamp(..., tz="UTC")` on strings that already contain `+00:00` is a portability hazard, and the flattening print dereferences `.columns` before the EventLog fallback, so that diagnostic can crash. Qualifier `fulfills` is reused for both dispatch and confirmation. The construction is usable; the interpretation of what discovery would show is not.
