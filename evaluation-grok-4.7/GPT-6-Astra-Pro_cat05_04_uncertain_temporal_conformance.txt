8.2

The interval arithmetic, timezone conversions, DST-fold filtering, and SLA and profile classifications are almost entirely correct. Independent reconstruction matches the source windows (HUB ±1.5 s, OPS-BER [−120, +149.999] s, SENSOR ±10 s, VENDOR-NY [−180, +239.999] s, LEGACY-BER [−60, +119.999] s, MOBILE-LON [−330, +330] s before entry delay), the June offsets, and the 2025-10-26 Berlin folds. The certain SLA violations are right: T-402/S1 at a minimum of 958.5 s, T-408/S2 at 3297–3303 s, and T-408/S3 at 138297–138303 s. The nine possible SLA breaches, the single surviving T-405 fold triple (early alert, late start, late containment), the T-404 and T-407 joint projections, the non-additivity of T-404/T-406, and the tolerance and zeta transitions all check out. Material assumptions are stated and recomputed. Sections 6 and 7 correctly separate what would make a result certain from what would only support enforcement.

The required program does not call PM4Py as that library actually works. The cited `conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, parameters=None)` reads `zeta` from the `parameters` dictionary and returns a per-trace list. The script always supplies `zeta` as a keyword, so a normal install raises `TypeError` before any comparison. `return_diagnostics_dataframe` is not part of that signature. The sentence claiming the call follows the documented API is inaccurate. The prose midpoint contrasts are numerically right (T-404/S2 at 2490 s, T-406 at 690 s, T-407 at 510 s, T-408 on the 3300 s boundary, T-409 near 270 s), and the answer does say the PM4Py path was not run.

Smaller defects: T-401/S3 is 518.5 s past 24 hours, which is 8 minutes 38.5 seconds, not “approximately 24 hours 9 minutes.” The SENSOR note “Ten-second rounding” names only the rounding step of a ±5 s plus ±5 s window. The fourteen missing-closure S3 and C→X results are classified in prose and left out of the required row set. The `sandbox:/mnt/data/...` link does not point at a supplied file. Minute-precision strings parsed with `datetime.fromisoformat` succeed on Python 3.11 and later only.

Under the stated baseline, the classifications can be relied on. The script’s PM4Py comparison cannot, until `zeta` is passed inside `parameters`.
