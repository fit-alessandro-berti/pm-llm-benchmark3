The answer has the right overall posture—refusing a single “automation cuts cycle time by X%” claim, separating availability from use, and recommending an embedded evaluation—but it is not quantitatively or logically reliable enough to score higher.

Adopter pre/post levels for Alpha, Beta, and Gamma are mostly right, and the required sections, estimands, mechanisms, and threat list are present. Those strengths are undercut by concrete failures. Pre-adoption `automated_fast_path_pct` is 4–6%, not zero. Never-treated contrasts are miscalculated (Delta Jan–Mar median is about 34.5, not 32.7, so the change is about +6 h, not +7.7 h; Epsilon Apr–Aug median is about 44.7, not 46.1; several p90 contrasts are also off). The Alpha p90 sensitivity figure (101.4 h, +2.9 h) does not match Apr/Jul/Aug (about 102.7 h, +4.2 h).

The larger problem is that section 7 ignores the control comparison in section 5. Never-treated p90 and median rose by as much as or more than the adopters’ raw changes, so a differenced reading does not support “p90 likely worsened by +5 to +12 h” or an escalation effect of +2 to +3 pp. Much of the escalation step is the March fraud-desk jump, visible in every branch before adoption. Gamma’s median drop is also timed with the July–August easing in Delta and Epsilon, so the “conditionally causal” benefit is not earned.

Required prose is short (the strategy justification is ~90 words versus 150–250; the rollout section is ~210 versus 250–400). The stated design is a cohort event study, but the code fits static two-way fixed effects, never uses relative time, mis-codes month lags via `days//30`, does not print the five outcome trends, and does not warn about aggregated medians. Adjusting for post-treatment complexity and arrivals is treated as harmless. Sticky repeated rework and escalation values, and transportability from an easier Delta pilot to Epsilon, are missed.
