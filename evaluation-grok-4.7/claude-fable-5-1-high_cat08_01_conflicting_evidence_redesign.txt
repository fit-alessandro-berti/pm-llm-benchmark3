7.4

The diagnosis is unusually strong process-mining work: it uses the frequency and performance DFGs, fitness versus 68% fitting traces, low precision, R1–R3, alignment deviations, rework rates, segment outcomes, and the capacity knee, and it correctly treats the fast-path contradiction as a segment mix rather than a blanket speed-versus-quality rule. The three redesigns are genuinely different (risk routing, rework-engine fixes, split control regimes), each states a sacrifice, and the experiment section watches abandonment, lagged complaints, cost-to-serve, and queue migration.

Those merits do not make the answer nearly flawless. Several claims are wrong or internally inconsistent.

The 24-hour service-credit cliff is stated only for Premium low-risk accessories. Redesign B, its failure mode, and the comparison table apply that cliff to Premium high-value devices, whose stated exposure is R1/R2 and an EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost. The pre-dispatch gate is limited to high-value cases, so the credit story is attached to the wrong segment and the wrong control.

“R1 violations disappear by construction” is not earned. R1 is an EUR 400 rule. The written tiers key off device class; mid-risk devices never appear; a low-risk accessory above EUR 400 can still be fast-approved. Declared value is named as an input and then not used as a hard gate.

Redesign C’s accessory stream is specified as instant approval and ship with evidence only on audit or repeat claims. That breaches R2 for Standard accessories. The table later adds an intake completeness step the flow does not contain.

The current process is marked “Dominated? Yes” even though every redesign is worse on at least one of cycle time, resource pressure, or complexity, and the text later says dominance cannot be shown. That cell violates the problem’s own constraint.

“56% of manual reviews end in an evidence request” treats 780/1,400 outgoing arcs as the review population, then the same section shows 2,600 inbound versus 1,400 outbound. The 56% figure is reused in Redesign A’s failure mode after the data are declared unreliable. The imbalance account is also incomplete: right-censoring can explain missing ends and missing manual outflows, but Dispatch replacement has more outgoing arcs (2,580) than incoming (2,365), which censoring does not produce.

Smaller overclaims accumulate. Summing four aggregate edge means into “~10h end to end” is not a variant cycle time. “Most of the 260” dispatch complaints and a cost advantage “well beyond 9%” are not supported. “120 cases decided without Manual review” reads a directly-follows bundle as proof those traces never touched manual review. The 12.5% repair-rework rate is once stated for high-value devices rather than repaired high-value devices. Stage 1 pilots routing and the part gate together, so a reopen drop cannot be attributed to either mechanism.

The artifact use and redesign differentiation are well above a generic Lean list. The repeated factual mis-location, the compliance overclaims, and the dominance label are large enough that the answer is not in the top band.
