The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape and names the right trouble spots (ev08/PK-8, ev10’s `N-421?`, ev11’s `N-42*`, INV-44 versus ORD-B, PAY-2 versus INV-44). It also avoids inventing events or objects and does not treat the 230 total as proof. Those merits do not offset basic OCEL mistakes, an internally inconsistent repair, and process-mining claims that would mislead.

The relation table classifies event–object links as O2O. ev10–DEL-Y, ev11–DEL-X/DEL-Y, ev11–ORD-A, ev12–ORD-B, and ev14–INV-44 are E2O. The JSON then stores ev10 and ev11 in `o2o_additions` with event ids in `ocel:oid`. That would fail against the object table. The one real E2O addition is ev08–PK-8. The structural twins a coherent repair needs are missing: O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` (the observed qualifier is `packs`, not a new `contains`) and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, parallel to the observed `DEL-X carries PK-7`.

Already-present facts are proposed again as repairs: ev11–ORD-A, `PAY-1 settles INV-44`, and PK-8 with IT-A2. PK-8–IT-A2 is already observed; only IT-B1 is open. Bundle A, the table, and the JSON disagree: the prose links ev11 to both deliveries, the table marks that provisional, and the JSON adds only DEL-X, while the 0.95 ev10–DEL-Y link is filed under the wrong relation kind. Bundle C “unapplies” the observed `PAY-1 settles INV-44` link and treats PAY-2 as settling an order. That is not a competing reading of missing data.

The payment logic in the final table and JSON is wrong. “130 matches ORD-A but the invoice is 230” is not evidence against `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. PAY-1 is already 100 against that invoice, and 100+130=230 is the partial-payment pattern domain rule 7 allows. Absence of an observed edge is why a candidate exists, not evidence against it. Confidence 0.95 on `N-421?` is too high: the question mark, identical NORTH routes, and the same 10:00 timestamp as ev09 leave DEL-X versus DEL-Y underdetermined. Rule 5 also allows Confirm Delivery to name only the order. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries at 0.60, and calling that conservative, skips that alternative. ORD-A is split (IT-A1 in PK-7/DEL-X, IT-A2 in PK-8), while ORD-B rides only with IT-A2. That split is the main reason ev11 is ambiguous, and it is never used.

The lifecycle audit does not cover every object type, does not separate independent E2O support from O2O support, and states a generic warning about circularity instead of checking whether an O2O edge was inferred from an assumed E2O edge and then reused as confirmation. “Over-billing” misstates an exact 130+100=230 match. Calling ORD-B delivered in the unrepaired log is false.

Downstream effects confuse the object-type graph with a lifecycle graph. Record Payment already relates to Payment through PAY-2; the missing ET–OT edge is Invoice. Order flattening only keeps events related to the order, so Pack and Dispatch do not appear on ORD-A unless those E2O links are added. The three frequency semantics (events, unique objects, total object relations) and concrete duplicated fragments are not compared. Saying Bundle B reduces DFG noise is unsupported.
