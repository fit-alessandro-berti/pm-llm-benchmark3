6.1

The write-up correctly separates April’s schema break, June’s mix shift, the later parts-delay regime, and October’s immature labels, and it covers all six required sections. It is still well short of a strict pass on mechanism, label timing, remediation, and the process-mining sketch.

The process-drift row is the largest analytical error. It says variant topology stayed structurally unchanged and limits process drift to slower `Order Part -> Part Received` times from July onward. The same answer documents the contrary: V2 rises from about 35% to 52% in June, and V3 rises from 22% to 35% in August before Partner Z exists. June is then labeled pure covariate drift because within-variant breach rates and AUROC 0.79 look stable. That does not establish that \(P(Y \mid X)\) was unchanged. The score is issued immediately after diagnosis, while Dispatch, Remote Fix, and Order Part occur after that point, and the answer never establishes that the route is in the feature vector. Mean predicted risk also moves from about 0.19 to 0.20 while the observed breach rate falls to 0.15. A visible shift toward a safer route would pull predictions down. The claim that features “shifted toward remote execution indicators” is unsupported and conflicts with that gap. July is grouped into the same covariate row on “stable” within-variant rates, but July’s matured V3 breach rate is already 0.46.

Label maturity is handled well for October’s headline 0.16 rate and badly elsewhere. August is 93% mature, and the matured V3 rate of 0.64 is already a real outcome shift, yet section 4 bins August with September as unsafe immature data. That contradicts the later training line “Jan–Jul + mature Aug/Sep.” September’s Partner Z rate of 0.45 versus 0.27 is repeatedly treated as a true base-rate gap even though the prompt states that Partner Z labels mature more slowly. October is also placed under concept drift although variant outcomes are explicitly too immature to estimate, and the narrative says long cases “had not yet breached.” The stated failure is an outcome-API backlog on long paths, which is label missingness, not evidence that the breach event itself has not occurred. September is written as the population-driven collapse, but AUROC is already 0.68 in August with no Partner Z; September only moves it to 0.66 while V3’s matured breach rate eases from 0.64 to 0.61.

The remediation can act on the wrong regime. A flat +0.25 V3 offset is unjustified and can exceed 1. Variant-stratified recalibration fit on June–July learns June’s over-prediction (predicted 0.20 versus observed 0.15) and July’s still-small V3 move, then applies the wrong direction to the August regime, where V3’s matured breach rate is 0.64 against a mean prediction of 0.19. Variant-specific heads are recommended without a check that the variant is known at score time. Maturity gates conflict across the answer: 85%, 90%, and 95%. Section 6 correctly warns against training on October and calibrating April’s broken scores, but it overstates that retraining on mature post-July labels without a supplier feature “fails completely.” A stable new \(P(Y \mid X)\) can be learned from those labels; what fails is tracking the next supplier move. That claim also fights section 4’s own V3 offset.

The tests contain the right contrasts, then weaken them. Test 5 fits the reference model on January–May, including corrupted April, and scores June–September without restricting to mature labels. Test 6 reweights the 0.45 versus 0.27 gap without aligning maturity. The maturity curve counts cases that have not closed by day \(t\) as non-breaches, which recreates the censoring under study. Thresholds such as day-30 breach about 0.12, AUROC below 0.60, residual above +0.25, and \(r > 0.85\) are presented as expectations without being derived from the tables.

The monitoring design omits intervention and deployment monitors for the April migration, May patch, June policy, supplier failure, and Partner Z go-live. Suppressing all AUROC/ECE alerts below 85% maturity would mute September’s 82% cohort, including real process and population signals that do not depend on the outcome label. Every drift type is marked high confidence despite those overlaps.

The code sketch does not implement the requested audit. `format_dataframe` is called with `case_id_key`, which is not the public parameter (`case_id`); after a real call the original `case_id` / `activity` / `timestamp` names used later are gone. Only a case-start cohort is built. There is no event-calendar cohort, no note on how those cohorts differ, and no `filter_time_range`, `discover_dfg`, or `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. `discover_performance_dfg` values are treated as dicts with `'median'`; the default edge value is an aggregated duration, normally the mean in seconds. Duplicate dropping is present, but it erases the duplicate rate instead of reporting it, and it cannot show the post-20-April, new-source-only rule.
