**7.3**

The design covers all eight required sections and respects the hard constraints: non-stationary demand, calendars, service versus waiting, latent objections, cross-case dependence, no optimality claim, and a PM4Py path that avoids `pm4py.read_csv`. It is not nearly flawless.

The main quantitative error is arrival scaling. About 31,200 cases in 18 months is roughly 57 per day on average, and 1.7× is relative to January–February, while 2.2× is Monday versus Wednesday. The skeleton sets `base_cases_per_day = 58` and then multiplies by month factors of 1.0/1.7 and weekday factors of 2.2/1.0/0.1. Those indices are not normalized and are not tied to the stated baselines, so peak volume is overstated by roughly a third before weekday effects. Section 2’s intensity formula never says to normalize \(s_{\text{month}}\), \(s_{\text{weekday}}\), or \(s_{\text{hour}}\), or to define \(\lambda_0\) as the January–February Wednesday rate. That undermines every later comparison.

Other issues: `discover_performance_dfg(..., variant="performance")` does not match the usual PM4Py signature; `process_tree.utils.parse` and `process_tree.parser.parse` are the wrong modules (`utils.generic.parse` / `pm4py.parse_process_tree`). Try/except hides this rather than calling a correct API. `get_variants` is treated as variant→count, but several PM4Py versions return trace lists. D2 needs “no prior violations,” which is not in the log and is missing from the missing-data assumptions. Right-censoring of open cases is ignored. Weekend rates and Tuesday–Thursday = Wednesday are invented in code and not stated as assumptions. SLA medians (for example 15 days for simple cases) sit above the observed clean-path median of 12 days, so the targets can accept non-improvements. Stress scenarios and hour-of-day effects stay in prose and are not represented in the runnable configuration.
