7.5

The answer is complete, well calibrated, and faithful to nearly every reported figure. It keeps raw approval gaps distinct from discrimination, treats support steps as burden rather than as something to delete, and refuses to treat risk and completeness as neutral. The residual gaps, staffing concentration, appeal ambiguity, and bad-control problem are handled with real care. It is not nearly flawless.

The main analytical error is the share-weighted illustration in section 3. “Needs language support” is 57% and accommodation requests are 62%, but the language and accommodation variants are only 24% (V4) and 31% (V5). Multiplying the conditional median waits of 3.8 and 4.7 days by the need rates produces “about two days” and “about three days,” then sets those products beside the adjusted gaps of +2.6 and +4.1. That weight is not the share of cases that traverse the step. Medians are not additive contributions to an adjusted mean difference, and the adjusted model already partials out documentation, channel, and region. The caveats do not keep the paragraph from presenting specialist-queue time as the plausible main component of the residual delay.

That error is tied to a missed contradiction. In V4, Language Support Contact is followed by Completeness and Request Missing Documents, not by Eligibility Assessment. The 3.8-day interval is therefore not the same kind of direct edge as Accommodation Review → Eligibility in V5. Treating it as a pure bilingual queue can fold document-request time into the language wait and double-count it. “Those shares track need” also smooths over variant prevalence running at roughly half of stated need, which is itself relevant to under-provision versus abbreviated path summaries.

Section 1 is too firm that Group B’s +8-point supervisor residual “is not the language step” because V4’s summary omits supervisor review. That variant is not every language case, and the later sections correctly leave this more open. “Extra hearings” is not in the log. The claim that the 52-caseworker spread is what keeps Group A fast asserts a cause the tables do not isolate.

The PM4Py plan names the right analyses, but several calls would not run. `filter_case_performance` is missing required `max_performance` and is given `retain`, which that function does not take. `filter_paths_performance` is also given `retain`. `applicant_group` and `region` are never mapped to case attributes, yet the filters and `trace.attributes` reads assume case level. `get_all_case_durations` is computed and then unused, despite the section’s purpose being shares and durations. The directly-follows filter on Language Support Contact → Eligibility Assessment would miss V4 if that path summary is literal.

The bottom line itself is scoped and does not overclaim unjustified denials. The weaker part is that its confidence about support-step delay partly rests on the inflated queue arithmetic above.
