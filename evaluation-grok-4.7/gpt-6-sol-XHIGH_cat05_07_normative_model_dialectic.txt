8.1

The brief is unusually strong: both sides are argued at real strength, concessions are specific, hidden non-flow violations (SUP-712, SUP-715) are not mistaken for control-flow fitness, and PD-3, FD-7, CRM linkage, duplicates, and label drift are kept distinct. The judgment is case-specific and the layered revision is the right architecture. It is not nearly flawless.

The burden of proof is applied unevenly. Section 1 correctly treats exception authority as something the claimant must establish. SUP-702 then becomes NONCOMPLIANT because PD-3’s waiver power and master-data validation are unproved, even though the dated instrument, eligibility, sanctions-before-activation, and next-business-day review are present. SUP-704 becomes JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION while issuer delegation and cumulative spend against the EUR 20,000 cap are still open, with reversal defined as later proof of no authority. Those are different burdens. The directive-versus-minutes distinction is real, but it does not justify assuming FD-7 competence while treating an actual steering decision as a non-waiver. Medium confidence does not remove the inconsistency.

SUP-706 is over-attributed. The case has no date. N4 supports distrusting a mutable, late text link; it does not establish that this case is a post-1 July CRM loss. The indeterminate classification can stand on linkage weakness alone, but the matrix states migration as the overlay.

The final section slightly restates strawmen. Both briefs already reject “every M0 miss is misconduct” and “useful practice is authority.” Rejecting those implications is fair only as a rejection of each brief’s strongest lean, not of claims those briefs still make.

The POWL is mostly faithful and the loop direction is right: `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)` is zero or more visible requests, sanctions and quality are truly concurrent on the standard route, notification sits before closure on every branch, and eligibility is correctly left outside the XOR. Two limitations are understated. Pilot `Quality_Review` is unconstrained against sanctions, validation, auto-approval, and notification, which is broader than “before or after activation.” Emergency edges force both retrospective quality and manual approval after activation, so a mixed order fits neither the emergency nor the standard branch, and neither exceptional route can reject or unwind if the later review fails. Withdrawal is described in governance prose and then omitted from the control flow. The dash-versus-“None” legend is a small traceability slip.

No classification is reckless, dates and aggregates check out, and SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-711, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are handled with the right dominant reasons. The remaining gaps are authority-burden inconsistency, one unsupported migration attribution, and a candidate model that is stricter and less complete than its commentary admits.
