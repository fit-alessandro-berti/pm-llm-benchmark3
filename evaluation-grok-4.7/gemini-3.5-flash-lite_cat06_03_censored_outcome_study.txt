5.5

The primary estimand is directionally defensible: 90-day default only among accounts that actually activate, with rejects, withdrawals, and open cases left undefined rather than coded as non-defaults. The leakage inventory, label-lag buffer, and the reason a random split fails are also real strengths. The design then breaks on identification, accounting, and implementation.

The cohort table is not a partition of 48,000. “Clean labeled” 18,000 is neither the given 18,900 non-defaults nor a justified remainder, the status text says those rows contain both \(Y=0\) and \(Y=1\), and a separate 2,700 default row is added anyway. The listed rows sum to 47,100, while the total is still printed as 48,000. Transferred cases are simultaneously excluded, imputed, and treated as a competing event.

Prediction time is not one estimand. Section 1 uses the second Credit Review or, alternatively, the first three events; section 4 switches to \(k\in\{3,5,7\}\) and 24/72-hour windows. Those are different decisions. The first three activities only reach the first Credit Review. Padding traces that died before \(k\) invents a prefix and can encode the terminal outcome. “Sliding window” is the wrong object for a prefix.

Maturity and validation contradict each other. Activation on or before 13 October 2025 is a correct rendering of a 90+35-day buffer against 15 February 2026, but the test window is starts from 1 September through 31 December 2025, offered as coverage of the 15 October migration. Under that same buffer, almost no post-migration activation has a mature label, and recent starts are kept only if they activated unusually fast. The split is by start time and is never restricted to mature activated cases, so the reported AUC, PR-AUC, Brier, and ECE population is undefined. Training only before the policy change also means the fitted model never sees the policy under which it would be used.

Censoring is misclassified. Still-open applications do not have a post-activation survival clock. A fixed 35-day buffer does not “completely eliminate” delay bias when 35 days is only the common range. The unlinked 40% of transfers are not plausibly MAR, and imputing a default label for them invents an outcome the study says is undefined. Adding `is_partner_originated` and letting trees split on missingness does not test MNAR. The required testable / partially testable / untestable classification is absent, and Heckman or IPW is invoked without an exclusion restriction, positivity, or a statement that those corrections target a different estimand from \(P(Y\mid\text{activated},X)\).

Feature rules have the same as-of holes. Full-case variant IDs are not known at the cut. Out-of-fold resource default rates still leak future labels unless they are point-in-time. Service and waiting times computed on the uncut log use the next event after the prefix. Broadcast `default_90d` and possibly overwritten `risk_score` values on early events are not audited.

The skeleton misses required case attributes and does not implement its own rules. Terminal state, censoring indicator, and a survival or competing-risk handoff are absent. `activation_time` is computed from the timestamp series, so the activity test never matches and `is_mature` is uniformly false. `label_available_at` is parsed and ignored. Validation cases are unused. Prefixes are taken from entire training traces, while the leakage assertion only inspects `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` output, which would not contain `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, or `default_90d` even when those fields remain on the prefix rows. Discovery results are computed and discarded. Deduplication and the second-review cut from the prose are not in the code.
