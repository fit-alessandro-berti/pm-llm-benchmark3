6.2

The case-level bounds, sojourn accounting, and WIP integrals are largely careful and checkable, but the temporal reconstruction and Little’s Law section contain material factual and conceptual errors, and several summary statistics are wrong or overclaimed. Under a strict reading, those defects keep this well below a near-flawless analysis.

The missing-start intervals for P05, P07, P11, and P15 follow the right constraints: queue entry, predecessor completion on the same resource, own completion, and nonnegative service. Central case waits, services, and elapsed censored times for the fully timestamped cases match the log. Station sojourn and station WIP are correctly treated as invariant to start imputation. The case-minute totals 2085 (S1) and 1340 (S2) give mean WIP 5.79 and 3.72 and system WIP 9.51. Open cases are retained as elapsed service or elapsed wait, and busy time includes P15, P17, and P21 rather than completed cases only. The imputation arithmetic behind the utilization bands is internally consistent: S1 busy time 535/595/605 minutes and S2 515/570 minutes reproduce the stated 74.3–84.0% and 71.5–79.2% gross figures. End-of-window state is also right: S1 holds P21 in service and P22 waiting; S2 holds two in service and four waiting. PM4Py is restricted to the 11 fully observed two-activity cases and is explicitly labeled case-level, including the fact that its waiting time omits the external-arrival queue.

The WIP path, which the task specifically requires, is not reliable. S1 does not sit at exactly 8 for the whole 10:15–11:30 interval. It oscillates between 7 and 8; the time average on that 75-minute window is 7.6, not 8.0. S2 is not a flat 5 from 11:30 to 13:00 either: it moves through 4, 5, and 6, and the peak of 7 occurs only in 13:45–13:50. The subperiod Little’s Law test is therefore built on an invented plateau. Flow balance and the 6.47 prediction are not a valid check against “L = 8.”

The server form \(\bar{B}=\lambda_{\mathrm{dep}}\bar{S}\), then patched with open elapsed service, matches busy time by accounting identity. Calling that match 100% empirical confirmation overstates what was tested. The global numerical illustration uses a completed-case mean sojourn of 147.5 minutes; the 14 completed totals sum to 2050, so the mean is 146.4 minutes. The claimed S1 completed-wait median of 67.5 is also wrong: the central 20 waits have middle order statistics 70 and 75, so the median is 72.5. The further claim that eventual mean system sojourn would exceed 220–250 minutes is not derived. Elapsed time only forces a mean above about 156 minutes; the higher band is an unsupported extrapolation.

Other statements are stronger than the evidence. S1 service “range [20, 35]” is not the central scenario, whose maximum is 30, and it is also not the full bound, which reaches 0. S2’s completed-wait range is given as [5, 65] even though the same document allows P11 to wait 80 minutes. Capacity is called an exact ceiling of 3.0 versus about 4.25 cases per hour while the text alternates between a 38.2-minute mean and a 40-minute figure and ignores gap time and censoring. The 15-minute C2 outage is a real capacity loss of roughly 15 server-minutes, but it does not by itself explain a six-case S2 residue, and P08 was not strictly unable to start until 11:15: C2 was free at 10:50–10:55. Assumption A9 (work-conserving service) also sits poorly with I2’s idle interval from 13:45 to 14:00 while P22 is waiting; that conflict is noted later, but the assumption is not revised. “Only cases with flow time ≤ 230 minutes completed” misstates censoring: the horizon is case-specific, and 230 is merely the longest observed completion.

The required seven headings, explicit required cases, labeled assumptions, and three-way sensitivity split are present. The Python distinguishes MISSING, OPEN, and blank, and the static scenario logic agrees with the prose totals, but it was not executed here, so PM4Py column names and runtime behavior remain unverified. Rounding is discussed as ±5 minutes and then left outside the case table and the code scenarios.
