8.3

The final answer is a strong, mechanism-aware drift diagnosis: March is correctly treated as logging/semantic drift, later rework and queueing are not collapsed into that, and supplier, staffing, priority, seasonality, and their interactions are separated with real causal restraint. Format, word counts, required segments, and the monitoring design are met. Most baseline deltas, fitness-recovery fractions, DFG L1/TV shares, and the October-versus-July senior-edge contrast check out.

It is not nearly flawless. In the change table, “largest MoM step” is wrong for fast-variant share (−10 in May; the largest move is −12 in October) and, if read as largest absolute change, also for cases (−100 in December, not +90 in October), median (−6.7 vs +6.3), and p90 (−22 vs +20). The “Δ Nov vs base” column is then used for July/October DFG rates. The August–September row cites `Assess → Request Info` 29→20, but 29 is July’s rate; August is not in the DFG fragment. The May–June row states `Assess` service “4.8→~5.1 h,” which is not observed (only 4.8 then 5.4 in July–August). The median argument equates “71% non-rework” with “71% fast path”; fast-variant share is already 39% in July, so that step is invalid even though the broader queueing claim can still hold if rework cases are the longest ones. The within-supplier residual is called “~5 pp” when 24% − 17.5% is 6.5 pp.

The PM4Py plan is mostly faithful, but `conformance_temporal_profile` is passed `deviation_threshold` rather than `zeta`, `isinstance(..., object)` is always true, log-skeleton getters do not match the usual API (`get_always_after`, directly-follows, not `get_*_relations` / eventually-follows), and `stratified_report` claims to pool small cells but only flags them. Those defects sit inside an otherwise rigorous, reproducible design, so they lower the score without overturning the attribution.
