6.4

The base offered-load arithmetic is the strongest part of the answer and is largely correct: mixture midpoints (6.94 / 26.3 / 32.82 / 38.5 / 15.74), net staffing of 2490 minutes after breaks, block loads, and the 16:00–20:00 collapse all audit. Scenario totals (~3578, ~2834, ~3068, Chen at 2040/2190) are also about right, and the recommendation correctly refuses to pick a policy only because it looks best on base mean delay.

That does not carry the decision. Section 4’s delay and SLA ranges are not derived from the load model, and they contradict the code. With the code’s own burden, capacity factor, and wait formula, base hybrid load/capacity is about 1.4 and mean delay is on the order of 30 hours, with P1 attainment near 70%. Section 4 states 3–5 hours and 85–95% P1. Those cannot both be the result. The SLA gaps are also partly inserted through `p1_shift` / `p2_shift`, so the ranking is assumed rather than produced by dispatch, speed, or queues.

Other material gaps: strict specialization already contains overflow, so it is not a clean contrast with the hybrid; skill capacities double-count multi-skilled people while fairness is defined on skills rather than resources; utilization figures above 100% are offered load; complex ME/ES service can exceed the 120-minute P1 clock with zero queueing and that constraint is never checked; the U5 intake freeze is reduced to a flat 30 minutes. The event log assigns off-shift resources, starts work with no queue, labels sequential ME/ES branches as parallel, and never builds stage-level waiting time, so the required PM4Py calls cannot show the delays, handovers, or bottleneck migration the prose describes. Preemption and setup are omitted rather than specified as omitted mechanisms.
