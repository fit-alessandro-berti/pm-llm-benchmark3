The case labels are mostly defensible, but the write-up is not coherent, and the required checker does not implement the rules it describes.

TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-307, TX-309, and TX-311 are classified correctly, with TX-305 properly left indeterminate because the release amount is unknown and the later settlement figure is not an immutable release payload. TX-306 and TX-308 are reasonably treated as compliant on the ground that a sub-20% increase and a decrease do not invalidate an otherwise valid enhanced review. TX-310 is a legitimate indeterminate call given the post-release certificate and the interested witness. TX-311 correctly uses `event_index` and evaluates R1 on the release-time jurisdiction, which is DE.

TX-302 is the serious case-reasoning error. The enhanced review at 09:20 is after case creation and before release, and nothing after that review invalidates it, so R1 is already met. EX-77 is valid additional evidence, not the reason the case passes. Section 4 is therefore wrong that loss of the 09:05 signature would make TX-302 noncompliant.

Section 1 defines every attribute update as a material change. Section 3 then treats the TX-306 increase and the TX-308 decrease as not resetting R1. The pseudo-logic does not state R1 as review-after-invalidating-change or a valid exemption, so the formalization does not match the findings. R4’s preserve-on-small-increase case is also left implicit.

The code follows the requested pipeline only superficially. It forward-fills, but `latest_mat_chg` includes creates and any later update, and R1 passes only if the latest enhanced review is after that timestamp or a hardcoded “STRONG” exemption exists. That marks TX-306 and TX-308 noncompliant and TX-310 noncompliant, contradicting the narrative. The indeterminate condition (`missing exemption id` and `WEAK` evidence) cannot fire. The R4 ratio uses the second-to-last row’s amount, so it misses TX-309, and it is not used in classification. Scope, expiry, signature time, standard-packet approvals, and same-timestamp ordering are not actually evaluated. Evidence quality is a lookup, and there is no executed trace-order check.

Sections 6 and 7 are the strongest part: the six control-flow failure modes are real, and the logging recommendations match the stated gaps.
