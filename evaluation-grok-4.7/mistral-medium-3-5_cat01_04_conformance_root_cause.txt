4.8

The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape and correctly treats logging and provenance defects as the main drivers of the measured fitness collapse, with policy drift and a possible residual compliance problem left open. It is not reliable as an abductive or PM4Py analysis. Several central inferences are reversed, staged numbers are mis-attributed, two required prose lengths are missed, and the reproduction code omits required methods and would not run.

Evidence ledger. Twelve rows exist, but the supports/weakens columns often treat coexisting mechanisms as mutually exclusive. The August sign-off rule is scored as weakening “real behavioral process change”; that rule, and the 210 recorded sign-offs, is direct evidence that the prescribed and recorded process changed while the tree did not. Missing `Compliance Check` is scored as supporting an obsolete model; the July memo still requires the check, so those traces bear on bypass versus under-capture, not on model drift. The appeal-rate row puts a weakening into the supports column. Reliability 100 on the omitted amount attribute confuses a certain data gap with a certain causal conclusion. The 11-of-70 screenshot sample is generalized, and it is read later as if it showed real reversals. Unused discriminators include the 30 lingering `Classify` labels, `Queue Routed` on most but not all cases, sign-off on both the standard and enhanced paths, the rise in events missing a resource (1.2% to 6.4%), and stable cycle time and approval rate.

Ranking. Label change, technical insertion, and order distortion belong at the top as explanations of the June drop, but plausibilities of 95, 90, and 85 are not tied to explanatory share, and “contradicting evidence: none” ignores partial adoption and the large residual after each repair. The screenshot result is listed as contradicting the timestamp hypothesis; those 11 screenshots support it, because they indicate that `Decision` actually preceded `Notify`. Model obsolescence at 80 is not time-scoped: it cannot explain June–July fitness of 32%, only part of the August–September residual. The ranking never separates “cause of the conformance drop” from “remaining operational concern,” and the strongest real-process evidence is assigned to the model hypothesis instead.

Causal account. The adjacency list points `policy_change` at `normative_model`, even though the failure was that the tree was not updated, and it points `measured_fitness` at `business_outcomes`, reversing the dependence. `Queue Routed` is treated as a label change. The prose is about 150 words against a required 180–280, and it only argues that stable appeals do not rule out noncompliance. It does not argue the other required half: stable appeals also do not prove that the deviations are only data error.

Counterfactuals. The 58%, 81%, and 89% figures are cumulative staged results, but they are predicted for single repairs. Source ordering plus dropping `Queue Routed` is assigned 89% even though that figure also includes the label map. Extending the model is predicted to make new variants appear in the DFG; a model edit does not change the log. The invented “about 90%” August figure does not match the stated 66% after preprocessing or 86% after the candidate model. The enforcement row predicts that appeals may rise if hidden bypasses are corrected, which is not a coherent outcome prediction.

Diagnostic program. Four phases and the right column set are present, and phase 3 does mention precision and overgeneralization. The program still bundles label remap and technical-event removal, so it does not isolate the effects the alignment table already separates, and the PM4Py column names families rather than the artifacts each phase would produce.

Code. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` is not used. `pm4py.discover_dfg` is never called. Precision is never computed, so the required August–September fitness-and-precision comparison is absent. `convert_to_petri_net` is not unpacked into net and markings, and `alignments.apply` is called without them. Only a subset of the supplied variants is loaded. The normalizer deletes `Manual Override`, contradicting both the evidence and the earlier claim that it is a real user action; the reference preprocessing left that move visible. Sorting on `source_sequence_number` uses a field the synthetic log never creates. The candidate tree allows sign-off only after enhanced-review compliance check, so it cannot represent the 60 standard-review sign-offs. The printed “fitness” is a perfect-trace ratio, not alignment fitness. The EUR 50,000 caveat is present and is one of the better parts of the block.

Conclusion and governance. Section 7 is about 235 words against a required 250–400. The “60–70% of the deviation” claim is inconsistent with the same paragraph’s 32% to 89% recovery. The 86% figure is described as the result of preprocessing; 86% is the candidate-model result, and preprocessing alone is 66%. The conclusion never states that the optional-sign-off model can buy fitness by overgeneralizing. Actions are otherwise aimed at the right owners, with the sequence-number field loosely called “source timestamps.” The six governance rules do cover versioning, semantic contracts, technical events, timestamp provenance, data-aware constraints, and post-release validation.

The headline is directionally right and employees are not simply blamed for log moves. The discriminating evidence, quantities, causal graph, and executable comparison required to sustain that headline are not.
