The answer is complete, well-structured, and substantively strong: it refuses to treat Site A’s 41-to-32-hour change as an identified effect, keeps originals before document checking, separates true overlap from recorded overlap, and stages a narrow pilot. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and implementation defects are material under a strict reading.

The transport argument is mostly sound. The fork–join account is right: with independent checks, perfect overlap saves the shorter branch, about 3.1–3.5 hours at A and 3.4 hours at B, and 0.38 × 3.4 ≈ 1.3 hours if spread over all B cases. Confounders are itemized (mix, +2 FTE, API, huddles, timestamps, no concurrent control). The digital versus originals split matches the statute. DFG figures are used carefully (2,108 = 62% of 3,400; 360/1,360 supplements). The conditional-go call, first cohort, and eight assumptions fit the evidence. Required headings, columns, node list, and word limits are met.

The main quantitative contradiction is in §5. The text calls 3.4 hours the no-original “ceiling,” then immediately says larger queue gains are possible, and the optimistic row reports an all-site median of −3 to −6 hours. That upper bound does not follow from the author’s own dilution arithmetic. Identity overlapping the 18-hour originals delay can add at most about 3.4 hours, and only where the case lock currently prevents a concurrency that rules 2–3 already allow. A coherent optimistic all-site range is closer to −1 to −4 hours. The digital-only row is tighter, but its all-site −3-hour end still requires nearly the full 38% share and the top of the effect range. “Median” changes are also approximated by share-weighted averages after the author notes that medians do not combine that way.

Decision thresholds disagree. The pilot pauses if rework rises by more than 3 points or p90 worsens by more than 15% (about 16 hours on a 110-hour baseline). §9 abandons universalization at +2 rework points or +10 p90 hours and expands only if rework is within +1 point and p90 within +5 hours. The pilot can therefore keep running after the abandonment rule has already been met. The +35-point overlap gate never states whether the denominator is the eligible stratum or all cases; on the full caseload that bar is barely feasible if only the first cohort is treated.

§7 is otherwise the right design: case-level assignment, phase-1 restriction to no-original, document-complete, non-legal claims, a supported arm versus a lock-workaround arm, Assessment-ready time as the proximal outcome, and explicit interference checks. With four identity reviewers and two lawyers, individual randomization plus “virtual lanes” is still a weak interference control. Assumption 7 names a cluster-period fallback, but that is not the primary design.

§8 builds the right POWL shape: partial order, Assessment joined to both branches, originals before document checking, and a legal loop whose redo is supplement → document recheck → reassessment. The limitation comment covers data guards, capacity, locks, and durations. The helper is not faithful to PM4Py: `DiGraph` has no `add_transitive_edges()`, and `StrictPartialOrder` has no `validate_partial_orders()`. Those calls raise `AttributeError` before conversion or playout. Transitive edges are unnecessary because the order relation is already transitive. `add_only_if_fm_is_reached` is not a playout parameter. `noTraces` and `maxTraceLength` are plausible keys.

Smaller issues: `assessment_capacity → waiting_before_assessment` is only valid if that metric includes assessor queue time after the join; WIP caps of 4 and 2 are unexplained; transport-confidence mixes “this confound is real” with “this number will recur,” so the 5-versus-80 scores are not one scale.

The recommendation itself is the correct policy: transfer the partial order for document-complete, non-dependent cases, do not copy the rollout or the nine-hour delta, and do not open document checking before originals. The defects are inconsistent magnitudes, misaligned stop rules, and non-runnable POWL code, not a wrong transport conclusion.
