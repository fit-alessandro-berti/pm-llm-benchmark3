5.5

The dataset core is usable: 12 objects, 17 timezone-aware events, amounts only on M1/M2, required O2O edges, and the stated integrity checks are present and mostly coherent. The analysis does not reliably describe that same model, and several claims fail against the code.

The construction contradicts its own policy. Section 1 says invoice and payment events are linked directly to the orders they monetize. The relation rows and the Section 3 activity map attach Record Payment only to Payment and Invoice. Section 4 then puts payment edges on the Order DFG. A reader cannot tell which relation policy was actually built.

E7 is miscounted twice. The rows are P1, I1, I3, O100, and O200: 5 objects, 3 types, 5 relations. Sections 3 and 8 call that 6 objects across 4 types, and check 3 would fail on the generated table.

Downstream logistics are treated as source facts. Reserve, pack, and invoice participation is explicit in the narrative. Dispatch and confirm name only a package and a delivery; linking those events to orders is containment propagation. The write-up says every E2O row is an explicit business fact and that O2O edges are never materialized into relations.

Qualifiers do not encode roles. The same verb is copied onto every participant (`packed`, `dispatched`, `paid`), so the qualifier column cannot distinguish the package from the delivery or the invoice from the payment. Both payments use `partially_settles`, which matches “partial payments,” while the prose also says V1 is closed after M2 without an invoice total.

Flattened predictions do not match the relations. O100 is Create, Add, Add, Reserve, Pack, Pack, Dispatch, Dispatch, Issue Invoice, Confirm, Confirm, Close. O200 omits the P2/D2 events. Shared events include E13 as well as E6, E7, E9, and E11. Neither order trace contains Record Payment. The repeated Pack/Dispatch/Confirm labels are the real Order-DFG distortion; the payment-branch and “duplicated payments” account is not. Payments are sequential across two days, so the invoice view does not show optional parallelism. Delivery traces are short, but D1’s 3-hour and D2’s overnight gaps are not instant shipping.

Object-centric expectations confuse the formalisms. An OTG is a type graph; O100–P1–O200 is an instance path, and there is no direct Package–Order or Delivery–Order O2O edge. An ET–OT graph is bipartite, not a tripartite hypergraph. In an object-centric Petri net, M1 and M2 are payment objects, not transitions, and a typed place does not merge order tokens into V1. The interaction summary is described as transitive and as a token-marking result. M1 co-occurs only with V1. O200 closes five minutes before O100, and O200 is not on D2; D2’s later confirmation does not explain O200 finishing first. The net is also described as one determined place structure.

The pipeline is only partly executable-looking. `from pm4py.objects.ocel import OCEL` does not match the long-standing `pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL` location, and the 2.4.1-versus-2.5 path story is not credible. `ocel_flattening` takes `ot`, not `object_type=`. `ocel_activity_frequencies`, `ocel_object_type_frequencies`, and `view_oc_petri_net` are not the stable top-level names. Failure modes are single sentences; “breaks silently” is the wrong failure for a bad import.
