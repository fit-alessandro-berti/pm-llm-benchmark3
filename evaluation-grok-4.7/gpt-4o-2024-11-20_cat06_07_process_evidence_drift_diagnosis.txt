5.5

The audit correctly separates the period into an April pipeline break, a June mix shift, a late-summer V3 deterioration, and an October labeling problem, but it is not reliable enough. Drift types are misassigned, July is missing, several claims contradict the tables or each other, and the required code would not run or produce monthly evidence.

The diagnosis table uses the right columns and the right six types, yet the assignments are loose. June is the clearest covariate shift: V2 rises from about 35% to 52%, while matured V1, V2, and V3 breach rates stay near 0.16, 0.09, and 0.35. That month is omitted from covariate drift and instead described as if the lower aggregate breach and Brier score showed stable predictions. Mean predicted risk stays near 0.20 against an observed breach rate of 0.15, and ECE rises to 0.060. Partner Z’s rural distance and device age are treated as covariate evidence even though those features are absent from the model. Population shift is dated August–October, but Partner Z’s share is 0% through August; the row’s own caveat admits this and still leaves August in scope. Concept drift is rated medium-low on the claim that there is no evidence the feature–outcome relationship changed. The matured variant table is that evidence: V3 moves from about 0.33–0.35 to 0.46 in July, 0.64 in August, and 0.61 in September, while V1 and V2 do not. Section 2 then says the parts delays “validate concept drift,” so the table and narrative disagree. Label delay is confined to October. July is 98% mature, August 93%, and September 82%, with median label delay already 42 days and slower Partner Z maturation, so September’s headline AUROC, Brier, ECE, and 0.45-versus-0.27 gap are not fully mature either. Pipeline failure is correctly centered on April, but May is brushed aside despite 2% unknown activity, 3% missing queue features, AUROC 0.78, and ECE 0.050. The cached pre-diagnosis count and duplicate removal only after 20 April are mentioned too loosely to define the repair.

Section 2 is not the required January–October narrative because July is absent. That is the month the supplier first misses targets, the parts edge moves from 42h to 65h, and matured V3 breach reaches 0.46, before the August extreme of 130h and 0.64. April’s observed breach rate of 0.19 is presented as part of the collapse; January–March were already 0.18–0.19. The April failure is the jump in mean predicted risk to 0.28, AUROC 0.67, Brier 0.184, ECE 0.110, 17% unknown activity, and 18% unmapped variants. August and September are merged, which hides that August’s V3 share of 35% occurs with no Partner Z, whereas September’s 42% is largely the new source: 24% of cases, 58% of them V3, versus 36% V3 elsewhere. October’s low observed breach rate of 0.16 is rightly treated as non-comparable, but the mechanism is reversed: longer V3 paths are disproportionately immature, not “disproportionately mature.” Evidence and hypothesis are not separated, and falsifiers are not stated in the diagnosis.

The test list touches every requested theme, but several expectations are not operational. Fixed-vintage backtesting is said to show “stability” under genuine process failure; the contrasting result is that censoring-only cohorts move back toward the pre-August V3 rate near 0.33 once matured, while a real path-conditional change remains near 0.60. The pipeline test covers unknown labels only, not rename normalization, duplicate removal, and recomputation of the cached event count. Stratification omits month and does not say that within-variant gaps after holding path fixed favor concept drift, whereas gaps that vanish after path and source stratification favor mix. Reweighting does not specify the source and target distributions, nor the falsifier that missing rural and device-age features can leave a gap even when shared covariates are transported. Lineage checks are generic rather than tied to the 1 April rename, the 20 April duplicate rule, and the 6 May partial backfill.

The remediation section has the right stage names and correctly keeps raw October, and unrepaired April, out of training. It then recommends temporary recalibration to historical Partner Z proportions, which cannot exist before September. Putting parts-delay into recalibration weights would leak post-decision information: prediction occurs at diagnosis, and the delay is downstream. September is not held out despite 82% maturity. There is no safe-month rule separating label-clean history from the current concept: January–March and June are mature and pipeline-usable but pre-supplier-shock; July–September can train the new V3 relationship only after a maturity cutoff and a check that the supplier shock is still the intended target; October cannot. A variant-specific model is endorsed in one sentence. V1 and V2 stability supports a V3 calibration or overlay first; a Partner Z model is not justified before stratification on variant, distance, and device age, especially while its labels mature more slowly.

The monitor list names the main metric families and says a label-delay alarm should pause retraining. It is not an architecture: there is no deployment or intervention monitor for the rename, mapping patch, routing policy, supplier failure, or partner onboarding, and no gate that lets performance-DFG alerts fire without labels while blocking AUROC-driven retraining below a maturity threshold. The code imports the requested libraries and does not invent a drift detector, but it is not an auditable monthly extractor. `format_dataframe` renames columns to the standard PM4Py names, after which `activity`, `case_id`, and `timestamp` are invalid. Normalization is applied to the old name after that rename. `filter_time_range` is called with `start_timestamp` and `end_timestamp` rather than the actual time bounds, over one global window. The case-start object is only the `Case Created` rows, is unused, and has no note that event-month cohorts time-stamp edges while case-start cohorts follow cases opened that month. `get_variants` is passed through `value_counts`, which does not yield a variant mix, and the temporal features are computed once and omitted from the summary.

The four required failure modes are present. The immature-label risk does not state the selection mechanism: finished cases are disproportionately short non-breaches, so October’s 0.16 rate would teach the opposite of the V3 experience. The pipeline mitigation says to rebuild features, but not to freeze any calibrator fit to April’s 0.28-versus-0.19 score inflation. The Partner Z mitigation does not warn that 0.45 versus 0.27 is itself an immature comparison.
