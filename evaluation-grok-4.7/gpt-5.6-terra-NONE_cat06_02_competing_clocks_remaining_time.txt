5.5

The write-up has the right skeleton: incubation is converted on a continuous clock, the join is a maximum, Sunday readout is separated from QA’s closed Sunday, excursion dependence is not treated as independence, and the six required sections are present. The forecast itself is not reliable.

The headline time mapping is wrong. From Friday 18 Sep 15:30, 120 hours is Wednesday 23 Sep 15:30, and Thursday 24 Sep 15:30 is 144 hours. Section 4 assigns 90–120 hours to Tuesday 09:30–Thursday 15:30 and starts the 120-hour tail on Thursday. The central 72–82 hour band is arithmetically Monday 15:30–Tuesday 01:30, but nothing can terminate in that overnight gap: QA closes at 20:00, so a Monday acceptance releases by about 20:45 (roughly 77 hours) and the next real window is Tuesday from 08:00 (about 88.5 hours). The prose’s “Monday late afternoon” window and the table disagree.

Friday chemistry completion is not a defensible best case under the answer’s own rules. One urgent sample outranks B-417. The analyst free around 15:54–16:24 has at most about 2.1 hours and is consumed by that urgent sample (minimum service 1.5 hours). The other analyst is free only around 16:42–17:30 and has at most about 1.3 hours, below the 1.5-hour minimum, so B-417 cannot finish by 18:00. The stated Friday 17:30–18:00 result contradicts that priority ordering. Because FIFO is the majority discipline and about four samples are still ahead, Monday 09:30–13:00 is an optimistic risk-priority case, not the typical spill. Chemistry will often bind the join on Monday afternoon or Tuesday, so the claim that microbiology “often” leaves a Monday morning/noon join is too soft. That also makes rank 5 for queue discipline too low.

Scenario probabilities do not match their labels. Ordinary no-weekend readout is about 55–75% and is the modal smooth path, yet Scenario C folds it into an 8–18% “delayed readout or confirmation” band. Scenario B’s 12–25% fits a rerun, not “rerun or Monday spillover,” and spillover is already inside the smooth path. Bands are allowed to overlap, but these ranges describe different events from the ones named.

A 4-hour documentation query opened Monday does not force Tuesday. QA is open 08:00–20:00, so a query found in the morning can return the same day and still release Monday. Scenario D’s earliest terminal time of Tuesday 10:00–15:00 drops that path. The conditional rerun band 0.20–0.30 given a confirmed excursion is an unstated numerical lift, and the completeness score 0.82 is never used to place the query rate inside 0.15–0.30.

Section 6 then overstates the secondary trigger. Section 3 correctly says a Sunday microbiology result barely moves a normal release because QA cannot start until Monday. The on-call decision matters mainly by moving a contamination confirmation earlier, not by shifting the normal-path terminal time from Monday 08:00 staffing to Sunday.
