4.5

The business reading, dependency table, approximation policy, and validation outline are substantial. Both executable models and several witness verdicts are semantically wrong relative to PM4Py loop semantics and to the prose in this same answer, so the formal core of the task does not hold.

**Process tree.** `Operator.LOOP` denotes do `(redo do)*`. With do = `E → I` and redo = `J → E → I`, the language is `(E I)(J E I E I)*`, i.e. one extra full cycle on every retry. The claimed language `E I (J E I)*` is the language of `loop(seq(E, I), J)` only. The duplicated `Execute Trial` / `Inspect Trial` leaves are therefore not a faithful encoding of repeat-until, and every multi-iteration claim that depends on `E I (J E I)*` is false (assumptions 8–9, the compact expression, §3, §6). W4’s trial shape `E I J E I` is outside this tree even before the supplier placement is considered. The stated reason for rejecting W4 (supplier before the first trial) is only one of the failures.

The block structure is also a coarser under-approximation than the text presents as inherent. `parallel(seq(Contain, Plan Verification), seq(Analyze, Design))` is a legal process tree and preserves the split dependencies. Joining both streams before a fixed `Plan Verification` then `Design` order is an extra, self-inflicted constraint. What a tree cannot express exactly is the later asymmetric synchronization: trial joins design and planning while the approval and supplier obligations, already enabled by root-cause analysis, join only at release. §3 treats the unnecessary early join as if it were that limitation.

**POWL.** Standard loop semantics are the same do `(redo do)*`, and the operator itself supplies the silent exit. Putting `XOR(pass_exit, Adjust Parameters)` in the redo child does not exit. The silent branch is an empty redo, so the model admits another `Execute Trial → Inspect Trial` with no `Adjust Parameters`, and a taken redo is always followed by another do-part. The compact description (`E → I → XOR(exit, J)`, with `J` returning to `E`) does not match the code (`LOOP(E → I, XOR(τ, J))`).

`e`, `i`, and `j` are members of nested models and also nodes of the root `StrictPartialOrder`, with no root edges. That is ill-formed POWL: partial-order nodes execute as root activities, parent links are not unique, and the claim that every element is downstream of registration and upstream of archive is false for those three nodes. `Operator.PARALLEL` is used for the high-risk pair even though the given POWL interface provides `XOR` and `LOOP`; unordered concurrency belongs in a `StrictPartialOrder` with no edge. The release edges `approvals → Release`, `supplier_choice → Release`, and `trials → Release` are the right idea and are undermined by the loop and the root node set.

**Witnesses.** W1 and F2 are the same visible string. Both models can produce it through the supervisor branch, so the POWL cell “Reject for the high-risk interpretation” uses a different standard from the tree cell “Admit structurally.” F3 is `E I E I J` before release. Under either the claimed language or actual `do (redo do)*`, a loop ends on inspection, and adjustment cannot be the last trial event. Both “Admit structurally” cells are wrong. The earliest structural break is the second `Execute Trial` with no preceding `Adjust Parameters`, not adjustment after an externally passing first inspection. For W2, W3, and W5, the earliest tree failure is earlier than “design before planning”: design or supplier occurs while `Contain Material` is still outstanding, and supplier/approval are still outside their serialized block.

**What stays sound.** The dependency table correctly separates precedence, exclusive risk routes, optional supplier, trial join, approval/trial independence, and release synchronization, and it correctly marks pass/fail as data the control flow does not observe. The preference for POWL on this non-block pattern is the right representational conclusion. The validation plan (convert, soundness, bounded playout, positive and negative replay, silent-label and duplicate-label checks, explicit limits of sampling) is a real test plan. It would not confirm the verdicts in §5, because those verdicts do not describe the models that were built. The self-critique names the data-guard gap and the tree’s lost overlaps, and it does not name the loop-language error, the ill-formed root, or the witness contradictions.
