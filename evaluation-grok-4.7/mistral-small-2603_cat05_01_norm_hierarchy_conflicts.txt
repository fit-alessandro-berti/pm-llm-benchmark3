3.5

The memo has the required seven-part shape, but it is not a reliable assessment. Classifications repeatedly contradict the memo’s own rules, several case facts are misread, and ambiguity is closed with high confidence.

The rule catalogue is not usable as drafted. Comparison and negation operators are missing, so triggers such as “Purchase EUR 50,000” and “requester/committer final business approver” do not state the norms. DE-B3 lists B2/B4 as an exception to the approved-supplier rule, while the pseudo-logic correctly says the emergency procedure does not waive that rule. DE-B2 is given a pre-commitment timing the SOP does not state. CFO-D1 is entered with no defeater even though the hierarchy treats the email as uncontrolled and limited by the integrity caveat. The first pseudo-logic line treats segregation (A1) as overriding PO sequencing (B1), which are different issues. B4’s express permission for commitment before the PO is not carried into the case logic.

Case results do not follow the stated method. P-501 is high-confidence NONCOMPLIANT for a PO-sequence breach and for ENG-1 approving the purchase. The four-hour stoppage fits the local emergency rule that allows commitment before the PO, the emergency form is inside 24 hours, and the file does not identify the Plant approver. The real delegation defect—the Plant Manager was reachable and had not delegated the Shift Lead—is missed. P-502 is high-confidence COMPLIANT with no deviations. It is a new-supplier commitment with Compliance clearance only at 18:30 and Finance approval only at 13:05, after commitment. That collides with A3, A6, and the memo’s own anti-cure rule. The CFO email of 15 July is also applied to a 14 July act. P-506 treats Finance approval as missing even though it is timestamped before commitment. P-507 says the CFO’s EUR 150,000 sufficiency cap “does not cover >EUR 100,000,” merging two thresholds, and then uses JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION while denying the waiver that would be needed for the absent Finance approval. P-508 does the same above EUR 150,000, where the email’s sufficiency sentence does not apply and Legal clearance does not replace A2. P-503 correctly sees the requester-committer-authorizer overlap, but it still marks ECD rules satisfied for three-day backup stock. P-504 correctly falls outside sterilization scope, yet lists DE-B2 and the violated supplier rule as satisfied. P-505 is COMPLIANT while its justification says the commitment fell after expiry. No case is INDETERMINATE.

The alternatives do not test the strongest opposing reading. For P-501 the live alternative is the local emergency sequencing rule, not a retroactive start of ECD-2. For P-505 and P-507, the paragraphs labeled as reasons the alternative is weak actually restate why Finance approval remains unmet. Section 6 repeats the invented P-501 segregation breach and the P-506 Finance error, and it calls late Compliance clearance “late evidence of a timely act.” The draft clause is clearer than the case holdings and would not support those COMPLIANT and JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION results, but dropped operators, an undefined 48-hour tail, and no explicit statement of which timing and supplier rules are displaced leave the clarification incomplete.
