The dialectical structure is strong: both briefs are steelmanned, concessions are explicit, case classes are individually justified, and the matrix plus burden rules are traceable. The verdict is not a split-the-difference. It still has material errors.

The revised POWL misses a stated minimum. Sanctions sits in a pre-decision block that must finish before the standard branch, so sanctions strictly precede quality. The required concurrency of sanctions and quality on the standard route is gone; quality is concurrent only with manual approval. That is a modeling regression: a CS-9-valid trace with quality before sanctions is now non-conformant, and M0’s actual concurrency is not preserved.

The judgment contradicts its own burden rule. B3 says an exception fails unless competent written authority is proved. U1 marks PD-3 competence unproved, and the overlay table stores `competence="UNPROVED"`, but SUP-702 is still JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION and the checker never reads competence. SUP-704 has the same gap (FD-7 competence only “to verify”) with a weaker presumption stated, not reconciled to B3.

The system statistic is wrong. The 312 Auto_Approve cases are June–July; the six August cases are outside that set. “20 out of 312, i.e. 6.4%” mixes denominators. The in-window ineligible share is 14/312.

SUP-708 is under-analyzed. The trace also places Manual_Procurement_Approval after Activate_Supplier, a second CS-9 breach with no overlay. The matrix, prosecution, and classification discuss only sanctions timing. The class (NONCOMPLIANT, critical) still holds.

Smaller defects: example rows invent risk, country, and group flags not in the evidence; `add_bd` is unused while the 2-business-day rule is a raw integer compare; fitness “1.0” is asserted without alignment. Case coverage, scope handling, CRM missingness, SoD versus the SUP-715 reviewer/approver COI, and the POWL-versus-guard split are otherwise careful.
