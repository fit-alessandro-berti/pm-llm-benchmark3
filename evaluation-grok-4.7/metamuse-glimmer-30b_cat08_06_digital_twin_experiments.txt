The write-up covers the required eight sections and the main domain constraints: seasonal and weekday demand, resource calendars, latent objections, rework, and architect/district coupling. It is still a sketch with several concrete errors, so it is not a strong design.

The process-tree claim is wrong. The given string is ordinary PM4Py syntax (`->`, `X`, `+`, `*`, `tau`), and the skeleton never calls `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. The real limits of that tree are different: no appeal path, loop-redo semantics that may not match the log, and no cross-case structure. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with `case_id_key`, which is not a valid parameter (`case_id` is). The excerpt is reduced to one row plus a comment. `ArrivalGenerator` and `Calendar` drop arguments they accept. There is no routing placeholder, interventions are unused, and nothing in the engine represents batching, architect linkage, or a future-event list. Only a combined inter-event delay is computed, while the estimation section proposed a service/wait split.

The simulation design names the right phenomena but does not specify discrete-event mechanics. Parallel review is not given fork/join state. Public notice is not a two-part delay (fixed 14-day minimum plus a latent extension). Objection “spill-over” in the scope section becomes an independent random extension in the assumptions, so related cases do not actually move together. Service times are assumed independent of case type even though the summaries say planning and inspection depend on type and district. Fire and environment triggers impute thresholds that are missing from the log without a rule for when the observed flags should be trusted. Prior violations required by D2 are not in the data model. Rework is memoryless despite unstructured reasons being a stated gap.

The experiment table lists baseline, D1–D10, and three stresses, but the effect sizes are single invented points (−30% loops, 30% travel saving, 0.7 smoothing) rather than ranges, and the trade-off cells do not state direction. Stresses are not clearly crossed with policies. A three-month warm-up and an extra two months of tail time do not match cycle times whose p90 is about 260 days, and steady-state deletion sits poorly on seasonal, non-stationary demand. Right-censoring of unfinished cases is ignored. Thirty replications and common random numbers are reasonable, but tail precision for rare complex variants is not justified, and the sensitivity list omits the service/wait split that the data gaps make most uncertain.

Validation renames resimulation as “replay” and skips conformance (fitness and precision) of the tree against the log. Utilization cannot be checked directly when start times are missing. Decision rules use an undefined SLA, an undefined disparity measure, and arbitrary 10% and 85% cutoffs, with no interval overlap test, cost, applicant burden, or defect risk. The design correctly refuses to treat objections as observed and does not claim optimality, but it is not yet a calibrated basis for choosing an intervention.
