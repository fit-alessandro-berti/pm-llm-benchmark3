The write-up has the required seven sections and a few sound local judgments (pair document completion with statement normalization, treat KYC false negatives as severe, and do not start with an activation bot). The portfolio decision itself is not reliable. Several load-bearing claims contradict the process data, the control findings, or the answer’s own earlier sections, and the year-1 plan breaks the budget.

**The recommendation rests on a false waiting-time claim.** It says A3 and A6 reduce the two largest waits. Median wait before KYC is 4.0 hours and before normalization is 20.0 hours. The long waits are external valuation (58.0h), customer signing (44.0h), entity-mismatch resolution (31.0h), and credit analysis (28.0h). The “bottleneck/synchronization” set (A3, A5, A6, A8) leaves out both explicit synchronization problems: entity mismatch, on the critical path in 82% of cases where it appears and looping back to KYC (1,160 of 1,750), and collateral valuation, required for 71% of equipment-finance cases, with 19% late external valuations. Committee packaging still waits on valuation, so A8 cannot clear that join. Weekly high-exposure committee cadence is never mentioned, so A8’s cycle-time effect is overstated. KYC and financial-document review are described as concurrent and then ignored.

**Waiting time is treated as time the activity’s automation removes.** Cutting a 35-minute contract-preparation service does not remove the 22-hour upstream wait; legal ops is idle when KYC is slow and overloaded at month-end. The same conflation is applied to registration, KYC, committee packaging, and signing. Downstream risk is repeatedly scored “low” because a flow “only” covers 1,750, 2,400, 7,300, or 7,600 cases. That is frequency ranking under another label, including for activities the matrix itself calls high process-level impact.

**Controls and dependencies are mis-specified.** Four-eyes is credit analysis versus committee packaging on high-exposure cases (5.8% violations in the month-end rush). The answer assigns it to A6 versus A8, then says the compliance portfolio (which contains neither) fixes it, while the bottleneck portfolio (which contains both) does not. A9 is made to depend on A8 even though committee decision feeds only 3,650 of 7,600 contract preparations. A4 is made to depend on A3, A12 on A9, and A10 on A9, while the legal gate is KYC clearance before signing. Provisional signing (6.5%, 1.1% remediation) and the 14% credit-line-renewal remediation rate are unused. Section 2 says A1, A2, A9, A10, and A11 need deep core-banking integration; the rollout then calls A1 and A10 lightweight. Which modules actually need core banking is not in the evidence, and the answer does not keep one story.

**The rollout is a different, over-budget program.** The limit is four modules. The phases deploy A1, A5, A3, A6, A9, A12, A8, and A10. A1 is justified by “Register is already 72% digital”; that note belongs to Receive application. A5 goes live before A6, which is the analyst-overload sequence the resource note warns about (credit analysis is the larger analyst load: 8,900 cases at 110 minutes, versus normalization at 5,200 cases and 75 minutes). A9 is scheduled before A8, against the answer’s own prerequisite. The KYC gate is a false-positive rate under 5%, although the severe failure mode is false negatives, and residual hits land on 3 specialist FTE concentrated in two seniors. The four-eyes gate is attached to A9 and A12, which do not enforce that separation. Effect statements still contain “→ ?” and claim the 58-hour wait is mitigated by A7 “if included,” in a portfolio that excludes A7.

**Internal scores do not support the conclusion.** The matrix is in identifier order, not ranked. First-year suitability is High for A1, A5, A9, and A10 and Low for A4 and A7; the recommendation then drops the High-rated modules and dismisses A4 for “lower direct benefits” after rating its process-level benefit High. The quick-win effects say document loops are not reduced while A5 is in that portfolio, and they describe analyst load as unchanged when cleaner files increase demand on the 9 analyst FTE. Compliance responsibility is named, but the chosen set never installs a hard KYC-before-signing gate, a four-eyes check, or an audit trail beyond the A3 row.
