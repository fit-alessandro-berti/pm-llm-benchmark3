The case reasoning is strong and mostly well calibrated, but the required executable check is broken and several smaller inconsistencies keep this short of a near-flawless answer.

The control-objective split is the right one. Nominal username separation is treated as distinct from preventing an effective erroneous or fraudulent transfer, and AV-2 is confined to duplicate, sanctions, bank-change, limit, and basic PO checks. Collusion, receipt fabrication, credential control, emergency abuse, outage, and recoverability are separated cleanly. The sufficiency test actually uses timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, evidence quality, and collusion resistance, and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the only standard is substantive. Case judgments avoid the two obvious traps: AP-204 is failed despite distinct users, and AP-205 is stopped before completion rather than scored as a completed failure. AP-202 and AP-208 are correctly treated as uncompensated, including AP-208’s ex post legitimacy. AP-209 is properly left indeterminate because the u12/u13 pattern is an alert, while the fabricated receipt is what makes AP-204 a failure. The post-release, pre-settlement manager action is given a coherent hybrid semantics, and the monitoring section keeps alerts distinct from proof.

The classifications are not mechanically wrong, but the rubric is not applied evenly. Section 2 puts post-release manager action in the partially equivalent, “tolerable” band, while AP-207 is then called fully met and “cleanly conforming.” ED-1 does allow approval before settlement, so the outcome is defensible, yet the test never states that exception. The case table also labels that same AP-201 and AP-207 review as preventive after Section 4 has defined it as hybrid. AP-203 is `OBJECTIVE_MET` at high confidence even though L3 status, an explicit ED-1 condition, is unproven; the source facts only support “mostly satisfied.” Severity is undefined, which makes AP-201’s “Moderate” hard to interpret beside a EUR 1,800 payment whose outcome was correct.

Section 7 does not run. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` takes `parameters`, not `keep_violations`. The default call correctly returns conforming cases, but that result is discarded, and `keep_violations=True` raises `TypeError` on the public signature. Violations require something like `parameters={"filter_type": "not_four_eyes_principle"}`. The surrounding pandas flags are thoughtful, yet they also over-encode facts: AP-208’s token is only “active,” not shown to precede preparation, and m21’s independence is stored as false without evidence. The rolling `BDay(1)` test can also disagree with a next-business-day reading, including AP-201, without being disclosed. The version note recognizes the right default behavior and then names a parameter the library does not provide.
