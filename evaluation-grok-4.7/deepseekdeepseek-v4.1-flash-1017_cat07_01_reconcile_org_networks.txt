The answer meets every structural requirement and handles the core contradiction correctly: handover and subcontracting measure directed, episodic routing, while working-together measures co-presence, so Mira can be central in the first two and peripheral in the third without either result being automatically good or bad. The four hypotheses are distinct enough, each has support, limits, and predictions, and the risk section stays properly non-accusatory. Bots and vendors are included, and the recommendation does not remove her without a replacement.

Deductions are for concrete inaccuracies, not tone. Hypothesis D says her events are “numerous on relatively few claims,” but 960 events on 905 cases is about one event per case, and her case-count percentile is 0.91—the highest among humans. That misstates the pattern the data-artifact hypothesis needs. Section 1 also asserts subcontracting loops “to adjusters”; the reported A→B→A patterns are Mira–Omar, Mira–Priya, and Bot_Rules–Mira, not Mira–adjuster–Mira. The executive summary then treats as fact that she “detects problems and chooses the next specialist,” and the awkward “sends cases to … reopens/complaints” mixes activities with destinations. The opening “best interpreted as” brokerage role also leans harder toward one label than the competing-hypothesis body supports. Useful calibrations are missed: handover lags to Omar and Priya closely match their own service times, and both specialists have higher exception rates than Mira.
