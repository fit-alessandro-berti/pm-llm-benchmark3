6.8

The required six-part structure is complete, the main pattern is read correctly (early slowness tracks a heavier case mix; later speed-up with rising errors appears only under high WIP and is steeper for less experienced staff; night’s slower late pace coexists with fewer errors than the overloaded day shift), and the hard constraints are respected. The score is held down by factual slips, over-identified causes, an internal contradiction, and analysis code that does not implement the models it claims.

**Mechanism table.** The rows and columns match the brief, and most cited rates match the aggregates. The “3.5–5.9 min” first-20 vs later gap is wrong: the four groups differ by 4.1, 4.7, 5.9, and 2.6 minutes, so neither bound is 3.5. Night-staff service times (17.5 / 14.9) are attached to the night shift, which is a different cut from that shift’s last-two-hour mean of 15.2. The circadian row defines a post-lunch dip as about 13:00–15:00 and then treats the day shift’s 15:00–17:00 error rate as that dip, while those same hours are when the day queue grows from 38 to 75. “Near-zero” service time and “significantly lower” night errors add certainty the log does not contain. Queue length 46 is medium on the answer’s own WIP bands.

**Integrated account.** The three-phase story correctly refuses a single cause and states that late acceleration is a speed–accuracy tradeoff under queue pressure, moderated by experience, with fatigue mattering mainly when WIP is high. That conclusion is then overloaded. Seniors’ 15.9-minute early mean is offered as evidence that they take the hard carryover; it is the shortest early mean of the four groups, so it does not show that. Phase 2 is labeled true learning while complexity, priority mix, and service time are still drifting. Phase clock bounds (0–90, 90–270, 270–480) are not in the bin means. The 13.7% rework figure for sequence positions 76+ is attributed to superficial quality checks in the last 45 minutes; those are different slices. “Careful pacing” under late low WIP is inferred from error rates alone; later service time is not split by WIP. The diagram’s 7.9–10.9% error band mixes the position-76+ rate with the high-WIP, high-complexity cell.

**Analyses.** The prose names within-resource contrasts, position, WIP, complexity, shift and clock time, batching, experience interactions, and downstream rework. The code does not carry that design. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are called with `case_id_key` rather than the `case_id` parameter those functions take. `discover_batches` is given `max_inter_arrival_time` instead of merge distance, and the result is treated as objects whose `.events` are dataframe indices. `cluster_log` is given `no_clusters`, which is not its parameter. The error equation shows worker fixed effects and a WIP-by-experience term; the logit omits worker effects and interacts queue length with experience instead. Sequence position and minutes since shift start are entered together as linear terms despite near-duplication, so the position effect the section sets out to isolate is not identified. A performance DFG built after dropping events with WIP outside a band creates false directly-follows edges. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` counts repeated activities inside a case, which is not the 7-day rework flag. Splines, coarsened exact matching, and the arrival-rate instrument are described and then absent from the code.

**Interventions.** Rejecting moving complex work out of hour 1, random assignment, deferring quality checks, and penalties is consistent with the evidence and the constraints. The queue alert is correctly conditioned on pairing it with capacity relief, and the extra day-shift senior targets the shift where the queue actually blows out. Capping shift length is a weaker judgment: position 76+ sits at about 459 minutes, still inside the stated 8-hour shifts, and the write-up explains that pattern as sleep deprivation and extended shifts. The randomization monitor’s target of a zero standard deviation of complexity is not what random assignment produces.

**Ethics.** Selection, logging artifacts, management-created queues, bot/human shared error, and punitive misuse are all covered, and punishment is rejected. “Ecological fallacy” is the wrong name for case-mix confounding. Junior staff are summarized as receiving simple batched work, which the early junior means (19.6 minutes, 5.1% errors) do not show. Batching is narrated as prior review plus click-through, or as rubber-stamping, as if one of those mechanisms were observed.

**Dashboard.** Nine indicators cover service time, waiting time, WIP, rework, error rate, complexity mix, batching, handover, and load fairness, each with a definition, fields, display, and threshold. The fairness Gini treats equal complexity shares as the target and would rebalance above 0.40, which reinstates the equal assignment rejected in the intervention section; skill-based routing and that equity rule are not reconciled. Handover logic treats Eligibility Decision as completion. Target concurrency of 4 per worker is an unstated constant. The panel sketch also promises a bot confusion matrix and a micro-break guard that the indicator list never specifies.
