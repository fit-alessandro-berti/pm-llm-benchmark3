3.5

The write-up matches the required seven-section skeleton, and a few raw facts are real: B02 interrupts B01, C05 is priority 1 with a tight deadline, D05 can only be done by D-F, and the tables do name decision times. The reconstructions those conclusions rest on are not reliable. Eligible sets repeatedly include jobs already in service or already complete and omit jobs that are waiting, rule predictions are inverted, and every primary label is contradicted by the episodes the tables skip or mis-score. Confidences of 70–90 overstate what this evidence can identify.

**QA is FIFO with one appointment jump, and priority is rejected.** A1 is a single server with a hard gap at the recorded calibration window 08:52–08:58. Service order is A01, A03, A02, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08. At 08:20 the waiting set is A02 (08:04, priority 3, estimate 20), A03 (appointment 08:20, estimate 10), and A04 (08:13, priority 1, estimate 7). A01 has just finished. FIFO selects A02, priority selects A04, and SPT selects A04 because 7 < 10. The observed start is A03. The table instead lists A01 as eligible, drops A04, and assigns A03 to FIFO, priority, and SPT together. At 08:30 A02 starts over the still-waiting priority-1 short job A04, which is the clean rejection of both priority and SPT. At 08:58 A02 is already complete; A04 is the FIFO head, the earlier priority-1 job, and the SPT job, so that row discriminates nothing, and predicting FIFO = A02 is a finished-job error. At 09:05 the waiting set includes A05, A06 (appointment 09:15), A07 (estimate 9), and A08 (priority 1). The start is A05. FIFO accounts for it; priority would take A08; SPT would take A07; starting A05 also overruns A06’s appointment, which then begins at 09:20. The table’s 09:20 row still treats A05 as waiting and lets priority select A06 while A08 is waiting. A08 runs last, after three lower-priority jobs, so it is evidence against priority. Calling the primary discipline appointment scheduling, with priority as the secondary, inverts the weight of the evidence: one exact appointment hit (A03), one missed protection (A06), and a residual order that is arrival order. Every realized duration in QA equals `est_min`, so the SPT ranking was knowable and still loses at 08:30 and 09:05.

**QB is a preemptive, exception-ridden hybrid, and the stated label contradicts the stated evidence.** The supporting episode is B01 stopping at 09:16 for B02, with the recorded reason “urgent arrival.” That is preemption. The primary label is non-preemptive priority. After B02 ends at 09:31, B03 (priority 2, arrived 09:20) is waiting and the server resumes B01. The table never opens that decision. At 09:48 the recorded reason is dispatcher reassignment and the start is B03, but B04 (09:35, estimate 8) and B05 (09:46, priority 1) are also present. Recorded arrival is a scan time that can lag true arrival by up to two minutes, so a stamp of 09:46 means B05 was already there at 09:48; the delay caveat does not remove B05. SPT on that set selects B04. The table’s eligible set is only B01 and B03, and it scores SPT as B03. At 10:00 B05 does start, which is a real priority-consistent choice once B01 is already stopped. At 10:14 B01 is resumed again ahead of B06 (priority 2). At 10:28 the waiting set is B04 (priority 3, estimate 8), B06 (priority 2, estimate 20), and B07 (arrived 10:08, appointment 10:30, estimate 6). The start is B04, and B04’s eight minutes pushes B07 past the appointment; the note says the appointment was not honored. Priority selects B06. SPT selects B07 if B07 is eligible. The table omits B07 and scores priority as B04, which hides the contradiction of its own diagnosis. Segment lengths sum to the estimates, so these rankings are not an artifact of a bad `est_min`.

**QC does not behave as deadline-aware priority.** C1 is free at 10:10 with C01–C04 waiting. C03 is the only priority-2 job and has the earliest deadline (12:30). The start is C02, estimate 8, the SPT job, whose deadline is 14:00. The table’s eligible set is C01 and C02, so it misses C03, calls those two priority-equivalent, and then defines the deadline rule as “deadline farthest,” which is the reverse of earliest-deadline dispatch. The same inversion reappears at 11:34: C06 has the loosest deadline (15:00), C07 the tightest (12:00), and the table says the deadline rule predicts C06. The 10:54 eligible set is C03, C04, and C05. C03 finished at 10:32 and C04 finishes at 10:54; the jobs actually waiting are C01, C05, C06, and C07. C05 is priority 1, so priority and a deadline rule are observationally tied there, and the job’s own note says the 10:50 deadline was already at risk. By 10:54 it is already missed. C05 waited through C03 and C04 with no preemption. At 11:40 C01 (estimate 36, deadline 13:00) starts ahead of C07 (estimate 18, deadline 12:00). Starting C07 would finish about 11:58, inside the deadline, and C01 would still finish before 13:00. The observed choice misses C07. That is direct evidence against an SLA-triggered switch. The initial backlog order C02 → C03 → C04 → C01 is the SPT order of jobs present at 10:10, and the note that C01 is last among that backlog matches SPT. Later insertions only partly fit: C05 is a priority-1 exception, C06 is the SPT job at 11:34, and C01 ahead of C07 breaks SPT, priority, and earliest deadline together. Confidence 80 on deadline-aware priority, with FIFO as the secondary, is not tenable. Realized QC durations again equal the estimates, so C06’s shortness was visible when C04 and C05 were chosen over it.

**QD treats a hard skill constraint as if it were the dispatch policy, and one observed choice is wrong.** Three resources run in parallel: D-M mechanical until 12:30, D-F firmware and electrical with a meeting 12:00–12:20, D-X mechanical and electrical only from 11:20. At 11:00 D03 has not arrived. D01 and D02 start together because only D-M can take mechanical D01 and only D-F can take firmware D02. That is eligibility, and it is not a ranking contest between them. At 11:25 D03 is already in service on D-F (started 11:21). D-M’s waiting mechanical jobs are D04 (estimate 10), D08 (priority 1, estimate 8, arrived 11:16), and D09. The start is D04. FIFO accounts for it; priority and SPT both select D08. The table says the observed selection is D03. D-X is idle from 11:20 until D06’s appointment at 11:30, with D07 already waiting and feasible only on D-X. That idle interval is the appointment evidence, and the QD table has no appointment column. At 11:48 D07 starts ahead of D10 (priority 1, estimate 12). D06 has already finished at 11:48, so “D07 selected over D06” reverses the trace. D-F finishes D03 at 11:36 and does not start D05 (estimate 30, which would run into the meeting) and also does not take D10 (estimate 12, which would fit before 12:00). D05 then starts at 12:20 because it is the only remaining D-F job after a calendar block, not because the earlier choice reserved D-F: at 11:21 D-F took D03, which D-X could also have done, and left the unique firmware+electrical job waiting. The 12:20 row lists D06 and D07 as eligible after both have completed. Priority is a poor secondary label: D04 before D08 and D07 before D10 both violate it. Confidence 90 is the least justified number in the diagnosis table, and it is attached to the inference the traces are least able to separate from a constraint.

**Later sections do not repair the decision errors.** Section 3 gives one supporting and one contradicting ID per queue, often the wrong mechanism, and does not say whether a violation is a known constraint or an undocumented exception. A08 is cited against the QA diagnosis for its long estimate; under a priority secondary, a priority-1 start would be support, and the actual force of A08 is that it was delayed until it was alone. Section 4’s QA test uses 08:58, where a corrected eligible set makes FIFO and priority agree on A04, and the proposed extra observable is a priority flag the table already contains. The QB test does not state the two next-job predictions at 09:48, which diverge once B05 is included: a priority rule selects B05, while the recorded reassignment selected B03. Section 5 ranks four exceptions and does not say how any of them would change a diagnosis. A two-minute scan lag cannot reorder waits of tens of minutes, so putting it first is the wrong scale. Perfect equality of estimate and realized duration across these jobs weakens “strategically noisy estimates” as an explanation of the SPT losses. The recorded B01 reasons, the calibration and meeting blocks, and the explicit missed appointment on B07 are stronger, and they are already in the file. Section 6 is a generic five-item wish list. It does not target the actual forks: whether a 30-minute job may be started into D-F’s meeting, appointment lateness tolerance (A03 exact, A06 five minutes late, B07 about 26 minutes late, D-X idle until 11:30), remaining time at B01’s stops, per-resource versus shared queues on QD, and the feasible set at 11:40 when C07 can still make 12:00. Section 7 calls QD the most identifiable queue and priority-only the dangerous inference. QD is the queue where skill eligibility, appointment idling, and FIFO are most easily collapsed into one label; QA is the trace a corrected decision set can actually pin down. The synthesis also warns against pure priority while Section 1 assigns priority as QC’s primary mechanism and as a secondary mechanism on QA and QD.

Assumptions used above, each forced by the file: recorded arrival is the scan time, so true arrival is no later than the stamp; a job with a stamp before the decision is eligible unless a skill or calendar rule removes it; priority 1 is highest; SPT uses the arrival `est_min` because remaining-time fields are absent; on QD, a resource only ranks jobs its skills and availability window allow. Under those readings the defensible sketches are: QA, non-preemptive FIFO plus an appointment override when the resource is free at the appointment instant; QB, preemption for an urgent priority-1 arrival, resumption of B01 ahead of priority 2, one documented manual reassignment, then a FIFO tail that misses B07; QC, a partial SPT backlog with a late priority-1 insertion and an unresolved C01-before-C07 choice that creates an avoidable miss; QD, skill and shift constraints as filters, within-resource FIFO, and one exact appointment hold on D-X. The submitted document does not reach those comparisons because the eligible sets and the rule predictions are already off.
