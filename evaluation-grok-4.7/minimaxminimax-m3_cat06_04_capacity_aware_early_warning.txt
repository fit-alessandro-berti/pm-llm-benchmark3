The final policy is structurally complete and gets the central qualitative calls right: I-03 is rejected on stage grounds, I-05 is suppressed as a no-new-event refresh while I-07 is re-opened because of the new log and then declined on value, time, and spillover, and capacity is capped at three deployments plus a reserve. Calibration shifts, effect heterogeneity, false-alarm burden, and queue externalities are all recognized, and the base-scenario arithmetic (direct benefit €19,950, cost plus burden €4,750, spillover €5,400, net about €9,800) is auditable.

The allocation does not follow the stated rule. Section 1 ranks by midpoint effect times value, minus cost, burden, and a 0.6 spillover haircut. That ordering places I-08 clearly above I-09 and I-02, yet the portfolio skips I-08 and takes I-02. Labeling I-08 “B/C” and invoking adverse fragility does not repair this, because I-02 is also adverse-negative and has a much smaller base net. The claim that the Strategic Enterprise overprediction “discount [was] already absorbed in robustification” is false: the score never uses calibrated probability, and absolute risk reduction is never checked against baseline risk.

Other concrete defects: optimistic spillover is set at 0.005 per case-slot, below the given 0.01–0.03 band; alert count is declared irrelevant and then used to promote I-02 and I-09; I-04’s long deadline is wrongly said to reduce congestion spillover on other cases; the holdout design ignores interference from the same spillovers the policy emphasizes; and the I-07 rationale is garbled (“consumes ~ of remaining time”). The result is a strong memo with a real decision-rule break and several smaller inaccuracies, not a nearly flawless one.
