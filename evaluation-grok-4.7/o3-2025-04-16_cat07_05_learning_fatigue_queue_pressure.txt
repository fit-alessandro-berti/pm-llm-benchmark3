6.5

The required six-part structure is complete, the hard constraints are respected, and the multi-cause spine is right: early slowness is largely case-mix, mid-shift speed-up mixes warm-up with easier work, and later speed with rising errors is conditional on WIP and experience. The score is held down by repeated factual misreads, one inverted mechanism, and analyses that would not identify the pattern they describe.

The mechanism table misuses the supplied aggregates. Positions 1–5 (queue 46, error 3.4%) are treated as the Early shift’s first hour; Early’s actual start queue is 61. Night’s 15.2 min is the last two hours, when the queue has risen from 29 to 55, so it is not “high service time with lower WIP.” “Seniors on Night do not deteriorate more than Mid-levels” is not in the tables; night staff are a separate group. The “2%” low-WIP bot-correction rate and “higher variance” after Bot_Risk are invented.

Fatigue is internally inconsistent. Its expected signature is slower or flat service time, but the supporting evidence is a further 2.1 min speed-up. That speed-up is then called a classic fatigue effect in §2, while the same section also, correctly, attributes late speed to queue pressure. Late shift is grouped with Day as a shift where arrivals exceed service and WIP climbs; Late’s queue moves from 70 to 66. “Errors 7–14%” mixes the 7.9% error rate with the 13.7% seven-day rework rate. Junior batching is said to lower service time without harming quality, which the log does not show.

Automation is reversed. The log says humans correct Bot_Risk more often when queue length exceeds 70. The table reads that increase as complacency, and §2 says workers accept the bot under high WIP and therefore generate more corrections. More correction is more detection, or harder cases, not acceptance. That story also conflicts with §5, which correctly treats human “errors” as possible bot fixes. §2 says end-of-shift Quality Check batching swells late errors; §5 says batched checks change when errors are observed. Both cannot be the operating claim.

§3 names the right comparisons, but the code would not execute them. `view_events_distribution_graph` is not a WIP-by-error scatter; `discover_batches` is given nonexistent `time_window` and `case_id` parameters; `get_rework_cases_per_activity` has no `activity` or `max_wait` argument. Service time is never built from `start_timestamp` and `time:timestamp`. A linear `sequence_position` term misses the error pattern that falls, then rises after position 50. `filter_case_performance` does not adjust for complexity.

Intervention 2’s main risk is omitted: moving complex work out of the low-error opening and into the high-WIP window can worsen quality, and seniors’ early selection may be protective. Intervention 6 claims fewer late errors from deferring Quality Check without counting the detection displacement §5 acknowledges. The fairness Gini uses raw event counts, so equal counts can still hide seniors carrying the complex work.
