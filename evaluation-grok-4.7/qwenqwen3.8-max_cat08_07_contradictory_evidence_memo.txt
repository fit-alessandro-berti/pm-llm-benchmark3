7.5

The memo meets the required structure, stays within the word limit, and lands on a defensible hybrid: keep the simple-house gains, stop survey-skipping and billing-before-activation where the tail and rework worsened, and gate any wider rollout on new evidence. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated; the five metric conflicts are explained with the fitness/precision gap, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal profile, and resource concentration; A–E are judged with conditions rather than treated as one dominant option.

The score is held down by several concrete weaknesses. The complex-segment gates say complaint rate, manual activation, and second visits should “return to at least baseline levels.” For those metrics lower is better, so that wording points the wrong way and conflicts with the earlier “does not increase” test. Low precision is partly described as allowing “undesirable” behavior; precision is about excess permitted behavior, not desirability. Segment results are treated as solid enough to scale simple houses even though segment and building-risk attributes are missing before pilot week 3, and that limit is not carried into the confidence of the recommendation. Rural installs (worse tail, first-time-right, and complaints) are never assigned a path. Ranking conformance monitoring first for decision value is weak once those behaviors are already paused; survey-skip validity, a stratified experiment, and 60-day cost would move the scale decision more. The 85% coordinator cap is an unanchored example.
