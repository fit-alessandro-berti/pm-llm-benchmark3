6.5

The design correctly refuses to recode rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and treats activation as selection rather than ordinary censoring. The maturity date is right: activation on or before 2025-10-13 is the conservative point at which 90 days plus a 35-day feed lag has elapsed by 2026-02-15. Those strengths do not offset a label leak in the required skeleton, an estimand that drifts across sections, and a validation plan that confounds the only scored test with the policy break.

The primary estimand is `Pr(default_90d = 1 | prefix at L, eventual activation under the regime in force at L)`. Conditioning on a future activation is a coherent retrospective association, and the competing-outcome alternative is the right contrast. Three definitional gaps remain. The regime that selects activation is the regime at the decision, which need not be the regime at L when a case crosses 1 July 2025. The fitted population is narrower than that estimand: section 2 drops any case whose first three events already contain Accepted or a later milestone, while section 1 only says “before any terminal pre-activation exit.” Complete-case fitting then conditions again on the feed having arrived, so the estimator is for activated accounts with observed labels, not for all historical activations. The alternative `Y` lists five terminal states and has no slot for still-open cases or activated accounts whose label is immature or missing, even though those are exactly the censored and selectively unobserved rows the pathway model must retain.

The cohort table’s totals add up, and the split of the 3,600 unlabeled activations into “not yet mature” versus “overdue after the maximum lag” is the right operational cut. Withdrawn cases are described as not exposed to credit; withdrawal can occur after credit review, so that cell overstates what is known. The table is also still an extract-time outcome inventory. It does not show the further loss from the pre-decision landmark, so the 21,600 labeled rows are not the analysis cohort.

The missingness taxonomy is the strongest conceptual section. Ordinary censoring, feed delay, activation selection, transfer, and permanently missing partner feeds are separated, and ignorability of missing feeds is correctly marked untestable. The primary strategy then quietly drops overdue missing labels. Inverse-probability weighting and worst/best-case bounds are appropriate sensitivities for a rate, but they are underspecified for a predictive model: filling every missing label with 0 or 1 and refitting changes ranking, not just prevalence. Transfer follow-up on the linked 60% does not state that a partner platform’s default definition may be non-transportable.

Prefix rules are only partly coherent. Fixed `k` and elapsed-time landmarks are both specified, short traces are not padded, loops are kept, and renaming is applied before prefixing. “`k = 3` corresponds to first Credit Review” is an assumption, not a landmark: variable paths mean event 3 may be a document request or may already be a decision, and fast accepts are removed with no discussion of how that shifts default risk. Elapsed-time text says a case that ended before `t` is not at risk; the code keeps every event up to `start + 7 days`, including Accept, Reject, and activation. “Near-exact” migration duplicates are described in prose and implemented as exact key matches only.

The feature list has the right as-of idea: static attributes, within-prefix control-flow and timing, and historical resource or queue rates restricted to labels already observable at the prefix. It never notes that a mature trailing default rate is stale by roughly the 125-day horizon, or that a raw timestamp feature mostly identifies the policy era.

The leakage table itself is sound for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-prefix events, same-day `documents_total`, backfilled `risk_score`, extraction-time experience, `label_available_at`, and `default_90d`. The skeleton does not enforce it. After the inner join, `feature_cols` is “everything except a denylist.” That denylist names fields that were never joined. It leaves `cohort_state`, which is a deterministic recoding of `default_90d` once only observed labels remain, and it leaves `y_primary` inside the feature list that is then passed to the training frame again. `activation_time` is also retained, although activation is after the prediction point. A model fit on that frame can separate the classes without using the process. The printed audit only counts forbidden activities and does not assert an allowlist, so it cannot catch this. The same pipeline deletes unlabeled mature activations before the comment that says to fit a label-observation model, so the proposed weighting sensitivity cannot be run on `model_df`. Variants and the DFG are built on the full prefix log, including the later windows. The seven-day prefix is unused. Case starts are never restricted to 1 January 2024–31 December 2025. The block is also well beyond the requested length, roughly 240 lines against an approximate ceiling of 160.

The temporal split is justified, and a random split would mix the July 2025 policy change, the October 2025 rename, and unequal label maturity. The chosen windows undo much of that care. Train and validation are entirely pre-policy; the only outcome-scored test is 1 July–30 September 2025, so policy shift and temporal failure are the same contrast. Recalibrating the post-policy regime “if drift is material” has no post-policy labels except the test labels. September starts are mature only if activation occurred within about two weeks of 13 October 2025, so the scored test subset over-represents short cycles. The 1–14 October gap is neither a test nor a drift window. Metrics match a 12.5% binary complete-case outcome (2,700/21,600), including PR-AUC, Brier score, and calibration. They do not measure discrimination at the moment of scoring, when many prefixes will never activate.

Section 8 draws the right scientific boundary between association among historically activated accounts and a counterfactual default risk for people the lender never booked. The deployment mechanisms are concrete: the score can change who is activated, rewrite queues and prefixes, alter limits or collections enough to move `default_90d`, and change transfer and missing-feed rates. The recommended use, stratification inside the already activated portfolio, sits awkwardly on a three-event pre-decision score. Once activation is known, the first three events are a dominated feature set relative to the information available at activation.

The skeleton does call `read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, a start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, `discover_dfg`, and `get_variants`, and it does not invent a PM4Py survival routine. Conceptually the study is aimed at the right question. As an implementable, leakage-safe design it is not reliable: the training matrix contains the target, the validation cut cannot separate policy change from predictive failure, and the formal population changes from section 1 to the code without being restated as a new estimand.
