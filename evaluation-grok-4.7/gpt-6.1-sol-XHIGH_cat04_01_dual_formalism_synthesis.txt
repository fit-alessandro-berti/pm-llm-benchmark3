The models are semantically strong: the POWL partial order matches the intended cross-branch dependencies, the process tree is a real under-approximation rather than a disguised exact model, loop/XOR/optional-supplier structure is right, and the eight witnesses actually test the claimed gap. The score is not higher because several required artifacts are looser or less reliable than the core construction.

The dependency table does not stay in its own schema. D01 stuffs two boundary facts into one slash-separated source and target, and D03/D10 use directed columns for symmetric or negative relations. The rationale repairs this, but the table as specified is not clean.

The process-tree loss is identified correctly (`RC < PV`, `CM < DC`, `CM < B`, `CM < U`, including the invisible delay of the supplier skip). Calling that loss “the” compact single-label cost is plausible, but it is only motivated as “no straightforward nesting,” not shown. The language claim `L(tree) ⊊ L(powl) = L(intended finite completions)` is therefore a well-supported reading, not a proof.

POWL edge choice, high-risk `StrictPartialOrder` (both reviews, no order), supplier `XOR` with one silent skip, and `LOOP(do=ET→IT, redo=AP)` are the right operators and child order. Materializing the transitive closure does not change that partial order, but the comment treats transitivity as something `add_edge` might fail to enforce. In POWL, precedence is already the transitive closure of the edges; the extra edges are redundant and the justification is a semantic slip.
