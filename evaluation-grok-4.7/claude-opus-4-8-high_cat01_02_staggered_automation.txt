The required structure is complete and the causal framing is often sophisticated: availability is separated from dose, SUTVA and treatment-version problems are explicit, staggered TWFE negative weights are correctly rejected, median and p90 are not collapsed, CSAT is treated as unidentified, and “reduces cycle time by X%” is refused. Those strengths do not rescue the quantitative core.

The sensitivity interval that drives §§5 and 7 does not follow from the inputs given. Gamma’s full-window median change is −8.3%. Subtracting a never-treated June→August change of about −7% to −16% produces roughly −1% to +8%, not the stated −3% to +2%. The windows are also incomparable: a January–June mean versus an adopter’s own past is not a June-to-August point change. The same section never reports the comparison the primary design requires. Over January–March versus April–August, Alpha and Beta median changes are about −0.4h and +1.9h while Delta and Epsilon rise by about +6.0h and +4.4h. Their p90 rises are also smaller than the never-treated rises (about +10h versus +15h to +18h). That contrast is still confounded, but it is not “no movement, possible tail harm,” and the published ranges do not contain it.

Other quantitative statements are wrong or over-strong. Gamma’s pre-period escalation mean is 8.7, not 8.4. Within the post window, automated-fast-path share and median cycle time move inversely for Alpha and Beta, not “near zero/positive.” Averaging the whole post period hides the path the answer’s own dynamic estimand emphasizes: May–June is the bad period, and August, at peak dose, is Alpha’s best median. Selection on a persistently high level does not “guarantee” regression to the mean. Escalation jumps again by about 1 point at adoption after the common March fraud-desk shift, so “not attributable to automation” is too absolute. The lead observed claim treats all adopters as median-flat and p90-up about 10%; Gamma is −8% and −1%.

The mechanism table misses the capacity calculation. Alpha’s arrivals per FTE rise from about 30 to the low 40s while the median stays near 49 hours. Flat cycle time under a routing surge and a two-FTE loss is exactly the substitution-versus-capacity question, and it is never measured. Conditioning the preferred estimator on post-rollout arrivals and FTE is a bad-control problem and is not flagged.

Section 9 builds relative-time dummies and then estimates the single-coefficient branch-and-month FE model the prose condemns. It does not fit the group-time design, does not reproduce the §5 bounds, and cannot support the reported conclusion. The stepped-wedge recommendation is directionally sensible, but two branches cannot carry leave-one-out robustness or a pre-registered 5% scale-up rule, and no within-branch encouragement design is considered. The “conditionally causal” rung is a claim about a future experiment, not a conditional effect in these data.
