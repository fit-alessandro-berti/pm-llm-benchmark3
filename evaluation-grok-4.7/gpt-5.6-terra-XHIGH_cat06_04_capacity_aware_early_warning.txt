The policy is structurally complete and gets the decisions that matter right: I-03 is ineligible once diagnosis is complete, I-05 is suppressed as a refresh with no new evidence, I-07 is a valid re-entry that still does not earn a swarm, I-06 is held for data validation, and one slot is reserved. Calibration is applied in the right direction, effects are not treated as known causal quantities, and spillover is carried into both ranking and the scenario table. The midpoint scores and the optimistic/base spillover arithmetic check out.

The adverse result does not. Benefit €5.6–9.6k minus €4.75k minus harm €8.1–16.2k is about −€15.4k to −€3.3k. The table states that net as €3.3k to €15.4k. The following sentence treats the outcome as unfavorable, so this is a dropped sign on the downside number the allocation is supposed to survive. That is the most serious defect in the memo.

Section 1 is also not the rule that Section 2 executes. The stated score caps reduction at calibrated untreated risk and shrinks effects when the segment is sparse. The reported scores are raw midpoint reduction times value, minus €450, minus the full burden. For I-09, the sparse Non-Native-Language Support case, that rule would shrink the effect; the case is instead scored at a full 0.24 and placed second in the queue. Section 5 then says not to down-rank that case for uncertainty. Those instructions conflict, and the portfolio follows the version that spends a scarce slot on the least identified effect.

Charging the listed false-alarm burden with probability 1 is a conservative penalty, not an expectation, even though the benefit term is written as one. It does not reorder the top of this list, but it is not the expected-value rule the section claims to use. The same gap appears in the queue term: W_Q is correctly left unknown, then a €90–180k range is invented and the bold nets depend on it. At the top of that range, I-02’s own gate (W_Q < €135k) fails, while the three-case base net stays positive only because I-04 subsidizes a negative-marginal slot. The memo states the per-case gate and still presents the three-case program as the base case.

Smaller faults accumulate. The adverse row adds 45–90 minutes of delay on top of a breach-probability harm that is already the model of that congestion. The two-hour active-alert rule can still cover I-05 at a 30-minute refresh cadence, so “ineligible” is stronger than the temporal policy. The post-swarm cool-down (“before Diagnosis Complete or within 24 hours, whichever comes first”) does not say when a repeat becomes allowed. “Clinically” comparable is the wrong domain. The concave-value cap and the public-sector obligation floor are proposed and then not used on I-01, the case that would test them.

Section 6 is the strongest part: stage-at-delivery monitoring, segment calibration, interference-aware randomization on marginal cases only, treatment logging so later models do not train on post-swarm labels, and explicit triggers to change the reserve and the queue multiplier. The missing piece is a numeric downside budget, which Section 1 makes a deployment gate and never specifies.

This is a serious portfolio rule with the right case calls and auditable working, undermined by a wrong-signed adverse net and by a scoring rule that is not the rule used to award slots.
