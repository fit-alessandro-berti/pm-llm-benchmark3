6.5

The answer meets the required structure, respects the constraints, and is numerically careful about the tables it cites. It is not nearly flawless. The central causal reading is wrong, the implementation contradicts the stated strategy, and several claims are stronger than the evidence.

The preliminary adjustment controls for risk band, documentation completeness, channel, household size, prior cases, region, and month. It does not control for language need or accommodation request, which are the policy triggers that define Groups B and C. Sections 1, 3, and 8 still treat those coefficients as residual gaps “after measured complexity,” and Section 8 calls Supervisor Review the main unexplained difference. For C, V5 already pairs Accommodation Review with Supervisor Review and a 21.6-day cycle; with accommodation omitted from the model, +4.1 days and +5.5pp supervisor review are not shown to be unexplained. The same problem applies to B and language support.

That error is repeated in the code. Section 2 says to match on language need, but `spec_base` includes accommodation and risk and omits language. The appeal logit then controls for `has_super` and `has_reqdoc` after Section 2 said those post-treatment steps must be excluded from total-effect models. `unexplained_super` treats high risk or an accommodation request as justifying Supervisor Review, which contradicts the prose claim that no such rule exists.

The code also would not run as written: `numpy` is not imported, `month` and `caseworker_id` are never created, and caseworker random effects are not identified when bilingual and accommodation staff are assigned by group. A global log skeleton at `noise_threshold=0.0` is not a useful fairness check. `applicant_group` may be a trace attribute, so event-attribute filtering is the wrong operation. `get_variants`, `filter_variants`, and `filter_paths_performance` are never used.

Visible composition is not reconciled with the regression. Top variants cover about 82% of A and 99% of B. Supervisor Review appears mainly in V3 and V5, which is only about 14% of B versus 11% of A, plus whatever sits in A’s missing 18%. That does not by itself support an adjusted +8pp. “Needs language support” is 57% for B while V4 is 24%; accommodation is requested for 62% of C while V5 is 31%. The answer notes that V4 may be truncated, then neither tests under-provision nor treats abbreviation as blocking that comparison. High-risk share for D is 51% against a 28% V3 share, and that gap is also left unexamined.

Smaller overclaims accumulate. Adjusted cycle-time gaps are asserted to be means. The 3.8- and 4.7-day waits are described as support arriving “too late to be useful,” although those steps precede eligibility. Document request is called a loop. Dual-hatted specialists are described as the same person reviewing their own accommodation assessment. Section 4 says regional differences are not group-specific, contrary to the stated B and C handover patterns in two regions. “Largely explained by complexity” is stated in the same paragraph that says a within-stratum check is still required. Discrimination is defined in Section 2 as unjustified treatment attributable to process or rules, then narrowed in Section 8 to intent.

The capacity, SLA, handover, risk-score, and mediator points are the strongest parts, and the answer correctly refuses to read raw approval gaps as discrimination or to remove support steps. Those merits do not repair the misreading of what the adjustment identified.
