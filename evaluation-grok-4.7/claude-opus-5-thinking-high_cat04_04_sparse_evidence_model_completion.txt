The three candidates are genuine competing hypotheses, the 7-variant enumeration, the balanced 12-trace partial order, and the permissive length bounds 20/40/70 are combinatorially correct, and the evidence ledger, missingness/optionality split, and D7 concurrency caveat are unusually careful. The score is held down by real defects, not by presentation.

The permissive inductive bias is not internally consistent. It claims every rule-consistent behavior a practitioner could defend is in, then adds an unbounded Supplier Confirmation loop that the audit itself calls possibly clearly undesirable, while still banning Credit Check / Engineering Review rework even though no domain rule forbids that either and the log supports both bans equally (zero repeats). That is two different standards. The loop is also the main reason the language is infinite, so the “reasonable vs forbidden” boundary the task asks for is drawn past the author’s own risk labels.

Smaller inaccuracies: the D5 ledger cell calls `CC → SC` the weakest constraint consistent with the 23 Supplier Confirmation cases; §4 correctly treats it as the strongest fitting precedence, and those two claims conflict. In §6, `CC, ER, EX` and the other four balanced unobserved traces are called “balanced-only” even though the same section says the permissive language is a strict superset. “All tasks live” is used for workflow-net soundness; no-dead-transitions is weaker than liveness once `Register Order` has fired. Validation design, duplicate-label/scalability discussion, and the alternative mixture-of-sequential-policies interpretation are strong. The models themselves are not wrong on the observed log. The write-up is not nearly flawless.
