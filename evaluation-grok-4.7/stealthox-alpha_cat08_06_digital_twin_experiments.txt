6.3

The design is a real discrete-event twin, not an independent-case replay: waiting is emergent, objections stay latent, calendars and cross-case coupling are in scope, and optimality is correctly disclaimed. Those choices are sound. The score is held down by concrete statistical, identification, and experiment-logic errors that a municipality could not implement as written.

The lognormal quantile fit is inconsistent. σ is taken from median and p90, which fixes the median at exp(μ), but the code then sets μ = ln(median) − ½σ². Under `Generator.lognormal`, that makes the mean equal the reported median and shifts every service, applicant, and objection draw downward by about exp(−½σ²) (roughly 0.55–0.7 for the wider activities). `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as a 5-tuple; the public return is the performance graph plus start and end activities, so the saved “edge performance” is the end-activity map. Sojourn times are also labeled as waiting targets even though the same write-up correctly says those edges mix service, queue, and closed hours.

Several mechanisms are underspecified or contradictory. D6 is “reserve one planner,” but the state model and code switch every queue to aging priority and never hold a planner back. D1, D5, D8, and D9 have no numeric lever (how many missing-document loops disappear, when legal pre-check starts, which cases skip environmental review, how early warning shortens notice). Activity-to-pool assignment is missing, including the scheduler and the fact that decision drafting shares the planner pool. Baseline council-week frequency is never set, yet legal shutdowns and stresses S2/S5 depend on it. No-show probability is not identified from scheduling-to-inspection gaps; those gaps are mostly the stated 8-day wait. December ×0.8 and a 15-day applicant p90 are tagged as fitted although they are not in the summary. The supplied tree’s “Completeness rework” label does not match the repeated “Completeness check” in the log, and the parallel reviews will still create sequential DFG edges; neither pitfall is handled.

The experiment and decision rules do not support the claims made of them. E18/E19 are said to test D9, but they compare baseline with D10, and D10 is only D1–D4. S4 likewise confounds D1 with D2–D4. In-sample acceptance is median ±15% and p90 ±20%, while adoption requires a 10% median cut; that gap is never reconciled with paired common-random-number cancellation. Welch warm-up assumes a steady mean this seasonal process does not have. Absolute gates (85% SLA, utilization forced into 0.70–0.90, two of three stresses) can reject both the baseline and useful capacity-releasing policies, and completed-case cycle times ignore right-censoring of the long complex-variance tail.
