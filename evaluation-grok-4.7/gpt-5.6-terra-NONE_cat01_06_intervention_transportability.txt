7.1

The transport judgment is right, and the case data are handled carefully. The answer refuses to treat Site A’s nine-hour median drop as an identified effect, keeps Document Check behind statutory originals, and recommends a segmented conditional pilot rather than copying the all-case design. Format, required nodes, word limits, and the eight-assumption register are met. The score is held down by formal control-flow errors, an unsigned quantitative table, and a few internal contradictions.

The process comparison is accurate and covers the required mechanisms: concurrency, originals, legal review, specialization, locking, mix shift, tail, rework, and timestamp artifact. Co-interventions are separated from parallelization in the evidence table, with appropriately low confidence. The recommendation names the right first stratum and the right expansion, modification, and stop conditions.

The transport graph contains every required variable and uses selection nodes, but it is unsigned. Several arrows are inhibitory (`originals_required`, `IT_case_lock`, and `role_specialization` into `true_overlap`), yet they read as the same kind of cause as `true_overlap -> cycle_time`. `case_complexity -> originals_required` is not supported; those are separate Site B facts. `cycle_time -> appeal` is also a loose causal claim.

The scenario section includes a genuine median-up / tail-down case, but the change columns do not have a stable sign. Optimistic cells report “5 to 9 h” as an improvement, while other cells use “+” for worsening. One cell writes “average/median” as if those were the same functional. Eligible shares are not derived from the 62% originals, 40% legal, and 48% high-complexity margins, so the 20–30% digital-only share is unjustified once those constraints intersect. Receipt-aware “eligible share” is a phrase rather than a share. The directly-follows counts are unused, including the useful check that Originals Receipt precedes Document Check about as often as the 62% rule predicts, and that supplements are 360 of 1,360 legal cases.

The candidate design contradicts itself on originals. One place creates a blocked Document Check and releases it on receipt; another creates a non-executable Document Check only after receipt, which makes both “non-executable” and “cannot start before receipt” vacuous. The textual POWL does not close the supplement loop. Its supplement branch ends at Legal Review and then falls through to Notify, so a final Approve or Reject is not preserved. The prose says the path repeats; the expression does not.

The PM4Py fragment gets the Site A trees, the Petri-net conversions, and the identity/document AND-join into Assessment right. The legal fragment does not. `supplement_path` is a child of both the loop and a later XOR, so the POWL is not a tree and can fail conversion or duplicate behavior. That post-loop XOR can still take the supplement path and reach Notify with no Approve or Reject. Broad `try/except` blocks also mean a failed soundness or playout check still looks like a completed model. Capacity, locks, and data-dependent routing are correctly named as outside the net.

The pilot measures fidelity separately from cycle time and anticipates queue interference, but the assignment rule is muddled: case-level 1:1 randomization, a team/week stepped rollout, and contemporaneous controls are different designs. The “200 vs 200” support rule is not tied to the heavy tail it is supposed to stabilize. WIP limits equal to the four pools’ headcount are stated without saying what happens to blocked or absent-reviewer work.

These are material defects in model fidelity and quantitative hygiene, not presentation nits. The operational conclusion remains usable: do not copy Site A’s universal parallel task creation; test true overlap only where documents are already legally available, and watch the assessment and legal tails.
