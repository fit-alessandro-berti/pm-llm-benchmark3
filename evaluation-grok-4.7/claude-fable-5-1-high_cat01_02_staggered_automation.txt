7.5

The analysis is rigorous and well calibrated on the central decision: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is correctly rejected, median and p90 are kept separate, the rollout is treated as a selected bundle rather than a technology experiment, and the pre/post and never-treated arithmetic that can be checked by hand is largely accurate. The remaining defects are logical and interpretive, not cosmetic, and they sit in the places a decision-maker would quote.

The conditionally causal takeaway still says availability of automation reduced median cycle time by about 1–7 hours. That drops the bundle the rest of the answer insists on. Parallel trends against Delta and Epsilon identify automation plus the routing campaign, local practice changes, and unequal staffing, not availability alone. Section 7’s median interval of −1 to −7 hours also excludes zero while confidence is only 55, and the threat table itself allows about +1 hour if trends are nonparallel. Those three statements cannot all be the uncertainty claim.

Process reading is one step short of the data. Rework exceeds escalation in every pre-period (Alpha 13.2 vs about 8.5), so loops are not confined to the specialist path and a mechanical fast-path composition story does not automatically fit a −2 point drop. The p90/median widening is mostly a May, network-wide break coinciding with the complexity step; April ratios for Alpha and Beta stay near 2.01–2.02, and Beta’s Q1-to-August widening matches the controls. Grouping Alpha/Beta as extra tail amplification overstates Beta. Below roughly a 50% fast-path share the median case is still outside a skim-the-easy-cases path, so Gamma’s improvement at 25–32% share cannot be composition; that quantile point is never made.

The recommended 50/50 case randomization does not identify the section 2 “pure technology effect net of queue.” Both arms share specialist and fraud-desk queues. It also fights the requirement of three months at a 40%+ fast-path share. The code’s event-time indicators clip Gamma’s early months into k=−3 and pool k≥2, so the reported leads of +0.5 and +1.1 hours are not the clean k=−3/−2 checks the text describes. The primary stacked cohort estimator is argued and then not estimated. Smaller slips: the Q1 median range 46.9–50.4 hours omits Beta’s 45.4–46.4, “about 7 points per month” ignores the final +2 steps, and 45 times the stated CSAT intensity coefficient is not +0.11. Untreated branches sitting at a 4–6% automated-fast-path floor is noted and never interpreted.
