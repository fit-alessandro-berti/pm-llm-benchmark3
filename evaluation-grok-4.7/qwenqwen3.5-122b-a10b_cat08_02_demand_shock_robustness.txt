The redesign is structured correctly and uses many of the right quantities (peak mix shift, near-100% assay and technical-validation utilization, specialist and medical queues, intervention conflicts). It is not reliable process reasoning. Several claims contradict the logs, and the frequency and timing evidence that should drive the design is mostly unused or compared incorrectly.

The clearest factual errors are these. Repeat activity is described as “1140 normal → 1260 peak,” as if repeats barely rose. Those counts cover 8 weeks versus 3 weeks; as a share of assay outcomes the repeat path roughly doubles (about 6.7% to about 14.8%), and daily repeat volume roughly triples. The same unequal-window comparison appears for recollection (760 vs 780 “despite lower total volume”), which hides an approximate tripling of daily recollection. Scenario 2 treats routing Routine work to M2 as current logic. That routing is candidate I3, not the baseline. M2 is the machine already tied to repeats, yet P4 still sends Routine volume onto a degraded M2 and then omits P4 from the trade-off table, so the repeat consequence is never scored. P2 turns a specialist-review queue into a trigger for a medical-validation overtime shift. Those are separate pools; medical overtime does not cover the Friday specialist gap.

Process-mining use is thin relative to what the logs show. Batch-wait share rises from roughly 4% to 13%, Urgent bypass falls from 74% to 58%, technical-validation output going to medical validation rises from about 25% to about 42%, and hold-for-specialist rises several-fold beyond the complex-panel mix shift. The long performance edges—recollection return at 42h/60h, repeat return at 10h/26h, batch wait at 9h/22h, technical-to-medical at 8h/24h, hold-to-medical at 18h/48h—are almost absent from the migration story. Without those, the bottleneck sequence is a utilization narrative rather than a demonstrated flow.

The package also breaks its own constraints. R2’s next bottleneck is listed as “None.” R4 is called robust, but its guard (technical-validation queue under 4h) is false in the peak it is meant to fix, where that queue is 16h and utilization is 101%. A technical-validation queue is treated as a leading signal that medical validation is about to block, even though technical validation is itself over capacity and many cases release without medical review. Monitoring anchors Urgent performance to the historical p90 of 19h, which already misses the 18h penalty, and does not watch the Routine 72h target or complex-panel tails. Thresholds such as “50 cases” and “85% courier on-time” are not grounded in the data.
