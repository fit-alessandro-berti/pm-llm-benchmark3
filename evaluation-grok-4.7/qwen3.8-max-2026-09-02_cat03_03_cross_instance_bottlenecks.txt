The final write-up meets the required structure and most of the analytical brief: typed graph with observed versus inferred edges, several multi-type motifs, seven timed counterfactuals, a full ranking table that does not put machine capacity first, flattening blind spots, three multi-object narratives with precedence separated from speculation, a six-item intervention portfolio including sync, tools, shipment policy, and logging, and adequate falsifiers. Arithmetic on the required waits (J1 3 h 55 min, J2 3 h 40 min, J1 pack-to-dispatch 5 h 55 min / partner wait 5 h 40 min, J6 1 h 20 min with 50 min after 16:35, L7 3 h 30 min) is correct, and the L7-versus-B2 caveat is appropriately non-causal.

The score is held down by several concrete flaws. The FIX-A “conservative” avoidable delay of 1 h 40 min (10:05–11:45) is not conservative: CNC-2 is only documented idle for 42 minutes (10:05–10:47), so the estimate assumes machine availability the log does not show, and it conflicts with the same paragraph’s appeal to that idle window. Rank 1, “batch synchronization amplified by shared RACK-7,” over-weights the rack: B1’s start aligns with J3’s 11:55 readiness, and on B2 the rack is free at 14:35, before J5 is ready at 14:40, so it is a near-constraint rather than the observed binding one. That row also double-counts RACK-7 into rank 3, where the supporting evidence never mentions it. Narrative 1’s “because J3 is ready only at 11:55” treats succession as the cause. The job-flattening claim that dispatch may not be attached to the job sits poorly with Pack → Dispatch frequency 6; the median of 1.1 h hides J1’s tail, it does not remove the edge. Summing the two shipment medians (0.8 h + 0.3 h) is used as if it were a total handling median. The PM4Py block names the required functions but compares row counts, leaves OTG/ETOT unused, and never actually contrasts two object-centric frequencies.
