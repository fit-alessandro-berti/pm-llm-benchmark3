6.3

The redesign is structurally complete and often directionally sound, but it is not reliable as process analysis. Several claims contradict the logs, queueing direction, or the normal-period baselines the policies are supposed to use.

The strongest parts are the scenario split, the refusal to turn every manager idea on permanently, and the explicit Urgent versus Routine versus Complex trade-offs. Early triage, courier smoothing, a peak medical-validation shift, and conditional Urgent bypass are sensible. The table, redesign-caused vulnerability, and leading-versus-lagging distinction meet the requested form.

The evidence handling is weaker than the structure suggests. Repeat transitions are described as rising from 1,140 to 1,260, which ignores the 8-week versus 3-week windows; the weekly rate roughly triples and the repeat share roughly doubles. Accession corrections are said to rise from 500 to 420, which is arithmetically false, although the percentage claim is right. The 54% of cases with some queue over 12 hours is attributed to intake, yet peak courier queue is 7.5 hours; the long queues are accession, assay, technical validation, medical validation, and specialist review. Specialist holds and repeats also rise far more than the mix shift can explain, and that is missed.

Bottleneck migration reverses cause and effect in places. Clearing medical validation does not create new assay demand; those cases are already past assay. Smoothing courier arrivals reduces arrival variability, so it should not spike preparation. Recollection returns in about 42–60 hours, so they do not clash with the next same-day courier wave. Medical validation is treated as the primary winter bottleneck while technical validation is already at 101% and the assay queue is 18 hours. An evening medical-validation shift also cannot clear complex cases stuck in specialist hold.

The control rules are poorly calibrated. A 4-hour assay spill trigger equals the normal median queue. An Urgent preparation trigger of 2 hours sits below the normal 2.5-hour median. The cross-training rule requires a short technical-validation queue at the same time accession is above 10 hours, which the peak data do not support. Batch size is reduced from 24 even though that larger batch is never adopted. Spilling work off M2 is marked as increasing repeats, despite M2 being the less reliable machine. Early rejection is treated as neutral for Urgent work, without counting the 60-hour peak recollection loop against the 18-hour penalty. The dashboard then switches from hours to “cases,” and a p90 Urgent warning of 15–17 hours is already breached by the normal p90 of 19 hours.
