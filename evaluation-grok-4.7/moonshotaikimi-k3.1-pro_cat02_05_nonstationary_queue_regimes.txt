The final analysis is substantively strong and mostly numerically reliable, but it is not nearly flawless. Repeated comparison figures are concatenated into unreadable strings, and that defect hits the sections that carry the main quantitative claims.

What holds up:

- Regime coverage is a complete 28-day partition, and the reported rates, WIP, FTE ratios, net \(A-C\), and weekday/weekend contrasts match the table (for example, 701 vs 697 arrivals/completions, WIP 142, pooled 2519/2409, Apr 20–28 imbalance 106).
- Outage and recovery are kept separate from steady high throughput. Little’s Law is not fed median service time; opening-hour \(L/\lambda\) is distinguished from elapsed flow time; missing endpoints keep every window at “approximate only” or “not defensible.”
- The 08:00–09:59 mismatch with `peak_08_10_pct` is real (Apr 7: 16.8% vs 31; Apr 21: 9.6% vs 47; Apr 25: 11.8% vs 22). Hourly Fano/CV values, the Apr 21 top-3 share (72/115), and the Apr 20 complex split (means about 22.43 vs 43.29) are correct.
- Assumptions, alternative backlog-state segmentation, demand/mix/capacity/backlog separation, and the single pandas block (PM4Py only in comments, Little’s Law only on named windows) meet the requested format.

What keeps the score down:

- Core contrasts are garbled in the final text: “20.9%44.6%,” “2535 min,” “4783 min,” “114115/day,” “5.936.83,” “100.6121/day,” “44.337.0,” “8374,” “433632,” and “5235.” A reader can misread those as different statistics. Section 2 still has the clean ranges, so this is a presentation failure rather than a wrong model, but it damages the required pooled-versus-segmented comparisons.
- The pooled weekday/weekend service-median contrast (30.55 vs 32.0) is itself era-mixed, which is a weaker illustration than the within-era WIP/FTE reversal.
- A few displayed identities are rounded hard (18.667/7.185 = 2.60; lag-1 “about 0.93”), and one Little’s Law cell mixes “approximate only” with “not defensible” instead of a single verdict.

No material arithmetic or queueing-definition error turned up in the readable figures. The deduction is for clarity and a few soft contrasts, not for a failed analysis.
