7.5

The answer is a strong, mostly well-audited regime analysis, but it is not nearly flawless. Several volunteered numerical claims are false, and one central queueing consistency check does not hold.

The required eight-part structure is met, and the main operating story is right: weekday/weekend scale differences, 2026-04-15 as a capacity stop rather than a demand shock, 2026-04-16/17 as a transient drain that must not be used as a throughput benchmark, a 2026-04-20 mix/service step with flat completions and ρ crossing 1, and a weakly identified staffing change on 2026-04-29. Little’s Law is correctly kept in opening hours, arrival and completion proxies are compared, and the pooled window is rejected. The hourly audit is real: outage zeros confirm the 07:00 bin origin, raw Fano is correctly treated as confounded by the diurnal profile, and 2026-04-21 is a batch-like shape rather than a rescaled ordinary day. Core balances check out (A1 net +4 and λ≈8.35/h; D1 net +94 and λ=9.50/h; end cum_net +110 versus a WIP rise of only about 30). Assumptions about lognormal/Weibull means are labeled, and the Python block actually implements the segmentation, dispersion, autocorrelation, change-point scan, and windowed L/λ calculations, with PM4Py kept in comments.

The serious faults are internal, not omissions.

- The recovery comparison is arithmetically wrong. From 2026-04-28 to 2026-05-01, `mean_wip` falls 50→32 and `cum_net` falls 128→110. Both decline by 18 cases. “WIP recovered ~5× faster” is not supported. During the intervention the two accounts move together; the large discrepancy is in the earlier buildup. The sentence inverts the evidence it is meant to interpret.
- Baseline “mutual consistency” is false. A1 gives L≈20, X≈8.3/h and E[S]≈0.47 h, so about 4 cases are in service and about 16 are not, with implied wait near 2 hours. At ρ≈0.65 and 6 servers, Erlang-C / Kingman waiting time is on the order of minutes, and even the C=1 bound is only about 13 minutes. ρ≈0.65 and a 1.95 h server wait cannot be true together. The answer never confronts this level contradiction, despite correctly flagging the separate 128-versus-52 stock gap. That also makes the A1 verdict `applicable` too clean relative to the earlier claim that no level-based queueing law is safe until the counters are reconciled.
- The two-week clearance policy is inconsistent with the answer’s own drain rate. Current E1 already nets about −6 cases per weekday. Clearing +110 in ten weekdays needs about 11/day total, hence about +5/day, not “11 extra completions” and +0.6 FTE stacked on the current 7.0. Adding 0.6 FTE to a system that already drains 6/day would clear the stock in roughly a week and a half, not two weeks.
- Smaller false or over-tight figures accumulate. Mean staffing is 144/28=5.14 FTE, not ~5.4, and that wrong mean is reused in the flagship pooled-staffing counterexample. The 5.4 FTE overload is about 25 excess cases per weekday, not ~20. 2026-04-16 is 129/(7×12)=1.54 completions per FTE-hour, not 1.49. D1 WIP is not monotonic (46, 44, 43). Pooled median service time is 31.5, not 32.5, and the pooled p90/median ratio is not larger than both component ratios when D1 is given as 2.4. Weekend arrival intensity is 3.74/h, not ~3.6; all-weekday intensity is 9.0/h, not ~8.9. Weekend capacity share is about 15%, not 12%. “Pure composition” overstates the staffing–WIP correlation, since the within-weekday correlation remains positive.

Uncertainty handling, outage/recovery separation, and the circularity warning on the load-state alternative are better than the mistakes. Those mistakes sit on the quantities a staffing decision would use, so they keep the answer well below a near-flawless score.
