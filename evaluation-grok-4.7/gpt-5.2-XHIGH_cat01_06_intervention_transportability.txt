The answer is complete and directionally right (`conditional go`, no universal copy of Site A, originals kept as a hard start constraint, confounders named, mediator-based pilot logic). It is not reliable on the quantitative and design points the task makes decisive. Several internal contradictions are large enough that the transport ranges and the pilot cannot be used as specified.

The scenario table contradicts its own bounds. The optimistic row treats Site A’s −6 h pre-assessment change as an upper bound, then states an eligible median-cycle change of −6 to −10 h. The digital-only row caps the mechanism at about 3.4 h of identity service plus a modest queue effect, then states −5 to −8 h. Those intervals are also larger than what Site B’s medians can support. Document service is 8.1 h and originals delay is 18 h on 62% of cases, while overall median wait before assessment is only 20 h and observed overlap is 6%. The service-time slice that parallelization can remove is on the order of the shorter check (about 3.4 h), and only when that check sits on the critical path. Because originals-required volume is 62%, the overall median case sits inside that mass; a digital-only program should be expected to move the conditional digital median and leave the overall median near zero. The table’s overall −1 to −3 h is a weighted blend of medians, which is not the median of the mixture. The tail-worsening scenario is present and useful, but its +10 to +25 h p90 in the legal scenario is asserted, not derived from the 360/1,360 supplement base rate.

The pilot cannot execute its own support rule. Site B completes about 3,400 cases in six months, roughly 130 a week. A 20–25% digital, no-legal stratum at 50/50 assignment yields about 15 treated cases a week, or roughly 100–140 treated cases in the stated 6–8 weeks. The support rule demands at least 400 treated cases per stratum. Stage 1 as written ends in a feasibility sample while the analysis plan treats it as an effect estimate. Case-level randomization within day also conflicts with the rule that reviewers not mix arms on a shift. With pools of 4, 7, 3, and 2, holding cases for an arm-specific shift adds queue delay to the primary cycle-time outcome. The section 3 proposal to factor queue huddles against parallelization never appears in the treatment versions.

The transport graph is incomplete relative to the prose. The text cites `legal_capacity → rework`, and that edge is absent. `assessment_capacity` feeds `waiting_before_assessment` and does not feed `cycle_time`, so post-join assessment service and queue are missing, while identity and document capacity also skip `true_overlap`. `queue_huddles → true_overlap` has no mechanism. Selection nodes are used correctly, and the narrative on shared versus site-specific paths is otherwise sound.

Evidence handling is strong on confounding and correctly refuses to call the 9 h median drop a treatment effect, then weakens that discipline in section 5 by using the confounded drop as a numerical bound. Transport confidence is not one scale: the added-FTE row has transferable component “none” and confidence 70, and the missing negative control has confidence 80. Throughput 4,800→5,200 with verification FTE 18→20 is about 267→260 completed cases per FTE. That per-FTE decline is never used, so the throughput row still reads as a possible gain from parallelization.

The Site B partial order for the non-legal strata is the right join: assessment waits on both checks, and document execution waits on originals when originals are required. The supplement fragment is written as a forced cycle (`Legal Review < Request Supplement < … < Legal Review`) even though 980 of 1,360 legal outcomes do not supplement. The recommendation itself matches the evidence: transfer only the overlap-to-join mechanism, start on digital, no-legal, low-to-mid complexity, and expand only if true overlap, join time, p90, and supplement rates behave. Assumption 3 restates a rule the prompt already gives (identity may start after registration). The eight tests are otherwise operational. Coverage across the ten required sections is high; the grade is held down by the inconsistent effect ranges, the impossible pilot sample, the assignment–interference clash, and the non-executable soundness check.
