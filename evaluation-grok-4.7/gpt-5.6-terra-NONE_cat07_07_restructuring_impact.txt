**6.8**

The memo meets the required structure and is balanced across East versus North/South, tails, fairness, learning, and a phased pilot. It is not nearly flawless: it leaves the management tables untested, and several process-mining measures are specified in a way that would mis-measure the effects they are meant to detect.

The main analytical gap is in the mechanism map and the scenarios that take those estimates as operating points. Adding the stated frequencies gives about 343 handovers per 100 cases now and about 359 after the change. Caseworker–specialist traffic rises from 36+31 to 41+36 per 100, so the embedded loop is relocated and enlarged, not removed. Frequency times median wait rises from roughly 588 to roughly 722 hour-handovers per 100 cases, about 23% more coordination delay under management’s own medians. Legal/risk volume rises from 9 to 12 per 100 (+33%) while utilization is already 91–93%. That load is infeasible in steady state without new capacity (implied utilization about 1.24). A move from 9.6h to 10.5h is not a credible consequence of that load. The optimistic scenario still treats 4.0h, 3.0h, and 10.5h as achievable with “adequate staffing,” without stating the capacity increment those waits require. Weighted technical utilization is about 73% today; a referral increase of 36 to 41 per 100, plus difficult quality checks and triage on the same queue, is a different demand shock from a flat “+15% utilization” benefit.

Section 7 has concrete measurement errors. The invented SLA map (72/120/240 hours) cannot reproduce today’s 6.5–9.8% miss rates when medians are already 6.8–8.6 days, so `sla_miss` would flag almost every high-priority case. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="event")` drops shared-service events from local cases and breaks handover, DFG, and variant comparisons. With only `time:timestamp`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time` cannot separate service from waiting; a utilization ratio built from it is not identified. `discover_performance_dfg` is left on its usual mean aggregation, which is the summary the rest of the answer says is misleading. Handover edges are raw counts, not per 100 cases, and the inter-event gap is labeled wait. Event-level means of `rework_flag` overweight long cases. `team` taken as the first event is often the bot, so the East versus North/South comparison is easy to mis-attribute. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` compares activity profiles, not shared case context; lower similarity between caseworkers and specialists is a learning signal only where those roles already perform overlapping activities, which the current split of local technical assessment versus casework does not establish. Several network calls pass `resource_key` / `timestamp_key` / `case_id_key` into wrappers that, in current PM4Py, accept a log plus a small parameter set and would raise `TypeError`. The version caveat does not fix that.

Smaller problems remain. In the optimistic scenario, the “early warning signs” row lists confirmation that the scenario is holding, not warnings. Direction arrows are missing on handover pairs, so some edges are readable only from surrounding prose. The pilot has no sample-size check for team × complexity × specialty × segment, and Phase 0 redefinition of queue events is not treated as a break in comparability with the historical log. Rework conditional on the preceding handover, which is how the baseline table is defined, is not computed.

Coverage of the seven required sections, the saturation warning, the East versus North/South split, and the refusal to treat a lower average cycle time as success are solid. The score is held down because those conclusions are not tied back to the arithmetic already in the handover and utilization tables, and because the analysis sketch would systematically distort queueing, SLA, fairness, and role-learning results.
