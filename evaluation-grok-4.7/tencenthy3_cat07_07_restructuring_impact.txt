6.5

The final answer covers every required section and stays appropriately conditional: three scenarios, East versus North/South, fairness and learning, and a pilot rather than a full rollout. That completeness is real, but the forecast is not tight enough to score higher.

The mechanism map names every requested effect, yet it still treats “nominal handover reduction” as the headline even though management’s own frequencies rise on the specialist and approval paths (about 36 to 41 and 42 to 49 per 100). Resilience is framed only as a loss of local backup. The strongest quantitative constraint in the data is missed: Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91% utilization while routed volume goes from 9 to 12 per 100. That load is past capacity unless work content falls sharply. The optimistic scenario still calls a ~10.5h Legal/Risk wait “stable due to better triage,” which cuts against broader detection, not for it.

Scenario numbers are also unclear where the comparisons were stripped (`6.8d6.5d`, `22.8d18d`, `6  3`). Hour-scale queue waits are not reconciled with a 90th percentile “exploding” to 30 days, so the adverse tail is asserted rather than tied to unstable utilization. Resource-similarity loss is listed as a days-to-weeks leading indicator, although deskilling is a lagging effect.

The pilot has the right ingredients, but the comparison is confounded: South is the control while the design never says whether `S_Tech` is removed from South. A required East 90th-percentile improvement is mislabeled as a guardrail, and the North rework guardrail (about +2pp on an 8.2% base) does not match the 12% rollback trigger. East has no equivalent quality stop.

The PM4Py sketch hits most requested views and is allowed to be pseudocode, but it still mixes role labels such as “local caseworker” with activity-level waiting times, never filters `customer_segment`, does not define the before/after split, and unpacks `discover_performance_dfg` into two values when it returns three. Rework and SLA misses are named more than measured.
