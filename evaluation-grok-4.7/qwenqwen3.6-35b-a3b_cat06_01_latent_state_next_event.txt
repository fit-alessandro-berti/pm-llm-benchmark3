4.5

The primary state labels are mostly reasonable, and several obligations are identified correctly (C6-A needs technical, regulatory, and supplier work; C6-C needs regulatory assessment; C6-D’s worklist shows assessment already open; C6-E’s shared lot requires supplier analysis). The write-up also avoids treating C6-D’s missing evidence events as proof that the work never happened. That is not enough to make the forecasts coherent.

The central error is that the three “next” predictions are sequenced milestones, not a distribution over the immediate next log event. Reasoning repeatedly says later items occur after the rank-1 event, while their midpoint probabilities still sum to about 1.1–1.3 and their ranges overlap. `process_enabled` is true on every row, so enabled work is never separated from what the log is likely to show next. Downstream disposition and authority notification are marked enabled while mandatory predecessors remain open.

C6-B is the sharpest control-flow mistake. Evidence has been received and review is unfinished, so the dominant next completion is Evidence Review Completed. Fast Close is described at triage, and the frequency table only shows it directly after Initial Triage; ranking it first, with S1 as an alternative, contradicts the prefix. C6-A has the same immediate successor, so the two cases are differentiated in the wrong place. Technical or supplier completion is also far too likely before review on a normal feed.

C6-C’s alternative state goes backward after both review and technical investigation are already logged. “Can be reportable” is overstated as mandatory reportability, and notification is not a currently enabled next event; the 16:00 batch is ignored. C6-D recognizes migration but never predicts a delayed evidence event surfacing ahead of assessment completion, despite assessment having been opened only at 12:40 and needing 2–14 hours. C6-E omits another evidence request despite completeness 0.72 and an existing loop, and it misses technical and regulatory assessment as suspected obligations for a six-complaint lot.

Every case lists “None” under contradicting evidence, including where the prefix, rules, or frequencies plainly cut against the ranked event. Queue and median times are mentioned, but not used to test whether a predicted completion can actually be next by 14:00.
