The briefs, matrix, and case rulings are substantial and mostly well traced, but the answer is not reliable enough for a higher score. Several classifications and authority claims conflict, and the required control-flow model does not execute or mean what the prose says it means.

The strongest analytical results hold. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are correctly treated as serious control failures. SUP-704 fits FD-7. SUP-705 and SUP-714 lack authority after expiry. SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-712, SUP-713, and SUP-715 are separated into data, model, segregation, label, and disclosure issues with usable reversal conditions. Both sides concede real points, and the final section rejects specific arguments rather than averaging them. The collection proposal covers stable notification IDs, policy versions, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, and immutable timestamps.

Material defects remain. SUP-702 is grouped with PD-3 violations and then ruled compliant, while the equivalent authorized overlay in SUP-704 is a justified exception. SUP-715 is unrelated to PD-3. “FD-7 patterns” are treated as normalized practice from a single post-expiry case. SUP-711 is determinate duplication, not an indeterminate case. Master-data validation required by PD-3 is never scored. The system verdict calls the operation materially compliant in aggregate while also finding critical failures and unauthorized continuation. One rejection answers a prosecution claim that the brief does not make. Stipulated “facts” include evaluative conclusions, and unstated thresholds (EUR 50,000, five business days, 12 months, 48-hour ratification) are written into the proposed rules. Comparison symbols are missing, so several rules are unreadable.

The POWL is not a valid executable model. `SilentTransition` is called with a label, and XOR is taken from `OperatorPOWL` rather than `Operator`. Edges refer to submodels that are not nodes of `M1`, transitions are reused across parents, and the information-request construction adds a cycle to a strict partial order instead of using `Operator.LOOP`. Quality review is placed before every decision, so the auto and emergency routes do not actually allow post-activation review; the closing summary asserts that they do. The overlay artifact is a schema sketch, not a populated policy table.
