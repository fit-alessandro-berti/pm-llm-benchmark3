4.5

The write-up follows the required section order and gets several local facts right, but it is not a reliable reconstruction. Case bounds for the highlighted missing starts are mostly coherent, while the station summaries, bottleneck logic, sensitivity claims, and Python do not match the stated assumptions or the event data.

The special-case bounds that are defensible under a previous-task constraint are P05 (S2 start no earlier than 09:45, so wait 20–50 and service 0–30), P07 (I1 free only after 09:25, so S1 wait 40–75 and service 0–35), P11 (C2 free only after P08 at 12:00), and P15 (I1 free only after 11:40). P16, P17, P21, and P22 are correctly treated as right-censored, with elapsed service separated from unfinished service. Fully observed min–max ranges in the aggregate row are also right, as are S1/S2 arrival and completion counts (22 and 20; 20 and 14) and the coverage counts. The 08:00–10:00 Little’s Law arithmetic is real: 14/120, mean S1 sojourn of those arrivals about 76 minutes, \(\lambda W \approx 8.9\), against time-average S1 WIP of about 5.5. The explanation that full sojourns spill past 10:00 is the right reason the identity fails.

That is where the reliability stops. P18, P19, and P20 never appear. They are not fully observed unremarkable cases: each has a completed S1 and a censored S2 wait (at least 50, 35, and 15 minutes). Dropping them hides part of the end-of-window S2 queue. “Central” imputation is the earliest feasible start, which maximizes service and minimizes wait. That is an extreme corner, not a center. The latest feasible start is allowed to be the completion instant, so service can be zero, with no use of the observed 20–35 and 25–50 minute service clusters.

Section 4 does not follow from the case table. Nominal S1 waits for the 21 started cases average about 65 minutes, not ~60. S2 waits for the 16 started cases average about 37, and even substituting censored lower bounds for the four cases still waiting only moves the 20-case figure to about 39, not ~45. Those “means” sit near the midpoints of the reported ranges. Under the answer’s own WIP definition (arrived and not yet complete) and departure-before-arrival tie break, S1 time-average WIP is about 5.8 with peak 8, and S2 is about 3.7 with peak 7. Peak 8 and peak 7 are fine; mean ~4.5 and ~3.5 are not. S1 busy time at the earliest-start corner is about 605 minutes of 720 (about 84%), and about 535 minutes (about 74%) if the two missing services are zero. S2, after removing C2’s 15 unavailable minutes, is about 570/705 (about 81%) at that same corner and about 73% at zero imputed service, counting elapsed time on P15, P17, and P21 as busy. The published ~560 and ~532 minutes and the tight 75–80% and 72–78% bands do not match that corner, do not match a midpoint, and are narrower than rounding plus imputation can support. Open elapsed work is easy to omit if busy time is summed only over completed services; the quality bar forbids that.

Section 5 contradicts itself. It says the external arrival rate exceeds both capacities, then gives 3.7/h against S1 capacity 4.3/h and S2 capacity 3.1/h. Only S2 fails that comparison, and 22 arrivals over 6 hours dilutes a process that actually stops at 12:10. The morning burst (about 7/h for two hours) is a fair reason S1 backs up early. The later claim that queues grow monotonically is false for S1: station WIP rises to 8 and then drains to 2 by 14:00 (P21 in service, P22 still waiting), while S2 ends at 6 after a brief peak of 7. That crossover is the bottleneck evidence, and it is missing. C2 down from 10:55 to 11:10 is a one-server, 15-minute cut, and it does explain a concrete overtake the answer never mentions: P09 finishes S1 at 10:35 but starts on C1 at 11:00, ahead of P08, who has been waiting for C2 since 10:20 and only starts at 11:15. Calling this a 50% capacity loss “precisely when the bulk of S1 completions were arriving” overstates a short, local gap.

Section 6 names three scenarios and computes none of them. Optimistic and central are the two imputation endpoints already in the case table; “pessimistic = central plus a 5-minute rounding penalty” does not say which timestamps move or in which direction. Rounding was promised in the bound method and then not applied. Stability claims are therefore assertions. In particular, “monotonic growth is highly stable” does not survive the S1 departure path, which is identified without any missing-start imputation.

The code does not reproduce the report. `parse_time` maps `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank to the same `NaT`, which is exactly what the required parser was told not to do. The P22 row has too few fields, so `WAITING_FOR_S1` lands in `s2_start` and `to_datetime` can throw. Interval rows keep only pairs with both endpoints, so open elapsed service never enters busy time. Pandas prints a five-row head and never builds WIP or the three uncertainty scenarios. The PM4Py projection is appropriately limited to fully timestamped complete cases and is labeled case-level, but with external arrivals absent its waiting time is only the S1-to-S2 gap. The comment declining `convert_log_to_time_intervals` satisfies that one requirement. `format_dataframe` is called without an explicit start-timestamp key, so the later service/waiting call is fragile.

What remains standing is narrow: no bypass, S2 service typically longer than S1, a real morning S1 backlog, an end-of-window S2 backlog, C2’s short outage, and Little’s Law not being valid on 08:00–10:00. What does not survive is the numerical station table, the “both capacities are exceeded” sentence, monotonic queues, the misuse of “central,” and the claim that the code implements the reconstruction.
