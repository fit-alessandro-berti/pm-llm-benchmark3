The diagnosis and the three redesigns are specific, segment-aware, and tied to the directly-follows graph, timing, conformance rules, rework, and capacity data. A, B, and C are genuinely different mechanisms (pre-dispatch control, repair-forward suppression of the fast lane, and consented choice with decoupled close), each with an explicit sacrifice, and the experiment plan checks downstream displacement rather than average cycle time. That is the core of the task, and it is largely met.

The score stays well below excellent because several evidence and control-flow claims are wrong or over-precise. Edge counts into complaint are summed to 510 and then equated with the stated 9.2% case rate; 510/5000 is 10.2%, and directly-follows counts are not cases. Complaint to manual review (310) and complaint to escalation (120) are alternative successors, not a sequence. Summing activity medians to about a 6-hour fast path is left unreconciled with the given segment medians of 8, 13, 20, and 28 hours. Population shares of 14% and 21% are treated as the mix of the 2550 auto-approvals. “At least twice” is narrowed to exactly two evidence requests.

Redesign A’s compliance claim is materially overstated. R2’s exception is only Premium low-risk accessories; the green lane also fast-dispatches Standard low-risk, and the new gate checks part selection rather than evidence completeness, so it does not establish R2. Repair technicians are called already constrained even though the stated repair-quote delay is part-price confirmation. Point estimates such as EUR 2–5, about 10 hours, 70–90 hours, a 40–60% complaint cut, and fitting traces above 85% are not supported and conflict with the instruction not to invent exact effects. The pilot wording also blurs 1750 high-value cases with a 50% randomization of that group.
