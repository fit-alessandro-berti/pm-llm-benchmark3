5.8

The conservative case calls are mostly sound, and the write-up covers every required section. It is not reliable as a pipeline design: ordering, sensitivity numbers, the “evidence-based” scenario, and the PM4Py block contradict the stated rules and the given facts.

The decision tree names the six relationship types and the five terminals, but step 6 treats unresolved timestamp order as an identity quarantine. That rule would quarantine clusters the later sections keep and order. It is never applied. Exact technical duplicates are defined by keeping the first ingested copy; this log has none (distinct `source_event_key` and `payload_hash` on every row), and ingestion is the wrong canonicalizer under fact 9. Step 2a also fights the later e03/e04 treatment: differing payloads are both a reason to keep two events and, elsewhere, a discarded hash conflict.

Section 2’s conservative decisions match the facts on the mandatory pairs: merge reconciled e03/e04 and same-invoice e11/e16; keep e05 and e14; keep e06/e07 and e13/e15 and e14/e15 as abstraction pairs; split the harmonized e08/e09 labels; keep e09 before e10. That is the strongest part of the answer. The “Main risk” column sometimes states the risk of the chosen action and sometimes the risk of the opposite action (double-counting is what the e11/e16 merge prevents). e15 is renamed `Post Payment Summary (ERP)` here and `Capture Payment (ERP Summary)` in sections 4 and 6. The second name leaves a capture-like activity in the log. “Link e15 to M1 and M2” is unsafe wording next to fact 8, which gives e15 no Payment object. e08 before e09 is a reasonable lifecycle prior, not a measured handover buffer; all three logistics stamps are `10:15:00`.

Section 3’s rule list has the right layers and correctly marks ingestion and eid order as non-semantic. The worked orders do not follow those rules. PSP `source_seq` is 400, 401, 402, so e12 < e13 < e14 inside one source. `[e11, e14, e12, e13, e15]` is therefore illegal under rule 2, yet it is offered as admissible. Invoice-before-payment is assumed, not given. `[e08, e09, e10]` is presented as the unique physical order; fact 4 separates release from acceptance semantically, and fact 5 only fixes e09 before e10. The 10:00 picks are correctly left concurrent with each other and before pack, with e07 after e06.

The implementation then cancels that partial order. `source_seq` is sorted globally, so scanner 552 is forced after WMS 100 and the two picks become a false sequence. A second `ocel_sort_by_additional_column` on `semantic_rank` alone drops the source and eid tie-breaks (and pandas’ default sort is unstable). `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` then writes that serialization into `ocel:timestamp`. Section 3 forbids using synthetic microseconds to invent sequence; section 6 does it, and section 7 calls the result “ordered step delays.” Equal-timestamp picks and the two captures become directly-follows self-loops under ordinary timestamp discovery. `original_timestamp` is copied first, which meets the preservation constraint, but the column miners actually read is the synthetic one.

Conservative retention is right: drop only e04 and e16, keep 15 events, including e05, e07, e09, e10, e14, and e15, and store source keys on the merged rows. The helper does not implement the stated attribute union (location versus scanner telemetry); it stores source names and keys only.

Scenario A is not evidence-based. Facts 4 and 8 are reasons to keep e08 distinct from e09 and to keep e15 as one invoice-level summary. Merging release with carrier acceptance, and pasting e15 onto both M1 and M2, invents a shared dispatch and a split balance the ERP did not store. The 12-event arithmetic follows those merges (17−5). The stated damage (“transfer durations”) is the wrong harm: the stamps are already tied, so the loss is the responsibility boundary and the ledger event, not a real duration. Scenario B’s count of about 10 can be obtained, but the collapsed set is wrong. e10 is `Handover to Carrier`, so an activity+timestamp match does not include it. e11/e16 are the same activity, timestamp, and objects, so a mechanical pass does collapse them; the action list omits that pair. `have_common_object=True` is explained correctly for e13/e14: they intersect on O1 and V1 while M1 and M2 differ. The code never passes that flag and never calls `ocel_merge_duplicates`, which the required block demands, with a warning, separately from `ocel_drop_duplicates`. The drop is applied to the whole log, which section 9 itself defines as a failure mode. There is no justified exact-duplicate use. Referential checks cover orphan event ids only, not object ids. `numpy` is unused.

Sensitivity figures are not derivable from the table. Raw E2O count is 44 (1+1+3+3+3+4+2+3+3+3+2+3+3+3+2+2+3), not 41. After redundant unions, conservative relations are 39, not 37. The 30 and 22 figures are likewise unsupported. “16 sequential steps” counts a linearized trace length, not a DFG. Conservative “zero false loops” is false on the order perspective: e03 and e05 remain `Pick Item`, and e13 and e14 remain `Capture Payment`; the timedelta total order creates those edges. Item-level I1/I2 parallelism is real and is the right place to say rework disappeared. Aggressive “M2 lost” is true of a drop and false of a relation-union merge, which would attach M2 to M1’s capture. OTG “spurious overlaps” overstates a weight change as a type-graph change. ET–OT and OC-DFG frequency are named and not computed. M1 has an authorization and M2 does not; that lifecycle asymmetry is ignored. Fabricated quantities appear as facts: M1/M2 = $50, a 50% revenue gap, `wms_location=A-12`, `device_id=SC-09`.

The provenance schema has the required fields, and an immutable raw log is the real reconstruction path. The narrative instead writes discarded events back into `ocel_raw`. The sample record drops `h-pick-i1-alt` after section 2 promised to keep it, and it does not store each source’s original E2O set, so relation reversal is underdetermined. The code’s four provenance columns do not implement this schema.

Failure-mode coverage hits the eight required themes and the e13/e14 and whole-log warnings are right. Mode 3’s example does not work: e07’s business time is 10:00 and e08’s is 10:15, so an ingestion tie-break cannot place the pack summary after warehouse release, and the cited gap is minutes. The mode-6 safeguard is contradicted by the code and by the “step delays” claim. Dollar amounts show up again.

Net: a reader can take the conservative keep/merge list and the M1/M2 intersection warning. They cannot take the admissible 12:00 orders, the sort keys, the library collapse set, the E2O/DFG table, or the Python as a faithful PM4Py implementation.
