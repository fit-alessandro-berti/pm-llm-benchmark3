The write-up is complete and often sharp (QA as non-preemptive FIFO plus exact-time appointment insertion; QD as skill routing with per-resource FIFO; priority as the unsafe inference), but the decision-set work the task centers on is materially wrong in places.

QC eligible sets are incomplete at the decisions that matter. At 10:10, C03 (priority 2) and C04 are already waiting, so priority is not a tie and would pick C03, not C02. At 10:18, C05 (priority 1, deadline 10:50) is waiting, so priority does not predict C03. At 10:32, C06 (est 6, arrived 10:25) is waiting, so SPT does not predict C04. Those false predictions prop up the later QC story. The 11:34 choice of C06 over C07 (priority 2) is never reconstructed.

The QB FIFO column changes meaning without saying so: at 09:31 FIFO means resume B01; at 09:48 it means start B03 and leave B01 parked. The QC counterfactual then claims a 2-minute scan delay could hide C05 at 10:32, and that C04 might already have been in service. C05 arrived at 10:11; C04 starts at 10:32.

Every realized duration in the extract equals `est_min`, so ranking noisy estimates as a medium-plausibility reason that SPT is untestable contradicts the evidence. Section 7 calls QB the least identifiable queue while Section 1 gives QC the lower confidence (55 vs 60). Several “contradicting” episodes contradict rival rules (priority), not the stated primary rule. D-F’s idle 11:36–12:00 can fit D10 (12 minutes, priority 1) before the meeting; “reserved for D05” does not close that gap.
