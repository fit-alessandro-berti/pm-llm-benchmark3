The core conclusion is sound and well supported: the crude Orion gap (21.96 h vs 15.24 h, +6.72 h) reverses inside every complexity stratum, a pooled-complexity standardization is about −2.5 h, and a 50% transfer based on the crude mean is the wrong intervention. Those strengths do not make the answer nearly flawless.

The executive section runs about 320 words against an explicit 180–300 limit. In the role table, rework is called a collider “if risk causes both rework and assignment.” That structure is confounding (a common cause), not a collider. A collider would require arrows into rework from both group and risk. The remainder gap of ≈2.12 h vs ≈1.41 h is used as evidence that the clocks are not a closed decomposition by group. Within complexity, remainders match; the crude remainder gap is the same compositional pattern the answer otherwise handles correctly, driven by compliance and rework mix. Section 8 calls Low Night an empty cell. It is not: Orion has C01 and Nova has C15 and C20. The graph omits `complexity → queue_wait` even though, within Orion, non-compliance Medium waits are about 9–11 h against about 3.5 h for Low, so complexity lengthens waiting beyond the compliance path. `complexity → unobserved_case_risk` is a dubious direction and is not classified as fact or assumption. `routing_rule → shift` is drawn as a single direction while only the Orion–night association is actually given. “Rules out a large Orion penalty” is too strong for High–Nova n=2. The closing recommendation says to scale a routing change after waits fall or the lottery contradicts the within-stratum pattern; a wait reduction from extra capacity does not itself justify relabeling cases, and a lottery that confirms the within-stratum pattern would argue against moving Orion work to Nova.
