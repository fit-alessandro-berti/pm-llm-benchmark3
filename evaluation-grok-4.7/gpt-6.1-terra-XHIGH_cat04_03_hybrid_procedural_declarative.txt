7.5

The hybrid design is coherent: the R5/R6 split is explicit, the process tree matches the backbone, DECLARE directions are right, and the temporal arithmetic holds. It is not nearly flawless.

The log skeleton treats an empty `directly_follows` set as “no adjacency constraint.” In that model the set is an allow-list, so any consecutive pair missing from it is a deviation. After the stated six-label projection, T1, T2, T4, T5, T6, and T7 still have consecutive pairs, so those skeleton passes are not reliable. T3 can still pass (projection is only `Reject Vendor`). T8 still fails on `never_together` and `always_after`.

Smaller defects: missing emergency authorization is `not evaluated` in the checker and `policy-dependent` in the matrix, with no single status rule; “reject unknown labels before projection” can be read as dropping clarification and escalation, which would undo the T2 and T6 procedural passes; the skeleton’s payment obligation is stricter than DECLARE and that disagreement is only implied; the 24-hour bound is not fixed as inclusive or exclusive.

The remaining layering holds. Unconditional opinion-before-activation precedence is correctly avoided. R3’s outcome cutoff is separated from `altresponse`. R9 and R11 stay in the attribute layer. T7 is right: escalation-to-opinion is about 3.67 standard deviations late, and the 2-hour payment interval stays inside `zeta = 2`. T1–T5 and T8 are otherwise assigned to the correct layer. Legal priority and the emergency exception are kept apart, with the operational risk of the chosen rule stated.
