7.7

The audit correctly separates April’s schema failure, June’s routing-mix shift, the August supplier shock, September’s Partner Z shift, and October’s censoring. It uses the right metrics, keeps falsifiers, and gates retraining on data quality and label maturity. The score is held down by mislabeled evidence, a few unsupported quantities, and process-extraction code that recreates the censoring bias the prose warns about.

The drift table is mostly well evidenced. April’s flat 0.19 breach rate against a 0.28 mean score, 17% unknown activity, 18% unmapped variants, and 14% missing queue features is correctly treated as a pipeline incident. June’s V2 jump to 52%, with within-variant breach rates still about 0.16 / 0.09 / 0.35, is correctly read as a routing change with over-prediction (0.20 vs 0.15). August’s `Order Part → Part Received` move from 65 h to 130 h and the matured V3 breach move from 0.35 to 0.64, while the score stays 0.19, is real conditional-outcome drift. Partner Z is correctly barred from explaining August and correctly implicated in September. October’s 0.16 breach rate at 55% maturity is correctly refused as a comparable outcome.

Several cells are less careful. Covariate drift is supported partly by volume growth from 3,410 to 5,250. Volume is not a change in P(X). The same row uses the post-diagnosis V3 share as a pre-diagnosis covariate, which conflicts with the later point that the path is not known at scoring time. October is listed as a data-pipeline failure, but that row never gives October evidence; the outcome-API backlog belongs with label delay. May is tied to “pre-April 20 duplicates” and the unrecomputed April cache. Duplicate removal applies to the new source after 20 April, and the cache gap is an April-record backfill failure. May-started cases can still be hurt by 1–5 May and by 2% unknown / 3% missing queue, but not by April 1–19 duplicates. In the process-drift row, the prediction-time argument is put under contradicting evidence even though it explains the drift rather than rebuts it. The concept-drift cell says breaches “hit 0.28–0.31” across July–October. July’s observed rate is 0.19 against a score of 0.18, so aggregate calibration still held; the July signal is the V3 rate of 0.46 and AUROC of 0.76.

Section 2 recovers July cleanly, and the month split is the right one. It then states hypotheses as measurements: June cases “still predicted pre-June V1 probabilities,” August’s unobserved 7% “likely” pushes the breach rate above 0.28, September’s true rate is “likely >0.33,” and October’s true rate is “>0.32.” Those directions are plausible. The floors are not identified by the tables. Section 6 repeats October’s “>0.32” as fact after Section 1 correctly says the claim would be falsified if mature October stayed at 0.16.

The seven tests match the requested design and each has two competing outcomes. The transport weight omits an explicit statement that weights must be normalized, though a constant prior odds would cancel. The legacy-to-0.35 illustration is an unshown calculation. Test 1 specifies τ = 30 and then evaluates τ = 25.

The training gates are proportionate: January–March are clean, April–May are unsafe until recomputed, June is a separate routing regime, July–August need refreshed labels, September needs IPCW or a cleared backlog, and October is unusable. The two-stage decomposition is the right remedy, and a separate Partner Z model is correctly rejected. Stage 2 still calibrates toward September’s “0.31+ reality” while the same answer says that 0.31 is censored, and it uses route intent a stage before the plan extracts it.

Monitoring dependencies are sound: a data-quality failure suppresses drift retraining, and maturity below 90% blocks recalibration. The code sketch meets the library checklist and normalizes the renamed diagnosis before features. It also has material defects. Five-minute deduping is done within `(case, activity)` groups, so it can drop a repeated activity that was not consecutive. `discover_performance_dfg` values are treated as dicts with a `"mean"` key; in current PM4Py they are numeric durations, so the edge calculation can throw. Exact V1/V2/V3 tuple shares on the case-start cohort classify only completed traces. In October that undercounts open V3 paths and inflates apparent V2 share, which is the censoring artifact the audit is supposed to detect. Completed-edge means have the same length bias, and they are means where the evidence is medians. Calendar versus case-start interpretations are noted, but the returned summary does not mark censored variant shares as non-comparable.

The five failure modes are the right ones, especially pipeline recalibration, supplier lead time invisible at diagnosis, and parity adjustment for Partner Z without variant-stratified evidence. The October mitigation is weakened by the invented 0.32 floor.
