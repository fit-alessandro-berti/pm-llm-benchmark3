6.5

The final write-up covers every required section and gets many case-level calls right, but it is not internally consistent and it breaks constraints the policy itself treats as hard.

What holds up: hard versus soft constraints are separated; high-priority work is not given to an unsupervised trainee; the four-assignment cap is respected; continuity is correctly kept on C007 and C016; C001 is moved to a Security specialist; and skill 0 is correctly treated as a bar to Security even with supervision (Eva and Jonas). The worker explanation is clear and non-punitive, and the audit list includes every requested indicator.

What does not:

- C006 stays with R9 (overtime risk high, normal priority, 36h, low handover, low complexity) and the trade-off is recorded as “None.” Safer Quality specialists exist, notably R10. The same overtime rule is used to move C010 off R2, so the rule is applied unevenly.
- C003 is kept with R4 (overtime risk high, 14 open cases, 3 hours left, normal priority, 18h). R3 is a Diagnosis specialist with more capacity. The text calls this an “H6-style” override, but the stated H6 test is complexity ≥ 0.70 and high handover cost. C003 is 0.66 with medium handover cost, so the label does not match the rule.
- Both of those assignments would be rejected by the pseudocode filter that drops high-overtime resources when SLA remaining is over 12 hours. The published algorithm does not produce the published table.
- “No batch-mate at the right skill” for C006 is false. C012 is also a Quality Check, low priority, with 48h remaining, so both cases meet the batching rule.
- The C010 note is misread. It is a trainee Approval under supervision, and skill 1 may prepare while skill 2–3 finalizes. It is not blocked because R2’s training priority is Quality. Giving it wholly to R6, who already has two urgent cases (C002 and C011), also cuts against the stated resilience rule.
- SLA urgency is about 25% of the score but does not depend on the resource, so it never changes the ranking. There is no queue order, even though caps and workload make order material. The tie-break references `s_train` outside the per-resource loop. Supervised Security (skill 1–2 under R7 or R8) is excluded in Phase A, and the 20% training quota is never enforced, only reported afterward as 1/9.
- The PM4Py sketch names the right functions, but `filter_event_attribute_values` on exact complexities 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, and 1.0 matches none of the continuous scores, and `filter_case_performance` is called with both bounds null, so it does not identify SLA misses.

The comparison is directionally sensible and correctly refuses a single optimum, yet it never actually builds the two baseline assignments, and the specialist-bottleneck example is imprecise.
