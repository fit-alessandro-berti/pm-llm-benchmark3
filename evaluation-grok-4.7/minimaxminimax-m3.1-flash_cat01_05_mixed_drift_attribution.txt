The final diagnosis is substantively strong: March is correctly treated as mostly semantic/provenance drift, later mapped-fitness, rework, and cycle-time movement is not collapsed into logging drift, and supplier, staffing, priority, and seasonality are given different onsets. DFG total-variation distances, baseline fitness gaps, and the non-monotone distance path check out. Hypotheses, segmentation bias, and the seven decision rules are rigorous and usable.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 2 is about 292 words, above the 280-word cap, and “1229 per 100” and “5935%” are unreadable where arrows were dropped. “4–6.5 pp below baseline for eight months” is false: September is about 3.5 pp and December about 2.5 pp. In the change table, June (+100%) and November (+550%) senior-assessment rates are not in the supplied DFG and are invented. The cycle-time table does not match the stated 0.7–1.0 median damping or the Part A code, and the 1.6 complexity multiplier produces hour-level precision the aggregates cannot support. Section 3.4 says the 10% month-over-month rule flags the behavioral points, but that rule would not flag mapped fitness (May is only about a 3% relative drop). Section 9 runs well past 400 words. The reference cohort is described as both 35–46% and 12–20% of the same peak months.

Part B would not run against the PM4Py manual: `discover_dfg` is a 3-tuple, not `(dfg, activities_count)`; `get_variants` is a dict, so the `Counter` destroys frequencies; `discover_temporal_profile` has no `activity` or `granularity` argument; `convert_to_dataframe` uses `concept:name` / `case:concept:name`, not `activity` / `case_id`; and `align_petri` omits markings. The required single code block is also split in two.
