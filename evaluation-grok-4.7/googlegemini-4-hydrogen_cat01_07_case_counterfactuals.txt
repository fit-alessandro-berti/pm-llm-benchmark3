6.4

The write-up meets the required structure and gets the main process distinctions right: partial orders, four-eyes separation, preliminary compliance not replacing final review, K-337 as a decision-policy change, and earlier readiness not equaling earlier completion. Those strengths are outweighed by temporal errors and capacity claims that do not survive the given snapshots.

The K-337 clock is wrong by a full day. Register at Mon 11:00 through Notify at the following Wed 09:10 is 214.2 h, not 238.2 h. That inflated baseline produces impossible savings: only 118.7 h remain after Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), yet the text claims savings of 120–135 h. The same paragraph treats Mon 08:30 as about 103–118 h; it is about 165.5 h. Section 2 (103–175 h, 63–135 h faster), section 3 (−66.7 h, −120 to −135 h, worst 175–190 h or 300+ h), section 5 (63–135 h), and section 7 (66–135 h) do not describe one range. The “~31 h” of inter-stage wait is also not the 24.5 h implied by the trace.

K-104’s base and best cases jump C1 without a feasible slot. Tue 08:00 shows a 16 h C1 queue and Wed 08:00 an 8 h queue, but the counterfactual still starts final review at Tue 19:20 or Wed 08:00 while claiming other behavior stays fixed. The feasibility check only says C1 does not overlap itself; it never places preliminary work and the reserved slot on that backlog. Decision quality is also stated as unchanged despite an unresolved sanctions ambiguity.

K-219’s 14–20 h gain depends on D2 working from about Mon 21:30 through the night. Nothing in the trace shows that. The Tue 08:00 A2 queue of 6 h likewise makes a Tue 10:30 assessment start an optimistic best case, not a supported one. Shift limits are missing from the reversing evidence.

Smaller faults add up. Section 4 uses D2 service of 6–14 h against D1 at 4–9 h, ranges the guidance does not state, and applies them to standard handwritten cases. “Exponential” waiting-time blowup is the wrong queueing shape. K-104’s table base (54 h) does not match the derived 51.3 h. Alternate-day pilot assignment would confound the strong weekday load cycle. Section 7 names three of the four interventions as least transportable, and it repeats the impossible K-337 savings.

The ranking, interference themes, and pilot safeguards are conceptually sound, but the quantitative counterfactuals are not reliable enough for a higher score.
