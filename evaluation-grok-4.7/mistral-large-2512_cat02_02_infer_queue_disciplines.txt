3.5

The write-up has the required seven sections and a few real events (B02 interrupting B01, D05 restricted to D-F, D06 starting at 11:30, C05 already late when started). The diagnoses themselves are not reliable. Eligible sets are repeatedly incomplete, rule predictions are miscomputed, and three of the four primary labels are contradicted by the same logs used to support them. Confidence is far too high for that ambiguity.

QA is not preemptive priority. No QA job is interrupted: every `service_segments` value is a single interval, A02 runs 08:32–08:52 to completion, and A04 starts at 08:58 only after A02 has finished and after A1’s calibration block at 08:52–08:58. The cited support, “A04 preempts A03” in the table and “A04 preempts A02” in the text, is neither a preemption nor internally consistent. At 08:20 the waiting set is A02 (P3, est 20), A03 (P3, est 10, appointment 08:20), and A04 (P1, est 7). The table drops A04, so its priority and SPT predictions are wrong; both pick A04, while the observed start is A03. A04 then waits through A02. At 09:05 A08 (P1) and A06 (P2) are waiting and A05 (P3) is started; A08 still waits through A06 and A07. That is repeated priority failure. The defensible reading is non-preemptive FIFO, with A03 inserted when the server frees at the appointment instant. A06 starting at 09:20 does not independently show appointment priority: A06 is also the FIFO successor of A05, and A05 (P3) occupied the server through 09:15. Explaining that lateness by “A04/A05 priority” is backwards for A05.

QB is mislabeled. B02’s start at 09:16 is a preemption of in-service B01, with the stated reason “urgent arrival.” Using that episode as the strongest support for non-preemptive priority reverses the definition. At 09:31 the server is free and B03 (P2) is waiting; non-preemptive priority selects B03, not a resume of B01 (P3). The table’s “priority → B01 (non-preemptive)” invents a third rule. Later choices also break priority: at 09:48 B05 (P1, arrived 09:46) is already present and B03 (P2) is taken; a delayed scan would make the true arrival earlier, so “B05 was not yet eligible” is the wrong direction. At 10:14 B01 is resumed ahead of B06 (P2). At 10:28 B04 (P3) is taken ahead of B06 (P2) and ahead of B07’s 10:30 appointment. A coherent hypothesis is urgent preemption plus return-to-interrupted-work, with the second stop explicitly a dispatcher reassignment, not a clean priority queue.

QC’s EDF label is not supported by the decision sets, and several EDF cells are inverted. At 10:10, C01–C04 are all waiting. Earliest deadline is C03 (12:30), not C02 (14:00); shortest estimate is C02 (8). The table omits C03 and C04 and credits EDF with the SPT outcome. At 11:34, C07 (deadline 12:00, P2, est 18) is waiting and is omitted; among the jobs listed, C06 has the latest deadline (15:00), so EDF does not predict C06. The prose says C06 is less urgent than C01 and still treats selecting C06 as EDF. At 11:40 the only rules that select C01 over C07 are FIFO-like; priority, SPT, and EDF all select C07. That choice also misses a still-feasible deadline: C07 from 11:40 would finish about 11:58, before 12:00, and C01 would still finish before 13:00 if started afterward. Section 3 then says C07 was less urgent than C01, while section 4 says the opposite. Section 4’s 12:16 episode is not a decision: C07 is the only job left. Every `est_min` in these tables equals realized service, so the EDF-versus-SPT conflict is not an artifact of noisy estimates.

QD is the least bad label, because skill eligibility is a hard constraint, but priority is not a supported secondary rule and the contradicting episode is factually wrong. D08 runs on D-M at 11:35–11:43; D07 runs on D-X at 11:48–12:16. D08 starts before D07, on a resource that cannot do D07. The real anti-priority choice is D-M at 11:25: D04 (P3, est 10) over D08 (P1, est 8), which also beats SPT and fits FIFO. D-X is idle from 11:20 until D06’s appointment at 11:30 even though D07 is eligible only for D-X. At 11:48 D-X takes D07 (P3, est 28) over D10 (P1, est 12). The 11:21 row pairs D03 with D04, who are on different resources, and omits D05, the actual alternative for D-F. The 12:20 row includes D09, who finished at 12:03. D-F’s idle stretch from about 11:36 to the 12:00 meeting, rather than starting the 30-minute D05, is the availability constraint the table does not analyze. Skill filtering plus appointments plus FIFO inside a pool fits better than skill-based routing with priority as the tie-break.

Sections 4–7 inherit those mistakes. The two discrimination tests use one mis-specified resume and one non-choice; the proposed “observables” are attribute rewrites, not extra log fields. Section 5 ranks strategic estimate inflation as medium without noticing that every estimate matches realized time, ranks appointment tolerance as low while QA’s only clear inversion is an on-time appointment, and does not treat calibration, the D-F meeting, return-to-preempted-work, or a frozen initial QC batch. Section 6 misses eligibility set, appointment tolerance, and estimated remaining time at the decision. The synthesis names QD as most identifiable and appointment logic as the dangerous inference. On these logs, QA’s FIFO-plus-appointment pattern is clearer once preemption is tested, QC is the least identifiable, and the dangerous false inferences are exactly the ones asserted with 70–85 confidence: QA preemptive priority and QC earliest-deadline-first. Assumptions are not labeled, and several false event readings are stated as observations.
