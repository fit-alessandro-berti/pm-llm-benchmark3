The formal comparison is largely right: loop scope, one check per iteration versus one global check, both directions of counterexamples (including two-analysis traces), the P1/P2/P3 verdicts, the effect of optional approval and hidden `tau`, and the stricter simplified tree are all defensible and internally consistent. Structural counts (12 vs 12 nodes, depths 4 vs 5, six visible leaves, one `tau`) match the trees.

The required executable comparison does not work. Firing uses `transition.inputs` / `transition.outputs` and place `p.inputs` / `p.outputs`; PM4Py places and transitions expose `in_arcs` / `out_arcs`. `tuple(sorted(m.items()))` also sorts place objects, which are not ordered, so the BFS state key fails even before firing. Section 5 therefore does not actually derive or compare the bounded languages.

Section 6 misstates `discover_footprints`. Those footprints are start/end sets plus directly-follows and parallel relations, not co-occurrence sets that ignore order. Equal footprints still would not prove trace equivalence, but the reason given is wrong, and the normalization code does not match the real return value. `compute_emd` is also invoked on plain trace lists rather than stochastic languages (only partly excused by the try/except). The claim that any node/arc simplicity score must rank A at least as simple as B is not earned, since the Petri-net score is never derived.
