4.5

The memo covers every required section and stays attached to the candidate interventions, but it is not a reliable decision analysis. Core quantities are misread, several mechanisms point the wrong way, and the recommended package contradicts both the evidence and the memo’s own service findings.

Boundary and indicators are shaky. Scope 3 is defined to include upstream material production and then supplier upstream emissions are excluded. The write-up assumes path-level precision is good enough even though the log’s precision is explicitly low. “Scrap rate (%)” is defined as kilograms per order. Partial shipment is folded into a quality-loop indicator. Resilience is proxied by SP1/SP2 violations rather than by whether a Service-contract order still has an alternative route under R1. Obsolescence is not an indicator, despite the 14-day, 6% forecast-error finding. Q1 later is monitored with `package_material_kg`, which is packaging mass, not the part weight the rule depends on.

Intervention effects are repeatedly unsupported or factually wrong. CNC_B’s Made-to-order rework rate is 18% versus 9% on CNC_A, not 11% versus 9%; the 11% figure is the Made-to-order Regular segment rate. An 18% rework-rate gap is then treated as an 18% scrap-mass reduction. Stock-to-air (5%, 18.5 kgCO2e) is conflated with the entire Standard-stock Service-contract segment (16%, 7.8 kgCO2e), and those shares are later added. S1 and S4 are the same lever with incompatible cycle-time and lateness deltas. Service-contract orders are given the longer batch-wait cap even though they have less slack than Regular Made-to-order orders. Obsolescence is labeled as scrap. Packaging-rework counts of a few hundred orders are claimed to cut scrap about 30%, which production scrap volumes cannot support. Shared emergency lanes are assigned an invented drop from 52 to 10 kgCO2e and a neutral lateness effect, including via consolidated trucks whose wait is 12–24 hours against a Critical median of 14 hours. Many prose directions are simply missing, so the claimed increase or decrease cannot be read.

The outcome table and recommendation compound that. Weighted baselines are about 7.4 kWh, 10.3 kgCO2e, 0.35 kg scrap, and roughly 9.5% late, not 10.5 kWh, 8.0 kgCO2e, 0.5 kg, and 10–18% late. The “after” energy range is therefore not even clearly an improvement, while a system transport cut of 50–70% to 4–5 kgCO2e is not available without largely removing Critical dedicated-van emissions. That violates the requirement not to take the lowest-emission option where service or resilience would fail. The same section says air-to-ground causes no service degradation after earlier estimating higher lateness. S5 is used in the trade-off story but omitted from the package. The best-supported quality and energy lever, moving slack Made-to-order work off CNC_B, is dropped. Consolidation is treated as improving route diversity when it concentrates departures and can weaken R1.

Rebound coverage names the right themes, but inspection is said to shift rework into packaging when the evidence says rushed inspection creates those later defects, and the proposed batching mitigation prioritizes the wrong segment. Governance thresholds such as a 30% emissions cut, 20% scrap cut, and 24-hour disruption recovery are not measurable as specified and would reward the same unsafe transport shift.
