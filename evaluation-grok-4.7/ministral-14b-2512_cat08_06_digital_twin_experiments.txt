The write-up names the right topics (calendars, seasonality, applicant delay, batching, tails, holdout) and it has all eight required sections, but the operational design contradicts those topics, the process-mining layer is not PM4Py, and the skeleton cannot represent the process it claims to simulate.

**Process-mining grounding is not usable.** The estimation plan and skeleton call `discover_arrival_distribution`, `discover_performance_schema`, `discover_waiting_times`, `discover_service_times`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.process_tree.apply`, `performance_schema.apply`, `arrival_distribution.apply`, and `variants.apply`. Those are not PM4Py APIs. The required path is absent: a pandas frame for the excerpt, `pm4py.format_dataframe` on `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp`, `pm4py.parse_process_tree` on the given tree (or an explicit account of why that string is not a faithful model), then variants, a DFG, a performance DFG, and `get_case_arrival_average`, plus service/waiting columns only where timestamps actually support them. Variant share (26% “simple clean path”) is treated as a gateway probability. Inter-event time is split into service and wait by formulas the log cannot support: many rows have only `time:timestamp`, described as a completion stamp, so sojourn is observed and the service/wait split is not identified. Pointing `dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df` at an XES parser and calling that a calendar is a category error.

**The simulator is not a digital twin of this process.** Cases have one `current_activity` and one `next_activity`, so the AND-block of planning / optional fire / optional environment, and the rule that decision drafting waits for every applicable result, cannot be represented. The clock generates a monthly arrival lump, then overwrites `current_time` with each service draw and jumps 30 days. Resource calendars are stored and never consulted. Fire’s Tuesday–Thursday window, the Monday portal clerk, council-meeting weeks, holidays, and the environmental 40% non-permit reservation never change capacity. Waiting-time distributions are fit and then unused. `hash(activity) % n` pins every execution of an activity to one person. Replications reuse one simulator, so experiments contaminate each other. Public notice is a timer (14-day floor plus an unobserved extension), and “Receive missing documents” is an applicant event with zero municipal service; both are still queued like staffed work. Effective weekly hours (fire: 2 FTE on three days; environment: about 1.8 FTE of permit time) are never computed, so bottleneck claims have no capacity basis.

**Non-stationarity and cross-case dependence are asserted, then dropped.** Warm-up discards three months so queues can reach steady state. This arrival process is seasonal (1.7× in two seasons, 2.2× on Mondays) and interrupted by council weeks, so a steady-state mean is the wrong estimand; metrics need to be reported by season and weekday against an initialized WIP. `generate_arrival` ignores its time argument. Architect concentration is a 10% chance of three extra Poisson arrivals, not a cluster process on `architect_id`, and `related_cases` is never filled. Objection effects that hit several neighboring cases, inspector travel state, no-shows, and geographic batching (less travel, longer wait for the first case in the bundle) stay outside the execution model. A monthly step cannot see Monday bursts or a Tuesday–Thursday roster.

**Missing data are filled with invented numbers and then treated as measured.** Objection events are unobserved. The plan still sets Bernoulli probabilities 0.15 and 0.7, a 30-day duration threshold, and a stress level of 0.25. That converts a proxy into an identified flag. D1’s “40% fewer missing-document loops,” D3’s “30% less travel,” rework adding “20–50%,” and an “empirical” 70–85% utilization target are not in the log. Utilization is a poor validation target here precisely because start stamps are missing. Removed emergency decisions are a known selection bias; the arrival assumptions then say unobserved cases follow the observed distribution. Exponential system tasks are given mean equal to the median, which is inconsistent with the exponential distribution. D2 depends on “no prior violations,” an attribute the log does not have, and that gap is never carried into routing uncertainty. D6 is specified as reserving one planner and implemented as a priority rule; those are different policies. D5’s stated downside (“issues found late”) is the problem early legal review is meant to change. D9’s benefit is not identified from a log that has no objection events; it needs an explicit structural assumption and a sensitivity bound, not a point improvement.

**The experiment and decision layer looks complete and is under-specified.** The table lists baseline, D1–D10, and three stresses, but almost every policy is one invented effect size rather than levels. Stress 3 is the removal of D4, which is already the baseline. Policies are not crossed with demand, calendar, and objection-uncertainty stresses, so D10’s interactions are missed: D2 reduces inspection load and so shrinks the value of D3 and D4. Decision thresholds (15%, 30/60/120-day SLAs, queue under 50, architect ratio 1.2) are not tied to the reported medians and p90s (complex variance is already 140 days median and 260 days at p90) or to interval overlap. District and architect “fairness” ratios ignore case-mix and legitimate D3/D5 travel. Thirty replications justified by “CV < 10%” is implausible for these tails; seed-to-seed Kruskal–Wallis does not quantify uncertainty. Conformance fitness of a discovered tree is treated as simulation validation. An 80% utilization check would tune the model to a number the log does not contain.

The skeleton’s own notes admit simplified routing and no calendar handling. Those are the core of the requested twin, not leftover polish.
