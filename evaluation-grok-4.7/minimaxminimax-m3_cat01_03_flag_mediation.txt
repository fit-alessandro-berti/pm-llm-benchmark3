The final answer has the right overall shape: it treats the flag as a policy bundle, rejects natural direct/indirect effects because of exposure-induced confounding, separates review occurrence from queue waiting, and covers P0–P4 plus the required identification designs. The flag-versus-unflagged headline means are also right (triage wait about 14 vs 149 minutes, service about 36 vs 57, cycle time about 991 vs 490; review 10/10 vs 4/10; escalation 6/10 vs 2/10; T1/T2 vs T3/T4). That is not enough to carry the grade.

The timestamp table, which is checkable, is wrong in several required slices. R17 is High, not Medium, so unflagged Medium is n=3 and unflagged High is n=3, not 4 and 2. The Medium manual-queue figures are the pooled unflagged numbers, not the subgroup. Unflagged-Low triage wait is 144 (median 138), not 169.5 (median 168). Flagged high-load escalation queue averages 405, not 432. Escalated triage service averages 33 (median 30), not 34 (median 33); manual service averages 185 (median 180), not 183.3 (median 195). Non-escalated triage service averages 40.5, not 39, and that row’s manual-queue median is 270, not 300. “~4× unflagged-low” and “morning flagged batch” are also inaccurate.

Two mechanism claims are simply false. High complexity is 5 flagged and 3 unflagged, not 5/5 flagged. Rejects are 2/10 in both groups, not 5/10 versus 2/10, so the decision-quality pathway is built on a nonexistent outcome gap. The graph’s `arrival_load → flag` edge is unsupported, and the feedback paragraph then treats arrival load as a consequence of the index case’s flag even though it is measured at arrival.

Estimands are mislabeled. P2 does not set manual review to 0, yet E2 calls that contrast a controlled direct effect. A uniform review probability is not an interventional indirect effect. E5 is not a controlled direct effect of escalation. E8 is a stratification, not an intervention. Decision quality never gets its own estimand. The pilot stopping rule says a cycle-time drop below 20% means continue, and “one site versus one site” conflicts with shift-level randomization. Reversal 1 wrongly treats added review capacity as net-harmful if hidden risk is real. The Python is not reproducible: `pm4 discover_dfg` is a syntax error, so nothing runs, and the small-n warning is never applied to the small cells.
