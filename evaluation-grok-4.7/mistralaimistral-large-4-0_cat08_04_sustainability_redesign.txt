7.4

The memo is complete, path-specific, and correctly refuses a blanket ban on air or dedicated vans. It still contains several concrete misapplications of the evidence and one contradictory indicator, so it is not nearly flawless.

The effects table anchors S8 to the wrong counterfactual. Shared emergency lanes are compared with the Critical stock/ground exception at 4.8 kgCO2e and 9% late, against the dedicated-van path at 52.0 kgCO2e and 3% late. That 4.8 path is a different, explicitly riskier variant. No emission figure is given for shared courier lanes, so the “large” transport gain is overstated and the service risk is borrowed from a path the option does not actually adopt.

S1’s cycle-time cell treats the 24h air-versus-ground transport-time cut as an order cycle-time increase. The stock path medians are 17h by air and 34h by ground, a 17h gap. Those are different quantities, and the larger figure is then reused as the service impact of shifting Standard-stock Service-contract volume.

The composite “impact per conforming on-time order” is defined on orders that are already on time and free of rework, then described as protection against shifting burden onto late, cancelled, or reworked orders. Under the subset definition, removing troubled orders from the measured set improves the indicator. The anti-gaming purpose does not follow. Adding kWh, kgCO2e, and kg without a stated normalization is also not a usable single indicator. Express share can double-count `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, and `expedite_flag`.

S2 says scrap may fall because batches are “more stable.” The evidence links larger batches to lower energy per item, longer waits, a 1.7× heat-treatment lateness factor, and a 6% obsolescence proxy. It does not support a scrap reduction. The material cell then parks obsolescence as “separate,” so the required effects table does not carry that material loss.

S3 states that CNC_A energy per item is lower at batch size 12. The 2.8-to-1.3 kWh/item curve is CNC_A’s own batch curve, not a CNC_A-versus-CNC_B measurement. The 16.0 versus 32.0 kWh comparison also mixes heat treatment, batching, and rework loops. The same 32-versus-16 gap is placed in the S7 energy cell, so inspection, machine shift, and batching are each illustrated with an effect that none of them would deliver alone. The memo also underuses the explicit finding that CNC_B is faster.

Section 5 treats 410 as the cancellation count. That is only the `Availability check -> Cancel due to delay` arc. Backorder adds 60, and `Cancel due to delay -> END` is 530. The partial-shipment transport note, “7.2 versus ground 3.6 plus extra shipment,” double-counts: 7.2 is already the path average for the split-and-backorder variant. S11 also does not carve out Critical-downtime lines, where a partial ship can protect a down machine. S10 records lower late-delivery risk when promises are lengthened; that can be a change in the promise rather than faster fulfillment, and the service column does not separate those.

The boundary sentence that upstream extraction is inside the system “where represented by `material_scrap_kg`” blurs a mass of process scrap with upstream burden. The later claim that Critical-downtime triage cuts the 38.0 kgCO2e segment average without creating unacceptable downtime risk is stronger than the memo’s own misclassification warning.

The required sections, segment-and-slack distinctions, SP1/SP2/Q1/R1 controls, rework and obsolescence, empty-return vans, and post-implementation log design are otherwise tightly grounded in the DFG and path table. Those strengths hold the score well above a merely complete generic plan.
