4.5

The memo keeps the required seven-part structure and sometimes uses real log facts (SP1 violations at 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract, missing SP2 justification on 31% of dedicated vans, consolidation savings of 35–55%, 6% batch obsolescence, CNC_B rework 18% versus 9% on CNC_A). Those merits are outweighed by invented effect sizes, a few mechanisms that fight the policies, and a package claim that the path shares cannot support.

**Boundary.** The gate-to-gate cut (machine energy, transport kgCO2e, scrap, packaging, rework, batch wait) matches the attributes. It leaves out the impacts that dominate a spare-parts lifecycle: customer downtime when a Critical-downtime or Service-contract part is late, and the follow-on buy or expedite after the 530 delay cancellations. Assumptions that Regular buyers will accept longer promises, and that Service-contract buyers will accept negotiated dates, are stated without a log basis.

**Indicators.** Several metrics are not usable. Energy per order divided by `(1 + rework_flag)` and energy per item divided by `(1 + batch_wait_hours)` mix unrelated units and hide the trade-off the batching evidence is about. The cycle-time compliance formula is missing the comparison (`cycle_time` versus `promised_lead_time_hours`). Route diversity counted as unique `route_mode` values does not measure policy R1 (an alternate route still available in a carrier disruption). Returns appear in a formula although the log exposes `rework_flag`, rework production, and rework packaging, not a returns event. Obsolescence, partial-shipment rate, and expedite rate are the stronger constructs; only some of them are defined.

**Interventions.** Eight options are discussed, but the effect sizes are mostly unsupported, which the prompt forbids.

- S1’s 30–50% air reduction is not in the log. Stock-to-air is 5% of orders, median cycle 17h and 3% late, versus stock-to-ground at 34h and 8% late. “Maintains or improves” on-time delivery conflicts with that gap. Air is justified only as a 24h transport saving when slack fails the 85% SP1 test.
- S2’s caps (8–12 pieces, 24h / 12h / 36h) and “20–40% energy” and “15–25% late-risk” cuts are design guesses. The evidence is narrower: CNC_A falls from 2.8 kWh/item at batch 1–3 to 1.3 at batch 12–20 while median wait rises from 4h to 38h, and heat-treatment waits above batch 10 carry 1.7× late risk. Obsolescence is absent inside the option itself.
- S4’s “60–80% of this segment” is too large. Standard-stock Service-contract transport is 7.8 kgCO2e versus 4.2 for Regular, so closing the whole gap is about a 46% segment cut, and only on orders whose promise still clears ground plus priority pick. A guaranteed 24h ground service is stronger than the observed ground-path median of 34h. The segment median is already 18h with 5% late, so replacing the air subset can miss a 95% on-time claim.
- S5 correctly cites 35–55%, then cuts the wait to 6–12h. The log’s consolidation wait is 12–24h. “90%+ on time” is a softer bar than the current 7% late rate on Standard-stock Regular.
- S6 misuses the 40% figure. That 40% is the damage-return reduction from using reinforced packaging on heavy made-to-order parts, which also doubles `package_material_kg`. The example that reinforced packaging is unnecessary on ground conflicts with Q1 (made-to-order parts over 8 kg stay reinforced unless inspection certifies low damage risk). A compatibility check should stop route/geometry mismatches (260 standard and 360 reinforced packaging-rework events), not remove reinforcement.
- S7 treats the inspection bottleneck as a reason to add a second inspection. The stated mechanism is the opposite: inspection_cell queues cause rework because rushed checks miss defects (Inspection → Rework production 470; Rework → CNC 390). The 30–50% rework and 25–40% return cuts are not evidenced. CNC_B is the right machine to gate; heat treatment is not shown to be the high-scrap source.
- S8’s 50% van replacement and 30–40% critical-transport cut are untied to the evidence. The justified subset is the 31% of dedicated-van cases with no SP2 justification, plus precautionary urgents after a split. The emergency-van path is 8% of orders, 14h median, 3% late, and 52 kgCO2e, often with an empty return. The stock/ground critical exception is already slower and worse (23h, 9% late). “98%+ on time” raises today’s roughly 96% critical on-time rate while removing dedicated capacity.
- S10’s 20% longer default promise and 15–25% energy and transport savings are invented. Lengthening `promised_lead_time_hours` also lowers measured lateness for Made-to-order Regular (already 18% late, p90 180h) without shortening the physical wait.

S3 (move slack made-to-order work from CNC_B to CNC_A) and S11 (partial shipment → backorder, 7% of orders, 79h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e and a second dispatch) are the clearest path findings and are not analyzed. S9 is recommended later without a mechanism.

**Effects table.** Where a range matches the prompt (consolidation 35–55%), it is usable. The other cells (60–80%, 20–40%, 15–25%, 2–4h, 1–2h) are false precision. S5’s cycle-time band contradicts the 12–24h wait. S2 claims a scrap reduction from batching even though larger batches raise the 14-day unshipped share. S1’s service sign is left ambiguous in the table and positive in the text. Direction markers are missing on many cells, so energy, lateness, and resilience signs cannot be read.

**Rebounds.** Batch obsolescence correctly uses the 6% / 14-day finding, and packaging, urgent-status gaming, and flexibility loss are real. Risk 8 repeats risk 1. The transport rebound is generic congestion. The log-specific displacements are missing: a missed ground promise flipping `expedite_flag` back to air or van; heat-treatment batch savings followed by the 1.7× late risk and an express leg; extra inspection load making the queue worse; partial shipments doubling `transport_kgco2e`; customer plants sitting idle, which can dwarf a 52 kg van saving; and longer promises pushing inventory and scrap onto the customer.

**Package.** Phasing S1, S4, S6, and capped batching ahead of blanket consolidation is a sensible order, and critical vans are not banned outright. The claim that the package addresses 60–70% of transport emissions and 30–40% of energy does not survive the mix. Weighted transport is about 10.3 kgCO2e/order, of which the critical segment is about 41% and the dedicated-van path about 40%. Stock air is about 9% and the made-to-order air path about 13%. Replacing half of vans by a hypothetical 30–40%, plus shifting only slack stock air, cannot approach 60–70% without cutting service-critical transport the prompt says to protect. Energy is about 7.4 kWh/order, roughly 87% of it made-to-order. The rework path is 5% of orders at 32 kWh (about 22% of energy). Removing that penalty and moving the 6% immediate-CNC path (24 kWh) toward the batched 16 kWh path is on the order of the mid-teens percent, not 30–40%, once wait caps stop batches from reaching 12–20. S1, S4, and S5 are also added as if their transport savings were independent. Calling shared couriers and consolidation a resilience gain sits poorly with R1: dedicated capacity is what currently holds the critical path at 3–4% late.

**Governance.** SP1/SP2/Q1 reviews, an exception log, segment dashboards, and an A/B pilot on Standard-stock Service-contract are the right control loop. The numeric targets fight the baseline: critical on-time 98%+ and median 16h versus a current median of 15h and about 4% late; a 15% air-share cap on all Service-contract orders, which would pull made-to-order and critical freight onto the same rule as stock orders that merely have slack. Regular on-time at 90%+ blends a 7% late stock segment with an 18% late made-to-order segment. Obsolescence, CNC_A versus CNC_B scrap, empty van returns, and `expedite_flag` are not in the control set. The low precision of the discovered model, caused by emergency and partial paths combining in disruptions, is never used to separate steady-state conformance from disruption periods. A quarterly carbon budget by segment can pressure approvers to refuse a justified van.

The redesign is directionally aware of slack, segment, and rework, but the quantities, the S6/Q1 conflict, the understated consolidation delay, and the 60–70% transport claim are material errors rather than presentation nits.
