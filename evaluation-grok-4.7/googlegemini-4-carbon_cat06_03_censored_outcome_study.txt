6.5

The design correctly keeps non-activated applications out of the non-default class, accounts for all 48,000 starts, and separates administrative censoring, feed delay, activation selection, transfer, and permanent missingness. The estimand, the backtest, and the skeleton then contradict that identification strategy in several places that would change the fitted probabilities.

The primary target is not one operational estimand. Prediction time is simultaneously the first Credit Review, 24 hours from start, and a k-event prefix. The displayed conditional default probability conditions on activation time \(T_{\text{act}}\), which is unknown at prediction time. The realized-loss product then drops \(T_{\text{act}}\) and multiplies \(P(S_{\text{act}}\mid X_{\le t_p})\) by \(P(\text{default}\mid X_{\le t_p}, S_{\text{act}})\). That product equals the joint event probability only for the marginal conditional probability, not for a version still conditioned on \(T_{\text{act}}\). Cause-specific Cox models and Fine–Gray models are written as interchangeable, but one targets cause-specific hazards and the other the subdistribution hazard; a CIF comes from Fine–Gray or from combining all cause-specific hazards, not from either label alone. Calling the reject-as-non-default coding “collider bias” misstates a definition and selection failure. Section 3 also puts all 48,000 cases into the Stage-1 risk set after Section 2 has already removed cases that never reach \(t_p\) or have already terminated.

The cohort arithmetic and the 125-day buffer (90 plus a 35-day feed) are consistent, and the flow table assigns every start to a real status. Folding permanently missing partner feeds into the given 3,600 “not yet matured” accounts is an assumption the snapshot does not identify. The maturity rule is also applied only in prose: any arrived `default_90d` is treated as usable.

Validation names the right threats (policy date, migration, shared queues, immature labels, random `pm4py.split_train_test` splits) and then builds a biased test. Stage 2 keeps applications started 15 September–10 October 2025 only if they activated by 13 October 2025, so the labeled test set is the fast-activation subset. A start-date split at 1 July 2025 still trains on cases whose later events and decisions fall under the new policy. The June 30, 2025 as-of training rule is not the split that is implemented. The 12.5% base rate is the raw 2,700/21,600 export rate, not the mature, IPCW, or IPOW population being scored. Stage-specific C-index, IBS, PR-AUC, and O/E are appropriate; the combined realized-loss probability has no metric. “Manski bounds” includes a \(2\times\) scenario that is not an endpoint of the bound.

Prefix rules for canonicalization, short traces, loops, and elapsed time are substantively careful, and milestone prefixes are correctly preferred to pooled k. The skeleton then implements k=3 only. The leakage table’s keep/repair/drop decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-prefix events, `documents_total`, point-in-time `risk_score`, extraction-time experience, and `label_available_at` are the right decisions. Two feature definitions reopen leakage: a regional historical median that is not pinned to \(t_p\), and an “out-of-fold” variant frequency alongside a trailing window.

The skeleton is concrete and uses `read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, `get_prefixes_from_log`, a start-time split, train-only `discover_dfg` and `get_variants`, and a forbidden-column assert. It also undoes the design:

- `causal_res_exp` is `groupby("org:resource").cumcount()` on a frame sorted by case id, then timestamp. That order is not event time, so later events of lexicographically earlier cases enter earlier cases’ “experience.”
- A label is treated as known when `label_available_at` is missing, and any label already present is `Activated_Labeled` even if activation is inside the 125-day window. That recreates the fast-default / slow-clearance bias the text flags.
- `stage1_time_days` runs from case start while the sample is restricted to cases that survived to the k-th event. Landmark analysis needs time from \(t_p\), or left truncation at \(t_p\). The handoff passes the uncorrected time to a Fine–Gray model.
- Outcomes are built once at 15 February 2026. The code never refits labels as of 30 June 2025.
- `documents_total` is absent from the forbidden set and from the audit assert. `MIGRATION_DATE` is unused.
- The activity map is written as the real post-migration vocabulary.
- The `np.select` choice `"Unactivated_Competing_" + series` is not a reliable choicelist entry.
- The block is about 183 lines against the requested approximate 80–160, and the return annotation omits the prefix log.

Section 8 is the cleanest part: conditional default given activation under the observed policy is distinguished from counterfactual approval, with post-\(t_p\) underwriting information, and deployment feedback is specific (early rejection, queue sorting, channel gaming, transfer diversion). Those claims are narrower than the Stage-1/Stage-2 product in Section 1, which is the right direction, but the study as specified would still train and score a different quantity from the one it defines.
