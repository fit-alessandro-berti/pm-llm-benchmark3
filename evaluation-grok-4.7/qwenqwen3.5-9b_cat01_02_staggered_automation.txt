3.5

The write-up keeps the required 10-part skeleton and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, but the empirical core is wrong and the identification work does not meet the task.

The pre/post table in §5 is not a median analysis. Alpha’s Jan–Mar median is about 48.9 hours and its Apr–Aug median about 48.5 (a slight decline); Beta is about 45.9 to 47.8. The reported 98.5→107.6 and 93.4→101.2 figures are roughly p90 levels, and even those post means and the Gamma/Delta/Epsilon figures do not match the panel. Gamma’s “pre” value is copied from Beta. Relative to Delta and Epsilon, both median and p90 deteriorated less at Alpha/Beta than at the never-treated branches, so the claim that adopters worsened compared with controls is the reverse of the arithmetic. May–Jun spikes and Aug recoveries are shared by Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon, so Gamma’s post-July drop is not evidence of a pilot success. Escalation is not stable: it jumps in March at every branch and keeps rising. Rework falls in April at Alpha/Beta, then rises with the May complexity shift everywhere.

That reversal contaminates §7, §8, and the claim ladder. Median is called slightly negative, Gamma is treated as a beneficial contrast, and the “suggestive” bullet says adopter cycle times increased versus controls. A safe claim is only that a uniform percent effect is not identified; the descriptive contrast does not point the way the answer says.

Estimands are all marked identifiable. That is the central mistake. Availability is not fast-path use (`automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6% when `automation=0`, so “>0” is an empty contrast). Interference from the shared fraud desk and the routing shift, treatment-version differences (availability, intensity, learning, local practice), and CSAT response rates of 20–45% with no counts are never built into the estimands. The primary “staggered event study” does not address heterogeneous timing or two-way fixed-effects bias, adjusts for post-treatment arrivals, staffing, and case mix without a direct-versus-total-effect distinction, and the justification is well under the required 150 words. Synthetic control with three pre-months and two donors is not credible. Required threats—few clusters and dynamic effects—are missing, and several bias directions are generic or backwards (regression to the mean is not “failure to fall to the control level”).

The rollout choice, expand with an embedded evaluation, is the right family of decision, but it is argued from the false median increase and never names Delta versus Epsilon. A 50% phase-in is not a randomized assignment, the guardrail ignores the common May–Jun CSAT dip, and the scale-up rule is another short pre/post comparison with no control group.

The Python does not run and does not do the requested analysis: `pd.compat.StringIO` and `plt` are invalid, pre and post are the same mean so every contrast is zero, event time subtracts an automation flag rather than a treatment date, the “fixed effects” fit has no branch or month effects, and statsmodels cluster-robust errors are available but unused. Warnings and assumption comments do not repair that.
