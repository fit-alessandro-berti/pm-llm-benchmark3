The final diagnosis matches the right overall pattern: April is a pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change, August–September combine supplier performance, concept change, and Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable because maturity is 55%. The six required sections, the alert gate against retraining on label delay, and the four failure modes are present and mostly pointed in the right direction.

Those strengths are undercut by checkable errors and missed requirements. July’s ECE fell from 0.060 to 0.042, so “calibration error increased” is false. May’s V3 matured breach stays about 0.33, and part-wait time was already rising from January (28 h) through March (35 h); calling May early concept drift, and tying May’s 3% missingness to the April cache that was never backfilled, overstates the audit. Variant share after diagnosis is treated as covariate drift even though the score is issued at diagnosis, before the path is observed. The stable 3.8–4.6 h remote-fix edge is never used, and September’s V3 jump is not separated from August’s: Partner Z (24%, 58% V3) accounts for most of the September mix shift, while August’s rise to 35% V3 happens with no Partner Z.

Two required tests give an expected result under only one hypothesis, and falsifiers are not stated for each drift type. The sketch calls `filter_time_range(..., mode='cases')`, which is not a real mode, and treats performance-DFG values as lists of raw times instead of aggregates that already contain `median`, so the summary cannot run. Cohort comments name the filters but not how an event-calendar cohort differs from the case-start cohort used in the metrics. Recalibrating on pooled June–August data also blends the pre-break and post-break supplier regimes. Missing spaces throughout the table and narrative further reduce clarity.
