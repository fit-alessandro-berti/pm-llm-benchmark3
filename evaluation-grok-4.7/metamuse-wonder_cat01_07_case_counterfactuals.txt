6.5

The write-up meets the required skeleton and gets the main qualitative traps right, but its headline cycle-time ranges are not temporally consistent with its own derivations. Under a strict reading, that is a core failure, not a presentation nit.

The partial order, conditional constraints, four-eyes handling, and the split between timing, routing, and decision policy are mostly sound. K-337 is correctly treated as a policy change: the second information request is removed, the discrepancy stays visible, and final compliance still sits after Assessment 2 and before Decision. K-411 correctly separates earlier verification readiness from earlier decision completion, and the scale-up discussion covers C1 reservations, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment saturation, weekends, and false concurrency. Pilots, ranking, synthesis length, and the eight labeled assumptions all fit the requested form.

The numbers do not survive cross-checking. For K-104, the table gives a 4–16 h change with a worst case near zero, while the derivation’s best case is only a Wednesday 10:00–12:00 decision (about 6–8 h), its base case is 3–6 h, and Tuesday-evening completion after a 19:10 assessment finish is never reconciled with the 16 h upper bound. For K-219, the table’s base saving of 4–14 h and worst case of +12 to +36 h do not match the derivation’s best 12–16 h, base 0–8 h, and worst “+24 h or more.” “Cycle time 4 to 14 h” is also ambiguous between a saving and an impossible total duration. For K-337, “2 to 4 working days” cannot mean a move from Wednesday week 2 to Monday–Tuesday week 2, which is at most about two working days, and Section 3’s 1.5–3 working days is a third, still different, figure. Friday C1 availability is then left sitting behind a Monday–Tuesday decision with no M1 constraint to explain the gap.

K-411’s scenario labels overlap: best is 5–20 h and base is 2–4 h inside an overall 0–20 h band. The claim that both checks can finish by Tuesday 13:30 is too early given a roughly 3–4 h D2 queue after 09:35 plus about 4.5 h of service; the later 16:00–19:00 finish is the one consistent with those inputs.

Two further feasibility gaps matter. The partial order forces preliminary compliance to finish before assessment, which the intervention does not require. Across the factual traces, D1 is double-booked on Tuesday (K-219 09:30–13:30 and K-337 08:30–14:30), violating the single-interval rule; moving K-219 to D2 would also remove that clash, and the answer never notices it. Smaller defects include calling D1’s 11–13 h queue the longest queue while C1 is at 20 h, an unsigned “14 h to +36 h” benefit, and dropped “not equal” signs in the four-eyes checks.

The reasoning about interference and decision risk is strong enough to stay well above a failed answer. The counterfactual durations are not stable enough to score higher.
