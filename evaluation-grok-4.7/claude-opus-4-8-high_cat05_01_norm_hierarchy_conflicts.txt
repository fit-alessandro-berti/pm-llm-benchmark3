5.6

The framework is serious and often well aimed, but the operative classifications repeatedly detach from the answer’s own rules, and several holdings misread the sources. Under a strict reading, that is a substantial failure of consistency and evidential discipline, not a polish problem.

Section 1 correctly refuses a single global/newest/strongest-document ladder, puts the burden on the party claiming a waiver, treats silence as non-waiver, applies controls at the time of the act, and flags the A7 reading as an assumption. The necessity sentence overreaches: bare emergency necessity is not an independent waiver of timing or onboarding. A4 waives competitive tendering only; commit-before-PO and register relief come from B4 and, inside scope and window, from ECD-2.

Section 2 is the right kind of hierarchy: issue-specific, with scope, override, and defeater columns. The closing equation “integrity: A > B > C ≈ D” is not defensible. ECD-2 is silent on integrity; the CFO email affirmatively tries to restructure operational approvals and is an uncontrolled email. Those are not equivalent. The table also never resolves whether `Finance_Approve` is the A2 Finance Controller, or whether `Plant_Approve` is the independent budget owner. That distinction decides several cases and is left collapsed into “Finance.”

The rule catalogue is mostly faithful. The B4 pseudo-logic is not. `emergency_local(P) unless plant_manager_reachable and not delegated` treats a failed Plant Manager substitution as the absence of an emergency. B4 separates those ideas. A stoppage expected within six hours is an emergency, and in an emergency commitment may precede the PO. Substitution by an actively delegated Shift Lead is an additional permission, and only when the Plant Manager is absent.

That error drives P-501. A four-hour predicted stoppage meets B4. ECD-2 is correctly excluded at 08:50, and the missing delegation correctly blocks Shift Lead substitution for the Plant Manager. It does not follow that there was “no valid emergency” or that commit-before-PO was prohibited. `Plant_Approve` and `Finance_Approve` both exist; at EUR 38,000, A2 and A3 are not triggered, and B2 does not textually require those approvals before commitment. Actor identities for SoD are unknown. NONCOMPLIANT at med-high confidence, on that ground, is not supported. The reversible evidence is not a delegation PDF alone.

P-502 contradicts itself in one row. The controlling interpretation says A3 was “satisfied albeit late.” The deviation and justification cells correctly say a post-commit clearance does not meet A3 and is not cured under A6. Under the answer’s own predicates, an uncured integrity breach is NONCOMPLIANT, not JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. The joint authorization was recorded in about 2 hours 45 minutes, inside four hours, yet Section 6 calls that recording a logging defect. EUR 62,000 also triggers A2 before commitment. `Finance_Approve` at 13:05 is after the 10:15 commitment, and no budget owner is identified. That breach is omitted. Severity “low-medium” for an uncured new-supplier compliance failure is too light, and the supplied fact that prior directive practice had not extended to new-supplier compliance clearance is unused.

P-503’s NONCOMPLIANT result is robust because A2 and A3 fail whether or not the pumps are in ECD-2 scope. The row still says the ECD-2 timing override was satisfied while also saying scope likely fails. Those cannot both be true. A1 confidence is too high: joint authorization by PROC-3 and IC-1 shows two distinct Incident Command members, and “final business approver” is not clearly the same role as one of two emergency authorizers. Missing approval and a tainted authorization are related but not identical defects.

P-504 is rightly NONCOMPLIANT: cafeteria repair is outside ECD-2, the supplier is unapproved, and there is no B4 safety or six-hour production stoppage. Listing absent Plant and Finance approval as a deviation is wrong. EUR 24,000 is below B2’s EUR 25,000 trigger and below A2.

P-505 misapplies both A3 and ON-44. EUR 88,000 from an approved supplier does not trigger A3, so the 17:40 clearance is not “A3 satisfied.” ON-44 is the 14 July outage, under which ERP time can lag real time by up to 12 minutes. It is not the authority for an 18 July clock that may have been nine minutes fast. The supplier email at 17:59:31 is the stronger contemporaneous artifact, and the answer’s own evidential rule treats that as better than the 18:06 ERP stamp. Calling the result INDETERMINATE while Section 5 says the contrary reading is weak does not follow. If the commitment is inside the window, the unanswered A2 problem remains: `Finance_Approve` is at 19:00, after commitment, and no budget owner appears. The secondary label JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION repeats the P-502 error. No Create_PO, Plant approval, or four-hour authorization record is in the evidence, and none is flagged.

P-506 is a sound NONCOMPLIANT on expiry. A verbal overnight extension is not a written, scoped, time-limited exception, and 19 July is outside ECD-2. Section 6 then blurs A2: Finance at 06:05 is before commitment at 06:20, but that single event is not both an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller, and `Plant_Approve` is still after commitment. B4 is not separately tested against the contamination-recovery facts.

P-507 is the most coherent case. Pre-commit compliance, in-window eight-hour need, two Incident Command authorizers, and the 12:40 signed PDF against the 14:30 ERP stamp are handled in the right order. Primary NONCOMPLIANT for missing A2, with timing and supplier relief treated as justified only on those issues, matches the stated hierarchy. The Section 5 D1 alternative is the credible flip and is properly rated as limited to Finance mechanics.

P-508 does not follow that same rule. Compliance, Legal clearance, SoD separation from BUY-8, and line-restoration scope are real strengths. They do not supply the missing pre-commitment budget owner and Finance Controller for a EUR 155,000 purchase. D2’s Legal clearance is additional to the CFO’s EUR 150,000 sentence; it is not an A2 substitute, and D4 keeps essential integrity controls. The answer’s hierarchy says ECD-2 and the CFO email cannot displace A2 substance. Primary JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, at low-medium severity, contradicts P-507 and the pseudo-logic. “A1 satisfied” is also too strong when the final business approver required by A2 never appears.

Section 6 sorts P-503, P-504, and P-506 as substantive breaches, then omits the A2/A3 failures on P-502 and P-508 and mislabels P-502’s timely authorization record as a logging defect. Late performance of Compliance is not late evidence of a timely clearance. The monitoring items are concrete. Several remediation steps are usable, but “ratification” needs to stay mitigation under A6, and the P-506 note corrects itself mid-sentence.

Section 7 is the strongest part. It separates a COO/Incident Commander operational overlay from a CRO A7 exception, sets an objective criticality test, keeps SoD and pre-commitment Finance and Compliance as a floor, rejects verbal extensions, makes ERP time rebuttable, and adds a post-hoc review and escalation brake. The residual gap is the CFO: the clause never states that interpretive authority over Finance cannot replace the named Finance Controller and budget owner with Incident Command authorization. The “unless two IC members designate it” sentence also softens the 24-hour test.

The answer therefore shows real process awareness in the abstract and then fails the test it set. Where a pre-commitment integrity control is unmet and unwaived, the catalogue says NONCOMPLIANT. P-507 obeys that rule. P-502 and P-508 do not. P-501 rests on a misreading of B4. Those are outcome errors, not presentation issues.
