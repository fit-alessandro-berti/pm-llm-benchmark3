The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape, ranks machine capacity last, and gets the headline clock arithmetic right: J1/J2’s B1 waits (3 h 55 min and 3 h 40 min), J1’s pack-to-dispatch gap on S1 (5 h 55 min), and the 50 min from TEST-1 free at 16:35 to J6 at 17:25. That is not enough. The answer misses the coupling the evidence is built to show, and several claims are false.

The jobs are not independent, and the delays cascade. J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40 and is batch-ready only at 11:55, so B1’s 12:05 start can be downstream of that hold; J1 and J2 absorb the wait. The same fixture is on J4’s 11:45 CNC-2 start. J4 is batch-ready at 13:10, but B2 waits until 14:50 while RACK-7 is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35 and J5, held by L7 until the 13:50 release, is ready only at 14:40. J4 packs at 19:10; J1 has been packed since 13:30 and leaves only when S1 dispatches at 19:25. L7 also ties J2 to J5 across B1/B2 and S2/S3, and J2 entered the cure under a supervisor override while the lot was still quarantined. L8 (C1, C3 → J1, J3) is never mentioned. None of that chain is assembled. Mechanisms are listed side by side, batch sync is marked high-confidence, and the lot is demoted to a low-confidence logging quirk.

Graph and flattening claims are wrong in detail. The O2O graph is not bipartite; `B1 batches J1` is reversed; `requires` / `batches` / `consolidates` are not the job DFG edges. The third “motif” is Job–Shipment only, mislabeled as involving Component. Flattening never discusses duplicated shared events (one `Batch Cure` on B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3, C1–C3). The lot view is described as unlinked to J2/J5 even though those events already relate to them; the gap is the job extraction, which kept only events tied to the job at the source. “Batch processing time of 4 hours” is invented. CNC-2 is documented idle only 10:05–10:47, yet 09:50–11:45 is treated as 1 h 55 min of FIX-A waiting. S2/S3’s 25–35 min post-pack lags are short dispatch tails, not evidence that consolidation is slow; the long shipment damage is J1 waiting for J4. The 1.8-SD remark, which says the pain is common rather than a single outlier job, is unused. Temporal order is repeatedly treated as cause (“solely,” “purely,” “proportional”).

Two of the three lifecycle stories do not span four object types; “PACKER” is not in the log. Interventions name the right levers, but the spillovers are confused (splitting B1 does not harm J4/J5; shipping S1 early does not delay S2/S3), and none has an object-centric evaluation design. The pm4py sketch omits `discover_etot`, calls `ocel_flattening` without an object type, runs `discover_performance_dfg` on the OCEL, and then treats a higher Job–Tool interaction count as proof that the tool is the bottleneck — the frequency-versus-impact mistake the interaction table warns against. Several “new” fields (lot release, FIX-A release) are already in the evidence.
