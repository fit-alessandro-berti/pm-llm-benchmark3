The duplicate taxonomy and the case decisions are largely right: exact re-ingestion is the only drop, e03/e04 and e11/e16 are the only strongly supported merges, e05 and e14 stay, e08/e09 are a label collision, and e06/e07 and e15 stay as abstraction layers in the conservative path. Counts 15, 14, and 10 follow from those choices, and the warning about `have_common_object=True` on O1/V1 is correct. That is not enough for a high score.

The ordering section contradicts itself. Rules 1–4 are defined as semantic, including within-source `source_seq`, then e04 before e05 (scanner 551 before 552) and e13 before e14 (PSP 401 before 402) are treated as non-causal fallbacks whose order is interchangeable. Those sequence numbers are the strongest local evidence in the ties. Separately, e08 before e09 and invoice before authorize are stated as strict, with no admissible alternative, although the given facts only force acceptance before handover and PSP detail before the ERP balance update.

The implementation misses the constraints it names. `ocel_raw.events` is reassigned before any copy, so the raw log is modified in place. `ocel_drop_duplicates(..., variant="exact_technical")` and `epsilon_days` are not a real, justified use of those APIs; a timestamp-and-activity drop would remove e05 and e14, which the design forbids. `merge_verified_pair` never applies the authority map, so scanner-only attributes are dropped with the row rather than unioned. The evidence-based copy is taken after relabeling and epsilon shifting, so it is not an independent scenario. The audit schema is sound on paper and is not what the aggressive library call records, yet reversal is claimed for that path too.

Sensitivity analysis names the right damages, then misstates directly-follows structure. Duplicate picks and invoices create same-activity self-loops, not extra Pick→Pack or Issue→Authorize edges. The Order-flattened “Pick I1 → Pick I2” edge assumes activity labels the code never refines. ET–OT and OTG effects are not actually compared for all four datasets.
