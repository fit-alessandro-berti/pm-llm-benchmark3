The write-up covers every required section and the staffing recommendation is pointed in a defensible direction: hybrid routing is preferred over pure pooling because of tails, fairness, and shock behavior, not because it wins base-case mean delay. That is not enough to carry the answer. Several load figures are wrong, one dispatch rule is inverted, the policy comparison is not auditable, and the simulation does not implement the policies it is supposed to compare.

**Capacity arithmetic does not hold together.** Daily mechanical and electrical branch counts are high (about 19.1 and 25.3 versus about 18.1 and 24.3). Software at roughly 50% is already multiplier-adjusted capacity (981 base-equivalent minutes); calling that “50% before multipliers” double-counts speed. The 12:00–14:00 block subtracts four breaks when only Ben, Ada, and Chen are off, so nominal availability is about 510 minutes, not 480. The 18:00–20:00 band of 120–210 minutes does not match Emil plus at most 30 minutes of Deepa overtime. U4 means are inflated (mechanical about 29.6, not 30.7; electrical about 37.1, not 38.7; software about 44.5, not 46.6), and those inflated means times the stated volumes are about 340 minutes, not the claimed 250–300. U2 prose says electrical demand rises 60–70% and about 10 branches, while the code doubles E/ES shares and renormalizes at fixed volume, which lifts the electrical share only about 20%. “2 staff” is written as “21 staff.”

**Policies are not operationally consistent.** SPEC both forbids overflow and allows Ben, Deepa, and Farah to overflow. The pooled rule is labeled SERPT, but `priority_weight × remaining` sorted descending is weighted longest-remaining-time within a priority class. O2 and the hybrid urgency score are missing coefficients, so the stated objectives are not computable. Section 4’s SLA bands (for example hybrid base P1 at 87–93%) are not derived from the offered-load math or from any shown Kingman calculation; ±25–35% does not make those bands auditable. Hybrid U4 at 82–88% is then described as near the 90% P1 target.

**The code does not test the argument.** Cases are list-scheduled in arrival order, so a later P1 cannot pass an earlier P3. `is_available` is never used, so breaks and shift ends are not enforced. “Preemption” adds 10% of the P1’s own duration to the P1; it does not interrupt a victim. Parallel branches are still executed back-to-back, then charged the 8-minute coordination penalty, so parallelism is strictly harmful and SPEC/POOL always pay it. Team-transfer and setup are applied to intake/validation, not only diagnostic family changes. Reservation, overflow thresholds, and the evening mode are absent, but the closing note only admits missing breaks, overtime, and overlap. Case IDs omit policy, so three policies are stacked into one log before `format_dataframe` and the handover network. End hours at or past 24 can crash timestamp construction. Five seeds are averaged with no dispersion.

Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest: handover versus touch time is labeled correctly, gaming and P3 starvation have concrete controls, and the pilot protects P1 while keeping pooling in shadow mode. Those sections cannot offset an inconsistent load model and a scaffold whose omitted core is the dispatch logic the recommendation depends on.
