The write-up has the right skeleton and the right primary pipeline, but it is not a reliable audit. It treats an unrun conversion as validated, and it gets the silent legal branch wrong in the invariant table and again in the tests.

The source model is an exclusive choice (`Operator.XOR` with a silent skip), not an “inclusive XOR.” After tau-hiding, Register → Assess Risk → Prepare Evidence → Configure Trial → Inspect Trial → Release Change → Archive Change is a legal complete trace: the skip can close the legal branch with no visible event. INV-09 lists that same visible sequence (minus Archive) as a negative witness of release “before Legal resolved,” and §5 repeats it as `neg_witness_premature_release`. That contradicts INV-03, whose positive witness is exactly the skip path. The illegal case (release while the XOR is still open) is a marking property. It is not a distinct visible trace, so “observable after hiding tau: Yes” for that synchronization is wrong. INV-03’s negative witness (Archive with no Release) tests finality, not optionality. Several other negative cells are “N/A.” There is no invariant that the loop body occurs at least once, even though minimum iteration count was required.

§4 marks almost every POWL→Petri, Petri→BPMN, and BPMN round-trip cell `validated preserved` and says the behavior was verified by reachability exploration. Nothing was executed. `validated preserved` is the wrong term; those cells are at most `expected preserved` or `not tested`. Calling the Petri→BPMN step `approximated` for optionality and the round trip `validated preserved` is inconsistent. The concurrency paragraph says Petri-net semantics are non-interleaved partial orders. PM4Py firing is interleaving; language checks cannot separate true concurrency from all linear extensions. “Dual input transitions/arcs” into Release is the wrong join picture (AND-join is two preset places on one transition).

The conversion script follows the required PM4Py calls and does trap the process-tree step, but any exception in Pipeline 2—including a missing marking, a soundness error, or a failed save—is printed as proof that the net is not block-structured. Pipeline 1 treats markings as booleans, so an empty `Marking` is indistinguishable from `None`. Pipeline 2 never checks markings before `check_soundness`. The §5 explorer puts the `Marking` itself in a `set` key; `Marking` is a `Counter` and is unhashable, so the routine crashes before any comparison. The prose promises reachability-graph analysis; the code never calls `convert_to_reachability_graph`. There is no playout. Footprints on `sequence` and `parallel` only are not equivalence. The bound is `max_visible_len=9`, while one `Correct Defect` redo already needs about 10–11 visible labels, yet §5 says the bound covers “0 and 1 loop iterations” and “formally proven” equivalence, and §9 says “1–2” iterations. Zero body executions are outside the language; one redo is outside the bound. The negative check would be accepted by a correct explorer.

§6’s layout warnings are useful, but the ASCII net is not a faithful picture of the asymmetric join, and it omits the loop. Criterion 6 in §7 quarantines any failed intermediate, including the process-tree failure that §2 correctly treats as an acceptable outcome. §8 is the strongest part: POWL stays authoritative, BPMN is a picture, and edits should not go through the derivatives. It still treats footprint equality plus a bounded trace set as a 100% gate. §9’s data-and-atomicity item is outside the control-flow model; the real leftovers are bisimulation versus bounded language, tau-timing of the skip versus Release, and BPMN gateway semantics versus the net.
