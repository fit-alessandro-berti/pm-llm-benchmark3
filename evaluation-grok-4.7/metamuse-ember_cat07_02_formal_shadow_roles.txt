The role model is coherent and constraint-aware: complexity, authority, substitution, and shadow status are used, all 14 resources are placed, and sections 3, 4, and 6 meet the required coverage. It is not nearly flawless.

The Mateo row calls all 44 A9 events “preliminary.” Only the 21 week-5 events are described that way, and 13 of those were rechecked. That overstates his standing approval authority.

Giorgio’s A7/A8 totals are labeled “regular,” but they include the absence spike (31 A7 and 18 A8). Baseline commercial volume is lower, especially for pricing. That blurs substitution with steady-state competence, which the scenario explicitly warns against.

The quality-role cell is the weak classification argument. A11 is about 300 events against roughly 35–38 A10 events, so frequency clustering would keep Ivan and Julia together. A5 Configure (26 and 22) is also omitted, even though it is larger than the A3 counts that are cited and is the activity most likely to blur quality with technical work. Chen’s late shift versus Dina’s day shift is likewise unused as a confound for the complexity gap.

The code does not implement the analysis it describes. The “entropy” line is −∑p²: `apply(lambda x: x)` never takes a log, so the 0→NA guard does not produce Shannon entropy. `case_complexity` is aggregated at case level and then never used; the profile is event-weighted, which reintroduces the frequency bias the text rejects. `0.65` is an unexplained cutoff. `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` and `discover_organizational_roles(..., case_id=..., timestamp_key=...)` do not match PM4Py signatures, and `.items()` assumes a dict when the usual return is a list of role records. Those calls would not run as written. Service time is never computed, so the 38-versus-74-minute substitution result is not operationalized.
