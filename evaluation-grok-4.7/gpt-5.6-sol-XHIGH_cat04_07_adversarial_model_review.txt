8.4

The review is rigorous, use-case-aware, and technically strong on the central process-tree questions. Model S and Model C are read correctly under standard `->` / `+` / `X` / `tau` semantics; B1–B8 are classified correctly; fitness is not treated as policy truth; and the third design keeps mandatory checks mandatory while parking missing source and affected-check data in an explicit non-compliant/uncertain state. The dissent is a real closed-world rebuttal, not a token objection. The score is held down by concrete defects in the required checker, the audit matrix, and the validation skeleton.

The POWL backbone is the right shape: both initial checks sit on the path to approval, supplier and expedite are silent-choice nodes rather than global optionalization, approval is a true XOR, and `xor(τ, *(cycle, τ))` is a correct zero-or-more correction cycle whose body cannot skip the recheck. Probe claims that follow from that language (reject B3, B4, B8; admit B6 only as Technical-only recheck; admit compositional traces A1–A3; reject A4 and A5) match the expression. Local duplicate labels are justified and are not Model C’s global copying problem.

The policy function does not match that care at the boundary it tells the board to test. After recording missing Credit Check and Technical Review, an Expedite trace with neither check still evaluates `max([])` and raises before returning `nonconformant`. That path is in the proposed negative suite (expedite with both checks removed). A control checker that crashes on a hard-negative trace is not governance-ready code.

Section 8 then blurs the hybrid the rest of the paper defines. The normative result is structural membership intersected with `evaluate_policy`, and unknown `source_type` is always an uncertainty. B1 and B2 are nevertheless recorded as unqualified “Accepts / Valid,” while B6 is “conformant” given only `affected={Technical Review}`. Under the supplied function those traces are at best `conditionally_conformant` until source is internal; an external label makes the no-supplier trace nonconformant. B5 and B7 are qualified correctly, so the omission is inconsistent rather than a global convention. The added rule that every correction waits for both initial checks, and that expedite waits for the latest recheck, is a real restriction beyond P4/P6 (it rejects a one-check-then-correct-then-the-other-check trace). Expedite-after-correction is flagged for ratification; both-checks-before-any-correction is not.

The evaluation program is well aimed—stratified seeds, compositional holdouts, negative traces, separate metrics, a rejection rule that ignores aggregate scores—but the skeletons are not reliably executable. `case_features` sorts on a nonexistent `event_index`; both-checks-missing is coded as `T_first`; a single leftover `__rare__` stratum still breaks `StratifiedShuffleSplit`; and unbounded `play_out` on the correction loop is not a soundness proof. Alignment costs are only sketched at uniform visible-move cost, despite mandatory-skip cost being the sensitivity that matters.

No metric was fabricated, S’s `tau` permissiveness is correctly separated from the expedite-ordering mistake, and C’s precision is correctly treated as possible variant memorization. Those are the right governance conclusions. The remaining gaps are local but real: the compliance oracle crashes, the behavior matrix overstates full conformance, and one ordering assumption is stricter than the ratified policy.
