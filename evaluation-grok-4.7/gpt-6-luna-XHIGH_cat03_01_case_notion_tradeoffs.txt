7.5

The answer is requirement-complete, identifier-rich, and internally consistent on copied events, partial settlement, and the decision not to crown a lead object. It is not nearly flawless: one central DFG mechanism is missed, and several claims are looser than the log supports.

The strongest material is concrete and correct. Transit times (D1 3h, D2 4h55m, D3 22h45m), V1/V2/V3 membership (e18/e21, e19/e22/e25, e20/e26), payment singletons (e21, e22, e25, e26), and e16/e24 being one D2 occurrence copied into P2 and P3 and into O1–O3 are all right. Treating a copied event as both participation evidence and a non-occurrence is applied consistently in the matrix, the V2 and M2/M3 traps, the e16 reconciliation, and the 1/n sensitivity check. Position C is argued with a real counterargument and explicit change conditions, and the code matches that choice by omitting `cluster_equivalent_ocel` while still separating relation-row counts from distinct event ids.

The main analytical gap is divergence as a DFG defect. Defining divergence as work split across traces, then rating it High for almost every type, does not capture what order flattening actually does. O1’s two strands are forced into one trace, so independent strands form directly-follows edges: e11→e12, e15→e16, and e23→e24. O3 does the same across packages and deliveries: e13→e14, e16→e17, and e24→e25→e27. Those edges are spurious causality and biased sojourn times, not merely “more complex” packing. Distortion 2 calls O3’s two packs informative and warns about frequency, but never states the false Pack→Pack or Dispatch→Dispatch edge. Frequency inflation from convergence is covered well; intra-case divergence is not.

Smaller inaccuracies add up. “Picking” is not an activity in this log. Distortion 10 overstates variant collapse: O2 and O3 share e13, e16, e19, e22, e24, and e25, but O3 alone has e09, e14, e17, and e27, so a variant summary would separate them. Distortion 3 claims the package view hides which strand is delayed, yet I21 and I31 share e13/e16/e24, while P3’s 16:00 confirmation versus P4’s next-day e27 is visible once packages are the cases. The heading of distortion 4 implicates delivery flattening in copy inflation, although e16 and e24 each relate to only one delivery. Create-to-close order duration is anchored on unshared e01–e04 and e28–e31; shared-event attribution affects intermediate waits, not that endpoint duration. The OTG sketch O1→I11→P1→D1 follows physical flow, but the stored qualifiers run O1 contains I11, P1 packs I11, and D1 carries P1. “Very high” delivery convergence beside “low” duplication, and High/High convergence/divergence on most rows, blurs hub structure with replication risk. “Guaranteed by construction” is stronger than a three-case pattern. Criterion 2 in the lead-object decision is a counting rule the analyst can apply to any type; it does not by itself rule out a qualified order lead.

So the paper is a strong, usable analysis with the right refusal to treat the longest traces as truth, but the missed false directly-follows edges and the scattered overclaims keep it well short of excellent.
