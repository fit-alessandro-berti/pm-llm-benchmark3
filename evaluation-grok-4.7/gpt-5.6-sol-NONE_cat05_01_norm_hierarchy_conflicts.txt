7.1

The assessment is a serious, source-faithful analysis with the right overall architecture, and several classifications are stable under its own premises. It is not nearly flawless. The method is stated more carefully than it is applied, and those application gaps are large enough to move results and confidence, not merely wording.

The framework is strong where it stays issue-specific. GPIP-4 is kept in control of segregation, pre-commitment Compliance, and the reserved Chief Risk Officer exception. ECD-2 is confined to timing and supplier-register relief inside its window. The CFO email is refused as a Compliance or segregation waiver. Late ERP times are subordinated to the supplier acknowledgment in P-505 and the queued PDF in P-507. Post-commitment approvals are treated as mitigation. P-501 is correctly placed before 09:00, P-504 is correctly placed outside sterilization scope, P-506 is correctly placed after expiry, and P-502’s late new-supplier Compliance clearance is a sound NONCOMPLIANT finding on the stated hierarchy. The catalogue, dimensional hierarchy, defect taxonomy, and replacement clause all address the required issues.

The central flaw is that three different Finance theories are in force at once. Section 1 treats joint Incident Command authorization as sufficient for Plant approval and for Finance approval up to EUR 150,000, while leaving only the independent budget-owner half of A2 open. Section 2 then narrows the CFO email to a rule about how Finance approval is evidenced. The case table reimposes a separate Finance event: P-502 calls the 13:05 approval a deviation, and P-507 and P-508 treat the absence of a Finance event as part of the breach. Those holdings cannot all be true. The pseudo-logic makes the same merge explicit: the CFO email may satisfy ordinary Finance workflow “unless” A2 role elements are unproved. That `unless` ties two different rules together.

Burden of proof is also applied unevenly. Assumption 9 says an unproved high-risk pre-commitment approval is INDETERMINATE when the act may still have complied. P-505 follows that rule. P-507 and P-508 present the same unproved budget-owner and Finance Controller roles, and Section 6 later calls those gaps inability to prove, yet Section 4 classifies both NONCOMPLIANT, P-508 at high confidence. P-508 is the clearest example. On the stated premises it has pre-commitment Compliance, pre-commitment Legal, two Incident Command authorizers distinct from the requester and committer, covered scope, and a live directive. High-confidence NONCOMPLIANT is earned only by treating missing role proof as an affirmative breach. The paper never chooses one burden and then keeps it.

Two alternatives do not do what they claim. For P-503, moving backup pumps inside ECD-2 still leaves no Compliance clearance on a EUR 140,000 purchase and still leaves PROC-3 as requester, committer, and joint authorizer. Under the paper’s own refusal to let ECD-2 waive A3 or A1, that scope change does not produce INDETERMINATE. For P-506, the offered class is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, but the same paragraph says a six-hour SOP emergency would leave the Plant-approval and budget-owner defects in place. That alternative does not change the primary class.

P-501 is overconfident. B4 expressly relaxes PO-before-commitment and allows a delegated Shift Lead to stand in for an absent Plant Manager. B5 says Finance is not waived. Neither sentence states that Plant_Approve or Finance_Approve must precede commitment below the GPIP EUR 50,000 line, and A6 cures only a missing pre-commitment approval that the applicable rule actually required. Plant approval was later given by the reachable Plant Manager, so the failed Shift Lead substitution is not, on these facts, a failed Plant_Approve. NONCOMPLIANT is arguable. High confidence is not.

Smaller defects accumulate. Several rule triggers drop the relational operator and read as “Amount EUR 50,000,” “Amount EUR 25,000,” and “up to” only by implication. P-502’s date is not in the evidence and is not flagged, although the ECD conclusion depends on it. P-504 lists lack of Incident Command authorization as an apparent deviation in a case the same row holds to be outside ECD-2, and “only one person acted” is not itself a breached rule below EUR 25,000. P-505 omits the missing PO time, the missing four-hour authorization record, and the unknown requester and committer. P-503’s controlling interpretation leads with the contestable three-day scope point even though absent Compliance clearance is independently decisive inside or outside ECD-2. D1’s EUR 150,000 cap is not cleanly separated from D2’s added Legal step in the P-508 primary holding. The 14:30 entry is called 5.5 hours after 08:55; the interval is 5 hours 35 minutes.

The result tilts toward NONCOMPLIANT wherever proof is incomplete. That is a methodological choice, but it is not the choice announced in Assumption 9, and it leaves JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION unused even as a fully worked primary alternative. The paper meets the required structure and is reliable on the clearest scope, expiry, and new-supplier points. It is not consistent enough, or precise enough about timing rules and burden, to score higher.
