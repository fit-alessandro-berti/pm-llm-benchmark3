7.7

The answer is complete, well structured, and strong on the points that matter most: minimal edits, decision-policy versus pure delay, queue absorption, four-eyes/compliance ordering, and scale interference. It is not nearly flawless. Several timings contradict the queue premises used to justify them, and one rework tail drops mandatory downstream work.

The feasibility model is mostly right and usefully separates timing (K-411), routing (K-219), and decision policy (K-337). The rework block is not. `Compliance Review[j] < Request More Info[k] < … < Compliance Review[j+1]` is written as an imposed order, not as an alternative to an assessment-issued request. Applied literally, it places compliance before the rework loop and conflicts with K-337’s actual, rule-compliant order and with rule 5. `Identity Check || Document Check[1]` in the common skeleton is also broader than rule 3; the originals constraint only partly repairs that.

K-104 is the best derivation. Fixed identity, document, and assessment timestamps, a non-substitutable preliminary screen, the M1 signing boundary, and C1 displacement are all handled. The adverse tail is not. A precautionary `Request More Info` on a high-risk case also forces a new document check, assessment, and compliance review. “+8–36 h” counts only the external wait, so the tail is too short. The synthesis then states a “4–10 h” gain and drops the 0 h case argued earlier.

K-219 has a direct temporal contradiction. The text assumes an A2 queue of about 6 h, then says assessment can end Tue 11:30, start near Tue 09:00, and in the best case finish late morning (−19 h). With a 6 h queue and a ~2.5 h assessment, an overnight document completion still yields a mid-afternoon finish, not late morning. The −14 to −18 h band is plausible; −19 h and the 11:30 bound are not, under the answer’s own assumptions. D2 at 5–9 h also slips outside the stated 4–8 h standard range. The 20–40% rework rate is disclosed as illustrative, but it is then turned into a break-even of 35–45% and an expected-value range, which is more precision than the evidence supports.

K-337 correctly treats the intervention as a change of decision, exposure, and defensibility, and the Friday/Monday split plus post-decision confirmation is the right mechanism. The best case is not consistent with the cited queue: Thu’s 5 h forecast from Fri 10:30 implies a start near 15:30, and a slow 4–6 h review then misses a Friday signature. A 12:30 start and −111 h is the optimistic corner, not a consequence of that forecast. “Escalation deadlock” also sits outside the intervention’s allowed approve-with-conditions or reject choice.

K-411 is careful: earlier enqueue is not the same as overlapping service, intervening arrivals determine the gain, and assessment/M1 can absorb it. The Tuesday-night best case is stretched but labeled as such. Calling the intervention “timing only” underplays the lock workaround and logging risk, though those risks reappear later.

Capacity, ranking, pilots, and the assumption audit do what was asked. The 100-case discussion covers C1 reservations, D1/D2 migration, rework, assessment, weekends, and false concurrency, and the pilot designs are operational. Least-transportable is split across K-219, K-104, and K-337 rather than decided. Cross-case D1 overlap is a real feasibility catch and is handled with appropriate doubt.
