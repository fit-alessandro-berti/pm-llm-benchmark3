The latent-state reading is mostly sound, and the C6-A versus C6-B split is the strongest part of the answer: same evidence-received prefix, different obligations, and Fast Close correctly dropped once C6-B has already entered an evidence cycle. C6-C’s country-rule reportability signal, C6-E’s six-complaint lot trigger for Supplier Analysis, C6-D’s migration/worklist treatment, the queue arithmetic, and the DFG caveats are also real process reasoning. The write-up still misses the prediction target often enough that it is not a high score.

The repeated failure is treating post-gateway activities as alternative next log events. Customer Evidence Requested and Close — No Safety Issue are reached from Evidence Review Completed (medians about 1.3 h and 3.1 h), and Disposition Recorded follows a finished Regulatory Assessment (median about 2.2 h). Under a normal feed those predecessors must appear first. C6-A rank 2, C6-B ranks 2–3, C6-C ranks 2–3, and C6-E rank 2 assign those downstream events material next-event probability anyway. C6-B’s review-completion interval is then lowered to 0.35–0.55 to make room for a close that the same DFG places hours after review. C6-E makes the timing contradiction explicit: 40 minutes and an untouched review are used to doubt Evidence Review Completed, while a further evidence request, which cannot be logged until that review finishes, still receives 0.15–0.30.

`process_enabled` is not applied consistently. C6-C marks Disposition Recorded and Close — No Safety Issue enabled while Regulatory Assessment is still the open mandatory obligation. C6-D marks the analogous disposition false. C6-C’s close is also the wrong terminal branch for a live reportability signal: a reportable result requires Authority Notification before closure. C6-D is the case where a delayed historical completion and a forward-enabled action come apart, but Evidence Received is still marked enabled on the theory that a source-system attachment will surface as that specific event.

Rule 5 is over-applied on C6-A. One complaint covering two devices from one lot, plus three merely “similar” complaints, does not establish two or more complaints on the same lot or supplier component. The scenario reserves an explicit lot link for C6-E. Labeling Supplier Analysis mandatory changes C6-A’s open obligations.

Several evidence bullets fight the observation model. The log has no start events, so “no investigation-start event” is not evidence that work has not begun. C6-C’s claim that a normal feed means a missing Regulatory Assessment completion shows the task has not finished is stronger than the stated 8–15% delay for cases younger than 48 h. High-severity overtake is documented for Technical Investigation and is assumed for Evidence Review. Rank-1 and rank-2 intervals overlap for C6-B and C6-D, so the published ordering is not stable inside the stated uncertainty.
