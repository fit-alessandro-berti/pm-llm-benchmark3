4.3

The write-up keeps the right overall shape and correctly flags the crude-versus-complexity reversal, but it is not reliable enough for the decision it is advising. Several self-computed contrasts are wrong, some of those errors reverse the qualitative claim, and causal vocabulary is applied to the wrong variables.

## 1. Executive causal assessment
The verdict label is in the allowed set, the length is inside 180–300 words, and the crude gap (21.96 vs 15.24, about 44%) plus the within-complexity reversal are real. Confidence 75 is too high for 30 completed cases and almost no overlap on complexity × shift. “Direct effect” is used for a complexity-stratified association that is still mixed with shift, load, compliance, and queueing. The required total / direct / mediated split is not actually made, and the success path for the 50% move is missing.

The main evidence-against claim is false: 19.47 h vs 11.12 h is High-Orion wait against Medium-Nova wait. Within High, the supplied waits are 19.47 (Orion) vs 22.75 (Nova). Within every complexity band, Orion’s mean wait is shorter. Crude rework (28.6% vs 18.8%) is also treated as a mechanism that offsets a benefit; within Low / Medium / High, Orion rework is 0%, 16.7%, and 50% against Nova 10%, 25%, and 50%. The crude rework gap is case mix.

## 2. Causal graph
This is Graphviz, not the required directed adjacency list. `routing_rule` is an edge label rather than a node. `case_completed -> cycle_time` reverses selection: completion is a sample filter downstream of duration and risk, and conditioning on it can open bias. It does not cause cycle time. `compliance_performed -> review_service` fights the measurement definition: compliance delay sits in `wait_before_review_h`. The stated feedback, queue wait causing arrival load, confuses a case’s arrival condition with later congestion. Supported arrows, assumed arrows, and one suppressed dynamic are at least named, and the note is under 120 words.

## 3. Variable-role table
Columns match. `resource_group` as a routing-policy bundle is the best cell. The rest mislabels roles. Complexity is pre-routing and a confounder of group versus cycle time; conditioning on it does not create collider bias “if mediated by resource_group.” `compliance_required` is fixed by policy and complexity, not a mediator of group. Rework is post-treatment, so it is a mediator or a proxy for risk, not a confounder. Shift is entangled with the coverage bundle; calling it a collider because “coverage is endogenous” does not specify a common-effect structure. `case_completed` never appears as the selection node.

## 4. Descriptive decomposition
The three complexity rows match the supplied aggregates, and the “not allowed” idea is partly respected there. The adjusted column otherwise repeats the crude number, so no adjustment is shown. Required arithmetic is missing, and several new calculations are wrong:

- Orion day is one case, C02, at 7.8 h. The table says two cases and 17.8 h.
- Nova night is (8.0 + 9.8 + 18.2 + 34.0) / 4 = 17.5 h, not 21.4 h. Orion night is 13 cases, not 12.
- Orion compliance-required cycle time is 240.2 / 9 = 26.7 h, not 29.4 h. Against Nova at 27.0 h, the claim that Orion’s compliance cases are slower does not hold.
- Appeals are C14 and C24 only: 1/14 and 1/16, not 2/16. “Orion may have better quality” is unsupported.
- High review service is about 9.17 h for Orion (55 / 6) and 9.3 h for Nova, not 8.9 and 9.2.
- Rework is left unstratified, so the second reversal is missed.
- Section 4’s High queue pair (19.47 vs 22.75) contradicts Section 1’s pair. Allowed cells still say Orion “may be faster,” “better queue management,” and “better quality.”

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests
Five rows exist and the requested themes are only nominally covered. Hypothesis 1’s “supporting” evidence is a shorter service time, which cuts against a harmful skill effect, and 19.47 vs 22.75 is called “longer” when it is shorter. Hypotheses 3 and 4 again cite 19.47 vs 11.12 as a High-complexity wait gap. The day-only test pools complexities and rests on a single Orion day case. The front-door sketch estimates an effect of group on `compliance_required`; that flag is not caused by group, so the criterion is not met. Hypothesis 5 mixes confounding by unobserved risk with truncation to completed cases, and “no abandoned-case data” is not contradicting evidence. Matching is a relevant test, but positivity is ignored: Medium-night non-compliance and High-day have no Orion/Nova overlap.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan
A randomized pilot and a compliance-capacity phase are present, and blind 50% reassignment is rejected. The pilot then drops compliance-required cases, which are most of Orion’s hard work, caps night load in a way that reintroduces selection, and calls the resulting contrast a direct effect. Running capacity expansion and dynamic routing at the same time contaminates that contrast. DFGs do not measure the queue-time change these phases target. Appeal and wait stop-rules are not tied to the control arm or to baseline rates near 1–2 appeals in 30 cases. The closing recommendation is about 95 words, below the 120–200 requirement, and it still talks about “Orion’s longer cycle times” after the stratified reversal.

## 7. Reproducible Python diagnostics
Pandas loading, crude and complexity-stratified cycle times, Nova-mix standardization, and the complexity × shift × group counts are the right diagnostics. Series alignment saves the weighted mean. The event-log step does not. Traces are stored as lists and passed through `format_dataframe` as if each list were one activity, so `discover_dfg` cannot recover directly-follows relations. `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` is never used. Variant frequencies are never computed. C30’s trace is corrupted to `Compliance Check>Request Info`, inventing a directly-follows pair that is not in the data. Warnings fire only for cells under three rows; a populated Orion cell with no Nova counterpart is not flagged as poor support.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
Five uncertainties are present, and each has one concrete data fix. Reviewer identifiers, risk score, compliance timestamps, arrival timestamps, and open or abandoned cases are the right fields. Item 3 slightly misstates the prompt: the analyst is told to assume a required check was performed; the extract simply does not record performance. This is the only section that is close to the brief.

The usable core is the refusal to treat 21.96 vs 15.24 as an effect and the preference for a small routing trial plus a capacity change. That core is undercut by inverted waits, a missed rework reversal, impossible day-shift counts, a reversed selection arrow, a front-door that reverses time, and process-mining code that does not discover the traces it embeds.
