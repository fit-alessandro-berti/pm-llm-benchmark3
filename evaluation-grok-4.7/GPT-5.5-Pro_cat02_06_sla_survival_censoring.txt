7.5

The estimand design is strong and mostly internally consistent: time origin, the 72-hour horizon, competing withdrawal/rejection, open cases kept in the risk set, and already-observed breaches versus pre-horizon censoring are separated correctly. S03, S06, S08, and S09 are handled in the right way, the 9 / 18 / 5 horizon partition checks out (and the pre-72 transfer shift to 16 / 7 is right), leakage and immortal time are treated seriously, and naive resolved-only analysis is rejected with real case IDs.

The main failure is the transfer sensitivity. Under the coded Aalen–Johansen and any-terminal Kaplan–Meier, “adverse” and “competing” are the same functional: both remove the case as a non-resolution event, so \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) and \(P(T>72)\) do not change. Section 6 still says adverse coding can make P1 and cohort B look worse than competing-event coding. Only the censoring scenario actually moves those estimates, and under that scenario the Kaplan–Meier print still says “no internal terminal disposition” after transfers have been recoded as censorings. Cause-specific CIFs for withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are never produced, so the recommended primary decomposition is not shown.

Smaller problems: section 3 names only S05 and S23 as rejections and drops S14 at 63 hours; late-resolution \(t^\star\) is the sample maximum rather than a pre-specified horizon; long inactivity is too readily called informative censoring rather than a predictive state at an administrative cutoff; and the n=32 stratified point estimates have no intervals or nonparametric bounds despite five cases still open before 72 hours (the implied resolved-by-72 bounds are 9/32 to 14/32 under the success-specific coding). Those gaps keep the answer short of nearly flawless.
