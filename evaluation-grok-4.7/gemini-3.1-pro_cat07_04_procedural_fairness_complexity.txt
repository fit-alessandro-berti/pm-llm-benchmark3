The answer covers every required heading, the main constraints, and the central staffing story, but several claims are materially wrong or stronger than the evidence.

The largest analytical error is the waiting-time reading. The 3.8-day and 4.7-day figures are the gaps from Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review to Eligibility Assessment. Section 2 instead targets waits immediately before those activities, and Section 3 calls them waits “for” language support and accommodation review. Those are different edges. The same section treats the 1.6-day Completeness Check → Request Missing Documents gap for Group B as justified by missing documents, although the note attributes that wait to bilingual staffing.

Section 1 says case mix does not explain the gaps. The adjusted figures show the opposite pattern for several metrics: most of Group D’s raw approval gap (about 20 points raw versus 3.1 points adjusted) and much of Group C’s approval gap is associated with the covariates. What remains is a residual, and it differs by group and metric. The write-up treats those residuals as one unexplained disparity.

The bottom line then calls the whole process procedurally unfair and says the system structurally disadvantages minority and accommodated applicants. The stronger support is narrower: Groups B and C incur extra queue time and handovers on rights-preserving routes that have no separate timeliness target, and some adjusted residuals remain. Decision-standard discrimination, appeal merit, and how much of the residual time is necessary extra work are not identified. Group D’s adjusted appeal gap (+5.7 points), the largest appeal residual, is underweighted relative to the staffing narrative.

Mechanisms are too few and partly mis-aimed. One legitimate mechanism is given. The risk-score mechanism is used to explain outcomes even though the adjusted model already controls for `risk_score_band` and `prior_case_count`. That control means a biased score would distort the adjustment; it does not itself account for the residual. “Structurally inaccessible” support overstates delay as denial of access. “Served by 52 caseworkers” overstates exclusivity; the log says Group A cases are spread across 52 workers.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested technique families, but the variant step is incorrect: `list(variants.keys())[3]` is frequency order, not scenario variant V4, and durations are not split by group. The log-skeleton `always_before` check on the pooled log cannot establish that language support precedes eligibility, because most cases never contain that activity. Waiting-time extraction is only a comment. Group D is omitted from the filters.

Mitigations and limitations are the most complete parts. Even there, an audit is said to require suspending automated risk routing, and the matching set drops risk, documentation completeness, and prior-case count while later treating those as the sensitive covariates.
