6.7

The memo reaches the right posture: hybrid deployment, not national scale and not full rollback, with the harmful elements suspended and the simple-house gain preserved. Structure, fact/interpretation/assumption separation, and the five metric-conflict explanations are largely sound. It is not nearly flawless.

The final decision is internally inconsistent. The memo says to scale coordinator ownership for all segments, while the closing actions only retain it in the four pilot regions and also pause national rollout. Geographic scope is never settled. That same recommendation treats coordinator absence as a scale gate, yet M5 is ranked last and then dropped from “what to measure next,” even though high utilization and the longer failed-visit → second-visit delay are direct evidence against scaling ownership.

The alternatives table misstates the evidence: apartments plus old buildings are 47%, but the parenthetical adds business and rural (66%). The +12 to +25 day p90 and 8–11pp first-time-right drops apply only to the first pair, not to business (+5 days, −4pp) or rural (+9 days, −5pp).

Several rollout thresholds are unusable as written because the comparison operator is missing (“p90 … 31 days,” “cost … baseline”). Other claims are stronger than the log: the skip rule was not shown to be tuned on simple houses, segment results are not an “unbiased test,” and “six months” for alternative D is invented. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) and the temporal-profile delay under high coordinator load are largely unused. Value-of-information is ordered, but “per unit of time/cost” is asserted without cost or time, and M3/M8 do not actually say which decision would flip.
