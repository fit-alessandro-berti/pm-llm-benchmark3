The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and chooses an activation-conditional 90-day default estimand rather than a convenience cohort. That choice is allowed. The execution then breaks the as-of logic the estimand needs, and the skeleton does not implement the cohort it describes.

The primary estimand is \(P(T>t\mid X_{\text{prefix}},\text{Activated})\), while the prediction time is the third event or 48 hours, which is before activation is known. The answer never says whether scores are issued only after activation or applied to every application. Those are different estimands. Still-open cases are described both as right-censored at the last event and as excluded from the risk set. The maturity rule, activation on or before 17 November 2025, ignores the stated 7–35 day feed lag, so many “mature” labels are not yet knowable on 15 February 2026. The 3,600 unmatured-or-delayed accounts, and permanently missing partner feeds, are then treated as ordinary right censoring. A late or missing binary feed does not establish event-free survival until a censoring time. `label_available_at` is never used to separate those mechanisms. Testable, partially testable, and untestable assumptions are not mapped; the IPTW sensitivity also changes the question to an all-activated counterfactual without stating exchangeability, positivity, or consistency.

Validation lines up with the policy and migration dates, and a random split is rightly rejected, but the test window (starts through 31 December 2025) is exactly where 90-day outcomes plus feed delay are mostly immature. Brier score and time-dependent AUC are named without inverse-probability-of-censoring weights. Pre/post policy and migration flags are proposed as features even though both are constant in a pre-policy training window, so they are not identified. Transfer linkage, reappearance under a new id, and the 40% unlinked share barely enter the cohort rules.

The leakage table drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, label timing, and the label itself, and it repairs `risk_score` and resource experience. That part is sound. It still misses same-day snapshot semantics for `documents_total`, warehouse overwrite of `risk_score`, and as-of construction for queue and historical approval features.

The code is in range and uses `format_dataframe`, a start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, and column dropping. It does not build terminal state, a censoring indicator, or label maturity from a configurable cutoff and feed delay. It does not restrict the feature table to the activated risk set. The commented event time, \(\min(90,\text{extraction}-\text{activation})\), is not time-to-default. `get_prefixes_from_log` returns a log, not a frame with `.columns`. The DFG is fit on full training traces, including post-prefix and default events.
