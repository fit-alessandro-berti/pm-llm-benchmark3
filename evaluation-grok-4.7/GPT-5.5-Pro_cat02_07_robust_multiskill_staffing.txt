6.7

The capacity arithmetic in §2 is mostly right and is the strongest part of the answer. The conditional-uniform means (6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50, 15.74), the block loads, the day total of about 2721, U4’s diagnostic means and roughly +242 minutes, U1’s 53.2 cases / ~3442 minutes / P1 rise from ~5.4 to ~9.1, and U2’s electrical move from ~341 to ~405 with almost no change in total load all follow from the tables. Shift capacity of 2490, or 2670 if every resource takes 30 minutes of overtime, is correct, and because every multiplier is at least 1.0 that 2721 is a lower bound before handover, setup, and coordination. The parallel-versus-sequential critical paths, including the ~81-minute Chen/Emil sequential ES path, are also right. Bottleneck migration across the day and across U1–U5 is directionally sound.

The decision sections do not live up to that arithmetic.

The §4 utilization figures contradict §2. Minimum base work already exceeds regular capacity and even full-overtime capacity. If the denominator is shift time including the 30-minute cap, max utilization cannot sit at 85–92% for a policy that still completes the work. Hybrid’s base row shows that range without a backlog, while strict specialization is labeled “100% plus backlog.” Average load is at least 2721/2670 ≈ 102% before slow multipliers and the 7/5/5/8-minute penalties, so the maximum is at least that high. The dilution escape — dividing by a multi-day drain horizon — is not stated, and it would not be the prompt’s planned within-shift utilization. The same table’s ranges overlap on mean delay, the tail, and both SLAs in essentially every scenario, yet §5 says the hybrid “dominates” strict specialization. The qualitative columns are ordered so the preferred policy wins; they are not an audit of the offered-load logic or of the code.

Objective A is not the lexicographic rule it claims to be. A 10-point P1 shortfall costs 100, while p95 is added in raw minutes, so a few hours of tail movement swamp a material P1 miss. P2’s weight of 200 and P3’s of 50 have the same unit problem. Objective B adds undefined regret, imbalance, handover, and skill-erosion terms. The recommendation cites both objectives without a scale on which the trade-off can be checked. P3 SLA is inside Objective A and absent from the comparison table.

The code does not implement the policy that is recommended. Hybrid and full pooling use the same eligible sets and the same crude preemption rule. There is no reserved P1 slot, no queue-threshold overflow, no Validation gate, and no stress mode. The 0.15 fairness weight and 0.25 slow-skill nudge cannot produce the fairness and resilience gaps in §4. Those omissions are not declared, so this is not an honest scaffold. Other mechanics diverge from the prose or the prompt: U4 doubles tail probabilities for Intake and Validation, not only diagnostics; service draws are taken during execution, so policies do not face the same realized services; preemption can charge a second handover because the predecessor resource is still the previous activity’s resource; preempted busy time is omitted from utilization; the burden ratio adds base-minute restart penalties to elapsed handover and coordination; and the stated guards (material P1 gain, more than ~20 minutes remaining, no marginal preemption) are absent. `coordination_min` is always 0, so the 8-minute rule never appears in the log.

PM4Py use is only partly competent. Stage queue times are computed separately, which is required, but parallel ME/ES intervals are not handled. Case-level service is a sum of intervals, so overlapping branches can push service above sojourn and make waiting negative, and a timestamp sort can invent handovers between concurrent branches. Preempted fragments are logged under the same activity name. The caveat says only that case-level waiting is not stage waiting.

Two interpretation choices tilt the horse race. Strict Software is Emil alone, despite Chen at 1.25, so morning Software failure is built into the strawman. The recommendation also never converts the structural deficit into a staffing or shift action, even though the analysis shows Emil is absent until 12:00, mechanical coverage collapses after 16:00, and a repeating day is unstable. §9 names the repeating-day problem and then leaves the primary recommendation as a routing rule that cannot meet the 90% targets in steady state. “Slightly above” capacity is true only for bare service versus the overtime ceiling; with handovers it is a large deficit. The §4 numbers are not outputs of the model, so calling them auditable overclaims what was shown.
