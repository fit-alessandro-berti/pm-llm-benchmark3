6.5

The redesign is structurally complete and operationally serious: all seven required sections are present, the four scenarios vary by mix and disruption type, bottleneck migration is traced station-to-station, downstream overload is discussed, policies have on/off triggers, the trade-off table has the required columns, one redesign-caused vulnerability is identified, and the dashboard separates leading from lagging signals. It also avoids blanket capacity adds and average-only optimization.

That credit is cut by unreliable use of the process-mining evidence. Normal and peak counts cover 8 weeks and 3 weeks, but absolute frequencies are compared as if they were rates. Pre-analytical to test preparation is described as dropping from 16,860 to 8,530, when the weekly rate actually rises and only the path share falls. Assay-to-repeat is treated as a small move from 1,140 to 1,260, while the weekly rate roughly triples and the exit share rises from about 7% to about 15%. The same window error makes the large shift from direct technical release toward medical validation and specialist hold easy to miss.

Several claims are factually off or internally inconsistent. The package says the frequency graph shows recollection dropping; peak recollection is flat in raw counts and roughly doubles as a share of receipts (about 4.2% to 8.0%). Routing routine work onto M2, the faster but higher-failure machine, is then credited with lowering repeats. Cross-training technical validators for accession correction is softened by keeping them “primarily” in technical validation, which does not resolve a pool already at 101% peak utilization against a 14-hour accession queue. Quantities such as an 18% M2 failure rate, a 30% loop reduction, and a 14-hour to 6-hour accession improvement are stated without data. Missing arrows also collapse key figures into unreadable strings (16,8608,530, 22%54%, 1,1401,260).

Scenario design and controls are directionally sound, but S1’s “+30%” mix is ambiguous and not anchored to the observed 18% to 29% urgent and 13% to 20% complex shift. Repeat-after-failure, open work-in-process, and right-censoring (peak releases 8,040 of 9,900 starts) are underused. The complex p90 target below 110 hours is tighter than the normal-period p90 of 118 hours without a capacity basis.
