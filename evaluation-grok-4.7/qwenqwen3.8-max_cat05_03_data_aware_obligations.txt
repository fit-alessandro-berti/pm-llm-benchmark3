**8.0**

The case states, ordering, and most rule outcomes are right, and the write-up covers temporal semantics, the rule table, pseudo-logic, ambiguities, control-flow limits, and logging fixes. It is not nearly flawless.

TX-302 is mislabeled. At release the state is GB, CORPORATE, EUR 180,000, with an Enhanced_Review at 09:20 and no later material change. That review already meets R1. EX-77 is valid (Head_Compliance, effective 09:00, qualified signature 09:05, in scope, unexpired), but it is redundant. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION means the exception is what makes the release acceptable. The right headline is COMPLIANT. Section 4 admits that a rejected exemption would still leave the case compliant, then keeps the exception label anyway.

TX-310 is over-hedged. R1 is established: AE, EUR 200,000, Standard_Review only. EX-88’s displayed 08:50 approval is not conclusive, the qualified certificate was created at 10:30 after the 10:00 release, and the only witness is the requester. Under R5 that exemption is not shown to have existed at release, so the better classification is NONCOMPLIANT. INDETERMINATE fits an unprovable trigger (TX-305), not a proven trigger with a failed defense. The alternative readings are clear, which limits the damage.

The checker has a real logic error. If an exemption is INVALID_COARSE or REVIEW_REQUIRED, R1 returns immediately and never tests whether an Enhanced_Review already satisfies the rule. That does not flip these eleven coarse labels, but it is the wrong control structure. R4 is also marked REVIEW_REQUIRED on TX-305 merely because the release amount is unknown, even though there is no pre-release increase to classify. EX-77’s certificate time is invented as 09:05; only the signature timestamp is given. Sanctions matching ORs the forward-filled case jurisdiction, so a list update for another jurisdiction could false-trigger.

Smaller gaps: several pseudo-logic predicates are used undefined; R3 is extended to “no screen ever,” which the rule does not say; expiry is treated as inclusive without saying so; TX-302 severity “Low” and TX-305 severity “Unknown / Medium” are vague. The trace-order check is only first-occurrence sequencing, though that meets the optional requirement.

TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 are reasoned correctly, including the 20% test, the exact EUR 100,000 threshold, decreases, same-timestamp `event_index` on TX-311, and refusal to copy TX-305’s later settlement amount backward.
