The final recommendation is a coherent, artifact-grounded portfolio: A5, A6, A10 with a KYC-clearance gate, and A4, in that rollout order, with A9, A8, and A11 held back until upstream data and controls are reliable. All seven required sections are present, all twelve candidates are ranked on process leverage rather than frequency, and the dependency, exception, resource-shift, and four-eyes arguments are specific and mostly accurate.

The score is held down by several concrete weaknesses. Effect sizes such as 8–15% and 20–35% are unsupported, and they are hard to reconcile because A10’s 44-hour signing wait sits in both the quick-win and recommended portfolios. Calling A10 the control-strengthening module stretches a reminder/adoption candidate; the compliance requirement is met only by redesigning it, while four-eyes is left to procedure. The two scarce core-banking slots are assigned to statement write-back and a KYC-state read without a tight case that those, rather than registration, contracts, or activation, are the true core integrations. Portfolio Q is also inconsistent on slot use: A10 is defined as consuming a deep slot, yet Q is described as using only A1’s slot. The 2,350-to-1,980 valuation gap is treated as leakage, and the Phase-2 gate lands at month 7, just outside the six-month integration limit.
