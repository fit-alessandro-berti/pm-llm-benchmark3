5.5

The regime structure is competent, but the quantitative claims are unreliable and sometimes contradict the formulas and conservation checks in the same document.

The segmentation itself is the strongest part. Weekday versus weekend, the 15 April outage, the 16–19 April recovery, the 20 April complexity jump, and the 29 April staffing change are the right breaks. Recovery is correctly refused as a steady high-throughput regime, median service is not substituted for mean flow time, and Little’s Law is withheld for the outage, recovery, and trending windows. The alternative segmentation and the pooled-staffing policy warning are real operational points.

Those strengths are undercut by calculation integrity. The stated utilization formula uses a 12-hour day, and the pre-outage and high-complexity weekday figures (0.82 and 1.44) instead match a 10-hour day; several weekend utilizations match neither. Pooled arrivals are given as 93.7 and completions as 94.0; the table sums to about 90.0 and 86.0, so the claimed near-zero pooled balance is false and the net surplus of arrivals is about 110 cases. High-complexity weekday completions are about 100.6, not 103, over seven weekdays rather than five, and the resulting cumulative deficit is not “consistent” with WIP rising only from 37 to 50. Bookend WIP is essentially flat (18 versus 19), so the flow imbalance is an unresolved conservation problem, not evidence of balance. Lognormal service means and all four Fano factors are off (for example, 21 April is about 9.3, not 11.2). The weekend result is internally contradictory: utilization 0.45 is called a light load while the implied sojourn is 234 minutes, yet carry-over risk is rated very low on only two days that appear to drain Friday’s WIP.

Format and code compliance are incomplete. Confidence is not a column in the regime table, verdict labels drift from the required set, and the code only prints a cumulative deficit rather than implementing a change-point rule. The prose statistics would not be reproduced by that code. The 29 April boundary is treated as a clean regime start even though the intervention time is unknown, and the hourly vectors are only partly audited: totals match the daily counts, but every peak share conflicts with `peak_08_10_pct`, not just 21 April.
