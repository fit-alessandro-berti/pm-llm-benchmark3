The state labels, rule citations, and A-versus-B split are largely competent, but the forecasts are not a coherent next-event distribution. Several ranked “next” activities are downstream successors, and two cases contradict their own observation model.

C6-A is in S3, and Evidence Review Completed is the only strongly enabled next completion. Rank 2 is then invalid on the answer’s own terms: it calls Technical Investigation Completed a “second-observation” and “not the immediate next,” yet assigns 0.12–0.28. A completion whose historical median is about 8.5–9 h, and which review is what opens, cannot carry that mass while the review event is still unlogged on a normal feed. Rank 3, Customer Evidence Requested, has the same ordering problem. Supplier Analysis is only suspected: one complaint covers two devices of one lot, and the three similar complaints are not shown to share that lot, so “likely mandatory” overstates Rule 5. `process_enabled: true` also conflicts with the claim that investigation is enabled only after review.

C6-B repeats that error and then contradicts it. The caveat says the next observed event must be Evidence Review Completed even if a closure decision already exists. Rank 2 still gives Close — No Safety Issue 0.15–0.35. Those cannot both be right on a normal feed. The duplicate score, completeness 0.98, and the fact that triage already refused Fast Close are used as color, not as constraints on that interval.

C6-C is the most stable forecast. Regulatory Assessment ahead of disposition is a justified departure from the raw TI→Disposition count of 119 versus TI→RA of 74, given the country rule. Disposition is still marked process-enabled while the same text says it is blocked if assessment is mandatory. Supplier Analysis Completed at 0.05–0.18 is a weak third: lots differ, no analysis is visible, and typical duration is 6–36 h. An unconfirmed root cause makes another evidence or technical step more competitive than an unopened supplier completion.

C6-D inverts its own timing. Regulatory Assessment was opened at 12:40; at 14:00 only 1 h 20 m has elapsed, below the stated 2–14 h window. The contradicting-evidence text says a delayed evidence event is more likely to appear before assessment completion, but assessment is still rank 1 at 0.28–0.55. Evidence Review Completed is listed as a suspected delayed obligation and then omitted from the three predictions. “Proves” that the attachment is a completed request/receipt cycle is too strong, and the historical Initial Triage→Technical Investigation count of 126 does not show that those historical cases were feed losses.

C6-E correctly treats six complaints on one lot as a real Supplier Analysis obligation and correctly blocks a low-severity shortcut. Rank 3 again places another Customer Evidence Requested ahead of the review completion that would create it. With receipt only 40 minutes old, normal-feed probability that review completion is next should dominate more than 0.45–0.70 once successor activities are removed from the competing set. Rank 2 can be a true concurrent completion only if analysis was already opened after the first review; that is stated, but `process_enabled: false` then fights the probability.

The cross-case discussion correctly refuses a shared DFG successor for C6-A and C6-B and correctly describes concurrency, migration, and queue priority. That discussion does not repair the numeric rankings. Midpoints are arithmetically orderly while referring to events that are not mutually exclusive immediate successors. The result is process-aware commentary wrapped around mis-targeted next-event probabilities.
