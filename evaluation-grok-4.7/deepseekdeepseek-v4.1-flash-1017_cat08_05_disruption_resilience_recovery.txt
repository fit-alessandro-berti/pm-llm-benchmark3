The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and stays faithful to the timeline, conformance figures, and A–L patterns. It correctly treats hospital protection, manual authorization, deferral, backlog ranking, and reconciliation as bounded adaptations, and it correctly refuses to equate skipped authorization, unscanned dispatch, or post-close audit repair with resilience. The recovery model includes triggers, risk lanes, evidence capture, backlog rules, and a reconciliation exit gate, and the indicator and trade-off sections cover throughput, service, traceability, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness.

Deductions under a strict reading:

- The recovery rules conflict. P0 places urgent hospital-critical ambient orders with high-risk hospital orders, P1 also lists hospital-critical ambient, and “urgent” is undefined. Later backlog ranking says product risk comes first, which would place a wholesale controlled order ahead of hospital ambient, while the control table still calls wholesale the lowest priority. Wholesale cold-chain, controlled, and recall-sensitive orders never receive an explicit tier.
- The DFG frequencies are underused. About 1,450 of 1,650 temporary releases still flow to dispatch without scan, and the manual evidence chain drops from roughly 3,250 unscanned dispatches to 2,600 phone proofs, 2,200 spreadsheet holds, and 2,100 closes. Skip-authorization is about 1,600 of 5,100 triaged orders. The write-up treats temporary release as strong controlled-substance resilience without tying that claim to this continuation and attrition.
- Timeline coverage of the given metrics is incomplete. Baseline p90, T2 wholesale p90 (180h versus 72h), and all T3 p90s are omitted. Stabilization is described as medians improving to 8h/28h/58h, while only the hospital median is back to baseline; pharmacy and bulk medians remain 28h versus 23h and 58h versus 36h.
- Proposed thresholds (15 minutes, 5%, 2 hours, 500 cases, 24h/3d/10d) are not tied to baseline cycle times or to the stated temporal deviations, including backlog-to-manual-pick waits above baseline p95 and the highly variable unscanned-dispatch-to-phone-proof delay.

No metric is materially misstated, and the main resilience-versus-noncompliance judgment is sound. The priority inconsistency and missed flow-level interpretation keep it short of a near-flawless score.
