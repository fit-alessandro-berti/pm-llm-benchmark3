8.4

The design is strong on the criteria that matter: non-stationary demand, resource calendars, cross-case coupling, latent objections, and a real separation of service, queue, external, and statutory time. It is not nearly flawless.

What holds up. Scope is a calendar-aware DES with case, architect, neighbourhood, and inspection-bundle state, not an independent-case replay. The estimation plan uses real PM4Py entry points (`format_dataframe`, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average, token replay, inductive tree, `log_to_features`) and correctly refuses process-tree playout as the engine. The elapsed-versus-effort feasibility check is the best piece of reasoning in the answer: lognormal means from the stated median/p90 pairs really do imply planning/fire/legal loads many times stated FTE, so treating those figures as work content would saturate every scenario. Assumptions cover all nine requested classes and mark objections, area thresholds, prior violations, and the wait/service split as unidentified. The experiment table has a baseline, D1–D10, interactions, and seven stresses, with mechanisms and trade-offs. Warm-up is terminating and season-aware, CRN is case-locked, and aleatory versus epistemic uncertainty is nested. Validation has numeric gates and an honest non-validation of D2/D9. Decision rules use median, tail, SLA, district and architect fairness, utilisation, rework, backlog, and robustness, and they do not claim optimality.

What keeps it below 9. The skeleton reuses one `ProcessTree()` tau node across several XOR/loop parents. PM4Py assigns `parent` in the children setter, so that tree is not a valid process tree and `convert_to_petri_net` in `conformance_sample` is not reliable. Public-notice time is mis-clocked: with one timestamp per activity, the 14–60 day statutory/objection duration sits in the gap *after* `Public-notice period`, but `clock_class` labels the notice row itself and leaves the following gap as municipal sojourn. Applicant delay is attributed the other way (via `prev_activity`), so the two external clocks are inconsistent. D3 is estimated as already implicit in the log, then switched off in B0 and turned on as a boolean policy, so the baseline and the intervention are not the same mechanism. Fire demand is hardcoded at 45% (all new residential plus all commercial) although the variant table only supports roughly the high-20s to mid-30s; the infeasibility conclusion survives, the number does not. `paired_bootstrap_ci` is an ordinary percentile bootstrap of a mean and is documented as “BCa-lite”. `run_experiment(..., thetas=[])` never enters the loop, so the printed experiment plan is empty, and `metrics_from_log` reads `local_ts` that `emit_simulated_log` does not create.

Those are local defects, not a wrong design. The capacity arithmetic, objection mixture, and CRN treatment are sound.
