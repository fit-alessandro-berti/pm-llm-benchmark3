7.4

The hybrid design is unusually careful: the R5/R6 split is real, strict precedence is chosen without silently deleting the emergency exception, the accepting net’s 27-marking account is consistent with the arcs, R10 is correctly kept at “at most once,” and the T7 payment interval (2 h, \(z \approx 1.94\)) is correctly left inside \(\zeta = 2\). The verdict narrative for activation-before-opinion versus a merely late opinion is also right. The score is held down by a formalism error that falsifies a required result column, plus several smaller modeling and consistency gaps.

The log skeleton’s empty `directly_follows` is not “no adjacency constraint.” In PM4Py, that set is an allow-list: every consecutive pair in the trace must belong to it. The empty set therefore rejects every trace of length greater than 1. `skeleton_for_log` widens `activ_freq` only, so it does not repair this. Under the dictionary as written, the Log Skeleton cells for T1–T4, both T6 rows, and T7 are `fail`, not `pass`. T5 and T8 remain `fail` for the reasons given. That is an executable mismatch with Section 8, not a presentation quibble. The frequency-adapter claim and the `always_before(A, B)` = “B before A” orientation are the credible readings; the directly-follows prose contradicts the same checker.

The net also admits `Change Bank Details` on `open` before activation, while a second `Verify Bank Account` is enabled only on `post`. A change after the single pre-activation verification cannot be re-checked before classification or payment. R11 does not cover that case, and Section 9 never states the hole. `Enhanced Due Diligence` followed by `Reject Vendor` is impossible in the net (both consume `classified`) but is allowed by the DECLARE and skeleton exclusion sets; only manager/compliance are paired with rejection. Escalation and clarification are impossible after activation; that is disclosed for escalation and is a reasonable reading of R3, but it is still a scope cut beyond R7.

Section 10 says unresolved critical evidence blocks a complete pass. Section 8 still gives T1–T3 an overall `pass` while `risk_class` is missing and route-to-class matching is `not evaluated`. The coverage note explains the intent, but the two sections do not use the same acceptance rule. Citation [1] points at `conformance.py` for ordinary Petri-net read loops, which that file does not establish. “Nonsuccession” is only paraphrased, not named. The lower ends of all three temporal windows are exactly 0, so this profile can never flag “too fast”; the table shows it, but the text never says the lower tail has collapsed.

The rest of the mapping holds together. Conditional legal precedence is correctly kept out of binary `precedence` so T1 is not forced to contain an opinion. Alternate response is correctly distinguished from the procedural idle-gate (request, activate, response). Count equality is correctly called insufficient for one-outstanding clarification. R6’s 24-hour clock is not confused with the R12 warning band, and T6 is reported under both policies, including `not evaluated` when joint authorization is absent. Those are the right modeling judgments; the skeleton allow-list error and the unstated pre-activation re-verification gap are why this is not a top-band answer.
