7.6

The pipeline is careful and mostly right on the hard cases: keep e05 and e14, do not treat shared O1/V1/P1 as identity, split e08 from e09, refuse to fold the 1:n ERP payment summary into either capture, and merge only e03/e04 and e11/e16 in the conservative path. Event counts 17/15/14/10 and E2O counts 44/39/37/28 are arithmetically consistent with those choices. Ordering is explicitly partial, ingestion is demoted, original timestamps are retained, and the false-story argument about synthetic microseconds is concrete. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The decision tree does not actually decide the two mixed groups in the data. T2 is a terminal test on a whole activity–timestamp group (“does each event name a different discriminating object?”). For {e03, e04, e05} the answer is no, because e03 and e04 share I1. For {e13, e14, e15} the answer is also no, because e15 names no Payment. First-match-wins then falls through T3–T5, which are written as one relation for the whole group, and lands in quarantine. The correct behavior — partition by discriminating object, keep the missing-object bucket separate, then test identity only inside a bucket — is never stated. Section 2 does those pairs properly, so the tree and the table disagree on the procedure that produces the table.

Section 3 and Section 7 disagree on how fixed the order is. Cross-object `source_seq` is described as weaker, and e09–e10–e08 is “not excluded,” but the code inserts e04≺e05 and e13≺e14 as hard tier-2 edges and always applies e08≺e09. The raw Order DFG is then reported as if it were a property of the log: 16 edges and five self-loops, including Dispatch→Dispatch and Issue→Issue. Those two loops exist only under one linear extension. The admissible order e09, e10, e08 removes the dispatch loop; ingestion order without the invoice-before-payment assumption removes Issue→Issue and creates Capture→Issue. Pattern 5 admits the second case, then the comparison table still treats the loops as the raw baseline. “16 / 14 / 13 / 9 edges” is also just n−1 follows in a single O1 trace, not the aggregated DFG. Unique raw Order arcs are 14, the same count as conservative; what changes is identity and frequency (Pick→Pick 2→1, plus relabeling). Calling the sum “DFG edges” overstates the structural gap.

“Release-to-acceptance is measurable” in the conservative column conflicts with the same section’s rule that tie durations are unresolved and must not be read off synthetic offsets. Both events remain at 10:15:00. `co-birth` for I1/I2 is the wrong object-graph notion; the real change is a new co-occurrence at Pick. “Four of five loops are artefacts” does not identify a clean real loop: Pick and Capture are mixed, and the other three are label artefacts.

Smaller consistency gaps: e08’s `warehouse_release` qualifier is in Section 2 and absent from the code; tier-2 weakness is not implemented; reconstruction “from the cleaned log alone” is true for snapshotted merges and not for the library merge, which only stores group ids. The PM4Py block is serious and mostly aligned with a relation-level `ocel_drop_duplicates`, but `primary_column=` is an unstable public signature, and the guarded drop plus the post-hoc `emptied` logic will mis-delete if the installed function drops events by `(activity, timestamp)` before relations. Hedging “as I understand the implementation” while still naming survivors e05/I2 and e14/M2 is stronger than the hedge.

The substantive recommendations are conservative and the provenance/failure-mode design is usable. The score is held down by an incomplete decision procedure on the actual mixed groups and by sensitivity results that are tighter than the partial order the answer itself allows.
