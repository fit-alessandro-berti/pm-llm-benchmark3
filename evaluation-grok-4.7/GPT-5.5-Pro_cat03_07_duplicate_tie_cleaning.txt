The domain decisions are mostly sound and the required constraints are respected, but the write-up is not nearly flawless. Several ordering and measurement claims conflict with the code, and the implementation can corrupt the very scenarios used in the sensitivity comparison.

The decision tree covers every requested class, and the event table treats the hard cases correctly: merge e03/e04 and e11/e16, keep e05 and e14, keep e06/e07 and e13/e15/e14/e15 unless an explicit abstraction scenario is chosen, and split the harmonized e08/e09 labels. Event-count arithmetic is right (17 → 15 → 14). The raw E2O total of 44 and the conservative and evidence-based totals of 39 and 37 follow from union-deduplication. The aggressive collapse to about 10 events and 28 relations is the right reading of activity/timestamp grouping with a shared object, and it correctly leaves e10 unmerged because the activity differs. The explanation that `have_common_object=True` cannot protect e13 and e14 is right: O1 and V1 are shared context, while M1 and M2 discriminate. Provenance, reversibility, and the failure-mode list are concrete and include the required eight cases.

The ordering prose is weaker than it looks. e09 before e10 and e12 before e13 are given. e08 before e09 is only a logistics assumption, yet it is still emitted as the intended total order without a concurrent alternative. Invoice issuance before authorization is likewise not a stated fact. e13 before e14 is within-PSP `source_seq`, which the rule list calls semantic and the later text calls merely deterministic. The microseconds warning itself is correct.

The code then diverges from those rules. The topological order is discarded in favor of `timestamp_ns + global_rank`, so a real cross-timestamp constraint would lose to the clock. The heap tie-break adds an undocumented source-priority ranking and compares raw `source_seq` across systems, which section 3 and failure mode 9 both forbid. `evidence_based` is built from `conservative` only after sorting and `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, then ordered again, so tie groups break and synthetic deltas stack. The verified merge always unions relations, does not encode per-source attribute authority, and assigns list-valued provenance cells back through `.loc`, which commonly raises in pandas. `refine_labels` does not update relation activity columns. The “scoped” `ocel_drop_duplicates` call is applied to the union of groups, so distinct exact-duplicate groups that share an activity and timestamp can still collapse. After `ocel_merge_duplicates`, hardcoded edges are silently skipped when surviving identifiers change, and ingestion time becomes an ordering input again.

Two sensitivity claims do not hold. Unioning e07 into e06 cannot increase Pack–Item ET–OT weight: e07 contributes no item relations, and duplicate E2O rows are removed. “Conservative cleaning preserves delays” is also wrong on this log. e06 and e07, and the payment cluster, already share a business timestamp; the pipeline’s index timedelta manufactures a delay rather than preserving one. OC-DFG and OTG effects are named, but frequency changes are left qualitative, and the contaminated evidence-based timestamps make the four-way comparison non-commensurable.

Section 8’s schema would allow reconstruction, yet the code only stores a subset of it. Length is also past the requested 1,900–2,900 words of prose. The scientific choices are conservative and mostly correct; the implementation and a few measurement claims are not reliable enough to score higher.
