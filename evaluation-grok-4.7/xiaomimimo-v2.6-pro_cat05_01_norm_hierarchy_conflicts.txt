5.5

The later conclusions show real command of time-of-act analysis, evidential weighting, and the integrity-versus-workflow split, but they do not hold together as a finished, consistent assessment. Several primary labels contradict the decision rules the answer itself adopts, and the required case, remediation, and policy products are left unfinished.

What holds up is the treatment of timing and of the cases that fail on the integrity floor. Commitment is tested when it occurs: P-501 is correctly kept outside ECD-2 because an ERP time of 08:50, read with the outage rule that recorded times run late, is still before 09:00. The CFO email is correctly denied retroactive effect for P-502. P-506 is correctly placed after 18:00 on 18 July, so an unwritten overnight extension cannot carry the directive. Supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31, not the 18:06 ERP stamp, is the right evidence for P-505’s commitment time. The signed PDF at 12:40, not the 14:30 ERP entry, is the right evidence for P-507’s four-hour record. P-502 (new supplier, compliance only at 18:30, dual approval after commitment, and no CFO email yet), P-503 (PROC-3 as requester, committer, and co-authorizer; EUR 140,000 with no compliance clearance; three-day backup stock), P-504 (cafeteria freezer, unapproved supplier, commitment before the PO, EUR 24,000 so the EUR 25,000 plant/finance trigger does not apply), and P-506 (EUR 51,000 with only finance before commitment) are defensible NONCOMPLIANT results. A6 is applied as mitigation rather than cure. ECD-2 is not treated as a Chief Risk Officer exception.

The classification rule then breaks. COMPLIANT is defined as full conformity, including controls validly displaced. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is defined as a departure that a valid derogation excuses. P-501’s commitment-before-PO is exactly what SOP clause B4 permits in a genuine sub-six-hour stoppage, yet it is labeled JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. P-507 and P-508 depart from the same local sequence and from the approved-supplier rule under ECD-2 and are labeled COMPLIANT. Those labels cannot both follow from the stated rule. P-501’s delegation problem is also left unresolved: the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1, which defeats B4’s substitution condition if SHIFT-1 exercised plant authority, and the 15:10 approver is unidentified. Calling that JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION treats an unproved exception as granted.

The larger error is A2. The facts say Incident Command members are not established budget owners, and they confine the CFO to interpreting finance controls, not waiving segregation or compliance. The conclusions still treat joint Incident Command authorization, plus the 15 July email, as satisfying both the independent budget owner and the Finance Controller before commitment. That reading is then used to mark P-505, P-507, and P-508 COMPLIANT. P-508 is the clearest miss: EUR 155,000 is above the email’s EUR 150,000 sufficiency line; “Legal clearance as well” adds a legal gate, not a budget owner or Finance Controller; there is no plant or finance event. COMPLIANT at high confidence does not follow. P-505 is also over-cleared: finance approval is at 19:00, after commitment and after expiry, there is no plant approval and no PO in the record, and the committer’s identity is unknown. P-507 assumes the seals serve the affected line and assumes the email replaces A2, with no separate finance event.

A further contradiction: positive duties are said to require proof, and the emergency form is said to survive ECD-2 as operational evidence. P-505, P-507, and P-508 are still COMPLIANT with no form, and P-505 with no PO and no recorded time for the Incident Command authorization. Under the stated burden rule those are unproven positive duties, not clean compliance. “Unapproved” is also leaned on as “presumptively new” for P-504’s compliance trigger; the sources do not equate those categories.

The hierarchy, the express-waiver canon, the scope test, and the planned alternatives are serious work. They do not repair an unstable label scheme, an A2 substitute broader than the CFO’s stated competence, or an answer that never settles the case table, the remediation, or the replacement clause.
