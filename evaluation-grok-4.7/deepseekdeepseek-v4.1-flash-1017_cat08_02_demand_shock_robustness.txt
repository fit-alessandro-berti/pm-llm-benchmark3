The final answer meets every required section and is well grounded in the logs: case-mix shifts, station utilizations and queues, recollection and repeat paths, M2 failure risk, and the stated conflicts among I1–I10. It avoids blanket capacity additions and mean-cycle-time optimization, separates robust rules from triggered policies, and includes redesign-caused vulnerabilities plus leading-versus-lagging thresholds.

The main defect is a repeated reversal of the process flow. Specialist review is upstream of medical validation (`Technical validation → Hold for specialist review → Medical validation`). The answer treats an evening medical-validation shift as pushing more cases into specialist review in the migration analysis, the redesign package, the trade-off table, and the residual risks. Extra medical-validation capacity can clear the downstream queue; it does not enlarge the specialist hold. That is a material bottleneck-migration error, not a wording slip.

A second gap is underuse of a key variant: urgent batch-wait bypass falls from 74% to 58% in the peak, which is central to the urgent p90 moving from 19h to 33h. Registration is also listed as the first S1 bottleneck despite a 2.8h peak queue, far behind pre-analytical, assay, technical validation, and specialist review. Several dashboard cutoffs, especially “1–3 urgent breaches,” have no denominator against roughly 130 urgent cases per peak day.
