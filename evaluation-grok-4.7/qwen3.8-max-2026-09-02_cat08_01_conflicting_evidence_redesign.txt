The final answer matches the required structure and is strongly grounded in the supplied artifacts: frequency edges, performance medians and tails, fitness versus fitting traces, R1–R3 and alignment deviations, rework and post-rework cost, segment outcomes, and resource limits. The three redesigns are genuinely different layers (eligibility routing, evidence capture, fulfillment control), each with an explicit mechanism, target population, and sacrifice. The comparison stays qualitative and correctly refuses exact ROI or Pareto dominance. Failure modes and bottleneck migration are specific, and the experiment plan uses post-close cost, complaints, conformance replay, and capacity rather than average cycle time alone.

The main deduction is process-mining inference. The 3.3% repair-path reopen rate and the 5–6% “reviewed/repair” rates are treated as evidence that repair or review causes better outcomes. Those figures come from selected paths: customers who reach and accept repair, and cases already routed to review, are not comparable to fast-approval cases. That confounding is never stated, yet Redesign C defaults high-value work toward repair and the pilot treats an 18–22% to 5–6% drop as a go target. A second flaw is sequencing: B is justified as creating manual capacity for A, while the live A pilot is placed before B. “Comformance” is a small clarity error. Those issues keep an otherwise defensible redesign from being nearly flawless.
