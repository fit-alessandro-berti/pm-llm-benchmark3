4.5

The required six-part structure is present, and the forecast correctly treats the join as a maximum, keeps weekend closures in view, and reports ranges rather than a single timestamp. The numerical spine does not hold up.

The incubation anchor is wrong. Readiness 46–54 h after Friday 14:00 is Sunday 12:00–20:00, not Sunday 16:00–Monday 20:00. The whole primary-readiness window sits on Sunday, so without on-call the readout is always Monday 08:00 onward. The contamination clock is then built on that wrong window and is internally inconsistent with it: Sunday 16:00 plus 20–30 h is not Wednesday 12:00–Thursday 02:00. Confirmation after a Monday-morning readout lands on Tuesday, not midweek.

Chemistry queueing is also off. Four samples are waiting ahead, so B-417 is fifth, not fourth, and the two analysts’ residual jobs are extra. Taking the 45% risk-priority rule as the base case is the less likely discipline, and the claim that FIFO costs about 30 minutes is not defensible: the Monday backlog can move B-417’s start from early Monday into the afternoon or Tuesday. Section 3 then starts a full triangular service at Monday 08:00, which erases that wait. The stated triangular mean, 2.75 h, does not match (1.5 + 4×2.5 + 4.5)/6 = 2.67 h.

The QA story contradicts itself. Five batches on hand, about three weekend arrivals, and 5–6 median-based completions leave a residual queue, but the ledger and the smooth path both say the queue has cleared and the wait is about 0.5–2 h. Throughput uses the 1.5 h median even though the 3.5 h 80th percentile implies a heavier mean. Scenario probabilities mix joints and marginals: “rerun and documentation query” at 15–20% is about the rerun rate alone, and a laboratory investigation at 20–25% is high once the 60–80% documentation-only share is applied.

Section 6 then breaks the parallel-join logic. An on-call Sunday readout cannot pull the central release forward by 8–12 h when chemistry is closed all weekend and QA is closed Sunday; under the answer’s own smooth-path maximum, chemistry is already the later branch. Several remaining-hour labels do not match the stated clocks (Monday 14:00 is about 70.5 h, not 68 h; the severe-tail Monday is not 204 h).

The central envelope, Monday afternoon through Tuesday midday, is still in a plausible band, and temperature-excursion dependence is treated as a weight shift rather than an independent add-on. Those merits do not repair the readiness date, the queue, or the contradictory on-call update.
