3.5

The write-up has the required seven sections and a full case table, but it misreads several control-critical cases, contradicts the policy overlay, and supplies code that neither runs nor meets the reproduction requirements. Those are core failures for this task, not presentation nits.

**Materiality.** Section 1 lists the right factors and never turns them into a scale: no band definitions, no rule for combining exposure, irreversibility, and evidence, and no usable distinction between severity and confidence. The confidence example treats a missing `Assess Loss` as both low-confidence and possibly high-severity without saying which fact drives which judgment. Silent `T(tau)` moves are described as model gaps. The notation defines them as technically fitting.

**Case judgments.** The alignment literacy breaks on model moves versus log moves, and on rules the tree does not encode.

- CL-609 is the clearest zero-cost policy failure: EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk, so P1 forbids Auto Approve. The row calls it low-value, P1-compliant, a silent move, and severity 1. There is no silent move. This inverts the case the cost discussion is built to catch.
- CL-607 was denied at coverage pre-screen, with no payment intended. `M(Assess Loss)`, `M(Auto Approve)`, and `M(Pay Claim)` are model insertions. The row says the denial proceeded through auto-approval and payment, treats disputed P6 as a violation, and assigns severity 5.
- CL-602 is `S(Manual Approve)` with `M(Fraud Screen)` on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim whose bank changed the day before. The row calls it auto-approved. P2 is the right rule, but the path is wrong, and “approver misled” is not in the evidence.
- CL-606 has synchronous Fraud Screen and Manual Approve after a signed adjuster report. P2 is not the failed control. The gap is core-log evidence of assessment. Severity 4 and “severe P2 violation” overstate a logging/linkage question.
- CL-608’s portal/CRM message is at +3 hours. P4 allows portal or CRM within 24 hours when linked evidence is retained. The mutable text field weakens confidence in that link. It does not establish a timing breach or a severe P4 violation.
- CL-605’s `T(tau)` is the XOR routing silent transition and is fitting at cost 0. The row calls it a structural flaw to remove, then also scores it severity 1 and fully compliant.
- CL-604 correctly sees payment at 10:03 and manual approval at 10:20, and P3 is the right overlay. Confidence is then set to Low and the likely cause is given as clock mismatch, which the timestamps do not support. P2 also applies because the amount is EUR 90,000. The immediate action (“reject payments”) does not address a payment the bank already accepted.
- CL-612 is a withdrawal with no model branch. Phantom `M` moves inflate cost. The row calls this an operational deviation and a severe P6 violation, then recommends a withdrawal approval step. The same row’s last cell says high cost and low risk.
- CL-610 correctly flags a synchronous P1 breach after a bank change. It omits P2, which independently requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve after that change. Severity 3 is light for an irreversible payment to a bank changed one hour earlier.
- CL-601, CL-603, and CL-611 are largely right: telemetry noise, a same-message-ID retry, and an `Assess Damage` / `Assess Loss` label split. CL-603 still narrates two notifications rather than one retried transport event.

The pattern table then says retry duplicates create 24-hour delay risk, limits out-of-scope handling to one denial, and drops withdrawals despite the evidence that denials and withdrawals drive most raw costs at or above 4. “Four synchronous Auto Approve policy failures” is cited while CL-609 is marked compliant in the case table. Section 4 has six thin rows. Several hypotheses are already disfavored by the given evidence (pre-screen failure on a claim the context says was denied; manual retry when one connector explains 74 duplicates). No row states that a model move is not proof the activity did not occur. Section 5 attaches CL-608 to a pre-payment fraud-screen block, omits CL-609 from data-aware auto-approval repair, and gives 75–100% risk-reduction figures with no basis. Control redesign, event correlation, model-branch repair, and case investigation are only partly separated. Several “corrective” items are future preventive controls.

**Cost versus risk.** Section 6 uses five cases, but CL-605 and CL-608 are argued from the wrong facts, CL-609 is absent, and “data conditions” is illustrated with external evidence rather than amount, risk, and bank-change state. The uniform unit cost, CL-601’s telemetry cost of 3 against CL-602’s control cost of 1, scope-driven cost on CL-607/CL-612, and irreversibility of an already accepted payment are not held together in one comparison.
