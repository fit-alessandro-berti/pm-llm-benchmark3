4.5

The writeup hits the required seven-part skeleton, distinguishes East from North/South, and correctly refuses an unmonitored full rollout. The forecast is still not reliable: headline quantities contradict the handover table and basic queueing arithmetic, the pilot cannot identify the effects it is supposed to test, and the PM4Py sketch does not run and measures the wrong objects.

**Handover arithmetic is backwards.** Summing the typed frequencies gives about 343 handover events per 100 cases today and about 359 under management’s own post-change table. Specialist out-and-back traffic rises (36+31 to 41+36), approval traffic rises (42+39 to 49+43), and legal/risk rises (9 to 12). Only cross-team and intake-to-caseworker fall. The central claim of a 10% nominal reduction is inconsistent with that table. The adverse “+7%” is closer to the given numbers than the “most probable” case. The mechanism map then cites “41 vs 36” as evidence of fewer local loops, which is an increase, and it treats the local-supervisor loop as removed even though only one supervisor post is cut and only medium/high-value cases move.

**The scarce queue is missing, and the capacity math is unstable.** Legal and Risk already run at 93% and 91%. A rise from 9 to 12 referrals per 100 cases is a 33% demand increase. With unchanged headcount, utilization goes to roughly 1.2, so the queue does not settle at management’s 10.5 h; it grows without bound. None of the three scenarios forecast legal/risk wait. The Little’s Law line is also incomplete (`Wq = ·(util/(1-util))` omits service rate and server count), so pooling of N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech is never actually calculated. Optimistic median cycle time “−0.8 d (6%)” does not match the volume-weighted baseline of about 7.5 d, where 0.8 d is closer to 11%. “Cross-team 50%” and several unsigned “pp” moves have no defined base. The optimistic “early warning” (utilization above 85% for five days) is a failure threshold, while the other scenarios use early warnings as signs that scenario is occurring.

**Mechanism and risk gaps.** The claim that the same-local-caseworker loop survives in East contradicts merging E_Tech into the shared service. A single FIFO story in the map conflicts with the later claim that `priority` sets order. Section 4 lists six real risks in one sentence each. Fairness is only a clause inside high-complexity starvation, with no team, language, or `customer_segment` comparison. Mix shift is ignored: more automation on simple eligibility can improve the average while high-complexity and North/South cases worsen. Learning is named and then left without a time path.

**Indicators.** `get_service_time` is used for a waiting-time percentile. `groupby.max(reassignment_count)` is not “reassignment after Tech_SS.” Subcontracting depth is not duplicate approvals. Weekly SLA miss is an outcome, listed as leading. The 90th-percentile cycle-time row has an empty target. Legal/risk load, `local_expertise_flag`, `handover_reason`, and cross-team rate are absent. “Rural” and a 10% similarity-entropy rule are invented.

**Pilot.** East is fully treated and has no within-team control; North/South contribute only a 20% high-complexity sample. Treatment is confounded with team and complexity. Staffing is unspecified: moving only E_Tech does not test a three-person pool, and moving all three techs contaminates the control. Volume in the pilot is far below full-scale load, so it cannot reveal the bottleneck the recommendation says appears only under load. Tech and approval change together, so attribution fails. Legal/risk has no guardrail. A three-month window is short for the learning loss the answer itself places months out. `local_expertise_flag` is treated as a survey checkbox.

**Code sketch.** `raw_df`, `df_pre`, `df_post`, and `cases_by_team` are undefined. The cohort loop looks up `df_before` / `df_after` instead. `from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.sna import handover` is not the public API the brief names (`discover_handover_of_work_network` and the matching working-together and subcontracting calls). Working-together and subcontracting run only on the last loop value. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` has no `activity` argument and writes case-level `@@waiting_time`, so it cannot describe the Tech or approval queue. `discover_organizational_roles` expects a log, not a similarity matrix. On a dataframe, `get_variants` returns counts, so `len(l) > 10` throws; keeping frequent variants also does the opposite of tail analysis. Specialty and customer segment are never filtered. Event-attribute filters are not shown at case level with full traces retained. Rework is not compared before and after. The sketch cannot support the comparison it claims to specify.

The recommendation to pilot, watch tails, and withhold judgment from early averages is the right decision class. It is not operational: there is no pass rule for scaling, no correction for East composition inside the average, and no condition that legal/risk utilization stay below the point where waiting explodes.
