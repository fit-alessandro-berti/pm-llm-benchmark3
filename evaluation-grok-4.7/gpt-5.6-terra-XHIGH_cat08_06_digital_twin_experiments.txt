7.6

The design is strong on architecture and humility, but it is not nearly flawless: a few executable-model contradictions and measurement biases would change cycle times and intervention rankings.

The twin is correctly a calendar-aware discrete-event model with shared queues, not an independent-case replay. Non-stationary arrivals, architect bursts, district inspection batching, latent objections, service-versus-wait separation, right-censoring, common random numbers, and the refusal to treat an unvalidated run as optimal are all handled well. PM4Py is kept on the log/model side, and the skeleton uses `pandas.read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, a guarded `parse_process_tree`, variants, a DFG, a performance DFG, an inter-arrival mean, and explicit timing columns. Assumptions are separated as required, and D1–D10 plus combined and stress runs are present.

The serious gaps:

- Public notice is grouped with the parallel technical branches, but the process tree places it after the AND-block, and P001 posts notice only once fire review has finished. Parallelizing it would shorten sojourn time relative to both the tree and the excerpt. The later DAG notes treat notice as its own clock, so the spec contradicts itself.
- Council meetings are said to shock public-notice cases, while notice is also defined as pure calendar time outside municipal calendars. Those cannot both be true without a hearing-alignment rule. That rule is missing.
- The primary duration clock starts at submission. D1’s pre-submission delay and abandonment then fall outside the KPI that is used to rank policies, so D1 is favored by construction. Applicant burden is mentioned, but it is not in the decision metric.
- Rework is only “return to an earlier stage.” Destinations after planning, inspection, legal check, and appeal are not probabilities. Those destinations determine which pools re-congest.
- The nominal tree does not fit the excerpt: P001 repeats Completeness check with no “Completeness rework” activity. Weekend and Saturday timestamps (completeness, fire review) also contradict the Monday–Friday and Tuesday–Thursday calendars. Neither inconsistency is diagnosed. Public notice is a single timestamp for an interval; duration estimation from that point event is not specified.
- The arrival intensity multiplies conditional case-type and architect factors and then adds another architect burst process, with no normalization. That double-counts peaks.
- A 70–90% utilization target can reject a policy that correctly unloads a pool. D3 travel reductions are not protected against service times that already contain historical travel. Reject-application’s 30-minute service has no pool. D6 does not say what happens if the reserved planner is one of the two simple-renovation-only planners.
- Uncertainty treatment is otherwise careful, but the “add replications until the CI is narrow enough” rule ignores optional-stopping bias. “Risk-adjusted” district fairness and “non-inferior in 80% of draws” have no margin or adjustment model. Simulator direction checks are labeled validation.
- `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is less stable than `pm4py.get_variants`. Naive timestamps are stamped UTC, which shifts a local calendar. The text requires separate random streams; the skeleton uses one generator.

Coverage is high and the simulation reasoning is mostly realistic. The parallelism error, the D1 clock bias, and the unresolved notice/calendar and rework-destination gaps are enough to keep this well below a near-flawless score.
