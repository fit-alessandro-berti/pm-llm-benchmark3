The answer covers all seven required sections and reaches the right broad split: hospital priority, manual authorization, deferral, and backlog rules can be resilience, while skipped authorization and scanless dispatch cannot, and post-close audit work is not real-time compliance. That thesis is undermined by repeated quantitative and temporal mistakes.

The manual-phase DFG is misread. Dispatch without scan is fed by 1,800 packs plus 1,450 temporary-release cases, so about 3,250 handoffs, not 1,800. Only 2,600 reach phone proof of delivery. Temporary release therefore flows into the traceability break; treating F as a success and G as a separate 1,800-case shortcut misses that link. Pattern K sits on Inventory reservation → Reconcile manual reservation → Pick/Cancel during stabilization, before pick. The answer places it after dispatch and invoice and then uses that false sequence to reclassify E and H. The 2,300-case tail is L (Invoice close → Audit remediation), which is conflated with K.

Period volumes are compared on unequal windows. Baseline is 42,000 cases over 21 business days, about 2,000 a day. T2’s 5,600 cases are roughly normal daily volume, not “13% of normal load.” T3 and T4 run above that daily rate. “Backlog begins” in T3 is wrong; the backlog queue is already in the T2 DFG. T0 is written as Day 30 to Day 1 instead of Day −30 to Day −1. T4 medians are called near baseline, but pharmacy median is 28h versus 23h and wholesale 58h versus 36h, with p90 still 74h versus 49h and 120h versus 72h. Those tails are omitted.

Domain inferences are overclaimed or wrong. The 2.9% controlled-substance dual-control gap is assigned to unsampled ambient orders, which are outside that metric. “No rework or complaints” for the skip-authorization cohort is not in the data. Snapshot “validation,” a 4–6 hour / mid-morning staleness point, a baseline 100% accept rate, an 80–90% rework reduction, and a 15–30% capacity loss are invented. DEA Form 106 is the theft/loss form, not a routine dispatch record, yet it is prescribed repeatedly.

The recovery model includes the required elements, but it conflicts with the answer’s own standard. It allows a standing-authorization skip after retiring C, tells the reader to retire L while Phase 5 still accepts gaps, treats a QA sample as a dual-control proxy, reconstructs cold-chain performance from silence plus proof of delivery (“assume reasonable thermal performance”), and backdates proof of delivery. T3’s specific hazard—intake restored while inventory sync and scanning are untrustworthy—is not a hard gate. Several indicator thresholds are missing operators or arbitrary, and an unrequested conclusion breaks the “exactly these sections” format.

The management trade-off is directionally usable, but it inherits the bad dispatch count, the unsupported rework cause, and the false recovery arithmetic. Completeness keeps this above a failing paper; the DFG timing errors, fabricated cohort facts, and a redesign that recreates concealed compliance do not support a higher score.
