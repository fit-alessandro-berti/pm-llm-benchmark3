The write-up has the required ten sections, a real evidence ledger, a sound warning that two DFG directions do not prove concurrency, and a useful data-collection plan. The three models themselves do not do what they claim.

The conservative tree is not a fit of the log. `xor(CC, ER)` selects one check, while all 30 cases contain both, and `sequence(...)` / `optional(...)` is not PM4Py process-tree syntax (`->`, `X`, `+`, `*`, with optionality as `X(a, tau)`). The prose says both checks occur on mutually exclusive orders; the code deletes one of them. That tree also cannot place Supplier Confirmation between the checks, so it misses the 2-case variant even under a charitable reading. Claiming about 100% fitness is false.

Both POWL candidates set `is_optional = True`, which PM4Py does not interpret, so Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review are mandatory. Nodes are never added, and edges are attached with `add_edge` rather than `order.add_edge`. As written, the balanced order also forces both checks before Supplier Confirmation, so it rejects the observed `Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review` cases and contradicts its own D5 rationale. Only the two expedite traces contain every activity; perfect fitness is nowhere near 100%. The permissive net still allows Supplier Confirmation before Register Order, outside the D1 boundary the text claims to keep.

The unobserved-behavior audit is largely wrong. Several “unobserved” traces are the 12-, 7-, and 1-case variants actually in the log, and several “admitted” traces violate the edges just defined (Expedite Review before Engineering Review; Supplier Confirmation before Engineering Review in the balanced order).

The validation script would not run: activity labels do not match the log, `fitness_alignments` returns a dict that cannot be formatted as `.3f`, the positive and negative traces are only printed, and the “balanced” net is copied from the permissive edge set, dropping `CC→SC` and `ER→SC`. Sampled playout is correctly described as non-exhaustive, but it never becomes a real check.

Section 3 also calls overfitting (rejecting unseen but plausible orders) an underfitting risk. The recommendation and confidence percentages describe the intended stories, not the languages of the assigned nets.
