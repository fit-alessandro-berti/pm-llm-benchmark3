6.5

The dialectical structure is strong: stipulated facts are separated from authority and scope disputes, both briefs are genuinely argued, required acknowledgments are present, and the case rulings mostly track written instruments, expiry, and burden of proof. That quality is undercut by model-semantics errors and an ill-formed POWL, which the task makes central.

The executable model does not represent the language it describes. The same `Transition` objects for sanctions, quality, activation, manual approval, and rejection are inserted into multiple parents; each later constructor overwrites `.parent`, so a parent-based reading leaves sanctions on the abbreviated reject branch, quality on the full reject branch, and activation on the emergency branch. The standard route is therefore not a well-formed POWL. `skip_quality_pre` is unused. Distinct activity instances per branch were required.

The `Request_More_Info` loop is also in the wrong place. `LOOP(skip_info, request_info)` correctly yields zero or more requests, but it sits entirely before the decision XOR, while sanctions and quality sit inside the routes. That language cannot generate SUP-707 (`Sanctions_Check`, then two requests, then `Quality_Review`). The limitation notes miss this interleaving failure. Reactivation is only a global silent skip of registration, so SUP-710 still has no quality-waiver path, and withdrawal is deferred in one sentence. PD-3’s mandatory automated master-data validation is never an activity and is never applied to SUP-702. CS-9 is open-ended and overlaps PD-3 and FD-7 with no precedence rule.

Case analysis slips where concurrency matters. In SUP-708, quality before sanctions is allowed by M0’s unordered checks; the matrix lists it as a conformance issue and omits the independent breach that manual approval occurs after activation. The severe classification itself is still right. SUP-711 is a explained same-transaction duplicate, yet it is labeled indeterminate while SUP-713’s label alias is treated as compliant. SUP-715 is factually a missing COI disclosure; the write-up calls that gap unproved and uses a muddy MIXED rationale. Several predicates are unreadable because comparison and arrow operators are missing (`requester manual approver`, `2%29%`, certification-validity and cap rules). The defense also overstates FD-7: provisional activation was permitted, not required. SUP-714 is counted beside the six August auto-approvals even though it falls inside that aggregate.

Authority handling is otherwise careful: in-window SUP-702 and SUP-704 are justified, post-expiry SUP-705 and SUP-714 are not, scope stays open for SUP-709 and SUP-710, and serious breaches (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, ineligible auto-approvals) are not excused by model debt. The layered control design and the data-contract contents match the case failures. The grade stops here because the normative model artifact does not actually encode those distinctions.
