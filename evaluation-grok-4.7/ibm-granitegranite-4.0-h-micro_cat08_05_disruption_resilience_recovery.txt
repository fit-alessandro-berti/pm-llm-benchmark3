The answer covers the seven required sections and the A–L table, but its central judgments are unsafe and several claims contradict the event data.

The timeline misplaces the manual-phase variants. The workaround DFG places dispatch without scan, phone proof of delivery, and deferral in T2, alongside skip-authorization and the fast lane. The write-up assigns those first three to T3 and omits reconciliation from T4, where the stabilization DFG shows it. Baseline is described as meeting all compliance requirements despite 0.8% traceability exceptions, 0.4% missing cold-chain documents, and 0.2% missing dual control. Hospital resilience is described as a shorter cycle time; the hospital median moved from 8h to 10h. The 91% hospital fill rate versus the 61% pharmacy fill rate, case volumes, and fitness drop from 0.96 to 0.71 are unused.

In the table, retiring temporary release control discards the control that reduced controlled-substance error; the cold-chain gap calls for extending that check. Retiring deferral discards the capacity choice that kept hospital supply moving, and the harm is assigned only to wholesale-bulk although pharmacy fill and dissatisfaction are the measured damage. Skip-authorization is tied to hospital and pharmacy orders rather than ambient product shipped on assumed standing authorization. Manual authorization is limited to hospital-critical orders although that path is much larger than the fast lane. “Keep” for the fast lane conflicts with the cell’s own call for formal rules. Dispatch without scan is treated as a critical-item speedup; it is the dominant handoff.

Section 3 then contradicts itself: backlog prioritization is both true resilience and the sole ambiguous pattern. Snapshot checks, paper picks, pharmacist release, phone delivery confirmation, deferral, and audit remediation are grouped as concealed noncompliance. Reconciliation is omitted. That erases the required split among real resilience, shortcuts, and appetite-dependent choices.

The recovery model has the requested headings, but the controls are attached to customer tier: dual control and temperature checks are hospital rules, while bulk work is left to periodic audit. Product risk is independent of customer tier. “Halt all automation,” system entry within 30 minutes, and daily or weekly cleanup do not match a WMS outage or the multi-week remediation tail. Partial recovery, when intake is back but sync and scanning are unreliable, is absent. Recall-sensitive product is never mentioned.

Indicators name the requested topics and do not define leading versus lagging measures, baselines, or segment splits. Throughput is defined as processing without downtime. Recommendations are generic, share one “high effort / low residual risk” rating, and leave controlled-substance, cold-chain, and fairness risk looking closed. The trade-off tells management to reject deferral, pharmacist release, and remediation, and it gives no concrete evidence that would change that decision.
