The write-up keeps the requested section skeleton and a partly right headline sequence (March label break, May rework, June capacity loss, October mix, December improvement). It does not meet the quantitative, temporal, or process-mining standard the task set.

**Timeline.** Several intervals attach the wrong month’s numbers and the wrong mechanism. Median cycle time and fast-variant share cited for May–June are March values (31.2 h and 57%); May is already 36.5 h and 45%. `Assess → Request Info` doubles by May (12 to 24 per 100 cases), before the July priority rule, yet that doubling is assigned to June–July. Senior assessment is present from January (about 4 per 100) and jumps in October (22), not July–September. October raw fitness does not recover (0.56 to 0.55). Duplicate `Open Case` and the fitness collapse begin in March, so the March–May row is mistimed. “Staffing recovery” is not in the timeline. Alternatives often restate the preferred mechanism. December strength 85 ignores the stated confound that load, complexity, and Supplier Z share also fell.

**Raw versus mapped fitness.** This section is about 120 words, short of 180–280, and inverts the required claim: mapping is treated as proof that process structure was unchanged. Mapped fitness only returns to about 0.90–0.91 in March–April and then sits near 0.86–0.89; that residual is small, so “remains low” conflates it with raw fitness near 0.50. Missing resources are treated as cycle-time distortion. The persistent raw–mapped gap shows the label break continued; it does not show that post-May directly-follows and waiting-time changes are logging artifacts. That distinction is asserted in one sentence.

**Quantities.** The baseline table uses a fabricated “2021” baseline (500 cases, 18% high complexity, 10% Supplier Z, 30 h) and percentage changes that do not match 2026-01/02 or the later months. Jaccard figures are not computed from the rate fragment; January is given distance 0.05 from an unnamed reference, and listed “missing” edges are edges that are present. Cycle-time parts (+15, +10, +2, +23 h) sum to about 50 h against a median increase of about 16 h from January to October. Recorded `Assess` service time rises by about 0.6 h, not 2 h. Only one change point is given for logging, behavior, and performance; two were required. The conclusion that change points are mechanism-specific is the sound part of the section.
