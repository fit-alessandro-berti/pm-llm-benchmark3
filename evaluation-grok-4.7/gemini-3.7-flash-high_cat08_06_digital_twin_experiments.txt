5.5

The write-up covers every required section and names the right machinery (shared resources, calendars, latent objections, seasonality, CRN, tail metrics, validation tiers). It is not a reliable design: several load-bearing quantities contradict the given log summary, the stated model, or the code that is supposed to implement them. Those are not presentation nits. They would change intervention rankings.

**Arrival calibration is inconsistent with the stated demand.** The log says March–May and September–November are 1.7× January–February, not 1.7× the annual mean. Section 2 sets peak ≈ 1.7× the overall base and January–February ≈ 0.6× that base, so the peak/winter ratio is about 2.8, and the month weights cannot average back to 1 unless the unnamed months are near zero. The code then ignores the 0.6 factor and multiplies the already-average rate by 1.7 for half the year (and by 2.2 on Mondays), which inflates annual volume by roughly a third before any stress factor. Capacity interventions are then compared on the wrong load.

**Routing probabilities contradict the variant table.** “New residential with fire + public notice” is 18% of cases and new residential is 28%, so both fire and notice probabilities for that type are at least about 0.64. The text sets P(notice) = 0.40, which cannot produce that joint share. Commercial with fire, environment, and legal is 13% of 17%, so P(environment | commercial) cannot be 0.75. Completeness-loop probability 0.42 is the simple-renovation missing-doc share, then applied to every case type. These are presented as estimated parameters, not as assumptions.

**Service distributions do not match the percentiles they cite.** For a lognormal, σ ≈ 0.55 with median 25 minutes gives a p90 near 50 minutes, not 90. The same mismatch appears for planning (claimed p90 14 h, parameters give ~9 h), fire, legal, site inspection, and applicant delay. Environmental Weibull(5.2, 0.85) does not have median 4 h under the parameterization used in code. The inspection Gamma is scale 2.5 days in the text and scale 20 hours in code. Public notice is misread: the table’s 60 days is the p90 column (“with objections”), not the median of a contested component, and 78/22, median 60, and p90 120 are invented. Section 3.9 then calls durations above 14 days an “exact proxy” for objections, which treats a missing event as observed and ignores that the mixture weight and the delay law are not separately identified.

**Control flow and rework do not match the process that was supplied.** The tree is submit → completeness loop → parallel reviews → optional notice → inspection loop → decision → optional legal → outcome, with rework after planning, inspection, legal check, and appeal. The diagram is mostly sequential; the code starts public notice in parallel with reviews, skips inspection scheduling, draws legal with a flat 0.15, and ends in an 8% coin-flip reject. Planning rework, legal rework, and appeals never appear, so the complex-variance tail (median 140 days, appeal/rework) has no mechanism. D2’s “~75% of simple cases” is not implied by the data, and the code’s own simple-value lognormal puts P(value < 50,000) near two-thirds before the required prior-violation filter, which is never implemented.

**The engine does not implement the dependencies or calendars it claims.** Architect bursts are an i.i.d. 31% label, not a Hawkes cluster. Objection contagion is an independent 0.22 draw. `get_effective_capacity` (Monday clerk holdout, 1.8 environmental FTE, peak inspector, council weeks) is never applied. SimPy capacities stay fixed, inspectors are created at FTE+2, and off-shift behavior only pauses work already holding a token. D7 lowers the Monday multiplier and deletes demand instead of shifting it. D6 is specified and then absent. D1’s pre-submission delay is absent. Batch release is timed from original submission, not from entry to the inspection queue. One seeded run is labeled as a replicated, CRN experiment; separate streams are not there, so policy branches consume different random numbers.

**Uncertainty and decision rules overclaim.** The sample-size formula is for a mean and does not evaluate to N = 35 without an s; it is then used for median and p90. Welch warm-up assumes a steady state this seasonal system does not have. Completed-case filtering right-censors the long complex and objection tails and that bias is never mentioned. Baseline KPIs (38.5 days, 92 days, 68.2% SLA, and the rest) are not in the log. A simple-renovation SLA of 98% within 15 days is not credible against a clean-path p90 of 24 days and a missing-doc median of 22 days. Equity thresholds disagree between the scorecard and the table. Replay “100% structurally valid” is true by construction if the simulator only emits conforming paths. A large-sample KS test at α = 0.05 will reject trivial, practically irrelevant differences.

**PM4Py section is only partly compliant.** The excerpt is loaded with pandas, `format_dataframe` is used correctly, and DFG, performance DFG, and variants are computed. Case arrival average is required and not computed. Inter-event time is stored as one column while the comment calls it a service/waiting split. The process-tree string is neither parsed nor accompanied by the parser’s limitations; the hand-built tree is never called. That separation of mining from simulation is the right architecture, but the skeleton is not a faithful executable of the design above it.

The skeleton is modular and the experiment list includes D1–D10 plus stresses. The numerical and logical breaks above are enough that the twin, as specified, should not be used to choose a policy.
