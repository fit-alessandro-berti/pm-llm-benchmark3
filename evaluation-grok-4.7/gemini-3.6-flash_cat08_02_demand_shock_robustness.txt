5.5

The write-up has the required seven-part shape and a real migration story, but too many of its numbers and mechanisms do not survive the logs. It is not a reliable redesign.

**Scenarios are internally inconsistent and partly uncalibrated.** “35% above normal” does not produce ~550 cases/day: 18,400 cases over 8 weeks is about 329/day on a 7-day lab, and 35% above that is about 444/day (peak is already about 471/day over 3 weeks). The diagram’s Urgent-plus-Complex shift “49% → 60%+” does not match the prose mix (32% + 22% = 54%). Scenario 3’s “WIP stalls at 85% completion,” doubled clerk error rates, and “queue 36h → 72h+” are invented. “Sole” specialist over-reads “one specialist unavailable every second Friday.” M1 outage is treated as a ~50% capacity loss even though M2 is the faster machine.

**Process-mining use is frequent and often wrong.** Repeat rates of 7.2% and 17.4% are repeats divided by successful technical-validation exits (1,140/15,890 and 1,260/7,240). Of assay outcomes, the rates are about 6.7% and 14.8%. Raw repeat counts (1,140 vs 1,260) are compared across an 8-week window and a 3-week window. Scenario 3’s diagram labels the performance edges 18h/48h as a queue explosion; the queue table is 5.0h / 36.0h. Observed peak queues (pre-analytical 9.5h, medical 22h, specialist 36h, technical validation 101%) are then reused as the results of new, more severe scenarios, so historical description is passed off as counterfactual migration. The urgent batch-bypass rate falling from 74% to 58% at peak is missed; that is evidence that the bypass is already failing when load rises. Time bases are not normalized, open cases are ignored (about 1,960 normal and 1,320 peak starts never reach END), and recollection→cancel rising from about 35% to about 42% is unused.

**The main redesign rests on a misread map.** Recollection already leaves at pre-analytical check, before preparation and assay. I6 cannot stop samples from “traversing assay and validation,” and it does not cut `Request recollection → Receive sample` from 60h to <24h; that edge is mostly return time, not detection delay. Assay repeats are a different branch (`Assay run → Repeat assay`) and are not prevented by intake rejection. “Up to 70% fewer data-entry errors,” queue cuts to <1.0h, and a 4–6% repeat reduction are unsupported. Routing both Urgent and Complex-panel work to M1 under a mix shift (together already ~49–54% of cases, with complex panels needing multiple assays) is the overload that policy is supposed to prevent. Having technical validators screen “normal” results for medical release misses the note that medical validators are needed for Urgent and Complex-panel cases; the peak flow shift is into that medical/specialist branch, not into already-releasable normals. Cross-training draws on the pool that is already at 101% at peak.

**Scenario policies conflict with the constraints, the SLA, and the dashboard.** Policy A’s automated release of non-complex Urgent results bypasses medical validation that the resource note says Urgent cases need, with no safety downside. Policy B sends Urgent work to partner labs without reconciling a 12h transport leg to an 18h penalty clock, and it adds undefined backup bench capacity. Policy D triggers on the pre-analytical queue and then changes preparation batch size, which does not drain that queue. Repeat-rate bands are green below 5% while normal operation is already ~7%, red above 8% while Policy B trips at 12%, and pre-analytical red at 4.5h while Policy D trips at 6h. Lagging targets (Urgent breach <1%, peak Routine p90 <60h, Complex p90 <110h, rework <6%) are tighter than normal-period tails (Routine p90 68h, Complex p90 118h, rework 7.5%) and contradict the table’s own claim that Routine median time gets worse.

**What holds up.** Case types are not treated as one average. I1/I2/I9 are not adopted wholesale. Downstream stations are named for the standing package. The table has the required columns and some real trade-offs (Routine delay, specialist queue after the evening medical shift, 12h outsourcing delay). One residual is actually created by the redesign (auto-registration moving bad metadata into pre-analytical check). Leading and lagging metrics are separated, and on/off triggers exist. Those merits keep the answer in the middle of the scale; the numerical and causal errors keep it from being operationally sound.
