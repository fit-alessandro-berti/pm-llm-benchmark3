The brief is structurally complete and often case-specific, but it is not sound on the two points the task makes decisive: authority, and POWL semantics. Those failures are large enough to keep the work well below a high score.

The burden rule in §1 says a relaxation never authorized to weaken CS-9 fails regardless of merit. §5 then treats in-window PD-3 as a justified exception for SUP-702, with medium-high confidence, without answering the Compliance observer’s later denial or the fact that PD-3 was never taken into CS-9. FD-7, a dated directive, and PD-3, minutes plus a retracted “monitored exception,” are given the same legal force. The 289 apparently eligible June–July auto-approvals and the 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes are never classified under that holding, so the system judgment does not cover the aggregate the brief itself stipulates.

SUP-703 is mishandled on the facts. The EUR 20,000 cap is FD-7 only, and FD-7 was not in force on 2026-06-20. PD-3 states no spend cap. “EUR 180k > cap” in the brief and the matrix is a category error; the real breach is ineligible high-risk auto-approval and exposure.

SUP-715 is grouped with CS-9 separation of duties. CS-9 separates requester from manual approver. This case is quality reviewer and approver, spouses in one reporting line, with a disclosure duty under a separate conflict-of-interest policy and an express statement that the relationship itself is not prohibited. Non-disclosure can be a violation; calling it a high-severity CS-9 separation-of-duties breach overstates both source and harm. SUP-712 is the actual same-human rule breach.

SUP-711 is described as a migration duplicate with one source transaction id, severity none, confidence high, then labeled DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE. That label is for an unresolved conclusion. The stated facts support a resolved logging duplicate of an otherwise conforming case. SUP-706 is undated, yet the 2026-07-01 CRM migration is treated as the explanation; the missing case date is not listed as missing evidence. SUP-713’s mapping was approved a week after the event, and the compliant label does not deal with that retroactivity. SUP-710 is treated as out of scope even though CS-9’s general pre-activation quality rule has no reactivation carve-out; only rejected leads are expressly ambiguous. SUP-709 underuses the abbreviated pre-screen sentence and slightly favors “probably out of scope” while the process owner says the opposite.

The revised model does not accept the cases the judgment relies on.

- `LOOP` with children `[Request_More_Info, silent]` is “do, then optionally redo”: one or more info requests on every path, including SUP-701 and SUP-702. The comment’s “0..n” reading is the wrong cardinality.
- `Sanctions_Check` sits inside the standard, auto, and emergency branches, while the reject child is only `Reject_Supplier`. A trace cannot both clear sanctions and reject, so SUP-709 is not in the language, and rejection can occur with no sanctions check.
- The info-request loop is entirely before the exclusive choice. SUP-707 requests information after sanctions and before quality; M1 cannot place it there.
- One shared `Transition` object for sanctions, quality, approval, and activation is reused across exclusive branches. In PM4Py those are node identities, so the XOR branches are not cleanly separate models.
- The auto branch leaves quality unordered against the whole sanctions/auto-approve prefix, so quality may precede sanctions. That over-approximation is not disclosed.
- §6 says the four routes come after concurrent checks. The code does not do that for auto or emergency. The DECLARE gloss “Activate response-precedes Sanctions_Check” names the wrong direction and is not a real constraint template.
- The overlay table then folds conflict-of-interest disclosure into CS-9 compensating controls after §6 correctly treats it as a separate rule, and separation of duties is not applied to emergency manual approval.

The data-collection list is the strongest part: stable `notification_id`, rule versions, eligibility snapshots, alias resolution, delegation, and separate event versus record timestamps are concrete and case-linked. The briefs do argue both sides and concede real points. Those merits do not repair the authority contradiction or a normative POWL that rejects the compliant baseline, the in-window pilot pattern, sanctions-then-reject, and the info-request trace it was built to allow.
