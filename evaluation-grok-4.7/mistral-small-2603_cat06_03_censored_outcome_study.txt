4.2

The write-up names the right hazards (activation selection, immature labels, policy change, migration, transfer linkage) and partitions all 48,000 cases, but the design does not hold together. Several core rules contradict each other, and the code implements the biased labeling the study is supposed to avoid.

**Target.** Time-to-default after activation is a legitimate primary question, and refusing to code rejects as non-defaults in that analysis is right. The estimand is not identified as stated. Prediction is placed after the credit decision, while the population is everyone who eventually activates. Activation is then still in the future, so conditioning on it selects on a post-prediction event. “Censoring, e.g. cases still active after 90 days” misstates the 3,600: those outcomes have not matured. Survival past a fully observed day 90 is a non-default under this label, not censoring. The alternative calls still-open a multinomial class and uses the ambiguous event “Activated-within-90d-default,” so it is not a competing-risks model.

**Cohort and censoring.** The six main counts sum to 48,000. The same table then adds invented ~24,000/~24,000 policy rows. Permanently missing partner feeds are not a flow state. Maturity is defined as `data_extract_time - label_available_at ≥ 90 days`, which does not check whether the label exists by the cutoff. Section 2 censors the 3,600 at day 90; section 3 censors them at extraction; both cannot be right. Censoring at day 90 means the case was observed through day 90 without default. Transfers are called competing events in an activated-only survival analysis they do not enter, while the 40% unlinkable transfers are a missing-follow-up problem. The “intention-to-treat” sensitivity is not ITT: it labels non-activated cases non-default under a counterfactual the brief forbids using for convenience. Assumption labels are swapped. Whether rejects would have defaulted is untestable, not partially testable. Whether the July 2025 policy changed the feature–outcome association is empirically comparable across periods, not unverifiable.

**Prefixes and features.** The prefix is built from events at or after the prediction timestamp (in code, after the last Credit Review). That is the future of the decision point, not a prefix, and it drops the document-request loops that occur before the final review. Fixed-count and elapsed-time windows are described, but the code’s `PREFIX_EVENT_COUNT > 0` branch makes the time window dead. Migration duplicates are never removed. Section 5 treats event-time resource experience and queue length as available; section 6 correctly doubts the experience field. `final_limit` is “after the credit decision” in one section and “only after activation” in the next.

**Leakage table.** Required columns and fields are present, and dropping `collection_status`, extract time, and raw label timing is right. `final_limit` is dropped for the wrong availability reason. `decision_reason` is kept even though it is filled at the same decision point. The table says to drop future events while prefix extraction keeps them. `default_90d` is marked Keep without a rule that it may be used only when `label_available_at` is on or before the as-of date. The code audit emits a row only if `final_limit` survived aggregation, so a clean frame produces an empty audit.

**Validation.** Splitting on case start and refusing a random split is justified. The test window 1 Jan 2026–15 Feb 2026 lies outside the event window, which ends 31 Dec 2025; that interval is only maturation time. Late-2025 validation cases cannot have a matured 90-day outcome plus 7–35 day feed by 15 Feb 2026, and there is no embargo between training labels and the next period. A model fit only on pre-July 2025 data does not “account for” the policy change. AUC and an unspecified Brier score are added beside the survival target without restricting to mature cases or using censoring-aware scoring. Section 8 then claims the study accounts for the policy change.

**Code.** Required `pm4py.format_dataframe` and `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` are never called. The skeleton is about twice the requested length. `tz_localize(None)` fails on both naive and aware timestamps. Terminal activity is taken with `iloc[0]` while the time is the minimum, and rows are not time-sorted before `head`. Maturity ignores `label_available_at` and `default_90d` and requires a flat 125 days, so true mature labels are marked censored. The target function sets `censored_activated` to 0. Non-activated rows stay in the split with missing targets. The survival comments describe a time and an event indicator that this target does not build.

Section 8 is the most usable part: association among activated accounts is separated from a causal or counterfactual claim, and three deployment feedback paths are named. That is not enough to make the estimand, censoring rule, prefix cut, backtest, or skeleton reliable.
