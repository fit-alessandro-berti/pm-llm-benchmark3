5.5

The write-up has the right skeleton (competing risks, open cases kept, escalation as immortal time, transfer assumptions, Aalen–Johansen as primary). It is not internally consistent, and several claims conflict with the supplied table or with the estimands as defined.

**Estimands.** Estimand 1 (CIF of resolution by 72h) is well posed. Estimand 2 is not one estimand: the prose simultaneously describes \(1-\mathrm{CIF}(72)\), a cause-specific hazard that censors resolution, and a binary “failed by 72h” indicator. Those answer different decisions. Under a binary breach indicator, S09 (open, age 92) has a fully observed breach at 72; calling it right-censored at 92 for both estimands contradicts the same paragraph’s “known SLA breach.” S08 (transfer at 68) is a pre-horizon exit, not an elapsed-time breach at 68. S03 (resolve at 76) is an observed late resolution, which counts as a breach only for the binary/clock definition, not for a model that censors resolutions. Open cases still younger than 72 (S15 at 35h, S31 at 23h, S10, S24, S29) are censored for every 72h functional; “remain open” in Estimand 2 does not say that. Withdrawal at 27h is a competing exit inside the window, not the same event as crossing 72h unresolved. Because Estimand 2 is mostly the complement of Estimand 1 once competing exits are coded as non-resolution, the two targets do not separate “never resolved,” “still open at 72,” and “resolved after 72.”

**Encoding.** The status table gives one resolution-oriented code, not estimand-specific indicators. Withdraw and reject have no alternative codings. Transfer assumptions A–C are the right three stories, but C is a different outcome model, and the table’s last column is not a sensitivity for non-transfer rows. Open cases older than 72 stay coded as censored only, so the known-breach estimand is never encoded.

**Bias section.** S15 versus S11 is the right contrast, and dropping S09/S26, S05/S06, and S08 is the right bias list. “Removes the tail” is too strong: completed-case duration still includes S03, S13, S20, and S28. “Transfers … about to breach” does not fit S21 (transfer at 26h).

**Methods.** The KM versus CIF inequality is stated in the right direction, and Aalen–Johansen matches the resolution functional. RMST to \(\tau=72\) is dismissed as “the mean time”; restricted mean time lost to the SLA horizon is directly relevant and is mischaracterized. Landmarking on “escalated by 24h” is recommended as the secondary analysis even though escalation times are not in the extract, which the multi-state paragraph and §9 admit. No variance, interval, or \(n=32\) caveat appears anywhere.

**Features and diagram.** Escalation and final `rework_count` are correctly treated as non-baseline. `AutoCheck`-first is called a safe baseline; relative to the cohort policy it is an early post-treatment mediator, and its delay after `Submit` is unknown. Every terminal row has `last_event_gap_h = 0` and every open row has a positive gap, so the gap perfectly labels censoring; that leakage is missed. The diagram omits the observed `Escalate → Review` edge (S03, S29) and adds unobserved `AutoCheck → Withdraw`. Repeated `Review` with `rework_count = 0` (S03) is not separated from rework.

**Sensitivity.** Scenario 1’s reversal story is directionally useful, but “Cohort B relies heavily on late transfers” is one case (S32); S21 is early. Scenario 2 recodes long gaps as withdrawals at `age − gap` (S11 at 26h, S26 at 32h) and would delete known breaches; it never says that conclusion can reverse, and it is not the same as informative-censoring weights. Scenario 3 claims B may look better because easy P3 volume moved to B. In the table, P3 is 5 versus 5 and P1 is 5 in B versus 3 in A. Withdrawal/rejection coding is never varied.

**Operations.** The non-causal reading of escalation is sound. The 60% alert at “Review at 48h” needs timestamps the file does not have. Reporting the resolution CIF as the SLA metric still leaves the contractual question unsettled: a 27h withdrawal is non-resolution, not a 72h clock breach. Calibration is named, not specified (no predicted-versus-AJ check, no score, no stratum sample sizes).

**Code versus the plan.** The 32-row table matches the prompt, open cases are retained, and the log is built without invented timestamps. The fitted comparison does not implement the prose. `event_cr` lumps every non-resolution terminal as one competing code (acceptable for CIF of resolution only). `event_res_censored` censors withdrawal, rejection, and transfer together, but the print calls it “Transfer=Censored.” `event_sla_fail` marks early withdrawal/rejection/transfer as events, leaves S03 and S09 as non-events, and is never fit — so it matches neither Estimand 2 nor a 72h breach indicator. Priority stratification is absent. `AalenJohansenFitter(calculate_variance=False)` is not a valid constructor argument in current lifelines, and `predict` is not the documented CIF accessor (`cumulative_density_`), so the primary branch raises rather than falling through. The `except` covers only `ImportError`. The fallback is one sentence, not a formula. `pm4py.get_variants` is used, but the comprehension assumes values are traces; versions that return counts throw. `numpy` is unused.

**Limits.** Timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, calendar time, workload, and non-random rollout are the right gaps. Propensity weighting is suggested with no small-sample caveat. Differential follow-up under a single cutoff is only implicit.
