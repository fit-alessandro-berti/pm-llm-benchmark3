2.8

The submission is only a partial skeleton of the required OCEL 2.0 pipeline, and the in-memory model does not preserve the stated business facts.

Modeling assumptions conflate event-to-object roles with object-to-object facts. The declared E2O vocabulary is `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`, and `closes`, while the relation rows also use `contains`, and those same words are reused as O2O qualifiers. The stated O2O direction, “from Order to the related objects,” contradicts both the required relationships and the table that was built: packages point at items, deliveries at packages, and the invoice at an order. Logistics and finance involvement is described too coarsely, and O2O is mislabeled as analytical propagation. The actual safeguard required here is to keep observed E2O links separate from links inferred by walking O2O. The import `from pm4py.ocel import OCEL` is not isolated as a version-specific adjustment; the usual class path is `pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL`.

The event table has 17 unique IDs and the object table has the right 12 objects, but the facts are already wrong. Confirm Delivery D1 is timestamped 10:00 instead of 13:00, and Close Order O200 is timestamped 10:00 instead of 10:30. Those two errors invent timestamp ties the narrative does not have. Timestamps remain strings, so they are not timezone-aware datetimes. M1’s 100 and M2’s 150 never appear as event attributes.

Relations are not a valid E2O table. Add Item rows point at O100 or O200 while declaring type `Item`, so I1, I2, and I3 are not attached to the add events. Reserve Stock links only I1 and uses `packs`. Pack P1 links only P1, omitting I1, I3, O100, and O200. Pack P2 omits I2. Both dispatches omit the delivery. Both payments point at V1 with type `Payment`, so M1 and M2 have no E2O row. I2, I3, M1, and M2 therefore never occur in `relations`. O2O gets the order–item, package–item, and delivery–package links, then drops V1–O200, points the invoice at the payments under a generic `settles` qualifier, and adds a mutual `closes` link between O100 and O200 that is not a business relationship.

The analysis block does not implement the required checks or views. There is no validation of unique IDs, orphans, activity/timestamp agreement, or duplicate relations. `ocel_object_type_activities` is missing. `ocel_flattening` is called once without an object type, and the per-type dictionaries rerun discovery on that same log. `discover_otg` and `discover_etot` are passed the flattened log rather than the OCEL. The commented views target a classic DFG and a classic Petri net, not the OC-DFG and object-centric Petri net. `activity_frequency()` and `object_type_frequency()` are not a reliable OCEL API.

The audit disagrees with the code: Dispatch is said to touch Delivery, Record Payment is said to touch Invoice and Payment, and Reserve Stock and Confirm Delivery are omitted. `closes` is reported as an O2O cardinality. The consequential case is Record Payment; the convergence/divergence that matters is Reserve Stock and Pack P1, where I1 and I3 from O100 and O200 meet, and V1, which bills both orders.

Section 4 stops mid-list. “Trace shape: O100, O200” names objects rather than activity sequences, and Package, Delivery, Invoice, DFG edges, copied events, and the weak Payment view are absent. Sections 5–8 are absent, so there is no OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, object-centric net, or interaction reading for P1, V1, M1, M2, O100, and O200, no alternative E2O policies, no eight failure modes, and no reviewer assertions.
