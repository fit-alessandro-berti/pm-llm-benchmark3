6.4

The review is serious and mostly well structured, but it is not nearly flawless. It misstates the current model, applies its own burden of proof unevenly, mislabels at least one case, and ships a revised POWL whose loop cannot generate the very behavior it is meant to legalize.

The opening description of M0 is semantically wrong. Register is not concurrent with the checks. M0 is Register, then a partial order in which only Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are unordered, then an XOR between the manual-approval sequence and Reject_Supplier, then Notify_Requester, then Close_Case. Calling that whole object a strict partial order also flattens the XOR operator. That is a bad start on a task scored for model semantics.

Section 1 is otherwise the strongest part. Supported facts, real disputes, and unproved points are separated cleanly, and the burden is explicit: preponderance after resolving reasonable ambiguity, with the party claiming an exception required to show authority, time window, every condition, and reliable evidence. The later judgment does not keep that standard. PD-3’s power to derogate from CS-9 is listed as disputed and the steering committee’s override authority as undocumented, yet SUP-702 becomes JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at medium-high confidence without deciding that authority question. The contemporaneous “monitored exception” note can support a temporary pilot, but that reconciliation is never actually made.

The briefs are full and cite the right cases, but they miss the required concessions. Prosecution acknowledges the missing loop and the absent resource rule, then still treats departures from M0 as the default control failure. It does not honestly concede the CRM break, duplicate registration, label change, or scope gaps. Defense is stronger on those defects and correctly handles SUP-702 and SUP-704 as overlay behavior, but it does not acknowledge genuine risk or the limit of informal practice. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are left to a matrix cell. Post-expiry continuation in SUP-705 and SUP-714 is argued as continuity and imminent policy rather than conceded as an authority failure. Dialectical fairness is therefore only partial; the judgment later rejects those defenses, which the defense brief itself should have limited.

The matrix covers every case, including control-flow-fitting SUP-712 and SUP-715, which is the right scope. Several rows do not match the judgment or the record. SUP-707 is judged a model defect, while its reversal cell still treats the current conclusion as a violation that would become a defect only after formal adoption. SUP-704 is medium risk in the matrix and low severity in the judgment. SUP-711 is a high-confidence migration duplicate of an otherwise fitting trace, so DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE is the wrong class: the log is dirty and the business trace is conformant. SUP-713 is called an interpretive, retrospective mapping in section 1 and then COMPLIANT at medium-high confidence. SUP-708 is correctly severe, but the trace also puts Manual_Procurement_Approval after activation, and that second CS-9 breach is dropped. SUP-702 never shows the automated master-data validation PD-3 requires before activation; the revised model treats that validation as its own activity and then does not test this case against it. SUP-709 includes Register_Supplier, which cuts against a simple “M0 has not started” story and is not discussed. The system judgment names SUP-703, SUP-708, and the post-expiry cases, but it drops the rest of the 14 clearly ineligible auto-approvals and the 9 unproved eligibility rows from the operational conclusion.

The classifications that hold up are SUP-701 compliant, SUP-703 and SUP-708 noncompliant and severe, SUP-704 a justified emergency exception, SUP-705 and SUP-714 noncompliant because expiry was not extended in writing, SUP-712 a real separation-of-duties breach outside M0, and SUP-706 indeterminate because a mutable link added two days later does not identify the notification. SUP-710 and SUP-715 are reasonable. Avoiding MIXED as a refuge is right. The final rejection of both “every M0 miss is misconduct” and “normalization extends expired authority” is the correct system result, even though the 14 ineligible pilot cases are underweighted.

The layered architecture is the right design: POWL for flow, overlays for pilots and emergencies, data checks for amount, risk, country, and time, and organizational controls for identity and conflicts. Withdrawal is only a placeholder. The separation-of-duties constraint is garbled in the architecture and overlay table. Rejected-lead and reactivation rules are clearer.

The code has a blocking semantics error. `LOOP(Request_More_Info, Request_More_Info)` denotes do (redo do)*, so it can produce only odd counts. SUP-707 contains two requests and is not in the language. The defensible pattern is a loop with one visible request and a silent redo, optionally skipped, which yields zero or more. The auto and emergency routes are also unsafe and the limitation is not stated: after Sanctions_Check there is no failure exit, so a failed sanctions check is still followed by approval and activation. Those routes also force Quality_Review to finish before Notify_Requester. SUP-702’s notification at +2 hours and next-business-day quality review likely has the opposite order. Post-activation quality is an allowed delay, not a required order. Route choice, caps, deadlines, and identity are correctly left outside POWL, and the collection proposal includes stable notification IDs, rule versions, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, authority, and immutable timestamps.
