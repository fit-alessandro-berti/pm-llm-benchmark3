The recommendation is directionally sound: hybrid, segment-limited deployment rather than national scale or full rollback, with survey-skipping and post-billing activation held back and with an explicit rollout threshold. The memo is decision-oriented, the five metric conflicts are all addressed, alternatives A–E are each given advantages, risks, and conditions, and the evidence table keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions in separate columns.

That score is held down by several non-minor defects. “Halved handovers” is false: 34% to 12% is a drop of about two-thirds. Process-mining generalization (0.56) is misused as evidence of organizational fragility; generalization is a model property, not resilience. Temporal-profile deviations and the DFG edge shifts are effectively ignored, despite being required artifacts. Aggregate deterioration is stated firmly in the memo without the pilot’s heavier apartment/old-building mix, and the incomplete cost estimate is treated as a demonstrated rise to EUR 207. The table sometimes duplicates the same causal claim across interpretation and assumption; the handover row even assumes fewer coordination failures, which cuts against the resilience evidence. Value-of-information ranking is incomplete: several of M2–M8 never say how a result would change the A–E choice, M8 is reduced to branding, and M5 is ranked last even though coordinator absence is a reason not to scale ownership. Section 6 then recommends scaling that ownership for simple houses without a near-term absence control, and its threshold loses the comparison direction that the memo had stated clearly. Segment labels are also treated as operationally ready even though risk attributes are incomplete before week 3.
