6.2

The final diagnosis labels are directionally defensible, and the required seven-part structure, confidence scores, and decision-set approach are present. The write-up is not reliable at the decision level: several predictions, timestamps, and discrimination tests are wrong or incomplete, and those errors change the interpretation of the same episodes used to justify the labels.

**Decision-set errors**
- QD at 11:30 lists FIFO as D07. D06 arrived at 11:10 and D07 at 11:14, so arrival-order FIFO selects D06, the same job the appointment rule selects. The observed idle of D-X from 11:20 to 11:30, while D07 was already eligible, is the actual appointment evidence, and that decision is not reconstructed.
- D08 is given arrival 11:14. The supplied arrival is 11:16. The D-M ranking is unchanged, but the eligible set is misstated.
- QB’s FIFO column treats the suspended job as outside the arrival order, so 09:31 is scored as a FIFO miss. Under arrival order, B01 remains ahead of B03. That assumption is never stated, yet it decides whether 09:31 and 10:14 support or contradict FIFO.
- The clean QB comparison at 10:28 is absent. B04 (priority 3, arrived 09:35) is taken over waiting B06 (priority 2). That single choice cuts against general priority and fits FIFO, independently of the resumption story.
- QC’s table stops before 11:40, where C01 is chosen over C07. SPT, priority, and earliest deadline all select C07; arrival-order FIFO selects C01. The prose calls this starvation or a policy switch and does not record those competing predictions.

**Mechanism and equivalence gaps**
- QC’s secondary rule, “priority-1 override when the deadline is at risk,” does not fit its own timing. C05’s 10:50 deadline was already unreachable at 10:18 and again at 10:32, and C05 was passed over both times. Earliest-deadline order matches priority at 10:54 and diverges at 11:34, where C06 is served and earliest deadline would take C07. Deadline equivalence is named in the identifiability cell and then not tested.
- QD’s meeting explanation covers why D05 did not start at 11:36: a 30-minute job would run through the 12:00 meeting. It does not cover D-F sitting idle until 12:00 while D10, an electrical job estimated at 12 minutes, was waiting and could finish before the meeting. D-F is qualified for electrical work and had already taken D03. The note that D-F was unavailable until 12:20 is about the later start, not this gap. Confidence 75 treats skill routing as cleaner than this idle allows.
- D06 on D-X is described as bypassing a skill-matched D-F. At 11:30, D-F was occupied until 11:36, so D-F was not a free alternative.
- For QA, A03 is both the appointment component and the strongest “contradicting” episode. Under the hybrid actually claimed, A03 is a confirming case. A07 over A08 is cited as FIFO support even though SPT makes the same pick. The discriminating QA cases are 08:32 and 09:05, not 09:32.
- A06’s 09:20 start is given a five-minute appointment tolerance. A05 occupies the server from 09:05 to 09:20, so non-preemptive FIFO already produces that lateness.

**Sections 4–7**
- The QA counterfactual reverses the usefulness of scan delay. A delay of at most two minutes still leaves A02 visible at 08:20, which the text itself grants, and a queue-entry time of 08:20 is outside that bound. The proposed observation cannot separate appointment priority from FIFO.
- The QC counterfactual says slack may be unchanged between 10:32 and 10:54. Slack falls throughout that interval, and the deadline passes at 10:50. Starvation is also the wrong alternative: it selects C01, which is already the SPT prediction, so it cannot explain a choice of C05.
- Section 5 lists six real exceptions and ranks them only in bands. It then gives one collective sentence on impact. It does not say how scan delay, appointment tolerance, the meeting block, the dispatcher reassignment, estimate error, or a mid-morning rule change would each move a specific queue’s label. Realized service on QB and QC matches `est_min` exactly, so estimate noise is a weak explanation for those two queues; the exception is not conditionalized that way.
- The collection plan names the right field types and is only loosely tied to the hardest ambiguities: the QB requeue rule, the QC deadline-versus-priority split, and D-F’s eligibility for D10 before 12:00.
- The synthesis is the strongest section. QB as least identifiable, and blanket priority as the most dangerous inference, follows the cross-queue evidence. QD as the most identifiable queue is the weak call: the skill partition is obvious, while the D-F idle and the appointment-versus-FIFO confound are unresolved. QA’s repeated unique FIFO wins are the cleaner identification.

The labels QA = FIFO plus appointments, QC = SPT, QD = skill routing, and QB = low-confidence preemption hybrid are reasonable summaries. The supporting reconstructions contain prediction errors, one wrong timestamp, a secondary QC rule that misses its own earlier decision epochs, and counterfactuals that do not discriminate the stated alternatives.
