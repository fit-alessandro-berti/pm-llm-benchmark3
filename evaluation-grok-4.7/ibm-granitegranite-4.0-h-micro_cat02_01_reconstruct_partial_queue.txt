The write-up keeps the seven required headings and a few correct primitives (two single-task resources, ±2.5 min rounding, OPEN versus completed service, three named imputation scenarios). Almost every quantitative and definitional claim is wrong, incomplete, or internally inconsistent, and several required analyses are only named.

Assumptions are not labeled A1, A2, …. MISSING and OPEN are treated as ordinary rounded timestamps in [t−2.5, t+2.5]. Blank, MISSING, and OPEN are not given distinct semantics. Maintenance is miscalled censoring. OPEN “final service” is defined as time until the next resource, which is not what right censoring means. No simultaneous-event convention is stated. Imputation is described as a single previous-case replacement, which the task forbids as a silent point fill.

Formulas do not separate S1 wait (start − external arrival), S2 wait (S2 start − S1 end), active service, station sojourn, and case sojourn. The only wait identity is S2 start − S1 end; the only service identity is an S1 duration; sojourn is S2 end − S1 start, which drops pre-S1 wait and is undefined for censored cases. Queue length is defined as start ≤ t < end, which is occupancy in service, not the waiting line. WIP is then equated with that quantity. Arrival rate is called throughput. Little’s Law is written as L = W, with the rate symbol empty, and it is never applied on any interval. Lower, central, and upper bounds are not operationalized.

The case table is not a reconstruction. P01’s S2 wait is 5 minutes (08:25 → 08:30), not [0, 0]. P02’s S1 service is 25 minutes and its S2 service is 40 minutes, not the copied [20, 25] and [30, 35]. The note that nothing is missing contradicts the ranges. The ellipsis drops P05, P07, P11, P15, P16, P17, and P21, all of which were required. The one special row, P22, is false: P22 arrives at 12:10 and is still waiting for S1 at 14:00 (about 110 minutes, censored), with no S1 or S2 service. The row gives S1 service [15, 20], zero S2 time, flag WAITING_FOR_S2, and “waiting for S2 at 13:45” (that clock time is P20’s S1 completion).

Station metrics are a repeated template, not counts from the log. External arrivals are about 7, 6, 4, 4, 1, 0 across 08:00–14:00, not 5 in every hour through noon. Completions are not 5 per hour at either station. S1 services cluster near 25–30 minutes; observed S2 services are mostly 40–50. Identical wait, service, WIP [1, 2], and utilization [0.5, 0.67] for both stations are incompatible with the log. The window after 12:00, where S2’s queue reaches four waiting cases plus two open services (WIP 8 at 14:00), is omitted. The 10:55–11:10 row sets arrivals, completions, WIP, and utilization to zero at both stations. Only C2 is down; I1, I2, and C1 are busy (P11 and P12 in S1, P09 in S2). Data-coverage is absent. Rows are hours, not one row per station as specified.

The bottleneck claim that the stations match and that the outage does not matter does not follow from the evidence. S2’s longer service at the same nominal capacity, the post-outage delay of P08 to 11:15, and the afternoon S2 queue are the relevant observations. Evidence is not separated from hypotheses. No resource-feasibility bounds are built for the missing starts (I1 gap for P07 after P05, C1 gap for P05 between P03 and P07, C2 gap for P11 after P08 and the outage, P15 before P17).

Sensitivity analysis names optimistic, central, and pessimistic scenarios and then asserts stability. No imputed intervals, no recomputed waits, busy time, WIP, or rates, and no statement of what would change. That stability claim is also inconsistent with section 1’s single-point rule and with the code’s end − 2.5 min fill.

The Python block does not meet the spec. The table is `[...]`. Imports (`pm4py.util` / `pm4py.statistics` as `pm4py`) are invalid. `pd.notna` does not separate MISSING, OPEN, and blank, and subtracting a timedelta from those fields will not parse. The event rows omit `case:concept:name`, duplicate each activity incorrectly, and call a nonexistent `pm4py.df_from_events`. There is no `format_dataframe`, no `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, no `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` with a case-level label, no `convert_log_to_time_intervals` or a comment justifying a pandas substitute, no uncertainty calculations, and no printed result tables. The calculations are explicitly omitted.

What remains is a heading skeleton plus a few correct terms, attached to fabricated metrics and a non-runnable script. It would mislead a reader about waits, capacity during maintenance, and which station is congested.
