The stage table is arithmetically reliable: medians, means, conditional support, and the flag, complexity, load, and escalation cuts match the timestamps, and absent stages are kept as missing rather than zero. The required estimands, pathway split, interference caveats, and pilot structure are all present, and natural direct/indirect effects are correctly set aside.

The resource mechanism is not consistent with the same timestamps. M2 service intervals overlap (R01 with R09, R03 with both, R10 with R03, R13 with R18 and R20), so M2 is not a single exclusive server, yet the resource pathway still treats M2 as that bottleneck. R01, R02, and R03 also wait 300–540 minutes after triage while their eventual reviewer has no earlier observed case, so shared WIP does not explain the longest morning waits. That evidence is only gestured at as a backlog or batch-release alternative.

P2’s spillover is overstated. Medium flagged reviews sit on M1/M3; the long cycles and senior queue are high-complexity M2 cases that the pilot explicitly keeps on mandatory review. Copying unflagged manual-wait distributions is also a different intervention from adding protected capacity. Smaller defects are the unsupported “clinical” checklist, a fabricated escalation resource id, and an ill-specified direct flag-to-escalation edge in a process where escalation never occurs without review.
