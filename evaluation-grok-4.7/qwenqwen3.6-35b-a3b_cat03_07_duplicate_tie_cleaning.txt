5.5

The write-up follows the required nine-part outline and gets the main conservative calls right: merge e03/e04 and e11/e16, keep e05, e06/e07, e10, e13, e14, and e15, relabel the e08/e09 harmonization collision, keep original timestamps as a stated rule, and treat ingestion time as a last resort. That is not enough to carry the answer. Several load-bearing claims are factually wrong, the ordering policy is not applied to the evidence in the table, and the PM4Py section misses a required call and ships a broken merge.

The aggressive scenario is the clearest failure. e10’s activity is `Handover to Carrier`, not `Dispatch Package`, so an activity-plus-timestamp merge cannot fold e08, e09, and e10 into one dispatch event. The stated retained count of 13 is already wrong under the groups the answer itself lists: three events becoming one is a reduction of two, so 17−1−1−2−1 = 12. Under the stated rule (`activity`, `timestamp`, `have_common_object=True`) the groups are worse: e03/e04/e05 share Pick Item, 10:00, and O1/P1; e06/e07 share Pack Package and O1/P1; e08/e09 share Dispatch Package; e11/e16 share Issue Invoice; e13/e14/e15 share Capture Payment, 12:00, and O1/V1. That retains 10 events and deletes e05, which the task forbids. Section 7 then says merging e05 is “unlikely with distinct objects,” which contradicts the section 5 point that sharing O1 and V1 is enough to collapse M1 and M2. The e13/e14 explanation itself is right; it is not carried through to the other shared-object traps.

Ordering is similarly over-claimed. `source_seq` is meaningful inside one source and is never used: SCANNER 551 before 552 orders the I1 scan before the I2 pick, CARRIER 1 before 2 confirms e09 before e10, PSP 400/401/402 orders e12 before e13 before e14, and WMS 100 before 110 supports pick before pack. Instead, e03 is placed before e05 by eid, and e08 before e09 is called domain-confirmed when the only given logistics fact is that handover follows carrier acceptance. Fact 7 orders e12 before e13 for M1 only; placing e14 after e12 is extra. The 10:00 and 10:15 blocks are then emitted as one total order. Section 3 correctly warns that synthetic microseconds invent causality, and the conservative code then calls `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` anyway, with sort keys 12 and 12.1 for the two captures.

The implementation does not meet the spec. `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` never appears. The candidate-group pre-check is a comment. `merge_verified_pair` drops `e2o` rows for the losing eid and only then tries to relink them, so those relations are gone. Provenance keeps the survivor’s source and stores keys, not both source names; relabeling e08/e09 overwrites `activity` without first copying `original_activity` onto every event. Referential integrity is an index membership assert, not an object-table check. `ocel_raw` is deep-copied, and the other three PM4Py calls are at least present.

Sensitivity does not compare the four logs on the required measures. Evidence-based merge almost disappears. OTG relations, lifecycle, and performance are not analyzed. “E2O stable” is wrong once duplicate e03/e04 and e11/e16 links are removed (raw relations sum to 44; those two merges remove 5). Merging two captures into one event is also described as a Capture self-loop. The six named patterns stay qualitative, and several depend on the false e10 merge.

The decision tree, provenance columns, and eight required failure modes are present and mostly usable. e06/e07 is correctly left ambiguous at medium confidence, but only one scenario is given, and quarantine is never applied. The audit schema can explain a merge; it does not store full raw rows, so the reversibility steps cannot actually rebuild the source log.
