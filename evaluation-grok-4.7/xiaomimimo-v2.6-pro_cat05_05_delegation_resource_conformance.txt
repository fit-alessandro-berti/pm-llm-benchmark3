The final report is a strong event-time analysis: alias normalization, D-QM-01’s bitemporal evidence, Eva’s role cutover, the void D-IC-04 subdelegation, the cooling-system scope failure, and the CHG-503 versus CHG-509 session split are all handled correctly, and collusion is not inferred from repeated pairing. It is not nearly flawless.

The required checker misimplements the business-day rule it is meant to test. In prose, a Tuesday 11 August trigger has a standard deadline of 13 August, an inclusive deadline of 12 August, and a “generous” deadline of 14 August. The `deadline()` function returns 12 August for both the excluded and included conventions and 13 August for the generous one. CHG-505 would therefore be printed late under every convention, contradicting the written claim that the generous reading makes 14 August 16:00 timely. The binary result for these two cases happens to survive, but the deadlines and labels do not.

The case table also misses that Ben’s certified sick leave runs through 14 August 23:59, so the CHG-505 retrospective review at 16:00 is itself an absence-conflict event, not only late and out of scope. The code then resolves `u_bk` to Ben and would mark the CHG-506 approval as a valid substitution, with no sick-leave or denial flag, despite the narrative classifying it as an identity failure. D-IC-03 and D-QM-02 are given invented instrument timestamps. Clara is called the drafter on 10/10 cases; CHG-508 was drafted by Ben. CHG-505’s primary label, valid substitution, is softer than RR4’s own “justified only when” retrospective condition, though the secondary unauthorized label limits the damage.
