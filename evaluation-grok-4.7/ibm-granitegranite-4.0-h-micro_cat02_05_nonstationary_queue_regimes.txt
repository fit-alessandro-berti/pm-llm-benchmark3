The write-up names the right topics and then fills them with dates, units, and quantities that the table does not support. That fails the benchmark’s core tests: use of the supplied evidence, internal consistency, and queueing definitions.

**Format.** The required top-level heading for the alternative segmentation is missing; that discussion sits inside section 2. Pooled comparisons are then written twice, as both “# 3” and “# 4”. The Python block is cut off inside the arrivals list and does not run.

**Regimes do not match the series.** “Normal weekday” for 2026-04-06 through 2026-04-12 includes Saturday and Sunday. The outage window 2026-04-13 through 2026-04-18 mixes two ordinary weekdays, the outage, the recovery spike, and a Saturday. Complex mix is dated from 2026-04-19, but `complex_pct` is still 23 that Sunday and jumps on 2026-04-20 (41 and above). That same regime swallows the staffing change that begins on 2026-04-29. “Post-intervention normal” is placed on 2026-05-03 to 2026-05-04: the series ends on Sunday 2026-05-03, and that day still has median service 34, p90 78, and `complex_pct` 39, against a week-1 service median near 24–26. Every regime is given the same arrival and completion summary, “median 100, p90 105,” including weekends whose arrivals are about 36–53.

The alternative segmentation inverts the mix shift, calling 2026-04-06 through 2026-04-14 “high complexity” while `complex_pct` is about 18–24, and it leaves 2026-04-22 onward unassigned.

**Quantities are not estimates from the table.** Pooled arrival intensity is given as 100.3 cases per hour. Over 07:00–19:00 the 28-day totals are 2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions, about 90 and 86 per day, or roughly 7–8 per open hour. Segmented arrival and completion intensities are set equal in every regime, which erases the actual imbalance: weekdays after the mix change complete well below arrivals, 2026-04-15 completes 58 against 105 arrivals, and 2026-04-16 completes 129 against 112. Outage “service time” of 180 minutes is `outage_min`, not handling time; that day’s median service is 31. Staffing of 7.0 is attached to the outage day, whose `staff_fte` is 6.0; 7.0 is the next day. Mean WIP is stated as 22.5; the 28 daily snapshot means average about 30. Capacity pressure is reported as the FTE number itself.

**Little’s law is misstated and the flow time is impossible.** The formula is written \(L = W\). The claimed sojourn of 0.33 hours (20 minutes) is below the active service median, so it cannot be a system flow time for a WIP that includes cases in service. The same 0.33 hours is reused for the early period and the invented post-intervention window. The outage/recovery row is marked “approximate only” without a throughput proxy, a 12-hour versus calendar-hour window, or a boundary argument. Recovery throughput is backlog drain after a three-hour stop, with WIP 52 and slower service, so a steady-state application there is not defensible. Arrivals are also described as dropping during the outage; the hourly vector keeps arrivals flowing while completions are 0, 0, 0 from the late-morning bins.

**Dispersion figures do not come from the vectors.** For 2026-04-07 hourly arrivals (sum 101, mean about 8.4), the index of dispersion is about 3 and the coefficient of variation about 0.6, not a Fano factor of 1.12 and a CV of 0.25. For 2026-04-21, spikes of 34 and 24 imply a Fano factor near 10, not 1.56. The CV is then read as variability of service time. Lag-1 of 0.42 is given with no series, sample size, or method. Cross-regime movement in `mean_wip` is called overdispersion; that is a change of level, not variance relative to the mean of a count. The 2026-04-21 vector also fails to reproduce `peak_08_10_pct` of 47, and 2026-04-15’s peak share of 32 is ordinary, not a burst. Neither check is made. Weekday/weekend composition and a Simpson-style reversal (high completion days that are drain events, not higher capacity) are not quantified.

**Operations follow the bad segmentation.** Recommendations include lower staffing during the outage and a post-recovery arrival surge. The outage is lost operating time at FTE 6.0; the following spike is completions, under a temporary move to 7.0 FTE, while the mix stays hard. The pooled-versus-segmented policy warning stays generic and conflicts with that reduced-outage-staffing advice.

**Code requirement.** The single block does not finish the daily table, does not build the hourly vectors, and does not compute regime summaries, dispersion, serial dependence, a change-point heuristic, windowed \(L/\lambda\), or the requested PM4Py comments. What is printed in the prose is therefore not reproducible from the code.

A few cautions are echoed correctly: open hours, overnight carry with no closed-hours service, completions not tied to same-day arrivals, an approximate 11:00–14:00 outage, an intervention whose clock time is unknown, and the fact that four illustrative days cannot identify a stochastic model. Those points do not offset invented boundaries, impossible flow time, and non-running code.
