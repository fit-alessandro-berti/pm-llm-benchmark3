6.5

The required structure is present, and the three central traps are handled correctly: I-03 is ineligible once Diagnosis Complete has occurred, I-05 is suppressed as a repeated alert with no new evidence, and I-07 is re-opened because of the new log but still rejected on value and horizon. Spillover is priced, segment bias is named, and the scenario arithmetic that is shown is mostly reproducible. The policy is nevertheless not internally consistent enough to deploy as written.

The calibration adjustment does not actually govern the decision. Section 1 defines `p_cal` and a score with expected false-alarm cost `(1 − p_cal) × B`, but the Section 2 scores match `δ_mid × V − 450 − B − 1800` exactly (I-04 7,150, I-08 2,750, I-09 1,750, I-02 900, I-06 825, I-07 −1,000, I-10 −310). Under that formula, `p_cal` never enters. SME uplift and Strategic Enterprise haircut therefore do not change eligibility, rank, or the selected set. Section 3 then switches back to `(1 − p_cal) × B`, cutting false-alarm cost from €7,800 to about €4,733 and making the same portfolio look roughly €3,000 better than the rule that selected it.

The slot price is also mis-derived. Six cases × a 0.01–0.03 probability × €20,000 is €1,200–€3,600 per slot, so the midpoint is €2,400, not the stated €1,800. That €1,800 figure is what makes the marginal scores positive. The same portfolio then violates the answer’s own fairness cap: three additive slots at the base 0.02 increment add about 0.06 to each untreated case, above the 0.05 daily limit in Section 5. The safety-net slot and the “value bands rather than raw value” rule are likewise not the rule used in Sections 1–2; today’s reserve is already committed to a possible regulatory case.

The allocation is myopic in a way that matters. Only two specialists can deliver a 45-minute swarm, and the swarm dies after Diagnosis Complete. I-08 is in Triage with 8 hours left, a €5,000 false-alarm burden, and a lower-bound gross benefit of 0.05 × €100,000 = €5,000. After €450, the false-alarm term, and a slot externality, that lower bound is negative. I-02 is already in Diagnosis Started with 3 hours left and a positive midpoint score, but it is parked until 13:30, while the regulatory window runs to 15:00. The robust marginal action is to swarm I-02 now and leave I-08 as the release candidate. The answer does the reverse because it sorts a static midpoint score. It also does not sequence I-09 ahead of the long-horizon Strategic cases.

Section 3 does not close that loop. Adverse reductions are 0.12, 0.02, and 0.16, which are not the lower bounds 0.10, 0.05, and 0.18, and the 15–30 minute adverse delay is never converted into harm beyond the ordinary 0.03 probability. The resulting net of about −€4,500 is interpreted as “stop further releases,” without dropping I-08, the case that creates the downside. Non-monetary duty is only an escalation footnote; I-01’s public-sector exposure never enters the score. The monitoring plan is concrete, but the waitlist comparison is confounded by the same score used to allocate slots, and the 0.05 spillover tripwire would already be breached by the recommended base case.
