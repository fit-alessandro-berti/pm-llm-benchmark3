The pilots measure logged versus true concurrency, reservation idle time, four-eyes breaches, and non-target C1 delay, with stopping rules tied to those failure modes.

Concrete defects.

- K-104’s base is internally inconsistent. Decision at Wed 10:30–12:30 versus a factual notify at Wed 18:40 is about 6–8 hours, not the stated 6–12. The table’s 4–22 hour saving also drops the derivation’s same-or-later worst case, and “finishes with you or later” is not a readable outcome.

- Assumption 5 overclaims. A clean D1 check on a file that was routed to D1 is not evidence that D2 has a higher clarification rate. That rate is a stated risk, not an observed skill gap.
- The symbol `⊀̸` is undefined, and “may overlap waiting for originals, but not DocumentCheck” can be read as forbidding identity–document overlap even after originals exist. Rule 3 does not clearly impose that ban.
- The weekly rollout asks for 100 similar cases. The compliance arithmetic uses 20 preliminary screens, and the K-337 paragraph never shows what 100 Friday C1/M1 arrivals would do to a single reviewer. “More than two reviewer-days” for 40 hours is also an oddly low description.
- K-411 is ranked “High” robustness while the same answer says the lock workaround may be impossible without corrupting the record. That hard failure is missing from the ranking’s risk statement.

These are calibration and consistency failures, not a missed control-flow model. The required insights are present and mostly well argued, which keeps the score well above a merely adequate write-up, but the self-contradictory ranges are enough to bar a high mark.
