The write-up has the required seven sections, a complete trade-off table, and a few real peak queues (pre-analytical 9.5h, assay 18h, medical validation 22h, specialist review 36h). Those strengths do not make the redesign reliable. Several causal claims run in the wrong direction, key numbers are misread or invented, and two always-on actions conflict with the capacity evidence.

**Metric and evidence errors.** Performance-edge medians are treated as station queues. Test preparation → batch wait is 6h/14h, so “batch wait of 14h” and “6h → 3h” are the wrong edge; batch wait → assay run is 9h/22h. Technical validation → medical validation at 24h is likewise an edge, while the medical-validation queue is 22h. Courier intake at 7.5h is assigned to Receive sample. Recollection “760 → 300” uses an 8-week count as if it were a rate. Weekly recollection is about 95 in the normal period and about 260 in the peak period, because 760/18,120 (4.2%) becomes 780/9,730 (8.0%). Accession “62% → 40%” uses normal utilization and ignores the peak of 91% with a 14h queue. The +20/+30/+40% speed and −1/+2/+3% rework figures are not derivable from the logs.

**Bottleneck logic.** An M2 capacity loss reduces assay output, so it does not create a technical-validation spike. A hold after assay does not move the bottleneck upstream into assay or create repeats. The specialist scenario names a medical-validator absence, then starts the chain at Hold for specialist review, which is a different pool (already 36h at peak, and one specialist is out every second Friday). Winter-spike pre-analytical failure is attributed to complex panels; the variant evidence ties complex panels to medical validation (3.1×) and specialist hold (2.4×), not to recollection. Urgent bypass falling from 74% to 58% at peak is unused, as is the TV → medical-validation share rising from about 25% to about 42% and the repeat-assay share rising from about 6.7% to about 14.8%.

**Policies that fight the data.** I4 is always on even though technical validation is the only pool over 100% (101%, 16h). Moving two validators onto accession correction is the shortage the conflict note predicts; the failure mode only mentions quality, not lost validation capacity. I2 is always on and claimed to shorten batch wait. Larger batches lengthen fill time, and the peak already shows batching at 12.7% of preparation exits versus 4.4%, with a 22h batch-wait edge. The package says I2 is safe only if paired with I1, but I1 is winter-only. The core bundle’s “next bottleneck: none” misses assay at 98% after earlier triage, technical validation after I4, and pre-analytical check at 96% after smoother arrivals. The M2-outage rule sends all routine work to M2, the failed machine with the higher repeat rate, while the trigger “utilization >95% for 2h” is already true across the peak (98%). Section 6 then says the opposite: move routine work onto M1 if M2 degrades. I1 is scored as speeding complex panels by 20%. Urgent-first dispatch is the rule that starves both routine and complex-panel work. I10 is triggered at a 24h specialist queue in one section and a 36h queue in another, and the residual-risk note treats it as an urgent-SLA threat even though the policy outsources complex-panel overflow only. Normal urgent p90 is already 19h against an 18h penalty (33h at peak). That breach never enters the design.

**Dashboard.** Several bands sit inside normal performance, so they would alarm in the stable regime: urgent p90 warning at 18h versus a normal p90 of 19h, repeat-assay warning at 5% versus about 6.7% normally, accession warning at 4h versus a 3h normal queue. Technical-validation queue, medical-validation queue, and urgent/complex mix are absent, despite mix shift being the feature that makes peak demand different from volume. Case-mix, recollection rate, and the share of cases with a queue over 12h (22% → 54%) would be the leading signals; urgent p90 and repeat rate are lagging and are not baselined.

**Scenario design.** The four required disruption families appear, but each scenario stacks an unrelated mix shift on the disruption, so migration cannot be attributed. Courier stress is in both the winter spike and the courier collapse. The courier policy pauses registration and “prioritizes night-shift samples” in a scenario defined by no intake after 16:00. I6’s stated failure mode pushes the queue back to Receive sample; the downstream load is preparation and assay. Residual risk correctly flags I1 fragmentation and I3 starvation, then mitigates I1 by pausing urgent priority when batch wait exceeds 12h, which is when urgent cases most need the bypass.
