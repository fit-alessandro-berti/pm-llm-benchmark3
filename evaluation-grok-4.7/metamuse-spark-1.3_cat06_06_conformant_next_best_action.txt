The ranking direction is sound: a conformant second-officer plan is first, P-901 is protected unless a governed fallback says otherwise, and A and B stay last because a better deadline forecast cannot cure a missing control. The score is held down by false or unreadable control statements, inconsistent timing arithmetic, one misapplied mandatory rule, and several unsupported numbers.

The comparison symbols that the reasoning depends on are missing, and several sentences are false or reversible as written. “Initial 78 and current 72 both 70” is not true as printed. “7270,” “S4 R17,” “S2R17,” “R17second officer,” and “SAR authorization” hide the thresholds and the four-eyes / SAR rules. “S4-early => 1>DS4” and “Ranking 12” contradict the table, where rank 1 is the S4 plan. The clock math does not hold together. C is totaled as 40–90 minutes and, in the same row, said to miss at the upper end; 75+15 equals 90.

Rule 6 is misstated on the evidence branch. Second review is required while the score is at least 70 or the fuzzy match remains unresolved. The E branch “resolved as false match + score <70 -> still needs Second Review” keeps the requirement after both triggers are stipulated to be gone. Action D’s own caveat is that rescreening does not substitute while current conditions remain; it does not make the requirement permanent after those conditions clear. The quantitative comparisons are looser than the prose admits.
