5.1

The final write-up covers every required section, uses real identifiers, and reaches a defensible Position C. It is not reliable enough on the log itself or on the flattening concepts the matrix is built to test.

Convergence is only partly right (shared events replicated across cases). Divergence is redefined as “short lifecycles,” so that column never captures false directly-follows edges. The clearest miss is Order: O1’s I11/P1/D1 path and I12/P2/D2 path, and O3’s P3 versus P4 paths, are forced into one sequence. Delivery and Payment are then scored “severe/extreme” divergence for truncation, which the brief treats as a separate defect.

Event membership is wrong in several places that the ratings depend on. I11 is e05, e11, e15, e23, not three events ending at e23. I41 is e10, e14, e17, e27, not Add–Pack–Confirm. The Order “preserved” cell drops e24 for O1 even though convergence later copies e24 into O1. O4 has eight directly related events, not seven. Order convergence omits shared e14, e17, e19, e22, e25, and e27, and “at least 5 (e16×3, e24×3, e13×2)” does not add up.

Other conclusions do not match the tables. Invoice traces have no Close Order event, yet the invoice DFG is given an Issue → payment → Close shape. e22 and e25 are both activity Record Payment; partial versus complete is an object–object qualifier, so an activity DFG hides that distinction instead of showing two differently named payment steps. Counting e16 in O1, O2, and O3 does not inflate the percentage of those orders dispatched; O2’s only dispatch and confirm are e16 and e24. Calling Dispatch → Issue Invoice a spurious edge is wrong for O2 and O1, where both events are directly related and time-ordered. The code comment ties e16 to D3; it is D2. O2 and O3 are not equivalent executions: O3 also has the second item, P4, and D3. The sensitivity check that drops shared events would strip O2 of dispatch and delivery confirmation and strip O3 of both of its dispatches, which is not acknowledged.

The mandated themes (P3, P4, D2, V2, M2/M3, singleton payment/delivery) and the lead-object argument are substantively on track. Those do not offset the systematic divergence error or the incorrect event sets.
