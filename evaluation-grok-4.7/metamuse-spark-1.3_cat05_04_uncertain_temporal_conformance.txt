6.5

The interval arithmetic, offset direction, timezone conversions, and DST-fold pruning are mostly right, and several borderline cases are handled with real care. The write-up is still not reliable as a decision record: one duration contradicts the author’s own interval, the sensitivity matrix disagrees with the prose, weak-evidence labels violate the author’s own classification rules, and the required code never actually produces the classifications it claims to implement.

The strongest work is the normalization. HUB as recorded ±1.5 s, OPS-BER floor-minute plus [−90, +120] s after CEST conversion, SENSOR nearest-10 s before ±5 s offset, VENDOR-NY EDT floor-minute plus ±180 s, and the weak T-409 ±12 min substitution are all constructed in the right order. T-405 is the best piece: the eight fold combinations are reduced to the only ingestion-feasible triple (alert on the first 02:50 occurrence, investigation and containment on the second fold), and the surviving durations [720, 1080] s and [2040, 2400] s follow from those intervals. The 3 s and 1.5 s boundary cases (T-404 S2, T-408 start→containment at zeta 2, T-403 alert→start at zeta 3) are real and correctly spotted. Entry delay is treated as an explicit occurrence-time assumption and is correctly identified as flipping T-406 S2. One-sided SLA limits are not confused with two-sided profile bands.

The duration table is not internally consistent. Section 2 places T-406 investigation, with entry delay, at [12:03:30Z, 12:19:30Z] and containment at [12:51:58.5Z, 12:52:01.5Z]. That pair yields [1948.5, 2911.5] s. Section 3 and Section 4 use [1948.5, 2971.5] s, and the no-delay upper bound is likewise 2671.5 s instead of 2611.5 s. S1 for the same case uses 12:03:30 correctly, so this is not an alternate model; the S2 bound is 60 s too high. Classifications happen not to flip, but the reported feasible interval is wrong.

Section 4 then misstates the consequences. T-401 S1’s slack under a +2 min tolerance is 28.5 s (1020 − 991.5), not 71.5 s. The matrix cells for T-403 S1 and T-406 S1 at +5 min say possible violation, while the adjacent prose correctly changes both to certain pass because 1141.5 < 1200 and 1171.5 < 1200. A matrix that contradicts its own correction is not usable.

The indeterminate labels are not applied according to the rules defined in Section 1. Negative alert→start time satisfies “no later than 15 minutes”; it is not untestable. Under the latency bound the author actually uses, T-407 S1 is [−183, 1023] s, which straddles 900 s, so it is a possible violation. Imposing receipt ≤ start only changes that to [0, 1023] s and does not create testability. T-407 containment-after-alert is [2097, 3303] s, wholly inside the zeta-2 band [1260, 4140], so the author’s own rule says certain pass; the table says indeterminate. T-409 is graded on two standards at once: S2 is certain pass on the weak ±12 min interval, while the temporal pair on that same interval is indeterminate. The asterisk and the “if trusted” asides acknowledge the problem without resolving it.

The code does not close these gaps. It builds intervals and has a classifier, but it only runs that classifier on one T-408 example. The T-405 prune result is printed and then discarded in favor of a UTC-hour filter. Raw events stay a list of tuples rather than a dataframe. The midpoint comparison is a hardcoded print, not a comparison against PM4Py output. `zeta=` is passed as a keyword; current PM4Py takes zeta inside `parameters`, so the call falls into the handler. The handler is allowed, but then nothing computes zeta 1, 2, and 3 or reconciles those diagnostics with the interval results.

Sections 6 and 7 are substantively aligned with the casework, including the right split among synchronization, missing receipt/start, duration evidence, fold logging, and entry-delay semantics. They cannot carry the numerical and classification errors above.
