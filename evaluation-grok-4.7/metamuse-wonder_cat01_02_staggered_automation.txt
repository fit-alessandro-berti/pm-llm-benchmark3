7.6

The answer is structurally complete and causally careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Quantitative mistakes and a gap between the stated design and the executed analysis are large enough to block a high score.

The framing is strong. Section 1 separates availability, intensity, targeted adoption, concurrent shocks, and aggregate measurement without a causal claim. The estimands distinguish ITT, actual fast-path use, medians versus tails, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamics, and fraud-desk interference, and they correctly treat transport to Delta/Epsilon as an extra assumption. The preferred staggered group-time design, the rejection of naïve two-way fixed effects under heterogeneous timing, and the small-donor and short-baseline critiques are all appropriate. Mechanisms, threats, and the claim ladder keep median recovery, tail risk, rework, escalation, and CSAT separate. `expand with an embedded evaluation`, with Delta phased in and Epsilon held out, matches the learning problem.

The arithmetic is not reliable enough. Gamma’s January–March escalation baseline is given as 8.2; it is 7.87. Delta/Epsilon January–March to July–August escalation changes are given as +1.5/+0.8; they are about +1.83/+2.13. That understates control escalation movement and weakens the escalation comparison. The Gamma p90 control change is given as −7.1 hours and the DiD as −8.5; the control average is about −7.95 and the DiD about −7.6. That incorrect −8.5 is repeated in the effect table. Epsilon’s May–June to July–August rework change is called flat despite a small increase. The stated 10% and 5% arrival increases are campaign facts; Gamma’s post-July series does not show a clean 5% step.

The quantitative section also does not carry out the comparison the identification section recommends. Alpha/Beta are judged mainly on July–August, after Gamma is already treated, rather than on an April–June contrast against not-yet-treated Gamma plus the never-treated branches. The code builds relative-time indicators but never uses them, and Gamma’s event times −6 through −4 fall into the zero-dummy reference. The only fitted panel model is the naïve branch-and-month fixed-effects regression the text itself treats as an invalid headline, with post-treatment complexity and load controls and with five clusters against roughly thirteen parameters. Guardrail and scale-up cutoffs are not tied to the observed month-to-month volatility.
