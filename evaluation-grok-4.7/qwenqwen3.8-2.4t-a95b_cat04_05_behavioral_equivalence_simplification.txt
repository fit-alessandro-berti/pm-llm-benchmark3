The formal comparison is strong and, on the central claims, correct. Equivalence is conditioned on explicit observation boundaries; loop scope, the single global check versus a per-iteration check, optional approval, and hidden `tau` are handled accurately; the P1/P2/P3 verdicts match those semantics; and the five visible traces are valid witnesses in both directions, including multi-analysis cases. The P2 abstraction \((\#A,\mathbf{1}_C,\mathbf{1}_R)\) is the right reading of that view, and the simplified tree really does preserve it while being structurally smaller (7 nodes, depth 3 versus 12 nodes and depth 5/6). Losses of per-iteration checks, approvals, and interleaving are stated rather than papered over, and bounded search is correctly distinguished from a proof.

The main defect is in the bounded explorer. Standard PM4Py silence is `label is None`, while `name` is a non-null id such as `tau_0`. The code classifies silence from `name` and emits `name` as the visible label, so operator and `tau` transitions can be counted as visible events. That can corrupt lengths, the Open/Close filter, and the printed symmetric difference even though the marking search itself is otherwise well structured. The stochastic snippet also never passes the 50/50 choice and loop probabilities it declares, and Petri-net simplicity is only deferred to an unrun call. The P1 discussion slightly hedges `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` after already making approval placement part of the P1 observation.
