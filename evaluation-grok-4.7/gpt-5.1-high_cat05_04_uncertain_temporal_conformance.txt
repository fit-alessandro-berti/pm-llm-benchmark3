6.4

The normalization framework is mostly right, and the baseline SLA calls are largely defensible, but the write-up is not reliable enough to score higher. Several required results are missing, internally inconsistent, or arithmetically softened exactly where the boundary is the result.

Clock direction, resolution-before-offset, and the June zone conversions are handled correctly. HUB is about ±1.5 s (reported as ±2 s), OPS-BER `10:14` CEST is UTC `[08:12:00, 08:16:30]`, SENSOR `08:51:10` is `[08:51:00, 08:51:20]`, and VENDOR-NY `04:00` EDT is `[07:57:00, 08:04:00]`. The T-405 fold reduction is also right: ingestion order leaves only alert in CEST and the later two events in CET. T-404’s start envelope and the mobile entry-delay shift to `[12:03:30Z, 12:19:30Z]` match the stated assumptions. Against a 0-minute SLA, the certain results hold: T-402 S1 is a certain violation (fastest delay is about 15 min 58 s), and T-408 S2 and S3 are certain violations (about 55 min and 38.4 h).

Section 3 then overclaims. “Omitted pairs are CERTAIN_PASS” is false. T-409 alert→start is about `[0, 17]` min, so at ζ = 2 it is a possible violation of `[4, 16]` min, not a pass. Every missing close→profile pair is untestable and should be indeterminate, the same treatment S3 already receives. The profile table also drops the required severity and confidence columns and does not give one row per testable pair.

The sensitivity section misses changes it claims to cover. T-409 S1 becomes a certain pass only at +5 min. T-408 alert→containment is a certain profile violation at ζ = 1 (about 65 min versus a 57 min upper bound) and a certain pass at ζ = 2; the text instead says only T-402 and T-403 have ζ = 1 alert→containment effects. “Border effect” also flattens T-402’s ζ = 1 certain violation. Duration endpoints are rounded inward: T-403’s own timestamps give about `[11 min 58 s, 19 min 2 s]`, not `[12.0, 19.0]`. That 19.0 min figure sits on the ζ = 3 upper bound of 1140 s, while the unrounded maximum is just above it.

T-407’s prose says the receipt window was intersected with the investigation-start interval. That intersection is `[14:16:58Z, 14:17:02Z]`, not the stated `[13:59:58Z, 14:17:02Z]`. The printed interval happens to be the right marginal cap, so the description and the number disagree. Global precedence is also imposed on T-409 even though the early part of `[15:52:00Z, 16:17:00Z]` is before every feasible alert; the table keeps that impossible tail while the duration is floored at 0. Same-system offset correlation is never discussed.

The code does not reproduce the analysis. It omits mobile entry delay, never infers T-404’s start or T-407’s receipt, and keeps both DST folds. Inverted fold pairs become a spurious `(0, 0)` duration, and classification collapses a union to its outer hull. The midpoint of the unresolved T-405 folds falls in the gap between them. PM4Py is only called on that lossy log, with no comparison to the interval classes, and directly-follows checking is not distinguished from the non-consecutive alert→containment profile. Section 6 then misstates T-402 S1 as still ambiguous, calls T-406 uncertainty “hours-level,” and treats T-405 as though the fold were still unresolved.
