4.9

The write-up follows the required outline and correctly recommends a monitored pilot rather than an immediate full rollout. It is not a reliable forecast: several quantitative claims contradict the given tables, the pilot does not test the proposed pooling, and the PM4Py sketch misuses core functions.

**Mechanism map.** The eight themes are named, but the causal account is thin and partly wrong. Management’s own routing table does not show nominal handover reduction: tech touches rise from 36 to 41 per 100 cases, legal/risk from 9 to 12, and approvals from 42 to 49; only intake-to-caseworker and cross-team arcs fall. The map still treats “fewer handovers” as an effect. “Reduce local technical knowledge transfer” is listed as an action when it is a consequence. Resilience is only “single point of failure,” omitting the opposing effect that a pool can cover individual absence better while failing under correlated load. Legal/Risk at 91–93% utilization, plus difficult quality checks entering the same queue, is the main congestion mechanism and is barely modeled. `discover_organizational_roles` does not show “fewer unique roles” because one supervisor post is removed.

**Scenarios.** All three have the requested fields, and the central case correctly separates a stable median from a worse tail and treats East differently from North/South. The optimistic case is not coherent with the data: waits “below 3.0h” cannot cover a legal/risk baseline of 9.6h that the proposal pushes to 10.5h at higher volume, and “no early warning signs” is not a monitoring plan. The adverse case says East “cannot scale,” but East’s specialist is removed into the central pool; East does not operate that queue. Hour-scale waits (4h to 8h) are then used to justify day-scale cycle-time moves, including a doubled 90th percentile, without a mechanism that could add roughly 8–20 days. Rework “12%+” is barely above East’s current 11.5%. Learning and absence resilience are missing inside the scenarios.

**Indicators.** The leading/lagging split is the right idea and mis-assigned. `get_service_time` is service time, not median wait after handover. Role discovery does not measure utilization. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` does not measure queue depth. A performance DFG is not case-level median and 90th-percentile cycle time. Monthly resource similarity is not a days/weeks leading indicator. Fairness, learning, resilience, `reassignment_count`, `local_expertise_flag`, and `handover_reason` are absent as measures even though they are in the log.

**Hidden risks.** Tail delay, complex cases, local knowledge, fairness, learning, and accountability are all real. Exception handling, which was required, is not a distinct risk. Calling skill decay “invisible in logs” undercuts the task: activity profiles, subcontracting, and `local_expertise_flag` versus outcome are observable proxies. Segment and priority effects stay generic.

**Pilot.** A Technical Shared Service used only by East, with North and South frozen, does not create the proposed pool of N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech. If only E_Tech serves East, the pilot removes local expertise without pooling capacity. East is already the slow, high-rework, high-utilization site (8.6d, 11.5%, 9.8% SLA miss, E_Tech 82%), so it is a confounded comparison, not a control. Guardrail operators are missing, and the implied thresholds are already breached by East’s baseline (SLA miss 9.8% versus about 8%; satisfaction 3.8 versus 4.0). Legal/Risk saturation and the shared approval queue are not piloted. Rollback exists; adjustment does not. Eight weeks cannot detect the learning loss the answer itself places years out.

**Recommendation.** “Pilot first,” plus an explicit warning not to read early average cycle time as success, matches the constraints. The exit rule does not: meeting those guardrails for eight weeks on this pilot would not identify pooling, North/South expertise loss, fairness, or resilience, so it does not justify full rollout. There is no modify path for the already-saturated legal/risk queue.

**PM4Py sketch.** Handover, DFG, variants, roles, similarity, and rework are attempted. Working-together, subcontracting, and SLA misses are missing, as are real filters for team, specialty, and customer segment. `format_dataframe` is called on an XES event log with incorrect parameter names; `filter_event_attribute_values` is given a dict instead of attribute, values, and level; `get_service_time(..., param="wait")` is not a real wait-time API. Before/after comparison is commented out, so the sketch never produces the per-100 frequencies, median waits, and rework rates the scenario is built on.
