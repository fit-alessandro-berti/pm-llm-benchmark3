6.1

The write-up has the right overall shape and the right headline (S is too loose to enforce policy, C memorizes an incomplete variant list, a block-structured model plus explicit rules is the sensible third path). It is not reliable as a governance review. Several required judgments are factually wrong, one required table is incomplete, and the third model is described as enforcing constraints that its own expression does not enforce.

The behavior audit is the clearest failure. Model C is an exclusive choice of eight fixed sequences. The only Auto Approval sequence is Register, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order. Probe B2 is Register, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order, so Model C rejects a case the policy marks valid. The matrix marks it accepted and treats that as “basic training-set concurrency.” That misses the cleanest evidence that C’s high precision is incomplete enumeration, not policy capture. The same carelessness reappears in the adversarial traces. Model S places each activity once, in `X(activity, tau)`, inside a single parallel block, so Supplier Review occurs at most once. Adv 3 (two Supplier Reviews) is therefore rejected by S, just as B8 is rejected for a second Credit Check. The audit says S accepts Adv 3, which contradicts the answer’s own B8 reasoning and means that trace does not distinguish the third model from both inputs. Section 8 also omits P3, P7, B2, B4, B5, and B7 even though the required table was supposed to cover P1–P7 and B1–B8. One of the three new traces (Adv 1) does separate the models; Adv 2 does so only if the post-hoc rule is counted, while the cell itself says “Rejects.”

Model S is otherwise read correctly: `X(a, tau)` under `+` makes Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, and Expedite Review independently skippable and freely ordered, so B3 and B4 are admitted, B1/B5/B7 are admitted, and Correct Dossier is outside the alphabet. The intentional-versus-accidental split is still backwards. The discovery note says S was built to tolerate missing events; the tau skips are that mechanism. The accidental damage is applying the same skip to mandatory checks, using parallelism so Expedite Review can sit before or between those checks, and deleting Correct Dossier so rework cannot be represented at all. Calling S a “spaghetti model” is the wrong defect: the tree is small and block-structured, and the failure is semantic. The five “undesirable” behaviors also collapse: item 4 is items 1–2 combined, and Supplier Review interleaved with Credit Check is not forbidden by P2–P4.

Model C’s broader critique (branch explosion, duplicate labels, brittle correction, maintainability, precision computed only on observed positives) is directionally right and then over-sold. Training fitness is 0.973, not 1, so C does not even replay its own training log; “memorization of the log” is too strong. Holdout fitness 0.744 is compatible with overfitting, but F4 also says some Supplier Review events are missing and some holdout combinations were never in training. A precise model should lose fitness on missing events. The drop does not, by itself, prove that every rejected holdout trace is a valid variant. Duplicate-label count is explicitly undocumented; tying it to “33 means near-complete unrolling” is an unsupported gloss. There are no silent transitions or loops in C, so acceptance of B1–B8 is not uncertain — which makes the B2 error harder to excuse.

The third tree is a real improvement in intent: mandatory `+(Credit Check, Technical Review)`, then a loop whose do-part is optional Supplier/Expedite, whose redo is correction plus rechecks, then exactly one approval, then release. On the language, that correctly rejects B3, B4, and B8 and accepts B1, B2, B5, and B7. The prose then misstates the tree. `*(A, B)` is A(BA)*, and the redo is `Correct Dossier` followed by `+(X(Credit Check, tau), X(Technical Review, tau))`, so both rechecks may be silent. The claim that correction “must repeat at least some behavior” is false of the procedural model. The patch, `ChainResponse('Correct Dossier', 'Credit Check' OR 'Technical Review')`, is not a DECLARE constraint; ChainResponse constrains the immediate successor, and the OR is informal. `DataRule_Correction` depends on `System.DeficientItems`, which the log does not contain, and it does not itself forbid an empty recheck. `DataRule_Supplier` only encodes Supplier ⇒ external, not external ⇒ Supplier. Credit Check and Technical Review are duplicated across the initial block and the redo, which is exactly the impact-analysis problem the review treats as fatal in C, and it is never discussed. B6 is therefore accepted for any correction, not “only if Technical Review was the affected check.” Section 8’s “correctly handles” is an overclaim. Limitations are mentioned, but the formal semantics section contradicts them.

The metric table at least refuses a single weighted score and says something useful about every reported number. It still treats the two teams’ figures as comparable observations. No seeds, preprocessing, cost settings, or held-out cases were supplied, so the 80/20 claim is unauditable; that is the first comparability concern, not a side note. Silent transitions are described as inflating S’s alignment state space and causing permissiveness. S is the fast model (0.018 s). The taus matter because skip moves are in the language and, under usual zero cost, do not reduce fitness. That is the optionalizing-fitness pathology, which is listed separately and explained better than the silent-transition paragraph. Ranking is broadly sensible; several entries say “proves” where the evidence only suggests.

The validation plan names the right experiments, then the skeleton would not run. `fitness_alignments` returns one aggregate dictionary, not per-trace rows with `'fitness'`; per-trace rejection needs `conformance_diagnostics_alignments`, which the prompt already pointed at. Generalization is required in the report and absent from the code. The split is described as attribute-stratified and implemented as an ordinary `train_test_split`. The rejection rule (true-negative rate under 100 percent, or arc-degree simplicity under 0.5) is arbitrary, ignores the declarative half of the proposed model, and is not carried into the deployment gate. Section 10 deploys the third model for compliance and simulation if IT can evaluate the post-hoc rules. Simulation is unsupported: there are no frequencies on the tau/XOR choices. Compliance deployment is recommended before soundness, the negative suite, or a stakeholder protocol has been passed. “Watermark S, retire C” is a defensible interim stance; it is stronger than the evidence and the untested artifact.

The dissent is long enough and is not a token objection, but it repeats the false premise that C already contains every approved pathway. B2, B5, Exp-before-Sup, and training fitness below 1 all contradict that. The evidence that would flip the decision (runnable synchronous rules, or a measured false-positive cost) is relevant and narrower than the gaps above: a corrected probe matrix, a non-empty recheck operator that does not depend on a missing attribute, and a holdout decomposition into missing events versus unseen but policy-valid combinations would also change how much weight this recommendation deserves.
