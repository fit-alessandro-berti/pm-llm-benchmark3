The write-up follows the required nine-section structure and states two real objectives, three operational policies, shock-wise failure modes, and a fallback rule. That scaffolding does not survive the quantitative claims it depends on.

The offered-load analysis double-counts labor. Skill capacities are computed as if each multi-skilled person’s full shift were available to every skill at once, so reported base utilizations of about 12–54% are not feasible. Aggregate standard work is about 2,720 person-minutes against about 2,490 available minutes even at multiplier 1.0, before slower resources, handovers, setup, and coordination. The 16:00–20:00 staffing cliff is missed, including no mechanical coverage after 18:00. ES volume is also wrong (4.99 cases, not 5.99), which inflates electrical and software load, and the U4 software mean increase is understated.

Section 4 is inconsistent with Section 2. Expected critical-path service alone is roughly 50–80 minutes, so mean completion times of 25–40 minutes are impossible. The table’s SLA and utilization figures are not produced by the code, and hybrid U5 can fall below the 90% P1 target that Section 5 treats as achieved.

The Python does not implement the comparison it claims. Hybrid and pooling use the same assignment; the utilization cap, overtime, breaks, and shift ends are unused; the inter-team penalty never fires; preemption is overwritten; U5 incorrectly applies the U2 mix change; and strict routing crashes when Chen is absent. Omitted mechanisms are not all identified, so the scaffold allowance is not earned.
