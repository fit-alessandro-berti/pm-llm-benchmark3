6.7

The design gets the central scientific point right and avoids the failure modes that would make it weak: rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are not recoded as non-defaults; the primary estimand is explicitly conditional on historical activation; the 48,000 cases add up; leakage fields such as `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, and `collection_status` are excluded; and validation is temporal rather than a random case split. The alternatives, sensitivity analyses, and deployment boundary are also real.

Those strengths do not make the study statistically coherent.

The target is called a conditional hazard, but the stated estimand is a 90-day survival probability or cumulative incidence. The schema supports a delayed binary label, not a continuous time to default. The primary analysis nevertheless puts all 25,200 activated accounts into a survival likelihood. In the skeleton, every matured default and non-default is given the event time `activation + 90 days`, so observed outcomes are completely tied at the horizon. Immature cases are assigned the absolute timestamp `extraction − 35 days`, which can fall before activation and is never converted to a duration. With that construction, a Cox model, Harrell’s C-index, and IPCW time-dependent AUC do not estimate a meaningful hazard. Delayed label maturity has been named and then collapsed into ordinary right censoring.

Prefixing is also leaky. Fixed lengths K=5 and K=7, and elapsed-time windows, can contain Accepted, Contract Signed, or Account Activated, but there is no milestone cutoff before the decision. The leakage table claims those future events are removed by prefix truncation; the code only keeps the first K events and drops columns. The audit assert cannot catch activity leakage.

The validation dates contradict each other and the process history. The prose test window is 1 July–30 September 2025 and is described as partly post-migration, but the migration is 15 October 2025. The code instead sends all of July–December 2025 to test. Training is entirely pre-policy, so `pre_policy` has no variance in training, while regime is attached to application start rather than decision or activation time. Long cases that start in 2024 and are decided after 1 July 2025 are treated as pre-policy. Queue features are thin, and `documents_total` is “repaired” with an event-time document count the schema does not provide.

The skeleton is concrete and uses `format_dataframe`, a start-time split, DFG, and variants on the prefix log, but it does not call `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`, is well over the requested length, and the survival columns would not support the model described in the comments.
