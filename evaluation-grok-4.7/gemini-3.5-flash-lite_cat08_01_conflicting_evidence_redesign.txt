The write-up follows the required structure and uses many real figures (2,550 fast approvals, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen, 31% to 9% cost gap, 0.91 fitness / 68% fitting traces, EUR 410, 82% utilization, 30–45% portal effect). The three redesigns are distinct, each names a sacrifice, and the pilot sequence plus lagged-reopen check show the right kind of question. It is not a generic Lean list.

It still misreads the evidence in ways that would change the decision.

The fast path is treated as a uniform “error-propagation engine.” That ignores the segment split the prompt requires. Premium low-risk accessories are 8 hours and 6% reopen on the fast path versus 46 hours and 3% on the manual path, with service-credit exposure above 24 hours. Standard low-risk customers tolerate 72 hours. Slowing those paths is not an improvement. The 22% vs 5% premium high-value gap is real, but the paths are not comparable populations; the text treats the gap as causal proof.

Process-mining metrics are mishandled. Fitness of 0.91 is not “poor”; the stated problem is low precision plus specific deviations, and 68% fitting traces means 32% deviate, which is less than one-third, not more. The 140-hour figure is the median cycle time of the unclear-defect segment, not a ceiling on evidence loops. Rework-repair (12.5% of repaired high-value devices) is conflated with wrong replacement-part selection, which the performance profile ties to dispatch-to-complaint. R3 (reopens must pass manual review) and the idle quality-check inspectors are barely used. The 24-hour service-credit rule is stated only for premium low-risk accessories and is then applied to all premium customers.

Redesign B aims the evidence portal at low-risk and “clear-defect” cases. The evidence failure sits in the 22% unclear-defect segment (140-hour median, highest abandonment, 26% repeat requests, p90 88 hours back to manual review). Redesign C’s concierge track does not show how premium high-value replacements still satisfy R1 and R2, while Track 1 “streamlines” standard cases and the sacrifice/table say standard cycle time does not change. Both of C’s failure modes are labeled “Failure Mode 2.” The escalation-board bottleneck migration is asserted without a mechanism. “High-risk accessories” is not a segment in the data.

The Pareto row marks the current process “Dominated? Yes,” which the prompt forbids claiming from this data. The portal pilot is run on standard low-risk accessories and uses a go bar of median cycle time under 10 hours, below an already-observed 13-hour fast path that often never enters the evidence loop. The concierge go bar (complaints under 3%, service credits down more than 50%) is stricter than the best observed premium high-value path (5%) and uses a service-credit baseline that is not in that segment. Complaint return is described as 14–30 days; the performance graph puts it at median 50–70 hours and p90 150–180 hours.

Correct scaffolding, uneven and sometimes misleading use of the mining evidence.
