The answer is strong on the hard causal points and still not nearly flawless. It correctly refuses natural direct and indirect effects, treats the flag as a policy bundle, separates review occurrence from review waiting, and keeps absent stages out of the zero-length calculations. Most stage medians check out, the positivity failure (scores 0.28–0.61 versus 0.64–0.93) is real, and the PM4Py sketch does not invent skipped events.

The numerical and design errors are concrete. For flagged high-load cases the residual-to-close median is 1.1 h (1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 1.5), not 1.0. “Residuals are already ~1 h in every row” is false: residuals run from 1.0 to 2.0 (R14 is 2.0). Several conditional medians are one- or two-case statistics presented beside full-subgroup medians; the narrative never gives those supports even though it says it does. The P2 cycle claim is miscalibrated: medium flagged cases have about a 5 h review wait plus about 2 h of service after a fast triage, so dropping review does not move them “toward” the unflagged-medium median of 6.2 h, which is mostly slow triage. E1 defines P1 as display suppression plus auto-rules off, while P1 in the prompt and in section 6 is display-off only. E3 and E8 then call that same mandate contrast an indirect effect and a controlled direct effect. The pilot randomizes at case level inside a shared review queue after section 5 correctly says interference requires a cluster design; cluster-robust standard errors do not remove the spillover bias. The “>4 h” high-complexity guardrail is already tripped by every flagged high case in the log (waits 5–10 h), so enrollment would freeze under the status quo. The graph also duplicates `triage_wait → cycle_time`, lets triage duration cause review, and never lets the badge affect reviewer judgment, which the text itself treats as a live pathway.
