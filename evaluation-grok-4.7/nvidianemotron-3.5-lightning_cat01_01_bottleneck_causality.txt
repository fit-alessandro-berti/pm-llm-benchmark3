The central finding is right: Orion’s higher crude mean (21.96h vs 15.24h) reverses inside every complexity stratum, so the 50% reassignment rests on case mix and can backfire. “Mainly confounded,” the pilot, and the queue/compliance phase are defensible. The rest is not reliable enough for a committee.

The night comparison contradicts itself. Orion night is given as about 23.1h and Nova night as 17.5h, yet the text says Orion is still faster and that holding shift fixed reveals an Orion benefit. 23.1 is slower than 17.5. Nova day volume is also wrong (12/16, mean about 14.5h, not 11/16 and about 15.3h). Low Nova is 9.64h in the supplied table, not a 9.6–10.5h range. Low Orion review service is about 3.3h, not about 6h. Medium rework is lower for Orion (16.7% vs 25%), not higher. There are no low-complexity compliance-required cases, so Orion cannot be “faster within compliance = yes” in Low, Medium, and High. Within complexity, Orion’s wait is lower in every stratum; the claim that higher review service offsets longer waits misstates both queueing and service.

The DAG does not connect shift to routing or resource group, so shift is not a confounder on the graph the answer itself draws, while the tables treat it as one. The rework-rate gap is cited as support for a causal arrow. Total, direct, and mediated effects are named more than separated, and within-stratum speed is treated as evidence of a beneficial Orion effect despite n = 2 and almost no common support. The graph note runs past the 120-word cap, and a non-allowed node is added.
