The stage arithmetic is accurate and the absent-stage handling is right, but the argument blurs the intervention it was asked to keep sharp and leaves the mechanism graph inconsistent with its own interference story.

The medians in §3 match the timestamps, including midnight crossings: flag 0 vs 1 triage-start 2.50h vs 0.20h, triage service 0.95h vs 0.60h, manual wait 1.75h (n=4) vs 6.75h, cycle time 5.75h vs 16.45h, and the load and escalation splits. Review rates 100% vs 40% and escalation rates 60% vs 20% are correct. Rejecting natural direct and indirect effects because of shared queues and exposure-induced confounding is the right framework, and the pilot, reversal list, and PM4Py sketch meet the required shape. Absent stages are not coded as zero.

The main defect is P1. The assigned contrast is suppressing display while leaving the score and other human information in place. E1 marks that contrast well-defined only by stipulating that suppression also removes the priority lane and automatic routing, and §6 then treats slower triage as the expected P1 effect. That stipulation is a different policy. §5 later tests pure display suppression, so the memo uses two incompatible definitions of the same contrast.

The adjacency list does not support the interference paragraph. `U_queue_state` has no parents, so flag, priority, and review never change the queue that supposedly mediates waiting. `risk_score` points only at `flag`, so the graph cannot represent a hidden flag with the score still visible. Treatment-confounder feedback is attributed to unspecified “triage outputs” rather than to priority changing when the case reaches the review queue.

Several deterministic patterns in the event data never enter the mechanism account. Flag 1 is triaged only by T1/T2 and flag 0 only by T3/T4. All seven M2 reviews are escalated, against one of four M1 reviews and zero of three M3 reviews. Flag and `risk_score` are perfectly separated (flag 1 minimum 0.64, flag 0 maximum 0.61), so every unadjusted flag contrast is a score contrast. §3 explains the gap with complexity and hidden risk and does not state that collinearity. The complexity gradient partly restates the flag split: low complexity is entirely unflagged. The n=1 medium-complexity escalation “median” is presented beside the n=10 results.

§5 uses triage service as a negative control after §4 treats that service time as an effect of lane and resource assignment. The unobserved-risk sensitivity check is only a clause inside the g-computation row. Shift alternation is called cluster randomization even though measured review and escalation waits of about 4–10 hours cross shift boundaries, so the proposed clusters contaminate each other.

§9 then says waiting-time mediation dominates and that a P3 pilot isolates it and yields clean evidence. Occurrence of review and escalation, score collinearity, resource assignment, and carryover are still open, and added capacity can change reviewer behavior as well as waiting.
