4.5

The write-up keeps the six required sections and correctly treats the period as several overlapping problems, with April as a pipeline incident and October as label censoring. Those headlines are outweighed by a repeated mechanism error, factual mistakes, tests that would not separate the competing causes, and a code sketch that corrupts the log and misses the required PM4Py operations.

## 1. Drift diagnosis table
The taxonomy is misapplied. Parts wait occurs after the prediction point (immediately after diagnosis). The July–October rise in `Order Part -> Part Received` (65 h, then 130 h, 148 h, 155 h), with V1/V2 matured breach rates staying near 0.16–0.19 and 0.09–0.13 while V3 moves from about 0.33 to 0.46, 0.64, and 0.61, is a change in P(breach | path). That is concept drift together with process-performance drift. It is listed as covariate drift, including April, at high confidence. April’s signature is the opposite of a real covariate shift: observed breach stays 0.19 while mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28, unknown-activity share is 17%, missing queue features are 14%, and unmapped variants are 18%.

June is placed under concept drift. V2 share rises to 52% and the overall breach rate falls to 0.15 with full label maturity, while the remote-fix edge stays at 3.8–4.6 h and V2’s own breach rate stays near 0.09–0.11. That is a routing mix change. Predicted risk remaining at 0.20 explains the ECE of 0.060 without a new within-path failure mode.

August is the clean supplier month: Partner Z share is still 0%, yet V3 matured breach is 0.64 and predicted risk stays 0.19 against an observed rate of 0.28. The table calls concept drift only “early” and dates it with June and September. September–October population shift is real (Partner Z 24% then 27%; 58% of Partner Z cases are V3 versus 36% elsewhere), but rural distance and device age are described as features that were introduced. The evidence says those covariates were absent from the model. October’s Brier of 0.205 is offered as unfavorable comparison material even though September’s Brier is 0.214.

The “contradicting or insufficient evidence” cells often restate supporting facts: incomplete May backfill, the flat remote-fix edge, and the V3 rate jump. May is also fused with April, despite May’s partial recovery (unknown activity 2%, AUROC 0.78, ECE still 0.050) versus April’s broken prefixes. Label delay is confined to October even though maturity is already 98%, 93%, and 82% in July–September, median label delay rises from 36 d to 42 d, and longer paths are the ones the backlog hits.

## 2. Timeline
January–March and the October censoring story are usable. April is rightly called a schema incident, then over-closed: the 6 May patch did not recompute the cached pre-diagnosis count, and duplicate removal applied only to the new source after 20 April. May and June are narrated as one block, so residual feature corruption is blurred into the remote-routing policy.

July’s aggregate breach rate of 0.19 and AUROC of 0.76 are treated as “not yet catastrophic” covariate onset. Matured V3 breach is already 0.46, hidden by a 50% V2 share. August is explained as higher V3 mix plus covariate drift. Mix alone cannot do that: August shares at pre-summer within-variant rates (about 0.16 / 0.10 / 0.32) still produce an aggregate near 0.19; the conditional V3 rate is what moves the outcome, and predictions do not follow. September reports Partner Z’s 0.45 versus 0.27 without the stated slower maturation, so an immature contrast is used as population evidence. October’s Brier is called the highest, which is false, and the sign flip versus August–September (predictions 0.23 above the immature observed rate 0.16, after months of under-prediction) is not used as the censoring signature.

## 3. Diagnostic tests
The seven topics are present only as a binary “pipeline versus true drift” contrast, so they cannot separate supplier-driven concept drift, routing mix, Partner Z, and censoring.

Fixed-vintage backtesting is redefined as “features from that month only.” The relevant test is to freeze a case-start cohort and rescore it after labels mature. April is described as a label-maturity failure; April outcome maturity is already 100%. The pipeline hypothesis is said to imply that the parts edge is anomalous only in April and steady afterward, which the measured July–October durations already contradict. Reweighting Partner Z is expected either to do nothing (pipeline) or to restore AUROC (population). It cannot explain August, when Partner Z is 0%, and it should leave a residual V3 gap in September if the supplier effect is real. Normalization-and-dedup recomputation is not given its own before/after metric on the same prefixes. `discover_performance_df` is not the library function.

## 4. Recalibration versus retraining
The staged outline admits the right ingredients and then binds them to the wrong months. June–August is one recalibration window, with full retraining deferred unless concept drift “persists beyond August.” August is already the month in which V3 risk roughly doubles and the score stays near 0.19. A global recalibration fitted there would shift V1 and V2, whose matured rates barely moved.

January–March are never named as the mature, stable training base. April as a “stale-data holdout” evaluates models on corrupted prefixes. September is called safe above 80% maturity and, in the same paragraph, unsafe until maturity exceeds 80%, while the table already shows 82% and slower Partner Z labels. `supplier_delay` is proposed as a case feature although that duration is unknown at diagnosis time; a trailing supplier state would be the leakage-safe version. Variant-specific models and a Partner Z submodel at an arbitrary 10-point AUROC gap are asserted, and fairness is claimed before section 6’s own caution. A November “cutover” is not in the evidence.

## 5. Monitoring and code
The monitor list covers the requested families, and a maturity alarm is said to suppress retraining. The thresholds would still miss the history they were built from. Predictive alerting requires AUROC below 0.66 and ECE above 0.05, so April (0.67 / 0.110), August (0.68 / 0.120), and September (0.66 / 0.140) do not fire. Maturity suppression starts only under 60%, so September’s 82% biased cohort can still train. After data-quality and maturity clear, predictive alerts “resume normal retraining,” which collapses the recalibrate-versus-retrain distinction.

The code sketch does not implement the audit. `Series.map` on only `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, then `fillna('UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY')`, relabels every other activity, including an already correct `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`, as unknown. Section 4 canonicalizes in the opposite direction. Deduping on `(case_id, activity)` happens before time sorting, and the duplicate count is computed after the rows are gone. `case_start` is unused, so there is no event-time cohort versus case-start cohort and no statement of the two interpretations. `format_dataframe` is called with `id_column` / `timestamp_column` rather than the library’s case, activity, and timestamp arguments. `filter_time_range` and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are absent. `discover_performance_df` is not `discover_performance_dfg`. `get_variants` is treated as a monthly timestamp frame. The returned object is one global dict with a maturity TODO, not monthly summaries.

## 6. Risks
The four requested failure modes are all present and the Partner Z fairness caution is the right one. The supplier row calls the same phenomenon concept drift after the earlier sections called it covariate drift. Its mitigation adds the same post-decision delay feature. The maturity gate is “≥ 80%” after section 4 disagreed with itself about September. The June overgeneralization row is fair.

Net: a reader would still hesitate to train on raw October labels and would still repair April mappings before trusting April scores. They would also recalibrate through the supplier regime, treat June as concept drift, read September’s immature Partner Z gap as stable performance, and run a sketch that manufactures an unknown-activity incident. That is partial structure with unreliable diagnosis.
