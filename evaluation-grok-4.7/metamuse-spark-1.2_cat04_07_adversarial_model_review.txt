6.0

The review is structurally complete and often sharp on Models S and C, but it is not reliable as a governance artifact. The third model, which is the main deliverable, does not have the language the audit assigns to it, and several metric claims are technically wrong.

Sections 1–2 and 6 are the strongest. Use cases are separated, every reported measure is interpreted without a fake aggregate, and the ranking of pathologies correctly puts optionalization-driven fitness, split opacity, positive-only precision, and missing-event bias ahead of the informal comprehension score. The S-versus-C probe readings are mostly earned from the expressions: S accepts B1–B5 and B7 and rejects B6 and B8 as language members; C accepts only B1 and B7. Calling that “S accepts 5/8” is a plain counting error. The claim that a trace-level alignment on B8 is about 0.8–0.9, and the appeal to the model’s 0.98 holdout fitness as if it were that trace’s fitness, invents results the brief forbids.

Model S is diagnosed correctly where it matters: `X(activity, τ)` inside `+` makes Credit Check, Technical Review, and Expedite Review skippable and unordered, so B3 and B4 are in the language, while Correct Dossier is absent. Point 4 of that attack is muddled, and Supplier Review before the checks is treated as a defect the policy never states. Model C is fairly charged with memorizing eight variants, missing B2 and B5, and encoding only “correct, then both checks.” The further claim that those process trees can become unsound or non-free-choice after conversion is false; block-structured trees are sound by construction. Holdout fitness 0.744 is also not “one in four cases rejected.” Alignment fitness is a cost ratio, not a case-rejection rate. That mistake reappears in the dissent and in calling C “unsound.”

The POWL alternative is the decisive failure. `StrictPartialOrder` executes every node once; “precedes if both occur” is the wrong semantics, even though `X(a, τ)` wrappers would have repaired optionality for Supplier Review and Expedite Review. The loop is the standard `do (redo do)*` with Register Dossier and an approval inside `do`, Release only after the loop, and another approval inside the redo. A corrected case is therefore forced through an approval before Correct Dossier, a second approval in the redo, a fresh Register Dossier, and a third approval. B6, A2, and A4 are not in that language, yet §8 marks them accepted. The same code puts `t_CC2` and `t_TR2` in both the XOR and the inner partial order, so the POWL is not even well-formed, and `Operator.SEQUENCE` is not a POWL operator in the usual PM4Py API. The model also reintroduces duplicate labels, then §9 rejects any model whose duplicate-label count exceeds 5. P5 is rewritten as “one approval per iteration” without saying that the code emits three. The base partial order alone would have handled B1–B5 and B7; the correction layer undoes the claim that this is safer than S and more general than C.

The validation plan is broad, but the missing-event probe deletes Supplier Review, the optional activity, and then treats S’s insensitivity as proof of over-permissiveness. Deleting Credit Check or Technical Review would have been the actual test. The dissent is a real alternative, not a token, and it stays inside the length bound, but it opens by refusing to retire either model and closes by archiving C, while resting the operational argument on the fitness-as-rejection fallacy.

The S/C critique would be usable board material after the arithmetic and soundness corrections. The third model would need to be rebuilt before any of the deployment gates in §10 could be meaningful.
