The required six-section structure is present, the allowed conformance labels are used, and Actions A and B are correctly placed last and treated as hard violations that a favorable forecast cannot repair. Screening is not treated as a substitute for independent second review, and the asymmetry between bounded operational loss and regulatory exposure is stated in the right direction.

The ranking itself is not reliable. P-884 has 90 minutes and P-901 has 70, yet the final recommendation calls P-901’s window “slightly longer,” which reverses the spillover comparison made earlier. Action C is called a reliable, deterministic fit inside 90 minutes, but a 35–75 minute review plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute reaches about 40–90 minutes and can miss the deadline. The S4 sensitivity is arithmetically wrong: it leaves “10–25 minutes for review” and adds screening time to waiting time. The stated review takes 35–75 minutes, and screening can run while waiting for S4, so the 50-minute cutoff does not follow from the facts and can reorder C versus D. The 45-minute P-901 interrupt used for the +0.12–0.28 spillover is also shorter than the upper review time, so that spillover is understated.

Other conclusion-level problems: branch probabilities such as 0.3, 0.4, and 0.7 are invented; F’s “benefit” of €100k–€500k is the harm of unlawful execution, which a conformant review already avoids; B is ranked above A without the same regulatory range, although a false four-eyes record followed by execution is at least as serious; G, an exception that is not enabled, outranks fully conformant E; and the continuations do not develop the sanctions hold/SAR outcome or the risk that an evidence request tips off the customer. D is labeled conditional even though the plan includes the second review, while C is labeled conformant now despite the same independence condition. The primary recommendation therefore rests on a timing and spillover analysis that the case numbers do not support.
