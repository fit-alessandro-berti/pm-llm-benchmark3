4.5

The draft keeps the right high-level refusal — rejected, withdrawn, and open cases are not coded as non-defaults, and the primary estimand is named as default given activation — but the design contradicts itself, the maturity arithmetic is wrong, and the skeleton does not implement the study it describes.

## Target and analysis do not match
The stated estimand is a binary probability, \(P(\text{default\_90d}=1\mid\text{activation},\text{prefix})\). The primary analysis is then a cause-specific Cox or a Fine–Gray model on the activated subpopulation, with inverse-probability-of-activation weights. Those pieces answer different questions. Among activated accounts, rejection and withdrawal have already occurred, so they are not competing events, and Cox and Fine–Gray are not interchangeable. Weighting activated cases by the inverse probability of activation moves the estimand toward a pseudo-population of all applicants, which is the counterfactual Section 8 says is out of scope. A binary conditional-on-activation estimand is fit on matured activated cases; it does not need activation weights.

The prediction time — “first event after Credit Review, or the third event, whichever is later” — is undefined once Credit Review repeats in a document loop, and it can land on Accept or another decision event. The alternative target puts default in competition with rejection, withdrawal, and transfer inside 180 days of start. Default is defined only after activation and only on a 90-day post-activation clock, so that competing-risk set mixes two time origins.

## Cohort and maturity claims are false
Activation on 15 November 2025 reaches day 90 on 13 February 2026. Extraction is 15 February 2026, so the rule leaves about two days for a feed that the brief says arrives 7–35 days after the horizon. The sentence claiming that this cutoff lets the window and the feed mature is incorrect. Equating that cutoff with the given 21,600 matured labels is also incorrect: those 21,600 are the warehouse’s already-matured activated accounts, not the subset that meets this design’s dates.

The flow adds to 48,000 only by relabeling the problem’s counts. It never removes starts after 31 August 2025, post-policy exclusions, or undeduplicable migration cases, even though Section 2 states those exclusions. Still-open cases are called right-censored default observations; under the primary estimand they never enter the default risk set. Linked transfers are assigned downstream default labels the linkage table is not said to contain. Section 2 then excludes post-1 July 2025 cases “until volume exists,” while Section 7 uses 1 July–31 August 2025 as the test set.

## Missingness strategy
Open cases, late activations, delayed feeds, partner feeds that never arrive, and activation selection are named, then collapsed. For a binary 90-day label, an immature activation is excluded as not yet mature; it is a censored survival time only under a different estimand. Complete-case analysis is listed as a sensitivity even though the primary rule already keeps only matured feeds. Multiple imputation under MAR does not stress the stated mechanism, which is permanently missing and elevated for partner-originated accounts. The untestable claim is the whole counterfactual default risk of cases never activated, not only the special assumption that their risk is zero. Positivity is ignored: some prefixes are rejected by policy with probability essentially one.

## Prefixes, features, validation
As-of timestamping, a rename map, and a rework count are the right ingredients. Fourteen elapsed days is an unexplained second prefix rule, and fixed length 3 is not the same cut as “first event after Credit Review.” In-sample DFG path frequency, unless frozen from history strictly before each case’s prediction time, leaks later cases into the encoding.

The leakage table correctly drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, and `label_available_at` as a predictor, and it repairs resource experience. `risk_score` is marked Keep. The file can repeat a later score on earlier events, the same way `default_90d` is repeated once known, so the as-of value has to be taken from the event that wrote it, which is a repair. `documents_total` at the prior midnight is a conservative repair of the nightly snapshot.

The temporal split is the right family, and a random case split would mix the July 2025 policy regimes. The design still fails as a backtest. There is no label-availability freeze at each prediction date, no embargo for queue features shared across the March/April boundary, and no post-policy validation fold. Requiring activation by 15 November 2025 makes the test set the fast activations only. The split does not isolate the 15 October 2025 migration, because later events of earlier starts still cross it. Time-dependent Brier score and cumulative/dynamic AUC belong to a survival target this section never adopted; with a 2,700/21,600 event rate, precision–recall and calibration slope on the conditional-on-activation population are the metrics that match Section 1.

## Limitations
Section 8 is the most coherent part: historical association given observed activation, selection into activation, and no license to impute default for never-activated cases. The three deployment shifts — threshold changes, applicant response to a visible score, and resource reallocation — are real changes to the data-generating process.

## Skeleton
The code is about 50 lines, short of the requested 80–160, and it will not carry the design.

- `cutoff` is unused. `label_ready` calls `.max()` on the whole `label_available_at` series, so it is a single global timestamp passed to `Series.map`, not a per-case maturity date. `mature` never consults the feed.
- Terminal state, censoring reason, and competing outcomes are never built. The printed cohort lumps every non-mature case into “Rejected/Withdrawn/Open/Transferred,” including immature activations.
- There is no validation fold. `pm4py.filter_log_on_case_id` is not a PM4Py function. `get_prefixes_from_log(..., k=3)` does not match the usual `length` argument.
- Prefixes are the first three events of the full log. `default_90d`, `collection_status`, `final_limit`, and `decision_reason` are never removed. The leakage helper is an uncalled stub whose comment treats columns as if they had timestamps.
- `discover_dfg` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` run on the full training log, so post-activation and collections timing enter a step the comment calls pre-prediction. No comment marks where a survival or competing-risk model would attach, and the unused CSV and timestamp imports do not substitute for that.

The draft would not support an honest early-warning claim. The estimand, the risk set, the maturity rule, and the code each describe a different study.
