The answer covers every required section and gets the main operational story right: flagged cases are triaged faster, almost all of them are forced into manual review, and most of the extra cycle time is queue time plus extra stage entry. That is not enough for a high score. Several checkable claims are wrong, key estimands are mislabeled, and the causal diagram drops the waits that the argument treats as the mechanism.

**Estimands.** E1, E6, and E7 are real policy contrasts. E2 is not a controlled direct effect: triage wait is exactly where priority acts, and “assign the flag while holding the triage policy fixed” is not a coherent intervention. E3 and E5 intervene on review and escalation rules and then call those contrasts interventional indirect effects. There is no estimand for mediation by manual-review waiting, as distinct from review occurrence or from spillover onto other cases. “Interventional Disproportionate/Mediated Effects” is not a framework. The prose correctly rejects cross-world natural effects because of interference and exposure-induced confounding, then the table still marks most contrasts “well-defined: Yes” and puts risk-score adjustment into the mediation design, which removes overlap if the flag is a function of the score.

**Graph.** `triage_wait`, `manual_queue_wait`, and `escalation_queue_wait` never point to `cycle_time`. `U_unmeasured_complexity` is a dead end. There is no path from `risk_score` or `complexity` into discretionary review, despite unflagged cases entering review by judgment. The second figure is a loose layout, not a directed time-ordered mechanism.
