5.5

The policy framework is structured and covers every required heading, but the concrete assignment plan breaks the policy’s own safety rules, misstates portfolio compliance, and cannot be reproduced from the scoring procedure.

The hard-constraint failure is C015. Security is defined as skill 3, or skill 1–2 only under R7_Gita or R8_Hugo, and skill 0 is defined as not allowed. R5_Eva’s Security score is 0. Assigning her to perform the review, with R8_Hugo supervising, is ineligible. The same is true of R10_Jonas. The legal supervised candidates were skill 1–2 resources such as R3_Celine or R4_David. The plan treats supervision as waiving a skill-0 ban, which contradicts both the scenario rules and Section 1.

The training attestation is also wrong. Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, and C015: nine cases, not eight. Of the three claimed development assignments, only C009 matches a documented priority at a legal skill level (R1_Amal, Diagnosis skill 1, normal priority, slack, checkpoint). C013 gives Eligibility to R3_Celine, who is already skill 2 and whose documented priority is negotiation. C015 is illegal. Valid documented-training share is 1/9, below the 20% requirement. The obvious missed candidate was a legal quality-trainee arrangement for C012, which the queue explicitly marks as trainee-suitable and which matches R2_Bruno’s quality priority, subject to his overtime constraint.

Overtime and capacity are applied more loosely than the stated exception. High overtime risk is allowed only for urgent SLA risk when no safer alternative exists. These assignments miss that test:

- R4_David already has 14 open cases, 3.0 hours, and high overtime risk, yet receives both C003 (normal, 18h) and C014 (normal, 25h). R3_Celine and R8_Hugo are legal Diagnosis/Rework alternatives with more time and low overtime risk.
- R2_Bruno receives C010 despite high overtime risk, 12 open cases, low complexity, low handover cost, and about 20 hours of slack.
- R9_Iris retains C006 despite high overtime risk, low complexity, low handover cost, and 36 hours of slack.

C003 has the strongest continuity argument because a further handover would push reassignments above two. That still does not meet the urgent-SLA exception, and adding C014 makes the capacity problem worse. No recorded exception is attached, although Section 1 requires one.

Security routing fights the resilience objective. C001 and C008 both go to R7_Gita (10 open cases, 4.5 hours, medium overtime risk). R8_Hugo has skill 3, fewer open cases, more hours, and low overtime risk, and the plan removes his current case C015. C001 should have been the specialist assignment to R8, with C008 retained by R7. The plan records the resulting concentration as a risk rather than correcting it.

Several other decisions are sound: C002 and C011 stay with R6_Farid, C005 stays with R5_Eva, C007 stays with R3_Celine, C009 is a genuine supervised training assignment, and C016 correctly favors continuity because Eva’s Diagnosis skill is 2 on a high-complexity, high-handover, high-priority case. Those do not repair the portfolio.

The weighted rule is not operational. Components have no normalization, handover delay is not defined as zero when ownership is retained, and “best feasible portfolio” has no search or repair step. No component scores are shown, so Section 3 cannot be audited against Section 2. Batching C006 and C012 across two owners is also undefined. The baseline comparison is directionally balanced and correctly refuses a single optimum, but it never computes the two baseline allocations. Specialist-only tie-breaking by open cases would concentrate Security on R8 and Quality on R10, not spread them across all maximum-skill resources.

The worker explanation is clear and non-punitive, and the indicator list is substantive. The PM4Py sketch names the requested discovery, filtering, timing, and SNA functions, but it would not run on the stated log. It assumes `supervisor`, `completion_timestamp`, `training_assignment`, `policy_deviation`, and `overtime_risk` without deriving them. The violation rule omits Final Approval skill 1 finalizing, the four-assignment cap, and overtime exceptions. The skill join is not time-versioned, and the handover denominator does not match the “per 100 cases” indicator.

The design is explainable in principle and serious about trade-offs. The recommended schedule itself violates eligibility, overtime, capacity, and the training floor, then reports that those constraints were satisfied.
