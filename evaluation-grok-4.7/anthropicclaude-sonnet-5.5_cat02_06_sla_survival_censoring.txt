8.6

The document meets the required nine-section structure, separates estimands from estimators, and handles competing risks, already-observed breaches, leakage, and non-randomized cohort assignment at a high level. The deductions are for incorrect reported figures, one blurred estimand, and a few smaller inconsistencies.

The main pooled Aalen–Johansen row is wrong at the precision it claims. With transfers as a competing event, the values at 72 hours are F1 = 0.303, S = 0.383, F2 = 0.139, F3 = 0.101, and F4 = 0.074, so E1 = 0.697. The reported 0.304, 0.380, 0.140, 0.075, and 0.696 are a drifted partition that still sums to 1. E2 over (72, 96] is 0.145, not 0.143. The same drift produces the pooled transfer sensitivities: censoring transfers gives E1 ≈ 0.689, not 0.687, and counting transfers as resolutions gives E1 ≈ 0.623, not 0.621. The statement that the code should reproduce the table is therefore false. The cohort and priority rows, by contrast, are correct to three decimals, as are the P1 reversal (0.75 to 0.47), the A-versus-B bound overlap, the naive Kaplan–Meier contrast (0.361 versus 0.304), and the completed-case means.

Section 6 then calls both 1 − F1 and 1 − KM “E1” and says that quantity ranges from 0.64 to 0.70. Those are different estimands. The crude bounds [0.28, 0.44] and [0.50, 0.72] are also placed next to the code’s best/worst risk-set variants without saying that moving the five early censoring times does not recover the Manski bounds. S10, at 47 hours, is discussed under an “age ≥ 48” alert rule. The conditional risk at 24 hours is slightly off: P(still active at 72 | active at 24) is 0.471, not 0.467.

The rest is strong. Right censoring, competing withdrawal/rejection/transfer, and known breaches are distinguished with the right case IDs. Open cases are kept. Transfer is given several explicit assumptions, and the P1 composition of all three transfers is used correctly. Escalation, rework, repeated review, and `last_event_gap_h` are classified as post-baseline or leakage rather than baseline covariates. The state diagram matches the observed prefixes, and the sojourn-time non-identifiability point is right. AJ as the primary analysis, with cause-specific hazards secondary and landmarking deferred, is justified by the 13 resolutions, nine open cases, and missing event times. The code encodes the table, keeps open cases, stratifies, runs the transfer sensitivities, and builds an order-only PM4Py log without invented timestamps.
