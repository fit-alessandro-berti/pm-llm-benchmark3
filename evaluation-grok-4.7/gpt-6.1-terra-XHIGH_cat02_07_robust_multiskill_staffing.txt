6.5

The write-up is careful and well structured, but it is not nearly flawless. Several results that drive the story are either arithmetic mistakes, assumed inputs presented as comparisons, or policies the code does not implement.

The clearest calculation error is U2 at 12:00–14:00. Doubling E and ES and renormalizing gives electrical-branch weight \((0.56+0.20+0.24)/1.40 = 0.714\), up from 0.60. The text uses undoubled E, gets \((0.28+0.20+0.24)/1.40 = 0.514\), says the fraction “rises” to a smaller number, and concludes midday electrical pressure can fall. The code doubles both columns, so the prose contradicts the program. The 14:00–16:00 figure of about 240 base minutes is the consistent one. Base offered-load totals, shift and break minutes, service means (6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50, 15.74), U1 volumes, U3 Chen hours, U4 tail means, and the Farah U5 software check are otherwise coherent.

Section 4 does not estimate the required decision metrics. Mean delay, p90/p95, and both SLAs are “—” in every cell. `D` is unallocated fluid work with each block restarted empty; `C` is computed from the policy dictionary (`handovers`, `retained`, `parallel`), not from dispatch. Hybrid is given 0.8 handovers, 60% retention, and a 0.20 setup fraction against 1.7 / 0.2 / 0.40 for pooling, then looks better on deficit and coordination. The text flags the U1/U5 specialist “win” as an input, but still calls hybrid strongest on the lexicographic P1 objective in the scenarios where that same input favors it. Max utilization is 100% because load exceeds the cap, and 0 pp spread is the LP equalizing fractional load. Those columns do not measure fairness or resilience.

The specialization rule is a weak comparator. It keeps Deepa off Mechanical even though she is the only Mechanical resource after 16:00, and it keeps Farah off Software during the only morning window when she and Chen can do that work. Failures from those coverage holes are built in before any queue discipline is tested. The hybrid narrative (lend-back of reserve, 10-minute retention band, 15-minute parallel threshold, 30-minute overflow) is not what the LP does; the LP is a per-block 85% cap on non-P1 minutes plus scalar setup and restart factors. The U5 intake constraint cannot bind: the cap is about 270 staff minutes against roughly 90 minutes of intake, so the outage and post-outage burst are absent from the only quantitative U5 result.

“Exceeds capacity by 81 minutes” at 14:00–16:00 compares base minutes with clock minutes while every multiplier is at least 1, so the overload is understated. The extra 5-minute team penalty is applied to ES as well as ME, although the prompt puts electrical and software on the same side of that boundary. Parallel versus sequential is never given a critical-path example (about 59 sequential diagnostic minutes versus about 33 plus 8 minutes of coordination). The interval log is a two-case fixture, not output of the allocator, so the PM4Py calls do not audit the policies. Case-level service and waiting time are at least labeled correctly, and the omitted mechanisms are stated.

What holds the score up is the problem framing: elapsed SLA clocks, separate priority denominators, labeled uniform and independence assumptions, two explicit objectives, evening skill holes, the fact that routing cannot create Chen or post-18:00 Mechanical capacity, and a pilot that refuses to starve real P1s. The recommendation to pair hybrid routing with more late-day cover is directionally sensible and heavily caveated. It is not earned by the table.
