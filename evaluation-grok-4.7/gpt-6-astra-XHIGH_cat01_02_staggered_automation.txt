The answer is a disciplined causal-policy analysis: required sections and table schemas are complete, median and tail outcomes stay separate, and the rejected “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is the right endpoint. Checked pre/post means, donor changes, window DiDs, last-pre-month event contrasts, load ratios, p90−median gaps, donor-exclusion ranges, and the CSAT arithmetic all match the panel. Availability, completed-path intensity, the routing-inclusive package, and a software-only effect are kept as different estimands, with interference and nonresponse stated explicitly.

Points come off for quantitative and decision looseness. The ±5 hour, ±8 hour, and ±0.20 nonrespondent allowances are assumed scales, justified partly by common shocks that differencing already removes, yet they become the headline ranges in the effect table. Rework and escalation are piecewise constant for long stretches, so the panel has fewer independent outcome shifts than 40 branch-months; that measurement grain is never used to discount the event-study or clustered standard errors. The equal-weight Delta/Epsilon donor mixes a low-complexity path with Epsilon’s rise to 38% complexity; leave-one-out keeps the sign but is not a complexity-aligned contrast. The design names Gamma as an optional not-yet-treated donor and never reports that contrast. Gamma’s escalation contrast is rounded to +0.88 where the displayed donor change of 1.13 implies +0.87. Escalation confidence of 55 is high beside post-adoption plateaus and the May mix shift.

The rollout choice is directionally sound and under-specified at the decision rule. Starting with Delta because complexity is lower and staffing recently rose recreates selection on operating conditions and delays learning in the high-complexity setting most relevant to Epsilon. “P90 rises over 10%” is not clearly tied to concurrent controls, and untreated month-to-month p90 moves already exceed 10%. Scale-up can be triggered by a 5% median gain or a 5% productivity gain, and productivity is not an outcome in the historical panel.
