6.3

The completed analysis is technically serious and often correct, but the document is unfinished and contains several real semantic and methodological errors. It cannot be treated as nearly flawless.

The answer stops mid-sentence in §10. Model S’s retirement conditions are stated; Model C’s recommendation is cut off; deployment conditions for the third model are missing. §11 is absent, so there is no dissenting rebuttal and no statement of what evidence would change the decision. Those are required parts of the deliverable, not optional close.

Where the analysis is present, the probe readings for S and C are largely right: S accepts B3 and B4 because the four reviews are optional and concurrent; C’s eight sequences reject B2 and B5 and force a full CC-then-TR replay after the single correction branch; B8 is outside both languages. The pathology ranking correctly treats optionalized mandatory checks, positive-only precision, and permissive token-based generalization as decision hazards rather than quality. The third design is a real alternative: initial `+(CC, TR)`, `*(tau, correction)` for zero or more corrections, one approval, and an explicit post-hoc layer. A1–A5 do separate it from both submissions. The claim that unit-cost fitness can hide a skipped mandatory check is sound.

The errors that hold the score down:

- Fitness and precision are conflated. Training fitness 0.973 means log behavior is outside C; precision 0.941 means model behavior is outside the log. The sentence that both show “training behavior absent from C” reverses the precision direction.
- Holdout fitness 0.744 is then glossed as “about a quarter of unseen traces need repair,” despite the answer’s own admission that log-level versus trace-average fitness is unknown.
- `decide(..., external=None)` appends an R6 flag on every trace, so B1, B2, B5, and B7 return `accept_with_flags`. §5 and §8 call those clean accepts. The prose says the flag applies to traces without Supplier Review; the code flags traces with it as well. The flag therefore does not mark the cases it is said to mark.
- R5 hard-rejects an empty recheck. P6 requires that only affected checks repeat; it does not say a correction must repeat at least one check. That case should be escalated, not encoded as a violation.
- Supplier Review is placed after both checks and all corrections. P3 does not require that order. `RD, SR, CC, TR, MA, RO` is rejected by the third model and by C, and the limitation is not disclosed. The same is true of a check interleaved with supplier review.
- The stratified split puts every singleton variant in training and keeps every test variant in training, so it cannot measure unseen-variant generalization. §9.5 does not say this. The perturbation metric also counts a now-forbidden edit of a valid trace as a sensitivity failure.
- Smaller faults: B5 is called valid without qualification; both B6 rejections are called non-policy, which is inaccurate for C; N3 is omitted from the zero-acceptance target; the SR/ER `X(tau, …)` wrapper is redundant; “25-fold” is 24.4.

§1–§9 would be a strong draft. As a governance document it is incomplete, and its executable decision procedure does not match the behavior table.
