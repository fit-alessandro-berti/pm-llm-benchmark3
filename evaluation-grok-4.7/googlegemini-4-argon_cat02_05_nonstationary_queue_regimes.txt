6.5

The regime framework is strong, but the write-up is not reliable enough for a higher score. It separates weekday and weekend load, the 15 April outage, the 16–17 April recovery, the 20 April mix shift, and the 29 April staffing change, and it correctly refuses to treat recovery completions as a normal capacity regime. Little’s Law verdicts for the stable baseline, the outage, the drain, and the overloaded weekdays are directionally defensible, and the pooled-staffing policy trap is the right operational point. Those strengths are undercut by conservation and arithmetic failures.

The central logical gap is that cumulative arrivals minus completions is treated as a physical backlog. The text claims about +106 cases by 28 April and “~120+” cases entering 29 April, and the pooled Little’s Law row says the horizon leaves a large stock in system. Measured `mean_wip` does not support that stock. It is about 18 on 6 April and 19 on 3 May, and overloaded weekdays sit near 37–50, not 120. Day-level changes also break simple conservation: 16 April has completions above arrivals while average WIP rises, and 24–26 April WIP falls sharply while net flow stays positive despite the stated rule that nothing is served overnight. Net flow is a valid imbalance indicator. It is not, on these aggregates, an identified backlog level. That error leaks into recovery timing, the “cases left in system” claim, and the WIP target in the recommendations.

The comparison table does not foot. Arrivals sum to 2519 and completions to 2409, so net accumulation is +110, about +3.9 per day, not 2520, 2444, +76, and +2.71. Weekday completions average 103.15, not 101.2. Weekday net flow and the all-days row are therefore wrong, and weekday plus weekend completions and net flows do not equal the pooled totals. Several medians are also wrong: pooled arrival median is 103.5 rather than 104.5, service-time median is 31.5 rather than 32.5, WIP median is 23 rather than 27.5, and the weekday completion median is 103 rather than 101. R4’s own net imbalance is +94, not the +96 stated in the segmentation. On 21 April the running intraday net peaks at +27, not +39.

Smaller but repeated precision problems remain. Lognormal means are a labeled assumption, yet utilization is quoted to a tenth of a percent and sometimes called utilization when the offered load is above 1. The R1 cell subtracts median service from mean sojourn to get a waiting time, which the audit itself says not to do. A stationary six-server system at roughly two-thirds offered load cannot hold a WIP near 20; that tension is never used to challenge `E[S]`, productive FTE, or the WIP definition. Welch’s t ignores the serial dependence the same section reports. Narrative Fano factors and standard deviations do not match the sample-variance definitions in the code, so the prose and the required script are not one calculation.

The code does satisfy the requested mechanics: in-memory tables, regime summaries, dispersion and lag indicators, a non-definitive split heuristic, windowed Little’s Law, and an explicit statement that these aggregates are not a PM4Py event log. The analysis is well organized and often operationally insightful. It is not numerically or conservationally tight.
