6.8

The final report is a strong, structured analysis that gets most case outcomes right, but it is not nearly flawless. Several required distinctions are missed or over-resolved, and the checking code would not reliably produce the network evidence it claims.

The best judgments are the clear violations and identity splits. CHG-502, CHG-508, and CHG-510 are correctly treated as drafter–approver breaches after alias normalization. CHG-507 is correctly unauthorized because Eva was still only an independent verifier on 9 August and had no QM delegation. CHG-510 correctly rejects D-IC-04 as prohibited subdelegation and also rejects Ben’s cooling-system retrospective as outside D-QM-01. CHG-503 and CHG-509 are properly separated by session strength. CHG-506 is rightly an identity problem rather than automatic misconduct. The calendar, the two-business-day count that makes 14 August late, and the refusal to treat the Clara–Ben pattern as proof are sound. All seven required sections and the case columns are present, and collusion is handled with benign and adverse alternatives.

The classification scheme is not followed carefully. VALID_SUBSTITUTION is never used. CHG-501 is a QE acting under D-QM-01, so the apparent deviation is a valid substitution, not a standing authorization. Calling it AUTHORIZED with high confidence also underweights the fact that the QMS record did not exist until 12 August. CHG-509 has the same problem: weekend off-duty is not established “absence,” Asha’s leave had ended, and the report’s own rule section calls that reading interpretive, yet the case row still says AUTHORIZED at moderate-to-high confidence. CHG-504 is a closer call, but AUTHORIZED is still stronger than the unresolved credential-sharing evidence supports.

A material fact is missed in the written findings. Ben’s sick leave runs through 14 August 23:59, and CHG-505’s retrospective is at 14 August 16:00. The report discusses sick-leave identity only for CHG-506, so it misses a second disputed use of Ben’s account on an already late, out-of-scope emergency review. The CI-44-only limit is also equated with every cooling_system event, and RR4’s demand for a credible safety rationale is not carried into the missing-evidence column. Section 7 then says “seventeen of 23” after section 4 correctly used 18 of 23. Ranking that pattern above CHG-505 or CHG-503 as a top investigation is weakly justified.

The code covers the requested libraries and checks, but delegation validity never tests the principal-absence condition, the business-day calculation is timestamp-fragile, and handover and working-together results are parsed as dictionaries. In current PM4Py those calls return NetworkX graphs, so the required network evidence falls into the exception path.
