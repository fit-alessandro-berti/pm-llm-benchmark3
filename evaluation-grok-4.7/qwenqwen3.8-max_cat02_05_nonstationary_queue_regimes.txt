7.5

The final write-up is a strong process-mining analysis: the required headings and tables are present, regimes are separated by demand, mix, outage, recovery, and staffing, recovery completions are not treated as steady capacity, and Little’s Law is limited to approximate use on the baseline windows with explicit units and boundary caveats. The reproducible code matches that design and correctly refuses to treat the daily aggregates as an event log.

The score is held down by numerical and consistency defects in the final tables. Post-change `complex_pct` is given as 43.9 in both the alternative segmentation and the pooled comparison, while the stated method is an unweighted mean of daily values, which is 43.3 (606/14). Baseline weekday median service is reported as 24.9 rather than 25.0. In the burstiness table, ordinary-day completions are given as CV 0.40 and Fano 1.31; the vector’s population moments are about 0.43 and 1.56. Those prose figures would also disagree with the code if it were run. The Little’s Law matrix leaves the baseline comparison operator blank (“Arrivals  completions”), and the recovery row’s window includes 15 April non-outage hours while the displayed flow time uses only 16–17 April. The large gap between baseline implied flow time (~2.4 opening hours) and median active service (~25 minutes) is noted in general but not used as a diagnostic of waiting, multi-step sojourn, or snapshot coverage.
