The arithmetic, section structure, and causal restraint are strong. Baseline deltas, the six fragment distances, and the March raw-to-mapped gap of 0.35 all match the tables. March is treated as a logging and semantic break, while May rework, post-June queue growth, the July priority rule, and the October renewal are given separate onsets, confidence levels, and tests. Sections 2 and 9 fall inside the required word ranges. The eight hypotheses, six discrimination cuts, segmentation support rules, twelve monitors, and seven decision rules cover the requested mechanisms.

These defects keep it short of a near-flawless score:

1. The section 1 separator has six cells under a seven-column header, so the required table is not valid Markdown.

2. The fragment L1 adds `Assess -> Request Info` and `Request Info -> Collect Docs`, which move almost one-for-one. That double-counts one loop: about 24 of May’s 40 points and about 34 of July’s 59. Month order is unchanged, but the magnitudes overstate how many distinct relations moved. The falling decision edge is also under-interpreted. Longer cycles can reduce terminal-edge rates through incomplete observation. Censoring appears later, but not in the distance write-up, where it affects 11 of July’s 59 points.

3. Part B stores UTC timestamps and then calls `pm4py.filter_time_range` with naive timestamp strings. Aware-versus-naive comparison fails in current pandas, so `event_window` can error before the half-open filter. The `MonthBegin` step and the duplicate-mask contract are otherwise sound.

4. One rank list scores hypotheses that answer different questions. A section 1 strength of 55 for redistribution sits beside section 9’s “low confidence” without a shared scale. Rule 4 fires on a relative wait shift and omits the complexity adjustment that section 5 requires, although priority is assigned from complexity.

5. “Confirmed” duplicate removal is stronger than the mapped-fitness definition. “Independent patterns” in section 2 is stronger than that same paragraph’s warning that the operational metrics were not normalized.

6. Benjamini–Hochberg on one monthly family does not handle serial dependence. Separating critical alerts only partly addresses that.

The residual mapped-fitness decline of about 0.06 by July is reported and is not dismissed as logging. No fabricated monthly figure turned up in the baseline table or the L1 sums.
