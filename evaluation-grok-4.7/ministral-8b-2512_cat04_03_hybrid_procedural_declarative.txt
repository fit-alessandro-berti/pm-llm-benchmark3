3.1

The write-up has the required ten sections and a few sound placements, but the three control-flow artifacts are mutually unsatisfiable, the PM4Py code cannot run, and the test matrix misses the layer split those cases were built to expose.

What is sound is narrow. R5 versus R6 is recognized as a real conflict. R9 is correctly left out of activity-only models. R12’s three pairs are converted to seconds correctly, and the warning-versus-reject distinction is stated. `noncoexistence` of Reject with Activate, and of the two approval routes, matches R1 and R8. `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` matches R7. T8’s overall failure is the contradictory terminals.

Those points do not rescue the model.

**Policy choice is not one policy.** Option 2 drops the joint authorization and treats “opinion within 24 hours” as sufficient. The implemented choice is then described as Option 2 while the procedural layer still demands opinion-before-activation. That is the silent merge the task forbids. The 24-hour emergency bound is also conflated with R12’s escalation-to-opinion band (mean 12 h, sd 6 h, ζ = 2 → 24 h). R6’s clock starts from the emergency activation; R12’s clock starts at escalation. T6’s opinion is 10 h after activation and 17 h after escalation, so it meets both clocks and still cannot pass, because the authorization is not in the log.

**DECLARE is unsatisfiable and uses the wrong templates.** `exactly_one` on both Manager Approval and Compliance Approval, together with `noncoexistence` of that same pair, accepts no trace. `absence` on Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor, Release First Payment, and Close Onboarding forbids every legal ending. Unary values are bare floats; the required `{support: 1.0, confidence: ...}` shape is missing. R4 is response (escalation obliges a later opinion), not precedence. R3’s “before the next request” is alternate response; `chainresponse` demands the immediate next event, and `nonchainsuccession` only forbids adjacency, so it does not keep an open request from reaching approval. `precedence(Classify Risk, Request Clarification)` forbids T2’s legal order, and `precedence(Receive Clarification, Classify Risk)` rejects every case that never asks a question. The explanation table assigns precedence the meaning of response and chain response the meaning of alternate response.

**The log skeleton is also unsatisfiable.** `activ_freq` of `{1}` means exactly once. R10 is at most once, so the sets must be `{0, 1}`. Exactly-once Reject and exactly-once Activate plus `never_together` of that pair accepts nothing. `never_together` of Request/Receive Clarification with Classify Risk, the approvals, and Activate Vendor rejects every completed clarification, including T2. `always_before` from Obtain Legal Opinion to Activate Vendor, and from Activate Vendor or Release First Payment to Close Onboarding, cannot hold on both the accepted path and the rejected path. `(Verify Bank Account, Verify Bank Account)` is a vacuous equivalence. `directly_follows` omits the legal interleavings of the three concurrent checks and their edges into Classify Risk, so T1 itself is illegal. `always_after` is oriented as (later, earlier) while `always_before` is (earlier, later). The prose then says the skeleton allows Request directly before Classify; the set forbids those activities from co-occurring at all.

**The procedural code is not a PM4Py accepting net.** Places are given activity names, transitions are unlabeled routers, and there is no final marking. `PetriNet.places.add("start")`, string markings, and `conversion.log.converter.apply(petri_net=...)` are not the API. One token in `start` makes Capture, Screening, and Verify a conflict, not an AND-split, and there is no AND-join before Classify Risk. Clarification places are never marked. The post-activation cycle returns to Activate Vendor, which breaks R10, and it does not implement R11’s later Verify-before-Release. Petri nets can express unbounded loops; the text says they cannot.

**Resource code does not check the rules it names.** The four-eyes helper compares an activity pair with `resource` value counts, so it never fires, and it labels that check R4. R4 is the opinion obligation; four-eyes is R9. The emergency branch never tests 24 hours, treats a missing opinion as non-violation (`min()` of an empty series), and has no fail-closed behavior for a missing `resource` or authorization attribute. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is unused.

**The matrix contradicts both the artifacts and the cases.** Under the dictionaries as written, T1–T3 fail DECLARE and the skeleton, yet they are marked pass. T4 is a pure R9 failure: the activity sequence is T1’s, so procedural, DECLARE, and skeleton stay pass. T7 obeys R5 (opinion at 40, activation at 43); elapsed escalation-to-opinion time is 34 h, not 40 h, which is a temporal warning, not a control-flow fail, and 2 h to payment is inside 72 ± 2·36. T6 is not a temporal warning: 17 h is inside the ζ = 2 band. The second T6 row marks the resource layer pass under R6 and the overall verdict warning while still failing procedural, DECLARE, and skeleton. R6 cannot pass from this log. T5’s missing reply is not a temporal-profile hit; that pair is absent, and the payment gap matches T1.

Section 9 never mentions the unsatisfiable cores (`exactly_one` with `noncoexistence`, `{1}` with `never_together`). It claims support is low where the dictionary sets support to 1.0, and it says redundancy is harmless. Deployment then names that skeleton as the authoritative policy layer and also says to prefer the procedural net when DECLARE disagrees, which leaves R3, R5, and R9 without a single owner.
