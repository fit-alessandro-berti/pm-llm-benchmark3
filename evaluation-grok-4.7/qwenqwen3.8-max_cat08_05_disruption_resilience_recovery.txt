The final analysis is complete, numerically faithful, and decision-useful, but it is not nearly flawless.

Sections 1–7 are all present and aligned with the evidence. Baseline, manual, partial-recovery, and stabilization volumes, cycle times, exception rates, and conformance figures are stated correctly. The A–L table uses the right columns and the right disposition logic: hospital triage, manual authorization, deferral, backlog ordering, and reconciliation are treated as conditional resilience, while skipped authorization and unscanned dispatch are treated as control loss. The recovery model includes triggers, risk-based triage, minimum controls, manual evidence, backlog rules, and a separate reconciliation/audit path, and it correctly refuses to treat later remediation as real-time compliance. Indicators cover throughput, service, traceability, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness, and the recommendations carry effort, benefit, and residual risk.

The main weakness is classification clarity. Several adaptations, especially A, F, I, J, and L, are placed in more than one of “true resilience,” “shortcut,” and “ambiguous,” so the required distinction is softer than the evidence allows. The timeline also under-specifies the manual-phase Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk cycle times, and it does not use the stated temporal-profile deviations (backlog wait beyond baseline p95 and highly variable phone-proof delay) with the same precision as the audit tail. A few DFG readings are slightly loose, notably “3,920 downstream pick activities” and “2,600 manual-phase dispatches,” and “shifted volume” is unclear. Proposed numeric gates are reasonable design choices, but several are asserted without being tied back to the baseline distributions.
