The primary survival analysis is unusually careful and, on the quantities I recomputed, numerically accurate. Time origin, the 72-hour horizon, competing events, administrative censoring, and already-observed breaches are separated correctly. The code implements that estimator without dropping open cases.

The score stays well below excellent because of estimand errors in the sensitivity layer.

T3 and T4 are labeled “lower bound on E1” and “upper bound on E2.” They are scenario-specific Aalen–Johansen imputations, 0.621 and 0.456. The assumption-free bounds, which the same answer computes elsewhere, are wider: E1 can fall to 0.50 if the two pre-72 transfers are credited as resolved and the five open cases younger than 72 all resolve, and E2 can rise to 0.50 if those transfers remain pending and those five cases are all still open. Calling the imputations bounds contradicts the answer’s own [Est] versus [Bound] distinction.

C1 places a “silent exit” at `t − gap`. For S11, S25, and S26 that instant is before 72 hours, while the idle interval runs through 72 and the case is still recorded open after 72. The 72-hour status is not identified; the coding asserts a disposition the extract does not show. That is why C1 E2 = 0.271 falls through the stated 28% floor. Section 6 then says that “at least 28% have no disposition at 72 hours” does not reverse, despite C1 in the same table.

Two smaller faults sit on top of that. The in-sample conditional risks for S10 and S29 are S(72)/S(47) = S(72)/S(51) = 0.655, so 0.65 to two decimals, not 0.66. “P1 is worst on E2 in every scenario” fails under C1: recoding S11 and S25 drops P1’s E2 to about 0.19, below P2 and P3. The 1.000 naive Kaplan–Meier limit is also partly an artifact of S13, the last time point, being a resolution.

S06’s E3 cell (“stays in the denominator”) does not match a cumulative-incidence increment, and E4’s definition mixes hazards with the cumulative incidences actually reported. Those are wording defects. The primary recommendation — Aalen–Johansen occupation probabilities, with landmarking held back until timestamps exist — remains the right analysis for these 32 cases.
