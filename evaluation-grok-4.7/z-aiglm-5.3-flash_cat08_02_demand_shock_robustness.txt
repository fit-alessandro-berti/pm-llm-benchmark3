7.5

The final write-up meets every required section and is unusually well grounded: the mix decomposition (routine weekly volume flat; urgent ~2.3× and complex ~2.2×; MV entries ~2.6×, holds ~5.6×, batch-wait ~4.1×), DFG share shifts, queue nonlinearity, and variant evidence are calculated correctly and used to reject average-cycle-time optimization, blanket capacity, and simultaneous priority for every case type. Downstream overload, on/off triggers, redesign-caused risks, and leading-versus-lagging signals are all present.

It is not nearly flawless. Pooled performance-edge medians are treated as urgent-specific and added (technical validation → medical validation 24h plus medical validation → release 5h = 29h, hence “structurally unachievable”). Those edges are not case-type stratified, medians are not additive, and the peak urgent median cycle time of 17h directly contradicts naive summation. The same pattern appears in the 16.5h “before checking starts” construction. A second quantitative hole is left unresolved: ~2.6× medical-validation entries cannot sit beside 93% utilization unless capacity already expanded sharply, yet both figures are used as if compatible. Smaller faults add up: “every station ≥93%” is false (several pools are 85–91%), an M2 outage “roughly doubles” the assay queue only if the machines are equal (not given), “~2 medical validators” is invented, overtime is over-causally tied to the accession load, and early triage is credited with cutting assay repeats rather than recollection loops. Slack-based dispatch also collapses toward the strict urgent-first rule the package rejects, and the quota fix is asserted more than stress-tested.
