The audit is strong, complete, and correctly organized around the required five sections. It covers every top-12 feature, every sequence pattern, and every local-only feature; it keeps queue length and elapsed time as admissible signals; it separates the legally repeated `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` pattern from the same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` artifact; and it consistently distinguishes prediction, causation, actionability, and fairness. The case JSON is valid, does not narrate leaked futures as facts, and Case Z is properly quarantined rather than rewritten.

The score is held down by several concrete errors.

The most important is a process-model mistake: `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is treated as the fast-track path and as a disguised fast-track indicator. Under the stated process, that succession is the ordinary path after damage assessment for both fast-track and evidence-cycle claims. Fast track is the skip of the document cycle, not that edge. The proposed substitute, low damage complexity, therefore repairs the wrong signal.

`same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6` is called impossible because no `Documents Requested` has occurred. The lineage only supports that a 23:00 snapshot can include later uploads. Registration-time uploads can exist without a document-request event, so impossibility is not established. The leakage finding itself is right.

`remaining_slack_hours = 240 − elapsed` is stated as if the 10-day target were 240 clock hours, while the same answer later says the calendar-versus-business-day definition is unsettled.

`initial_reserve` and `claimant_channel` are placed in `credible_drivers` while their point-in-time lineage is still unattested. `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, whose defect is known, is correctly excluded. That split is inconsistent with “defensibly available at t0.”

The narratives overstate what removal implies. Residuals of about +0.20 remain, and retraining would reallocate importance, so “the score will very likely fall substantially” and “surviving drivers are small” are stronger than the attributions support. Case Z’s status says the whole trace ordering is unreconstructable when only one same-timestamp pair lacks a sequence index.

Smaller technical slips remain. `NotChainSuccession` is used as if it were the natural statement of terminality, and `CoExistence(Appeal, second decision)` is too strong because an appeal need not already have produced a reconsideration. A negative directly-follows edge is read as “documents arrived promptly,” which a binary pattern does not say. The fairness plan twice invokes a “4% rule,” which confuses the four-fifths rule.

None of these overturn the main leakage inventory or the appeal-versus-ordering distinction, but they are real inaccuracies in a process-aware audit.
