7.2

The write-up meets the required eight-part structure and is strong on regime logic: outage is separated from recovery, recovery is not treated as normal high throughput, mix, calendar, capacity, and backlog are distinguished, and Little’s Law is refused where the boundary conditions fail. Most regime means, traffic-intensity lower bounds, and hourly dispersion figures check out.

It is not nearly flawless. The primary-regime evidence cells are corrupted where arrows dropped out, so “52→35”, “43→36→32”, “22→19”, and “2.5→3.0” read as 5235, 433632, 2219, and 2.53.0. The claim that `peak_08_10_pct` aligns with 09:00–10:59 fits 2026-04-07 and 2026-04-25, but not 2026-04-21 (about 36.5% versus the reported 47). Pre–20 April complexity is given as an arrival-weighted ~21%, while both the weighted and unweighted figures are about 22.5%, and the code computes an unweighted mean. Implied sojourn times of roughly 147 and 239 minutes in the low-utilization baseline regimes are left unreconciled with \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) near 0.6 and with active handling of 25–29 minutes; that tension matters because these are multi-step cases, so system time and active service are different objects. The 16 April rise in WIP from 47 to 52 while completions exceeded arrivals is also missed. Smaller slips include weekend hourly variance stated as 4.98 rather than about 5.02, and calling outage-day service times backlog-affected even though those fields exclude waiting.
