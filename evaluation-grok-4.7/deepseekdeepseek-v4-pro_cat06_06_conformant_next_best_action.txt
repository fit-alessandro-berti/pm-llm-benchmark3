The final recommendation correctly keeps immediate execution and self-certification at the bottom, treats the override as not yet enabled, and prefers a conformant independent second review with parallel rescreening and an explicit P-901 spillover tradeoff. That core judgment is sound, but the final conclusions are not reliable enough for a high score.

The counterfactual and the reranking trigger both say that a resolved match plus a score below 70 permits Auto Clear and abandonment of second review. Auto Clear is available only with no alert and a score below 40. A score from 40 to 69 still requires the analyst approval path; only the extra second-officer gate drops, and only if the fuzzy match is actually resolved. Recommending Auto Clear in that band would skip a mandatory control. Section 4 says rescreening does not replace review while the triggers remain, so the final recommendation also contradicts the conformance section.

The T+50 fallback cannot support the claimed high chance of meeting the 90-minute deadline or the “low” breach risk. A review takes 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute, so an officer who starts at T+50 meets the deadline only on the shortest path. The typical path breaches, and the stated earliest continuation does not match that cutoff. Calling this plan dominant over interrupting S2 overstates a real tradeoff between P-884’s deadline and P-901’s spillover.

The fallback is also inconsistent: if the expedited screening call cannot be started, the stated fallback is Action D, which itself requires that call. The benefit and harm cells stay qualitative despite the supplied euro and probability ranges, the 0.55–0.75 approval probability and the 12-minute-old score are barely used, and a hold while a report is filed is largely missing from the outcome trees.
