6.5

The pipeline is complete and the in-memory tables are mostly referentially sound, but the write-up misstates several consequences of its own relations and adds facts the narrative does not support.

The code does include all 12 objects and all 17 timezone-aware events, keeps 100 and 150 on the payment events, repeats activity and timestamp onto every E2O row, and checks uniqueness, orphans, activity/timestamp agreement, and duplicate relations. Cross-order packing is encoded correctly: `e6` and `e7` touch `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200`; `e8` stays on `I2`/`O100`; `V1` bills both orders; `M1` and `M2` point at `V1`. Flattened trace membership for Order, Package, Delivery, and Invoice largely follows those links, and the Payment-flattening critique is right: each payment object has a one-event trace.

The O2O vocabulary misses a required distinction. Package–item links use `contains`, the same qualifier as Order–item, instead of `packs`. Those two business relations cannot be separated by qualifier. The audit then calls package–item cardinality N-to-M. In this data each item sits in one package, so the observed pattern is one package to many items.

Several analytical claims contradict the tables. `I2` is absent from `Reserve Stock`, so the Item directly-follows graph includes `Add Item` → `Pack Package` for `I2` and `Add Item` → `Reserve Stock` → `Pack Package` only for `I1` and `I3`. Section 5 gives every item the reserved path. `O100` co-occurs with `I3` and `O200` on `e6`, `e7`, and `e11`; `O200` co-occurs with `I1` and `O100`. The interaction summary drops those pairs and therefore misses the convergence the case is built around. Section 3 says `e12` and `e15` radiate into both order processes. Those events are linked only to `Payment` and `Invoice`, and section 4 correctly excludes them from the order traces.

Order and invoice discovery is over-simplified. `O100` has `Add Item` → `Add Item` and `Pack Package` → `Pack Package`. The invoice object has `Record Payment` → `Record Payment`. Section 4’s invoice DFG includes the second edge; section 5’s OC-DFG drops it. The object-centric Petri-net paragraph says the net “correctly” synchronizes every pack as one token pattern, which both over-determines a discovery result and ignores `I2`.

Two reviewer checks fail on inspection. `100 + 150 = 250` is labeled the invoice total. The narrative gives payment amounts only and calls both payments partial; it never states an invoice total. The orphan check asks for a strict subset of object identifiers. Every object appears in `relations` or `o2o`, so the union is equal to the object set.

Section 1 also overreaches: withholding dispatch, confirmation, and payment from orders does not make order throughput match physical fulfillment. The version note (`≥ 2.3.0 / 2.7.x`, plus a second import path) is too loose to isolate a real constructor difference.
