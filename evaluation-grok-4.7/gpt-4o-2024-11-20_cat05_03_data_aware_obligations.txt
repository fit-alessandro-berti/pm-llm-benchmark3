The write-up keeps the seven required headings and a few right instincts, but the actual decision work is unfinished and several of the decisions it does make are wrong. That is a failed assessment, not a near-miss.

**Section 1.** Blanks as “no update,” no backward copy of the final case, and `event_index` as the same-timestamp tie-break are stated correctly. The rest is too loose to use. “Material change” is expanded to jurisdiction updates, which R4 does not do. Effective time is described as routinely different from event time; the policy makes event time controlling unless evidence establishes another effective time. The four time notions are named, not operationalized.

**Section 2.** The table is only loosely aligned with the requested columns, and the pseudo-logic does not evaluate obligations. `amount/2`, `jurisdiction/2`, and `risk_level/2` are untimed, so they cannot express “latest value at release.” R1 is encoded as a `release_funds` success rule that *requires* `not valid_exemption`, so a valid waiver makes the clause fail. R5 treats `Record_Exemption` as the approval and never checks expiry, amount cap, customer type, or the difference between a displayed backdate and proof that approval existed at the trigger. R3 drops the real trigger (list addition after the latest screen and before release, for the jurisdiction current at release). R4 does not define the baseline (amount known at the latest review), the strict “more than 20 percent” test, the rule that a decrease does not invalidate, or the unknown-baseline case. R2 ignores the instruction that a standard-packet `Compliance_Approve` does not cure a missing `Enhanced_Review`.
