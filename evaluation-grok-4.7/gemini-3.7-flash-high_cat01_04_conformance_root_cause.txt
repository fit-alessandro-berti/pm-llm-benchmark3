6.4

The write-up is complete, well structured, and right about the main June collapse: label remapping, dropping `Queue Routed`, then source ordering recover fitness in that order, while August’s remaining gap is largely an unupdated model plus an optional sign-off that can overfit. Counts for the listed variants, the 620/530/70/210 splits, and the staged 32 → 58 → 81 → 89 and 66 → 86 figures match the brief. The code encodes the given tree, builds both nets, expands the variant tables, discovers DFGs, and compares a normalized log with an optional sign-off model, including the EUR 50,000 overgeneralization warning. Governance covers all six required topics.

Those strengths do not make the argument reliable.

The load-bearing error is treating unresolved residuals as established behavior. The brief states that no audit has shown whether the 25 and 30 enhanced-review traces without `Compliance Check` are bypasses or missing events. Sections 3 and 7 nevertheless say those 55 cases “bypassed” the check, and the conclusion puts 85% confidence on genuine compliance vulnerabilities. The July 10 memo is scored as support for noncompliance at 75 even though it reports no known rise in violations. Posterior 78 for employee noncompliance is far too high for evidence the answer itself says is unaudited. Confirmed-incident stability is barely used as contrary evidence.

“Real behavioral process change” is mis-specified and ranked last (75). The August policy is a real process change; model obsolescence is the failure to encode it. The 60 standard-review sign-offs are what an amount-based rule predicts, not evidence of rogue escalation. Manual override is classified as supporting a technical-event artifact even though IT says it is a real user action.

Section 4 has decision-relevant mistakes. A model that requires sign-off before every `Decision` would reject the many traces with no sign-off and lower fitness; the row describes that repair but predicts the optional model’s rise from 66% to 86%. The precision move “from ~0.92 to >0.98” is invented. “+3.1% and +5.6%, reaching ~95%” does not add. Resource enforcement is predicted to cut mean events from 6.8 back to 5.4, but those extra events are recorded activities, not missing resource fields. Stable KPIs are used in the ledger and ranking as if they supported logging artifacts and weakened real change, then correctly described in section 3 as proving neither claim.

The causal graph invents `reviewer_workload_fatigue`, routes systematic `Queue Routed` emission through hidden “routing glitches,” and draws `UI_release → true_process` after the ledger treats the release as behavior-preserving. The “weakens” column often treats coexisting mechanisms as mutually exclusive. “66%” is the post-cleaning August fitness, not raw August conformance. June’s listed variants average about 6.12 events per case, not the stated 6.3; that reconciliation is missed.

The required code never computes alignment diagnostics, only aggregate fitness. The caveat hardcodes 55.6% → 89.5% (perfect-trace rates on the aggregates) while calling them fitness and while the prose uses 66% → 86%. Phase 4 names dubious helpers (`generalization_tbr`, `simplicity_petri_net`) and drifts into governance instead of isolating a hypothesis. The order repair swaps adjacent `Notify, Decision` and thereby assumes the defect under test.

Format, coverage, and the staged-artifact story are strong. Calibration, a few counterfactuals, and several asserted mechanisms are not.
