The answer is strong on structure, mechanism, and decision design, but it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative claims are internally inconsistent or arithmetically wrong, and the inferential confidence outruns the design.

The required skeleton is complete: availability is separated from intensity, staggered selection and co-occurring shocks are described without a causal claim in §1, the seven estimands cover ITT, dose, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, and dynamic/heterogeneous effects, and SUTVA plus multi-version treatment are explicit. Most pre/post arithmetic checks out, including Alpha/Beta April on-impact DiDs (−1.57h median, −2.20 pp rework, +0.90 pp escalation), Gamma’s June–July DiDs (−3.65h median, −6.70h p90, −2.00 pp rework, +1.00 pp escalation), and the long-window Alpha-versus-Epsilon median contrast. Median and p90 are not collapsed. The rollout choice, case-level logs, fraud-desk monitoring, and refusal of a single “reduces cycle time by X%” line match the decision problem.

The quantitative core slips. Gamma’s January–June load is about 32.7 cases per FTE, not 32.9. The §5 bounds do not follow from the cited inputs: −2.5h to −3.7h does not contain the −1.57h early DiD used to justify it; the “upper” median bound is given as −6.5h to −8.0h and then as −6.9h to −8.2h; the adverse p90 scenario is +2h to +7h in §5, +6.9h to +9.5h raw and −11h to +4h in §7. Fraud-desk contamination of controls biases a DiD benefit by about S, not 2S, and that 2S adjustment is not actually applied to the −6.5h p90 DiD. March movements are not a common +2h to +3h rise: Beta is +0.5h and Epsilon is +0.9h, so the parallel-pre-trend premise is overstated. Calling continuous cycle-time scaling an “arithmetic fact” is false; Alpha’s median rises from 48.3h to 53.5h while fast-path share rises from 25% to 39%. Regression-to-the-mean is not refuted: Gamma adopts at its own peak (45.4h, 45.9h), and August declines are shared.

Identification is softer than the labels imply. Rework and escalation steps are real and replicated, but they are perfectly timed with the routing campaign, so E4 is not “Yes” identified as an automation effect. Aggregate rework can fall mechanically when cases move onto a no-rework path; confidence 85, and escalation confidence 90 with “no counterevidence,” ignore that bundle. The dose model’s clustered p < 0.01 is not credible: branch and month fixed effects plus covariates are about 14 parameters on 5 clusters, so the cluster-robust covariance is rank-deficient, which §6 and the code warnings only partly admit. FTE is treated as a confounder in the adjustment and as a possible consequence of automation in the mechanism table; those are different estimands. Callaway–Sant’Anna, stacked DiD, and imputation are named as one primary strategy and then not estimated. The code builds relative-time indicators and instead fits the binary TWFE specification the text rejects. The 80/20 within-Epsilon holdout reintroduces shared specialist and fraud-desk interference, the main spillover the answer itself treats as central. Gamma at 32% is not steady state.

The scientific bottom line—do not publish one percentage, separate median from tail, and expand only inside an experiment—is sound. The score is held down by bound errors, the 2S mistake, overclaimed pre-trends, and confidence levels the design cannot support.
