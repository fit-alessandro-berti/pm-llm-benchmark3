4.5

The design correctly refuses to code rejected or withdrawn cases as non-default and drops the obvious post-decision fields, but it is not a coherent study. The primary estimand, the cohort arithmetic, the censoring taxonomy, the as-of rule, and the code disagree with each other, and several of those disagreements would produce a misleading result if implemented.

## 1. Target definition
The chosen family — 90-day default conditional on activation — is the right primary family. The write-up then contradicts it. The unit is activated accounts, while the population is all 48,000 applications. Prediction time is “the first event after KYC,” which is before activation, so P(default | prefix, eventual activation) conditions on a selection event that has not happened yet. That post-prefix selection is the central identification problem, and it is never stated.

The horizon ignores the stated 7–35 day feed lag, so “90 days after activation” is not when `default_90d` becomes known. The alternative is not a clean competing-outcome target: still-open is censoring, rejected is missing from the state list, and Fine-Gray is invoked while the text simultaneously talks about a counterfactual default that a competing-risk model of observed exits does not require. Time-to-default with administrative censoring and a clearly labeled policy counterfactual are not developed.

## 2. Cohort construction
The labeled export is 21,600 = 18,900 non-defaults + 2,700 defaults. The table sets the labeled row to 18,900 and writes “Default=0/2,700” in the status cell. The displayed counts sum to 45,300, not 48,000. The narrative repeats the false claim that 18,900 cases have `default_90d`.

Transferred cases are marked “no default possible,” which erases both the 60% linkage and the possibility of follow-up under a new identifier. There is no case-level as-of rule tying activation time, the 90-day horizon, feed delay, and the 15 February 2026 extract. Partner accounts with permanently missing feeds do not appear in the flow.

## 3. Censoring and missing labels
The four mechanisms are named and then assigned incorrectly. Still-open applications have not started the post-activation clock, so they are not ordinary right censoring of `default_90d`. The 3,600 immature activated accounts mix “horizon not reached” with “horizon reached, feed not in.” Transfer is treated as a delayed label rather than as a competing exit and/or loss to follow-up. Permanently missing partner feeds are called a subset of the 21,600 labeled rows; a permanent miss is unlabeled by definition.

Kaplan-Meier or Cox is proposed for a target that was defined as a binary probability. KM does not use the prefix. Neither method is tied to a landmark: features are taken before activation, while the clock would start at activation. The sensitivity analysis single-imputes transfers at 2,700/21,600. That forces a competing exit into the activated-only estimand under a strong and unstated MAR assumption. Policy shift is called testable via an interaction; migration is described as if it were already known not to affect default risk. Independent censoring, linkage-based missingness, and permanent partner missingness are not classified carefully.

## 4. Prefix extraction
Three different prediction times appear: the first post-KYC event, the first five events after KYC, events within seven days after KYC, and one global timestamp of 15 October 2025. A fixed length of five can include Accept, Contract Signed, activation, and collections. Requiring five events conditions on remaining in process that long. Padding short cases with nulls is not a prefix policy.

The seven-day window is justified as a fix for `documents_total`. The actual defect is same-calendar-day snapshot leakage, which a longer window makes worse. Renamed activities are left as-is, so the 15 October 2025 migration splits one activity into two vocabularies. Duplicate events are not collapsed. Repeated document cycles are dropped, which removes the rework signal. The snippet calls `get_prefixes_from_log` with `variant_selection="first"` and no length, and it keeps whole traces that merely touch KYC or Credit Review before a global date.

## 5. Process-aware features
The requested categories are all mentioned, but several definitions do not work. `documents_total` is a nightly snapshot, not a loop activity. `pm4py.get_variants` yields a log-level variant map; a count of unique sequences is constant across cases and leaks if it is fit on the full log. “Time to decision” is defined as time to the first Credit Review and can sit after the prediction point. Employee experience is not in the event schema and is stored at extraction, not at the event. Queue length is proposed without an as-of reconstruction rule.

## 6. Leakage audit
Dropping `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and post-prefix events is right. The rest of the table is not reliable. `documents_total` is “repaired” both by a seven-day cutoff and by an upload-before-prediction rule, which are different rules. Experience cannot be repaired from this extract. `label_available_at` is marked Keep. That timestamp is an outcome-feed fact: early label arrival can itself indicate default. It belongs in the maturity filter only and must not enter the feature table. The audit also misses the likely layout in which case-level fields are repeated onto early events, so non-null on a row is not the same as known at that timestamp.

## 7. Validation design
A start-time split is the right idea, and a random split would mix the July 2025 policy change with the October 2025 migration. The stated windows do not implement that idea. Validation runs through 31 October 2025 and is described as pre-migration, but the migration is 15 October 2025. The test window is starts from November–December 2025. With a 90-day horizon plus up to 35 days of feed lag, most of those cases cannot have a mature label by 15 February 2026. Label maturity, which the task requires the design to address, is absent. There is no as-of backtest, no maturation embargo, and no within-policy fold to separate misfit from policy shift. Integrated Brier score assumes a survival predictor that was never specified; AUC on finished activated cases ignores censoring and selection.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary
The association-versus-counterfactual distinction is partly stated, including the invalidity of a default risk for rejected or withdrawn cases. Selection is still not separated as its own limit: a score fit only among those who were activated under the historical policy is not an application-time default probability. “Post-1 July 2026” is the wrong extrapolation boundary. The three deployment items are historical nonstationarity and missing feeds. They do not describe how use would change the process: underwriters changing who is activated, retraining labels becoming missing by design, or reviewers changing document and escalation behavior so the control-flow features move.

## 9. Implementation skeleton
`pandas.read_csv`, timestamp parsing, and `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the specified columns are present. The pipeline does not do what the section claims. `format_dataframe` returns a dataframe; iterating it does not yield traces. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called without a prefix length. Cohort flags are event-level. `is_censored` treats every missing `default_90d` before the extract date as censoring, which marks rejects as censored. There is no case start, activation time, terminal state, or label-maturity date. There is no train/validation/test split by case start. Post-prefix columns are not removed before feature construction. `any(...) > 1` is always false because `any` returns a bool. `prefix["org:resource"].mean()` does not match a trace of event dicts and treats a resource id as experience. Comments never mark where a survival or competing-risk estimator would attach. The skeleton would not run, and a repaired version would still implement a global calendar cutoff rather than a per-case prefix.

The submission recognizes the right target family and avoids the worst labeling shortcut. The cohort does not balance, censoring is misclassified, prediction time is not an as-of landmark, the test period is mostly unlabeled, and the code does not implement the audit it describes.
