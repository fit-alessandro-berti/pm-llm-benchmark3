3.5

The write-up follows the required outline and separates outage, recovery, mix, staffing, and weekend effects in name, but its estimates are not reliable enough to use. Definitions, date boundaries, dispersion statistics, and the code disagree with the supplied table and with each other.

The largest conceptual error is treating \(\lambda/\text{completions}\) as staff utilization \(\rho\). Over a stable horizon that ratio is near 1 by flow conservation even when servers are not saturated. Baseline days are about \(0.97\)–\(1.02\) on that ratio, not \(0.8\)–\(0.9\). The outage day’s \(105/58 \approx 1.81\) is unmet flow, not a utilization of 1.9. Section 7’s 7.5 FTE and 6.5 FTE “average staffing” recommendations are built on this false \(\rho\). True utilization cannot be identified: only median and p90 handling times are given, not mean service.

Regime boundaries do not match the evidence attached to them. “Baseline weekday” 6–12 Apr includes Sat–Sun, when staff is 2.5 rather than 6.0. Recovery (16–19 Apr), post-mix (20–28 Apr), and staffed (29 Apr–3 May) likewise swallow weekends, while the reported \(\lambda\), WIP, and service ranges describe only the weekdays inside those spans. 13–14 Apr is labeled with median/p90 up to 29 and 60 minutes; those days are 25–26 and 48–50. The outage row says arrivals were 112 and \(\lambda=9.3\); 15 Apr had 105 arrivals (\(8.75\)/h) and 58 completions. 112 is 16 Apr. The mix break is 19 Apr (23%) to 20 Apr (41%), not “after 13 Apr.” The alternative then calls 15–19 Apr “high-complexity, low-staff,” even though complexity is still about 23–27% and 16 Apr is staffed at 7.0 for backlog drain. Every regime is marked high confidence.

Pooled figures are off. Mean arrival intensity is \(2519/(28\times 12)\approx 7.5\)/h, not 7.2. Mean completion intensity is about 7.17/h, not 7.5. Weekends are \(8/28\approx 29\%\) of days and about 14% of arrivals, not 20% and 10%. Mean WIP is about 29.9, not 28.5. The composition point is real, but the text says pooling “understates weekend burstiness,” which is the wrong direction: the pooled rate sits far below weekday intensity and far above weekend intensity.

Section 5 invents results. Lag-1 ACF \(0.35\) (\(p<0.01\)) and a CUSUM \(p<0.001\) at 13 Apr are not computed. Fano factor and \(CV^2\) are equated, and the index of dispersion is the same ratio copied twice. For 7 Apr arrivals, population \(\mathrm{Var}/\mathrm{mean}\approx 3.0\) and \(CV^2\approx 0.35\), not 1.8. For 21 Apr arrivals the Fano factor is about 9.3, not 12.5. Completions on 7 Apr are overdispersed (Fano about 1.6), not near-Poisson at 0.9. The 34-arrival spike is the 10:00–11:00 bin, not 09:00–10:00, and that vector does not reproduce `peak_08_10_pct` of 47. That contradiction is never flagged. Four profiles are correctly called insufficient, but serial dependence is only asserted.

Little’s Law uses \(W=L/\lambda\) and correctly refuses to treat median service as flow time. The verdicts are still too strong. Unknown 07:00 backlog, closure with no overnight service, 15-minute snapshots, and net imbalance make even the calm early weekdays approximate only. Carry-over is called low because WIP is 12–21; that stock is why carry-over matters. Post-mix flow times are not \(3.7\)–\(5.56\) h (weekday ratios are about \(4.1\)–\(5.1\)). Recovery is absent from the matrix, which is the window where high completions are discharge of the outage stock. From 20–28 Apr, arrivals exceed completions by about 106 cases, while mean WIP rises only from the high 30s to about 50; the staffing days claw back only about 18 cases. “Staffed weekday / applicable / stable” misses a slow drain and an intervention whose start time is unknown. Cumulative arrivals minus completions (\(+110\) over the extract) is also not reconciled with mean WIP.

The Python block cannot run: 28 dates versus 27-element columns, and the dropped values differ by column (arrivals omit 105, completions omit 46, and so on), so rows would be scrambled even if lengths were forced. Later `loc` assignments label 25 Apr–3 May, including the intervention weekdays, as “Weekend Post-Mix,” so the code’s segmentation is not the table in section 2. `acf` is imported and never used. The PM4Py note names the requested functions and implies an event log is absent, without saying plainly that these daily aggregates are not a PM4Py log.

What remains is a usable skeleton: do not pool weekday with weekend, do not read 16 Apr throughput as steady capacity, and do not apply Little’s Law on the outage day. The numerical layer, the utilization identity, the change-point date, and the required reproducible code fail.
