7.3

The answer is well structured, constraint-compliant, and generally well calibrated. It keeps several live hypotheses, separates what each network can show from what it cannot, treats centrality as ambiguous, includes bots and vendors, and refuses both removal and an accusation of misconduct. The required sections, risk types, and follow-up analysis classes are all present, and the executive summary is inside the word limit. It is not nearly flawless.

The handover definition is too loose. Handover-of-work is direct succession of resources, not “a later event, often immediately afterward.” That softness matters because the rest of the piece depends on handover meaning transfer points. The absence result is also placed among handover observations even though it is a separate natural experiment, not a property of the network.

Several counts are over-read. Bot_Rules → Mira frequency 920 is called “920 cases,” but edge frequency is not a case count. “Ordinary low-value claims were unaffected” is repeated in the interpretation, the bottleneck hypothesis, and the summary; the evidence is only that the median did not change. “Would likely increase formal escalations and specialist overload” blends the operations manager’s opinion with the absence result, which shows queue buildup and a higher exception-case 90th percentile, not those two outcomes.

The strongest validity gaps are missed contradictions in the given tables. Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules cover almost the same cases but have exception shares of 0.19 and 0.31, so `exception_flag` cannot be treated as a stable case attribute; the data-quality section only gestures at endogeneity. Mira’s own share is 0.48, so almost half of her cases are not flagged, which limits a pure exception-desk story and is never stated. Mira → Priya elapsed time equals Priya’s median service time (51 minutes), and Mira → Omar (42 minutes) is close to Omar’s 37 minutes, yet the answer never asks whether “elapsed time” is mostly the successor’s service time. `lifecycle:transition` is never used, despite service-time and waiting-time recommendations that depend on start/complete pairing.

The subcontracting reading is directionally right but incomplete. Specialist loops are emphasized, while the larger Mira → adjuster handovers do not appear as return loops. That release-versus-consultation contrast is the clearest structural finding in the two networks and is left mainly as a future check. Hypothesis 1’s prediction is also too strong: unstable, discretionary routing would not falsify a formal exception desk. “Variants containing Mira” is not what `filter_variants` does; that filter selects activity sequences, not resource participation. The phrase “the prompt explicitly notes” breaks the analytical frame.
