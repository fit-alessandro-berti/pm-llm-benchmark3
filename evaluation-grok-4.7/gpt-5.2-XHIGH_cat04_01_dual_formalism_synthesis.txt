7.5

The POWL model is the right canonical object and is largely faithful: registration is the unique source, containment and analysis are unordered, verification waits only on containment, design and both optional/approval obligations wait only on analysis, the trial waits on both work products, approvals and supplier consultation stay incomparable with the trial, release is a real three-way join, and the loop body/redo order keeps `Adjust Parameters` between iterations. The process tree is a genuine under-approximation rather than a disguised exact model, and A5 is a well-chosen witness of that cut. Format, control-flow treatment of risk and supplier choice, and the decision not to duplicate labels are all sound.

The reasoning is not as exact as the models. F3’s earliest violation is not `Adjust Parameters`. Under the answer’s own rule, the redo marks that inspection as a failure, so adjustment is still legal there; the first illegal event is `Release Material`, which occurs without a later passing `Inspect Trial`. The added claim that adjustment occurs after the passing inspection describes a different forbidden trace and contradicts assumption 3.

Section 6 then blurs the approximation boundary. Forcing `Plan Verification` to finish before the trial is required behavior. The tree’s extra constraint is that approvals and supplier consultation also wait for both `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action`. Listing the trial in that overconstraint makes a correct synchronization look like an error.

Smaller gaps: “true concurrency” is used for what is actually interleaving of completion events; the dependency table never states supplier overlap with planning, design, approvals, and trial as its own relation; “mildly” undersells how much early approval behavior is cut, without comparing the alternative cut that would preserve early approvals and sacrifice trial overlap; and the risk-route row calls a required data abstraction an over-approximation. The self-critique restates known limits and misses these slips.
