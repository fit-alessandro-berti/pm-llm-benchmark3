The pipeline is internally consistent and unusually careful about counting. The 17 events, 12 objects, 35 E2O rows, and 12 O2O rows match the narrative and the chosen inference; flattened Order/Package/Delivery/Invoice sequences and DFG frequencies are arithmetically right; shared events (e06, e07, e11), P1’s convergence, O100’s split across P1/P2, and the 100/150 amounts are not double-counted as extra executions or allocations. Provenance on the single inferred link, e08–O100, is explicit, and the OCEL-versus-projection distinction is maintained.

Deductions are for local failures, not for the model.

The full-operational alternative does not follow from its own construction. Confirmations are specified as adding only the carried package, but the effect claims Order and Item lifecycles gain confirmation events and that “D1 operations” are copied onto both orders. Those consequences follow for dispatch only if confirmation is also given items and orders, which the construction sentence does not say.

In the same section, the undirected path is written O100 → V1 → O200. That reverses the declared `bills` direction, Invoice → Order.

Failure mode 14 is not inspectable as written. “No Reserve Stock event for I2 establishes neither a reservation nor a stock violation” stacks a negative subject with “neither,” so a reviewer cannot tell whether the absence is being treated as non-evidence of reservation, non-evidence of a stock violation, or both.

“Same-type interactions also exist between O100/O200 and I1/I3” is similarly ambiguous; only the later “self-type” clause rescues it. Discovery will also treat `relation:origin` as invisible, so the baseline analysis mixes one inferred participation into every E2O result while the safeguard stays outside the miner. That is disclosed, but it weakens the claim that provenance keeps analytical links from being read as observations.

These are narrow defects in an otherwise rigorous answer, which is why the score stays high and why it is not near 10.
