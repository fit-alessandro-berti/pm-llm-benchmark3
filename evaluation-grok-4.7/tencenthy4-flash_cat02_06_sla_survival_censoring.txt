7.2

The estimand framework is strong and mostly coherent: time origin, horizon, right censoring, competing exits, and already-observed breaches are separated, and E1–E4 are tied to distinct decisions. Transfer is not treated as ordinary censoring in the primary analysis, open cases are retained, and escalation/rework are correctly flagged as post-baseline leakage. Cohort, priority, and most case-level examples are used concretely.

The score is held down by concrete errors. AutoCheck is in 13 of 17 cohort-B prefixes, not 12; S17 is listed both as AutoCheck-after-Screen and as having no AutoCheck. S29 is grouped with long or transferred escalated cases although it is open at 51 hours. The state diagram has no arrows, includes an unobserved Review self-loop, and omits observed transitions such as Screen→Escalate, AutoCheck→Escalate, and Rework→Resolve.

The required estimator does not match the prose. `aj_cif` updates the at-risk survival only at times of the cause of interest, so competing events never reduce \(S(t-)\). With no tied times, that is not Aalen–Johansen, and the printed “primary” and sensitivity probabilities are biased. The PM4Py path also counts AutoCheck-without-Screen traces as AutoCheck-before-Screen. A second fenced block sits beside the single required code block.
