The final write-up is a strong event-time analysis: blanks are non-updates, state is forward-filled, `event_index` breaks the TX-311 tie, and release-time snapshots are not replaced by final case values. R1–R7 are tabulated with explicit ordering, and the case results are almost all well supported.

Correct primary calls: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are NONCOMPLIANT; TX-305 is INDETERMINATE; TX-306 and TX-308 are COMPLIANT once a sub-20% increase and a decrease are treated as non-invalidating; TX-302 is COMPLIANT because the 09:20 enhanced review already meets R1, with EX-77 only a second valid path; TX-311 is COMPLIANT because jurisdiction at release is DE, so R1 is not triggered. Percentages (56.25, 4.55, 24.21, 22.88, 27.78) are right. Sections 4, 6, and 7 cover the required cases and failure modes with usable counterfactuals.

Deductions: `ord_key = timestamp_ns + event_index` is not unique across cases (TX-302’s enhanced review and TX-310’s standard review share 09:20 and index 2). `amount_at_last_review` is looked up by that key alone, so TX-310’s review amount can be read from TX-302. Rule flags happen to survive; the required diagnostics column does not. The exemption join also ignores `waives` and injects a hardcoded `evidence_supports_prior_approval` boolean, so TX-310’s contradictory record is settled in code rather than left as `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The pseudo-logic is looser than the table, especially on the R1 disjunction and on a standard-packet approval not counting as R2. The 08:45 witness time versus the 08:50 displayed time is over-read as proof they cannot be the same approval.
