7.5

The month-by-month diagnosis is the right one: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June is a routing mix shift with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September combine parts-path performance collapse, V3-specific outcome shift, and Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. Numbers are used carefully, the six drift types are all addressed, the remediation is staged, and label-maturity gates correctly block automatic retraining. That core is not enough for a high score.

Drift types are blurred. June’s V2 jump is post-diagnosis control-flow mix, not demonstrated covariate drift of features available at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, yet covariate drift is marked high-confidence for June. October’s long-path backlog is censoring, and it is still listed as covariate drift. April is given high-confidence process drift in the same row that admits the business process may not have changed; the 18% unmapped mass is logging and prefix failure. The May residual (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050) is pinned on the April feature-cache miss, but the audit only states that April’s cached event-count was not backfilled, while May still has its own 2% unknown-activity rate around the 6 May patch.

Censoring direction is mishandled for August–September. The backlog hits longer, higher-risk paths, so cutoff breach rates are biased down. Mix-weighted matured variant rates are about 0.31 in August and about 0.34 in September, against observed 0.28 and 0.31. Delay therefore masks deterioration; it does not partly explain it. October is treated correctly, August–September are not. V1 also creeps from about 0.16 to 0.19 by September, so “V1/V2 slightly improve” is false. A separate V3 model is recommended without stating that the variant is not known at the required scoring prefix unless a diagnosis-time routing decision exists.

Test 3.1 is internally inconsistent. Hypothesis A is “October concept/performance drift” but predicts that fully matured labels will still show a low breach. Continued deterioration from the August–September regime would show a high matured breach; a persistently low breach would be improvement or a different regime. The other tests are the right design, including conditional-on-path checks and Partner Z transport.

The code sketch would not audit the stated failure. `MonthEnd(1)` plus one second drops almost all of the last day. `filter_time_range` is called with non-existent `start_timestamp`/`end_timestamp` keywords. DFGs and variants are built on event-truncated logs, so edge durations and control-flow are distorted, while the case-start cohort is used only as a case count. `n_v1_standard` counts variant keys containing `Dispatch Technician`, which includes V3 and does not count cases. The performance DFG value is stored as a median without a median aggregation. Renamed labels are normalized, but prediction prefixes ending at diagnosis are never built, so the April prefix bug would not be detected. Cohort interpretations are one-line comments, not the truncation versus right-censoring distinction.

Section 6 is sound, including the June overreaction and fairness misread. The answer is a strong audit with a correct headline timeline and several concrete reasoning and measurement errors.
