4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and stays attached to segments, policies, and several real paths, but it is not a reliable redesign. Multiple mechanisms are inverted, baselines are misread, and the effect table reports unsupported savings in the wrong impact category.

System boundary. Machine energy, transport, scrap, and packaging belong inside the boundary, and upstream production plus end-of-life are reasonably left out. The uncertainty that the prompt flags is missing: low model precision, disruption variants that combine, and forecast error. Treating rework and obsolescence as pure inefficiencies contradicts the stated batch-wait, scrap, and 14-day obsolescence trade-off. Dedicated vans are also treated as justifiable for Service-contract orders in general. SP2 requires a Critical-downtime justification.

Indicators. Batching and obsolescence never become their own indicators. Cycle time is defined as the median or p90 of `promised_lead_time_hours`, which is the promise, not elapsed cycle time. `order_weight_kg` is not an event attribute, so kgCO2e per kg and the 0.5 kgCO2e/kg truck target are invented. Several “targets” are worse than the log: about 1.5 kWh per Standard-stock order versus an observed 0.8–0.9, and a 5% Standard-stock rework target versus an observed 3–4%. CNC_B energy per item is not in the evidence, so the 2.0 kWh/item target has no baseline. The quality formula adds an implied-returns term the log does not contain.

Interventions. Eight options are named, but several controls point the wrong way.

- S1’s “70% of ground/air violations” is not in the log. SP1 is violated in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases. Air is then allowed for Service-contract broadly, which keeps the unjustified stock-air behavior the evidence describes.
- S2 tells the process to enforce a 38-hour CNC wait and a 72-hour heat-treatment wait. The evidence gives 38 hours as the median wait cost of batches of 12–20, and 1.7× late risk for heat-treatment waiting. Neither figure is a waiting cap. The claimed 30–50% energy cut of CNC_A versus CNC_B compares a known CNC_A batch curve (2.8 to 1.3 kWh/item) with a machine whose energy was never measured. The 48-hour slack rule is also invented.
- S3 sends queued inspection to CNC_A. CNC_A is a production machine; inspection is `inspection_cell`. It also applies reinforced packaging only after a low-damage certificate. Q1 requires reinforced packaging for made-to-order parts over 8 kg unless that certificate exists.
- S4 quotes a 90% emission cut to customers. Air is about 4–6× ground, so the transport reduction for a mode switch is roughly three-quarters to five-sixths, and only on the transport portion. The 6% air share is a whole-log path share, not the Made-to-order Regular air share.
- S5 aims consolidation at Standard-stock Service-contract, whose median cycle time is 18 hours. A 12–24 hour consolidation wait fits non-urgent Regular stock better. The drop from 7.8 to 3.5 kgCO2e treats a 35–55% saving versus ground parcels as if 7.8 were a ground baseline. That 7.8 already mixes in air.
- S6 uses incompatibility to send orders into rework packaging. A pre-ship check is meant to keep orders out of the 260 and 360 rework-packaging arcs. The 40% damage-return figure belongs to reinforced packaging, and “260 to 100 cases/year” treats one log arc as an annual volume.
- S7 batches Critical dedicated vans into two or three daily waves. That path’s value is a 14-hour median and 3% late rate. Shared-courier emission factors are absent, so 52 to 25 kgCO2e is unsupported.
- S8 labels the segment “Made-to-order Critical-downtime” and assigns 38 kgCO2e to ground. Critical-downtime Service-contract is its own segment; 38 is its average, while the stock/ground exception is 4.8 kgCO2e with late delivery rising from 3% to 9%.

The partial-shipment path, 7% of orders, 79-hour median, 24% late, and a second shipment, is left without a control. Standard-stock does not pass through CNC batching, yet a later mitigation still ranks it against made-to-order for batch priority.

Effects table. The required columns are present, but direction is often unclear and frequently wrong. Air and van changes are scored as large energy savings: S1 at 10–30%, S7 at 50–60%, S8 at 30–40%. Stock air versus stock ground is about 1.0 versus 0.9 kWh. S2’s transport cell is explained as “batch energy.” Batching and consolidation are shown as small late-delivery and resilience improvements even where the log shows longer waits, 1.7× lateness, and less route diversity. The ranges are finer than the evidence.

Rebound. Obsolescence at 6% is correctly recalled, and the inspection-to-expedite and customer-acceptance risks are real. The set still collapses batching and obsolescence, then repeats batch waiting as a sixth risk. It misses the main displacement chain: batch or consolidation wait consumes slack, `expedite_flag` flips the order to air or van, and the transport rebound cancels the energy gain. Weight gates of 50 kg and 100 kg are not attributes. Shifting volume onto CNC_A can form a new queue and push overflow into CNC_B, emergency production, or cancellation. Those 410 direct cancels and 60 backorder cancels are unmonitored.

Recommendation and governance. The package correctly tries to keep true downtime fast, move slack stock off air, prefer CNC_A when due-date slack exists, negotiate dates only with Regular made-to-order, and log policy overrides. The quantified “40–50% transport” and “rework from 18% to 9%” claims treat a 5% CNC_B path rate as the portfolio rate. Consolidation-plus-van-wave remains in the core package for the tightest service segments.

SP1 monitoring sets an 85% ground/truck mode share. The 85% in SP1 is the probability that ground still meets the promise on that order. Q1 is monitored as 100% reinforced packaging, which erases the certified low-damage exception. R1 is relaxed to 90% coverage. Validation adds surveys and daily dashboards, and it does not specify trace checks for batch size against wait and lateness, the 14-day ship rate, partial-shipment doubles, cancel-due-to-delay, `expedite_flag` after a green recommendation, or variant precision during disruptions.

The skeleton matches the candidate list. The operating rules and numbers do not match the log closely enough to implement safely.
