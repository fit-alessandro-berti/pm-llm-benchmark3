5.5

The assessment is structured and directionally right on several cases, but it is not reliable. It closes open authority questions too early, contradicts its own finance rules on the largest purchase, and reports high confidence where actors, thresholds, and evidence are unresolved.

The hierarchy is a single rank order, despite the need for issue-specific orderings. An ordinary CFO email is placed above both the signed incident directive and the board rule that other controls survive an emergency unless the Chief Risk Officer issues a written exception. “Interpret finance controls” is treated as authority to waive pre-commitment approval. The email’s own limiter — judgment, and no removal of essential integrity controls — never becomes a defeater. The open question whether a COO and Incident Commander directive can count as that Chief Risk Officer exception is recorded as “None.”

The rule catalogue then hardens those choices. Segregation and compliance are “always” required, while finance is waived for every in-scope purchase at or under EUR 150,000 during the whole directive window. That logic conflicts with the later claim that the email was inactive on 14 July. Local plant and finance approval are given a pre-commitment deadline the local procedure does not state. The global rule, which does state that deadline, also requires an independent budget owner and applies only from EUR 50,000; the budget owner is never analyzed. The emergency rule fuses purchase-order timing, shift-lead substitution, and the 24-hour form. Legal clearance above EUR 150,000 is mis-attributed to the incident directive rather than the email. “New supplier” and “unapproved supplier” are never distinguished.

Case results reflect those gaps. P-502, P-504, and P-506 are sound noncompliance calls: late compliance for a new supplier, a cafeteria purchase outside sterilization scope, and an unwritten extension after expiry. P-505 correctly prefers the 17:59 supplier acknowledgment to the 18:06 system stamp. P-507 correctly prefers the 12:40 signed PDF to the 14:30 system entry. P-501 is labeled noncompliant with high confidence for the wrong reason: at EUR 38,000 the global finance rule does not apply, the local approval rule does not say “before commitment,” and the shift-lead sentence concerns an absent plant manager’s approval, not who may commit. P-503 is noncompliant, but “no rule satisfied” ignores dual authorization and an approved supplier. P-508 is the clearest error. At EUR 155,000 the answer’s own waiver stops at EUR 150,000, and “obtain Legal clearance as well” does not replace finance or budget-owner approval. It is marked compliant, with no deviation and high confidence.

Uncertainty is understated. Six of eight cases are high confidence, none is indeterminate, and missing evidence is repeatedly “N/A” despite unknown committers, missing budget owners, an undated P-502, and unproven four-hour recording. The outage of 14 July is later blurred into the 18 July clock issue. Of the alternatives, the P-503 discussion does not change the classification, while the two compliance calls that depend on the email are not stress-tested. Remediation merges late proof of a timely act into “logging defect,” reviews only two of four unapproved or new suppliers, and proposes discipline for the weakest actor theory. The draft clause still leaves “critical” undefined, leaves finance above EUR 150,000 unclear, and allows a committer to be a joint approver after treating that pattern as fatal.
