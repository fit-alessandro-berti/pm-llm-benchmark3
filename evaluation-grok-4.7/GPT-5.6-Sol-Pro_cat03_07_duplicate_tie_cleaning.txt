8.1

The design is strong and mostly faithful to the facts: the decision tree separates the six required cases, e05 and e14 are retained, e08/e09 are treated as a harmonization collision, e15 stays an invoice-level summary, and ingestion time is not the business order. The retained counts 17/44, 15/39, 14/37, and 10/28 follow from the stated unions, and the warning that `have_common_object=True` still collapses M1 with M2 through O1/V1, and I1 with I2 through O1/P1, is correct. Provenance, original timestamps, and the false-causality warning about synthetic offsets are handled seriously.

It is not nearly flawless. The prose says e08→e09 and e11→e12 are valid only if those lifecycle rules are accepted, and that a conflict should be quarantined or downgraded; the code always inserts both edges and raises on a cycle. That is the same false-story failure the ordering section warns against. `conservative_ordered` then writes index-based deltas into the primary timestamp, so ordinary DFG and waiting-time code on that object will treat concurrency as 1 ms causality unless a caller remembers the side view. “Without deleting legitimate concurrency” is therefore stronger than what the pipeline emits.

Smaller accuracy gaps remain. Scanner values are not actually source-namespaced; non-key relation attributes keep the first row; exact-duplicate survival is lexicographic eid order, not the “later copy” rule. Section 7’s “one Pick, one Pack, one Dispatch, one Invoice, and one Capture” obscures that Handover and Authorize survive, and Authorize→Capture is a weak member of the “eight changed interpretations.” The guarded `ocel_drop_duplicates` call is so neutralized that it does not exercise cross-event duplicate behavior, and the audit-table schema is richer than what the code records.
