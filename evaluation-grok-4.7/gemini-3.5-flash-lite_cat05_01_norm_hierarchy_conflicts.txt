The paper has the required seven-part structure, a dimension-specific hierarchy, and several directionally sound results (P-504 out of scope, P-506 after expiry, P-503’s self-approval, P-501 before 09:00 and without delegation). Those merits do not carry the assessment. Classifications repeatedly depart from the paper’s own rules, one core prohibition is misread, and the evidential method stated in section 1 is not applied to the facts that test it.

## Consistency with the stated method
Section 1, the hierarchy, R-GPIP-3, and the pseudo-logic all preserve pre-commitment Compliance clearance for a new supplier. P-502 is then labeled `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` at high confidence because clearance at 18:30 is treated as waived by “emergency necessity.” That waiver is the move A4 and A6 reject, and it is the move the paper itself rejects before the case table. The same cell admits an “interpretive tension” while assigning high confidence. EUR 62,000 also triggers A2; Finance_Approve at 13:05 is after commitment, and no Plant_Approve appears at all. Neither gap is carried as a live deviation.

R-GPIP-2’s exception column is “None,” while R-CFO-1 creates a finance substitute up to EUR 150,000. The catalogue and the case logic are not one system.

## Misread controls
A1 bars the requester or the committer from being the final business approver. It does not bar one person from both requesting and committing. P-508 states that neither Incident Command authorizer was BUY-8, so A1 is met on the given facts. The paper treats BUY-8’s dual request-and-commit role as the breach, repeats that theory in sections 5 and 6, and aims remediation at “role consolidation.” The defensible problem is different: EUR 155,000 is outside D1, D2’s Legal clearance was obtained and does not by itself replace the Finance Controller and independent budget owner, and no finance event exists.

P-503 is rightly `NONCOMPLIANT` because PROC-3 approves, requests, and commits. The row still says satisfied rules are “None” and never lists the missing A3 clearance. EUR 140,000 is above EUR 100,000 on an approved supplier, and no Compliance_Clearance occurred. Under the paper’s own preservation rule, that omission alone blocks the section 5 flip to `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. The 24-hour scope theory is also applied as if it were settled, despite C6.

## Time, scope, and evidence
P-501’s stoppage was predicted in four hours, so B4 allows commitment before the PO without ECD-2. The solid defect is the reachable, non-delegating Plant Manager, not sequencing. The emergency form at 07:50 on 07-15 is inside 24 hours of 08:50 and is never credited. The section 5 alternative inverts ON-44: recovery timestamps run later than the act, up to 12 minutes, so a logged 08:50 cannot be moved into the 09:00 window.

P-505’s supplier call at 17:57 and acknowledgment at 17:59:31 are the better contemporaneous evidence, and a fast ERP clock would place 18:06 earlier, not later. The row treats 18:06 as evenly balanced with that record and asks for an ON-44 clock audit. ON-44 ended on 07-14 at 13:00. The pseudo-logic then subtracts exactly 12 minutes for any in-window ERP time, beyond the stated “up to 12 minutes.”

P-507’s signed PDF at 12:40 is inside four hours of 08:55, and Compliance_Clearance at 08:50 precedes commitment. The paper cites ON-44 again; the case describes a document-system outage on 07-17. Section 4 calls this a justified exception. Section 6 calls it a logging defect. Pre-commitment compliance, the point that separates P-507 from P-502, is absent from the satisfied-rule cell.

P-506 is fairly `NONCOMPLIANT`, but Finance_Approve at 06:05 preceded commitment and Create_PO at 06:45 did not. The satisfied-rule cell lists events, and the reasoning stops at the verbal extension.

## Requirements the draft does not close
Section 5 offers four alternatives, and the P-508 alternative stays `NONCOMPLIANT`. The task requires alternatives that change the primary classification. The strongest P-508 alternative — Legal plus separated Incident Command authorization as a complete finance reading above EUR 150,000 — is not the one written. The P-501 and P-505 alternatives rest on the evidential mistakes above. Assumptions are not marked off from facts, despite the section 1 claim that they are.

Section 7 addresses authority, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, records, expiry, and cafeteria scope creep. Clause 12.2(c) then makes Finance Controller approval for purchases at or above EUR 50,000 non-waivable by an incident directive or a CFO email, and 12.3 makes joint Incident Command authorization sufficient for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000. That is the same finance ambiguity the clause was supposed to close. “Statutory budget owner” has no basis in the materials. Backup-line parts with multi-day stock remain undefined.

The result is a complete template whose hardest holdings — P-502, P-505, P-507, and P-508 — are not reliable under the interpretation the paper announces.
