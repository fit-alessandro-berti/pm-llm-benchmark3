The write-up keeps the right multi-mechanism skeleton: March is mostly semantic, mapped fitness rebounds, and later rework, queueing, mix, and seasonality are separate. It also fills every required section. The quantitative layer and several mechanism labels do not hold up, and those are central to the task.

The baseline-relative table is mislabeled as month-to-month and contains real arithmetic errors. March median cycle time is about −1.0% versus January, not +0.4%. May mapped fitness is about −4.3%, not −5.4% (that figure matches June). July supplier share is about +214%, not +220%. May median is +15.9%, not +16.2%. June is called a cycle-time peak; the median keeps rising through August and again to 49.0 in November.

The DFG “Euclidean” figures (2.1, 12.4, 18.7, 22.3) are not the Euclidean distances of the supplied rates. On the stated 0–100 scale those distances are about 1.7, 19.1, 27.7, and 29.2. Part A of the code would instead emit 0–1-scaled distances and was not reconciled with section 3. The cycle-time split adds invented parameters the aggregates do not contain: “+10% cycle time per +10pp complexity,” “8–12 hours per rework loop,” and “+20pp complexity from January to December” (December is 34 versus 21, so +13pp; +20pp is October). Shares of 30/40/25/5 therefore look more exact than the evidence. A median also cannot be partitioned like a mean. Rework is later described as +24pp; the May level is 24%, which is about +12pp from January and +11pp from April.

Fitness is treated as if more allowed rework lowers it. The normative model already permits Request Info. Token replay fitness does not move just because an allowed loop becomes more frequent; precision or variant shares would. The mapped series is about 0.90–0.91 in March–April and bottoms at 0.86, so a “persistent 5–10pp gap” overstates it. October’s senior-assessment surge does not further worsen mapped fitness, which cuts against using that activity as the explanation of the residual gap.

July is the wrong break for control flow. Assess→Request Info is already 24 per 100 cases in May, before the priority rule, and fast-variant share only moves from 41% to 39% in July. Priority reorders waiting; it does not by itself create rework. Evidence strength 75, and the October complexity spike offered as support, conflate that rule with seasonality. Section 9’s 60% confidence contradicts section 1. Missing resource identifiers after migration are logging/measurement drift, not resource/organizational drift. “Stabilization” of duplicate Open Case in May is not a second logging change point. The annual renewal is seasonal, and May, October, and December are mixed; the onset column never uses those labels. “Exceeds historical trends” in December has no prior-year series.

The discrimination table often records cross-sectional differences as behavioral drift. Higher rework inside supplier Z or high-complexity cases is the population/input contrast. A longer Assess queue with stable service time is performance drift, not behavioral drift. “None” does not state the pattern that would have indicated population drift.

Several monitors would fail on this very incident. Raw fitness below 0.85 is true in every month after February, so it never resets. Mapped fitness below 0.85 never fires (the low is 0.86). Missing-resource alerts at 10% sit above the observed 6.5% peak. Complexity above 35% “except October” false-alarms in November, the seasonal peak. Benjamini–Hochberg is invoked for thresholds that are not p-values. Variant distribution and within-class queue time, the two artifacts that would separate priority redistribution from aggregate delay, are missing. There is no explicit event-ordering monitor.

Part A crashes: subtracting the January row from a datetime column, with a further divide-by-zero on duplicate Open Case. The change-point rule flags the whole post-March regime, not change points. Part B never imports pm4py, `compute_monthly_artifacts` does not aggregate by month, and the duplicate filter drops every Open Case that shares a timestamp rather than one extra copy. `discover_dfg` returns a three-tuple. The embedded tables themselves match the source data, and the PM4Py names are mostly the right ones.

Section 9 is the strongest prose: March logging is properly separated from later drift, and priority and the December campaign are marked as weakly identified. It still repeats the +24pp error, says supplier Z “directly” raised rework without a composition bound, and treats the campaign effect as real at 70% while also saying load and the intervention cannot be separated. The seven rules have the right shape, but rules 5–7 inherit the same miscalibrated cutoffs.
