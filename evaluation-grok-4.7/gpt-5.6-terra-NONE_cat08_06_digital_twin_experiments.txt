7.5

The design is strong and usable: scope, calendars, latent objections, queue-generated waiting, non-stationary arrivals, cross-case coupling, validation, and the no-optimality rule are handled at a high level. It is not nearly flawless. Several specification errors and design/code contradictions would bias intervention comparisons if followed literally.

The arrival regression is misspecified. A single daily count cannot contain additive district and case-type coefficients; those belong in a stratified count model or in a separate allocation model. As written, the mean would not correspond to a generative process.

The seed scheme contradicts common random numbers. `hash((scenario_id, replication))` changes the stream by scenario, and Python’s salted `hash()` is not reproducible across processes. The skeleton then uses one generator, so any policy that changes the number of draws (D7’s thinned Monday Poisson, bursts, skipped branches) desynchronizes later service, rework, and objection draws. Paired differences would not be the CRN estimates described in the text.

Warm-up and measurement do not match the tails the design itself cites. A 6-month discard is short relative to Complex-variance medians of 140 days and p90s of 260 days, and end-of-horizon cases are never censored. Completions inside the window would over-represent short cases. Historical initialization is mentioned, but the primary design still measures a biased cohort.

D5 is not operationalized. “Legal pre-check” never states whether the later legal check is replaced, shortened, or duplicated, which is the difference between a capacity saving and a new bottleneck. D6 likewise does not say how a reserved planner interacts with the two Simple-renovation-only planners.

The executable path is an independent-case lognormal, not the discrete-event model argued in sections 1–5. Resource pools, calendars, join logic, and the bundler do not affect completion time, yet the demo still compares B0 with D10. That comparison does not estimate the interventions. Related logic errors: public notice is released in parallel with reviews, against both the process tree and section 1; architect bursts are added on top of a background rate that already contains their volume; D7 multiplies Monday volume by 0.80 and never reinserts the displaced demand; unknown prior violations are treated as “no violations,” which inflates D2 eligibility.

Process-mining use is mostly conventional (`format_dataframe`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average, no fake `read_csv`, tree-parse failure explained). Two gaps remain. Conformance against a tree whose redo labels are `Completeness rework` and `Inspection rework` will flag artifacts, and that is not tied back to the estimation plan. Performance-DFG times are also not identified as invalid on interleaved parallel branches (planning, fire, environment).

Validation and decision rules are thoughtful, but the temporal holdout can easily be one season, and replay “arrivals within ±10%” is nearly automatic if submissions are inputs. Thresholds are appropriately non-binding. The remaining problem is that the uncertainty apparatus is more careful than the generator that would feed it.
