The answer has the right qualitative skeleton: A and B stay last and nonconformant, G is not treated as available, a second officer is not replaced by re-screening, self-certification, or a SAR, and P-901 is the reason to prefer S4. That is not enough. The ranking is justified with timelines, probabilities, and expected values that contradict the case data and, in places, the answer itself.

The primary plan’s clock does not work. S4 arrives in 35–65 minutes and a second review is given as 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute, so total time is about 75–155 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. Meeting the deadline is only the fast tail, not the claimed ~60% (breach 0.35–0.55). Section 6 then shortens review to 30–45 minutes and calls execution at 75–105 minutes a 60% success. The same error appears in the trace that labels execution at minute 80–100 as “SLA MET.” If S4 arrives at 65 minutes, the remaining 25 minutes cannot contain that review; calling the miss probability “0.50+” understates a near-certain miss.

The fallback is timed too late. Interrupting P-901 only at minute 50, then adding a 35–75 minute review, does not “guarantee” P-884’s deadline; the earliest finish is about minute 90 and the usual finish is after it. Waiting that long can also spoil P-901’s 70-minute deadline. The option value of interrupting now versus waiting is never calculated.

Action D is misidentified. D already includes a second-officer review by S2 or S4. Rank 1 is essentially D with S4, while rank 5 redescribes D as re-screening without that review and assigns the label “CONFORMANT IF PURSUED IN PARALLEL WITH C1/C2,” which is not one of the four required statuses.

The benefit–harm section is not usable. Fuzzy-match confidence 0.46 is treated as P(true sanctions hit), once as 46% and once as a 54% true-positive rate. False-rejection probability is taken as the complement of the 0.55–0.75 approval chance; the chance a now-rejection is false is closer to that approval probability itself, and the complement also mixes in holds and true rejections. The 0.46 factor is applied twice for F. Interval arithmetic is wrong: 10–17.5 minus 2.2–14 minus 2.5–7.5 is not 0.8–8.5. Net figures flip between signed gains and unsigned losses, so the summary can show A/B/G with large positive “net benefit.” P-901 is given an invented baseline breach below 0.30 and an invented €18–50k loss; API calls are priced at €200–500; director response, override grant, and re-screen failure rates are invented. A completed Rule 10 override is also treated as still an unlawful execution with the same €100–500k exposure. Residual sanctions risk is real; equating a valid exception with A/B is not.

The document disagrees with itself. Section 1 ranks E above F. Section 3 calls E dominated (about −€13k to −€72k) and F strongly positive (€18–103k) if the rejection threshold holds. The closing table reverses E and F and changes the nets again. Confidence 0.72–0.80 averages conformance certainty with an operational success rate the time bounds do not support.

Conformance mapping for A, B, F, and G is the soundest part, and A/B are correctly refused as forecast-proof. The operational recommendation a reader would implement—wait for S4, expect the deadline to be met more often than not, and interrupt P-901 at minute 50—is not supported by the stated durations or by a coherent loss model.
