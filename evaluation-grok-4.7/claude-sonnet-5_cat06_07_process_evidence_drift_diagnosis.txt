7.4

The diagnosis correctly separates April’s schema failure, June’s routing-mix change, the July–September supplier shock, Partner Z’s arrival, and October’s censoring, and the six required sections, falsification tests, retrain gates, and risk cases are all present. It is not nearly flawless: several claims are mis-scaled or mis-attributed, and the required code sketch contradicts its own cohort definitions.

The August composition shift is understated. V3 moves from 22% in July to 35% in August, before Partner Z exists. Holding July’s mix and applying August’s matured variant rates gives an aggregate near 0.25; holding August’s rates and applying only the mix change gives about the same increment. Mix and the within-V3 jump (0.46 to 0.64) are similar in size, so “modest mix” is wrong, and August is missing from the covariate-drift row. The V3 share jump itself is left unexplained. September is called the worst AUROC/ECE pair, but October’s AUROC is 0.64 versus September’s 0.66. “36% baseline” is September’s non–Partner Z V3 share, not the historical ~20% mix.

May’s 3% missing-queue rate is tied to the cached “events before diagnosis” feature. The audit says that cache was not fully backfilled for April, which is a different statement from May-cohort missingness. June is described as a clean arithmetic mix shift, while mean predicted risk stays at 0.20 against an observed breach rate of 0.15 and ECE rises to 0.060, worse than May, after data quality has mostly recovered. That calibration gap is the predictive consequence of the mix change and is only gestured at later as a “small” ECE bump. April’s over-prediction is real; the claim that truncated prefixes are what pushed scores to 0.28 is a mechanism the tables do not show. Duplicate removal only on the new source after 20 April is never used, so April is treated as one homogeneous pipeline month. Label-delay confidence is marked as having no material counterevidence, which is stronger than the table can support before a fixed-horizon check.

The remediation overreaches in two places. A parts-delay feature is recommended for scoring immediately after diagnosis; the case’s own `Order Part → Part Received` time is future information, and the answer never separates that ex-post duration from an ex-ante supplier signal. A separate V3 model is marked justified even though eventual variant may be unknown at the prediction point, and even though the same section’s lead-time feature would remove the stated reason to split. Immediate containment quarantines training data and does not stop use of scores that sit near 0.19–0.21 while August–September observed breach rates are 0.28–0.31. Test 1 treats immature August–September aggregates (~0.28–0.31) as the mature-October target; with V3 still ~41% and the parts edge at 155 h, that band is not a clean falsifier.

The code sketch calls the requested libraries and refuses a fictional drift detector, but it does not implement the cohorts it describes. `mode="traces_contained"` keeps only cases that both start and end inside the month; with median label delay of 33–52 days, that is a short-case sample, not a case-start cohort, and it recreates the October survivorship bias the narrative warns against. There is no pm4py mode for “first event in month.” `format_dataframe` renames `timestamp` to `time:timestamp`, so the later `df["timestamp"]` lookup does not run. Duplicates are flagged and then removed, so `duplicate_share` on the returned frame is identically zero. `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` is turned into missing activities and left in the log passed to DFG discovery. The one-second adjacent-row rule is an arbitrary proxy and does not measure time since the previous same activity. Event-sliced performance DFGs also drop edges that cross the month boundary, which is exactly the long parts edge under study.
