The answer is complete, well structured, and appropriately cautious on discrimination versus procedural burden. It hits every required heading and constraint, and its adjusted-gap arithmetic (about 63%, 76%, and 85% of the raw approval gaps) is correct. The score is held down by several concrete analytical and technical errors.

The caseload comparison is not valid. “About 100 support cases per specialist versus about 70 for Group A” divides a needs subset (57% of B, 62% of C) by specialist headcount, then compares that with all of Group A’s cases across 52 workers. “Disproportionately assigned” is also treated as if those cases were handled only by the 7 bilingual staff, and the 3 overloaded regional officers are dropped. Those figures cannot support the overload mechanism as stated.

The Completeness Check → Request Missing Documents wait is misread. The source note attributes the 1.6d versus 0.4d gap to bilingual-staff queueing before the request is issued. Section 3 instead ties that fourfold wait to inaccessible documentation requirements, which would show up after the request, on an edge the table does not measure.

The “+12 pp / +4 pp Request Docs excess” (V2+V4 versus incompleteness) is over-precise. Those variants are mutually exclusive lower bounds, the column is a path summary, and other variants are unseen. Section 2 calls this suggestive, but section 3.2 promotes it to a firm “excess beyond incompleteness.”

The PM4Py sketch has real logic and API mistakes despite the version disclaimer. `supervisor_without_trigger` counts every case with Supervisor Review and never removes legitimate triggers. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with `activity_key` rather than `concept_name_key`, and `filter_paths_performance(..., keep=True)` is not a valid argument. The E-value helper is defined for risk ratios and never connected to the percentage-point or day estimands actually used.

Smaller overclaims remain: Submit → Identity Verification group medians are called “a channel effect” without within-channel splits, and the percent-of-gap-“explained” figures subtract conditional adjusted effects from raw aggregates. Path-summary gaps (V3 missing eligibility, V4 missing notification, 57% versus 24%, 62% versus 31%) are caveated often enough that they do not dominate the grade.
