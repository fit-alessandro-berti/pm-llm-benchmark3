6.2

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and covers every required section, but several analytical and specification failures keep it well short of a high score.

The recommendation correctly rejects national scale and full rollback, preserves the simple-house gains, and pauses survey skipping and post-billing activation. Alternatives A–E are all evaluated with advantages, risks, and conditions. Most headline figures match the evidence, and the five metric conflicts are each addressed.

The evidence table violates the required separation. Incomplete building-risk attributes, sparse start timestamps, and cost data that stop after week 4 are stated facts, yet they are filed as uncertain assumptions. The handover/precision row also fuses two mechanisms: coordinator ownership can explain fewer handovers, while low precision is the model allowing extra behavior.

The fitness/precision account is technically loose. Precision 0.51 means the model permits behavior beyond the log, not behavior that was “not intended.” Observed harmful paths (billing before activation, repeated visits) are log behavior and alignment move-on-log findings; treating them as a precision result confuses the two metrics. Generalization’s drop from 0.70 to 0.56 is never used.

The segment explanation misattributes cause. The survey skip applies to medium-risk addresses, and the simple-house timing gain is on validation-to-scheduling. Crediting the skip rule and post-billing activation for the low-risk improvement is not supported. Permit-check absence is also collapsed into that same skip rule. The pilot’s harder mix—more apartments and older buildings—is omitted from the tail explanation even though it is a direct confound.

Rework figures are misstated as “+11% to +18%,” “+7% to +15%,” and “+6.1% to +9.8%” rather than levels moving from 11% to 18%, 7% to 15%, and 6.1% to 9.8%. “Doubled” for 7% to 15% is also inexact. DFG edge shifts are essentially unused, as is spare field-survey capacity (78% to 52%) against second-visit load (66% to 92%).

Value-of-information ranking names an uncertainty for each item but rarely states the decision fork that would follow. M6 is ranked above a randomized skip trial without noting that skipped cases lack survey outcomes to validate against. M5, the direct test of coordinator absence, is ranked last while coordinator ownership is what the memo proposes to scale. Rollout thresholds lack comparison direction, the 85% utilization cap is arbitrary, and business and rural segments (19% of the pilot) receive no explicit action.
