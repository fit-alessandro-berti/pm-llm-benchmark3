7.1

The answer is complete and often directionally right, but it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative claims are wrong, and the July priority rule is repeatedly treated as a control-flow break the traces do not show.

March is correctly identified as mostly semantic drift: raw fitness falls from about 0.93 to 0.55 while mapped fitness stays near 0.90, and the later mapped-fitness decline is not waved away. The L1 distances on the six non-constant DFG rates are arithmetically right (March 3, May 40, July 59, September 28, October 64, December 36). Most January-relative changes are right, supplier rework is properly confounded with pre-existing complexity, December is left unidentified, and the monitoring set, segments, and seven IF/THEN rules cover the required mechanisms. Word counts and table schemas comply.

These errors are material. March median cycle time is 31.2 versus 31.5, a change of about −1.0%, not +0.5%. Mapped fitness is worst in July (0.86), not August (0.87). Median month-to-month gains are about +4.5 h in May, +4.3 h in June, and +6.3 h in October, versus +3.4 h in July and +0.8 h in August, so “steepest acceleration in July–August” is false. p90 rises from 69 to 121 (×1.75), which is not “nearly doubles.” Missingness moves from 4.8 to 6.5 and back to 5.4; it is not flat. Distinct labels rise by two, so the increase is not “only” the eligibility rename. `pm4py.discover_declare` is not the manual’s discovery call.

The causal timing is blurred. Median, p90, supplier share, and rework all jump in May, before the 15 June staffing cut, yet the first performance change point is placed at staffing and May–June are one interval with evidence strength 80. The p90/median ratio moves from about 2.33 in June to 2.35 in July, with no July break. Senior Assessment stays about 7–9 per 100 cases through September and jumps to 22 in October, so “post-July” routing is the wrong date. May-versus-July therefore confounds staffing with the priority rule. The priority rule is a queue policy, not a demonstrated control-flow change.

Process-mining caveats are missed. The two rework edges move almost one-for-one, so L1 double-counts them; the decision-edge decline can be windowing as cycle time grows; fitness is worst when DFG distance is not, which is the signature of frequency change inside an allowed model. `supplier_z_pct` tripling is not what “incomplete bundles” predicts, and mix versus within-supplier rate is never separated. Fast-plus-rework stays near 68–70 until October–November, when it falls to about 64, an unused third-variant signal. Benjamini–Hochberg is invoked on threshold alerts that are not p-values. The preferred “case-level” population is a four-way aggregate that likely crosses priority with the complexity flag that defines it, and the pre-July queue baseline already contains the supplier and staffing shocks.
