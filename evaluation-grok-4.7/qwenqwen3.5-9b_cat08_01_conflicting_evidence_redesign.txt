The diagnosis names the right tension and cites real artifacts (fast-approval complaint rate 14.1% versus 3.3% on the completed repair path, the 140-hour unclear-defect median, the 26% repeat-evidence figure, R1–R3, and the 72-hour and 96-hour complaint edges). The three redesigns are specific to this process, each states a sacrifice, and the experiment section does check whether harm moves to the escalation board. That is not enough to carry the answer, because several load-bearing readings of the log are wrong and two redesigns would act on those misreadings.

The central cost claim is reversed. Fast-approval cases stay about 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits; the advantage shrinks from 31% to 9%. Calling that path “significantly more expensive” after rework changes the decision. Edge counts are also added (3600 + 2550 + 2300) as if they were case volume. Log fitness 0.91 is reported as “68% fitness”; 68% is the share of fully fitting traces. The complaint-to-manual edge (310 cases, mean 40 hours) is the R3-conforming handoff, not evidence that reopened claims bypass manual review. The stated deviation is complaint after close. Fraud/risk is treated as underused; the staffing note says those two specialists are the peak bottleneck, and quality check is the underused pool.

Redesign A sends premium low-risk accessories onto the manual path. Their fast median is 8 hours, the manual median is 46 hours, and service-credit exposure is already high past 24 hours. R2 also carves out exactly this segment. The stated sacrifice then swaps medians across segments (“standard” moving from 8 hours to 13 hours). Those 8 and 13 hours are the premium and standard fast-path medians, not a before/after for one segment. The remaining standard fast path still has no evidence check, so the claim that this routing eliminates fast approval before evidence completeness does not follow.

Redesign B and its table disagree: the sacrifice text says unclear-defect cycle time rises, while the table says it improves. The portal evidence only supports a 30–45% cut in repeated requests, plus abandonment risk, not a collapsed loop or removal of fast-path complaints. Redesign C’s rule set conflicts with itself (premium forced to repair quote, while low-risk accessories stay fast), so premium low-risk accessories have no single destination. The 3.3% complaint rate is treated as a property the path will keep if volume is pushed onto it; that rate is for cases that already finished repair quote, approval, and repair. The pre-dispatch gate from the brief is a replacement-part control, then applied to repair quotes. Forcing repair also ignores the 12.5% rework-repair rate on high-value devices and the EUR 410 versus EUR 95 wrong-replacement costs.

The comparison marks the current process Pareto-dominated and all three redesigns undominated. The brief says dominance cannot be settled from these data; several cells also contradict the redesign text. Pilot thresholds are attached to the wrong segments and baselines: a 24-hour stop for standard customers, whose tolerance is about 72 hours, and a stop if unclear defects exceed 100 hours when their median is already 140. A two-week repair-quote mandate on 35% of volume also cannot see complaints whose medians are 50–70 hours and whose tails run to 150–180 hours.

The write-up is complete and non-generic, but the arithmetic, conformance reading, segment SLAs, and causal use of path rates are unreliable at the points an executive would use them.
