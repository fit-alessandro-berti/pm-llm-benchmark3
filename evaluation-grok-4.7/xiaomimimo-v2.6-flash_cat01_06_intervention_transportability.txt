7.5

The transport conclusion is right — conditional go, limited to the no-originals stratum, with Site A’s nine-hour drop treated as a confounded bundle — and the pilot, modifiers, and stop rules are operationally serious. The score is held down by concrete errors in process structure, causal edges, DFG arithmetic, and the Petri/POWL code.

Section 1 misstates Site B: Legal Review is not a prerequisite of Assessment. The DFG and the rules put it after Assessment (Assessment → Legal Review 1,360; Assessment → Approve/Reject otherwise). The same row’s “consequence” cell then says Legal Review stays downstream, so the table contradicts itself.

The transport graph includes `legal_capacity → waiting_before_assessment`, which cannot hold: legal work is post-assessment. `digital_documents → originals_required` and `treatment_parallelization → identity_capacity/document_capacity` blur selection, load, and capacity. `cycle_time → appeal` is an unsupported edge. The prose is inside the word limit and correctly names the originals gate, the lock, and co-interventions.

Quantitative work is directionally strong (eligible share ~34–38%, structural saving well below 9h, one scenario where the median improves and p90 worsens, Little’s-law suspicion that “service” medians bundle waiting). Two counts are wrong. Identity volume is given as 2,050 + 1,020 = 3,070; those DFG rows are not additive event counts. Document-check volume is given as 3,260 + 345 = 3,605, which double-counts supplement re-entries that already sit inside Document Check → Assessment. Workload is `n × median`, with no skew caveat, on a process whose tail is the point. The 4.2h “structural” piece mixes before-period sums (3.5+7.2) with after-period maxima (max(3.1, 6.5)); holding service times fixed yields about 3.1–3.5h. The utilization conclusion survives these mistakes; the arithmetic does not.

The intervention and pilot are the best parts: statutory gating, blocked-not-started document tasks, pool throttles, fidelity versus mediators, saturation caps, and an MDE near 12% (~5h) at ~578 cases per arm. Gaps are smaller: no negative-control process, and scenario S6 uses the confidence column for attribution purity rather than scenario credibility.

The POWL/Petri block would not reliably run. Current PM4Py exposes `StrictPartialOrder.add_edge` and `OperatorPOWL` with `Operator.XOR` / `Operator.LOOP`, not `POWL(nodes=, order=)` plus `add_node`/`add_order`. The fallback import `pm4py.objects.powl.converter` is the wrong path (`pm4py.objects.conversion.powl.converter`). Approve and Reject are both ordered after Assessment, so every trace is approved and rejected; the optional legal branch is a second forced model, and `ExclusiveChoice` is not a real class. Unrolling the supplement loop is explained, but it is not the loop operator the library exists to provide. Comments on capacity, locks, durations, and data-dependent gates are accurate.

Assumption register format and count are correct, and the recommendation’s expand/modify/abandon triggers match the analysis. The answer is a strong transport memo with too many local falsehoods, especially where model fidelity was an explicit criterion, to score higher.
