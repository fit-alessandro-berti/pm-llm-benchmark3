6.5

The role model is complete and mostly well aimed: all six required sections are present, roles are not assigned by raw volume, complexity and substitution are used, and shadow roles get both benefits and risks. That substance is undercut by repeated factual and logical errors.

The main table misstates evidence a process owner would rely on. Giorgio does not perform “59% of [Fatima’s] A7/A8 volume during absences”; 72/122 is his full-period A7 count over hers, while the absence window is 31 A7 and 18 A8. Nora is 690 low-complexity cases out of 775 (690+85), not “690/765,” and that fraction is cases, not A1/A2 events; the same wrong fraction is repeated later. Mateo’s A7 count is 10, so “A1–A12 all >10” is false. Giorgio’s A7+A8 share is about 19.5% of ~820 events, not 18%.

Several claims are stronger than the log. Giorgio already holds a pricing certification, so the absence cover was not done “without matched certification.” No handover network is in the evidence, yet Mateo’s cross-activity work is said to be what “the handover network shows.” His 16 A6 events are a 10-week total, not events “during weeks 7–8,” and Erik is not described as unavailable in that episode. Erik’s A5/A10/A14 counts are not shown to be on security-tagged cases. Quality A10 is not shown to be re-execution after failed checks. Hana does not “own” reopen decisions (Mateo has more A13 events). Calling 0.11 a complexity ceiling ignores Nora’s 85 medium cases, and Erik’s 9 KB updates are not a “high” A14 load. Variant 1 also names the temp agents, so it is not dominated by Amira and Ben. Grouping Chen and Dina as one role, despite 0.71 vs 0.48 median complexity and 42 vs 9 critical cases, fights the required caution that similar profiles are not similar risk; the later caution does not repair the grouping.

The code covers the requested PM4Py calls, but it is not coherent. `discover_organizational_roles` is iterated as a dict; `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with parameter names that are not the PM4Py ones; `substitution_share` is the share of substitution rows belonging to the role, not the share of the role’s work done as substitution; the broker score does not measure the diversity the comment describes; similarity and working-together results are unused; and the guardrail refers to a nonexistent resource, “Kim.” Core vs peripheral is also blurred for Giorgio when slower substitution performance is treated as evidence that the activity is peripheral.
