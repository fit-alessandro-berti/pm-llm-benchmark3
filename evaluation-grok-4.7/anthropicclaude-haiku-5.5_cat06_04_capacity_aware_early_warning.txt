6.2

The answer is a complete six-part policy with the right traps handled and with portfolio arithmetic that largely audits. It is not coherent as a deployable rule: the stated gates do not permit the portfolio that is then selected.

The probability screen requires adjusted central risk at least 0.50 and adjusted lower risk at least 0.35. I-04 is Strategic, so \(0.48 - 0.085 \approx 0.40\), and even raw \(p_{now}=0.48\) fails 0.50. The same row reports that adjusted probability and still awards slot 1. I-08 at about 0.35 fails both cuts and is still marked eligible. The evidence gate requires a 0.10 smoothed move or a defined material event, and it says a refresh is not an event. I-02 moved by 0.08 and I-09 by 0.07, with no new artefact, stage change, or feature correction. Both are then selected, and I-02 is mislabeled evidence-changing. Section 4 later treats only a third unchanged alert as closed, so the two sections state different persistence rules. Ranking by robust score, with time only as a tie-break, does not support taking I-02 at about \(+€25\) ahead of I-08 at about \(+€250\). “About equal” is not what those scores say.

The robust-score arithmetic is otherwise tight. Uniform 25th percentiles, the NNLS and imputed 10th percentiles, \(€450\), full false-alarm burden, and \(6 \times 0.02 \times €15{,}000 = €1{,}800\) reproduce the reported scores for I-01, I-02, and I-04 through I-10. I-03’s robust figure of about \(−€550\) omits that same \(€1{,}800\); its midpoint of about \(−€2{,}050\) includes it. The scenario sums also check: benefits \(€14.1k/€5.4k/€4.6k\), \(€11.4k/€4.8k/€3.75k\), and \(€6.0k/€3.6k/€0.5k\), costs \(€4.75k\), and spillover \(€2.7k/€5.4k/€8.1k\). Optimistic I-04 uses absolute reduction 0.235, above the section 1 cap of \(0.5 \times p_{adj} \approx 0.20\). Full false-alarm burden is charged again in the optimistic row, where the assumed effect is the upper half of the range, so that row mixes a “needed intervention” effect with a certain false-alarm penalty. The \(€15{,}000\) untreated value is disclosed, but it is unanchored and decides most deferrals. No sensitivity is shown.

I-03 is correctly barred by stage. I-05 is suppressed for an unchanged third refresh. I-07 is reopened because of the new log and still refused a swarm; at \(€5{,}000\), even the top of its effect range cannot clear \(€450 + €50 + €1{,}800\). The reserve is kept, and the adverse row correctly puts one selected case near zero. Those are the strongest parts of the allocation.

The later safeguards conflict with that allocation. A one-in-four floor for cases under \(€10{,}000\) would have required a low-value slot among today’s four; none is given, and there is no rule for when the floor overrides a negative score. A \(€1{,}800\) per-segment daily spillover cap is the same size as one base slot, while the decision deploys three. NNLS randomization is proposed, then I-09 is treated deterministically. Refitting calibration offsets when the treated-minus-untreated outcome gap moves by more than 0.05 treats a real intervention effect as miscalibration. Retiring the policy after three “adverse-scenario” weeks is not operational, because that scenario is a constructed stress case rather than an observed result. The 14-day outcome window is also unexplained for SLA breaches that resolve on the incident clock.

This is a serious, mostly auditable draft whose own eligibility rule would reject its selected portfolio. That blocks a high score.
