3.5

The fragment is a partial, internally inconsistent draft. Easy completed-case arithmetic is mostly right, and the resource-feasibility bounds for P05, P07, and P11 are the correct non-overlap intervals, but the required analysis is not delivered and several stated claims contradict the data or the answer’s own definitions.

The required document stops during the P13 row. Sections 4–7 are absent: there is no station table, no arrival/departure or WIP trajectory, no utilization numbers, no bottleneck comparison, no three-scenario sensitivity analysis, and no Python. PM4Py is never used. P15, P16, P17, P21, and P22, all mandatory, never appear, so right-censored waits and open service are defined in A6–A8 and then omitted from the reconstruction.

That omission collides with an explicit error in §2: P15 is grouped with P05, P07, and P11 as having an exact total sojourn. P15 is `OPEN_IN_S2` (S2 end open; S1 start missing). Only its S1 sojourn, 12:15−10:10, is fixed. Final service and case sojourn are censored. The same paragraph says a missing start leaves wait+service fixed. That is true for P05 and P11 only because both the queue-entry timestamp and the end are known. The P07 note then explains the fixed 75 minutes by “I1 free 09:25–10:00,” which is a 35-minute window. The 75 minutes are 10:00−08:45. The number is right; the justification is not.

A1 says every two-timestamp duration carries an extra ±5 minutes, while §3 calls those sojourns fixed. The printed A1 interval is also missing the lower sign, “[t2.5, t+2.5].” Rounding is never actually propagated, so the table’s integers look more precise than A1 allows. Midpoints make this worse. P05’s central service is ~15 and P11’s is ~12.5, both below every fully observed S2 service (shortest is P10 at 25). For P11 the feasible service is [0, 25] only because C2 is occupied by P08 until 12:00 and P11 ends at 12:25. A credible central imputation is a start near 12:00 and service near 25, not the midpoint 12.5. Zero service is left as an unmarked operational endpoint.

Resource accounting is wrong: “two of the four resources” have missing or open work. I1 (P07, P15, P21), C1 (P05, P17), and C2 (P11, P15) do; only I2 is fully bracketed. Busy time and utilization are collapsed into one sentence. Little’s law is restricted to intervals whose WIP returns to the same level. The sample-path identity does not need that, and equal endpoint WIP is only a weak stationarity proxy. “W for cases active in the interval” mixes left- and right-censored sojourns. The WIP sentence treats “still present” as a departure event, which would drop open cases out of WIP at 14:00.

The P08 remark over-attributes a 55-minute S2 wait to the 15-minute C2 outage. About 30 minutes of that wait is ordinary queueing from 10:20 to 10:50, while C1 is on P07. P09 starts on C1 at 11:00, during the outage, and P10 later waits 50 minutes on C1 after C2 is back. The outage is real capacity loss; it does not by itself explain the wait jump.

Fully observed rows P01–P04, P06, and P08–P13 match the clocks, and the P05/P07/P11 bound endpoints match resource release and completion. Those checks cannot carry an answer that never measures queues, WIP, throughput, utilization, or the bottleneck, and that drops every censored case.
