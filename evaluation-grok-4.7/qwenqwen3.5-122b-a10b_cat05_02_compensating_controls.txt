4.5

The case verdicts are only partly reliable, and the supporting analysis and code contain material errors.

The nine classifications are directionally better than a mechanical four-eyes count: AP-202 and AP-208 fail, AP-205 is stopped before completion, AP-203 and AP-207 can meet the objective through ED-1, and distinct users do not automatically pass. That is the main credit. Several of those rows are still inaccurate. AP-203 and AP-207 omit the manager action from both the preventive and detective columns even though that approval is the central compensating control; the rationales mention it and the columns do not. AP-206 places a pre-release manager approval under detective controls, contradicting Section 4’s own rule that pre-release manager action is preventive, and it treats the token timestamp as a proven ED-1 failure when the facts leave logging delay unresolved. AP-204’s rationale says collusion was detected; the fabricated receipt was found, while collusion is denied and the two-person cell explanation is unresolved. AP-201 lists emergency-token status as missing although the facts state there was no token. AP-205 is marked a completed four-eyes violation even though release never completed. Corrective-capacity reasons are also wrong in places: AP-204 is “none” because the receipt was fraudulent, and AP-208 is “none” because the amount was high, rather than because recall timing had passed. INDETERMINATE is never used where the record is explicitly ambiguous.

Sections 1 and 2 cover the required topics but do not operate as a real sufficiency test. Bank-detail change is conflated with compromise of the operator account, ED-1 is treated as though it were itself a preventive control, and the four equivalence levels are not tied to a decision rule using timing, independence, coverage, override, recoverability, evidence quality, and collusion resistance. Section 4’s release-versus-settlement semantics are coherent, yet Section 5 reads “which control breaks the causal chain” as the point of failure, assumes collusion or backdating, and for AP-201 falls back to the expected-loss reasoning Section 2 rejected. Section 6 hits most monitoring topics and distinguishes alert from proof, but shared-workstation monitoring is not a separate rule, and the 5%, 2%, and 10-per-month cutoffs are presented as rules without being labeled illustrative.

The code reconstructs the log and labels its output as evidence, but it is not a sound checker. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called without `keep_violations=True`, so a real PM4Py install returns compliant cases, not violations, and returns a log rather than case IDs; the later concatenation can crash, and only `AttributeError` is caught. The facts table marks manager-before-settlement true for AP-204, AP-205, and AP-209, where no manager acted, and marks 24-hour audit true for AP-204 and AP-205, where audit was late or absent. Required AV-coverage and repeated-pair flags are not emitted, and emergency eligibility is reduced to a token flag, so the ineligible high-value emergency case is not flagged.
