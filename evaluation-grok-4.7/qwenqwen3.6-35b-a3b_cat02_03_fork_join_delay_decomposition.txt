The case-level decomposition is the only part that holds up. The formulas for upstream time, branch queue, branch service, `join_ready = max(end)`, downstream queue, final service, and total flow time are the right max-based fork-join identities, and the 12-row table plus the F02, F04, F07, and F11 walkthroughs match the timestamps. Critical labels in that table are also right: A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, B on the other eight.

Everything built on that table is inconsistent with it.

The aggregate comparison says B is critical in 9/12 cases and C in 1/12. The same document’s table shows 8 and 2. Reported means are not the means of the reported columns: B service is 37.5, not 38.3; C service is 20.2, not 19.5; B queue is 19.2, not 18.8; C queue is 10.4, not 8.6. Mean fork-join stage time is 61 minutes, not 56.7; 56.7 is the mean of B’s own queue-plus-service, and it is placed under A. The “mean synchronization wait when not critical” row (10, 0, 35) is F02’s three waits, not an average. B’s sync wait is not 0: F01, F04, F08, and F11 all finish B before the join. The tail claim that total flow time is driven by B’s queue “combined with A’s fixed service” over-attributes A. On the long cases, F07 and F12, B’s path is 75 minutes and A is strictly inside it.

The counterfactuals do not recompute the join. Cutting a critical branch only saves time up to the gap to the runner-up. A −20% (10 minutes) saves 5 minutes on F01, because B ends at 08:55, and 10 minutes on F11, not 10 and 10. B queue −50% does not save ~85 minutes over 9 cases. B is critical in 8 cases, and on F05 and F10 the runner-up absorbs part of the cut; the flow-time saving is 92.5 minutes if joins are recomputed and later stations are held fixed. The zero-benefit list (F01, F04, F08, F11) has four cases, which contradicts the claim that B is critical nine times. Downstream queue sums to 176 minutes (mean 14.7), not 171 (mean 14.25).

The final-station diagnosis is not earned from the resource log. Several waits occur while the assigned server, and sometimes both servers, are idle: F01 joins at 09:00 with F1 and F2 free and still starts at 09:08; F07 joins at 10:55 while F1 has been free since 10:40. F10 is not capacity-bound for most of its 20-minute wait. Recommending dynamic F1/F2 routing, and calling final capacity the binding constraint on F09/F10, treats a pattern the timestamps already reject. The limits section names release policy versus queue as a possibility, but the redesign section does not use the evidence that distinguishes them. The “B queue below ~10 minutes makes A binding” rule is also the wrong comparison: A binds when `Q_B + S_B` falls to 50, and `S_B` varies from 30 to 45.

The Python block does not implement the required analysis. Converting every column, including `case` and `final_resource`, with `HH:MM` parsing throws before any result is produced. The critical-branch chain overwrites an A-critical case with C whenever C ends after B, so the stated A > B > C tie rule is not what the code does. The counterfactual subtracts 20% of A service or 50% of B queue whenever that branch is already critical, with no new `max`. Only aggregate savings are printed, and they are signed as negative. The PM4Py frame has one fork-join row and one final row, not one row per A, B, C, and final activity; timestamps are minute integers; `format_dataframe(..., format="log")` does not match the library’s arguments; and `case:concept:name` is never set. The comment that directly-follows performance cannot recover synchronization wait is the right caveat, attached to code that never computes that wait.

Section 7 does cover non-identifiability of causality, branch capacity, preemption, and release policy, and the two redesigns at least name throughput, tail, utilization, a new bottleneck, synchronization, and a pilot. Those sections cannot carry a document whose central frequency, influence table, savings, and reproducible check all disagree with its own case table.
