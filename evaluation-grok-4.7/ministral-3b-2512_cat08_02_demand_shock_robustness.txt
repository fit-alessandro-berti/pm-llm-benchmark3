The write-up has the required seven sections and table columns, and it sometimes names real conflicts (M2 repeat risk, technical-validation loss from cross-training, routine starvation, partner-lab delay). Those merits do not survive the load-bearing errors. Several recommendations are operationally impossible or point the wrong way, and the process-mining evidence is mostly unused or misread.

**Factual reversals and misreads.** M2 is the faster, higher-failure analyzer; the outage scenario calls M1 more error-prone. “760 recollections = 7.5% rework” and “11.4% repeats” do not match the logs (recollection is about 4% of normal orders; assay-repeat share is about 6.7% normal and 14.8% peak). The normal-period arc Technical validation → Medical validation = 3,900 is a transition count over eight weeks, not a backlog. Medical validators and the specialist-review resource are repeatedly treated as one pool, despite different utilizations, queues (22h vs 36h at peak), and the every-other-Friday absence applying to the specialist. The claimed drop of the accession queue from 3.0h to 1.5h is invented.

**Bottleneck migration is causally backwards.** Specialist absence is said to create a technical-validation backlog, and routing onto M1 is said to cause pre-analytical delay. Both stations are upstream of the constraint being discussed; a downstream stop piles WIP backward, it does not create that mechanism. Scenario mixes are incomplete or garbled and do not sum to 100%. Scenario 1 bundles mix shift, validator loss, and courier delay, so migration cannot be attributed. Performance edges are almost unused: recollection return at 42h/60h, hold → medical validation at 18h/48h, batch-wait → assay at 9h/22h, and repeat → assay at 10h/26h. The sharpest variant fact is missed: urgent batch-wait bypass falls from 74% to 58% at peak, meaning priority already fails under load. Repeat-after-failure (worst tails, double assay consumption, clustered after night shifts and M2) never receives a policy.

**The package is not robust.** I6 and I2 are said to reduce complex-panel volume and to add no new bottleneck. I6 does not shrink complex demand; at a 9.5h pre-analytical queue it also cannot guarantee a 2h reject without a separate lane, and faster rejects feed the 60h recollection loop and clerk rework. I2 (batches of 24) fights the evidence that batch-wait share rises from about 4% to about 13% in the peak, and it is a poor always-on rule when urgent share is rising. I4 is always-on even though technical validators are already at 101% peak. The “no new bottleneck” summary contradicts the assignment constraint and the answer’s own failure-mode column.

**Scenario policies contradict their triggers.** Under an M2 outage the prescribed rule still routes routine work to M2 and speeds registration (I8). That accelerates inflow into a broken assay resource; intake should be throttled. The trade-off row then marks Urgent, Routine, and Complex-panel all “Excellent,” which violates the rule that case types cannot be prioritized simultaneously and is implausible with the faster machine down. For the winter mix (urgent + complex as the majority, and complex assays counting more than case share), the stated next risk is M2 overload; the likely overload is M1. “Specialist review > 50% queue” is not an operable trigger, and the Friday absence is predictable, so the policy waits for a queue the calendar already announces. I6 and I7 are both baseline and the courier-specific policy, so that scenario does not actually switch rules. Rework cells list “transport rework” and “increased priority rules,” which are not rework.

**Monitoring does not separate controls.** End-to-end urgent Register → Release time is labeled leading; it is a lagging outcome. Medical-validation and specialist-hold queues are merged, so the dashboard cannot choose between an evening validator shift and partner overflow. A 95% assay-utilization alarm is late relative to the jump from a 4h to an 18h queue. Urgent p90 < 16h is tighter than the normal-period p90 (19h) and tighter than the 18h penalty line, which would lock in urgent-first starvation. Complex-panel and repeat tails, the worst cycle times in the log, are absent from the lagging set.

The residual-risk section does note self-inflicted harm from I3 and I10, but it misses the larger redesign failures: always-on larger batches, validator cross-pull at 101% utilization, and faster intake during an assay outage.
