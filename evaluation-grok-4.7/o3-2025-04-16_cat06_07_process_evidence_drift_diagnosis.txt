The month-by-month story is the right one: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June is a routing mix shift, August–September stack supplier delay with a real within-V3 breach jump and then Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is censored. That is not enough for a high score. The write-up states unobserved analyses as facts, mislabels some of those facts, contradicts itself, and the required code sketch would not run or meet the stated spec.

The diagnosis table invents evidence. Nothing in the data supports “even after conditioning on part-wait buckets, late-August cases still breach more often,” or a V1-only AUROC “pre-test.” Those claims are later proposed as future tests, so the same result is both a finding and an open question. Technician shortage and SLA throttling are guessed causes. “Jan–Aug labels fully mature” is false (July 98%, August 93%). May’s residual gap is blamed on the April cache miss, which the audit limits to April. Within-V3 breach doubling and an unobserved lead time are filed under covariate drift; flat mean risk near 0.19 is what you expect if the model still applies pre-crisis variant rates, which is concept or omitted-variable drift, not mix shift. October is also stuffed into the pipeline row when the predictive failure there is censoring. Arrows and dashes are gone throughout, so figures collapse into unreadable tokens such as “0.810.67.”

The tests name the right checks, then botch several expected results. If October is only immature, matured breach should rise toward the long-wait, V3-heavy regime, not “converge to earlier months.” Fixed-vintage backtesting is named and not specified. Rebuilding April without recomputing the cached diagnosis-count feature can leave a pipeline bug that the test would misread as concept drift. The Partner Z reweight is a false dichotomy: failure to restore AUROC also follows from supplier delay and differential label delay. Stratified calibration is required and omitted.

Containment is directionally right and then inconsistent. Section 4 pools June–August for Platt scaling and trains through 31 August at 93% maturity; section 6 refuses cohorts under 95%. June and August are different regimes, so one scaling layer mixes a V2 mix shift with a V3 rate shift. A V3 head is defensible; a multi-task network is not earned. Realized part-wait must not be fed in at diagnosis time, and that leakage risk is missed in the immediate actions.

The monitor list and the maturity gate on retraining are sound. The code is not. It never builds a case-start cohort or states how event-time slices differ from case-start cohorts; `cases_started` actually counts cases with any event in the month. It does not call `pm4py.filter_time_range`, `pm4py.get_variants`, or `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. `timestamp_filter.filter_timestamp` and `get_variants_df` are not the public API. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a tuple whose edge values are aggregated floats, so `.get(...).total_seconds()` breaks, and the field is named median under a default mean aggregation. Event-month filtering also drops edges that cross the boundary, which biases the exact supplier wait this audit turns on. `format_dataframe` renames the keys the later lines still use.

Section 6’s five failure modes are the cleanest part, and the Partner Z fairness warning is proportionate. They do not repair the earlier contradictions.
