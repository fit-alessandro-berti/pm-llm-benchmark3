The case-level dialectic is structured and often directionally right, but it is not reliable enough to score higher. Several high-stakes calls are sound (SUP-701 compliant; SUP-703 and SUP-708 noncompliant; SUP-704 justified inside FD-7; SUP-705 and SUP-714 unauthorized after expiry; SUP-707 a model gap; SUP-712 a real segregation-of-duties breach). Those conclusions do not offset factual, semantic, and deliverable failures.

The briefs miss required concessions. Prosecution never squarely grants the genuine model and data defects (the information-request loop, ambiguous rejected-lead scope, reactivation, duplicate registration, and label equivalence). Defense argues post-expiry continuity and minimizes sanctions-after-activation rather than accepting severe risk and the limits of informal practice. Aggregate evidence is essentially unused: 289/312 apparently eligible auto-approvals, 14 ineligible, 9 incomplete, 47 safe information requests, the rise in missing notifications from 2% to 29%, six early-August auto-approvals, and Internal Audit’s focus on SUP-703 and SUP-708.

The evidence matrix repeatedly treats given facts as missing: stable `notification_id` (SUP-702), existing daily monitoring (SUP-704), no pre-clearance transactions (SUP-708), a certification already said to be valid (SUP-710), resolved aliases (SUP-712), and a mapping date already known to be one week late (SUP-713). SUP-708’s trace problem is understated: quality occurred before activation, while sanctions and manual approval occurred after. SUP-709 misreads CS-9, whose quality requirement is tied to activation, not to every rejection. SUP-715 is misclassified legally: no rule prohibits spouses; the proven breach is missing conflict disclosure, and it is wrongly equated with the requester/approver identity rule. The matrix would let a retroactive CEO waiver reverse SUP-705, contradicting the stated burden of proof, and that CEO authority is invented.

The revised model is not executable and is semantically invalid. `activate_node = Activate` raises `NameError`, so the function cannot return a model. The last construction puts manual approval, auto-approval, activation, and rejection into one strict partial order, whose nodes are all required; the XOR operators therefore do not create exclusive routes. The information request is mandatory rather than repeatable and optional, the loop construct has the wrong child arity, and unused silent transitions never create a skip. Quality is ordered before activation, so neither the pilot route nor FD-7’s provisional route is in the language. Data rules cannot add traces that a partial order forbids. The emergency pattern—sanctions, then activation, then quality and manual approval—is absent.

The architecture omits DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints and withdrawals. The “policy-overlay table” is only an unpopulated, non-normalized schema, without delegation evidence, frozen eligibility snapshots, or separate effective and record timestamps.
