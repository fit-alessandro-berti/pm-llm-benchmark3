**6.5**

The design covers every required section and gets several hard constraints right: waiting is emergent, objection events stay latent, calendars and non-stationary demand are in scope, PM4Py is separated from the engine, and no optimality claim is made. Those strengths do not offset multiple methodological contradictions and intervention mechanisms that cannot produce the effects the experiment table claims.

**Estimation and validation errors**
- Effective capacity is written as `c_eff ≈ λ E[S] / ρ_target`. That is staffing to a target utilization. It does not estimate effective capacity. Activity-level λ, rework, and part-time calendars are also omitted, so the comparison with stated FTE is not identified.
- Trace-driven validation replays observed inter-event gaps and still claims this tests pools and calendars. Those gaps already embed waiting, so queueing and shift calendars are bypassed. A resource check needs observed arrivals and service, with waiting left emergent.
- Decision metrics are quantiles of finished cases only. Policies that lengthen the tail censor more cases and can look better on a conditional median and p90. `censored_share` is only a stability flag. There is no Kaplan–Meier or other censored quantile.
- Section 6 defines utilization as busy time over calendar time, which cannot exceed 100% in this DES. The “no pool above 100%” instability check is then vacuous. Instability shows up as growing WIP.
- With about 31,200 cases, a chi-square or KS test will reject trivial gaps. The tolerance remark does not fix using those tests as acceptance criteria.
- Observed utilization via Little’s law reuses the same inferred service times the model was given, so it is not an independent check.

**Contradictions between the spec, the assumptions, and the generator**
- Public notice is a 14-day statutory minimum plus an extension. The sampler draws a lognormal with median 14 days, so about half of notices finish before the legal minimum. That contradicts Section 2.4 and the variant table.
- Section 2.4 treats the environmental gap as an exogenous consultant delay (about 8 days plus 4 hours of service). The engine puts only a 4-hour service on the env pool and expects the 8 days to emerge as queue time. Those cannot both be the baseline. D8 (triage of the municipal queue) has a different effect under each story. Rounding 3 × 0.6 = 1.8 FTE up to 2 makes the queue story optimistic.
- Site-inspection p90 is “3h + travel.” The sampler uses 3 hours and never adds district travel, while D3/D5 fairness and batching benefits depend on that travel.
- Baseline no-show probability is zero. The problem states that no-shows matter. S4 only tests 15% and 25%.
- “Restarts at the same activity, not at a fresh queue position” and “the queue position is re-acquired” cannot both be true.
- Section 1 couples public notice to the council calendar. The generator samples an uninterrupted delay. Business-day counting versus blackout days is undefined.
- Assumption text says reject probability is conditional on planning or legal conflict. The generator uses a flat 0.10, and there is no conflict state.
- The first missing-document cycle increments `rework`. Ordinary incomplete filings and true rework are then the same metric, so the “+2 percentage points” rework guardrail is not interpretable. D1 moves that mixed metric by construction.

**Experiment design that does not match the mechanisms**
- Planning rework, legal rework, and appeal are part of the stated process and of the 140-day complex tail. The tree omits them, which the answer notes, but neither Section 1 nor the generator specifies the return point, probability, or council coupling. Tail rankings for D5, D6, D9, and D10 are not identified until that exists.
- D5’s stated cost is extra legal rework when planning changes. Nothing in the model changes planning after legal starts, so that trade-off cannot appear. Only an earlier legal arrival would.
- D8’s levels are classification-error rates. There is no rule for what triage changes: skip, shorten, reassign, or delay. False negatives have no process consequence.
- D3 is specified as same-architect, same-district bundling. The candidate intervention is district batching, and the brief treats related-case bundling as current practice. The baseline has no bundling. The coded placeholder adds the full window as wait for every case and does not reduce travel, so the smoke-test effect has the wrong sign.
- D4 is `Policy("D4")` with empty `extra_inspector_months`, and capacity is fixed at construction. D10 is defined as D1+D2+D3+D4 but is coded as D1+D2+D3 only.
- Headline cycle time is pooled across peak and off-peak arrivals. D4 can only act in peak months, and there is no peak-season duration metric, so the decision rule dilutes the intervention the study is built to test.
- S2 doubles top-architect session intensity without stating that total volume is held fixed. Burstiness is confounded with demand.
- Section 4’s roughly 2,000–4,000 runs omit the level grid and the Section 5 plan to carry an ABC posterior through every policy. Nested Sobol (each sample itself a stochastic DES) is not costed, and it is unclear whether one Sobol point is one replication or a fully replicated experiment. Optional stopping on the CI half-width also nudges coverage.

**Skeleton defects relative to the design**
- Attribute generation is independent of the joint structure the estimator describes: `requires_fire` is ignored, value is not stratified by case type (`Lognormal(10.5, 1)` makes commercial values look like small renovations), architects are uniform over 300 ids (top 20 share about 6.7%, not 31%), and `ArchitectBurstOverlay.assign` raises `NotImplementedError`. The runnable path is an independent-case NHPP.
- `get_variants_as_tuples` is version-sensitive. In PM4Py builds where the values are counts, `len(traces)` throws. Variant statistics are a required output.
- Calendar logic is real, but council `closed_days` stay empty, inspection scheduling is charged to the clerk pool despite `scheduler_1` in the log, and the dedicated Monday portal clerk is a TODO. Float equality in `_acquire` happens to be safe only because `next_open` returns the same float when the pool is open.
- CRN preserves arrivals and case attributes across policies in the same scenario. Any policy that changes the number of service or routing draws (D2 is enough) shifts every later draw. Per-case streams keyed by case id are the fix the text gestures at and does not implement.
- Placeholders such as `P_FIRE["New residential"] = 0.3` and `P_NOTICE = 0.6` imply about 5% of cases with both, against an 18% variant share. They are labeled illustrative, and a smoke test against the variant table would still be meaningless.

**What keeps the score from falling further**
The boundary, entity state, shift calendars, NHPP-plus-sessions arrival plan, “do not sample waiting time,” GPD treatment, paired bootstrap, holdout by season, and explicit non-claims are sound. The PM4Py path correctly avoids `pm4py.read_csv`, uses `format_dataframe` with the XES keys, computes a gap column with an explicit wait-plus-service flag, and separates discovery (`discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, token replay) from SimPy. That is a real design, with too many internal contradictions to treat as a decision-ready twin.
