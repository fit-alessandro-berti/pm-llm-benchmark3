7.0

The assessment covers every required heading, uses the supplied figures accurately, and reaches a calibrated conclusion: timeliness, burden, and staffing look procedurally unfair for Groups B and C, while residual approval gaps and Group D remain indeterminate. It also respects the hard constraints. Raw approval gaps are not treated as proof of discrimination, case mix is not treated as a full excuse, `risk_score_band` is not treated as neutral, support steps are kept as possible burdens, and no mitigation removes those steps just to shorten cycle time. Sections 5, 6, and 8 are the strongest.

The core inference in §3 overreaches. A +9.5 percentage-point `Request Missing Documents` gap after controlling for `documentation_completeness` is a within-level residual. It does not show that staff request documents from files already marked complete. Completeness is a coarse, possibly endogenous label, and the activity is not the same variable as the attribute. The same paragraph then says language support “happens too late,” while the parenthetical correctly notes that variant V4 places `Language Support Contact` before `Completeness Check`. V4 is defined as a path that already contains `Request Missing Documents`, so that path cannot show that language support causes the document request. “Routes directly” to supervisor review is also wrong: the V5 summary is Accommodation Review → Eligibility → Supervisor Review.

§2 misstates the groups. Language support is 57% in Group B and accommodation requests are 62% in Group C. Those traits do not almost perfectly define either group, and the published adjustment does not control for `language_preference` or `disability_accommodation_requested`. The two-estimand idea is sound, but later sections still treat those adjusted gaps as unexplained by legitimate routing. “Significantly higher” also implies statistical significance that no standard error supports. The +0.9 day Group D cycle-time gap is conditional on the covariates, not a comparison with high-risk Group A cases specifically.

§4’s capacity, handover, and defensive-escalation mechanisms are plausible. The risk mechanism is not. Group D is defined by high prior-case count and high risk, so their co-occurrence does not show that the score “likely” treats prior applications, wrongful denials, or income volatility as fraud features.

The §7 listing is broader than a sketch, but it is not executable. `case_outcome_binary` is never created, month is never derived from `time:timestamp`, and the fit is an OLS linear probability model rather than the three sequential specifications in §2. `get_service_time` is called without an activity, and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with no start timestamp even though the log has one timestamp column. Repeated activity names are a poor proxy for the given reopen/rework rate. The “> 30 days” filter is mislabeled as the 90th percentile; those percentiles are 21.4, 34.2, 38.8, and 29.5 days. Exact `filter_variants` on a summary path that omits `Case Closure` can drop the cases the plan means to study. Queue ratios use only ~712 Group B language-need cases and ~508 Group C accommodation cases, leaving out the same needs in other groups.

Smaller wording problems accumulate: Group C is repeatedly called disabled applicants, “healthy/low-risk” is unclear, and V5 supervisor review is called mandatory although the policy text does not say that. Sampling uncertainty is named in the §2 introduction and then left out of the design.
