The write-up covers every required section and is often careful (notably C015, C007, C016, and the hard/soft split), but the concrete plan does not implement the stated policy, and several justifications contradict the rules or the formulas.

The overtime rule is inverted in the queue. Rule 4 and the policy’s own exception allow an overtime-high resource only when the case is urgent and no safer eligible resource exists. C003, C014 (David), C006 (Iris), and C010 (Bruno) are normal or low priority with substantial slack, and safer skilled alternatives exist (Celine or Hugo on diagnosis/rework, Jonas on quality, Farid or Eva on approval). The C003 text treats “ample SLA” as a reason to keep David. Ample slack is a reason to refuse the overtime exception. David has 14 open cases and 3.0 hours left and is still given two new cases. Hours remaining never enter the eligibility filter, so that assignment is not even feasible.

Section 3 does not follow section 2. With the published weights, workload term, and continuity override (high complexity and high handover only), C006 should prefer Jonas over Iris, and C010 should not select Bruno while Farid and Eva remain eligible. C006 and C012 are described as one quality batch but are given to two different people, so the batch gate is not actually applied. The stated processing order is not least-slack order: C009 (22h) precedes C010 (20h), and C006 (36h) precedes C014 (25h) and C015 (27h).

C001 misstates a hard rule. H3 allows Security at skill 1–2 under Gita or Hugo. Celine is skill 1, and rule 5 prefers continuity when supervision is feasible. The answer says H3 “forces” a specialist and that Gita is already committed to C008, but C008 is later in the author’s own order. A specialist handover can still be defended with the 3h SLA versus the stated +3.3h weak-skill service penalty and +2.7h handover wait, but that evidence is not used.

The training quota is missed and not repaired. Only C009 clearly supports a documented training priority. That is 1 of 9 normal/low cases, about 11%, against a 20% rule, and the algorithm has no repair pass. C010 “Partial” is wrong: Bruno is approval skill 2 and is not the trainee. C013 does not match Jonas’s training priority (security). C012 is the explicit trainee-quality case and matches Bruno’s priority, but the overtime conflict is never stated. The C015 skill-0 block is correct and well argued.

The score itself is not operational. `est_service_time`, `SK_gate`, `wU`, `max_open`, and `scarcity_pressure` are undefined. The extra `wU*U*SK_gate` term sits outside a weight table that already sums to 1. Clipping the workload score at 0 means the worst load and overtime only forfeit about 0.15–0.20, while continuity is worth 0.25, so fairness cannot win against an incumbent. The specialist penalty of 0.15 on easy work still leaves skill 3 above skill 2. Resilience and batching stay as stubs.

The baseline comparison names all seven dimensions and avoids a fake single optimum, but it never assigns the 16 cases under either baseline, then still claims the lowest SLA risk and the best resilience. Those superlatives are not shown. The worker text is clear and non-punitive, yet it promises overtime protection and supervised pairing that this batch does not deliver.
