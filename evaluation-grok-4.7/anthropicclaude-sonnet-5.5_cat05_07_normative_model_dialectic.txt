The answer is strong and mostly reliable, but it is not nearly flawless. It keeps authority, scope, logging, and control-flow fitness apart, argues both sides without strawmen, and classifies all 15 cases with explicit severity, confidence, and reversal conditions. The deductions below are for concrete errors, not for contestable judgment calls.

The August count is wrong, and it is repeated. The materials report six post-pilot `Auto_Approve` cases in the first week of August. SUP-714 is dated 2026-08-02, so it is one of those cases unless the text says otherwise. Section 2.2 and section 5.2 both say “SUP-714 and six further August cases,” which turns six into seven. That inflates the main evidence of post-expiry drift.

Section 5.3 overclaims the misfit breakdown. It says 6 of the 12 non-fitting cases are “model, scope or data artefacts,” including SUP-706. Section 5.1 correctly calls SUP-706 `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`. An unresolved mutable CRM link is not an established artefact. The “half are artefacts” line, used to answer Operations’ obsolescence claim, is therefore softer than the write-up presents. The 12/15 and 3-fitting counts themselves are right, and 701, 712, and 715 are the correct fitting set.

The burden in section 1.4 is stricter than the labels in section 5.1. An exception must rest on written authority from a body competent to vary CS-9. PD-3’s competence is explicitly unproved, and FD-7’s issuer is unproved, yet SUP-702 and SUP-704 are still `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. Dual attribution and “conditional on” language make this a disclosed choice rather than a hidden compromise, and Medium confidence helps. It remains a softer landing than the stated burden. The sharper calls are sound: SUP-703 critical, SUP-708 high despite no transactions, SUP-705 and SUP-714 noncompliant despite sympathetic facts, SUP-707 not a violation, and SUP-712 noncompliant despite a perfect M0 fit.

The POWL is largely the right approximation. Zero-or-more `Request_More_Info` uses loop order `[silent, activity]`, which is `tau (A tau)*`. Sanctions and quality are unordered on the standard route and both precede manual approval. Rejection no longer forces `Quality_Review`, which correctly fixes M0 against CS-9. Notification sits inside every route and `Close_Case` follows the route, so notification-before-closure holds. Overlay routes are parameterized by time. Those are real semantic choices, not decorative structure.

The code still has defects. `build_reactivation_powl` builds and then discards partial orders, including an unordered approval fragment, and probes a `.children` attribute `StrictPartialOrder` does not have. The returned model is the rebuilt one, and the limitation note admits the scaffolding, but the required executable block should not contain it. `sod_violation` returns true when an alias is unresolved, collapsing “unknown” into “violation,” which contradicts the burden in section 1.4. The auto route orders sanctions before `Auto_Approve`, not merely before activation, and emergency `Request_More_Info` must finish before activation. Neither over-constraint is listed in the limitations. The pandas demo can print `OK` for SUP-704 from window and initial spend alone, with no criticality check and no cumulative-cap check, while the prose treats both as open.

Calendar work is careful: 2026-07-11 as a Saturday, and the resulting Monday follow-up after FD-7’s expiry, is labeled as inference. The pre-migration CRM anomaly on SUP-702 is a real catch. Aggregate arithmetic 289+14+9=312 is right. The architecture table puts eligibility, caps, identity, conflict-of-interest, and sunset rules outside POWL, which is the correct split.
