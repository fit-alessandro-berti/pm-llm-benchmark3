6.4

The estimand framework, censoring discussion, and leakage analysis are strong, but the reported survival numbers are internally inconsistent and do not match the estimator the answer itself specifies. Under a strict reading, those errors are large enough to reverse one of the main qualitative claims.

The time origin (Submit), horizon (72h), and three estimands are well separated. Estimand 1 is \(P(T^*>72)\) for any internal terminal state. Estimand 2 is \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\). Estimand 3 is late resolution mass, \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(t_{\max})-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\). S03 (resolved at 76), S09 (open at 92), S06 (withdrawn at 27), and S08 (transferred at 68) are assigned to the right risk sets, and open cases with age under 72 are correctly treated as still censored at the horizon. Withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are not silently coded as ordinary censoring. The completed-case critique is concrete: 4/13 resolved cases exceed 72h, while S09, S11, S25, and S26 have already passed 72h and are dropped by that analysis; resolved-only P1 (S04, S18) shows a 0% breach while three P1 cases are transferred and three remain open. Cohort B compliance is read correctly from the prefixes: all 5 P1 and all 7 P2 cases start with `Submit>AutoCheck`, and all 5 P3 cases start with `Submit>Screen`.

The method section is specific to this extract. Aalen–Johansen plus restricted mean time fits \(N=32\) and the absence of intermediate timestamps. Cause-specific hazards are separated from cumulative incidence. Fine–Gray is flagged as a poor fit for time-varying internal activities. Escalate, rework, repeated review, and prefix length are classified as time-varying, with immortal time explained. The observation that `last_event_gap_h` is 0 for all 23 terminal rows and positive for all 9 open rows is correct and is a real leakage trap. Last-event entry times for open cases are correct (S11 entered Escalate at 26h, S26 entered Review at 32h, S24 at 63h). The state diagram matches the observed edges, including S27’s `Rework>Resolve` path and direct escalation being confined to 7 of 8 P1 cases.

The numerical layer does not hold.

- For the baseline competing-risk encoding, the product-limit probability of still being internally active at 72h is about 38.0%, and \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\) is about 30.4%. The answer reports 29.7% and 30.9%, and a late-resolution incidence of 19.7% against a correct increment of about 30 percentage points through S13. Restricted mean active time to 72h is about 50.4h, not 47.8h. The code’s Kaplan–Meier / Aalen–Johansen loop would produce the former figures, yet the text says the script produced the latter.
- Cohort active probabilities are about 33.9% (A) and 41.6% (B), not 28.9% and 31.1%. P2’s resolution CIF is about 43.1%, not 38.9%. P1 is an exception: 46.9% still active, 25.0% resolved by 72h, and 53.1% if the two pre-72 transfers count as success, are right.
- Compliant Cohort B (\(n=12\)) has about a 44.4% probability of still being active at 72h. Non-compliant Cohort B (\(n=5\)) has 40.0%. Sections 6 and 8 instead give the compliant active rate as 30.6% and 27.3%, so the claim that compliance lowers the active-at-72 probability runs in the wrong direction. The unresolved comparison (about 63% versus 80%) does go the way the text says. The same section pair also swaps the S2a/S2b labels: narrative S2a is optimistic transfer, while the code’s S2a censors transfers.
- Scenario S3 overwrites transfer and selected open times to a censoring time of 75h. That invents an event-free interval after an observed transfer (S21 at 26h, S08 at 68h) and, under independent censoring, reallocates their mass as if they might still resolve. The comment calls this a pessimistic non-resolution bound. On this encoding the active-at-72 probability is about 46.9%, not the reported 39.8%. `event_any` is computed and then never used, so the comment that transfers still end internal sojourn time is not what the estimator does.
- “Fastest observed Review→Resolve is at least 8–15h” and “gap ≥ 15h is the 75th percentile of productive stage duration” are not in the extract. Terminal rows have a zero gap, and intermediate sojourns are unobserved. The text makes that point and then uses the invented durations to declare S24 an inevitable breach.

Small-sample uncertainty is mentioned in the method table, then replaced by 0.1-point percentages and a “major reversal” on strata of 5 and 12. P1’s sensitivity, the open-versus-terminal gap artifact, and the P3 non-compliance pattern are the parts that stay trustworthy. The pooled SLA levels and the compliance comparison on remaining-active probability do not.
