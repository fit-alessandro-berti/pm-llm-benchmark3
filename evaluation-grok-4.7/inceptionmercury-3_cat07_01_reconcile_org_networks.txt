6.8

The write-up is complete, readable, and rightly treats the centrality gap as a metric difference rather than a paradox. It keeps several hypotheses in play, avoids accusing Mira of misconduct, and correctly refuses to remove her without replacing the coordination function. Most cited figures match the tables, and the executive summary is within the required length.

It is not nearly flawless. The activity table gives Mira 960 events and 905 cases, about 1.06 events per case, while the subcontracting frequencies (126 with Omar, 98 with Priya) each require her to reappear. Those summaries cannot both be raw counts from the same log. The answer never flags that conflict, then treats the loops as evidence that she routinely takes work back and acts as the “controlling party.” That overreads a sequence metric and builds a main mechanism on unresolved data.

The working-together argument is also off. A 22% case share does not cap the reported co-case rates: Alice–Carla at 27% is already incompatible with Alice covering only about 16% of cases if those percentages share an all-case denominator. The later test is muddled in the same area. Removing “Assign Specialist” and “Request Missing Information” would not be expected to raise her working-together centrality, and the second activity is real work shared with juniors and Temp_1, not a pure routing label. `filter_variants(log)` does not select traces that contain an activity.

Smaller validity and calibration problems accumulate. “Receives them back” is shown for the specialists, not the adjusters. “High variance” cycle-time contribution is read as her decisions creating waits, although outbound elapsed times closely track the target resource’s own service time and the six-day effect is a queue for an unstaffed role. One uncontrolled absence is called strong proof of key-person dependency. “Lacks an audit trail” is stated as fact. Low working-together is used against the boundary-spanner hypothesis after being used to define brokerage. Mira’s assignments to the four adjusters are fairly even, and the operations manager’s specialist-overload claim is not reconciled with the fairness risk. About half of her cases are not exception-flagged. Vendor_Pay sits on the team-lead release path, and Temp_1–Vendor_Verify subcontracting is left unused, so bots and vendors stay peripheral despite the instruction to account for them.
