6.4

The write-up is a serious capacity argument with the right headline, and it is not a reliable quantitative recommendation. Day-level offered load is mostly audited correctly, and the policy discussion is operationally specific. The same document then contradicts its own arithmetic, treats hand-set scores inside its stated error bands as a ranking, and ships a simulator that does not implement the policy it describes.

What holds up is the base-load identity. Mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74), branch volumes (18.09 / 24.30 / 12.85), 2,720.6 standard minutes against 2,490 net minutes, and ρ ≈ 1.09 are right. U1 to 3,442 minutes (ρ ≈ 1.38), U3 to 2,040 minutes (ρ ≈ 1.33), and U4 to 2,962 minutes (ρ ≈ 1.19) follow from the stated rules. The morning software hole, Chen as the only fast electrical resource before 10:00, Emil as the only person after 18:00 with no Mechanical skill, and the U2 mix-versus-volume ambiguity are the correct structural findings. Labeling assumptions, separating deferral from delay, and warning that pm4py sums overlapping ME/ES intervals are real process-mining competence. The pilot (shadow mode, day-level randomization, no live Chen removal) is the right shape of experiment.

The capacity section then mis-adds the blocks that are supposed to locate the slack. On-shift minutes are 600 in 10:00–12:00, not 570, so that block is ρ ≈ 0.98, not 1.03. In 12:00–14:00 Ada, Ben, and Chen are all on their 30-minute breaks, so net capacity is 510, not 570, and ρ ≈ 0.89, not 0.80. The “only slack window,” later used as the training slot, is an artifact of rolling the previous block forward and forgetting the clustered lunch breaks. Under reading A, doubling E and ES weights and renormalizing adds about 8 standard minutes, not ~60: total work stays near 2,729 and ρ near 1.10, not ~2,780 and 1.12. The stranding story is right; the load number is not. The 58-minute gap between the 231-minute global shortfall and the 290-minute residual is unused Chen/Deepa/Emil time at multiplier 1.0, and the text calls it multiplier waste. Symmetric triangular noise on the same endpoints does not raise the mean by 2–4%. Parallel ME critical path is 7 + 32.82 + 8 = 47.8, not 48.1.

Those slips feed the recommendation. Section 2.1 puts effective ρ at 1.15–1.30 after handovers. Section 4’s 18–24 overhead minutes per case is 750–1,000 resource-minutes, about +0.30 to +0.40 on ρ, so effective load is nearer 1.4–1.6. The claim that a 6-minute-per-case handover cut (~250 minutes) closes the capacity gap uses only the 231-minute multiplier-free gap. The claim that dropping the blocking-handover reading moves utilization by 6–8 points is arithmetically 450–600 minutes over 2,490 minutes, i.e. about 18–24 points. Policy C’s own base max utilization is 0.93, which already violates the 90% rule the text says only C respects. U4’s ρ of 1.19 does not trip a day-ahead trigger set at 1.20, yet U4 is listed as firing it. J2 is defined as CVaR₀.₂₅ over scenario-seeds, described as minimax, and coded as the worst scenario-mean. Policy A is justified as family-disjoint; Chen sits in both the Electrical and Software pools, so ES can be sequential on one person. B’s base mean delay of 205 versus C’s 220 sits inside the stated ±25% band, and the P1 gap of 0.13 sits on the edge of the stated ±0.10 band, but both are written as established orderings.

The §4 table is judgment, as disclosed, and the code would not reproduce it. Completions run after assignment, while a resource with `busy_until == t` is already free, so a busy system overwrites `in_service` and drops the stage transition. Under the ρ > 1 regime that is the paper’s point, multi-stage flow mostly disappears. Preemption does not shorten the logged interval or give back unused busy time. `tier1_wait` is fixed at 0, so the wait-based tier and overflow rules never open. The parallel rule omits the SLA test. Reservation omits the idle-time and “P1 already in service” releases. Dwell omits the 45-minute and slack < 30 exceptions. Policy A is scored with B’s least-slack ratio, so a tight P2 outranks a loose P1. C-triage is not implemented. The scaffold note still says these mechanisms are implemented. P2/P3 attainment is computed inside a horizon that ends at 20:30 against clocks of 8 hours and 24 hours; that censoring is named in §9 and then left inside J1.

The robust conclusion that survives is qualitative: this roster cannot clear the day, specialization strands Mechanical while Electrical queues, pooling buys mean delay by loading slow multipliers and fast people, and shocks U1/U3/U5 are staffing failures. The numerical policy ranking, the 90% fairness claim, and the handover-closes-the-gap claim do not survive the document’s own figures, and the required reproducible comparison does not run.
