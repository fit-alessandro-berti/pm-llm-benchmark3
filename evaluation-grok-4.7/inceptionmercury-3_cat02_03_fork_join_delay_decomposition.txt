7.5

The fork-join arithmetic, case table, critical-branch counts, and the three counterfactual totals are correct, and B is rightly ranked above A despite A’s longer service. The answer is still short of the required standard because of a utilization error, an incomplete flow-time counterfactual, and a script that would fail as written.

The definitions are the right ones. \(U_c=G_c-R_c\), \(q_{c,i}=S_{c,i}-G_c\), \(s_{c,i}=E_{c,i}-S_{c,i}\), \(J_c=\max_i E_{c,i}\), \(Q_c=FS_c-J_c\), and \(T_c=U_c+(J_c-G_c)+Q_c+P_c\) telescope to \(FE_c-R_c\). The identity \(\sum_i w_{c,i}=3(J_c-G_c)-\sum_i(q_{c,i}+s_{c,i})\) is correct. The sentence that introduces it switches from summing \(q+s+w\) (which equals \(3(J-G)\)) to summing \(w\) alone, so the double-counting argument is less exact than the algebra. Synchronization slack is defined and illustrated for four cases, but it is not part of the 12-case table, so the requested case-level separation is only partial.

Every reported case duration I can recompute matches the log, including F02, F04, F07, and F11, the service and queue means, critical frequencies 2/8/2, and mean sync waits 11.0, 4.3, and 30.4. The ranking uses frequency, queue plus service, and the 75-minute B tail, and it records the 5- and 7-minute near-ties. That is the right influence argument.

Counterfactual 1 saves 5 and 10 minutes of join time on F01 and F11 only (15 total). Counterfactual 2’s floor rule is explicit and the eight positive savings sum to 92. Counterfactual 3’s downstream waits sum to 176. The write-up correctly says these are join-ready savings and that a fixed final start absorbs them. It does not turn that caveat into a resource-feasible flow-time delta. The F11 masking note is plausible; the F01 note is weaker, because F1 is already idle across the original 8-minute gap, so an earlier join does not by itself remove that gap.

Section 5 inverts utilization. Cutting B queue time does not by itself lower B busy time; busy time is the service interval. Removing final wait likewise leaves the 20-minute services unchanged, so final staff are not freed for redeployment. Later F1 work is already back-to-back from 11:10 to 12:10, so redeploying on that basis can create a new queue. The throughput sentence is also loose: in this extract every case finishes, so these changes cut flow time and WIP under fixed arrivals rather than raising completed throughput.

The Python rebuilds the table, uses a deterministic alphabetical tie break, and implements the same three scenarios. `discover_performance_dfg` is called with `start_timestamp_key`, which the standard PM4Py signature does not accept, so the reproducible block raises `TypeError` before printing the DFG. The comment that directly-follows performance cannot recover \(J_c\) is right; the same call would also invent sequence edges among concurrent branches.

The limits section correctly withholds causality, names preemption and release policy, and treats the savings as ceteris paribus bounds. It leaves unused a direct capacity bound in the timestamps: B is triple-overlapped around 10:10–10:30 and again around 10:50–11:20, so B’s delay cannot be a single-server queue.
