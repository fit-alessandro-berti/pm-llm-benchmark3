7.5

The memo is complete, numerically faithful to the tables, and appropriately refuses to treat raw approval gaps as discrimination or to “solve” delay by dropping language and accommodation steps. The verdict is calibrated, and the best material (post-treatment controls, dual-hatted specialists, SLA gaming, equality-by-erasure) is real. It is not nearly flawless.

The main logical slip is in how the published residuals are used. The adjustment set does not include `language_preference` or `disability_accommodation_requested`, yet Sections 1 and 3 treat the +9.5 pp / +8.0 pp (B) and +4.2 pp / +5.5 pp (C) gaps, and the +4.1 day cycle-time gap, as what remains after legitimate routing. Those residuals still mix mandated support branches with excess discretion or queue time. Section 2 notices the missing flags; the findings section then writes as if the preliminary model had already held them fixed.

A second gap sits in the variant table. B’s listed paths cover about 99% of cases and only V4 contains language support (24%), against a 57% language-need share. C’s paths cover about 98%, and only V5 contains accommodation review (31%), against a 62% request rate. D’s only fraud path is 28% against a 51% high-risk share. Calling V3/V4/V5 “policy-aligned” from relative enrichment never tests under-provision. If those summaries are full traces, coverage itself is a fairness finding; if they are abbreviated, that limit needed to be stated.

The sensitivity plan names an invalid design: “instrumenting” documentation completeness with channel and language. Both have direct effects on routing, waits, and appeals, so they fail the exclusion restriction. Bounds and leave-one-covariate-out checks are fine; that IV is not. “Case mix explains a large share” is also too smooth: most of B’s appeal gap and about half of C’s cycle-time gap remain.

The PM4Py sketch would not do what the prose claims. After `format_dataframe`, these covariates are event attributes unless prefixed as case attributes, but `case_table` reads `trace.attributes`. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is never aggregated, while `get_service_time` answers the less relevant component. Declare and log-skeleton are fit on the pooled log, which is majority Group A, so they cannot show a constraint that holds for B/C and not A. The cell loop drops prior-case count, household size, month, language, and accommodation after the text required them. Default performance DFGs are means, not the medians and tails the argument uses.

Smaller issues: 3.8-day and 4.7-day edges are unweighted by path incidence, so they are not group-level median effects; testing “language support implies later eligibility” against Group A is nearly vacuous; variant durations are compared across traces that end at different activities (notify vs activate vs appeal). None of these overturn the staffing-and-handover conclusion, but together they keep the answer short of a near-exact assessment.
