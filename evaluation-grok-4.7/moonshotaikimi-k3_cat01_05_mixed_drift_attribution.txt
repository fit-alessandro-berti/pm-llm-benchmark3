The final report is complete, well structured, and strong on the criteria that matter: it separates mechanism-specific change points, treats the March fitness collapse as semantic/provenance drift while keeping the later rework and cycle-time movement as real, and ties rework to Supplier Z, queueing to staffing, delay redistribution to the July priority rule, and the October–November surge to seasonal case mix. Sections, columns, word counts, the seven decision rules, and the monitoring design (seasonal references, no universal threshold, multiple-testing control) all match the requested format. The baseline deltas and L1 distances check out.

It is not nearly flawless. The March gap between raw 0.55 and mapped 0.90 is 0.35, not a “37-point” gap. Calling p90 “3× faster” than the median compares absolute hours (+53 vs +17.9); relative to their baselines the stretch is about +78% versus +57%, so the tail claim is overstated. The DFG “month-over-month” distances are distances between supplied snapshots and skip February, April, June, August, and November, so the May jump of 37 is not a one-month change. Cycle-time shares are honest ranges, but the 1.5–2× mix assumption is not reconciled with median versus mean, and the stated hour bands do not sit cleanly on a ~31h baseline. Section 9 calls July–August deterioration superadditive after Section 4 correctly says the aggregates cannot prove that. The gradual pre-October complexity rise (about 21% to 29%) is not given its own drift type, and the residual mapped-fitness drop is called behavioral without saying which moves an alignment would penalize once allowed rework is in the model. The Python plan is substantively right, but it is two blocks rather than the required single block.
