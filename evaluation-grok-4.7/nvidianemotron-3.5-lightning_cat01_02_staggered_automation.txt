4.5

The memo has the right skeleton: staggered adoption, availability versus intensity, endogenous targeting, small-cluster limits, median versus tail, and a refusal of “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” with an embedded-evaluation rollout rather than a blind expansion. That conceptual coverage is real. It is not enough to carry the grade, because the quantitative core is unreliable and several final claims repeat those errors.

The arithmetic does not survive a check against the panel. Alpha’s April–August mean median is 48.5 hours, not 46.5, so the change from 48.9 is about −0.4 hour, not a 2.4-hour decline. Alpha’s post mean p90 is about 108, not 106. Delta’s p90 path is badly misstated (pre about 72, post about 89). Epsilon’s post median is about 44.7, not 88.5; that figure mixes April’s median with later p90s, even though no median in the file exceeds 53.5. Epsilon rework is not flat. Section 5 then builds the sensitivity bounds, the control comparison, and the Section 7 ranges on those figures. The p90 row also swaps supporting and counterevidence relative to a “worsening” assessment.

The never-treated comparison is the wrong contrast. Raw levels are not a staggered comparison. Over the same calendar window, Delta and Epsilon’s medians and p90s deteriorate by more than Alpha and Beta’s, so a simple difference-in-differences has the opposite sign from the memo’s tail story. July–August improvement is common to treated and untreated branches, and the March escalation jump hits every branch before adoption. Those patterns are absent, while a regression coefficient, standard error, and p-value for fast-path intensity are stated without being estimated.

Final claims do not repair this. “Median changed by at most ±2.5 hours” is false on the memo’s own Gamma and Delta figures. The “conditionally causal” bullet calls a raw range a difference-in-differences under a stable case-mix assumption the data violate. The suggestive bullet depends on the invented intensity coefficient. Estimand E7 says baseline medians exceeded 90 hours; they did not. Section 1 incorrectly gives Epsilon an extra FTE.

The code does not implement the analysis it is offered as. `avg()` always reads the pre window, so every printed pre/post delta is zero. Relative-time labels are mis-binned and printed with the wrong branch name. The fitted model is the naïve two-way fixed-effects regression the narrative set aside, not the event study. Dynamic treatment effects are missing from the threat table. The within-branch randomization answers a different question from branch rollout and collides with the memo’s own shared-queue mechanism, and the scale-up rule (“effect is +1 hour, i.e. no worsening”) is not an operational decision rule.
