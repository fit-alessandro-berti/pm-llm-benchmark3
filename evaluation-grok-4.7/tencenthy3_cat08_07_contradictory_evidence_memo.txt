6.5

The memo reaches the right decision (segmented hybrid, not national scale and not full rollback) and covers every required section, including a real fact/interpretation/assumption split, all five metric conflicts, all five options, and a ranked value-of-information list tied to process-mining evidence.

It is not nearly flawless. Alternative A is credited with median, handover, and cost gains “across all segments,” which the segment table contradicts (old buildings are slower; several segments lose first-time-right and complaints). The rollout rule then misuses the portfolio p90/p95 (31/45 days) as if those were the apartment and old-building baselines, sets first-time-right at 80% against an 82% baseline, and leaves NPS as “toward +21,” so the gate is not a clean threshold. Condition 1’s “or skip remains paused” also lets Redesign R expand into worse segments without a positive test that coordinator ownership itself is safe there, while the randomized skip experiment is left off the gate despite the region-mix and missing-attribute confounds. Several comparison statements are also unreadable as written (“8%19%,” “cost  EUR 198,” “returns to  baseline”), which breaks the decision rule an executive would have to apply.
