The final recommendation has the right skeleton—unlawful shortcuts last, a conformant second review first, and some attention to P-901—but the operational plan itself is not reliable.

The primary plan’s success branch is nonconformant. It says that if the match clears and the risk score falls below 70, cancel the second officer and auto-clear. Auto-clear is allowed only when there is no alert and the score is below 40. A score from 40 to 69 still needs the analyst path already taken; it does not authorize automatic execution. Labeling that composite `CONFORMANT NOW` is therefore wrong. Re-screening also does not, on the facts given, remove a score of 72: amount and a sanctioned-border destination can keep the score at or above 70 even if the name hit is a false match, and while either condition remains, a second officer is still mandatory.

The timing model does not support the ranking. S2’s review is treated as 35–75 minutes, while S4’s review is treated as 5–15 minutes. Screening and the wait for S4 are added end to end, although they can run together. The delayed S2 fallback is then called a “zero-deadline-risk” route. Starting that review only after a 15–30 minute screen pushes the path to roughly 60–120 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. Section 2 also says S2 is pre-engaged immediately, while the final recommendation says S2 interrupts P-901 only if screening fails. Those are different spillover profiles.

The counterfactuals assume execution. For the S2 branches, both the fast and slow cases end in “executed safely,” despite an approval probability of only 0.55–0.75. Rejection, hold, and a suspicious-activity report are missing from the top traces, and filing a report is never distinguished from permission to pay. The five traces also skip the fifth-ranked action and analyze immediate execution instead.

Benefit reasoning does not hold together. Customer harm of €15,000–€80,000 is the cost of a false rejection or prolonged hold, but it is applied as if every rejection were false. The stated “0.65 × €20k ≈ €13k” figure uses only the bottom of the deadline-value range, ignores the chance of missing the deadline, and does not offset that customer-harm range. Regulatory exposure is described as a 5–10× dominance, which the stated ranges do not support at their edges. Several comparisons are also unreadable because the inequality signs are missing.

What remains sound is the refusal to let a deadline forecast justify immediate execution or self-certification, the conditional status of rejection and override, and the recognition that S4’s 35- versus 65-minute arrival should change the S2-versus-S4 choice. Those points are not enough to carry a primary plan whose stated success path breaks the auto-clear rule.
