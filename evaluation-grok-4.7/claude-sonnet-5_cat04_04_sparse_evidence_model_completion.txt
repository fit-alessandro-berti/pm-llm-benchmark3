5.6

The three candidates are real, competing hypotheses, and the evidence ledger correctly refuses to treat bidirectional DFG edges as concurrency. That is not enough to carry the assignment. Two required closings are missing, the validation program is cut off, and several claims about the models are tighter or looser than the languages actually defined.

The ledger covers the grouped variants, D1–D7, and S1–S7, and the three-way account of CC↔ER and SC↔ER (true concurrency, case-level XOR, or completion-timestamp/ingestion artifact) is the right reading of D7 and S7. Shared assumptions correctly fix completion semantics, the RO…AC→RP→AR skeleton, loop-freedom, and the conflation of stocked-component skips (S4) with the 5–10% missing SC events (S5). Weaker spots: distinct no-SC and expedite variants are collapsed into single rows, and OV-EarlySC is described as SC starting after CC alone. Those logs only order completions.

The conservative tree is an XOR of the seven observed sequences, so its complete language is exactly the log. Duplicate labels and linear growth with variants are discussed properly. Underfitting risks (tie-break reorderings, missing SC on the expedite shapes, no compositional generalization) match that bias. Calling the resulting fitness only “very high” is unnecessary hedging: on this log it is perfect by construction.

The balanced POWL is the best technical idea. CC ∥ ER, EX after both, SC after CC or after ER via an XOR of partial orders, both optionals silent-skipped, then AC→RP→AR. That union’s complete traces are exactly the OR-precedence language (38 is not the count here; this model has 14 complete traces, 7 of them observed), and all seven variants are included, including CC, SC, ER and ER, CC, EX, SC. The write-up then misstates its own result: the XOR-of-partial-orders is trace-language-equivalent to “SC after at least one check,” not a behavior-changing substitute for a dynamic OR. “Least assumption-laden” oversells concurrency; S1, S2, and D7 are still open, and concurrency commits to every interleaving. “AND-branches” is the wrong name for those partial orders. The API hedge also leaves `model_balanced` short of the required self-contained program. RO, ER, SC, CC, EX is admitted, but citing D5 only “loosely” is wrong: D5 directly licenses SC after either check.

The permissive tree, SEQUENCE(RO, PARALLEL(CC, ER, XOR(SC, τ), XOR(EX, τ)), AC, RP, AR), is a genuine superset (2!+3!+3!+4! = 38 traces, not “up to 4!×2×2 = 96”). D1, D2, both checks before approval, and at-most-once EX are kept; D5 and D6’s AND-gate are intentionally dropped. That boundary is clear. “Spirit, if not the letter, of D5” is not: SC completing before both checks violates D5’s stated enabling condition, which the audit itself later calls risky. The matrix’s fitness line (“high”) should be perfect inclusion for all three models; precision risk low / moderate / high is directionally right.

Section 6’s excluded traces and earliest failure points check out, and the conservative model correctly admits no unseen complete trace. Section 8 only rebuilds the 30-case log and opens a soundness loop. It never defines the models, never computes fitness, precision, generalization, or simplicity, never does bounded playout, and never runs a positive/negative suite. There is no limitation statement. Sections 9 and 10 are absent, including the recommendation, the timestamp/attribute collection plan, calibrated confidence, and the alternative that would change the recommendation — even though §§1 and 4 cite §10. The S1-only sequence model is the obvious missing alternative: it would reject the 11 ER-before-CC cases and should not be the operating model unless new lifecycle data revives it.
