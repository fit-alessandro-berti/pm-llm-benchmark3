7.6

The calculations check out, the required sections and mechanisms are all present, and causal claims are usually labeled as counterfactuals. It is not nearly flawless.

The top rank, “batch synchronization, amplified by RACK-7,” over-attributes the tool. In B1 the documented gate is J3’s readiness at 11:55, ten minutes before the 12:05 cure; nothing shows RACK-7 delayed that start. In B2 the rack is free at 14:35, while J5 is ready only at 14:40, so the rack is not the final constraint on the 14:50 start. J4’s 100 minutes and RACK-7 are then also used to support rank 2, without a clean partition. Rank 2 further calls rack cleaning and CAL-3 recalibration “contention,” although those are maintenance states, not two jobs competing for a tool.

The strongest missed coupling is temporal but striking: J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40 and is batch-ready at 11:55. That possible path from J3’s fixture hold into J1/J2’s multi-hour B1 wait is never stated, even as speculation, so the two largest joins stay disconnected. J2’s 220 minutes are likewise treated as partner synchronization despite the L7 quarantine overlapping 10:20–12:05. The unlabeled 17:20–17:25 gap is called a CAL-3 “handoff.” Section 2 is also written with raw LaTeX (`12{:}05`, `\max`, `\mathbf`), so several required results are harder to read than the arithmetic deserves.

The PM4Py outline has the right design: multiple OC-DFG frequency semantics, flattening comparisons, a lot-to-job link audit, and waits built from readiness milestones rather than machine gaps. As executable code it is fragile. Canonical relations often lack activity and timestamp until merged with events; `compute_edges_performance` and the feature flags may be invalid kwargs; and the unused `discover_otg` / `discover_etot` unpacking can abort the script before the useful analysis.
