7.5

The core interval arithmetic is largely right: offset direction, June zone conversions, HUB/SENSOR/OPS bounds, the single surviving T-405 fold triple, T-404’s retained duration range, T-406’s two entry-delay readings, T-407 left indeterminate, and the zeta/tolerance flips in §§2–4 all check out. That is the hard part of the task, and the classifications at tolerance 0 and zeta 2 match those intervals.

§6 does not. Several “minimum evidence” claims contradict the intervals already computed. T-401 S1 stays possible under a perfect OPS clock (minute floor plus HUB’s early edge still reaches about 901.5 s), so an NTP residual of ±15 s is not enough. T-402 S2’s long tail is produced by the +120 s side of the offset; “offset ≥ −30 s” moves the late edge and leaves 09:16Z reachable. T-404 S1 fails on a short investigation (late start); a duration upper bound near 14.9 min would make that miss certain, while a certain pass needs the duration lower bound near 20 min. A T-405 fold tag only recovers the intervals already classified as possible across 900 s. “Tighter early bound” on T-402’s AR→IS profile is the wrong end of the interval.

Same-clock offset is never treated as one draw. Independent subtraction is stated for different systems and then used for LEGACY–LEGACY and HUB–HUB as well. On T-405, a shared offset collapses AR→IS to roughly [840, 960) s, which is a certain profile pass at zeta 2 rather than a possible violation.

The PM4Py write-up mixes SLA language with a profile-only check. At zeta 1 the cited midpoints for T-401, T-404, T-405, and T-409 are profile deviations, not passes; the code prints midpoint labels without the interval class beside them, and half-open ends are collapsed by 1 µs inside `apply_offset`. Governance and the primary sensitivity matrix are otherwise tight. The score is held down by wrong resolution claims and the missing common-mode clock case, not by the main tables.
