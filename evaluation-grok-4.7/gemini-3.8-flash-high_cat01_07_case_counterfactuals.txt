5.5

The write-up is complete and often conceptually right: K-337 is treated as a decision-policy change, final compliance and four-eyes are kept, and capacity externalities are discussed. The numerical and control-flow counterfactuals are not reliable enough for a high score.

The partial-order model misstates the rules. `{Assessment_k, Compliance_Review} < Request_More_Info` reads as a conjunction, while only one of those activities may issue the request. The concurrency constraint is written as a biconditional, so originals-not-required would force parallelism rather than permit it. K-411 is described as compressing verification to `max(duration)`, which ignores staggered queue starts. The K-104 path puts preliminary compliance inside the join that gates Assessment, so a late C1 screen would hold a case the intervention does not require to wait.

Case timelines contradict the snapshots and each other. K-219 calls D1’s backlog 16 h at Mon 17:30; the 16:00 snapshot is 11 h, and 16 h is the later realized wait until Tue 09:30. The derivation then has D2 issue `Request More Info`, which Rule 6 does not allow, while the table correctly places that request after Assessment. The reversal threshold is wrong: with the stated formula `(1-p)·30 + p·75`, expected time crosses 48 h at about p = 0.40, not 0.25. K-104’s table band is 48–53 h, but the scenario section spans 38.3–56.5 h. A Monday-afternoon C1 pre-screen is assumed against an 18–20 h compliance backlog without an explicit preemption account. K-337 says Friday backlog is about 4 h and that C1 starts at 13:30; those two claims do not add up. K-411’s factual cycle time is given as both 29.5 h and 29.3 h, and an 85-minute earlier assessment arrival is turned into a Wed 08:00 start against a factual overnight wait to 09:30.

At weekly scale the direction of interference is right and the required topics are covered, but the magnitudes fail. One hundred handwritten checks at an extra 2–4 h each are 200–400 h of added D2 work, which is unstable for one generalist; a queue “over 20 h” understates that. The same section says K-411’s system effect is strictly positive and then describes worse assessment queues. `ρ ≈ 0.95` and the M/M/1 waiting-time formula are invented and do not fit a reservation load above capacity. The 85% power claim has no variance. K-219’s pilot information value is rated low even though the handwriting-error rate is what would reverse the decision. Assumption 8 is an external-timing assumption labeled as quality.
