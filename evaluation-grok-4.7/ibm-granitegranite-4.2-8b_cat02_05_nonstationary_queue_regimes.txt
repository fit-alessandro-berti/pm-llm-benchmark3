The write-up follows the required section structure and shows some real queueing awareness (service time is not flow time, outage completions are not a healthy throughput regime, Little’s Law needs a stable boundary and a stated throughput proxy, weekdays and weekends should not be pooled blindly). Those strengths do not carry the analysis, because the segmentation and the numbers contradict the supplied table.

The primary regimes are not a defensible reading of the series. Normal steady state is limited to 6–12 April and then overlaps a “recovery” window that starts on 11 April, before the only outage. 13–14 April and 18–28 April are left outside every primary regime. That omitted stretch is the main operating break: on 20 April `complex_pct` jumps from 23 to 41 and stays near 40–47, median/p90 service rises to about 34–36 / 78–88 minutes, and weekday completions stay below arrivals while WIP sits in the high 30s to 50, all at roughly 6.0 FTE. Treating 11–17 April as the recovery regime both double-counts the pre-outage weekend and misses that backlog crisis. 16–17 April are also misstated: staff is 7.0 and 6.5, not “back to ~6.0.”

The alternative split is the right idea and the wrong facts. There is no 30% complex day and 14 April is a Tuesday at 24%, not a Thursday at 30%. The clean mix break is 19–20 April. The text then contradicts itself about whether 15 April is low-complex or already in the high-complex world.

Pooled figures are not reproducible from the table. Mean daily arrivals are about 90 and mean completions about 86, not 105 and 107. Hourly means are likewise wrong (7 April’s vector sums to 101 over 12 bins, so the mean is about 8.4, not 12), which makes the reported CVs and dispersion indexes unreliable even though the formulas are the right family. The implied flow time for the intervention example is also miscalculated: \(43 \times 12 / 121 \approx 4.3\) hours, not 3.5. Capacity claims such as “pressure low” rest on median service and full-day use of mean FTE, which overstates capacity for a right-skewed service distribution and is already falsified by the weekday completion deficit from 20–28 April.

The Python does embed the right daily rows, but its masks do not implement the prose regimes, the intervention mask is largely wiped out by the WIP rule, and the printed “output” matches neither the code nor the data. PM4Py is correctly flagged as inapplicable to these aggregates, yet the named helpers are described inaccurately.

So the document looks complete and is directionally literate, but a reader who trusted the boundaries, the pooled comparisons, or the dispersion and flow-time figures would get the operating story wrong.
