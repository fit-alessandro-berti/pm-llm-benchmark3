7.4

The final JSON is structurally complete and often genuinely process-aware: state labels track the normative abstraction, A versus B is split on obligations rather than the shared prefix, migration delay is not treated as proof a task never happened, and completion-only logging is used to separate an enabled task from the next visible event. The cross-case discussion correctly treats the DFG as confounded observational frequencies.

Those strengths do not make the forecasts coherent.

C6-A’s lead reasoning says high-severity work can overtake the evidence-review queue. The operating note allows overtaking only in Technical Investigation; Regulatory Assessment is prioritized by reportability risk. Evidence Review is described as an ordinary 1–5 h queue with two reviewers. That misread props up the top prediction. R-T4 is also weak support: the same resource triaged C6-B, a low-severity duplicate.

Ranks 2 and 3 repeatedly treat a downstream successor as if it could be the next logged event. For A, B, and E, a second or third evidence request, Technical Investigation completion, or a closure is control-flow-after Evidence Review Completed, and those items are marked `process_enabled: false`. The cited 101/356 and 173 review-exit counts are conditional on review already being logged. With a normal feed, the probability that the next completion is the review should sit much higher, and the disabled successors much lower, unless a concrete skip or parallel-work mechanism is established. That mechanism is only really available for C6-D.

C6-C’s Regulatory Assessment range is malformed: `"high": "0.60"` is a string. The contextual preference for assessment over disposition is defensible under the repeated-malfunction rule, but the answer never confronts the stronger directly-follows count from Technical Investigation to Disposition (119) versus to Regulatory Assessment (74). “After pickup” also slides from the stated 2–14 h after assignment to time since the 11:45 investigation completion.

C6-D puts an opened, unfinished Regulatory Assessment under `completed_obligations`. The worklist correctly shows it in progress; it is not a completed obligation. The delayed-receipt interval (0.12–0.24) also sits above the 8–15% delay rate without a clear decomposition of “occurred” versus “occurred and still delayed at 14:00.”

C6-E correctly bars Fast Close and treats Supplier Analysis as the distinctive obligation, then spends the third prediction on a Technical Investigation completion that is neither required by severity nor likely to be logged before a second review. A, B, and E also share one two-reviewer queue, and that dependence is never scored.

Intervals are appropriately coarse and the residual mass is acknowledged, but the next-event probabilities are systematically loosened by successor frequencies and one queue rule is imported from the wrong resource.
