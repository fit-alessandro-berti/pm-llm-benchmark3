The answer covers every required section and stays appropriately conditional: East is treated differently from North and South, fairness and learning are carried through the scenarios, and the recommendation is a monitored pilot rather than a full rollout. Under a strict reading it is still not a reliable forecast, because several central claims are inconsistent with the numbers supplied in the prompt.

The mechanism map misstates the handover arithmetic. Local technical handovers move from 36 + 31 to 41 + 36 per 100 cases, and approval handovers from 42 + 39 to 49 + 43. Cross-team transfers fall only from 6 to 3, and intake-to-caseworker from 88 to 83. The visible network therefore gets slightly larger, not “flat/slightly lower,” and “collapses” is the wrong description of that change. The map also never quantifies the supervisor cut: one of three supervisors is removed while approval volume rises from 42 to 49 per 100, which is roughly a 75% load increase on each remaining approver. That capacity shock is more important than the stated move from a 2.4h to a 3.0h median.

The same problem reappears in the scenarios. Legal/risk demand rises from 9 to 12 per 100, about +33%, on resources already at 93% and 91%. Fixed capacity cannot absorb that; the central case is overload, not the management estimate of 10.5h. The central scenario nevertheless adopts 4.0h, 10.5h, and 3.0h, while the optimistic scenario still gives Legal/Risk a stable ~10h wait. Those scenarios contradict the queueing point made earlier. The case-weighted baseline is also off: the average of team medians is about 7.5 days and the case-weighted 90th percentile is about 18.6 days, not 7.3 and 18. “70% simple cases,” the 15–20% clarification rate, and the satisfaction drops to 4.0 are presented as if they were measured.
