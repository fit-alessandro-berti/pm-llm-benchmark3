The write-up is complete and directionally right about a multi-mechanism episode, but it is not reliable on timing, measurement, or the monitors a user would actually run.

The March logging break is correctly separated from later operational drift: raw fitness falls from 0.93 to 0.55 while mapped fitness stays near 0.90, with partial label coexistence (500 `Eligibility Screen` versus 140 `Validate Eligibility`). Multiple mechanism-specific change points are the right global conclusion, and the January-relative percentage table is mostly accurate. That is the strongest part of the answer.

Temporal binding is repeatedly wrong. The May–June interval uses April baselines and July endpoints: rework 13% to 29%, `Assess → Request Info` 12 to 29 per 100, and median cycle time 32.0h to 44.2h. June itself is 27% rework, about 40.8h median, and 24 request-info edges in May. Section 9 then calls that smeared 12-to-29 move a precise Supplier Z alignment. By May, before the June 15 staffing cut, median cycle time is already +5.0h and p90 +15h of the eventual July gaps (+12.7h and +35h). The July decomposition still assigns about 55% of the p90 gap to post-June queueing.

The distance section has visible arithmetic and labeling errors. May’s senior-assessment term is written as \((2)^2\) although \(7-4=3\), producing \(\sqrt{361}=19\) instead of \(\sqrt{366}\approx 19.13\). The vector labeled November is the October row; November has no normalized DFG. Omitted edges are not given an explicit assumption there, and the nearly duplicate rework pair is counted twice. March’s small normalized distance is described as a logging shift even though normalization has already removed the label change. September 10 is listed as a second logging change point; that date is Supplier Z’s operational recovery, while the label count stays at 11 and missing-resource rates stay elevated. July 1 is a weak behavioral break: senior assessment moves only from 7 in May to 9 in July, then back to 7 in September, and jumps to 22 in October with the complexity spike. The priority rule is also treated as if it increased high-complexity share and senior-assessment frequency. It reorders the queue. The aggregates cannot show the redistribution the timeline itself flags.

The cycle-time split is manufactured precision. Queueing +19.2h, rework +8.8h, case mix +4.2h, and service +2.8h sum exactly to the +35.0h p90 gap, with no identification method. The only service observation is a median rise from 4.8h to 5.4h, which contradicts a +2.8h service component. Rework and queueing are not separable because extra `Request Info` loops add queue visits. Mapped fitness is called stable and “entirely blind” to rework, yet it falls from 0.93 to 0.86 and is worst when rework peaks. Section 5’s “complete replacement” of `Validate Eligibility` contradicts the 78% figure in Section 1. In the staffing hypothesis, the modest service-time rise is placed under evidence against, although it supports queueing over slower work. “Proves,” “unequivocally,” and “fully restores” overclaim a residual mapped-fitness gap and confounded supplier effect.

The monitoring design would mishandle this year. Mapped fitness below 0.85 never fires (the minimum is 0.86). Missingness above 2% never clears after March. Assess service above 7h never fires. Week-over-week conformance misses the gradual May–July slide. The DFG monitor states Levenshtein distance and then alerts on Euclidean distance. A rolling Inductive Miner fit against a model learned from the same window is nearly tautological. Fixed cutoffs contradict the seasonal-baseline paragraph. Benjamini–Hochberg on ten dependent operational checks would suppress the coincident alerts that actually mark a supplier or staffing shock. The primary population drops Supplier Z, high complexity, and incomplete resources, so it removes the main drift paths and conditions on informative missingness. The Mar–Jun phase also pools migration, supplier failure, and staffing while claiming to isolate them.

Part A does not validate ranges or continuity, embeds only six monthly fields, changes only case volume, and prints hardcoded change points instead of applying a rule. Part B’s `format_dataframe` call passes `case_id_key` and `resource_key`; the PM4Py arguments are `case_id`, `activity_key`, and `timestamp_key`, so formatting fails. The normalizer deletes every named activity rather than duplicated `Open Case` events and does not compare raw versus mapped logs.
