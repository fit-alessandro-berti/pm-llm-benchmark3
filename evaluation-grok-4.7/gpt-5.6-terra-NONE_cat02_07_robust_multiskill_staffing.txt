6.7

The write-up is complete, auditable in structure, and right about the decision that matters: do not pick a policy for base-case mean delay, treat Chen’s absence and the late mechanical cliff as structural, and prefer protected overflow over pure specialization or pure pooling. It is not reliable as a quantitative argument. Several load claims contradict the stated formulas, and the simulation does not implement the policies being compared.

The activity-count identities are correct (day totals 42 cases, 18.09 mechanical, 24.30 electrical, 12.85 software; U1 morning volume 27.2). The service means are not. The mixture of range midpoints is about 6.9 / 26.3 / 32.8 / 38.5 / 15.7 minutes for Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, and Validation, not 7.5 / 27.3 / 33.2 / 38.0 / 16.8. Workload minutes built from those figures (about 2,810) are therefore high, and the U4 “about +16%” shift is only a rough match to doubling the diagnostic tail probabilities.

The mechanical-coverage claim contradicts the skill table and itself. Deepa is mechanical-qualified at 1.25 through 18:00 (18:30 with the overtime cap). Emil cannot do mechanical work. The opening statement that neither Deepa nor Emil can do mechanical work after 16:00, and the later line that 16:00–20:00 has no mechanical-qualified resource, are false. The 16:00–18:00 problem is that mechanical work is pinned to one shared resource, Deepa, while Emil carries software. Zero mechanical coverage starts at 18:00, or 18:30 if overtime is actually used. Policy 4’s 18:30 cliff is the consistent version; Section 2 is not.

U2 is mis-quantified under the interpretation the text adopts (double E and ES shares, then renormalize, hold block volume fixed). Electrical activity expectation moves from 4.20 to 5.00 in 12:00–14:00 and from 6.20 to about 7.32 in 14:00–16:00, not to 6.0–6.5 and 8.5–9.5. Under that same renormalization, software activity counts stay flat to slightly down, so “software also rises” does not follow. No block-level offered-load balance in effective minutes (after multipliers, breaks, and shared Intake/Validation) is shown, so midday bottleneck labels stay judgmental. U1’s “+8 percentage points” is also implemented incorrectly: adding 8 to the P1 weight and dividing by the new sum raises P1 by about 6–7 points, not 8.

Section 4’s ranges overlap enough that hybrid does not dominate on the numbers given, and those ranges are not produced by the code. Objective A also double-counts P1 misses through the extra \(\gamma\) term. Thresholds drift across sections (overflow close at 75%, normal mode at 80%, surge at 90% versus “exceeds capacity”).

The Python meets the checklist only on the surface. Real gaps:

- Preemption is declared implemented and `PREEMPT_RESTART_FACTOR` is unused. No in-service interruption exists, so policy differences on preemption are untested.
- The 8-minute coordination delay is written to the log and stored as a future queue-entry time, then dispatch ignores that timestamp and can start Validation immediately.
- Strict homes omit Emil (and Ada/Farah) from Electrical, and strict parallelism is hard-coded off, which is harsher than Policy 1’s stated rule.
- Hybrid mode is a single P1 E/S hold on Chen, Deepa, and Emil, not the reserve, slack, idle, and shift-cliff rules in Sections 3 and 5.
- Setup treats Electrical and Software as one family, against the stated M/E/S setup rule.
- An activity that cannot finish before shift end still records handover/setup and then locks that resource until shift end.
- Shift start, break end, and the U5 intake-outage end are not events, so dispatch can miss the moment capacity returns.
- Unfinished cases are stamped at 20:30 and can still “pass” P1 or P2 if elapsed time is under target, which contradicts assumption A4. Busy time includes breaks while the denominator excludes them.
- Service draws are not common random numbers across policies. PM4Py is aimed at a log that still contains Handover, Setup, Pause, and Coordination, and activity intervals span breaks, so case service time is not active service time. The stage-wait split itself is the right interpretation.

Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest: secondary risks are concrete, the event attributes cover the requested audit fields, case-level PM4Py waiting time is correctly separated from stage queue time, and the pilot protects P1 with shadow mode and rollback. The live pilot deliberately avoids outages and absences, so it cannot by itself confirm the robustness claim; the backtest would have to do that.

Use the hybrid recommendation as a reasoned hypothesis. Do not use the delay, SLA, or utilization ranges, or a run of this simulator, as evidence for it until preemption, coordination, calendars, U1 priority shares, U2 loads, and Deepa’s 16:00–18:00 mechanical capacity are fixed and the table is regenerated from that run.
