What holds up:

High severity, reversible injury, two devices on one lot, and three similar complaints correctly force Technical Investigation, Regulatory Assessment, and Supplier Analysis, while C6-B’s low severity, no injury, single device, and 0.93 duplicate similarity point toward a short review and non-investigation closure.

- C6-D uses the migration flag, the source-system attachment, and the 12:40 worklist opening. C6-E correctly makes Supplier Analysis mandatory for a lot tied to six complaints and keeps Fast Close out.
- Primary states for A, C, D, and E match the completion-only log. Counts, elapsed times, and queue bands are generally accurate. Interval midpoints are ordered and not fake point probabilities. The cross-case note correctly limits what the DFG can say.

Material defects:
- Several “next” activities are not enabled successors. Another Customer Evidence Requested event cannot be next until Evidence Review Completed is logged (C6-A, C6-E). Close — No Safety Issue and Fast Close are not immediate successors of Evidence Received (C6-B). A further Technical Investigation “triggered by Regulatory Assessment” would follow that assessment, not precede it (C6-C). Disposition Recorded is not enabled for C6-D while Regulatory Assessment has been open for about 1 h 20 m of a 2–14 h activity. `process_enabled` is nevertheless true on all 15 rows, so the required distinction between an enabled action and the next log observation is missed.
- C6-B’s alternative state is S1 TRIAGED after both evidence-request and evidence-receipt events. That state is already behind the prefix. Fast Close was available at triage at 09:11 and was not taken; giving it a top-three slot up to 0.20 after the evidence cycle is weakly grounded. R-T4’s reportable-case role is noted, then not used to build a credible third successor.
- C6-D says the DFG has no direct Technical Investigation Completed → Regulatory Assessment Completed path. The supplied table has that edge 74 times, median 5.0 h. The 12:40 opening versus the 13:05 technical completion is also the clearest concurrency evidence in the set and is left undeveloped. With only 1.3 h of a 2–14 h assessment elapsed, a delayed evidence event is a strong candidate for the next analytical-feed observation while assessment remains in progress; the ranking still puts assessment completion first at 0.40–0.75 and calls disposition enabled.
- C6-A’s Technical Investigation Completed band of 0.10–0.30 is high against an 8.5–9 h median, seven items waiting, and one of three engineers unavailable. C6-E’s completeness “dropped” to 0.72; no earlier score is given. C6-A lists the dominant Evidence Received → Evidence Review edge as contradicting evidence. C6-C treats a normal feed as strong proof that Regulatory Assessment is not complete, which is firmer than the completion-only log and the 8–15 % delay allowance support. Shared review-queue contention among A, B, and E is unused.

The answer is well above a DFG-copying baseline, but the next-event errors, the unused `process_enabled` flag, the invalid C6-B state, and the false DFG claim keep it well below an expert-grade forecast.
