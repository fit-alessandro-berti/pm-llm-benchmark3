The answer meets the required seven-section structure and stays inside the operational boundary of the log. It separates energy, transport, scrap, and packaging, refuses a single invented CO2e score, and keeps air, dedicated vans, batching, and reinforced packaging conditional on segment, slack, Q1, SP1/SP2, and R1. Indicators, rebound coverage, the package recommendation, and the conformance/rollback plan are specific to the DFG and the stated policies.

The effect table is the main failure of grounding. Several “effects” are contrasts between multi-factor path averages, not effects of the named change. CNC_B → CNC_A is scored as 32.0 vs 16.0 kWh and 1.8 vs 0.70 kg, and rework is said to “avoid [a] 160h loop.” Those figures are full-path medians and averages for a rework-conditioned variant versus a different path that also changes heat treatment, batching, and mode. The 160h figure is that path’s median cycle time, not the duration of the rework loop. The same pattern appears when S10 is credited with 24.0 vs 16.0 kWh and 23.0 kgCO2e. The 18% → 9% rework shift is stated as if the machine gap transfers fully.

Partial shipments are blurred. The log splits 1,010 partials into 790 direct packs and 220 backorder waits, while 79h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e, and “second shipment” belong to the 7% backorder variant. S11 then treats double transport as the general partial-shipment problem.

Volume bases are not reconciled. The critical van variant is 8% of orders (~960), while reinforced → dedicated van is 620 and van → confirm is 610. The answer cites both the 8% / 52.0 path and the van-justification rate without flagging that they cannot be the same population. Stock air → ground is called a small-to-moderate cycle-time increase, while the path medians are 17h versus 34h and the stated transport gap is about 24h.

Those are attribution and population errors in the quantitative core, not missing sections. Direction and governance remain sound, which keeps the score above a merely adequate answer.
