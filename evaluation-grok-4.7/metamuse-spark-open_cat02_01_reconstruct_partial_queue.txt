The case-level reconstruction is the strongest part: fully observed waits and services are correct, the four missing starts are bounded with the right resource constraints, sojourn is correctly treated as invariant to the wait/service split, and open versus waiting cases are not dropped or treated as finished. Assumptions, rounding, and the O/C/P framing are also conceptually sound, and the time-varying S2-bottleneck story is directionally defensible.

Those merits do not survive the aggregate results. Stated bins are `[08:00,10:00), [10:00,12:00), [12:00,14:00)`, but S1 completions are counted as 7/7/6 rather than 6/7/7. That error produces the false late-window claim that S2 arrivals equal S2 departures; under the stated convention the late window still adds about one S2 case (7 versus 6). Total WIP peaks at 13 after P20 at 11:30 and again at 11:50, not 12, and that peak does not depend on the tie-break the answer credits for it. End-of-day WIP of 8, with 6 at S2, is correct.

The C2 gap is misread. P08 entered the S2 queue at 10:20, so C2 was not idle after 10:50 for lack of work; maintenance plus short buffers explains the gap. The same paragraph then equates a 15-minute outage with both 0.375 lost jobs and one full case. Scenario labels are reversed in the busy-time table: earliest-start O maximizes service, but O is assigned the zero-service totals 535 and 515. For P05 and P11 the stated central imputation equals O, yet the S2 mean-wait row still separates O and C. The central S1 busy time of about 570 is also arithmetic error; the listed central services sum to 595. Per-interval S1 utilization above 100% in 08:00–10:00, and a peak S1 WIP of 5 in that window, are not supported by the event trace.

Little’s Law is applied to the full nonstationary window and reported two incompatible ways: L≈8.9 in the sensitivity table, versus λ=14/6 and mean WIP ≈5–6 in the code. The code would actually compute peak WIP 13, then prints hardcoded literals instead of the variables it builds. The PM4Py calls are wrapped so failure is silent, are aimed at an event log rather than the dataframe those helpers expect, and never print their case-level outputs. Station metrics are transcribed constants, not pandas calculations over the parsed table.
