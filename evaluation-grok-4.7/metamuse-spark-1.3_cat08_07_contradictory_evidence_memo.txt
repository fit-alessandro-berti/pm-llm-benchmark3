7.4

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and covers every required section, artifact, and constraint, but it is not nearly flawless. Several inaccuracies and decision-logic gaps keep it well below a top score.

The executive memo misstates the segment evidence. It says apartments, old buildings, business, and rural cases “all improved median slightly.” Old buildings (18% of the pilot) moved the other way: median +1.0 day and p90 +25 days. That is a directional error in the opening decision narrative, not a wording nit. The same paragraph treats the pilot aggregates as if they were a comparable national effect and never tells management that the pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings, that risk attributes are missing before week 3, or that cost and start times are censored. Those caveats appear later, but an executive memo that omits them overstates how cleanly Redesign R “broke the exceptional path.”

“Proven” is used for the simple-house bundle. The result is a non-randomized, six-week, case-mix-skewed observation with incomplete segment data. The evidence table is careful; the decision sections are not. Immediate scaling of that bundle, including beyond the four pilot regions, is not gated on fixing classification quality or on the 60-day cost the memo itself ranks as the highest-value measurement. Rollback is mentioned only after the fact.

The rollout threshold is not operational. “p90 31d,” “complaint 6.5%,” and “full 60-day cost per case baseline” have no comparators, while utilization alone uses “<80%.” Complaint 6.5% is looser than the 6.1% baseline with no reason. The text then says to keep the hybrid if thresholds are met “only for simple houses,” which conflicts with reading those same numbers as portfolio gates. The activation rule is also incomplete: “no billing before a successful activation test” never says how the enlarged manual-activation path (7% to 15%, DFG 8% to 19%) is allowed to bill.

Smaller faults accumulate. Seasonality is introduced with no evidence. Queueing “explodes above 85–90%” and is then gated at 80%. M6, a rule-validity check, is reduced to “correlation.” The fitness/precision, median/tail, handover/resilience, visible/full-cost, and segment explanations are otherwise substantively right, and A–E plus the M1–M8 ranking are decision-linked rather than generic. The recommendation correctly refuses both national scale and a blanket rollback. The remaining gaps are exactly the ones this task penalizes: a factual slip, fact and interpretation collapsed at the decision point, and a threshold management could not apply.
