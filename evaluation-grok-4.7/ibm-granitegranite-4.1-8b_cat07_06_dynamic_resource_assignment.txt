# Algorithm defects

The pseudocode does not implement the seven required scoring ideas in a usable way.

- Resources are treated as having one `required_skill`. Skills are activity-specific, so eligibility cannot be evaluated as written.
- Security eligibility omits supervised skill 1–2 and does not restrict supervisors to Gita and Hugo. Negotiation, quality, and rework are hard-blocked below skill 2, which removes legitimate normal/low training assignments, including Celine’s negotiation priority.
- The SLA term divides remaining time by itself, so it collapses to a constant urgency weight and does not rank resources or distinguish a 2-hour deadline from a 9-hour one.
- Skill match subtracts a skill name from a number. The sign is then interpreted as “resource too high,” which is the opposite of a lower-skill result.
- Handover comparison is lexicographic on strings. Continuity is only a small additive boost and never tests the rule 5 exception (owner skill 0 or 1 and supervision infeasible).
- The overtime condition reads the undefined name `resource`, mixes attributes with dictionary keys, and does not check whether a safer resource exists.
- `open_cases + 1 > 4` confuses the cap of four new batch assignments with total open cases. Every resource already has at least four open cases, so the fallback iterator matches nobody.
- Workload penalty equals the open-case count and dominates the small skill, continuity, and training bonuses, recreating first-available behavior.
- Resilience and bottleneck avoidance are absent from the score. Hours are adjusted with an undefined `estimate_time`. Tie-breaking by lower remaining hours prefers the more exhausted resource and is not in the code. Cases are not ordered by urgency, so the result depends on queue order. Supervised pairs are not represented, yet the table emits them.

# Comparison, audit, and PM4Py

The comparison covers the requested dimensions and correctly describes the baselines’ directional weaknesses. Its numbers are unsupported: roughly 90% of first-available throughput, less than 80% of specialist delay, and a 30% error reduction do not follow from the stated 1.8× rework, 1.4× appeal, 2.7-hour, or 3.3-hour evidence. Neither baseline is actually run on C001–C016, so the comparison never tests this queue. The claim that the proposed policy has low SLA and compliance risk is inconsistent with the illegal security and high-priority assignments.

The worker note is understandable and not punitive, but “urgent urgent” is broken prose, and promises of visible numeric scores and routine continuity do not match the formula or the table.

The audit section names every requested indicator, yet several computations are not PM4Py behavior. `filter_event_attribute_values` filters; it does not count owners. `get_workload_snapshot`, `filter_case_attribute_values`, and `discover_organizational_roles(..., activity=...)` are misused or nonexistent. `filter_case_performance` does not accept `'sla_missed'`.

The implementation sketch misses `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `discover_batches`, and `get_variants`. It invents `read_dataframe`, `save_dataframe`, `compute_handover_frequency`, `get_case_metric`, and `compute_skill_mismatch`. `format_dataframe` is called with `nominal_attributes`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is given the wrong argument, and `view_sna` is applied to role-discovery output rather than an SNA metric. Setting `index_col` to the case id drops the identifier PM4Py needs, and overwriting historical `org:resource` rewrites the log instead of recording a new assignment decision.
