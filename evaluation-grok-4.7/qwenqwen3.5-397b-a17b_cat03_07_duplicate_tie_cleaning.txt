4.5

The write-up has the required nine-section shape and several locally correct judgments, but it is not a reliable cleaning design. The decision procedure, the conservative scenario, the order, and the code disagree with each other and with the stated facts.

The decision tree does not implement the six required case types. Exact technical duplication is reduced to identical event IDs, which never occurs in this log. Same activity, timestamp, and a shared object set are resolved before harmonization can be tested, so e08/e09 cannot reach the harmonization branch. A summary with a subset of objects (e06/e07, e13/e15, e14/e15) is caught earlier as “concurrent same-label” and would be retained unchanged. Section 2 then does the opposite: it refines those labels and merges e08/e09. The tree and the table are not one policy.

Merging e08 with e09 at high confidence is the central error. Fact 4 distinguishes warehouse release from carrier acceptance; the collision is a shared harmonized label, not a verified duplicate. A minimum-damage conservative pipeline should keep both events and refine the labels. Putting that merge in the conservative path, and counting 14 retained events from it, makes the “conservative” log already lossy. e03/e04 and e11/e16 are the pairs with real same-event evidence; e05 and e14 are correctly kept.

Ordering is only partly grounded. e12 before e13, carrier acceptance before handover, WMS pick-before-pack, and the ERP summary after the payment messages follow the facts. Invoice-before-authorization does not. Concurrent picks are forced into a total order and then described as the intended order, without a clear statement that the total order is not the physical order. Within-source sequence is declared semantic, but it is not scoped in the 12:00 list: e13 before e14 is actually PSP `source_seq`, not an arbitrary payment-id sort. Alternative admissible orders for the ties are largely absent.

The evidence-based count is wrong. From the stated 14-event baseline, folding e07 into e06 and dropping e15 yields 12, not 11. The mechanical scenario also claims e09/e10 would merge on activity plus timestamp, but those activities differ. The `have_common_object` warning for M1 versus M2 is right and is one of the strongest passages.

The Python block does not meet the implementation contract. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is never called. `ocel_merge_duplicates` is commented out. The candidate-group pre-check is unused. The verified-pair helper overwrites the keeper hash, does not union objects or attributes, does not keep the dropped `source` and `source_event_key`, and does not remap E2O relations. Referential-integrity validation is explicitly omitted. `sort_index = source_seq` orders across systems and contradicts the narrative order, yet index-based timedeltas are then added on that sequence.

Sensitivity analysis is not a four-way comparison across event/E2O counts, flattened DFGs, OC-DFG, ET–OT weights, OTG, rework, and performance. It misstates effects: aggressive merging is said to inflate pack frequency and to collapse the object-type graph as if M1 and M2 disappeared. Merging events does not merge payment objects; unioning relations can attach both payments to one event. The claimed WMS-dispatch to ERP-summary duration is not tied to a real pair.

The provenance list and the eight failure modes are the most complete parts, but the code does not implement that schema, and the safeguards forbid merges and synthetic durations that earlier sections introduce. The result is a structured draft with the right prohibitions named and the wrong conservative decisions applied.
