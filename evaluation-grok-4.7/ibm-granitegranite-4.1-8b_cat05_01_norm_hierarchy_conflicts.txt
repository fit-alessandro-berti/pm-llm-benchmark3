The memo keeps the required seven-part form and occasionally names the right pressure points: ECD-2 is time-boxed and scope-limited, the CFO email is not a controlled amendment, the Chief Risk Officer issued no A7 exception, and a cafeteria purchase sits outside the contamination directive. Those merits do not carry the assessment. Case conclusions repeatedly detach from the stated hierarchy, from the amounts and clocks in the record, and from the instruction to judge each act at its own time.

## Method and hierarchy
The opening method states a usable burden (unproven mandatory controls are not compliance) and a dimension-specific hierarchy. The later sections do not apply it. Section 2 says ECD-2 probably cannot displace segregation of duties or compliance clearance. The pseudo-logic then drops compliance whenever C2 allows an unapproved supplier, and drops segregation whenever commitment may precede the PO. C1 is a timing permission. It is treated as a waiver of A1 and of finance. The GPIP row also fills “what it can override” with what may override GPIP, so the hierarchy table does not answer its own columns.

The rule catalogue omits operative tests that decide cases: the six-hour emergency definition, shift-lead delegation, the 24-hour emergency form, A6’s non-cure rule, and the four-hour recording clock as an evidence rule. The “512 lines” label does not describe the logic that follows. Threshold symbols are missing, and “EUR 150,000” is used both as a ceiling and as a floor.

## Case facts and classifications
Several primaries are driven by misread facts.

- P-501 is classified COMPLIANT under ECD-2. Commitment is 08:50 and the directive starts at 09:00. The outage note, if it affects that stamp at all, places the real act earlier, not later. The reachable Plant Manager and the absence of a delegation are never analyzed. High confidence is not available on that record.
- P-502 is EUR 62,000, so A3 is triggered by the new supplier, not by the EUR 100,000 limb, and D1’s EUR 150,000 ceiling is the relevant CFO sentence. The memo states both thresholds in the wrong direction. Compliance at 18:30 is treated as good enough after a 10:15 commitment, which is the post-hoc cure A6 and the task both reject.
- P-503 stacks a requester-committer-authorizer, no compliance clearance on EUR 140,000, no finance event, and a backup line with three days of stock. The write-up calls the segregation gap a failed A7 exception and still returns JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION because the purchase was urgent.
- P-504’s NONCOMPLIANT label is the most defensible primary in the set, and the mechanism is wrong. EUR 24,000 does not trigger the EUR 25,000 plant-and-finance rule. The memo doubts whether the supplier is approved after the table says unapproved, and it never centers the new-supplier clearance that GPIP actually imposes.
- P-505 is EUR 88,000 from an approved supplier, so A3 is not triggered. The supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31, and a possibly fast ERP clock, are the commitment evidence. The memo instead treats the 18:06 ERP stamp and the 19:00 finance entry as if they reopened the expiry analysis, then reaches JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION by assuming an unwritten extension.
- P-506 imports BUY-8 from P-508, argues with itself in the cell (“Wait”), finds A2 satisfied because finance at 06:05 precedes commitment at 06:20, and still explains the breach as an A2 failure. NONCOMPLIANT is plausible on other grounds: no written extension, commitment before the PO, plant approval after commitment, and no shown budget owner. Those are not the reasons given.
- P-507 records Compliance_Clearance at 08:50, before commitment at 09:00. The assessment says no compliance record exists and builds JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION on an implied waiver of a control that was performed. The signed PDF at 12:40, inside four hours of the 08:55 authorization, is the contemporaneous recording; the 14:30 ERP entry is the late log. That distinction is missed.
- P-508 restores the affected line, which is a stronger scope fit than P-503’s three-day backup stock. The memo treats the pump as outside scope and the backup pumps as inside. Compliance at 15:50 and legal clearance at 16:00 precede commitment; segregation is actually cleaner because BUY-8 is not an authorizer. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is then rested on operational need and on legal clearance standing in for finance above EUR 150,000, which D2’s “as well” does not say.

The pattern is emergency normalization. Five of eight cases become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, including cases the memo’s own hierarchy would leave noncompliant. Confidence labels stay medium or high where the controlling fact was misread.

## Alternatives, evidence, and the draft clause
Section 5 covers four cases, so the count is met. The P-507 alternative is another version of the false “no compliance clearance” premise, so it cannot change a classification for a real reason. The P-505 alternative inherits the expiry framing and does not test the acknowledgment-versus-ERP-clock premise that would actually move the case. P-501, P-502, and P-508, where the primary is most fragile, are not given a classification-changing alternative.

Section 6 has the right columns and then copies the factual errors: P-501 and P-502 are logged as no breach, P-507 as missing compliance, and P-505 as a post-window finance problem. The P-503 remedy, treating dual authorization as an implied A7 exception after the fact, is the cure the policy refuses. Late logging, a timely act proved by a PDF or a supplier acknowledgment, and a control that never happened are not kept apart in the cases where that distinction is decisive.

Section 7 is the strongest part. It separates timing and supplier-register relief from segregation and compliance, requires an explicit finance deferral, converts oral authorization to a record within four hours, and adds an exception log. The scope definition still points the wrong way: “stoppage exceeding six hours” would miss the SOP emergency, which is a stoppage expected within six hours. Budget-owner independence, ERP-clock drift, and the ban on serial informal extensions remain thin.

## Score
A usable skeleton and a few correct institutional cautions keep this above a failed outline. The assessment is not reliable on the decision the file asks for. Wrong thresholds, one directive applied before it exists, one performed clearance treated as absent, one actor copied across cases, and exception labels that contradict the hierarchy are central failures of evidential discipline and time-of-act reasoning, not presentation nits.
