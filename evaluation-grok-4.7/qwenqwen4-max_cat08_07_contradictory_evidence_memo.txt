The memo is a strong, decision-ready unbundling of Redesign R: keep coordinator ownership and the simple-house merge, stop post-billing activation and unvalidated survey skipping, and tie any wider rollout to measured cost, tail, and conformance gates. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated; the five metric conflicts, A–E conditions, and M1–M8 value-of-information ranking are specific and usable. Arithmetic on the EUR 37 break-even and the segment-weighted deltas is correct, and process-mining evidence is actually used.

It is not nearly flawless. The claim that the six-week window truncates the cycle-time tail, and that this bias is known to flatter R, sits badly with a reported p95 of 62 days, which cannot be produced by a hard 42-day completed-case window. Complaint immaturity is asserted as fact rather than left as an assumption. “Deliberately harder” pilot regions is unsupported. The executive framing treats harm as 47% of volume, while worse p90, first-time-right, and complaints cover about 66%. “Fewer handovers” is treated as a fifth lever although it is largely the result of coordinator ownership, and redeploying survey capacity is recommended even while skill fungibility is still uncertain.
