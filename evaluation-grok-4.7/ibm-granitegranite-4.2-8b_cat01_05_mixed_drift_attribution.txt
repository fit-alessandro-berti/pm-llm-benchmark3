The write-up keeps the required section and table structure and gets the broad sequence right: March is mostly label and provenance drift, later rework and cycle-time growth are real, and the mechanisms do not share one change point. It is not reliable enough to act on. Load-bearing metrics are misread, the quantitative section does not reproduce its own inputs, and the monitoring rules would fire on the wrong mechanism.

The central construct error is `fast_variant_pct`. The series moves inversely with `rework_variant_pct` (about 58–60 with rework near 12 in January–February, 39 with rework 29 in July, 50 with rework 20 in September). That is the straight-through variant share. The answer treats it as the priority-queue share and then uses that reading in the July timeline row, hypotheses 3, 4, and 8, the priority segmentation, the fairness monitor, the attribution, and decision rules 3 and 6. The priority rule is a queue-order change. These aggregates cannot show whether high-complexity cases waited less and standard cases waited more. Rule 6 would treat a rework-driven drop in the fast variant as a priority-scheduling failure.

The cycle-time identity is also wrong. Median case cycle time minus an Assess service time of about 5 hours is every other activity, every queue, and every rework loop. It is not reconstructed waiting time before Assess. That identity is reused in sections 4, 5, 7, 9, and 10. Service itself moved only from about 4.8 hours to about 5.4 hours, roughly 0.6 hour of the roughly 12.7-hour median increase by July (about 5 percent). The stated shares — case mix 20–30, rework 40–50, service 10–15, queueing 30–50 — overlap and sum well above 100, and the service share contradicts that 0.6-hour fact.

Section 3’s arithmetic does not match the tables. On the six named edges, Manhattan distance from January is 2 in March, 33 in May, 48 in July, 24 in September, 54 in October, and 31 in December. The answer reports 1, 30, 67, 32, 59, and 33, so the claimed July peak is an addition error. October is the larger DFG move because Assess→Senior Assessment goes from 4 to 22 per 100 cases. The baseline-relative column is misaligned, December raw fitness is given as about +4 percent versus January when it is about −37 percent (0.58 versus 0.92), and the “duplicate” series is largely the missing-resource series. March duplicate open-case is 4 percent, not 4.8 percent; 4.8 is missing resource. December duplicate is 4, not 5.4. The June Request Info rate of 28 is not in the DFG fragment.

October’s senior-assessment counts are unused. Assess→Senior Assessment is 167, then 106 approvals and 54 rejections. Recorded senior assessment is 4 per 100 cases in January at 21 percent high complexity, and 22 per 100 in October at 41 percent high complexity. That is a larger shift than mix alone, and it is exactly where population drift, routing, and historically inconsistent recording have to be separated. September is also missing as its own interval: supplier Z falls from 46 percent to 24 percent, rework from 27 percent to 20 percent, median cycle from 45.0 hours to 41.5 hours, and the fast variant recovers from 42 percent to 50 percent, before the October seasonal surge. Mapped fitness is wrong at the baseline (January is 0.92, not 0.90) and is described as stable near 0.87. It actually eases from 0.92–0.93 to 0.86 in July, which is the residual conformance loss after label repair. Hypothesis 1’s predicted signature says mapped fitness would be about equal to raw fitness under pure logging; the logging signature is the opposite gap. The rank column puts pure logging at 1, while the ranking note calls it the weakest hypothesis and the interaction the strongest.

The Python does not implement the requested PM4Py workflow. `pd.compat.StringIO` is not valid in current pandas. `from pm4py.log import log_to_df` and `from pm4py.discover import discover_dfg` are not the public API. `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.filter_time_range`, and `pm4py.discover_temporal_profile` are required and are never called. `discover_dfg(..., normalization=False)` is not the real signature; the real call returns a directly-follows graph plus start and end activities, and that return value is discarded. Outer-merge `fillna(0)` turns months that were never in the DFG fragment into huge false distances. Raw-to-mapped recovery is never computed as a series. The logging rule is `raw < 0.92 - 0.4`, so March at 0.55 does not qualify and April at 0.51 does.

Monitoring coverage has the right headings, but conformance is duplicated with label fitness, outcomes are absent despite the October approve/reject counts, and there is no temporal profile. The missing-resource rule at 4 percent would alarm every month after March, because the post-migration level sits at about 4.8–6.5 percent. Seasonality is mentioned, yet the seven operating rules do not compare a month with the same month in a prior season, so recurrence and new drift stay mixed. Section 2 runs past the 280-word limit and section 9 past the 400-word limit. The usable core is the March-versus-post-May split and the supplier, staffing, season, and December-confound narrative. The numbers, the priority measurement, the queue definition, and the code are not.
