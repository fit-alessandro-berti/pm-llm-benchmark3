The case-level bounds for the required cases are largely right, and the S1-dominant bottleneck reading is supported by the growing S1 waits. The station metrics and the code do not reliably match that narrative.

Full-window resource busy ranges are coherent: I1 245–315, I2 290, C1 270–300, C2 245–270 over 345 available minutes, and end-of-window WIP of 8 is correct. Missing starts are bounded rather than replaced by one silent value, open cases are kept, and service is separated from sojourn. Assumptions and the seven required headings are in place.

The station table is not. S1 is 21 started and 20 ended, not “20 started (19 ended, 1 open).” During 10:55–11:10, P07 completes at 11:00, so completed services are not 0, and C1 is busy for the whole 15 minutes (P07, then P09), not 10/15. P09 is already queued and P10 enters at 10:55; the row only counts P11 plus P08. Post-outage C1 busy time is about 150 minutes, not 130, so 76% is too low. S1 queue-plus-active peaks near 8 around 11:00–11:30, so “peak 5–6” is too low and sits inconsistently next to a mean of 4–6 on a ramp that starts at 0. “First 55 minutes” for P01–P07 is actually 45 minutes. Arrival and departure rates are counts, not rates.

Little’s Law is named and then never computed. Section 6’s scenario table is conceptually sound, but the reproducible code does not implement it: `prev_end` keeps ends `<=` the current end, so each missing start collapses to its own end, and utilization is hardcoded rather than calculated. The PM4Py projection also mixes partial cases, so the case-level service/waiting calls are not on a log where those semantics hold.
