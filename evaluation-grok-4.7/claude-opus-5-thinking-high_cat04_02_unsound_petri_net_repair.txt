6.5

The diagnosis of the degenerate waiver join is right, the marking tables match the net, R1 is a sound one-arc repair, and the standalone code implements that repair. The write-up still makes several claims that are false or unproved, including in the minimality argument the task specifically weights.

The verdict says the token `t_split` places in `p_analysis` is never consumed on the waiver route. `t_analyze` consumes it. What cannot be consumed is the successor in `p_analysis_done`. Calling that token stranded in `p_analysis` or `p_analysis_done` repeats the slip: `[p_analysis, sink]` still enables `t_analyze`. Only `[p_analysis_done, sink]` is dead. The later prose admits the unique dead marking and the tables correctly list `t_analyze` as enabled at step 5, but the verdict and the sentence that calls `[p_analysis, sink]` “dead as well” are wrong.

The cost model is explicit and R1’s cost of 1 is right, but the uniqueness proof is not. It treats “add one arc” as if it had to be a second input of `t_join_waived`, then only checks alternative presets of that transition. Other cost-1 arcs are never ruled out. Adding `p_analysis_done → t_waive_review` also yields a workflow net that can reach exactly `[sink]` with analysis mandatory; it merely forces the waiver to wait until analysis has finished. That may be rejected for weakening parallelism, but then the rejection has to be stated. As written, “provably the unique cost-1 repair” is a non sequitur. The same overclaim is repeated in the deployment recommendation. Section 8 is also over the 250-word limit (about 290).

R1 is misclassified as free-choice. After `p_analysis_done → t_join_waived`, `t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived` share `p_analysis_done` and have different presets, so the repaired net leaves the free-choice class. The original net is free-choice; R2 remains free-choice. “No behavioral risk” is too strong for that structural change, even though the upstream XOR keeps the two joins from being enabled together and R1 is still sound and 1-safe.

The bypass checks do not do what §7 says. The “producers of `p_decision` must read `p_analysis_done`” invariant still holds under R3, because `t_skip_analysis` writes `p_analysis_done` rather than `p_decision`. A directed path from the review branch to `t_authorize` that avoids `t_analyze` exists in the corrected R1 net as well; an AND-join is not a cut of the underlying graph. The reachability test “`t_authorize` enabled while `p_analysis` is marked” also accepts R3, since the skip empties `p_analysis` before authorization. Those three tests would miss or falsely flag the repairs this answer itself proposes.

R2’s edits, cost 8, language relative to R1, and the warning that orphaning `p_waived` creates a second input-free place are correct. R3 is a real policy change, its guard on `p_waived` does keep the skip off the standard route, and its cost of 7 matches the stated model. The diagnostic block calls the required PM4Py functions on both nets, and the firing helper refuses a disabled transition. Expected differences (workflow-net true for both, sound only after the repair, CE-A blocked, repaired markings a strict subset) follow from the arc addition. Extra comments in §5 go beyond clarifying the repair, and “cost 1 admits three shapes” incorrectly includes a cost-0 relabel, but those are secondary.
