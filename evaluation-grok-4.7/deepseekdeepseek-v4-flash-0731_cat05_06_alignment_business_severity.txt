The case-level reading is mostly accurate and well separated: telemetry and retries are treated as noise, the silent tau is treated as a fit, zero-cost auto-approvals are treated as policy failures, and raw cost is not used as the ranking. The required sections, columns, and main remediation themes are present. The score is held down by concrete logic and consistency defects.

The clearest policy error is CL-606. Fraud Screen and Manual Approve are synchronous and precede payment, so P2 is satisfied on the supplied alignment. Conditioning P2 on acceptance of the external assessment is wrong; that evidence bears on the missing Assess Loss model activity, not on P2.

Pattern 6 then blurs a distinction the case table had right. CL-602 lacks a fraud screen but shows Manual Approve before Pay. CL-604 has both a missing fraud screen and payment before approval. The pattern states those as one combined failure for both cases.

The materiality section lists the right factors and correctly separates confidence from severity, but it never defines what severity 1 through 5, or the confidence numbers, mean. That leaves CL-610 at severity 5, equal to the EUR 480,000 case, without reconciling the EUR 4,500 exposure against the framework’s own amount factor. Withdrawal is also folded into the P6 scope dispute, which names denied pre-screen leads and reopened claims, not withdrawals. “Recent” in P2 is treated as P1’s seven-day window without that assumption being flagged.

The code follows the requested PM4Py calls and keeps raw cost separate from a manually assigned severity, but the rollup does not aggregate risk level or policy-overlay flag, CL-607 is given an invented amount of 0 and risk “UNKNOWN,” and CL-608 is `policy_violation: False` after the narrative says the P4 linked-evidence condition is not met.
