6.5

The write-up is complete, well partitioned, and often numerically careful, but it is not reliable enough for a high score. Several required quantitative claims contradict the table, each other, or the attached code.

The primary regime partition is the strongest part. The 28 days are covered once, outage and recovery are not treated as normal high throughput, and most regime means that can be recomputed from the daily rows are right (baseline weekdays 100.1/99.6, overload weekdays 114.0/100.6 and net +13.4, intervention weekdays 115/121 and net −18, month totals 2519 arrivals, 2409 completions, net +110, mean WIP 29.9). Weekday/weekend bimodality, the April 20 mix/service step, and the refusal to read April 16’s 129 completions as steady capacity are sound. The alternative state split is genuinely different, and its warning that pooling both recovery episodes hides the mix change is fair. Hourly Fano factors and CVs for the four vectors check out (about 3.23, 4.14, 10.12, 1.29). Several Little’s Law rows are also arithmetically right, including baseline week 1 at 143.7 operating minutes and the outage divergence of 583 versus 322 minutes, with a correct `not defensible` verdict.

Material errors:

- Full overload window (20–28 Apr) is given as 914 arrivals, 820 completions, and net +94. The rows sum to 892, 786, and +106. The +94 figure is only the seven overload weekdays. The stated rates 0.141 and 0.127 per minute are therefore wrong. The code window would compute the correct sums, so section 6 and section 8 disagree.
- Pre-outage 6–14 Apr is given as 802 arrivals and 799 completions. The rows sum to 781 and 776. Mean WIP is 18.67, not 18.8, so 152.1 minutes is not the implied flow time.
- Completions per FTE do not fall from 16.9 to 14.7. Baseline and overload weekdays are both about 16.8–17.0 completions per FTE-day. Threat 3 and the Simpson discussion invent a productivity drop. What the data support is flatter throughput while median and p90 service rose.
- “Every day from 20–28 Apr arrivals exceeded completions by 10–16” is false: 25 Apr is +8 and 26 Apr is +4. Cumulative net through 28 Apr is +128 only from 6 Apr; that interval itself adds +106, not +128.
- The April 15 inventory story is impossible as written. End-of-hour net flow peaks at +55 and finishes at +47. Opening ≈14, peak ≈69, and closing = 47 cannot hold together. Closing would be about 61 if the opening calibration is taken seriously. Threat 5 separately treats 16 Apr mean WIP of 52 as the opening buffer. Those are different quantities, and the opening backlog is not observed.
- The Little’s Law cell called `applicable` says overnight carry is under 4 cases. That matches daily drift, not inventory level. With WIP near 20, a stable overnight stock on the order of the late-day queue is plausible. Drift was confused with level, which is the boundary condition used to justify `applicable`.
- The same cell subtracts median service (25) from mean sojourn (144) to get “queue wait ≈ 119.” That is the substitution the answer itself forbids. Section 1 then states congested flow times of 200–350 minutes as fact, while section 6 marks those windows `not defensible`.

Further problems that keep the score down: `peak_08_10_pct` is declared a mislabeled 09:00–11:00 share because 7 Apr matches, but 21 Apr does not (indices 2–3 are 36.5%, not 47%; literal 08:00–09:59 is 9.6%). Service means of ~28 and ~43 minutes are unlabeled interpolations between median and p90, then turned into 7.2 FTE, ρ = 0.95, and ρ = 1.32. Active handling time on 16–17 Apr is attributed to “aged” cases even though waiting is excluded from the service metric. The prompt’s coding-change alternative is collapsed into “fundamentally more demanding tasks.” Completion Fano ≈ 0.98 is called nearly deterministic; that is Poisson-scale, and 21 Apr still starts at 3–4 completions per hour. The state segmentation is partly tautological because it cuts on net flow, WIP, and FTE, and pooled weekends repeat the mix confounding the answer criticizes elsewhere. The Python “change point” hard-codes the dates already chosen. Confidence is High on two-day cells.

Conceptually this is a serious regime analysis. As a quantitative document it contains too many internal contradictions in the audit, the paradox example, and the Little’s Law matrix to score higher.
