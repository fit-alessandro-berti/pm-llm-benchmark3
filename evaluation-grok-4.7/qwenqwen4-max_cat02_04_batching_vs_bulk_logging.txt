7.5

The final report is a strong process-mining analysis: it keeps PM4Py batch labels separate from physical execution, distinguishes logging batches from operational ones, and the G1–G8 rankings, G6 zero-service/inflated-wait mechanism, and censor-or-exclude adjudication are mostly coherent. It is not nearly flawless.

The final conclusions still contain numerical contradictions. Section 7’s G7 waiting bound of 0–19 minutes does not follow from the stated start window `[13:45, 14:15]` with upstream arrivals left at minute resolution (M05’s feasible wait is about 0–4 minutes; M01’s is about 0–17). That 19 comes from adding ±30 minutes to a −11 minute wait, which also censors `queue_arrival` by 15 minutes. The same adjudication repeats “flow inflated by up to 22 minutes” for G3; if E01’s completion is the next start (11:10), the logging delay is 20 minutes, and 22 is E01’s wait.

Section 3 defines `U_sum` with capacity in the denominator, then reports G1 as `12/3 = 400%` and leads G5 with 233%. The mandated code divides by capacity, so it would print about 100% and 78% instead. Section 2’s G7 row (mean wait `+4.20`, arrivals before first start `5/5`) contradicts both the data and the later, correct claim of four negative waits; only M01 arrives before 14:00. The code also says E04 is right-censored at 11:30 but leaves that proxy as `NaT`.

Conformance comments over-claim control-flow effects. These fragments are one activity per case, so equal timestamps do not by themselves make an Alpha Miner or DFG “look parallel.” Resource-time and ordering bias are real; trace-variant bias is mostly not, on this extract.

The hypothesis matrix, assumption labels, capacity-versus-identity split, and collection redesign are otherwise careful and usable. The deductions are for repeated arithmetic and estimator inconsistencies in the final tables, not for the overall diagnosis.
