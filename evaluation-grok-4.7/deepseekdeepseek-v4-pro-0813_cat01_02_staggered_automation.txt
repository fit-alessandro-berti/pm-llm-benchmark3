7.5

The arithmetic on the pre/post windows, control contrasts, and within-Alpha/Beta intensity slopes is largely correct, and the answer keeps median and p90 separate, treats rollout as endogenous, and refuses a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim. Estimands, mechanisms, threats, and the claim ladder are well matched to staggered adoption, intensity, spillovers, and small-N uncertainty.

The score is held down by quantitative and identification slips. Control-adjusted p90 differences of about −3 to −10 hours are labeled “percentage points” in §§5 and 7, and those ranges are then called “absolute” while written as percents. The median “−3 to −8 h” band drops Beta’s weaker late contrast (about −1.7 h versus Epsilon). The §3 justification is only ~110 words against a required 150–250. The final “conditionally causal” bullet treats five clusters as an identification failure rather than an inference problem. The Python model is a branch-FE regression of binary availability with no calendar time and never uses the relative-time indicators it builds, so it does not implement the stated primary design and leaves `automation` nearly collinear with `automated_fast_path_pct`. Branch-months are equally weighted despite large volume differences, and the nonzero 4–6% fast-path share when `automation=0` is never examined. The rollout (Delta then Epsilon) adds a holdout and guardrails, but a two-branch stepped wedge does not fix the small-N problem the answer itself diagnoses, and the non-inferiority margin is undefined.
