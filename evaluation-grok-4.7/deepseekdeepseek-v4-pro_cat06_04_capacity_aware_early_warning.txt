6.5

The policy is well structured and gets the hard constraints right: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed for an unchanged repeat, I-07 is reopened because of new evidence, spillover is inside the objective, and segment calibration, fairness, and monitoring are concrete. The portfolio (I-04 and I-09, one slot reserved) is internally motivated rather than a sort on `p_now`.

The quantitative core does not audit. Section 1 sets today’s spillover at €2,400 on a €120k base, while every priority score subtracts €2,000. Section 3 then says the exposed set is I-02 (€25k), I-06 (€15k), and three unnamed cases at €20k, which sums to €100k, but still uses €120k. The published scores (for example I-04 at +€5,030) are not recoverable from the Section 3 effect of 0.156 or from the stated €2,400 spillover.

The adverse row misses a required case. “Little or no benefit” is implemented as I-04 at 0.075 and I-09 at 0.18, so both effects stay material and the −€2,800 net is too mild. Optimistic uses the top endpoint, not the upper half of the range.

Rescaling the supplied absolute-effect ranges by `p_adj / p_now` treats observational miscalibration as a proportional causal effect. That is exactly the identification step the brief warns against, and it changes Strategic Enterprise and SME scores. The score also ignores time-to-deadline and option value, so a 10-hour triage case outranks diagnosis-stage cases with 2.5–3 hours left. Treating a diagnosis-stage case shrinks the spillover set; that benefit is never credited, even though spillover is endogenous to the chosen portfolio. Section 5’s own rule—intervene only if the pessimistic end stays nonnegative after spillover—does not hold for I-09 at the high spillover rate. Causal monitoring never addresses interference, so treated-versus-untreated comparisons would absorb the spillover the policy is built on.
