The final write-up has the right regime story and the required eight-part structure, but its calculations are not coherent enough for a high score. Several headline figures contradict the table, each other, or the code.

The pooled balance is reversed. Over all 28 days there are 2519 arrivals and 2409 completions (about 90.0 versus 86.0 per day). The answer reports 88.9 and 92.1 and then says pooled completions exceed arrivals, so 6.0 FTE looks sufficient. That inequality is the opposite of the data, and the section 7 policy contrast is built on it. The high-complexity staffing point is still directionally right (weekday arrivals about 114 versus completions about 101 before the intervention, then a real drain on 29 April–1 May), but the pooled foil is false.

Primary estimates are loose or undefined. Baseline weekdays are about 100.1 arrivals, 99.6 completions, and WIP 20.3, not 99, 98, and “~19 weekday.” The 15–19 April completion figure of 88 matches neither the five-day mean (77.2) nor the three weekdays (99.7), while 105 arrivals is only the weekday slice. Section 4’s WIP values (19.2 and 43.4) do not match either all-day or weekday means.

Little’s Law is internally inconsistent. The baseline row says 138 minutes; the notes use L = 19.2 and 98 completions/day and get about 141 minutes; neither pair is the baseline weekday or nine-day mean. The 283-minute “high complexity” result is the 20 April snapshot (WIP 37, 94 completions), not the regime (weekday flow time is closer to 315 minutes). The 248-minute pooled figure matches the answer’s own arrival rate, while the matrix labels the proxy as completions. Subtracting median service (25) from mean flow time to get a 116-minute wait is not valid. Marking 6–14 April, which still mixes weekdays and a weekend, as `applicable` is too strong given unknown overnight backlog.

Burstiness is overstated. 7 April’s hourly mean is 101/12 = 8.42, not 9.6; that error is what produces Fano ≈ 2.76. A population Fano is about 3.0 versus about 9.3 on 21 April, so the overdispersion contrast survives, but the printed magnitudes do not. Coefficient of variation is never discussed. The representative hours also fail to reproduce `peak_08_10_pct` (7 April’s 08:00–09:59 share is about 17%, not 31%), and that audit is missed. The staffing recommendation then uses a pooled 35% peak even though post-20 April weekdays concentrate about 46% of arrivals early.

Uncertainty handling is incomplete. The prompt’s coding-change versus true mix ambiguity is never treated; service-time and p90 widening would have been the right evidence. Assumptions are not labeled. The code hard-codes dates, so it does not implement the required change-point heuristic, does not print a pooled summary, and its all-day regime means would contradict the prose tables. The PM4Py comment correctly says these aggregates are not an event log.

Qualitatively, outage versus recovery, the 20 April mix break, the 29 April staffing change, weekend heterogeneity, and the confounding of extra FTE with worse regimes are the right process-mining distinctions. Those strengths do not offset reversed aggregates and flow times that are not computed from the windows they are attached to.
