4.5

The required nine-section structure is present, base service means and case-mix arithmetic check out, and the specialization–pooling tradeoff, tail/SLA objective, and P1-protected pilot are substantively framed. The capacity conclusion that drives the recommendation does not.

Total base offered work at speed 1.0 is about 2,720 minutes (291 intake + 476 mechanical + 797 electrical + 495 software + 661 validation) against about 2,490 staffed minutes after breaks, or about 2,670 with the 30-minute overtime cap. Multipliers above 1.0, handovers, setup, and coordination push clock time higher still. The day is already at or over capacity. Section 2 instead sums the same people into every skill pool they can perform (mechanical “35%,” electrical “47%,” software “57%”) and treats those non-simultaneous supplies as utilizations. That error is never corrected. The base rows of the scenario table, with maximum utilization about 0.55–0.78, contradict the answer’s own workload totals.

Scenario loads are also not auditable. U2 adds the 12:00–14:00 and 14:00–16:00 E+ES shares (0.40 + 0.42 = 0.82) and then asserts electrical load rises to about 1,150 minutes and software to about 770, with no derivation. Doubling those shares and renormalizing, or doubling those arrival counts, produces a much smaller increase. U3’s “afternoon software = 495/480 ≈ 103%” assigns the whole day’s software load to Emil and ignores Farah’s morning capacity. U1’s intake bottleneck is implausible: intake is short and several resources can do it. A true time-block and skill account, required by the task, is never calculated, despite Deepa starting at 10:00, Emil at 12:00, and Farah leaving at 12:00.

The comparison table is presented as seed-averaged simulation output. The code cannot produce it. `random.Random` has no `poisson` method, so the program fails immediately. Even if arrivals were fixed, priority is stored and then ignored, `case.last_resource` is never updated so handover cost stays zero, preemption is hard-coded off, breaks do not interrupt service, and whole cases are booked before later arrivals. The omitted-mechanism note does not disclose that priority dispatch and handover do not affect results. Seed counts conflict across the text (50), the narrative code claim (30), and `__main__` (3). Section 4’s SLA bands, preemption storms, and handover rankings are therefore unsupported.

Smaller consistency failures add up: objective formulas drop operators; the pooling score is said both to reward the highest score and to penalize handover; preemption triggers differ (60 versus 15 minutes); “~100 minutes is 4% of one shift” is about 22% of one shift; stage “wait” is start minus case arrival, so later stages include all earlier sojourn; queue-entry and pause/resume attributes are missing. The monitoring list and phased pilot are the strongest parts, but they inherit the unsupported claim that pooling’s handover burden has already been shown to be unacceptable.
