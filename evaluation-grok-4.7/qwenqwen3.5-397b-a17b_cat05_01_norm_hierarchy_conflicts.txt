5.5

The final assessment has the required seven-part structure and a usable working theory: GPIP-4 as an integrity floor, ECD-2 as a time-bounded operational overlay, and the CFO email as a limited finance interpretation. Several outcomes are directionally defensible, especially P-502 on post-commitment compliance, P-504 on scope, and P-506 after expiry. It is not reliable enough to score higher.

The method section never states a burden of proof, a later-in-time rule, or a specificity rule, although those were required. The hierarchy also treats GPIP-4 as able to displace the local SOP. A5 makes GPIP-4 a floor and leaves stricter local controls in place.

The formalized SoD rule is wrong. A1 bars the requester or the person who commits from being the final business approver. The pseudo-logic instead prohibits the requester from being the committer. That contradicts the P-508 reasoning, where the same person requests and commits and SoD is treated as satisfied. The compliance rule covers only new suppliers and drops the above-EUR-100,000 trigger.

That omitted trigger breaks P-503. Under the answer’s own hierarchy, ECD-2 and the CFO email cannot waive compliance, yet the case is explained only as a segregation breach. The missing pre-commitment compliance clearance is the clearer violation, and the section 5 alternative would not change the result while that breach remains. P-502 also omits the A2 finance-timing breach. The CFO email did not exist at 10:15 on 14 July, so it cannot be used at the time of that commitment.

P-501 calls the emergency form late and lists it as satisfied. The commitment was 14 July 08:50 and the form 15 July 07:50, inside the 24-hour limit. A6 is also applied to a EUR 38,000 purchase that does not trigger GPIP-4’s pre-commitment finance rule.

The outage note is misapplied. It concerns ERP entries during recovery from the 14 July 08:30–13:00 outage and says those timestamps may be up to 12 minutes later than the act. P-505 is on 18 July, and its separate fact is that the ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast. The 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment can still support a pre-expiry commitment, but the stated evidential basis is wrong. P-507’s signed PDF at 12:40 independently falls within four hours of 08:55. Calling that case a justified exception because of a later ERP timestamp contradicts the answer’s own rule that contemporaneous evidence controls. Section 6 then treats the same fact as a logging defect.

P-508 is marked compliant, with high confidence and no deviation, despite no finance or budget-owner approval above the email’s EUR 150,000 sentence. The four-hour authorization record is also unproven for P-505 and P-508. The unresolved point that Incident Command is not expressly a budget owner is left unused in the case table. No case is indeterminate, and severity is never defined.

Section 6 says a later CRO signature can cure P-502 and P-503, while also saying later approval cannot cure an A6 failure. The recommended clause is concise and includes an anti-normalization safeguard, but it does not resolve the numeric thresholds or the budget-owner problem the cases turn on.
