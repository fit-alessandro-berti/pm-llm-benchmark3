7.5

The policy is unusually careful and largely auditable, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and one false invariance claim keep it well short of an excellent mark under a strict reading.

What holds up: every required section is present; all ten cases are decided; I-03 is refused because it is past `Diagnosis Complete`, not because its probability is low; I-05 (refresh with no new event) is suppressed while I-07 (new log) is reopened and then rejected; spillover enters the go/no-go rule rather than appearing only as a caveat. The base net-benefit arithmetic checks out. With \(S \approx 6 \times 0.02 \times €15{,}000 = €1{,}800\), the reported figures match the stated formulas: I-01 \(0.265 \times 9{,}000 = 2{,}385\), NB \(\approx -115\); I-04 \(0.19 \times 0.85 \times 60{,}000 = 9{,}690\), NB \(\approx +5{,}440\), RB \(0.075 \times 60{,}000 - 2{,}450 = +2{,}050\); I-09 midpoint NB \(\approx +1{,}750\) and halved-lower-bound RB \(= +550\); I-05 uplifted NB \(\approx +150\); I-08 NB \(\approx +1{,}250\) but RB \(\approx -1{,}700\). Section 3’s scenario bounds also add up, including the marginal-slot check on I-02 (\(-€0.5\mathrm{k}\) to \(+€1.8\mathrm{k}\)) and the illustrative \(0.25/0.50/0.25\) blend \(\approx +€6.9\mathrm{k}\). The two-proportion sample-size claim (\(\approx 95\) per arm for a \(0.20\) absolute reduction from a \(0.60\) base) is correct. The monitoring section has real triggers, a near-tie experiment, doubly robust estimation, a shadow model, and an explicit rule for when parameters may change.

The main conceptual break is between Step B and Step C. Step B says adjusted probabilities are never the effect. Step C then multiplies the supplied absolute-risk-reduction ranges by \(p_{\mathrm{adj}}/p_{\mathrm{now}}\) (\(\times 0.85\) Strategic, \(\times 1.10\) SME). Calibration error is not a causal-effect error. The supplied ranges are already absolute effects; the defensible use of calibration is a cap at adjusted untreated risk, which is the \(0.8 \times p_{\mathrm{adj}}\) rule. That cap never binds here, so the ranking is driven by the extra, unjustified rescaling. Calling that rescaling “symmetric, so neither group is treated preferentially” is also wrong: it shrinks Strategic effects and inflates SME effects. The flat factors are not even the case-specific ratios the rule states. For I-08, \((0.43-0.12)/0.43 \approx 0.72\), outside the stated Strategic band \(0.75\)–\(0.92\). Using the case-specific low ratio instead of \(0.85\) drops I-08’s base net benefit from about \(+€1{,}250\) to roughly zero. A deployable rule that does not reproduce its own table is not yet deployable.

The false-alarm claim is false. Weighting burden by \(1-p_{\mathrm{adj}}\) does not “roughly halve” it: the factor is about \(0.31\) for I-09, \(0.37\) for I-05, and \(0.66\) for I-08. Applied consistently to the robust bound as well as to net benefit, I-08’s robust bound rises from about \(-€1{,}700\) to roughly zero or positive and its net benefit moves toward \(\sim€3\mathrm{k}\), ahead of I-02 and I-06. The claim that the portfolio ranking is invariant is therefore not earned.

Feasibility is knife-edge and under-tested. \(L_{\mathrm{post}} = 90\) minutes is an invented hard gate. It correctly leaves I-07 out on the clock, but I-07 is already out on net benefit, so that decision is robust. I-09 is not: a \(12{:}00\) start finishes at \(12{:}45\), plus 90 minutes, against a \(14{:}30\) deadline, leaving about 15 minutes. I-02 is scheduled at exactly its latest feasible start, \(12{:}45\), so any overrun makes the supplied effect unrealizable under the same rule. There is no sensitivity to \(L_{\mathrm{post}}\). The reserve story then contradicts the same clock. I-05’s latest start is \(14{:}45\), the reserve releases at \(15{:}00\), and the reserve-override hurdle is NB \(\ge €3{,}000\), while I-05’s NB is about \(+€150\). Listing I-05 as second in line for the released slot is not feasible under the policy as written. The reserve’s \(q \approx 0.1\)–\(0.3\) and \(\mathrm{NB}_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx €10\mathrm{k}\) are likewise assumed, not derived; they are what make one reserved slot look mandatory.

Smaller defects accumulate. I-06’s “artefact” effect of about \(0.09\) and the \(0.45\) verification cutoff do not follow the stated “halve the lower bound” rule. Robust bounds stack the low effect with the low calibration factor for Strategic cases but use the raw low effect for SME cases. Hysteresis refers to a probability trigger the value-based rule never defines. Required backfill is not fed back into \(S\), so the decision uses unmitigated spillover while the operating plan assumes mitigation. The \(20\%\) low-value slot floor can override NB \(> 0\) without a reconciliation. “Do nothing forgoes about \(0.5\) breaches” is a gross figure; net of \(0.18\)–\(0.54\) spillover breaches it is much smaller. The \(20\)–\(25\%\) adverse weight is asserted and then turned into a point expectation.

The selected portfolio itself — I-09 at \(12{:}00\), I-02 or verified I-06 at \(12{:}45\), I-04 after confirmation, one slot held to \(15{:}00\), no swarm for I-03, I-05, or I-07 — is still a coherent conservative answer, and Sections 4–6 are concrete enough to run. The grade is held down by contradictions in the rule that produces those numbers, not by missing the problem.
