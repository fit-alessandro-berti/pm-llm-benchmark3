The write-up matches the required headings, tables, path lists, and scenario labels, and it gets several structural points right: preliminary compliance does not replace final compliance, four-eyes is kept, K-337 truncates the path only by changing decision policy, and C1 reservation plus false concurrency are treated as spillovers. Those merits do not offset the timing analysis.

The central quantitative claim is wrong. K-337 runs from Monday 11:00 through a Friday request, a weekend external wait, the next Monday’s third document check, Tuesday assessment and compliance, and a Wednesday decision — about nine days. The answer repeatedly treats that factual cycle as about two days and then places the counterfactual at 2.0–3.0 days, so the worst case is presented as slower than the factual case. That inverts the intervention. The same section also says compliance load falls because “only one” compliance review is needed; the factual path already had exactly one.

Queue state is misread, and earlier readiness is treated as earlier completion. For K-219, D2’s Monday 08:00 queue of 2 h is used to start work at 17:30, ignoring the Monday 16:00 snapshot of 5 h. Assessment is then started the moment documents finish, despite A2’s multi-hour queue, and the base case still lands on the same Wednesday 08:30 decision while the table claims a shorter cycle. Arithmetic fails inside that story: 17:30 plus 6 h is 23:30, not Tuesday 01:30. K-411 calls D2 free against a 4 h Tuesday queue, starts both checks at 09:35, and its base case has a 6 h check ending at 21:35. The best case also clears A2 immediately even though Tuesday’s assessor queue was about 6 h, one assessor was in training, and the factual case still waited until Wednesday. K-104’s best case says C1 starts immediately after Tuesday’s assessment but only shortens a 2.5-day case to 2.2 days, and it never applies the stated 1–3 h saving from not reading the file from scratch.

The 100-case discussion repeats the readiness error by saying K-219 shrinks the assessment queue, misses the word limit, and the K-219 pilot stopping rule imports a C1-reservation trigger. Five of the eight “assumptions” are restated rules rather than the load-bearing claims about idle resources, decision windows, and calendar span.
