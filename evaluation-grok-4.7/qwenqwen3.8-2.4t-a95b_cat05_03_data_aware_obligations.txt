The case results are largely right: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant; TX-305 is indeterminate; TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are compliant; and TX-310 is properly noncompliant at medium confidence because EX-88’s certificate post-dates release and the only witness is the requester. The main classification flaw is TX-302. R1 is already met by the `Enhanced_Review` at 09:20, with no later material change. EX-77 is valid, but it is surplus. **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** says the case is acceptable only through the waiver. The later alternative reading notes **COMPLIANT** and does not adopt it.

Formalization is weaker than the case table. R3’s trigger is true whenever any earlier screen precedes the list update, so a later rescreen still leaves the rule “triggered.” The code’s latest-screen test is the correct reading; the pseudo-logic is not. R1’s material-change predicate calls a release-level `>20%` test that does not actually depend on the candidate change. R5’s prose uses expiry as a strict cutoff, while the code treats `release_time <= expires` as still valid.

The pandas logic matches the intended outcomes on this log, including forward-fill without future leakage and `REVIEW_REQUIRED` for EX-88 and the unknown TX-305 amount. Status handling is not monotonic: a later indeterminate R2 or R3 assignment overwrites an earlier `FAIL`. `approved_by` is never checked, witness time is unused, and `latest_material_change` includes `Create_Transaction`. Those defects do not flip these eleven cases, but they keep the checker short of a reliable rule engine. Sections 6 and 7 are specific and usable.
