The answer has the right compliance posture and covers all six required sections, but its operational ranking rests on inconsistent timing math, invented parameters, and one material misreading of the rejection rule.

The ordering is directionally sound. A and B are correctly treated as currently nonconformant and inadmissible despite their speed. G is correctly left disabled until both Rule 10 conditions exist. The quantitative case for Rank 1 does not close. Branch D1 states a 25–50 minute “focused” review and a 65–95 minute total, but 40 + 25–50 + 5–15 is 70–105. Section 5 then uses a different compressed review-plus-execution band of 35–55 minutes to get 70–90. Neither compression appears in the case. When S4 is late, the answer switches back to the stated 35–75 minute review and gets 105–155. Compression is applied only on the favorable branch, which biases D toward beating the 90-minute deadline. The S2 fallback at t=30 uses a 35–65 minute review; S2’s stated review time is 35–75, so 70–110 should be about 70–120. The t=30 gate “ETA ≤ 40 minutes” is also too loose to protect a 90-minute deadline once a full second review and execution are added.

Action C is labeled conformant now, while the case makes conformance conditional on an independent review. The table’s 40–90 minute total also drops the extra analyst-review step the narrative adds, so the claim that C has the best conformant chance of meeting the deadline is not cleanly measured.

The conditional approval range 0.65–0.85 is invented. The only given approval range is 0.55–0.75, with an explicit warning that it may not transport. The 0.48–0.64 baseline SLA-breach range is cited and then barely used, even though it is the main prediction against which D should be compared.

Section 4 misstates what would make F valid. Rule 6 requires a second officer before Approve or Payment Executed, not before Reject. The case itself leaves only the documented rejection-threshold uncertain. Requiring a new policy just to give Rule 6 its existing scope is an error in a section that specifically asks for F’s validity conditions.

The S4=65 sensitivity compares euros (0.55–0.75 × €20,000–€35,000) with P-901’s 0.12–0.28 probability increase. Those are not commensurate, and P-901 has no loss-given-breach figure. The table also places avoided regulatory loss inside some harm cells and describes the sanctions-escalation branch as Action E in the table, then as an S4 reject/hold/SAR in the trace.

The recommendation would likely survive these corrections, but the deadline, spillover, and rejection-threshold reasoning is not reliable enough for a higher score.
