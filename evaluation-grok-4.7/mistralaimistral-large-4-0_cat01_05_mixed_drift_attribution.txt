6.5

The diagnosis is complete and the main story is right: March is a semantic and provenance break, May is supplier-driven rework, June is capacity and queueing, July is an unobserved redistribution, October is seasonal mix plus a recording ambiguity, and December is confounded. Baseline arithmetic, the seven-edge L1 distances, the raw-to-mapped recovery of 0.35 (0.35/0.45 ≈ 78%), and the separation of change points by artifact all check out. The score is held down by repeated anchor errors, self-contradictory thresholds, over-precise cycle-time splits, and a Part B PM4Py layer that does not match the manual.

Supplier exposure is repeatedly given as 16% to 43%. April is already 18%, so the May step is 18 to 43. The senior-assessment jump is repeatedly given as 9 to 22. The last pre-season fragment is September at 7, and October is 22. Incidence per high-complexity case goes from about 4/21 in January and 7/26 in September to 22/41 in October, so the October rise is larger than case mix. That ratio is never computed, which leaves the logging-versus-behavior split weaker than the data allow.

The July behavioral change point is not earned by the cited deltas. Fast-variant share falls from 60 to 41 between February and June, then only from 41 to 39 in July, and rebounds to 50 in September. `CollectDocs→Assess` moves from 91 to 89 and rework from 24 to 29 only by stretching May into July. The priority rule remains plausible because the prompt says the monthly aggregate hides redistribution. It is not identified by those DFG moves. Part A would not flag July on the stated rule, while it would flag March’s mapped-fitness drop of 3 pp as performance and flag duplicate-`Open Case` moves of +1 pp in April, July, and October as new logging breaks. Section 3.4 and the code do not describe the same candidates.

The cycle-time split manufactures precision the aggregates do not support. Service evidence is one activity, about 4.8 h to 5.4 h. Extra rework executions raise total touch time even when that duration is nearly flat, so the “service ≈ 3% / +0.5 h” bucket misses multiplied work. The stated shares are 60 + 25 + 15 + 3 = 103, and 10.7 + 4.5 + 2.7 + 0.5 = 18.4 h against a median gap of 17.85 h. May is assigned 25–35% queueing with no May queue measurement. `Request Info` is an allowed path, so the doubled loop explains variants and duration. It does not, by itself, explain mapped fitness falling from 0.925 to 0.86. That residual implies deviations outside the supplied fragment, and the answer treats the fitness drop as if it were the rework signal.

Rule 4 says service time within ±10% of baseline indicates queueing. The observed move is 5.4/4.8 − 1 = 12.5%, so the rule excludes the episode used to justify it. Rule 1’s “>80% of the gap,” read with section 2’s denominator of 1 − raw fitness, is 78% in March and would miss the canonical logging case. Section 5 places null results in columns that ask for positive indicators, including “case mix stable” as population drift and “DFG ordering unchanged” as behavioral drift. June’s monthly bucket is used at evidence strength 90 for a supplier interval ending 14 June and again for a staffing interval starting 15 June.

Part A is transparent and reproducible. Part B is not manual-consistent. `format_dataframe` takes `case_id`, not `case_id_key`. `filter_time_range` takes `dt1, dt2`, not `timest_start` and `timest_end`. `discover_declare` is called with `min_support`, `max_support`, `max_declare_cardinality`, and `include_data` instead of `min_support_ratio` and `min_confidence_ratio`. `discover_temporal_profile` is given `min_time` and `max_time`. `normalize_labels` and `remove_duplicate_open_case` walk an EventLog, while `format_dataframe` returns a dataframe, and `len(sublog)` is stored as cases. Those calls would fail or silently measure the wrong object.

Monitoring covers the required surfaces and avoids one global threshold. A frozen January–February reference permanently alerts on labels, duplicates, and missing resources after an accepted migration. Same-month-prior-year references are specified with no prior year available. Benjamini–Hochberg is applied to monitors that move together, without a dependence caveat. The primary population also requires a priority field that the timeline introduces only on 1 July.
