The review is rigorous, behaviorally grounded, and nearly complete. It separates language membership from alignment score, refuses to collapse the metrics into one winner, and correctly treats Models S and C as unfit for release control for different reasons. The third design is a real alternative: one visible transition per activity, mandatory checks that cannot be skipped, expedite gated on current results, exactly one approval, selective recheck, and missing sourcing or correction scope forced to review instead of silently optionalized. Manual replay of B1–B8 and A1–A5 matches the stated verdicts, including the important split between existential silent correction routes and the evidence guard.

The deductions are for concrete defects, not tone. In the metric skeleton, `sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(...)` is wrong for the documented API: `check_soundness` returns a bool, so that call crashes and the promised diagnostics are never produced. `policy_verdict` documents trace-index scope keys but ignores a scope attached to a non-correction index; a mis-keyed known scope then becomes “unknown,” which can soften a FAIL into REVIEW. “Correction event indices” is also ambiguous between trace position and correction ordinal. The alignment-cost helper is only a sensitivity sketch, and marking Supplier Review as always critical does not encode the external-only rule the guard itself states. Those are limited faults in an otherwise sound governance argument.
