6.7

The policy is complete and often sharp: I-03 is correctly barred by stage, I-05 is suppressed as refresh noise while I-07 is re-opened on the new log and then refused on value and timing, spillovers are priced into the score, segment offsets match the stated biases, and the monitoring and label-integrity safeguards are concrete. The arithmetic of the published base scores is mostly reproducible. It is not decision-theoretically tight enough for a high mark.

The deployable rule is broken by its own allocation. Step 3 makes a first snapshot non-actionable unless the deadline is too close to wait, and I-08 is held for that reason, but I-04 — also a first alert, with 10 hours left — is given slot 1 immediately. No override is stated. “Watch level” is never defined, so the persistence test is not actually operational. Step 1’s “conservative end” applies to the calibrated probability, which barely moves the score because it enters only through \(FA\times(1-p)\); the binding uncertainty is \(\Delta\) and congestion. Shading \(\Delta\) for Strategic Enterprise is announced and then not used: I-02’s \(+€650\) and I-04’s \(+€7{,}400\) are unshaded midpoints. Calibration error is also treated as if it were an effect-size error.

The I-02 rationale is numerically wrong. At \(\Delta=0.08\), \(0.08\times€25{,}000=€2{,}000\) against about \(€450+€250+€2{,}400\), which is roughly \(−€1{,}100\), not break-even. Break-even under their own base spillover is about \(\Delta\approx0.12\). I-02 and I-09 also flip negative at the high end of the spillover band they themselves quote, yet both are committed. The flat \(€2{,}400\) charge ignores the stated congestion dependence, so the third slot is priced as if the first two had not already been used. “In that order” (I-04, then I-02, then I-09) is score order, not urgency order, and the two-specialist constraint is never translated into serial timing.

Section 3 has further audit problems. With optimistic effects \(0.18, 0.24, 0.27\), \(P(0)\approx0.46\) and \(P(1)\approx0.41\), so the mode is zero breaches avoided, not “most likely 1 of 3.” Base spillover is written as \(3\times6\times0.02\times€15\text{–}25\text{k}\) but the upper figure \(€8{,}100\) is not \(€9{,}000\). Adverse stacks the full \(€3{,}400\) false-alarm bill on top of lower-bound effects; the specified adverse case does not say every alert is false, and base instead uses expected false-alarm cost. I-04’s “\(€2{,}000\) priced in” is also false under their formula: only about \(€1{,}200\) is priced in.

The fairness machinery is better than the decision it is attached to. Step 5 says to apply the equity floor, then all committed euros go to I-04, I-02, and I-09, with no statement of why the risk-based slot is not binding today. Non-monetary duty is mostly just the regulatory reserve; Public Sector cases are ranked as ordinary euro scores. Releasing that reserve at 14:30, against a before-15:00 arrival risk, is unexplained. The untreated-case value of \(€15\text{–}25\text{k}\) is an external assumption that decides the marginal slots and is not stress-tested case by case.
