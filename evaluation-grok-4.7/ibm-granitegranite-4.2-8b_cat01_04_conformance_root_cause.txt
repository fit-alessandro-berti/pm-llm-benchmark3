4.5

The write-up keeps the required eight-part shape and reaches a partly sound high-level view: the June collapse is mostly a logging/measurement failure, employee blame is not the lead explanation, and the August sign-off rule can make the control-flow model stale. That is not enough to carry the grade. The final sections still contain process-mining errors that would send the investigation the wrong way.

Fitness is systematically inverted. The supplied table separates fitting-trace rate from mean alignment cost (raw June–July: 32% and 0.74; after label map, `Queue Routed` removal, and source-sequence ordering: 89% and 0.12). The conclusion says normalization makes “alignment fitness drop to below 0.15” and that precision rises above 0.90, “matching” those summaries. Cost fell; fitting rate rose; precision was never reported. The same inversion reappears in the ranking tests, counterfactual numbers, phase decision rules, and the governance trigger.

The skipped compliance check is misidentified. `Manual Override` traces are `Manual Override, Standard Review, Decision, Notify`. Standard review does not include `Compliance Check`. The unresolved traces are the enhanced-review traces that go directly to `Decision` (25, then 30). The ledger, causal write-up, and conclusion nevertheless treat override traces as missing checks, and the recommended audit targets override cases. That audits the wrong population. The model also does not treat an inserted override as a skipped check.

Provenance is blurred. The 31 May label change and the 3 June `Queue Routed` gateway event are separate; the causal list and conclusion attribute the technical event to the UI release and then call that insertion an ordering defect. Ordering is the 15 June ingestion-time change. Only 11 of 70 reversed `Notify`/`Decision` traces were checked on screenshots, but later text treats the reversal as established for most cases. The directly-follows evidence is `Standard Review -> Notify` and `Notify -> Decision`, not a `Receive -> Standard Review -> Notify` edge.

The staged alignment ladder is the discriminating evidence and is not used quantitatively. Label mapping, technical-event removal, and source ordering account for successive jumps of about 32→58→81→89. The same preprocessing leaves August–September at 66%, and an optional sign-off model returns it to 86%. Model obsolescence is therefore a main cause of the later residual, not a weaker hypothesis than a real behavioral change. Ranking real process change above the obsolete model, while cycle time, approval, and appeal barely move, is not supported. The required point that stable outcomes neither prove a data error nor clear noncompliance is replaced by the stronger claim that the memo and stable appeals show noncompliance was not widespread. Detection was explicitly incomplete, and no audit has resolved the missing enhanced-review checks.

Several falsifiers are reversed or confounded: a result that would support noncompliance is placed in the falsify column, the label-change repair bundles three mechanisms, and the optional-sign-off row predicts a fitness decrease when the observed repair increases fitting traces. The causal arrows also point the wrong way for outcomes and for the policy change: the policy changed the process and the tree was not updated; it did not update the normative model. `business_outcomes -> true_process` is an inference, not a cause.

The code does not meet the executable checklist. It never calls `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`, or `pm4py.discover_dfg`; alignment uses a nonexistent routine; `total_nonconforming` is undefined; and the “optional” sign-off net makes sign-off mandatory after the check while also adding `Enhanced Review -> Decision`, which permits the bypass the policy forbids. The EUR 50,000 overgeneralization caveat is printed, but the model itself does not implement an optional, value-conditioned sign-off or a precision comparison.

Section 7 also runs well past the 250–400 word limit, and several required headings and column labels lose the specified hyphens. The six governance rules are the strongest part and cover the six requested topics, but they inherit the fitness-direction ambiguity.
