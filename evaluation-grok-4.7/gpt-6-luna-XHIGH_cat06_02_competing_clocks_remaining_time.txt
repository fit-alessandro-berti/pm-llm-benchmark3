7.2

The forecast is a serious scenario envelope: the join is treated as a maximum, weekend time counts while work is paused, the temperature excursion is handled as a common cause, scenario bands are allowed to overlap, and rejection is left unquantified because no critical-defect rate is given. Those choices match the problem. The score is pulled down by clock errors, an inconsistent earliest bound, and a smooth-path probability that is too low for the rates given.

The headline earliest time is too early and disagrees with the scenario table. Section 2 puts the smooth-path floor at Monday 09:30–11:00. Section 4 puts earliest completion at 65–70 hours, Monday 08:30–13:30. Monday 08:30 requires chemistry already finished on Friday, an on-call microbiology result on Sunday, and about 0.5 hours for QA review plus release. Release alone is 0.25–0.75 hours, and QA review has a median of 1.5 hours. A defensible floor is about Monday 09:15–10:30 even on that favorable path: if chemistry is already done, readout still takes 0.5–1.5 hours from Monday 08:00 unless the on-call path finished on Sunday, and QA then still has to review. The two sections do not describe the same bound.

Friday chemistry is directionally right (“completion unlikely”) but the 16:30 cutoff is not tied to the two analysts. From 15:30, one analyst is free only at 16:42–17:30 and then has 0.5–1.3 hours before 18:00, which is shorter than the 1.5-hour minimum service, so that analyst cannot finish any new sample on Friday. Only the analyst who frees around 15:54–16:24 can finish a short sample, and only if B-417 actually gets that slot ahead of the urgent sample. A risk-priority Monday start can also be 08:00 on the second analyst if only the urgent sample outranks B-417, which makes a Monday mid-morning chemistry result a normal priority case. “Late morning through afternoon” underweights that case. A smooth-path chemistry result slipping to Wednesday is not supported: two analysts and at most four samples ahead still leave B-417 able to finish on Monday or, if paused at 18:00, on Tuesday morning.

The no-on-call contamination clock is long by about half a day to a day. A Monday 08:00 readout takes 0.5–1.5 hours, so an indication is known about 08:30–09:30. Adding 20–30 hours puts technical readiness at about Tuesday 04:30–15:30, which is read Tuesday 08:00–17:00. “Tuesday 04:00–Wednesday afternoon,” and “typically Tuesday or Wednesday” for the usable result, need a readout delay the log does not support. The same one-day pessimism appears on documentation queries. After a Monday-morning join, a 4-working-hour query can be supplied Monday afternoon and still leave time for resumed review and release before 20:00. The query row’s earliest terminal time of Tuesday drops that path.

The 25–45% smooth-path band is low relative to the stated margins and relative to the answer’s own dependence story. Query 15–30%, rerun 10–20%, contamination 5–12%, and a 4–10% investigation rate when tests are normal do not, even stacked, force only a 25% clean path. Because the excursion raises rerun, contamination, and investigation together, failures cluster, which puts more mass on the fully clean path than an independence product does. A band nearer 35–60% fits the same assumptions. The low band pulls the central window toward Tuesday.

Section 6 mis-states the investigation trigger. A contamination indication itself moves investigation probability to 0.30–0.55; that shift does not require a confirmed excursion. The excursion flag remains the right secondary trigger, and the primary choice of the microbiology readout is defensible because it both moves the join by about a day and changes the investigation odds.

Smaller gaps: the P80-like window (about 95–150 hours) starts inside the central window, so the quantile labels are loose; the severe tail stops at Wednesday 30 September, while a laboratory investigation opened Wednesday and lasting 7 calendar days, followed by resumed QA, can land in early October (Section 2’s “or later” is the better statement); and the 2–5 hour queue figures are used without the shortcut being stated. Analyst free times, pre-Monday QA capacity (4.5 Friday hours plus 4 Saturday hours), and the 0.35 arrival rate never get an explicit residual-queue sum.

The central story — usual terminal event electronic release, mass on Monday afternoon through Tuesday, right tail driven by a laboratory investigation — still holds after those corrections. The errors move edges by hours to about a day. They are concrete enough that the forecast is not nearly flawless.
