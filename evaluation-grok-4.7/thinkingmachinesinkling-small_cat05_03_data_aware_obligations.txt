6.3

The narrative is a serious event-time analysis, but it is not nearly flawless. One case is reasoned incorrectly, and the required checker does not actually check the rules.

TX-302 is the clearest case error. At release the state is GB, CORPORATE, EUR 180,000, so R1 applies. `Enhanced_Review` at 09:20 is after creation and before release, and nothing material changes in between, so that review already meets R1. EX-77 is also valid on the stated evidence (Head_Compliance, qualified signature at 09:05, matching scope, unexpired, outage explaining the 11:00 insert), but it is redundant. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` with “Exemption satisfies R1” treats a satisfied primary control as a waiver. Section 4 then draws the wrong counterfactual: disproving the 09:05 signature would not make TX-302 noncompliant while the 09:20 review still stands.

The other ten classifications are defensible. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are straightforward violations. TX-306 and TX-308 correctly refuse to treat a sub-20% increase or a decrease as invalidating the prior enhanced review. TX-311 correctly applies `event_index` and turns R1 off once jurisdiction is DE at release. TX-305 is rightly `INDETERMINATE`. TX-310 as `NONCOMPLIANT` is a supported reading: scope would match, but a certificate created at 10:30, a requester-witness, and a backdated PDF do not meet R5; the alternative is stated.

Section 1 is otherwise sound on blanks, forward-only state, record time versus evidence time, and no final-state leakage. The materiality bullets are not: a decrease is listed as material and, in the same bullet, as not resetting R1. Sections 6 and 7 are the strongest part of the answer. The six control-flow failure modes are distinct and tied to these cases, and the logging recommendations cover release payloads, signature versus record time, scope, provenance, rule version, and ordering.

The code misses the specification. It does call `StringIO`, parse timestamps, keep `event_index`, call `pm4py.format_dataframe`, and forward-fill. It then hard-codes `COMPLIANT` / `NONCOMPLIANT` by case id, which was explicitly forbidden, and that lookup calls TX-302 `COMPLIANT` while the narrative says `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. `state_at_release` is the last event in the case, so TX-305 would pick up the next-day settlement amount; that frame is unused, but it is the leakage the task is about. Latest reviews are not cut off at release. `review_amount` and the amount updates are computed and never used, so there is no R4 test and no “latest material change” timestamp. `exemption_valid_coarse` is true for any id in the register, including EX-88, whose own record says `valid_for_R1: False`. R1–R3 flags are trigger or existence bits, not ordering checks. The closing claim that this diagnostics frame evaluates the obligations overstates what it does.
