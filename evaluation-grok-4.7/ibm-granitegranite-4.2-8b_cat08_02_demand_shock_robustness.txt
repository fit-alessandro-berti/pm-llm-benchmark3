6.5

The required seven-section structure is complete, and the redesign is appropriately scenario-based rather than a blanket capacity or average-cycle-time fix. Triggers, deactivation rules, trade-off columns, a self-inflicted vulnerability, and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard are all present. The score is held down by several material reasoning errors.

The answer treats invented machine facts as given: M1 is said to run about half of assays, and M2 is called the smaller machine. The log only says M2 is faster and fails more often, and that repeat-after-failure work clusters after M2 and night shifts. The demand-mix policy then routes repeats onto M2, which is the opposite of that evidence and would be expected to raise rework.

The M1-outage trigger requires M2 utilization below 95 percent. At the stated peak, assay utilization is already 98 percent, so the contingency is unlikely to turn on in the outage it is meant to handle. The same policy says both “one urgent per M2 batch” and “max 4 urgent slots,” which cannot both be the rule.

Diverting technical validators into accession correction is labeled robust across every scenario even though that pool is already at 101 percent peak utilization. A 20 percent cap limits the damage but does not create spare capacity; it takes time from the saturated station the conflict notes already flag.

Performance-edge times are used as queue lengths (batch wait cited as about 14 hours). Complex-panel and urgent work are asserted to drive accession corrections without support in the variant evidence. “Technical-only release” is treated as an acceptable way to miss the 18-hour urgent limit by up to 6 hours, without reconciling that medical validation is required for those cases. The flexible-priority rule also talks about urgent bypasses occupying batch slots, which is incoherent if those cases have left the batch.

DFG frequencies are cited more than they are used. Path rates that actually move between periods—recollection, batch-wait share, assay repeats, technical-to-medical share, unfinished cases—are not calculated, and urgent p90 (19 hours normal, 33 hours peak) versus the 18-hour penalty is never used as a quantitative design constraint.
