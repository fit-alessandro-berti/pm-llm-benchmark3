5.5

The final write-up matches the required structure and is strong on control-flow: prerequisites, four-eyes, preliminary versus final compliance, and the fact that K-337 changes decision policy rather than only deleting time. K-411 and K-104 are directionally sound, and the spillover discussion (C1 reservations, D1/D2 substitution, assessment congestion, false concurrency) is the right kind of analysis.

The K-337 clock is wrong, and that error reverses the main result. Factual `Request More Info 2` runs Friday 10:30 to the next Monday (“weekend delay”), then Document Check 3, Assessment 3, compliance, and notify on the following Wednesday. Elapsed time from Monday 11:00 is about 214 hours, not ~46 hours. A Friday-evening decision is roughly 110 hours shorter, not 30–60 hours longer. The ~46-hour baseline is Monday 11:00 to Wednesday 09:10 in the same week, which erases the weekend the case itself records. The Tue 08:00 / Wed 08:00 compliance-queue snapshots are the first week, not the Tuesday of the factual compliance review. That inverted sign is then repeated in the K-337 derivation, the robustness rationale, and the synthesis.

Absolute durations for K-104 (~82.5 h) and K-219 (~72 h) are each about 24 hours high (actual spans are ~58.5 h and 48 h). Some deltas survive only because both ends are shifted. K-219’s worst case is +15 h in the table and +48 h / ~120 h in the derivation. Section 4 says K-337 is faster per case; sections 2, 3, 5, and 7 say it is slower.

Capacity arithmetic is not feasible: ~8 identity or document cases per person per day, and 50–100 compliance reviews per week at ~6 h/day, contradict the stated service times. Tuesday 08:00 D2 backlog is 4 h, not 2 h. A 6 h assessment queue from 08:00 does not free the assessor at 10:30 or 12:00. Jumping an 8 h Wednesday compliance queue is treated as about a 3 h displacement. The synthesis is also just over the 320-word cap, and its “most dangerous” claim denies the speed that the corrected K-337 counterfactual actually has.
