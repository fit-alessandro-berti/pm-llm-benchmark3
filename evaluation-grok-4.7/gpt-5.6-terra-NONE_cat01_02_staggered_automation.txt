6.5

The causal framing is strong, but the quantitative core is not reliable enough for a higher score. Sections 1–4, 6, 8, and 10 correctly separate availability from use, reject a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim, avoid naïve two-way fixed effects as the causal estimator, and tie rollout to learning, queues, staffing, and interference. The recommendation is operationally and inferentially sensible.

Those strengths do not offset errors in the evidence used to support them.

- Alpha’s pre-period fast-path share is 4, not 4.7, so +33.3 pp is wrong.
- Alpha’s April–August rework mean is about 13.7, not 13.1. Rework rose by roughly 0.5 pp; the reported slight decline is the wrong direction. “Modest reduction” and “rework did not increase” are therefore false for Alpha, despite Section 4 correctly describing an April dip followed by a rise.
- Delta/Epsilon April–August means are about 42.6 median hours and 93.2 p90 hours, not 43.8 and 94.3. The relative-effect narrative is built on those incorrect benchmarks.
- Alpha’s post escalation mean is about 11.4 (+2.9 pp), not 11.2 (+2.7).
- Leading minus signs are missing throughout the contrasts, so losses, declines, and improvements are ambiguous in the main table.
- The “0 to 8 h” median interval is not reproducible from the stated calculations.
- The code builds event-time indicators and then never estimates them. Its fitted model is the saturated branch-and-month fixed-effects specification the text warns against. Month fixed effects also contradict the intensity model’s comment: they remove the within-branch learning path and leave a cross-branch comparison. The cluster warning says five clusters, but the intensity regression uses three, with more regressors than clusters.

The estimands, threat list, and claim ladder are otherwise careful. The grade is held down because the bounded descriptive results exclude the true Alpha rework change and the control-trend comparison is arithmetically wrong.
