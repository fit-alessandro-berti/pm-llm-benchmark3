7.5

The design is strong on scope, non-stationary demand, calendars, latent objections, experiment structure, uncertainty, validation, and decision rules. It is not nearly flawless because the PM4Py grounding and skeleton contain concrete errors.

The supplied tree is closer to PM4Py than the rewritten one. `tau` is the standard silent activity, and `+` is parallel, not a construct that must be replaced by a sequence. `X(tau, activity)` under `+` is the usual way to express optional parallel reviews. Replacing that block with `->(...)` drops the partial parallelism the process requires. The notes that `tau` is invalid syntax are wrong.

`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns a 3-tuple `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`. The skeleton looks for length 2 and then takes index 1, so it keeps the wrong object. `format_dataframe` returns a formatted DataFrame, not an EventLog; the docstring states otherwise. `pm4py.discover_log` is not a stable entry point.

Resource logic is inconsistent with the stated model. Environmental capacity is both `round(3)` SimPy servers and a 0.60 availability factor, then `reviewer_absence` is applied twice. A positive `legal_blackout_weeks` zeroes legal capacity for the whole run, not only those weeks. `beta(4, 4)` makes the expected objection probability half of the named base rate. Peak multipliers can stack on seasonal factors that already encode the 1.7 ratio, which does not match the “2.0 instead of 1.7” stress.

Sections 1–7 otherwise cover entities, queues, cross-case coupling, service/wait separation, D1–D10, combined policies, stresses, warm-up, paired replications, tails, and validation without claiming optimality. Those strengths keep the score well above the middle, but the operator error and executable mismatches are too material for a high mark.
