7.4

The answer covers every required section, stays conditional rather than presuming the change is beneficial, and correctly treats East differently from North and South. The 343 versus 359 handover-count comparison is accurate and useful. The recommendation to pilot with explicit stop-rules matches the evidence. It is still short of a tight forecast because several quantitative and measurement claims are wrong or biased.

The mechanism map understates the Legal/Risk constraint. Referrals rise from 9 to 12 per 100 cases, about a one-third volume increase, on specialists already at 91–93% utilization. That is not a small demand increase: without added capacity it pushes those resources past full load. The same section sums handover counts but never weights them by the stated waits. Doing so with the management figures gives roughly 588 versus 722 median-wait hours per 100 cases, about a 23% increase under the proposal’s own assumptions. Edge-level waits that look modest therefore hide a larger queue burden, and the optimistic scenario never confronts that aggregate.

The three scenarios include the requested dimensions, yet they stay qualitative against baselines that are numeric (cycle times, P90s, rework, SLA misses, utilization). Arbitrary warning thresholds (5–6 hours, 10–15%, 1–2 points) are not tied to those baselines or to sampling variation.

The pilot confounds treatment with team: East is treated and North/South are the main control, which is the comparison least able to separate pooling from East’s existing overload. A small North/South subset is mentioned, but it is not the design that identifies effects. Windows of 8–12 weeks also ignore right-censoring when East’s P90 is already 22.8 days, so late-starting tail cases will not be observed.

The PM4Py sketch calls the requested discovery functions, but several measures would mislead if run. Period is assigned event by event, so in-flight cases contaminate both sides. Grouping or filtering by `team` before discovery cuts traces and drops the shared-service edges the study needs. The per-100 summary is by activity pair, while the decision is about resources; the shared-queue flag matches activity names, would mark ordinary Quality Check as shared, and is never used. Inter-event time is labeled wait even though a single `time:timestamp` cannot separate queue delay from service, and `get_service_time` needs a start timestamp the log does not have. “Rework after handover” is true if any later event is rework, so early handovers inherit downstream rework. Case team and cycle time come from the first remaining event. Broad `except: pass` blocks hide these failures. `filter_event_attribute_values` and `filter_variants` are not actually applied.

Section 4 and the indicator table are the strongest parts: tail risk, learning, accountability, fairness, and exception load are all separated from the mean. Those strengths do not offset the missed capacity arithmetic or the biased before/after measures.
