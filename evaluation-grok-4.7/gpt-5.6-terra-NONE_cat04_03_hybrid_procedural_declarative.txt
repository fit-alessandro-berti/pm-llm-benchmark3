4.5

The write-up handles the R5/R6 conflict clearly and gets the temporal arithmetic right, but the three activity models contradict the chosen policy and the verdict matrix. Under the artifacts as written, the canonical low-risk trace T1 is illegal in the process tree, in DECLARE, and in the log skeleton, while section 8 marks all three layers pass.

**Policy choice versus DECLARE.** Resolution A is conditional: after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor`. The dictionary instead encodes unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`, and section 4 states the consequence accurately: every activation needs a prior legal opinion. That rule rejects T1, T2, T4, T5, and T8. T1 and T2 are still marked DECLARE pass. The mapping’s limitation statement also aims at the wrong defect: the rule does not merely punish an opinion that exists for an unrelated reason; it makes a legal opinion mandatory on the low-risk route, which never escalates. A conditional obligation is not a plain precedence template. It belongs in the external checker (if escalation and activation both occur, the opinion’s timestamp must be earlier), which is the same place R6 already has to live.

**Process tree language versus the matrix.** PM4Py `LOOP(do, redo)` executes `do` at least once. `LOOP(Request Clarification → Receive Clarification, τ)` therefore requires one or more clarification cycles. The prose calls this zero or more. The zero-or-more tree is `LOOP(τ, Request → Receive)`. As coded, T1, T3, T4, T6, T7, and T8 are not in the language, yet all of those except T5 and T8 are marked procedural pass. T2 is the only accepted trace the loop actually allows, and its procedural pass is the one that is earned.

The high-risk branch compounds this. `XOR(τ, Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion)` before `Enhanced Due Diligence` admits only two fragments: no escalation, or escalation immediately followed by the opinion and only then due diligence. T6 has escalation, due diligence, approval, activation, and only then the opinion. T7 has the opinion between due diligence and compliance approval. Neither word is in the tree. Both are marked procedural pass. Section 3 says legal order is left to DECLARE and that the tree only makes escalation optional. The tree in fact welds escalation to an immediate opinion. That approximation is undisclosed, and the verdicts assume it was not made.

**Log skeleton.** PM4Py evaluates `always_before (A, B)` as a necessary predecessor: if `B` occurs, `A` must already have occurred. It evaluates `always_after (A, B)` as: if `A` occurs, `B` must occur before `A`. It treats `directly_follows` as an allow-list. Under those rules the dictionary rejects every supplied trace:

- `(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` together require both approvals before every activation, which also contradicts R8.
- `(Close Onboarding, Reject Vendor)` and `(Close Onboarding, Release First Payment)` together require both rejection and first payment before every closure.
- `directly_follows: set()` allows no successive pair, so every trace of length greater than 1 fails the default checker.

Section 5 defines the weaker reading “whenever both appear, check order,” and the matrix follows that reading. That is a different relation from the one `conformance_log_skeleton` checks. `never_together` and the frequency bounds for R10 are the parts that match the library.

**R2 is not encoded where the mapping says it is.** “Neither approval in a rejected case” is `noncoexistence` of `Reject Vendor` with each approval. The dictionary uses `nonsuccession(Reject Vendor, approval)`, which still allows the approval earlier in the trace. T8 happens to fail for other reasons (`noncoexistence` of reject and activate). A rejected case whose approval precedes rejection is not covered. Section 2 promises an external check for “exactly one approval if accepted, none if rejected.” Section 7 never implements it.

**Resource code.** The main four-eyes comparison is the right layer, and T4’s Cora/Cora pair is a real R9 violation. `if activate_resources:` treats `None` as “do not check.” `all_resources` returns `None` when `Activate Vendor` exists but `org:resource` is missing, so that data-quality failure is dropped, against the rule stated in the same section. `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps cases whose two activities have different resources; the snippet names that result `violating_log` and describes it as the violating traces. The R3 outstanding-request scan and the R11 post-activation scan are the right kind of stateful checks, and the R6 function correctly refuses to certify an emergency when the authorization attributes are absent.

**Temporal profile.** The three pairs, the conversion to seconds, and `zeta = 2.0` are right. The bands are `[0, 96]`, `[0, 24]`, and `[0, 144]` hours because each lower end is `mean − 2·sd = 0`. T1’s 71 hours, T2’s 26 and 84 hours, T6’s 17 hours from escalation to opinion, and T7’s 34 hours are classified correctly at this layer. T7’s payment delay of 2 hours has `|z| ≈ 1.94`, so it stays inside the band. Treating these as warnings is the right reading of R12.

Sections 1, 9, and 10 are the strongest prose: two explicit policies, no silent merge, vacuity, the R10/R11 tension on a second payment, missing emergency attributes, and separate finding messages. They do not compensate for models that reject the backbone they claim to implement. A deployable revision keeps the legal-first choice, deletes unconditional opinion precedence, fixes the loop to `LOOP(τ, cycle)`, moves escalation and legal opinion out of the block-structured tree into an explicit conditional check, and rebuilds `always_before` / `always_after` / `directly_follows` so exclusive approvals and the two terminal endings remain satisfiable.
