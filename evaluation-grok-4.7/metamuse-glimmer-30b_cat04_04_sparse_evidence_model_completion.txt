The write-up has the required sections and a usable evidence discussion, including a correct account of why both DFG directions do not prove concurrency. The three candidates are not reliable competing models, because the prose, the formal expressions, and the code disagree, and several audit and metric claims are false.

The conservative design (an XOR of the seven observed middles) is the right idea, but the tree is not valid PM4Py: the operators must be `X` and `+`, not `xor` and `and`, and the required discussion of duplicate labels and scalability is missing. The balanced POWL puts Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review on every case. That rejects the 7 traces with no supplier event and the 28 with no expedite event, so the stated “~100%” fitness and the claim that the model “allows skip” / “keeps SC optionality explicit” are false. The permissive tree is the same invalid syntax, and because the checks, supplier confirmation, and expedite review are parallel, it admits expedite-before-checks and supplier-before-either-check, which its own boundary calls forbidden. The validation snippet repeats those trees, never calls playout, never checks the positive/negative suite, and wrongly says the permissive language is infinite (there is no loop).

The unobserved-trace audit lists observed V6 and V3 as unobserved permissive traces. Two “excluded” traces are incoherently described, and `RO, CC, Exp, ER, …` is admitted by the permissive code while being reported as excluded. The recommendation then treats the broken balanced net as the compliance model. The data-collection plan and the confidence ranges are the soundest part of the answer, but they do not repair the models.
