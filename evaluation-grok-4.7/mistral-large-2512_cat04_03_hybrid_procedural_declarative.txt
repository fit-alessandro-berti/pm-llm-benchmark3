3.5

The write-up covers every required section and correctly treats R5/R6 as a real policy conflict, R12 as a warning band, and segregation of duties as something activity sequences cannot see. Those strengths are outweighed by formal models that are invalid or unsatisfiable, and by a verdict matrix that does not follow from those models.

The process tree is not a runnable PM4Py object: the imports and constructor do not match the library, a `LOOP` is given one child, silent steps are empty activity labels, and a sequence is appended onto the leaf `Run Sanctions Screening`. The language is also wrong. `Activate Vendor` sits inside each accept branch and again in a suffix that still runs after `Reject Vendor` and `Close Onboarding`, so the tree both duplicates activation and activates rejected cases. Clarification is described as a self-loop on `Classify Risk`, omitted from the compact expression, and coded after classification; T2 and T5 request clarification before classification. Sanctions escalation is optional and, in T6–T7, occurs after classification, but the code tries to force it inside the opening parallel block and never orders `Obtain Legal Opinion` before activation. The chosen “hybrid” therefore is not what the tree implements.

The DECLARE dictionary does not use the required shape (template → activity or pair → `{support, confidence}`). Unary keys are invented strings. `exactly_one` on both `Manager Approval` and `Compliance Approval` cannot be satisfied in any case, and it contradicts `noncoexistence` of those two activities and R2 (one approval on an accepted case, none on a rejected case). `absence` is given a `max` parameter the template value will not honor; standard absence would forbid the activity entirely. Precedence from `Obtain Legal Opinion` to `Activate Vendor` is unconditional, so every normal activation fails it. R5 is conditional on escalation; when activation occurs and the opinion does not, the rule is violated, not vacuous. R3 needs alternate response (a receive before the next request and before a final outcome). Plain response does not encode that. The semantics table covers only a subset of the rules and never separates alternate response from chain response, or noncoexistence from nonsuccession. Confidence `0.9` is then treated as a warning for T6 only, which conformance checking will not do.

The log skeleton is internally impossible for any completed case. `always_after` requires, whenever activation occurs, both approvals, and whenever close occurs, both rejection and first payment, while `never_together` forbids the two approvals and rejection with activation. `directly_follows` has no edges among `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account`, so the concurrent prefix in T1 is illegal; the text admits this and the matrix still marks T1 as pass. Pair orientation is explained as “if A occurs, B is before A” while the pairs are filled as chronological `(earlier, later)`. `activ_freq` pins `Verify Bank Account` to `{1}`, which contradicts R11 and the tree’s second verification. Equivalence being empty is a fair choice.

The temporal profile itself is the right three pairs, in seconds, with `zeta = 2.0`. The bounds are two-sided. T2’s clarification gap is 26 hours; the band is `[0, 96]` hours, so it is inside the band. The write-up calls 26 a breach of 96 and marks a warning. T6’s escalation-to-opinion gap is 17 hours, inside `[0, 24]`, but the matrix says warning under R5 and pass under R6 for the same timestamps. R6’s hard 24-hour activation-to-opinion rule is a different interval and is not a zeta warning. T4 and T5 contain `Activate Vendor` → `Release First Payment` and should be scored, not left “not evaluated.”

The resource code does not match the matrix. Four-eyes is computed on resources pooled across the whole log, so a verifier in one case and an approver in another is a false violation. Missing attributes are not handled except by a generic exception that becomes a failure. The clarification check compares dataframe index values, ignores a lone receive that precedes its request, and does not require the receive before a final outcome. The emergency check auto-passes whenever the opinion is within 24 hours of activation, including when there is no escalation and when the opinion is absent, and it never flags the missing out-of-log authorization. T6 therefore passes this code; the matrix says fail under R5 and warning under R6. T4’s explanation claims DECLARE `noncoexistence` and log-skeleton `never_together` detect Cora’s four-eyes breach. Those layers only see activity names.

The matrix then marks T1–T3 as pass on procedural, DECLARE, and log skeleton. Under the artifacts as written, those three traces fail all three. T4’s control flow matches T1, yet T4 is a procedural fail and T1 a pass; the prose says the tree does not express four-eyes. T5’s prose says the loop allows an exit without a reply, while the cell is a procedural fail. T6’s prose says the tree still fails under the operations reading, while that cell is pass. T7 is a real temporal warning (34 hours against an upper bound of 24) and is otherwise marked pass only by ignoring `exactly_one` and the directly-follows whitelist. T8’s overall fail is justified.

Section 9 names redundancy, the missing emergency flag, the missing risk label, and the danger of reading support and confidence as law. It does not notice that the declarative and skeleton models accept no trace, that precedence vacuity was reversed, or that the suffix undoes the claimed mutual exclusion of reject and activate. Deployment then recommends this tree as the canonical model and a check order whose first three stages would reject the happy path.
