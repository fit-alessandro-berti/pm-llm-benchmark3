The diagnosis is the right one: April is a schema and feature-construction failure, June is a routing-mix change, August–September stack real parts-path deterioration with a concept shift and then Partner Z, and October is mostly censoring. The six-section structure, competing hypotheses, training quarantine, and maturity-gated alerting all track that split. The score is held down by a material cohort error and several places where the metrics are over-read.

The code does not build a case-start cohort. `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_contained")` keeps only traces whose entire lifecycle sits inside the calendar month. That is the short-case slice (remote fixes that open and close in-month), and it drops precisely the long V3 cases this audit turns on. The comment calls that slice “cases whose first event falls in the month” and “the correct denominator for label-maturity accounting.” Both claims are false. Copied as written, every monthly DFG and edge time would repeat the October censoring bias. The event-calendar filter is the right mode and is then unused except for a row count. Exact `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` dedup is a strategy, but it is silent on timestamp jitter and on legitimate same-timestamp repeats. Timezone-aware timestamps against naive bounds are a likely comparison failure. Edge values are dumped raw, so the sketch never actually reports a median `Order Part → Part Received` time.

“Holding path fixed” is too strong for the V3 rates. V3 is a control-flow bucket. Inside it, `Order Part → Part Received` moved from about 40 hours to 130–155 hours, so P(breach | V3) changing does not show that P(breach | same path and same timings) changed. The omitted-variable caveat later in the row only partly repairs the earlier wording. June is the largest shift in the variant distribution (V2 34% → 52%) and is omitted from the covariate-drift months, which start at July–September. That mix shift is also why July’s headline breach rate can stay near 0.19 while V3’s matured rate rises to 0.46: extra V2 volume offsets worse V3 outcomes. “V3 is still a minority” does not explain the offset.

June is treated as near-baseline. ECE 0.060 is more than double January–March, mean predicted risk 0.20 sits above an observed rate of 0.15, and a constant predictor at 0.15 has Brier about 0.128 versus the model’s 0.139. Discrimination held up; calibration did not. The same base-rate check is missing where it changes the story: September’s Brier of 0.214 matches p(1−p) at the observed rate, so skill is about zero, while October’s Brier of 0.205 is far worse than the censored base rate. October also flips the calibration gap (predicted 0.23 versus observed 0.16) after August–September under-prediction. That sign flip is direct evidence of censoring and is not used.

The concept-drift contrast trains the “before” window on January–June, which includes April’s broken prefixes, so a positive result would not separate concept drift from the pipeline incident. August–September’s rising median label delay is partly the parts path taking longer, not the same mechanism as the October outcome-API backlog; the label-delay row treats them as one measurement defect. “Safe” training months correctly drop raw April and immature September–October, but July is pooled with May–June without saying that, until part-wait is a feature, July labels a different conditional outcome for the same V3 control flow.

The containment sequence itself is proportionate: quarantine April, do not fit calibration on April or October, repair lineage and add part-wait, travel, and device age before a global retrain, and keep any segment model to a V3 or Partner Z calibration head. Those decisions match the evidence. The cohort bug and the missed metric triangulations are what keep this below a near-flawless audit.
