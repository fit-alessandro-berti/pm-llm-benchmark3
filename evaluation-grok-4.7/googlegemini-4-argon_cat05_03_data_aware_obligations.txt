7.6

The case outcomes are largely right, but the formalization is not sound enough for a high score. Several rules are mis-scoped, one temporal formula is actually incorrect, and those errors are copied into the checker.

The reconstructions at release are right for all eleven cases, including the hard ones: TX-305 stays unknown rather than inheriting 150,000; TX-308 treats 100,000 as triggering R1 and a decrease as non-invalidating; TX-309 measures 145,000 against 118,000 (+22.88%), not 95,000; TX-306’s +4.55% does not invalidate; TX-311 is DE at release only because `event_index` 4 follows 3; TX-304 is sanctions-only; TX-303 and TX-307 fail R2 for different missing legs; EX-77 is timely and EX-88 is not. COMPLIANT / NONCOMPLIANT / INDETERMINATE are defensible, and TX-310’s NONCOMPLIANT reading is the stronger one given the post-release certificate and the interested witness.

The formal layer does not match that care. The R3 trigger puts an unconstrained `¬∃ e_ss2 ∈ (e_slu, e_rel)` inside a universal over prior screens, so any intervening event of any type kills the trigger, and the trigger already means “violation.” That contradicts both the English gloss and `Sat_R3`. `ValidER` folds `MatAmtInc` into R2, but R4 invalidates a review only for amount-dependent obligations; a >20% increase would wrongly fail an otherwise valid PEP/HIGH approval. The prose also adds “any sequential step >20%,” which the baseline formula and the code do not implement. Section 1’s exemption biconditional omits approver, scope, waiver target, and expiry. R4, R6, and R7 are repeatedly listed as triggered or satisfied obligations when they are validity or interpretation rules; TX-306 and TX-308 “satisfy R4” even though the threshold never fires. TX-302’s cell hedges COMPLIANT and JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. The TX-311 decisive-evidence sentence credits the tie-break for ordering 16:20 before 16:30, which the timestamps already do.

The code meets the requested pipeline: in-memory CSV, `event_index`, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, forward-fill only, release snapshots, prior-event tracking, exemption join, diagnostics, and a trace-order contrast. On this log it would likely match the narrative labels. It still inherits the R2/R4 conflation, sets `er_valid` only by falling through a default, and never checks foreign-to-foreign jurisdiction changes. Sections 6 and 7 are concrete and cover the six required failure modes.
