6.5

The final recommendation is structurally complete and directionally sound, but it is not reliable enough for a higher score. A4, A6, A7, and A12 are a defensible four-module portfolio: the answer respects the module limit, includes an explicit control module, defers activation and contract generation until upstream controls improve, and uses critical-path, rework, staffing, and audit constraints rather than frequency alone.

The concluding quantitative case is flawed. Registration is an 8-minute activity, yet the answer first calls the receive-and-register pair 11 minutes and then calculates “3 min × 12,000 ≈ 600 hours” as their combined labor. It then compares those labor hours with waiting-time case-hours. It also misstates Little’s Law: cycle time and throughput are different, and an external synchronization wait does not by itself bound throughput. The claim that registration frees no meaningful capacity overreads after-15:00 slack in a pool that is otherwise the visible queue.

There is also an unresolved contradiction. The dependency section says financial-statement normalization cannot reach its quality bar without the missing-document module, while the recommended portfolio deploys normalization and defers that module. Waiting-time claims are likewise overstated: the effects section says the portfolio removes most of the 58-hour valuation wait and the 20-hour normalization wait, but the rollout targets only reduce them to 36 and 12 hours. The quick-win assessment says rework barely changes despite that portfolio containing the modules aimed at the missing-document and contract-remediation loops.

Concurrency between KYC and financial-document review, the equipment-finance concentration of collateral cases, and the weekly committee cadence are largely unexamined. Those gaps and the inconsistent final evidence keep the answer well short of nearly flawless.
