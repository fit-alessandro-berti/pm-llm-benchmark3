The case results are substantively right: event-time forward fill, no final-state leakage, `event_index` ordering, the 20% invalidation threshold, and the EX-77 / EX-88 evidence split are all handled correctly. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 are classified on the right state and the right rule. TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT, rather than a proved waiver, follows R5. Sections 6 and 7 are concrete and map to the actual failure modes.

The score is held down by accumulated looseness, not by a wrong case outcome.

The findings table misuses “Satisfied obligations.” TX-304’s pre-update screen, TX-309’s invalidated enhanced review, and TX-305’s amount-blind review are listed as satisfied while the deviations column says they do not count. A scanner of that column gets the wrong control result.

The formalization never pins down “material data change,” which is the predicate R1 turns on. R4’s pseudo-logic says an unknown review amount makes the effect indeterminate, omitting the requirement that a later increase actually occurred. Unknown jurisdiction is indeterminate in the table and absent from the R1 formula. The TX-306 alternative is aimed at a new compliance approval; the live question is whether the sub-20% increase is an R1 material change that demands a new enhanced review.

The checker diverges from those rules. Any later risk or customer-type edit fails R2, not only the edit that made HIGH or PEP true, and the code never tests the standard-packet limit on `Compliance_Approve`. A post-review move from one non-DE jurisdiction to another does not invalidate R1. Unknown amount forces `REVIEW_REQUIRED` even if jurisdiction is DE and no amount-dependent rule can fire. Sanctions matching overloads the case jurisdiction column. `evidence_strength` is a hand label. `format_dataframe` is not tied to `event_index`, so the PM4Py object can drop the same-timestamp order the rest of the answer treats as decisive, and only `ImportError` is caught. The in-code reason control-flow conformance fails is one sentence.

Those are precision and faithfulness gaps on an otherwise strong analysis, not a near-flawless formalization.
