6.5

The required seven sections are present, the headline phase metrics and fitness figures are mostly copied correctly, and the core judgment is sound: hospital prioritization and deferral can be resilience, skipping authorization and unscanned dispatch are concealed noncompliance, and post-close audit work is not real-time control. Those strengths do not offset repeated factual and structural errors.

The process reading is unreliable. T2 is drawn as temporary release **or** unscanned dispatch, but the manual DFG is largely sequential: 1,450 of 1,650 temporary-release cases still go to dispatch without scan. Unscanned dispatch is therefore about 3,250 inflows, not the 1,800 cited. T4’s dominant path is still Receive → Validate authorization (33,500); backlog prioritization (1,500), manual-reservation reconciliation (1,400), and audit remediation (2,300) are side branches, yet they are written as the main variant. T3 has no DFG, and “batched post-pick barcode scans” is invented. Pattern J is said to have cleared the 11,400 T3 queue; that figure is total partial-recovery volume, not the stabilization backlog. Audit remediation is described as closing 2,300 files when 2,100 reach end.

Segment metrics are mislabeled. The 7.8% T2 cancellation rate and the 2.1% baseline rate are overall rates, not pharmacy and hospital rates, yet the fairness indicator is built on that split. T4 “approached baseline” omits pharmacy p90 still at 74h versus 49h and wholesale p90 at 120h versus 72h, with traceability still 2.2% versus 0.8%. Section 7 then treats a pharmacy p90 of about 50h as the disruption concession, which is baseline performance, and Section 5 sets an incident target of ≤48h. Those statements contradict each other and the timeline.

Causal claims go past the evidence: dual-control “lowered to 2.9%,” prevented major safety events, wrong lots and expiries, impossible recall tracing, a 4–6 hour snapshot limit, 2–5% inventory drift, and a 14-day reconciliation duration. The 9.5%, 4.8%, and 2.9% figures are phase aggregates, not effects measured inside patterns F or G. Wholesale p90 of 180h is attributed to deferral, although only about 960 cases enter the backlog queue.

The redesign covers trigger, triage, product controls, paper evidence, backlog order, and reconciliation, but it misses the case’s actual partial-recovery failure: intake can return while inventory sync and scanning stay unreliable. A <200ms latency exit test does not catch that. Recall-sensitive product is in the problem and absent from the control matrix. Spreadsheet invoice hold, a named T2 deviation, never becomes a rule to withhold invoicing until evidence exists. Compliance-staff capacity, the stated cost of manual authorization, is not scheduled. Rework is a required indicator and is omitted. Recommendation priority 2 and 3 swap places between the matrix and the narrative, and reconciliation is 24 hours in the model and 48 hours in the roadmap.

Section 3 also drifts from the more careful table: the hospital fast lane is labeled pure resilience after the table records an authorization bypass, and audit remediation is both “ambiguous” and concealed noncompliance. Temporal-profile findings—the p95 backlog wait, the highly variable phone-POD delay, and the long compliance tail—are barely used.
