4.5

The memo has the right skeleton and the right refusal of a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, but the quantitative core is not reliable enough to support the claims it then tells leadership they may safely make.

Section 1 misstates selection and intensity. Gamma was piloted in July; only Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor cycle time. Alpha’s fast-path share in April is 25%, not 4%. The fraud desk received more borderline cases from automated triage; it did not divert cases away from automation.

Section 5 does not survive arithmetic. Alpha’s January–March median is \((50.4+46.9+49.4)/3=48.9\), not 49.7, so the reported −1.3 hour change is wrong. Beta’s April–August median is \(238.8/5=47.8\), not 47.3. Gamma’s stated April–June average uses January and February (36.4 and 36.5); the real April–June median is about 43.7, and July is 38.4, not 36.4. June p90 for Gamma is 101, not ~82, so “dropped from 82 to 87” is neither the right base nor a drop. Beta’s p90 is about 106 in May–July, not stable at 92–99 or at 10–12. Rework is about 11–14%, not 9–10%. The same swaps of p90, rework, and escalation appear in the mechanism table.

The intensity result is invented. A slope of −0.8 to −1.2 hours per percentage point of fast-path use is incompatible with a ~40-point intensity rise and cycle-time moves of only a few hours, and the reported \(p=0.2\) is not computed. The sign language for FTE is also internally inconsistent. Pre/post windows, later “clarifications,” and the broken Beta row contradict one another.

The central contrast is missed. Never-treated medians and p90s worsen sharply after the May complexity shift. Alpha’s own median is nearly flat and its p90 is worse in absolute terms, but both look better relative to Delta and Epsilon. Beta’s median does not fall below its pre-period except marginally in August. Alpha’s p90 stays above its pre-period average through August (June is about +20 hours, not a 10–15 hour cap). Section 7’s “safe” 3–5 hour reduction for Alpha, Beta, and Gamma, and the claim that tails return to or below baseline by month two, are therefore false. March’s common +2 point escalation jump, before any adoption, is repeatedly treated as an automation-period change from a 7.8% March base.

Identification is weaker than the write-up admits. Building a fraud-desk covariate from `escalation_pct` is a bad control. Parallel trends are described as a property of covariates and then declared to “hold” without a real control contrast. Section 2 says nothing is identifiable; sections 7 and 10 still issue conditionally causal hour estimates. The one-month pre-period in the Delta pilot cannot support the event study the memo itself recommends, and several decision-rule inequalities are missing.

The code would not run (`pd.compat.StringIO`, `str > None`, `Period.astype`). Relative time collapses to −1/0/1, the “panel” model is Alpha-only, clustered errors are never estimated, and not all five outcomes are shown. The threat list and the decision not to expand blindly are the parts that hold up.
