4.3

The design is only a partial outline of the right problem. It would still produce misleading default estimates if implemented, because the primary estimand, the risk set, censoring, and the code do not describe the same analysis.

The primary target confuses three different objects: a binary 90-day default label observed only after activation, a survival time whose origin is Account Activated, and a competing-risk process that starts at Application Started. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open cases never enter a post-activation default clock, so they are not competing events or ordinary right-censored default times. Conditioning the training population on eventual activation also uses information unavailable at an early prefix. The stated estimand, “default within 90 days given the prefix,” does not match a population defined by a future activation. Prediction time is simultaneously “event 3” and “day 7,” while the clock is said to start only at activation. The warehouse label is `default_90d`, not a demonstrated event time, and the 7–35 day feed lag is not part of the target definition. The alternative multi-state target is the analysis the prose actually describes, so the two formulations are not separated.

The cohort counts sum to 48,000, but the statuses are wrong for the stated target. “Exclusion: None” contradicts an activated-only population. Rejected cases are written as a competing outcome “(no default),” which is the convenience coding the study was required to avoid. The 3,600 immature activations mix an unfinished 90-day window, a delayed feed, and possible permanent missingness. The 2,900 transfers are one “partially observed” row, with no split of the 60% linkage, the 40% unlinked, or possible double-counting when a linked case returns under a new identifier. Still-open cases are called right-censored without a time origin or a terminal-state rule. There is no as-of maturity rule tying activation time, the 90-day horizon, feed delay, and the 15 February 2026 extract.

Censoring mechanisms are collapsed. Permanent partner missingness is treated as independent right censoring. Delayed labels are not given a maturity buffer. Selection into activation has no strategy beyond redefining the population. The sensitivity analysis imputes every censored case as default or non-default, including cases whose default outcome is undefined. Testable, partially testable, and untestable assumptions are reduced to two bullets. Independent censoring, linkage missingness, partner-feed MNAR, and stability across the July 2025 policy change are not classified.

Prefix rules are not operational. Fixed-length truncation contradicts the claim that document loops are preserved. Padding short cases with `NA` invents history. Migration duplicates are ignored. Cases already rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or activated before the prediction point remain eligible, so the prefix can contain the outcome it is supposed to anticipate.

The feature set omits the calendar and policy-cohort features required by the known 1 July 2025 and 15 October 2025 breaks. `discover_dfg` is proposed as if a global graph were a case-level prefix feature. Rework is attached to the wrong loop. Queue features and an as-of construction for historical experience and historical risk are only named.

The leakage table correctly drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and future events, and it repairs `documents_total` and resource experience in the right direction. `final_limit` is mis-timed to Contract Signed rather than the final credit decision. `label_available_at` is marked Keep. `default_90d` on event rows is absent from the audit. Nothing in the pipeline actually removes those columns from the feature table.

Validation uses a case-start split, which is the right family, but the migration boundary is wrong: the test fold begins 1 October 2025, while the migration is 15 October, so pre-migration cases are labeled post-migration. Train, validation, and test also confound policy shift, migration, seasonality, and label immaturity. Late-2025 activations cannot have a matured 90-day label plus feed delay by 15 February 2026, yet there is no walk-forward gap or label-availability cutoff. The C-index is undefined without event times and a censoring model. Macro-F1 requires a final state for open and censored cases. Pre/post-policy subgroup checks cannot be done inside an all-post-policy test fold.

The decision boundary correctly says the fitted association is not a counterfactual default risk for rejected applications. Two of the three “deployment” effects are external drift and existing feed latency. Missing are score-driven changes to who is activated, analyst gaming of prefix features, and earlier collections changing the default outcome. Those are the ways deployment would move the selection mechanism the model is conditioned on.

The skeleton does not implement the design. `unique()` arrays do not support `&` or `-` as set operations, so the cohort print fails. Open cases are defined as events after the extract date, which the observation window does not contain. Activated cases with a missing `default_90d` are counted as non-defaults. Prefixes keep label and post-outcome columns. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied without lifecycle timestamps. The closing comment excludes non-activated cases from survival modeling after the text included them as competing risks, and it points multi-state prediction at a heuristics miner. The code is also short of a complete as-of case table for start, activation, terminal state, censoring, and label maturity.
