The answer meets the required structure and is operationally serious: four distinct stress types, migration paths tied to the logs, guarded use of the candidate interventions, a trade-off table with every required column, a redesign-caused failure mode, and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard. It does not collapse into “add capacity” or into average cycle time. The score stays well below excellent because several process-mining figures are wrong, the control logic conflicts with itself, and one case class is designed out of the system.

Process-mining arithmetic is systematically biased. Batch-wait “share” is batch-wait events divided by the Pre-analytical → Test preparation arc, so the claimed ~4.5% → ~13.8% should be about 4.4% → 12.7% of preparation exits (760/17,310 and 1,180/9,320). Assay → Repeat “~6.9% → ~15.5%” uses Test preparation → Assay run as the denominator (1,140/16,550 and 1,260/8,140). Of assay exits the rates are about 6.7% and 14.8% (1,140/17,030 and 1,260/8,500). Peak Technical → Medical “~47.2%” and Technical → Hold “~8.4%” divide by Assay → Technical arrivals (7,240). Peak technical decisions are 8,080, so those shares are about 42.3% and 7.6%. Normal happens to balance, which hides the inconsistent base. Recollection ~4.2% → ~8.0% is the one share computed on a balanced activity. These errors do not reverse the story, but they are repeated in the narrative, the table, and the red bands, so the dashboard is calibrated to the wrong baselines.

Constraint choice is incomplete. Peak utilization ranks technical validation highest at 101%, then assay at 98%, pre-analytical at 96%, and medical validation at 93%, while median queues rank specialist, medical (22h), assay (18h), then technical (16h). R3 names assay and medical as the drums and caps WIP only in front of those two. A cap in front of medical parks flow on the pool already over 100% utilization. At 101%, the technical queue is not a stable median; backlog should be growing through the three-week peak. That instability is never used. The 4-week cross-training lead time is also unused, so R5 is treated as available inside a wave that is shorter than the training time.

Repeat-after-failure is folded into the Routine lane even though it is 8–9% of volume, consumes assay capacity twice, clusters on M2 and night shifts, and has the worst peak tails (median 145h, p90 240h). Some of that class also enters medical validation (Repeat → Medical 80 normal, 120 peak). A three-stream rule that guarantees Urgent, protects Complex, and gives Routine/Repeat only a residual rate will starve the class that most damages the drum.

Scenario policies do not stay scenario-specific under the supplied peak. Medical queue 22h, specialist queue 36h, urgent p90 33h, courier queue 7.5h, and a mid-teens repeat rate trip P1, P2, P3, P4, and P5 together. Precedence is undefined. P2 tells the lab to keep Routine off a failing M2 and, in the same playbook, to enlarge Routine batches while Urgent/Complex occupy the reliable machine. If the machine that is down is M1, those instructions cannot be executed together. P5 is armed by lagging p50/p90, which the dashboard itself says not to steer by. P3’s amber specialist band starts at 18h; the dashboard’s amber band starts at 12h. Tightening Hold-for-specialist criteria has no clinical-release risk attached, which matters in a validation lab. Case-mix share is a P3 trigger and the defining feature of S1, yet it is absent from the daily leading set. WIP caps, the main throttle, have no numeric or even qualitative limit.

Residual risk and the I1/I2/I3/I4/I8/I10 conflict handling are the strongest parts. I9’s routine release hold is simply left out rather than rejected, despite peak routine p90 already at 120h against a 72h target. Courier smoothing, early reject, and conditional flexing are directionally sound; their failure modes are named. The package is usable as a draft operating concept and unreliable as a calibrated control design until the shares, the technical-validation drum, the repeat stream, and the simultaneous-trigger rules are fixed.
