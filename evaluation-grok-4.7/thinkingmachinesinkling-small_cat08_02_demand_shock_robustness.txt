**6.5**

The required seven sections are present, and the package does real bottleneck-migration work: it rejects blanket capacity, strict urgent-first dispatch, larger routine batches, and holding routine releases, and it names a redesign-caused failure (faster clean flow exposing validation and specialist review). Triggers, switch-off rules, a next-bottleneck column, and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard are all there.

The process-mining evidence is mishandled in ways that change the conclusions. Normal frequencies cover 8 weeks and 18,400 cases; peak frequencies cover 3 weeks and 9,900 cases. The answer treats raw counts as comparable volumes. Medical validation to release is described as “only 4,170 versus 3,990 despite higher input because release is clogged.” Absolute inflow is not higher (about 4,240 versus 4,100), and the peak weekly rate is much higher. The same unnormalized reading makes assay repeats (1,140 versus 1,260) and recollection (760 versus 780) look almost flat, when the peak rates roughly double or triple. Open cases are also ignored: peak ends about 8,580 of 9,900 starts, so lower completion counts are partly unfinished work-in-process.

Resource logic slips. An evening medical-validation shift is treated as relieving Hold for specialist review, which is a different pool (one specialist, absent every second Friday). Batch-wait time is given as 6 h to 22 h; those figures are different edges (preparation to batch wait is 6 h / 14 h; batch wait to assay is 9 h / 22 h). The “median / p90” line only restates normal-to-peak medians.

The control rules conflict with the peak they are meant to cover. Urgent cases are capped at 30% of assay slots while the peak mix is already 29% urgent and 20% complex, and repeats consume a second assay pass; no slot budget shows this fits. The flex pool fires only when correction wait exceeds 10 h and technical-validation wait is under 8 h, but the observed peak has both queues high (14 h and 16 h), so the pool is off in the joint overload it is scored as covering. Clerk overtime error after 9 h shifts is never guarded in the intake surge policy.

The scenario set, trade-off table, and monitoring bands are usable, but the frequency comparisons and the infeasible peak rules keep the redesign from being operationally reliable.
