6.7

The forecast is complete and often sharp: handover count versus topology, East versus North/South, utilization versus throughput, and a conditional “pilot first” recommendation are the right core claims. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative claims are unreadable or inconsistent with the given baseline, the pilot does not test the mechanism it relies on, and the PM4Py sketch would mis-measure the comparisons it is meant to support.

The mechanism map’s best point is real. Management’s own table replaces the local loop (36 + 31 per 100 cases) with queue handoffs (41 + 36), so nominal handover reduction is a topology story, and the event count can rise. That point is then undercut in the same table, where A1’s “expected direction” is still “nominal handover reduction.” Before/after figures are systematically jammed (`3.24.0h`, `8883`, `63`, `9.610.5h`, `912`, `3943`, `8.28.5%`). As written, “cross-team handovers fall 63 per 100” is not a readable restatement of 6 to 3.

The optimistic scenario contradicts the baseline and the waits it cites. Legal and Risk are already at 91% and 93%, and this stream is estimated to rise from 9 to 12 per 100 cases. “No queue exceeds 85% utilization” cannot sit beside that increase unless capacity is added, which this scenario does not add. If those referrals are most of Legal/Risk load, offered utilization scales by about 12/9 and lands near 1.21–1.24, which is an unstable queue, not a modest move from 9.6h to 10.5h. The central scenario’s 14–18h Legal/Risk wait is therefore also too mild under that load assumption; the answer never states the assumption or the arithmetic. “High-complexity cases receive specialist attention faster” conflicts with the same scenario’s 3.2h to 4.0h technical wait and 9.6h to 10.5h legal/risk wait. Optimistic “early warning signs” are success conditions, while the other two scenarios use actual warnings.

Leading indicators will false-alarm on day one. “Utilization >90% for TSS, Legal, and Risk” is already true for Legal and Risk. A Legal/Risk p90 wait above 12h is not anchored to a baseline whose median is already 9.6h, so the p90 is likely already past 12h. Cross-team handovers are treated as a one-way risk (a rise means failure), while the resilience discussion also treats the planned fall from 6 to 3 as loss of cover. The fairness trigger “East–North gap widens” has no direction: East is already slower (8.6d versus 6.8d), so East improving narrows the gap, and a widening gap can mean opposite operational outcomes.

Section 4 covers the required hidden risks, including tail latency, complexity, local knowledge, fairness, learning, accountability, and absence. It states the cross-team reduction from 6 to 3 as if it occurs, rather than as management’s estimate.

The pilot is phased and has a control, a stabilization window, segmentation, staff feedback, and rollback language, but Phase 1 does not test the shared service. Moving only E_Tech and routing only East high-complexity cases creates no pool with N_Tech (71%) or S_Tech (68%). East’s hypothesized gain is relief of an 82% specialist by that pool. Phase 1 instead adds queue friction without extra capacity, and failure blocks Phase 2, where East and South would finally share capacity. Pass criteria are missing comparison operators (“TSS utilization 85% sustained,” “East CSAT baseline 0.2,” “cross-team handover baseline”), and the band between pass and rollback is undefined (85% versus >90% utilization, +0.5 versus +1.0 reassignments). Customer segment and specialty are segmentation dimensions, then absent from pass/fail rules. “Gap widens” repeats the ambiguous fairness metric.

The recommendation correctly refuses a full rollout, refuses to read +15% utilization as productivity, and lists modifications that match the capacity and learning risks: keep difficult quality check off Legal/Risk, keep local assessment for simpler cases, preserve local approval for sensitive cases, add priority queueing, name an owner, and fix case notes. It does not say plainly that a passing modified pilot would not validate the original design, including difficult quality check on the same scarce queue and removal of a local supervisor.

The analysis sketch calls the right families of functions, then implements several of them so they return the wrong comparison. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key` argument. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting results are SNA objects whose weights live on `.connections`, not `.edges` or a dict, so `handover_edges` returns an empty map and centralization falls through to `None`. `format_dataframe` returns a dataframe, but waiting-time and duration code iterates it as an event log. Trace case ids live on `concept:name`, not `case:concept:name`, so durations will not join back to cases. An empty segment filter returns the unfiltered log. Inter-event gaps are labeled waiting time even though they include the previous activity. `discover_performance_dfg` returns `(dfg, start, end)` and `view_performance_dfg` needs those three arguments separately. Hard-coded `sla_days=10` contradicts the published miss rates: North misses SLA on 6.5% of cases while its 90th percentile is 16.4 days, so the SLA threshold is beyond 16.4 days, and East’s 9.8% miss rate with a 22.8-day 90th percentile puts it near the low-20s, not at 10 days.
