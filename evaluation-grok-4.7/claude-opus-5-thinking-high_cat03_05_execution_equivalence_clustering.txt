6.2

The write-up is complete, formally ambitious, and often sharp on the real object-centric distinctions (externality versus identity, X5 versus X6, qualifier-only divergence in X7, freight-count confusion of X4 with X8, flattening failure modes, `max_objs` connectivity). It is not internally reproducible, and several claims contradict the author’s own definitions.

The preferred relation is defined as equality of α, and α includes `packaging_mode`. Under that definition, and under `semantic_signature`, X1/X2 (`single`) are not equivalent to X3 (`multi_consolidated`). The assignment table nevertheless puts all three in S1, while printing two different “defining signatures.” Section 6 then asserts that regrouping by `semantic_signature` merges X1 with X3. The code does the opposite. Someone implementing the published signature will not recover the published clusters. The S1.a/S1.b aside admits the split but does not repair the definition, the code, or the later claim that ≈B itself merges X1–X3.

That break spreads. Section 4 says collapsing M41 and M42 into one payment moves X4 into S1. α still has two deliveries, two packages, and `multi_split`, so X4 stays out of S1. Section 8 says only typed structural equivalence separates X1 from X6 and X7. ≈B separates both (shared finance; substitution). Section 7 says that under label-poset equivalence X3 and X4 “stop being distinguished.” They have different event counts (one Dispatch and one Payment versus two and two); a Hasse isomorphism cannot identify them. The same section says label-collapse drops X4 into S1, then summarizes that S2 survives at least four perturbations. Those statements cannot both be true, and the collapse argument also mixes activity clusters with semantic cluster names. S2’s object counts are untouched by label collapsing.

≈L is mis-located. It does not sit strictly between activity-sequence equivalence and typed isomorphism: equal per-object lifecycles do not fix the global interleaving, and equal label sequences do not fix lifecycles. As defined — EXT objects kept as their own types, lifecycles taken from incidence — ≈L separates X5 and X6 from X1 (extra external lifecycles) and separates X7 from X1 (I72 is on Dispatch; I11/I12 are not). Section 5 says ≈L merges X1, X5, X6, and X7.

Prose and code disagree on the signature that is supposed to make clustering reproducible. `episodes` is defined as maximal Pack⁺→Dispatch cycles and implemented as `len(deliveries)`, duplicating `deliveries`. `packaging_mode` is defined by whether one dispatch covers every package and implemented as “some delivery carries more than one package.” Ignoring O2O qualifiers blanks `carries`, so that implementation reclassifies X3 from consolidated to split; the robustness discussion never says so. `composition_change` matches bare qualifier strings, so X8’s package-level `replaces` is distinguished from X7’s item-level `replaces` only because `repacks` is checked first.

The canonizer is not the exact individualisation-refinement procedure it claims to be. It greedily commits to one cell-minimum and does not search the remaining tree, so renaming invariance is not guaranteed once more than one symmetry has to be broken. Colour refinement itself is a reasonable core, and it is deterministic.

The PM4Py outline is directionally right to re-key library clusters and to treat raw identifiers and unbounded connected executions as hazards. The control flow is not executable against the usual contract (one OCEL value per equivalence class, not a list of per-order executions already split out). “Ordered activities plus type counts” is only a rough account of the key, not an exact one.

Borderline commentary on X1/X2, X1/X5, X1/X6, X3/X4, and X4/X8 is the strongest part of the answer, and the purpose-specific recommendations are sensible once the false “only ≈T” claim is removed. The grade is held down because the preferred clustering, the canonical signature, and several robustness and refinement claims do not describe the same partition.
