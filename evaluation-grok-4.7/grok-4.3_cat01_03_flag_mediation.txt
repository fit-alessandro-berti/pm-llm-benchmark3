The required skeleton is present, and the broad story is right: flagged cases are triaged faster, then spend much longer in review and escalation, the flag is a policy bundle, and natural cross-world effects are a poor fit. The execution is not reliable enough to carry that story.

**Estimands.** Several contrasts are mislabeled. P2 keeps priority triage, so it is not a direct effect on triage completion. P0 versus P2 on cycle time is a total policy effect, not an interventional indirect effect through review occurrence. P3’s effect on manual wait, and P4’s effect on escalation wait, are effects on those times, not effects mediated by them. E5 conditions on escalation while P4 changes who is escalated, then still marks the estimand well-defined. E1 and E6 duplicate the same total effect. E8 is not a separable effect. “Controlled,” “interventional,” and “separable” are treated as one framework. P1 is never specified as display suppression versus disabling the automated flag bit, which is the distinction the problem requires.

**Graph.** The edge list omits paths from unobserved risk and complexity into review, escalation, service time, and the decision. Queue wait is drawn as causing service duration. `underlying_risk` is unobserved but not marked as such. The time-ordered diagram is one chain and drops the flag, score, complexity, and load. Interference is named, then most case-level estimands are still called well-defined.

**Timestamps.** Counts and stage times do not match the log. There are 10 flagged and 10 unflagged cases, not 11 and 9. Six of 10 flagged cases escalate, not 7 of 11. Two unflagged cases escalate (R09, R20), so escalation for flag 0 is not structurally absent. Rejects are R03, R09, R13, and R20, so “reject only among escalated flagged cases” is false. Reported components are round numbers that add exactly to the reported cycle time, including rows where some stages do not occur; they are neither means nor medians. Flagged high-complexity mean cycle time is about 1344 minutes, not 1143; unflagged high-complexity mean cycle time is about 924, not 564. That last error hides the main competing pattern: unflagged high-complexity cases are also very long. Required slices are missing: flagged normal load, flagged cases without escalation, and low/medium complexity. The follow-up is about 50 words, below the required 100–180.

**Pathways and identification.** P2 is assigned to the priority-triage pathway even though P2 keeps that pathway. T1/T2 versus T3/T4 is perfectly collinear with the flag, which is much stronger than “labels differ.” The proposed threshold window 0.65–0.75 lies entirely on the flagged side of the actual cut between 0.61 and 0.64, so it cannot identify a discontinuity. Flag is effectively a deterministic function of the score, so a within-score contrast has no overlap. All arrivals are on one day, so day clusters and between-day contrasts are undefined. The mediation row assumes away exposure-induced confounding for an interventional estimand, which reverses the usual reason for using that estimand. The negative control is not tied to any event this process records.

**Policy and pilot.** P1 is given “strong” evidence of no effect. Nothing in the log separates automated routing from human response to a displayed flag. The −15–25% and similar cycle-time figures are unsupported; review and escalation waits are a much larger share of flagged cycle time. The pilot conflates encouragement, flag suppression, and removal of mandatory review. Its safety stop uses the raw reject rate, although a higher reject rate can be better or worse decision quality. It does not measure false approvals, queue length, or outcomes for cases left in the shared queues.

**Code and close.** The Python block is a non-runnable stub with `flag,...` instead of the CSV. It does not parse times, build an event log, call `format_dataframe`, discover either DFG, or calculate stage times. The comment claims those steps were implemented. The synthesis is about 100 words, below the required 180–260, and calls shared-queue occupancy directly observed when only timestamps are observed.

What keeps this above a failing mark is coverage: the right policy menu, a real attempt to avoid natural effects, the six reversal themes, and a pilot aimed at separating priority triage from mandatory review. What pulls it down is that the timestamp decomposition is invented, central empirical claims are false, the estimands do not match their labels, and the required program is absent.
