6.5

The pipeline is complete and the in-memory tables are mostly referentially sound, but the write-up contradicts its own model and several predicted discovery results are factually wrong.

The data layer covers all 17 events, every stated object, timezone-aware timestamps, payment amounts on events, and the required E2O/O2O columns. Shared facts for reserve (I1, I3, O100, O200) and pack-P1 (P1, I1, I3, O100, O200) are encoded, and the validation checks that are present would pass. That is the strongest part of the answer.

The modeling story does not match that code. Section 1 says logistics and financial events are not propagated to orders and calls the policy source-minimal. The relations still attach Reserve Stock, Pack Package, and Issue Invoice to orders. Qualifiers are also uneven: both the package and the items receive `packs`, while dispatch treats the package as a weak `relates_to` and the delivery as `dispatches`. O2O uses `contains` for both order membership and physical packing, so the audit’s “contains: 6” erases a distinction the scenario asks to keep. `completes_settlement` on M2 asserts that 150 finishes V1; the narrative only says both payments are partial and no invoice total is given.

Validation is short of the spec: activity agreement is checked, timestamp agreement is not. The constructor fallback is appropriately isolated.

Section 4 does not follow the relations just built. O200 is tied to six events (create, add, reserve, pack P1, invoice, close), not about five. Pack Package (E7) is copied into both order traces and is omitted from the “copied shared events” list, even though Section 3 correctly calls E7 a convergence point. Dispatch Package is also absent from the order flattening and is not listed among the omitted lifecycle steps. The single DFG chain drops Add Item → Add Item and Pack Package → Pack Package. The claim that duplicated Issue Invoice events double revenue does not follow: amounts sit on Record Payment, and those events are not linked to orders.

Object-centric expectations overreach. OC-DFG edge colors are invented, and there is no cross-type “handoff” edge from orders to the invoice flow. An object-type graph taken from these O2O rows has no Package–Order edge; P1 and V1 also share no event, so “high coupling among O100, O200, P1, and V1” is false. The Petri-net paragraph states one binding as fact, including a “Pack Package transition for P1.” Pack Package is one activity with two different bindings (E7: both orders; E8: O100 only). That violates the requirement not to treat the object-centric net as uniquely determined. Payment flattening is the analysis that is actually precise: two length-1 traces and no DFG edges.

Alternative 3 turns O2O links into synthetic activities. Propagating O2O into extra E2O rows would change discovery without adding events; the written alternative changes the event story. Semantic check 4 also reverses direction (“Items to Orders,” “Packages to Deliveries”) relative to the container-to-constituent table.

Failure-mode coverage hits the required eight topics, but several stay generic, and the revenue and API claims inherit the same looseness.
