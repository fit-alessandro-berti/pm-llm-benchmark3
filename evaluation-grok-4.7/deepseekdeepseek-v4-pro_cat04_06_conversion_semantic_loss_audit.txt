5.5

The document covers all nine required sections and largely understands the source: registration first, unordered assess/prepare, optional legal review, at least one configure-then-inspect iteration, redo between iterations, overlap between legal review and verification, the release join, and archive last. The primary pipeline is the right one, the expected process-tree failure is well motivated, and the rejection and canonical-storage guidance is usable.

It still misses the assignment’s main standard, which is to separate what is stated, what is only expected, and what was actually validated. The audit marks POWL→Petri and the BPMN round trip as “validated preserved,” and the later prose reports identical trace sets and “manually confirmed” witnesses. None of that was executed. Bare “preserved” is also used for markings, outside the required assessment vocabulary. Petri→BPMN is called approximated while the round trip of that same model is called validated, which is inconsistent.

The invariant table mishandles witnesses. The skip trace and the legal-after-verification trace are allowed behaviors, but both are placed in the negative column. There is no explicit invariant, with a violating witness, that verification cannot start until both Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence have completed, and no negative witness for zero loop iterations. Label uniqueness is also confused with how often a label may occur in a trace.

The validation code is not reliable. `pm4py.execute_transition` is not part of the given API, so the explorer would not run. `check_trace` is only `pass`, so the positive and negative witnesses are never checked. Playout is omitted. The loop bound only counts a `Correct Defect` label, and the write-up treats a bounded search as if soundness made the languages identical beyond that bound. Soundness does not establish language equivalence.

The residual claim that the POWL itself cannot distinguish a taken skip from a branch that never started is wrong: the XOR must complete, and only the tau-hidden projection loses that distinction.
