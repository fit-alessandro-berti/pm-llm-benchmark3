The final write-up is complete and often insightful, but it is not internally consistent on the relation it asks the reader to use, and several concrete claims are false under its own data and definitions.

The activity-sequence partition is right: X1, X2, X5, X6, and X7 share one label sequence; X3, X4, and X8 each stand alone. X1 versus X2 is handled correctly as renaming. The borderline discussions of X1–X5, X1–X6, and X1–X7 correctly show that labels hide external sharing and substitution. Purpose-specific recommendations in the last section are sensible.

The preferred relation does not hold together. Section 1.3 defines business-semantic equivalence as sharing the typed-structural signature plus external flags, qualifiers, and pack/dispatch shape. That relation can only refine the typed partition. The table and later sections then merge X3 with X4, X5 with X6, and X7 with X8. Section 8 also calls typed equivalence finer than the preferred relation. Those cannot all be true. The same section says pack/dispatch interleavings are equivalent, while component (a) of the definition preserves total order, and Section 7 treats partial order as a later perturbation.

The canonical signature therefore does not implement the preferred clustering. The code is an event-level type/external/qualifier tuple. It assigns different signatures to executions that the preferred column places in one cluster, so it is not reproducible for that relation. It also never encodes the O2O facts that actually carry `partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`, `replaces`, and `removed_before_dispatch`; it reads a nonexistent `event.qualifiers` field. The lead-object filter (`startswith("O")` or `id == "Order"`) is accidental rather than correct.

Cluster interpretations inherit that looseness. The logistics-split “signature” is a disjunction (one or two deliveries, one or two payments), not a signature. The claim that label-only flattening absorbs X8 into the split-logistics cluster is false: `Return Item` and `Inspect Return` make X8’s activity sequence unique. Logistics-only equivalence, as defined, also cannot merge X4 and X8, because those return events touch Order, Item, and Delivery and would survive the stated filter.

Robustness has a hard error. Ignoring O2O qualifiers does not put X7 with X1. `Dispatch` on X7 still carries `Item:I72`, so the execution’s own E2O signature still differs. The partial-order perturbation likewise suggests X3 and X4 might merge once payment multiplicity is dropped, but one versus two deliveries remains. X4’s payments are also misstated as two partial settlements; M42 completes settlement.

The PM4Py outline misses the required lead type: `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is called without `Order`. The post-processing puts the raw event id into the supposed canonical key, so renamed but equivalent orders still do not match. Exact lifecycle equivalence is also described as answering whether two orders used the same resource at the same time, which is shared identity, not equivalence. `max_objs` is treated only as a speed knob, not as an incomplete, order-dependent cut.
