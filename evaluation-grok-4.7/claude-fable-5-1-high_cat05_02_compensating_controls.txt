The review is substantively strong: it separates nominal four-eyes form from the control objective, refuses to treat a clean outcome or a logged activity as sufficiency, and lands the difficult cases in the right neighborhood (AP-203 met under ED-1, AP-205 stopped before completion, AP-204 failed despite two user IDs, AP-208 failed despite a legitimate outcome, AP-209 left indeterminate). The seven required sections are present, the expected-loss critique is real, and the hybrid reading of a post-release, pre-settlement manager action is explicit. The score is held down by contradictions inside that framework and by several concrete inaccuracies.

The sufficiency rule in §2 says an independent human who acts before irrevocability without evidence of a substantive review is merely mitigating, and that the objective is then not met. AP-201 has only the bare word “reviewed,” and AP-206 has “approved,” with no record of invoice, bank details, or receipt. Both are nevertheless classified OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET. AP-207 is locked as OBJECTIVE_MET while the same write-up says the rating holds only if a pre-settlement stop actually existed, and then lists that stop as missing evidence. The decision rule and the case table do not implement the same test.

The recall clock moves. AP-201’s audit is treated as outside a 24-hour window measured from release (about 24.9 hours; settlement was 10:00, so the same audit is about 23 hours after settlement). AP-208’s ratification is called “at the edge” because it is exactly 24 hours after settlement, not after release. §3 also calls the AP-208 audit four business days / about 115 hours later, while §4 calls it “+5 days.” AP-201’s corrective-capacity cell says the pre-settlement window was real, while the missing-evidence cell says it is unknown whether m04 could halt settlement. The column key says severity is about deficiency and exposure, not realised loss, but AP-202 is Critical and the larger, less controlled AP-208 exposure is only High.

Smaller factual slips accumulate. Eight months is about 240 days against a 180-day ED-1 floor; calling condition 3 “marginal” is wrong. §1 credits AP-201 with a partial receipt review the facts do not state. §4’s parenthetical that AP-201’s missed audit is “irrelevant because settlement preceded” does not explain anything. §5’s claim that AP-201’s convenience path “made AP-202 possible” overreaches. O4 says ED-1 removes a human and therefore fails collusion, while O2 correctly notes that condition 6 still inserts a manager.

The code meets the structural brief: inline CSV, UTC parsing, `format_dataframe`, `filter_four_eyes_principle`, a separate blocked-attempt path, joined facts, the requested flags, and an evidence-not-verdict label. The version discussion of `keep_violations` is the right hazard, and the pandas cross-check is the right mitigation. It still invents a 5-year tenure for AP-205’s “established” vendor instead of leaving it unknown, and it claims to cope with either PM4Py behaviour while not catching a `parameters`-dict signature or a non-DataFrame return. Those calls would crash before the complement check runs. The joined facts also omit shared-workstation and the 37-payment pair count, so the repeated-pair flag only sees the two in-log cases.

The judgments a reviewer would most want preserved are preserved. The write-up is not nearly flawless, because the classification rule, the recall anchor, the severity principle, and the stop-mechanism standard are each applied differently in different sections.
