5.5

The construction is broad and often careful, but it is not executable as written, and its central Order analysis contradicts its own relations.

The O2O validation rejects the intended data. `duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"])` fails because both M1 and M2 use `partially_settles` toward V1. That assertion runs before `OCEL(...)`, so the pipeline stops before any discovery. `"P1" if False else "P2"` happens to yield P2, but it is dead, misleading code.

Provenance is implemented incorrectly. `e2o(..., basis="derived_order_membership")` stamps every row of E07–E10 and E13–E14, including the explicit Package, Item, and Delivery links. Section 1 and the audit say only the extra Order links are derived. Filtering to `explicit_in_description` would drop the real pack, dispatch, and confirm facts. The advertised source-versus-analysis split does not hold.

The Order flattening in Section 4 does not follow the code. O100 is tied to E08, E10, and E14, so its trace has 12 events: two packs, two dispatches, and two confirmations, and no payments. The written trace has about eleven steps, inserts both `Record Payment` events, and omits P2/D2. The same subsection then says E12 and E15 appear in neither Order projection, and that O100 omits P2 and D2. Those claims cannot all be true. The predicted Order DFG and log skeleton inherit this error: a payment self-loop that the relations do not create, and missing `Pack Package`, `Dispatch Package`, and `Confirm Delivery` self-loops that they do. Section 6 repeats the omission by limiting this policy’s Order effect to E06, E07, E09, and E13.

Other factual slips are smaller but clear. Check 6 says E12 and E15 relate payments to V1 through `partially_settles`; that qualifier is O2O, while the E2O qualifier is `payment_against_invoice`. V1 has three events, not “approximately four.” D2 runs from 10:15 to 09:00 the next day, which is 22 hours 45 minutes, not 23 hours 45 minutes. The ET–OT discussion claims 17 activity names; there are 17 events and 9 activities. The OTG is described both as a type graph and as a hub among the instances V1, O100, O200, M1, and M2. “At most one object of each type per role” is false for E06, E07, E09, E11, and E13. Calling O100 and O200 distinct Petri-net colors confuses tokens with types, and the OC-DFG is overstated as retaining instance identities such as P1 and M1.

The object inventory, event ledger, amounts, O2O directions, refusal to invent `completes_settlement`, I2’s absence from reservation, Package and Delivery control-flow shapes, Payment-flattening weakness, alternatives, and failure-mode list are largely sound. Those parts do not repair a validation crash or an Order projection that disagrees with the relations it is interpreting.
