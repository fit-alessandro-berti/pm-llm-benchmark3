6.5

The write-up meets the required outline and is often careful about counterfactuals, observed versus inferred links, lot-to-job linkage gaps, and non-machine constraints. It is not nearly flawless. Several central quantitative and causal claims are overstated, and those errors flow into the ranking and the interventions.

The B1 arithmetic is sound: J1’s 3 h 55 min splits into 3 h 45 min until J3’s 11:55 readiness and 10 min to the 12:05 start; J2’s 3 h 40 min splits into 3 h 30 min plus the same 10 min. The S1 arithmetic is also sound (13:30 to 19:25 is 5 h 55 min; excess versus a 25- or 35-minute lag is 5 h 30 min or 5 h 20 min). Those parts match the evidence and are properly conditional.

The FIX-A figure does not. J4’s 09:50-to-11:45 gap is 1 h 55 min, and 11:40−10:05 is 1 h 35 min, but CNC-2 is documented idle only from 10:05 to 10:47 (42 min). The remaining time until 11:40 is an unknown machine state. Treating 1 h 35 min as waiting on an available machine while FIX-A is held extends the claim past the evidence. The 5-minute post-release lag is the only fully aligned slice. Narrative B then carries that inflated delay through to J4’s 19:10 pack and J1’s S1 wait. B2 breaks that cascade: J4 is already batch-ready at 13:10, while B2 cannot start before J5 at 14:40 and actually starts at 14:50, so an earlier CNC start is absorbed before shipment.

The B2 rack decomposition has the same attribution problem. J4 ready 13:10, RACK-7 free 14:35, J5 ready 14:40, start 14:50. The rack is free before the partner job. The binding prerequisite is J5, then about 10 min of start overhead. Labeling 14:35−13:10 as 1 h 25 min of rack-caused delay ignores the overlap with J5’s unreadiness. The marginal rack delay, given J5’s timestamp, is about zero. That mistaken 1 h 25 min is reused as the benefit of a second rack, so the intervention portfolio inherits a non-binding constraint. Oven idleness from 12:55 to 14:50 shows that OVEN-1 was not binding; it does not show that the rack was.

Bundling “batch synchronization + shared rack” as rank 1 therefore mixes a well-supported B1 AND-join with a B2 rack story the timestamps do not support. Global oven utilization of 44% also does not show that OVEN-1 was free during J1/J2’s morning wait. Shipment consolidation, with a cleaner ~5–6 h effect on J1, is ranked below a smaller and weaker tool effect without a stated ranking criterion. The CAL-3 estimate is closer: 17:25−16:05 is 1 h 20 min, and TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25, but recalibration ends at 17:20, so 50 min is 45 min of recalibration plus 5 min of residual, and another 30 min is J6 queued behind J5.

The “no job beyond 1.8 SD” remark is explained as shared delay. S1’s wait is concentrated on J1 (packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25) while J4’s pack-to-dispatch is only 15 min, against a Pack→Dispatch median of 1.1 h. That concentration is not reconciled with the flattening summary. The recalibration edge is marked observed as relating to TEST-1; the source says CAL-3 was recalibrated and TEST-1 was idle, not that one event links both. Narrative C correctly keeps the lot override, the rack, calibration, and S3 apart in strength, but it is several mechanisms joined by theme rather than one causal chain.

Section 4’s flattening critique and the combinatorial account of idle machines with waiting jobs are strong. Falsifiers and extra fields are specific. The PM4Py block names the requested functions and does not derive waits from machine gaps, but the sync-wait loop keeps events at or before the cure time and then takes the max timestamp. For any job related to the cure, that maximum is the cure itself, so the computed wait is zero. The length is also well above the 1,800–2,800 word aim.
