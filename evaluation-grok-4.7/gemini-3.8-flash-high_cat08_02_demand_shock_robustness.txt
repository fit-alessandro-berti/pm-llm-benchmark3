6.5

The required seven-part structure is complete, and the bottleneck-migration story is often pointed in the right direction: I8 dumping into pre-analytics, I4 stripping an already over-full technical-validation pool, I1 starving non-urgent work, I3 concentrating repeats on M2, and courier compression creating starvation followed by a night-shift shock. Many source figures are used correctly (utilizations, median queues, recollection 4.2% to 8.0%, accession correction 2.8% to 4.3%, technical-validation-to-medical 24.5% in the normal period). It is not a reliable redesign. Several control rules contradict the package, and multiple “evidence” figures are wrong or invented.

Scenario 1 does not close. Routine volume “~1,350/week, falling to 37%” of ~3,350 is 40%, not 37%; urgent “+150% volume” at a 32% share is also inconsistent with that total. Weekly peak volume is 3,300 (+43.5%), not ~3,350 (+45%). Specialist demand “fivefold” is the historical peak ratio, not a consequence of the stated +140% complex-panel volume. Scenario 3 treats “1 of 2” medical validators as fact; only accession correction is specified as two people. The 25% clerk-time claim is invented. Scenario 2’s “~140%” on M1 after an M2 outage conflicts with pooled peak utilization of 98% and with M2 being the faster machine: equal machines would put about 196% on the survivor, and losing the faster machine would be worse.

Section 2 miscomputes peak rates by dividing by assay-to-technical-validation (7,240) instead of technical-validation exits (3,420+4,050+610 = 8,080). Medical-validation share is 42.3%, not 47.2%; hold-for-specialist is 7.6%, not 8.4%. Those holds are then mislabeled as the rate at which complex panels need specialist review. Peak repeat rate is 1,260/8,500 = 14.8%, not 14.6%. The I4 claim that diverting two validators pushes utilization “above 125%” assumes an unknown team size.

The robust package and the scenario policies fight each other. Dual-track batching is supposed to protect routine work with a size-16 batch and a 3-hour cap. Policy A freezes non-urgent batching whenever the preparation queue exceeds 3 hours, and turns off only below 1 hour. Peak median preparation queue is 11 hours, so in the demand scenario this policy is meant to handle it latches into permanent urgent-first dispatch — the failure mode attributed to I1 — and the “routine protected” table cell is false. Hysteresis for Policy B (medical-validation queue under 2 hours) is likewise the normal median, so the evening shift and outsourcing stay on through any peak.

Outsourcing savings are arithmetically overstated. A 12-hour transport leg is subtracted from a 36–48 hour local specialist queue as if the partner’s own assay and review time were zero. Auto-validation is said to clear 35–45% of routine technical-validation work and thereby cut peak technical-validation utilization from 101% to 65–70%; removing that share of routine work does not remove 30-plus points of total load. Direct release is only about half of peak technical-validation exits. “Eliminates the 22-hour medical queue” and an urgent p90 target under 16 hours are not credible while the peak receive edge is 8 hours and the assay median queue is 18 hours, with no new analyzer capacity.

The dashboard would misfire. Combined pre-analytical recollection plus accession correction is already about 7.0% in the normal period and 12.3% at peak, so a green band under 5% never matches the stated definition. Validation WIP “red” above 80 cases is far below a Little’s-law reading of the peak queues (medical validation alone is on the order of 150–200 cases at a 22-hour median). Courier Policy D trips at 1.5× while the dashboard turns red only above 1.8×. The dashboard also activates a “Policy E” that is never defined; the evening shift lives inside Policy B.

Residual risks do include one real redesign failure (urgent-label gaming), plus partner-lab weather exposure and post-9-hour error rise. Clerk cross-training during an 85% registration peak is recommended without a clear registration-overload consequence, and aggressive early rejection is not tied to the already high recollection-to-cancel share (370 of 890 peak recollection paths).
