The role model is strong and mostly faithful to the evidence: automation, temps, permanent front office, Giorgio’s hybrid profile, tiered technical work, Fatima, Erik, quality, Mateo, and Hana are separated for the right reasons, and complexity, substitution, and authority are not collapsed into activity counts. The required sections, benefits/risks of shadow roles, and the two staffing cautions are all present.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 2 states interpretations as facts that Section 4 correctly treats as unresolved: Chen is said to work “alongside” Erik, and Mateo’s 16 security events are assigned to the Weeks 7–8 filter, although the matrix gives only 10-week totals. Giorgio’s 20 approvals are called “likely” minor delegated sign-offs without evidence. “Only Chen” has security adjacency is too strong (Dina has 6, Ivan 4). Erik is “~1.4%” of events; the counts sum to 10,534, so 170 events is about 1.6%. “Rechecked” is also upgraded to “re-approval,” and “existential” risk and “margin leakage” go beyond the data.

The PM4Py sketch is well aimed but would not do what it claims. `Role` exposes `originators`, typically a list, not an `originator_importance` dict, so the membership loop fails. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` does not create event-level `@@service_time`, so the A7 slowdown is not actually measured; `get_service_time` is also not resource-split. Network results are returned rather than inspected.
