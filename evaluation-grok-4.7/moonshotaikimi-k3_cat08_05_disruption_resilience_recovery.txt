8.4

The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and is strong on the criteria that matter: it separates the operational and compliance clocks, treats T3 as a false-recovery phase, and refuses to equate throughput or later audit cleanup with real-time control. The A–L judgments, control-floor redesign, and accept/reject trade-off are concrete and mostly well grounded in the DFGs and rates.

It is not nearly flawless. “Day 301” does not clearly denote Day −30 to −1. The T3 claim that misses were “3–7×” baseline is loose; the traceability, temperature, and dual-control rates are about 6–7×. “Unlawful” for controlled-substance snapshot counts goes beyond the stated requirements. The ~3,070 cases without a visible END are conflated with ~4,900 later remediation touches, although unfinished work, backlog, and compliance gaps are not the same population. The highly variable dispatch-to-phone-proof delay is underused, and the expected dual-control baseline is rounded inconsistently (~10 versus about 11 on actual T2 volume).
