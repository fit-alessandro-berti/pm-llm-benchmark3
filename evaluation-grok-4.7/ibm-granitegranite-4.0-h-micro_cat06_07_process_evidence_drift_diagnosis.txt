3.5

The audit is structurally complete and directionally aware of the April rename, the parts-delay surge, Partner Z, and the October backlog, but the taxonomy, numbers, and decisions are unreliable enough that an operator following them would mis-time and mistype the failure.

## 1. Drift diagnosis table
The required six-way split is incomplete: data-pipeline failure has no row, so the dominant April incident (activity rename, 17% unknown activity, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped variants, AUROC 0.81→0.67, mean predicted risk 0.28 against a stable 0.19 breach rate, ECE 0.110) is absent from the diagnosis that is supposed to drive action.

Covariate drift is assigned to Aug–Oct on the `Order Part → Part Received` duration spike. That edge is a post-decision process measurement, unavailable at the prediction point immediately after diagnosis, so it is performance/process evidence. The real covariate shifts are elsewhere: June’s routing mix, and September’s Partner Z attributes (rural distance, device age) that were never in the model.

Concept drift is dated July–Oct and tied to “V2 adoption and Partner Z.” V2’s matured breach stays about 0.09–0.13 the whole year, and Partner Z’s share is 0% through August. The conditional change that is actually visible is V3: matured breach moves from about 0.30–0.35 through June to 0.46 in July, 0.64 in August, and 0.61 in September, while mean predicted risk stays near 0.19–0.21. The cited “predicted risk (0.18→0.31)” swaps September’s observed breach rate for the model’s mean score. Confidence is marked High on that misread.

Process drift is narrowed to July–Oct durations and scored only Medium, while June’s policy shift (V2 35%→52%, V1 45%→30%, overall breach 0.18→0.15, remote-fix edge still 3.8–4.6 h) is left out. V2’s stable duration does not weaken the V3 finding; it localizes it.

Label delay is pinned on “V3 maturity of 55%” and “unknown activity 2%→3%.” The 55% figure is October’s overall maturity, not a V3 rate. Unknown-activity share peaked at 17% in April and is about 0.7–0.9% from July onward; that series is a pipeline signal. August at 93% maturity, with a matured V3 breach of 0.64, is mostly a real outcome change. October’s observed breach of 0.16, against a predicted mean of 0.23 and a 52-day median label delay, is the censoring signature, and it is not isolated to V3 by any table in the source.

Population shift is correctly placed in September–October, then “contradicted” by a claim of no V1/V2 shift. June already moved those shares, and Partner Z’s slower maturity means the 0.45 vs 0.27 September gap is not a finished population effect.

Falsifiers are not stated. The contradicting-evidence cells mostly restate a different variant’s stability.

## 2. Timeline
January–March is fine. April names the rename and the partial backfill, then inverts the duplicate finding: removal applied only to the new source after 20 April, so early-April duplicates remained; they were not removed in a way that emptied the cohort. Prediction prefixes broke; outcome labels did not (maturity 100%, breach still 0.19). Calling these “label prefix errors” mixes the feature pipeline with the outcome label.

May–June is summarized as stabilized, with duplicates still lingering and “no drift detected.” May is a partial pipeline recovery (unknown activity 2%, missing queue feature 3%, AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050) because the cached pre-diagnosis count was not fully recomputed. June is a real control-flow mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, so it is process/covariate mix shift without concept drift. Collapsing those months, and declaring no drift, misses the separation the task requires.

July attributes the 1 June remote-routing policy to July and reports a breach-rate “shift” of 0.19→0.19 and a maturity “drop” of 98%→98%. July’s actual new fact is the parts edge moving 42 h→65 h and matured V3 breach 0.35→0.46, at 98% maturity.

August’s V3 share is given as 25%→35%; 25% is August’s V1 share, and V3 goes 22%→35%. The duration cited, 35 h→65 h, is March-to-July, not August (August is 130 h). The breach “spike” 0.28→0.31 crosses into September and omits the matured V3 rate of 0.64. September then calls V3’s breach rise “0.30→0.45”; 0.30 is about March’s V3 rate, 0.45 is Partner Z’s immature observed rate, and September’s matured V3 rate is 0.61 after August’s 0.64. Label maturity “82%→55%” assigns October’s figure to September.

October correctly invokes the outcome-API backlog, then states that V3’s breach “remained high (0.61).” The source marks all October variant rates as insufficiently mature. That 0.61 is September. The overall 0.16 is an immature-cohort rate, not a recovered process.

## 3. Diagnostic tests
The seven requested tests are named, and the expected results do not discriminate.

Maturity curves are expected, under covariate drift, to show stable matured V3 breach rates. Stable matured rates would argue against a conditional change; they are not a prediction of covariate drift. The informative contrast is: fixed-vintage V3 rates staying near 0.64 in August means the August drop is real; October’s 0.16 rising once the same cases age means October was censoring.

Relabeling and deduping are expected to fix “label delay” by shrinking unknown activity. Unknown activity is the April pipeline fault. Under a pipeline failure, April AUROC and ECE should recover after canonical labels, duplicate removal, and recomputing the cached count; under concept drift, matured V3 breach stays high after that repair.

Stratified calibration is expected to “align” under process drift. The deployed scores stay near 0.19 while matured V3 breach reaches 0.64, so the process change should show miscalibration and, given AUROC 0.68, a discrimination loss. Label delay should distort October toward apparent over-prediction (0.23 vs observed 0.16), not leave calibration looking stable.

Performance DFGs are expected to look stable under label delay. Edge times come from event timestamps, not from the breach label; October’s 155 h edge is visible whether or not the outcome API has caught up. That independence is the point of the test.

The conditional-outcome test compares V3 with V1/V2. Those variants already differed in January (about 0.32 vs 0.16 vs 0.10). The test has to hold the path fixed and compare months. A concept-drift result is matured V3 risk changing after June while V1 and V2 do not; a pure mix result is June’s overall rate falling while each variant’s rate stays put.

Reweighting is expected to improve Partner Z’s own performance. Transport checks ask whether aggregate error shrinks after restoring the pre-September mix, and whether non-Z V3 cases still breach at the post-July rate. Partner Z cannot explain July–August, because its share was still zero. Label maturity has to be aligned before that comparison, because Z’s labels arrive later.

Missingness is tied to “missing delivery-time features” as covariate drift. The measured missingness spike is April’s queue feature. Delivery time is not a documented missing column.

## 4. Recalibration versus retraining
The staging does not match the evidence and conflicts with section 6.

April is correctly treated as a pipeline containment problem, without an explicit ban on training until remap, dedup, and feature recompute. May–June is assigned V2/V3 recalibration while also being declared unusable for training. June’s labels are fully mature and the within-variant rates are stable; any June gap is mostly mix (predicted 0.20 vs observed 0.15), and V2’s conditional risk did not move. July–August calls for variant-specific recalibration of V2 and V3. V2 is stable. August’s AUROC of 0.68 means a monotone recalibration cannot restore rank order; parts-delay or supplier state has to enter the feature set, on labels that have actually matured.

September–October is then chosen for full retraining. October is 55% mature, the backlog hits long paths first, and the observed 0.16 breach rate is the wrong base rate to learn. September is only 82% mature, slower for Partner Z, and still mixed with the supplier shock. Safe material is January–March, June after the pipeline is confirmed clean, and July with a light maturity filter. August–September can enter a retrain only as matured, path-stratified cases once new delay and Partner Z features exist. October is unsafe until the vintage matures. A separate V3 model is not justified merely by a supplier shock if delay and variant are features; a Partner Z segment model is premature until stratified mature metrics show error inside Z beyond the missing covariates.

## 5. Monitoring architecture
The eight monitor names are present as one-line descriptions. There are no thresholds, no cohort definitions, and no alert dependencies. A label-maturity or median-delay alarm is not wired to suppress retrain and calibration pages; a data-quality alarm on unknown activity or the queue feature is not wired to block scoring; a performance-DFG alarm is not required to wait for mature labels before a concept-drift declaration; population alerts are not required to stratify by variant and maturity before a fairness claim. Deployment annotation of 1 April, 6 May, 1 June, 10 July, and 1 September is absent, so those monitors would refire the same undifferentiated AUROC drop.

The code sketch does not implement the requested extraction. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called. The renamed diagnosis is written to `normalized_label` and then unused, and the replacement runs old→new rather than collapsing both strings to the label the feature pipeline scores. Timestamps are never parsed, so `.dt.month` fails. There is no duplicate key strategy. `pm4py.filter_time_range` is unused. Discovery is called with an `event_stream=` argument and the results are treated as objects with `.shape`. The variant call is `discover_variants` on the full frame, not `pm4py.get_variants` on a cohort. Temporal features are extracted once from the unnormalized log. Event-time buckets and case-start cohorts are not built and not distinguished: an event-calendar cut splits in-flight cases and will distort October and April, while a case-start cohort is what the metrics table means by “cases started.” The loop prints shapes instead of returning auditable monthly edge medians, variant shares, and cohort counts.

## 6. Risks
The four failure modes are the right topics and are undercut by the earlier sections. Retraining on immature labels is warned against, after section 4 already recommends a September–October retrain; the mitigation does not say that learning October’s 0.16 rate would train the model to understate long-path breach risk. Calibrating around a pipeline bug is to be checked on a held-out mature set, which still carries April’s bad features unless the cache is recomputed first. Supplier-driven concept drift is answered with transport reweighting, which is a mix tool and does not put parts delay into the model or keep August’s discrimination loss in view. The Partner Z fairness caution is the closest to a sound mitigation, and it still folds “variant-specific models” into a fairness fix without a mature, stratified residual.

Across sections, April is not classified as the pipeline incident, June is explicitly called drift-free, August–September numbers are crossed across months and mechanisms, and October’s immature breach rate is still used as if a V3 rate of 0.61 were known and trainable. Those are the distinctions the task exists to test.
