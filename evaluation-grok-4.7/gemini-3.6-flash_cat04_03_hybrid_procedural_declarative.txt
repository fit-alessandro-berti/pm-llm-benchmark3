4.0

The write-up has the required shape and a usable backbone, but the implemented rules contradict each other and the verdict matrix. Under a strict reading it is not a coherent hybrid model.

The adopted policy is Resolution A, yet the resource checker implements Resolution B: activation before the opinion is only a warning when the opinion arrives within 24 hours of activation. T6 under Resolution A is then marked `fail` in that layer. The same checker, on T6 (opinion 10 hours after activation), returns a warning. No policy flag exists, so one program cannot produce both matrix rows. Resolution B is also scored as a procedural, DECLARE, and log-skeleton pass even though those artifacts are not replaced. That combines the two policies in the way the task forbids. The 24-hour clock is taken from activation, not escalation, without being stated as an assumption. Joint authorization is only a comment, not a missing-attribute rule.

The process tree for the happy path is structurally right: start, AND of the three checks, classify, then XOR of reject, low-risk, and high-risk, each ending in close. It still cannot support the matrix. `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, and `Close Onboarding` are single node objects reused across branches, so the value is not a tree. Clarification, escalation, opinion, bank change, and suspension are omitted, which is disclosed, but T2 is then a procedural pass and T5/T6-A a procedural fail. With ordinary alignments, T2’s extra clarification events are deviations. If those events are ignored, T5’s remaining projection is the valid low-risk path and should pass. The tree also contains no legal-opinion gate, so it cannot fail T6 under Resolution A and pass it under Resolution B.

The DECLARE dictionary is the clearest break. `precedence (Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is global: every activation needs a prior opinion. It is not “scoped to escalation,” despite the mapping and the semantics table. T1, T2, and T4 fail it. `precedence (Receive Clarification, Classify Risk)` at confidence 0.8 requires a clarification before every classification, so T1, T3, T4, T5, T6, T7, and T8 fail it. The matrix marks T1–T4 and T7 as DECLARE passes. The semantics table omits that second precedence entirely. R3 is only `response`, so a request may be answered after the next request or after activation; the task asked for alternate versus chain response, and for noncoexistence versus nonsuccession. Those distinctions are absent. `response (Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` also fails T8, which the explanation does not mention.

The log skeleton’s stated pair orientation is reasonable, and several `never_together` and `activ_freq` choices match R1, R8, and R10. It does not encode R5: there is no pair that forces the opinion before activation. `always_before (Obtain Legal Opinion, Sanctions Escalation)` invents the converse of R4. `directly_follows` lists only two pairs. In PM4Py that relation is the allowed succession whitelist, so T1–T8 all deviate, contradicting the pass cells. If it is not a whitelist, T6 does not fail the skeleton for the R5 reason given. `Request Clarification` and `Receive Clarification` are missing from `activ_freq`, so T2’s pass depends on an unstated treatment of unknown activities.

The temporal profile is the sound layer. The three pairs, the second conversions, and `zeta = 2.0` are right, and deviations are correctly warnings. T7’s 34 hours exceeds 12 + 2×6 = 24; T1’s 71 hours and T6’s 17 hours do not. That does not rescue T7’s overall `warning`, because the DECLARE model already fails T7.

R9’s resource comparison would catch T4 (Cora verifies and approves). R11 only inspects the first post-activation bank change. Timestamps are assumed to be datetimes with `total_seconds()`, which the hour-indexed cases are not. Missing resource or timestamp aborts the whole check; a missing emergency-authorization attribute is never defined.

Section 9 notes some duplication and vacuity, but not the false failures on the low-risk path, and it never discusses the required risk of treating support/confidence as normative truth. The 0.8 rule is exactly that problem. Deployment then runs a warning-producing R6 check before a DECLARE model that hard-fails the same cases, without saying which finding wins.
