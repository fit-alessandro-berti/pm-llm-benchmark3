The answer has the right policy skeleton and the wrong load-bearing numbers. Conformant second review is ranked above self-certification and immediate execution, screening is not treated as a substitute for that review, Action G stays disabled until both Rule 10 conditions exist, and P-901 is the reason Action C is not first. That is the part that tracks the case. The quantitative story used to justify the ranking does not.

The primary plan is sold as executing at T+70–85 with the €20,000–€35,000 deadline benefit, €0 regulatory risk, and confidence 0.92. Second-officer review is only timed in the prompt for Action C: 35–75 minutes, then 5–15 minutes to record and execute. Applied to an S4 arrival of 35–65 minutes, the critical path often finishes after 90 minutes. A late arrival plus a long review breaches by far more than the stated 10–20 minutes. The given SLA-breach probability under continued normal processing, 0.48–0.64, is never used, and transport of that prediction to this intervention is never discussed. Parallel screening fills the wait; it does not shorten S4’s clock. Invented review times of 15, 20, 25–35, and 30–45 minutes create the appearance that the deadline is met.

Benefit accounting is not comparable across rows. Actions D, C, and E are credited with €100,000–€500,000+ of “regulatory penalties avoided,” which is the cost of an unlawful execution, not a differential benefit of one conformant path over another. D and C are also credited with the full €15,000–€80,000 false-rejection range as if choosing them avoids that loss outright. Action E’s hold harm is cut to €15,000–€40,000 without a basis. Action C’s harm column invents a €0–€10,000 residual and leaves the P-901 spillover out of the harm figure. “€0 regulatory risk” on a conformant approval erases the remaining chance that an authorized reviewer clears a real sanctions match.

The dominance argument multiplies fuzzy-match confidence 0.46 by the penalty range. That 0.46 is a name-match score, not a probability of unlawful execution. Control-breach exposure attaches to executing without the required review even if the supplier is clean, so the product both mislabels the input and locates the harm in the wrong event. The same paragraph’s “99% operational success” claim is a different argument and is not connected to the product. Expected false-rejection harm of €8,250–€60,000 multiplies the outer ends of two ranges and treats post-review approval probability as the probability the payment is legitimate.

Section 3 says D strictly dominates C. Section 5’s own late-S4 case is a tradeoff in which P-884 loses the deadline value. That value is tied to meeting the deadline, so a miss is the full €20,000–€35,000, not a minor partial delay. The traces and the timeline then treat P-901 as breached outright. The prompt’s effect is +0.12–0.28 breach probability for a 45-minute interruption, and P-901’s baseline probability is unknown. The diagram’s “done at T=50” also fails its own 45-minute arithmetic, and “arrive at 40, review 35, done at 80” is 75.

The branch probabilities are fabricated and not a partition: screening-clears 0.60, match-persists 0.25, and “ambiguous and S4 delayed” 0.15 mix an outcome with an independent arrival time. Approval at 0.65, customer-response 0.70/0.30, director reachable 0.50, and director approves 0.30 are presented as estimates. Nothing in the case supports a 0.60 chance that rescreening drops the score below 70. The fallback adds a risk threshold of 85 and a T+50 cutoff, then forbids Action F “under no circumstances” after Section 4 correctly left F conditional. Rule 10 is also over-specified into cryptographic sign-off and a narrow list of infrastructure incidents.

Conformance labels and the A/B prohibition are the strongest part. The selection-bias point is real: 0.55–0.75 is an approval rate after independent review and does not travel to an unreviewed execution. Mapping D to Rules 2 and 5 is loose, and Action F is tightened beyond the prompt into a requirement for a confirmed list match. Those are secondary. The grade is held down because the recommended plan’s deadline benefit, dominance claim, spillover certainty, and 0.92 confidence are not supported by the ranges the answer was given.
