3.5

The write-up has the required ten sections, names the R5/R6 clash, picks a fail-closed reading, and gets the R12 arithmetic and the T7 timing flag right. The artifacts that the task asks to be executable do not implement that reading, and the verdict matrix does not follow from those artifacts.

The procedural model is not a usable process tree. The imports and constructors are not PM4Py’s process-tree API, so `procedural_model` would not build. The operator tree is also the wrong language for the backbone it describes: an `AND` makes Receive, the three checks, both clarification events, Classify, and Sanctions Escalation concurrent and mandatory, and the inner `XOR` offers Reject, or all of Manager Approval plus legal opinion plus EDD plus Compliance Approval, or Activate, or payment, or Close, as alternatives. A conforming low-risk case cannot follow approval, activation, payment, and close. Process trees do have a loop operator; the claim that a clarification loop cannot be written is false. The prose says the three checks precede classification and that clarification is a loop before Classify. The code does neither. Post-activation behavior is omitted, and R11 never reappears as code.

The DECLARE dictionary is not a PM4Py DECLARE model. Template names are absent, so each pair is only `{support, confidence}` and cannot be checked. The unary rule is `("Activate Vendor", None)` rather than an activity key. The semantic table then assigns templates that contradict Section 2 and the requirements. R4 is `precedence` in the mapping and `response` in the table; the requirement is “if escalation occurs, an opinion eventually occurs,” which is `response` or `responded_existence`, not precedence. `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` forces an opinion before every activation, so T1 and T2 fail, but both are marked DECLARE pass. `exactly_one` on Activate rejects the legitimate zero-activation rejection in T3; R10 is at most once. R2 is not unary `exactly_one` and is not `noncoexistence`: neither encodes “exactly one of the two approvals in an accepted case, and neither in a rejected case.” R8’s Compliance/Manager exclusion is missing. R3, the natural `altresponse` (not `chainresponse`, because other work may sit between request and reply), is missing. `response`, `precedence`, `altresponse`, `chainresponse`, `noncoexistence`, and `nonsuccession` are never actually contrasted; T8 is the case that shows why R1 is `noncoexistence` rather than one-directional `nonsuccession`.

The log skeleton has the six required keys and a defensible `never_together` set, but `activ_freq` assigns `[1]` to Activate, Reject, and Release First Payment together. That means exactly once, not at most once, and it contradicts `never_together` on Reject and Activate: no trace can contain both exactly once and contain neither pair. Values are lists, not occurrence-count sets. An empty `directly_follows` whitelist, under ordinary log-skeleton conformance, rejects every direct succession, so every test trace fails. The orientation note does not define whether `(a, b)` means “a before b whenever b occurs.”

Section 7 checks only the first Verify resource against the first approval resource, on an ungrouped frame, using `activity` / `resource` / `time`. The Capture-versus-Activate rule is absent. There is no case id, no behavior for a missing resource or timestamp, and no `filter_four_eyes_principle`. The emergency function ignores any authorization attribute and any 24-hour bound; it only rechecks order, then the matrix marks T6’s resource/data cell pass. R11 is named in the mapping and not implemented.

The matrix is internally inconsistent. T5 and T8 are overall `fail` while every layer is `pass`, so the decisive violation has no locus. T8 violates the skeleton’s own `never_together` and the table’s own Reject/Activate `noncoexistence`. T3 and T1 violate the skeleton frequencies and the unconditional opinion-before-activation rule. T6 is `pass` on the resource/data layer whose function returns a violation for that trace. The R6 alternative is described as a clean pass; the log still has no joint authorization, so that reading is `policy-dependent` even though the opinion falls inside 24 hours (activation at 13, opinion at 23). “Earliest decisive violation, then later ones” is not carried through the rows.

Section 9 mislabels ordinary satisfaction of a negative constraint on T4 as vacuity. The real vacuity is rules whose trigger never occurs. It also misses the modeling trap that “payment at most once” plus “a later payment after a post-activation bank change” cannot both be read literally without saying whether the bank change happens before the single payment. Support and confidence are hardcoded to 1.0 and then treated as if that were a discovered certainty. Deployment drops the log skeleton from the check order and treats post-hoc conformance as if it blocked downstream execution.

Temporal seconds for 48/24, 12/6, and 72/36 hours are correct, zeta is 2.0, and T7’s 34-hour opinion lag is about 3.7 standard deviations while the 2-hour payment gap stays inside 72 ± 72 hours. That local result does not repair the control-flow model or the verdicts.
