6.5

The write-up is structurally complete and analytically serious, but it is not internally consistent enough for a high score. Sections 1–3, 5–7, and 9 are strong; sections 4 and 8 do not reliably support the recommendation.

Service mixture means are correct (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74), and the block loads derived from them match the demand mix. Shift, break, and multiplier arithmetic is mostly right, including the important result that base offered work is about 2721 minutes against 2490 regular minutes and at most 2670 with full overtime. Because every multiplier is at least 1, that comparison is a valid lower bound, not just a loose analogy. Bottleneck migration is also well argued: software around 16:00–18:00, no mechanical capacity after 18:00, electrical under U2, Chen’s absence under U3, and intake-to-diagnostic cascade under U5. The 14:00–16:00 electrical capacity of 401 should be about 391 (Ada 88.9 + Chen 120 + Deepa 90 + Emil 92.3). That does not change the bottleneck call.

Assumptions are labeled, both required objectives exist, and the three policies are operational rather than mere names. Specialization versus pooling, speed, shift coverage, parallel-versus-sequential work, handover, and fairness are all treated. Recommending hybrid reservation rather than base-case mean delay is the right kind of conclusion, and the crisis triggers and pilot protections are concrete.

The comparison table is the main analytical failure. Those delay, SLA, and utilization ranges are not computed from the capacity model or from the simulator. No queueing relation connects the proven overload to a base mean delay of roughly 165–210 minutes and a pooled p95 near 480. With utilization above 1 even before multipliers, a large unfinished tail should remain; the reported base tail is too optimistic relative to the author’s own capacity bound. Uncertainty is named, but the ranges are not auditable.

The simulator is substantial and encodes the right tables, three policies, seeds, and PM4Py calls, but it is wrong on the main path. `branches_open` stays 0 for M, E, and S. On diagnostic completion it becomes −1, so Validation is never created for those cases. Most cases therefore never finish and are scored as a 1440-minute delay. ME/ES can complete; ordinary cases cannot. Printed policy metrics are dominated by that bug. Other implementation gaps compound it: dispatch is speed-blind despite the speed matrix; overtime is identical across policies; hybrid “slow overflow only when queue age justifies it” is not implemented; preemption penalizes remaining time that can still include handover; resuming the same resource can add handover again because `last_resource` is updated only at completion; absent Chen remains eligible. The code would run, but it would not produce the section 4 comparison, and the answer never reconciles the two.

Format, process-mining design, and the limits/pilot section meet the request. The grade is held down by the unaudited central table, its conflict with the capacity bound, and a simulation defect that removes Validation for most cases.
