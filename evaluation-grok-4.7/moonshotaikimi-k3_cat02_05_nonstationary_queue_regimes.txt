The final write-up is a strong, internally coherent queueing analysis: regimes are evidence-based, outage recovery is not treated as normal capacity, Little’s Law is applied only with an explicit open-hours convention and proxy, and pooling is shown to reverse balance, mix, staffing, and flow time. The reproducible code matches the stated design, and the main regime totals check out (2,519 arrivals, 2,409 completions, net +110; CUSUM peak +128 on 2026-04-28).

It is not nearly flawless. The CUSUM “climbs +76 over Apr 20–28” is wrong; that window’s net flow is +106. Utilization rests on a single lognormal matched to each day’s median and p90, without noting that those summaries come from a simple/complex mixture, so \(\hat\rho\) (and the “about 7 FTE” rule, which only reaches \(\rho\approx0.99\) before peaks) is softer than presented. Lag-7 and residual lag-1 are quoted as if they were the code’s estimates, but the lag-7 hand figure uses a different correlation than the code, and the residual estimator is biased toward zero. R1 is marked “applicable” while opening backlog is still unknown and the 146-minute sojourn versus \(\rho\approx0.66\) is left unresolved.
