7.4

The candidate languages are real alternatives, and the seven observed variants sit in all three. The score stays well below the top because the required validation script crashes, the permissive model is described in two incompatible ways, and a few quantitative claims are sharper than their evidence.

**The validation script fails before it checks anything.** `pm4py.check_soundness(...)` returns a bool. `sound[0]` raises `TypeError` on the first net, so the metric block, playout, and the hand-written suite never run. The surrounding calls match the current API (`convert_to_petri_net`, alignment fitness/precision, `generalization_tbr`, `simplicity_petri_net`, `play_out`), the suite’s expected accept/reject vector matches the three languages, and the sampling caveat is stated correctly. The script still does not do the job Section 8 requires.

**D5 is not the same constraint in Section 1 and Section 5.** The ledger says the permissive tree honors the disjunctive reading (supplier confirmation after either check). The tree does not. Supplier confirmation is parallel to the whole `(Credit Check ∥ Engineering Review) → (Expedite Review xor τ)` block, so `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is admitted with no check finished first. Section 5 later calls that an intentional overshoot of D5, and the audit calls the same trace risky and arguably undesirable. The boundary the task asked for is therefore blurred: the extra behavior is both “reasonable generalization” and “arguably undesirable.” The disjunctive rule itself (supplier confirmation after at least one of the two checks) is a different language from the one that was built.

**Smaller inaccuracies.**
- “Skip rate 23% > loss rate 5–10%” compares a case-absence rate with an event-loss rate. The count in the same cell is the usable one: about 23 observed supplier-confirmation events imply roughly 1–3 missing events, against 7 absences, so both true skips and loss are plausible.
- “Keep every ordering constraint with zero observed counterexamples” overstates the balanced model. Expedite Review occurs only together with Supplier Confirmation (2/2), and that co-occurrence is dropped. That can be a reasonable choice; it is a choice.
- The conservative exclusion line about “SC after ER without a preceding CC…ER order other than the two observed shapes” does not pick out a clear set of traces.
- The matrix states fitness as 1.0. Inclusion of all seven variants makes perfect fitness a fair structural expectation; the table was asked to stay qualitative or interval-valued.

**What is solid.** The DFG recount, the 11/30 Engineering-Review-first count, and the 23/23 Credit-Check-before-Supplier-Confirmation regularity are right. The ledger’s account of bidirectional DFG edges is right: the same pair of directions is compatible with concurrency, a case-level XOR of two sequences, overlap, or ingestion-index ordering of tied timestamps. The conservative model is exactly the seven variants, with a concrete duplicate-label and scalability discussion. The balanced partial order (checks unordered, Credit Check before Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review unordered with Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review after both checks and unordered with Supplier Confirmation, fixed approval–release–archive tail, both optionals as XOR with τ) accepts V1–V7 and rejects the excluded traces it cites. `XOR(Supplier Confirmation, τ)` is explicitly disclosed as mixing stocked-part skips with integration loss. The S1 alternative (those 11 Engineering-Review-first completions are tie-breaks or late logs, so the operational model should move toward sequence) would actually change the recommendation. The data plan targets the decisions that matter: the external/stocked flag, start and complete timestamps, tie-free ordering, and future traces that would kill Credit-Check-before-Supplier-Confirmation, the concurrency hypothesis, or the expedite guard.

The modeling argument is strong. The executable check is broken, and the permissive candidate is mislabeled relative to D5.
