3.1

The write-up has the required seven-part frame and does not collapse every case into one label, but it is not a reliable compliance assessment. Several classifications rest on inverted timelines, misread clauses, and evidence that belongs to other cases. Stated method and case results also contradict each other.

The hierarchy is one stack, not an issue-specific ordering, and it lets ECD-2 override GPIP-4 segregation, finance approval, and compliance clearance. That reading is not earned. A4 keeps every control except competitive tendering unless a written A7 exception exists. C5 does not mention those integrity controls. A7 does not say a COO–Incident Commander directive is a Chief Risk Officer exception, and the CRO never ratified ECD-2. A5 lets the local SOP add stricter controls; it does not let the SOP defeat GPIP-4. The CFO email is then treated as both non-binding and as a waiver of GPIP-4 finance rules. C5 is cited sometimes as a waiver and sometimes as a non-waiver.

The rule catalogue repeats those errors. D1 is attributed to ECD-2. B4 is treated as waiving finance approval even though B5 says the emergency procedure does not waive it. The segregation rule states a waiver “if” and then leaves the condition blank while noting that ECD-2 never mentions segregation. Threshold operators are missing, so several rules do not say whether they apply at or above the stated amount. Evidence requirements are generic and do not capture the four-hour authorization record, the 24-hour emergency form, or the requirement of a written CRO exception.

Case application is the decisive failure. P-501 is classified compliant on the claim that commitment at 08:50 followed PO creation at 12:20. The sequence is the reverse, and commitment occurred before ECD-2 began at 09:00. The Plant Manager was reachable and had not delegated SHIFT-1. Same-day plant and finance approvals are treated as curing that pre-commitment sequence, contrary to A6 and the answer’s own method. The purchase is also below the EUR 50,000 GPIP-4 threshold that the answer applies. P-506 reverses finance timing: finance approval at 06:05 preceded commitment at 06:20, yet the row calls it post-commitment and missing. P-507 had compliance clearance at 08:50, before commitment at 09:00; the 12:40 PDF and 14:30 ERP entry concern the Incident Command authorization, and the PDF falls inside the four-hour recording period. P-508 had legal clearance at 16:00, before commitment at 16:10, but the row lists that clearance as missing while calling the case compliant and simultaneously saying the EUR 155,000 amount is outside the email’s EUR 150,000 sufficiency statement. P-505 uses the 18:06 ERP stamp as the commitment time and mislabels the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment as an emergency form, despite the instruction to prefer contemporaneous evidence and the separate point that the ERP clock may have been fast. P-503 is noncompliant for partly fair reasons, but the stated 24-hour scope assumption is never applied to three-day backup stock. Similar missing finance events are compliant in one case and noncompliant in another without a stable premise. Signed PDFs and telephone logs are assigned to cases that do not contain them, and the July 14 ERP outage is used to explain later dates.

The alternatives do not repair this. For P-508, the stronger reading is presented as a secondary view and depends on legal clearance being absent when it is present. For P-507, the alternative misidentifies the PDF as compliance evidence. For P-503 and P-506, the alternatives depend on records lost in an outage that was not occurring on those dates. Remediation repeats the category errors, proposes after-the-fact waivers as cures, and recommends a CRO exception for the cafeteria purchase that is plainly outside sterilization scope. The draft clause reaches authority, thresholds, records, and expiry, but its Chief Risk Officer safeguard can be waived by the CFO, and its compliance wording does not settle new-supplier treatment or whether later paperwork cures a missing pre-commitment control.
