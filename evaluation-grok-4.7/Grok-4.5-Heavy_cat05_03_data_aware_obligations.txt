7.3

The case dispositions are mostly right, and the temporal model is clear. The formalization and the checker do not reliably implement that model, and one required counterfactual defaults missing amount evidence in the favorable direction.

Temporal reconstruction is sound. Blanks are non-updates, state is forward-only, `(timestamp, event_index)` is the order, and event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are separated correctly. TX-311 is read in the right direction: index 3 still has `US`, index 4 sets `DE`, and Release_Funds therefore sees `DE`. Final attributes are not pushed backward. EX-77 (qualified signature at 09:05, documented outage) is separated from EX-88 (certificate created at 10:30, after release; interested witness).

The eleven release states and the primary labels hold up:

- TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant for the right operative reasons: no enhanced review of a foreign amount at or above 100,000; high risk without `Compliance_Approve`; `XZ` listed after the only screen; PEP covered only by a standard packet; 118,000 to 145,000 is about 22.9% after the enhanced review.
- TX-302, TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are compliant. The enhanced review on TX-302 already meets R1, so EX-77 is redundant. The 220,000 to 230,000 move is about 4.5%. The drop from 140,000 to 100,000 does not invalidate the review, and 100,000 still meets “at least 100,000.” `DE` at release switches R1 off.
- TX-305 is indeterminate. The next-day 150,000 settlement is not an immutable release amount.
- TX-310 as noncompliant, medium confidence, is a defended reading of R5: the reliable timestamp shows the signing certificate did not exist at release, and the displayed 08:50 time is not enough. The interested oral statement is treated as the weaker reading, not as proof.

Section 2 is where the logic slips. The R1 table and the pseudo-logic both return indeterminate whenever amount or jurisdiction is unknown. That is broader than R6. A known `DE`, or a known amount under 100,000, already excludes R1 even if the other field is missing. The R2 table has the same over-width: unknown risk or unknown customer type becomes indeterminate even when the other field is already `HIGH` or `PEP`. The R2 pseudo-logic is tighter (“leaves HIGH/PEP possible”) and therefore contradicts the table. The table also requires the enhanced review to follow the activating change; the R2 formula drops that constraint. `latest_material ≺ er` is undefined when no material change exists, so the formula does not actually say that a pre-release enhanced review succeeds in the ordinary no-change case. The code later special-cases a missing material timestamp; the formula does not. “Material data change” is also widened to every activating or deactivating risk, PEP, or jurisdiction change, while R4 only defines amount invalidation, and only for amount-dependent obligations. That extension is not tied back to which obligation it can defeat.

The TX-305 alternatives overreach. A release payload at or above 100,000, with “no known” post-review change, is treated as compliant. The review document has no amount, and R4 says an increase from an unknown amount is not classifiable. No logged `Update_Amount` does not prove the reviewed amount was within 20% of the newly revealed figure. The same favorable step appears when the settlement figure is substituted for the release amount: R1 would become decidable as triggered, and satisfaction would stay indeterminate. TX-301 is slightly mis-caused as “R1 via R4.” A standard review never discharges R1; the failure is the absence of any enhanced review after the amount is 125,000 and the jurisdiction is `US`. R4 is real there, but it is not what makes the standard review inadequate.

The code would likely flag this particular log in the same coarse way, and it does keep the TX-305 settlement after release. It does not implement the rules it states. Materiality is `new / previous_known > 1.20`, and `amt_at_last_review` is written and never used. R4’s baseline is the amount at the latest standard or enhanced review, so a path such as 100,000 at review, then 115,000, then 130,000 is a material increase that this loop will miss. Comparisons use raw timestamps, so equal timestamps are not ordered by `event_index`. `format_dataframe` sorts by case and timestamp only, with an unstable sort, after the code has ordered on `event_index`. R3 does not check that the list update added the release-time jurisdiction; any later list event after an earlier screen is enough. Exemption scope treats a missing amount as inside the cap. Narrative “activating changes” never become material events in the loop. Any referenced exemption is forced to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, including TX-302, whose enhanced review already satisfies R1. `discover_declare(...).items()` is an unsupported assumption about the return type and can throw after the diagnostic print. Those are fixture-fitting gaps, not a faithful checker.

Sections 6 and 7 are the strongest remainder. The six requested control-flow failures are present and tied to the right cases, and the logging recommendations cover immutable release payloads, split timestamps, rule version, exemption scope, change provenance, and a stable sequence number.
