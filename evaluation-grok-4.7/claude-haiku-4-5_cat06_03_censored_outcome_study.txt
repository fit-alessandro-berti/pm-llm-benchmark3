5.0

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, and open cases as non-default, and the leakage decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, same-day `documents_total`, extraction-time resource experience, and `label_available_at` are the right ones. Those strengths do not survive the estimand, censoring, validation, and implementation errors below.

The primary target is not a coherent survival estimand. The population is restricted to activated accounts whose labels have already matured, which removes ordinary right censoring, yet non-defaults are then described as censored at 90 days and analyzed with Kaplan–Meier and Cox models. A fully observed non-event at a fixed horizon is an outcome, not a censored time. Prediction time is defined in incompatible ways: after the first \(k\) events but before any decision, at the last pre-decision event, and, in the code, at the \(k\)-th event of the untruncated log. The estimand also conditions on future activation while the stated use is pre-decision early warning, so the score is not operational at the time it would be issued. Inclusion compounds this: defaults may enter before the 90-day window closes, while non-defaults may not. That outcome-dependent entry inflates the default rate.

The alternatives are not estimable as specified. The competing-risk state list puts rejection, withdrawal, still-open status, and default within 90 days after activation on one cumulative incidence scale. Those events do not share a time origin, and still-open follow-up is censoring, not an absorbing competing state. The second alternative then censors rejection and withdrawal, which treats outcomes that preclude activation as if default could still occur. Linked-transfer accounting is internally inconsistent: the exclusion table calls 1,160 cases “60% unlinked” of 2,900, while 60% linked is about 1,740, and those linked cases are also described as added back into a partition that already sums to 48,000.

The censoring strategy repeats the category error. The primary write-up claims weighted Kaplan–Meier under MCAR, but the procedure fits an unweighted model on a cohort whose censored count is explicitly zero. The 3,600 not-yet-mature activations are conflated with permanently missing partner feeds. Sensitivity analysis that right-censors the 5,100 still-open applications at extraction, using time since application start, changes both the risk set and the time origin. The proposed MCAR check compares defaults with non-defaults; that comparison should differ when covariates predict default. Stability of AUC after dropping partner cases does not test whether missing feeds are informative.

Prefix construction does not enforce an as-of cut. Fixed-length and elapsed-time rules are applied to the full event list, so Contract Signed, Account Activated, collections, and Default Recorded can fall inside \(k=10\) or \(k=20\) and inside a 30- or 60-day window. The activity-renaming map is illustrative only, and the one-minute deduplication computes a rounded timestamp and then ignores it. Feature lists are otherwise useful, especially the split among static attributes, prefix features, and historical resource data, but example traces still contain Accept.

Temporal validation is motivated correctly and then specified incorrectly. Train, validation, test, and holdout sizes do not reconcile with 21,600 or 25,200. Maturity is tied to activation by 30 November 2024, which is far stricter than 90 days plus a 7–35 day feed lag before 15 February 2026. The holdout is placed in January 2026 even though starts end on 31 December 2025. There is no fold-specific as-of date that requires `label_available_at` to precede training. C-index above 0.65 and illustrative figures such as a 0.70–0.75 C-index or a 25% top-decile risk are unsupported thresholds.

Section 8 states the right causal boundary and then crosses it. It correctly says that default risk for applicants who were not activated is unidentified, while also treating the conditional-on-activation probability as a basis for declining applications. The three deployment feedback loops are substantive.

The skeleton misses required PM4Py behavior and does not implement the design. It never calls `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`. Prefixes are manual slices of the full log. `time_to_event_days` is `label_available_at` minus activation, then clipped at 90; because the feed arrives 7–35 days after the horizon, defaults and non-defaults collapse toward the same time and the survival outcome is destroyed. The \(k=5\) and \(k=10\) loops emit separate rows, so the merged analysis table double-counts cases. The leakage “check” is a hardcoded table, not a test that feature times precede prediction time. Several imports are unused and point at old internal modules. Censoring is not encoded as of a cutoff, and the block is several times the requested length.

Taken together, the answer identifies the selection problem and the obvious leakage fields, but a study built exactly as written would estimate the wrong quantity, validate on an ill-defined calendar, and train on prefixes and times that do not match the stated target.
