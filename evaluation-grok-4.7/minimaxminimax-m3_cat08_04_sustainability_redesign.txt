6.5

The memo meets the required seven-part structure and stays tied to segments, DFG paths, SP1/SP2/Q1, and the batching, rework, and transport evidence. It also refuses to strip Critical-downtime of fast transport and treats air and dedicated vans as slack- and segment-dependent. Those are real strengths. The grade is held down by several analytical errors that change the result.

The effects table does not state direction in most cells. Symbols are blank, and words such as “large” or “up to 12–24 h” do not say whether the metric rises or falls. That misses the required directional comparison.

Section 6 then contradicts the earlier analysis. Capped batching is described as raising energy per item (2.8 kWh versus 1.3 kWh), yet the package treats S2 as one of the largest energy reductions. Moving work from CNC_B to CNC_A is also credited with removing air freight on a different immediate-CNC path. The 40% damage-return figure, which the evidence ties to reinforced packaging of heavy made-to-order parts, is applied to packaging-route checks. The 24% late rate and 7.2 kgCO2e belong to the backorder variant, not to all 1,010 partial shipments. Opportunity size is overstated: the 4–6× air factor is applied to the whole 16% service-contract stock segment, and the 35–55% truck saving to the whole 39% regular-stock segment, despite the answer’s own slack limits.

The obsolescence indicator is mis-specified as cycle time over 14 days. The evidence is unsold batch stock when the forecast is wrong, while made-to-order p90 cycle time is already about 7.5 days. Reverse-logistics savings are counted after return transport was placed outside the boundary. The 18 h and 24 h batch caps and the 15% rework cutoff are presented as if derived from the log. “A/B” is used for a before/after quarter comparison, and the validation plan does not match a twelve-change rollout.
