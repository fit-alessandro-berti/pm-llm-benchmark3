The write-up has the required sections, table columns, case labels, and a few correct classifications, but the counterfactuals are not temporally or procedurally reliable enough to use.

The partial order is one informal skeleton. It is not a relation set, and it collapses distinct case orders despite the instruction to keep them apart. Originals-before-document, identity-before-originals, and escalation are missing as explicit relations. Calling K-104 “timing only” misses the inserted S1 packet and the C1 reservation. K-219 as routing and K-337 as a decision-policy change are the right categories.

Cycle-time figures are unlabeled and internally inconsistent. Factual durations are about 58 h, 48 h, and multi-day for K-104, K-219, and K-337, so “cycle time 8 to 18 h” cannot be an absolute duration. Section 3 then treats larger numbers as better, which only works if they are savings, while K-219’s table (“6 to +4 h”) disagrees with its own best/base/worst (10 / 6 / +4). Worst cases still assume a gain. Incomplete packets, slow handwriting, and queue preemption can produce zero or negative change.

K-104 does not make identity or document checks finish earlier. D1 still runs Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40 and A1 still finishes Tue 19:10. Final compliance cannot start Tue 20:00–23:00 unless the reserved slot preempts the Tue 08:00 C1 backlog of about 16 h. That preemption is asserted, not derived. The path also drops S1.

K-219 misses the binding constraint. Document check ended Tue 13:30 and assessment started Tue 20:00, so readiness was already early relative to A2. Assessment ended Tue 22:30 and M1 signed Wed 08:30, so a 1–1.5 h earlier assessment still lands on the same morning decision. A slower D2 can erase the earlier start. “D2 starts Tue 08:00” ignores originals at Mon 17:30, D2’s Mon 16:00 queue of about 5 h, and the overnight work already observed for D1.

K-337 is the hardest failure. The case is high-risk and high-value, and no compliance review has occurred by Assessment 2 on Friday. The intervention stops the second information request. It does not waive final compliance or four-eyes. The stated path goes from Assessment 2 straight to Decision, while the feasibility paragraph claims compliance is satisfied. “Thu–Mon” is the wrong window, and “Decision on Tue 11:00–14:00” does not exist downstream of Friday 10:30. Tying 60 h versus 36 h to approve-with-conditions versus reject is unexplained. The removed tail is on the order of several days if compliance can occur Friday, and much less if C1 is busy. The omitted control makes the range non-operational.

K-411 misreads the trace. Both checks already finish by Tue 19:30. Wed 09:30 is assessment start. The real serialization is identity ending Tue 13:20, the document task appearing only then, and D2 running Tue 15:00–19:30. Parallel creation might pull document completion forward by a few hours. It does not move check completion from Wednesday to Tuesday, and a Tue evening finish still faces the A2/M1 overnight gap. The claimed 4–9 h saving contradicts the stated finish time. One case does not inflate the assessment queue by 1–2 cases.

The 100-case arithmetic is impossible on the stated pools. C1 is one reviewer at 3–6 h per final review. One hundred K-104 cases are an order-of-magnitude overload, not “30–40 % of C1.” The same is true of shifting one hundred document checks onto D2 and calling the result 78 % utilization. K-337’s system total of 180–240 h is about 2 h per case, against a case-level claim of 36–60 h. Avoided rework is also treated as having no compliance-queue spillover. The scale-up discussion is about 100 words against a required 250–400, and it does not use the daily snapshots.

Ranking K-337 first for “high robustness” equates queue-independence with safety. That intervention changes loss exposure, appeal risk, and compliance defensibility, and the path used to rank it is infeasible. Reversibility is rated low in the table and then K-104 is called the hard-to-reverse danger in the synthesis. K-411’s logging uncertainty is real, so “low” pilot information value is the wrong way round. The synthesis is about 100 words against a required 220–320.

The pilots inherit that ranking. K-337’s primary outcome is cycle time, its spillover cell says “none expected,” and appeal power at 60 cases is unaddressed. K-219 eligibility drops the handwriting feature that caused the D1 routing. Assumption 1 says C1 can reserve capacity without displacing other high-risk cases, which contradicts the spillover claim. Assumption 8’s week-long queue stability is already false in the Monday-to-Thursday snapshots and is mislabeled as interference.

Credit is limited to skeleton compliance, the routing-versus-policy distinction, the correct direction of C1 displacement and D1/D2 substitution, and the presence of reverse-evidence and pilot rows. The clocks, mandatory compliance path, binding constraints, and scaled capacities do not hold.
