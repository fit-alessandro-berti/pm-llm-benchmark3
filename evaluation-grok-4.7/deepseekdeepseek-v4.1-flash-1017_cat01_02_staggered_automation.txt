The answer is a strong, well-calibrated causal assessment: availability is separated from uptake, median and p90 are kept apart, staggered and endogenous rollout is treated as a design problem, and the rollout choice preserves a comparison branch. The pre/post and DiD arithmetic checks out, the claim ladder matches the evidence, and the code is labeled descriptive with small-cluster and aggregation warnings.

It is not nearly flawless. The section 3 justification is about 140 words, short of the required 150–250. Section 1 places the FTE changes in May/June and misses Beta’s April gain. The headline results are an unadjusted two-period DiD, including Gamma’s two-month post window, even though the identification section itself rejects naïve TWFE under dynamic effects and time-varying confounding. Arrivals, FTE, and complexity are in the data and are not used to produce a workload-adjusted contrast. The constant 4% fast-path rate in never-treated branches is not examined as a measurement floor. The sensitivity ranges are judgment intervals, and the event-time regression is likely collinear with branch and month effects. Escalation is given confidence 60 despite the shared March fraud-desk shock.
