7.2

The design correctly refuses to treat the 21,600 matured activated accounts as the scoring population and refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, transfers, or open cases as non-defaults. The competing-outcome estimand, leakage table, temporal split rationale, and deployment feedback are substantively strong. It is not nearly flawless: several statistical claims conflict, and the skeleton does not implement the cohort it describes.

The primary estimand is mostly well specified: landmark at first Credit Review, states that keep non-activation separate from default, and a clear split between the joint probability and the activation-conditional probability. Calling censoring an outcome state, then saying it is not a class to predict, muddies that definition. Section 3 then calls a discrete-time multi-state model “equivalently” a one-row multinomial with IPCW. Those are not equivalent. Section 1 fixes the unit of analysis at one row per case, which fits only the multinomial. A flat multinomial also treats “activated, default label not yet mature” as missing the whole state, even though activation is already known. That understates activation and is not ordinary right censoring. The text never resolves this partial-observation problem.

Cohort arithmetic does sum to 48,000, and the table correctly separates labeled, competing, open, and immature activated cases. It does not identify selectively unobserved cases. Permanently missing partner feeds are absorbed into the 3,600 “delayed label” row. Sections 2 and 3 contradict each other on transfers: inclusion rules follow the linked 60% in the primary cohort, while the primary analysis treats all transfers as competing events and moves linkage to a sensitivity. The maturity rule, activation plus 90 plus 35 days even when `label_available_at` is already known, is a defensible guard against outcome-dependent feed timing, but the cost is not stated. With extraction on 15 February 2026, it leaves mature post-1 July 2025 activations only through about 13 October 2025.

That omission weakens validation. Putting July–December 2025 starts in the test set is the right regime-shift idea, and the critique of a random split is right. Positivity is poor for that test: late-2025 starts cannot reach a mature 90-day label by extraction, so IPCW is least stable where performance is claimed. The evaluable subset further over-represents fast activations. The migration slice is only a feature-shift check; outcome labels for post-15 October activations are not mature. Split prose and code also disagree: `pd.cut` without `include_lowest` drops starts on 1 January 2024, puts all of 1 January 2025 in train, and puts 1 July 2025 in validation.

Prefix rules in prose are sound (no padding, keep loops, map renamed activities before cutting, truncate before feature extraction). Features correctly separate static attributes, prefix aggregates, and point-in-time historical joins, including the extraction-time experience trap. The leakage table covers every required field and the repairs for `documents_total` and `risk_score` are the right ones. Same-timestamp attributes on the Credit Review event itself get less attention than later events.

The code does not carry this design. Timezone-aware event times are compared with naive `data_extract_time` and `label_available_at`, which raises in pandas. `format_dataframe` output is passed straight into log iterators; conversion is not shown. The still-open prefix filter is computed and then discarded with `if False`, so a prefix can contain `Rejected` or `Account Activated`, and `Accepted` is not even in the exclusion list. `terminal_act` is `iloc[0]` on the unsorted CSV while `terminal_time` is the minimum timestamp. A mature activated row with missing `default_90d` falls through `== 1` into `activated_no_default`, which is the bias the study is supposed to prevent. The leakage assert only checks output key names, so it cannot fail. `risk_score_at_pred` is a list. `get_variants` is labeled “top” without ranking, and the temporal-feature frame is unused. Assumptions and a later IPCW/Aalen–Johansen hook are commented, and no fake survival API is invented, but this skeleton is not runnable as the audit pipeline it claims to be.
