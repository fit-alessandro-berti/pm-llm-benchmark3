The delivered analysis identifies the right broad story—logging and extraction artifacts drive most of the measured collapse, the August sign-off is a model gap, and missing checks are still unresolved—but it repeatedly mishandles the quantitative evidence it is supposed to discriminate with, and the required reproduction code does not implement the comparison it claims.

The evidence ledger has the required shape and covers most source types, yet several support/weaken assignments are inverted or unconnected. Stable appeals are listed as supporting employee noncompliance while the reliability note says they suggest process integrity. The July memo is likewise listed as support even though the note says it does not confirm a breach. Source-sequence availability is treated as weakening the technical-event hypothesis, and omitted high-value attributes are treated as weakening noncompliance. The directly-follows relations and the 25/30 enhanced-review traces without a compliance check are not ledger items, so the two sharpest structural facts are missing. The June–July fitness drop is also pinned to the 15 June timestamp change, although that period also contains the 31 May relabel and the 3 June `Queue Routed` insertion, which the staged alignments show are larger.

The ranking does not separate “cause of the drop” from “real operational concern.” Label change, ordering, and technical events are all scored as if nearly certain and nearly equal, despite the given path 32% → 58% → 81% → 89%: ordering is the smallest repair (+8 points), not the second cause. A real process change is scored at 30 even though sign-off appears on 210 of 620 August–September cases. “Increased cycle time” is cited as support, although 26.0 to 27.4 hours, with flat approval and appeal rates and an event-count rise matching the inserted activities, points the other way. The proposed discriminating tests for the top three hypotheses are the repairs already reported in the alignment table.

The causal list points `true_process` into `normative_model`, which is the opposite of the stated failure: the policy changed the process and the tree was not updated. Technical events are wired into `recorded_labels`. No hidden variable represents the untested bypass-versus-missing-event ambiguity or the EUR 50,000 attribute. The prose explanation is under the 180-word minimum and says the preprocessing “proves” behavior largely matched the old intent, which overclaims an 89% June–July fitness and a 66% August–September fitness.

The counterfactuals contradict the supplied results. Label mapping alone is predicted to lift fitness above 80%; the given result is 58%. Source ordering is predicted to add more than 10 points; the given result is about 8. Reverting the UI is assigned to real process change, so it tests the wrong hypothesis. Enforcement of compliance checks is predicted to lower fitness. Two of the eight rows are not hypotheses, and neither a missing-check audit nor a reviewer-level `Manual Override` test is included.

The four-phase program merges label remapping with technical-event removal, so it does not isolate the mechanisms already separated by the staged alignments. Variants and precision are not in the program, and the optional sign-off model is not checked for the precision loss the later caveat mentions. `sort_event_log` and `align_log_replay_fitness` are not real PM4Py operations.

The code block does not meet the executable-comparison requirement. The normative string has no top-level `->`, activity names containing spaces are unquoted, and `convert_to_petri_net`’s marking pair is ignored. Variant counts are not expanded; June–July is never built; trace boundaries are collapsed with `::`. Removing `Queue Routed` leaves empty labels. `discover_dfg` is imported and never called. Alignment and precision calls are commented out. The candidate tree places `Supervisor Sign-off` after `Decision` in an exclusive choice, so it does not accept the observed sign-off-before-decision pattern on both branches. The script, as written, cannot parse and does not print a raw-versus-normalized or fitness-versus-precision comparison.

The conclusion is the strongest section and stays inside the word limit. It still attributes the majority of the 93-to-32 drop to the label change and the ETL inversion, using the three-step recovery as evidence for a two-cause claim. It also folds the sign-off policy and the missing compliance checks into one residual concern, and “60% confidence in the absence of noncompliance” is stronger than an unaudited gap plus incomplete incident detection allows. The six governance rules do cover the six requested controls and are specific enough to be usable.
