6.8

The fork-join accounting in §§1–2 is sound and the case table checks out, but the required counterfactuals, one aggregate, and the Python do not. That is enough to keep this well below a near-flawless score.

**What holds.** Upstream, queue, service, sync, \(T^{FJ}=\max_i(q_i+s_i)\), downstream, final service, and total flow time are defined correctly, and the identity \(TFT=\mathrm{upstream}+T^{FJ}+d+s^F\) is the right anti-double-count. The explanation that \(\sum_i w_{c,i}^{\mathrm{sync}}\) stacks overlapping idle intervals is correct. All 12 rows and the F02, F04, F07, and F11 walkthroughs match the log: critical counts are A 2, B 8, C 2, with no ties. Ranking B over A, despite A’s flat 50-minute service, is the right conclusion, and the zero-benefit logic for a non-critical branch is explicit. Assumptions are labeled. §§5 and 7 cover the requested redesign dimensions and the main identification limits.

**Numerical and logical errors.**
- CF2 row F03 contradicts itself. \(B\) end moves from 09:45 to 09:35, while A ends at 09:30, so the new join is 09:35, still \(B\), and the optimistic cut is 10 minutes. The write-up sets the join to 09:30, switches the critical branch to A, and books 15 minutes. The optimistic total is therefore 92.5 minutes (mean about 7.7), not 97.5 (mean 8.1). The claim that criticality moves to A on F03 is false.
- Exact ties are dropped even though §1 says ties are listed jointly. CF1 F11 lands on A and B together at 11:20. CF2 F02 and F06 land on A and B together. All three are reported as A alone, so the shift toward A is overstated.
- Mean A synchronization wait when A is not critical is 13.2 minutes (132/10), not 11.7. The all-case mean of 11.0 is the figure that actually follows from the table. The 11.7 cell is inconsistent with the author’s own case data.
- §4 leaves a discarded draft in the first CF1 table (“Wait…”, “09:0008:55”, “2030”) before the corrected bound table. The worked flow times are printed without a minus operator.

**Downstream evidence left unused.** The task asks whether final-station congestion masks branch gains and whether \(d_c\) is a queue or a release rule. Same-resource precedence is in the log and was not computed. Of 176 downstream minutes, only 73 are blocked by the previous case on that resource (F05, F08, F09, F11 fully; F04 and F10 partly). The other 103 minutes occur while the assigned server is already free, including the whole waits of F01, F02, F03, F06, and F07. A crude 55% window utilization and “may need a third server” therefore overstate capacity as the mechanism. §7 correctly says policy cannot be ruled out, but the pilot in R2 asks to build a Gantt the timestamps already support. The O versus P bounds are a real strength; they are not a substitute for that split.

**Python does not reproduce the write-up.** Optimistic mode sets `final_start = new join`, so the printed CF1/CF2 “savings” equal the join reduction plus the entire original downstream delay. That is not the 1.25 and 8.1 minute means in §4. Pessimistic mode matches the prose (zero for CF1/CF2). `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(..., start_timestamp_key=...)` is not part of that function’s signature, and the call sits outside the inner `try`, so a normal PM4Py install fails before the later prints. `get_service_time` is the call that actually fits. Fork-join quantities are correctly marked as custom pandas, and tie handling in the base log is deterministic.
