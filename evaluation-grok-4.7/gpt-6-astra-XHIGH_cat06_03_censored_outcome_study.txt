8.2

The design is statistically serious and usually precise: activation is treated as selection, rejected and withdrawn cases are not recoded as non-defaults, O is not used as a dumping ground for censoring, and the February 2026 snapshot is not mistaken for a complete prospective test after the July and October changes. The deductions below are internal failures, not a preference for another estimand.

The primary six-way target is coherent. R, W, T, A-D, A-N, and open-at-180 are mutually exclusive under a declared 180-day disposition horizon, and the CIF factorization correctly keeps activation time out of the feature vector. The alternative targets are actually different questions. Two soft spots remain. q90(X, u) is not explicitly barred from using events between the landmark and activation; if it uses them, the integral is not the landmark estimand. O also pools cases that will activate after day 180 with cases that never activate. That matches the horizon definition, but it is a decision-relevant mixture and is only lightly flagged.

Cohort accounting uses all 48,000 and correctly refuses to split the 3,600 or 5,100 without dates. The 12.5% rate is confined to the labeled activated subset. Censoring types are separated cleanly, the d/n to (d+m)/n bounds are right, and positivity failure is not “fixed” with weights. The primary estimator is slightly blurred: administrative +35-day complete-case gating and IPW for still-missing feeds are both described as the main analysis.

The published follow-up dates check out: 17 November 2025, 13 October 2025, 4 July 2025, 16 April 2025, and maturity of May 2024 starts by 2 April 2025. The 1 May 2024 fit does not. Under the stated activation+90+35 rule, the earliest in-window activation cannot be lag-eligible before about 5 May 2024, so that fit has no valid default labels. “Sufficiently early activations can inform” the default component is therefore false under the paper’s own rule; using whatever feeds have already arrived reintroduces the fast-positive selection the paper forbids. The escape clause (“delay if inadequate”) never actually corrects the chosen date. The prose refit is all eligible history through 2 April 2025, while the code’s `refit_pool` is only the middle slice, described as ending in March 2025 but implemented through 1 April 2025.

Prefix rules are strong: no padding, no future length indicator, loops retained, ties not ordered by row number, and mappings restricted to what was knowable. Features respect the missing lifecycle pairs, so service and waiting times are correctly withheld. The leakage table is the right set of decisions, including point-in-time repair rather than trusting event-row cell values.

The skeleton follows that allowlist and censors at the coverage cutoff rather than the last event. Later activation does not overwrite open-at-180. Those are the right hard parts. It still does not implement the design it states. The runnable path labels Y at the February extraction and only then slices on start time; historical `case_table` calls are disabled. `observed90` requires `min(label_available_at) >= activation+90`, so one early stamp can discard a mature label, including a real early default. Quarantine does not clear `target`. Censored and feed-missing rows are NA in `target` while R/W/T/O are filled, so a naive complete-case fit on `target` biases the CIFs the comments say to estimate. `snapshot_group` cannot reconcile 18,900 versus 2,700. The forbidden-column assert cannot fail given the allowlist. For k>1, keeping cases whose stacked prefix length equals k is only correct if `get_prefixes_from_log` returns exactly one length-k trace per case.

Deployment limits and the rejection-versus-safety warning are sound. This is an unusually careful study with a few real calendar, estimand, and executable-audit errors, not a nearly flawless one.
