6.5

The write-up meets the required section structure, uses concrete object IDs and timestamps, separates some speculation from observation, and correctly keeps machine capacity last. The central ranking is still wrong on the timestamps it cites, and that error is repeated into the intervention benefits.

The decisive mistake is the B2 decomposition. J4 is ready at 13:10, RACK-7 is free at 14:35, J5 is ready at 14:40, and B2 starts at 14:50, with OVEN-1 idle throughout. Under the same full-batch rule used for B1, the start time is `max(13:10, 14:40, 14:35, 12:55) = 14:40`. The rack clears five minutes before J5 is ready, and the batch does not start at 14:35, so the rack is not binding. Calling 13:10–14:35 (1 h 25 min, “~85%”) tool delay, and only 5–15 minutes synchronization, treats overlapping necessary conditions as if the earlier one caused the start. Section 3 then ranks shared tools first because “RACK-7 sets B2’s start,” which sets J4’s pack and S1. Section 5 repeats this as “B1’s late cure … gates B2, J4 and S1.” Section 6 prices a second rack at “1 h 25 min for B2.” J4 also has slack into B2: speeding FIX-A does not move a start pinned by J5. The dispatch-leverage justification for Rank 1 does not hold. FIX-A’s 42-minute CNC-2-idle window and CAL-3’s 45-minute recalibration remain real local evidence; they do not establish the cross-instance chain.

The CAL-3 split is also off. J6 is ready at 16:05 and starts at 17:25 (1 h 20 min). J5 occupies TEST-1 from 16:10 to 16:35 (25 minutes, not 30). 16:05–16:10 is unexplained, and during the test CAL-3 is required as well, so that interval is joint machine-and-tool occupancy. The clean tool-only piece is the 45 minutes from 16:35 to 17:20, when TEST-1 is idle, plus about 5 minutes to 17:25.

Calculation (7) compares incompatible intervals. 08:10 is batch-readiness, not job release, while 0.4+1.1+0.3+1.0+1.1 = 3.9 h sums job-DFG median elapsed times from release through dispatch, and those medians already contain waiting. The “at least 7 h 20 min (~65%) represented nowhere” claim does not follow. J2 was not “never legitimately ready”: readiness at 08:25 precedes the 10:20 quarantine; the conflict is the override that still puts C2 into the 12:05 cure. Calculations (3) and (6) lack the explicit counterfactual required for every calculation. The 08:25-versus-10:20 “conflict” in the falsification list misstates that timing. The portfolio sentence that I1, I2, and I5 all turn into extra S1 dwell is right for pulling J1 forward and wrong for speeding J4 or J6.

The PM4Py block names the required functions and compares more than one frequency notion, but it would not run as written. `ocel.relations` does not carry activity and timestamp; those live on the events table (or an extended table). Grouping by object-type pair and taking `nunique` of `ocel:oid_2` does not count distinct pairs. `total_objects / unique_objects > 1` marks repeated traversal by the same object, not one Batch Cure fanned out across J1, J2, and J3. The comment that `barrier_wait` would return 3.92 h and 3.67 h assumes the readiness instants are already events.

What remains sound is the B1 arithmetic (3 h 55 min, 3 h 40 min, 10 min; 7 h 45 min aggregate; 7 h 15 min quorum plus 30 aggregate post-quorum minutes), the S1 dwell (5 h 55 min; 5 h 20–5 h 30 min against the 25- and 35-minute tails), the sample z-score bound `(n−1)/√n ≈ 2.04`, the 73-versus-115-minute oven contradiction, the lot-link and override caveat, the conjunction account of low utilization, and the logging-versus-operations intervention with perverse metrics. Those do not repair the binding-constraint error that organizes the ranking.
