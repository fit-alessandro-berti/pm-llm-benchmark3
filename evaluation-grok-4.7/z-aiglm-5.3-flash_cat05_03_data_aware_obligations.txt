The case analysis is strong and mostly correct, but the answer is incomplete against an explicit seven-part contract, and the code that was required as a judging deliverable is truncated and non-executable.

Sections 1–4 are the part that holds up. Causal replay, blank-as-no-update versus unknown-at-create, `event_index` tie-breaks, and the split among event, effective, record, and evidence time are stated precisely. The 11 classifications match a careful reading: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant; TX-305 and TX-310 are indeterminate for the right reasons (unknown amount, and EX-88’s certificate at 10:30 plus a conflicted witness); TX-302 is compliant because the enhanced review already discharges R1; TX-306 and TX-308 are compliant only under the R4-harmonized reading, which is disclosed; TX-311 is compliant because release is under DE. The 100,000 threshold is treated as inclusive. Percentages (about +56%, +4.5%, +24%, +23%, and the TX-308 decrease) are right. Section 4 correctly notes that reversing TX-311’s index does not flip R1, while a later effective time would.

Formalization is weaker than the prose. In the R3 clause, `ListUpd(...,J)` and `V(...,'jur',J')` are never unified, so the rule does not actually require the listed jurisdiction to be the case jurisdiction. R4 reuses `A` for both an amount and an event. R2’s violation line is not well-formed. The formal R1 silently adopts the “only R4-invalidating changes count” reading instead of parameterizing the strict alternative the prose admits. TX-301’s amount is labeled USD; the policy is in EUR and the log states no currency.

The decisive defects are missing required output. Section 5 stops mid-statement inside `pm4py.format_dataframe`’s exception handler. What remains does not forward-fill, snapshot release state, track reviews or material changes, flag R1–R4, join the exemption register, or emit diagnostics or a DECLARE/trace-order check. Sections 6 and 7 are absent, so the six control-flow failure modes and the logging recommendations were never delivered. Under a strict reading, a correct narrative that drops two required sections and ships non-runnable code cannot score higher.
