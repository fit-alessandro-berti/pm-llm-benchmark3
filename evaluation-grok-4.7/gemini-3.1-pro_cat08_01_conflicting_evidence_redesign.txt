The write-up follows the required structure and cites many real figures correctly (2,550 auto-to-fast-approval cases, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen, 31% vs 9% cost, dispatch-to-complaint mean 96h / p90 180h, premium high-value 22% vs 5%, evidence-to-manual median 26h, 7 reviewers and the 82% queue threshold, 6–18h gate, 30–45% portal effect, EUR 410). The three proposals are different levers, and the standard high-value pilot is a sensible scope. Those merits do not offset several decision-relevant errors.

The diagnosis misstates the evidence loop: repeated evidence requests are 26% of cases that enter that activity, not 26% of cases or of the 22% unclear-defect segment, and that statistic does not by itself produce the 140h median. “Resolving” complaints in 40h treats one hop (complaint → manual review) as full resolution. Fast approval before evidence completeness is an R2 problem; R1 is “manual or fraud review before fast approval,” not “no fast approval” and not “risk review” specifically. R3 and the post-close reopen deviation are ignored. The slow path is treated as the quality benchmark despite 12.5% repair rework on high-value devices.

Redesign A over-implements R1 by forcing claims above EUR 400 off fast approval entirely, discarding the observed fraud-review → fast-approval route, and equates “high-value” with “EUR >400.” Premium low-risk cases are already at an 8h median, so “beating 24h” is not a new gain. Routing premium high-value cases onto the reviewed path moves them from 20h to about 96h, which the temporal profile already flags as too slow for premium (manual review → repair quote). The table’s “++ premium CX” and “low compliance, hardcodes R1/R2” hide that. Standard processing is undefined, so R2 remains open for non-premium fast dispatch. The two fraud specialists are never loaded, although the flow sends work to risk review. “Compliance fines” are not in the data.

Redesign B calls 1.8h the fast-path median cycle time. That is only the fast-approval → dispatch edge. Segment fast-path medians are 8h, 13h, 20h, and 28h, so “becomes ~8–20h” is the wrong sacrifice. The drawn flow gates every fast approval (~2,300 dispatches) while the text claims a high-value target; two inspectors who are only slack outside repair campaigns cannot absorb that volume in steady state. The gate also leaves R1/R2 untouched, so “moderate” compliance is an unsupported upgrade. Not every reopen is a wrong part.

Redesign C’s table says the portal “eliminates” 140h loops. A 30–45% cut in repeats cannot do that. Forced pre-intake evidence also collides with the R2 premium low-risk exception and the 24h credit threshold; the stated sacrifice covers only digital literacy and abandonment (390).

“Dominated? Yes” for the current process violates the constraint that dominance cannot be established from this data, and it contradicts the table’s own negatives. Stop/go rules do not match: go wants reopen under 5% and error cost down more than 60% with under 12h added; stop fires only above 18h or if complaints fall under 50% (18% → 9%, still worse than the 6% reviewed-path rate). A part-selection gate cannot be expected to replicate full review quality. The pilot keeps standard high-value claims on a fast path that still breaches R1. Token fitness is a poor proof for B, because a gate absent from the normative net can look like a new deviation. The 15h “queue” cutoff is the median of manual review → repair quote, not a queue baseline.
