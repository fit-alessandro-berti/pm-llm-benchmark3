7.5

The interval arithmetic, time-zone conversions, DST-fold pruning, SLA breakpoints, and zeta boundaries are largely exact and internally consistent. That is not enough for a near-flawless score. Several stated conclusions are false or stronger than the evidence and the code.

The common-mode claim in A3 is wrong. Shared LEGACY-BER offset does not leave every label unchanged. Independent T-405 Alert_Received→Investigation_Start is [720, 1080] s. Cancelling a shared offset leaves [840, 960) s. At zeta 1 that moves from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to CERTAIN_VIOLATION (wholly above 780). At zeta 2 it moves from POSSIBLE_VIOLATION to CERTAIN_PASS (wholly inside [240, 960]). Checking only S1, which stays possible, does not support “does not change any classification.”

The PM4Py section reports a run that standard `conformance_temporal_profile` would not produce. That checker scores directly-follows pairs only. Alert_Received→Containment_Complete is never directly-follows here, yet the zeta-1 “flags” include T-402 (3720 s), T-403 (3450 s), and T-408 (3900 s). Keeping Investigation_Complete in the T-404 log also sits between Investigation_Start and Containment_Complete, so that pair is not checked either. “Every certain interval verdict matches the midpoint verdict” is therefore false for T-402 Alert→Containment at zeta 1: the interval result is a certain violation and PM4Py would not emit that pair. The 4-tuple `(duration, n_std)` assumption is narrower than the usual `(diff, mean, std)` diagnostics; a shape guard that never checks the inner tuple would not catch it. The hand-computed midpoints themselves (855, 1095, 930, 810, 900, 510, 3300, 138300) are correct. Attributing the non-consecutive flags to a PM4Py run is not.

Joint dependence is applied only to T-404. The same identity matters for T-406: S1 + S2 = Alert→Containment ≈ [3117, 3123] s, which is less than 15 min + 45 min. Both marginal rows are rightly POSSIBLE_VIOLATION, but they cannot be violations together. A11 says that case is noted where it matters and then omits this one. Separate Minkowski bounds also slightly overstate the coupled extremes.

Smaller defects: the prose “smallest |z|” rule misclassifies a union that lies entirely outside a band but on both sides (the code’s per-piece test does not); clipping a wholly negative ordered duration with `max(lo, 0)` can invert the interval; T-409 S2 is already a certain pass under the weak ±12 min bound without the order clip, so the order constraint is not what saves it; “needs the full 5 minutes” for T-403 is the wrong quantity (the breach tail is 241.5 s, not the 420 s width).

What holds up under recomputation: resolution-then-offset windows, CEST/EDT/BST/CET instants, the single surviving T-405 fold triple, T-404’s [597, 1023] and [2277, 2703] with the 20:03 / 24:57 joint clearance, T-402’s 1.5 s profile sliver and the unreachable S2 certain breach inside +120 s, T-408’s knife-edge at 3300 s and the 138300 s close, the 0/2/5 min and zeta 1/2/3 matrices, and the Section 6 offset thresholds that were checked (including T-401, T-403, T-406, and T-409’s −598.5 s / −898.5 s figures). Baseline certain SLA breaches are exactly T-402 S1, T-408 S2, and T-408 S3.
