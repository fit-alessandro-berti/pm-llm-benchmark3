7.5

The memo is a real decision document: it rejects national scale and full rollback, keeps the simple-house gain, stops survey-skipping and billing-before-activation, and ties rollout to explicit thresholds. The evidence table keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions apart, and the value-of-information section mostly says what would change the choice. Those strengths sit next to several errors that matter for this task.

The executive memo runs about 750 words, past the 700-word cap. It calls simple houses the “easy majority path”; they are 34% of the pilot, a plurality. It then treats coordinator ownership plus merged validate/schedule as a “proven slice.” The pilot changed four things at once, and the memo’s own assumption table says those pieces were not identified separately. The address-validation → scheduling speedup for simple houses supports the merge. It does not identify ownership as the cause. “The bundle deleted … permits” overstates the redesign: missing permit checks are an alignment finding, not a stated design change. “Survey capacity sat idle” overstates a drop from 78% to 52%. Business (8%) and rural (11%) never receive an operating path, and immediate routing on low-risk houses is ordered while risk attributes are still incomplete before pilot week 3. That gate is named as a risk and then left out of the action list.

Section 3 misstates precision. Precision is extra behavior the model allows relative to the log, not distance from a “sensible or intended” process. Observed alignments (billing before activation, repeat installs, missing permits, complaints after billing) explain fitness and normative deviation. They do not explain precision of 0.51, which is behavior allowed but not seen. Low generalization is then glossed as overfitting. That reading fights the low-precision reading and is left unresolved. The median/tail account is also too narrow. Overall median fell 3.4 days, and median improved for apartments (−2), business (−3), and rural (−1) as well as simple houses (−6). Only old buildings worsened at the median (+1). The distinctive simple-house result is the tail (p90 −8) against p90 deterioration everywhere else. “The median is dominated by the 34% segment” does not follow.

Value of information is concrete, and sequencing M7/M6/M3/M4 ahead of the skip RCT is coherent. Ranking the ownership-under-absence test last is weakly matched to a recommendation whose immediate scale action is single-owner coordinators at 93% utilization. The p95 of 62 days versus a six-week pilot window is also left unexamined.

The decision itself remains defensible: do not scale the bundle, do not discard the simple-house result, restore survey, permit, and activation gates, and require tail, rework, conformance, and fully loaded cost to clear baseline before any wider rollout. The grade is held down by the precision conflation, the median attribution, the unseparated “proven” components, the missing business/rural path, and the word-limit miss.
