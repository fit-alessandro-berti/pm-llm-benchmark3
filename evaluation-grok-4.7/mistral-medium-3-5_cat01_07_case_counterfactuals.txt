The write-up keeps the requested skeleton, but its counterfactuals are temporally inconsistent, internally contradictory, and in one case control-flow infeasible. Those are core failures, not presentation nits.

The feasibility model serializes Identity Check before Document Check before Assessment. That contradicts the rule that the two checks may run concurrently when originals are not required, and it conflicts with the K-411 path later in the same answer. Relation symbols are absent, so the partial order is not actually stated. Conditional constraints omit four-eyes, the rule that a preliminary compliance screen does not replace the final review, and the originals/identity split. K-411 is classified as a routing change even though both checks stay with the same pools; the change is task creation and concurrency. K-104 is only partly a routing change: its legal content is earlier advisory work plus a shorter final review after assessment.

K-104 is the decisive feasibility error. Final compliance is started at Tue 06:00 and, in the best case, finished before Assessment has even started. Factual Assessment runs Tue 15:10–19:10, and both rule 5 and the intervention require final compliance after that assessment. The feasibility paragraph asserts the opposite of the timeline it approves. The claimed Tue 14:00 decision is therefore impossible. The derivation also ignores the Tue 08:00 compliance backlog of about 16 hours. The table’s −12 to −24 h band excludes the derivation’s −6 h worst case, while the reversal paragraph says an incomplete packet can erase the gain entirely. Preliminary work saving 1–3 hours on a post-assessment review is never modeled.

K-219 assumes D2 starts at Mon 17:30 when the Mon 16:00 snapshot gives D2 about 5 hours of queue, and assumes A2 starts Tue 08:00 when that snapshot gives A2 about 6 hours. Service is pinned near the fast end of the handwritten range. The table then says −4 to +2 h while the derivation says −16 to +2 h. A clarification loop of 8–36 hours is reduced to a +2 h worst case without showing the arithmetic.

K-337 correctly treats the second information request as a decision-policy change and keeps a compliance review by C1, with A1 and C1 distinct. The timing does not hold. Thursday 08:00 compliance backlog is 5 hours, not 18. A Monday 08:30 decision against a Wednesday 09:10 factual finish is about 49 hours, not the stated best case of −72 hours. Best, base, and worst dates are ordered differently from the numbers attached to them. The worst case also reintroduces evidence-gathering that the intervention forbids.

K-411’s parallel path is the right control flow, and the logging caveat is real. The table’s −2 to −4 h and the derivation’s −2 to −18 h cannot both be the conclusion. Document start at Tue 11:30 does not account for D2’s Tuesday morning queue, and a 4-hour service is the floor of a 4–8 hour band. Readiness is repeatedly treated as final completion.

The capacity table points K-104’s displaced load at Assessment rather than other compliance work, and says K-337 affects no other cases while removing D1, A1, and later C1 load. The 100-case discussion is about 145 words against a required 250–400. It invents 20–40 hour delays, 10–20% rework, 5–10% appeals, and 12 assessment cases per day without a capacity derivation. Weekend, external-response, D1/D2 substitution, and timestamp-versus-concurrency effects are each a clause.

Ranking K-337 as high robustness inverts the risk. It has the largest clock-time upside precisely because it changes the allowed decision on a high-risk, high-value case with an unresolved discrepancy. That is the intervention that is dangerous despite speed. K-104 is the least transportable local result: one reserved slot on the only compliance reviewer helps the target by displacing everyone else, and one hundred reservations do not scale. The pilot pair inherits that ranking. A 20-case, 50/50 split cannot support the stated power to detect a 20-point appeal increase, and cycle time is the wrong primary endpoint for a policy that weakens evidence.

The synthesis is about 145 words against a required 220–320, repeats the ranking error, and calls K-411’s local effect both a few hours and, earlier, up to 18 hours. Two of the eight assumptions restate given rules. The 8–36 hour supplier assumption is already contradicted by the factual weekend response. The 10–20% handwriting-rework figure is unlabeled invention, filed as a resource assumption. The audit omits the assumptions that actually drive the timelines: immediate queue jumping, evening or weekend manager availability, and backlog snapshots being ignorable.
