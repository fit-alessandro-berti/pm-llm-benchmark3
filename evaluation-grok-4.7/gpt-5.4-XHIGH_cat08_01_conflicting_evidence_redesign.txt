7.4

The diagnosis and the three redesigns are specific, segment-aware, and tied to real artifacts, but the write-up is not nearly flawless. One performance claim is false, the frequency graph is treated as more complete than it is, and a few mechanisms over-claim what the edges can support.

The modal-path contradiction is handled correctly. Fast approval to dispatch is 2,300, the cited edge times (0.8 / 1.0 / 3.0 / 5.0 hours) match the performance graph, and 14.1% versus 3.3% reopen, the shrink from 31% to 9% cost advantage, the high-value splits (22% vs 5%, 18% vs 6%), fitness 0.91 with only 68% fitting traces, the three move-on-log deviations, R1–R3, the 26% repeat-evidence rate, EUR 95 / EUR 410 wrong-replacement costs, and 12.5% versus 4.0% repair rework are all used accurately. The redesigns are different mechanisms: segment gates on straight-through processing, a one-request evidence lane with assisted fallback, and a fulfillment gate plus an exception cell. Each states a sacrifice. The pilot order, holdout logic, and “measure through reopen, not just to close” test are defensible. Slow review is not treated as uniformly better.

The clearest error is the claim that `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` at 34 mean hours (p90 88) is “the single largest delay jump in the prompt.” It is not. Means of 96, 72, 54, and 40 hours are larger (`Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`, `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen`, `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board`, `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review`). Even if time-to-complaint is set aside as an outcome lag, the two post-reopen edges still exceed 34 hours. That misstates the performance graph the diagnosis depends on.

Process-mining rigor stops short of the frequency graph’s own arithmetic. Listed endings are 3,015 + 220 + 390 = 3,625, not 5,000. Listed inflows to manual eligibility review (intake 1,400 + evidence 890 + reopen 310) far exceed listed outflows (1,400). Complaint inflows sum to 510 against outflows of 430, and 510/5,000 is about 10.2%, not the stated 9.2% case rate. Those gaps mean 1,430 “entries” into missing evidence and “2,300 of 5,000” are edge counts in a partial graph, not established case volumes. A redesign sized off them needed that caveat.

Smaller reasoning slips accumulate. Calling a 6–18 hour pre-dispatch gate “much cheaper” than a EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost mixes cycle time with a conditional error cost and ignores the unknown error rate. “Underused” quality check is inferred from a repair stream of about 360 checks; that does not show spare capacity for high-value pre-dispatch. Redesign C’s exception cell is not explicitly shown to satisfy R3. Redesign B’s failure modes omit holding fraud cases in evidence completion and the Premium 24-hour service-credit exposure. The 72-hour tolerance of Standard low-risk customers, if status is visible, never becomes a design lever. Marking A and B “not dominated” is stronger than a partial, conflicting log can support; “conditional” was the claim the data allow.

The structure, trade-offs, and segment logic are well above a generic cycle-time program. The false superlative and the unexamined flow imbalance are enough to keep this out of the top band.
