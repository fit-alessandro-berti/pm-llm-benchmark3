7.5

The answer meets the required structure and is strong on scenario design, case-type trade-offs, on/off triggers, and redesign-caused failure. It is not nearly flawless: a few process-mining claims are wrong or misleading, and some of the supplied evidence is left unused where it would change the design.

The scenario set is valid. S1–S4 vary mix and disruption type, not just volume, and the four required stresses are all present. Mixes add to 100%, the 3,300/week peak rate is right, and urgent-bypass rates, queue times, specialist absence, and the 18h/72h targets are used rather than a single average cycle time. Priority is explicitly ordered, with caps so Urgent, Complex-panel, and Routine are not treated as simultaneously first. I4 and I9 are rejected for the right reasons, and each main bundle names a downstream station that can absorb the displaced load.

The clearest error is in S1: “only 8,530 reach Test preparation.” That 8,530 is the direct Pre-analytical check → Test preparation edge. It is not the count of cases that reach preparation. Of the 420 Manual accession correction cases, 310 continue to Test preparation and 110 join recollection, so the diversion story is overstated. In the same table, “Technical validation → Medical validation rising to 3,420” is a bad cross-window comparison: the normal-period absolute count is 3,900 over eight weeks, versus 3,420 over three weeks. The weekly rate and share do rise; the raw total does not. Those bypass percentages are variant statistics, not frequency-DFG edges.

Bottleneck ranking is too confident relative to the queue table. Calling pre-analytical the first bottleneck (9.5h) does not reconcile specialist review at 36h, medical validation at 22h, and assay at 18h. Migration can still start upstream, but the answer states the ranking as if queue magnitude supported it. The most diagnostic performance edges are largely unused: Technical validation → Medical validation 8h/24h, Hold for specialist review → Medical validation 18h/48h, Repeat assay → Assay run 10h/26h, and Manual accession correction → Test preparation 5h/16h. Night-shift clustering of repeats and the clerk error rise after 9h shifts are also unused. P4’s extended receiving hours can create the recollection loop the package is trying to remove, and that link is not made.

Accession correction is under-designed. Two specialists, 91% peak utilization, and a 14h queue are described as unblocking many cases, but the robust package mainly refuses to pull technical validators sideways. That refusal is correct; it does not replace a control for the two-person constraint. The mix warning band is also loose: red only above 50% Urgent+Complex, while the observed failing peak was already about 49% and had urgent p90 of 33h. P2’s 15%/8% M2 repeat triggers are not in the data and are not labeled as provisional.

Residual risks and the leading-versus-lagging dashboard are otherwise concrete, including two harms created by the redesign itself. Those strengths keep the answer well above the middle, but the DFG misread, the unequal-window comparison, and the unused performance edges are enough to keep it out of the high band.
