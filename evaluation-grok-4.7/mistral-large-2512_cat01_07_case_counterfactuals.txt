4.5

The write-up preserves the required section skeleton, four-eyes separation, and the crucial point that K-337 changes decision policy rather than only elapsed time. Those merits do not rescue the counterfactuals. Cycle times, start times, and scale effects are systematically inconsistent with the case logs and queue snapshots, so the numeric conclusions are not usable.

Factual durations are misstated. K-104 runs Mon 08:10–Wed 18:40, about 2.4 days, not 4.5. K-219 is exactly 48 hours, not 2.5 days. K-411 is about 29.5 hours, not 2.2 days. K-337 is closer but still short of the Mon 11:00–following Wed 09:10 span. The same error reappears inside the derivations: Mon 08:10 to Wed 12:00 is not 3.5 days, and Mon 11:00 to Fri 15:00 is not 5.0 days. K-411’s claimed 0.4–1.0 day gain is an artifact of that inflated baseline. Against the real 1.23-day fact, the stated counterfactual band of 1.2–1.8 days includes no improvement and possible worsening, yet every reported scenario is still a gain.

Scheduled times ignore the snapshots that were supplied to constrain them. For K-104, C1 has 16 hours of queue at Tue 08:00 and 8 hours at Wed 08:00, but preliminary work is placed Tue 08:00–12:00 and the final review is given a Wed 08:00 reservation. That is preemption of the scarce reviewer, not a feasible gap. The claimed 2–3 hour saving is also inconsistent with a review block that is still about four hours. For K-219, D2’s queue is about 5 hours at Mon 16:00, so D2 does not start at originals receipt (17:30). A2 still has 6 hours of queue at Tue 08:00, so assessment does not start at 09:30 merely because the file became ready overnight. Handwritten-but-standard work is timed with D2’s complex band (8–14 hours) rather than the standard 4–8 hour band, and rework is treated as if it could itself be a rejection. For K-411, D2’s Tue 08:00 queue is 4 hours, so a task created at 09:35 does not start at 11:30. Same-day assessment is asserted despite the stated growth in the assessment queue. Parallelism is then booked as new I2 and D2 demand, when the work content is unchanged and only the overlap changes.

The partial order is not a reliable feasibility model. K-219 is drawn as a total chain through identity and originals and, at the same time, as identity parallel to document check. Originals constrain document check only; identity may proceed earlier, but unconstrained concurrency applies when originals are not required. K-104’s displayed chain still places escalation after assessment, while the intervention and the later narrative move it before assessment. Precedence and four-eyes are asserted in prose, but the relation set itself is too ambiguous to enforce them.

The 100-case analysis names the right mechanisms and then contradicts its own arithmetic. Taking 200–400 C1 hours against roughly 40 hours of weekly C1 capacity is weeks of backlog, not a 2–5 day delay. Shifting 800–1,400 hours onto D2 in one week is likewise beyond one person’s capacity; saturation and exploding waits are the result, not a modest queue swap. Avoided K-337 work is closer to the actual third loop (about 3.5 hours of document work and 2.5 hours of assessment) plus external calendar wait, not 8–14 staff hours per case. Earlier K-411 readiness does not add 200–500 assessment hours. Timestamp fabrication versus true concurrency, a required scale topic, is left out of the rollout. The rollout is also too thin for the requested 250–400 word interference analysis.

Robustness and pilot design inherit those errors. K-411 is ranked most robust and called insensitive to external conditions in the same answer that says assessment congestion can erase the gain. Assumption 8 assumes no nonlinear assessment effect while the ranking calls that effect the principal risk. The synthesis is under the 220-word floor and repeats the insensitivity claim. K-337 is correctly treated as the dangerous, hard-to-reverse policy change. The K-219 stopping rule halts the pilot if D1’s queue shrinks by more than half, which is the intended spillover, not a failure. Rework rates of 20–40 percent, a 5 percent D1 baseline, and a 60/40 approve-or-reject split are presented as operative numbers without evidence.

The qualitative skeleton is therefore only partly sound: compliance still follows the last assessment, four-eyes is kept, K-337 is not mistaken for a pure speedup, and C1 reservation is recognized as non-scalable. The timestamps, baselines, capacity arithmetic, and ranking magnitudes are not temporally or operationally reliable.
