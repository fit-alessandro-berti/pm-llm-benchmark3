7.5

The answer is specific, internally organized, and largely faithful to the given E2O and O2O tables. It still has a repeated category error in the comparison matrix and one real timeline mistake in a required distortion, so it is not nearly flawless.

The assumptions are sound. Flattening is limited to direct E2O relations, convergence is replication of one event across several cases, and divergence is several secondary lifecycles serialized in one trace. Shared copies are treated as both participation evidence and duplication. Propagation is reserved for a labelled sensitivity variant. Those choices stay consistent in sections 4, 5, and 7.

Most citations check out. `e13` is on `O2/O3/I21/I31/P3`; `e14` is on `O3/O4/I32/I41/P4`; `e16` and `e24` touch `O1/O2/O3`, `I12/I21/I31`, `P2/P3`, and `D2`; `V2` really is `e19`, `e22` (`M2`), and `e25` (`M3`). Item traces are the clean `Add Item → Pack Package → Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` paths. Package traces are three events. Delivery traces are two. Payment traces are singletons. The order reconstructions that produce `Pack → Pack`, `Dispatch → Dispatch`, and both payment/confirmation orders are real, especially on `O1`, `O2`, and `O3`. Section 7’s denominators for `e16` (1 event, 1 delivery, 2 packages, 3 items, 3 orders) and `e19` (1 event, 1 invoice, 2 orders) are exact. Decision B matches the matrix, and the code block contains every required discovery call, stores named outputs, and treats DFG frequencies as case-notion-dependent.

The matrix then misfiles several phenomena under divergence, against the definition in section 1. Package traces do not serialize order lifecycles: `P1`–`P4` each have one pack, one dispatch, and one confirmation. `P3` mixing `O2/O3` and `P4` mixing `O3/O4` is hidden aggregation. Delivery divergence is likewise low. `D2` does not contain repeated package steps; `e16` and `e24` carry `P2` and `P3` together, and pack events are not even delivery-related. Calling that “Medium as aggregation risk” names the right business issue and the wrong column. Invoice “Medium” is also justified by the wrong mechanism. `V2`’s `Record Payment → Record Payment` comes from `M2` and `M3`. `O2` and `O3` sit on both events, so two billed orders do not create the extra activity. The item cell has the same slip in reverse: splitting `O3` into `I31` and `I32` is fragmentation, while within-item divergence is low.

Point 7 states the `O2` timestamps correctly (`e22` 12:40, `e24` 16:00, `e25` 09:00 next day) and then misreads them. The post-confirmation edge is `M3` / `completes_settlement`. The partial instalment, `M2` / `partially_settles`, occurs before confirmation. The warning against a causal DFG reading survives; the instalment it assigns to that edge does not. Point 6 handles the qualifiers correctly, which makes this a local contradiction rather than a general misunderstanding.

Smaller gaps remain. Order convergence omits `e17` and `e27`, shared by `O3/O4` through `D3`. Co-presence on delivery events can over-attribute orders to packages: `P3`’s `e16`/`e24` also list `O1` because `D2` carries `P2`. The four orders have four different activity sequences, so equivalent-execution clustering separates them rather than grouping “split versus consolidated” orders. The prose also runs past the requested length. None of these overturn the lead-object decision or the main counts.
