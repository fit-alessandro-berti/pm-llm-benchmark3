5.5

The memo has the right skeleton and avoids the worst traps, but it is not a coherent, auditable policy. The scoring rule, the rank table, the fairness constraint, and the scenario arithmetic do not describe the same decision.

## What holds up
Stage eligibility is applied correctly: I-03 is refused because diagnosis is already complete, not because of its high probability. I-05 (repeat alert, no new event) is suppressed, while I-07 (new customer log) stays eligible. One slot is reserved. The base-case money for the chosen trio is internally consistent: midpoint effects 0.19, 0.10, and 0.15 give €11.4k + €10k + €3.75k = €25.15k, minus €8.95k cost and €5.4k spillover, net about €10.8k. Section 5 names the right failure modes (value bias, non-native uncertainty, segment miscalibration, label contamination, spillover incidence) and attaches concrete devices: a segment capacity band, an intervention flag with uplift modeling, weekly segment recalibration, and a delay trigger for the externality charge.

## Decision rule does not drive the decision
Adjusted probabilities are defined (SME +0.06, Strategic −0.08) and then never used. Expected gain is midpoint risk reduction times breach value, minus €450, minus the full false-alarm burden, minus a flat €1,800. Ranked on that rule, after the €1,800 charge, the order is I-04 (about €7,150), I-08 (about €2,750), I-02 (about €900), I-09 (about €310 with the 0.7 haircut), then negative cases, with I-07 last at about −€1,000. The table says something else: I-02 is tier 2, I-08 is tier 3 tied with I-07, and I-09 is tier 4 tied with I-01. I-08 is also the fragile case. At its lower bound the direct result is 0.05 × €100k − €450 − €5,000 = −€450 before any spillover, yet it is selected and does not change the ranking once the adverse row shows it can wipe the portfolio out.

The €1,800 charge is 0.02 × 6 × €15,000. The €15,000 average is not derived from the book, a constant charge cannot change rank, and it is large enough to push every non-strategic case through zero. That makes the later “20–40% SME/Public unless net gain < 0” safeguard vacuous. The chosen set is I-04, I-08, and I-02, all Strategic Enterprise, which is the segment the memo itself says is over-predicted. The fairness budget is not applied and not waived in writing.

I-01’s uncertainty note says under-prediction. Public Sector calibration is described as broadly acceptable; the 0.04–0.09 under-prediction belongs to SME.

The 0.7 factor is an unmotivated shrinkage. On I-06 it is applied to the causal effect when the defect is an imputed queue feature that may have fabricated the jump from 0.20 to 0.52. “Wait” has no trigger, no expiry, and no relation to time left.

## Scenario table is only partly auditable
Optimistic effects 0.24, 0.125, and 0.19 sum to 0.555, not the stated 0.70. The euro pieces of that row do add to about €31k. “+19k to +22k” has no shown second endpoint. Adverse spillover of €3k per slot is not derived from the 0.01–0.03 band or from the 15–30 minute delay. False-alarm cost stays €8.95k in every scenario, including the optimistic row, where the alerts are assumed real, and the adverse row, where the problem states that the false-alarm burden is exactly the quantity that should move.

## Timing and repeat-alert logic
Nothing in the rule uses hours to deadline or remaining eligibility. I-09 is already in Diagnosis Started with 2.5 h left and a lower bound that still clears cost (0.18 × €20k − €450 − €800 = €2,350 before the haircut). I-07 has 1.5 h left. Both are told to wait, while I-04 (10 h, triage) and I-08 (8 h, triage) consume slots now. A 2-hour cool-down is longer than I-07’s remaining life, and there is no deadline override. The “10% relative feature weight” test is not an operational definition. I-05 is marked eligible in the table while section 1 and section 4 say it fails the persistence filter. I-02 and I-09 are also second alerts; only I-02 is treated as cleared, with no stated new event.

## Monitoring
The dashboard, Brier score by segment, delay cap, and monthly capacity share are real controls. The 10% case-level holdout does not address interference: the same memo’s premise is that a swarm worsens untreated diagnosis-stage cases, so a naïve A/B contrast overstates the treatment effect. Power at four slots a day, contractual limits on withholding a swarm, and a definition of “net benefit” for the last-100-interventions review (realized outcome versus model-implied gain) are unspecified. Non-monetary obligations, beyond the reserved regulatory slot and a quota the portfolio ignores, are not in the score.
