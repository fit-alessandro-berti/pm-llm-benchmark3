The write-up has the required seven headings and several defensible labels, but it misstates the log, contradicts its own control model, and does not deliver the required checking code. Those are core failures, not nits.

Section 1 does not map AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 onto real FEP-1 threats. It says MG-4 and ED-1 fail to address single-actor initiation-and-release, which is the threat MG-4 exists to cover, and it says MG-4 does not consider relationships even though MG-4 requires independence and no unresolved conflict of interest. “ED-1 bypasses receipt review” is false: ED-1 still requires an independent pre-settlement approval, and a rubber stamp is a weak MG-4, not a permitted bypass. ED-1 is treated as a corrective control; it is a conditional permission. Compromised-account use, warning overrides, and post-settlement irrevocability are missing as threat scenarios. AV-2 is credited with preventing fraud and proving legitimacy despite the stated limits (no goods-receipt test, no collusion detection, no proof of who controlled an account).

Section 2 names four equivalence levels but does not build them from timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, and collusion resistance. Evidence quality is effectively absent. Full equivalence is defined by later absence of detected collusion rather than by ex ante design. The rejection of “same expected loss” is a short list, not an argument: it never explains prevention versus detection, accountability, moral hazard, or irreversible tail exposure.

Section 3 uses the allowed labels and is right that AP-202 failed, AP-205 was stopped before completion, AP-206 is indeterminate, AP-208 failed, and AP-203 can meet the objective. The rows then contradict the timestamps. AP-207’s manager ratification is at 14:40, after release at 14:05 and before settlement at 16:00; the audit is the next day, after settlement. The row says “MG-4 before release” and “RA-7 before settlement.” AP-203’s audit at 15:00 is also after the 12:00 settlement, but the row says the audit was before settlement. AP-206’s approval was before release, not merely before settlement. Overridden AV-2 is listed as prevention on AP-202. ED-1 is listed as a preventive control where its preconditions are exactly what is in doubt. AP-204 is only OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET, with moderate severity and a rationale about “paired behavior,” while the fabricated receipt, missing MG-4, shared workstation, and audit on 8 May outside the recall window are the actual control-objective failure. AP-201’s rationale is low dollar exposure, despite convenience rather than necessity already being known; “missing” necessity is not missing. AP-209 calls corrective capacity none although the audit fell inside the recall window, and it pairs moderate severity with low residual risk. Corrective cells repeatedly restate detection.

Section 4’s hybrid label for a manager action after release but before settlement is the right semantics and is explained in one sentence. The table does not compare AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1. ED-1 is miscast as a hybrid pre-release event. Post-settlement review inside 24 hours is called purely detective, with no recall distinction. Section 3 does not apply the hybrid rule to AP-207, the case that requires it.

Section 5 names five cases and misses the required chain for most of them. AP-202 never says the warning failed to break the chain or what remains exposed after non-recovery. AP-204 says another workstation “would detect” the fraud and that it “may evade RA-7,” although RA-7 already found the fabricated receipt and was too late to correct it. AP-208 treats the risk as vendor legitimacy; the open risk is bank ownership after a three-day account change and a dead validation service, and the payment happened to be legitimate. AP-206 and AP-207 do not identify a loss mechanism, the control that interrupts it, the residual exposure, or the effect of an independent releaser. Qualitative ranges are absent.

Section 6 lists the requested monitoring topics, then invents an unexplained 5% same-user alert and never separates an alert from proof of misconduct. That omission matters for the u12/u13 pair, which may be a payment cell. It also skips the evidence to collect and the 100% one-business-day audit rule for emergency same-user payments.

Section 7 is not executable and misses the specification. The CSV is one row plus an ellipsis. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`. A variant filter is not a four-eyes test. There is no blocked-release path for AP-205, no case-facts frame, and no flags for emergency eligibility, AV coverage, manager timing, audit timing, bank change, or repeated pairs. `'Emergency' in log_csv` cannot work: emergency facts are not in the event log. The print line does not label the output as evidence rather than a verdict, and there is no account of the main version-sensitive behavior: the four-eyes filter keeps cases in which the two activities have different resources, so nominal violators are the complement, while AP-205 has no completed release.
