3.1

The write-up keeps the seven required sections and a few real episodes (A03’s 08:20 start, B01’s urgent stop, D06’s 11:30 appointment, D05’s unique skill). Those fragments do not carry the diagnosis. Eligible sets, observed choices, and priority direction are wrong often enough that the queue labels are not usable.

Priority is treated backwards. The prompt defines 1 as highest and 3 as lowest. The text calls A02 (priority 3) higher priority than A04, and calls C02 (priority 3) higher priority than C03 (priority 2). Later tests inherit that inversion.

QA is labeled non-preemptive priority at confidence 85. The log falsifies that rule. Service order is A01, A03, A02, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08. A02 finishes at 08:52, before A04 starts at 08:58, so A04 does not overtake A02. At 08:20, A04 (priority 1, estimate 7, arrived 08:13) is already waiting and is skipped for appointment job A03. At 08:32, A02 (priority 3, estimate 20) starts while A04 is still waiting. At 09:05, A05 starts over A08 (priority 1). At 09:32, A07 starts over A08. The section 2 table also puts A02 in the 08:02 eligible set before A02 has arrived, drops A04 from the 08:20 set, calls 09:20 A06’s appointment time (the appointment is 09:15; the note says it started late), says FIFO would pick A07 over earlier A06, and states that estimate 12 is less than 9.

QB’s headline, FIFO plus a preemption, is the only primary label in the right family, and the body abandons it. At 09:00, B01 is the only job, so that start is not evidence of preemption. At 10:14 the resource resumes B01; it does not select B07. B07’s 10:30 appointment is explicitly not honored, and B07 runs at 10:56 after B04 and B06. The reconstruction omits the choices that damage strict priority: B01 resumed at 09:31 ahead of waiting B03, B03 started at 09:48 ahead of priority-1 B05, B01 resumed at 10:14 ahead of B06, and B04 started at 10:28 ahead of B06.

QC is labeled earliest-deadline-first at confidence 80. That rule fails the opening choices and the later ones. C1 is free at 10:10, and the run order is C02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C01, C07. C02 is not the earliest arrival; C01 arrived at 10:00 and is near the end, matching its own note. The 10:18 row records C04 as the selection; C03 started then. The 11:34 row records C07 and treats that as EDF confirmation; C06 started then, and C07 is last. The priority column is present and fully populated; the identifiability cell says it is missing. SPT is the rule that actually matches C02, then C03, then C06, and the text notices the first match and still files the queue as EDF. C05 is the one choice EDF and priority both predict, after C05’s 10:50 deadline is already past. C01 before C07 contradicts EDF, priority, and SPT together.

QD is labeled skill routing plus SPT, with preemptive priority. Skill eligibility is a real constraint: D05 needs D-F, and D07 needs D-X. It is not the ordering rule claimed here. D05 arrives at 11:07 and has one segment, 12:20–12:50; it is not served immediately and is not paused in service. At 11:25, D-M selects D04 (estimate 10, priority 3) while D08 (estimate 8, priority 1, arrived 11:16) is waiting. The table calls D04 the shortest mechanical job and omits D08. No QD job is preempted. Section 3 says D06 starts after D07; D06 runs 11:30–11:48 and D07 runs 11:48–12:16. D-X’s idle hold from 11:20 to the D06 appointment is real and is not analyzed against D07 and D08, both of which D-X could have taken.

Section 4 does not separate the live competitors. The QA test uses a dispatch timestamp relative to 08:20 even though the start is already on the appointment and a priority-1 job is waiting. The QC test predicts priority would select lower-priority C02, then uses a skill blockage on an all-general queue as the discriminator. Section 5 lists four exceptions, but appointment tolerance of ±2 minutes is imported from the arrival-scan bound, and the effects do not revise the wrong labels. Calibration 08:52–08:58, C1’s prior job until 10:10, D-F’s meeting versus the idle gap after 11:36, exact estimate-versus-duration matches, and an SLA policy switch are left unused. Section 6 names useful fields and still misses remaining time at B01’s stops, the 11:36 D-F state, and whether C06 was visible at 10:32. Section 7 calls QC the most identifiable queue on the strength of the EDF label. On these logs QC is the least stable single-rule inference; a FIFO-plus-appointment reading of QA, and FIFO on D-M, are much tighter. Confidence of 70–85 sits on top of misread decision sets.
