The answer covers every required section, uses concrete object IDs and timestamps, hedges causality, keeps machine capacity last, and treats the J2 supervisor override and the missing lot-to-job links as real issues. Those strengths do not offset several quantitative and causal mistakes that the requested decomposition and ranking depend on.

The CAL-3 split does not add up. J6 is ready at 16:05 and starts at 17:25, which is 1 h 20 min. J5 occupies TEST-1 from 16:10 to 16:35. From J6’s readiness to 16:35 is 30 minutes, while J5’s test itself is 25 minutes. The write-up calls that occupancy 25 minutes and then adds 45 minutes of recalibration and 5 minutes of handoff. That sums to 75 minutes, not 1 h 20 min. The opening 16:05–16:10 gap is dropped, so the 1 h 20 min headline over-assigns time to CAL-3 and the 45-minute correction is arithmetically inconsistent.

The RACK-7 claim is contradicted by the same answer’s own counterfactual. RACK-7 is free at 14:35, J5 is ready at 14:40, and B2 starts at 14:50. The last binding input is J5, five minutes after the rack is already available. The 10-minute tail matches B1’s ready-to-start lag. The FIX-A figure uses a different counterfactual from the one stated. “Start when the machine was free” is evidenced only for the recorded CNC-2 idle window, 10:05–10:47 (42 minutes). Reporting about 1 h 50 min as fixture-blocked treats the whole arrival-to-start span as tool delay. Two machine-summary conflicts go unremarked. OVEN-1’s longest recorded event gap is 73 minutes, but the cited idle is 115 minutes. TEST-1’s longest gap is 39 minutes, while 16:35–17:25 is 50 minutes. Those figures can be reconciled only by extra machine events or a different meaning of “idle,” and that distinction matters for the utilization argument.
