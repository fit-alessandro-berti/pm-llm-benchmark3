8.4

The design is strong on the hard parts of this problem: portfolio-level discrete-event state, calendars, latent objections, paired comparisons under shared demand shocks, separate submission and end-to-end clocks, and a real split between service, queue wait, and applicant delay. It still has enough unclarities and local slips that it is not nearly flawless.

The scope covers applications, external parties, work items, rosters, itineraries, and shared queues. Architect batches, neighborhood notice extensions, and bundled travel are real cross-case mechanisms, and in-flight cases keep age and appointments across a policy cutover. Rework is the weak part of that scope. “Reopen only affected work” and “invalidate stale results” never define the invalidation matrix. A planning correction that restarts a 14-day notice is a different process from one that only repeats the review, and appeal re-entry is named but not given a return stage, resource, or clock rule.

The estimation plan is process-mining literate. It uses the completion projection for variants and directly-follows graphs, refuses to read parallel completion order as precedence, and warns that replaying observed waits on top of simulated queues double-counts congestion. The planner check is a valid lower bound: about 400 planning reviews a week with a 3.5-hour median cannot fit in 320 scheduled hours even before drafting. Calling that load “about 700 hours” is only the degenerate bound in which every below-median review takes zero time, so the mismatch is understated relative to the lognormal sketch given in the same section. Fire capacity is correctly left as FTE to be reconciled. Inspectors are then stated as four people on a Tuesday–Friday field week, and clerks, planners, environmental reviewers, and legal officers are likewise turned into headcount. That sits badly with the later rule that hourly headcount and weekly FTE are different quantities. “Four clerks available for permit work on Monday” treats portal support as lost permit capacity without putting that reading in the assumptions.

Assumptions cover every requested topic and keep missing objections, violation status, area, and sensitivity latent. The Monday clerk rule and the inspector headcount claim should have been in that table as alternatives.

The experiment matrix has a baseline, D1–D10, targeted interactions, and four stresses, and it compares each policy with baseline under the same stress. D3 is specified as same-architect, same-district bundles. The process also needs geographic batching of unrelated cases in a district, which is the travel mechanism behind D3/D5 and the “batch by district” scheduling note. “Compatible” in the skeleton does not settle that. D10 says “the same levels” as D1–D4 while those arms are multi-level, so the combined arm is not identified.

Warm-up, paired seeds, keyed draws, censoring, heavy-tail quantiles, and the split between simulation error and input uncertainty are all sound. The code runs one fitted draw per stress. The outer loop over refits described in the uncertainty section is not in the runner. Per-type p90 gates of about 5% sit next to an admission that sparse type and district tails are weakly identified.

Validation is concrete, including conformance versus a free simulation, conservation of WIP, holdout with frozen information, and a pilot before any causal claim. The excerpt audit is thinner than the log that was supplied. P001’s Saturday completeness check is a real calendar contradiction. P002 also has a Sunday completeness check, a Saturday inspection, and Sunday drafting, which bears more directly on clerks, inspectors, and planners.

Decision rules track approvals, rejections, open cases, and pre-submission delay, so a faster rejection or a shifted submission date cannot silently improve the score. The stacked gates (median, every type’s tail, 90% of input draws, three of four stresses) can be statistically or substantively unresolvable. They are labeled as proposals.

The skeleton separates PM4Py log handling from the engine and uses a plausible 2.x surface: `format_dataframe`, variant and DFG discovery, performance DFG, `parse_process_tree`, and conversion to a Petri net. The 33-hour-9-minute submission gap and the two scheduling-to-inspection follows are right. Service and wait stay in separate columns and are left missing when only completions exist. The loader depends on an invented path and a blank-line split instead of an embedded frame. Routing covers flags, latent requirements, and the low-risk inspection waiver, including unknown violations staying on the ordinary path. Appeal, legal branching, and notice-restart rules are not in the placeholders.
