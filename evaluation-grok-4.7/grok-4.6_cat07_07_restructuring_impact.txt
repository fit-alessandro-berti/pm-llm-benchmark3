7.5

The forecast is well structured, covers every required heading, and correctly treats the proposal as rerouting plus queueing rather than a simple handover cut. East versus North/South, fairness, learning, and the ban on reading success from early averages are handled seriously. It is not nearly flawless.

The largest analytical miss is treating “Technical Shared Service” as a node in the handover-of-work network. That network is resource-to-resource. A shared queue shows up in the activity DFG, or in a handover graph only after resources are aggregated. The same slip appears in the mechanism map (“a new hub”), the optimistic scenario (“a star”), and the code comments. Activity-based resource similarity is also misstated: it is similarity between resources, not between caseworkers and activities.

Utilization is over-read. Pooled tech capacity is about 74% today; “15% higher” does not by itself push the pool to the 91–93% Legal/Risk region. “Less idle, so service time falls” mixes utilization with service time, and “higher utilization predicts longer tails, not shorter cycle times” is too unconditional for a pooling change. The claim that average cycle time is dominated by a large mass of cases the proposal does not move sits badly next to a 49/100 shared-approval path.

The 41-versus-36 tech gap is asserted as re-asks; it could be one-way completions or handoffs elsewhere. The 0.3–0.5 day P50 shift is unexplained precision. East cannot supply the stated ≥2,000 baseline cases in 12 weeks at 520 per month.

The pilot contradicts itself. Phase 1 sends `{legal, risk, security}` cases into Technical Shared Service, while the same design says not to merge Legal/Risk into that queue. “Any two amber or one red” never defines amber versus red. The tech wait tripwire is 2× an unobserved P90, which is loose relative to the tail risk argued elsewhere. “Rollback if warnings appear on both pilot teams, not only North/South” confuses the control with the pilots. Segment-level power for high-complexity rework and P90 is not addressed.

The PM4Py sketch has the right comparisons, but it would not run as written. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key`. NetworkX handover graphs do not yield weights via `edges.items()`. `case:service_time` / `case:waiting_time` / `case:cycle_time` are not the columns that API produces. With only `time:timestamp`, service time and waiting time are not separately identified, and the sketch never says so. `roles_alg` is unused, and the network results are discarded.

These are localized defects in an otherwise strong conditional “pilot, and do not ship the package” recommendation. They are still enough to keep this well below a near-flawless score.
