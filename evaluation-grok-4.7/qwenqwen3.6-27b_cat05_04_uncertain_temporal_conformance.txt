The interval method is set up correctly (offset direction, resolution before clock correction, stated mobile-delay and causal assumptions, and the only ingestion-consistent DST fold for T-405). Most SLA labels happen to survive that setup. The numerical work and several required conclusions do not.

T-408 is mishandled on the nominal timestamps alone. Investigation start 15:10 to containment 16:05 is 55 minutes, so S2 is a certain breach of the 45-minute limit under HUB’s ±1.5 s band, not a possible violation near 2700 s. Containment on 2026-06-01 16:05 to close on 2026-06-03 06:30 is 38 h 25 min, so S3 is a certain breach of 36 h, not a ±3 s overlap of 129600 s. Section 4 then claims +2 min tolerance clears both; it clears neither, and +5 min still leaves S2 a certain violation.

T-402’s alert-to-start profile row is also wrong on the answer’s own figures: [958.5, 1531.5] overlaps the stated zeta=2 band [240, 960], so it is a possible violation, not a certain one. The +5 min S1 sweep claims several maxima fall under 1140 s when section 3 lists 1141.5, 1160, 1171.5, and 1203.

Duration endpoints are repeatedly mis-subtracted (T-401, T-402 start-to-contain, T-403 vendor upper bound, T-404 start upper bound, T-405 legacy bounds, T-406 start-to-contain lower bound, T-409 upper bound). Many SLA labels stay the same only by luck. Almost every testable temporal-profile pair is missing, despite the requirement of one row per testable pair.

The Python block is not executable: the activity list is longer than the case and timestamp columns. It does not build DST alternatives, inferred starts, delivery latency, or interval classifications, and the PM4Py call is only a guarded stub. Governance recommendations are the soundest part of the write-up.
