The write-up covers every required heading and stays appropriately conditional, but several claims conflict with the supplied numbers, the pilot cannot identify the mechanism it is meant to test, and the PM4Py sketch misses required analyses and misuses functions.

**Handover arithmetic is wrong.** Current listed handovers sum to 343 per 100 cases; management’s post-change table sums to 359. The local specialist loop rises from 67 (36+31) to 77 (41+36), and the approval loop from 81 to 92. Section 1 still says centralization should decrease total handovers per case, and the optimistic scenario repeats that decrease. Those statements contradict the only frequency evidence given. The useful finding is that “fewer loops” is a narrative the routing table does not support.

**The main failure mode in the data is underweighted.** Cross-team handovers are rare (6 per 100) but already have an 8.9h median wait and an 18% rework rate, explicitly because case notes differ across locations. The new design sends about 41 technical cases per 100, plus legal/risk and shared approval, across locations. That scales the known bad handover, unless notes and ownership are fixed first. Lost local context appears later as a general risk; it is not tied back to that 18% baseline.

**Queueing is internally inconsistent.** Pooling three specialists can cut wait for a given utilization because East is a single server at 82% while North and South sit at 71% and 68%. Section 1 acknowledges load balancing, then Section 4 says higher utilization “mathematically guarantees” longer waits. Kingman’s approximation does that only if service time and variability stay fixed and the server count stays fixed. Pooling changes the server count. A guaranteed tail explosion is stronger than the formula allows. The repeated claim that early gains are East “clearing its backlog” is also unsupported: East’s 8.6d median and 22.8d 90th percentile, at steady monthly volume, describe chronic congestion, not a one-time stock.

**The pilot does not test pooling.** Moving only East into a shared service leaves a one-person “pool,” so load balancing cannot appear. Moving N_Tech and S_Tech into that pool while North and South remain the control group removes the control groups’ specialists and contaminates the comparison. The alternative—medium-complexity cases across all teams—is named and then dropped; the recommendation locks onto the East-only design. Guardrails are also mis-scaled. Legal/Risk already wait 9.6h at 91–93% utilization, and the plan itself expects 10.5h. A 6h cap, an 8h rollback, and a 95% utilization tripwire either fire on today’s legal/risk work or do not measure the technical queue. Four baseline weeks and eight pilot weeks are short relative to a 22.8-day tail: late-start cases are censored, and learning loss will not show in that window.

**Scenarios are not forecasts against the baselines.** Team medians, 90th percentiles, rework (8.2/9.0/11.5%), SLA misses (6.5/7.2/9.8%), CSAT, and handover-specific rework rates are barely used. “Rework drops,” “tail increases,” and “rework skyrockets” have no ranges. The central case adopts management’s frequencies, including longer specialist, approval, and legal waits, and still says average cycle time stays stable or improves, without showing why hour-scale wait changes would move day-scale cycle times.

**Indicator and code sections have concrete method errors.** `get_service_time` is service time, not specialist waiting time; waiting time is the role of `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, and this log has no lifecycle pair, so service time is not identified. Counting rows whose `org:resource` is already a shared-service resource counts work in service, not the queue. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe(...)['cycle_time']` is not a real cycle-time output; case durations need something like `get_all_case_durations`. The sketch never calls `discover_working_together_network` or `get_variants` / `filter_variants`, does not filter `required_specialty` or `customer_segment`, and does not compute SLA misses. `local_expertise_flag`, `handover_reason`, `outcome`, `priority`, and `rework_flag` are mostly unused, so the code does not connect the restructuring to the measures the log was built to support. Discovered networks are not compared.

The recommendation correctly refuses an unmonitored full rollout and warns that an early average can hide North/South and tail effects. That conclusion sits on a pilot and a metric plan that would not reliably detect those effects.
