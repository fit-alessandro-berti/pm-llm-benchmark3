The memo meets the required structure and lands on a defensible hybrid: keep coordinator ownership and merged scheduling for simple low-risk houses, stop survey skipping and billing-before-activation elsewhere, and gate any wider rollout. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated; the five metric conflicts are explained with fitness/precision, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal profiles, resource networks, and segment results; A–E and M1–M8 are all covered with decision conditions.

It is not nearly flawless. The executive memo cites the improved visible cost (EUR 184 to 161) and omits the worse estimated full cost (EUR 198 to 207), so the main decision section understates the central cost conflict. It also treats simple-house gains as “clear” while the case-mix difference, incomplete risk attributes before week 3, sparse start times, and six-week pilot sit mostly outside that memo. DFG and temporal rows drop the edge arrows, which makes those evidence lines harder to read. Ranking conformance monitoring (M7) above the randomized skip test (M1) and full downstream cost (M4) overweights an operational check relative to measurements that could reverse the strategic choice. The recommendation is labeled pure E while actually narrowing E by withholding full R even from simple houses. Thresholds such as “about 85%” utilization are stated without a link to the observed 93% load.
