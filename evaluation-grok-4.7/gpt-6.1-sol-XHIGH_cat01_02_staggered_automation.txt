The arithmetic, estimands, and claim discipline are unusually strong, and the checked pre/post contrasts, donor-specific DiDs, endpoint slopes, Manski-style CSAT bounds, and bias envelopes match the panel. Availability is kept distinct from completed fast-path share, median is not collapsed into p90, absolute deterioration is separated from relative contrasts, and the universal “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is correctly rejected. Cohort-wise comparisons, interference through the fraud desk, and regression to the mean are handled with real identification logic rather than estimator name-dropping.

The score is held down by gaps between the stated design and the evidence actually used. The primary strategy promises cohort event-time effects, but the reported analysis and the fitted models are static post-window averages; the constructed relative-time indicators are never estimated. That matters because the series are dynamic: Alpha/Beta medians peak in June and recover with the controls by August, and the rework dip is largely an activation-month drop followed by a rebound, while May’s rework jump is common across branches. Broad-window DiDs therefore mix a transient dip, muted deterioration during a shared shock, and a late recovery. The conditionally causal bullet then compresses donor-specific median contrasts of about −2.6 to −6.4 hours into “roughly −3 to −6,” under assumptions the answer itself says are not established.

Section 7’s safe median claim says adopters’ medians “improved” relative to comparison trajectories. Alpha is essentially flat and Beta’s raw median rises; the accurate statement is that they deteriorated less than the selected controls. The strategy table also lists baseline trend, complexity, capacity, and load adjustments that the coded availability model does not use. E3 defines a use contrast partly through completed fast-path share, which the same row correctly says is not assignable. The scale-up rule’s 5% median, 5% p90, 1 pp rework, and 0.10 CSAT cutoffs are not tied to a baseline, a loss function, or a minimum detectable effect, and “queue-sharing team clusters” is ambiguous about interference. Those are material unclarities in an otherwise rigorous answer, not cosmetic nits.
