The analytical core is unusually exact: event counts, copied-event inflation, transit-time reweighting, invoice-latency deflation, the vanished `Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` edge, the `O2`/`O3` payment cycle, and the per-order 1:1 log-skeleton coincidence that hides a 6:4:3 item–package–delivery graph all check out against the given E2O relations. Section structure, required distortion types, and the lead-object argument are complete and internally consistent.

The score is held down by a concrete failure in the required code, plus smaller constraint and wording problems. `ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"]` (and the sibling `unique_objects` / `total_objects` lookups) invert the real OC-DFG shape, which is `edges[object_type][(a1, a2)][...]`. That block is the script’s quantitative reconciliation, and it would crash. The prose also runs well past the 1,800–2,800-word aim. Calling the delivery-level mean the “true” transit time in the matrix sits awkwardly against the later, better claim that item weighting is valid for customer-experienced delay. The `Add Item → Add Item` self-loop on `O1` is a real sequential order history, so grouping it with parallel-subobject artifacts is slightly too broad.
