5.7

The design covers the required eight sections and correctly keeps calendars, non-stationary demand, service-versus-wait separation, and unobserved objections in scope, without claiming an optimum. It is not nearly sound.

Process-mining grounding fails the stated PM4Py contract. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` does parse this operator string; `tau` is a silent skip, not a queue delay. The parallel block is an AND of planning and optional fire/environment reviews, but branching is repeatedly described as exclusive “versus” choices, including public notice and inspection. Imports use nonexistent `pm4py.algo.mining` paths; performance DFG is imported and never computed; case arrival average is replaced by cycle time; no service or waiting columns are built; routing has no placeholder. Timestamp strings are subtracted with `.dt`, and the lifecycle join keys a column that is actually the index, so the skeleton would not run.

Statistical design is also inconsistent. Variant share 9% is converted to a rework probability with an odds formula. Warm-up of about 30 days and “steady-state” intervals are incompatible with 1.7× seasonal demand and 140–260 day tails. A Pareto shape of 1.2 has infinite variance, which undermines the proposed Sobol analysis. Service times stay pooled across case types, so type-specific policies are weakly identified. Objection mixture weights, the 30% batching gain, and SLA day targets are invented. Architect “fairness” is defined as variance of case counts, and D2’s inspection-quality risk never enters the decision thresholds.
