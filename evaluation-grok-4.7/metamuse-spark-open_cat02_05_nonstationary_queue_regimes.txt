6.5

The regime logic is strong, but the write-up is not numerically reliable enough for a high score. Demand, mix, outage, recovery, and the staffing change are separated; recovery is not treated as a normal high-throughput state; pooling distortions and Little’s Law limits are discussed in the required format. Those parts meet the quality bar. The grade is held down by repeated arithmetic errors, prose that contradicts the code, and several precise statistics that are unsupported or impossible under the stated definitions.

What holds up:
- The primary segmentation is operationally coherent, with an explicit outage day and a separate recovery window.
- Both alternatives are real alternatives, and the comparison of parsimony versus diagnosis is useful.
- Weekday/weekend composition, the complex-mix step, and the outage/recovery throughput illusion are the right pooling failures. Many core regime means check out (baseline weekdays 100.1 arrivals and 20.3 WIP; high-mix weekdays 114.0 and 44.3; intervention weekdays 115 and 121).
- The hourly-vector versus `peak_08_10_pct` mismatch is a genuine data-audit finding.
- Baseline and high-mix Little’s Law bounds are computed correctly (about 146 minutes versus 280–317 open minutes), and median service is not used as mean flow time.
- The Python block covers the required tables, dispersion, autocorrelation, a non-definitive change-point scan, windowed Little’s Law, and a clear statement that these aggregates are not a PM4Py log.

Material errors:
- Narrative R2 is four weekends with mean arrivals 42.8. Those four days average 41.75, and the code’s R2 mask is only 11–12 April (mean 40). 18–19 April are in the prose regime and in neither primary code mask.
- Daily arrival dispersion is wrong: variance is about 870, SD about 29.5, and Fano about 9.7, not variance 1,020, SD 31.9, and Fano 11.3. Within-baseline Fano is about 0.23, not 0.33. WIP variance is about 165 and Fano about 5.5, not 143 and 4.8.
- The 10–11 and 13–14 spikes on 21 April are 58/115, or 50%, not 60%.
- Change-point scores of 3.8 and 3.2 are incompatible with the stated score, `|post − pre| / pooled SD`. For arrivals, that score at 20 April is about 0.4. The quoted scores cannot be what the code prints.
- The 15–17 April Little’s window uses cumulative completions 289 and mean WIP 45.3. Completions are 299 and mean WIP is 44.7, so 8.03/h and 338 minutes are wrong. “(58→47→35)” mixes completions with WIP.
- Weekday mean WIP is about 34.9, not 33.9. Weekend staffing is 2.625 FTE, not 2.56. Recovery is about +21% versus baseline completions, not +14%; 18.1 cases per FTE is about 17.9.
- Complex-share median is 33, not 32.5. The unaccounted flow gap is about 110 cases, not 70–80.
- Burning about 90 excess cases at the observed 6/day surplus takes about 15 days. The text says both 7–8 days and about two weeks.
- The 1.13 pressure ratio was observed near 6 FTE, then reused as if it were the result of cutting to 5.5 FTE.
- Spearman −0.31, pooled correlation +0.05, arrival–completion correlations 0.92 and 0.12, detrended autocorrelations, and the CUSUM path are stated to two decimals but are not produced by the code. The “95%” band is also described as `1/√n`, which is the standard error, not the 95% band.

Conceptual limits: daily `mean_wip` is used as opening and closing stock after the audit correctly says those boundaries are unobserved. Flow balance, not the WIP level, is the relevant boundary check. The added “1.5 nights” that turns a 5-hour open-window sojourn into a 22–23 hour calendar sojourn is not identified from these aggregates. The baseline “applicable” verdict is still directionally defensible because arrivals and completions differ by only about 4 cases over 7 days, but the boundary argument given for it is the weaker one.
