4.1

The write-up has the right headline — S is too permissive for enforcement, C memorizes a handful of variants, and a constrained hybrid is the sensible direction — but the formal reading of both models is unreliable, and the proposed third model does not implement the policy it claims to implement. For a governance review, those are decision-breaking errors.

**Language membership is wrong on the probes that matter.** Model S is `Register` then a parallel of four `X(activity, tau)` skips, then `X(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)`, then `Release`. There is no loop and no `Correct Dossier`. So S does enforce exactly one approval, and it cannot emit a second `Credit Check` or any correction. Section 3 still lists both approvals and B8 as behaviors S admits, while Section 5 calls `X` exclusive. Those claims contradict each other and the tree. S correctly rejects B6 and B8; the matrix marks both as accepted. Tau can skip a modeled activity. It cannot absorb an extra visible label.

Model C is the union of eight fixed sequences, not a generator of combinations. It contains B1 and B7 only. It does not contain B2 (`Technical Review` before `Credit Check` with `Auto Approval`), B5 (alternate check order plus supplier, expedite, and auto-approval), or B6 (correction followed by technical review alone). T7 repeats both checks, in credit-then-technical order, with manager approval. Sections 4, 5, and 8 mark B2, B5, and B6 as accepted by C. That hides the exact failure F2 and F4 describe and makes C look more adequate than it is. C’s training fitness of 0.973 is never used: C does not even cover the training log, so “audit-ready mirror of frequent variants” is already false before holdout.

**The third model is not executable and does not mean what Section 7 says.** `ProcessTree("Register Dossier")` sets the operator, not the label, under the real constructor (`operator`, `parent`, `children`, `label`). A process-tree loop needs two children; this loop has one, so correction cannot be skipped and `convert_to_petri_net` is not well-defined. As ordered, the loop sits after approval, so a corrected dossier is approved before recheck and is never re-approved. B6 is therefore not accepted, despite the audit table. Rework cannot be technical-then-credit. Expedite is only after supplier review, so P4’s “before or after supplier” is missed while the table marks P4 satisfied. Supplier review is an `X` with a silent skip. That is exactly the forbidden move: solving a missing external/internal attribute by optionalizing the activity. The DECLARE fragment is a fabricated API (`DeclareModel`, `Condition`, `SimpleCondition`), only two constraints are actually built, and the stated P7 rule (“credit check without a prior correction”) would flag the mandatory first credit check. “Attribute guard” and “flags B8” are prose, not code.

**Metrics and validation inherit the same looseness.** Holdout fitness is described as generalization. The largest pathology is labeled leakage, but a fitness drop is evidence of overfitting or split shift, not leakage; leakage would inflate holdout. Alignment-cost “sensitivity” passes a bare integer where PM4Py wants move-cost functions. The stratified split imputes `external=False` when the attribute is absent, which is the data situation in the brief, and `train_test_split` then fails because stratify has a single class. The negative suite is never turned into an event log. The decision rule rejects only if fitness and precision are both under 80 percent, so a very permissive high-fitness model survives that clause. Thresholds of 90 percent, 85 percent, and 0.1 seconds are undefended after the brief warned against aggregate worship.

**Coverage gaps.** The dissent is about 85 words, not the required 150–250. The three “adversarial” traces do not separate the three languages: `Register, Supplier Review, Release` is rejected by S as well, because S still requires one approval. Section 8’s checkmarks are empty or unreadable, and several P-rows repeat the probe mistakes (S fails P5 and P7; C and the third model pass B5 and B6).

What keeps this above a failing review is structure and some correct calls: B1, B3, B4, and B7; S’s optional checks and pre-check expedite; C’s branch explosion, duplicate labels, positive-only precision, and holdout collapse; use cases kept separate; no single blended score. The board still could not rely on the matrix, the code, or the deployment recommendation.
