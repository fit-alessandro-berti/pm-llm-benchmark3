The write-up has the required nine sections and a usable pipeline sketch, but its audit and validator do not reliably separate source behavior, conversion, layout, and what was actually checked.

The baseline covers registration, the assess/prepare split, optional legal review, a minimum one-pass loop, the release join, and archive. It never states the decisive partial-order fact: there is no order between optional legal review and verification, and prepare-evidence orders verification without ordering legal review. That N-shaped dependency is why a process tree is not a faithful intermediate. Witnesses stay informal (“a trace where…”), and the binary-loop prohibition on ending on Correct Defect is only implied.

The tree pipeline is labeled “conversion failed” as if observed, while other rows say “not tested,” and the stated reason—concurrency plus optionality plus a loop—is wrong. Those constructs can be block-structured; this net is not series-parallel. Several audit cells use the disallowed term “preserved.” Petri-to-BPMN concurrency is called approximated, and silent optional behavior is called lost, because a diagram might hide or reorder them. That equates layout with language. Initial and final markings and binary do/redo exit rules are not really audited.

The bounded explorer hides `None` labels and checks exact final-marking equality, but `visited` stores markings only. The first path to a marking, including the final marking, suppresses every other interleaving, so a sound net yields essentially one trace. The comment then calls that comparison exhaustive under the bound.
