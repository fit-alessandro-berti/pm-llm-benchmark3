The answer is complete, well structured, and strong on the core judgment: it separates control-preserving adaptation from throughput bought by dropping authorization, release, scan, and custody evidence, and it treats post-close audit repair as a different event from real-time compliance. The T0–T4 narrative, the A–L table, the indicator set, and the recovery design all track the case. The score stays well below excellent because several precise claims are wrong or over-connected.

The timeline uses the given metrics correctly and the absolute-versus-rate point in partial recovery is real: about 616 traceability exceptions on 11,400 cases versus about 532 on 5,600, with cold-chain and controlled-substance counts essentially flat (about 262 vs 269 and 160 vs 162). Stabilization counts are also right: compliance hold 1,300, reservation reconciliation 1,400 with 300 cancels and 200 unresolved, proof-of-delivery remediation 1,200 with 800 invoiced, audit remediation 2,300 with 2,100 closed. Fitness, fill rates, and the main directly-follows frequencies are faithful.

These claims do not hold up:

- “4,500+ T2 orders” did not enter fulfillment. Snapshot outflow is 3,920 to pick and 580 deferred, which is 4,500 exactly, and deferral is not fulfillment.
- Packed orders sent straight to unscanned dispatch are 1,800 of 3,600, which is half, not “over half.” The 650 dispatches with no phone proof of delivery are exact, not “650+.”
- The stabilization directly-follows graph is a map of the 35,000 Day 7–21 cases, not a residue ledger. Linking the 650 missing T2 proofs of delivery to the 1,200 T4 proof-of-delivery remediations, and treating pattern G as what “fueled” all 2,300 audit-remediation cases, crosses period boundaries the logs do not support.
- The 450 backlog-prioritization cancels are not shown to be “due to age/stockout.” That cause is supplied by the answer.
- “Exception rates roughly halved” and “stayed 2x–2.75x baseline” fit traceability, cold-chain, and controlled-substance rates only. Rework and cancellation moved much less (12.5% to 9.0% to 5.8%; 7.8% to 5.2% to 3.0%, against 3.5% and 2.1%).
- Calling the snapshot path “stale” for all of T2 clashes with the later, better judgment that it was reasonable early and degraded as stock moved. Flow also does not conserve: 80 fast-lane cases, 200 manual authorizations, 100 skipped checks, and large pick-list gaps never get acknowledged, while arc counts are still stated as completed orders.

- The priority score looks quantitative, but its four inputs have no shared scale, so the weights are decorative. Day 7–21 are also labeled business days without the source saying that.

The classification itself is sound: B and formalized I/J as resilience, C and G as shortcuts to retire, A, D, E, F, and H as conditional, and K/L as necessary repair that must not be allowed to masquerade as in-line control. The redesign has triggers, tiered triage, product-level hard stops, offline evidence, backlog rules, and a provisional-close gate. That is the right recovery logic. The grade is 8.1 because those design judgments sit on a few temporal and numerical overclaims a strict reading cannot ignore.
