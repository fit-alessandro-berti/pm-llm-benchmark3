5.5

The memo covers every required section, respects the main interpretive constraints, and reaches a cautiously worded bottom line. It is not reliable enough, because several central inferences are numerically backwards or invented, and the PM4Py plan would not perform the analysis it claims.

The most serious error is the repeated claim that Group B is over-routed into language support. V4 is 24% of Group B, while 57% need language support, and the listed variants already account for about 99% of Group B with only V4 containing Language Support Contact. That is under-inclusion in a rights-preserving step, not over-use. Sections 1, 3, and 4 all draw the opposite conclusion, and section 4 adds an unsupported stereotyping story about majority-language applicants. The shares do not leave an excess to explain.

The resource calculation is also presented as fact without a basis. “Disproportionately assigned” to 7 bilingual workers does not mean those 7 carry all 1,250 Group B cases. Even on that false assumption, 1,250/7 is about 179, not 170, and 3,600/52 does not measure a dedicated Group A caseload. Calling “170 versus 70” an objective capacity gap overstates what the table supports.

Section 3 says the 3.8-day language wait and 4.7-day accommodation wait explain most of the extra cycle time. Those are segment medians on subsets (about 24% and 31%). Group B’s median of 13.7 days sits near V2 (14.9 days, 34% of Group B), and V2 lines up with a 46% incomplete-documentation rate. The sharper result is the residual +9.5 pp Request Missing Documents and +8.0 pp Supervisor Review after controls, which the path discussion never really uses. Justified composition is also under-specified: V3 for Group D tracks the 51% high-risk share, fraud-review waits differ only modestly (2.8 versus 2.2 days), and V6 (13% versus 4%) plus the +5.7 pp adjusted appeal gap is the larger unexplained D problem. The write-up never cleanly separates path mix that policy can explain from residual burden inside a path.

The comparison strategy names matching, variants, resources, waiting-time decomposition, covariate sensitivity, and region/channel splits, but it never says which contrast is disparity, which is legitimate routing, which would support discrimination, and which is only uncertainty. The matching set copies the preliminary controls and does not stratify on language need or an accommodation request, which are the actual policy triggers. Omitting possibly biased covariates is mentioned, yet the design still treats the first adjusted model as the main comparison.

The PM4Py sketch has concrete defects. `format_dataframe` is called on an XES event log with a list of columns, which is not that function’s signature. The first ten `get_variants` keys are not the ten most frequent variants. `filter_case_performance(..., max_performance=30 days)` keeps faster cases, but the result is named `slow_cases`. `get_service_time` is called and discarded. The log skeleton is not tested as a group-specific constraint. There is no within-variant duration comparison and no per-group performance DFG, yet the closing text claims variant frequencies, edge waits by group, and constraint violations.

Limitations and mitigations are the strongest parts: endogenous risk and documentation, coarse workload, thin outcome quality, capacity, service targets, fewer handbacks, explanations, monitoring, and score audits, each with a trade-off. Appeal quality is still only implied, and the audit’s “politically sensitive” downside is not an operational trade-off. The closing judgment correctly refuses both “raw gaps equal discrimination” and “case mix makes this fair.” That conclusion is better than the path arithmetic underneath it.
