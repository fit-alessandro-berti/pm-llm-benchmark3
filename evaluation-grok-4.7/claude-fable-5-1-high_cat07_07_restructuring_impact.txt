7.5

The answer hits every required section, separates East from North and South, treats fairness and learning as real effects, and withholds an unmonitored full rollout. The handover totals (343 vs 359 per 100 cases), the frequency×median wait totals (≈588 h vs ≈722 h per 100 cases), the supervisor load ratio of about 1.75×, and the Legal/Risk projection past 100% of capacity are correct. The pilot — East and South treated, North as a concurrent control, difference-in-differences, guardrails, rollback — is concrete. Those strengths sit next to checkable errors.

Quantitative problems:
- “Six of the eight expected medians go up; only cross-team goes down” is false. Five rise (tech in, tech out, approval in, approval out, legal/risk), two fall (intake→caseworker 1.1 h→1.0 h; cross-team 8.9 h→6.0 h), and bot intake is unchanged.
- The volume-weighted average of the published team medians is about 7.46 days: (6.8×820 + 7.4×760 + 8.6×520) / 2100. The central scenario’s “7.3 d → 7.0 d” starts from the wrong baseline. With East 8.6→7.6 and North/South each up 0.3–0.6 days, the same weighting stays near 7.4–7.6 days. An overall median of 7.0 while North and South worsen does not follow from the scenario’s own assumptions.
- “East −1.5 days, North and South +0.5 days produces a better weighted mean” computes to about +0.005 days: (−780 + 410 + 380) / 2100. The weighted mean is essentially unchanged. The 75% case-share figure (1580/2100) is right.
- +23% handover-wait hours is +1.3 hours per case, about 0.06 days against a ~7.5-day cycle. The opening treats that +23% as cycle-time-scale. The threat that can add days is queue instability, which appears later and is argued well for Legal/Risk.

Measurement and code problems:
- Keeping only cases with a Close event, with no maturity window, drops long post-change cases. With p90 cycle times of 16–23 days, that censoring can manufacture a better average — the false signal the recommendation warns about.
- The return-loop detector collapses runs with one non-recursive `replace`. A realistic shared→local→shared pattern such as `110011` becomes `11` and is missed.
- `rework_after_handover` counts any case that contains the handover and has `rework_flag` anywhere, including rework before the handover. That is a different quantity from “rework rate after handover.”
- Working-together density is read as substitutability. That network records co-presence on a case. Activity-based resource similarity is the measure that speaks to who can cover whom.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` returns per-case feature rows. It does not produce the weekly count dashboard described in the comments and the indicator table.
- SLA bands of 5/8/14 days, post-change prefixes `TSS_` / `Shared_`, and a 0.5-hour fallback service time are not in the log. Unchanged resource ids would make the after-period shared-service metrics empty.
- `get_service_time` is called with no guard for a missing start timestamp.
- The segment-filter helper and `SEGMENTS` are never applied.

Smaller issues: an optimistic technical-queue p90 of ≤12 h on a 3–4 h median is a p90/median ratio of about 3–4, which the same answer treats as a congestion alarm; a 20% drop in `local_expertise_flag` can fire mechanically once tech work leaves the team; single completion timestamps are labeled waiting time; “~4–5 resources per case” and “top-10 variants ~70%” are stated as if observed.

The recommendation — modify, then pilot; expand only if guardrails hold; do not read month-1 means as success — still follows from the solid parts: Legal/Risk capacity, the supervisor cut, East’s different baseline, and tail, fairness, and learning risks. The grade stays here because the central forecast’s headline number does not follow from its own team-level assumptions, one of the three opening arithmetic anchors miscounts the median changes, and several proposed PM4Py checks would measure the wrong thing.
