7.5

The memo is a competent competing-risk design: time zero is Submit, the horizon is 72h, open cases stay in the risk set, and withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are not silently treated as ordinary censoring. E1 as \(1-F_R(72)\), E2 as no disposition by 72h, and E3 as late resolution are the right family of targets, and Aalen–Johansen as the primary estimator fits a 32-case extract with one clock per case. The Python Aalen–Johansen and Kaplan–Meier routines keep opens, separate three transfer encodings, and build a timestamp-free PM4Py log. Cohort P1 counts (3 vs 5) and the three P1 escalated transfers match the table.

The required 68h transfer walkthrough is incomplete. S08 is not taken through E1, E2, and E3 under each transfer assumption. Under a factual internal-resolution estimand it is a competing event at 68h and is already a non-resolution at 72h; under loss-to-follow-up it is censored before the horizon so status at 72h is unknown; it cannot be an E3 late resolution. S32, transferred at 96h, is the sharper contrast: at 72h it is still unresolved and still in the risk set, so the transfer encoding does not change its 72h contribution. That case is never used.

The “already-observed breach” rule (any \(Y>72\) with no resolution yet) misses pre-horizon competing events. S06 at 27h is a deterministic E1 non-resolution even though \(Y<72\). Those cases are handled elsewhere, but the three-way split the task asks for stays blurry.

The state diagram drops observed edges that matter for stage hazards. S01 has AutoCheck→Resolve, and S27 has Rework→Resolve. The written graph only sends Rework back to Review, which would force an extra review sojourn that the log does not show.

The censoring sensitivity is methodologically off. `last_event_gap_h` is structurally 0 on every terminal row and is only positive for opens, so it is not a valid IPCW covariate for censoring; stage-at-cutoff has the same post-outcome problem. Priority, cohort, and a time-varying history would be the coherent adjustment set. The best/worst imputation is only a bound on the binary resolution indicator; it does not bound the competing-risk composition of the imputed failures.

Fine–Gray is described as acting directly on the cumulative incidence. The coefficients are subdistribution hazard ratios. Restricted mean “hours unresolved” is also ambiguous once withdrawal or rejection has already absorbed the case. The plan tells the reader to stratify cohort within priority and to report uncertainty; the code does only marginal strata and point estimates. With about eight P1 cases, that gap matters. The claim that the cohort ordering could reverse is plausible because the crude on-time counts are close, but it is not tied to any estimate the code is set up to print.

Assumption labeling is incomplete on inclusion: the analysis treats these 32 rows as a Submit cohort under administrative censoring. A prevalent or purged extract would be length-biased, and that assumption is never stated.
