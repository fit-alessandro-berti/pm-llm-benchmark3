3.5

The write-up has the required seven sections and a few defensible calls (alias collapse on CHG-508, Eva’s lack of QM authority on CHG-507, D-IC-04 as a prohibited subdelegation). Those do not rescue the analysis. Most case conclusions are wrong or reached by inverting the rules, and the required PM4Py checks would not run.

**Rule interpretation is misplaced and sometimes false.** Business-day counting is parked under RR1. A later QMS row is discussed with the wrong example: the 2026-08-12 migration record predates Asha’s 2026-08-13 approval and is not what that approval relies on. The signed 2026-08-09 PDF is never weighed against that lag. Treating every act by a principal on leave as unauthorized unless re-delegated strips Asha of her own QM role. “CHG-510 lacks an emergency flag” is false; the log has `emergency=true`. “Within two business days” is illustrated by claiming 2026-08-14 is in time for a 2026-08-11 event. Tuesday plus two business days is Thursday 2026-08-13, so Friday is late on every ordinary count.

**Case table.** CHG-501 is HIGH, so RR3 applies; Ben has no standing QM authority and no “implicit” QM power. D-QM-01 may still make this a valid substitution (PDF before the event, Asha on leave, production-equipment scope), but the stated reasoning denies the rule. CHG-502 is a plain RR1 breach: Clara drafts and approves; LOW risk does not waive separation. It is not a valid substitution. CHG-503 calls `svc_qms` a different verifier and marks RR2 satisfied. RR5 says a service account is not a human; the weak WS-DM-04 session points at the implementer, so independent verification is not shown. Ben’s 11:30 approval is also left untested even though D-QM-01 is in force by then. CHG-504 is high-confidence unauthorized despite a QM principal, a VPN window that covers 09:22, and an explicit credential-sharing ambiguity; the instructed result is indeterminate. CHG-505’s IC approval may fit D-IC-03, but the retrospective review is late, Ben’s delegation is production-equipment only, and he is on sick leave. Calling the exception valid, timely, and severity “none” misses RR4. CHG-506 says no delegation was recorded; D-QM-01 covers that timestamp and scope. Ben denies the act and the IP is an office machine, so identity is indeterminate, not a proven authority failure. CHG-507’s unauthorized label is fair, but Eva is still Independent Verifier on 2026-08-09, not a Quality Engineer, and Grace as a distinct verifier is ignored. CHG-509 asserts Asha was absent on Saturday 2026-08-15; recorded leave ends 2026-08-14, so the D-QM-02 absence condition is not met and high-confidence validity is unjustified. The strong Eva attribution for `svc_qms` never enters the row. CHG-510 is unauthorized, but the row misses Clara’s RR1 self-approval and Ben’s out-of-scope retrospective review.

**Matrix.** Eva’s role change, the point of the event-time instruction, is flattened to Quality Engineer. D-QM-01’s caveat is Ben’s sick leave, not the PDF-versus-migration conflict. Clara is listed as holding an IC delegation that D-IC-03 voids.

**Patterns.** Benign/adverse pairs exist, but credential sharing is offered as a benign account of Asha, and “remote access” is offered for Ben against an office IP and his denial. The adverse reading of CHG-503 never states the real control failure: the implementer may have verified his own work.

**Code.** CHG-509 is rewritten as 2025-08-15. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is invoked once without the two activity pairs, and its result is labeled as violations although that filter keeps cases that satisfy four-eyes. Delegation tests use string comparison, so `2026-08-11 12:00` does not cover an 11 August timestamp; Farid is then hardcoded as always authorized. Absence, subdelegation, sick leave, retrospective SLA, and service-account attribution are not in the checks. Pandas interval logic is absent. The script neither executes the required tests nor avoids automatic mis-flags.

**Conclusion.** The 60/30/10 split treats indeterminate identity cases as proven authority failures and assigns false precision. Priorities skip the clear SoD breaches and the late, out-of-scope QM review.

The product would send an investigation in the wrong direction on a majority of cases. Structure and two or three correct labels keep it above a token attempt.
