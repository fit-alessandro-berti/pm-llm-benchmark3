6.4

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, or open cases as non-default, reconciles all 48,000 applications, and picks a conditional post-activation target with a real alternative. That avoids the failure mode the brief warns about. It is not a sound, implementable study: the estimand, censoring clock, validation window, and skeleton disagree with each other, and several required as-of checks are only named.

The primary estimand is \(P(\text{default within 90 days}\mid\text{prefix},\text{activated})\) at a prefix that is usually pre-decision. Activation is then a future event. Restricting training to eventual activations estimates that conditional association only under the historical approval policy; it is not an early-warning probability for every application at prediction time. Section 3 then mixes that conditional target with a Fine–Gray subdistribution and an “upstream” competing-risk stage without defining the risk set or the time origin. For this target, still-open, never-activated cases are not right-censored default times. They have no activation clock. The cohort table labels all 5,100 that way anyway. Unmatured activated cases are correctly not coded as 0, but “censor at the extraction date” never converts follow-up to time since activation, nor to last date a feed could have arrived (activation + 90 days + 7–35 days).

The maturity arithmetic is the largest design miss. Extraction is 15 February 2026, so a 90-day outcome plus a feed lag is generally knowable only for activations up to roughly mid-October 2025 (earlier if the lag is nearer 35 days). The test window is starts from 15 October through 31 December 2025, so many of those cases cannot reach a mature 90-day label. Time-dependent AUC and a “survival Brier score” are named, but not IPCW versus naive Brier, not an embargo, and not a backtest as-of date at which the horizon is observable. Splitting on the July policy change and the October migration is right; using the post-migration slice as the sole test set also confounds data-quality breakage with predictive performance. The testable / partially testable / untestable taxonomy is one comparison of linked transfers plus the rejected-applicant counterfactual. Independent censoring, MAR versus MNAR partner feeds, and stability across the policy change are left unspecified.

Prefix rules leak. Taking every event “up to the terminal decision” for short cases puts Accept/Reject, and possibly `decision_reason`, inside the prefix. Elapsed-time prefixes do not define timestamp ties or same-day `documents_total`. Queue and resource features are not required to be computed as of the prediction instant from historical reference data.

The leakage table’s keep/repair/drop choices for the eight required fields are mostly right. The skeleton does not implement them. `EXTRACTION_DATE` is unused. There is no activation time, terminal state, censoring time, or label-maturity flag as of a cutoff, which the brief required. `default_90d` is aggregated with `max` and then omitted from `LEAKY_COLS`, so it can enter `safe_features`. The comment that `documents_total` is repaired is false; nothing computes event-time document counts or a last `risk_score` at or before the cut. Cohort counts cover only activated subsets, not the 48,000-way flow. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is run on the full training trace before truncation, so a next-event implementation would pull post-prefix time into the last prefix event. Assumptions are a two-line comment, and non-activated cases are again called competing events inside a conditional-on-activation pipeline.

Section 8 is the cleanest part: associational claims stay inside the historical activation policy, rejected-applicant default is off limits, and the three deployment feedback loops are real. That is not enough to carry the inconsistent estimand, the immature test window, or a skeleton that does not enforce the as-of rules it describes.
