The memo hits every required section, uses most DFG counts and period metrics correctly, and draws the right core line: manual authorization, release control, deferral, and backlog rules can be resilience, while skipped authorization and unscanned dispatch are concealed noncompliance, and post-close audit work is not real-time control.

The decision quality is weaker than the structure. T4 is called operationally near baseline, but pharmacy and wholesale cycle times, exception rates, rework, cancellations, fitness (0.90 vs 0.96), and fitting traces (76% vs 91%) are still materially worse; only the hospital median has recovered. The fast lane is described as sometimes bypassing authorization, although that path goes straight to the inventory snapshot with no authorization step. Traceability exceptions are called a 10x rise; 9.5% versus 0.8% is about 12x. Pattern F is labeled keep while the rationale changes it, and the closing trade-off then allows ambient skips and unscanned dispatch that the table retired for all products.

The recovery model covers triggers, tiers, evidence, backlog, and remediation, but the thresholds are unexplained, the notation is not POWL, recall-sensitive lot capture is missing, and there is no overflow rule for the compliance-capacity limit that produced the 1,600 authorization skips. Temporal findings—backlog sojourn beyond baseline p95 and highly variable proof-of-delivery delay—are barely used. Leading indicators also misname completion rate as utilization.
