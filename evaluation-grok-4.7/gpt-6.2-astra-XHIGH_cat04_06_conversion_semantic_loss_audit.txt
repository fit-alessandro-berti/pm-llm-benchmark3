The answer is a strong, source-faithful audit: the N-shaped partial order, `CI(DCI)*` loop, silent skip, and 45-word bound are right, and it correctly refuses to treat a tree, a drawing, or a shared visible word as equivalence. It is not nearly flawless.

The dedicated negative-witness check only looks at completed words. Early `C`, early release, and other illegal prefixes that deadlock are exactly the cases sections 4 and 7 say must fail, but they are left to a generic prefix diff that prints only the shortest extra and sets no verdict. A silent livelock that never deadlocks is likewise invisible to `bad_deadlock` once `(marking, word)` states are merged. `semantics.execute` sits outside the stated API and can abort the whole oracle; the footprint `set(...)` comparison is unguarded and skips quietly if the relation key differs. Petri→BPMN precedence is marked `expected preserved` while the same converter’s join and concurrency are only `ambiguous`, and section 4 never rises above a pre-execution guess.
