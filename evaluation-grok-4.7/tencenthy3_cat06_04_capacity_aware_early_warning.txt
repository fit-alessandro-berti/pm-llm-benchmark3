6.4

The six required sections are present, I-03 is correctly rejected as past the effective stage, I-05 is not treated like I-07, and the portfolio is not ranked by `p_now` alone. Those are the right core calls. The policy is still not decision-theoretically coherent enough to deploy.

The stated rule and the allocation contradict each other. Section 1 ranks by adjusted net value and funds the top of that list, with a repeated alert eligible only after a new event, a +0.10 move, or a first alert. I-04 has the highest displayed score and is deferred. I-02 moves only from 0.58 to 0.66 and I-09 from 0.62 to 0.69; both are second alerts with no new event, so both fail that rule, yet both are the selected interventions. Time urgency and data-quality holds are doing the real work, but they are not part of the scoring rule.

The repeated-swarm constraint is also misstated. The ban is on another swarm after one has already been used, unless something material changes. I-05 has repeated unchanged alerts and, unlike I-02 and I-09, is not described as already swarmed. Suppressing a refresh-driven duplicate is right; saying policy forbids any swarm is not. I-07 is correctly reopened because of the new log, then left waiting despite 1.5 hours to the deadline and a 45-minute intervention that may consume the only remaining window.

Calibration is mixed into the causal effect without support. Overprediction of Strategic Enterprise breach probability does not justify a 30% cut to the supplied risk-reduction range, and that cut is not used in the scenario table anyway. The SME upward shift never enters the value score. The €500 spillover allowance is far below the later €2.4k–€7.2k spillover model, so the rule systematically underprices queue harm relative to the impact table.

The scenario arithmetic is only partly auditable. Base midpoint benefits, €2.3k combined cost, and the €4.8k / €7.2k spillover figures check out under a stated €20k assumption. Optimistic “breaches avoided” starts at 0.39, which is the base midpoint sum, not the upper-half sum 0.465. The base row shows the full 0.26–0.52 range. False-alarm burden is subtracted in full and folded into “intervention cost.” The deployable cutoff is also unreadable as written: the comparison operator is missing from “Adjusted Net EV  0,” and the same happens in the base arithmetic check.

Two specialists, possible non-parallel delivery, and interruption of ordinary diagnosis are not scheduled. The fairness floor can force a high-probability, low-value swarm whose spillover exceeds its benefit. Non-monetary obligations are thin, and Non-Native sign-off is required in the rule but not attached to the I-09 decision. Monitoring covers the right categories and has a concrete recalibration trigger, but it does not estimate the breach-probability externality itself.
