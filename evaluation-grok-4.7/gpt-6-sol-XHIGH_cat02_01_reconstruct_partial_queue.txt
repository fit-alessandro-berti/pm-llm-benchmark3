The score is held down by several concrete weaknesses.

The scenario labeled central is not central in the feasible windows. P05, P07, P11, and P15 are each placed only five minutes after the earliest feasible start, so S1 busy time sits at 595 against an earliest/latest span of 605 versus 535. “Roughly comparable service” is also a weak description of the P11 calibration point (20 minutes beside neighboring C2 jobs of 45 and 50). Section 4 then calls 74–84% and 73–81% a plausible utilization range, while Section 6 correctly says the short-service endpoint includes zero-duration services and is not an operational schedule. Those two statements do not sit together.

The total-WIP peak value of 13 is right, but “around 11:30–11:50” is not. Nominal WIP is 13 from 11:30 to 11:40, falls to 12 from 11:40 to 11:50, and returns to 13 from 11:50 until 12:00. The hourly table never shows 13, so the prose interval is doing work it does not do accurately.

The rounding envelopes “roughly 433–708” and “440–645” resource-minutes are not derived. They are in the right neighborhood of an unconstrained ±5 minutes per service on top of imputation, but an unevidenced pair of bounds should not be stated as a result, even with “roughly.”

Two analysis gaps remain. No nominal resource-overlap audit is reported, despite half-open ties such as C1’s 11:00 handoff from P07 to P09 and the prompt’s warning about apparent conflicts. Busy time is only pooled, so C2’s 15-minute outage never appears as its own busy-time series. The 4.2/h versus 3.2/h capacity comparison also stays smoother than the log: with a long S1 queue, 10:00–12:00 throughput is about 3.5/h, and the repeated five-minute gaps mean the two inspectors are not continuously busy. The text flags the rates as descriptive, but it does not reconcile them with that partial idleness.
