The final answer meets the required structure and is internally consistent: all eight executions are assigned, local identifiers are not treated as meaningful, X1 and X2 are correctly merged, and the six required borderline pairs are explained through genuinely different equivalence definitions. Activity-sequence groupings are right, including the baseline grouping of X1, X2, X5, X6, and X7 and the separation of X3, X4, and X8. The interpretations cover behavior, risk, flattening, and stability, and the robustness section tracks the stated definitions.

The score is held down by flaws in the required reproducible signature and by over-precise claims about PM4Py. In `normalize_events`, the total-order path sorts on `(time, activity, objects)`. The supplied executions have order but no numeric timestamps; if `time` is missing, every event sorts by activity name, so `Dispatch` precedes `Pack Package`. That contradicts the claim that the signature preserves event order, which is the center of the canonicalization requirement. The business-semantic signature also includes the full normalized trace, so on this dataset its partition is identical to typed-structural equivalence; qualifiers never change a class. The answer does not say that, and the default code does not actually ignore the order of independent events as the prose claims.

The PM4Py section asserts that the key retains raw `(object_type, object_id)` pairs and does not rename identifiers, then treats `max_objs` as silent truncation. Those are stronger than the interface supports. The outline also assumes cluster values are object-style sublogs with `.omap`, rather than showing a canonicalization that starts from ordinary OCEL events, relations, and lead-object ids. The preferred clustering is defensible but nearly the finest partition, despite the instruction not to treat the finest partition as best.
