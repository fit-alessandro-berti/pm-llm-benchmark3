7.5

The case analysis is strong on the main traps: alias-normalized self-approval on CHG-508, Eva’s event-time role on CHG-507, invalid subdelegation plus RR1 on CHG-510, service-account non-attribution on CHG-503, sick-leave identity on CHG-506, and pair repetition treated as an indicator. Those calls, the business-day reading that makes CHG-505’s Friday review late, and the separation of technical SoD from control purpose are the core of the work. The score is held down by internal contradictions, one over-strong classification, and checking code that is not reliably executable.

The CHG-504 alternative contradicts the answer’s own absence rule. Section 2 says a principal who is demonstrably in session cannot be treated as absent, and the code implements that (`in_session` forces `PRINCIPAL_PRESENT`). The CHG-504 row then says Ben’s delegation was still valid at 09:22, during Asha’s 09:15–09:40 VPN session, and that the act stays authorized if the true actor was Ben. An action recorded as `u_as` is not Ben exercising D-QM-01. During that window the absence condition also fails under the answer’s own test. The primary standing-QM reading is sound only if the actor was Asha.

CHG-509 is labeled `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` while a condition the answer itself treats as mandatory — Asha’s absence on 08-15 — is unproven. That is an indeterminate authority result with a possible valid substitution, and the provisional wording does not remove the overclaim. The same split appears on CHG-510: the case row treats Ben’s cooling-system retrospective review as outside D-QM-01, then the control-objective section counts that review as a timely QM-side review that partly met RR4. Under the strict reading, RR4’s reviewer was not established.

The 60-day Clara–Ben figure is over-read. “Preceding 60 days” has no anchor date, so it can overlap 10–14 August, and three in-log Clara→Ben high-risk production approvals fall inside D-QM-01. The conclusion treats the 18 approvals as pre-10 August prima facie RR3 failures. Ben’s denial is also widened: the source denial is the CHG-506 approval, while the 14 August review is only covered by the broader statement that his account was used during sick leave.

The code’s design is mostly the right design: normalize `b.koch` before four-eyes, keep unresolved `svc_qms` from creating a false RR2 pass, re-test an adverse Diego attribution, and keep network output from becoming a collusion finding. It is still a weak executable artifact. `filter_four_eyes_principle` and the SNA discoverers are called with `resource_key=` rather than the long-standing `parameters` dictionary keyed by `pm4py:param:resource_key`. On the classic signature that is a `TypeError`; if kwargs are stuffed in under the literal name `resource_key`, pm4py silently keeps `org:resource`, so the pm4py four-eyes and handover results are not alias-normalized. Flags are printed only after the unprotected network calls, so a throw there drops the flag report. D-QM-02 and D-IC-03 timestamps and Eva’s role start are invented, CI-44 is hardcoded into an automated `VALID` for CHG-505, and `DILIGO`/`replace` is noise. The pandas cross-check would catch a silent resource-key miss only if execution got that far.

Severity cells such as “Low if the PDF is genuine; High if not” leave the required severity field unresolved. “Approver independence” on CHG-507 is the wrong name for an authority failure. The required headings, RR1–RR6 formalization, weekend handling, and the refusal to treat handover weight as proof are otherwise in good shape.
