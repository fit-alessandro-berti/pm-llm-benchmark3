7.5

The write-up meets the required structure, keeps several live hypotheses, calibrates the risks, and does not treat centrality as a verdict or recommend removing the role without a replacement. The core reconciliation is sound: about 1.06 events per case, spread across many partners, produces high directed-flow centrality and thin co-occurrence at the same time. The 1,640/1,580-versus-960 counting paradox, Bot_Ingest’s working-together degree, Vendor_Pay’s zero out-degree, and the single-absence caveat are all real strengths.

The score is held down by quantitative and semantic overclaims.

Outgoing mean elapsed times are set beside the target’s median service time and used to call Mira responsive and to rule out a queue at her. A queue at Mira would appear on edges into her. Outgoing gaps are the wait until the next resource. For Carla and Diego those gaps (18 and 19 min) are shorter than median service (22 and 24 min), so those edges do not “contain” the target’s processing. Mean versus median is never flagged.

The Alice–Carla 27% check correctly kills “share of all 4,100 cases” (1,107 > Alice’s 665). The next step does not hold. A Jaccard reading of Mira–Carla at 8% is about 131 shared cases; that does not contradict 350 handovers once edge frequency is event-level, which is the answer’s own reading, because one case can carry several direct handovers. The same paragraph then revives “8% of all cases ≈ 328, so 36% of her caseload” after that denominator has already failed for other rows of the same table.

Subcontracting counts are turned into return rates (126/210, 98/180) after Section 1 has said the handover frequencies may not be direct-succession counts on the 960-event set. “Not a strict gate” depends on those ratios sharing a unit. “About a third” of medical reviews is fair ((330−210)/330 ≈ 36%). “A quarter” of legal reviews is not ((260−180)/260 ≈ 31%), and “at all” is stronger than a direct-edge table. H3 then treats 160/210 and 140/180 as “76–78% of her referrals send a handover back,” an aggregate weight ratio left unresolved against the 60% and 54% within-two-events figures.

H5 joins Bot_Rules’ 0.31 exception share to Mira’s re-screening loop. Read as event-weighted against a ~19% case base, 0.31 does imply about 1.9 Bot_Rules events on a flagged case, and 4,750 − 4,090 = 660 extra bot events. The fragment that actually includes her occurs 62 times. The limit given is “62 is small beside her 960 events,” which misses the comparison with those 660.

Handover frequency is also stated as a share of distinct cases (“nearly half” of Elise’s 610 and Farid’s 590). “Escalate Complaint and Assign Specialist appear only in her profile” reads the main-activity column as a full profile. The missing activity name is correctly noted for Assign Specialist and not for Escalate Complaint.

The PM4Py sketch filters to `lifecycle:transition == complete` and then calls `insert_case_service_waiting_time` with no start timestamp. Service time here depends on start/complete pairs. The later prose mention of a start timestamp “if available” does not repair that pipeline. Out-degree 0.82 versus in-degree 0.51 is partner diversity on a normalized graph; “distributor rather than collector” is easy to read as volume. The executive summary’s “usually decides” and “difficult cases waited” are firmer than the body, which notes the unreported median for flagged cases during the absence and coverage of only about 56% of flagged cases.
