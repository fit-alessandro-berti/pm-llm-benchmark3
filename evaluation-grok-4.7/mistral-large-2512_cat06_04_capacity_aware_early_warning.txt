**4.0**

The write-up has the required six-part shape and a few sound qualitative calls, but it is not an implementable policy. The allocation contradicts itself and the capacity constraint, the scoring rule is the wrong estimand, and the impact table states quantities that the case data cannot generate.

The stage gate is right: I-03 is out because it is already at Diagnosis Complete. I-05 (unchanged refresh) is treated as stale, and I-07 (new customer log) is treated as new evidence. Segment calibration is acknowledged, and fairness and monitoring are at least enumerated. Those are the parts that keep this from collapsing further.

The decision itself does not cohere.

Capacity is exceeded and the chosen set is unstable. Four slots exist. The table marks five cases Intervene (I-01, I-02, I-04, I-07, I-08). The named portfolio is I-01, I-02, I-04, I-08. The prose then says I-07 is included. It also reserves one slot. Four named interventions plus a reserve is five uses of four slots. The published scores would not produce that portfolio anyway: I-09 at about €2,200 outranks I-08 at about €2,000 and I-01 at about €1,200, yet I-09 is told to wait and I-01 is told to go. The allocation rule, the score column, the action column, the named portfolio, and the rationale are five different answers.

The eligibility rule is misapplied. A repeat alert is eligible only with a new event or a +0.10 move in `p`. I-02 moves 0.58 → 0.66 (+0.08) with no new event and no prior swarm, and I-09 moves 0.62 → 0.69 (+0.07). Both are marked eligible. The same rule is used to bar I-05. Section 1 also lets a material new event break the 4-hour swarm cooldown; Section 4 says the cooldown holds regardless of new alerts.

The score is not the decision quantity this problem specifies. Expected direct benefit is absolute risk reduction × breach value − swarm cost − false-alarm burden. The written score multiplies by adjusted `p_now` again, so a 0.20 absolute reduction is treated as if it were 0.20 × p. That systematically shrinks every benefit and reorders cases toward high predicted probability. The spillover term is the same euro amount on every case, so it cannot rank anyone, and it applies 0.02 twice. Widening the Non-Native-Language Support effect range by 50% leaves the midpoint unchanged, so that “robustification” does not enter the score. Time to deadline and the option value of waiting (I-04 at 10 h, I-08 at 8 h, a possible regulatory arrival before 15:00) are absent. The € scores are not recoverable from the formula: they sit near `p × value × some reduction` with costs sometimes ignored.

Under a hurdle that uses the midpoint effect, cost, false-alarm burden, and the answer’s own spillover of about €3,000 per slot (6 × 0.02 × €25,000), I-01 is about €1,700 of direct net benefit and should not take a slot. I-08’s lower bound is about 0.05 × €100,000 − €450 − €5,000 < 0, with Strategic Enterprise overprediction and 8 hours of slack, yet it is selected. I-09’s midpoint direct net is higher than I-01’s and I-02’s, and it is the case the answer later says the policy will starve.

Section 3 is not auditable. The worked base arithmetic gives about 0.37 probability-points and about €12,490. The base row says 0.8–1.2 breaches and €80,000–€120,000. The optimistic row says 1.2–1.8 breaches and €120,000–€180,000. The upper ends of the four selected effect ranges sum to 0.35 + 0.22 + 0.28 + 0.15 = 1.00, and the maximum gross benefit is about €40,450. Both headline ranges are impossible. Spillover arithmetic in the same section is €12,000 at the midpoint and a stated range of €3,000–€9,000; the table’s base range matches neither, the optimistic band matches one slot, and the adverse band matches four slots at the base rate. Adverse congestion of 15–30 minutes is never converted. False-alarm burden on the named set is €250 + €600 + €2,000 + €5,000 = €7,850 and is omitted. Labor of €1,800 is treated as the full intervention cost. Adding that burden and the €12,000 spillover midpoint to the worked €12,490 benefit makes the base case negative, while the interpretation still says positive with moderate confidence. I-08 is also adjusted by 0.05 in the arithmetic after Section 1 fixed the Strategic Enterprise shift at 0.08. Effect ranges are plugged in as if they were known causal effects.

Smaller factual and policy errors compound this. I-06’s false-alarm burden is €300; the rationale calls it high. The real I-06 problem is the imputed queue feature. The value cap (at most one case above €50,000 per day) is already broken by selecting both I-04 and I-08. The segment quota is broken by a portfolio with no SME and no Non-Native-Language Support case. Exploration is 10% in Section 5 and 5% in Section 6; 10% of four daily slots is not an operational sample, and randomizing only below-threshold cases does not identify an unbiased effect. “0.04 spillover per slot” and “effects decline by >20%” have no defined baseline. Instrumental variables are named with no instrument. Workflow-change triggers, data-quality failures, and logged propensities for policy-induced label drift are thin or missing. Non-monetary obligations (regulatory exposure, public-sector duty) never modify the euro score.

An operator cannot tell which cases get today’s slots, and a manager cannot trust the claimed net benefit. The qualitative skeleton is real; the decision theory and the arithmetic are not.
