7.4

The review is unusually complete and often precise, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and traceability errors sit on the exact points the task makes decisive: necessity versus sufficiency, authority, exception conditions, and system-level counts.

The opening rule is logically broken. Section 1.1 says fitting M0 is necessary but not sufficient for CS-9 compliance, then immediately says that failing to fit M0 does not establish a CS-9 violation. Those claims cannot both be true. The second is the right one. SUP-707 meets the substantive CS-9 checks and still cannot fit M0. Later classifications follow the correct rule, so the sentence is not the operative standard, but it is still a foundational error.

SUP-702 is mishandled against the review’s own burden. PD-3 requires automated master-data validation before activation, and the trace does not show it. The matrix says failure to evidence that control would reverse JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Section 5 then says every PD-3 condition was met and that confidence falls only if validation cannot be evidenced. It already cannot. The same upgrade happens in aggregate: “289 appear eligible” means risk, country, and group, not that validation, timing, and authority were shown for all 289. Calling those cases correctly performed overstates the record. The June 15 CRM note is also mislabeled as the post-2026-07-01 mirror gap.

The system counts do not add up. The case table is 3 compliant, 2 justified exceptions, 6 noncompliant, 3 model/scope defects, and 1 data-indeterminate. Section 5.2 reports 6 model/data, 2 justified, 3 compliant, and 5 noncompliant. That sums to 16 and reverses the comparison being argued. On these labels, noncompliance outnumbers model and data findings. The 15 cases are also a constructed set, not a sample from which a compliance rate can be inferred; the review correctly demands a denominator from Operations and then treats this set as one.

SUP-714 is one of the six first-week-of-August auto-approvals, but the briefs and system judgment repeatedly say “SUP-714 and six August cases” or “six more.” That inflates the normalization evidence. The defense’s claim that approval preceded first spend in every case except SUP-703 is false for SUP-704 and SUP-705 and unproved for SUP-708, and the judgment never corrects it. SUP-706 has no date, yet it is asserted to fall inside the 29% gap; the missing date is not even listed as missing evidence.

Authority is not stable across sections. The burden and judgment treat a disputed steering-committee pilot as a case-level justified exception and a system-level governance defect. Layer 5 says an unsigned “monitored exception” creates no overlay. Section 7.1 then invents a DISPUTED status that restores JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Those can be reconciled only by an explicit prospective-versus-retrospective distinction, which is not made. PD-3’s own word “provisional” is also dropped: the emergency route gets a distinct provisional activity, while the pilot route uses ordinary activation with a later quality review and does not explain the difference.

The POWL is nevertheless one of the stronger parts. Concurrent sanctions and quality checks, separate manual, auto, emergency, rejection, and reactivation routes, notification before closure, and a true zero-or-more `Request_More_Info` loop use the correct PM4Py loop polarity (`LOOP(silent, redo)` versus `LOOP(do, silent)`). Data-dependent route eligibility is kept out of the net and stated as a limitation. The overlay table and collection proposal cover rule versions, snapshots, aliases, delegation, authority, and immutable timestamps. Case outcomes for SUP-701, 703, 705, 707, 708, 709, 711, 712, and 714 are well supported, and both briefs actually concede the points the task requires them to concede.

The remaining case calls are defensible but softer: SUP-710 is a scope gap only if silence in CS-9 creates an implied certification substitute; SUP-713 treats a mapping approved after the event as present compliance; SUP-715 correctly separates the spouse issue from the requester/approver rule, then uses NONCOMPLIANT for a disclosure policy whose text is not in the normative materials. None of those is wild. The grade is held down by the contradictions and miscounts, not by the architecture.
