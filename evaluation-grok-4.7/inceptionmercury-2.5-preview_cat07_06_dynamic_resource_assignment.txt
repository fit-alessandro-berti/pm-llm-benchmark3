4.5

The write-up covers every required section and gets several urgent or continuity-driven assignments right (C001 to R8, C002 and C011 to R6, C005 to R5, C007 to R3, C008 to R7, C016 to R5). The recommended plan and the scoring rule still conflict with the stated constraints and with each other, and the audit design would not measure the policy.

**Objectives.** SLA, skill, fairness, continuity, and training are listed, and hard rules are separated from preferences. Resilience is absent as an objective. Remaining hours never become a constraint. The 20% training quota is stated as unconditional, dropping the “only if SLA risk stays acceptable” clause. Continuity is written as a hard ban, while the scenario treats it as a preference unless the owner is skill 0–1 and supervision is impossible. Quality batching is turned from a permission (normal/low and more than 24h) into a routing preference.

**Policy.** The score cannot implement the decision it claims to make. SLA urgency is `1/(hours+1)` for every candidate of a case, so it never changes who is chosen, and there is no case-ordering step even though the cap of 4, overtime, and the training quota depend on order. Workload at `-5 × open cases` swamps skill (`×10`) and continuity (`+15`); a training bonus of `+20` can outrank a competent owner on a high-priority case. Handover cost, complexity, and hours remaining are not scored. Overtime is a hard exclusion for every non-urgent case, with no “only if no safer alternative” test, while the later training override can put that case back onto a high-overtime resource. Eligibility requires skill 3 for Security and skill 2–3 for Approval, then a later step allows skill 1–2 under “a” skill-3 supervisor. Those two steps contradict each other, and Security supervision is not limited to R7 or R8. The supervision check does not cover ordinary high-priority work, so constraint 2 is unenforced. Tie-break and “no eligible resource” handling are only sketched.

**Queue.** The table violates rules the text itself names. C015 goes to R10 under R8, but R10’s Security score is 0, and rule 6 allows supervision only for skill 1–2. R5, the other person named in the note, is also Security 0. C012 (low, 48h) goes to R2, who is overtime-high, already has 12 cases and 4 hours, and is removed by the policy’s own non-urgent overtime filter. C003, C006, C010, and C014 likewise add non-urgent work to overtime-high resources (R4 has 14 cases and 3 hours; R9 has 11) while lower-load legal alternatives exist. R10, the lightest legal Quality specialist, is used on an illegal Security case and loses C012. C009’s diagnosis training idea fits R1, but the supervisor chosen is R4 rather than low-overtime R3. Factual cells are wrong: R6 is overtime-low, yet C002 is marked “Overtime High” and C011 “Overtime” / “very high workload” despite 5 open cases; C013’s complexity is 0.21, not high; C006’s trade-off and risk are “None” on an overtime-high owner. C010 misses the prepare-versus-finalize rule. Running the published weights does not reproduce C001, C003, C006, C010, C012, C014, C015, or C016.

**Baselines.** All seven dimensions appear, but only as unlabeled adjectives. Specialist-only is called highly fair; concentrating work on the top skill score is the less even pattern. First-available is called low SLA risk without accounting for the stated 3.3h service-time penalty of weak-skill ownership. The proposed column wins every row, so the comparison does not hold the required trade-off: training and continuity cost throughput and sometimes immediate skill.

**Workers’ note.** The tone is plain and non-punitive. It still describes the cap of 4 as burnout protection while the real harm in this batch is existing load and overtime, and it presents C012 as development after the assignment has already breached the overtime rule.

**Audit and PM4Py.** Every requested indicator is named. Several definitions point at the wrong phenomenon: handover is described as changes of `case:concept:name` (the case id); skill mismatch compares a skill name with a numeric level; rework/appeal is equated with `reassignment_count > 2`; bottleneck “queue time” is tied to `get_service_time`; `filter_case_performance`, `filter_event_attribute_values`, and `discover_performance_dfg` are cited for computations they do not perform. `assignment_reason`, `handover_cost_band`, `workload_snapshot`, and `discover_batches` are unused. The sketch references `format_dataframe`, a handover network, and an event-attribute filter, then calls an undefined `high_overtime_resources`. It does not show role discovery, resource similarity, working-together or SNA, waiting time, performance DFGs, variant analysis, or case-attribute filtering, and it does not show how log outcomes would set the weights.
