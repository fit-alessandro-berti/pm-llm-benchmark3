5.5

The write-up is well organized and conceptually careful, but the quantitative reconstruction of the queues is not reliable. Fully observed case times, completed-case sojourns, and the 14:00 inventory are largely right; the missing-start bounds, intermediate WIP, busy time, and several rate claims are not.

**Missing-start bounds contradict the stated resource rules.** A7 says a missing start is bounded by queue entry and the same resource’s previous completion and next start. That rule is applied only for P15. For the other three cases the numeric interval is the loose queue-entry-to-completion window, and the central midpoint is taken from that window.

- P05 on C1 cannot start at 09:25. P03 occupies C1 until 09:45, and P07 starts at 10:20, so the reported-time start interval is 09:45–10:15. Wait is 20–50 minutes and service is 0–30, not 0–50 and 0–50.
- P07 on I1 cannot start at 08:45. P05 occupies I1 until 09:25, so the start interval is 09:25–10:00. Wait is 40–75 and service is 0–35, not 0–75 and 0–75. The published midpoint 09:22:30 overlaps P05.
- P11 on C2 is the clearest error. The text says P08 ends at 12:00 and then claims the 11:05 queue entry “dominates” the lower bound. The binding lower bound is the later of the two times, 12:00. P13 starts at 12:30, so the start interval is 12:00–12:25. Wait is 55–80 and service is 0–25, not 0–80 and 0–80. The midpoint 11:45 overlaps P08 by 15 minutes and the stated lower bound 11:05 also falls inside the C2 outage.

**P15’s S2 wait is exact and is reported as a range.** S1 completion is 12:15 and S2 start is 13:30, so S2 wait is 75 minutes. The range 75–110 (central 92.5) is S2 start minus a varying S1 start. That mixes S1 service with S2 queue time. Section 6 then lets this false wait move from 75 to 110 across scenarios. The S1 bounds for P15 themselves, 11:40–12:15, are the ones that follow the resource constraint.

**The queue and WIP path is inconsistent with the same answer’s waits.** At 11:00, after completions then starts, S1 has P11 and P12 in service and P13–P18 waiting: queue 6, busy 2, WIP 8. The snapshot table says queue about 2 and WIP about 4. The same undercount appears at 09:00 (queue 3, WIP 5, reported as about 2 and about 4), 10:00 (queue 6, WIP 7), and 12:00 (WIP 7). Waits of 70–105 minutes with two servers and roughly 30-minute service imply a queue of about 6, so the table also contradicts section 3. Total WIP is consequently low through the middle of the window: about 11 at 10:00 and about 12 at 12:00, not 7–9. The 14:00 row is exact and imputation-free: S1 is 1 waiting and 1 busy, S2 is 4 waiting and 2 busy, system WIP is 8. The shape is wrong: system WIP peaks near midday and then falls to 8, rather than drifting from about 7 to about 9.

**Busy time and utilization are not reconciled to the case table.** Fully observed completed S1 service sums to 505 resource-minutes, not about 540. Adding P21’s elapsed 30 and the answer’s own loose P07/P15 services gives about 535–645, not 570–680. Fully observed completed S2 service sums to 480. The figure 545 equals 480 plus the central P05 and P11 services, so it is not a total “before missing intervals.” Open elapsed time is 35, and the loose service bounds do not produce the stated 580–680 band. Against 720 and 705 available resource-minutes, the corrected central busy time is roughly high-70s percent at both stations. The published central picture, S2 near 90% and possibly above S1, depends on these inflated totals and on service maxima of 75 and 80 minutes that resource sequencing does not allow.

**Subperiod counts and the Little’s Law check are softer than presented.** External arrivals in 08:00–10:55 include P17 at 10:45, so that bin has 17 arrivals, not 16; 11:10–12:00 has P19–P21, so 3, not 4. The errors cancel in the daily total of 22. S1 completions do not depend on P07’s start: the completion at 10:00 is observed, which the text later says and the rate table denies. Over 11:15–13:30, S2 departures are P08–P13, six completions, about 2.7 per hour, not 4–5 completions and 1.8–2.2 per hour. The nonstationarity caveat is appropriate; the departure count is not.

**What holds up.** Observed S1 and S2 waits and services for timestamped cases are correct, as are the 14 completed sojourns, their mean of about 146 minutes, and their median of 145. Those sojourns really are identified by arrival and S2 completion alone. Open work is kept as elapsed service with a final-time lower bound, and blank timestamps are not treated as missed completed activities. The stable conclusions are the right ones: S1 waits become large on fully observed cases, S2 finishes 14 services against 20 S1 releases, S2 holds six cases at 14:00 against two at S1, and a 15-minute one-resource outage is too small to be the sole cause of the later S2 backlog. The time-varying bottleneck reading is supported by the waits and the terminal inventory even though the intermediate queue table does not support it.

**Code.** The script builds the table, separates MISSING, OPEN, and blank, constructs an interval-event frame, calls `format_dataframe`, and keeps the PM4Py service, waiting, arrival, and finish enrichments on a fully observed complete-case projection with an explicit case-level label. Station calculations are in pandas, with a comment on why that is safer. The numeric core is the same loose bounds as the prose. Open services are given an occupancy end at 14:00 and that end is processed as a departure, so `busy_at_14` drops in-progress work and will not reproduce the prose inventory of two busy S2 cases. The enrichment calls use `timestamp_key` / `start_timestamp_key`; the usual PM4Py argument names are `timestamp_column` / `start_timestamp_column`, and the `try/except` would swallow that failure instead of demonstrating the functions.

The score is held down by errors in the quantities the task centers on: feasible starts, queue length over time, and utilization. It is not lower because the end state, the completed sojourns, the observed case table, the censoring distinction, and the bottleneck direction are coherent and would survive a corrected imputation.
