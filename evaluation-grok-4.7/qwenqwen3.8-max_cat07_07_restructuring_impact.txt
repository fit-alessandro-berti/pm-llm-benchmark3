The forecast is complete, balanced, and structurally compliant: all seven required sections are present, East is treated differently from North and South, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The handover arithmetic is correct (343 vs 359 per 100 cases; about 588 vs 722 proxy wait-hours), and the main claim — that management’s own table does not show a nominal handover reduction — is sound. Scenarios, indicators, hidden-average risks, fairness, learning, and rollback logic are all covered.

It is not nearly flawless. The PM4Py sketch discovers the required objects, but the handover-wait example filters `org:resource` on labels such as `"local caseworker"` and `"Technical Shared Service"`, which are not resource IDs, while the defined `resource_group` mapping is never applied there. Those waits are also computed only after the change, and the handover, working-together, subcontracting, similarity, and performance-DFG results are stored rather than compared. The 10-day SLA cutoff is inconsistent with the stated miss rates of roughly 7–10% and p90 cycle times of 16–23 days, and the fairness table uses an `"SLA miss"` string match that the log does not define. Median wait times multiplied by frequency are called waiting-hours without noting that this is only about 1.3 hours per case against multi-day cycle times. Scenario rework and cycle-time deltas are plausible but not tied back to the given rework-after-handover rates, and the local specialist path itself rises from 67 to 77 handovers per 100 cases, which is a sharper contradiction of the “fewer loops” claim than the aggregate 343 vs 359 figure.
