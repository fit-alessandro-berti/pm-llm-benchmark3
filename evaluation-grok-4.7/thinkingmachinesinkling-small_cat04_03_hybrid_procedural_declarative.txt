4.8

The write-up has the required shape and a real R5/R6 split, but the executable models are not coherent with each other or with the verdicts. That is decisive for a task whose point is constraint semantics.

The chosen activity-only reading of R5 is encoded as unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`. In DECLARE, that means every activation is illegal unless an opinion already occurred. It is not vacuous on non-escalated cases, and it does not “do no harm”: T1, T2, T4, T5, and T8 all violate it. Section 9 states the opposite and calls the resulting false alarms “false negatives.” The same dictionary then adds `exactly_one` on `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment` (support not even 1.0) and `absence` on `Sanctions Escalation`. Those rules forbid the rejected trace and every escalated trace. Section 4 admits rejected cases break `exactly_one`; section 8 still marks T3 and T7 DECLARE `pass`.

The log skeleton is likewise unsatisfiable. `always_before` puts both `Reject Vendor` and `Release First Payment` before `Close Onboarding`, so every closed case must both reject and pay. `always_after` requires activation to sit after both `Manager Approval` and `Compliance Approval`. `directly_follows` is treated as a handful of mandatory adjacencies; in PM4Py it is the allow-list of direct successions, so every real trace deviates. Section 8 still marks T1–T5 and T7 as log-skeleton `pass`, and it says T6 fails because opinion follows activation — a pair the skeleton never states. Section 1 claimed the skeleton enforces R5.

The process-tree design (parallel checks, then classify, then an XOR of reject / low-risk / high-risk) is the right backbone, and the exclusions are disclosed. The constructor is not. `ProcessTree(operator, parent, children, label)` is called as `ProcessTree(label, operator, children)`, so the object is not the tree described. Traces that contain clarification, escalation, or a legal opinion are still graded procedural `pass` even though those labels are absent from the net.

What holds up is narrower. The seconds conversion and `zeta = 2.0` are right, and T7’s 34 h escalation-to-opinion gap is a genuine warning (z ≈ 3.67). T1’s z-score is not: 80 − 9 = 71 h against a 72 h mean is about 0.03 standard deviations, not 8.8 h and z = 0.24. T4’s four-eyes failure, T5’s missing reply, T8’s double ending, and the two-way T6 reading are the right business outcomes, but they are not what these dictionaries would return. R3 never gets alternate/chain response, despite the requested distinction. R11 is left without the external check the mapping itself says is required. The duty and emergency sketches exist, but the emergency check compares index order and calls `.total_seconds()` on the scenario’s hour offsets.

Deployment order is sensible only if the authoritative DECLARE and skeleton layers are trustworthy. Here they are not.
