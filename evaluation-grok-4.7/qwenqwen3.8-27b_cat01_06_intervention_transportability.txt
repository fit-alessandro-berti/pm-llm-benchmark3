6.6

The final answer is complete and well organized, and its core transport judgment is sound: Site A’s before/after gain is confounded, Document Check cannot be started before originals when they are legally required, and the right call is a segmented conditional go rather than a full copy. The comparison, confounder table, failure modes, and stop rules are substantive. It is not nearly flawless.

The POWL code does not faithfully encode the Site B process. `POWL`, `POWLNode`, and `POWLEdge` with `add_node` are not the PM4Py POWL constructors; a strict partial order is `StrictPartialOrder`, and optional choice and a supplement loop belong in `OperatorPOWL` with XOR and LOOP. As written, the import fails, so the Petri-net checks and playout never run. Even as a dependency sketch, `Decision` waits on both `Legal Review` and `Legal Review (Supplement)`, so legal review and the supplement path are mandatory rather than optional. `convert_to_petri_net` also returns `(net, initial marking, final marking)`, which is never unpacked, and the fallback conversion is outside the exception handler.

The quantitative design has concrete errors. Detecting a 4-hour shift with an SD of 20 hours at 80% power needs roughly 390 cases per arm, not about 100; the stated minimum of 30 per stratum cannot support that endpoint. In the digital-only scenario, a 38% subgroup shift of −3 to −6 hours is converted into an overall median shift of −1 to −3 hours as if medians averaged linearly. The optimistic 100% scenario also treats identity-during-originals as new concurrency, although Site B’s rules already allow both to start after registration; the directly-follows counts were not used to estimate how often that overlap is already available. Selection nodes are inconsistent: Site A is given `legal_capacity` despite having no legal review, and both sites select the treatment.
