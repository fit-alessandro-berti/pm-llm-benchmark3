The answer is not a deployable policy. It preserves the six-section shape and makes two qualitatively right calls — I-03 is past the effective stage, and I-05’s unchanged repeat is not treated like I-07’s new log — but the rule, the table, the portfolio, and the scenario math contradict each other, and several inputs are simply the wrong case’s numbers.

Eligibility is not operational. The rule restricts swarms to Diagnosis Started, then marks Triage cases eligible and selects three of them. A false-alarm burden above €200 is declared disqualifying, which would remove I-01, I-02, I-04, I-06, I-08, and I-09, yet those cases are marked eligible and several are selected. I-06 is excluded until its imputed queue feature is repaired, marked “Intervene,” and then left out of the portfolio. “Top 50% of the ARR range” is never defined or applied. The priority tiers do not follow the answer’s own net figures: I-08 and I-04 are the largest reported nets and are labeled Medium, while smaller nets are High. The action column marks seven cases “Intervene”; the portfolio names four. “Wait” is never used, so horizon and option value are ignored. Four named swarms plus a reserved slot require five slots; only four exist.

Calibration is assigned to the wrong segments and the wrong sign. SME underprediction of about 0.04–0.09 is the bias that should raise risk; the answer gives SME no adjustment and subtracts 0.04–0.09 from Public Sector, whose calibration is described as acceptable. Strategic Enterprise overprediction is named and then never applied to a score. A further 10% ARR haircut is applied to Public Sector and Strategic Enterprise, which mixes a probability-calibration problem with the intervention effect. Non-Native-Language Support is given both “no adjustment” and a “neutral bias,” which leaves the uncertain estimate looking usable.

Section 2’s arithmetic and case facts do not hold. Midpoints are not used: the plugged ARR values include 0.21, 0.15, 0.08, 0.13, and 0.21, and 0.08 is below I-06’s stated floor of 0.15. I-07 is scored with €20,000 value and an €800 false-alarm burden; those figures belong to I-09. I-07 is €5,000 and €50. Reported nets do not match the subtractions printed beside them: I-04’s €9,000 − €450 − €2,000 is €6,550, not €7,550; I-06’s €1,200 − €450 − €300 is €450, not €750; I-10’s €2,310 − €450 − €150 is €1,710, not €1,860. I-04 and I-08 list their ARR ranges as “spillover.” The selected set is I-01, I-02, I-04, and the mis-valued I-07, which is not the ordering implied by the scores in the same table. Deadline pressure is omitted: I-07 has 1.5 hours and the swarm itself takes 45 minutes, while I-04 and I-10 have 10 and 12 hours.

Section 3 does not implement the three required scenarios. Optimistic and base spillover are identical, use 0.15 rather than about 0.01–0.03, and hit only I-06 and I-08 instead of six untreated diagnosis-stage cases per slot. Adverse spillover is smaller than optimistic spillover, so the stress case is the mild one. Adverse also keeps every selected case at a substantial effect, despite the requirement that at least one selected case receive little or no benefit, and it uses 0.05 for I-04 when I-04’s floor is 0.10. The same wrong €20,000 I-07 value is repeated in all three rows. Displayed benefits do not add up: the optimistic components total €31,850, not €25,950; the base components total €17,750, not €19,750. The stated nets do not equal benefit minus €1,800 minus the spillover terms shown. False-alarm burden disappears. Euro amounts are placed in the “breaches avoided” column, and the “benefit range” cells are single points. “High confidence” is attached to the favorable scenario because the assumptions are favorable, which reverses what confidence means. Delay of 8–20 minutes, or 15–30 minutes in the adverse case, is never connected to the spillover claim.

The repeat-alert distinction is only partly a policy. I-05 versus I-07 is directionally right, but the write-up treats I-05 as if the only gap were 30 minutes, misses that I-07’s probability fell from 0.75 to 0.72, and justifies swarming I-07 with I-09’s economics. Cool-down is 30 minutes, shorter than the 45-minute swarm and inconsistent with “no repeat swarm without a material new event.” Suppression at three unchanged alerts and a 24-hour hold is an arbitrary patch. Escalation at probability 0.75 puts back a single probability threshold. Minimum evidence change, second-alert cases I-02 and I-09, and re-entry criteria are not specified tightly enough to run.

Fairness names the required topics and then offers safeguards that the earlier sections do not use. A minimum intervention rate for low-value cases can force negative-value swarms. A standing Non-Native-Language Support buffer, on top of the regulatory reserve, is not feasible at four slots. Calibration monitoring is described as adjusting ARR, which is the wrong object. The label problem is answered with a holdout and quarterly retraining; post-swarm outcomes would still enter future labels as if they were untreated. Spillover is said to be inside the decision rule, but the score in Section 1 has no externality term. Non-monetary duties — public-sector obligation, the regulatory arrival, harm not captured by the euro burden — are not weighed against the euro score.

Monitoring lists the right headings and does not specify a measurement design that could survive this intervention. A chi-square test is not a calibration check; expected calibration error, reliability slope, and segment bias would be. Case-level A/B testing is contaminated by the queue spillover the policy is supposed to measure. The 0.1 cumulative-error trigger is undefined. A negative realized impact is set to trigger model recalibration rather than a change to the allocation rule. There is no logging plan for assignment, reason code, queue depth, specialist interruption, or suppression decisions, and no immediate override for the known Strategic Enterprise workflow shift.

Taken together, the answer would not produce a coherent same-day allocation, and its expected-impact conclusion of a positive net in every scenario is not supported by its own arithmetic or by the spillover model it was told to use.
