6.5

The write-up is complete, segment-aware, and built around three genuinely different designs, with explicit sacrifices, bottleneck migration, and a staged pilot that tries to detect downstream displacement. Those strengths are undercut by repeated process-mining and causal errors, plus numeric claims the evidence cannot support.

The diagnosis cites real artifacts (edge volumes, performance tails, fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces, the 14.1% vs 3.3% complaint split, the 31%→9% cost reversal, the 82% utilization cliff, and segment medians). It then mishandles them. Directly-follows counts are treated as end-to-end variant volumes and unique cases: 2,550 auto→fast approvals are called the dominant full path, and 510 incoming complaint edges are called total complaints, while the case rate of 9.2% implies about 460 cases and complaint outflows sum only to 430. Several activities do not conserve flow (manual review inflows far exceed listed outflows; dispatch and repair are also unbalanced), and that incompleteness is never flagged. Medians are added (0.5h + 0.4h). “Percentage of fitting traces” is mislabeled trace fitness, and non-fitting traces are equated with proven R1/R2 breaches. “Complaint after close,” one of the three named alignment deviations, and rule R3 are largely absent from the diagnosis. Repair arrivals are stated as 390, omitting escalation and rework inflows. The 3.3% figure is the completed repair-quote path only, not the reviewed path; quote→complaint (80) and approval→abandon (70) show unhappy cases leaving before that statistic is measured. “Fast path is net-negative for high-value devices” is not in the data. The aggregate fast path is still 9% cheaper after rework.

Redesign A is the right kind of split (keep accessories fast, force high-value through review), but its mechanism is not grounded. Accessories are asserted to be exactly the ≤€400 population and to account for ~2,150 manual-review cases, so reviewer load falls by >40% and utilization goes below 65%. The log does not show segment mix inside manual review, and the prompt’s own rule-update effect is a 15–35% increase in manual inflow. Amending R2 for all sub-€400 accessories is then scored as near-elimination of compliance risk. Projected trace fitness above 95% and a 48–72h device cycle time (versus observed reviewed medians of 96h and 120h) are invented. The diagram’s “35–57%” device share is inconsistent with the 35% high-value share used in the text.

Redesign B correctly targets the evidence loop and wrong-part tail, and it does register digital-literacy abandonment. It over-claims the payoff: a 30–45% cut in repeat evidence requests (rendered as 35–45%) is turned into a drop from 140h to 36–48h, and the allowed 6–18h gate is narrowed to 6–12h. All 260 dispatch→complaint edges are treated as wrong-part events the gate will remove. A “5-minute” and, elsewhere, “10-minute” sign-off confuses touch time with multi-hour transition times on a resource that queues sharply above 82% utilization. Fitness “above 98%” ignores remaining R3 and post-close complaint deviations.

Redesign C is a real third design (repair-first, kill the price-quote wait, retire the 45-minute board). It still treats the selected 3.3% repair-path complaint rate as the rate you get by forcing devices into repair, and it claims quality check will cut high-value rework from 12.5% to under 4%. Repair→quality check already has 360 observations against 45 rework exits, so inspection is already on the main repair path; the 12.5% rate is what remains after that check. “100% trace fitness” and “zero” compliance risk are not available from this log. Adding mean sojourn times to “52 hours eliminated” overstates what a price catalog can remove. Swarming is not charged the coordination tax on the two fraud specialists who are already the Monday bottleneck.

The comparison table violates the instruction to avoid unsupported exactness and not to declare Pareto dominance from this evidence. Cost bands (−15% to −22%, −18% to −25%, −10% to −18%), utilization under 65%, and fitness targets are fabricated, and the current process is marked dominated. Conditional dominance by segment is the result the data can actually support. Standard low-risk customers who tolerate 72 hours with visibility are not a dominated case.

The experiment is the strongest section: premium accessory versus premium high-value pilot, conformance and utilization metrics, rollback if the review queue breaks, a long observation window, and checks for call-center and warehouse displacement. It inherits the bad baselines. Abandonment “6.4%” is 320/5,000 evidence-loop exits, not 390/5,000 total abandons (7.8%). A p90 of 180 hours is 7.5 days, so a 14-day readout does not miss that edge’s p90. The go rule “net cost decreases by 12% to 18%” excludes both weaker and stronger results. Phase-1 sequencing assumes the express lane frees reviewer capacity; if accessories are already mostly on the fast path, enforcing R1 does the opposite and the later phases start from a tighter bottleneck.
