The answer detects the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat complexity stratification as identification in the comparison table, separates queueing from service, and gives a safer phased alternative to the 50% reassignment. Those are the core requirements, and most stratum counts, means, and the pooled standardization (about −2.5 h) check out.

It is not close to flawless. The diagnostic script never runs: the CSV column is `group`, but every calculation uses `resource_group`. `discover_dfg` is also not unpacked, so the directly-follows comparison fails even after that fix. Section 1 is far over the 180–300 word limit, and the recommendation is over the 200-word limit.

Causal discipline slips in several places. The selection hypothesis has the bias sign backward: dropping long Orion cases would make completed Orion cases look faster, yet that pattern is listed as contradicting positive selection, and the predicted “upward” bias conflicts with the stated mechanism. Within-stratum review service is slightly lower for Orion in every complexity band, not “slightly higher.” In the high band both groups are fully compliance-required and have the same 50% rework rate, so “more compliance/rework” is wrong there. The recommendation claims the reversal survives adjustment for compliance and shift; only complexity was standardized, and shift barely overlaps. The graph writes two `quality_outcome` edges backward and treats `resource_group → shift` as a supported causal arrow when the facts only show bundled coverage. The executive verdict and recommendation then lean on “Orion is faster because of certified night capacity” more strongly than the non-identification caveats elsewhere allow.
