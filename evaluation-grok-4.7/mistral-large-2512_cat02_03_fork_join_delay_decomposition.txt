5.5

The case-level fork-join arithmetic is the strongest part of the answer, and it is not enough to carry the rest. Sections 3–6 contain quantitative errors, one inverted influence judgment, counterfactuals that ignore slack and final-station masking, and Python that cannot run.

**What holds.** The eight definitions match the stated semantics: upstream is `fork_release − arrival`, branch queue is measured from `fork_release`, service is start-to-end, `join_ready` is the max of the three ends, downstream queue is `final_start − join_ready`, and total flow is `final_end − arrival`. The per-branch identity `queue + service + sync = fork_join` is the right reason that summing synchronization waits cannot be added into elapsed case time. All 12 table rows check out, including critical branch (A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, B on the other eight), and the four worked cases are correct. B is rightly separated from “longest mean service.”

**Section 3 is numerically wrong.** From the answer’s own table, mean B service is 37.5, not 38.3; mean B queue is 19.17, not 20; mean C service is 20.17, not 20.5; mean C queue is 10.42, not 10.8. Mean synchronization wait is about 11.0 (A), 4.3 (B), and 30.4 (C), not 18.3, 0, and 18.3. B’s mean sync is zero only on the cases where B is critical. The F04 illustration treats the 18-minute downstream wait as C’s sync wait; C is critical there, so its sync wait is 0. Large sync wait is evidence of low control over `join_ready`, so using it to rank C is backwards.

**A versus C is mishandled.** Critical frequency is a 2–2 tie, which the ranking does not treat as a tie. A’s fork-to-end time is 50 on every case, and A is first or second on all 12 cases. C’s path is usually near 23–28 minutes and becomes critical only in the F04 and F08 queue spikes. A is the usual constraint on how much a faster B can move `join_ready`. Ranking C above A is not supported.

**Counterfactuals contradict the case table and the slack.** A 20% cut in A service is 10 minutes, but F01’s slack over B is only 5 minutes, so `join_ready` can fall by 5 before B takes over. F11’s slack is 10, yet F11’s final start is back-to-back with the previous F1 job, so that gain is absorbed by downstream delay. Halving B queue is not a uniform 10-minute saving: the eight B-critical queues are 20, 20, 25, 30, 30, 25, 20, and 30. Join-level effects are further capped by slack (about 5 minutes on F10, 10 on F05, and about 15 on F06, F07, and F12). The write-up states full flow-time savings for every currently critical case and then mentions masking only as a possible side effect. “Secondary effects: none” for the A cut is also too strong: the critical branch switches once the slack is used up.

**Recommendations use a misread scenario.** A and B are technical; C is administrative. Pooling B with C as “both administrative” is not an assumption, it is a contradiction of the prompt. Starting A before `fork_release` is described as reducing A’s service time; it would change the release offset, and A’s queue is already zero. Final service is exactly 20 minutes on two alternating resources, arrivals are every 15 minutes, and several downstream gaps occur while the assigned F resource is already free. That pattern is unused, even though `final_resource` was supplied and it is the right check on whether added F capacity would move flow time.

**The script does not implement the claims.** `apply_counterfactual` is run on the result table but reads `A_start`, `A_end`, `B_end`, `C_end`, `fork_release`, and `final_start`, which that table does not have. With final start held fixed, `(join − fork) + (final_start − join)` cancels, so the A and B counterfactuals would save nothing even after the column error was fixed. That result also disagrees with the 10-minute savings in the prose. `pm4py.get_service_time` is called without the activity it requires, and `discover_performance_dfg` is unpacked as two return values. The interval-log construction and the comment that directly-follows performance does not recover max-based synchronization are the parts that match the requested design.

**Section 7 is directionally useful and still soft.** Causality, unlogged branch capacity, preemption, and release policy versus queue are the right limits. Overlapping intervals still identify a lower bound on concurrent branch occupancy, A and F durations are deterministic, and the F1/F2 alternation is observable. Those identifiable facts are left out, while several redesign claims are stated more strongly than twelve cases and an unidentified waiting mechanism allow.
