The write-up covers all ten required sections, correctly isolates the R5/R6 clash, and gets the business outcome of several traces roughly right (T4 segregation, T5 open clarification, T7 late opinion, T8 double terminal). The formal artifacts do not implement those judgments, and several encodings are unsound.

The procedural model is not executable PM4Py. `apply_leaf`, `apply_sequence`, `apply_parallel`, `apply_xor`, and `apply_optional` are not the process-tree API. The code also disagrees with its own sketch: screening is sequenced after a two-way parallel, so the required three-way concurrency is gone, and T2’s screening-first order would not fit. `Close Onboarding` is missing on the reject branch, so T3 does not fit the tree. `Obtain Legal Opinion` never appears, while `Sanctions Escalation` is trapped before classification even though T6 and T7 escalate after it. `Skip Clarification` is not an allowed label, and the “loop” is a single optional sequence. Section 8 still marks T1–T3, T5, and T7 as procedural passes.

DECLARE mis-states two obligations. Unconditional `precedence` of `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Activate Vendor` fails every activation that never escalates (T1, T2, T4, T5, T8); R5 is conditional on escalation. `response` from `Verify Bank Account` to `Release First Payment` fails every rejection, including T3, and is not what R11 says. R3 needs alternate response (a receive before the next request, and before a final outcome); plain response plus precedence allows stacked requests and a receive after activation. The matrix marks T1–T4 DECLARE passes anyway. Section 9 then claims confidence is uniformly 0.95, but the dictionary uses 0.95, 0.90, and 0.85.

The log skeleton would reject every trace. `directly_follows` is an allowed-relation whitelist, not a sample of the initial fork; only three pairs are listed. Path-specific `always_before` pairs such as manager-before-activate, compliance-before-activate, and reject-before-close do not hold on the other route. The claim that `always_before` forbids interleaving confuses it with chain precedence. Those cells are still marked pass.

The temporal profile itself is the soundest piece: the three R12 pairs, hour-to-second conversion, and `zeta = 2.0` are right, and T7’s 34h escalation-to-opinion gap is a real warning (|34−12|/6 > 2) while the 2h payment gap stays inside zeta. The resource check would catch T4. The emergency check treats activation-before-opinion within 24h of activation as an exception even though joint authorization is absent from the log; under the chosen strict reading, missing authorization cannot clear R5. The 24h anchor (activation versus escalation) is never stated.

Section 8 therefore grades an informal policy, not the models above it. That breaks the coherence the task asks for, even though the conflict discussion and the deployment ordering are sensible.
