6.5

The design covers every required section and is usable as a first draft: non-stationary arrivals, role calendars, architect/district clustering, inspection bundling, latent objections, a full D1–D10 plus stress matrix, common random numbers, and a modular split between log handling and the simulator. It still has several design errors that would bias intervention comparisons.

The central simulation mechanism is inconsistent. Queues, pools, and calendars are specified so that waiting should be an output, but the event logic and estimation plan also sample waiting times as inputs (“separate random variables” for service and waiting, inferred by subtracting an imputed service time from the inter-event gap). Capacity, priority, batching, and smoothing policies (D2–D4, D6, D7) act through queues; feeding them historical waits double-counts congestion and mutes those effects. Exogenous clocks (applicant response, the 14-day public-notice minimum, consultant reports) are the only delays that should be drawn. That split is never made.

Service times are defined as raw timestamp differences. Planning, legal, and inspection work that spans nights, weekends, holidays, or council weeks would be stored as service and then delayed again by the same calendars. Estimation and the skeleton both use elapsed hours. The provided medians (for example 3.5 h planning, 14 h at p90) only make sense as working time. Statutory public notice is calendar time; those clocks are not distinguished.

Warm-up treats a seasonal process as if it had a steady state. Welch’s method on mean cycle time and WIP assumes convergence to a flat mean, which March–May and September–November peaks do not have. A one-year average then hides the only period where D4 and D7 matter. Decision metrics are not stratified by season or by peak versus off-peak backlog.

Out-of-sample validation is a first-12-months versus last-6-months split on an 18-month log. That confound season with forecast error: each peak season appears at most about twice, so the 1.7× factor is weakly identified, and the holdout month mix differs from the calibration window. The same section never checks control-flow fitness. The tree’s redo labels, “Completeness rework” and “Inspection rework,” are not log activities; PM4Py loop semantics would insert those labels and would not reproduce a second “Completeness check.” Planning, legal, and appeal rework are absent from the tree but still sampled in `should_rework`. Conformance replay is omitted, so that mismatch would not be caught. Eighteen months also cannot separate seasonality from trend or from the removed emergency cases.

Several mechanisms the comparison depends on are unspecified or missing. D9 has no state change beyond “extra communication,” and objection intensity cannot be identified from a duration proxy. D2 eligibility needs prior violations, which are not in the log, and no-shows are not separated from inspection rework. Environmental “triage” is implemented as shorter service time even though the summary attributes that variability to consultant reports. Reject (30 min–2 h) has no resource pool; schedulers appear in the log but not in the calendars; the excerpt’s same-planner continuity is ignored, so pooling gains are overstated. D1’s pre-submission delay sits outside lead time, so cycle-time KPIs favor it by construction. Complex-variance “median < 150 days” is weaker than the observed median of 140 days, so the baseline already passes. Fairness uses an undefined “X days.”

The skeleton matches the requested shape: pandas load, `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the three standard columns, variant/DFG/performance-DFG/inter-arrival attempts, and placeholder arrival, routing, calendar, and experiment types. The imports are not current PM4Py: `pm4py.objects.conversion.log.factory` and `pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg.factory` are the removed factory API, and `variant="performance"` is not a valid argument to the frequency DFG discovery call. High-level `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`, `pm4py.get_variants`, and `pm4py.convert_to_event_log` are the consistent surface. When start timestamps are absent, the code writes the full inter-event gap into `waiting_time_h`. `parse_process_tree` is attempted, but the docstring only hedges that the helper might be missing; it does not state the redo-label, missing appeal/planning/legal loops, or silent-transition limitations. Seeds and stream assignment never reach `ExperimentRunner`, so the common-random-number plan is unimplementable when D2 drops activities and desynchronizes a shared stream.

Assumptions are honestly labeled and objections stay latent. The draft does not claim optimality. Those are real strengths. As written, a municipality could still rank D4, D6, D7, and D10 incorrectly because waiting, working time, and seasonality are not identified cleanly enough for the policies under test.
