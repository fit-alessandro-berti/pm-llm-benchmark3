The write-up keeps the required eight-part outline and reaches a few defensible high-level judgments: K-219 is a routing change, K-337 changes decision policy, K-411 is mainly enablement of concurrency, and K-337 is the most dangerous of the four. Those judgments are not backed by feasible, minimal-change paths or by timings derived from the given clocks and queue snapshots.

The partial order is too coarse and internally inconsistent. `Assessment < Compliance Review` conflicts with preliminary compliance running during identity and document checks. `Compliance Review < Decision` is stated unconditionally, while several cases have no compliance activity. Missing from the order are escalation advice, the rule that a request for more information can come from assessment or compliance, and the high-risk requirement to reaffirm compliance after later assessment. The constraint list does capture four-eyes, single-resource occupancy, originals, and the idea that queues are forecasts.

The case table is not a reliable artifact. Unescaped `||` breaks the Markdown columns, and the assumptions cells are filled with side effects while the side-effects column is empty. The paths are activity lists, not timed, resource-assigned, partially ordered executions.

K-104 drops S1, leaves preliminary compliance ambiguous relative to assessment, and asserts a 6–10 hour cycle-time cut without reconciling it to a final compliance start that cannot precede Tuesday 19:10 or to C1 backlogs of about 16–20 hours. The stated propagation direction is wrong: a C1 reservation displaces other compliance work; it does not push delay upstream.

K-219 serializes identity before originals and shows no rework branch. It invents a 10–20% error increase, ignores the 8–36 hour external-rework range, and gives a document-task interval rather than an end-to-end range tied to the Monday 17:30 D2 queue.

K-337 is infeasible. The intervention stops only the second information request after Assessment 2. The path deletes the first rework cycle, skips the mandatory compliance review on a high-risk, high-value case, and therefore also drops four-eyes. A 12–24 hour saving is temporally inconsistent with the Friday-to-Monday external wait: removing that loop removes on the order of several days, while compliance and a conditional or negative decision still remain. Section 3 describes the correct intervention and then conflicts with that path.

K-411 has the right concurrency shape, but the 4–8 hour saving is not derived. Document completion, not identity, was the gate, and the case already waited overnight for assessment. The base case both claims hours saved and says the assessment queue is unchanged.

The capacity table mis-states several spillovers. Early C1 preparation is said to grow the identity and assessment queues. Rework suppression is said to grow compliance and is paired with an appeal “reduction.” At 100 cases, routing document work to D2 would saturate D2 and idle D1; the write-up instead says corrections overwhelm D1. It also calls the K-104 population low-priority even though those cases are high-risk and high-value. The required 250–400 word rollout is about 110 words and omits weekend and external-response effects and timestamp integrity versus true concurrency. It never uses the single-server fact that C1 cannot honor provisional reservations for 100 similar cases.

The ranking is broadly sensible, with decision-policy change last and parallelization first, but “high” robustness and “moderate to high” benefit for parallelization conflict with the congestion analysis. The pilots name every required row and still fail as designs: eligibility is vague, compliance eligibility would flood the scarce reviewer, sample logic is undefined, and parallelization’s spillover is recorded as minimal. The synthesis is about 90 words against a required 220–320, names K-411 and K-337 correctly at headline level, and then asks only for generic “queue and timing data.” Assumption 8 is an interference label on a quality effect. The other assumptions are broad enough that they do not expose the load-bearing claims about reservation, overnight work, or weekend waits.
