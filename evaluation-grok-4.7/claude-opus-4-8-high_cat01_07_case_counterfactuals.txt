The write-up matches the required structure, keeps the four cases on different control-flow skeletons, and gets the main qualitative distinctions right: K-337 changes the decision standard, a preliminary screen does not discharge final compliance, four-eyes stays intact, queue figures are forecasts, and local gains can move congestion. Those strengths do not offset several timing and minimal-change errors that sit in the middle of the reasoning.

The K-104 clock is mis-anchored. Monday’s 18–20 h compliance forecast is treated as this case’s critical-path wait. The file is not compliance-ready until Tuesday evening, after assessment and the factual escalation, and the Wednesday 08:00 forecast is already about 8 h. The realized wait is the post-eligibility gap into Wednesday 13:10. The same section says the intervention only adds a triage packet, while the path deletes the Tuesday 19:20–21:20 escalation block. That deletion changes both the activity set and the moment final compliance can start. The parallel brace `{prelim ∥ identity ∥ document}` also overstates what C1 can do on Monday: C1 is the congested single server, two urgent compliance cases arrive the same day, and S1’s advisory overlap does not create an early C1 reading slot. Service arithmetic is loose as well: a 4.5 h review with a 1–3 h warm-start saving leaves about 1.5–3.5 h, not a 2–4 h review, and the table’s −4 to −12 h band excludes the derivation’s own near-zero case.

K-337’s magnitude fights its mechanism. From Friday 10:30 to the factual Wednesday 09:10 finish is on the order of 119 calendar hours. A Friday compliance-and-decision turnaround would save far more than 40 h; a weekend shutdown with Monday completion would still save about two days. The stated −24 to −40 h band only fits a future in which compliance and sign-off remain late, which undercuts the claim that the weekend external wait is the main source of the gain. The path also leaves Rule 6 open: compliance can still issue Request More Info, so prohibiting the second assessment loop does not by itself force a terminal decision. C1’s Friday capacity is never placed on a snapshot.

K-219 and K-411 are directionally sound and still loosely timed. D2 at originals receipt faces the Monday 16:00 queue of about 5 h, not the stale morning 2 h figure, and M1’s next-morning sign-off can absorb an earlier Tuesday assessment so Notify need not move. The 25–40% handwriting-clarification rate is an unsupported point probability. For K-411, D2’s Tuesday 08:00 backlog is about 4 h, so document work does not become free at I2’s 11:30 start; the table’s −3 to −5 h cycle-time range again omits the near-zero assessment-absorption case argued in the derivation. The Tuesday “assessor in training” note, which bears on that absorption, is unused.

Section 4 is the best part: single-server reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework displacement into appeals, assessment bottleneck migration, weekend dependence, and false concurrency are all real effects, and the rollout length fits the requested window. The robustness rank is less stable. Spillover is a required ranking criterion, and the same answer calls broad C1 reservation zero-sum and the least transportable local result, then ranks that intervention first by narrowing it to a “disciplined” reservation. The pilot table, assumption count, and synthesis coverage are complete. Assumption 6 is a policy-validity claim filed as structural, and “benefit is convex below utilization 1” misnames the nonlinearity.

Net: feasible, constraint-aware counterfactuals with the right adverse-effect story, weakened by inconsistent clocks, one non-minimal path, an under-sized K-337 range, and a ranking that softens the intervention it then awards.
