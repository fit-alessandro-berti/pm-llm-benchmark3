6.8

The write-up reconstructs eligible sets and keeps FIFO, priority, SPT, appointments, and skills apart often enough that the four diagnoses land in a defensible region. It is not nearly flawless: a central preemption claim is misstated, one discrimination test reverses its own logic, and several causal glosses are stronger than the decision history.

**Mechanism errors**
- B05 is used as evidence that priority-1 work preempts. B05 is recorded at 09:46; B03 (priority 2) occupies B1 from 09:48 to 10:00; B05 starts only when B03 ends. That is a jump at a completion epoch. The 09:48 and 10:00 rows already show this, while the QB close and exception 1 call it preemption. The preemptive versus non-preemptive distinction is the point of QB.
- C05 is treated as an SLA override that explains 10:54. C05’s deadline is 10:50 and `est_min` is 40, so the deadline is already infeasible at 10:11, 10:18, and 10:32, when C03 and C04 are started instead. The story does not say why the emergency is ignored until the deadline has passed. At 11:40, C07 can still finish before 12:00 and C01 is chosen anyway, which cuts against a stable deadline rule. Priority and deadline stay confounded.
- Test 2 gets the observable backwards. If C06’s queue-entry time is after 10:32, C06 was not eligible and dynamic SPT on the true eligible set still holds. That result does not confirm a frozen snapshot. Snapshot batching needs C06 already queued and absent from the candidate set the dispatcher actually used.
- At 10:14 the appointment cell says “B07 if exact.” B07’s appointment is 10:30. An exact-time rule does not start B07 then. The relevant comparison is whether B01’s remaining 14 minutes finishes near 10:28, before that appointment.

**Overclaims and missing decision points**
- A07 before A08 is listed as headline FIFO support. At 09:32 SPT selects A07 as well. The separations are 08:32 (A02 over A04), 09:05 (A05 over both A07 and A08), and 09:20 (A06 over both A07 and A08). The 08:58 release is omitted: A04 is simultaneously FIFO-next, shortest, and the earlier priority-1 job, so that start separates none of the three.
- Section 3’s line “A07 at 09:32 is earlier than A08, not A07 runs first” does not state a coherent claim.
- “C04 before C06 can be explained only by a snapshot or an unrecorded exception” is too closed. A locked initial-backlog SPT order, which the C01 note itself suggests, is another live reading, and it still leaves C06 ahead of C01 while C07 is not.
- Given a decision to remove B01 at 09:48, the earliest other waiter is B03. That agrees with FIFO among the remainder and disagrees with priority (B05) and SPT (B04). Labeling B03 as the strongest contradiction of a FIFO-based hybrid erases that split. The real stress on FIFO is stopping B01, and then taking B05 at 10:00 instead of resuming B01.
- Exception 1 merges QB’s logged “urgent arrival” with QC deadlines. QB has no deadlines. The logged “dispatcher reassignment” is direct evidence and outranks a generalized SLA mechanism.
- Resumption cost is required and barely used. Every preempted total equals `est_min`, so these logs show no measured processing-time loss on stop/resume. Visible overhead does not explain the failures to preempt.
- QD is aimed correctly: D04 before D08, and D07 before D10, falsify both priority and SPT inside the skill-feasible sets. The table skips 11:20, when D-X becomes free and stays idle until D06 while D07 is already waiting. Confidence 72 is high beside D-F’s idle stretch from 11:36 to 12:00, when D10 (electrical, priority 1, 12 minutes) would finish before the 12:00 meeting. The 11:21 priority cell calls D03 as if priority were decisive; D03 and D05 are both priority 2.
- The collection plan is specific and usable. C01 does not belong in the remaining-time row; it is never partly served before it starts. Scan delay is set aside too broadly: C05 is recorded at 10:11, so a delay of up to two minutes can put the true arrival before C1’s 10:10 release. SPT still picks C02; the priority counterfactual at 10:10 changes.

The score stays in the 6s because the eligible-set tables, B01 remaining-time arithmetic (31, then 14), the estimate-equals-realized check, the QA/QD rejection of priority, and the closing warning against global preemptive priority on QB and pure SPT on QC are real process-mining work. The mistakes above sit on the exact distinctions the case was built to test.
