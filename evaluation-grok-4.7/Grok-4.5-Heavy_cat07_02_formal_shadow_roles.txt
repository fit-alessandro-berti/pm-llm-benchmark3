The role analysis is careful and mostly faithful to the counts, similarities, complexity medians, and substitution episodes. Required sections, the table columns, the three mandated shadow roles, paired interpretations, and staffing cautions are all present, and shadow work is treated as a resilience mechanism with measurable downside rather than as misconduct. The score is held down by overclaim and by code that would not do what the prose says. Erik’s security authority is called absolute and non-substitutable, yet Chen (24), Mateo (16), Dina (6), and Ivan (4) also perform A6; 96 of those events is dominance, not an exclusive gate. Chen’s 24 security-adjacent events are read as higher risk authority, which the later Chen/Dina ambiguity then walks back. The PM4Py sketch names the right high-level functions, but several parameters are wrong. `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key`. Role discovery’s variant is `CLASSIC`, not `ACTIVITY_ROLE`. Roles expose `activities` and `originator_importance`, not `.resources` or `.label`, and the join is left as an empty `role_map`. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., [0.6, 1.0])` keeps exact values, so the “high-complexity rediscovery” does not select a range. Complexity is aggregated per event, so long cases outweigh short ones relative to the case-level table. Those gaps are in the section that was supposed to show how not to over-read frequency clusters.
