The answer is a serious, case-complete conformance review with the required headings, a real two-sided argument, and a layered revision that is mostly traceable to the record. It is not nearly flawless. Several classifications conflict with the answer’s own burden of proof, and the revised POWL silently drops a legitimate rejection path.

## What holds up

The stipulated, disputed, and unproved split is accurate. CS-9, the PD-3 window, the FD-7 window and conditions, the CRM missingness jump, the 47 information-request cases, and the 312 / 289 / 14 / 9 / 6 auto-approval counts are all used without invention. SUP-703 is correctly separated from an in-window low-risk pilot case: high-risk, ineligible, EUR 180,000. SUP-704 is correctly kept inside 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, with sanctions first, spend under EUR 20,000, monitoring, and next-business-day retrospective controls. SUP-705 and SUP-714 are correctly treated as post-expiry uses. SUP-712 is correctly read as a CS-9 different-human breach that M0 cannot see. SUP-715 is correctly not converted into that same rule: the problem stated in the record is reviewer–approver relationship plus missing conflict disclosure. SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are tied to the right mechanisms (missing loop, ambiguous intake scope, duplicate source transaction, delayed label map). Both briefs concede specific cases, and the system judgment names a bounded noncompliant set rather than equating every M0 deviation with misconduct.

The PM4Py sketch is in the right idiom: partial-order concurrency on the standard sanctions/quality track, an n-ary XOR over routes, a two-child loop, notification before close, and data rules kept outside the net. The loop comment correctly describes a silent do-child plus `Request_More_Info` redo. The overlay table and event schema cover stable `notification_id`s, rule versions, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, and separate event versus record timestamps.

## Material flaws

**The burden of proof is announced and then not applied.** Section 1 says a justified exception requires explicit authority plus immutable proof that every overlay condition was met, with that burden on operations. PD-3’s power to displace CS-9 is left disputed, and the Compliance observer’s later disclaimer is part of that dispute. SUP-702’s trace also has no automated master-data validation, which PD-3 requires before activation. The matrix lists that evidence as missing, then says the finding flips only if authority is rejected or eligibility facts are false. Section 5 still returns `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That is a lower bar than the one the answer set, and it is the clearest place where a contested overlay is resolved by accommodation. The prosecution brief creates the same slack: it argues that minutes and email never amended CS-9, then describes SUP-702 as “much better defended” instead of as the test of that theory.

**The revised net cannot reject a fully checked supplier.** M0 decides after both checks. M1’s reject branch is only sanctions then `Reject_Supplier`. The standard, auto, and emergency branches all end in activation. A trace such as register, sanctions, quality review, reject, notify, close is outside M1, and so is any pilot or emergency path that fails sanctions after those route-specific activities have started. The task allows an approximation only if the limitation is stated. It is not. Withdrawal and reactivation are explicitly left out; post-check rejection is simply absent.

**Two further POWL choices over-constrain the overlays without being named.** On the auto route, quality is ordered after activation. PD-3 permits quality up to two business days after activation; it does not forbid quality before the provisional activation. An eligible pilot case that finished quality first would fit neither the auto route nor the manual route. `Master_Data_Validation` is also mandatory in the language, but it appears in none of the observed auto-approval traces. Until logging exists, M1 would fail the cleanest pilot cases for a documentation gap. Deadlines and euro caps are correctly pushed to data rules; these two control-flow mismatches are not disclosed.

## Smaller faults

The defense never addresses concurrency, although that was a required topic. M0’s unsynchronized sanctions and quality checks are not what produces the false deviations; sequential check traces still satisfy the partial order. That point should have been made.

SUP-711 is classified `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` at high confidence, while the stated reason is that the shared source transaction id already explains a duplicate over a compliant body. “Indeterminate” and that degree of confidence do not match. SUP-706 has no case date, so “possible CRM-migration effect” is an unsupported link; the mutable late text join is enough on its own. SUP-703 is not the same pattern as SUP-702: SUP-703 contains `Notify_Requester`. SUP-705’s matrix treats continued flood conditions as “real emergency facts”; the record only has operations’ assertion, which Section 1 correctly leaves unproved. The defense’s “289 appear eligible” slide treats attribute eligibility as if master-data validation and the two-business-day quality condition were shown for that population. They are not.

Confidence is uneven. SUP-712 is high-confidence noncompliance while alias-resolution confidence is still listed as missing. SUP-715, whose relationship and missing disclosure are given in the same way as the alias fact, is only medium. SUP-708 and SUP-703 are both critical, despite no transactions before sanctions clearance in SUP-708 and EUR 180,000 of spend in SUP-703. Conditional “unless an overlay applies” rules are placed under DECLARE even though the answer elsewhere correctly says those predicates belong in data checks. The FD-7/PD-3 instants are encoded as UTC with no source timezone. No listed case sits on a midnight boundary, so the practical effect is nil, but the assumption is silent. SUP-704’s missing-evidence cell asks for the critical-supplier document and not for cumulative spend until retrospective approval; only initial spend is in the record.

## Bottom line

Case selection, expiry handling, separation of control-flow blindness from resource and conflict rules, and the data-collection proposal are strong. The grade stops well short of excellent because the exception standard is not applied to the case the answer itself flags as authority-contested and evidence-incomplete, and because the proposed normative language both omits ordinary rejection and adds unstated constraints that would mis-fire on otherwise valid pilot behavior.
