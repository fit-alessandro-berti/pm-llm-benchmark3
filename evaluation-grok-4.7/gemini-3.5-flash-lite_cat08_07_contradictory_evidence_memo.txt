6.5

The memo reaches the right family of decision (hybrid E: keep the simple-house gains, stop the harmful mechanisms, do not scale nationally, do not full-rollback), hits the required sections, and mostly quotes the core numbers correctly. It is not decision-safe. Several claims a management team might act on are invented, internally inconsistent, or misread the process-mining evidence.

The executive memo overstates certainty (“proves,” “backfired,” “high churn,” cases stalling “indefinitely”). Churn is not measured; NPS only moved from +21 to +17. Billing-before-activation was a designed pilot rule when the signal looked acceptable, not demonstrated metric-gaming. Regional mix (more apartments and older buildings), segment and risk attributes missing before week 3, cost data cut off after week 4, and a 6-week window are recorded in the table and then ignored when the memo locks a hard segment split.

Cross-section contradictions are material. The memo restores surveys for complex work and reallocates survey staff back to those segments; the final recommendation invents a “15%” transfer of survey capacity onto second-visit teams. Restoring surveys for roughly two-thirds of volume should raise survey demand and, if the skip rule was the cause, lower second visits, so that transfer fights the stated fix. Rural orders are left on the redesigned path in the memo and fully reverted in the final section. Immediate measurement is M2, M3, and M4 in the memo, M6, M3, and M4 in the ranking, and M3, M4, and M6 in the close.

The rollout bar is miscalibrated. Complex segments must beat baseline p90 and also hold 30-day complaints to 3%, against an overall baseline of 6.1%. A false-negative rate “below 2%” is undefined and unsupported. “Six additional months” for alternative D is invented, and A, B, and D are narrowed to strawman conditions.

Process mining is only partly understood. High fitness with precision 0.51 is explained as operator improvisation and a model that allows “virtually any path.” That misses the stated reading (many cases follow the simplified path, while the model still permits too many combinations of skip, manual activation, second visit, and billing). Generalization also fell, from 0.70 to 0.56, which a pure over-permissive model does not explain, and it is never used. Temporal-profile deviations, the handover network versus the working-together network, overall first-time-right (82% to 76%), and the SLA split are thin or absent. The evidence table is the right shape, but several “interpretations” (gaming, vague rules, contractor delay) are not supported by the log.

Value of information is complete but ordered for a decision already treated as settled. The confounded skip rule makes the randomized stratum test (M1) more decision-relevant than an observational rule check (M6). Conformance monitoring is framed as catching “rogue” behavior rather than watching a policy the pilot deliberately changed.
