3.5

The decomposition frame is recognizable, and the branch queue and service columns match the log, but the answer is not reliable on the quantities that decide the question. Flow time, the critical branch, and the counterfactuals are wrong often enough that the ranking and the redesigns rest on a corrupted table.

**Decomposition and case table.** Upstream time, all branch queues, all branch services, and final service (20 minutes on every case) are correct. Join time, the critical branch, and downstream queue are wrong for F04, F08, and F11. C ends at 09:52 on F04 and 10:50 on F08, so C is critical there; A ends at 11:30 on F11, so A is critical there. The true critical counts are A 2, B 8, C 2. The table reports A 3, B 9, C 0, and section 3 then reports B as 8/12. Those two counts cannot both be true, and 3+8 does not cover 12 cases. The worked F11 note sees that A is latest and then leaves the table uncorrected. Total flow is right only for F01 and F02. The other ten values are inflated (F03 is 120, not 140; F07 is 130, not 230; F12 is 125, not 285). Those totals also break the identity upstream + (join − fork) + downstream + final service, including when the answer’s own join time is used. The F04 arithmetic 610+20−515 is 115, not the stated 155.

**Definitions.** Queue, service, downstream queue, and flow time are defined correctly. Join-ready is written as \(\max(A_{A,c}, A_{B,c}, A_{C,c})\) after completion was defined as \(E_{i,c}\), so the max is not a usable formula. Fork–join stage time, \(J_c - \text{fork\_release}_c = \max_i(Q_{i,c}+S_{i,c})\), is never stated. The double-count remark has the right intuition and the wrong accounting: each branch satisfies \(Q_i+S_i+W_i = J-\text{fork}\), so summing the three synchronization waits adds \(3J-\sum E_i\) of parallel slack that is not in flow time at all. That identity is not shown.

**Aggregates.** A’s mean service is 50 and B’s is 37.5, not 37.9. C’s mean service is \(242/12 \approx 20.2\), not 19.4. Mean queues are about 19.2 for B and 10.4 for C, not 21.7 and 12.1. Reported mean synchronization waits do not match the corrected joins (A about 11, B about 4.3, C about 30.4). The sentence that B dominates service time is false; A has the longer service on every case. B is still the most frequent critical branch, but C does control join twice, both times through queue rather than service, which is exactly the distinction the task requires. Near-ties are omitted: F10’s B lead is 5 minutes, F08’s C lead is 5 minutes, and F01’s A lead is 5 minutes. There is no tail contribution to join-ready.

**Counterfactuals.** A 20% service cut is 10 minutes, but slack caps the join movement. On the real A-critical cases, F01 saves 5 minutes before B takes over, and F11 saves 10. F04 and F08, treated as the beneficiaries, save nothing because C remains critical. The stated 30-minute total is therefore the wrong set and the wrong per-case amount. Halving B’s queue saves join time only up to the next branch: about 92.5 minutes if the final start can move with the join (F10 saves 5 of the 10-minute queue cut; F05 saves 10 of 12.5), not “100–110,” and not on F11. Zeroing downstream queue is not supported by the F1/F2 trace. Several waits line up with the previous case on the same resource (F05, F08, F09, F11); others continue after that resource is free (F02, F03, F06, F07, F12). The claimed sum, 203, matches neither the correct downstream total, 176, nor the answer’s own column total, 198. Secondary effects are asserted in one scenario and denied in another.

**Code.** The script rebuilds the log and would compute correct timedeltas, so it does not reproduce the published table. It never computes synchronization waits, never prints a case or per-case savings table, and does not document tie-breaking. The A-service counterfactual clips \((J_{\text{new}}-J_{\text{old}})\) at zero and then subtracts it, which forces that savings term to zero and contradicts section 4. The interval frame uses `start`/`end` rather than `start_timestamp` and `time:timestamp`. `pm4py` is not imported; `format_dataframe` and a performance method are not called. The directly-follows comment is one sentence.

**What holds.** Section 7 correctly flags unknown branch capacity, unobserved preemption, non-identified causality, and the possibility that final waiting is a release rule. The two redesign directions, B’s queue and the final station, are plausible only after the critical-path and capacity facts above are repaired. Throughput is never compared with the observed load: arrivals every 15 minutes versus two 20-minute final servers.
