5.5

The design correctly refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, or open cases as non-defaults, defines a conditional-on-activation estimand, and prefers a temporal split to a random one. Those choices avoid the worst failure modes. The write-up is not statistically reliable enough to implement or to trust as a study protocol.

The as-of cohort is wrong in a material way. The 5,100 still-open applications are incomplete follow-up: many can still activate after 15 February 2026. Treating them as known non-activations, and dropping them with rejects and withdrawals, conditions on a future terminal state and drops recent, post-policy cases. Transfers are not marked as selectively unobserved despite 40% missing linkage and possible reappearance under a new id. The flow table never separates labeled, right-censored, competing, selectively missing, and not-yet-mature cases, even though those counts are given exactly (they sum to 48,000; the “approximate” totals are unnecessary).

Censoring is mis-specified. Ordinary right censoring (horizon still open and default not yet seen), a matured horizon whose external feed is merely 7–35 days late, and a permanently missing partner feed are different mechanisms. The primary analysis folds all unknown labels into right censoring at extraction. A late binary feed does not give a censored survival time; it gives a missing label. “No selection bias” is too strong: activation is policy selection. The conditional estimand is coherent only if that selection is the target, which this answer never cleanly separates from early-warning risk.

Prediction time is inconsistent. Scoring at Account Activated makes `final_limit` and `decision_reason` temporally available, but that is no longer “the first few events,” and it lets the credit decision into the features. Section 4 then also describes first-k prefixes. Elapsed-time prefixes are named and not specified. The 15 October 2025 rename and duplicate events are waived with “use the recorded name,” so the same step becomes two activities across the migration. Resource experience at extraction time is lookahead and should be dropped or rebuilt from events before the cutoff; keeping it “with a flag” does not fix it. `decision_reason` and `risk_score` can be backfilled, and that is not repaired.

Validation does not embargo label maturity. A test window of activations from November 2025 (or from 15 October in the code) still has horizons and feeds open on 15 February 2026, so calibration on the “known” subset selects fast feeds and early defaults. Section 7 and the code disagree on the validation cut (31 October versus 15 October). Suggesting that censored test cases be scored as non-defaults, even “for illustration,” reintroduces the bias the design rejects. The three “deployment” effects are mostly exogenous process changes, not feedback from using the score (who gets activated, early collection, behavior change).

The skeleton would not run and does not meet the spec. Case-level label logic indexes with `loc[case]` on a row index, `groupby` order does not match `unique()`, flags drop the case id before the merge, document-loop counting uses label `loc` on a filtered frame, and features are merged on a default RangeIndex that was never set to the case id. The split is by activation time, not case start, and non-activated cases are dropped first. There is no case table of start, terminal state, censor time, and label maturity. Post-prefix fields stay on the event table; the audit checks column-name substrings and does not remove them. `get_prefixes_from_log` is only demoed for one case, with an invented return shape. Survival or competing-risk connection is not marked in the code. The block is far longer than the requested 80–160 lines.

Taken together, the stance on labels is right, but cohort timing, censoring, leakage repair, validation maturity, and the implementation are not.
