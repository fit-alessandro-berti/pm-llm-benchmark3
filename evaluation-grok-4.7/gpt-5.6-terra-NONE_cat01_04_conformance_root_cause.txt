7.5

The write-up is well structured, covers every required section, and reaches the right qualitative conclusion: the June collapse is mostly label change, `Queue Routed`, and ingestion-time ordering, August adds a real sign-off rule the tree never absorbed, and missing compliance checks stay unresolved. It correctly refuses to treat the fitness drop as employee misconduct, and it flags that an optional sign-off branch can raise fitness while ignoring the EUR 50,000 condition. That is not enough for a near-flawless score. Several claims are arithmetically or temporally false, and the ranking treats irreconcilable summary statistics as high-reliability measurements.

The alignment staircase does not match the exhaustive variant lists under ordinary perfect-fit semantics. Every June–July trace contains `Risk Screen`, `Queue Routed`, or both, so perfectly fitting traces against the normative tree should be about 0%, yet the ledger rates the reported 32% at 85–96 and treats 32% → 58% after renaming as a controlled effect. After renaming alone, only the 90 traces with no `Queue Routed` and a legal order are in the language, which is about 14%, not 58%. Label-map plus dropping `Queue Routed` does land near the reported 81% (535/650 ≈ 82%). Full repair of all 70 `Notify`/`Decision` swaps would then land near 93%, not the reported 89%. August–September after the same three repairs is about 56% perfectly fitting on the old tree, not 66%, and about 90% on the optional sign-off tree, not 86%. June–July mean events per case implied by the variants is 3975/650 ≈ 6.12, not the stated 6.3, while April–May (≈5.43) and August–September (≈6.78) do match. April–May fitting of 93% is exactly the share left after dropping the 40 `Request Info` traces, which `*( 'Standard Review', 'Request Info' )` should accept. Those inconsistencies are inside the evidence the ranking calls 95–97 reliable. A provenance-aware reading would have demoted 32% and 58%, kept the rename itself (release notes plus the 40-case UI audit), and treated 81% as the one June–July fitness step that actually reconciles.

The ledger also misstates timing. `Manual Override` is already a June–July variant (20 cases). The 2026-08-12 patch only changed how an existing action was logged; it did not introduce the label. That error blurs technical-event insertion, semantic-logging change, and real user behavior. The ETL switch on 2026-06-15 sits inside the June–July bucket, but the timestamp hypothesis never requires a within-period split, so the 70 reversals are not shown to post-date the pipeline change. Screenshots cover 11 of 70 only. Posterior 91 is too high for the claim that ordering defect explains that pattern as a class.

Hypothesis scoring is uneven. Label change, gateway events, and an obsolete tree are fairly ordered as coexisting causes. “Real behavioral process change” at 72 mixes a documented August policy change with the separate question of what caused the measured drop. Noncompliance is split (43 versus 63); this one is not. The enhanced path was 180/180 checked in April–May and only later shows 25/170 and 30/270 without a recorded check. That onset is concurrent with the logging changes and is stronger evidence than the memo, which the ledger rightly discounts, but the before/after contrast is left implicit. “Overfit” in the causal narrative is the wrong fault: the candidate tree overgeneralizes and will lose precision. Later sections correct this.

The causal list includes the required nodes and the prose (about 210 words) does explain joint mechanisms and why stable cycle time, approvals, and appeals neither clear the log nor clear a small high-risk subset. The direct edge `true_process -> alignment_moves` still skips the recording layer the rest of the argument depends on.

The four-phase program is the strongest operational part: each phase isolates a different confounder, and precision is required beside fitness so an optional sign-off model cannot win on fitness alone. Counterfactuals are specific and falsifiable. Governance rules match the six requested controls and are concrete.

The PM4Py block hits the required calls: process tree, Petri net, `parse_event_log_string`, DFG, alignment diagnostics, alignment fitness, rename plus optional `Queue Routed` removal, raw-versus-normalized comparison, and an August–September fitness/precision comparison against `X ( tau, 'Supervisor Sign-off' )`, with an explicit EUR 50,000 caveat. Two weaknesses remain. Perfect-fit share is inferred from optional `fitness` / `trace_is_fit` keys even though `cost` is already in hand, so that print can be silently zero. The script also never checks its own perfect-fit rates against the published 32/58/81/89/66/86 figures, which is the check that would have exposed the bad steps.

Net: right mechanism, right restraint about staff blame, right warning on an overpermissive model, and a real failure to audit mixed aggregates before assigning posteriors.
