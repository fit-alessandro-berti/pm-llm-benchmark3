3.0

The paper keeps the required headings and tables, and four primary labels (P-503, P-504, P-506, P-508) point in a defensible direction. Those labels are not earned. The method contradicts itself, the rule catalogue misstates the sources, and the case rows mix timelines, invent facts, and swap evidence between files. A reviewer could not rely on it.

## Method
Section 1 states three incompatible decision rules at once: GPIP-4 is the highest norm, ECD-2 is most authoritative inside its window, and the most restrictive control always wins. No rule decides which meta-rule prevails. Facts and assumptions are not separated. A6 (a later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval), A4 (emergency status waives competitive tendering only), A7 (only a written, scoped, time-limited Chief Risk Officer exception), and the open question whether a COO/Incident Commander directive is an A7 exception are never given a working treatment.

The hierarchy is one flat ranking, despite the request for an issue-specific ordering. “What it can override” is reversed for several rows: GPIP-4 is described as overriding finance approval and compliance clearance, which are controls it creates. The Aachen SOP is described as able to override GPIP-4, although A5 allows local rules to be stricter and forbids them to weaken GPIP-4 silently. The CFO email, an ordinary message outside the controlled repository, is given power to override GPIP-4, the SOP, and ECD-2, while the same row says the CFO cannot waive finance segregation. ON-44 is placed in the norm hierarchy. The source itself says the outage changes evidential weight and is not a normative exception.

## Rule catalogue
The catalogue invents and misstates duties. R4 requires Legal_Clearance for every unapproved supplier before compliance and before the PO. The only legal-clearance text is D2, and it is an amount rule above EUR 150,000 in an informal email. R3 assigns Compliance_Clearance to the Finance Controller and times it to Create_PO. A3 requires it before supplier commitment, for a purchase above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier. R8 limits Finance_Approve to purchases above EUR 50,000 at Aachen. B2 already requires Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve at EUR 25,000, and A2 requires both an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller before commitment at EUR 50,000. R6 says the PO must precede commitment and lists no exception, while B4 and C1 are express timing exceptions. R1’s request-before-commit rule is not in the sources. R9 and R10 are duplicates, and both assign the emergency form to the Incident Commander without a source. A1, the segregation rule, is absent from the catalogue. That omission drives the later case errors.

The pseudo-logic widens the conflict. It lets ECD-2 override plant and finance approval, treats C2 as a waiver of Compliance_Clearance, and creates a condition that the CFO email must arrive within one hour of the ECD-2 start. The email was sent on 15 July at 07:20, about 22 hours after the directive began. Case analysis then applies the email without that invented limit.

## Cases
Rules are applied to the wrong time, and several rows argue against their own classification.

- P-501 is classified NONCOMPLIANT because commitment preceded the PO, while the justification says ECD-2 and the CFO email allow that sequence. Commitment was at 08:50 on 14 July. ECD-2 began at 09:00. The clock caveat makes the real act earlier, so it cannot be pulled into the window. The CFO email is dated the next morning. The four-hour stoppage is a B4 emergency, the form at 07:50 the next day falls inside 24 hours, and EUR 38,000 does not trigger A2 or A3. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. None of that timing is used. Telephone recordings are cited for this file; the file does not contain them.
- P-502 is classified JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Compliance_Clearance at 18:30 and Finance_Approve at 13:05 both follow the 10:15 commitment, and the CFO email did not yet exist. A3 and A2 apply, and A6 blocks a post-hoc cure. The row states that there was no finance approval. The joint authorization was recorded at 12:55, inside four hours of 10:10. Section 6 recommends recording it as though that had not happened.
- P-503 is noncompliant on any serious reading, because there is no compliance or finance approval on a EUR 140,000 purchase and PROC-3 is requester, committer, and one of the two authorizers. The row misses that segregation breach. It imports P-502’s 12:55 recording, treats C1 as permission to skip plant, finance, and compliance, and ignores the three-day backup-line scope dispute.
- P-504 is rightly outside the sterilization scope, but the row lists R2 and R7 as satisfied and then lists R2 as the deviation. EUR 24,000 does not trigger plant or finance approval. The clean breach is B3, together with B1 once B4’s six-hour or safety test is not met. The justification says ECD-2 does not require Incident Command authorization for non-critical purchases, which does not support NONCOMPLIANT.
- P-505 is called COMPLIANT with high confidence because the ERP commitment is 18:06 and finance approval is 19:00. The supplier acknowledgment is 17:59:31, the call starts at 17:57, and the ERP clock may be nine minutes fast. Those facts are the center of the evidential instruction, and they are not used. Finance approval after commitment does not satisfy A2. Plant approval is absent. Severity is scored Low on an EUR 88,000 boundary case.
- P-506’s NONCOMPLIANT label is available because a verbal overnight extension has no written record and the directive ended at 18:00 on 18 July. The controlling-interpretation cell adopts that extension as the governing norm. Finance approval at 06:05 actually precedes commitment. Plant approval is late, and the PO follows commitment. Section 6 then remediates this file as an unapproved supplier above EUR 100,000 requiring legal clearance.
- P-507 is called COMPLIANT with high severity. Compliance at 08:50 precedes commitment, two Incident Command members authorize at 08:55, and the signed PDF at 12:40 falls inside four hours even though the ERP entry is 14:30. That is late evidence of a timely record. The row instead cites a legal clearance at 16:00, which belongs to P-508, and treats an invented legal-clearance rule as the controlling issue. No finance or plant event exists on EUR 110,000. High-confidence COMPLIANT does not follow.
- P-508’s NONCOMPLIANT label fits the missing finance and plant approvals on EUR 155,000. D1’s own ceiling is EUR 150,000, and D2 adds legal clearance “as well,” which was obtained at 16:00 and does not replace A2. Compliance preceded commitment, and neither Incident Command authorizer was BUY-8, so segregation holds. The row treats BUY-8’s separation from Incident Command as the defect.

## Alternatives, evidence, and redraft
Section 5 does not meet its own test. The P-502 alternative remains JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. The P-501 alternative uses the next day’s email. The P-507 alternative discusses waiving the EUR 100,000 threshold on a file where compliance was already obtained before commitment. The stronger reversing premises are missing: B4 for P-501, A6 for P-502, the acknowledgment and fast clock for P-505, and the EUR 150,000 cap for P-508.

Section 6 never separates a policy breach, a logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove compliance. P-508 is omitted. Several remedies tell the site to collect the missing approval after the fact. That is the cure A6 rejects. P-504 is told to obtain finance approval below both thresholds. P-506 and P-507 receive each other’s facts.

Section 7 ranks ECD-2 above board policy, then says the CFO email is interpretation only, then writes an exception that lets that email waive finance segregation up to EUR 150,000 and, with legal clearance, above it. The same clause allows a retrospective Chief Risk Officer exception to justify any missed approval. The four-hour record and 30-day review are a real anti-normalization control, and they are undermined by that retrospective cure. Scope of “critical” lots, expiry, verbal extensions, segregation as a non-waivable integrity control, and the difference between supplier onboarding and Compliance_Clearance remain unresolved.

The recurring failure is discipline under time and evidence. Window edges, the direction of the ERP clock, the PDF-versus-ERP record, and which approval existed before commitment are the questions the materials were built to force. The paper states a caution about ERP timestamps in Section 1 and then decides the boundary cases from the wrong clock, the wrong file, or a later approval.
