The memo reaches the right decision (hybrid E: no national scale, no full rollback, restrict the redesign to simple low-risk houses, and buy information before any wider rollout) and it hits every required section, including a real fact/interpretation/assumption split, all five metric conflicts, A–E, and a full M1–M8 ranking.

It is not reliable enough to score higher. The headline gain is wrong throughout: median cycle time fell from 14.2 to 10.8 days, a 3.4-day improvement, not 2.4. Simple houses are 34% of the pilot, not a majority. Segment results are called unambiguous even though the pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings than baseline and segment/risk attributes are missing before week 3, so the aggregate and segment contrasts are confounded. The fitness/precision account then slips: low precision means the discovered model allows unobserved behavior, not that “the log captures all deviations” or that this is not a modeling problem. The rollout threshold is internally inconsistent (simple-house p90 already beat baseline, yet success is defined as converging to baseline; EUR 175 is not “at least as good as” EUR 184). Temporal-profile deviations, the survey-team vs second-visit utilization shift, NPS, and the generalization drop are largely unused, and “resolves >70% of the uncertainty” is an unsupported figure. Hyphen-stripped wording further weakens an otherwise well-structured memo.
