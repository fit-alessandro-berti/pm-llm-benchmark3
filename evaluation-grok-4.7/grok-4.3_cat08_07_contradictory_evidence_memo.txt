5.5

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid and respects the bans on blind national scale and blanket rollback, but it is not decision-safe. Option C is reversed in both the memo and §4: C already suspends survey skipping and post-billing activation, yet the answer treats it as leaving those mechanisms untouched and then gives a “reasonable if skip is not causal” condition that points the opposite way. That error makes the E-over-C choice unsupported.

§3 misstates the fitness/precision conflict. High fitness with precision 0.51 means a permissive model, matching the mining team’s “too many combinations” reading; calling this overfitting is the wrong quality dimension, and the dropped generalization (0.70 to 0.56) is ignored. “Unobserved or invalid” also conflates discovered-model precision with alignment deviations against an expected model. The median/tail account is only partial: apartments themselves improved on the median (−2 days) while the p90 worsened (+12), so the conflict is within-segment, not just mix between simple houses and hard buildings. Temporal-profile and DFG evidence are essentially unused.

The opening bundles business customers into “p90 +12–25 days” and “complaints +5–7 pp,” but that segment is +5 days and +4 pp. “Manual activation doubled” is an overall 7%→15% result, not a shown within-segment fact, and “improved accountability” was an expectation, not a result. Case-mix bias (more apartments and older buildings), the 6- vs 10-week windows, sparse start times, and segment attributes missing before week 3 never constrain how hard the aggregate or segment claims are pushed.

§5 ranks measures but does not say how each result would change the decision, so the value-of-information requirement is missed. M5 is ranked last even though coordinator ownership is what they would scale and absence-resilience is an open causal question. M1 and M6 are treated as additive rather than largely redundant for the skip-rule decision.

§6 then contradicts itself: activation-before-billing is “enforced,” while post-billing activation is paused only outside simple houses. Thresholds also sit worse than baseline (complaints ≤7% vs 6.1%, first-time-right ≥80% vs 82%, p90 up to baseline+2 days) and cannot be met by “four consecutive weeks” plus 60-day cost on the same cohort. Permit control for old buildings is sound; apartment treatment, utilization shift back onto survey teams, and a conformance gate for billing-before-activation are not specified.
