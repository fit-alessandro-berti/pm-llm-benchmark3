4.6

The arithmetic in the base load and dedicated skill-capacity tables is mostly auditable and correct (service means, block loads, break-adjusted upper bounds, U2 renormalization, U4 means, and the 981→621 software bound). Assumptions are labeled, three policies are specified in operational language, and the monitoring attribute list covers the requested fields. That is the credit.

The deliverable is incomplete and internally inconsistent. Section 9 is absent. The Python block stops mid-function, never applies handover, setup, parallel coordination, preemption, shifts, or breaks, never emits an event log, never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, or `discover_handover_of_work_network`, and never prints a comparison. Section 4 nonetheless reports cross-seed ranges as if that scaffold had been run.

Those ranges are not a safe basis for the recommendation. HYB beats POOL and SPEC on essentially every cell, with wide overlapping intervals treated as dominance. Section 5 then says POOL can produce better P2/P3 delay, which the same table does not show. \(Z_1\) and \(Z_2\) are never scored. In \(Z_1\), raw mean delay in minutes swamps the SLA terms, so the stated P1 bias is not what the formula implements. Fairness and resilience are not on a defined numeric scale.

Policy design ignores the speed matrix the load section just computed. SPEC and HYB home Ada (1.35) and Farah (1.40) to Electrical and Chen (1.25) to Software, while Chen and Deepa at 1.00 Electrical are homed elsewhere. Ada is the reserved P1 resource but cannot do Software, so morning software P1s have no reserved server. HYB overflow is contradictory: a wait threshold is stated, then any home-lane work blocks leaving the lane, and “overloaded” parallelization is undefined. The SPEC evening rota moves the only fast software resource onto Electrical.

Bottleneck reasoning overstates non-additive upper bounds. Skill capacities cannot be staffed at once. The 16:00–18:00 problem is joint load of about 392 standard minutes versus about 210 clock minutes on Deepa and Emil, not merely software 92 versus 90. Emil has no Mechanical skill, so post-18:00 mechanical work cannot “compete for Emil.” U3 is illustrated with 16:00–18:00, when Chen is already off shift. U1’s “+8 percentage points” is diluted in code because P1 is renormalized with P2 and P3. The 72-hour horizon is fed only one day of arrivals, and breaks are coded in `split_windows` but not used by the scheduler.

`get_service_time` is described as returning per-event service times; it is an activity aggregate. Case-level `@@case_waiting_time` is not separated from queue, handover, synchronization, and off-shift gaps, which the required PM4Py checks depend on. Parallel-versus-sequential critical path, penalty load, and per-resource planned utilization above the 90% fairness line are not quantified before the policy is chosen.
