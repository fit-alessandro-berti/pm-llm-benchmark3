The final answer is structurally complete and substantively strong on transportability: it refuses a blanket copy of Site A, treats the 9-hour median drop as confounded, keeps statutory ordering, segments by document channel, and designs a pilot that can separate mechanism failure from implementation failure. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and internal contradictions are large enough to undermine the scenario analysis, the intervention controls, and the pilot.

Section 2 overruns the 180–280 word limit (about 300 words). The graph also sends `S_legal_requirement` into `legal_capacity` and `originals_required` into `document_capacity`, conflating routing demand with pool size. Effect-modifier arrows are unsigned, so enabling and blocking paths look identical.

Section 5’s arithmetic does not hold together. Overall median change is treated as eligible share times a subgroup median shift (`0.38 × 6 ≈ 2.3 h`), which is not how a median moves. The all-case 20-hour wait before assessment is applied to digital-only cases, which should not carry the 18-hour originals delay. The optimistic row’s “−4 to −7 h” is then described in prose as a subset effect, while the same column is an overall effect in the next row. “40% legal + 38% digital” is not an eligible share. “Rwork” is a typo.

Section 6’s WIP rules contradict themselves. Assessment is capped at 8 per assessor (24 total) and also described as 80% capacity above 24. Legal is capped at 6 per reviewer (12 total) while the 80% tripwire is 16.

Section 7’s volume math is wrong by roughly fourfold: about 1,310 cases in 10 weeks cannot yield ~700 cases per stratum per arm. The Mann–Whitney “80% power” claim has no variance. Stratum shares of 23/15/37/25 are stated as volumes while assuming independence of originals and legal review. A case-level crossover is mentioned without a coherent unit.

Section 8 documents the right limitations, and assessment does wait on both checks, but `StrictPartialOrder(..., order=[...])` is not the PM4Py constructor, and the suggested `add_order` fallback is also wrong. The code as written should raise on the central Site B model. The fallback text still calls the net sound “by construction.”

Section 9 exceeds the 250–400 word cap (about 530). It first calls the first-wave group “38% … digital-only and do not require legal review,” then later correctly uses about 23%. The conditional-go decision itself, and the expand/modify/stop logic, are the right recommendation.
