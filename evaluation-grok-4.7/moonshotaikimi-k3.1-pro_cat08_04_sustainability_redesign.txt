The answer meets the required seven-section structure and is unusually well grounded in the supplied segments, DFG arcs, path variants, batching curves, route evidence, and SP1/SP2/Q1/R1. The boundary is explicit, energy, transport, and material are kept in separate units, and the order-weighted screening shares (MTO about 87% of logged energy and 85% of scrap; Critical-downtime about 41% of transport) follow from the given averages. All twelve options are tied to a path or segment, service and resilience effects are conditional, and the critical stock/ground variant is correctly rejected despite its lower emissions (23 h / 9% late versus 14 h / 3% late). Rebound coverage is specific, including batching-to-express, obsolescence, packaging-versus-returns, queue displacement, partial-shipment fallback, customer padding, and case-mix artifacts. Validation uses segment baselines, decision-time conformance, and stop rules rather than a pooled before/after.

Deductions are for small precision and clarity problems, not for the overall design. The 22% SP1-violation cohort is treated as the air-to-ground cohort even though a violation only means ground or consolidation was feasible, not that the chosen mode was air. Twice-daily consolidation is still assigned the full observed 12–24 h wait instead of distinguishing a scheduled departure cap from today’s consolidation delay. The CNC_B rework path’s 32 kWh and 1.8 kg are used a little loosely as the effect of the inspection gate, although the path also includes batching and machining. The effects table is so heavily bidirectional that several gated interventions never receive a primary expected direction. These are limited faults in an otherwise strong multi-criteria recommendation.
