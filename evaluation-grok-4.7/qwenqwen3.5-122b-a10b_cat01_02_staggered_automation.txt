The write-up has the right overall shape: availability versus use, endogenous staggered rollout, median versus tail, small-cluster caution, and a refusal of “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” with expansion tied to an embedded evaluation. Those strengths do not survive the quantitative and identification errors.

Load-bearing arithmetic is wrong. The stated Jan–Mar versus Jul–Aug window is not applied consistently: Beta’s post p90 is August only (99.2) rather than the Jul–Aug mean (102.4); Epsilon’s p90 contrast is March versus August; Gamma’s pre median is given as 38.2 rather than 37.5. Epsilon’s post median of 45.8 and “+13.6%” match Delta’s percentage applied to Epsilon’s baseline, not (44.8+40.8)/2 = 42.8 (about +6%). The pooled correlation of automated fast-path share with median cycle time is reported as −0.35; the cross-section is positive, because the long-cycle branches are the ones with high use. Alpha’s April-to-August intensity story skips the June median/p90 spike. Jul–Aug as the post window for April adopters drops the adverse early post period.

Those figures drive weaker claims. Section 7’s “controls worsened” support, and the top “Observed” bullet that Delta and Epsilon both significantly deteriorated while Alpha and Beta stabilized, overstate the record. Epsilon’s median ends near its early-2026 level. Gamma’s cleanest contrast—May–June deterioration, then a July reversal at constant complexity and FTE—is washed out.

Bias directions are reversed on two core threats. Regression to the mean after selecting historically slow branches inflates apparent gains; the table says it underestimates benefits. Losing FTEs should lengthen cycles and mask benefits; the table says staffing overstates benefits. Escalation is given confidence 60 as an automation effect even though every branch jumps in March with the fraud desk, before rollout. The preferred design conditions on post-treatment arrivals, FTE, and case mix without treating them as bad controls. Interference and treatment version (shared fraud desk, routing bundled with go-live, learning as a different dose) are not stated on the estimands. Measurement aggregation is missing from the required threat list. The mechanism evidence cells are unreadable, and several immediate-path facts are missing: April rework drops at Alpha and Beta, then rework rises with the May complexity shock.

The code does not validate the panel, prints only median trends rather than the five outcomes, builds relative-time indicators it never uses, and fits the binary two-way specification the task warned about. The rollout section is under the 250-word minimum, and within-branch 50/50 randomization ignores shared specialist queues. The closing “not supported” rung is the right policy conclusion, but it sits on an empirical ladder that is not reliable.
