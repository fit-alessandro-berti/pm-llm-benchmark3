4.5

The write-up is well structured through the policy and monitoring sections, and several real constraints are handled carefully: mixture means, shift/break timing, the post-18:00 mechanical gap, Emil’s 16:00–18:00 software tightness, Chen’s absence under U3/U5, the U2 mix-versus-volume ambiguity, preemption penalties, and the specialization–pooling trade-off. Objectives cover both SLA tails and fairness/robustness, and the recommendation is not chosen only for base-case average delay.

That is outweighed by a central capacity error. Chen is qualified and on shift from 08:00 for both Electrical (1.00) and Software (1.25), but the 08:00–10:00 ratios count only Ada and Farah. Correct base software load/capacity is about 0.37 and 0.48 in the first two blocks, not 0.78 and 1.01, so software is not a base morning bottleneck. The same omission turns U1 morning Electrical (~0.73, not ~1.23) and Software (~0.63 and ~0.81, not ~1.34 and ~1.72) into a false overload story. Related multiplier mistakes (Deepa’s electrical rate at 16:00; Emil’s software rate around midday) are smaller but show the same audit failure. Bottleneck migration, the scenario table, and the claim that hybrid P1 reservation is robust in the morning all rest on that mistake; reserving Emil does not protect 08:00–12:00 software work, which Chen actually holds.

The comparison metrics are not derived from the load ratios or from a completed run. They are presented as three-seed scaffold ranges even though no simulation results exist. Section 8 stops inside the skill matrix, so it does not implement three policies, handover/setup/preemption, repeated-seed metrics, an interval event log, or the required PM4Py calls. Section 9 is missing. Those are required deliverables, not optional polish.
