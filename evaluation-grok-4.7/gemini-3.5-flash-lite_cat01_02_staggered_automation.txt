5.7

The write-up follows the required section structure and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” slogan. Availability, intensity, targeted rollout, co-occurring shocks, small-cluster inference, and median-versus-tail separation are all recognized. Several quantitative and identification conclusions are still unreliable.

Section 5’s adopter window is January–March versus July–August for Alpha and Beta only. Gamma, the later adopter, never receives a pre/post contrast, despite the request for every adopter. Never-treated comparisons then switch to January-versus-August endpoints. On that endpoint, Epsilon’s median moves from 41.4h to 40.8h (−1.4%). On the same July–August window used for adopters, Epsilon’s median average is about 42.8h against a January–March average of about 40.3h. The control comparison is therefore not the comparison the adopter arithmetic describes. Alpha’s stated −6.5% median change is also slightly off its own averages (45.6 versus 48.9 is about −6.7%). The full April–August mean, which the code actually computes, leaves Alpha’s median nearly unchanged and Beta’s higher. That window dependence is not shown.

The tail bound is the largest substantive error. Absolute p90 rose for Alpha and Beta, but it rose more for Delta and Epsilon over the same months. A staggered comparison of the kind named as the primary strategy points toward smaller tail deterioration among adopters, not a robust +5% to +12% automation penalty. Section 7 nevertheless rates tail inflation at confidence 75 and treats it as a claim leadership may make. Rework timing is also misread: Alpha and Beta fall in April (13.2 to 11.2 and 12.8 to 10.4), while the later rise lines up with the May complexity shift. Escalation’s March jump precedes adoption in every branch; the additional post-adoption gap versus Delta is nearer 1 percentage point than the reported +2.0 to +4.5, so confidence 85 is too high.

The intensity result, about −0.8 median hours and +0.4 p90 hours per 10-point fast-path increase after branch fixed effects, is not derived in the text and is not estimated by the code. The code fits pooled OLS of median cycle time on the binary flag or the intensity share plus FTE, arrivals, and complexity, with branch-clustered errors and no branch or month effects. Relative-month indicators are built and then unused. With five branches, the pooled availability coefficient mainly compares slow, high-volume branches with faster ones. Printed output is first month to last month, so the May–June hump is invisible. Assumptions appear as a closing warning rather than as comments attached to a clearly descriptive within-branch panel model.

Estimands have the right headings, but several are misspecified. TOT-01 labels a jump to 100% fast-path use a LATE, although observed intensity tops out near 50%. HET-01 contrasts complexity levels rather than an automation effect across subgroups. SAT-01 uses a 4–8 month horizon when Alpha and Beta have only five post months. ITT-01 is marked identifiable while the design section says conditional parallel trends are highly exposed to regression to the mean.

The preferred Callaway–Sant’Anna strategy is a reasonable alternative to two-way fixed effects, and the two-cohort, five-branch limitation is stated. The routing rule is still treated as a covariate problem. Extra volume is assigned on the same dates as adoption (+10% for Alpha and Beta in April, +5% for Gamma in July), so availability and the routing package are not separately identified. Arrivals are also recommended as controls even though they are partly consequences of that package. The instrumental alternative is called shift-share, and an over-identification test is named for what is effectively one instrument.

The rollout choice, expand with an embedded evaluation, matches the uncertainty. The implementation is weaker. Delta is described as structurally similar to Epsilon; Delta is smaller, lower-complexity, and faster, while Epsilon resembles the original high-complexity targets. A significance rule on one next branch conflicts with the small-cluster warning, and the p90 and CSAT guardrails are not calibrated to untreated volatility already visible in the panel. The specification is also a little under the required 250–400 words. The closing ladder correctly ranks the blanket X% claim as unsupported, while the conditionally causal −2 to −4 hour median offset and accompanying p90 inflation are more specific than the reported contrasts can carry.
