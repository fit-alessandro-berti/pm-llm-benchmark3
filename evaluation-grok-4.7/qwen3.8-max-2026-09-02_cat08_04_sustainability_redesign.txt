7.2

The final recommendation is complete and well aligned with the required structure: boundary, indicators, all twelve options with path, mechanism, service/quality/resilience effects, and monitoring evidence, a multi-column effects table, eight rebound risks, a phased package, and conformance-plus-exception governance. Segment distinctions, SP1/SP2/Q1/R1, rework, returns, and obsolescence are handled, and air and dedicated vans are not banned outright.

It is not nearly flawless. The effects table is the main failure. Direction symbols are missing, so many cells are fragmentary (“; possible from…”, “small for non-urgent…”) and the required energy, transport, material, cycle-time, lateness, rework, and resilience directions cannot be read reliably. Several remaining claims also put transport changes into the energy column (consolidation, shared vans, fewer partial shipments), conflating `energy_kwh` with `transport_kgco2e`. The 35–55% consolidation reduction is attached to the recommender without stating that it is versus individual ground, not versus air.

The core path trade-offs are named more often than measured. The already-late batched CNC_A ground path (118h, 21% late, 16 kWh, 5.8 kgCO2e) is not set against immediate CNC plus air (70h, 9% late, 24 kWh, 23 kgCO2e), and the CNC_B rework path (160h, 32% late, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap) and dedicated-van path (14h, 3% late, 52 kgCO2e versus 23h, 9% late, 4.8 kgCO2e on the stock/ground exception) are not used as decision numbers. Longer Regular made-to-order promises can lower the late rate by moving the target; validation tracks promise changes and lateness but does not separate that KPI effect from a real cycle-time improvement. Per-closed-order indicators also drop the cancellation path.
