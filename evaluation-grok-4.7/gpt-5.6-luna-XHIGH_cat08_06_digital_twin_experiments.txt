8.2

The design is strong, constraint-compliant, and unusually careful about latent data, calendars, heavy tails, and not treating an unvalidated simulator as an optimizer. It is not nearly flawless: several modeling and experiment-design gaps would matter in implementation, and the skeleton has a concrete PM4Py inconsistency.

The scope correctly rejects an independent-case replay. Architect bursts, shared queues, inspection bundles, and a latent neighborhood-objection state are first-class, and calendar time, queue wait, municipal service, and external response are kept as separate clocks. Estimation is likewise well grounded: arrivals come from submit events, service is not read off completion gaps, routing is conditional, objections stay latent, and PM4Py is limited to formatting, variants, DFGs, performance DFGs, and conformance rather than being mistaken for the simulator. Assumptions are separated as requested and are mostly realistic. Validation (replay versus generative timing, holdout seasons, no refitting on the test window, Little’s law only in stable periods) and the explicit non-optimality caveat are the right epistemic posture.

The material gaps are these. Rework topology is underspecified relative to its effect on the tail. The tree’s redo labels (`Completeness rework`, `Inspection rework`) are not reconciled with the log, where rework appears as a repeated `Completeness check` and no such redo activity occurs; planning, legal, and appeal return arcs are only “the appropriate point.” The Monday portal-support clerk, which cuts completeness capacity on the 2.2× arrival day, is dropped while the other special calendars are enumerated, and D6 never says whether the reserved planner is simple-renovation-only or a generalist. Stress rows are not clearly crossed with policies, so a baseline-only shock would not identify robust interventions. “Positive net benefit in 80% of scenarios” is a decision threshold with no defined net-benefit function. Warm-up invokes Welch plots, which look for a flat mean, on a cyclostationary seasonal system that should not flatten; the 365/365/260 scheme also never states the arrival cohort, whether drain arrivals are shut off, or how peak months are scored separately from the annual mean that would hide the bottleneck D4 and D7 target. Public notice is grouped rhetorically with the parallel reviews even though the tree places it after that AND-block. Utilization “70–85%” does not say whether the denominator is nominal FTE or calendar-available hours, which matters for fire, environment, and legal.

The skeleton matches the excerpt, avoids `pm4py.read_csv`, calls `format_dataframe` with the required keys, refuses to invent a service/wait split, and keeps routing, calendars, clustered arrivals, and bundles outside PM4Py. Two code issues remain. `discover_dfg` is unpacked as a triple, but `discover_performance_dfg` is not, despite the same return shape in current PM4Py, and nothing warns that those edge times are inter-completion delays. Section 5’s separate random streams collapse to one shared generator. Parse failure is handled safely rather than papered over.

Taken together, this is a credible twin design with professional uncertainty handling, pulled down by an incomplete rework semantics, an experiment matrix that does not fully cross shocks with policies, a seasonal run-length story that mixes steady-state tools with a non-stationary process, and a few API/code inconsistencies.
