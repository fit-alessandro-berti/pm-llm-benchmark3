5.5

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activated applications as non-defaults and frames the primary estimand as 90-day default conditional on eventual activation, with a distinct multi-state alternative. The cohort arithmetic accounts for all 48,000 applications, and the leakage table makes sensible keep/repair/drop calls for the dangerous fields. Those strengths do not carry the study.

The maturity rule is wrong. Label availability is determined by activation time plus 90 days plus the 7–35 day feed lag, relative to 15 February 2026, not by case start. A start before 30 April 2025 does not guarantee a mature label, because activation can occur much later. The same error makes the validation window incoherent: the November–December 2025 holdout is almost entirely unlabeled even with immediate activation, and the July–October test set retains a biased subset of fast activations. Requiring mature labels inside each fold does not repair a split that was not built from an as-of extraction date. Train cutoffs also disagree across sections (30 April versus 1 April 2025).

The inverse-probability sensitivity analysis names the wrong estimand. Weighting activated defaults by the inverse probability of activation estimates the covariate-standardized conditional risk, not P(default and activation). The 60% transfer linkage is unused, and the 3,600 immature activations are treated as ordinary delayed labels despite permanently missing partner feeds. Conditioning on a future activation is stated, but collider bias and the fact that activation is unknown at an early prefix are left thin. Elapsed-time prefixes and “use the full trace if the case is short” can include the credit decision, which conflicts with the no-post-decision rule.

The skeleton is below the requested length and does not implement the audit it describes. Activation time is derived with `idxmax` and then merged onto a column of the same name; label maturity is assigned from a case-indexed series onto a reset frame, so it will not align. Terminal state and censoring reason are never built. The leakage check only asks whether forbidden columns exist on the raw extract, and `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` run on full traces that include activation and collections. Prefixes are computed and then unused, so post-prefix events are never removed from a feature table.
