6.8

The write-up covers every required section, uses concrete object IDs and timestamps, keeps machine capacity off the top of the ranking, and usually separates precedence from causation. Those strengths do not offset several material errors in the calculations, the binding-constraint logic, the falsification list, and the code.

The synchronization subtractions for J1 and J2 are right (235 and 220 minutes), as are the raw S1 intervals (355, 340, and 15 minutes), the 210-minute L7 window, the 42-minute CNC-2 gap, and the 80/50/45-minute CAL-3 splits. The S1 benchmark pair is not. Against a 25-minute post-pack lag the excess is 330 minutes; the answer states 350. The 320-minute figure, from 355 − 35, is the one that is arithmetically correct. The same table then treats J4’s fixture delay as the 5 minutes after FIX-A is released at 11:40. That interval is the handoff after the tool is already free. J4 is at CNC-2 from 09:50, FIX-A is held by J3 until 11:40, and CNC-2 is recorded idle for 42 minutes inside that hold. The assumption “otherwise ready at release” simply defines away the contention window the timestamps suggest. Ranking shared-tool contention down because “FIX-A explains little of the 115 minutes” follows from that assumption, and the release-then-start pattern is used as evidence against the fixture rather than as evidence consistent with it.

B2 has a related binding error. RACK-7 is free at 14:35, J5 is ready only at 14:40, and B2 starts at 14:50. The closing counterfactual (a 14:40 start, ten minutes earlier) is the right marginal result, but the rank table still cites rack unavailability through 14:35 as positive support. From 13:10 to 14:35 the rack constraint is confounded with J5’s absence; after 14:35 the rack does not bind. CAL-3 is cleaner than the answer’s lead number: the 80 minutes from 16:05 includes J5’s occupation of TEST-1/CAL-3 until 16:35. The machine-idle recalibration, 16:35–17:20, is the delay that can be tied to CAL-3 alone. The answer does report the 50-minute alternative, under a counterfactual that has to assume away that conflicting test.

The top row of the ranking table assigns J3 an “08:25–11:55” path. 08:25 is J2’s batch-readiness; J3’s is 11:55. Section 4’s “54–58%” utilization range drops OVEN-1’s 44%. The graph prose reverses the supplied qualifier to “Job batches Batch,” while the adjacency fragment `J2C2L7` is not an edge list. Calling “contained in” an inference blurs the recorded `L7 contains C2` relation; the inverse direction is the inferred part. Motifs 1–4 are real multi-type structures. Motif 5 uses only Job and Shipment.

Two of the six supposed falsifiers of batch synchronization do not weaken it. A specification that common cure is physically required supports synchronization as the coupling mechanism. A split-batch trial that produces earlier feasible starts without quality or setup damage supports it as well. That leaves four genuine weakeners, short of the required six. Narrative 3 also stitches L7 to J6, CAL-3, and an S3 “rule” the log only shows as a 35-minute observed lag.

The PM4Py block names the required functions and avoids reading machine-event gaps as waiting, but it would not perform the comparison it claims. `ocel_objects_interactions_summary` is treated as a `source, target, count` edge table and then summed into one co-occurrence total. `graph_type="interacts"` does not match the object-graph API. `discover_ocdfg` returns an activity DFG, which has no `.edges("Job", "Batch")`. The flattened Job and Shipment logs are counted and then ignored; `discover_performance_dfg` is called on the OCEL with arguments that function does not take. The printed “frequency” comparison therefore does not compare two object-centric frequency metrics.

The flattening account, the utilization paradox, the lot-extraction caveat, the non-additivity warning on B1, and the intervention spillovers are the parts that hold up. They are not enough, under a strict reading, to carry the wrong benchmark, the inverted fixture window, the mislabeled J3 timestamp, the backwards falsifiers, or the non-executable frequency comparison.
