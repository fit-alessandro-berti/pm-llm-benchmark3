5.5

The audit inventories the obvious full-trace leaks and keeps a usable five-part structure, but several process and time-semantics errors are material, and the local narratives contradict their own constraints.

Section 1 catches the main unavailable fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) and the subtler defects in `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, and `risk_score_current`. That inventory is incomplete in kind. `total_case_duration_hours` is the 10-day breach label in another form, not only a post-closure covariate. `num_document_requests_total` repaired to a pre-`t0` count is structurally empty: at this `t0`, immediately after the first coverage check, document cycles have not started. The 65% overlap of `final_handler_team` with the team at `t0` is the non-leaking part; the informative 35% is exactly the post-`t0` reassignment. Valid rows are labeled severity “Low,” so the severity scale does not mean defect severity.

The sequence block misstates admissibility. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is normatively allowed — an appeal follows Partial or Deny and can cause a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` — and is inadmissible only because it is future and disabled at `t0`. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is the different case: same-timestamp ordering with a missing sequence index, and impossible if the logged order is real. Section 3 then puts both under “violate normative process order,” undoing that distinction. `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is future-possible and parallel-legal, but the remediation “retain if timestamped at/after `t0`” keeps post-decision information. `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` and `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` are likewise post-`t0` on a conforming prefix; conditioning them on “timestamps ≤ `t0`” yields no conforming mass. Those rows are also full of dropped comparison operators, so several remediations are not executable as written. `initial_reserve` is declared frozen at registration with no lineage support.

Section 2 names the right check families, then specifies some of them incorrectly. Exclusive choice between the evidence cycle and damage assessment contradicts the model: both routes reach damage assessment, and the evidence cycle is the long route into it. Withdrawal can put `Claim Closed` on a path the hard precedence constraint does not allow. Appeal legitimacy is checked only as Decision–Appeal–Decision, without the Partial/Deny precondition. Same-timestamp ties are assigned alphabetical order, which manufactures a trace. Enabled, disabled, and merely future-possible activities are not actually separated: appeal is disabled until a qualifying decision, settlement is future but not enabled at `t0`, and fraud opening is conditional.

Section 3 states the four standards only loosely, then slips mechanism language back in. Queue length is both a generic predictor and the congestion signal. `calendar_week` is both a fairness proxy and a drift proxy. Channel is “defensible” here and a proxy in the case write-up. The repaired-lineage class never says what a repaired prior-claim count may claim.

Section 4 is the weakest application. Case X correctly drops the appeal flag and `final_decision` and should be recomputed, but `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0 = 1.2 h` with a negative contribution is a short prefix and remaining slack; the text calls it an older claim whose slack was reduced, which reverses the sign. Case Y lists `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` as a credible driver “after SQL fix” and as a driver that must be removed until the SQL is fixed, then narrates “multiple prior claims, suggesting complexity” from the unrepaired value. Region and reserve are given base-rate and mechanism claims a local contribution does not establish. Residuals of about +0.17 to +0.21 are ignored, so the surviving features are over-credited. Case Z is rightly invalidated, and the same-timestamp, mutable score, closure role, and same-day snapshot problems are named; week 52 is omitted even though it is the supplied holiday/policy confounder.

Section 5 covers every requested control at a sensible general priority. It still omits sequence-index integrity, point-in-time training rows, and the rule that explanation text may not outrun predictive association. Conformance tooling is named without correcting the declarative mistakes above. Drift monitoring is ranked below the failures that make the published ranking unsafe.

The answer would be a usable first pass after those corrections. As written, it is not a reliable process-aware audit.
