7.4

The review is strong on use-case separation, disaggregated metrics, Model S/C language behavior, precision-inflation via duplicate labels, and the selective-recheck loop problem. It is not nearly flawless: several confident technical claims are false, the policy checker does not match its own specification, and the deployment gate reintroduces a pathology the review itself ranks as critical.

The headline modeling claim is wrong. A block-structured tree can express mandatory unordered `Credit Check`/`Technical Review`, optional `Expedite Review` strictly after both, and optional `Supplier Review` interleaved with all three, with no duplicate labels:

`+(->(+(CC, TR), X(ER, τ)), X(SR, τ))`.

Shuffle preserves order inside the first child, so `ER` cannot precede `CC` or `TR`, while `SR` can sit before, between, or after them. The parenthetical that a tree must put `SR` wholly before `ER`, wholly after it, or duplicate labels simply omits parallelism with a sequence. POWL is a valid encoding, not a mathematical necessity. That error is the stated reason for the architecture.

Leaf accounting is also wrong. Model C has 48 leaves, not 45 (5+5+6+6+6+7+8+5). Calling the XOR “8 disjoint parallel paths” confuses exclusive choice with concurrency. In `model_third`, `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` each occur three times (initial, only-recheck, and both-recheck), and `Supplier Review` and `Expedite Review` each occur twice. That is 6 extra labeled transitions, not “at most one duplicate per check” and not 4. The “0-duplicate exploration variant” is never defined.

The probe matrix for B1–B8 against S and C is correct, and the third net does accept B2, B5, B6, A1, and A3 and reject B3, B4, B8, and A2. The audit layer does not live up to the table. `affected_checks={'TR'}` crashes because a set is not indexable; a list of activity-name strings is exploded by `set("Technical Review")` into characters; labels in the example (`TR`) do not match code labels (`Technical Review`). `POLICY_RULES` is unused and lists `Supplier Review` as a sufficient recheck candidate, while the code requires `CC` and/or `TR`. The prose earlier says “CC, TR, or SR”; the code and F6 do not. Section 8’s “CONDITIONAL_VALID if internal” is the wrong condition: a known internal order with no supplier review is strictly valid, and a missing attribute warns on every trace.

“100% behavioral accuracy on P1–P7 and B1–B8” overclaims. The net always accepts policy-dependent B6, cannot enforce external sourcing, and rejects corrections that start before both initial checks have finished, which P6 does not forbid. The evaluation skeleton never calls `audit_trace_policy`, puts B6 in the unconditional positive suite, and fails any model whose holdout fitness is below 0.980. That last rule rewards the optionalizing of poorly logged steps, which Section 3 correctly calls a semantic flaw rather than noise tolerance. A1 does not separate the third model from S (both accept); A2 separates it from neither. Only A3 distinguishes it from both. Section 3 lists four admitted bad traces of S, then a fifth behavior S rejects; the requirement was five admitted behaviors.

The fitness reading of 0.992 is too strong. Event-based alignment fitness stays high when many traces contain one extra `Correct Dossier` among many events; that does not show the activity is rare or cost-discounted. The dissent is real, inside the length bound, and correctly attacks split control and unsigned interaction effects, but “any arbitrary combination” of rechecks is looser than the XOR of only-CC, only-TR, or both-once.

What keeps the score here is the quality of the rest: no single composite score, a correct S-versus-C probe reading, a real explanation of why positive-log precision flatters an enumerated XOR of sequences, mandatory checks that are not silenced, an explicit missing-attribute limitation, and a validation plan that includes seeds, stratification, a negative suite, cost profiles, soundness, and a task-based comprehension protocol.
