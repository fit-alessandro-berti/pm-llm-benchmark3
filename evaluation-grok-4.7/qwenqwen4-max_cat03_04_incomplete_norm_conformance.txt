The answer is strong on the dimensions that matter: open- versus closed-world separation, scope and abstraction, and not treating unmodeled edges as violations. All eight required sections are present, norm IDs and counts are used, benign relations (Validate Order → Issue Invoice, Manual Compliance Review–Customer, Package–Delivery codeath, Reopen Order → Close Order) stay non-violations, and N1’s “eventually” versus directly-follows trap is handled explicitly. Deductions:

- The semantic classifier is binary on edge presence. Any positive weight becomes conforming, so the real partial gaps (N1 98/100, N4 198/200, E2 1448 vs 1450, E6 198/200, E8 92 vs 93) are invisible unless the separate eventually recheck happens to see them. That undercuts the sketch relative to the prose.
- `norm_ocdfg`, `norm_otg`, and `norm_etot` are passed only into the PM4Py calls. Status, scope, and semantics are re-hardcoded and never read from those structures, despite the comment that metadata lives there.
- “T1 and T3 are consistent with their ET–OT counterparts” is too clean: Add Item–Order is 1448 against Item 1450, and Record Payment–Payment is 93 against Invoice 92.
- N4’s table cell leads with “control skip,” and E6 is High / E10 Critical, a bit above what carrier attributes and a suspected identifier join support. Later sections correct this; the table itself still overstates.
- Length looks above the 1,700–2,600 word aim (excluding code), mainly from the 21-row table plus eight norm gaps.

No major misclassification of forbidden, mandatory, typical, or unregulated behavior. The uncertainty close correctly separates robust counts, norm incompleteness, suspected data quality, and what graph summaries cannot decide.
