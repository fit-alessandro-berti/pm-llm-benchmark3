The write-up follows the required 10-part outline and names the right problems — staggered, targeted adoption, intensity versus availability, staffing, case mix, the fraud desk, and survey nonresponse — but the quantitative core is unreliable, the identification claims are too strong, and the code cannot be run. Those are central failures for this task, not presentation nits.

## Arithmetic and data facts

The adopter table does not match the stated Jan–Mar versus Apr–Jun windows.

- Alpha’s post median is about 50.4 hours, not 50.8; the change is about +1.5 hours, not +1.9. The p90 change is about +11.5 hours, not +15.3. Rework is essentially flat once April is included, not +0.8. CSAT changes by about −0.01, not −0.05.
- Beta’s post median is about 48.4, not 47.5 (change about +2.5 hours, not +1.6). The p90 change is about +10 hours, not +3.8. Rework falls on that window; +2.8 has the wrong sign. The duplicated +2.8 on rework and escalation looks copied, not computed.
- Gamma is internally inconsistent: 38.4 minus 38.2 is not −1.0 hour, 38.4 is July’s median rather than the Jul–Aug mean, and only two post months exist. The −8.4 hour p90 figure is not recoverable from a transparent window.
- Delta’s “36.2 to 42.6” and Epsilon’s “40.2 to 46.6” are single months labeled as period averages. March Delta median is 36.2; the Jan–Mar mean is about 34.5. May Epsilon median is 46.6; the Apr–Jun mean is about 46.0 and June is 49.4.

Other factual mistakes feed the conclusions. Gamma’s fast-path share is 25% then 32%, not 25–36%. Alpha’s fast-path share rises through August to 48%; it does not peak in June and fall to 43%. That 43 is August median cycle time. Epsilon has the highest complexity (30–38%), not low complexity. Beta’s pre-adoption medians drift up (45.4, 45.9, 46.4), so “Alpha/Beta were trending down” is false. Alpha itself is 50.4, 46.9, 49.4, not a clean decline. Pre-period fast-path shares of 4–6% while `automation=0` are never flagged.

The intensity result, −0.12 hours per 10 points with p=0.08, is not computed in the answer. The code block does not contain the data. Reporting a p-value from an unrun model is not an approximation.

## Identification

Four estimands are marked identifiable. Availability was chosen because Alpha and Beta were large and slow; demand was redirected onto them; FTEs and complexity moved with the rollout; the fraud desk is a shared queue; two branches remain untreated. None of that is repaired by a post dummy plus covariates. The honest cell is “not identified from these data,” or “only under assumptions the design itself contradicts.”

The primary design is named as a staggered event study and then specified as pooled OLS on one post indicator. That is the naïve specification the task said to avoid, with no cohort-specific effects, no not-yet-treated contrast, and no negative-weight or treatment-timing discussion. Gamma is not-yet-treated in April–June and is unused as a comparison. Cluster-robust standard errors with five branches do not “account for” small-cluster uncertainty; they are themselves unreliable. Adjusting for arrivals, FTE, and complexity is treated as innocuous, although the routing campaign and staffing moves are post-rollout and can be mediators or bad controls.

The synthetic-control alternative is a single donor. Delta’s cycle-time level is far from Alpha’s, so it is not a synthetic control and it is a poor match. Interference is assumed away in the identifying assumptions even though the brief states that automation sent more borderline cases to a shared fraud desk and that routing moved volume off the control branches. Control contamination is the main comparison problem, and it stays peripheral.

Section 3’s justification is about 90 words against a required 150–250, and it treats non-random timing as useful variation rather than the reason parallel trends fail.

## Mechanisms and effect claims

Beneficial and harmful signatures are reversed for specialist congestion and for the tail: tail slowdown is listed as the beneficial signature, and faster triage as the harmful one. Several cells are directionless, so the mechanism table does not actually state a testable signature.

The effect table then says median cycle time likely improved, citing Gamma’s −1.0 hour and the unrun regression, while the same answer’s Alpha and Beta rows worsened. June-to-August medians also fell at untreated Epsilon (49.4 to 40.8) by almost as much as at Gamma (45.9 to 36.0). Epsilon’s p90 fell about 16 hours over that span. Gamma’s post-July drop is largely a shared rebound from the May complexity shock plus easing late-summer volume, on top of a June FTE loss that inflated Gamma’s pre-adoption baseline. Confidence 60 for a median benefit, and 70 for a rework increase, are far too high. Immediate rework at adoption fell at Alpha (13.2 to 11.2), Beta (12.8 to 10.4), and Gamma (14.6 to 12.6). March escalation jumps about two points in every branch before any go-live; later automation increments should be measured from that common shift.

Regression to the mean is required and not analyzed. Alpha and Beta were selected for bad historical cycle times. Workload is never calculated. Alpha’s arrivals per FTE rise from about 32 in March to about 43 in June, which is the obvious congestion shock against which flat-to-worse medians and much worse p90s have to be read. The claim ladder then leads with a conditionally causal “1–3 hour reduction” in high-complexity Alpha and Beta, the branches whose own table shows longer medians. Ranges also disagree across sections 5, 7, and 10 (−2.5 hours, 0–5%, and 1–3 hours).

## Decision and code

`expand with an embedded evaluation` is a defensible category, but the design does not buy identification. Delta is not the pre-trend twin of Alpha or Beta; it is the smallest, fastest, lowest-complexity branch. Treating it next by analyst choice, then Epsilon, leaves one contaminated control and repeats endogenous order. The two remaining branches were the last chance to randomize order or hold one out under a pre-registered contrast against the other. The baseline window includes September, the proposed go-live month. Guardrails disagree with the scale-up rule (3% versus 4% median; 3% versus 1% escalation) and are raw thresholds, not control-adjusted differences. Process data to collect stay vague: no activity waiting times, variant shares, queue length, or repeat-loop counts.

The Python block fails the reproducibility requirement. The CSV is replaced by an ellipsis. `pd.compat.StringIO` is not a valid reader. Adoption month is the first observed month for every branch, so the relative-time variable is calendar time and the post indicator is degenerate. The formula uses `FTE` rather than `fte`. Cluster-robust fitting is not `groups=` on `fit`. There are no branch or period effects, no real relative-time dummies, no panel validation, and plots of only two of the five outcomes. Never-treated adoption lines will fail. Assumptions are not written as comments; the printout calls a post dummy an event study.

Format compliance and a wide threat list keep this off the floor. The numbers, the estimator, and the lead causal sentence do not support a decision.
