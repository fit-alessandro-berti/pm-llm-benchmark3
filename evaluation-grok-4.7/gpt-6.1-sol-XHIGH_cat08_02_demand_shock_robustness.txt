8.4

The answer is factually careful and structurally complete: mix math, weekly rates, queue/utilization figures, cited performance edges, repeat transitions per 100 registrations, and specialist-hold counts all check out, and it respects non-conserving DFGs, the flattened order log, and the ban on treating pooled averages as the objective. Bottleneck migration, downstream overload, case-type conflicts, and redesign-caused fragility are handled explicitly, and I1–I10 are conditioned or rejected rather than adopted wholesale.

It is not nearly flawless. Several of the sharpest process-mining signals are never computed: technical validation’s direct-release share falls from about 74% to about 50%, batch-wait incidence rises sharply, pre-analytical recollection incidence roughly doubles, and urgent batch bypass falls from 74% to 58%. Those omissions weaken the migration argument relative to the evidence given. Accession correction is also left unresolved: at peak its queue is 14 hours, while the only proposed donors are technical validators already at 101%, and the 85% cover gate makes redeployment unavailable precisely then; section 6 does not state that hole. Dashboard “slack” bands are easy to misread as clock time left, because the slack definition is not restated where the thresholds appear. Overall rework, which the answer itself treats as lagging and window-censored, is placed in a leading-indicator row. “Unchanged daily volume” in S4 is ambiguous across mixes, and I4 is once worded as a permanent transfer even though the proposal is contingent cross-training.
