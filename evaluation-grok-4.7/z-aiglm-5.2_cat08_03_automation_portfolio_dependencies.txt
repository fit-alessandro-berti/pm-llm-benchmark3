The write-up has the required seven sections, stays inside the four-module cap, correctly refuses to lead with registration or an activation bot, and keeps humans on exceptions and audit. The core process reasoning is still not reliable enough.

The matrix is an ID list, not a ranking, and it underrates collateral triage: a 58-hour external wait on 2,350 cases is a synchronization constraint, not a medium process effect. The dependency section then assigns deep core-banking integration to contract generation and activation, while the rollout assigns it to statement extraction and contracts. Those claims conflict, and statement extraction is not established as core banking.

The effects analysis misreads the event log. Registration is already 11,880 of 12,000 cases, so auto-registration does not flood the process or worsen end-to-end cycle time; it mostly fails to move the constraint. The 22-hour wait before contract preparation is not removed by cutting an 8.5% remediation loop, the 28-hour credit-analysis wait is not fixed by “cleaner inflow,” and the 8-hour wait before missing-document requests is not the customer delay the checklist actually targets. “Eliminates misrouting” overstates a recommender with a remaining wrong-path risk.

The recommended set also misses the evidence the question stresses. Entity-mismatch resolution is on the critical path in 82% of the cases where it appears and sits on a 3-FTE specialist bottleneck; that statistic is never used. KYC and financial review concurrency, the 71%/18% collateral split, the weekly high-exposure committee cadence, and provisional signing (6.5%) are likewise not designed for. A “compliance-first” portfolio with no KYC hard stop, and with both control modules deferred to months 7–12, does not meet a first-wave control requirement. The final trade-off explains why not to automate registration first, but it does not justify leaving the longest waits and the critical-path specialist loop untouched.
