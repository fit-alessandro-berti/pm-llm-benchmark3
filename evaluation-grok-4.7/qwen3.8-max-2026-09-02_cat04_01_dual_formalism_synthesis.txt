The final model is coherent, and the witness verdicts match the stated semantics. It is not nearly flawless.

The process tree is a real conservative under-approximation: `(C→P) ∥ (A→D)` must finish before the trial, the risk XOR, and the optional supplier. That preserves the trial precondition and release join, and it correctly excludes unsafe orders, but it forbids approvals or supplier work that the narrative allows as soon as root-cause analysis is done. A3 (`R, A, S, …`) is the right witness for that loss. The POWL edges encode the same dependencies directly: `A` enables design, risk, and supplier; `P` and `D` jointly enable the trial; trial, risk, and supplier join at release. Loop and XOR structure matches the repeat-until and at-most-once claims under ordinary PM4Py `LOOP` / `XOR` semantics.

The deductions are for over-strong wording and a validation sketch that would not run:

- Section 6 calls the POWL “exact” for approval–trial overlap and supplier overlap, while the witness A5 (`G, E, U, Q, I`) is allowed only if concurrent composite nodes shuffle internally. That assumption is stated as an open risk in the self-critique, but the table and the POWL verdict treat it as settled.
- The playout check treats traces as lists of labels or `None`. PM4Py playout returns an event log; silent steps are not reliably `None`. As written, the assertions do not implement the check they describe.
- The single-occurrence counter does not check risk-route mutual exclusion or that each `J` sits between cycles. Those invariants are only described in prose.
- The duplicate-label row says that avoiding duplicate labels contributes to the tree’s under-approximation. The missing behavior comes from the block-structured join, not from label uniqueness.

No allowed/forbidden verdict in the eight-row table is wrong under the assumptions the answer actually adopts, and the required sections, approximation policy, and cross-branch counterexample are all present.
