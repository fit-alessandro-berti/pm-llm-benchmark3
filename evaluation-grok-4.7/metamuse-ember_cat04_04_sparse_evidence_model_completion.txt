The three candidates are genuinely different, internally consistent hypotheses, and the control-flow claims match the code. Conservative is an exact seven-trace XOR. Balanced’s XOR of partial orders really does encode “S after at least one of C or E” and “X after both,” and it accepts all seven observed variants, including A C S E P R Z. Permissive is a real superset that drops exactly those two constraints. The unobserved-behavior audit breaks the evidence link the task requires. A S C E P R Z is admitted because permissive drops D5, not because D5 or S7 support early supplier confirmation. D5 says S may begin after one check, which forbids S before both. A E X S C P R Z is not justified by V7: V7 has X before S only after both checks.
