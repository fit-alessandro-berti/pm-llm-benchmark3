The forecast has the right skeleton and the wrong clocks. Incubation, elapsed-hour conversions, QA capacity, and the contamination tail are each off by about a day or more, and sections 2, 3, 4, and 6 do not describe the same scenarios.

**Incubation is dated a day early.** Readiness is 46–54 h after Friday 14:00, so Sunday 12:00–20:00. The write-up uses Saturday 12:00–Sunday 16:00, with a mode near Sunday 12:00 (that instant is 46 h, not ~50 h). Saturday readout, Saturday on-call dispatch, and a Sunday 10:00–14:00 terminal time are impossible. Saturday QA (09:00–13:00) closes before the batch can be technically ready, and QA does not work Sunday. On-call can only move readout from Monday 08:30–09:30 to Sunday afternoon or evening. It does not save a working day on the smooth path. Its real value is starting a confirmation incubation on Sunday rather than Monday, which this forecast never uses.

**Hour totals and named weekdays do not match.** Friday 15:30 plus 67 h is Monday ~10:30, not “Tuesday 11:00 (2 days 19 hours).” Tuesday 11:00 is about 91–92 h. Friday 15:30 to Wednesday 09:00 is about 113.5 h, not 65.5 h. In the percentile table, 62–66 h is mapped to Monday evening–Tuesday morning, 75–86 h to Tuesday afternoon–Wednesday afternoon, and 120–144 h to Saturday–Sunday. Those pairs disagree by roughly 15–48 h. The headline envelope is not usable.

**Section 2 contradicts section 3.** Scenario A’s table gives Monday earliest, Tuesday central, Wednesday tail. The decomposition then says earliest Tuesday 10:00, central Tuesday 10:30–12:00, tail Tuesday afternoon, after chemistry fails to finish Friday. Scenario B’s table is Sunday–Monday; the body is Tuesday 11:00–12:00 and “negligible difference.” Scenario C’s table is Tuesday evening–Wednesday; the body is again Tuesday late morning. A, B, and C are not materially different outcomes. The ledger says microbiology dominates the smooth path; the decomposition says chemistry and the QA queue make on-call irrelevant.

**Chemistry queueing is not the queue in the prompt.** Waiting ahead are one urgent and three ordinary samples, and both analysts are still occupied (0.4–0.9 h and 1.2–2.0 h). Both in-service jobs finish before 18:00. At most one new minimum-length job can finish before close; the later analyst cannot finish a 1.5 h minimum. B-417 does not plausibly start around 16:20–17:00 and leave a neat 1.5–2.0 h. Under FIFO it is behind four waiting samples. Under risk-priority the urgent sample outranks “high,” but the second analyst can start B-417 at Monday 08:00. Scenario C then has those four samples completing Saturday 14:00–16:00 while stating that the lab is closed Saturday.

**Monday QA is staffed with the wrong number of reviewers.** From Monday there are two reviewers. A join near Monday 09:30–11:00 and a net queue of roughly 6–8 batches is on the order of half a day of parallel work, so service can start Monday afternoon and may finish before 20:00. The decomposition prices that queue as 9–12 h on one server, says B-417 cannot start Monday, then adds another 1.5 h Tuesday for “one more batch” already included in the wait. Scenario E switches to “~4 reviewers,” which do not exist, and a Friday join queue of 12–15.

**The contamination clock is long by about two days.** Monday ~09:00 plus 20–30 h is Tuesday morning through Wednesday afternoon, with readout the same working day. The text lands confirmation on Thursday 14:00–20:00 or Friday 09:00 and the join on Friday. It then pushes QA to the following Monday. A confirmed indication raises investigation probability to 0.30–0.55. It is not a 0.5 chance of rejection. That 0.5 also fights the ledger’s overall rejection band of 0.01–0.03.

**Path probabilities are not a coherent mixture.** Midpoints sum past 100% even after the stated overlaps. D’s weights 0.575 + 0.25 + 0.125 do not match the claimed 57.5% / ~30% / ~12.5% split. Folding a 3–7 calendar-day lab investigation into `5 days × 8 h` and then into an 8.7 h expected add-on is the mean-stacking shortcut the task forbids. “Plausible earliest” is set to the modal Tuesday release and given “70% confidence,” which is neither an early envelope nor a probability of that instant. Section 5 ranks on-call at ±2–3 days and chemistry discipline at ±6–12 h, then says both are about 0 h. Six drivers are listed. Section 6’s primary event is Saturday 12:00–18:00 readiness, which cannot occur, and the text then says the central forecast does not move.

The assumption ledger, max-versus-sum language, weekend pause, rerun and query tails, and a qualitative temperature dependence are the parts that hold. They do not rescue a forecast whose dominant biological clock, calendar arithmetic, server count, and scenario table disagree with its own decomposition.
