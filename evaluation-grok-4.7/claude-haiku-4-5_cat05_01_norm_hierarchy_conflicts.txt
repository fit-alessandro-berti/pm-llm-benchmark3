4.4

The assessment covers every required heading and shows real sensitivity to time windows, A6 non-cure, the July 14 outage, and the difference between an ordinary email and a controlled exception. That coverage does not hold together. The hierarchy, the rule logic, and the case results contradict one another, and three outcome-driving classifications rest on a grace period and “pending” finance approvals that no source provides.

The method in §1 is partly sound: GPIP-4 is the baseline, A7 was not invoked, ECD-2 is time-boxed, silence is not a waiver, and later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment control. It then abandons those premises. §2 says ECD-2 probably cannot override segregation, Finance approval, or Compliance clearance. §3 Rule 2 and Rule 4 nevertheless treat joint Incident Command authorization as allowing commitment before Finance, and §4 uses that override for P-502, P-507, and P-508. §1 correctly reads B5 as not waiving Finance or the approved-supplier rule. The issue table and R6 then say the B4 emergency waives B2/B3 and the register. The pseudo-logic is not usable as formal logic: comparison operators are missing, so several rules do not state the constraint they name, and Rule 4’s compliance defeater drops A3’s new-supplier trigger.

Case results do not follow the stated hierarchy.

- P-501 is directionally defensible as noncompliant: 08:50 is before 09:00, the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate, and commit-before-PO is not otherwise permitted. The closing summary then alleges R1 and R2 breaches that the case row itself rejects (different requester and committer; EUR 38,000 is under the EUR 50,000 rule). The “decisive” phone recording is not in the evidence. The outage covers 08:42 and 08:50 and is not used.
- P-502 should be noncompliant under the author’s own hierarchy. It is a new supplier, Compliance clearance is at 18:30, and Finance approval is at 13:05, after a corroborated 10:15 commitment. A6 does not cure that. The four-hour recording rule was met (10:10 to 12:55), but §6 calls that recording a logging defect. The JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION label depends on an invented grace period.
- P-503 is the strongest call. EUR 140,000 with no Compliance event and no Finance event fails A2 and A3 even if backup-line scope is conceded, and PROC-3’s triple role is a serious segregation problem. The §5 alternative does not change the classification, so it misses the required “would change the result” test.
- P-504 lands correctly: 16 July 14:00 is inside the ECD-2 clock and outside its purpose. The row opens by placing it outside the window, then corrects itself, and it imports ECD-2’s two-member rule into SOP B4. EUR 24,000 is also under B2’s EUR 25,000 line, which is never used.
- P-505 wrongly says EUR 88,000 to an approved supplier triggers Compliance clearance. A3 does not apply. Plant approval is omitted. Contemporaneous call, acknowledgment, and the nine-minute-fast clock converge on a pre-18:00 commitment; INDETERMINATE underuses that evidence. The July 14 outage is also blended into a 18 July timing dispute.
- P-506 is sound. A verbal overnight extension with no record does not move the 19 July 06:20 commitment back inside ECD-2.
- P-507 has pre-commitment Compliance clearance and a signed PDF at 12:40, inside four hours of 08:55. The author never checks that deadline, then classifies a total absence of Finance approval as a low-severity justified exception by assuming a later Finance act. That contradicts the burden rule in §1.3.
- P-508 is not compliant. EUR 155,000 is above D1’s EUR 150,000 cap; D2’s Legal clearance is “as well,” not a substitute; there is no Plant or Finance event; the Chief Risk Officer did not ratify anything. COMPLIANT at high confidence, while the same cell says a strict reading is noncompliant and Finance approval is “assumed pending,” breaks A6, the evidence rules, and the author’s hierarchy.

Confidence is systematically too high where the prose is already conditional. §7 is a full manual rather than a concise hierarchy clause, and its “baseline” column misstates A3 for the EUR 50,000–150,000 band, including new suppliers. The sunset and Chief Risk Officer ratification ideas are useful; they do not repair the case results.

The paper is a complete first draft with several locally correct outcomes. It is not a reliable compliance opinion, because the classifications of P-502, P-507, and P-508 are reached by relaxing the rules the earlier sections said could not be relaxed.
