6.5

The forecast has the right skeleton: the weekend is treated as a hard gate, the join is a maximum, chemistry is shown to miss Friday close, and the heavy tail is tied to correlated rework rather than to summed stage means. The elapsed-time bookkeeping, scenario probabilities, and several binding-constraint claims do not hold together tightly enough for that skeleton to be reliable.

The smooth-path clock is internally inconsistent. Friday partial service is estimated at about 0.4–3.4 h remaining, which puts risk-priority completion near Monday 08:30–11:30, while FIFO is placed at Monday 13:30–15:30. Those two supports are then replaced by a blended Monday 10:30–13:30 window that is not the mixture of either interval. The maximum is taken against that blend, so queue discipline disappears before the join. The same write-up then calls Sunday on-call authorization a zero-hour effect. That is only true when chemistry finishes after microbiology readout. On the fast residual (minimum service 1.5 h minus up to ~1.3 h already worked), chemistry can be done before a Monday 08:30–09:30 readout, so the on-call branch does move the left edge. The stated earliest completion, Monday 12:00–13:30, assumes a fresh 1.5 h of Monday chemistry service and therefore misses the residual-work case already calculated in Section 3. A defensible earliest release, with an empty QA queue and a short review, sits earlier on Monday morning.

Scenario probabilities do not match the clocks attached to them. Scenario 1 is described as mutually exclusive of Scenario 2, yet FIFO (about 55%) is inside Scenario 1’s decomposition and also inside Scenario 2, whose 15–22% band can cover a rerun (0.10–0.20) or FIFO, not both. Scenario 4’s 18–28% band is the right order of magnitude for a documentation query or a documentation-only investigation; the Wednesday–Thursday central window is the duration of those delays stacked. The joint of a query and an investigation on an otherwise clean batch is only a few percent. Most of that probability mass ends Tuesday, not Thursday. Scenario 3 mentions a Sunday on-call start of the 20–30 h confirmation, which can return a usable result Monday afternoon, then reports the join only as Tuesday 09:30–16:30. A long Monday rerun is likewise capped at Monday 17:30 even though the chemistry laboratory closes at 18:00 and the rerun maximum is 3.0 h.

The required summary numbers do not match the calendar. Wednesday 23 September 15:30 is 120 h from Friday 15:30, and Thursday 24 September 16:00 is 144.5 h, but Tuesday 29 September 12:00 is 260.5 h, not 250.5 h; Tuesday 29 September 15:30 is 264 h, not 250 h; Thursday 1 October 18:00 is 314.5 h, not 306.5 h. Section 3’s +3/+5/+7 calendar-day examples are dated correctly and then converted incorrectly in Sections 2 and 4. The Saturday-afternoon QA freeze is real, but the dormant gap from a 16:00 completion to Monday 08:00 is 40 h, not 43 h.

The top uncertainty driver contradicts the ledger. The ledger shows that even a 16:42 start cannot finish by 18:00, because 1.3 h remains and minimum service is 1.5 h. Driver 1 then says a 15-minute Friday slip is what creates the 62 h weekend jump. That jump is already certain on this prefix; Friday closure is a level shift, not the main spread. Path and dependence uncertainty (excursion, investigation class, contamination) move the distribution by days. The excursion update itself uses quantities the case does not supply: contamination “up to 0.35,” a rejection band of 8–12% that consumes essentially all of Scenario 3 despite a false-positive release branch in the same scenario, a median shift of +40 to +75 h, and a variance cut of more than 70%.

QA arithmetic is only partly specified. Clearing about two batches in 4.5 reviewer-hours implies a mean service near 2.2 h, which is a plausible reading of a 1.5 h median and 3.5 h 80th percentile, but that mean never enters the ledger, and 7 reviewer-hours are later said to clear 3.5 batches. Overdispersion is named and then reduced to a 1.0–2.5 h wait. The central Monday-afternoon release and a Tuesday P80-like band remain a reasonable sketch of the bulk of the distribution; the earliest edge, the scenario table, and the severe-tail hour labels are not accurate enough to carry the same weight.
