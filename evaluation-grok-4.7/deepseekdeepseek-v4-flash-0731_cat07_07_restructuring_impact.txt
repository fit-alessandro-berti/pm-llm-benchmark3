The required seven sections are present, and the forecast is directionally balanced: East is treated as already overloaded, legal/risk capacity near 91–93% is treated as a constraint on the claimed utilization gain, and the recommendation is a limited pilot rather than a full rollout. Those points are not enough to make the forecast reliable.

Section 1 never maps resilience to absence, despite cross-team cover being the current absence path (6 per 100 cases, 8.9h, 18% rework). The optimistic handover rates (36 / 31 / 9–10) are the current embedded-specialist rates, not a post-change forecast. The central scenario states 19 legal/risk handovers per 100 cases; the management table states 12. The adverse scenario says rework “rises to 8–0%” and that workload is “idle 100%,” which reverses a bottleneck. Several scenario sentences do not state a usable claim (“tactile work,” “plarry,” “Eastagains,” “crossteam handover not middle”).

Section 4 names the required hidden risks, then invents a “Latinos” segment that is not in the log and cites `discover_8_activity_based_resource_similarity`. The pilot’s utilization guardrail (shared resources between 45% and 80%) is already incompatible with Legal at 93% and Risk at 91%. The pilot window is 4 weeks pre and 6–8 weeks post, while the recommendation states 46 and 68 weeks. Guardrail labels such as “10% no claims against waiting” and “< 10 p.p.” are not operational.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested artifacts, but the script calls undefined `format` and `dfs`, passes `complement=` and a bare string to `filter_event_attribute_values`, treats `len()` of a handover network as an edge count, invents `get_all_case_features`, and ends with invalid syntax. It does not compute a before/after comparison.
