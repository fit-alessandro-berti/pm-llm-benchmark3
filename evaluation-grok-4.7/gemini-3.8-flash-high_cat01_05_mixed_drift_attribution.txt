6.4

The write-up covers every required section, keeps the main timeline in the right order (March semantics, May rework, June capacity, July priority, October mix, December confounding), and the baseline deltas and 7-edge \(L_1\)/\(L_2\) figures check out. It still fails the standard it is being scored on: quantitative restraint. Several headline numbers are invented or misread, medians are decomposed as if they added, and a number of “refutations” are stronger than the aggregates allow.

The arithmetic that is presented as measurement is not measurement. \(\rho \approx 0.95\), a 2.0 h complexity differential, a 6.0 h “dual-assessor” senior review, and a pre-May Supplier Z complexity of 25% do not appear in the evidence. The July residual \(13.05 - (0.60 + 0.84 + 0.15) = 11.46\) h (87.8%) treats the median of a sum as the sum of medians, counts one extra `Assess` at 4.8 h after first calling the loop 5.4 h, and then puts that residual in the timeline as an observed “~88% of delay.” The same 88% is later the whole queueing bucket, which also contains supplier re-entry, so the staffing share is not identified. November is assigned October’s senior-assessment rate of 22 per 100; that month is not in the normalized DFG. The March sentence calls 0.38 the raw-versus-mapped collapse; \(0.90 - 0.55 = 0.35\), and 0.38 is the February-to-March raw drop. January’s p90−median gap is 37.5 h, not 37.2 h.

Mechanism language overruns the design. March cycle time is essentially flat and mapped fitness near 0.90, so a logging/semantic account is the right primary reading, but “process execution remains unchanged,” “definitively driven,” and repeated “refuted” are not available from monthly totals. Supplier Z was already somewhat more complex; rework only partially reverted; June’s 40.8 h already mixes pre- and post-15 June cases. Most of the p90 rise is April 71 → June 95, before the 1 July priority rule; June 95 → August 110 is the smaller piece. The priority rule reorders the queue. It does not, by itself, raise `Assess → Senior Assessment`. Intake mix from May through August is not stable. The 9 September–10 October row describes the September recovery, while October is the renewal break. December is labeled mixed and then the seasonal alternative is said to be refuted by an 8 pp rework drop that also coincides with lower volume, lower complexity, and lower Supplier Z share, on a monthly total that includes five days before the intervention.

The distance section is calculated correctly and then over-interpreted. Rows skip February, April, June, August, and November, so `.diff()` is not month-to-month. `Assess → Request Info` and `Request Info → Collect Docs` move almost in lockstep, so \(L_1\) double-counts the loop. That partial \(L_1\) is not an exact lower bound on graph-edit distance. Token replay does not clear duplicate `Open Case` events with silent transitions; extras are log moves. Silent transitions are model-side. A second logging “change point” on 1 July is the maximum of a post-March plateau, on the priority-rule date, with no logging event. The code repeats that mistake: every month from March stays flagged because labels stay at 11 and raw fitness stays near 0.5. That is a level shift, not a change point.

Monitoring is broad and the seasonality / false-discovery discussion is real, but several rules miss the episode they are meant to catch. p90/median never exceeds 2.5 (November is \(121/49 \approx 2.47\)). Assess service rose about 12.5%, so a “within 10%” gate would not open for the staffing episode. Supplier share is not a defect rate, and share never returns below 15% after May, so the habituation rule cannot fire in September or December. A missing-resource alert at 3% against the January baseline is on forever after March. A 30-day rolling label set absorbs a bad label. `is-exactly-1(Open Case)` plus “Open Case precedes Register” is violated by essentially the entire pre-migration log, where that event is absent. STL seasonal bands are not estimable from one observed season. The proposed primary population (non-Z, standard, no duplicate open, resource present) is a useful control and a poor primary monitor: it drops the supplier, complexity, and seasonal mass the drift actually lives in, and conditions on post-migration missingness.

PM4Py names (`format_dataframe`, `filter_time_range`, `discover_dfg`, `get_variants`, `discover_temporal_profile`, log skeleton, DECLARE) match the high-level interface, and raw versus mapped copies are separated. Assigning `org:resource` from the pre-format frame after `format_dataframe` reorders rows can attach resources to the wrong events. Deduping with `duplicated` without a timestamp sort does not keep the earliest `Open Case`.

The narrative skeleton is the right one. The score is held down by fabricated precision, one misdated rate, median arithmetic presented as a decomposition, and decision thresholds that would not have detected the series they were built from.
