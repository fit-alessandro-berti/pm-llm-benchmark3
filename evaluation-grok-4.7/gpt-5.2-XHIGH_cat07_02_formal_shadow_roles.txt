7.4

The role model is largely faithful to the counts, complexity medians, fragments, and substitution episodes, and it respects the main constraints: temps stay separate from permanent front office, Chen is not treated as interchangeable with Dina, Giorgio’s cover is not treated as equal competence, and Hana’s approval authority is separated from Mateo’s routing. Shadow roles include benefits and risks, and the ambiguity tests are specific.

The analysis still overreaches in several places. Mateo’s A1/A2/A4/A12 volume is about two-fifths of his events, so describing that work as coordination “breadcrumbs” understates real execution. Hana’s A13 is peripheral in the table and part of the core in section 2. The claim that frequency clustering would merge Nora with Tier-1 is weak: her profile is only A1/A2, while agents also carry large A4/A12 mass. “Final authority” from the Week 5 rechecks ignores that 8 of 21 preliminary approvals were not rechecked. “More supervised” for Dina and “specialist idle time” if Mateo is removed are not supported. Certifications, tenure, shift, and home team are almost unused, so authority is inferred from activity mix more than the attribute evidence allows.

The code is only partly coherent. `critical_share` is a within-resource 95th percentile, so it is about 0.05 for every resource and does not measure critical exposure. Case complexity is aggregated on events, which double-counts long cases. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting results are never inspected. Role membership reads `resources` rather than the usual `originators` key, and the discovery calls pass keyword arguments these PM4Py functions generally do not accept. The pipeline therefore does not reliably join mined clusters to complexity, substitution, or authority.
