6.5

The core comparison is right: under standard loop semantics, Model A ties one Check Revision to every Analyze Revision, while Model B has exactly one Check Revision concurrent with the whole loop. The four visible counterexamples are valid in both directions, including two-analysis traces, and the effects of optional Approve Revision and hidden tau are explained correctly. Visible-trace, P3, and P2 verdicts follow from that invariant, and the bounded-versus-unbounded warning is sound.

The structural table is not. Model A does allow Check Revision between Rework Case and the next Analyze Revision, because each iteration starts with a parallel of those two activities. The claim that A forbids that placement is false.

The simplification is also mis-scoped. P1 explicitly keeps the number and placement of approvals visible. Section 1 defines P1 that way, but Section 8 drops Approve Revision and still says P1 is preserved and that the simpler tree should replace Model A for compliance. That is a contradiction, not a stated compromise. The simplicity comparison also counts 4 visible leaves instead of 5 (Open, Analyze, Check, Rework, Close).

Petri-net sizes are given as “expected exact” figures (~8/~9 places and transitions) without a derivation; a standard process-tree conversion yields 10 places and 10 transitions for both nets. The bounded search only accepts a trace when a visible transition enters the final marking, so a silent step into the final marking would be dropped. The required name `simplified_model` is never assigned.
