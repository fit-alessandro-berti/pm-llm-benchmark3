The analysis is strong on abductive structure: it separates the measured fitness collapse from residual operational risk, uses the staged 32% → 58% → 81% → 89% repairs in the right order, treats the August sign-off rule as model obsolescence rather than misconduct, and warns that an optional sign-off tree buys fitness by overgeneralizing. All eight required sections, column sets, and the six governance topics are present, and the routing shares, 26/23/8-point decomposition, and 6.7% April baseline are calculated correctly.

It is not nearly flawless. Directly-follows count 530 is repeatedly treated as 81.5% of June–July cases; the Classify → Queue Routed variant makes the case share 560/650 (86%). Counterfactuals invent costs (0.30, 0.09) that contradict the given 0.44 and 0.12, and they say inverted edges fall from 700 rather than 70. Phase 2’s “18 unconfirmed” June cases contradicts the correct 70 − 11 = 59 used earlier. The missing-resource jump is attributed to resourceless Queue Routed events, but adding a resourceless event on ~86% of cases would push that rate to about 15%, not the observed 5.9%. The ledger cell “no inkas” is garbled, and section 7 runs long of the 400-word cap.

The PM4Py block follows the requested call sequence and states the EUR 50,000 / precision caveat, but it is not reliably executable: top-level imports of `PTTransitionNode` and `process_tree.grammar.Operator` do not match stock PM4Py, `discover_dfg` is used as an object with `.graph` although the high-level API returns a tuple, and precision depends on a nonexistent `pm4py.algos.conformance.precision_ga` path whose fallback is activity coverage, not precision.
