6.5

The policy is complete, mostly constraint-legal, and unusually honest about trade-offs, but it is not executable as written and several of its own rules do not generate the assignment table.

The hard/soft split, the worker explanation, and the audit list are the strongest parts. High-overtime resources are held in reserve, high-priority work stays at skill 2–3, security and approval legality are respected, C007 and C016 keep continuity, C006/C012 are a valid quality batch, and the Eva/Jonas security note is correctly rejected because both have skill 0. The comparison refuses a fake optimum. Those outcomes are the reason the grade stays above the middle.

Section 2 does not implement that table. `U` depends only on the case, so it is a constant inside each argmax and cannot change the resource. Fairness, resilience, and the “large” short-SLA penalty are never given formulas, so the score is not reproducible. The overtime test calls `no_safer(opts)` while `opts` is still being built, and the continuity gates reassign `opts` inside the resource loop, which wipes candidates mid-scan. H9 turns a historical association (>2 reassignments) into a hard force-owner rule. The prose weight shift includes “SLA ≤ 4h”; the sketch shifts only on `priority == "urgent"`. The sketch also drops every high-overtime resource on non-urgent work and never applies the “no safer alternative” exception.

The queue decisions diverge from that machinery and from the stated evidence. R6 receives C002, C010, and C011 — three cases — but the batch check says four. C001’s SLA is 3h and the cited handover penalty is a median 2.7h of wait; that path is expected to consume the deadline, and a supervised owner is also expected to add service time, yet the case is not escalated. C014 is extra rework on R8, who already has the enterprise security case and C015, with no scored comparison against other rework skill 2–3 resources. Hours remaining are never estimated against R5 (5.0h, two high-priority cases, eight open), R6 (two urgent SLAs), or R8 (5.5h, three new cases). Moving C003 off R4 makes `reassignment_count` cross the >2 threshold; that consequence is missing from the risk column. The training shortfall is real, but the write-up never tests the other documented diagnosis priority, R6, nor the fact that both approval-priority resources have approval skill 0.

The PM4Py sketch names the right functions and the right audit lifecycle, then misuses two of them: `filter_event_attribute_values` is exact-value membership, not a numeric `≥ 0.75` test, and `filter_case_performance(0, 1e9)` is a no-op that treats cycle time as if it were an SLA deadline. Several constraint sentences are also missing their operators, so H5, H8, H9, and S3 are not unambiguous rules.
