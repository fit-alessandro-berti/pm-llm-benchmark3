7.2

The audit judgment is well calibrated: routine pairs stay low-risk, emergency channels are treated as plausible, Lena/Kim is isolated for review, and the report avoids calling collusion a fact. The required sections, severities, and support/refute tests are present, and the cited counts (42, 9, 17, 16, 84, 62, 72%, 83%, 18–21%) match the log.

The retrospective-justification test is misspecified. `log_clean_sys` drops `quarter_close_upload`, so the 12 batch cases never reach the check. `filter_paths_performance(..., 0, 3 * 86400)` uses three calendar days, not three working days, and keeps same-person justifications when the gap is short. Discovering DECLARE or a log skeleton from that subset does not encode a different resource or a deadline, and conformance is fit on overlapping cases, so the 17 same-person breaches and late justifications would not be reliably flagged.

Same-person four-eyes breaches are also blurred with two-person handoffs. The 9 bank/payment violations are same-resource cases; the 16 fast approvals and 44 co-cases are Lena→Kim sequences. Section 6 still says the pairing “exhibits 9 four-eyes exceptions.” Section 1 treats the `legacy_erp` bot mapping as already a partial explanation of the 42 PO breaches, while the data only says some bot events are mis-mapped. Create PO→Approve PO (8 and 10 minutes; 84 and 62 cases) is blended with Emergency Override→Approve PO (11 and 14 minutes; 118 and 96 cases).

In the code, `log_legacy` is unused, and the note describes legacy-versus-clean and cross-segment network comparisons that are not run. `discover_network_analysis` sets the in, out, and both node columns to `org:resource` on an event log, so it does not show a resource network conditioned on `vendor_id`. The four-eyes calls also assume the filter returns violators, with no `keep_violations` (or equivalent) check. The “~45 late cases + 17” volume can double-count, and a few mechanisms are more specific than the log (cooling-off window, phone/radio, Kim holding invoices).
