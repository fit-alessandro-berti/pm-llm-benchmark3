6.5

The write-up covers every required section, avoids a fake optimum, keeps high-overtime staff off the batch, respects the four-assignment cap, and correctly refuses Security work for Eva and Jonas (skill 0 cannot be supervised into eligibility). Those parts are sound. The core decision rules are not applied consistently enough for a higher score.

The largest error is C001. Complexity is high, handover cost is high, the SLA is 3 hours, and the stated evidence is that an owner change on a high-complexity case adds a median 2.7 hours of waiting. Constraint 5 prefers continuity unless the owner is skill 0 or 1 and supervision is not feasible. R3 is skill 1 and R7/R8 can supervise, so the exception does not apply: R3 should perform Security under specialist supervision. The table instead makes R8 the performer and calls the delay “slight.” That understates a wait that can consume almost the entire SLA.

C003 has the same kind of problem. Its reassignment count is already 2, and the evidence says more than 2 reassignments raises rework and appeals. Moving it from specialist owner R4 to trainee R6 crosses that line. Avoiding R4’s high overtime is reasonable; using a repeated diagnosis loop as the training case is not.

The pseudocode would not produce the recommended queue. Supervised pairs are constructed only for Security, yet C003 and C009 are diagnosis pairs. Quality batching and the global 20% training quota never appear in the algorithm, though both are used to justify the table. High overtime is only a score penalty for urgent/high work, not “allowed only when no safer alternative exists.” Urgency is added equally to every candidate, so it does not affect who is chosen. Several helpers (`supervised_case`, `estimated_activity_time`, `relax_soft_constraints_in_order`) are undefined, and the weights are unexplained for a policy that is supposed to be auditable by workers.

Capacity is also overclaimed. R6 receives two urgent cases, a normal approval, and a trainee diagnosis inside 6.5 hours. R3 is capped at four while owning a 6-hour high-complexity rework and supposedly supervising two diagnoses plus C001 context. Section 4 still calls the result more resilient because “R8/R7 share Security,” while R8 actually takes two of the three Security cases plus rework.

The PM4Py sketch names the right families of functions, but `training_priority` is not on the log, the skill matrix is not shaped for the merge shown, `get_service_time` is left as an ellipsis, and `filter_case_performance` is never used. It audits a policy more than it learns one, and parts of it would not run.
