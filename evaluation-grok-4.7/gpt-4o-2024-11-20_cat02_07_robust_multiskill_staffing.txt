The submission is an unfinished fragment. It covers only a thin version of sections 1–2 and then stops. Sections 3–9 are absent, including every required policy, the policy-by-scenario table, the recommendation, secondary effects, the process-mining design, the Python block, and the validation plan. Against a format that demands nine specific sections and an executable comparison, that omission is decisive.

What is present is not reliable enough to carry the missing work.

**Assumptions.** Uniform draws inside the given ranges are a labeled and acceptable choice, and priority order plus a one-preemption cap are restated correctly. Several load-bearing assumptions are wrong or too vague to use:

- Parallel ME/ES is described as “splitting active time.” Concurrent branches consume two resources at once; case elapsed time is the maximum of the branches plus the 8-minute coordination delay, not a split of one activity. Sequential work adds the two services and avoids that coordination delay. “Without handover” is true only when one cross-skilled resource keeps both branches. A resource change still costs 7 minutes, and a mechanical-to-electrical/software team change costs another 5 unless that same person retains the case.
- SLA attainment is defined from activity time plus setup, coordination, and preemption penalties. The targets are elapsed completion times. Queue wait, shift gaps, breaks, and the 09:30–10:00 intake outage dominate those clocks and are left out.
- The restart rule is missing: an interruption adds 10% of remaining active service, and a case may be preempted at most once. “Cannot be further displaced” is not the same rule.
- Uncertainty is misstated. U4 doubles complex-tail probabilities, so the service mixture changes. U2 doubles E and ES from 12:00–16:00 and renormalizes the mix. U5 adds a hard intake blackout, not only an arrival or roster change. “Service times and prioritization unchanged” is false for this set.
- Fairness as max/min utilization among whoever is on shift does not implement the stated rule that planned utilization above 90% for any person is unfair. Shift lengths differ sharply (Farah is four hours; Emil starts at noon), and breaks and the 30-minute overtime cap never enter the definition.
- The “objectives” are slogans. There is no formulated objective: no metric, weights, constraints, or aggregation across U1–U5. Tail/SLA risk and fairness are named, which meets the topic list only, not the request for two usable formulations. Decision variables stay at the level of “degree of pooling.”

**Offered load.** The table is not auditable. No block, no skill-hour identity, no resource-speed adjustment, and no available-minute denominator appear. The magnitudes do not match the demand and service tables. A day has 42 expected arrivals. Intake mixture mean is about 7 minutes, so intake work is on the order of 290 minutes, not 20–40. Mechanical touches M and the mechanical leg of ME, about 18 activities, with a mixture mean near 26 minutes before multipliers, so roughly 450–500 minutes, not 120–150. Electrical (E + ME + ES) is larger still; “~200+” has no stated scope. Bottleneck notes are also off: morning mechanical is available to Ada and Ben at multiplier 1.00, Deepa from 10:00 at 1.25, and Farah at 1.40, so it is not “Farah only.” Afternoon electrical is not a Chen/Emil dependency; Deepa is the fast electrical resource through 18:00, and Ada remains eligible but slow. U1 is one sentence. U2, U4, and U5 are not analyzed. There is no offered-load versus speed-adjusted capacity by block, so bottleneck migration cannot be identified.

**Missing analytical core.** No policy states eligibility, score, reservation, preemption, parallel-versus-sequential choice, handover avoidance, or overflow triggers. Specialization versus pooling versus reserved P1 capacity is never operationalized, so the central trade-off is not examined. There is no base-versus-U1–U5 comparison on delay, SLA, utilization, fairness, handover burden, or failure mode. Robustness is undefined. Nothing is said about starvation, gaming of expected times, skill erosion, validation becoming the new bottleneck, or interpretable dispatch. No event attributes and no PM4Py analyses are specified. There is no code, so none of the required simulation or capacity scaffold, event log, `format_dataframe`, service/waiting separation, or handover network exists.

The fragment shows that the author read the control flow, the SLA thresholds, and the scenario names. It does not compute from the tables, does not respect elapsed-time or concurrency semantics, and does not deliver a policy comparison. That is an early sketch with material errors, not an analysis.
