The write-up has the required seven-part shape, but the assignment itself is not reliable, and the scoring procedure would not produce the table or enforce the stated rules.

The skill matrix is misread on the decisions that matter. C002 is given to R4_David as “highest skill (3)” for Final Approval. R4’s approval skill is 0, overtime risk is high, the open-case count is 14, and only 3.0 hours remain. Final Approval requires skill 2–3. The legal specialist and current owner is R6_Farid (skill 3). Hard rules are both incomplete and then broken. The cap of 4 new assignments, hours remaining, high-priority work below skill 2 only with a real supervisor, and the security rule (skill 3, or skill 1–2 only under R7_Gita or R8_Hugo) are not actually implemented. Urgent approval is given to a skill-0, high-overtime resource. Non-urgent C014 adds work to that same overloaded resource while R3_Celine and R8_Hugo have rework skill 3 and low overtime risk. Almost every row records the trade-off as “None.”

The pseudocode does not match the log or the rules. `required_skill` is treated as a number rather than a skill name. The SLA term is identical for every candidate, so it cannot rank anyone. Continuity is also case-level, and `1 - handover/3` gives high handover cost a score of 0, which is the reverse of the intended preference; the later owner tie-break never fires. The “resilience” term down-scores Risk and Quality, the teams that hold the security and quality specialists. There is no batch state for the 4-assignment cap, the 20% training share, hours, or supervision. The table cannot be the output of this function.

The baseline comparison stays generic. First-available and specialist-only are not run on C001–C016, while the proposed policy is credited with low SLA risk and high fairness despite the C002 and R4 outcomes. The worker note cites “R6_Farid on Approval with supervision,” which is neither the table nor R6’s skill. Audit names cover the requested indicators, but several PM4Py mappings are the wrong tool (`get_variants` for workload, activity similarity for skill mismatch, organizational roles for queue length). The sketch would not run: `format_dataframe` is applied to an event log, a nonexistent `skill` field is compared with skill-name strings, handover and performance-DFG calls do not match the public API, and service time, waiting time, batches, and the working-together network are only name-dropped.
