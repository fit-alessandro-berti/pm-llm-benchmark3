7.5

The two models are largely the right objects: the POWL partial order matches the stated cross-branch DAG, and the process tree is a real under-approximation rather than an unsafe one. The write-up still misses the standard the prompt sets for witness precision and for not treating unchecked checks as evidence.

The POWL construction is the strongest part. `RD` precedes both openings; `CM→PV` and `ARC→DCA` stay independent; the approval `XOR` and the optional supplier `XOR` are enabled by `ARC` and joined only at release; `PV` and `DCA` both precede the trial loop; the approval node, supplier node, and loop are unordered with each other; `RM→AC` closes the case. High-risk `QR‖RR`, at-most-once supplier consultation, and `LOOP(ET→IT, AP)` are the right abstractions of the data and pass/fail conditions. That language really does allow T04 (reviews before planning/design) and T02 (reviews between `ET` and `IT`) without allowing T06 or a release that skips the chosen route.

The process tree matches the claimed policy. Phasing `(CM→PV)‖(ARC→DCA)` before `trial ‖ approval ‖ optional supplier` preserves the trial gate, approval–trial overlap, the loop, the risk choice, and the release join, and it does not add illegal orders. The cost is exactly the one named: approvals and `Consult Supplier` cannot start until both work products exist, so they cannot overlap `CM`, `PV`, or `DCA`. Keeping that overlap out, instead of the alternative block structure that would serialize approval before the trial, is a defensible choice given how the narrative highlights approval–trial overlap. Duplicate labels are avoided consistently. Parent links and `.order.add_edge` are used in the way the API reminder requires.

The witness and validation sections are not at that standard.

T07 is not a clean loop counterexample. `RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, ET, IT, AP, RM, AC` is already illegal at `RM` because no approval route has occurred. `AP` itself is still a legal prefix under the fail-and-redo reading; the loop rule is broken only when `RM` appears instead of a new `ET→IT`. The reason text calls this “AP after the passing inspection,” which is a different prohibition from “redo not followed by another cycle,” and it never mentions the missing approval. Verdict cells happen to stay “forbidden,” but the earliest condition is not identified. T04 and T06 are doing the real work; T08 is the clean missing-approval case.

The silent-behavior check repeats that mistake. `RD, CM, ARC, PV, DCA, ET, IT, RM, AC` is T08: supplier skip is not the only thing going on, and the approval route is absent. Using it as the positive “silent correctly skipped” oracle would accept a net that drops reviews. The canonical-choice paragraph also states that the POWL “converts to a sound workflow net” before the plan has performed that conversion.

Section 4’s concurrency gloss is wrong in a smaller way. `CM→PV→trial_loop` puts containment strictly before the trial, not merely before release. Grouping “CM vs opt_cs/trial” as one residual case understates that precedence. Section 6 also switches between “overconstrains” and “under-approximation” for the same language loss, and “false negatives” is the wrong conformance word if it means spurious deviation alarms.

Residual self-critique items (silent skip ordered after `ARC`; whether a sequence inside a parallel is interleaved) are real and correctly limited. They do not repair the bad oracle or the confounded trace.
