6.2

The framework is serious and mostly usable: facts are separated from assumptions, rules are applied at commitment, issue-specific hierarchies replace a single ranking, post-hoc approval is not treated as an automatic cure, and timestamp conflicts are often handled with the right evidence. The write-up still does not apply its own method consistently, and several classifications rest on misread controls.

The decisive break is P-505. Under the stated hierarchy, EUR 88,000 from an approved supplier does not trigger A3, so pre-commit Compliance is not a satisfied requirement. A2 does apply. Finance_Approve at 19:00 is after commitment, no independent budget owner is shown, A6 is said not to cure, and D1 is given only low-to-medium weight. That combination supports NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, with low-moderate residual severity, does not follow. The same unresolved A2/D1 gap is treated as fatal for P-507, where joint Incident Command authorization before commitment fits D1 more closely than P-505’s later Finance event. Assumption 6, which resolves integrity-critical ambiguity against a clean pass, is also not applied to P-505. Section 5 then attacks only the weaker timestamp premise. The supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 and the fast-clock note already favor an in-window act; the classification turns on A2.

P-501 is overstated. SHIFT-1 committed at 08:50; Plant_Approve is a separate 15:10 event. The answer treats that later approval as an undelegated Shift Lead substitution. Below EUR 50,000, A2 is not in play, and B2 does not state that Plant and Finance must precede commitment. Commit-before-PO is allowed on the four-hour stoppage and is still listed as an apparent deviation. NONCOMPLIANT is arguable; medium-high confidence and the stated rationale are not.

P-503’s primary NONCOMPLIANT call holds because EUR 140,000 had no pre-commit Compliance or Finance. The alternative does not. Scope inclusion, D1, and a softer view of PROC-3 still leave A3 unmet, so those premise changes do not produce JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or INDETERMINATE. C3 is also blurred: two distinct authorizers can satisfy “distinct,” while the real defect is A1 if PROC-3 is a final approver. Calling the recorded 10:55 authorization only a partial attempt undersells the record.

“Unapproved” is equated with A3 “new supplier.” That is explicit only for P-502. It inflates P-504 and makes the Section 6 grouping of P-503 “as new supplier” inaccurate; P-503 is an approved supplier above EUR 100,000. P-504 remains noncompliant on scope, B3, and unilateral commitment, but not on that A3 theory.

The hierarchy table reverses its own columns for several GPIP rows: statements about what cannot defeat a clause are placed under what that clause cannot override. The issue-specific bullets are clearer than the table. Broken comparisons (“D1150k,” blank operators, “BUY-8  IC authorizers”) make the formal catalogue harder to apply. B6 is checked for P-501 and then largely disappears. Severity has no stated scale, so High for the cafeteria purchase and Low-moderate for P-505 are not comparable.

P-502, P-506, P-507, and P-508 are broadly aligned with the stated rules: late Compliance on a new supplier, an unwritten extension after 18:00, a PDF-versus-ERP recording distinction, and Legal clearance not substituting for a Finance Controller above EUR 150,000. Section 7 actually resolves authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, records, expiry, and repeat-waiver review. Those strengths do not repair the break between the announced burden of proof and the one case labeled justified.
