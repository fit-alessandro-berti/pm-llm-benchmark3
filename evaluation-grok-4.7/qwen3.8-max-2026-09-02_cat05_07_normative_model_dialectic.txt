7.5

The final review is strong on structure, case coverage, and dialectical balance, but it is not nearly flawless. Two final-conclusion problems keep it well below a 9.

The case analysis is generally careful. All 15 cases are classified with a dominant reason, severity, and confidence. Authority, expiry, scope, CRM logging, and resource rules are separated from raw trace shape. SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715 are handled convincingly. Both briefs acknowledge the other side’s real points, and the system judgment does not treat every M0 deviation as misconduct.

The judgment is inconsistent on SUP-702. Section 1 correctly lists automated master-data validation for that case as unproved, and the stated exception burden requires authority plus every material condition. PD-3 requires sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation; the trace shows neither a validation event nor an undisputed incorporation of PD-3 into CS-9. Calling SUP-702 a justified exception, while the same gap leaves the 289 similar cases only “potentially” justified, does not follow that burden.

The model revision has a concrete semantics error. Section 1 says POWL cannot express loops. It can: `Operator.LOOP` is binary, with a do-child and a redo-child. The code builds a one-child loop. In current PM4Py that construction raises, and even without the check its semantics are undefined. The fallback to `Operator.XOR` is also one child, so it fails the choice-arity rule as well. Repeatable `Request_More_Info` was required, and neither the loop nor the claimed approximation is executable. A finite 0/1/2-occurrence unrolling would have been the defensible substitute.

Smaller model limits remain. The auto route forces quality after activation, although PD-3 only permits that delay. The hardcoded `QA_Clearance` effective date is not established by the case. Those are secondary to the loop defect and the SUP-702 contradiction.
