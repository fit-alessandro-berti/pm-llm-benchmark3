7.8

The answer is unusually strong on estimands, staggered-adoption design, median-versus-tail separation, interference, and most of the arithmetic, but it is not nearly flawless. Several factual, quantitative, and design errors are material under a strict reading.

The pre/post levels, log-point difference-in-differences, leave-one-control-out ranges, and event-time ATTs that were checked against the panel are correct to ordinary rounding. Examples include Alpha’s late median DiD of about −16.5 log points, Gamma’s May–June to July–August median DiD of about −10.6, the branch-weighted event-time median path from −6.9 to −12.6, and the escalated-volume totals of 412 to 543. The distinction between availability and `automated_fast_path_pct`, the point that fast-path completion is partly an outcome, the fraud-desk SUTVA problem, and the refusal to treat static two-way fixed effects as the estimand are all right. The claim ladder matches that caution. The code meets the requested panel, contrast, relative-time, clustered, and intensity requirements and does not pretend branch-month aggregates are an event log.

The quantitative discipline breaks in the regression-to-the-mean paragraph. Gamma’s May–June median is about +22% versus January–March (+19.7 log points), not +16%. Delta and Epsilon rose by about +24% and +19% (+21 and +17 log points). Gamma’s idiosyncratic excess versus those controls is about zero, not 2–4 log points, so the claim that reversion could explain up to a third of Gamma’s −10.6 effect is not supported by the comparison that is named. That error leaks into the −3% end of the median range. Section 1 also says arrivals peaked in June in every branch; Gamma peaked in July, which is its adoption month. The May complexity increase is +5 points in every branch; the “5–8 points everywhere” wording folds in Epsilon’s later June step.

Identification is slightly misstated. The wild cluster bootstrap is model-based resampling. Calling it design-based inference blurs it with randomization inference, which the threats table later names correctly. The preferred group-time contrasts are also unadjusted, while the strategy table lists complexity as an adjustment variable. “April, a flat month” is inaccurate: control medians rose from March to April.

The process section sees that rework need not scale with use, but it never computes the obvious composition check. If rework cannot occur on the automated fast path, Alpha’s rise from about 4% to 48% fast-path share would have cut the rework share sharply. Recorded rework instead ends higher than its pre-period level, which implies a large increase in rework among the remaining cases. “Can be pure composition” and “definitional artifact” are therefore too narrow.

The rollout choice, case-level randomization, routing freeze, fraud-desk monitoring, and pre-registered non-inferiority rule are the right policy shape. The protocol contradicts itself. Randomization is 50% for weeks 1–6 and 80% for weeks 7–10, then “full availability,” while the primary endpoint is weeks 9–16 and the scale-up rule requires a randomized contrast at week 16. Delta is both scheduled to adopt after 16 weeks and allowed to adopt only if five conditions hold. Epsilon is fixed as the next site and also left to a coin flip. Those are design errors, not wording nits.

The safe descriptive claims are mostly earned. The answer should not have attached a specific reversion share to Gamma, should not have called the bootstrap design-based, and should not have proposed a primary window that outlives the randomization.
