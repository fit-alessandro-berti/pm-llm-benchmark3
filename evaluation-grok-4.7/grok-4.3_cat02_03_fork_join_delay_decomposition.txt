4.5

The fork-join formulas in §1 are right, and the case table is right for 11 of 12 cases, but the analysis is not reliable enough to score higher. The error is on a required worked case, the aggregates contradict the table, and the code does not run the required counterfactuals.

**Critical-path error.** For F11 the branch ends are A 11:30, B 11:20, and C 11:03. `join_ready` is 11:30, the critical branch is A, and downstream queue is 11:50 − 11:30 = 20. The table, the worked line (“join_ready=680 (B)”), and the later counts all treat B as critical and downstream queue as 30. Critical frequency is therefore A 2/12, B 8/12, C 2/12, not 1/12, 9/12, and 2/12. The Python `max` would have labeled F11 as A, so the published table also disagrees with the code.

**Aggregates do not match the table.** From the printed case rows, mean B service is 450/12 = 37.5, not 38.3; mean B queue is 230/12 ≈ 19.2, not 20.8; mean C queue is 125/12 ≈ 10.4, not 9.2. Mean sync waits are about 11.0 (A), 4.3 (B), and 30.4 (C). Reporting A sync as 40.8 and then saying A waits a long time because it finishes early reverses the evidence: A’s slack is often 5–15 minutes, and A is the runner-up, not an early branch. No exact ties exist, but F08 and F10 have 5-minute margins; those near-ties are never stated. Tail contribution to `join_ready` is absent.

**Counterfactuals are incomplete and partly wrong.** A 20% A-service cut (10 minutes) changes F01 and F11 only. On F01 the second branch is 5 minutes behind, so join time falls by 5, not by the full cut. A 50% B-queue cut leaves F01, F04, F08, and F11 unchanged, and on F05 and F10 the gain is capped when A becomes critical (savings 10 and 5, not “10–15” on nine cases). Downstream masking is asserted for F10–F12 without using the F1/F2 calendars. Several of those waits occur while the assigned final resource is already idle, so they are not shown to be capacity queueing. Eliminating every downstream wait is also not jointly feasible on two servers: F09 and F11 would overlap. The stated mean downstream delay of 15.9 matches neither the correct sum (176/12 ≈ 14.7) nor the answer’s own column (186/12 = 15.5).

**Redesigns overclaim mechanisms.** B is still the main flow-time controller, but “75% of cases,” “B utilization increases,” and a new `fork_release` bottleneck do not follow. The binding secondary effect is A, whose path is fixed at 50 minutes with zero queue. Two final servers already have idle gaps and a 20-minute service against a 15-minute arrival spacing, so a third F resource is not identified as the throughput lever. Assumptions (end time shifts one-for-one with queue, `final_start` can move to `join_ready`, no induced congestion) are not labeled.

**Code requirements are missed.** Sync wait and elapsed fork-join are never calculated. Tie-breaking is implicit. The three counterfactuals are a print statement claiming a “full script” that is not there, so there are no per-case or aggregate savings. §7 correctly names capacity, preemption, causality, and queue versus release policy, but it stays generic and is not tied back to the idle-resource evidence or to uncertainty on the estimates.
