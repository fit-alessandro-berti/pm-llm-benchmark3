## Assumptions

The case-notion paragraph is usable. The convergence and divergence definitions are not applied consistently later. Convergence is defined as merging several lifecycles into one case, and divergence as splitting one lifecycle across cases. The matrix then rates Order convergence High because D2 “merges” O1, O2, and O3, even though an Order flattening yields four separate cases and copies shared events outward. Package divergence is rated Low while O1 is split across P1 and P2 and O3 across P3 and P4. Payment divergence is rated None while V2’s settlement is split across M2 and M3, which section 1’s own definition would call divergence. The same split is filed under Invoice convergence.

The propagation rule is also unstable. Section 1 limits propagation to one declared hop and uses D2–P2–O1 as something that would not be inferred. e16 and e24 already relate D2 directly to O1. Section 7 then proposes a three-hop chain, `carries → packs → contains`.

## Matrix

Ratings often describe the wrong phenomenon.

- Order does not lose P3, P4, D2, or D3. e13, e14, e16, e17, e24, and e27 are already on the relevant order traces. What that flattening damages is duplication of those shared events and interleaving of O1’s two shipment paths and O3’s two shipment paths. Divergence is therefore high for O1 and O3: repeated Pack, Dispatch, and Confirm Delivery activities inside one case. The shared-event list omits e13, e14, e17, e25, and e27.
- Item convergence is high, not low. e13 is on I21 and I31; e16 and e24 are on I12, I21, and I31; e14 and e27 are on I32 and I41. “Items are unique to orders” does not address that. Item traces also drop Create Order, Issue Invoice, Record Payment, and Close Order. The claimed item DFG “handoffs” such as I11 → P1 → D1 are object paths. An activity DFG on these traces is Add Item → Pack Package → Dispatch → Confirm Delivery for every item.
- Package duplication is mis-identified. e17 and e27 contain only P4. The only package-level copies are e16 and e24, shared by P2 and P3. P3 and P4 share no events. A package DFG does not produce P3 → D2 → P2 edges.
- Delivery duplication is absent. Each of e15, e16, and e17 relates to exactly one delivery, so each delivery case is exactly two events: Dispatch then Confirm Delivery. D2’s cargo is O1–O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, and P3: three orders, three items, two packages. The matrix’s “3 packages, 4 items” folds D1’s I11 and P1 into D2. Section 3 later lists the correct cargo, so the two sections disagree.
- Invoice events are likewise one case each. e22 and e25 appear once, in V2. They are not copied across invoices. Suitability labels such as “order for order questions” and “payment is a poor end-to-end view” are broadly sensible, but they sit on these incorrect risk ratings.

## Distortions

Coverage of the required themes is nominal: P3 and P4, D2, V2, instalments, and a short lifecycle all appear. Several of the eight write-ups cite edges or traces the log does not contain.

1. P3 really is e13 → e16 → e24. Calling Pack → Dispatch evidence that P3 was dispatched alone over-reads an activity edge. At package level that edge occurs once per package, which matches four packages. The real inflation is counting one dispatch event, e16, in both P2 and P3.
2. P4 is e14 → e17 → e27. Pack → Confirm Delivery is not a direct edge. e27 is D3 with O3, O4, I32, I41, and P4. It is not a delivery shared with P3 or D2.
3. D2’s flattened trace is e16 → e24 only. It does not contain the other events of O1, O2, and O3, so it is not a monolithic order DFG. The two-event path is real, and it does omit e12 versus e13. The stated mechanism is the recurring flattening error.
4. V2’s sequence e19 → e22 → e25 is the actual instalment history. In an invoice log those two Record Payment events are two of the four payment events in the table, not copies. Order flattening is where e22 and e25 would be doubled, once into O2 and once into O3.
5. The payment isolation claim is the strongest item. M2 and M3 have no internal edge, and the partial-versus-complete relationship exists only through V2. That also contradicts the Payment cell of the matrix.
6. For O1, e12 is followed by e15, then e16. Pack(e12) → Dispatch(e16) is not a directly-follows edge. The concrete interleaving is e11 → e12, e12 → e15, and e15 → e16, and e15 is separated from its own confirmation e23 by the second shipment and by payment. Two dispatches inside O1 match two packages; inflation appears when e16 is also counted in O2 and O3.
7. I11’s path e05 → e11 → e15 → e23 is four event types. That is a short item lifecycle with sibling context removed, not a near-singleton. The objects with one event type are M1–M4. Deliveries have two.
8. O3’s operational order is e13 → e14 → e16 → e17. The cited edges e13 → e16 and e14 → e17 are not direct. The direct cross-path edge is e14 → e16, and the two shipments are forced into one sequence. “Artificial parallelism” reverses that effect. A later real interleaving, e24 → e25 → e27, is left unused.

The e15–e16 gap used in section 4 is five minutes, from 11:00 to 11:05, not one hour. That comparison also pairs an inter-dispatch gap with D2’s cycle time, e16 at 11:05 to e24 at 16:00, so the “contradiction” is two different measurements.

## Decision, code, and checks

Position B is an allowed conclusion, and Order plus Package and Invoice matches the commercial, logistical, and billing questions in this log. The supporting claim that Order avoids extreme convergence and divergence does not survive the table: O1 and O3 have repeated shipment activities, and e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, and e27 are multi-order. The residual-risk example is also unsupported. O1’s close, e28 at 11:00 on 4 February, is after D2’s confirmation at 16:00 the previous day. Nothing in the log marks a “primary” object that could be used to drop e16 from O1.

The code names every required algorithm and stores results. `pm4py.ocel_flattening` takes the object type as a positional argument; the `Parameters.OBJECT_TYPE` form does not match that entry point. `get_object_types`, `get_object_type_counts`, and `get_attribute_values` on an OCEL are not a dependable inspection path. `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is included because of “secondary lifecycles,” which does not say what equivalence would be tested. A relevant use would be whether O1, O2, O3, and O4 are execution-equivalent. They are not: O1 has two shipments, O2 one, O3 two shipments plus a shared invoice.

Sensitivity checks 1 and 4 are aimed at the right quantities. Check 1 still depends on an undefined primary object. Check 2 cannot show the stated effect: e15, e16, and e17 already contain both the orders and the deliveries, so propagating `carries → packs → contains` does not newly attach those dispatch events to orders. Check 3 correctly notes that a Package lead drops V2, M2, and M3.

The assignment asked what each flattening preserves, copies, truncates, or turns into false directly-follows structure. The response answers that question with several traces and edges the snapshot does not generate, and with a lead-object justification that rests on those traces. The structure and the genuine payment, invoice, and consolidation examples keep it from collapsing, but the mechanics are not trustworthy enough to use.
