The diagnosis and the three redesigns are segment-aware, mechanism-specific, and tied to real artifacts (fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces, 14.1% vs 3.3% complaints, 31% vs 9% cost gap, R1–R3 alignments, evidence-loop times, 82% review cliff, EUR 95 vs EUR 410). That is the right problem framing, and A (authorization), B (evidence timing), and C (fulfillment/recovery) are genuinely different.

The score is held down by process-mining errors that the conclusions rely on. Summing edge medians (0.5+0.4+1.8+3.2) is treated as an ~6h end-to-end median and repeated in the table, which medians do not support and which conflicts with the segment fast-path medians of 8h, 13h, 20h, and 28h. DFG arc counts are repeatedly read as unique cases and as one full trace (2,300 “cases” on the five-step path; 890 “cases”). The 30h Repair quote → Customer approval edge is attributed to part-price confirmation and then used as Redesign C’s lever; that edge is the customer-approval wait, while the prompt locates part-price delay in the quote resource. “~78% never need evidence” equates the 22% unclear-defect segment with non-entry to the activity. Right-censoring (3,625 terminals vs 5,000 starts) is well noticed, but the claim that it makes slow paths look worse is the wrong direction.

The Pareto section breaks its own rule: current is marked Dominated = Yes, and A–C are said to dominate current on frontier regions, after stating that dominance cannot be established. Current remains rational for low-risk accessories even after rework. The experiment plan is concrete, but Stage 1 pilots the accessory lane the fast path already serves, so it does not test the high-value routing claim, and later stages can confound the gate with that routing change. Several stop/go points (rework 12.5%→9%, 30% complaint cut, 140h→<100h) are unsupported point targets. Mid-risk devices and the EUR 400 R1 threshold are never operationalized separately from the “high-value” label.
