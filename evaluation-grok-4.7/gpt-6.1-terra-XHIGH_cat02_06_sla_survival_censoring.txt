7.5

The estimands, censoring distinctions, leakage discussion, and case-level bias examples are largely correct and carefully hedged. The grade is held down by a false sensitivity conclusion, a horizon-irrelevant transfer count, and thin use of the control-flow evidence.

The two 72-hour targets are well defined: internal non-resolution `1 − F_R(72)` under competing withdrawal, rejection, and transfer, versus `P(T_terminal > 72)` for internal workload. Time zero, the inclusive deadline, and the four required cases are handled correctly. S03 (resolved at 76) and S09 (open at 92) are known failures and known still-open at 72; S06 (withdrawn at 27) and S08 (transferred at 68) are competing exits and known to have left the internal workload. Right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches are not collapsed. The finite-sample bounds 4/15–6/15 and 5/17–8/17 are correct for both estimands, and it is right that the equality is coincidental: on-time resolutions are S01, S02, S04, and S07 in A and S16, S17, S18, S19, and S27 in B, whereas known survival past 72 is a different set (A: S03, S09, S11, S13; B: S20, S25, S26, S28, S32). The unresolved contributors are the open cases younger than 72: S10 and S15 in A, and S24, S29, and S31 in B. Section 3 never names S29 or S31, so the B upper increment of +3 is not auditable from the narrative.

Completed-case rates 4/6 and 5/7, P1 imbalance (3 vs 5), and the below-72 versus above-72 open cases that are named are accurate. Withdrawal and transfer are not treated as ordinary non-informative censoring. Escalation, rework, full prefixes, and `last_event_gap_h` are correctly kept out of the baseline covariate set; terminal gaps are in fact all zero, so that field leaks endpoint status. The Aalen–Johansen primary analysis and terminal-event Kaplan–Meier secondary analysis match the two estimands, and the warning against reading a competing-risk-censored Kaplan–Meier as real-world resolution risk is right. The hand implementation of Aalen–Johansen is the standard product-limit form and does not drop open cases.

Section 6 does not survive that implementation. At 72 hours the resolution cumulative incidences are about 0.292 (A) and 0.316 (B) with transfer competing, 0.292 and 0.332 with transfer censored, and 0.377 and 0.379 if transfer is credited as resolution. Censoring withdrawal and rejection as well leaves them essentially tied (about 0.361 vs 0.362). Nothing reverses. Only pre-deadline transfers can matter: S21 at 26 and S08 at 68. S32 at 96 stays in the risk set through 72 under every coding, so “B has two transfers and A one” is not a reason the 72-hour comparison would flip. Crediting transfer actually moves A more than B, because S08 occurs in a risk set of 5. The ordering that really is unidentified is the partial-identification one: the cohort intervals overlap because of S10, S15, S24, S29, and S31, which the sensitivity write-up does not use.

The prefix evidence is underused. Cohort B often starts `Submit>AutoCheck` while A does not, which is the observable trace of incomplete screening-policy compliance, but the text only notes that S16 and S17 differ in order. The state sketch is explicitly provisional, yet the bidirectional Review/Rework arrows are stronger than the observed traces, and S27 goes `Rework>Resolve` rather than back to Review. The code calls `pm4py.get_variants` only to print a variant count; the repeated-Review flag is a full-prefix pandas count, correctly labeled descriptive, but it is not a PM4Py-derived, time-safe feature. The plan asks for uncertainty and the bounds are the assumption-light companion, but the code prints neither.
