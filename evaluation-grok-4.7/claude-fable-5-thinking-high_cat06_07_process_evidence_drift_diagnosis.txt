7.5

The six-section structure is complete, and the main split is right: April is a schema/pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September stack supplier performance drift, V3 concept drift, and then Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is a censored subsample. Tests, monitor gates, and the five failure modes are concrete and mostly proportionate. The score is lower because several claims used to justify that split contradict the metrics or the cohort those metrics describe.

The June mechanism is wrong. Mean predicted risk is 0.20 against an observed breach rate of 0.15, so the score distribution did not compress toward low risk; ECE 0.060 versus a Q1 baseline near 0.024 is also not a mild softening. Aggregate P(breach | diagnosis-time features) moved even though P(breach | variant) did not. The right operational conclusion — calibration check, not a panic retrain — is left standing on a false reading of the table.

May is mis-attributed. The unrecomputed “events before diagnosis” cache and the 1–20 April duplicate window belong to April cases. May’s own 2% unknown activity and 3% missing queue line up with a patch on 6 May, i.e. a partial month of still-broken prefixes, which the write-up never isolates.

Training advice is internally inconsistent. July is the stated onset of V3 concept drift (matured V3 breach 0.46, parts edge 65 h) and is still placed in the unconditionally safe training set, while August–September are barred unless supplier features exist. Fitting July without those features embeds the same unobservable regime the answer warns against. Calling V3’s defect “mostly calibration” also underplays the AUROC drop to 0.68 in August and 0.66 in September; an intercept fix cannot restore rank order if lead time varies inside V3 and is absent from the features.

Smaller overclaims accumulate. Rural distance and device age are described as out-of-support model inputs and as something to flag now, although they were never model features and collection is only proposed later. “No new activities or reordered transitions” is stronger than variant shares plus two edges can show. August’s V3 share jump from 22% to 35% predates Partner Z and has no documented policy cause, yet it is folded into the September mix story. “Lower bounds distorted estimates” for the August/September rates is unclear, and the 0.28/0.31 order does not follow the September-then-August maturity clause.

The code meets the requested API list and correctly separates event-month from case-start cohorts, but `discover_performance_dfg` returns a statistics dict, not a median in seconds, so the parts-edge series the audit turns on is never actually extracted. Exact `(case, activity, timestamp)` deduping also misses the likely migration duplicate: the same instant emitted as `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` and as a real label. Temporal features are computed once and never enter the monthly summary.
