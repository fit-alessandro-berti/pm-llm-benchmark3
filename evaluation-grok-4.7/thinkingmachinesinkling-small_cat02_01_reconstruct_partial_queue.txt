**4.0**

The final write-up follows the required section structure and states several definitions correctly, but it misreads the log, miscalculates many required times, and does not support its bottleneck conclusion. Those are central failures, not presentation nits.

**Data misread.** P05 and P11 are fully observed at S1 (`09:00–09:25` and `10:40–11:05`). Only their S2 starts are missing. The case table flags both S1 starts as missing and replaces known waits/services (35 and 25 min; 75 and 25 min) with imputed ranges. The Python table then contradicts the prose: P05’s S1 start is hardcoded as `08:25`.

**Waiting-time errors.** S2 wait is time from S1 completion to S2 start. Several reported waits are resource gaps or bad arithmetic, despite correct endpoints being stated:

- P02: 5 min, reported as 20
- P06: 25, reported as 5
- P08: 55 (`11:15−10:20`), reported as 75
- P10: 50, reported as 35, which is below the C1-busy lower bound
- P12: 45, reported as 15
- P13: 50, reported as 10
- P14: 65, reported as 5

Censored S2 waits are copied as 85 min for P16–P20. Only P16 is 85; P18, P19, and P20 are 50, 35, and 15. P16 and P19 S1 waits are 100 min, not 105.

**Conclusion does not survive the corrected times.** Later S2 waits are about 45–75 min. At 14:00 both C1 and C2 are busy and four cases are queued, while S1 has one case in service and one waiting. That is a time-varying bottleneck, with S2 saturated in the afternoon. Calling S2 capacity-adequate and “starved, not overwhelmed” depends on the understated waits. Subperiod arrival counts are also wrong (for example, 08:00–10:00 has P01–P13, not P01–P10), so the station table’s WIP and utilization figures are not reliable. No numerical Little’s Law check is actually computed.

**Censoring.** For P15 S2 and P21, elapsed service is already about a typical service time, but the “central” estimate adds another full mean (about 60 and 55–57 min). A central censored service time is not elapsed plus an unconditional mean.

**Python.** The required script is not reproducible. P18–P22 rows have too few fields for the declared columns, so the frame will not build. Scenario code subtracts digits from case IDs, leaves a placeholder `manual` map commented “not real,” and never computes queue, WIP, or scenario metrics. The PM4Py calls sit in a bare `try/except` and do not print case-level service, waiting, arrival, or finish results.

What holds up is the labeled assumption list, the formal wait/service/sojourn definitions, correct handling of several fully observed S1 intervals, and reasonable resource bounds for the genuinely missing S1 starts on P07 and P15. That is not enough to carry the reconstruction.
