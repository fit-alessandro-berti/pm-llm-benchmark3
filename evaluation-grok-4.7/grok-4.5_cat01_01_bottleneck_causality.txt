**7.5**

The answer is a competent, well-structured causal and process assessment: the Simpson reversal is real, the arithmetic checks out, stratification is not treated as identification, and the 50% reassignment is correctly rejected as a confounded policy. It is not nearly flawless. Several graph and mechanism errors are concrete, and they matter for the estimands the task asks for.

The provisional verdict “mainly confounded” (68/100) is a defensible reading of the crude gap (Orion 21.96h versus Nova 15.24h, about +6.7h) against the within-complexity reversal (Low 7.50 vs 9.64, Medium 17.17 vs 19.38, High 31.57 vs 35.00). The complexity-standardized contrast is auditable. With weights 12/30, 10/30, and 8/30, \(0.4\cdot7.50+0.333\cdot17.17+0.267\cdot31.57\approx17.15\) and \(0.4\cdot9.64+0.333\cdot19.38+0.267\cdot35.00\approx19.65\), so about 2.5h in Orion’s favor. Section 4 correctly forbids reading either number as a causal effect. Word counts, required columns, five hypothesis types, a capacity phase, and a randomized medium-case pilot are all present. Crude rates match the case file (6/14 high, 13/14 night, 9/14 compliance, 4/14 rework).

The causal graph contains real mistakes.

- `quality_outcome -> case_completed` reverses time. The 30-day appeal indicator is defined after completion and can be affected by post-process factors. Completion can select whether quality is observed; quality cannot cause completion. `cycle_time -> case_completed` and `unobserved_case_risk -> case_completed` are coherent selection arrows; the quality arrow is not.
- `unobserved_reviewer_skill` never touches `resource_group` or `routing_rule`. The graph therefore says certification is independent of Orion, while the text’s main beneficial mechanism is that Orion has more certified reviewers. The prose also claims arrows “from group to … quality,” and no such edge exists.
- `unobserved_case_risk` does not point into `routing_rule`. The residual-confounding story in Sections 3 and 8 is missing from the graph. `complexity -> unobserved_case_risk` also runs the wrong way if coded complexity is a coarse proxy for pre-existing difficulty.
- Shift is only downstream (`routing_rule -> shift`, `resource_group -> shift`, then `shift -> arrival_load`). That encodes shift and load as mediators. Section 3 correctly says shift may instead be a pre-assignment confounder. Those two choices imply opposite conditioning strategies for a total effect, and the graph note treats “Orion–night coupling” as a supported fact rather than a contested direction. Arrival load, as recorded, behaves like an arrival-state covariate, not a consequence of the group label.

The mechanism analysis is uneven in a way that affects the verdict. Within every complexity stratum the Orion advantage is almost entirely `wait_before_review_h`, not `review_service_h`: about −3.3h of wait versus −0.1h of service at High, −1.9h versus −0.2h at Medium, and −1.6h versus −0.4h at Low. Section 4 says this. Hypothesis 2 then cites shorter waits as support for a skill effect on service and rework. Certified skill is a poor explanation of a queue gap. The same table uses Orion’s long absolute waits (17–21h) as support for a harmful Orion effect, even though the two Nova high cases wait 22.6h and 22.9h. The day-only falsification test is not a test: Orion has one day case, and a pooled day contrast would remix complexity.

A further process identity is missed. `cycle_time_h - wait_before_review_h - review_service_h` is about 0.8h on the plain path, about 2.2h when compliance is required, and about 2.3h (no compliance) or 3.6–3.7h (with compliance) when rework is present. Request-info delay sits outside both measured durations. “Rework mediates cycle via extra review service” is therefore incomplete, and `compliance_performed -> cycle_time` is doing real work the write-up never measures.

Smaller faults: “front-door” is used for a wait-versus-service split that does not meet the front-door criterion; the day-versus-night row reports a complexity stratification rather than a shift comparison; uncertainty 5 names two remedies after asking for one; the code leaves `Counter`, `defaultdict`, and `numpy` unused and builds the log by hand instead of `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`, though `discover_dfg` on trace order is a legitimate frequency DFG and the diagnostics requested are otherwise implemented, including support warnings.

The recommendation is still the right operational one: do not move half of Orion’s book, measure open cases and atomic times, add queue or compliance capacity, and randomize only where overlap exists. Phase 2 still under-specifies whether a medium night case randomized to Nova carries its shift into a day-staffed group. That is the same shift-versus-group bundle the graph failed to pin down.
