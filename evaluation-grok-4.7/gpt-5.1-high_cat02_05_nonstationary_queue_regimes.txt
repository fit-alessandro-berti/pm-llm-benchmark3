The analysis is complete, numerically mostly careful, and sound on the main operating question: outage recovery is separated from steady throughput, mix, staffing, and backlog are distinguished, and Little’s Law is withheld where the boundary conditions fail. It is still short of a high score because several quantitative claims and cross-checks are wrong or internally inconsistent.

The pooled high-mix illustration is factually off. Over 2026-04-20 through 2026-05-03, arrivals are 1,335 and completions are 1,247, a surplus of about 88 cases (roughly +6 per day). Weekdays alone are about 114 arrivals per day against about 107 completions. That window does not look like “arrivals ≈ completions.” The pre/post sign change is real (about +106 cases before 2026-04-29, about −18 after), but pooling attenuates the deficit; it does not make the period look balanced. That was the section’s central quantitative comparison.

The hourly vectors contradict `peak_08_10_pct`, and the audit never checks them against each other. With bins starting at 07:00, 08:00–09:59 is the second and third hours: about 17% on 2026-04-07 versus a reported 31, about 18% on 2026-04-15 versus 32, about 10% on 2026-04-21 versus 47, and about 12% on 2026-04-25 versus 22. The 2026-04-21 mass sits at 10:00–11:00 and 13:00–14:00. Burstiness measured from the vectors and “morning peak concentration” measured from the daily column are not the same evidence. A shifted 09:00–10:59 window fits three days and still fails 2026-04-21, so the conflict is not a simple off-by-one.

Regime R4’s headline rates repeat the composition error the essay criticizes. The four-day averages of about 75 arrivals and 82 completions per day mix Thursday–Friday catch-up (211 arrivals, 241 completions) with a balanced weekend (87 and 87). The same split is done for R5 and R6 and omitted for the recovery regime.

The alternative segmentation does not match its own rules or the code. The prose requires `|arrivals − completed| ≤ 5` and `mean_wip < 30` for balance, and `< −5` for draining. 2026-04-29 is −5 with WIP 43, so it meets neither rule, yet it is called marginally draining. The code drops the WIP condition and labels that day roughly balanced, and it also keeps the outage day out of the building class while the prose puts it in both. The printed primary-regime means also recombine weekdays and weekends inside R5 and R6, so the script does not reproduce the split estimates in the narrative.

Dispersion is interpreted too loosely. A Fano factor near 3 on 2026-04-07 is the expected signature of a smooth diurnal rate; it does not, by itself, show extra-Poisson burstiness or non-stationarity. The factor near 9 on 2026-04-21, with isolated spikes, is the stronger burst evidence. Coefficient of variation is computed and never discussed. The jump heuristic flags ordinary Monday/weekend WIP swings along with the real breaks, and that confounding is left unstated.

Smaller problems accumulate. Implied flow times are rounded upward past the arithmetic: 20.3/0.139 is about 146 minutes, not 150; 44/0.14 is about 314, not 320. Calling the transient ratios upper bounds is not justified. Averages of daily medians and of `complex_pct` are unweighted, which matters once completion volume ranges from 58 to 129. R2 (two days) and R6 are both labeled high confidence even though the intervention time inside 2026-04-29 is unknown and that day’s WIP (43) still looks like the prior regime. The coding-change caveat is stated once, then the recommendations treat the complex-share jump as a real mix shift requiring specialists and separate queues. The finite-window Little’s Law discussion is directionally right and correctly refuses the outage and the growth phase, but it never compares implied sojourn with median service, so the waiting-time gap stays implicit.

Format, totals (2,519 arrivals, 2,409 completions), the main regime arithmetic, the Fano arithmetic, the decision not to treat median service as flow time, and the “6 FTE looks fine in the pool” policy trap are all in good shape. Those strengths support a competent analysis. The missed contradiction, the false pooled balance, and the prose/code segmentation mismatches are large enough that the result is not nearly flawless.
