The analysis is strong on regime structure, outage-versus-recovery separation, and Little’s Law restraint, but repeated net-flow sign errors and one backwards dispersion argument keep it short of nearly flawless.

The audit is careful and mostly exact. Scheduled-hour rates, the +5 / +47 / −30 / +106 / −18 path to a cumulative +110, arrival-weighted mix (33.8%, 22.5% vs 43.9%, 20.9% vs 44.6%), equal-staff weekday contrasts, and the outage-plus-recovery pool (99.7 completions/day hiding 58 then 120.5) all check out. Refusing to treat median handling time as mean flow time, withholding utilization without \(E[S]\), and keeping April 29 as a mixed transition are the right queueing judgments. The hourly-vector versus `peak_08_10_pct` break (31% vs 16.8%, 47% vs 9.6%) is a real data defect, and the Fano, CV, and lag figures that were recomputed match the text. The single code block implements the required tables, dispersion and lag indicators, a non-definitive one-split heuristic, restricted Little’s Law windows, and a clear PM4Py comment that these aggregates are not an event log.

The serious defect is the sign of net flow wherever completions exceed arrivals. Net is \(A-C\) in the accumulating rows (+0.6, +13.4, +3.9) and in the code (`arrivals - completed`), but the same quantity is written as an unsigned positive in the drainage rows: recovery “net 15/day” instead of −15, April 29 “Net 5” instead of −5, April 30–May 1 “6.5” instead of −6.5, and the intervention block “Net/day 6.0” instead of −6.0. That last table is the staffing comparison. Read against the +13.4 row, 6.0 says accumulation slowed, while 115 arrivals and 121 completions say the flow reversed. Section 6 repeats the slip (“Net 13”, “net 5”) inside a matrix whose other cells use signed \(A-C\). Nearby prose often says “drainage,” so the conclusion is recoverable, but the reported comparison numbers are internally inconsistent and directionally wrong.

A second logical slip is in the daily Fano discussion. Weekday arrival Fano ≈ 0.63 is correctly computed with population variance, and the warning that a daily total can look calm while April 21 is bursty within the day is right. The further claim that mixing regimes blocks an underdispersion reading is backwards: low-mix, outage, recovery, and high-mix weekdays have different means, so pooling them inflates variance. A pooled weekday Fano still below 1 is stronger within-regime evidence, not weaker. For an inhomogeneous Poisson process the daily total remains Poisson, so this daily Fano is about the integrated count, not the hourly profile. Those two statements should have been separated.

Smaller gaps: WIP/FTE being higher on weekends is a rescaling, not a Simpson reversal (the outage/recovery cancellation and the staff–WIP confounding are the real ones); outage-day service quantiles are taken from the 58 completed cases with no selection caveat; several \(n=1\) or \(n=2\) regimes are still quoted to two decimals. Confidence labels limit the damage. The implied open-hour times that are shown (about 156, 147, 237, and 301 minutes) are arithmetically consistent and correctly barred from being turned into waiting time by subtracting the service median.
