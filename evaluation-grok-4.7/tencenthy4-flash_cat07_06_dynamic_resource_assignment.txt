The policy covers all seven required sections, respects most hard skill and overtime rules, and gives a usable worker-facing explanation plus a real PM4Py audit sketch. It is not reliable as a complete assignment or as a scoring procedure.

The assignment table omits C009. The later check says C009 is one of two documented diagnosis-training cases and that this reaches 2 of 9 normal/low cases (22%), but it never names the performer, supervisor, trade-off, or risk. The cap claim that R6 receives exactly three cases is only consistent if C009 goes to R1, which is an inference, not a stated assignment. The required C001–C016 table is therefore incomplete, and the training-quota claim is not fully supported by the table.

The scoring model does not do what it claims. `urgency_score` depends only on the case, so adding it to every candidate cannot change which resource wins. Skill, continuity, fairness, and training are also on incompatible scales, so the stated weights do not balance those factors. Expected handling time is never defined, so the hours filter is not operational. R5, with 5.0 hours and medium overtime risk, still receives two high-priority cases plus C010.

The “at least 20% training” rule is labeled a hard safety constraint, but the greedy algorithm has no batch-level check or repair to enforce it. The same section folds the four-assignment cap and overtime rule into safety/compliance, which blurs the separation the question asked for. Supervisor load is outside the cap: R3 keeps fragile C007, takes C013, and is the supervisor for C003 and the implied C009. The code also disagrees with the prose: high-overtime urgent work is allowed without checking that no safer alternative exists, skill-1 approval preparation is rejected outright, and there is no search over supervised pairs.

C003 is the weakest case decision. It creates a third reassignment and gives a repeated diagnosis loop to a skill-1 performer who already holds two urgent cases. That stacks both historical risk patterns the answer itself cites. The other choices are mostly coherent: security and approval rules hold, high-overtime resources R2, R4, and R9 get no new work, C007 and C016 stay with competent owners, and Eva/Jonas are correctly blocked from C015 because their security skill is 0.
