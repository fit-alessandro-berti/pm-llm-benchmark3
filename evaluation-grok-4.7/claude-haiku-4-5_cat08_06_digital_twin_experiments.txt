5.4

The write-up covers every required section and does treat demand as seasonal, resources as calendared, and objections as unobserved. It is not a sound simulation design. Quantitative errors, control-flow mistakes, and a Python skeleton that misses the required PM4Py contract are large enough that the plan would mis-size capacity and mis-rank interventions.

The process state cannot express the model it claims to simulate. A case has one `current_activity`, and each resource has one `current_case`, while the tree’s `+` block runs planning, fire, and environmental review concurrently and the pools are multi-server (8 planners, 5 clerks, 4 inspectors). Join semantics are also misplaced: the AND-join is before public notice, public notice is before inspection, and legal check is after decision drafting. The prose instead blocks decision drafting on a flat predecessor list and never models applicant appeal, which the brief explicitly includes.

Fire capacity is wrong by a wide margin. “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is encoded as 24 hours/week. Two FTE is on the order of 80 person-hours/week, merely restricted to those weekdays. That error alone invents a fire bottleneck. Site inspection is also given as about 30 minutes despite a stated median service time of 1 hour plus travel. Statutory public-notice time and applicant delay are paused on holidays along with staff, although those clocks are calendar time.

The arrival model contradicts itself. Section 3 sets λ to the overall mean of about 57.8 cases/day, then applies 1.0× for six months and 1.7× for six months. Those multipliers average 1.35, so the twin generates about 35% too many cases. The estimation code normalizes month and weekday factors to a mean of 1; the assumptions do not. Architect clustering is described both as 3–5% of arrivals and as the mechanism for 31% of volume, without stating whether cluster events replace or add to the Poisson process.

The objection sub-model does not match the only data available. A latent 15% flag plus an added Lognormal(μ=1.1, σ=0.5) extension has median about 3 days and 90th percentile about 5.7 days, not the stated “p90 ~12,” and it cannot produce the empirical public-notice 90th percentile of 60 days. That rate is also absent from the sensitivity list. Repeated activities are treated as rework, so the designed completeness loop is counted as failure. Routing is declared deterministic by case type, then also attribute-driven; the sample log already shows a New residential case with a legal check, which the type rules would skip. Right-censored open cases in an 18-month log are ignored, so long complex traces will bias cycle times downward. A hard cap of three rework loops further truncates the tail the study is supposed to protect.

The experiment table is not a factorial, despite that label. Stress scenarios are not crossed with D1–D10, so the later robustness gate is not identified by the design. Effect sizes are not hypotheses: D1 is credited with an 8-day median cut although missing-document cases are only 19% of volume; D10’s 25-day median and 50-day p90 cuts are implausible for a mix whose tail is commercial and complex-variance work that D1–D4 barely touch; D2 is said to increase inspector utilization while removing inspection load. Stress-1’s tidy +8 days at 1.3× demand ignores queueing nonlinearity and is said to hit every policy equally, including policies that add or remove capacity. SLAs are invented and then called a council agreement. Interventions are ranked with day-savings and euro thresholds before any calibrated run, which violates the brief’s bar on claims from an unvalidated model. D7’s reserved slots for the largest architects are scored as a quick win with no fairness penalty.

Warm-up is the wrong concept for this system. Four weeks cannot fill queues whose complex cases last 140–260 days, and a seasonal process has no steady state to reach. The runner then generates arrivals only after the warm-up window, so the warm-up is empty, and collected cycle times are not filtered. Ten replications use a normal 1.96 interval instead of t with 9 degrees of freedom. Common random numbers are specified with one shared seed and, a few paragraphs earlier, with `42 + 10*exp_id + rep_id`. The skeleton implements the second scheme.

Replay of 500 extracted cases cannot validate queues, because waiting is produced by the rest of the workload. Odd/even-month holdout leaks long cases across months and breaks seasonality. Utilization checks compare event counts with busy time. Bottleneck targets are the same medians used as inputs, so matching them is calibration. “MAE < 10%” and “±50% CI” coverage mix prediction intervals with parameter intervals.

The skeleton does not do what section 8 requires. It never imports PM4Py, never calls `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, or case-arrival average, and it never builds a performance DFG. The earlier pseudocode uses `case_id_key` and `resource_key`, which are not `format_dataframe` parameters (`case_id` is), treats `discover_dfg` as a single graph, and calls `get_variants_as_tuples(...).value_counts()`. The claim that `parse_process_tree` expects a Petri-net format is false; `->`, `X`, `+`, `*`, and `tau` are process-tree syntax. Inter-event gaps are stored as waiting time even though, with one timestamp, they mix wait and service. Service is then added with `timedelta` and a dummy lognormal, ignoring calendars. No arrival generator, router, rework handler, or cross-case dependency is actually executed.

What is usable is the boundary list, the separation of applicant delay from staff service, the statement that objections are latent, and the checklist of metrics. Those do not repair the capacity arithmetic, the non-stationary rate, the parallel state, the experiment claims, or the PM4Py contract.
