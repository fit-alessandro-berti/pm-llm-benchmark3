7.5

The write-up is strong: required sections are present, most primary statistics check out, logging batches are separated from physical batches, and uncertainty is usually labeled. It is not nearly flawless.

Verified figures include G1 wait 4.25 and 400% naive load, G2 waits 12.25 versus reconstructed 23.0 and service 17.5 versus 6.75 (+159%), G3 wait 19.5 and service 16.75, G5 peak concurrency 4 against a cap of 3 and 70/90 = 77.8% pool load, G6 mean wait 724/7 = 103.4 with zero service, G7 mean wait −4.2, and G8 mean wait 48.2. The G6 identity \(W_{obs}=W_{true}+S_{true}+D_{defer}\), \(S_{obs}=0\) is the right mechanism. PM4Py category predictions at 60 s / 900 s and minimum sizes 2 and 5 match the temporal patterns. G5’s 8-minute four-way overlap surviving ±1 minute censoring is correctly argued.

Material problems:

- G8’s split is wrong. Subtracting Q01’s 38 minutes from every wait and calling the mean remainder 10.2 “queue congestion” and ~75% “downtime” mis-allocates a constant. With the safety window ending at 15:35, downtime exposure is 35, 33, 31, 30, and 27 minutes (mean 31.2, about 65% of 48.2) and post-return wait is 3, 10, 17, 24, and 31 minutes (mean 17.0), not 0, 5, 10, 16, and 20.
- G7’s censoring contradicts itself: “[13:58, 14:15]” is 17 minutes, not “≤15,” and that window is then used to justify a ≤3 minute serial mean. Flooring a displayed 14:15 completion gives \([14:15, 14:30)\), not \([14:15, 14:20)\). The 14:20 bound comes only from `recorded_at`.
- Bias language is inconsistent in the same table. Service uses relative error (+159%, ≥+139%), while utilization puts the naive level itself in parentheses (259%, 239%, 400%, 500%) and calls that the bias.
- “6–8 min inter-completion spacing” for 10:08, 10:14, 10:21, 10:27 is false; those gaps are 6, 7, and 6. The 8 minutes is S01’s own start-to-complete duration.
- “Standard shift end” is used as G6 evidence. The prompt never establishes that 17:00 is end of shift. “Zero throughput for 2.5 hrs” is also loose: 14:05 to 17:00 is 2 hours 55 minutes. “Violates Little’s Law” misnames a utilization–delay contradiction; \(W=W_q+S\) still holds as 103.4 = 103.4 + 0.
- The required script is unlikely to run on PM4Py as documented. `discover_batches` takes a `parameters` dict, not `merge_distance` / `min_batch_size` keywords, and the loop unpacks a 3-tuple `((activity, resource), count, type_dict)` that the usual return value does not have. Diagnostics design is otherwise careful: zero-duration G6 and negative-wait G7 are retained, overlap, lag, 15-minute grid, and precision/format mismatch are computed, and the sensitivity grid is the right one.

Those execution and decomposition errors keep it well below a near-flawless score, even though the cluster ranking, G5 headcount contradiction, and exclusion adjudication are process-mining work of a high standard.
