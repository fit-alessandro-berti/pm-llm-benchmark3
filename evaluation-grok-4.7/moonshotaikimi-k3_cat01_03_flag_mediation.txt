7.5

The final write-up is complete, numerically careful, and causally serious: stage means match the timestamps, absent stages are not zero-filled, natural direct/indirect effects are rejected for the right cross-world and interference reasons, and P0–P4 are not collapsed into one “flag” treatment. It is not nearly flawless.

The policy arithmetic does not follow the stated mechanism. Escalation is correctly described as occurring only after review, yet P2 is scored as landing flagged cycle time near 7–10 hours by stripping review wait and service while leaving the senior-queue block in place. Removing mandatory review also removes most of that escalation path, so the expected cycle-time cell is inconsistent with sections 3 and 4.

Section 2 treats unflagged triage wait of 3.1h under high load versus 1.9h under normal load as support for displacement. That contrast is exactly the load variable; it does not separate interference from congestion. “Morning high-load clustering” is also overstated: two of the six high-load flagged cases arrive at 17:30 and 18:30.

“Single-world mediation formulas” are said to fail. Interventional effects are the single-world estimands; what actually fails is identification when queue state is an unmeasured, treatment-affected confounder. E4 further blends an always-reviewed principal stratum with an interventional indirect effect without separating them.

The pilot is concrete, but the guardrails (queue > 8, p90 > 4h, senior queue > 4) are unanchored, 12 week-clusters are thin for rare quality events, and restricting to medium complexity avoids the high-complexity escalation mass that dominates the observed 21.4h versus 9.2h gap. That limit is not stated. The regression-discontinuity row likewise does not say the current score gap (0.61 to 0.64) is an empty bandwidth.
