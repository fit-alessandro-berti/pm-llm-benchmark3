7.1

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, separates the conditional-on-activation estimand from a counterfactual “default if activated” estimand, and reconciles all 48,000 applications. The leakage table, competing-risk vocabulary, and temporal-split rationale are strong. It is still not a coherent study, because the primary estimand, the cohort labels, and the skeleton describe three different targets.

The primary endpoint is ill-specified. `activated_default90` and `activated_survived90` run on an activation-plus-90-day clock, while \(H=180\) runs from the prefix. A case can be activated, fully followed through \(\tau+H\), and still not have a resolved 90-day outcome. That case is neither rejected/withdrawn/transferred, nor either activated state, nor `no_qualifying_event_by_H`, nor censored under the stated rule that censoring requires follow-up to end. Accepted-but-not-activated is the same hole. Calling \(P(Z(H)=z\mid X)\) “equivalent” to the cumulative incidence is wrong once \(Z\) is undefined under censoring: the CIF keeps censored cases in the risk set; the conditional probability given an observed state does not.

Label maturity is defined three ways. Section 2 waits until activation + 90 + 35 days unless an earlier guarantee exists. Section 3 treats `label_available_at` at or before the as-of date as sufficient. The code requires both the 125-day buffer and a non-missing label, so an early feed is discarded and \(H\) is never used. Section 2 then assigns all 18,900 and 2,700 matured accounts to those activated states with no check that the composite event falls inside \(H\).

The censoring model is partly misspecified. Transfer linkage, partner identity, and “observed follow-up” are not prefix-time covariates, but they are listed as prefix information for IPCW. IPCW is also both the primary estimator and a sensitivity analysis. Conditional independent censoring is correctly called untestable; the rest of that paragraph is looser, treating observed facts as if they were testable assumptions.

Validation does not match the clocks. Training and validation are entirely before 1 July 2025, so `policy_era` and its interactions are constant in-sample and cannot be fit, while the only post-policy evidence is the locked test. That test runs through 31 December 2025 against an extraction date of 15 February 2026, so most test cases cannot complete 180 days, and many cannot complete activation plus 90 plus feed delay. Horizon metrics will be dominated by the censoring model. AUPRC is added without an IPCW or horizon definition. The rolling-origin label rule (“origin plus evaluation lag”) can admit labels that were unavailable at the training cutoff.

The skeleton contradicts the design. `get_prefixes_from_log` is computed and discarded; the feature table is a different filter that keeps only cases with exactly three events and no decision inside them, so early rejects, withdrawals, transfers, and accepts are silently removed instead of receiving the specified short prefix. The output target is a static cutoff label, with open and immature activated cases as classes rather than censored time-to-event records. `prior_30d_event_count` counts the case’s own earlier events, so it is essentially \(K-1\), not resource workload. Sorting is unstable despite the stated tie rule. DFG and variants are fit on the full post-decision log, including the test period. The leakage assert only checks column names, which those fields never enter.

The useful parts — the alternative conditional target, the 48,000 disposition table, the leakage decisions, and the warning about deployment changing activation and labels — are real. They do not repair the unidentified state, the infeasible test horizon, or code that would train on the wrong outcome.
