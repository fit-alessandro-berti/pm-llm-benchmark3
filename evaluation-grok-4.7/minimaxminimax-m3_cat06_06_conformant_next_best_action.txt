6.5

The final recommendation is in the right family: independent second-officer review stays mandatory, immediate execution and self-certification stay at the bottom, alternate screening runs in parallel and does not replace that review, and P-901 is treated as a real spillover with an S4-timing sensitivity. The six required sections, conformance labels, and A–G coverage are present.

The operational comparison that supports the ranking is not reliable. S4’s review is treated as about 5–15 minutes after a 35–65 minute wait, while the only stated review duration is 35–75 minutes and the 5–15 minute band is the later record-and-execute step. That compression makes S4 look only marginally slower than interrupting S2 and produces the claimed 5–45 minute deadline buffer. The same table puts execution at t=45–95 against a 90-minute deadline, which is a buffer of about −5 to +45 minutes, so the “likely meets, with a positive buffer” claim overstates the plan.

Delayed interruption is described as leaving P-901 with more slack. P-901’s 70-minute clock runs during the wait, so a later 45-minute interruption leaves less slack and can make breach much more likely than the stated +0.12–0.28, which applies to interrupting now. The final trigger also escalates to that interruption if screening confirms the fuzzy match. A confirming hit weakens the case for rushing execution and makes the spillover harder to justify.

A properly completed override is misstated in the final recommendation: once a Compliance Director has authorized it and a genuine incident identifier exists, that path is the permitted exception. The answer says that even then it would not displace the second-officer rule. Rejection remains correctly conditional, SAR non-disclosure is respected, and the primary plan is still a conformant second review, but the timing arithmetic, spillover direction, escalation trigger, and override statement are material errors.
