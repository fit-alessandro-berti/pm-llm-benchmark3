The completed sections are analytically strong but the submission is structurally incomplete, and a headline quantitative claim is wrong. Sections 6 and 7 are missing entirely, and section 5 stops after a single unfinished paragraph, so the required pilot (scope, comparison group, windows, segmentation, guardrails, feedback, rollback), the conditional recommendation, and the PM4Py sketch are all absent.

What is present covers the mechanism map, three differentiated scenarios (including East versus North/South), a leading/lagging indicator table, and more than six average-cycle-time blind spots, with fairness and learning treated seriously. Most handover arithmetic checks out (343 vs 359 events, 588.3 h vs 722.2 h, approval fallout 7.1% vs 12.2%).

The case-weighted cycle time is misstated as 7.2 days; (820×6.8 + 760×7.4 + 520×8.6) / 2100 = 7.46 days. The claim that handover waiting is only ~3.4% of cycle time, and thus the whole programme can move the median by only about ±1%, rests on that error, on summing frequencies × medians as if they were expected total wait, and on treating hour-scale waits and day-scale cycle times as commensurate without a working-time versus calendar-time distinction. Scaling all current tech utilisation (2.18 FTE) linearly with the 36 specialist referrals also treats local assessment and informal work as if it were the same load the shared queue would inherit. Erlang-C figures are internally consistent but presented with more precision than the M/M/c assumptions support.
