8.5

The memo is a strong, decision-ready synthesis: it unbundles Redesign R, refuses both blanket scale and blanket rollback, and ties the choice to segment effects, conformance deviations, cost-window truncation, and process-model quality. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated; all five metric conflicts are explained coherently; A–E are judged with conditions; and M1–M8 are ranked by how they would change a live decision, with sequencing and pre-committed rollout gates.

Deductions are for precision, not structure. “Gained … 1pp complaints” omits the sign and can be read as a complaint increase. “A third of volume” treats the pilot mix as the volume at stake before the national mix is known. “Scale what is proven” attributes simple-house gains specifically to coordinator ownership and merged scheduling, although those elements were never isolated from survey skipping and post-billing activation. Absolute-zero permit and billing gates also sit uneasily with the memo’s own logging-gap assumption, and several numeric cutoffs (85%, <2pp, +2 days) are asserted rather than justified. The rollback trigger limits the practical damage of those overclaims.
