The answer is a strong, internally careful conformance analysis: all twelve alignments are read correctly, model moves are not treated as observed events, zero-cost synchronous traces are separated from data-aware policy breaches, and raw edit cost is never used as the ranking key. Scope, label substitution, retries, silent routing, and external evidence are distinguished with appropriate uncertainty. The required sections, columns, and PM4Py/pandas separation of technical cost from business severity are present.

It is not nearly flawless. P3 is applied only to CL-604. CL-609 and CL-610 also paid without the applicable manual approval; an ineligible Auto Approve is not that approval. Scoring both as 4, and never naming P3, understates CL-610 in particular: mandatory fraud screening and manual approval are both absent after a bank change one hour before payment. Urgency language and triage priority 1 only partly repair that classification.

CL-608 is scored 2 even though the author’s own severity-3 definition covers an unresolved evidence gap that materially impairs oversight. A mutable text link is the only support for notice on a EUR 250,000 payment, so “plausibly satisfied” is ahead of the evidence. The same flattening appears in case triage, where that notice gap, the external-assessment gap, and the label mismatch share one priority after the portfolio has correctly separated them.

The code largely reconstructs the supplied moves, keeps unknown bank history unknown, and avoids turning cost or amount into a fake loss score. Two implementation choices are still unsafe for an audit script: CL-604’s real 10:03/10:20 times are mixed into an otherwise invented timeline, and CL-606’s offsets fabricate control times around a report that is correctly excluded from the log. Severity cells such as “4, urgent” and “2, provisional” also blur the required 1–5 scale.
