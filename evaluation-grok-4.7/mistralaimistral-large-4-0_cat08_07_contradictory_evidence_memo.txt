7.4

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision, covers every required section, and explains the five metric conflicts with real process-mining evidence. It is not nearly flawless: several claims are over-certain, one alternative is internally illogical, and the rollout gates do not match the evidence.

The recommendation correctly rejects national scale and full rollback, keeps the simple-house gains, and stops survey skipping and billing-before-activation. Fitness, precision, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal deviations, resource networks, and segment splits are all used. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated, and M1–M8 are ranked by whether they could change the decision.

The serious problems are these. Rollback is said to “leave” field-survey utilization at 52%. That utilization is an effect of skipping surveys; restoring the baseline should push survey work back up and second-visit load down. The apartment/old-building gate treats overall baseline p90 (31 days) as those segments’ baseline. Their baselines are not reported—only changes of +12 and +25 days—so the gate is invented. Section 6 then allows overall p90 to remain at the pilot’s worsened 38.5 days, which conflicts with the tighter memo gate. Business (8%) and rural (11%) cases never receive an operating rule. “E incorporating C” also leaves it unclear whether coordinators still own complex cases. Utilization is 93%, but nothing concrete reduces it to the 85% gate. “Proven,” “clear win,” and “process mining confirms the mechanism” overstate a non-comparable 6-week, mix-skewed pilot whose risk attributes are missing before week 3. Low precision is treated as proof the process is uncontrolled; a permissive model also inflates fitness, so high fitness is weak evidence of a clean simplified path. A p95 of 62 days inside a 6-week window is noted only as possible right-censoring and then dismissed, despite threatening the tail comparison. Alternative C is charged with possibly dropping merged validate/schedule, which C does not do. The hard activation-before-billing gate ignores billing delay and how much of the median gain it would return.

Notation such as “14.210.8” and unsigned “FTR 8pp” makes deltas hard to read, but the underlying figures are otherwise the right ones.
