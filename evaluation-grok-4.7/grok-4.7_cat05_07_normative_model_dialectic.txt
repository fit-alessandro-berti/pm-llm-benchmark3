The brief is unusually strong: POWL semantics, authority versus fitness, and the case outcomes are mostly well controlled. It is not nearly flawless. Several traceability and model-semantics errors are concrete, and they sit in the parts the task made decisive.

What holds. M0 is read correctly: one execution of each partial-order node, precedence rather than required overlap, both checks mandatory before the XOR, manual approval before activation on the approved child only, no loop and no resource view. CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 are kept apart. The prosecution actually concedes logging, relabeling, duplicates, and the missing information-request loop, and still isolates SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, expiry, and ineligible auto-approval. The defense actually concedes those breaches and the inability of practice to extend a lapsed instrument. SUP-715 is not misread as a CS-9 requester/approver breach. SUP-709 is not forced through a quality-before-reject rule the standard does not state. The population split (289 / 14 / 9, six August continuations, 2% to 29% notification missingness, 47 information requests) is used as population evidence rather than as a rate computed from the fifteen curated cases. The sliced languages correctly make SUP-703 fit the pilot alphabet and fail the guard, while baseline after 2026-07-31 rejects SUP-714. The zero-or-more loop puts the silent child first, which is the right PM4Py loop shape, and the limitation is stated. Reactivation is not smuggled in by silently skipping registration.

Defects that keep this below a near-flawless mark.

FD-7 is restated inaccurately. The directive requires daily monitoring and Quality_Review within two business days, and it caps spend until retrospective approval. It does not impose a two-business-day deadline on manual approval. Section 6, the overlay table, and `emergency_guard` treat that deadline as part of FD-7. A timely quality review, an intact cap, and a later retrospective approval would be condemned by the proposed norm and permitted by the instrument.

The revised POWL couples late quality to notification. `auto_route` and `emergency_route` leave quality inside the XOR child, and the parent orders that child before `Notify_Requester`. PD-3’s own justified pattern is sanctions, auto-approval, activation, notification, then quality within two business days. SUP-702 is the exemplar: quality on the next business day, CRM notification at +2 hours under a stable id. Inserting that notification at its recorded time puts it before quality, which this language rejects; leaving it out makes closure fail the mandatory notification edge. The task required awkward POWL limits to be stated. This one is not, even though it hits the exception the judgment accepts. The prose guard (“quality before closure”) and the code (quality before notification) also disagree.

The burden is stricter than the judgment, and the code is stricter than both. An exception is accepted only when every condition of the written authority is shown. Master-data validation before activation is a PD-3 condition and is unproved on SUP-702 and on the 289. The judgment still labels SUP-702 `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That lower confidence is noted, but `auto_guard` then fail-closes the same missing fact. The identity section admits that prospective fail-closed checks are not historical findings. The master-data control never receives that distinction, so the three layers do not decide the same case the same way.

SUP-710 is misdescribed in the defense brief and the matrix as “fresh sanctions and prior manual approval.” The trace contains a current `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before activation. The prior artifact is the quality certification. Section 5 later states the facts correctly, which shows the slip was not the holding, but the matrix is the evidence record and it is wrong.

SUP-704 is graded high confidence although the file gives initial spend of EUR 18,000, and the same answer lists total pre-approval spend as the fact that would reverse the exception. SUP-713 is high confidence while the answer itself treats mapping-approval timing as debatable. The SUP-708 judgment cell, “No transaction mitigates harm only,” does not say the proposition the brief actually defends.

Smaller modeling nits. `overlay_in_force` compares timestamp strings with no stated format precondition. FD-7’s own `enabled_routes` includes auto-approval and relies on a note that the real authority is overlapping PD-3. Conflict disclosure is specified for SUP-715 and then absent from the executable guards, so the code as written certifies that case.

The classifications of SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-708, SUP-709, SUP-711, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are defensible, and the rejection lists are genuine rather than a split. The score stops at 8.2 because the controlling emergency rule is overstated, the endorsed pilot trace does not fit the proposed language, and the burden, the judgment, and the guard disagree on master-data validation.
