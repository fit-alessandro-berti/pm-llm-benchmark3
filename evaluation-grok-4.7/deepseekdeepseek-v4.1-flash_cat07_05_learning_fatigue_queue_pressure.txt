6.5

The required structure is complete and the constraints are respected: fatigue is not treated as the sole cause, early complexity and late queue pressure are both recognized, punishment from raw late-shift error rates is rejected, and quality checks are not dropped to raise throughput. The cited figures match the tables, and the mechanism, intervention, ethics, and dashboard sections are all present.

The core synthesis still overclaims. Most of the service-time drop, from 18.8 to 12.9 minutes, occurs while mean queue length falls from 46 to 31 and complexity falls from 0.72 to 0.50. The later move from 12.9 to 10.8 minutes is small even as queue jumps to 62 and then 88. Queue pressure is well supported by the WIP cross-section and by the late error spike; it is not a strong explanation of the overall decline. The closing claim that service time is “driven strongly” by warm-up is also ahead of the evidence: sequence position, complexity, priority, and queue move together, and no within-resource, complexity-adjusted result is shown.

Seniors are described as quality-stable even under high WIP. Their later error rate rises from 2.2% at low WIP to 4.9% at high WIP. That is a smaller degradation than for other groups, not stability. “Early-shift errors” also blurs the Early shift with the first events of any shift. Positions 1–5 have a 3.4% error rate, above the mid-shift trough.

The analysis code would not do what it claims. It filters a `wip_band` column that is never created; `filter_event_attribute_values` defaults to case-level retention, so it would not stratify events by WIP; `discover_batches` is called with keyword arguments that do not match its parameters-dict API; and the time filter is one arbitrary calendar day. The dashboard also alerts when service-time p90 rises, which is the opposite of the observed speed-up with rising errors. Intervention 2 is credited with improving early error rates even though the error problem is concentrated later and under high WIP.
