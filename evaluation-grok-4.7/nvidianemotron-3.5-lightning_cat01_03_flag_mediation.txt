The write-up has the right section skeleton and a defensible preference for interventional or separable effects over natural direct and indirect effects, given shared queues and exposure-induced confounding. That conceptual frame does not survive the data.

Flag counts are wrong: the file has 10 flagged and 10 unflagged cases. R20 is unflagged and is treated as flagged, so the headline table uses n = 11 vs 9. Cycle-time medians are therefore wrong (reported 1152 vs 318 minutes; correctly computed, about 987 vs 345). Triage-service and handoff figures are also off, including R06 service time and R18’s cycle time. Section 4 repeats false support: manual review is 10/10 vs 4/10, not 11/11 vs 2/9; escalation is 6/10 vs 2/10, not 7/11 vs 1/9; high complexity is 5/10 vs 3/10, not 6/11 vs 2/9. Resource medians (T1 vs T4) are likewise incorrect.

Estimands miss the brief. T2’s contrast is arrival-to-triage-done, but the outcome is triage service only. T5 and T6 are effects on waiting and escalation, not effects mediated by them. “Set the flag to zero” is not separated from changing post-flag routing. The graph omits `cycle_time` and `decision_outcome`, and the process diagram is garbled. The within-score design cites a bin with essentially no overlap. The pilot randomizes individuals despite the interference the text itself flags, and the “−30% to −50%” cycle-time claim is unsupported.

The Python block is truncated at `pd.read_csv(pd`, so it neither runs nor does any PM4Py step, and several embedded rows drop timestamps and shift columns. Sections 8 and 9 are absent.
