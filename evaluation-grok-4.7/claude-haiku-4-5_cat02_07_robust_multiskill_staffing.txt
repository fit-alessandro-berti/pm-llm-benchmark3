The write-up covers every required section and states several mechanisms correctly (parallel critical path as max-branch plus 8 minutes, sequential sum without that coordination delay, one preemption, 10% remaining-time penalty, 7/5/5 handover-setup rules, SLA clocks, and a staged shadow-mode pilot). Those pieces do not survive the quantitative core. The capacity model, scenario arithmetic, skill rules, comparison table, and code contradict each other and the given data, so the recommendation is not an audited result.

Capacity math is not auditable. Case-type totals are printed as 10.34, 10.66, 8.31, 8.25, and 4.98, which sum to 42.54 rather than 42; the block rows themselves sum to about 9.84 M, 11.06 E, and 7.86 S. Weighted means are wrong: electrical is 32.82, not 32.4; software is 38.5, not 39.5; validation is 15.74, not 15.82. U4 means are also wrong (mechanical 29.6 not 30.4, electrical 37.14 not 37.8, software 44.5 not 45.5), and intake/validation tails are doubled even though U4 applies only to diagnostic activities. The displayed electrical load product does not equal its own factors: 17.275 × 32.4 is about 560, not 616.4. Every skill-capacity identity is false (mechanical terms sum to about 1431, not 1077; electrical to about 1579, not 1224; software to about 981, not 714; intake to about 2367, not 2541).

Two structural choices dominate the bottleneck story and are mishandled. ME/ES still consume a full service on every branch; the 0.5 factors understate resource-minutes by roughly a quarter and confuse critical-path time with offered load. Summing each person’s whole shift into every skill they can perform, then reporting intake 11%, mechanical 35%, electrical 50%, software 60%, and validation 27% together, treats mutually exclusive uses of the same minutes as simultaneous capacity. There is no time-block assignment despite the shift pattern being the actual constraint: before 10:00 only Ada, Ben, Chen, and Farah are present; after 16:00 software is essentially Emil; 18:00–20:00 is Emil alone. Noon breaks, Farah’s 12:00 exit, multipliers on the assigned resource, handover, setup, coordination, and the 30-minute overtime cap never enter the load.

Scenario scaling repeats the problem. U1 morning volume is 7×1.7 + 9×1.7, so the day is about 53 cases, not 70. The written expression 42 − 16 + 11.9 + 15.3 + (12+10+6+3) is not 70 either, and the intake increment (11.2 × 6.96 ≈ 78) is stated as 37. Applying 70/42 ≈ 1.67 to the whole day double-counts the unscaled afternoon and then feeds U5. U2 simultaneously says demand doubles, others are scaled down, net +4 E and +2 ES, 48 cases, and “+50%,” while the added minutes use the ordinary electrical midpoint and another 0.5 on ES. U3 removes Chen’s full shift from electrical and from software at once. The later claim that Chen is 33% of software capacity and that remaining capacity is about 750 versus a 714 base does not match 360/714 or 714 − 360.

Policies are not the three policies requested. Strict specialization and “full pooling” both drop qualified people: Ada (mechanical 1.00), Chen (electrical 1.00), and Farah (mechanical and electrical 1.40) are missing from work they can do. Farah is later called not multi-skilled; she is the only person with every skill, and the natural sequential ME/ES resource who avoids the team-boundary penalty. Gamma then sends work to unqualified people (Ben or Emil on skills marked “-”, Ada on software) under an unlabeled cross-training assumption. Percent reserves are not a dispatch rule, overflow conflicts with the later U3 claim that Ada and Ben stay idle, and the Beta-lite fallback allows two preemptions although a case may be preempted at most once. Objective A omits P3; neither stated objective is ever scored. On the paper’s own U1 numbers Beta has better delay and P1 attainment than Gamma, yet the synthesis cites “80% vs 82%” as favoring Gamma.

Section 4 is false precision. Delays, Ginis, handover percentages, and SLA rates are not derived from the load model or the code, have no intervals, and conflict internally: U3 software is “unbounded” and also a 250-minute mean; 68% P1 attainment is said to miss 10–12 P1 cases when the paper’s own P1 volume is about 5–12; U5 still uses 5.4 P1 cases after a surge; 22% handover is converted to 527 minutes on the wrong base. P3 is mostly absent. “Observation” is used for speculative effects.

The Python does not implement the comparison. `run()` never calls the three dispatchers; every policy assigns Intake to Ada, one diagnostic activity to Ben, and Validation to Ada on a single global clock, ignoring arrivals, shifts, breaks, contention, U2, the IT freeze, setup, parallel branches, and preemption (`TODO`). `get_service_time('M'|'ME'|...)` is a KeyError against the service table, and complexity can be applied twice. U2 is absent from the scenario loop. There are no repeated seeds. `pm4py.get_service_time` is never called, and `format_dataframe` is not given the start timestamp, which is the exact service-versus-waiting pitfall the task requires to be labeled. A non-running stub would be acceptable only if every omitted mechanism were identified; “simplified assignment” does not do that. Section 7’s attribute list and drift ideas are the strongest process-mining material, but they describe a log the simulator does not emit.

An extra “Conclusions” heading also breaks the required heading set. A hybrid with explicit overflow and preemption thresholds is a plausible policy shape, but this document does not establish it.
