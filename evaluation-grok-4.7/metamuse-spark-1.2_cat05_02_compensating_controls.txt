7.4

The case judgments are mostly disciplined and avoid the obvious traps (same-user is not automatically a failure; distinct users are not automatically success; a clean outcome is not a sufficient control; AP-205 is a prevented loss). The objective split, the settlement-versus-release timing model, and the AP-202 / AP-204 / AP-208 / AP-209 calls are the strongest parts. The score is held down by contradictions, a few factual slips, and a code section that does not do what it claims.

The equivalence test conflicts with itself. Section 2 treats a complete ED-1 package as fully equivalent to four-eyes. Section 4 then says AP-207’s post-release, pre-settlement ratification is hybrid and not fully equivalent. AP-207 cannot be both. ED-1 also does not restore four-eyes; it is a conditional permission. “Fully equivalent” overstates it. The coverage rule is additionally broken on the page: “Clean AV-2 pass  proof of no defect” drops the negation, and the same missing operator leaves “unresolved independence failed” and “Distinct IDs independence” unreadable. Elsewhere the text correctly limits AV-2, so this is a drafting failure in the test definition rather than a consistent misconception.

AP-201’s audit timing is misstated. Release was 4 May 08:06 and audit was 5 May 09:00, so it was not the “same business day.” The problem’s 25 hours is measured from release; settlement was 10:00, which is about 23 hours before the audit. Treating recall as already closed, and corrective capacity as low, ignores that reference-point ambiguity. The independent pre-settlement review is also discounted harder than the stated “reviewed” fact supports. OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET is still a reasonable label.

AP-206’s classification is weaker than its own evidence. Independent approval occurred before release, AV-2 passed, amount, vendor age, and bank history fit, and the audit fell inside both 24 hours and one business day. The two-minute token gap makes ED-1’s permission indeterminate; it does not make corrective capacity conditional on that timestamp. INDETERMINATE, while “leaning” tolerable, ducks the distinction the answer itself draws. AP-208’s failure call is right, but “vendor only 8 months” is placed with the ED-1 breaches even though eight months clears the 180-day condition. The explicit misses are amount, bank change, unavailable validation, post-settlement ratification, and late audit — five conditions, not four. L3 is never evidenced for the managers treated as MG-4, yet AP-203 and AP-207 are OBJECTIVE_MET at high confidence.

Counterfactuals are directionally useful, then over-specific. “Only four-eyes” would have stopped AP-202 is too strong; a non-overridable bank-change block or an independent override holder could have stopped it. The AP-204 illustration assigns fabricated-receipt detection to AV-2, which by definition does not check receipt or commercial substance. Several probabilities are labeled illustrative, but 90%, 5–15%, and 70–90% still pretend to a precision the facts do not support. m11’s review is called a full review although the facts only say “approved.”

The code reconstructs the log, parses UTC timestamps, calls `format_dataframe` and `filter_four_eyes_principle`, and separates AP-205, but it does not join the case-facts frame to the log despite printing “Case facts joined.” Manager timing is hardcoded, then echoed back as a “check.” AP-204 and AP-209 are flagged “same-user not ED-1 permitted” even though the releaser is u13. Vendor ages of 30 and 999 days are invented. The set-difference does not handle both four-eyes return conventions; it assumes a conforming return, so the version-robustness claim is false. An unguarded `import pm4py` also means the manual fallback never runs if the library is absent. The verdict disclaimer is clear and appropriate.
