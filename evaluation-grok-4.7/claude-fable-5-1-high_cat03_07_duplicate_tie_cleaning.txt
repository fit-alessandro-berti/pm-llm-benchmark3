7.5

The design is careful and mostly coherent: the six-way taxonomy is sound, e05 and e14 survive the recommended pipelines, ingestion is demoted to a tie-break, original timestamps are retained, and the partial-payment and `have_common_object` argument is right. It is not nearly flawless. Several required figures and the ordering story disagree with the pipeline that is actually specified.

The conservative counts 17 → 15 events and 44 → 39 E2O relations are right, as are evidence-based 13 events / 35 relations and library merge 10 events / 28 relations. The label claim is not: distinct activities go from 10 to 13, not 12. Relabeling e09, e07, and e15 adds three labels and retires none, because `Dispatch Package`, `Pack Package`, and `Capture Payment` still have events.

The required decision table misstates canonical identity on the ambiguous rows. For e06/e07 scenario A, and for both readings of e13/e15 and e14/e15, more than one event is kept, or e15 is dropped rather than folded into e13 or e14. The “preserved canonical eid” cells still say e06, e13, and e14. That column is wrong for those scenarios. “Singletons at their timestamps” is also wrong for e10 and e12: they share a second with other activities and are merely singleton activity groups.

Section 3 over-promotes assumptions into semantic edges. Known facts force e12 ≺ e13, e09 ≺ e10, and e15 after the payment messages. They do not force e08 ≺ e09 or invoice issuance ≺ authorization. Those are template choices. The code then makes them hard edges through an Order lifecycle while the prose files pick ≺ pack and invoice ≺ payment under R1. Because every event carries O1, that Order template turns almost every cross-activity tie into an R3 “about the world” edge. R4 makes the opposite default: PSP outranks ERP, so without the template e12 would be placed before e11. That conflict is never discussed. `concurrent_with` uses missing direct edges, so a pair with only a transitive path is recorded as concurrent. The 12:00 ingestion illustration also puts e13 before e14 even though both have `ingested_at` 12:00:03.

The sensitivity narrative partly undoes the earlier caution. Two picks of I1 and I2 are real; “Pick → Pick, legitimately I1 then I2” treats the R4 serialization as business order. Pattern 5 marks `Issue Invoice ⟲` on V1. In eid order and in ingestion order the two issue events are not consecutive: payments sit between them. The real raw artifact is a second issue plus, under ingestion order, Capture → Issue Invoice, which the text does mention only as an extra.

The implementation does not do what Sections 4–5 and 8 say it does. Authority maps name `location`, `device_id`, `invoice_total`, `gl_account`, and similar fields that are not columns. The columns that actually differ (`payload_hash`, `source_event_key`, `source_seq`) default to the canonical row, so BILLING and SCANNER values become discarded conflicts. Scenario B therefore does not keep ERP status attributes on e06; `invoice_balance` is also absent, so dropping e15 does not move a balance onto V1. `merge_verified_pairs` always unions relations and never checks the identical-or-subset rule. Provenance for A, B, and C is appended to shared lists without `scenario_id`, so the audit log cannot be reversed per scenario. Full reconstruction to a raw OCEL is claimed anyway. The justified `ocel_drop_duplicates` path never runs on this log; the call that runs is the whole-log diagnostic. `filter_ocel_events` is not a reliable top-level PM4Py entry point, and `len(discover_objects_graph(...))` counts nodes if the return value is a graph, so that sensitivity field does not measure interaction weight.

The C′ relation-stripping account (12 events, 28 relations, e05 left with I2 only, e14 left with M2 only) matches the usual `drop_duplicates` on `(activity, timestamp, oid)` and is appropriately hedged. Sections 8 and 9 are the strongest part of the answer: the provenance fields are the right ones, and the failure list covers the eight required modes with real controls. The prose also runs past the 1,900–2,900 word target, roughly into the mid-3,000s excluding code.
