4.7

The write-up has the right skeleton: join on the slower branch, separate queue from service, keep reruns and investigations off the smooth path, and treat the temperature excursion as a common factor. Those choices are then undercut by calendar arithmetic that is internally inconsistent and, in places, impossible.

The incubation anchor is wrong. Friday 14:00 plus 46 h and 50 h are correctly Sunday 12:00 and Sunday 16:00, but 54 h is stated as Monday 20:00. It is Sunday 20:00. That error is repeated in the assumption ledger and the stage decomposition, so the “late primary ready Monday evening, readout Tuesday” branch does not exist. Primary readiness is entirely Sunday afternoon–evening.

That mistake drives several required outputs. Scenario C’s central contamination window (Wednesday–Friday) is about one to two days late: Sunday 12:00–20:00 plus 20–30 h is Monday morning through early Tuesday, and a weekday microbiologist can usually read that on Monday or Tuesday morning. The smooth-path claim that microbiology staffing dominates, and that chemistry is not binding, does not survive the corrected clock. Without on-call, readout is only about Monday 08:30–09:30. Chemistry that never clears the Friday queue often finishes later on Monday, so the Monday join is frequently the chemistry clock. On-call readout cannot move release to Sunday: QA has no Sunday hours, and release occurs only after QA acceptance. Earliest plausible release is Monday morning, roughly 65–75 h from Friday 15:30, and only if the queue and service are both short.

Section 4’s earliest band, 30–46 h mapping to Sunday night–Monday morning, fails both ways. Thirty hours after 15:30 is Saturday 21:30, before the incubation can finish (earliest technical readiness is about 44.5 h out, Sunday 12:00). Forty-six hours is Sunday early afternoon, not Monday morning. The same section’s 170 h lower edge is Friday 25 September, not Monday 28 September.

Dates contradict each other. Sections 2 and 3 call next Monday “22 Sep” (that date is Tuesday) and shift the whole near-term week by one weekday. Section 4 then says next Monday is 21 Sep and leaves the scenario table uncorrected. Far-tail labels such as Monday 28 Sep happen to be the right weekdays, so the document does not even use one calendar.

Other quantitative problems are smaller but real. A documentation query of 4–12 QA working hours, on a Monday join, returns the batch Monday evening or Tuesday, not Wednesday–Friday. Scenario A’s modal Monday readout and scenario C’s “weekend staffing delay” are largely the same path, so the 35–50% and 30–45% bands double-count the base case. “About 85–92% Electronic Release” and “rejection only a few percent” do not partition. Friday’s residual FIFO queue (urgent plus ordinary samples still unfinished at 18:00) is collapsed into “chemistry usable Monday 09:00–11:00,” which is early for the 55% FIFO branch. Using mean chemistry service to dismiss a Friday finish also hides a narrow left tail: a 1.5 h service started by about 16:30 can still clear 18:00.

Section 6 is therefore aimed at the wrong observation. Weekend on-call authorization changes the smooth path by roughly the Monday readout duration, not by a day, because QA is shut on Sunday and chemistry often still sets the join. The update that actually moves the forecast is confirmation or clearance of the temperature excursion (and, next, a contamination indication), because that is what fattens or collapses the investigation tail.

What keeps this above a failing score is the join logic, the explicit overlap warning, the refusal to multiply anomalies as if independent, and a P50 neighborhood (Monday 21–Tuesday 22) that is still in the right region for a clean or lightly delayed release. The task is calendar decomposition, and the incubation endpoint, the earliest terminal time, the weekday labels, and the top-ranked driver are wrong.
