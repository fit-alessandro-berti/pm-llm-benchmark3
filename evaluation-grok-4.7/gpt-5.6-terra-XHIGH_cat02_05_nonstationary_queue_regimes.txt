Little’s Law is not fed median service time. The eight required sections and the single pandas script are present; PM4Py is confined to comments and is not applied to these aggregates.

The alternative segmentation does not cut the days differently. The pooled median-service screen is given as about 0.75. Under the answer’s own outage-adjusted definition the ratio is \(78149/102600=0.76\). The 0.75 figure appears only if the 180 outage minutes are left in the denominator, which contradicts the formula used for every regime.

Recovery is described as WIP falling from 52 to 35. Those are the 2026-04-16 and 2026-04-17 daily means. The outage-day mean is 47, so the sampled mean peaks on the first clearance day. Net completions above arrivals on 2026-04-16 can sit alongside that peak; the write-up does not separate those two facts.

Section 4 leans on means for two- and three-day cells. The lag-1 correlations (about 0.41 and 0.61) are left as raw mixture correlations, with a warning but without a day-type-stratified check. The outage-day workload screen applies that day’s observed service median to all arrivals, although the completed cohort that day can be a selected subset. The script itself is consistent with the prose definitions and does not treat the `complex_pct` split as ground truth.

These are design and precision gaps in an otherwise careful separation of demand, mix, capacity, and backlog. They are enough, on a strict reading, to keep the answer below a near-flawless mark.
