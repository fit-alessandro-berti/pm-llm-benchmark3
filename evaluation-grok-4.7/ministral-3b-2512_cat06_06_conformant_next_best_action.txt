4.5

The ordering instinct is acceptable: unlawful execution and self-certification are last, a second-officer path is first, and P-901 is acknowledged. The analysis under that ranking is not reliable enough to act on.

The table’s benefit and harm figures are systematically false. Ranks 1–3 and G claim a 0% deadline breach. S4’s availability is 35–65 minutes, recording and execution take further time, and an S2 review is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes. Arrival is not completion. At a 65-minute arrival, a real review plus execution cannot fit inside 90 minutes, so 0% breach is impossible on the recommended plan. The same rows claim 0% false rejection or hold. Conformant approval is only 0.55–0.75, and a score of 72 with an unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match can still end in rejection, hold, or a report. Those zeros also make the €20k–€35k “benefit” look captured with certainty. Rank 2’s unexplained haircut to €15k–€30k does not repair that. Action F puts customer loss in the benefit column, caps it at €15k against the stated €15k–€80k, and calls a non-execution “0% deadline breach.”

The top plan’s own branches contradict the table. Rank 1 treats alternate screening as resolving the match and then calls approval likely because the score is 72. A score of at least 70 still forces an independent second review even if the name match clears, and screening may confirm the match rather than clear it. Action D’s “no change, therefore reject and file a report” ignores the approval range and treats a Suspicious Activity Report as automatic. Action E inverts the evidence branch: resolution of the fuzzy match is given as a reason to reject. Report filing is also inserted into rejection and hold branches without the prohibition on telling the customer.

Conformance mapping is wrong where it matters. Action A skips the second-officer review and a valid approval path; it is not a four-eyes breach, and Rule 4 does not apply because analyst review has already occurred. The binding control is the score-or-fuzzy-match second-review rule, plus execution only after a valid approval. Action F is tied to an invented confidence cutoff of 0.40 or 0.46 and, if that cutoff is missed, to Rule 5. Rule 5 is the evidence cycle. The case states that the rejection threshold is not established. What is missing is the institution’s written rejection standard, not a made-up number. Labels are also not the required ones: E is “CONFORMANT,” and F uses a custom label. Rank 1 is marked conditional without stating the condition, while consuming an API call and still listing spillover as “None.”

Section 5 gets the timing decision backwards. If S4 will arrive near 65 minutes, interrupting S2 then spends P-901’s capacity after P-884 can no longer finish inside the deadline. An early signal that S4 is late is what would make an immediate S2 interrupt worth considering. Waiting and then interrupting is the dominated sequence. Alternate screening is optional information; it is not required for compliance and does not replace the second review. The API quota is a real scarce resource, and the exception path can burn 20–60 minutes and still fail. Neither point is analyzed as a decision constraint. Section 5 also calls the P-901 interrupt “not acceptable” while the table ranks it third.

The close contradicts itself. The body falls back from late S4 to S2 under Action D; the formal fallback is Action F, contingent on the invented 0.40 threshold. Confidence of 90% and 75% is not supported by a 12-minute-old score, an unresolved match, a 30-minute officer-arrival band, observational predictions that may not survive intervention, and an unmeasured P-901 loss. Reranking triggers omit the screening result and an updated score, and “P-901’s deadline expires first” is already known because 70 minutes is less than 90.

The regulatory range is cited and A and B are correctly kept off the recommended list, but the write-up never uses dominance cleanly: the low end of unlawful-execution exposure already exceeds the high end of deadline value before non-monetary consequences. Favorable breach forecasts therefore cannot rehabilitate A or B. That point is asserted by mislabeled rule numbers rather than argued.
