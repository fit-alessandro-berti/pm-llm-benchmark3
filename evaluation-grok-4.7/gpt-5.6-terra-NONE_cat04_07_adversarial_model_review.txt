6.5

The review is strong on diagnosis and governance, and unreliable on the model it asks the board to adopt. Sections 1–6, 9, and 10–11 meet the brief: use cases change the fitness/precision trade-off, every reported measure is interpreted without a fake aggregate, Model S’s optional `X(activity, tau)` leaves are separated from mere noise tolerance, Model C is treated as variant memorization, B1–B8 are judged as language membership rather than alignment cost, and the dissent is a real allow-list argument inside the 150–250 word bound.

The third alternative does not implement the semantics it claims. `Operator.PARALLEL` and `Operator.SEQUENCE` are not POWL operators; concurrency and order belong in `StrictPartialOrder`. The same `credit` and `technical` objects are reused across the initial block and the recheck XOR, so the tree is not well-formed. `Operator.LOOP` with `[correction_cycle, SilentTransition()]` executes the cycle at least once. That language rejects B1, B2, B5, and B7, while Section 8 says the third model accepts them. The compact expression sequences Supplier Review before Expedite Review; the partial order leaves supplier, expedite, and correction unordered; the prose and audit allow either supplier/expedite order. Those three specifications disagree.

The policy checker is closer to the intent and still inconsistent with the audit. Missing `external_internal` is always indeterminate, so B1 and B2 are not unconditional accepts. B5 and B7 remain acceptable for a known internal case because an extra Supplier Review is not forbidden, yet the audit marks every non-external reading indeterminate. Supplier after approval is missed for internal cases. Recheck coverage uses sets, so a second required check after one correction is invisible, and one earlier correction satisfies every later repeat. The checker also does not enforce the backbone’s supplier-after-both-checks order.

Smaller slips match the same pattern: “approval before mandatory checks” is skip, not reorder, in Model S; expedite-before-approval there is structural, not “usually”; duplicate-label count 33 is called plausible without a defined count. The validation program would be a sound way to discover these failures, but the artifact as written would not pass it.
