7.5

The case-level, event-time analysis is strong and usually precise, but the answer is not nearly flawless. Several classifications overclaim relative to the answer’s own rules, and one portfolio conclusion is affirmatively wrong.

The clearest error is the 60-day Clara–Ben pattern. Ben has no standing QM authority and the only evidenced QM delegation is D-QM-01, five days from 2026-08-10 through 2026-08-14. The statistic is 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production approvals over the preceding 60 days. Section 3 calls that pattern “not violations of any stated rule,” and Sections 4 and 7 treat it mainly as structural concentration caused by Asha’s leave. That leave cannot account for a 60-day rate. Absent per-event delegations, most of those approvals are potential RR3 breaches or a missing-delegation gap, not merely an RR6 collusion indicator. Refusing to call it collusion is right; clearing it of authority failure is not.

CHG-509 is internally inconsistent. Section 2 and the code require principal absence, Asha’s recorded leave ends on 2026-08-14, and the code therefore returns NO AUTHORITY. The case table still leads with VALID_SUBSTITUTION on the theory that a weekend is formal unavailability. That theory is unevidenced. The primary label should be INDETERMINATE.

CHG-505 has the same overclaim in smaller form. Lateness is solid: implementation on Tuesday 2026-08-11, review on Friday 2026-08-14, and Friday is outside two business days under every ordinary convention. Scope failure is also solid, because D-QM-01 is production-equipment approval, not cooling-system retrospective review. The row nevertheless says the IC approval leg alone “would be VALID_SUBSTITUTION.” CI-44 is not in the log, and no safety rationale is in the record, so that leg is unproven as well. Sick-leave identity is a third suspicion, not a third established failure.

CHG-504 as primary AUTHORIZED is a judgment call with real support (Asha’s statement plus the 09:15–09:40 VPN window covering 09:22), but credential sharing is still open. Medium confidence and a secondary identity label keep it from being a hard error. Section 2 is slightly too strong in treating an unattributed service account as already nonconformant rather than conformance-not-demonstrated; the CHG-503 classification itself is more careful and appropriate.

The code covers the requested workflow and encodes the important manual judgments, but it is not as verified as claimed. Delegation checks are row-wise Python datetime tests, not pandas interval logic. D-IC-03 is coded as every `cooling_system` change, wider than CI-44. The RR4 loop uses `emergency == True` while the approval loop string-normalizes the flag, so RR4 silently prints nothing if pandas leaves the column as strings. The claimed “verified output” is exactly what a mental simulation would produce, including the CHG-509 absence failure that the case table then declines to adopt.

The matrix, RR1/RR2/RR5 alias and service-account handling, the stale-master trap on CHG-507, CHG-508 after alias normalization, the void D-IC-04 subdelegation, the PDF-versus-late-database distinction, and the separation of technical SoD from weaker control objectives are all handled at a high level. Those strengths do not remove the authority misread or the contradictory CHG-509 verdict.
