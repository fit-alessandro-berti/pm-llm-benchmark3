7.5

The memo is complete, decision-oriented, and strategically sound: it unbundles Redesign R, rejects both full national scale and wholesale rollback, and lands on a gated hybrid with explicit pauses, measurements, and rollout gates. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated cleanly; all five metric conflicts are explained with the right process-mining artifacts; and the M1–M8 ranking is sequenced and decision-linked.

It is not nearly flawless. Generalization is misread as temporal instability or drift; low generalization means the discovered model overfits the sample, not that the process drifted. Segment benefits are repeatedly called “proven” even though the same memo treats case mix, incomplete pre-week-3 attributes, and non-comparable regions as open. The apartment/old-building p95 gate of 45 days applies the portfolio baseline to segments whose own baselines are unknown. Post-billing activation is kept for simple houses without segment evidence, while being treated as a compliance failure elsewhere. Scaling that slice nationally while capping utilization at an invented 85% (from an observed 93%) omits the staffing required to do both. Thresholds that allow FTR and complaints to stay worse than baseline are not justified, and M6 is treated as able to kill the skip rule outright without noting that skipped addresses have no survey outcome.
