7.6

The answer is complete, numerically careful, and conceptually serious, but it is not nearly flawless. Several identification, bounding, and design problems are large enough to undercut an otherwise strong analysis.

The arithmetic that can be checked by hand is accurate. Adopter pre/post contrasts, the three comparative differences, arrival and load changes, and the stated pre-trend slopes all reproduce. Availability is cleanly separated from intensity, selection and co-interventions are catalogued, median and p90 are not collapsed, CSAT nonresponse is treated as fatal to a customer-outcome claim, and the universal “reduces cycle time by X%” statement is correctly rejected. The recommendation to expand only with an embedded evaluation is the right class of decision. The mechanism and threat tables are specific to this rollout rather than generic.

The main failure is estimand slippage. E1 is defined as a package effect of making automation available, while the preferred design adjusts for load per FTE and complexity and the narrative still calls that target a package effect. Those covariates are exactly the routing, staffing, and case-mix shocks bundled with rollout. The unadjusted differences actually reported in section 5 are a different contrast again, and the intensity slopes (about −0.62 median hours and −1.01 p90 hours per +10 percentage points) are never reconciled with the roughly −4 to −7 hour comparative differences. Section 9 then estimates the branch-and-month fixed-effects specification that section 3 correctly distrusts, builds relative-time indicators, and never uses them. With about 15 regressors and 5 clusters, the cluster-robust variance has rank at most 5; warning that standard errors can be tiny does not state that most of them are not identified.

The headline windows also hide the sharpest pattern in the panel. In April, Alpha and Beta rework falls by about 2 points and escalation rises by about 1 point while controls are flat, whereas median cycle time barely moves and Beta’s median and p90 worsen. March escalation rises by exactly 2 points in every branch, which is the fraud desk, not automation. May–June p90 then rises by about 11–23 hours in the April adopters. A decision interval of 0 to −5 hours for the median excludes short-run harm the raw series show, and −7 to +3 hours for p90 cannot cover the observed tail spike. Full-window “avoided deterioration” is a real relative arithmetic result, but it is partly shared May reversal and a common August volume drop.

The rollout design has the same unfinished reasoning. Within-branch 50% eligibility in a shared specialist and fraud queue violates the no-interference condition the design needs, and that is not stated. A 0.05 CSAT stop rule contradicts the claim that CSAT is not estimable at that resolution. Four-week phases conflict with the multi-month learning ramp. Delta-first is safer operationally, but it is the branch least able to reveal the high-complexity tail failure mode already visible at Alpha and Epsilon. The pre-period automated-fast-path share of 4–6% while availability is still zero is also left unexamined, as is case-weighting of branch-month medians.

So the claim ladder is directionally right, and the descriptive contrasts are trustworthy, but the preferred causal contrast, the sensitivity bounds, and the learning design are less tight than the writeup’s confidence suggests.
