The write-up follows the nine-section outline and gets a few load-bearing judgments right: e05 and e14 stay distinct, e10 follows carrier acceptance, ingestion time is not business time, synthetic microseconds invent causality, and shared O1/V1 does not make e13 and e14 the same payment. Those points are not enough. The required ordering, PM4Py block, and sensitivity analysis all fail in ways that change the result.

The 10:00 order omits e07 entirely, even though e03–e07 was an explicit group. It then sequences e03 before e04, two records of one physical scan, and places e05 after them with no cross-system evidence. The same error repeats at 12:00: e11 before e16 treats one invoice issuance as two successive events, and e13 before e14 is presented as the intended business order when those captures are concurrent partial payments. Within-source `source_seq` (WMS 100 then 110, scanner 551 then 552, PSP 400–402, ERP 40 then 41) is never actually applied. Rules are named, but the published order is not marked semantic versus merely deterministic, so the total order reads as a physical story the task forbids.

Duplicate policy is inconsistent across sections. e03/e04 is “retain both” and also “union payload,” with canonical id e03. e08/e09 says “refine label” but never states the refined labels, so the harmonization collision (warehouse release versus carrier acceptance versus handover) is not actually repaired. The conservative count of 17 is only coherent if nothing is merged; it conflicts with the union policy. The evidence-based scenario’s damage claim is wrong for a union: merging e03 with e04 should keep the WMS location, not lose it. The mechanical collapse is given as “~10” and highlights e13/e14, but never states that the same activity-and-timestamp merge would also destroy e05.

The Python block does not implement the design. It deep-copies once, then `pass`es on candidate groups. It never calls `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` or `pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates`, never writes `original_timestamp`, `original_activity`, `source`, or `source_event_key`, and never runs the verified-pair helper on e03/e04 or e11/e16. Sort is by timestamp and source name, which is not the stated order. Index-based timedelta is applied anyway, contradicting the failure mode that forbids synthetic durations. Referential-integrity validation is a nonexistent method call.

Sensitivity analysis skips most of the required comparisons: order-, item-, and payment-flattened DFGs, OC-DFG frequency, ET–OT weights, OTG relations, rework, and lifecycle or performance effects. Of the six named “edges,” e01→e02 is explicitly unchanged, and several others are not follows-relations at all. The item-level loss of I2 and the payment-level loss of M2, the two clearest damages, are absent. The audit schema is mostly present but has no reason field and no real reconstruction of relations or durations. The eight failure modes are listed, yet several safeguards contradict the code or would not catch different payload hashes on M1 and M2.
