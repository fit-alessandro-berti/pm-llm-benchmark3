The forecast meets every required section and constraint: all seven headings are present, the mechanism map covers every mandated link, the three scenarios each include the requested dimensions, East is treated differently from North and South, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The best analytical point is the handover arithmetic: pairing the current tech loop (36+31) against the proposed shared-service loop (41+36), and approvals (42+39 vs 49+43), correctly shows that “fewer handovers” is not what management’s own routing table implies, while Legal/Risk at 91–93% cannot absorb 9→12 referrals per 100 cases.

Points come off for concrete flaws. The sketch’s SLA rule (High 5 days, Medium 7, Low 10) is inconsistent with baseline medians of 6.8–8.6 days and SLA-miss rates of only 6.5–9.8%, so it would classify most current cases as misses. The custom “wait” is sojourn since the previous timestamp, while `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called and then ignored. Rework is compared as raw counts, not rates. The segment loop can hit empty logs with no guard, and its results are never used. The pilot both treats “selected North/South” cases and offers South as the control, so the comparison group is contaminated. Optimistic “early warning signs” are disconfirming signals, not signatures of that scenario, and several percentage bands (5–10% median, 1–2 pp rework) are stated without a capacity or queueing model.
