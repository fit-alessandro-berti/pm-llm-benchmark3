The write-up covers every required section and correctly treats the task as a multi-objective trade-off, with a usable hard/soft split, a worker-facing explanation, and a full indicator list. Several central claims are still inaccurate or not executable.

The queue plan misreads the skill matrix. Approval and Security scores of 0 are “not allowed,” while 1 is the trainee level. C010 still calls R8_Hugo an approval trainee and gives him a shadowing role even though his Approval score is 0; the legal preparers are the skill-1 resources. C015 likewise calls R5_Eva a security trainee although her Security score is 0, then moves the case from current owner R8_Hugo (skill 3, fewer open cases, low overtime) to the more loaded R7_Gita for no compliance gain. C012’s rationale that Bruno is the only Quality trainee is false: David and Eva are also skill 1. The stated 3/9 training share therefore counts at least one invalid training assignment.

C001 is allowed only in a narrow reading (skill 1 plus R8 supervision) and is a poor choice. The case is urgent, has 3 hours of SLA, complexity 0.88, and is explicitly marked as needing a specialist. The given evidence says a weak-skill owner adds about 3.3 hours of service time, which already exceeds that slack, while the answer describes the skill gap as slight and never uses the 3.3h or 2.7h figures in the decision. The same plan also gives Celine four batch roles (unskilled urgent security, a repeated diagnosis, high-priority rework, and supervision) against 6 hours and 6 open cases, without a portfolio-level capacity check. That conflicts with the answer’s own feasibility rule.

The scorer would not reliably reproduce the table. `SLA(c)` does not depend on the resource, so the largest urgent weight cannot change the ranking. Continuity, resilience, and normalization are only partly specified; supervised pairs are not generated systematically; workload and hours are not updated inside the batch; case order is undefined; ties may be random. The 20% training quota and the Quality batching rule are stated and then left out of the algorithm. Baseline comparison stays generic and never applies first-available or specialist-only routing to these 16 cases, so the throughput and SLA claims are not grounded in this queue.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested analyses, but `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` is not a real parameter, and `filter_case_performance` is called with `performance_metric` and `min_value` instead of `min_performance` / `max_performance`. The SLA check does not parse `sla_deadline`, and deviation detection compares a single `org:resource` with a possible supervised pair. Most individual assignments besides C001, C010, and C015 are defensible, which keeps this above a failed answer, but the skill errors, inert objective term, and non-reproducible decisions are material.
