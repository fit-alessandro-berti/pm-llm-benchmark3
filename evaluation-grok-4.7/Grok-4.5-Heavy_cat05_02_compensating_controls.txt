7.5

The analysis is disciplined and mostly non-mechanical: nominal four-eyes status is separated from the control objective, AV-2 is confined to the checks it actually performs, and the hard cases are not collapsed into amount or user-count rules. It is not nearly flawless. Several timing, classification, and code defects are material.

The decomposition and sufficiency test are the strongest parts. Six threat scenarios are mapped to prevent, detect, correct, or miss for AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1, including collusion, compromised credentials, and goods-receipt gaps. The seven-dimension test (timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance) and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the sole standard are substantive. What is missing is an explicit crosswalk from fully equivalent / partially equivalent / merely mitigating / ineffective onto the five required classifications, so AP-203 can be called fully objective-met while the same section treats full equivalence as rare on any same-user path.

Case judgments that hold up: AP-202 is a genuine objective failure (same-user override of a bank-change and limit warning with a supervisor’s credential, conflicted and late ratification, audit after recovery was realistic, unrecovered loss). AP-204 meets the form of FEP-1 and still fails the objective because a fabricated receipt passed a non-independent pair and AV-2 does not test receipt. AP-205 is correctly loss-prevented rather than a completed violation. AP-206 is correctly left indeterminate on the two-minute token gap. AP-208 correctly fails despite a legitimate payment. AP-203 is a fair objective-met reading of ED-1.

Weaker judgments:
- AP-207 is only partially met because ratification followed release. ED-1 condition 6 is approval before settlement, and that ratification was before settlement, independent, and evidence-based. The pre-release versus pre-settlement distinction is real for strength, but it is overstated as a failure of the permission the case is being tested against.
- AP-209 is classified as partially met mainly from the repeated u12/u13 pair and shared workstation, while section 6 says that pattern is an alert, not proof, and may be a legitimate cell. With the design documents missing and no defect in this payment, INDETERMINATE fits the answer’s own rule better.
- AP-201’s audit is about 24 hours 54 minutes after release, which is outside a 24-hour release clock, but settlement was at 10:00, so the same audit is about 23 hours after settlement. “Borderline” and “slightly late” are asserted without fixing which clock RA-7 uses. That clock is part of why the case is withheld from objective-met.
- The AP-208 counterfactual adds a working AV-2 to the independent second releaser, so it does not isolate what the second releaser would change during an outage. “Vendor <1 year” is also placed beside the ED-1 failures; eight months still clears the 180-day tenure rule. Amount, recent bank change, unavailable validation, unsigned checklist, post-settlement ratification, and late audit are already sufficient.

The preventive/detective/corrective table is coherent, and calling post-release, pre-settlement manager action hybrid is a defended semantic choice aligned with MG-4. Monitoring recommendations cover every requested indicator and correctly separate alert from misconduct, which makes the AP-209 classification more inconsistent, not less.

The code meets the structural requirements (embedded CSV, UTC parsing, `format_dataframe`, a four-eyes call, blocked-attempt handling, a facts join, and an explicit non-verdict label) but its manager-timing evidence is wrong. After `unstack`, cases without `Bank_Settlement` have NaT, and `NaT is not None` is true, so the “settlement missing” branch never runs. A timestamp compared with NaT is false, so AP-201 is flagged as neither before nor after settlement even though the essay’s main compensating control is pre-settlement ratification. The comment claims this branch is facts-based; the intended default (manager present and no settlement event means before settlement) would instead have marked AP-202 true. `keep_violations=True` is stated as pm4py 2.7.x behavior. That parameter is not part of the stable documented signature; the try/except and pandas cross-check limit the damage but do not make the API claim reliable. The facts frame also invents 365 vendor days for AP-205 and treats m21 as independent when only m04, m02, m11, and m15 are established. The 36-hour “one business day” proxy would mark AP-201’s audit as timely while the prose calls it late.

Section 7 is labeled as evidence rather than the verdict, so these bugs do not overturn the written classifications. They do make the required executable checks unreliable on the timing question the rest of the answer treats as decisive.
