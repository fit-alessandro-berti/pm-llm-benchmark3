7.5

The audit is strong on the main leakage cases and on the required distinctions, but it is not nearly flawless. Several severity, classification, and narrative problems keep it well below a top score.

The final write-up covers every global feature, every sequence pattern, and the local-only fields, and it uses the required five-part structure and valid JSON. It correctly treats post-closure duration, anywhere-appeals, full variants, final decision, current risk score, and last resource role as unavailable at prediction time. It keeps queue length and elapsed time since registration as usable process-state and prefix-age signals, and it does not turn region or channel into causal delay drivers. Appeal followed by a later settlement decision is correctly separated from the impossible-looking close-before-damage pattern, including the same-timestamp problem in Case Z. Case Z is withheld rather than narrated from leaked events. The validation and remediation plan covers point-in-time features, prefix recomputation, conformance, recalibration, explanation stability, fairness, and drift.

The defects are still material. Employee experience joined as of extraction date is a mutable-lineage leak, but the answer places it with calendar and drift proxies and never repairs it. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is under-ranked as high: `claim_created_at <= extraction_date` can pull in the claimant’s later history, not a small boundary error. Closure-time `final_handler_team` is likewise soft-pedaled as high even though the stored value is the final team. Case X’s narrative says the repaired explanation is dominated by queue, complexity, and a young prefix, but the residual “other features” contribution is about +0.21, larger than complexity and far larger than the −0.03 elapsed-time effect, and channel is omitted from that sentence while still listed as a driver. Those directions are also taken from SHAP on a leaked model; the recompute status does not say the signs can change after refit. Channel and initial reserve are treated as credible despite unverified lineage. Footprint, prefix-alignment, and declarative checks are named more than explained, and the same-timestamp discussion implies a sequence index could salvage Close → Damage Assessment, which remains normatively impossible even if the order is real.
