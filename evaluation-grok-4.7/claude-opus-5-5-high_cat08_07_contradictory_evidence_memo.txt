7.3

The memo is a strong, usable decision document: it selects a segmented hybrid rather than national rollout or full rollback, keeps facts mostly distinct in the evidence table, explains the five metric conflicts with real mechanisms, and ranks M1–M8 by whether the result would change the decision. The arithmetic that is checkable (cost gaps, segment-weighted p90/FTR/complaints, ρ/(1−ρ)) is largely correct, and the normative-versus-discovered reading of fitness 0.94 versus frequent alignments is sound.

The recommendation still mis-specifies the intervention it would scale. The pilot skipped survey for some medium-risk addresses, and the “often wrong” note sits on apartments. Simple low-risk houses improved as a segment, but nothing shows that survey-skipping was applied to them or caused that gain. A 13-point drop in the scheduling→survey edge is consistent with skips concentrated in the medium-risk slice. Treating “survey skip for low-risk houses” as part of the winning package, rejecting option C partly for “giving up” those savings, and extending that skip in a stepped wedge before M1/M6, puts an unvalidated rule into the action plan. The same memo’s own value-of-information section says a low-risk harm finding should remove skipping.

Several controls are internally inconsistent. The scale gate allows first-time-right at baseline−1pp and complaints at +0.5pp, while the kill rule reverts any first-time-right drop and waits until complaints exceed +1pp. A 100% permit gate ignores the table’s own logging-gap assumption. Precision is barred as a target in §3.1 and then used as a rollout gate. “Five changes,” “proven gain,” “every component removes a check or a handover,” and regulatory exposure are stated as settled in the decision sections after the table correctly marks causality, logging, and legal status as open. DFG shares are later converted to “per 100 orders” after the table notes those shares are not a partition (69%+38%>100%). Generalization falling alongside precision is explained as the natural result of a looser model; on the usual fitness/precision/generalization trade-off, a model that allows many extra traces tends to generalize more, not less. Rural deterioration (11% of volume) is left out of the “damage is concentrated in 55%” claim. The 150,000-order stake is labeled as assumed, but it sits above the baseline run-rate implied by 24,000 cases in 10 weeks.

Strategically the direction is right: stop billing before a passed activation, restore permit checks, do not nationalize R, do not discard the simple-house speed gain, and buy M1, M4, and M6. The operational package is not yet reliable enough to execute as written, because it expands the wrong component and gives management two conflicting sets of numbers.
