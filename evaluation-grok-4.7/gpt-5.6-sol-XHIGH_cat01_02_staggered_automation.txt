7.5

The answer is a careful, mostly well-disciplined refusal of “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” but it is not nearly flawless. The central numerical claim is framed more strongly than its own design and arithmetic support, and the key tables are not safely readable.

The conditionally causal ladder item is the main logical failure. A −4.5 hour Alpha/Beta and −4.2 hour Gamma difference-in-differences, divided by treated pre-period medians, becomes “about a 9% median reduction” if parallel trends, no anticipation, stable implementation, and modeled fraud-desk interference hold. That assumption list omits the treatments that actually switch on with availability: the adopter-only routing increase, branch-specific FTE changes, and the May complexity shift, which begins after Alpha and Beta are already treated, so it has no within-regime pre-period for the April cohort. Parallel trends in the outcome do not separate software availability from that bundle. “Stable implementation” does not cover it. The same bullet also converts a relative contrast into language that sounds like an absolute reduction. Alpha/Beta’s own post-period median change is about +0.73 hours, and their p90 change is about +9.7 hours. Alpha’s June median (53.5) and p90 (119.9) are worse than any pre-adoption month. Section 7’s “safe” median sentence has the same slip: controls are already called “counterfactuals.”

Section 5’s primary tables compound this. Pre and post values are concatenated (`4.038.0`, `48.9048.50`, `2548`), and decreases appear without minus signs (`(0.40)`, `4.49 h`, `8.45`, `1.93 pp`) while increases keep “+”. The surrounding prose and the stated definition “treated change minus control change” let a careful reader reconstruct the signs, and those reconstructed figures check out, but the required quantitative exhibit is not reliable as printed. The ±5 hour and ±10 hour bands are labeled as scenarios, yet they are not tied to a pre-trend slope, a leave-one-control-out, or a complexity-restricted contrast. The executed analysis never produces the cohort-specific group-time estimates, Gamma-as-not-yet-treated contrast, or leave-one-branch-out checks that Section 3 says are necessary. The suggestive ladder line also puts a p90 improvement of six to eight hours beside the median result, despite raw tail worsening and a Section 7 confidence of only 30.

The code meets the checklist and correctly refuses a causal reading, but it estimates one pooled, binned two-way event study for the median only. That is the specification the prose warns against. `month_index` via `astype("int64")` and adoption timing via `Period.ordinal` are not guaranteed to share a scale, so the relative-time indicators can fail silently. The written DiDs are not recomputed in the script.

Smaller gaps: branch-equal averages of medians are not arrival-weighted; load per FTE is discussed but never shown, despite the capacity-versus-mix question; Delta (complexity about 18–23) and Epsilon (about 30–38) are treated as one evaluation pair even though a result in one does not transport to the other; a one-versus-one branch delay has almost no power once the shared fraud desk is acknowledged; and the 5%, 1 point, and 0.10 guardrails are not connected to observed month-to-month noise or to survey sample size.

What keeps the score from falling further is real: availability is separated from fast-path intensity and from case-level treatment; median and p90 are never collapsed; selection, interference, compositional rework, censoring, nonresponse, and five-cluster inference are handled explicitly; the arithmetic that can be reconstructed is accurate; and “expand with an embedded evaluation,” with randomized order plus saturation rather than another targeted launch, is the right decision.
