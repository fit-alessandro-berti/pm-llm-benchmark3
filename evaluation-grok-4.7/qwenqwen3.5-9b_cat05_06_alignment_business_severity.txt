It is not reliable enough to score higher. Policy application, severity, and the code deliverable contain material errors.

The severity scale is internally inconsistent and does not use the factors it claims to use. CL-610 (EUR 4,500, wrong approval path) is rated 5, while CL-602 (EUR 480,000, high risk, bank change, no fraud evidence) is rated 4. CL-607 and CL-612, with no payment, are rated 3, above CL-608 (EUR 250,000, weak notification evidence) and CL-606 (EUR 75,000, missing core assessment). The written definition of severity 3 requires financial exposure, then it is applied to no-payment model gaps. Severity 5 is defined as systemic or intentional, then applied to a single mid-band case.

CL-609 is misread. EUR 18,000 and medium risk violate P1, but they do not meet P2 (above EUR 50,000, high risk, or a recent bank change). The overlay leaves the EUR 5,000–50,000 medium band unspecified. Calling this a P1/P2 violation at severity 5 collapses that gap. CL-608 is marked “P4 satisfied” despite the mutable text link, which makes the portal/CRM evidence unreliable; that case should stay inconclusive. CL-611 is treated as a settled label synonym with high confidence even though the mapping table was changed after the case. CL-604’s cost of 3 is described as confirming severity, although the same cost on CL-601 is only UI noise, which is the point of the comparison. The silent XOR routing move in CL-605 is correctly called fitting, then later treated as a possible hidden control bypass.

Section 3 merges the 74 same-message-ID retries with CL-608’s channel/evidence problem and never states that two of the nine external-adjuster cases lack a stable document identifier.
