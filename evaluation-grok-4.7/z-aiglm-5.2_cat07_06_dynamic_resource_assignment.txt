That is not enough to make the policy coherent.

The assignment table contradicts both the stated rules and the pseudocode. High overtime risk is hard-filtered unless priority is urgent, yet low-priority C013 is given to Bruno, and the worker explanation claims high-overtime resources were avoided. C011’s owner is Farid, not Eva; the table and comparison nevertheless treat the move to Eva as continuity. C016 has a skill-2 owner, high complexity, high handover cost, and an explicit continuity note, but it is removed anyway. C001 could have stayed with Celine under Gita/Hugo supervision, which the continuity rule requires when supervision is feasible. C012 is marked as training even though Jonas is already a quality specialist and his training priority is security. Two real training matches, C003 and C009, barely meet the 20% target, while the stated trainee-approval opportunity on C010 is discarded.

The scoring procedure cannot reproduce the table. Resilience is calculated and then omitted from every total. Skill 0 is blocked only for security and approval. Handover bands are divided as though they were numeric. Normal cases ignore SLA urgency, and no score is shown for any case. Celine is also left with two high-complexity cases plus two supervisions inside six hours, so the plan is operationally infeasible.

The baseline comparison misstates first-available routing: the evidence says it reduces waiting time while increasing mismatch, whereas specialist-only creates the security and approval bottlenecks. Avoiding David does not improve diagnosis resilience when Celine becomes the sole specialist used. The PM4Py sketch names relevant functions, but `filter_case_performance` filters case duration rather than waiting time, similarity, working-together, and batch discovery are absent, and nothing shows how the log would calibrate the policy weights.
