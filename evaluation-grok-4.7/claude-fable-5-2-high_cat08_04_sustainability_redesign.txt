8.4

The answer meets every required section and constraint, stays inside the log, and is unusually careful about units, slack, segment differences, and rebound. The deductions are for a handful of analytical overclaims, not for missing structure or a reckless recommendation.

The boundary is sound. Energy, transport, scrap, packaging, rework, and obsolescence stay inside; embodied materials, inbound logistics, and customer-side downtime stay outside, with the last correctly used to block a false “green” choice on true downtime. Refusing to add kWh, kgCO2e, and scrap without factors is the right multi-criteria stance. Count gaps are real and correctly flagged: 11,200 closes + 530 cancels = 11,730; cancel inflows are 410 + 60 = 470 against 530 outs; dedicated-van path share (about 960) disagrees with the 620 DFG edge. Hot-spot arithmetic also checks: critical orders are about 41% of transport (0.11 × 38 / ~10.3), MTO is about 87% of energy and 85% of scrap, and the 5% CNC_B rework path is about a fifth of energy and a quarter of scrap.

Two attributions are too tight. The 42% “stock → standard pack → ground” path is labeled “Standard-stock Regular,” but that segment is 39% of orders across modes and has different averages (4.2 kgCO2e and 0.8 kWh, not 3.6 and 0.9). The 5% stock–air path is likewise treated as the 22% SP1 violations among Standard-stock Service-contract orders (~420 of ~1,920). Those sets overlap; they are not the same cut.

The clearest logic slip is O8. The 9% late stock/ground critical path versus 3% on the van path is called an “observed bound” for precautionary orders moved off vans. Those paths also differ in whether the part is already in stock versus emergency-produced, so 9% is not a bound on slowing emergency work. The 32 kWh versus 16 kWh contrast for O5 has the same problem: the paths differ in heat treatment, batching, and rework together, not in machine alone. The packaging-flow claim overreaches: 420 rework outs to reinforced versus 360 ins from reinforced makes a standard-then-reinforced repack possible, not something the aggregates show. O2’s proposal to put heavy reinforced parts on consolidated trucks never faces the damage and route-geometry incompatibility the answer itself uses to explain packaging rework. Small extras—reusable-packaging backhaul, less packaging from merged shipments—are not in the evidence.

Those slips do not flip the recommendation. Air and vans stay available by segment and slack; true downtime keeps the van; large-batch waiting, dropping reinforced packs over 8 kg, and retiring CNC_B are rejected; the shared slack budget is flagged as dependent on grid factor and items per order. Rebound coverage is specific across batching, expediting, obsolescence, low truck fill, carrier-capacity loss, packaging returns, inspection queues, customer gaming, and accounting displacement. Governance pairs SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance with late rate, absolute van counts, and calibration, so cleaner logging cannot masquerade as lower impact.
