The final write-up is structurally complete and directionally right about the conformance collapse, but it mishandles the central quantitative evidence, breaks the causal graph, and does not deliver a coherent model-comparison program.

The primary story in §§3 and 7 is the right one: June’s drop is mostly logging and extraction (`Risk Screen`, `Queue Routed`, warehouse-time order), August’s residual is largely an unupdated model facing `Supervisor Sign-off`, and stable cycle time, approval, and appeal rates do not license blaming reviewers. Employee noncompliance is correctly kept secondary, missing checks and `Manual Override` stay open, and the six governance rules cover the required controls. Format, phase isolation, and the “do not claim yet” caveats are met. Section 3 is about 216 words and §7 about 300, both inside the required ranges.

Those strengths are undercut by repeated misreading of the alignment ladder. Mapping `Risk Screen` alone moves fitness from 32% to 58%, not ~81%. 81% is the joint result of mapping plus dropping `Queue Routed`; 89% is that pipeline plus source-sequence order. §§2 and 4 assign the joint 81% figure to each repair separately, and §2’s “residuals vanish after remapping” is already false on the given table. That is the main evidence in the case, used incorrectly.

The ledger row for stable outcomes lists real process change as both supported and weakened. Incomplete incident detection is treated as evidence against noncompliance; it only weakens the appeal-rate argument. `policy_change → normative_model` (the failed update) is missing, while `business_outcomes → reviewer_behavior` and `recorded_labels → normative_model` reverse or misstate causation. Real process change at 35% is too low and is “contradicted” by fitness recovery that does not remove sign-off: after the same preprocessing, August–September is still at 66%, with sign-off the dominant move (210 of 620 cases). High-value omission, the 30 mixed `Classify`+`Queue Routed` traces, the 11/70 screenshot sample, and the events-per-case rise (~+0.9, in line with `Queue Routed`) are not used as first-class evidence.

Section 3 then says an optional sign-off model “confirms” the process is largely conforming. Extra fitness from a more permissive tree does not show that, and precision is never actually compared. The code does not expand the variant-frequency tables, so normalization is a no-op; removing `Queue Routed` mutates a list being iterated; source sequence is not applied to order; precision is never computed; August–September is never built. The candidate tree places mandatory `Supervisor Sign-off` after `Notify`. The log shows sign-off before `Decision` on both the standard and enhanced branches, so that tree would not produce the claimed fitness gain and is not optional or data-conditioned on EUR 50,000.
