The final answer is a serious, well-structured causal assessment: it refuses a single “X%” claim, separates availability from use, treats rollout as endogenous, keeps median and p90 apart, and recommends expansion only with an embedded design. Those judgments match the evidence. It is not nearly flawless, and the quantitative core is the main failure.

Section 1 is slightly over the 150–250 word limit and says high complexity rose 5–8 points “everywhere.” Beta moved from 25% to 28% (+3), after a dip to 23%. The baseline fast-path share is also not 4% everywhere; Beta is 6%.

The estimands and mechanism tables are strong. Interference through routing and the fraud desk is recognized, and the mechanism signatures are tied to the panel. E1’s interpretation still says the availability contrast is “net of all concurrent operational changes,” while the same row says those changes confound it.

Identification is directionally right—staggered event study rather than a naïve static two-way fixed-effects regression—but the prose says pre-trend checks are the coefficients at t = 3 and t = 2. Those are post-adoption lags. The pre-period coefficients are t = −3 and t = −2. That sign error is repeated in the threats table. The pooled event study also does not confront cohort heterogeneity: Alpha/Beta and Gamma are forced into the same relative-time coefficients despite different shocks.

Several reported numbers are wrong and then reused. Delta’s April–August rework is about 13.5%, not 13.0%, because May–August are 13.9 and only April is 11.7; the same month miscount makes escalation 9.2, not 9.1. Those errors shift the Alpha/Beta-versus-Delta rework contrasts. The Gamma-versus-Delta “−9.4 hour” median contrast mixes Gamma’s January–June window with Delta’s January–March to April–August change. The cited p90 range out to −13.2 hours is not in the DiD table. Among treated branch-months, the fast-path/median correlation is about +0.20, not −0.52; the sign is reversed, so the “higher use, lower cycle time” correlation claim is false. Bounds then wander across 0 to −9 hours, −1 to −9, −8 to −12, and 3 to −7.

The rollout choice is the right one, but “synthetic control” here is one donor (Epsilon), weekly toggling ignores carryover, and the guardrails are miscalibrated: rework of 18% is above the sample maximum, not the 90th percentile, and CSAT of 3.7 is below every observed month, not the 10th percentile. Section 8 also exceeds 400 words.

The code would recompute several means correctly and does warn about five clusters and aggregated medians, but it never requests branch-clustered standard errors even though statsmodels allows them. The event-study baseline also pools never-treated months with Gamma’s relative times −6 to −4, so it does not match the stated t = −1 baseline. The “Observed” ladder rung attributes about 1 point of escalation to automation; that is a differenced claim, not a bare observation.
