6.5

The write-up covers every required heading and all twelve cases, and it gets the central inversion right: raw edit cost is not exposure. Zero-cost auto-approvals (CL-609, CL-610), telemetry and retries (CL-601, CL-603), the silent tau (CL-605), the label pair (CL-611), and high-cost denial/withdrawal traces are classified in the right direction. That is not enough for a high score. Several policy readings, confidence calls, pattern statistics, and the top remediation are wrong or internally inconsistent, and the code section presents an unrun result table.

The materiality scale contradicts itself. Severity 3 is defined as inverted authorization without fraud or loss, while severity 5 is also “out-of-sequence disbursement before approval.” No case is scored 3, so the timing dimension never actually discriminates. AML is added as a control objective the overlay does not state. P6 is a scope dispute between process owner and model owner, not a control a case can “violate,” yet CL-607 and CL-612 are scored as P6 violations at severity 1 with high confidence. That erases the dispute the case supplies.

Per-case policy reading is incomplete or overconfident where it matters most. CL-609 fails P1 twice: EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk. The row, the cause, and the later rule-engine fix mention only the amount cap. CL-602 is scored severity 5 with high confidence and the cause “approver bypassed the system,” even though the trace only shows a missing log event plus an uncorroborated verbal claim. That conflicts with the answer’s own confidence definition and with the rule that a missing event is not proof the activity did not occur. “Freeze payment if uncollected” also fights the trace: Pay Claim is synchronous. CL-604 correctly flags pay-at-10:03 versus approval-at-10:20 and P3, but the narrative and cause never treat the separate M(Fraud Screen) on a EUR 90,000 claim, which is an independent P2 miss (amount, not risk). CL-610 is the cleaner P2 breach — auto-approve after a same-day bank change, with no fraud screen and no manual approval — yet it is tied with CL-609 at severity 4 and then ranked below it by amount. CL-608’s mutable CRM link is treated as if notification had been executed and only logging failed; the facts support an evidence gap, not a completed control.

Pattern evidence is stretched. The four synchronous P1/P2 breaches and the 74 same-message-id retries are used correctly. “Systemic across historical logs” for Assess Damage is not in the evidence; only CL-611 exists, and the mapping table was updated after that case. Pattern 3 glues the external-adjuster Assess Loss gap (the nine high-value cases, two without a stable identifier) to CL-608’s CRM notification link and then assigns that frequency to the combined pattern. Those are different controls, systems, and evidence problems. Pattern 1’s risk line frames the failure as auto-pay above EUR 5,000, which does not cover CL-610.

The hypothesis tests are thinner than the table layout suggests. CL-602’s alternative says the “adjuster” omitted the log entry; the speaker is the manual approver. Both CL-606 hypotheses assume the assessment occurred and differ only by webhook failure mode, so they never test a mis-linked or non-equivalent report. The denial/withdrawal alternative is “the database lost state,” not the actual dispute over whether M0 is payable-claims-only. CL-611 is never given competing hypotheses.

The portfolio’s first preventive control does not do what it claims. A hard block until an approval state is locked stops CL-604. It does not stop CL-609 or CL-610, because Auto Approve is the approval state. Those cases need data guards (amount, risk, bank-change lookback) and a detective data-aware conformance check. The task asked for data-aware conformance explicitly; the portfolio never adds one. The corrective freeze line includes CL-609 and omits CL-610. Label repair is bundled into a telemetry/retry filter. Expected reduction of “€100k+” is an unexplained figure.

Section 6 is the strongest part and hits cost design, scope, tau, data conditions, synonyms, duplicate telemetry, external evidence, and irreversibility with real cases. The tau paragraph then speculates that silent moves can hide routing anomalies, which CL-605 does not support and which undercuts the earlier “model is sound” conclusion.

The Python sketch does call `parse_process_tree`, `convert_to_petri_net`, a dataframe log, and `conformance_diagnostics_alignments`, and it keeps supplied cost and business severity in separate columns. It does not build a dataframe of the supplied move summaries, and it does not aggregate move patterns. CL-607’s amount 0 and LOW risk are invented. The printed “PM4Py_Calculated_Cost” column matches the unit costs exactly. Default PM4Py move costs are not 1, the script shows no evidence of having been run, and the comment that alternative alignments may differ is contradicted by a table in which every calculated cost equals the supplied cost.
