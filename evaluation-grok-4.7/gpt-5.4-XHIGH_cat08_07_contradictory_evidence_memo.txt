The memo is a strong, decision-oriented synthesis: numbers match the evidence, national scale and full rollback are correctly rejected, and the hybrid recommendation tracks the segment split, tail, rework, and billing-before-activation problems. Fitness versus precision, median versus tail, handovers versus resilience, visible versus downstream cost, and simple houses versus apartments and old buildings are explained in the right causal direction. The evidence table is mostly clean, and the value-of-information ranking is tied to decisions rather than listed generically.

It is not nearly flawless. The opening recommendation suspends survey skipping and post-billing activation only for higher-risk segments, while the later decision pauses post-billing activation as a standard path for everyone. “Short” experiment conflicts with a 60-day downstream-cost gate. The 85% utilization cutoff is arbitrary, and “commercially meaningful” overclaims what duration and complaint deltas show. One assumption wrongly ties NPS to incomplete post-week-4 cost data; other assumption cells slide into interpretation. Survey-team utilization falling while second-visit utilization rises, the SLA split, and business and rural segments are underused. Coordinator ownership is given credit for simple-house gains the pilot never isolated, and conformance monitoring is ranked above the ownership test even though ownership is what the recommendation would scale.
