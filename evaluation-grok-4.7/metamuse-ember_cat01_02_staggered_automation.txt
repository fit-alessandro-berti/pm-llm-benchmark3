7.4

The answer is strong on structure, estimands, staggered-adoption reasoning, median-versus-tail separation, and the refusal of a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and design errors are material under a strict reading.

The adjusted intensity slopes are wrong for Gamma. Late-minus-pre fast-path intensity rises about 24.5 points, not the 43 points used for Alpha and Beta. Dividing Gamma’s roughly −3.9 hour relative median contrast by ~43 points produces the reported −0.9 hours per 10 points; the consistent calculation is about −1.6. That error is what makes the three slopes look artificially alike, so the “consistency” sentence is not earned. The headline DiD also mixes windows: Gamma’s −3.9 uses January–March, while April–June is the adjacent untreated pre-period and is set aside for the number that enters the claim ladder.

“January to February values are very stable” is false for the main outcomes. Alpha’s median falls 3.5 hours and its p90 falls 6.6; Delta and Epsilon also move. Pre-trend language is therefore overstated for the key early adopter. The constant 4% (Beta 6%) automated-fast-path share in every pre-availability month, and the long runs of identical rework and escalation figures, are treated too lightly for metrics that drive the dose analysis.

The code meets the checklist and does not invent regression output, but it does not reproduce the reported DiDs or intensity slopes, and it fits the constant two-way fixed-effects model the text correctly warns against. The event-time indicators are built and then unused. Cluster-robust standard errors with five clusters and a saturated dummy set are fragile; that limitation is warned about, not handled.

Smaller issues remain. Scenario bounds disagree across sections (−7 to 0, −7 to +1, and a Delta-only −8.1). Escalation is given the highest confidence while its automation-specific share is called unidentified. The embedded rollout is well aimed, but team- or week-level assignment still shares specialist and fraud-desk queues, so the proposed ITT is not free of the interference the earlier sections emphasize.
