The final policy covers the required structure and the main traps: I-03 is refused because the swarm is past its effective stage, I-05 is suppressed as an unchanged repeat while I-07 is reopened on new evidence, and spillovers, segment calibration, and label feedback are addressed with concrete controls. The direct-benefit arithmetic for the three scenarios is internally checkable.

Decision-theoretic coherence is weaker than the presentation. The stated score is midpoint absolute-risk reduction times breach value, minus €450 and the full false-alarm burden. That ranking puts I-08, I-09, and I-06 ahead of I-01, yet the selected set is I-01, I-02, and I-04. Ambiguity aversion can justify skipping I-08 and imputed I-06, but the deployable rule does not encode that criterion, so an operator following Section 1 would not reproduce Section 2. The same break appears in escalation: adjusted I-02 falls below the 0.60 cutoff, while I-09 meets Diagnosis Started, under 4 hours, and adjusted risk above 0.60, yet I-09 is deferred and I-02 is taken. The proposed 60% segment cap is also missed by a two-of-three Strategic portfolio.

I-07’s fallback is operationally impossible. Its deadline is about 13:30, and a swarm takes 45 minutes, but eligibility is postponed until a 14:00 reserve release. There is no marginal test of whether I-01’s roughly €1.7k base benefit exceeds one slot’s spillover onto six untreated diagnosis-stage cases, including shorter-clock I-06 and I-09. Spillover euro ranges are not tied to stated case values. The effect-evaluation plan uses untreated queue cases as controls despite the policy’s own interference, so those estimates would be biased by the spillover being measured.
