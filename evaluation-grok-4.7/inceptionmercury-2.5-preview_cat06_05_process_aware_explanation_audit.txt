4.7

The audit covers every required item and correctly flags the obvious full-trace leakages, but several judgments are substantively wrong, two case revisions invent or drop drivers, and both revised predictions are left standing on explanations the audit itself dismantles.

The leakage table gets the clear exclusions right: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, closure-time team, complete-trace document counts, the tied `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern, mutable `risk_score_current`, and end-of-case `last_resource_role`. The SQL defect on `prior_claim_count_before_t0` and the nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot` are also identified. Those calls are not enough to carry the section. `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is marked valid with severity none, and the remediation says to retain it when `t0 < fraud open`. At this prediction point that edge is future; the inequality keeps the leaked event. `final_handler_team` is only a lineage defect, although it is populated at closure and matches the t0 team in about 65% of claims with no as-of timestamp. `initial_reserve` is declared known at registration with no lineage support. `calendar_week` is a mild proxy, without the policy-change and holiday alignment. Appeal followed by another settlement decision is removed as future, but it is never stated that this repetition is normatively allowed after Partial or Deny, which is the distinction that separates it from the impossible or tie-broken closure pattern. Severity “None” is outside the required scale. “Usually after t0” for `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is too weak: under this t0 that cycle has not started.

The validation suite names prefix time, order, ties, complete-trace aggregation, and repeat decisions, then stops short of the required split among enabled, impossible, and merely future-possible activities. The process is called a normative DAG even though document cycles, parallel fraud investigation, and post-appeal reconsideration are not acyclic. Conformance is mislabeled causal. A frequent directly-follows edge is never distinguished from a normative rule, and section 5 later treats a DFG as if it were a normative model.

Section 3 preserves complexity, elapsed time, and queue length, and it puts region and channel in a fairness bucket rather than a causal one. It also invents `coverage_check_passed_time_delta`, lists `calendar_week` twice, and allows a claim of holiday operational bottlenecks. Repairable fields such as as-of team, frozen risk score, and prefix document counts are absent from the repair class, while `final_handler_team` is placed in a removal class whose boundary denies even repaired predictive use. The four standards — predictive validity, causal interpretation, actionability, and fairness review — stay collapsed into short “may / may not” cells.

Case X and Case Y are both marked retain. Appeal and the full variant dominate those local attributions, so the published probabilities need recomputation. Case X’s narrative says high complexity still leaves a standard fast track possible; high complexity is the signal that points away from the low-complexity fast track. Channel is retained in section 1 and then disappears. Case Y adds `initial_damage_complexity`, which is not a listed local driver, and drops the admissible negative queue signal and the unresolved reserve. Case Z correctly withholds a repaired narrative for the missing sequence index, post-t0 risk score, final role, and same-day document snapshot, but week 52’s holiday and policy confounding is left out.

The closing plan hits point-in-time storage, as-of timestamps, prefix recomputation, refit after removal, a thin fairness check, and drift monitoring. Explanation stability is absent, and conformance is aimed at a DFG rather than an explicit normative model.
