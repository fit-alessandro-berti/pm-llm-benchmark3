5.5

The required seven sections and the A–L table are present, and several directional calls match the case (C and G are unsafe shortcuts, D is time-limited, B is the control-preserving path, pharmacy service was sacrificed, and an audit tail outlived apparent operational recovery). The analysis then breaks on the distinctions it is supposed to make, and the redesign contradicts its own safety rules.

**Temporal reading is incomplete and partly wrong.** T2 omits the actual gateway (Emergency triage, 5,100 of 5,600), deferral, backlog, and spreadsheet invoice hold, even though invoice hold is a named move-on-log deviation. Cycle time, rework, and cancellation are dropped for T2–T4, so the before/during/after comparison the timeline is asked to make is mostly exception rates plus two T3 medians. T3 is given Backlog prioritization and Reconcile manual reservation as dominant variants; those activities appear in the stabilization DFG, and partial recovery has no DFG. T4 is summarized as approaching baseline. Hospital median did return to 8h, but pharmacy median/p90 remained 28h/74h versus 23h/49h, wholesale 58h/120h versus 36h/72h, traceability 2.2% versus 0.8%, cold-chain 0.9% versus 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control 0.5% versus 0.2%, rework 5.8% versus 3.5%, and fitting traces 76% versus 91%. Fitness and fitting-trace figures are never used. “2,300 cases in the audit-remediation queue” misreads the DFG: 2,300 entered audit remediation and 2,100 reached END.

**The resilience split contradicts the table and the DFG.** Section 2 correctly marks A as modify because authorization was bypassed, and H as modify. Section 3 then labels A true resilience and H concealed noncompliance. The fast lane does not pass through Manual authorization check: Emergency triage splits into manual authorization (2,500), skip authorization (1,600), and fast lane (1,000) directly into the snapshot. A is prioritized service with a missing control, not evidence that “balance was achieved.” F is labeled true resilience on the prompt’s claim that it reduced controlled-substance errors, without the residual 2.9% missing dual-control (baseline 0.2%) or the cold-chain documentation miss. J is labeled true resilience although the case states it is resilient only if the rules were explicit. K is separated from L even though both are post-hoc repair; L is described as having “fixed” and “restored” compliance, which treats after-the-fact remediation as equivalent to real-time control. The worst T2 path is missed: 1,800 packs go to dispatch without scan and without temporary release, and only 2,600 of the dispatch-without-scan volume then receives phone proof of delivery.

**The recovery model fails its own risk rules.** Customer tier is used as a proxy for product risk. Tier 3 (wholesale) is authorization plus deferral after four hours, so a controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive bulk order has no dual control, temperature record, or release gate. Several “minimum” controls require a scan while the scenario is a WMS/scan outage; there is no offline evidence substitute that can actually run in T2. Quality/release is not reinstated as a hard pre-dispatch gate. Backlog rank 3 is wholesale margin, which discards clinical and compliance risk and builds unfairness into the future process. Snapshot staleness is flagged at four hours in reconciliation, but release is still allowed on a ship-then-remediate SLA. The notation is labeled POWL and is unexplained indented text.

**Indicators and recommendations do not lock the lesson.** Targets such as hospital fill >95%, pharmacy fill >85%, and customer-satisfaction delta are not in the data (the only fill rates given are 91% and 61%). Nothing stops cycle time from looking recovered while the audit tail is open; conformance fitness is not an indicator. Controlled-substance and cold-chain protocol work is priority 3, below commercial and communications items, despite those exception rates rising about twelvefold to fourteenfold. Residual-risk text is often a restatement of the recommendation (“requires strict criteria,” “requires real-time remediation”).

**Section 7 reverses the analysis.** It accepts temporary relaxation of ambient authorization, which is pattern C, after the table retires C for every customer and product and Section 3 calls C concealed noncompliance. It also cites A as the proof that protection and control can be held together, which the fast-lane path does not show.
