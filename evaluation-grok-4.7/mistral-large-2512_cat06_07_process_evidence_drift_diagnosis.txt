6.2

The audit correctly separates the period into a pipeline break, a routing-mix change, a later performance shift, and an immature October scoreboard. That macro reading is the right one. The write-up still has factual reversals, mis-filed evidence, a retraining plan that would miss the new regime, and process-mining code that would not run and would corrupt the log it is meant to audit.

**1. Drift table.** All six types appear, and April-as-pipeline plus October-as-censoring are the right primary calls. Several cells put evidence in the wrong role. June’s lower V2 breach rate is evidence of a benign mix change; filing it as contradicting process drift treats a real routing change as if it were not process change. “No outcome-definition change” does not weigh against concept drift. Partner Z’s absent rural and device-age features are a reason the model cannot represent the new population, not evidence against a population shift. “No systematic censoring bias” is incompatible with the stated October backlog, which hits long resolution paths first, and with the observed breach rate falling to 0.16 while part waits and V3 share worsen. Covariate drift is under-called: June’s V2 share moving from about 35% to 52% is the clearest P(X) shift in the series, and discrimination mostly held. Concept drift is timed too late. Matured V3 breach is already 0.46 in July at 98% label maturity, against about 0.30–0.35 earlier, while V1 and V2 stay flat. July–October as one high-confidence label-delay block overstates July (98%) and August (93%).

**2. Timeline.** January–March, April’s rename and broken prefixes, May’s incomplete backfill, June’s remote-routing policy, and October’s non-comparable labels are handled in the right direction. The June paragraph reverses the variant table: V3 is 22% in May and 18% in June, then 22% in July. “18% → 22%” in the June confounder sentence is the July change. April’s 18% unmapped share is described as a slight distortion; it is the variant-level face of the same mapping failure as the 17% unknown-activity share. July is told as a small aggregate dip (AUROC 0.76, still-good calibration in the large: predicted 0.18 vs observed 0.19) and never uses the V3-specific jump that shows the parts path’s outcome rate has already changed. August and September are one block. August has no Partner Z, 93% maturity, part-wait 130 h, V3 breach 0.64, and predicted risk still 0.19 against an observed 0.28. September adds Partner Z (24%, slower labels, observed 0.45 vs 0.27) on top of a V3 conditional rate that is already 0.61. The stable 3.8–4.6 h diagnosis-to-remote-fix edge is unused, so the write-up never shows that the remote path’s duration did not move. Sign of miscalibration is also thin: April over-predicts (0.28 vs 0.19), June over-predicts (0.20 vs 0.15), August under-predicts (0.19 vs 0.28). October’s 0.23 vs 0.16 can be an immature-label artifact rather than a further model failure.

**3. Tests.** The required menu is present, including maturity curves, a recompute, stratified scores, a conditional-path check, reweighting, and missingness. Expected results cross wires. April is already 100% mature, so April metrics will not improve because labels mature; recovery belongs only to label repair, duplicate cleaning, and recomputing the cached pre-diagnosis count. Fixed-vintage backtesting is given that feature-repair result, which is a different experiment. A vintage test answers whether August–October rankings survive a common follow-up window. Lineage is named in the prompt and then collapsed into missingness. Partner Z “missing” rural and device-age fields were never in the model; that is absent specification, not item missingness, and no differential-missingness fact is supplied. Reweighting is not specified to be null in August, when Partner Z’s share is zero, and only informative in September. Within-variant AUROC versus ECE is not used as the split between a V3 level shift and a ranking failure. Control-flow DFG structure versus performance-DFG duration is not separated, yet “no new activities” is one of the claims that split would test.

**4. Decision.** Containment correctly freezes October and broken April features. The short-term plan then recalibrates on June–July because those months allegedly have stable variant mixes. They do not: June is the policy break, and July is the first month in which the matured V3 breach rate leaves the historical band while part-wait goes from 42 h to 65 h. A global calibrator fit there would chase June’s safer mix and July’s still-mild aggregate rate, then sit below the August base rate. Full retraining on January–March plus June–July, with August–September excluded as “overlapping drift,” leaves the model without mature cases from the supplier-delay regime it must learn once those labels finish. May is neither safe nor unsafe. Variant-specific modeling is left as a hypothetical even though the matured V1/V2 versus V3 table already shows a path-specific outcome change. There is no caveat that the score is issued at diagnosis, so the eventual variant is only a segment if the route is known at that prefix.

**5. Monitoring.** The monitor set and the maturity gate on AUROC, Brier, and ECE are the right containment logic. Data-quality failures are not given the same hard block on retraining. The sketch misses `discover_dfg`, calls `get_variants_sorted` and `filter_time_range(..., mode='cases_start')`, which are not the public APIs (`get_variants`; modes such as `events`, `traces_contained`, `traces_intersecting`), and never states what the two cohorts mean. Event-time slices count in-month activity and, after dropping out-of-window events, can invent directly-follows links; case-start slices match the monthly “cases started” table and are the right base for variant mix. Variants and the performance DFG are both taken from the event-filtered log. `format_dataframe` renames `activity`, `case_id`, and `timestamp` to the standard PM4Py names, after which `df['activity']` and the duplicate subset fail. Every `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` is rewritten to `Initial Diagnosis Completed` before measurement, which erases the April data-quality signal and can manufacture a second diagnosis. Deduping only identical case, activity, and timestamp misses the migration duplicates the audit described. The month-end bound sits at midnight on the last day, so that day’s events fall out. Case ids are read from event attributes while an event log iterates traces.

**6. Risks.** The four failure modes match the case and the mitigations point the right way: immature October labels bias breach risk down, an April calibrator would fit broken prefixes, the supplier shift has to be tested inside V3, and Partner Z needs a stratified comparison before any fairness claim. The rows stay generic. They do not cover the June global recalibration trap, training that never sees post-shift mature V3 cases, or the code’s own failure mode of imputing unknown activities as diagnoses and then treating the repaired log as evidence.

The grade stays near 6 because the month-by-month story a reviewer must reach is mostly visible and the six required sections are filled with real numbers. It stays well below 8 because a reversed mix statistic, a denied censoring mechanism, July’s V3 onset, a recalibration window built on a false stability claim, and an audit script that crashes and relabels unknowns are material, not cosmetic.
