The policy is decision-theoretically sound and operationally usable. It correctly refuses I-03 after Diagnosis Complete, separates I-05’s unchanged repeat from I-07’s new log, adjusts probabilities by segment, treats effects as uncertain rather than equal to `p_now`, prices queue spillover, keeps a reserve slot, and backs fairness and drift controls with concrete triggers. The base arithmetic for I-04, I-02, and I-09 checks out, and the adverse case is allowed to go net negative.

It is not nearly flawless. The stated “robust direct net exceeds spillover” rule does not actually clear I-02: its lower-bound net is about €950, below even the low spillover of roughly €1,200–€3,600 per slot, yet it is still Tier 1. The €20k untreated-case exposure is unexplained and a bit low versus the listed diagnosis-stage values, and the optimistic spillover band (€2k–€4k) sits under the answer’s own low-end formula (€3.6k). Adverse spillover (€8k–€16k) and the top of the optimistic benefit range are not derived. The robust-versus-expected-value criterion is never defined, so waiting on I-08 (second-highest midpoint net, lower bound negative) is a judgment, not a rule. Reserve review at 13:30–14:00 also largely misses I-07’s 1.5-hour deadline.
