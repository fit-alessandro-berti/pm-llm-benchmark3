3.5

The write-up covers every required section and the right mechanism list, but it is not a reliable analysis. Numbers are repeatedly attached to the wrong slice of the data, signatures contradict the evidence cited for them, and the proposed code does not measure service time.

The mechanism table fails its own job. Learning is defined as falling errors, then supported with 2.5% to 2.7%, a rise, and bins 16–30 are merged with the 31–50 figures. Fatigue is defined as slower work, then “supported” by the 76+ bin at 10.8 minutes, which is the fastest cell in the table. That cell is the speed-under-pressure pattern, not fatigue. Circadian evidence says late-shift service time “peaks” at 11.4 minutes despite lower WIP; night is slower at 15.2 minutes, early-late is 12.7, and the late shift’s queue stays about 66–70. Juniors are said to take simpler cases first; the source note says early complexity and later pressure both matter for them. Batching is described as 9–18 events at 15.2 minutes; 15.2 is the 6–15 position mean, while those batches are very short. The Bot_Risk contrast of 13.7% versus 4.8% rework is not in the data; those are the 76+ and 16–30 rework rates. Night staff are also conflated with the night shift.

The narrative repeats those swaps. Day shift is described as WIP falling into the 30–70 band; its queue grows from 38 to 75. Medium-WIP low-complexity service time (11.6 minutes) is paired with high-complexity error (5.6%). The all-resource high-WIP low-complexity cell (9.4 minutes, 5.8%) is attributed to juniors. Sequence position 76+ (10.8 minutes, 7.9%, queues 62 then 88) is treated as the late shift. Seniors’ later mean of 11.8 minutes is called stable even though they start at 15.9, and 2.2% is their low-WIP error, not their 4.9% high-WIP error. The speed-versus-quality point is stated, but the arithmetic under it is not trustworthy.

Section 3 names the requested comparisons and then implements different ones. `minutes_since_shift_start` is used as the outcome everywhere, including the regression, although it is elapsed shift time and is nearly collinear with sequence position. Service time is never computed from the two timestamps. `format_dataframe`, `cluster_log`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `filter_event_attribute_values`, `discover_batches`, and `get_rework_cases_per_activity` are called with parameters those functions do not have; the junior filter has a duplicated `value` argument and would not run. The experience split takes the first 20 dataframe rows per experience-month value, not the first 20 events of each resource-shift. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, path-performance filters, and a real within-resource model with WIP × complexity × position are absent.

Intervention 8 is given a benefit of incentivizing quality, which endorses punishment from raw late-shift errors. Intervention 6’s benefit is throughput from deferring quality checks. Capping every shift is not tied to the fact that 76+ events already occur inside a normal shift (about 459 minutes) or to day-shift queue growth. Random assignment is not linked to the risk of moving complex work onto juniors. Monitoring columns are generic rates, not mix-adjusted event-log checks.

The ethics section names selection, management-created pressure, automation, and punishment, then undercuts itself: it claims juniors get more complex work later, while aggregate complexity and high-priority share fall across the shift, and it still leaves the punitive option looking useful. Waiting time is defined as `start_timestamp - time:timestamp`, which reverses the usual service interval and is not waiting time. `queue_length_at_end` and `wip_at_end` are not in the log. Fairness is raw service-time spread, so seniors who absorb early complex work would look unfairly slow. Headings are not the required single top-level set.
