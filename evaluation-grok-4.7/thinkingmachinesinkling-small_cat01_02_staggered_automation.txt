6.5

The conclusion that a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not identified, and that expansion should be an embedded evaluation with a holdout, is the right decision. Estimands, staggered-adoption design, mechanisms, and threats are structured carefully and usually distinguish availability from use, medians from tails, and description from causation. That framework is undercut by quantitative errors large enough to change the sign of the main comparison.

The adopter pre/post table does not consistently report window means. Beta’s April–June fast-path share is 34, not 39, and rework is about 11.9, not 12.7. Gamma’s January–June rework and escalation averages are about 13.1 and 8.7, not 12.3 and 8.0. Delta and Epsilon pre-period escalation is pulled down to the January–February plateau, so the March fraud-desk jump inside the stated pre window disappears. The same plateau substitution hides Alpha and Beta’s April rework drop.

The prose then contradicts the table: April–June median increases are about +1.5 h (Alpha) and +2.5 h (Beta), not +7–10 h. Over that window Delta and Epsilon rose about +6–7 h, so the descriptive difference-in-differences is roughly −4 h, not “possible harm of +0 to +4 h.” The reported −5% to +8% median range is built on that sign error. July–August improvement is also mirrored in never-treated branches (Epsilon falls about as much as Alpha from the June peak), so the learning interpretation is stronger than the parallel recovery supports. The intensity split is wrong as well: Alpha’s high-intensity months average about 48 h once June is included, not 45.6 h.

Smaller but real problems: section 1 is over the word limit and makes causal claims it then denies; a common post-March fraud-desk indicator is collinear with month fixed effects; alternate-day assignment is mislabeled as randomization; and the relative-time code divides by a month-unit timedelta, which is not a reliable calendar-month offset. Cluster warnings and explicit non-causal comments are appropriate, but the arithmetic a reader would rely on is not.
