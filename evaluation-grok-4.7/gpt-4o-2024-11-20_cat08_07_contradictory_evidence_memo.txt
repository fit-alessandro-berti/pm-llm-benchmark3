The memo chooses a defensible hybrid and covers every required section, but it is not reliable enough for an executive decision. The central trade-off is stated backward: overall p90 moved from 31.0 to 38.5 days, yet the memo calls that a “reduced p90” and assigns the aggregate figure to complex cases. Those segment tails are different numbers (+12 days for medium-risk apartments, +25 for permit-sensitive old buildings, −8 for simple houses).

The evidence table fails the required separation. Its “fact” cells are causal claims: “rising efficiency,” “complexity amplified,” “increased errors,” “streamlined ownership,” “missing task leads to SLA breaches.” Supported facts are the metric movements, sample windows, model scores, alignment moves, and DFG shares. Complaint-within-30-days (6.1% to 9.8%) is also merged with the billing-to-escalation edge (3% to 8%). Fitness 0.94 is treated as evidence that paths were simplified; fitness only says the model can replay the log. The precision drop to 0.51, against a baseline of 0.79, is the signal that skipped survey, manual activation, repeat visits, and early billing remain freely combinable. Generalization (0.70 to 0.56) is omitted.

Metric-conflict explanations point the right way and stay too causal and too thin. They leave out the mix shift (pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings), segment labels missing before week 3, cost truncation after week 4, and completion-only timestamps. They also skip the mechanisms that make the conflicts intelligible: install-to-activation delay in apartments, the new billing-to-complaint tail, failed-visit-to-second-visit delay where coordinator load is high, survey-team utilization falling from 78% to 52% while second-visit utilization rises from 66% to 92%, and context dying when a coordinator is absent.

Alternatives A–E are present, but C and E blur together, and scaling coordinator ownership is not conditioned on 93% utilization or the absence test. Value-of-information labels M1, M4, M6, and M7 “high” without saying what result would reinstate survey skipping, keep billing after activation, or stop simple-house rollout. M1 and M6 are near-duplicates. M5, the measurement aimed at the ownership model being scaled, is ranked only medium. The rollout “threshold” is “controllable risk and cost,” which is not an evidence bar.
