7.5

The memo is a complete, constraint-compliant hybrid recommendation (E, not national scale or full rollback) with real fact/interpretation/assumption separation, correct use of the main process-mining artifacts, and a usable value-of-information ranking. It is not nearly flawless.

Material problems:
- “Easy majority” is wrong. Simple low-risk houses are 34% of the pilot; apartments plus old buildings are 47%. The sentence overstates who benefited.
- “Handovers fell from 34% to 12% of cases” misstates the metric. The evidence is cases with 3+ handovers.
- The apartment segment that drives the skip-rule decision (median −2 days, p90 +12, first-time-right −8 pp, complaints +5 pp, 29% share) is never quantified. Business and rural figures are also omitted.
- Coordinator scope is ambiguous. The memo both limits R to simple houses and “retains coordinator ownership” generally, so the operating model for about two-thirds of cases is not implementable as written.
- Rollout gates conflict: p90/p95 “not more than 5% worse” versus p90 “non-inferior within 2 days,” while the false-negative bar is only “low enough.” Several other cutoffs (85%, 8%, 6.5%, 8 weeks, 99%) are unexplained.

Smaller problems:
- Low generalization is glossed as the model being “not disciplined,” which confuses generalization with precision. Fitness versus precision itself is explained correctly.
- One interpretation cell ties the skip rule to simple-house gains; the redesign skipped surveys for some medium-risk addresses, and simple-house gains line up with merged validate/schedule and fewer handovers.
- The memo underplays the pilot mix shift and incomplete segment attributes before week 3, then still recommends scaling the simple-house path.
- M1 is ranked first without the harm of re-randomizing a skip rule already associated with large tail and quality losses, or early-stop limits. M7 is correctly treated as an immediate control despite a lower information rank, but the memo’s funding order (M6, M1, M4, M3) does not match that ranking and drops M7.

No invented core metrics, no claim that one design wins every metric, and the five required conflicts are substantively explained. Those strengths keep it well above a weak memo; the misstatements and the non-operational threshold/ownership gaps keep it well below an excellent one.
