7.3

The business reading is strong and mostly consistent: noise, scope mismatch, label drift, missing external evidence, true control failure, and zero-cost data-rule breaches are separated, confidence is not collapsed into severity, and CL-609/CL-610 are correctly treated as policy failures despite perfect fitness. The deductions are for concrete errors and gaps, not tone.

The materiality scale fits the later scores, including high severity with incomplete confidence on CL-602 and low severity with high confidence on retries and telemetry. CL-604 is rightly treated as a P2/P3 sequencing failure on a EUR 90k claim, not as a cheap reorder. Silent `tau` on CL-605 is correctly called fitting. P3 is not wrongly pasted onto every missing control.

The main analytical mistake is in section 6. CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous and contain no hidden transition; folding a “hidden-fit structural path” into those P1/P2 breaches conflates CL-605’s harmless `tau` with a data-predicate failure. That is exactly the distinction the section is supposed to hold. “P2 not fully triggered” on CL-609 is also loose: at EUR 18k, MEDIUM, and no bank change, P2 is not triggered at all; only P1 forbids auto-approval. The 5–50k band is therefore not the same control as Fraud Screen plus Manual Approve, but priority 2 still collapses it into that mandate while priority 10 reopens it.

CL-611 is scored as a benign alias while the same answer treats a post-hoc mapping change as a live alternative. The case action (“normalize synonyms”) is ahead of the discriminator. CL-604’s confidence is marked high while clock skew and an alias approval remain open. Pattern F mentions CL-604 only as sequencing pressure, even though Fraud Screen is absent from the log, not merely late.

Remediation covers prevention, detection, correction, model repair, correlation, and data-aware checks, but the portfolio overclaims. The payment gate does not by itself fix synchronous auto-approvals unless the token is rechecked against amount, risk, and bank-change age. Ranking investigation of the paid EUR 480k bank-change case fourth, behind build work, underweights an already irreversible exposure. Several validation metrics are not usable as written (`riskLOW`, blank targets, “unpaid control skips”).

The code keeps severity off the raw-cost column and records the right policy flags for the zero-cost cases, but it is not a faithful reproduction. `parse_event_log_string` consumes one comma-separated trace per line, so `CL-601` and the other case ids become fake activities and shift every alignment. CL-604 has five synchronous moves (Register, Validate, Assess Loss, Manual Approve, Notify), not four. The group-by aggregates deviation labels, cost, and amount; it does not aggregate move patterns, risk levels, or policy-overlay flags. The rank is still a severity-then-amount sort, and it disagrees with the closing judgment that places CL-610 ahead of CL-609.

Sections 1–4 would score higher alone. The hidden-move mix-up, the middle-band policy slip, and a required code block that does not reproduce the stated traces keep it well short of nearly flawless.
