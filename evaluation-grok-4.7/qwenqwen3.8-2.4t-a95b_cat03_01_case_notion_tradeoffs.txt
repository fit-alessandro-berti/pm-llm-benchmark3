It is not nearly flawless.

The divergence definition says a split produces branching in the flattened case. Flattening does the opposite: `O1`’s parallel `P1`/`D1` and `P2`/`D2` streams, and `O3`’s `P3`/`D2` and `P4`/`D3` streams, are forced into one total order. Section 3 never states the resulting false directly-follows edges (`e11`→`e12`, `e15`→`e16`, `e13`→`e14`, `e16`→`e17`). Those edges are the main DFG distortion of divergence, and the eight examples stay on duplication, hiding, and truncation instead.

The duplication ratings are not on one scale. Item copying is 24/16 (1.5×) and Order copying is 42/31 (1.35×), but Order is rated High and Item only Medium/high. Order “convergence” is also used for shared milestone timestamps, which is not the collapse-into-one-trace idea defined in section 1.

The Python block would not run against a PM4Py `OCEL`. `ocel.events` and `ocel.objects` are DataFrames, and event–object links live in `ocel.relations`, not in `event["ocel:related_objects"]`. The same object is then passed to `pm4py.ocel_flattening` and the discovery calls. The shared-event diagnostic that the outline relies on is therefore empty or an exception. `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is also described as a way to compare secondary lifecycles; equivalent-execution clustering only groups similar lifecycles.
