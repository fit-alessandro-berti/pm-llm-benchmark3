3.5

The write-up has the required nine sections and a real sense of the specialization–pooling trade-off, but the capacity arithmetic, resource ranking, scenario table, and code are not reliable enough to support the recommendation.

The offered-load section is the clearest failure. It treats the 08:00–10:00 block as three people and about 360 minutes, leaving out Chen, who is on shift. Breaks are 30 minutes, not about 10. Complex tails are roughly a doubling of the ordinary midpoint at probabilities 0.15–0.20, not a generic “+20%.” Expected diagnostic work is not about 23/28/32 minutes once those tails are included, and ME/ES cases add a second activity’s work content. Speed multipliers never enter the load. Farah at 1.40 is the slowest diagnostic resource, yet section 3 reserves P1 work to “Ada/Farah/Emil” as the fastest set. The evening block is only Emil, who cannot do Mechanical; that structural gap is missed. U2 and U4 are not given a load analysis. The “87.5% utilization” line is presented as an observation from an understated numerator and an understated denominator.

The objectives are not operational. Objective 1 is an unaggregated indicator with an undefined weight and an undefined P2/P3 target. Objective 2 adds utilization variance to a queue length times a handover penalty, which mixes units and does not measure robustness across U1–U5. Parallelism is “default parallel,” with no comparison of the 8-minute coordination delay against critical-path time, the 7-minute handover, or the extra 5-minute mechanical-versus-electrical/software team penalty.

The three policies omit most of the required dispatch machinery. Specialization does not say who runs ME/ES, who does Intake and Validation, whether P1 preempts, or how fixed queues avoid the team-change penalty. “Always sequential” is an extra choice, not a consequence of specialization. Full pooling names SERPT plus priority without a score, a tie-break, a handover penalty, or a preemption rule, and “go sequential if waiting exceeds 5 minutes” does not say what is being waited for. The hybrid has no reservation fraction, no once-only cap, and no 10% remaining-time restart rule. It lists both “reduces starvation” and “P2/P3 starvation.”

Section 4 does not meet the requested table. There is no scenario axis, no P2 SLA, and no utilization fairness. Cells such as P1 SLA 80/95/92 are point values with no model, no seed, and no uncertainty. Those figures also contradict section 5: pooling is shown with a better P1 SLA and high resilience, then hybrid is recommended because it protects P1. The fallback is likewise inconsistent: low availability switches to specialization, while the rationale says specialization fails when Chen is absent. A handover burden above 10% of time and a P2/P3 “delay” of 2 hours are undefined against an 8-hour and a 24-hour SLA. U2, U4, and U5 never affect the choice. All three policies are shown at or above the 90% individual-utilization fairness limit, and that constraint is not applied.

Secondary effects miss the validation pile-up the prompt asks for, the restart penalty from repeated preemption, and unfair load on the only in-shift skill. The gaming story is backwards: delaying Intake does not reset an elapsed SLA clock that has already started.

The event design omits queue entry, eligible set, skill used, planned versus actual service, pause/resume, handover, setup, shock flag, and SLA clock. Drift is reduced to comparing BPMN models. Bottleneck migration is not given a method, and case-level PM4Py service/waiting time is not distinguished from stage time.

The Python does not implement the paper model. Skill multipliers, shifts, breaks, overtime, queues, diagnostics, ME/ES branches, setup, conditional handover, and preemption are absent or unused. Demand is the first two blocks repeated three times (48 cases instead of the stated 42) on one global clock, so later activities can start before Intake finishes. The non-specialization branch assigns a resource name and then indexes it as a dict. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called; the importer path and `case_id=` argument do not match PM4Py’s dataframe API; analysis is skipped on import failure; and `df` is then returned unbound. Nothing prints a base-versus-U1–U5 comparison. That is a crashed stub, not a labeled scaffold of the omitted mechanisms.

Section 9 correctly notes that fixed means are not a Poisson process and that the range shape is assumed. It then proposes a backtest against historical P1 data the scenario does not supply, and a single-policy pilot that cannot separate the three policies.
