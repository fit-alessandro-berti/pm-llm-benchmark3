The delay decomposition is coherent and the case arithmetic checks out. Upstream, branch queue, service, join-ready as the max completion, synchronization, downstream lag, final service, and total flow are defined so that \(L_c=U_c+T_c+D_c+P_{cF}\) with \(T_c=\max_i X_{ci}\), and the write-up correctly shows \(\sum_i(Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci})=3T_c\). The F02 overlap example makes the double count concrete. Recomputed case rows, sync waits, critical-branch margins (A 15, B 120, C 12 case-minutes), cohort totals (235 / 732 / 176 / 240 = 1,383), and the three isolated counterfactuals (15, 92.5, and 176) all match the tables. Zero-benefit cases are identified for the right reason: a noncritical branch does not move the join. B is ranked by last-completion frequency, queue-plus-service, tail margin, and counterfactual gain, not by mean service, where A is longest. The F03 idle gap and the two-resource replay (only F11 waits 5 minutes; 171 case-minutes versus the log; a third resource adds only those 5) are the right way to show that final-station “queue” can mask or fake a branch effect. Assumptions and non-identifiability are stated explicitly.

The mandated script is the real weakness. `pm4py.get_service_time` is called with `activity_key` and `case_id_key` and then indexed as a per-activity map. The usual public signature returns one scalar and does not take those keywords, so the run can die before the required prints. The prose still states that this call reproduces A = 50, B = 37.5, C ≈ 20.1667, and F = 20. That claim is not established by the code as written.

Linear n=12 P90s (74, 124.5, 114.5, 21.8, 24) are disclosed, then used as tail KPIs they are too brittle to support. The lag-constant flow table (means 114.00 and 107.54) appears before the text shows that those starts are not a feasible two-resource schedule.
