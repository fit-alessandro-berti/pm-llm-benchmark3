The delivered write-up is strong on estimands, competing risks, and leakage, but it is incomplete and several headline numerical claims are wrong. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Format.** Required sections 8 and 9 are absent, and section 7 stops mid-sentence (“`P1` is designated highest priority”). There is no code block, so none of the seven code requirements (in-memory table, two estimands, three transfer encodings, risk sets that keep open cases, a stratified comparison, a PM4Py prefix parse, and printed summaries) are met. Limitations are only scattered earlier, not prioritized as requested.

**What holds up.** Time origin, the 72-hour horizon, and the split between already-observed breaches, pre-horizon censoring, and competing exits are right. \(E_1=S_{\mathrm{disp}}(72)\), \(E_2=1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\), and late-resolution \(E_3\) are well defined and tied to different decisions. The four worked cases (`S03`, `S09`, `S06`, `S08`) are encoded correctly. T1/T2/T3 are justified by the data: all three transfers are escalated P1 (`S08`, `S21`, `S32`). Section 3’s naive-case critique is concrete and accurate (resolved-only 33.3% vs 28.6%; means 54.7h vs 45.6h; P1 resolved-only breach 0/2; 5 of 9 already-realized >72h rows are non-resolved; P3 concentration of withdraw/reject; B’s 0/5 P3 `AutoCheck`-first compliance). Most Kaplan–Meier / Aalen–Johansen point estimates check out: A \(S(72)=64/189\approx33.9\%\), B \(735/1768\approx41.6\%\), \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\) 29.2% and 31.6%, T3b \(E_2\) 62.3% and 62.1%, and the P2/P3 stratum figures (47.6%, 20.0%, 34.3%, 40.0%, resolution 38.1%→48.6%). The SAP is specific to \(N=32\) and missing intermediate timestamps. The same shortcut gives about 48.7% for B, not the stated 48.5%; the proper \(S(72)\) is about 42.8%. Reported \(E_2\) values are inconsistent with those \(S(72)\) values (A’s components no longer sum to 1). A correct S4 leaves B’s \(E_2\) at 68.4% and raises A’s to about 71.9%, so the claimed \(E_2\) reversal does not occur.

- Direct standardization to the stated weights \(8/32\), \(14/32\), \(10/32\) gives \(E_1\) 35.4% vs 42.5% and \(E_2\) 68.8% vs 67.5%, not 36.4% vs 41.9% and 72.1% vs 67.4%. The standardized \(E_2\) gap is overstated by roughly 3.5pp. Stratum direction (P2 better, P3 worse) still stands.
