7.5

The diagnosis is strong and well structured: March is correctly treated as a semantic/provenance break, later rework and queueing are not collapsed into logging drift, and the monitoring design can separate recurrence from new drift. The required sections, columns, and seven decision rules are present, and most DFG distances and baseline deltas check out.

It is not nearly flawless. The queueing “sanity check” invents a team of about 13 and then contradicts itself: a 1.65 utilization multiplier cannot turn ρ 0.70 into 0.94 (that product exceeds 1). The service share of +1–2 h, and the claim that service explains “at most” 8–12% of the cycle-time rise, are not upper bounds supported by the only measured change (+0.6 h on `Assess`). “86–96% of every month” is false for July (about 85%). The “largest month-on-month step” column is wrong for fast-track share (−12 pp in Sep–Oct, not −10 pp in Apr–May) and reports the largest increase rather than the largest absolute step for cases, median, and p90.

Part A does not run: baseline selection duplicates columns, so `float(base[met])` fails. Part B does not match the PM4Py API: `format_dataframe` takes `case_id`, not `case_id_key`, and has no `resource_key`; `conformance_alignments` returns a list, not `(alignments, fitness)`; `get_variants` on a dataframe yields counts, so `len(v)` breaks.
