4.8

The write-up has the required eight-part shape and the right qualitative cast of regimes (baseline, weekend, outage, mix shift, later extra staff). It also correctly treats active service time as distinct from sojourn and refuses a steady-state reading of the outage day. Those merits do not survive the quantitative and logical failures below. Under a strict reading the report is not reliable enough to act on.

**Segmentation does not match its own estimates.** R0 is written as 2026-04-06–04-14, which includes the weekend, while 8.3 arrivals/h and 8.2 completions/h only fit the seven weekdays (about 8.35 and 8.30). Rw’s dates “2026-04-06/07/08/09/10 Sat–Sun” are Monday–Friday; the actual first weekend is 04-11/12. The quoted 3.7 and 3.6 per hour match all eight weekends, but the WIP band 12–16 and “baseline” label match only the early ones. R2 and R3 likewise fold weekends into ranges whose rates, staff, and service figures are weekday figures. R2 arrivals are 798/7/12 = 9.5/h, not 9.4; completions are about 8.4/h, not 8.5. R3’s 9.1 and 9.6/h are not the mean of either the three weekdays (about 9.6 and 10.1) or the five-day window.

**Backlog accounting is wrong where it matters.** On 04-15, arrivals − completions = +47. The 16th recovers only 17 and the 17th another 13, so the outage surplus is not cleared on the 16th. Mean WIP also rises from 47 to 52 on the 16th while net flow is negative. Calling R3 a “steady state” repeats that mistake: WIP is still falling (43, 36, 32), completions still exceed arrivals, and the intervention time inside 04-29 is unknown. Cumulative arrivals − completions from 04-06 through 04-28 are about +128 cases, while reported mean WIP only moves from the high teens to about 50. That broken inventory identity is never discussed, so Little’s law and “capacity pressure” rest on an unchecked balance.

**Utilization is internally inconsistent.** Assumption A1, mean ≈ median × (1+ln 2)/ln(p90/p50) ≈ 1.3 × median, is false for these ratios: at 46/25 the factor is about 2.8, not 1.3. The stated loads 0.70, 0.55, 0.95, and 0.80 do not follow from A1. Weekend ρ = 0.55 is especially implausible. With any mean at or above the median, weekend pressure is similar to or higher than baseline weekday pressure, because staff falls by more than demand. A1 applied to R2 pushes ρ through 1, which is the claim the completion shortfall actually supports; 0.95 understates it.

**Section 4’s pooled contrasts are mis-measured, and the paradox is not a paradox.** Weekday mean WIP is about 34.9, not 32. Complex share moves from about 21% in week 1 to about 44% in week 4; 19% and 47% are single days. The unweighted 33% complex figure is fine, but peak concentration pooled across days is about 32%, not 28%. Post-intervention median service near 33 minutes is worse than the baseline near 25 and slightly better than the high-mix period near 35. WIP falls only relative to that high-mix period, and it remains well above baseline. A pooled reader would not conclude that “service times improved.”

**Dispersion claims contradict both the vectors and the code.** Sample Fano factors (variance/mean, ddof=1) are about 3.2, 4.1, 10.1, and 1.3, not 1.6, 2.4, 6.9, and 1.2. Coefficients of variation are about 0.62, 0.69, 1.03, and 0.55, not 0.38, 1.12, 1.80, and 0.30. Lag-1 dependence on the smooth 04-07 and 04-15 arrival profiles is strongly positive; the outage trough is in completions (zeros from roughly 11:00–14:00), not in the arrival series the text describes. The Ljung-Box p-value is asserted and never computed. The code’s Fano, CV, and lag-1 definitions are the standard ones, so a rerun would falsify section 5.

**Little’s law is three different calculations.** For an opening-hours mean WIP, W = L/λ_hour = 20/8.3 ≈ 2.4 hours. The matrix instead reports 20/(8.3×12) = 0.20 and labels it days (4.8 hours), i.e. the calendar stock/daily-throughput ratio, after section 1 has defined WIP as an opening-hours mean. The code then does `W/(lam*12)/24`, about 0.008 days. R0 and weekends are marked `applicable` despite unknown 07:00 backlog, frozen overnight queues, and snapshot timing that the table does not identify. R2 is called near-stationary while WIP rises and about 90 weekday cases accumulate. Implied sojourn is never set next to the 25-minute median, which is the check that would show waiting dominates touching time.

**The code does not implement the prose.** `staff >= 7` fires before the date rules, so 04-29 and 04-30 become `HighStaff` and `PostIntervention` collapses toward 05-01. 04-17 matches none of the weekday rules and is labeled `Weekend`. There is no printed pooled row. PM4Py is correctly kept in comments and the aggregates are correctly described as not an event log; `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is omitted. The heuristic is not marked, in the output, as non-definitive.

**Implications inherit the bad numbers.** A 1 FTE increase “reducing WIP by 30%” is an endpoint comparison on a transient, not a steady effect (regime means are closer to a mid-teens percent drop). “Two days are lost” does not describe a 180-minute outage. The fixed-6-FTE policy warning is the right kind of Simpson/pooling point, and the data-collection list is directionally right, but both sit on top of the clearance, utilization, and flow-time errors.
