5.5

The write-up covers every required section and the recommendation is aimed at cross-scenario SLA risk rather than base-case mean delay. The numerical and implementation apparatus does not support the claims made from it.

The load math is wrong and not internally consistent. Midpoint mixture expectations are Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, and Validation 15.74, not 6.90, 26.15, 32.49, 37.50, and 15.42. Block-weighted type counts are about M 9.84, E 11.06, S 7.86, ME 8.25, ES 4.99, not 10.0, 10.9, 7.8, 8.1, and 5.2. Those inputs flow into the 473 / 786 / 488 minute loads. U2 keeps total arrivals fixed and only reweights the mix, so electrical-path volume in 12:00–16:00 rises by roughly 20%, not by a doubling. That assumption is never stated in Section 1, while Section 2 still calls the shock a doubled E/ES demand. The offered-load bounds of “15–20 person-hours” and “65–90%” are too loose to audit.

Staffing facts used for bottlenecks are wrong. Chen is software-capable from 08:00–16:00, so morning software is not Farah alone. Emil’s break is 16:00–16:30, not during 12:00–14:00. The sharpest base cliff is 16:00–16:30, when only Deepa is on duty; the text treats 16:00–18:00 as Deepa plus Emil. P3’s 24-hour target is almost non-binding inside a single 08:00–20:30 day, but O1 still treats P3 shortfall as if the one-day model can measure it. Unfinished cases are closed at 20:30 plus 120 minutes, which still passes P2 and P3, so the SLA rates are biased upward. That bias is not disclosed.

Policy text contradicts itself and the code. P-Spec simultaneously forbids overflow, allows Ada onto electrical when mechanical is empty, and gives Chen a static 50/50 E/S split. P-Pool’s published score (priority, expected remaining time, and aging) is not what the simulator sorts on. P-Hybrid’s prose uses stage wait, a 0.3 sequential-time parallel threshold, and a P3 donor-utilization rule; the code uses time since arrival, parallelizes every P1/P2 ME/ES case, and overflows P3 after 120 minutes. “A case may be preempted at most once” is applied to the preempting P1, and the victim’s stored remaining time is never used on restart.

Section 8 does not run. `order: bool = True` sits between `@dataclass` and the class, which is a syntax error. Every case is placed in the intake queue before the clock starts, and intake dispatch never checks arrival time, so later arrivals are eligible at 08:00. Handover and setup occupy a resource but are omitted from utilization. The Section 4 ranges are nevertheless attributed to this scaffold. With overlapping SLA bands, the statement that P-Hybrid dominates \(J_{\text{SLA}}\) is stronger than the answer’s own uncertainty allows.

Sections 5–7 and 9 are the strongest part: sticky routing versus pooling, Farah’s slow multipliers, validation as a downstream bottleneck, auditable overflow triggers, the required event attributes, and a P1-protected shadow then day-level pilot. Those arguments are why the grade stays at mid-scale. The capacity results, scenario magnitudes, and policy comparison table are not reliable enough to carry the recommendation.
