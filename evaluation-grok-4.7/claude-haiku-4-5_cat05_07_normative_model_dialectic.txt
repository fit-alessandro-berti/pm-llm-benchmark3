4.5

The review is structurally complete and many case labels are directionally defensible, but it is not reliable as a conformance judgment. It invents record facts, misstates controlling rules, contradicts stipulated evidence, and delivers a revised POWL that cannot express the overlays it claims to govern.

Stipulated evidence is mishandled. SUP-708 states that no transaction occurred before sanctions clearance; the brief, matrix, and judgment repeatedly treat that timeline as unknown and condition severity on it. Missingness rose from 2% to 29%, yet the write-up repeatedly uses 27% and an unsupported count of about 80 cases. Email chains, flood-status memos, a CRM backlog item, a 2026-05-15 PD-3 issue date, and a EUR 50,000 auto-approval cap are not in the materials. The EUR 20,000 FD-7 cap is also applied to SUP-703, a June pilot case outside that directive.

Authority is blurred. Notify_Requester is required by M0, not by the text of CS-9. CS-9 separates requester from manual approver; SUP-715 concerns the quality reviewer and approver under a disclosure rule, and the prosecution section’s “no undisclosed conflict” wording reverses the fact. SUP-712 is a segregation breach that fits M0, not a control-flow breach. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation is never analyzed. SUP-704’s critical status, cap, monitoring, and next-business-day review are given, then treated as unconfirmed. Retrospective label approval is accepted for SUP-713 while retrospective authority is rejected for SUP-714, without a stable theory of when later ratification counts.

The dialectic is uneven. Prosecution does not clearly concede the genuine defects it was required to concede: duplicate registration, label equivalence, CRM linkage, the missing information loop, and ambiguous scope. Defense invents “imminent amendment” evidence and understates SUP-703. Concurrency is required and barely argued. The 15-case “27% compliant” rate treats a curated edge-case sample as an operational compliance rate. Severity for SUP-708 is both severe and medium-high.

The revision misses the modeling task. POWL natively supports `Operator.LOOP`; the claim that it cannot express a repeatable `Request_More_Info` is false, and the XOR approximation allows only one request, so SUP-707 still does not fit. The code has no emergency branch in which activation precedes quality and manual approval. The auto-approval branch still sits after a mandatory quality check, so it contradicts PD-3 and excludes SUP-702. The collection rules then order quality after sanctions, contradicting the concurrent partial order. The same `Activate_Supplier` and `Close_Case` objects are reused across submodels. Withdrawals, required in the scope rules, are absent. The proposal is split across two Python blocks, and the second depends on the first.

Useful residue: SUP-701, SUP-702, SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-709, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are classified on broadly sound dominant grounds, expiry is taken seriously, and the data schema does ask for stable notification IDs, aliases, versions, and timestamps. Those merits do not repair the evidence and model-semantics failures.
