The diagnosis is evidence-heavy and mostly accurate: edge counts are not treated as a variant probability, fitness 0.91 is correctly separated from the 68% fitting-trace rate, the fast path’s cost edge is kept at 9% after rework rather than reversed, and segment medians are not fabricated by summing edge medians. The three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms—policy lanes, evidence acquisition, and a high-value resolution cell—and each states a real sacrifice. The experiment plan’s shadow mode, intent-to-treat warning, and refusal to use noncompliant dispatch as a control are strong.

The score is held down by concrete faults. “Escalation board takes 42 hours” misstates the complaint-to-board sojourn; the board itself is a 45-minute daily slot, and the 42 hours are the preceding edge (median), with p90 130. Listing `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close` (2,320) inside the fast corridor is inconsistent with `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (2,300) once escalation inflows are acknowledged. Flow is not conserved elsewhere either: listed terminals are 3,015 + 220 + 390 = 3,625 of 5,000 cases, and several activities have inflow/outflow gaps. That censoring is central to the “failure after close” claim and is never stated.

The evidence edge (mean 34 / p90 88 hours) is treated as process tail latency. Much of it is customer response time; the portal can shorten it, but the diagnosis does not separate customer time from reviewer time. Mid-risk devices, explicitly in scope, are never routed.

The experiment section contradicts itself. Stage 0 imposes the R1 hard stop immediately, which is the main manual-inflow shock, while Redesign B is sequenced first specifically to free capacity before that shock; no bridge is specified for the 82% utilization cliff. Redesign A’s go bar is still “fitting traces materially above 68%,” the pre-containment figure, after the plan itself says Stage 0 must not be credited to A. Abandonment go/stop bands leave a gap around 2–3 points, and one go metric is “per completed claim,” which abandonment can game. The 15–35% manual-inflow range is the prompt’s generic rule-update effect, then used as if it were this lane design’s estimate.
