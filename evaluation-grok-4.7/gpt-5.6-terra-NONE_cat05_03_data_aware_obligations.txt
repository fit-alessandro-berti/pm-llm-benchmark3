6.4

The write-up is complete and often precise, but it is not nearly flawless. One highlighted case is misclassified, the formalization does not match the case results, and the checker misses the same-timestamp semantics the narrative itself depends on.

The largest error is TX-302. At release the state is EUR 180,000, MEDIUM, GB, CORPORATE, so R1 triggers. `Enhanced_Review` at 09:20 is before release at 09:40, and nothing after that review changes amount, risk, jurisdiction, or customer type. R4 never invalidates it. R1 is therefore satisfied by the review. EX-77 is also well supported, but it is surplus. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`, “satisfied only by waiver,” and the section 4 claim that a bad signature would flip the case to `NONCOMPLIANT` are all false. The case is `COMPLIANT` even if EX-77 is ignored. That misses the contrast with TX-310, whose only review is a `Standard_Review`.

The other release classifications are largely defensible. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 follow the event-time state, the 20% tests (56.25%, 4.55%, 22.88%, 27.78%), the standard-versus-enhanced distinction, and the TX-311 `event_index` tie-break. TX-305 is correctly `INDETERMINATE`. TX-310 may be `INDETERMINATE` given contradictory approval evidence, but the findings row says the exemption is not established and the standard review does not meet R1. On that wording the result is `NONCOMPLIANT`, not indeterminate. The table overclaims relative to the later discussion.

Section 2 is uneven. The prose time model is sound: blanks are non-updates, fill is forward only, and event, effective, record, and evidence time are separated. The pseudo-logic then defines `MaterialChange` as any rule-relevant attribute change “including” a >20% increase. Under that definition, TX-306’s move from 220,000 to 230,000 sits between the enhanced review and release and would defeat R1. The finding still says `COMPLIANT`. R3’s satisfaction formula existentially quantifies a list-update variable that is not tied to the update inside `R3Trigger`. R4 is described as always requiring a fresh review, although it only invalidates a review for amount-dependent duties.

The code meets the structural checklist and does not use a case-to-label lookup. It still does not implement the stated semantics. Review, approval, screen, and material-change currency are compared on timestamps with `>=` / `<`, so a later same-timestamp change is treated as not after the review. That is exactly TX-311: jurisdiction becomes DE at index 4 after the review at index 3, but both stamps are 16:20 and the review is treated as current. Predecessor diagnostics also omit `event_index`, so the tie-break is invisible. R2 marks the trigger indeterminate unless both risk and customer type are known, even though either HIGH or PEP is sufficient, and it never checks that the enhanced review postdates that trigger. R3 fires on any later list update without checking that the added jurisdiction is the release jurisdiction. `coarse_status` labels the weak EX-88 path as a deviation rather than `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The code would not flag TX-302, which quietly contradicts the narrative.

Sections 6 and 7 are the strongest. The control-flow failure modes go beyond the six required ones and use the right cases, though TX-311 is event-time change rather than final-state leakage; TX-305 is the cleaner leakage example. The logging recommendations cover immutable release payloads, split timestamps, rule version, exemption scope, provenance, and stable ordering.
