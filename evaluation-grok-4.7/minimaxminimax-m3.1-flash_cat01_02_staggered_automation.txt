The answer is strong on the core scientific task: it separates availability from use, treats rollout as a selected package rather than pure software, keeps median and p90 apart, flags fraud-desk interference and small-cluster limits, and ties expansion to a design that improves identification. The hand calculations that can be rebuilt from the levels (pre/post means, control changes, and the −4.2 h / −6.2 h launch contrasts) are accurate, and the decision is operational rather than estimator-naming.

It is not nearly flawless. The main quantitative tables drop minus signs, so several rows do not audit: rework shown as 0.42 and +1.57 cannot produce 1.98, and the CSAT components do not produce +0.083 unless both changes are negative. Gamma’s median decline is printed as an unsigned increase of 6.47 h. Narrative and the claim ladder recover the correct direction, but the headline table can be misread. Estimand horizons are also loose: Gamma is described as “through month +2” when only t=0 and t=+1 exist, and the CSAT contrast refers to months 3–5 beyond the panel. Section 5 attributes a later absolute median improvement to Beta, whose mature median remains above its pre-period mean; section 4 had this right. Scenario endpoints (−10% to +5%, and the tail analogue) are stipulated rather than derived, and “credible” parallel trends in the claim ladder sit poorly with the stated failure mode that those trends are implausible.
