4.8

The design covers the requested sections and correctly keeps objections latent, applicant delays separate from staff work, and demand time-varying. It is not a sound simulation specification: several methods contradict the data or the non-stationary setting, and the code does not follow PM4Py or the stated policies.

The warm-up plan is the largest design error. Complex-variance medians are about 140 days, with a p90 near 260 days, and arrivals have multi-month seasons, so there is no steady state a 30-day warm-up can reach. Dropping the first 10% of a 24-month run can also cut into a peak season. A non-stationary twin needs a dated start, a realistic initial backlog, at least one full seasonal cycle, and metrics reported by season. Common random numbers for paired policy comparisons are absent.

Service and waiting time are handled inconsistently. The log’s inter-event gaps are sojourns (queue, work, and closed hours), while the brief already separates median service from median wait. Setting waiting time equal to the inter-event gap and then adding another service draw double-counts time. The assumption that short activities are low-variance conflicts with the given ratios (completeness 25 vs 90 minutes; planning 3.5 vs 14 hours; legal 3 vs 16 hours). Multi-day work that cannot finish inside one shift, planner skill limits (two planners do only simple renovations), fire Tue–Thu, inspectors Tue–Fri, Monday portal coverage, and council blackout weeks are not operational calendar rules. Appeals, no-shows, and objection spillover across related cases are missing, so the long tail and cross-case coupling are under-specified. “Unconditional” rework also conflicts with rework rates that differ sharply by variant.

The experiment table lists D1–D10 and three stresses, but the “factors and levels” are single invented effect sizes (60% fewer missing-document loops, 30% less travel, 40% fewer notice extensions). Those unidentified quantities are exactly the uncertainties a sensitivity analysis should vary; Morris/Sobol is aimed at other parameters instead. D7’s claim that smoothing raises planner utilization is wrong in a work-conserving system with no abandonment: arrival shape changes queues, not average utilization. Decision thresholds are tighter than current medians for several case types, so the baseline fails its own criteria. A 70–85% utilization band treats freed capacity from D2 as a failure. Gini and SLA cutoffs are undefined relative to baseline and have no paired interval rule.

Validation would not detect these problems cleanly. Utilization cannot be checked against HR calendars that were inputs, not measurements, especially with start times missing. A KS test on tens of thousands of cases will reject trivial gaps. Variant-level conformance against the simplified tree will fail because that tree has no appeal and no planning or legal rework. The temporal holdout is a good idea, but the last three months are one seasonal regime, not a generic out-of-sample test.

The skeleton does not meet the coding constraints. `pm4py.read_pandas`, `pm4py.variants`, `pm4py.directed_graph`, and `pm4py.performance_analysis` are not PM4Py entry points; the usual calls are `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, and `discover_performance_dfg`. Service and waiting columns are never built. The tree parser looks for the literal prefix `*(...`, does not split parallel children, treats `tau` as a real activity, and never calls `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, while the limitations of uncontrolled `tau` choices and missing rework paths are only one clause. `apply_routing` sends low-value simple renovations straight to decision drafting, skipping planning and the rest of the process, although D2 only drops site inspection. `run_experiment` returns the same hardcoded metrics for every policy and seed, and calendars store shifts but never use them.
