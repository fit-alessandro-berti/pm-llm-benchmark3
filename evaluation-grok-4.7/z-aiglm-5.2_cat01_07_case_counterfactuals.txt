6.6

The required structure is complete and the main qualitative judgments are sound: K-337 changes decision policy, final compliance and four-eyes are preserved, C1 reservation does not scale, and queue forecasts are treated as uncertain. Those strengths do not offset repeated temporal errors.

K-219 is the largest failure. Tuesday 08:00 already shows a 4-hour D2 queue, so a case routed there after originals arrive cannot start at 08:00–10:00 “after” that queue. A start near 12:00 plus D2’s slower 4–8 hour service can finish later than D1’s factual 13:30 completion. The claimed base saving of 0–4 hours, and the best-case finish, are therefore not supported and may have the wrong sign even without rework.

K-337’s own path is arithmetically inconsistent. A Monday 14:00 decision is about 1.8 days before Wednesday 09:10, and a Tuesday-morning decision saves about one day. The stated 2.5–3.5 day, 3.5 day, and “about 3 day” savings do not match those endpoints; a Friday-evening decision would save roughly 4.5 days, which is also not the figure used.

K-411 says the Tuesday assessment queue grows “to 7 h by Monday 16:00,” which reverses time. The derivation also treats document service as about 4 hours and completion by 17:00, while the stated 4–8 hour range can push completion to 20:00, later than the factual 19:30, without that case appearing in the worst case.

K-104 correctly keeps identity and document timing fixed, but the table’s Wednesday 10:00 compliance start does not follow from earlier readiness when Wednesday’s C1 forecast is still 8 hours. Reservation could justify cutting in, yet the write-up sometimes credits readiness instead. Smaller issues include an unsupported 30% queue bound, unexplained pilot power claims, and labeling four-eyes separation as behavioral.
