8.1

The response meets every structural requirement and is quantitatively careful on the supplied aggregates. Baseline deltas, the restricted L1 distances (3, 40, 59, 28, 64, 36), fitness recovery, and the March-versus-post-May separation all check out. March is correctly treated as semantic/provenance drift, later rework and queueing are not collapsed into logging, and the monitoring design can separate recurrence, mix, and new behavioral drift. That is why the score stays high.

It is not nearly flawless.

The cycle-time split is false precision. The 2–4 / 3–5 / 0.5–1.5 / 8–12 hour bands, and the 3+4+1+10 identity, are not identified by the aggregates. Service evidence is only an Assess median of about +0.6 h in July–August, there are no stratum cycle times, and mix and rework are mediated by the queue. The disclaimer does not make the residual accounting an estimate. The L1 also double-counts the lockstep request-info pair (those two edges differ by exactly 1 in every supplied month) without saying so. Rank order survives if the loop is counted once, but the reported magnitudes are inflated.

Attribution is a notch too tight in two places. June–August “queueing after staffing loss” at about 80% confidence blends a May performance break that predates the 15 June departures with the later capacity shock; Supplier Z share is still 43–46% through July. The June–July logging change point is misstated: missingness moves from 5.5% to 6.2% between May and June, while June to July is only 6.2% to 6.5% and duplicates 5% to 6%. “Normalized DFG rates remain close to January” for March–April cites an April fragment that was not supplied. “Aggregate rework peaks” at the July priority change ignores the November tie at 29%.

Section 5 misreads the record. The inconsistent historical recording is the Senior Assessment distinction, not the complexity flag. That limitation is then in tension with the ~90% seasonal reading of the October complexity jump. In section 4, “aggregates cannot show interaction” is absence of evidence, not evidence against the interaction hypothesis. “Complete-case resource analyses would be selected rather than representative” is not a coherent claim, though the intended selection-bias point is right.

Part A is transparent and internally consistent. Part B uses the requested PM4Py entry points and correctly avoids overwriting the source log, but it parses timestamps as UTC and then filters with naive `Period.start_time` / `end_time` bounds, which is a real comparison failure. The gradual January–August complexity rise (about 21% to 29%) is also never isolated from the October step.
