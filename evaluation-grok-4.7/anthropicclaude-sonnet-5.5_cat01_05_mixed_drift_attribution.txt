The answer is strong on the criteria that matter: March is treated as a semantic and provenance break, later rework and queueing are not collapsed into logging drift, supplier, staffing, priority, and season are given different change points, and the monitoring design can separate recurrence from new drift. Most of the arithmetic checks out, including baseline deltas, L1 distances, fitness-recovery shares, workload splits, and the single-split variance figures.

It is not nearly flawless.

The Jun–Aug timeline row is misaligned. Median 36.5 h, p90 84 h, p90/median 2.30, and the +21% Assess workload are May figures (680 × 1.24 / 694.4 − 1). June has no normalized DFG, so that +21% cannot be a June result. Placing May inside the staffing interval blurs the supplier-versus-capacity onset the rest of the answer is careful about. The same looseness appears in “p90/median 2.30→2.44.”

Smaller quantitative slips accumulate. Fast + rework is 68% in June and July, not “69–70%.” Mapping recovery is about 85–96% once April is included (~96%), not 85–93%. “Jan–Apr +3 pts of high-complexity and ~0.4 h per point” do not fit the same endpoints: January to April is +2 points, and baseline to April is +2.5. Section 2’s “median 32→45 h, p90 71→110 h” is an unlabeled April-to-August comparison; the baseline p90 is 68 h. The claim that labels and missing-resource rates do not reverse is too absolute: missingness eases from 6.5% to 5.4% and raw fitness rises from 0.48 to 0.58.

The cycle-time split is appropriately hedged, but the 20–40 h loop duration is assumed, and the ρ/(1−ρ) “about 4×” illustration is an M/M/1 factor while the same paragraph uses 16 servers. The saturated three-point rework equation is then used to say December shows no campaign effect; that is stronger than an illustrative, collinear fit can support. In the code, the inter-completion comment says the gap includes the predecessor’s duration; with completion timestamps it includes the current activity’s service time. `diff_constraints` also would not compare log-skeleton frequency bounds correctly.

The required structure, PM4Py calls, causal restraint, and segment-level monitoring are otherwise in very good shape, which is why this stays above 8 despite those errors.
