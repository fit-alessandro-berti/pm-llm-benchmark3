The memo meets the required structure and makes a defensible, non-dominant call: hybrid rollout (E) plus a controlled measurement program (D), with national scaling and full rollback both rejected for explicit reasons. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated in a 10-row table; fitness versus precision, alignments, the DFG, temporal profiles, resource and social networks, and segment effects are all used. The five metric conflicts and the M1–M8 ranking are tied to how the decision would change, and the close states what to do now, what to pause, what to scale, what to measure, and numeric gates for a wider rollout.

It is not nearly flawless. The executive lead says the redesign “failed for the other two-thirds,” but medians still improved for apartments (−2 days), business (−3), and rural (−1); the failure is tail, rework, and complaints, not cycle time overall. Section 3 says simple houses gained time “with no rework,” which the +3pp first-time-right change does not support, and it calls field-survey utilization of 52% “52% idle.” The line “Improved Installation→Activation for no one” reverses the stated temporal finding (that path worsened for apartments). “+60% complaint-tail” mixes a relative complaint-rate increase with the duration tail, and “>80% of decision value” plus a “tie” between M6 and M1 that is then ranked 2 and 3 are unsupported. The opening memo also barely surfaces the visible-cost versus full-cost conflict that later drives the top measurement.
