The memo gets the causal structure right: March is mainly a label and provenance break, later movement is real, and supplier rework, staffing queues, priority redistribution, and renewal season are kept as separate mechanisms with uneven confidence. The baseline-delta table and the seven-edge L1 figures check out, mapped fitness is not treated as proof the business process was unchanged, and the monitoring design uses different statistics, seasonal references, and a multiple-testing rule.

The quantitative presentation is still unreliable in places. In the timeline, lost range markers turn October senior assessment into “722 per 100,” and several other cells collapse into unreadable forms such as “12%27%,” “16%46%,” and “0.870.89.” Section 9 then assigns the May supplier story June’s peaks: supplier share 46%, rework 27%, and p90 of 95 hours. May itself is 43%, 24%, and 84 hours; 95 hours already includes the 15 June staffing cut.

The second “logging change point” is not a change point. Missing-resource rate keeps rising from 4.8% in March to 6.5% in July, so calling April–June a plateau is wrong for that series. The L1 distance also double-counts one loop: `Assess→Request Info` and `Request Info→Collect Docs` move almost in lockstep and supply 24 of May’s 40 L1 points. That dependence is never stated. The same fragment shows `Collect Docs→Assess` falling while rework rises, which is the opposite of what extra loops alone would do and is better read as assessment backlog; that reading is missed, as is the drop in decisions per 100 cases as open inventory.

The cycle-time bands are only loosely tied to the aggregates. Service time has a real basis (about +0.6 hours). The 6–10 hour queue band and 4–7 hour rework band do not, and the stated shares span roughly 77% to 135% of the same 17.5 hour median increase.

Part A is reproducible and the embedded numbers match the prompt. Part B does not match the PM4Py manual: `filter_time_range` is called with `case_id` and `timestamp` rather than `case_id_key` and `timestamp_key`, so the slice fails as written.
