7.5

The assessment is complete, well calibrated, and generally faithful to the evidence, but it is not nearly flawless. Several numerical claims are wrong or unreproducible, one important bound runs in the wrong direction, and the PM4Py plan would fail or mislead on points it presents as operational.

The strongest parts are the separation of raw parity from adjusted disparity, the refusal to treat case mix or `risk_score_band` as dispositive, the focus on support-step queues and handovers as burden rather than as proof of intent, and the explicit rejection of dropping language or accommodation steps to shorten cycle time. Sections 2, 5, 6, and 8 are conceptually careful. The 63/76/85 percent “gap removed” figures match the raw and adjusted approval gaps, and the variant shares, waits, and adjusted contrasts are mostly transcribed correctly.

Concrete problems:

- The support-coverage claim is logically reversed. The text says the paths are summaries, then treats non-mention of Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review as absence and concludes “at most” about 25% of B and 33% of C receive those steps. If a summary omits activities, non-mention is not an upper bound. That undercuts ranked concern 4, which is one of the sharper fairness findings.
- “Summing D’s excess median waits … gives about 0.7 d, which matches the adjusted +0.9 d” is not a valid decomposition and does not follow from the table. The unweighted excesses are about +0.3, +0.3, +0.6, +0.1, and 0 days. Sum of medians is not the median of the sum, and several of those edges are optional. The 0.7 figure is unreproducible without silently dropping the fraud-review excess.
- The binomial standard-error passage overclaims. Crude SEs of roughly 1.4–1.6 pp are single-group SEs, not SEs of a contrast with A, and they do not apply to covariate-adjusted effects. Section 8 then says the residuals come without uncertainty estimates. Those two claims conflict.
- The staffing comparison undercounts demand. Language need and accommodation requests also occur outside B and C (about 72, 74, and 49 language-need cases in A, C, and D, and about 144, 75, and 78 accommodation requests in A, B, and D). Comparing roughly 100 specialized cases with 3600/52 Group A cases is not like-for-like, even with the later caveat.
- V3-versus-high-risk “about half in both groups” is arithmetically fair for that one variant (28/51 and 9/18), but it is too strong as evidence that fraud routing is proportionate. Other variants can still contain Fraud Review, especially in the 18% of A and 10% of D outside the six listed paths.
- The PM4Py plan is broad, but `discover_performance_dfg` values are floats under the public API, so `m["median"]` fails. The Group A log skeleton will treat legitimate support paths as deviations. Common support is an unweighted stratum share, not the promised share of cases, and standardized mean differences are not computed. Stratification drops household size, prior-case count, and month after Section 2 requires them. Clustered uncertainty and censoring are described and then not implemented. `reopen` is computed and unused.

Minor clarity issues remain: the raw gaps “−9, −15, and −20” follow C, B, D order without restating that order, and 4.7/5.8 is about 81%, not 80%. The bottom line itself is appropriately indeterminate and does not equate unequal approvals with discrimination. The deductions are for the bad bounds, the invalid wait reconciliation, the invented precision, and analysis code that does not do what the prose says.
