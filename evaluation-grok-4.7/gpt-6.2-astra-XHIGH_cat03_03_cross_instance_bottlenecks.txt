The answer is a careful, numerically accurate treatment of the coupling. It keeps the required sections, separates observation from inference, and avoids treating machine capacity or raw time order as the explanation. The score is held down by a code defect, a few over-strong phrasings, and one missed measurement conflict.

The arithmetic checks. J1’s 235 minutes and J2’s 220 minutes to 12:05, the 225/210-minute splits at 11:55, J4’s 115 minutes to 11:45 with 110 minutes of fixture overlap and 42 idle CNC minutes, J1’s 355 and 340 minutes around 19:10, J6’s 80 minutes split into 5 + 25 + 45 + 5, and the 210-minute L7 window are all correct. The shared-start remark is also right: an 08:35 J1/J2 launch moves both jobs by 210 minutes, and 420 job-minutes are one oven shift. Counterfactuals are stated, including carrier windows, oven and rack availability, and the override. B2 is handled precisely: with fixed membership the joint opportunity is 14:40, earlier rack clearance alone does not move it, and earlier J5 readiness can move it only to 14:35. The CAL-3 figure of 45 minutes correctly leaves J5’s occupation of TEST-1 out of the tool-specific saving. Lot events are not forced onto J2: the override, the missing Job-log links, and the unknown C5 demand time are all explicit. Fixture delay is not pushed through to S1, because B2 still waits for J5. Flattening, perverse incentives, and the seven batch-sync falsifiers are specific and usable.

The PM4Py block would fail on a normal OCEL. `ocel.relations` carries event id, object id, and qualifier. Activity and object type live on the event and object tables, so filtering `relations` by `ocel.event_activity` and `ocel.object_type_column` raises `KeyError` before the cure-identity count. `ocel_objects_interactions_summary`, `discover_objects_graph`, `discover_otg`, `discover_etot`, and `extract_ocel_features` are called and then discarded. The OC-DFG comparison of event pairs with distinct objects is the right comparison and is consistent with PM4Py’s OC-DFG shape. Waiting is computed from readiness versus batch start, which respects the ban on reading machine gaps as queues.

Smaller analytical problems remain. The J2 row attaches the quarantine override to the early-load counterfactual, but 08:35 is before the 10:20 hold. The B2 clock split of 85 + 5 + 10 minutes can be read as an 85-minute rack cause, while the same cell’s counterfactual says earlier rack clearance saves nothing under full membership. Rank 1’s observability cell says the Job view “sees individual gaps,” which sits badly against the supplied medians and against Section 4’s correct claim that the 1.1-hour Pack→Dispatch median hides J1’s 355 minutes. The edge list never shows CAL-3 or TEST-1, and node types are only implicit. Reported idle windows, 12:55–14:50 on OVEN-1 (115 minutes) and 16:35–17:25 on TEST-1 (50 minutes), are longer than those machines’ longest flattened gaps, 73 and 39 minutes. That conflict is direct evidence that narrative idleness and inter-event gaps are different measurements, and it is unused. The partial-batch spillover says J3 might lose the next oven slot, although a morning cure of about 50 minutes and J3’s 11:55 readiness leave the oven free on the observed day. Speeding J1 can also lengthen J1’s measured wait for J4 inside S1. That adverse interaction is left implicit in the evaluation design.

The ranking itself is defensible: two last-member-plus-10-minute starts support batch synchronization as the main coupling pattern, while the larger S1 contrast is ranked second with the carrier-window alternative kept visible. Machine capacity stays last among the physical accounts. Nothing here is a wrong timestamp or a collapsed causal claim. The remaining gaps are enough to keep the answer short of nearly flawless.
