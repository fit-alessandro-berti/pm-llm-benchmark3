5.5

The six-section structure is complete, and the headline judgments are directionally right: I-03 is refused because the swarm is past its effective stage, I-05 is not treated like I-07, a slot is reserved, and spillovers, segment calibration, label feedback, and monitoring triggers are all addressed. Those points keep this above a weak `p_now`-only ranking. The decision rule itself is not coherent, and the portfolio numbers are not auditable.

The expected-benefit identity is wrong. The supplied ranges are absolute risk reductions, and the stated valuation is absolute reduction times breach value, minus swarm cost, minus false-alarm burden. The rule instead uses `p_adj × effect × value`. That treats an absolute reduction as a relative reduction and shrinks every benefit by the calibrated probability. The same identity is repeated in Section 3, where “breaches avoided” is about half of the absolute reductions. Under that arithmetic I-08’s midpoint value of avoiding breach is scored near −€2,000; absolute-reduction arithmetic puts the midpoint well above cost and burden, with only the lower bound near zero. The adverse scenario can still be negative, but the base-case “marginal positive” reading is an artifact of the mis-scaled benefits.

The stated rule is not the rule that is applied. Section 1 says time to deadline must clear about 2 hours and that cases are taken in expected-net-benefit order with a spillover penalty. I-07 has 1.5 hours and is marked eligible. I-09 is scored at about €1,800 and labeled Tier 2 Wait, while I-01 at about €750 and I-02 at about €1,100 are Tier 1 Intervene. The displayed scores also omit the spillover penalty the formula claims to include. Section 5 then caps concurrent swarms at two and calls for an SME or public-sector slot whenever net benefit is positive, while the selected book is three swarms and no SME case. Non-Native-Language Support is given an invented +0.05 bias in the rule and a 0.15 shrinkage later; the problem only says the segment is uncertain, not that it is biased upward.

I-05 is mishandled. No swarm has yet been used. A flat refresh is a reason not to raise priority and not to swarm twice, not a reason to declare a never-treated case ineligible. Deadline approach with a stable high probability is new operational information. I-07’s new log is correctly distinguished, but “wait” with 1.5 hours left is effectively a refusal, and Section 4’s escalation path does not appear in I-05’s allocation row.

Section 3 omits the false-alarm burdens that Section 1 subtracts (€250 + €600 + €2,000 on the selected cases). Inside the answer’s own benefit figures, that omission is enough to erase the base-case surplus. Spillover harm rests on an unstated €15,000 average breach value, and long-horizon cases (I-04 at 10 hours, I-08, I-10) are treated as act-or-drop, with no option value against the reserved regulatory slot. Public-sector and regulatory harm beyond the euro table is named and then not used.

Monitoring is the strongest section: segment calibration, a breach-relevant effect check, capacity, fairness quotas, and label drift all have triggers. It still targets time-to-diagnosis-complete more than deadline breach, and propensity matching will not identify effects well under a deterministic ranker. The safeguards are concrete, but several contradict the portfolio just chosen.
