7.6

The central conclusion is sound: the crude Orion penalty reverses inside every complexity stratum, so the proposed 50% transfer is not supported, and stratification is correctly treated as descriptive rather than identification. Coverage of the required sections, estimands, and intervention types is complete, and most cycle-time arithmetic checks out. The score is held down by causal and factual errors that a strict reading cannot ignore.

The graph is not a DAG. `review_service` and `rework` point at each other, so the adjacency list already contains the loop the note later treats as something a static DAG cannot represent. `quality_outcome → case_completed` reverses time: the 30-day result occurs after close and cannot cause completion. `routing_rule → shift` is labeled as fact, but the engine assigns a group, not a shift; night coverage is also compatible with `shift → resource_group`, and that choice decides whether shift is a mediator or a confounder. `shift → arrival_load` is likewise asserted rather than marked as an assumption.

The role table misstates collider bias. Conditioning on `queue_wait` is overcontrol of a mediator; “unobserved risk affects both wait and rework” is a fork, not a collider at wait. Compliance is correctly called pre-treatment in that table, then treated in hypothesis 4 as a mediator of the group effect. Policy requires the check from complexity, so it confounds group comparisons. It is not caused by Orion versus Nova. The within-compliance means are also not mix-adjusted: Orion’s compliance-required cases are about two-thirds high complexity, Nova’s about half, so near-equal means do not show that the group adds nothing.

Evidence is overstated in the lead. High-complexity rework is 50% versus 50%, a tie, not a reversal, yet sections 1 and the recommendation say rework reverses in every stratum. The claim that cycle time equals wait plus service plus a constant 1–2 hours is false: residuals are about 0.8 hours without compliance or rework and about 2–4 hours with them. The compliance-not-required mean is wrong on the authors’ own fraction: 203.1/17 is 11.95 hours, not 11.89. Hypothesis 3’s predicted check compares all Nova night cases with Orion medium cases. “Clinical oversight” does not belong in claims review, and “label change  capacity change” is an unfinished sentence. Uncertainties 1, 4, and 5 each bundle several data additions after asking for one. The recommendation then calls compliance and queueing “the true driver,” which is stronger than the stated uncertainty.

The Python sketch matches the requested diagnostics, uses a stated common complexity distribution, and separates control-flow DFGs from timestamps. Rounding in the standardized Nova mean (19.67 versus about 19.65) is minor. The substantive limit is interpretive, not formatting: this is a careful, actionable analysis with a few identification and arithmetic mistakes in exactly the places causal discipline is being judged.
