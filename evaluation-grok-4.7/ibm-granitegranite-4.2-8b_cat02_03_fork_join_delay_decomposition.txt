4.5

The final write-up has the right fork-join frame and the right main conclusion: B is critical in 8/12 cases, A and C in 2/12 each, so B controls completion more than A even though A’s service is longer (exactly 50 minutes in every case). Definitions of upstream time, branch queue, service, `join_ready = max(end)`, downstream queue, and total flow are mostly the right ones, and the double-counting point is recognized. Critical-branch labels in the table are correct. Limits (capacity, preemption, queue vs release policy) are relevant.

The required numbers are not reliable, and the document contradicts itself. In the case table, F02 upstream is 15 and `C_queue` is 10, while the F02 worked example correctly uses 20 and 5. Flow time is wrong for F03 (130 vs 120), F06 (125 vs 120), F08 (95 vs 110), F09 (150 vs 120), and F12 (115 vs 125). F06 `C_service` is 5 instead of 20; F08 uses join 10:55 and `C_service` 20 instead of 10:50 and 30; F12 swaps the downstream/final split (10 and 20, not 20 and 10) and still misses total flow. Upstream is 20 for every case except F01 (15); the table’s 15/35/25 values are not in the data. Mean C queue is about 10 minutes, not ~5, and max C service is 30, not 22.

Synchronization wait is interpreted backwards: the last branch has zero sync wait, so B’s mean sync wait is small because it is usually last. The text says B’s sync wait is higher because it is often last, and it reports A’s average sync wait as 0.

The counterfactuals do not cohere. The prose alternates between an earlier join shrinking downstream delay and increasing it, and it never cleanly measures how much of a branch speedup is absorbed when `final_start` is held by the final station. The code does not match the claims: A and B counterfactuals recompute a gap but leave `final_end` unchanged, so savings are identically zero; the B update sets `B_end` back to the original end, so the 50% queue cut is a no-op. Reported “~10–15 minute” savings were not produced by that code.

The Python block also misses the required PM4Py steps. It never calls `format_dataframe`, `get_service_time`, `discover_performance_dfg`, or `convert_log_to_time_intervals`; the imports would not reach those APIs, and the note only says why a custom join is needed. The base pandas decomposition in that block is largely right, which makes the hand table worse: it is not the table the code would print.

Format coverage is complete (seven sections, worked cases, two redesigns, one code block), but under a calculation-sensitive reading the quantitative backbone and the counterfactual evidence are not trustworthy enough to carry the otherwise sound ranking.
