8.2

The audit is unusually complete and gets the central distinctions right: post-closure duration and appeal/decision/variant features are treated as t0-invalid, `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is separated from the impossible same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern, queue length and elapsed time are preserved, and predictive, causal, actionable, and fairness standards are not collapsed. Coverage of the top-12 features, all five patterns, and the local-only drivers is essentially complete, and the recomputation stance is correct: contaminated probabilities are not repaired by deleting SHAP terms.

The score is held down by concrete defects.

The footprint check forbids “anything twice after `Claim Registered`.” That contradicts the normative model. Document cycles repeat, and a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate after appeal. The same section later treats those loops as zero-cost, so the proposed suite is internally inconsistent. The check is also ill-formed: an alpha-style footprint does not encode “occurs twice.”

`Response(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded)` is stronger than the process statement that an appeal can lead to another decision. Withdrawal during appeal would be false-flagged. `DOC-DOC` is asserted to mean two document cycles; it may instead be two document activities in one cycle. That reading is not supported by the variant string.

Case Y uses `initial_reserve` as a credible driver and narrates it as admissible evidence while also classifying it as unusable until a versioned snapshot is proven. The lineage was never given. The narrative then says the probability should be materially lower than 0.71 after removing the variant. That conflicts with the later, correct warning that deleted leakage can be absorbed by correlated proxies such as region. Directions from the leaked model are also narrated as established historical rates for channel, region, complexity, and queue.

Several claims are stated more strongly than the evidence. The 35% `final_handler_team` mismatch is described as non-random and concentrated on slow, escalated claims. Week 52 is identified with both the holiday period and the policy change, although the notes only say those factors align with certain weeks. Total duration is called the breach label’s answer key; closure time and the 10-day settlement target need not be the same quantity. The opening rule that every post-coverage activity is future “by construction” is later qualified for same-timestamp fraud triggers, but the absolute version is still too strong. Severity values such as “low/medium” and “critical (leakage), none (conformance)” fall outside the required scale.

These are judgment and consistency errors inside an otherwise strong audit, not a failure to see the leakage.
