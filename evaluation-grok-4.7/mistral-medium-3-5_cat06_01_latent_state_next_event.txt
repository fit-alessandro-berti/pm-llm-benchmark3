The answer follows the required schema and gets the primary next activity right for every case, but its state model and enablement logic are repeatedly wrong.

C6-A’s primary is written as a transition (S2→S3) even though Evidence Received has already closed S2, and the alternative is the same S3. Regulatory Assessment is marked not process-enabled and described as dependent on Technical Investigation and Supplier Analysis, contradicting the explicit concurrency rule. Supplier Analysis is treated as mandatory because one complaint covers two devices; the actual trigger is related complaints on a shared lot or component (only partly supported by the three similar complaints).

C6-B’s alternative state S1 is impossible after evidence receipt. Fast Close and Close — No Safety Issue are given substantial probability as the next logged event while the reasoning itself places them after Evidence Review. That misses the required distinction between an enabled outcome and the next observable completion.

C6-C’s alternative calls an in-progress Regulatory Assessment S5; S5 requires that obligation to be finished. Disposition is then justified by “assessment in progress,” which does not enable disposition, and Authority Notification is ranked as a next event despite needing a reportable conclusion, disposition, and the 16:00 batch.

C6-D’s primary (S4, Regulatory Assessment already opened by R-R2 at 12:40) is the strongest call, but the S1 alternative is incompatible with a completed technical investigation. Non-obligations are listed as open obligations, and Disposition remains too plausible only about 80 minutes after assignment against a 2–14 h regulatory queue.

C6-E’s alternative S2 is definitionally wrong once the second Evidence Received exists. Supplier Analysis is correctly mandatory, then marked not process-enabled, and its completion is over-ranked against a 6–36 h queue while review has only been waiting ~40 minutes. A six-complaint lot is not carried through to a suspected reportability obligation (Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment).

Cross-case comments correctly refuse identical forecasts for C6-A and C6-B and treat the DFG as observational, but the claim that C6-D has broader intervals is not true of the numbers given. Rank-1 choices are directionally sound; the formal states, enablement flags, and several lower-ranked successors are not.
