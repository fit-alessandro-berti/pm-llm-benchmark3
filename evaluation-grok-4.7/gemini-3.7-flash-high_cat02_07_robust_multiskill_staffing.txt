5.4

The submission covers every required section, encodes the tables, and gets the base identities right: mixture means (6.94 / 26.30 / 32.82 / 38.50 / 15.74), daily volume 42, skill task counts 18.09 / 24.30 / 12.85, pure work 2,720.6 min, staffed minutes 2,490, shift/break placement, and the 18:00–20:00 mechanical hole (Emil only, no mechanical skill, about 1.05 M/ME cases). The three policies are specified in operational language, and the Monte Carlo table does not simply crown the lowest base-case mean. That is the usable core.

The decision arithmetic and the recommendation do not hold up.

**Offered load is the wrong object, and several scenario totals are wrong.** Block “instantaneous load ratios” divide the full Intake–diagnostic–Validation content of cases that arrive in the block by labor present in that block. That ignores carry-in, carry-out, sequential precedence, and speed multipliers, all of which are ≥ 1. Calling 16:00–18:00 “186.7% instantaneous” overstates what was calculated. Aggregate base minutes are then compared with clock supply and overtime is called mathematically mandatory; multipliers and the fact that P3 can cross midnight are not in that comparison, and 30 minutes of overtime cannot close even the stated 231-minute gap. U1 morning P1 volume is given as rising from 1.05 to 5.56. Morning base P1 is 7×0.10 + 9×0.15 = 2.05; 1.05 is the evening mechanical count reused by mistake. U1 work is stated as 3,426.8 after a morning total of 1,752, but 2,720.6 − 1,030.8 + 1,752 ≈ 3,442. U2 (“E and ES double, then renormalize”, arrivals unchanged) changes 12:00–16:00 workload by only a few minutes; “+224 min, electrical +42%, software +28%, bottleneck shifts entirely” does not follow. U4 correctly recomputes diagnostic means (29.60 / 37.14 / 44.50) and then adds 421.5 minutes, about a 15.5% uplift on all work. Diagnostic-only, the increase is about 242 minutes. Intake and Validation tails were not supposed to double.

**The flagship policy is not the policy that was simulated, and the table contradicts the write-up.** Section 3’s hybrid uses a 20-minute overflow gate, stickiness, sequential ME/ES when a cross-skilled resource is free or delay ≤ 15, and parallel only for a tight P1 or a 35-minute backlog. The code sequences almost every non-late case, never checks idleness or backlog, and does not pin both branches to one person, so the claimed 7+5+8 minute saving is not assured. “Dynamic P1 reservation” is never implemented. Policy 1 both forbids preemption and permits it; the code forbids it. The overview’s “four queues” lists five. Objective 1 mixes probabilities with raw minutes (0.5×P90 swamps w1 = 100), α/β/γ are undefined, and neither objective is scored on the table. On that table, pooling has lower mean and P90 in every scenario and better P1 SLA in five of six (base 90.4% vs 79.7%). Hybrid never reaches the 90% P1 target. Hybrid’s setup minutes are the worst in every row, which is unremarked, while U5 handover of 191 vs 405 is treated as 214 minutes of recaptured capacity. Section 5 then says hybrid holds utilization below the burnout threshold; the same table shows hybrid max utilization 118–127%. U2 in section 2 is a crisis; U2 in section 4 matches the base case, which is what the code’s renormalization actually does. Those facts are never reconciled. P3 “100% and guaranteed by the 4-hour boost” is an unsupported guarantee.

**The simulator censors the outcomes the SLAs refer to.** Unfinished cases leave the event queue when the shift ends and are dropped. Delay, P90, and SLA use only completed cases; a priority with no completions is imputed as 100% SLA. There is no completion-rate column. The next morning is never modeled, so post-18:00 mechanical work — identified in prose as an SLA failure for P1/P2 — does not count as a failure. U4 doubles Intake and Validation tails as well as diagnostic tails. U1 Poisson means are `round`ed. Overtime is not a 30-minute cap: a task may start up to minute 29 of overtime and run long, and breaks do not interrupt busy work. Preemption rewrites remaining time from a finish clock that includes handover/setup, leaves the original full interval in the event log, and can count busy time twice. Cross-team tags use home team on Intake/Validation, so Farah’s “Gen” team creates penalties the rule did not clearly require. Setup fires on any diagnostic skill change, including inside one case, despite the rule’s “between consecutive cases” wording. Thirty seeds are mentioned, but the published table has no seed dispersion. With about five P1 cases a day, a 1–2 point SLA gap is noise; U5’s 80.9 vs 79.4 is treated as a ranking.

**Process-mining requirements are only partly met.** The log has the five required columns, and the code calls `format_dataframe`, `get_service_time`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, and `discover_handover_of_work_network`. Section 7 then calls `@@service_time` pure processing and `@@waiting_time` queue latency. In this log, handover and setup sit inside the activity interval, and PM4Py’s case waiting figure is sojourn minus service, not stage queue time. No stage-level queue metric is computed. The attribute list omits assignment reason, eligible-resource set, and planned versus actual service; pause/resume is only a boolean. Drift and bottleneck migration are named in a diagram and not tied to a concrete procedure. The 75–80% primary-skill time claim is not a simulation output.

What remains defensible is narrower than the recommendation: the day is structurally overloaded once multipliers and handovers are admitted; 16:00–20:00 and Chen’s absence are real coverage shocks; full pooling buys cycle time and P1 attainment with speed and coordination loss; strict specialization fails when the only morning software capacity is removed. A robust choice would publish completion-censored metrics with seed intervals, score the stated objectives in commensurate units, and treat hybrid as a handover-oriented alternative that currently misses the P1 target — or change the rule so it stops doing so. The pilot plan (shadow mode, stepped wedge, P1 circuit breaker) is the right shape of safeguard and does not repair the prior validity gaps.
