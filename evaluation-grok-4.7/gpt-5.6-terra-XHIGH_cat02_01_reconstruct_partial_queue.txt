7.5

The reconstruction is strong on definitions, missing-start bounds, censoring, and most arithmetic, but it is not nearly flawless. One reported flow identity is false, and the Little’s Law section does not test what it claims to test.

The case table, resource bounds, and full-window load figures hold up. Nominal waits and services for the fully observed cases match the clock. The feasible windows for P05 S2 (09:45–10:15), P07 S1 (09:25–10:00), P11 S2 (12:00–12:25), and P15 S1 (11:40–12:15) follow from queue entry, the same resource’s previous completion, the next start, and nonnegative service. P08’s 12:00 completion correctly dominates the C2 outage for P11. End-to-end sojourns for P01–P14, the 60–230 range, the mean near 146, and the median 145 are right, including the fact that P05, P07, and P11 have known totals despite missing starts. S1 residence 2,085 case-minutes (mean about 5.8) and S2 residence 1,340 (mean about 3.7) match a case-by-case sum. Busy-time ranges also reconcile: S1 535–605 around a central 570 (about 74–84% of 720), S2 515–570 around 542.5 (about 73–81% of 705), with the I1/I2 and C1/C2 splits adding to those totals and with open elapsed service included. The central half-hour WIP path is consistent with completions-then-starts-then-arrivals, and the off-grid S2 peak of 7 around 13:45 is real. Open cases are kept as elapsed lower bounds, zero service is treated as a mathematical floor, and section 6 correctly reverses “optimistic” for utilization versus waiting.

The subperiod S2 entry counts do not. S2 entry time is defined as S1 completion, and the S1 completion row is 6 / 7 / 7. The S2 entry row is 6 / 7 / 8. Those last-bin entries are P14 through P20 only, so 7, and 6+7+8=21 contradicts the same section’s statement that S2 has 20 entries. Section 5 repeats the false comparison “6 vs 8.” The sign is unchanged, since 6 completions are still fewer than 7 entries, but the afternoon imbalance is overstated and the two tables cannot both be true. The Python binning would print 7, so the written rates are not the code’s results.

The cohort “Little’s Law” equality is an accounting identity. With \(\lambda=N/T\), \(W=(\text{residence area})/N\), and \(L=(\text{residence area})/T\), \(\lambda W=L\) cannot fail. The steady-state rejection is justified, and the disclaimer that this only checks arithmetic is explicit, but it is not an independent consistency check on an interval where the assumptions nearly hold. No arrival-versus-service capacity is computed either. Mean completed service is about 28 minutes at S1 and about 40 minutes at S2 with two servers each, so a full S2 is near 3 cases per hour while a full S1 is nearer 4. That calculation is what would show early S1 buildup as demand above a higher intrinsic rate, and later S2 buildup as throughput near a lower rate. The time-varying conclusion is still the right one, and utilization is correctly treated as inconclusive because the imputation bands overlap, but the mechanism stays qualitative.

Smaller weaknesses: the \(\pm 5\) minute envelopes are pasted onto nominal bounds rather than re-solved as a joint rounding region; the S2 coverage line lists 14 observed starts and 2 open activities in a way that looks additive even though the opens sit inside the 14; and the indicator \(t<H\) drops censored cases at exactly 14:00 while the snapshot keeps them. The code’s missing-start rule also ignores a job that straddles the known completion, which happens not to occur here.
