5.5

The answer covers every required heading, all eight cases, and a real spread of classifications, and it handles several clear calls well: P-502 (new supplier, Compliance after commitment), P-504 (outside both the emergency definition and the directive’s subject matter), P-506 (after expiry, with no written extension), and P-508 (above the email’s EUR 150,000 line, Finance still missing). P-505’s use of the supplier acknowledgement over the ERP stamp, and P-507’s use of the 12:40 PDF against the four-hour recording rule, show genuine evidential discipline.

Those strengths are undercut by contradictions and misapplied rules.

P-505 is classified COMPLIANT while the same row lists the missing budget-owner approval as missing evidence. GPIP-4 A2 is triggered at EUR 88,000; A3 is not, yet A3 is listed as satisfied. No Plant approval appears at all. Finance approval is after commitment, and the CFO email is treated as a full waiver despite A4, A6, A7, and the email’s own “essential integrity controls” sentence. Under the answer’s own test—compliant only if every applicable rule is met—this case cannot be COMPLIANT.

That reading of the email is also applied inconsistently. The hierarchy treats the email as low-weight interpretation, the pseudo-logic then says it normally waives Finance approval up to EUR 150,000, P-505 becomes COMPLIANT on that waiver, and P-507, which raises the same question, is INDETERMINATE. Section 6 then calls P-507 a policy breach. The Finance-waiver issue needed one stated rule and one consistent consequence.

P-501 is internally inconsistent. The row classifies it as a justified exception while also saying the later approvals do not cure the timing gap. A6 does not apply in the way claimed: at EUR 38,000, neither A2 nor A3 requires a pre-commitment approval. The Shift Lead point is also mis-specified. The local emergency clause allows an actively delegated Shift Lead to stand in for an absent Plant Manager; it does not, by itself, decide who may commit. The four-hour stoppage and the pre-09:00 timing are identified, but the July 14 ERP outage is not applied to those timestamps.

Other gaps: “unapproved” is equated with “new supplier” without argument, which props up the A3 theory for P-504; P-506’s post-expiry purchase-order-after-commitment breach is not stated; P-507’s eight-hour need is not tested against the six-hour emergency test or the 24-hour audit view; prior practice on incident directives is unused; and the A1 catalogue line drops the prohibition, so the formalized rule does not say what it means. The P-505 alternative argues the clock, not the stronger point that the email cannot displace A2.

The skeleton is competent. The classifications on the contested cases do not follow from one stable hierarchy, so the assessment is not reliable as a compliance judgment.
