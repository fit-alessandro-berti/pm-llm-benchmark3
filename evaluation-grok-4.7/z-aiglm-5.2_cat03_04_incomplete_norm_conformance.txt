The answer covers every required section, respects open-world semantics, and correctly refuses to treat the four protected unmodeled relations as violations. N3 and T6 are rightly treated as forbidden, N1 is not collapsed into a directly-follows breach, and OTG presence is distinguished from per-event ET–OT gaps. Those are the core judgments, and they are mostly sound.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 1 says it distinguishes six classes and three normative statuses, then lists seven classes against a four-status norm. E10 is assigned to forbidden behavior, then to suspected identifier reuse, given Critical severity and High confidence, and later called both a robust violation and a data-quality contingency. That contradiction sits on the exact separation the task tests.

Count reading is also too strong. “2 of 1450 Add Item events” is inferred from edge weights; the summary never states that event count. “83 direct closes without invoice” is unsupported: 72 Validate→Issue Invoice plus 83 Validate→Close cannot be exclusive successors of about 100 orders. N5/E9/T4 are cited for edges they do not govern. The code sketch names the required functions and keeps a separate semantic layer, but `norm_otg.meta` / `norm_etot.meta` does not match the stated tuple types, and `expected_scope` is documented as possibly callable while compared as a number. Length also runs well past the 1,700–2,600-word target.
