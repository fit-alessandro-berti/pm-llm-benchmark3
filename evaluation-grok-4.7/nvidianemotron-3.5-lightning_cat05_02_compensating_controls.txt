5.5

The final write-up covers every required heading and often reasons from timing, independence, and recoverability rather than from headcount or amount alone. AP-202 as failed, AP-205 as stopped before completion, AP-208 as failed despite a legitimate payment, and AP-203/AP-207 as met only because the emergency conditions appear to hold are defensible. The equivalence test includes the requested dimensions, and the monitoring section separates alerts from proof of misconduct.

Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors and internal conflicts in the final text.

The preventive/detective section contradicts itself on the exact question it is asked. The manager-approval row treats approval after release but before settlement as detective, while the following row classifies that same action as preventive. The two standards are not reconciled with the equivalence test, which reserves full equivalence for action at or before release, even though AP-207 is later marked objective-met on a post-release, pre-settlement approval.

Case judgments slip in places that are factual rather than discretionary. AP-204’s row asserts that corrective action was taken; the record only says the audit found a fabricated receipt. A completed 220k payment, detected days after the recall window, is then left at moderate severity. AP-206 is classified as partially met, while the facts leave the token timestamp explicitly unresolved between a logging delay and a failed precondition; that is the clearest indeterminate case, and the classification options exist for it. The counterfactual for AP-208 treats the 900k as likely already irrecoverable, although the payment is described as legitimate and the harm is unrecoverable exposure, not a realized loss. AP-201’s rationale says the controls caught material errors; the case facts say the vendor and amount were correct. AP-205 is marked a completed four-eyes violation even though release never occurred and the nominal rule applies to completed payments.

The code does not support the narrative and would not reliably run. `format_dataframe` is called with `activity_id` and `activity_resource_key`, which are not the usual parameters (`activity_key` is). The result is then iterated as if it were a list of traces; `format_dataframe` returns a dataframe, so that loop fails. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is described as returning violators. In standard PM4Py it keeps cases in which the two activities have different resources, so the violators are the complement, limited to completed pairs. The violating-case and blocked-case results are never joined to the facts frame and never printed. The hand-built facts contradict both the source table and section 3: AP-201’s clean validation pass is coded as not a full pass; AP-204 is given a bank-account change although none occurred; AP-204 and AP-209 are coded as manager-approved before settlement although neither has a manager event. Coverage then labels the blocked and unavailable cases as warning/override. Emergency eligibility sets vendor tenure permanently true, treats a missing manager as satisfying the manager condition, and marks AP-206 eligible without testing whether the token existed before preparation. The printed flags are therefore false evidence, not a neutral aid to judgment.
