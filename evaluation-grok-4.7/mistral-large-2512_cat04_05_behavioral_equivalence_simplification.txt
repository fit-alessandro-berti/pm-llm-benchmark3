3.5

The write-up hits every required section and lands the right coarse verdicts (P1 and P3 not equivalent; P2 only under abstraction), but the operator semantics underneath those verdicts are wrong, and most of the concrete evidence does not hold.

Model A puts `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` in parallel inside each iteration. Either order is legal, and both must finish before the optional approval and before rework or loop exit. The answer treats that parallel as “no concurrency” and as a forced check-after-analysis sequence. Model B’s check is one leaf beside the loop, so every complete trace has exactly one `Check Revision`. The structural table instead gives B an unbounded check count. Loop exit is also misread: the redo child must be followed by another body, and `tau` only skips approval. It is not the loop exit. That mistake invalidates the `tau` discussion in §4.

Of the five traces, only T4 (B only) and T5 (A only) are real. T1 and T3 are in neither language (rework without another body; two iterations with no `Rework Case`). T2 is in both, because A allows check before analysis. The required two witnesses in each direction are therefore not supplied, and several “decisive differences” name the wrong constraint. The P3 definition says traces that contain 12 events and 3 analyses; the spec is at most 12 and at most 3. The P2 definition likewise says exactly one analysis and one check, not “at least one” plus the analysis count and a rework flag. Under the real P2 abstraction the observable pairs match (analysis count \(n \ge 1\), rework iff \(n \ge 2\), check always true, approvals hidden), so that verdict happens to be defensible, but not for the reason given.

The bounded search is the right idea (BFS, silent steps, both caps), yet it is not runnable: `pm4py.get_marking_after_transition` is not part of the stated API, and a pm4py `Marking` is a `Counter` and cannot be put in a `set`. Stochastic code claims invented probabilities and then never assigns any. Footprint/EMD limits are the one technically solid stretch.

Complexity figures are not derived. A and B each have 12 nodes and 7 leaves (6 visible, 1 silent). Reported counts are 11 and 10 nodes and 6 leaves, B’s depth is 5 rather than 4 under the answer’s own edge-count function, and the simplicity scores are guessed.

The “simplified” tree does not preserve P2. Check and rework are optional epilogues, rework no longer separates attempts, and the loop redo is the approval choice, so repeated analyses need no visible rework. It is also larger than either original (14 nodes, 3 silent leaves) while still being called simpler, with no structural comparison. Regression traces 2 and 3 are not in that tree; negative trace 2 (`Open Case`, `Analyze Revision`, `Close Case`) is accepted because check can be skipped. §9 then recommends that tree as the P2 replacement for B.

Bounded non-equivalence would already prove unbounded non-equivalence if the witnesses were valid. The closing warning blurs that direction.
