5.5

The write-up covers every required section and correctly avoids treating the log as steady-state, fully observed, or proof of optimality. It is not a sound design: several choices contradict each other or the process facts, and the skeleton does not follow PM4Py’s actual interfaces.

The baseline already includes district batching, while D3 turns that same policy on or off, so the incremental effect is unidentified. “18-month replay” in the matrix also conflicts with a generative, non-stationary arrival model used for counterfactual policies. Stress “1–3” is one collapsed row, not three scenarios with levels.

Warm-up of 3–6 months “until queues stabilize” assumes a steady state the assumptions section rejects. Complex-variance median/p90 durations are 140/260 days, so that warm-up is shorter than open cases and cannot clear initial bias. A moving-average convergence check will not settle under 1.7× seasonal swings.

The SLA line “30/60/90 days by case type” never assigns types. A 90-day limit is already below the complex-variance median, so the primary gate can be infeasible before any intervention. “Utilization 85% sustained” is undefined and can punish D4 for adding capacity. D10 “wins on four metrics” can pass while losing on tail duration and legal risk. D2’s skipped-inspection safety risk never becomes a constraint. The 15%/20% cuts are unanchored to statutory deadlines or baseline quantiles.

Architect bursts are not a non-homogeneous Poisson process. The text states both without a cluster, Cox, or compound construction. Routing is treated as deterministic despite missing area and sensitivity attributes. Monday demand (2.2×) is described as a calendar calibration, which mixes load with the separate Monday portal-clerk rule. Applicant delay, consultant delay, no-shows, and appeal re-entry after planning or legal review stay unspecified. Prior violations, required for D2, are absent from estimation.

Objections are correctly called unobserved, then turned into an identifiable mixture and a shared extension factor with no identification argument. Fitting log-normal or Weibull to sojourn times labeled an “upper bound” is not an estimator of service. Weibull and log-normal have finite moments at every positive shape; “shape <2” does not define a heavy tail. Case-level bootstrap confidence intervals ignore queue and batch dependence. In-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests on fitted service times do not validate the twin. A final 3-month holdout is shorter than complex cases, so it is heavily censored and season-specific. Throughput is used as if it were utilization, which the missing start timestamps cannot support.

The skeleton misses required sojourn or service/waiting columns and does not explain that one timestamp per event makes that split impossible. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a graph plus start and end activities and is not unpacked. `case_arrival.get_case_arrival_average` does not match the library function `get_case_arrival_avg`, and section 2 names a different wrapper. The parsed tree is an ellipsis that always fails. `["D2", ..., "D10"]` inserts `Ellipsis` rather than the omitted policies. Arrival, calendar, and routing objects are never used by `SimulationEngine`.
