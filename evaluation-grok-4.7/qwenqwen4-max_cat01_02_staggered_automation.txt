The final answer is a strong causal-policy assessment: it separates availability from dose, refuses a single “X%” claim, keeps median and p90 apart, treats the fraud desk as interference, and pairs a bounded observational reading with a case-level randomized rollout. Estimands, mechanisms, threats, and the claim ladder are specific and mostly well calibrated.

It is not nearly flawless. The Beta April–August median is wrong: (47.3+49.0+49.0+48.5+45.0)/5 = 47.76, not 47.96. That +1 addition error is repeated as raw +2.06 h (+4.5%), both-control DiD −3.16 h (−6.9%), mix-matched −2.38 h (−5.2%), the −8.5% log bound, and the final claim ladder. Correct figures are about +1.86 h (+4.1%), DiD −3.36 h (−7.3%), and mix-matched −2.58 h. Conclusions do not flip, but this is the quantitative core.

The hand-demeaned dose slope (−0.130 h/pp, cluster SE 0.032, t(4) CI excluding zero) is reported at false precision. The same arithmetic process already failed a five-term sum, the regression was not run, and a CI that excludes zero sits awkwardly next to a causal median range that includes zero. The Callaway–Sant’Anna primary design is never estimated; the evidence is collapsed 2×2 DiD plus a descriptive TWFE the text itself says can be negatively weighted. Section 7’s raw median range “−0.8% to +4.5%” omits Gamma’s −8.3%. The scale-up rule calls “upper 95% bound of p90 change ≤ 0” non-inferiority; at a zero margin that is a superiority requirement, and it conflicts with the +10% p90 pause guardrail.
