5.5

The report has the required seven-section structure and the right broad thesis: hospital continuity and deferral can be legitimate, throughput is not resilience when controls vanish, and the post-recovery audit queue is hidden workload rather than real-time compliance. Several phase metrics, the A–L table, and the keep/modify/retire instincts are directionally usable. It is not reliable as an incident analysis.

The manual-phase directly-follows graph is misread. Hospital-critical fast lane is its own branch (1,000 of 5,100 triaged orders), separate from skip-authorization (1,600). The table cites that skip count as evidence for pattern A, and the resilience section then claims manual authorization (B) closed A’s authorization gap. Those paths do not connect. D’s early-versus-stale timing is flattened into “risky shortcut,” while K and L are treated as risk-appetite ambiguity rather than a delayed compliance tail. Phase-level controlled-substance misses rose from 0.2% to 2.9%, which is never reconciled with the claim that F reduced those errors. T4 is called near baseline even though pharmacy and wholesale cycle times, traceability, cold-chain, dual-control, rework, and cancellations remain far above T0. Fitness is cited, but fitting traces (91% to 44% to 59% to 76%), move-on-log versus move-on-model deviations, POD delay variance, and the backlog wait beyond baseline p95 are largely unused. About 3,250 scanless dispatches versus 2,600 phone proofs is also missed.

The recovery model names the required topics, then conflicts with itself. Customer priority and product priority have no rule for a hospital order that is also controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive. Temperature documentation is not a release gate. Partial recovery, where intake is back but inventory sync and scanning are unreliable, is not designed for. “Pre-Cron / In-Cron / Post-Cron” is neither used nor explained. C and G are labeled retire while the rationale keeps conditional versions. Recommendations then assume the failed system can detect downtime and route work, and that dispatch software can hard-lock missing scans during an outage, which would also stop the hospital flow the report says to protect. The audit recommendation is marked low effort despite thousands of exception and remediation cases.

Indicators omit backlog age, throughput, and service protection, three of the required dimensions. The closing trade-off says the evidence that would change the recommendation is T2 traceability already being worse than baseline (9.5% versus 0.8%), then concludes the trade-off is still acceptable. That conditional does not follow.
