6.5

The memo is complete and well structured, and it covers every required heading, scoring factor, worker-facing explanation, and PM4Py hook. It is not reliable as a policy. Several load-bearing claims contradict the rules, the data, or the scoring procedure that the answer itself states.

The recommended queue is not what the stated scorer produces. `generate_valid_candidates` is never defined, so eligibility is a black box, and the published weights still contradict the table under a natural reading. After C016 updates Eva’s load, C005 scores higher for Amal (skill 2, fairness about 5.5, skill score 30) than for Eva (skill-3 score cut to 22 because complexity is below 0.60, fairness driven negative). C004 scores higher for Jonas than for Amal, because a skill-3 resource is capped at 22 on low complexity while Jonas combines a skill-2 score of 30 with the best load term. C014 likewise scores far higher for Jonas (about 42) than for Hugo (about 15 after the security-specialist penalty). The text still asserts that EDRA “yields” Amal, Eva, and Hugo. Reason codes in the table are never emitted by the pseudocode.

Hard rules are rewritten and then breached. Security skill 0 is not allowed, and constraint 6 permits skill 1–2 only under Gita or Hugo. Jonas is skill 0, yet C015 is given to him as a compliant supervised trainee. Approval skill 0 cannot prepare or finalize; constraint 7 limits preparation to skill 1. Hugo’s approval skill is 0, yet C010 makes him the preparer. HC4 adds “designated security trainees” without treating that as a governed exception. The same section says keeping Celine on C001 “violates compliance,” while HC3 and constraint 6 explicitly allow skill 1 under a level-3 supervisor. That supervised pair is also the option that avoids the cited median 2.7-hour handover on a 3-hour SLA. Calling the policy’s SLA risk “low” does not survive that arithmetic. Hours remaining are never decremented, so Hugo’s 5.5 hours and four involvements, and Celine’s complex stack, are unchecked.

The formula fights the prose. Documented continuity range is [−15, +35], but a non-owner high handover is −20 and an anti-loop bonus pushes the owner path to +50. SC3 says the 3.3-hour weak-skill penalty must outweigh the 2.7-hour handover; the continuity term does the reverse (−15 for a weak owner versus −20 for reassignment). Supervision counts as 0.5 in the code and as a full assignment in HC2. The fallback allows a batch of 5, breaking the hard cap of 4. Training share is given as both 4/9 (44.4%) and 3/9 (33.3%) in the same section. The legal training case the queue itself flags, Bruno on low-priority C012, is declined, and the 20% quota is then met with the two skill-0 assignments.

The specialist-only comparison is factually wrong on its own tie-break. Highest skill, then fewest open cases, sends diagnosis to Celine (6) ahead of David (14) and quality to Jonas (4) ahead of Iris (11). The claim that specialist-only overloads David and Iris is the opposite of that rule. First-available criticisms are only partly traced. Audit indicator 3 is said to check HC2, which is the batch cap, not skill. The appeal rate and the complexity adjustment are named and then omitted from the formulas.

The PM4Py sketch names the required functions, but it would not audit the policy as specified. The skill join assumes a long table with `skill_level`; the given matrix is wide. SLA failure is a fixed 8-hour `filter_case_performance` cut, so a 3-hour breach can pass and a long-SLA case can fail. `get_variants` is unused while the comment claims loop detection. The performance DFG printout does not compute the wait-to-service ratio. Nothing in the function learns or recalibrates weights.
