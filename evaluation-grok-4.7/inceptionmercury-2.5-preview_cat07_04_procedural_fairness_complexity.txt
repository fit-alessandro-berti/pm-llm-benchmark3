The write-up covers every required section and stays within the main constraints: it does not treat raw approval gaps as discrimination, does not write the disparities off as case mix, does not recommend dropping support steps, and its closing judgment is appropriately hedged. The data citations are mostly right, and the capacity story for language and accommodation queues is the correct center of the problem. It is not a precise assessment.

The adjusted results are mishandled. Supervisor review for Group B is +8.0 percentage points, not “+8.0%.” The +2.6 days is adjusted cycle time, not an adjusted waiting time. Those are different claims. The preliminary model does not control for language need or an accommodation request, so residuals for Groups B and C are not “after support needs.” The sharpest residual is missed: Group B is still +9.5 percentage points more likely to be sent for missing documents after documentation completeness is held constant. Group D’s +5.7 percentage point appeal residual is also left out of the path analysis, while fraud routing is called justified by risk score without the required caveat in that section.

The staffing claim is inflated. Not all 1,250 Group B cases need the seven bilingual workers; 57% need language support, and the three overloaded regional officers are omitted. “More supervisor review” is treated as stricter scrutiny. Those are not the same thing. Implicit bias and “valid eligibility” are asserted without evidence. Appeal rate and reopen/rework rate are collapsed even though Group D leads appeals and Group C leads rework.

Section 2 names the requested checks but never defines how disparity, legitimate routing, discrimination, and uncertainty would be separated. Propensity scores put possibly downstream covariates, especially risk band and documentation completeness, into the primary balance set. There is no within-variant comparison, and the published variant durations are not group-specific, so mix shift and slower treatment on the same path cannot be separated. No uncertainty is attached to a model given only as point estimates.

The PM4Py sketch would not run and does not answer the fairness question. `filter_event_attribute_values` takes an attribute name and a collection of values, not a dict. `filter_paths_performance` filters on numeric path duration, not an activity list, and the activity names themselves are shortened. Service versus waiting time is not recoverable from a single timestamp without saying so. Handovers, performance DFGs, and skeletons are computed for one group and never compared. Declarative checks that would test whether required support and fraud steps actually fire are absent.

Mitigations are present but their trade-offs are generic. End-to-end ownership by the same seven bilingual workers can worsen the bottleneck the proposal is meant to fix. Two accommodation specialists who also supervise are never treated as a concentration risk. The bottom line is calibrated, yet it blends a fairly direct structural burden—long support queues, scarce staff, no support-step time targets, extra handovers—with the genuinely indeterminate question of discriminatory eligibility decisions.
