6.5

The final write-up meets the required structure and gets the main conformance story right: June–July fitness recovers in stages under label mapping, `Queue Routed` removal, and source-sequence ordering, while August is largely an unmodeled `Supervisor Sign-off` plus a smaller unresolved residue of missing compliance checks and `Manual Override`. Employee noncompliance is not treated as the default explanation, stable outcomes are not over-read, and the optional sign-off model is correctly flagged as overpermissive. Word counts, section counts, and the six governance rules are in range.

Those strengths do not make it nearly flawless.

The ranked table is internally inconsistent. Timestamp/order defect is rank 3 at posterior 75, while obsolete normative model is rank 4 at posterior 85. The fitness increments also cut against that order: source ordering is the smallest staged gain (81 to 89), whereas the optional sign-off model is a much larger August gain (66 to 86) and is what keeps August at 66 after the same repairs that return June–July to 89.

The causal list has false or reversed edges. `policy_change -> normative_model` contradicts the stated fact that the tree was not updated. `policy_change -> high_value_status` reverses the actual conditioning relation. `real_operational_concern -> alignment_moves` runs inference backward into the measurement. `UI_release -> event_semantics_contract` treats a missing governance control as an effect of the release.

Two ledger rows put “weakens employee noncompliance” in the supports column, so those rows do not name a supported hypothesis. Manual Override is also used as contradicting evidence for the `Queue Routed` artifact hypothesis even though IT’s claim concerns a different event.

The PM4Py block is not a reliable reproduction. The log stays a flat list of dicts keyed by `activity` rather than an event log or dataframe with `concept:name`, `case:concept:name`, and `time:timestamp`, so `discover_dfg` and alignments cannot see the activities. The alignment calls use `align_conformance_checking`, `fitness_alignment_based`, and `precision_alignment_based`, not PM4Py’s alignment APIs, and failures are swallowed into `NA`. The move-cost fallback treats `>>` as a whole move, which is not how alignment moves are structured. The pipeline design is coherent on paper; as executable comparison code it does not do the job the section requires.
