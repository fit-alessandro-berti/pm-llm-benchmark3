6.5

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activation as non-default, and the primary estimand is a real conditional probability: 90-day default after activation, scored only for accounts that have reached an activation landmark without a default already observed. Population, horizon, unit, and the pathway and policy-counterfactual alternatives are stated clearly enough to use. The 48,000-case accounting adds up, the five observation mechanisms are separated, and the leakage table covers every required field with usable availability rules.

The validation narrative contradicts its own split. Training is 2024 starts, validation is 1 January–30 June 2025, and the locked test is 1 July–30 September 2025. That test sits entirely after the 1 July 2025 policy change, so it has no pre-1-July stratum. The 15 October 2025 migration falls in the shadow window, so the test is a pre-migration, post-policy period. The sentence that treats the test as spanning the policy change and as evidence about a post-migration regime is false. Because the landmark is activation plus 24 hours, a start-time split also lets a 2024-start case activate inside the validation or test calendar and contribute post-policy events and labels to training. No embargo ties eligibility to prediction time. “Train is pre-policy” is therefore not true for this target.

The fixed-length view is under-specified. A length-3 prefix is often pre-decision, while labels are restricted to eventual activations. That estimates default among applications that later activate, which is a different estimand from the landmark risk, and the selection is not written into the target.

The IPW proposal is only partly identified. Positives can enter through a `Default Recorded` event or `default_90d = 1`, while negatives require a separately audited complete feed. That is outcome-dependent verification. Covariate IPW does not repair it, and the write-up never states the asymmetric observation model, weight truncation, or whether metrics are weighted. The 35-day feed lag is calculated correctly in prose (activation on or before 13 October 2025) and then ignored by the code’s maturity flag, which is only activation plus 90 days versus 15 February 2026. The unexplained “not 27,300” also breaks an otherwise exact cohort reconciliation: activated cases are 21,600 + 3,600 = 25,200.

The skeleton is not implementable as written. `applications_events.csv` is one row per event, but the code asserts 48,000 event rows. `activation` is computed on the case table and then read from the event frame, which does not have that column. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` returns a log of truncated traces; unpacking it as `(case id, length)` pairs is invalid, and `pm4py.get_case_prefix` is not a PM4Py API. With `NEGATIVE_FEEDS_AUDITED = False`, only defaults receive `y90`, and the guard asserts only that some training ids exist, so a one-class prefix table can pass. Timestamp ties are resolved by stable CSV order after the text forbids that. The leakage asserts and the external-survival comment are the right idea; they sit after these failures.

Conceptually this is well above a labeled-export classifier. It is not a reliable study specification until the split clock matches the landmark, the test-period claims match the cuts, and the pipeline runs on event-level data with verified negatives kept out of the training set.
