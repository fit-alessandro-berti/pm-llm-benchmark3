7.5

The memo reaches a defensible constrained decision: do not scale Redesign R as a bundle, do not roll it back, suspend survey-skipping and post-billing activation, restore surveys and permit checks where risk is high, and tie any wider rollout to further evidence. The required sections are present, the numbers are accurate, facts are separated from interpretations in the evidence table, all five metric conflicts are explained, A–E are judged with conditions, and M1–M8 are ranked by how they could change the decision.

The decision itself is less precise than the evidence. The memo treats coordinator ownership as the proven beneficial piece and says to scale it, while also saying to run the ownership comparison only “when expanding beyond the pilot” and to “keep” ownership. Those are different actions, and the segment pattern does not isolate ownership from the merged simple-house path. Old and apartment cases, which also sat under coordinators, deteriorated. The 85% utilization cap is an invented control, and the plan does not operationalize business billing disputes, rural rescheduling, or unclear activation-specialist ownership.

Process-mining coverage is incomplete. Fitness, precision, alignments, the DFG, and segments are used well, but the temporal profile is largely ignored: slower installation-to-activation for apartments, the new billing-to-complaint tail, and longer failed-visit-to-second-visit delays where coordinator load is high. Low generalization is also lumped in with low precision as “overflexible,” which misstates what low generalization means. Several causal lines are stated too firmly for incomplete, non-randomized evidence (“mainly because,” “concrete mechanisms,” “cost-shifting artifact”). The highest-ranked experiment, a randomized skip trial, is not limited to strata where harm is still uncertain.
