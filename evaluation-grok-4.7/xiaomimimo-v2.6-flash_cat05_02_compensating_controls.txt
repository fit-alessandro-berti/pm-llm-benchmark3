The case reasoning is strong, careful, and mostly faithful to the log and the control rules. Nominal four-eyes status is separated from objective attainment, ED-1 is treated as a conditional permission, and the timestamps, recall margins, independence conflicts, self-override, validation outage, and u12/u13 pairing are handled accurately. AP-203 and AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET`, AP-202 and AP-208 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`, AP-205 as prevented before completion, and AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` are well supported. The preventive/detective/corrective semantics, especially post-release but pre-settlement manager action, are precise, and monitoring is correctly framed as alert rather than proof.

The score is held down by material defects. The required program does not run: `facts` is keyed as `case`, but the join is `on="case:concept:name"`, so the mandated facts join and evidence flags raise `KeyError` before any of that output. In the sufficiency test, overrideability is scaled so that a lower number is better, then written into columns and a rule (`OO1`) that treat higher numbers as better, so the formal test is ambiguous. AP-204 is labeled `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` even though the write-up says the authenticity objective “failed outright,” recovery had expired, and the author’s own worst-material-threat rule would push that realized, uncorrected loss to `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`. AP-208 also counts the emergency token as active before preparation although the facts only say a token was active.
