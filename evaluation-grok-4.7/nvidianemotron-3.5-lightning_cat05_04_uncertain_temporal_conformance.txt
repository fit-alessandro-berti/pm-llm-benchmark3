The final write-up has the right skeleton (offset direction, UTC conversion, fold handling, SLA vs profile, required headings), but several classification-changing mistakes remain in the finished tables, and the sections disagree with each other.

The closed-form interval arithmetic is not reliable. For T-401, Close_Incident is correctly placed on 2026-06-02 in the UTC table, then the duration is computed as about 9 minutes by dropping the day boundary. The true gap is about 24 h 9 min, inside the containment-to-close profile band, so that pair is a certain pass at zeta 1–3, not a certain violation. The SENSOR lower bound is also off by 5 s (08:51:05 minus 5 s is 08:51:00, not 08:50:55). For T-405 the fold choice itself is right, but 17 min 59.999 s is 1080 s, not 1140 s. For T-406, 13:14 minus 10.5 min is 13:03:30 local, not 12:53:30; the spurious negative durations and the “ordering violation survives every SLA tolerance” claim come from that arithmetic error.

Three cases are modeled against the wrong evidence. T-404’s 18–25 minute record is investigation duration ending at Investigation_Complete (10:35), not time back from Containment_Complete (10:55). The inferred start is about [10:10, 10:17], so S1 and S2 are possible violations, not a certain S1 violation and a certain S2 pass. T-407 treats delivery latency as receipt before send; receipt is send plus [0, 20] min, and with the unlogged causal cap the S1 result is possible or indeterminate, not a certain violation. T-409 keeps the generic OPS-BER offset of [−90 s, +120 s] and never applies the stated weak [−12 min, +12 min] bound, so its narrow S1 “certain pass” is unsupported. Evidence that the problem flags as weak is never scored indeterminate.

S3 and the containment-to-close profile are filled in for T-405, T-406, T-407, and T-409, which have no Close_Incident, using alert-to-containment durations of about 35–55 minutes. Those rows should be untestable. T-408’s 38 h 25 min S3 violation is one of the few close calculations that is right.

The Alert_Received → Investigation_Start acceptance region in section 3 is copied from the alert-to-containment band, [1980, 3420] at zeta 1, instead of [420, 780]. Some rows are then classified as if the wrong band were real (“all below 1980”), while others are classified as if the true band were used (“inside the band”) even when the printed region does not contain the interval. Section 4 mixes both bands again: T-403’s narrative uses 960 and 1140, T-409 uses 540, and partial overlap is sometimes called a certain violation. Severity and confidence are left blank.

The Python block does not meet the executable requirement. It loads only a stub of the log, uses timestamps that `fromisoformat` will reject, does not build DST alternatives or interval classifications, and leaves the midpoint-versus-interval comparison “not shown.” `conformance_temporal_profile` is called with a zeta keyword and without the supplied profile.

The governance list does cover UTC storage, raw timestamps, offset telemetry, resolution, lifecycle semantics, DST fold, causal ids, and an overlap policy. That, plus correct handling of the Berlin fold, the T-408 36-hour breach, and several HUB-only SLA intervals, is why this is not a failure. It is not a passing conformance analysis: too many certain/possible calls flip once the day boundary, the T-404 anchor, the latency sign, and the T-409 offset are fixed.
