The write-up follows the seven-section outline and all twelve candidates appear with the requested columns, but it is not a grounded portfolio analysis. The matrix is identifier order, not a ranking, and several ratings conflict with the evidence: normalization is tied to KYC even though statement normalization follows document review and runs concurrently with KYC; external-valuation triage is only a medium process benefit despite a 58-hour synchronization wait on the committee path; signing’s downstream risk is rated low despite the KYC-before-signing rule, a 6.5% provisional-signing rate, and activation that stops when controls are incomplete.

The dependency section names the required topics and then stays generic. It treats A3 as if it were KYC clearance itself, never uses the 3-FTE specialist bottleneck, the two-senior handover, analyst centrality, back-office utilization falling below 65% after 15:00, month-end legal load, or RM-driven document loops, and it says A1, A9, and A11 need deep core-banking integration. The rollout later deep-integrates A1 and A6 instead.

Portfolio construction has a harder failure. The recommended set is A1, A4, A5, and A6. Compliance requires at least one first-wave module that strengthens controls rather than only cutting labor; none of those four does that. A1 is also a weak bottleneck choice: registration is an 8-minute, 1.5-hour-wait activity in a pool that is already slack in the afternoon. The largest synchronization points, external valuation and customer signing, are left out of the “bottleneck/synchronization” portfolio without a reason. The compliance portfolio is mislabeled lower-frequency even though KYC and contract preparation are high-volume, and it claims no cycle-time effect while including the 31-hour mismatch path and contract generation.

Section 4 does not cite variants or directly-follows relations. Effects are broad adjectives. Portfolio 1 is said to cut rework only minimally while containing the checklist aimed at the 52% repeated missing-document loop (3,300 forward, 2,800 back). Portfolio 3 is said to lengthen cycle time despite A4 and A9. Resource effects are the wrong shape: A1 plus A5 increases clean inflow to the 9 credit analysts, and placing A6 a phase later creates the overload the resource network warns about. Freed specialist and analyst time, idle back office, and a later surge into 4 legal FTE are not worked through. The mismatch-to-contract bypass (380), the mismatch return to KYC (1,160), the 82% critical-path rate, concurrency, collateral rates (71% equipment, 18% working capital), four-eyes breaches (5.8% of high-exposure cases at month-end), and contract-term loops (8.5%, 14% on renewal) never enter the effects.

Risk coverage names audit trails, A2/A3 false results, escalation strain, and four-eyes only as a vague peak-period concern. Model drift is absent. Four-eyes is not given a compensating control tied to separate people for credit analysis and committee packaging. Provisional signing, silent extraction errors, and activation on incomplete controls are omitted.

The phase plan stays inside the two-deep-integration cap, then uses a 95% integration gate that is not a process gate. A5 precedes A6 with no analyst-utilization gate, RM resistance to customer friction is unaddressed, and no metric tracks control violations or end-to-end time. The recommendation says bottlenecks dominate frequency, while Phase 1 still spends the scarce deep integration on registration and never shows, from waits, rework, or the resource network, why that should go first or how the compliance constraint is met.
