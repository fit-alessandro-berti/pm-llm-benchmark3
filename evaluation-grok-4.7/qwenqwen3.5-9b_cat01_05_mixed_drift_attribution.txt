5.4

The write-up keeps the right overall shape and the right central split: March is mainly a label and provenance break, while later rework, waiting, mix, and seasonality are separate. That split is not carried through with reliable quantities, timing, or monitoring logic.

The raw-versus-mapped reading overreaches. Mapped fitness near 0.90 after renaming `Eligibility Screen` and dropping duplicate `Open Case` shows that the old model was mostly failing on syntax; it does not prove the business process was unchanged. Mapped fitness itself slips from about 0.93 to the mid-0.86s. The lasting raw–mapped gap is continued semantic and duplicate-event drift, not evidence that the post-March process changed. Duplicate `Open Case` is only about 4–6% of cases, so it cannot explain a drop from 0.92 to 0.55; the split label does. Missing resources are treated as an accountability nuisance, without saying what they do to organizational mining versus what duplicates do to traces, variants, and fitness.

The mechanism timeline mis-assigns the July priority rule. `Assess → Request Info` jumps in May (12 to 24 per 100 cases), with Supplier Z’s share and rework, before the rule exists. It peaks in July and then falls while the rule remains. `Assess → Senior Assessment` stays single-digit through September and jumps to 22 in October, with the renewal-season complexity spike, not with 1 July. The historical note that senior assessment was recorded inconsistently is never used. September’s partial reversal — Supplier Z, rework, fast path, and cycle time all improve while staffing is still unrestored — is missing as its own interval, so supplier and staffing effects stay tangled. October is called gradual rather than seasonal, and no interval is marked recurring. December’s drop is then treated as evidence that overtime worked, although complexity, volume, and the end of peak season move at the same time, and cycle time remains far above the January–February level of about 31 hours.

The quantitative section does not match its own measure. On the seven supplied rates, Euclidean distance from January is about 19 in May, about 29 in October, and about 17 in December, not 0.12, 0.24, and 0.18. March cycle time is 31.2 versus 31.5, not +0.0. The June `Request Info` delta has no June row in the normalized directly-follows fragment. The change table has five metrics, not the required six, and omits fast-path share, p90, supplier share, and both fitness series. The 25/25/15/35 cycle-time split sums to 100 without a range. Recorded assess service rises only about 0.6 hour while median cycle time rises by roughly 15–18 hours, so a 15% service share is not supported. Queueing is plausibly the largest piece; the percentages are not.

The code would not reproduce the narrative. Fitness recovery is coded as raw minus mapped, which is negative, so the “gap > 0.3 means logging” rule never fires. The distance threshold 0.15 is on the wrong scale for per-100 rates, so every non-baseline month becomes “behavioral,” including March. The monthly summary and September directly-follows row are not fully embedded, and nothing validates ranges, month coverage, or rate bounds. `format_dataframe` is given a nonexistent `log_format` argument; time slicing never calls `filter_time_range`; `discover_temporal_profile` is given an `activity` argument it does not take; the mapping helper mutates a frame and does not compare logs.

Monitoring is only partly usable. Event order, service time, and temporal profile are not covered as distinct monitors. Fixed cutoffs such as mapped fitness below 0.85, queue above 50 hours, and rework above 20% are not tied to the baseline or to a segment. A 12-month rolling reference cannot be built from this window and would include the drifted months. Benjamini–Hochberg is invoked for monitors that have no p-values. Making post-migration, non-Supplier-Z cases the primary population removes the segment that carries the rework signal. Rule 6 sends a `Request Info` spike to the priority rule, which is the wrong first check.

The strongest claim is the March semantic break. Supplier-linked rework and staffing-linked waiting are plausible and confounded. Priority redistribution, October senior assessment, and the December improvement are not cleanly attributed.
