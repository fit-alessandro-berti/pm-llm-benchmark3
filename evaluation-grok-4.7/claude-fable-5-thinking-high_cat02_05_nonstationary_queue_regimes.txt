The regime structure, outage/recovery split, open-hour Little’s Law setup, and most rate estimates are coherent and check against the table. The score stays well below the top of the scale because several highlighted figures are arithmetically wrong and a few inferential claims are stronger than these aggregates support.

Checked and consistent: arrivals 2519, completions 2409, gap 110; baseline weekdays about 8.3 arrivals/h and 8.3 completions/h (701 and 697 over 7 days); overload weekdays 9.5 vs 8.4 (798 and 704); recovery and intervention near 10 completions/h; pooled arrival rate 7.5/h, mean WIP 29.9, arrival median 103.5; staff–WIP correlation about +0.65; Apr 7 cross-bin Fano 2.96 and CV 0.59; baseline daily Fano about 0.23; lag-1 autocorrelation about 0.39 and lag-7 about 0.72; Little’s Law values about 2.4, 4.7–5.3, 5.4, and 9.7 open-hours. All four hourly vectors sum to the published daily totals. A one-bin shift of the morning peak matches Apr 7, Apr 15, and Apr 25.

Defects:

1. Mean `staff_fte` is 144/28 = 5.14, not 4.9.
2. Weekend arrivals are 359/2519 ≈ 14.3% of volume, not ~16%.
3. Folding Apr 16 into Thursdays moves mean Thursday completions from 109.3 to 114.3, about 4.5%, not ~25%. The 25% figure is 129 versus the Apr 9 Thursday alone.
4. “(32−20)×12 ≈ 12 cases” is not a valid equation. Excess WIP is about 12 cases; at roughly 6 net completions per day that is about two weekdays. The factor of 12 does not belong there.
5. 47/1.2 = 39.2 open-hours, not ≈38. After Apr 16–17, mean WIP is still 35, and the mix shift arrives before a three-day drain finishes, so “matching observation” is loose.
6. The Apr 18–28 conservation sketch starts from WIP 35, which is Apr 17. In that window WIP runs from 16 to 50; on Apr 20–28 it runs from 37 to 50 against net inflow of +106. The missing cases are concentrated in the overload window (net +94 versus a WIP rise of about 13). Baseline weekdays are nearly conserved (701 vs 697). Treating the gap as ±10–15% haze on every Little’s Law number, including the baseline window marked applicable, is too broad.
7. The bands E[S] ≈ 0.47–0.53 h and 0.67–0.75 h are unlabeled distributional assumptions. They sit near a lognormal fit to the median and p90, but they are not identified by the table. At the overload median of 35 minutes, offered load is about 5.5 erlangs and ρ is about 0.9 on 5.9 FTE. Persistent deficit and rising WIP support overload; ρ ≳ 1.05–1.20 should have been marked assumption-dependent. “Near-100% utilization” on the recovery days likewise requires mean handling near 40 minutes (60/1.49).
8. “Within-regime the sign is negative” is not a within-regime correlation. Overload staffing is almost constant, and the 7.0-FTE days still have high WIP. The pooled correlation of +0.65 is real and confounded by weekends and reverse causality.
9. Across the 20 weekdays, corr(daily median service, completions) is only about 0.17. That is too weak for the claim that a pooled regression would find slower service increasing throughput.
10. Apr 20 is a Monday. A Monday–Sunday split puts the mix shift on a week boundary. Alternative B does bury the Apr 16–17 recovery inside week 2; calling Apr 20 mid-week is wrong.
11. The first regime cell, “Apr 6–14 & Apr 18–19, Mon–Fri (7 d),” reads as if those weekend dates were part of the seven weekdays. The footnote is what actually places them.
12. For Apr 21, (8+34)/115 = 36.5% is the shifted window. The labeled 08:00–10:00 share is 11/115 ≈ 9.6%, so “either way” is wrong. “30–50% in all consistent readings” also skips weekends at 17–22% and baseline days near 26%.
13. At 6.4–7.1 erlangs, 7 FTE is about ρ 0.91–1.01. “≈ 7 FTE permanently” and “≥ 7 with any waiting-time target” in the same section are not the same recommendation.
14. The staffing heuristic `diff >= 0.9` misses the weekend step 2.5 → 3.0, which the prose treats as part of the intervention.

The single Python block builds both tables, separates pooled and regime summaries, reports Fano, CV, and lag autocorrelation, labels the change-point rule as a heuristic, restricts Little’s Law to chosen windows, and correctly says these aggregates are not a PM4Py event log while naming the three requested methods. The fluid example then hardcodes 47, 10.0, and 8.8 instead of taking them from the frame.

The operational core holds: do not treat recovery throughput as a baseline, and pooled average staffing or an early end to the surge both look acceptable in the aggregate and fail once mix, backlog, and day type are separated. The calculation and overclaim issues above are frequent enough that the write-up is not nearly flawless.
