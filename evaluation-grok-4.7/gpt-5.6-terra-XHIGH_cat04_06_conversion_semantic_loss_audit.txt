7.4

The source reading is largely right: the partial order, the silent XOR, the `(C I)(D C I)*` loop, the release join, and the non-series-parallel split between legal review and verification are identified correctly, and the script usually treats a successful conversion object as non-evidence. The required sections, pipeline split, exception handling around the process tree, witness polarity, and POWL-as-canonical recommendation are in place. Those strengths do not make the audit reliable as written.

The validation code has a concrete defect. `convert_to_reachability_graph` returns a transition system with `states` and `transitions`. The diagnostic reads `places`, so that count is always zero and the source-versus-candidate “reachability statistics” do not measure the graph. Section 5 then calls workflow-net and soundness checks without the guards used in Section 3, and the summary counts only thrown exceptions. A `False` soundness result can still be reported as zero diagnostic failures. The primary Petri-to-BPMN failure is re-raised before the Petri net is diagnosed, visualized, or sent through the process-tree attempt, so one derivative failure suppresses the rest of the audit.

The bounded explorer is thoughtful, but its exhaustiveness claim is too strong. On `marking == final_marking` it records the visible trace and does not expand. That is complete only when the final marking is dead. An unsound round trip that can leave the final marking and reach it again with a longer visible trace is silently under-enumerated. Dominance on consecutive silent steps is sound; this early stop is not, under the claim as stated.

Section 4 contains a direct logical error. A language that contains both traces with Legal Review and traces without it does distinguish an intentional skip from deletion of the Legal Review branch. Deleting that branch removes the Legal Review witnesses. What trace equality cannot show is the choice point or branching structure, which Section 9 states separately and correctly. The same section also marks Petri-to-BPMN “expected preserved” for the unordered legal/verification behavior while Section 2 says BPMN conversion can alter that concurrency. For this N-shaped dependency, those cells should start as `ambiguous`, not as a uniform expected preservation.

Three smaller inconsistencies remain. Criterion 5 literally says any reach of a non-final marking is grounds for rejection, which is true of every intermediate state. Criterion 11 rejects duplicate visible labels outright, while the code comments say duplicates are not automatically semantic errors. The stakeholder annotations tell the reader to label the silent skip, without warning that turning that annotation into a BPMN activity label would change the round-trip language and manufacture a false mismatch. The empty-symbol notation for tau makes several of those sentences harder to check than the underlying model is.
