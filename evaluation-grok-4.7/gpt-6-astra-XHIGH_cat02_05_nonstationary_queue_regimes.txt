9.2

The analysis is unusually rigorous: quantities and units are defined carefully, the outage is kept distinct from recovery, and the two data defects that actually threaten inference are both found and used. The hourly vectors sum to the daily totals, yet their 08:00–09:59 shares (16.8%, 18.1%, 9.6%, 11.8%) contradict `peak_08_10_pct`. The closed-system residual is also handled correctly: a zero start implies opening stocks of at least 88, 96, 110, and 112 on the four flagged days, so instantaneous WIP cannot fall below 45, 57, 59, and 65, against reported means of 23, 20, 22, and 19. Positive initial backlog would worsen that contradiction. Rates, residuals, Fano factors, the April 20 split (22.43% to 43.29%, about 95.1% SSE reduction), the Simpson reversal (within-stratum increases, pooled decreases), and both Little’s Law ratios (2.61 and 2.60 open hours) check out.

Regime boundaries, the flow-balance alternative, and the decision to mark every Little’s Law window `approximate only` or `not defensible` are coherent. Completions are the right primary throughput proxy; medians are not smuggled in as mean flow time; recovery throughput is not treated as ordinary capacity; and the pooled staffing reversal is the right policy trap. The code matches the prose and keeps PM4Py in comments on a real event log.

The remaining problems are small but real. “At least that many carried cases” points at minima of −3, −2, and −2, so the bound of 3, 2, and 2 is easy to misread. “Fixed daily totals” does not explain the low within-regime daily Fano factors; those groups were not selected on arrival variance, and the relevant distinction is simply daily totals versus intraday clustering. The open-hours flow times assume `mean_wip` is an opening-hours time average, while 15-minute snapshots could include the closed 12 hours; that specific exposure mismatch is never stated, even though a numerical sojourn time is reported. Placing the 34–36 minute median beside the 37.4 minute mean break-even threshold is qualified, but it is still the weakest comparison in the piece. The PM4Py note also never shows `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, one of the three methods named in the task. None of these overturns the argument.
