6.5

The write-up is a real survival analysis, not a generic essay: time origin, horizon, competing events, and open cases are separated; Aalen–Johansen is the right primary functional for “not resolved by 72 hours”; immortal time on `Escalate` / rework is handled correctly; and the main point estimates that can be recomputed from the table are right. Scenarios 1–3 match the data: overall \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) is 30.4% / 31.3% / 37.9%, Cohort A 29.2% / 29.2% / 37.7%, Cohort B 31.6% / 33.2% / 37.9%, P1 25.0% / 27.1% / 53.1%, P2 43.1%, P3 20.0%. The cause-specific 1−KM figure of 36.1% versus CIF 30.4% is also the correct direction and magnitude. The hand-rolled risk-set logic keeps open cases and, with no tied times, implements product-limit AJ properly for those three codings. That core is why the grade stays above a failure.

It is not close to clean, because several results that the narrative treats as findings are false or aimed at the wrong estimand.

The 28.1% “absolute lower bound” is the wrong floor for the estimand defined in §1. Estimand 1 counts every case that is not resolved by 72 hours, including rejection, withdrawal, and transfer. Known on-time resolutions are 9 (S01, S02, S04, S07, S16, S17, S18, S19, S27). Known non-resolutions by 72 hours are 18. Only the five opens younger than 72 hours (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are still unresolved as of the extract. The sample bounds on the proportion not resolved by 72 hours are therefore 18/32 = 56.3% and 23/32 = 71.9%. The quoted floor of 9/32 keeps only the four late resolutions (S03, S13, S20, S28), four opens past 72 hours (S09, S11, S25, S26), and S32, and drops nine earlier competing exits that §1 already defined as failures. Linking that 28% to the AJ figure of 69.6% presents a change of definition as if it were additional discovery. S03 does not “decrease” \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\); a resolution at 76 hours simply does not increment it.

Scenario 4 is not an analysis. The column is a copy of Scenario 2 in every stratum, while the stated mechanism (transfers rewritten as resolutions at 120 hours, so they stay in the risk set; opens past 72 hours fixed as failures) is a different estimator. Holding S21 in the risk set until a late resolution moves P1 \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) to 25%, not the reported 27.1%, and the pooled curve diverges from the censoring curve before hour 72. “Open and already past 72 hours = failure” does not change \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) at all, so the displayed metric cannot show the sensitivity being claimed. The code never implements this scenario. §6’s qualitative-reversal section is therefore only as good as Scenarios 1–3; the fourth column is unsupported.

The process model misstates the log. S16 is `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve`, so it is not an `AutoCheck→Resolve` path; the only `AutoCheck→Resolve` edge is S01. Observed edges absent from the diagram include `Screen→AutoCheck` (S01, S15, S17), `Screen→Resolve` (S16), and `AutoCheck→Review` (S15, S17). The `gap_to_age_ratio > 0.5` zombie rule also misses S09, which the same section treats as a stalled breach (31/92 ≈ 0.34).

Censoring is misdiagnosed. `last_event_gap_h` on S09, S11, S25, and S26 cannot bias \(F(72)\) or \(S(72)\): those ages are already past 72, and the AJ value at 72 is determined by the risk sets up to 72. A long gap is observed history, relevant to residual hazard after the last event. It is not, by itself, evidence that the administrative cutoff is informative. Transfer is the censoring mechanism that is plausibly informative, because it is an internal decision concentrated in escalated P1 cases (S08, S21, S32, all three of eight P1 cases). The §3 claim that ordinary censoring of dormant WIP “severely underestimates” 72-hour tail risk conflates those two problems. The same section’s cohort-maturity contrast (open share 4/15 versus 5/17) is too small to carry the confounding story; P1 mix (3/15 versus 5/17) and the fact that every transfer is P1 matter more. Calling every Cohort B P3 trace non-compliant is stronger than the extract: the prompt says rollout was incomplete, and it does not state that P3 was supposed to start with `AutoCheck`. The pattern is real; the policy-violation conclusion is not identified.

The analysis plan recommends procedures the extract cannot support. Landmark cause-specific Cox models at 24 and 48 hours, and clock-reset semi-Markov sojourns, need event times. Only case age and one end gap are observed, so stage at a landmark and escalation timing are not in the data. Those methods belong in §9 as designs that become possible once timestamps exist. Feasible primary work is AJ, with transfer coding as sensitivity, plus crude bounds that acknowledge five pre-horizon censored cases. No standard errors or intervals are given for n = 32 and slices of 8–14, so the P1-versus-P2 “reversal” is reported as an operational fact when three P1 transfers are the entire contrast. The escalation contrast of 63.0 h versus 41.5 h is a completed-resolved-case mean (five escalated resolutions versus eight others), which is the selection bias §3 argues against. §7 then says escalation effects are non-causal and, in the next claim, that mandating fewer escalations will increase breaches. That counterfactual is not identified.

Smaller encoding and definition problems accumulate. The §2 composite indicator sets a resolved case to 1 only if \(T \le 72\), which is a horizon indicator, not an AJ event indicator; the code correctly events all resolutions at their own times, so the table and the program disagree. The “worst-case” column still right-censors opens at or under 72 hours, so it is not a worst case. Estimand 4 is a cause-specific survival, \(P^*(T_{\text{Resolve}} > 72)\) under independent censoring of other exits, but it is headed as both “net failure” and “net resolution,” and the prose drops transfer from the set of removed causes while the S08 row censors it. \(F(\infty)\) is written as if it were known; with open cases, only the observed CIF increment after 72 hours is estimated. Calibration by priority and cohort is named in the task and replaced by unestimated 80/50/24-hour alert cutoffs. `pm4py.get_variants` is called, but the script assumes keys are activity tuples and values are trace lists. On current PM4Py that object is a count map, and the crash sits before any survival printout. There is no fallback.

What holds up, and should not be blurred with those errors, is the estimand-to-decision mapping for \(\psi_1\) versus on-queue survival \(\psi_3\), the three coherent transfer stories (absorbing internal loss, independent censoring, credit-as-resolution), the explicit refusal to drop the nine open cases, the correct on-time versus late resolved lists, and the warning that `escalated` and `rework_count` are post-baseline. The grade is held down by false precision in §3 and §6, a control-flow diagram that contradicts the prefixes, and a primary dynamic model that cannot be fit on this extract.
