6.7

The answer is complete and structurally strong, and the operational recommendation is the right one: do not copy an unconstrained AND-split, keep statutory ordering, and pilot a gated subset. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and causal claims are wrong or internally inconsistent, and those errors sit in the parts the task most cares about.

The process comparison is the best section. It hits the required features, separates control-flow from locks, roles, originals, and timestamps, and does not treat the 8% to 71% overlap jump as clean evidence. Minor slips remain: “strictly sequential” overstates Site A before, given the recorded 8% overlap, and “8% drop” confuses percentage points with a relative change.

The transport graph contains the required nodes and a real selection-diagram idea, and the prose correctly says an AND-split is not a transport theorem. `appeal -> cycle_time` is a causal mistake. The 90-day appeal rate is a post-notification quality outcome; it does not generate the cycle time that ends at `Notify`. `S_treatment -> treatment_parallelization` is also not a site-selection node.

Section 3 correctly refuses to identify the 9-hour median drop as a treatment effect and lists the co-interventions. It then invents a split of “about 3–4 hours from concurrency and 5–6 hours from everything else.” That precision is not in the data. Service-time drops are also pinned too narrowly on the API and the two added FTEs, underplaying the simpler mix.

Sections 5 and 9 walk that caution back. Digital-only transfer is given −6 to −9 hours and confidence 75, which reimports a confounded Site A magnitude that Section 3 scored at 25. The mapping treats `median(X+Y)` as the sum of medians and `median(max(X,Y))` as the max of medians, then multiplies by 0.38 and calls the result an overall median. Medians do not combine that way. The 38% “digital at registration” share is also an unstated identification of “originals not legally required” with Site A’s digital-at-intake construct, and 23% further assumes independence from the 40% legal share.

The steady-state queueing argument is wrong. Faster verification does not raise mean assessment arrival rate when external demand is unchanged. It can release a backlog or increase arrival variability. The jump from ρ ≈ 0.82 to 0.95 is invented: no assessment or legal service time is observed, so Kingman’s formula is only a name. The bottleneck-shift scenario does correctly show a better median with a worse tail, which is the required qualitative case. The IT-lock failure direction is also right, but confidence 80 on ranges as wide as +10 to +20 hours is not a calibrated interval.

The pilot undercuts its own causal purpose. Eight weeks of Site B volume is about 1,050 completed cases, not 1,200. σ ≈ 18 hours is implausible against a median of 44 and a p90 of 110; a normal approximation puts the scale nearer 50 hours, so the t-test power claim does not support the median/p90 estimand, especially inside four strata. Case-level randomization in shared specialist pools conflicts with the proposed quota split, so SUTVA is named and then not solved. WIP limits, huddles, the lock refactor, and parallel task creation are bundled, so a positive result would not isolate the Site A mechanism.

The candidate control flow is substantively right: identity may overlap originals receipt, document checking stays behind originals, assessment waits for both, and the supplement path is a loop rather than a fake sequence. The early legal “pre-notification” is not in the partial order. `check_soundness(...)[0]` is likely a crash if the facade returns a bool, which is the usual contract. The non-encodable list is accurate and covers data-dependent routing, capacity, locks, and durations.

The conditional-go decision, the first cohort, and the stop/expansion triggers match the constraints. The 6–9 hour benefit stated in the recommendation is still too large relative to the unidentified Site A contrast. The eight assumptions are well formed, but the independence assumption actually used for the 23% cohort is missing, as is the constant-arrival assumption behind the utilization story.
