7.5

The final write-up is a strong competing-risk design: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72, right censoring versus competing events versus already-observed breaches, and case-level contributions for a resolve at 76, an open case at 92, a withdrawal at 27, and a transfer at 68 are defined correctly. Primary Aalen–Johansen incidence with transfer as competing, landmarking as secondary, immortal-time treatment of `Escalate`/`Rework`, and the cohort/priority confounding discussion are the right analysis for these data. The case table, open-case ages, and P1 imbalance (3/15 vs 5/17) match the extract.

The score is held down by concrete defects.

The state diagram drops observed edges. `Screen→Escalate` occurs in S04 and S11, and `Rework→Resolve` occurs in S27. Both are written out of the transition list.

In the code, `1 − CIF` is printed as `No_Resolve_72` even when transfers or withdrawals/rejections are censored. Under that encoding the complement is not the actual probability of non-resolution; censoring changes the estimand. The prose states this correctly, but the printed sensitivity comparison undoes it.

`conservative_failure` is the same cause code as `competing`, so one advertised transfer scenario is a null contrast. The informative-censoring bounds described for open cases below 72 are never computed. Section 4 calls for bootstrap intervals; the code never produces them.

`pm4py.get_variants` commonly returns variant → list of traces. `int(v)` then raises, and the handler falls back to a manual count. The rework feature is therefore not actually taken from the PM4Py result in the usual API.

`CIF(∞) − CIF(72)` is stated as an estimand even though, with open cases, the infinite-horizon incidence is not identified. The conditional landmark version is the defensible one, and that is what the code implements.
