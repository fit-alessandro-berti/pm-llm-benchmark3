6.2

The write-up hits every required section, stays close to the process-mining evidence, and correctly rejects a frequency-first A1 start. Those merits are undercut by a broken year-1 constraint, a recommendation that was never effect-tested, and several control and timing overclaims.

**Year-1 budget is exceeded.** The case allows at most four modules in the first year. Phase 1 takes A2, A5, A9, and A12. Phase 2 is defined as the next six months of that same year and adds A6 and A4, with A7 and A10 in parallel. That is six to eight modules. The gates that follow are performance gates only, so the cap is never applied. The rollout spends capacity the problem does not grant.

**The chosen portfolio is not one of the portfolios that were analyzed.** Sections 3 and 4 score quick-win {A1, A5, A10, A9}, bottleneck/synchronization {A4, A5, A6, A7}, and compliance-first {A12, A9, A3, A2} on cycle time, waiting time, rework, handovers, resource demand, and control violations. Section 7 then recommends the hybrid {A2, A5, A9, A12}, followed by {A6, A4} plus A7/A10. That hybrid never receives the six-part effects treatment the format requires, so the decision actually advocated is the combination with the thinnest process case.

**The stated objective and the feasible set diverge.** The close argues that calendar time is dominated by entity-mismatch wait (31h, on the critical path in 82% of cases where it appears), external valuation (58h), customer signing (44h), and document loops. Inside a four-module year, the plan funds classification, document nudging, contract rules, and a remediation workbench. A4, A7, and A10 — the attacks on the three longest waits — sit outside the cap. Once the budget is respected, wave 1 does not maximize synchronization-delay reduction, which is the criterion the recommendation claims.

**Inflow is accelerated without the safety valve the answer itself requires.** Section 5 says any module that speeds clean work toward the nine credit analysts must be paired with A6 or a hard capacity gate, because those analysts are central in the working-together network. Phase 1 ships A5 and A2 anyway and parks A6 in an unfunded second wave. A WIP red line is a reactive stop, not that pairing. The same pattern appears in the quick-win set: A9 (contract generation) is included while clean KYC, entity resolution, and classification — its own stated data prerequisites — are absent, and that sacrifice is not named.

**Control impact is stated more broadly than the modules can deliver.** The recommendation says the chosen set hard-gates KYC-before-signing and addresses the 6.5% provisional-signing rate and the 5.8% four-eyes breaches. Phase 1 has no e-sign control (A10) and no packaging automation (A8). A12 can detect and open remediation; as specified, it does not block signing, and “freeze auto-packaging” has nothing to freeze until A8 exists. Four-eyes violations occur when the same person completes credit analysis and committee packaging in the month-end rush; those two activities stay manual. A9’s decision-to-contract match is a real control gain. The signing and segregation-of-duties claims are larger than the wave.

**A8 confuses upstream synchronization with service time.** Median wait before committee packaging is 26h because packaging waits on KYC status, normalized statements, valuation when required, and credit notes. Auto-assembly shortens the 50-minute service task and can refuse an incomplete pack. It does not remove that 26h. The matrix says it “removes 26h pack wait,” which is the local-activity error this case is built to expose.

**Concurrency is acknowledged in one closing sentence and unused in the analysis.** KYC and financial-document review often run in parallel even though the log records them in sequence, so cycle time on that span tracks the longer branch. Effects for A5 and for the KYC-side modules are written as if those waits were sequential and additive. The 98% “register in position 2” variant and the concentration of KYC handovers onto two senior specialists (inside a three-person pool) are likewise flattened or omitted.

**Ranking and gate language are weak.** The matrix walks A1–A12 in source order. Suitability labels differ, but nothing is ranked. Several pass conditions are unsupported point targets and, as written, are missing the comparison (“remediation  5%,” “down  30%,” “directionally  40%”), so the gate cannot be audited. The same dropped connectors break prerequisite chains (“registration  classify,” “ClassifyKYC 7250”).

What holds up: spending the two deep core-banking slots on A2 and A9, and keeping A5 and A12 as side cars, matches the six-month integration limit; A11 is withheld until control-completion signals exist; audit trail, false-negative escalation, extraction drift, stale contract rules, RM friction, and legal ownership of the rule library are concrete; DFG loops (3300/2800, 1750/1160, 650/510) are applied to the right activities; and the case against automating registration first — it would move the queue onto the scarce specialist and analyst pools — is the right process conclusion. The replacement portfolio does not fit the budget that conclusion has to live inside.
