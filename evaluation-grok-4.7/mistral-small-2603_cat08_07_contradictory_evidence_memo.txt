5.5

The recommendation sits in the right neighborhood—do not scale Redesign R nationally, do not fully roll back, pause survey-skipping and post-billing activation, keep coordinator ownership only where simple houses improved, and measure before any wider rollout. That judgment is undermined by factual, logical, and design errors that a decision-maker could act on incorrectly.

The required six numbered sections are not produced. The opening “memo” is a short action list with `[Your Name]` and `[Current Date]` placeholders, and it does not itself explain the trade-off. The closing section largely repeats it.

Several facts are wrong or unreadable. “3 handovers” is not the stated “3+ handovers.” Dropped signs turn “median duration change of −6.0 days” and “first-time-right +3 pp” into “median duration 6 days” and “first-time-right 3 pp,” and the same corruption hits the apartment/old-building row and the rollout gates. Apartments and old buildings are merged despite different p90 effects (+12 vs +25 days) and different mechanisms.

The evidence table attempts the required split but misfiles items. Exclusion of complaint cost after week 4 is a stated data fact, not an uncertainty. “Activation timing may have hidden failures” is an interpretation. Fitness 0.94, precision 0.51, generalization 0.56, p95 (45 to 62), sample windows, region mix, incomplete risk attributes before week 3, sparse start timestamps, permit-check absence, DFG shifts, and the survey-team vs second-visit utilization swing are missing. “Proven” benefits overclaim a short, non-comparable, compositionally harder pilot.

Metric-conflict explanations are directionally right but thin. Low precision is called “unrealistic paths” rather than an overly permissive model. Median-vs-tail is attributed only to segment treatment effects, ignoring mix, a 6-week vs 10-week window, and censoring. The cost story credits fewer handovers and skips the survey-skip vs rework-capacity shift. Temporal-profile and DFG evidence are barely used.

Alternative C is evaluated incorrectly: its risks are the practices C already suspends. D and E are conflated, then sequenced as if E comes only after D. The failure path labels a partial rollback as B.

Value of information lists M1–M8, but impacts are generic and the ranking conflicts with the decision. M5 is “low” even though coordinator ownership is what would be scaled; M1 and M6 are double-counted; nothing states the result that would reverse a choice.

The gates are not defensible. p90 ≤ 35 days, first-time-right ≥ 80%, and complaints ≤ 8% are all still worse than baseline (31 days, 82%, 6.1%), p95 and permit conformance are ungated, and aggregate thresholds do not match a high-risk-segment experiment. Pausing the skip rule while randomizing that same rule is unresolved, and mandatory permit checks for old buildings—the worst tail cell—are never an action.
