7.2

The answer meets the required seven-section structure and is unusually well grounded in segments, DFG edges, path variants, SP1/SP2/Q1/R1, and the batching, air, van, rework, and obsolescence evidence. It correctly keeps energy in kWh, refuses to treat all air and dedicated vans as unjustified, and does not make the low-emission critical ground path the default. The portfolio hot spots it uses — made-to-order at about 87% of energy and 85% of scrap, critical downtime at about 40% of transport from 11% of orders — match the case-mix table.

The quantitative layer is not reliable enough for a higher score. S5’s portfolio transport reduction of 8–18% overstates what 35–55% savings on already-low ground parcels can do. S3’s “470 → ~235” rework loops treats every inspection rework as CNC_B, although the CNC_B rework variant is only 5% of orders. S2’s −10–25% energy claim is not reconciled with wait caps that stop batches short of the 12–20 size and 38-hour wait where the 2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item gain appears. S9 pairs 760 emergency-production cases with an 8% path (960 orders) as if they were the same population. “Least-emitting observed variant” is wrong for critical stock/ground at 4.8 kgCO2e; stock → standard pack → ground is 3.6. The packaging-rework rate “620 of 10,910” uses a denominator that cannot be built from the log. Section 2 says every indicator is computable from the log, then relies on van load and a predicted on-time probability that are not attributes.

The governance plan also conflicts with the redesign. An abort whenever p90 exceeds the segment baseline would roll back S5, whose own table adds 12–24 hours, and can roll back S9, which lengthens precautionary critical orders. Those are internal contradictions, not cautious ranges.
