7.1

The redesign is structurally complete and unusually well grounded in the logs: weekly mix math, repeat rates (1140/17030 and 1260/8500), recollection and accession splits, clinical-routing shares (26.4% to 49.8%), and completion rates all check out. Scenarios differ by mix and failure mode, priorities are explicitly non-simultaneous, and downstream overload is discussed for the main levers. It is still short of a near-flawless process argument.

The clearest quantitative error is I10. A 12h transport delay is treated as if it replaces local assay, medical-validation, and specialist queues, including the claim that Complex-panel cycle time falls by more than 30 hours. Partner processing time is omitted, and median queues are added as though every Complex-panel case incurs all of them. That overstates both P2 and P3 and the trade-off row that depends on them.

P1’s gated routine release hold is aimed at the wrong resource. Medical validation is specified for Urgent and Complex-panel work, and most releases already leave from technical validation. Holding routine reports therefore does little to free medical validators. The 9h clerk cap and the upward flex of preparation staff are also underplayed against stations already at 85% and 89% with multi-hour queues.

Urgent SLA arithmetic uses unstratified edges. Register-to-receive is 8h at the peak median for all cases, not a measured Urgent transit time, so “44% of the 18h budget” is too precise. The same overconfidence appears in “guarantees” of a 1.5h prep wait and in the invented jump to “well above 105%” if two validators are pulled.

Process-mining use is strong but incomplete. Assay inflow exceeds outflow by about 1750 peak cases, the largest visible accumulation, yet technical validation at 101% is called the true throughput limit without reconciling that gap. Batch-wait is only about 4% of normal preparation exits and 13% at peak, but Batch-24 is still a standing pillar without that frequency. Peak repeat rates are also partly confounded with mix and volume. Trigger thresholds disagree internally: accession flex is “before 4h” in policy text and “>7h” on the dashboard.

Residual risks and the leading-versus-lagging dashboard are substantive, including redesign-caused digital defects and split-order traceability. Those merits keep this well above a merely competent answer; the I10 arithmetic, the mis-targeted I9 benefit, and the unstratified SLA claim keep it out of the high band.
