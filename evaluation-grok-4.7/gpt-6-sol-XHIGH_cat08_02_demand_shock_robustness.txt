The answer is a strong, constraint-compliant redesign: the seven required sections are present, case-mix arithmetic is right (about 2,300 to 3,300 orders per week, Urgent about 414 to 957, Complex-panel about 299 to 660, Routine flat near 1,400), and quoted queues, utilizations, edges, and variant rates match the logs. It designs to the 18-hour and 72-hour tails, rejects blanket I1, I3, and I9, and treats I2, I4, I5, I8, and I10 as conditional. Bottleneck migration is traced for each scenario, and every bundle names a downstream station that can saturate.

The score stays well below excellent because several controls do not survive the same logs.

The S2 trigger is miscalibrated. Assay-to-repeat versus assay-to-technical-validation is about 1,140/15,890 in the normal period and 1,260/7,240 in the peak period, roughly a 2.4× rise in repeat share. A gate at 1.5× the normal baseline would stay on through an ordinary winter peak, so it does not isolate machine degradation or an outage. The recovery line then says to restore M2 only, while the trigger itself applies to either machine.

Accession correction is identified (2 specialists, 91% peak utilization, 14-hour median queue, and a worse correction-to-recollection split) and then left vague: “provide help,” with I4 withheld because technical validation is already at 101%. That is a real migration warning without a workable relief path. The night-shift / M2 clustering of Repeat-after-failure work is likewise noted only in the residual tails, not given an operating rule.

Process-mining use is careful about period length, loops, and non-additive medians, but it stops short of the flow balances that would tighten the argument: peak assay entries far exceed exits, recollection cancellations rise from 290/830 to 370/890, and technical-validation-to-medical-validation roughly doubles per week while direct release does not. Those cuts are what show WIP accumulation and mix shift, rather than utilization alone.

Smaller defects add up. Specialist review turns on above 12 hours and off below 12 hours, so only the two-shift rule supplies hysteresis. After courier smoothing, the prose names assay and technical validation as the next constraint while the table and S4 narrative still put pre-analytical check next; peak check utilization is 96%. Nominal 85/95% load bands, and the 12-hour queue bands, would sit in alarm for most of the observed peak and would not discriminate a worse day inside that peak. The clerk rule that errors rise after 9-hour shifts is never turned into an explicit shift cap, even though those errors feed the two-person correction queue.

What remains sound is the pull limit, deadline-aware reserved capacity with an explicit breach contingency, reliability-gated machine use instead of a fixed M1/M2 split, receipt-based two-hour triage, and selective outsourcing only when it beats the local path after the 12-hour transport delay.
