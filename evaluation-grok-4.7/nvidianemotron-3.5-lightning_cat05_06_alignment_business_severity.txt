The final write-up covers every required section and, on the central point, correctly refuses to treat raw alignment cost as risk: telemetry and retries stay low severity, silent `tau` is treated as a fit, and the zero-cost auto-approve cases are called policy failures. That structure is not enough to carry the grade. Several conclusions misread the alignments or the overlay.

CL-606 is the clearest factual error. Its alignment is only `M(Assess Loss)` plus an external adjuster report and no core event. The answer calls that an Assess Damage versus Assess Loss label mismatch and a post-hoc data-mapping issue. Those facts belong to CL-611, whose alignment is the different pair `L(Assess Damage)` plus `M(Assess Loss)`. The same mix-up is repeated in the cost-versus-risk discussion, and Section 3 folds the nine external-adjuster gaps into “label mapping,” so the two patterns the task asked to keep apart are given one cause.

CL-610 is scored only as a P1 breach. A bank change one hour before payment also triggers P2: Fraud Screen and Manual Approve are required before payment. Severity 3 and a P1-only remediation understate that case relative to CL-609. CL-602 likewise stretches P3. Manual Approve is synchronous before Pay Claim; the failure is a missing fraud screen under P2, not payment before approval. CL-604 is the actual P3 case, and that severity-5 control never appears in the remediation portfolio.

CL-607 treats Notify Claimant as something that should not have occurred. The alignment marks it synchronous, and notice of a denial can be legitimate; the spurious moves are the model-only assessment, auto-approval, and payment. CL-611’s label evidence is explicit, but confidence is recorded as Low. CL-601’s narrative also says “amount 5k” for a EUR 1,200 claim.

The Python block would not run. `parse_process_tree` returns a tree, not a net and markings; `likely_cause` is not a column, so the evidence table raises `KeyError`; and the constructed log inserts model-only moves such as Fraud Screen, Assess Loss, Pay Claim, and Notify into traces where those are `M` moves. The policy flag then marks CL-609 auto-approve compliant and marks several correct manual-approval cases non-compliant, contradicting the narrative.
