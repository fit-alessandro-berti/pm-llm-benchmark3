4.8

The write-up keeps the right skeleton: June’s collapse is mostly logging and extraction, August adds an unmodeled sign-off, and missing compliance checks are still unresolved. That skeleton is undermined by inverted inferences, mis-sized effects, a causal graph that asserts unsupported links, missed length and confidence requirements, and a code block that does not implement the required experiment and would not run.

## 1. Evidence ledger
Twelve rows exist and the columns match, but several assignments are wrong or non-diagnostic.

- Stable appeal and incident rates are entered as support for a real behavioral process change. Those flat outcomes are evidence against a material operational change. The same row is later used, in the ranking, as a reason to doubt that hypothesis.
- `Manual Override` is scored as a technical-event artifact. The chronology says IT treats it as a real user action, and reviewers describe corrective use. The fitting row after technical-event removal still leaves `Manual Override` as a log move. That is a label or real-activity question, not a `Queue Routed`-style filter.
- The 32% → 81% row bundles label mapping with `Queue Routed` removal and calls the bundle isolating. The alignment table separates those steps: mapping alone reaches 58% and cost 0.44; removing `Queue Routed` then reaches 81% and cost 0.21. The omitted 58% step is the main quantitative discriminator, and it is absent from the ledger.
- Missing enhanced-review checks are said to weaken model obsolescence. Those traces are deviations from a rule the supervisor memo still affirms. They discriminate noncompliance versus missing events; they do not count against the August sign-off gap.
- “High-value attribute omitted from the report” is treated as support for an obsolete model and as evidence against a timestamp defect. An unused column is an analysis gap. It has no bearing on either hypothesis until it is joined.
- Reliability 95 on “function unchanged,” with no unresolved dependency, overstates a 40-case UI label check. That audit confirms the screen text, not end-to-end functional equivalence.
- Variant frequencies never appear as their own items: the 30 residual `Classify` traces, the 90 `Risk Screen` traces without `Queue Routed`, the 70 then 45 reversed `Notify` traces, and the 150/60 sign-off split are the actual frequency evidence.

## 2. Ranked hypothesis table
All six hypotheses are present, and employee noncompliance is correctly kept off the top. The probabilities are not tied to a defined claim, so they fit neither “share of the conformance drop” nor “this mechanism is real.”

- Label change at 92, with contradicting evidence “None material” and the expectation that fitness recovers after mapping alone, is contradicted by the supplied ladder. Mapping alone leaves fitness at 58% and mean cost at 0.44. Cost reductions are about 0.30 for the label map, 0.23 for removing `Queue Routed`, and 0.09 for source ordering. Posteriors 92 / 88 / 82 flatten that gap, and timestamp ordering is ranked as if it were nearly as large as the label shift.
- The expected observation for an obsolete model says the extension improves fitness and precision. The supplied result is a fitness gain from 66% to 86%. Precision is unmeasured, and an optional sign-off on every path is the pattern the brief warns can buy fitness by overgeneralizing. Section 4 later says precision may drop. Those two claims conflict.
- “Sign-off only on the high-value subset” is listed as contradicting evidence. The attribute was never joined, so that restriction is not an observation.
- Real process change at 25 is inconsistent with the August policy and with 210 of 620 later cases carrying `Supervisor Sign-off`. If the column means “cause of the June drop,” obsolete-model at 78 is far too high, because that gap appears after preprocessing in August. If the column means “the mechanism occurred,” 25 is far too low. The required split between cause of the measured drop and remaining operational concern is not in the table.
- The expected noncompliance pattern, concentration on high-value cases, is unmotivated. The EUR 50,000 rule adds sign-off; it does not predict more missing `Compliance Check`s. Enhanced review and high value are different partitions.

## 3. Causal-abductive explanation
The required names appear, and the prose correctly says flat outcomes can coexist with a small undetected bypass. The graph and the length do not meet the specification.

- The explanation is about 117 words against a required 180–280.
- `UI_release → technical-event` is unsupported. `Queue Routed` is a 3 June gateway change; `Manual Override` logging changes on 12 August.
- `true_process → normative_model` points the wrong way. The policy changed the process and the tree was not revised; the model did not update because the process changed.
- `normative_model` never points at `alignment_moves`, so the comparison that actually creates moves is missing.
- `high_value_attribute` is named and then left unlinked. It should condition sign-off, and it is the reason a control-flow optional task is the wrong repair.
- Arrow direction is mixed (`->` and `<-` in one list).
- The prose says the UI release “altered only surface labels” and that the ETL change “corrupted” `Decision`/`Notify` order. The first is a release-note claim plus a label audit. The second is established for 11 of 70 reversed traces, not for the log as a whole.

## 4. Counterfactual predictions
Eight rows are present. Two are near-duplicates, and several tests cannot discriminate.

- Removing `Queue Routed` and `Manual Override` together is not a test of the technical-event hypothesis. Deleting any activity the net does not contain reduces log moves, including a genuine user action. Fitness rising after that deletion is guaranteed by construction.
- “Fitness unchanged” is too weak a falsifier for the label, queue, and ordering rows. The observed pattern is partial recovery. A serious weakening result is the one already in the table: mapping alone stalls at 58%.
- Model extension is predicted to create new variants and DFG edges. Extending the tree leaves the log’s variants and DFG unchanged; it only reclassifies existing sign-off edges.
- The high-value stratification row predicts more missing checks and a rising appeal rate. Stratification does not change appeal rates, and the missing-check mechanism is not the EUR 50,000 rule.
- The data-aware guard row is the one sharp prediction: precision should improve relative to a blanket optional sign-off if and only if sign-off tracks the amount threshold. That row is inconsistent with section 2’s claim that the optional model itself improves precision.
- No row is a blinded provenance audit of the 25 and 30 enhanced traces that lack `Compliance Check`, which is the test that separates bypass from extraction loss.

## 5. Diagnostic program
Four phases, in a sensible order, cover DFGs, variants, remapping, source order, model comparison, stratification, and an audit. Isolation is only partial.

- Phase 3 runs source reordering and model extension together, so it cannot separate an ordering defect from model obsolescence without a factorial comparison.
- The risk cell for that phase never states that an optional sign-off raises fitness while accepting sign-off on low-value cases and allowing high-value cases to skip it.
- Alignment work is described as fitness loss, not as move-on-log versus move-on-model. That distinction is what separates `Risk Screen` / `Queue Routed` / `Manual Override` / `Supervisor Sign-off` from a missing `Compliance Check` or a missing `Decision`.
- `discover_variants` is not a PM4Py entry point. Precision is named once and never used as a decision criterion.
- The audit is aimed at “high-value cases,” while the unresolved traces are the enhanced-review traces with no recorded check. Those sets have not been shown to be the same.

## 6. PM4Py code
The block calls `parse_process_tree` and `convert_to_petri_net`, prints the EUR 50,000 caveat, and sketches a raw-versus-normalized comparison. It does not satisfy the reproduction requirements.

- Frequencies are not expanded. April uses three unweighted strings; June uses two; August uses one. `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` is not the call; the import path is `pm4py.objects.log.importer.parse_event_log_string`.
- The June sample omits reversed `Notify`, missing checks, `Manual Override`, residual `Classify`, and `Risk Screen` without `Queue Routed`. After the stated normalization, both retained traces fit the original tree, so the script reports a full recovery and hides every residual the investigation is about.
- `pm4py.replace_activity_names` and `pm4py.filter_activities(..., retain=False)` are not PM4Py operations. Fitness and precision are invoked as `evaluate` with mixed alignment and log arguments; the library entry points are `apply` / `fitness_alignments` and `precision_alignments`.
- Optional sign-off is written with `?`, which is not a process-tree operator. The coherent encoding is an exclusive choice with a silent skip, placed so both the standard and enhanced branches can reach it, matching the 60 and 150 counts.
- DFG discovery runs only on the April log and the result is never compared. August metrics are computed only for the candidate net, on a single already-normalized trace, with no precision comparison against the normative net.
- The script is therefore not executable and, if the API calls were repaired without changing the sampled traces, would be a misleading experiment.

## 7. Provisional conclusion
The central claim is right: label substitution, `Queue Routed`, and ingestion timestamps account for the fall from 93% to 32%, and the same preprocessing that returns June–July to 89% leaves August–September at 66% until sign-off is added to the model. The section is about 200 words against a required 250–400, and it never states a confidence level for either judgment.

It also calls the June artifacts “logging and model-maintenance defects” while listing only logging changes. Model staleness is an August effect. `Supervisor Sign-off` sitting outside the tree is then offered as a reason a compliance problem may remain. That presence is the policy change the tree missed. The open compliance questions are the un-audited missing checks, the 90 enhanced traces that have a check and no sign-off, and whether the 60 standard-path sign-offs match EUR 50,000. “Selective bypasses on high-value cases” merges those questions. Actions are aimed at the right three groups, but the audit target should be the missing-check traces plus a value-stratified sign-off check. The closing prohibition on claiming “no compliance problem” and on claiming that employees became noncompliant is the correct claim limit. A further limit is required: the 86% optional-sign-off model is not yet a valid normative model.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation
Six numbered rules cover versioning, semantic contracts, technical events, timestamp provenance, data conditions, and post-release validation. Rule 1 forces a normative-model revision for every UI change; a label-only release should force a mapping change under rule 2, and a model revision only when the allowed control flow changes. Rule 5’s “automatically annotated” overstates what governance can honestly promise. Rule 6 checks raw and normalized fitness and still does not require precision, a value-stratified guard, or a business-outcome comparison before a model is declared normative.

The grade is held down by the wrong ledger directions, the label-only recovery claim, the `Manual Override` misclassification, the unlinked and partly backwards causal graph, both shortfalls against explicit word counts, the missing confidence levels, non-identifying counterfactuals, and reproduction code that neither expands the variant tables nor can run.
