The write-up covers every required section, keeps several live hypotheses, and stays appropriately cautious on misconduct, removal, and centrality. The numbers it cites (centralities, the 8.4-to-13.9-day shift, the ~65% change, ~1.06 events per case, the 4–8% co-case band) match the tables.

The main failure is the reconciliation the question asks for. Working-together is defined as case co-occurrence, so a short routing event still counts if both resources appear on the case. Explaining Mira’s low working-together score by “brief” or “transactional” contact does not reconcile 350 Mira→Carla handovers with an 8% co-case rate. A valid account would separate repeated directed transitions, normalized pairwise overlap, and star-shaped brokerage (high handover betweenness, thin dyads) from event duration.

Hypothesis F misreads the subcontracting fragment: in Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules, the “Fraud Screen update” is the return of Bot_Rules, not work Mira performs. The executive summary is about 240 words against a 120–180 limit, is too metric-heavy for a non-technical owner, and says the absence “proves” her role. The proposed handover comparison of the full log with exception-only cases does not isolate the non-exception subset. Bot_Ingest and Vendor_Pay, the high-volume path that bypasses her, are effectively unused, and “ordinary low-value” median cycle time is widened to ordinary claims in general.
