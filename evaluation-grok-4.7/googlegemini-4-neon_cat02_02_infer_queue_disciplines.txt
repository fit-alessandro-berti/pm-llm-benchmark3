The reconstruction is unusually strong: decision sets are real, most clock arithmetic checks out, 32/32 `est_min` = realized service is a genuine finding, and QA/QB/QD are largely argued from competing predictions rather than start order. It is not nearly flawless. The defects below are enough to keep it well under a top mark.

**QC’s headline mechanism is misnamed.** `score = est - wait` is not dynamic aging. At a common decision time, `now` cancels, so `est - wait` is exactly the static index `arrival + est`. Relative order never changes while jobs wait, and no job overtakes another because it has aged. The write-up states the static equivalence, then still diagnoses “SPT with wait-time aging” and treats C04-over-C06 and C01-over-C07 as aging/anti-starvation evidence. Under that index C04 (10:29) always beat C06 (10:31), and C01 (10:36) always beat C07 (10:58); there is no crossover. HRRN, which does age, is correctly shown to fail at 11:40. The fitted rule is a static FCFS–SPT compromise plus a post-10:50 exception, not aging. That is a real interpretive error in the primary label, even though the ranking itself matches 10:10, 10:18, 10:32, 11:34, and 11:40.

**The C04–C06 margin is inside the noise the prompt warns about.** The index gap is two minutes. Logged arrivals can be up to two minutes late. That fragility is not applied to the one comparison that is supposed to reject pure SPT. Separately, “6 of 7 QC selections” is loose: there are six release decisions, and the index hits five. Priority and earliest-deadline-first never diverge on these waiting sets; “frequently co-predict” understates a total confound. Alpha = 1 is fit on the same seven jobs used to praise it.

**QB is slightly over-claimed in the summary statistics and the diagnosis table.** Strict SPT/SRPT fails at six releases (09:31, 09:48, 10:00, 10:14, 10:28, and 10:36), not five. “In-progress resumption bias” is not identified: every resume is also what FIFO would do, because B01 is always the oldest job, and 10:28 (fresh B04) already kills resume-first as a necessary rule. The second stop is time-aligned with B05, but the logged reason is reassignment and the job started is B03; calling both stops P1 safe-stop latency blurs that.

**Smaller issues.** QA’s 09:05 episode contradicts lookahead appointment protection, which the chosen diagnosis already drops, so it is a weak “contradicting” case. Test 2’s next-job fork is conditional and mushy (“B03 if the override still happens”). QD FIFO-over-SPT-and-priority really rests on one clean release (D-M at 11:25); 11:48 is explicitly scarcity-confounded and should not sit in the supporting cell as if it identified FIFO. Confidence 68 on QD is a bit high relative to the push/pull/HOL/unavailability equivalence the text itself describes.

What keeps the score here is the actual decision-level work: A03 versus A02/A04, A02 versus A04 at 08:32, B04 versus B06/B07 at 10:28, C05’s non-preemptive jump only after 10:50 while C07 was still feasible at 11:40, D04 over D08, and the D-F idle while D10 fit before the meeting.
