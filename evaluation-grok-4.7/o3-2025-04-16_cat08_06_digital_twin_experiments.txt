5.0

The design covers the required sections and recognizes seasonality, calendars, shared queues, and architect/inspection coupling, but several choices would bias intervention comparisons, and the code does not meet the PM4Py or estimation requirements.

The state model is too coarse for this process. Parallel planning, fire, and environmental reviews need an explicit join before decision drafting; that synchronization is absent. Appeal and planning/legal rework are named via flags, yet only the tree’s completeness and inspection loops are estimable, so the complex-variance variants cannot be generated. `objection_flag` is stored as if objections were observed, while the log only supports a latent mixture on public-notice duration. Withdrawal is inside the boundary with no mechanism. The scheduler role is missing, and clerk Monday assignment, simple-only planners, fire Tue–Thu limits, the environmental 40% reservation, and case continuity (the same planner drafting the decision) are not state. The inspection batch key `(district, architect_id, week)` does not implement district batching across architects, which is the actual D3 lever. Neighborhood objections that hit several cases are collapsed into independent longer notices, so D9 has nothing to act on.

Parameter estimation double-counts demand: a non-homogeneous Poisson process fit to historical counts already contains architect bursts, and a compound Poisson then adds them again. Monday seasonality is left as a broken `exp(_Monday)` term. Inter-event time is defined as waiting from the previous event in the case, which is invalid on the parallel fragment and mixes queue wait, service, calendar delay, and applicant delay. The provided median/p90 service and wait tables are unused. Start-time imputation is declared nearly unbiased after calibration, which is circular: service and wait remain non-identified. Council-week legal capacity is set to an invented 15%. `pm4py.resources.calendar` is not a real PM4Py API. Rework is a single geometric per case type, with no separate no-show, applicant-readiness, or appeal mechanism. Branching via gradient-boosted trees is not tied back to the variant-share table.

Assumptions omit the statutory 14-day public-notice floor and its point mass, treat prior violations as available for D2 when they are not in the log, and leave D1’s applicant burden outside a boundary that starts at portal submission. The experiment table has no numeric factor levels. Trade-off cells are stripped to phrases such as “Expect missing-doc loops,” so effects and costs are unreadable. One-at-a-time runs plus a single D10 bundle do not identify interactions among inspection skip, batching, and peak staffing. Stresses are only a flat +30% demand, one absent inspector, and both together; they do not cross peaks, Monday bursts, council weeks, or objection regimes. Eligibility under missing violation and area-threshold data is unspecified.

The run design treats a seasonal system as steady-state: a 90-day WIP warm-up “until steady” has no steady state here, and initial WIP for cases whose medians are 48–140 days is undefined. “Two full seasonal cycles” in a three-year horizon is wrong. Common random numbers are claimed, then undone by `base_seed + run_id` (and `seed+exp_id` in code, which also fails when ids are strings). Batch-means intervals are specified for independent replications; with seasonality those batches are not stationary. A Kolmogorov–Smirnov check at about 31,200 cases will reject a lognormal for trivial departures, and a lognormal cannot represent a fixed 14-day notice plus a heavy objection tail. Empirical resampling also cannot extend the tail beyond the sample. Morris screening does not propagate the service/wait split or latent objections into the decision metrics.

Validation confuses token alignments with timestamp accuracy. In-sample KS tests on the service and wait distributions the model samples from are circular. There is no check against the published variant mix or median/p90 table, no seasonal split of the three-month hold-out, and no check that training did not include that hold-out. Cross-case burst and batching structure is never validated.

Decision thresholds are unanchored. Goals of 30/60/90/150 days sit at or above current medians, so the baseline already passes some of them. Utilization of 60–85% conflicts with environmental time that is only 60% available for permits. Fairness is a district median gap under 10 days and ignores architects, who are the population D3 and D7 redistribute. Selection does not wait on a passed validation and has no uncertainty rule across ten policies and many KPIs.

The skeleton correctly loads the excerpt with pandas and calls `format_dataframe` with the three required keys, and it keeps a simulation layer separate from mining. It also imports removed `pm4py.objects.dfg.factory`, so it crashes before analysis. The tree is passed to a file importer and then discarded; `pm4py.parse_process_tree` is the matching API, and the comment that PM4Py needs PTML or JSON is false. Service time is set to 10% of mislabeled inter-completion “waiting,” which collapses the two quantities. There is no routing placeholder. Variant, DFG, performance-DFG, and arrival-average calls are only partly credible, and the arrival mean uses days that have submissions only.
