The analysis is strong and mostly auditable. Crude means, the within-complexity reversal (−2.14, −2.21, −3.43 h), pooled standardization (−2.51 h), the Kitagawa split (+9.44 / −2.72), matched night/high-load contrasts, the compliance/rework remainder identity (about 0.8 + 1.4 + 1.5 h), and the overlap problem are computed correctly. Stratification is not treated as identification. Queue time is separated from review service. Group is handled as a treatment label, a routing proxy, and a bundle. The pilot-plus-capacity plan, rollback rules, and five falsification tests are concrete and appropriately cautious. The Python is consistent with the stated PM4Py approach, avoids fake performance timestamps, and the claimed +6.71 h, −2.51 h, and 40 support warnings follow from the code.

The lead section still overreaches. The −2.5 h pooled contrast and the +9.4 / −2.7 Kitagawa split use different weights, but section 1 presents them as one decomposition. “Read as a total effect there is no sign of harm,” and 70/100 confidence that the crude penalty is “not an effect of Orion assignment,” claim more than the design identifies. The 85% wait share describes the residual association; it is not evidence for the confounding verdict, and calling it a mediated pathway treats that residual as an Orion effect. “Probably fail” because Orion’s mix would average 24.7 h at Nova’s stratum means is the forecast section 4 correctly marks as not allowed.

Smaller defects: “single-case cells” mislabels the Low contrasts (1 vs 2 and 1 vs 5). The DAG has no direct `shift → queue_wait` arrow despite the shift/load and calendar-time hypotheses, and several speculative arrows are only waved through as assumptions. `rework → review_service` is called definitional while the arithmetic puts rework’s extra time in the unlabelled remainder, with review service unchanged. The allowed column’s “runs through waiting” is a causal conclusion. The pilot send-back rule truncates Nova’s wait tail; intention-to-treat is named, but that bias is not. The 10-versus-36 case claim states neither alpha nor power.

Those are precision and identification slips in an otherwise careful argument, enough to keep it short of nearly flawless.
