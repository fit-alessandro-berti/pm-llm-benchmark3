4.7

The write-up covers every required section and keeps more than one role in play, but it is not a reliable process-mining interpretation. Several claims are factually wrong, the metric that the question turns on is misdefined, and the narrative treats a broker story as established.

The central contradiction is mishandled. Handover out-degree, in-degree, and betweenness on a normalized graph measure breadth and bridging, not handover volume or importance. Working-together here is case co-occurrence, not “multiple consecutive events,” joint execution, or parallel work. Mira→Carla is 350 handovers; against roughly 4,100 cases that is about 8%, which matches the stated 8% co-case rate. The apparent clash is mostly many moderate ties versus dense team cliques, plus short intermittent touches, not evidence that she rarely meets the people she hands work to. The text then says low co-occurrence means she is not a bottleneck. The absence window points the other way for exception cases, and low working-together cannot clear that. It also invents a Mira–Omar / Mira–Priya co-case ranking that the table does not contain, then uses that invented “no collaboration” point as evidence against the broker hypothesis. Low intensive co-work is what a broker looks like.

Other hard inaccuracies: 920/4,750 is not “~20% of triage claims” (Bot_Rules mixes triage and fraud screen; an edge count is not a claim share). Exception share 0.48 is a proportion, not a correlation, and it is inflated by routing into her queue rather than proof that she detects or flags problems. “High variance” cycle-time contribution is restated as a correlation with longer paths. Carla/Diego are given a Request Missing Information overlap that belongs to Elise/Farid and Temp_1. Omar/Priya are called the slow resources while Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h) are ignored. The 8.4→13.9 day shift is called a doubling; it is about 1.65×. “Lack of audit trail” and “fraud screening bypasses” are not in the evidence. The latter leans toward control failure without support. “Queue cleaner” is read as early quality detection; in context it is a process owner diminishing her. Subcontracting is sometimes reversed (Omar→Mira→next rather than Mira→specialist→Mira). Bot_Rules is described as a closed automated loop even though the cited fragment runs through Mira. Ordinary low-value cycle time was unchanged during her absence; that null result is dropped, and the summary calls her indispensable.

The follow-up methods would not do what is claimed. `filter_variants` does not filter on `exception_flag` or on time. Role discovery does not measure workload imbalance. Waiting time “before Mira’s events” is undefined in a window where she has no events. Subcontracting loops cannot show “automation failures” by themselves. Fast 4.2-minute events are treated as likely mis-assignments.

The recommendation correctly refuses removal and separates immediate action, monitoring, and deferral. It still imposes an immediate adjuster “second click” on specialist routing that may be the designed medical/legal path, and it underuses bots, vendors, and temps. Calibration slips throughout: “proving,” “fault,” “bypasses,” and a single winning label in the summary despite an explicitly ambiguous log.
