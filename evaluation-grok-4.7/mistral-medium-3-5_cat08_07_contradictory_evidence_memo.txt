5.5

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and covers every required section, but it is not reliable enough for an executive decision. Several load-bearing numbers are misstated, one alternative is inverted, and the process-mining explanation confuses fitness deviations with low precision.

The evidence table labels distorted figures as facts. Simple houses improved by 6.0 days median, +3pp first-time-right, and −1pp complaints; the memo repeatedly reports “median cycle time 6 days,” “first-time-right 3pp,” and “complaints 1pp.” Old buildings’ changes are +25 days at p90 and −11pp first-time-right. Apartments are +12 days and −8pp. The memo assigns “p90 cycle time 25 days” and “first-time-right 11pp” to apartments and old buildings together. Read as levels, a 25-day p90 would be better than the 31-day baseline, which reverses the segment conclusion. Second visits are 18% of cases; 17% is the failed-visit-to-second-visit edge. Those figures are mixed.

Alternative C is misread. C scales coordinator ownership while suspending survey skipping and post-billing activation. The table says C’s risk is that it does not address those two practices. The risk is the definition of the option, so the comparison among C, D, and E is not coherent. The recommendation then widens the blur: immediate action limits R to simple houses, while “what to scale” extends coordinator ownership to every case and adds an unsupported 80% utilization cap.

The fitness/precision account is technically backwards. Low precision means the discovered model permits many variants that the pilot log does not show. Billing before activation is a move-on-log, so it was observed and the model did not accept it cleanly. A missing permit check is a move-on-model: the model expected a step the log omitted. That is a fitness deviation, not an example of an overly permissive model, and it is not “unseen in the baseline.” Generalization’s drop from 0.70 to 0.56 is listed and never interpreted.

The decision also skips the main identification problems. Pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings, and segment and risk attributes are incomplete before week 3. Aggregate tail, rework, and complaint gaps are therefore confounded, while the 34% simple-house result is too incomplete to scale on. Median improvement despite a harder mix would actually strengthen that result; within-segment tails still worsen outside simple houses. Neither point is made. Idle survey capacity (78% to 52%) against saturated second-visit capacity (66% to 92%) is unused, as are the temporal deviations that tie apartment activation delay and second-visit delay to coordinator load.

The cost gates contradict the baseline. Estimated full cost is already EUR 198 baseline versus EUR 207 pilot, and the pilot estimate is incomplete. Rolling back above EUR 190, or requiring EUR 185 before expansion, would reject outcomes that beat the baseline. M5 is ranked last even though the memo would extend the centralized ownership model whose absence risk M5 tests. The 80%, 50%, 5%, and 2% thresholds are stipulated rather than tied to a decision loss.
