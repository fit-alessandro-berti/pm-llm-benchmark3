8.4

The diagnosis is unusually strong and matches the evidence. April is treated as a schema/pipeline incident (unchanged breach rate, cycle times, and mature variant rates alongside the UNKNOWN, missing-queue, and unmapped spikes). June is a routing mix shift with stable within-variant rates, so recalibration rather than retraining. July–August are supplier-driven process and concept drift, with censoring already pulling the cutoff rate below the mix-weighted matured rate. September adds Partner Z. October’s 0.16 is correctly rejected as a comparable breach rate, with the warning that downward recalibration would likely be the wrong direction. Required tests, staged training decisions, monitor gating, and failure modes are all present and proportionate. Falsifiers are specific.

Deductions are for concrete slips, not for the overall story.

The Partner Z gap is over-precise. Only the V3 shares (58% vs 36%) are given; the 0.42 vs 0.31 split and the “~0.03 residual” assume an unstated V1/V2 allocation. A sensitivity range was required there. Calling the October 0.55×0.16+0.45×0.60 scenario a “bounding exercise” is also wrong: that is one imputed scenario. The actual bounds are about 0.09 to 0.54. ECE is compared with the counterfactual matured gap (about 0.12 in August) rather than the cutoff calibration gap versus the observed 0.28 (about 0.09), so “calibration-in-the-large dominates ECE” is a bit stronger than the cutoff numbers support.

The required sketch would not run as written and contradicts its own prose. `format_dataframe` renames `case_id` / `activity` / `timestamp`, but later code still uses the old names. `discover_performance_dfg` is not unpacked, unlike `discover_dfg`. The JS divergence compares full trace-variant keys with a V1/V2/V3 baseline, so it does not measure the mix shift it claims to measure. Dedup drops `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` while section 6 says to keep `source_event_id` in the key.
