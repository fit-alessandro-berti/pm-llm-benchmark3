7.4

The design is complete, modular, and unusually careful about calendars, exogenous delays, latent objections, censoring, and not treating an unvalidated twin as an optimum. It still has several material specification errors that would mis-calibrate demand, bias paired comparisons, or leave an implementer with contradictory control-flow and resource rules.

The arrival model double-counts volume. Submissions are used to fit \(\lambda(t)\), and that same intensity is then used as the cluster-centre rate while every centre also spawns a size drawn from a distribution whose mean is greater than 1. Case intensity has to be the centre intensity times mean cluster size (or the centre process has to be fit on cluster starts). As written, both the estimation plan and `ArrivalGenerator` inflate arrivals by about the mean burst size. Cloning one attribute vector onto every case in a burst also overstates dependence relative to the stated “often shared” assumption.

Common-random-number pairing will not survive the interventions that change paths. Separate streams are necessary but not sufficient: D2, D5, and D8 skip or add activities, so sequential service draws shift. Paired median and p90 differences then mix policy effects with random-stream desynchronization. Path-stable keys per case and potential activity are required.

Public-notice timing contradicts itself. One clause starts the notice when planning finishes; the next says the simulator follows the tree, which starts it only after the whole parallel block. The excerpt is compatible with that join (notice begins after fire finishes), so the parenthetical treats a consistent trace as a conflict. Starting the notice at planning completion would overlap a 14-day delay with fire and environmental review and shorten the critical path.

The simplified tree cannot replay the illustrative log, and the conformance discussion misses why. PM4Py loop semantics require the redo child between iterations, but the log repeats `Completeness check` and would repeat inspection activities, with no `Completeness rework` or `Inspection rework` events. Token replay of P001 against the parsed tree should fail for that reason, not only because appeals and legal rework are absent. Performance DFGs are also treated too loosely as waiting-time references: on completion-only timestamps they measure inter-event flow, and they make a parallel review block look sequential.

Activity-to-pool mapping is incomplete where capacity is actually shared. Decision drafting is visibly done by the same planners as planning review; that contention is not specified. `Reject application` has no pool. `Inspection scheduling` is performed by `scheduler_1`, who is in neither the inspector nor the clerk pool. Monday portal support is recorded in the calendar prose but never turned into a reduction of completeness-check capacity, despite Monday arrivals at 2.2× being a main queue driver. Inspectors are additionally given Monday office work the source calendar does not state. In the skeleton, `blocked_fraction` is never applied, and `utilisation()` divides by elapsed horizon minutes rather than rostered minutes, so it does not compute the KPI defined in the decision section.

Uncertainty handling is ambitious but not reliable as specified. The outer bootstrap-plus-ranges distribution is a frequentist/sensitivity mixture, not a posterior, so the 90% “credible interval” used as a hard decision rule is mislabeled. Quantiles under the stated right-censoring need a censored estimator; dropping unfinished cases and quantiling only completions both bias the tail, and the skeleton’s `run` only returns completed cases. Sobol indices are attached to intervention rank, which is not a variance-decomposition output. “No events on a rostered day” confounds absence with low demand, and inter-inspection gaps confound travel with service and waiting. A seasonal process will not flatten on a Welch plot; the snapshot warm-up is the sounder of the two proposals.

The experiment matrix, service-versus-wait separation, latent objection mixture, validation scorecard, and explicit non-optimality claim are the parts that hold up. The PM4Py layer itself is largely conventional: pandas loading, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, DFG and performance DFG, case-arrival average, and NaN service/wait columns when only completions exist. Those strengths do not remove the calibration and comparison defects above.
