8.5

The answer is a strong, constraint-aware redesign and is right on the main judgments: made-to-order dominates recorded energy and scrap, critical orders dominate transport, air and dedicated vans are not equally unjustified, and the low-emission critical stock/ground path is not an acceptable universal substitute. The deductions below are what keep it below a near-flawless score.

The opening reconciliation overstates a data problem. Selected-path shares summing to 89% are expected for a partial variant table, not evidence of unmatched cases. Grouping that 11% with the real DFG gaps (11,730 terminal events versus 12,000 orders, and 9,410 listed arrivals at Confirm delivery versus 11,200 confirmations to Close) treats incompleteness as inconsistency. Those two DFG gaps are correctly added and correctly left unexplained. A related mismatch is missed: the critical emergency/van variant is given as 8% (about 960 orders) while dedicated-van frequencies are only 610–620, and emergency-production frequencies are 720–760. The answer makes reconciliation a premise, then uses the 8%, 14-hour, 3% late, and 52 kg figures without flagging that this variant cannot sit inside the van counts.

Section 4 sometimes turns observational contrasts into intervention effects. The 35–55% consolidation figure is the reported difference versus individual ground parcels, not a demonstrated result of twice-daily departures. The 75–83% air-to-ground figure is a fair reciprocal of the stated 4–6× ratio, and the 54% CNC_A figure is a fair reading of 2.8 to 1.3 kWh per item, but both are still local associations. The caveats and the warning not to add S1/S4, S2/S10, S3/S7, or S8/S9 savings partly repair this; the S5 cell remains too definite.

The 95% and 96% “on-time” figures are simply 100% minus the 5% and 4% late rates. That ignores 530 delay cancellations and 270 orders absent from the terminal counts, and it compares historical segment lateness with SP1’s prospective 85% ground-feasibility test. The later calibration warning helps, but the comparison is still looser than the surrounding claims.

The batch-wait formula is directionally sound, and flooring it at zero is coherent, but “negative uncapped budget” does not match the displayed \(W_{\max}\). The quantile is also never tied to SP1’s 85% rule, so two service standards can conflict. S1 is analyzed and then left only implicit in the three-stage package.

The weighted concentrations are arithmetically right under the stated assumptions: made-to-order is 34% of orders and about 87% of order-weighted energy and 85% of scrap; critical orders are about 41% of transport. The 22% SP1 rate is about 420 Standard-stock Service-contract orders, and that figure is properly not treated as 420 proven air substitutions. Segment distinctions, Q1, obsolescence, rework, empty running, and fallback capacity are handled without a blanket modal ban. Those merits are why the gaps above do not pull the score further down.
