The policy clears the main traps: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is re-opened on new evidence, calibration is segment-specific, and spillover is carried into the scenario table. The direct-benefit arithmetic for I-04, I-09, and I-02 is reproducible, and the six required sections are all present.

It is not tight enough to score higher. The deployable rule and the allocation contradict each other. Section 1 sets `p_adj = p_now − 0.085` and a 0.35 floor, which puts I-08 at about 0.345 and therefore ineligible, yet the table marks I-08 eligible. The same rule says Strategic effects should be taken from the lower half of the range, but every score uses the lower endpoint for every segment. I-06’s lower-bound net (~€1.5k) beats I-02 (~€0.95k), so the stated tier order would swarm I-06 first; the data-quality override that reverses this is not part of the rule. Section 5 then says spillover belongs in the score, which Section 1 never does.

The base-case “positive” result is an assumption, not a result. With six untreated cases, midpoint spillover of 0.02, and the stated costs, net benefit changes sign once average untreated value exceeds roughly €42k. That breakeven sits inside the value range of the book and is never shown. The adverse €24k spillover cap is also not derived. The adverse breach band (0.26–0.36) does not match “one selected case gets no benefit”: zeroing I-09 leaves about 0.18.

One reserved slot is assigned three jobs at once—regulatory hold, I-06 after validation, and a fairness set-aside for I-07 or I-01—without a priority order. I-04 is swarmed immediately despite 10 hours still in triage, with no act-now versus wait comparison, and I-08’s midpoint net (~€4.6k, second-highest) is dropped on an unstated maximin rule without the expected-value regret. False-alarm burden is subtracted as a sure cost in every scenario, including when the swarm still reduces risk.
