The framework is strong: wait, service, and sojourn are separated correctly; missing starts are bounded by resource precedence rather than filled in silently; open and still-waiting cases stay in the analysis; and the bottleneck claim is tied to the arrival burst, growing waits, and the 14:00 state. Those parts would survive a recalculation. The station-level numbers and several aggregates would not, and some of them contradict the answer’s own definitions and its own code.

Case bounds for the required rows are largely right. P05’s S2 start really is feasible only on [09:45, 10:15], P07’s S1 start on [09:25, 10:00], P11’s S2 start on [12:00, 12:25], and P15’s S1 start on [11:40, 12:15], with midpoints 10:00, 09:42:30, 12:12:30, and 11:57:30. Elapsed open service is correctly 30, 5, and 30 minutes for P15, P17, and P21, and total sojourn for P01–P14 really is independent of those imputations. The listed sojourns 60 through 230 are individually correct.

The aggregates built from that list are not. They sum to 2050 case-minutes, so the mean is 146.4 minutes and the median is 145. The answer states 2055, 146.8, and a median of 155. The P12–P14 S1 wait band is given as 85–95 minutes, but P13’s wait is 80.

Station WIP is an imputation-invariant area, because a missing start only moves time between queue and service. Over [08:00, 14:00] the exact means are 5.79 cases at S1 (2085/360) and 3.72 at S2 (1340/360), summing to total process WIP of 9.51. The answer reports roughly 4.3 and 3.3. The same table says S1 active WIP is about 0.7–0.8 while S1 utilization is about 76% of two resources. Those cannot hold together: 76% of two servers is about 1.5 cases in service. Central busy time is 570 minutes at S1, and 570/360 = 1.58. S1 queue means are about 4.11–4.31 across the three imputations, not 3.5–3.7. S1 peak WIP reaches 8 just after 11:30 (P13–P20 all still in the station), not 7. The 12:00 landmark is S1 WIP 7 and S2 WIP 5, so total 12, not 7, 6, and 13. The 10:00 and 14:00 landmarks are right, including S2 WIP 6 and S1 WIP 2 at the horizon.

Busy time is likewise off in a way the sensitivity table then repeats. With zero-length imputed service, S1 busy time is 535 minutes, central 570, maximum 605, hence about 74–84% of 720 resource-minutes. The published 510/545/580 and 71–81% are each 25 minutes low. S2 is 515 / 542.5 / 570 against 705 available minutes, about 73–81%. The published 510/538/565 omit P17’s 5 elapsed minutes, which A9 and A10 say are included. Started-case mean waits are about 65.2–68.6 minutes at S1 (21 cases) and 36.6–40.0 at S2 (16 cases), not 70–73 and 39–42. Completed S2 service means run about 34–38 minutes, not 33–37.

The two-hour rate table mixes bin edges. Arrivals and S1 completions behave as right-closed bins: 14, 7, 1 arrivals and 7, 7, 6 S1 completions, with the 10:00 and 12:00 events kept in the earlier bin. The same rule puts P08’s 12:00 S2 completion in 10:00–12:00 and yields S2 counts 3, 6, 5. The answer’s 3, 5, 6 moves only that one S2 event into the afternoon, so the late-morning versus afternoon S2 departure rates are not comparable to the S1 row beside them. The code uses half-open intervals and would print yet a third set (arrivals 13, 8, 1). Section 7 would also print the corrected busy times and wait means. The narrative figures were not produced by the code that is offered as their reproduction.

The time-varying bottleneck conclusion still stands on evidence the arithmetic does not touch: 14 arrivals by 10:00, S1 waits moving from about 5–20 minutes to about 80–105, and six of the eight unfinished cases sitting at S2 at 14:00. Calling the completed-service means “broadly overlapping” is the weak step. Central means are about 27 minutes at S1 and 36 at S2, and the imputation ranges do not close that gap. Equal headcount therefore does not imply equal cases-per-hour capacity. The C2 discussion is appropriately non-causal: the outage is only 15 minutes, P06 occupies C2 until 10:50, and P08’s 55-minute wait is only partly contemporaneous with maintenance.

Little’s Law is applied in the right place and correctly described as an accounting identity for P01–P14 over H = 350, not as a steady-state result. The identity is evaluated on the wrong sum, 2055 instead of 2050. The single-activity PM4Py projection makes case waiting degenerate, because the whole sojourn is coded as one service interval; the case-level label is present, but the call does not demonstrate a meaningful service/wait split. Format, assumption labeling, and the optimistic/central/pessimistic logic are otherwise in good shape. The score is held down by station metrics that are wrong by enough to contradict the utilization arithmetic, a sensitivity table that repeats those errors, and a reproducible script that does not reproduce the published figures.
