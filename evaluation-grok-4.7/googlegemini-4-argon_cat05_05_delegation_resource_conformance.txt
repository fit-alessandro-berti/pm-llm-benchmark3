The narrative is strong on the planted authority questions, but it is not nearly flawless. One high-confidence case call is under-supported, the required checking code would not reliably reproduce the findings, and several statements are firmer than the evidence.

The best reasoning is real. Alias normalization correctly makes CHG-508 a drafter/approver breach. CHG-507 is correctly unauthorized because Eva was still only an Independent Verifier on 2026-08-09, with no QM delegation. CHG-510 correctly stacks a prohibited subdelegation, Clara’s self-approval, and an out-of-scope retrospective review. CHG-501 correctly treats the signed PDF as the authority instrument and the 2026-08-12 QMS row as migration lag. CHG-504 stays indeterminate. CHG-503 is not treated as proof that Diego self-verified. CHG-505 is rightly late: Tuesday 2026-08-11 plus two business days ends Thursday 2026-08-13, so Friday 2026-08-14 misses RR4, and Ben’s production-equipment delegation does not cover `cooling_system`. Sick-leave use of `u_bk` on CHG-506 is properly treated as unauthorized account use. Section 4 gives benign and adverse readings without treating the Clara–Ben pair as collusion by itself.

CHG-509 is the main analytical miss. D-QM-02 requires Asha to be absent. Her recorded leave ended 2026-08-14. Saturday 2026-08-15 is a weekend, which does not by itself establish formal absence or unavailability. The finding nonetheless states that absence was met and labels the case VALID_SUBSTITUTION with high confidence and low severity. The missing-evidence cell never asks where Asha actually was. That case should be conditional or INDETERMINATE. The same assumption is hardcoded: approval logic can emit `VALID_DELEGATION(D-QM-02)` without testing principal absence.

The PM4Py section meets the checklist on paper and then breaks it in execution. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called without `keep_violations=True`. The default retains cases that satisfy four-eyes, while the prints describe those rows as RR1/RR2 violations, so CHG-502, CHG-508, and CHG-510 would be omitted and conforming cases reported as breaches. `discover_handover_of_work_network` and `discover_working_together_network` return an SNA object; `hw_net["connections"]` and `wt_net["connections"]` should be attribute access and should raise `TypeError`. D-QM-02 and D-IC-03 are given invented `db_recorded` timestamps. `LOW_RISK_STANDING_OK` is emitted even where RR1 fails. Service-account modes and the manual-judgment note are clear; the network step still does not run as written.

Smaller overstatements accumulate. Section 2 calls D-QM-01 cryptographically signed; the record is a signed PDF, and Section 3 correctly admits that a trusted timestamp is unproven. “48-hour elapsed business time” is actually 48 calendar hours. The matrix assigns Ben and Clara a standing LOW-risk approval franchise the role table does not give. Ben “may act only under a valid QM delegation,” so CHG-508’s `training_document` approval needed a scope analysis, not “N/A (LOW-risk).” Post-2026-08-11 verifications by Eva are scored as full RR2 passes without separating person-separation from the fact that her Independent Verifier interval had ended. CHG-503’s secondary UNAUTHORIZED label, and the phrase `u_dm == svc_qms`, are stronger than a shared terminal with no re-authentication. The conclusion’s “two” scope failures lists three cases and counts CHG-510 twice. CHG-502 and CHG-508 are the same LOW-risk RR1 breach, rated Medium and High.

Sections 4 and 6 are specific and usable. The three named investigations are sensible, with CHG-506 first. Those strengths do not offset an overconfident CHG-509 conclusion and checking code that inverts the segregation flags and crashes on the required networks.
