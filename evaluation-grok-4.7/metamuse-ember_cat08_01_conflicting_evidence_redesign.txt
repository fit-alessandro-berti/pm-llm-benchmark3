**7.5**

The memo is numerically careful, segment-aware, and specific enough to pilot. It is not nearly flawless. Several analytical overreaches would mislead a sponsor on effect size, compliance, and how complete the event picture is.

Checked counts match the prompt: 3,600/5,000 into auto-screen, 2,550 auto→fast approval, 240 fraud→fast approval, 2,300 fast approval→dispatch, evidence inflows 650+780=1,430 with 890 returns and 320 abandonments, complaint inflows 140+80+260+30=510, fraud inflows 400+360+220=980, fitness 0.91 with 68% fitting traces, reopen 14.1% vs 3.3% and 9.2% overall, cost gap 31% before rework vs 9% after, segment shares and medians, 15–35% manual-inflow risk, 30–45% portal effect, 6–18 hour gate, and EUR 95 vs EUR 410 wrong-part cost. High fitness with only 68% fitting traces is read correctly as local deviations in about one-third of cases. The memo keeps medians, p90s, and segment splits, and it states that exact dominance and ROI cannot be computed from this evidence.

The frequency graph is treated as a closed operating picture. It does not conserve flow. Manual eligibility review has about 2,600 listed inflows and 1,400 listed outflows. Fraud/risk review, repair quote, dispatch, repair, quality check, and complaint/reopen also fail to balance. Listed endings reach 3,625 of 5,000 cases. Edge counts are sometimes narrated as cases (320 abandoned, 310 sent back) in a log where loops are explicit: evidence repeats in 26% of cases that enter it, and 510 complaint edges sit above 9.2% of 5,000 cases (about 460). Those frequencies are usable as observed directly-follows counts. They are not a full case ledger.

The claim that 12.5% high-value repair rework is “matching” 45 rework edges against 360 repair→quality-check edges is wrong. 45/360 = 12.5% is an aggregate transition ratio. The prompt’s 12.5% is the high-value rate and 4.0% is the low-risk rate, so an aggregate that includes low-risk repairs should fall below 12.5%. Quality check also shows 390 listed outflows against 360 inflows, so that ratio is not a clean case rate.

Redesign A borrows reviewed/repair outcomes. The 96-hour and 120-hour medians, and the 5% and 6% reopen rates, belong to the reviewed/repair path, including quote, customer approval, repair, and quality check. A still allows reviewed replacement after a manual or fraud gate. Review delay is on the scale of the manual and fraud edges (medians roughly 5–16 hours on the cited edges), not automatically the full repair-path duration. Fast approval’s 14.1% reopen rate is tied heavily to dispatch and, in the temporal profile, to wrong part selection. A explicitly leaves part selection unresolved, yet the comparison table still gives A lower EUR 410 exposure and a reopen rate “toward 5–6%.” The pilot’s 10–12% go-range is more cautious than the table, so the document disagrees with itself. The same temporal profile says Manual eligibility review → Repair quote is often too slow for Premium cases. A’s first pilot pushes Premium high-value onto that edge. A priority slot only partly offsets that, and the redesign section itself does not confront the finding.

Redesign C conflicts with R3. R3 requires every reopened claim to pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial. The diagnosis correctly treats Escalation board exits (65 dispatch, 45 repair, 10 denial) as R3-ambiguous. C then replaces the full manual-review cycle with a delta review of only the disputed element, so a part-only dispute can close without manual eligibility review. Section 6 does not list that failure. C’s other failure modes are concrete: two inspectors, three technicians, wasted parallel quotes, and loss of the daily escalation slot’s satisfaction effect.

Redesign B is the cleanest design. It targets the evidence loop (1,430 entries, 890 returns, mean 34h / p90 88h, 26% repeats, 22% unclear-defect cases at a 140-hour median), keeps the 30–45% figure as a prompt-given portal range, and treats low digital literacy as a design constraint. It correctly limits itself: B does not enforce R1 and does not stop a wrong-part dispatch.

The experiment order is substantive: measure value and evidence completeness before claiming R1/R2 rates, pilot Premium high-value first, use the evidence pilot to free manual capacity before the 15–35% inflow shock, hold Standard high-value as a delayed comparison, and score post-close reopen, p90, credits, and reshipments. Shared manual and fraud capacity can still slow the comparison segment, so a cycle-time gap can appear because the control worsened. The 10–12% threshold is a chosen cutoff; the observed reviewed/repair rates are 5% and 6%, and review-gating is a weak instrument for reaching them.

In the delivered comparison table, directional signs are missing, so cells such as “touch cost,” “Strong for gated,” and “: target repeat” are fragments. All rows are marked conditional, which respects the dominance limit, while the current-process cell also calls high-value and unclear-defect dominated. That is a stronger claim than these artifacts can close.
