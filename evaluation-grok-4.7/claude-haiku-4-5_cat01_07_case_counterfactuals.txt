4.5

The required skeleton is present, but the counterfactual timings are not reliable enough to use. Magnitudes contradict the paths, and several paths contradict the queues and rules they are supposed to respect.

The partial order is not a usable precedence model. Identity Check is serialized before Document Check even when originals are not required, Assessment is placed directly after Triage, and Request More Info is placed after Compliance Review. K-219 is marked “timing only” even though the intervention is a resource reassignment. K-104 is also marked timing-only while it changes escalation routing and reserves C1.

K-104’s arithmetic does not match its own path. Factual cycle time is about 58.5 hours (Mon 08:10 to Wed 18:40), not 4 or 4.5 days. The table’s “2.5–5 h faster (Tue 22:30 vs Wed 18:30)” equates a 20-hour gap with a 2.5–5 hour gain, and the later “1.25 day” best case uses the wrong baseline. A final C1 review on Tue evening also jumps the Tue 08:00 16-hour compliance queue. Held fixed, that backlog is why factual compliance starts Wed 13:10. Preliminary reading can shorten service by roughly 1–3 hours; it does not by itself move the decision to Tuesday night. Wed 08:30 for M1 also collides with K-219’s factual decision.

K-219 is internally inconsistent. Section 2 ends the best case Tue 19:00 and calls that a 2.5-day improvement (actual gap versus Wed 09:00 is about 14 hours). Section 3 ends the same best case Wed 08:50, essentially the factual finish, and still calls it 1.5–2 days earlier. “D2 starts ~13.5 h earlier” does not follow from queues of 11 h versus 5 h or from the stated starts of Tue 08:00 versus Tue 09:30. Documents in the factual case were ready Tue 13:30, yet assessment waited until Tue 20:00. Moving document completion to ~13:00 does not justify an assessment start at 14:00–15:00. Overnight D1 work in K-104 is also ignored when D2 is held to a Tue 08:00 start. Rework as a downside is recognized; the no-rework gain is not.

K-337 correctly treats the intervention as a decision-policy change and keeps four-eyes between A1 and C1. The clock math still drifts across 3, 3.5, 5–6, and 5.5 days. Fri 14:40 is about 4.2 days from Mon 11:00, and about 4.8 days earlier than Wed 09:10, not those figures. Compliance is run in parallel with M1, and the text says M1’s decision does not depend on C1’s sign-off. That breaks “compliance after the latest assessment and before decision.” This is C1’s first review, on an unresolved high-risk gap, so compressing it because “the summary is enough” is the weak justification. The closing summary then assigns K-104’s “+1.25 day” figure to K-337.

K-411 states the right distinction and then drops it. The case table saves 1.5–2.5 hours and finishes documents Tue 16:00 while leaving the Wed 14:20 decision unchanged. The derivation finishes documents around Tue 20:00 and calls the base case neutral. Earlier queue entry is not the same as both resources starting at 11:30, and a Tue 21:00 A2 start is incompatible with K-219 occupying A2 from Tue 20:00–22:30.

The 100-case discussion names the right mechanisms: C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment congestion, weekends, and false timestamps. The accounting is still off. A 20-hour queue snapshot is subtracted from weekly capacity as if it were another week of flow; shifted assessment arrivals are counted as 240 new case-hours of demand; D2’s “20 h backlog” does not follow from 60 h inflow against 40 h capacity. The rollout is also far past the 250–400 word limit, and the synthesis is far past 220–320 words.

Robustness ranks the policy change first because it deletes waiting, while the danger that the problem highlights is exactly that this is not a pure time change. K-411’s audit-trail risk is real, but it is a poor “most dangerous despite speed” pick when its own analysis says speed is about zero. Pilots are required for the two highest-ranked interventions, in one two-column table. The answer instead pilots rank 1 and rank 3 in two separate tables. The K-219 pilot excludes originals, which is the constraint that defines that intervention. The power claims are wrong: 40 cases per arm cannot detect a 2% versus 7% appeal difference at 80% power, and n=80 does not give a ±5% interval around a 25% rework rate.

The assumption list is complete and mostly labeled, but it assumes C1 can overlap service intervals, which only S1 is allowed to do, and it states external weekend turnaround as 48+ hours when the observed loop is about 70 hours.
