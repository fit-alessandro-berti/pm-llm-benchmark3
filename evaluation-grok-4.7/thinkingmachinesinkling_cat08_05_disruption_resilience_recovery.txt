7.4

The final answer meets the required seven-part structure and is mostly faithful to the metrics, fitness figures, and the core distinction between service protection and lost controls. Timeline numbers, conformance scores, and the audit-flow counts (2,300 in, 2,100 out) match the source. It correctly refuses to treat throughput, deferral, or later audit closure as proof of resilience, and the recovery design covers triggers, triage, product-level minimum controls, parallel evidence, backlog priority, and a separated reconciliation path.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 3 mislabels skipped authorization as a move-on-model deviation after Section 1 correctly places it on the log. The hospital fast lane is treated as something that can be “paired” with manual authorization, while the manual DFG makes those mutually exclusive branches, and the 91% hospital fill rate is then credited to controls the fast lane does not pass through. Temporary release is classed as clear resilience even though dual-control misses rose from 0.2% to 2.9% and almost all released orders still flow to dispatch without scan (1,450 of 1,650). Pharmacy and wholesale are attributed to the skip-authorization and no-scan paths without segment evidence. Spreadsheet invoice hold is only listed as a deviation, so a potentially protective control is never interpreted. The backlog-duration breach of baseline p95 is essentially unused, T3’s hybrid state is thinly specified, the audit tail is overstated as still “unresolved,” and controlled-substance proof-of-delivery is set at 24 hours in the table and 4 hours in the process model.
