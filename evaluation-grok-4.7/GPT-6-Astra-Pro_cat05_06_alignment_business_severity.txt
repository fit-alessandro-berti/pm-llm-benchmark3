8.4

The analysis is strong, case-complete, and unusually careful about the difference between edit distance and control failure. It still has enough local overclaims, scale inconsistencies, and unverified implementation details that it is not nearly flawless.

The materiality framework uses the right factors, keeps confidence separate from severity, and correctly refuses to treat a missing model move as proof that work never happened. The euro total 480,000 + 90,000 + 18,000 + 4,500 = 592,500 is arithmetically right and is properly caveated as nominal claim value. Per-case readings of the alignments are accurate, including tau as a fitting zero-cost route, CL-604’s log/model payment pair as one displaced payment, CL-609/CL-610 as synchronous policy failures, and CL-603/CL-601 as technical noise. Patterns, competing hypotheses, and the remediation split across investigation, release control, data-aware rules, correlation, model scope, and label/retry cleanup all match the requested categories. Move totals are right: 65 synchronous, 8 log-only, 12 visible model-only, 1 silent, technical cost 20.

The main analytical softness is CL-609. P1 does forbid Auto Approve, and P2 is correctly not triggered. Calling Fraud Screen plus Manual Approve the policy-compatible route goes beyond the overlay. P2 reserves that pair for amount, HIGH risk, or a recent bank change; the process tree’s other branch is a model constraint, not an explicit rule for the EUR 18,000 MEDIUM band. The severity scale then compresses unlike problems: CL-602’s unsupported fraud evidence and CL-604’s timestamped payment-before-approval are both 5, while CL-611 is 2 even though label equivalence is explicitly unproven on a EUR 62,000 HIGH claim. The severity-1 definition is also muddy, and synchronous notification is scored as no material deviation while the 24-hour clock remains unverified. “Observation boundary” is the wrong term for a normative policy layer. Section 5 can be read as adding CL-609 and CL-610 on top of the four monthly auto-approval failures.

The code’s supplied-move accounting, nullable P1/P2 logic, CL-610 special case, and refusal to overwrite the authoritative alignment are careful. It was not execution-tested. Passing a positional trace-cost list and `SHOW_PROGRESS_BAR` through `conformance_diagnostics_alignments(**parameters)` is version-fragile, the `api/api` citations look malformed, and `affected_case_raw_cost` repeats full-case cost across overlapping move groups.
