4.5

The final design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, or open cases as non-default, and its conditional estimand, \(P(\text{default within 90 days}\mid\text{activated})\), is a legitimate primary target. The cohort arithmetic in the flow table adds to 48,000, the leakage inventory covers the required fields, and a random split is rightly rejected because of the July 2025 policy change and the October 2025 migration. Those choices are not enough to carry the study. Several required pieces contradict each other or cannot be executed on the stated calendar.

The prediction point is not a single estimand. Section 1 places it after the final credit decision and before contract signature. Section 4 places it at the last Credit Review while `decision_reason` is still missing. Those are different information sets: the first may legitimately know the decision and `final_limit`; the second must not. The implemented rule is worse than either description. Taking the last Credit Review in the completed case uses the later path, including whether another document loop occurred, so the prefix is defined with future knowledge. Fixed event-count prefixes and elapsed-time prefixes are waived rather than specified, and cases that never reach Credit Review are left unresolved. Activity renaming is mentioned; migration duplicates are not.

Label timing breaks the validation plan. The maturity rule requires activation at least 125 days before 15 February 2026, so activation on or before about 13 October 2025. The test fold is cases that start on or after 15 October 2025. Activation cannot precede start, so that fold is empty under the study’s own rule. Much of November–December is unlabeled even with a 7-day feed. The surviving late cases are fast activations, and that selection is not identified. Training only before the policy change and testing after it confuses a shift stress test with prospective performance under the current policy. Subgroup performance is essentially absent. Classification metrics ignore the censoring mechanism that created the estimation sample.

The censoring section names the right mechanisms, then miscounts them: rejected, withdrawn, and still-open are not 26,800 cases (they are 19,900; all non-activated outcomes are 22,800). Still-open cases are treated as undefined exclusions rather than administratively incomplete trajectories. The competing-outcome alternative lists censoring as an absorbing state beside default. IPCW is only a sketch, and the 60% transfer linkage and permanently missing partner feeds get no sensitivity design. A 30-day resource default rate is not knowable at prediction time, because the outcome itself lags by roughly 97–125 days. Section 5 also wants only the initial `risk_score`, while Section 6 wants the latest score at or before the cutoff.

The skeleton is below the requested substance. It never calls `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`, never builds case-level start, terminal, censoring, and label-maturity fields for the full 48,000, and does not use `label_available_at` after parsing it. `groupby.apply` returns one timestamp per case, and assigning that series back to the event table misaligns it. `get_variants` is computed and discarded. The leakage assert checks three column names after those columns were simply never aggregated. The closing survival remark has nothing to attach to, because immature activated cases have already been dropped.
