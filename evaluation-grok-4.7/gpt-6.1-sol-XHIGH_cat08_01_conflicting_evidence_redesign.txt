The diagnosis is unusually careful and almost entirely faithful to the given artifacts. Edge counts, performance statistics, the 14.1% versus 3.3% complaint split, the 31% to 9% cost comparison, segment medians, token-replay fitness versus 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, the EUR 95 versus EUR 410 replacement-error costs, and the 3,625 versus 5,000 end-count gap are used correctly. The answer also keeps elapsed directly-follows time distinct from labor time, separates claim value from the high-value product label, and refuses to treat the repair cohort’s lower complaint rate as causal proof.

The three redesigns are genuinely different: guarded replacement, repair-led service cells, and assisted evidence collection. Each names a target cohort, a mechanism tied to a measured failure, and a real sacrifice. The experiment order is coherent, compliance is treated as a constraint, and downstream shifting, censoring, and selection effects are explicitly monitored.

The score is held down by a few precise defects. In the Pareto section, the column asks whether a design is dominated, while the footnote defines “No” as “no demonstrated dominance.” Those are opposite claims, and the current-process cell uses the column in the passive sense. The 61–71% utilization statement compresses two alternative ceilings into one required band: 71% absorbs only a 15% inflow increase, whereas a 35% increase requires about 61% or less. The 26% repeat-request incidence is multiplied by 30–45% to produce 14–18%, although a reduction in repeat requests does not necessarily reduce the share of cases with two or more requests by the same proportion. Low model precision is also blended with the separate problem of non-compliant observed behavior. Several other numeric stop/go targets are disclosed as choices, but they still add false precision.
