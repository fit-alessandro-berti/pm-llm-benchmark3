7.5

The normative analysis is strong and largely well calibrated. Forbidden, mandatory, typical, and unspecified relations are separated correctly; open-world treatment is applied to unmodeled edges; N1’s “eventually validate” rule is not collapsed into a directly-follows violation; E10 is kept as a suspected join error; E8 is held against the missing unapplied-cash label; and the protected benign relations (invoice-after-validate, review-to-customer, package–delivery codeath, reopen-to-close) are not treated as violations. The severity order, containment focus on N3 and T6, and the uncertainty close are defensible and tied to norm IDs and counts.

Deductions are for concrete defects, not tone. The semantic layer does not classify: `_edge_to_norm_id` always returns `None`, so every edge becomes unspecified, `norm_ocdfg` / `norm_otg` / `norm_etot` are never used for interpretation, and absent mandatory edges are never derived. Present mandatory edges are also annotated as if they were missing links. That misses the required classification layer even as a sketch. Section 7 reopens N4 scope (“whether the package was not externally shipped”) after the summary already limits the 198/200 count to externally shipped packages. Row 16 attaches T4/T5 (Order–Customer and Order–RiskCase) to Manual Compliance Review–Customer, which those norms do not govern. Section 8 calls the two N3 cases “confirmed” while the table still requires object traces, linkage, and timestamps. N4 is given an eventuality hedge that the norm states only for N1. The write-up also restates the same findings across the table and sections 3, 4, 7, and 8, well past the 1,700–2,600-word aim.
