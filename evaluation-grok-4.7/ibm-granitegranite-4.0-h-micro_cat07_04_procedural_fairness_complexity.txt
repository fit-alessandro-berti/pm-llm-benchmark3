The write-up keeps the required eight headings and does not treat raw approval gaps as discrimination or recommend dropping support steps. That is where its reliability ends. It restates the rubric more than it uses the evidence, and several central claims are factually wrong.

Section 1 collapses outcome parity into adjusted disparity and never applies either idea to the tables. Section 2 names matching, variants, resources, waits, sensitivity, and subgroups, but it never defines an estimand or a way to separate legitimate routing, residual disparity, discrimination, and uncertainty. Stratifying on `applicant_group` treats the exposure as a control. No interval, standard error, or identification assumption is specified, even though the adjusted figures are described as preliminary.

Section 3 misreads the data. Group C, not Group B, has the worst timeliness: raw median 15.9 days and an adjusted +4.1 days, versus Group B’s +2.6. Accommodation Review is Group C’s path (V5, 31%, 4.7-day wait). Group B’s accommodation rate is 6%, and that wait is reported as n/a. Group B’s distinctive burden is V4, the 3.8-day language-support queue, the adjusted +9.5 pp missing-document rate, and +8.0 pp supervisor review. Group D’s fraud-review wait is only 2.8 versus 2.2 days and its adjusted cycle-time gap is +0.9 days; the residual that matters is appeals, +5.7 pp, and the V3/V6 mix. Group A is the low-appeal reference (6%, V6 at 4%), so an “Appeal Review disparity” for Group A is inverted. The seven bilingual workers, three overloaded regional officers, five specialists of whom two also supervise, and the two-region handover chains are barely used. Calling language-support delay “justified” because the step is authorized conflates a lawful activity with an untargeted queue.

Section 4 offers four generic mechanisms and calls concentrated specialist capacity “perceived” unfairness. It misses queueing with no support SLA, ping-pong handovers, documentation completeness as a language-mediated variable, supervisor backlog, and appeals as a response to residual burden. Section 5 lists the requested limitation topics, but “emotional support” is not the missing-need problem in this log, and it never explains overcontrol, post-treatment bias, or why conditioning on `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` can hide the disparity under study.

Section 6 includes every requested mitigation, yet several “side effects” are merely costs, and the audit item’s stated consequence is a benefit. It never trades capacity against fraud leakage, SLA pressure against rushed support, fewer handovers against loss of specialist accountability, or adjusted monitoring against false reassurance from biased covariates.

Section 7 would not run and would not estimate fairness. `filter_case_performance` filters duration, not group or risk. Filtering Group A to high risk and Group B to incomplete documentation is not adjustment. `discover_dfg` does not produce input for `get_variants`. `get_service_time` is called with an invented signature. `sensitivity_analysis` is not a PM4Py function. `load_xml` ignores the stated tabular log and `format_dataframe`. Performance DFGs, handover and working-together networks, organizational roles, `filter_variants`, path-performance filters, case durations, and log-skeleton or DECLARE checks are absent.

Section 8’s “partially fair” is softer than the residual evidence. After the stated controls, approval, cycle time, missing documents, supervisor review, and appeals still differ, and the longest waits sit on rights-preserving steps that have no timeliness standard. Discrimination remains unproven; procedural unfairness on those paths is already supported.
