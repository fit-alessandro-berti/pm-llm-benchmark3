7.3

The policy split, the strict-R5 accepting net, the DECLARE encoding, the temporal arithmetic, and the layered reading of T1–T5, T7, and T8 are largely coherent. The answer still has one executable-semantics error that breaks the log-skeleton layer, plus several smaller modeling and checker defects.

The log skeleton’s `directly_follows` entry is wrong for `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`. In that checker the set is a whitelist of permitted immediate successions: every consecutive pair in the trace must belong to it. It is not a mandatory chain. A model whose only pair is `("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding")` therefore rejects every realistic onboarding trace, including T1–T4, T6, and T7. Those matrix cells are marked `pass`. The useful observation that the skeleton does not encode strict R5, and so would accept T6, is wiped out once this key is actually checked. Leaving the set empty does not fix it; an empty whitelist is equally strict. The other skeleton relations are in the right orientation: `(A, B)` in `always_before` means B occurs before A, and the same shape in `always_after` means B occurs after A.

That skeleton is also internally uneven. Manager approval, enhanced due diligence, and activation are tied back to classification, but `("Reject Vendor", "Classify Risk")` is missing, so a rejection with no classification can still satisfy the hand-built skeleton.

The implemented net itself is the strongest artifact. Receipt is an AND-split, classification is the join, the classified token makes rejection, manager approval, and enhanced due diligence exclusive, clarification is an idle/pending side token, and escalation is enabled only from the post-screening sanctions token while `p_case_open` remains. Under strict R5, T6 is disabled at `Activate Vendor@13` and T7 is enabled because the opinion restores `p_sanctions_clear` first. Final markings on the strict accept and reject paths are clean. The comment that the `p_case_open` self-loop is what prevents post-outcome escalation is inaccurate: the self-loop preserves the token across an escalation; activation and rejection prevent a later escalation by consuming it and not restoring it.

The R6 row overclaims precision it does not demonstrate. “Allow activation while an opinion is pending” is not enough for an accepting net. In the strict net, activation is the consumer of `p_sanctions_clear`. If that input is simply removed, `Obtain Legal Opinion` after activation produces a residual token and the marking is not `{p_end: 1}`. The variant can be made exact only if some later transition consumes that token. The matrix still says the variant is a procedural `pass`.

Smaller defects: the segregation function returns `not evaluated` as soon as any relevant resource is missing, so a real four-eyes clash on the remaining events is discarded. `bool(activation["case:emergency_authorized"])` treats the string `"false"` as true. The escalation scanner does not consume a matched opinion, so one later opinion can cover several escalations. Pre-activation `Change Bank Details` is impossible in the net and is not listed among the deliberate exclusions. DECLARE response plus nonsuccession still allows an unsolicited opinion before activation, while the net does not; section 9 only discusses the post-activation case. T5’s temporal cell is `pass` even though the clarification pair never occurs, which sits awkwardly next to T3’s `not evaluated`, though the payment pair alone is inside the band.

The parts that hold up are the ones this task most cares about. R5 and R6 are not merged. Strict legal precedence is the implemented policy, the emergency exception is fail-closed on missing authorization, and T6 is reported both ways: hard fail under strict R5, and `policy-dependent` under R6 because the opinion is 10 hours after activation but joint authorization is not in the log. T7’s opinion delay is \((34-12)/6 \approx 3.67\) and the payment gap is \(|2-72|/36 \approx 1.94\), so `warning` rather than `fail` matches R12. Seconds, `zeta = 2.0`, and the 0–96 / 0–24 / 0–144 hour windows are right. T4 fails only on Cora verifying and approving. T8’s first impossible event is `Reject Vendor@10`, with the missing payment as a later failure. Resource absence is indeterminate, not a silent pass, and support/confidence are correctly treated as metadata rather than evidence.
