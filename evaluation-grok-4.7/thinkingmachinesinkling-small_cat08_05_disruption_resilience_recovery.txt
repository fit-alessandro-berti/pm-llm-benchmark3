The memo hits the required seven-part structure and gets the core distinction right: manual authorization, explicit deferral, and reconciliation can be resilience, while skipped authorization, unscanned dispatch, and post-close audit are not real-time compliance. Most baseline, workaround, and stabilization rates, multipliers, conformance figures, and move-on-log/model deviations are used correctly, and the recovery design covers triggers, evidence packets, and a separate audit track.

It is not nearly flawless. The stabilization directly-follows graph is assigned to partial recovery (compliance hold 1,300, reconcile 1,400, repack 900) and then repeated for stabilization, which breaks the timeline. “2,100 cases remain” misreads completed Audit remediation → END transitions. Defer noncritical (500) cannot feed a backlog of 960; snapshot and pick volumes of 3,920 are edge counts, not activity totals. The fast lane is a parallel branch, not a merge into skip-authorization. Section 2 says temporary release reduced controlled-substance dual-control failures, contradicting both Section 1 and the rise from 0.2% to 2.9%. Bulk median 58 h versus 36 h is not “near baseline.”

The redesign drops hospital-critical ambient and non-hospital controlled or cold-chain orders out of the triage levels. Audit-queue limits conflict across sections (0.5%, 5%, and 2%), and the exit bar of 85% fitting traces is looser than the 91% baseline. Phone proof of delivery is both a moderate control and “corner-cutting.” Throughput and p90 cycle time are weak as indicators despite being how apparent recovery masked the tail.
