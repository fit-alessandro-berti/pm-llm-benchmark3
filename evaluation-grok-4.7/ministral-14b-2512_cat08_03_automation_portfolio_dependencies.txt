4.6

The memo has the required seven sections, rates all twelve candidates, and correctly treats frequency as a weak guide to first-year value. That structure does not hold up under the process evidence. Several load-bearing claims are wrong, the portfolios break stated constraints, and the rollout does not implement the portfolio it recommends.

The candidate matrix is an ID-ordered catalog, not a ranking. A1 is given downstream-bottleneck risk of “None,” while the candidate note and the later recommendation both say faster registration exposes classification, KYC, and document review. A1’s labor case is also overstated: 12,000 cases at 3 minutes is a small hour saving, and back office already drops below 65% utilization after 15:00. A4 is described as critical for 82% of cases. The evidence says that, when entity mismatch is present (1,750 cases), it lies on the critical path in 82% of those cases — roughly one case in eight, not four in five. Waiting times are repeatedly treated as if automating the activity removes the queue before it. KYC’s own median wait is 4 hours; the long KYC-path delay is the 31-hour specialist queue. False negatives on A3 are described as blocking signing. A false negative clears a true hit and is a control failure; false positives are what consume the specialist pool. Suitability labels conflict with the same row’s risk labels, especially A10 (“High / low risk” beside a medium control risk and a 6.5% provisional-signing finding). Stripped symbols (“31h ?”, “8.5% ?”) leave several cells unreadable.

The dependency section names the required topics and then mis-wires them. A4 and A6 are declared the two deep core-banking integrations. Registration, contract generation, e-sign, and activation are the modules that actually write into core banking; statement normalization and entity resolution sit in document and KYC systems. Four-eyes is applied to contract generation. The finding is narrower: in high-exposure cases, credit analysis and committee packaging must not be the same person, with a 5.8% breach rate in the month-end rush. A5 is treated as a general data-quality prerequisite. It improves document completeness, not classification fields or identity data. Concurrency is missed: KYC and financial-document review often run together, so their waits do not add. The DFG sequence Credit analysis → Collateral valuation request (2,400) is the reason the 58-hour external wait stacks on the credit queue; triage does not by itself start valuation earlier. The edge Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation (380) is a control bypass and is never discussed. Specialist risk is blurred across compliance ops and the 3 FTE specialist pool, including the two seniors who already concentrate handovers.

All three portfolios are incomplete against the constraints. The quick-win set fails the rule that one first-wave module must strengthen controls, and it adds 12k + 3.3k + 7.3k cases as if those were distinct labor. The bottleneck set (A4, A6, A7, A5) is the one later recommended, yet none of those modules is a control module; A4 is an assistant with limited coverage. The compliance set leads with a probabilistic KYC pre-classifier, the candidate with the most severe false-negative exposure, and does not design the four-eyes separation or a hard stop on provisional signing. No portfolio is segmented for equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases, versus working capital, where it is required in 18%.

The effects tables invent a universal ~100-hour cycle time and then precise reductions. Cutting a 31-hour wait on about 12% of cases, or a 58-hour wait on about 2,350 cases, cannot produce a 30–40% average cycle-time cut, and neither change removes the weekly high-exposure committee or the 44-hour signing wait on 7,300 cases. Aggregate delay is larger in signing and in the credit-analysis queue than in entity mismatch. Claimed side effects are unanchored: entity automation of “60%,” KYC rework falling because of document tools, provisional signing falling below 3% without a signing gate, and four-eyes violations falling to 0% under modules that do not separate those two roles. The compliance-portfolio table credits A10 for a signing-wait cut even though A10 is not in that portfolio. Customer-response delay, which the activity note says dominates missing-document loops, is ignored when A5 is credited with large rework reduction.

Compensating controls do not work as written. Escalating “100% of false negatives” is impossible, because those cases were not flagged; auto-clears need sampling. The audit trail of suggestion, override, and final decision is not imposed on every module. A9’s outdated rule library is absent from the risk table. Four-eyes is renamed into a classification review and never turned into a month-end staffing or workflow control. RM behavior, the stated source of incomplete document instructions, is not a residual risk even though A5 is recommended and relationship managers will resist extra customer friction. Analyst overload from cleaner inflow is noted earlier and then dropped from the effects and the metrics.

The rollout contradicts the recommendation. The recommended four are A4, A6, A7, and A5. The plan builds A4, A6, and A5, then A3 and A9, and leaves A7 optional. That is five modules against a cap of four. The closing “next steps” add A5 again and then A3/A9. A6’s gate, manual override under 5%, can be passed by rubber-stamping. A3’s success line, “KYC check time <10h,” is weaker than the current 4-hour median wait. Metrics stay local to each bot: no end-to-end cycle time, no provisional-signing rate, no four-eyes rate, no specialist or analyst utilization, no product split.

The final argument is right that registration should not be first, and right that A11 is unsafe while upstream controls are open. It then rests that judgment on the 82% misread, a 30–40% cycle-time claim, and “indirect” control benefit. Indirect control does not meet a requirement that one first-wave module strengthen controls. Speeding mismatch handling, normalization, and valuer routing still lands on weekly committee and customer signing, so the same bottleneck-shift warning used against A1 applies to this portfolio and is not answered.
