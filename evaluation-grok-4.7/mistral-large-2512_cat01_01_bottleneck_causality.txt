The write-up sees the crude-versus-stratified reversal and rightly refuses a blind 50% reassignment, but the causal story built on that reversal is inconsistent, several required comparisons are numerically wrong, and the diagnostics code would not run.

The verdict “partly causal through mechanisms” is not supported by the argument that follows. Within every complexity stratum Orion’s mean cycle time is shorter, yet the text still treats Orion’s crude delay as mediated by night work, compliance, and rework. That is confounding and selection into case mix, not mediation. Compliance cannot be a group mechanism here: within Low, Medium, and High, compliance rates match across groups (0%, 50%, 100%). Rework likewise matches or favors Orion inside strata. Calling those mediators of a harmful Orion effect reverses the actual pattern.

Section 1 cites high-complexity waits of 19.47h versus 22.75h as “longer queue waits.” The cited contrast is shorter. The same section uses the within-stratum advantage as “evidence for causality” of a verdict about Orion-driven delay.

The graph encodes the same mistakes. `resource_group → compliance_required` is contradicted by the policy fact and by the within-stratum rates, and it is never labeled as an assumption. `shift → arrival_load` is the wrong direction for a load measured at arrival. `compliance_performed → review_service` conflicts with the definition that compliance waiting sits inside `wait_before_review_h`. `complexity` is pre-routing, so it is not a mediator of group. The table then calls `shift` a collider and `compliance_required` a collider for reasons that describe mediators, and those labels disagree with the graph.

Section 4 is not auditable. Night is 17 cases, not 12, and day is 13, not 18; means are about 21.7h versus 14.0h, not 19.5h versus 14.5h. “92.9% Orion” is Orion’s night share, not the share of night cases that are Orion (13/17). Compliance is 13 cases at about 26.8h versus 17 at about 12.0h, not 12 at 23.5h versus 18 at 10.0h. Orion’s mean wait is about 12.8h, not 11.5h. Day-versus-night and compliance-versus-not are also complexity contrasts; the allowed interpretations treat them as effects. Crude higher wait and service are composition effects: inside strata both are lower for Orion. Medium and High Orion cases are exclusively night, so those strata do not separate group from shift. That positivity failure is the main identification point and is never stated.

The hypothesis table covers the required types only loosely. Rank 1 is already contradicted by equal within-stratum compliance and rework. The “within-reviewer” test compares different groups’ reviewers. The night mean used as evidence is the wrong one. The front-door label is ordinary mediation language. Selection is the right concern, but the predicted check does not say what would remove Orion’s within-stratum advantage.

The pilot plan has the required phases and a cautious recommendation, yet Phase 1 claims a direct effect from randomizing the group label. Randomization would target a total effect unless shift and other post-assignment mechanisms are held fixed. Phase 3 relaxes a policy-required control rather than adding compliance capacity.

The Python does not compute an Orion-minus-Nova standardized contrast, only a reweighted Orion mean. `value_counts` on lists of activities will throw, and `convert_to_event_log` is not fed a PM4Py event log. The required `parse_event_log_string` plus `discover_dfg` path is not used, so the variant and directly-follows comparison is not reproducible.

Section 8 is the cleanest part: five real gaps, each tied to one data field or design change. That, plus noticing the Simpson reversal and advising a small routing pilot instead of a 50% move, is what keeps the score from falling further.
