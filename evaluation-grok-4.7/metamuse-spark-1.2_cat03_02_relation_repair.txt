5.5

The answer covers every required section and is strongest on ev08 (PK8-2 / PK8-1 precedent), on refusing a unique ev11 assignment in Bundle A, and on naming circular E2O/O2O reinforcement. Those merits do not offset material errors in the process-mining consequences, an internally inconsistent “conservative” bundle, and several misstated evidence claims.

The evidence model overstates its rules. Full lifecycle order is labeled a hard invariant while the same paragraph allows overlaps; completeness is labeled a strong default and then described as something that must not drive repairs. N3 is not a coherent negative: linking ev10 to DEL-Y assigns PK-8, so the stated “PK-8 unassigned” consequence does not follow. Customer=C17 and route=NORTH are treated as corroboration for specific links even though every relevant order, package, delivery, invoice, and portal row shares those values, so they do not discriminate. The 10:00 timestamp is cited in favor of DEL-Y specifically, although it supports simultaneity equally for a second delivery and for a shared delivery. Rule 4 is misapplied in Bundle B: a bulk dispatch is one event covering several packages, whereas the bundle keeps two dispatch events and hangs both packages on DEL-X.

The accounting story is blurred. W3 says PAY-1 matches the “INV-44 remainder.” After the observed payment of 100, the unpaid balance of 230 is 130, which matches PAY-2 and ORD-A’s declared value. The two identities 130+100=230 (order values) and 100+130=230 (payment amounts) are compatible with an invoice that bills only ORD-A plus fees and is settled by two payments. Adding both ORD-B and PAY-2 explains the same gap twice. That alternative is not separated cleanly from consolidation.

Bundle A is not a minimum-change repair. It still adds the invoice–ORD-B and PAY-2–INV-44 edges that section 8 later describes as over-repair that should stay unresolved. The proposal table marks the bulk edges reject (“do not add”), then Bundle B adopts them, so status is bundle-relative without being defined that way. Derived O2O scores are not discounted for circularity: PK-8 packs IT-B1 is 0.82 against an E2O of 0.85, and DEL-Y carries PK-8 is 0.78 against an E2O of 0.75. Bundle B promotes a 0.35 edge to acceptance without a new confidence.

Qualifiers are not stable. The table uses relates for ev12–ORD-B and ev14–INV-44; the JSON uses bills and settles; ev10’s JSON qualifier is the corrupted token dispdispatches; the code uses dispatches. E2O and O2O also share verb labels (packs, bills, settles) without a stated role convention.

Section 5’s gross_weight remark assumes an unobserved weight increase. The table gives one snapshot per package. Order lifecycles are written as if dispatch and payment events belong to ORD-A and ORD-B directly. They do not.

Section 6 is the largest failure. Standard flattening keeps events that cite the chosen object. Unrepaired ORD-A is ev01, ev03, ev04, ev11, ev12. It does not contain Pack, Dispatch, or Record Payment. Unrepaired PK-8 already has ev07 and ev10, so the repair adds a second Pack on PK-8; it does not newly add dispatch. The new package directly-follows fragment is Pack Item → Pack Item. IT-B1’s own sequence stays Add Item → Pack Item, because IT-B1 never gains a dispatch event. “Unique items at Pack Item go from 2 to 3” is false: IT-A1, IT-A2, and IT-B1 are already attached. “Unique invoices at Record Payment go from 1 to 2” is false: both payment links are still INV-44. The claimed ORD-B trace Create→Add→Pack→Dispatch→Issue→Record, and the “three extra events” variant effect, describe a graph that `ocel_flattening` will not produce. OC-DFG, O2O, and ET–OT edges are also merged: DEL-Y carries PK-8 is not an OC-DFG arc, and Pack Item already co-occurs with Item and Package through ev06 and ev07, so ev08 does not create that type-level pair.

The code copies the frames, appends the selected rows, checks referential integrity and duplicates, and calls the requested discovery and flattening functions. The fallback replaces the OCEL object with a dict and still passes that dict to discovery. The lifecycle check is a `pass`. Qualifier and type constraints from H1 are never checked. Original E2O rows in this extract have no qualifier column, which the duplicate key does not address.

Constraint compliance is partial: no new events or objects, arithmetic is verbally denied the status of proof, and Bundle A leaves ev11 unresolved with equal alternatives. The selected JSON nevertheless commits the financial closures, and Bundle B assigns ev11 to DEL-X only by assuming the bulk hypothesis it is then used to illustrate. The write-up also runs well past the 1,700–2,700 word aim.
