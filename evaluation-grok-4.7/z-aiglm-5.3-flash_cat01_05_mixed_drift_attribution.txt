7.5

The report meets every required section, column set, word-count band, and decision-rule count, and its main attribution is sound: March is logging/semantic drift, later rework and cycle-time movement are real, supplier and staffing are separated from seasonality, and the priority effect is correctly left unverified. Quantitative work on baselines, L1 distances, fitness recovery, and the supplier–rework correlation is largely accurate, and the monitoring design is specific rather than generic.

Deductions are for concrete errors, not tone. The timeline says Assess→Request Info moves 12→27 per 100 cases; 27 is June’s rework-variant percent, while the DFG rate is 24 in May and 29 in July, and no June DFG is supplied. It also says the fast-variant share “keeps sliding” after 1 July; it rises from 39 in July to 50 in September before the seasonal drop. June 15 is described as a steepening or acceleration, but median and p90 month-to-month increments are already largest in May and then shrink, so staffing better explains persistence than a new steeper slope. The 91→89 Collect Documents→Assess move is too small to carry that claim, and the November DFG rate is an interpolated range the aggregates do not contain. In the code, Open Case cleanup only drops identical timestamps, so it would miss the extra Open Case→Register events actually described, and `retain=True` is not part of the current PM4Py `filter_time_range` signature.
