The case verdicts are correct on all eleven traces, and the temporal model is sound. The score is held down by an unsound formalization, a few code defects, and one overstated alternative reading.

The reconstructions at release match the event-time rules. Blank cells are carried forward, not nulled. Final settlement and late exemption inserts are not copied backward. Same-timestamp order uses `event_index`. Amount increases above 20 percent invalidate the prior review, decreases and the TX-306 increase of about 4.55 percent do not, and an unknown amount stays unknown. Classifications follow from that state: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, TX-309, and TX-310 are noncompliant; TX-302, TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are compliant; TX-305 is indeterminate. TX-302 is rightly compliant because the enhanced review at 09:20 already meets R1, with EX-77 only a second, valid path. TX-308 correctly keeps R1 active at exactly EUR 100,000 and still treats the earlier review as valid. TX-310 correctly refuses EX-88: a certificate created at 10:30 cannot prove approval at a 10:00 release, and the requester’s oral claim does not repair that. TX-311 correctly releases as DE, so R1 is off, because the jurisdiction update is event_index 4 after the review at event_index 3.

Section 1 draws the right distinction among event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and logging fixes, with the right cases attached. The rule table is usable and the ambiguity discussion hits TX-302, TX-305, TX-310, and TX-311.

The pseudo-logic does not implement the three-valued logic it claims. `Trig_R1` is classical, so `UNK >= 100000` is undefined, and `Sat_R1 := not Trig_R1 or ...` does not yield indeterminate for TX-305. `AmountInvalidated` mixes `exists` with conditional arrows, so several updates do not aggregate to true, false, or unknown. `Sat_R2` omits the table’s own constraint that the enhanced review must follow the high-risk or PEP assignment and stay uninvalidated. R6 is only a comment.

The code would likely label these eleven cases the same way, but it is not reliable as written. `last_er["state_amount"] is not np.nan` is an identity test and is true for both numbers and missing values, so that conjunct does not check anything. EX-77 is given a certificate-creation time of 2026-01-01, which the evidence does not state. EX-88 is given `hash_valid=False`, which the evidence also does not state. The conflicted-witness flag is never used. R4 is folded into a reason string rather than a coarse flag. The material-change timestamp treats any raw HIGH or PEP value as a material change and is not the predicate the rules use.

The TX-305 alternative is too strong. A later payload of EUR 150,000 still does not make the review safely classifiable: R4 says an increase from an unknown amount stays unclassified without more evidence. That path should remain indeterminate, not become noncompliant, unless the amount at review itself is shown.
