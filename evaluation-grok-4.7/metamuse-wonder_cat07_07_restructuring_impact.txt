The answer covers every required section and stays balanced: East is treated differently from North and South, fairness and learning are carried through, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The best insight is that management’s own handover table increases specialist-facing traffic (tech 36→41, legal/risk 9→12, approval 42→49) while only relocating it. Baselines are mostly quoted correctly.

The decision-relevant error is the claim that the “+15% specialist utilization” gain is arithmetically impossible because Legal and Risk already sit at 93% and 91%. That 15% is the stated benefit of pooling the embedded techs (about 68–82%). A volume-weighted tech load near the low 70s can rise by about 15% and still land near the mid-80s. The real capacity break is separate: legal/risk referrals rise by a third (9→12) with almost no slack, and the same hub is also asked to absorb difficult quality checks. The recommendation joins those facts incorrectly. “Specialist-facing handovers rise; only their location changes” is also internally inconsistent.

The optimistic scenario compares a new p90 wait with the old 3.2h median. Several point forecasts (East median 7.5d, shared-service waits of 6–10h, rework of 12–15%) are asserted without a derivation. “Remove one local supervisor” is stretched into local approval accountability disappearing. The pilot routes all East technical work into the treatment arm, so the team expected to differ has no concurrent control.

The PM4Py sketch calls the requested discovery, variant, rework, role, similarity, and service/waiting functions, but it does not filter a `customer_segment` cohort, does not compute SLA misses, treats a likely case-level wait column as if it were an activity wait, and would mis-count handover edges. The log has only one timestamp per event, so true resource service time and utilization are not identified, and the sketch does not say so.
