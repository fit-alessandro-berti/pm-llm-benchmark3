2.5

The write-up keeps the required nine-section skeleton and a few local facts (both models need at least one check; Model B has at most one; P1 and P3 are not raw-trace equivalent; footprint or EMD agreement would not prove language equality). Those points are outweighed by inverted semantics, invalid witnesses, non-executable code, and a simplification that drops the process it claims to preserve.

Equivalence notions are not usable. `tau` is treated as a time delay rather than a hidden transition. P1 both demands identical sequences and then dismisses `Approve Revision` as silent, contradicting the requirement that approval count and placement stay visible. P2 turns the check-boolean into an approval-boolean, drops the analyze count and rework flag, and then says both that order is irrelevant and that sequences must match. The duplicate-label note assigns occurrence position to P2 and P3 and then says only identity matters for P2.

The structural table misreads `+`. In A, `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` are concurrent inside each iteration, so the check is not strictly after analysis and revisions are not concurrent. In B, the single check is concurrent with the whole loop, not sequenced once before it. B still contains `X('Approve Revision', tau)` and an unbounded analysis loop. The join description does not match either tree.

All four traces fail as discriminators. Under the stated binary-loop rule, `Rework Case` must be followed by another loop body, so T1 is in neither language. T2 (`Analyze`, `Approve`, `Check`) is in B and not A, the opposite of the table. T3 has two analyses and no rework, so it is in neither. T4 is in both, so it shows no difference. No witness has two valid analysis attempts, and there is no inclusion proof. Optional approval is also described incorrectly: hiding `tau` still leaves `Approve Revision` visible, and A forbids approval before that iteration’s check.

P2 should be equivalent only under the dashboard abstraction: both always open first, close last, perform at least one analysis, perform at least one check, hide approval and `tau`, and have rework exactly when the analysis count is at least 2. The “not equivalent” verdict, and the claim that B lacks multiple revisions and approvals, are false. The P3 reason confuses the observation bound with a bound on Model B and misses that one in-bound counterexample already refutes unbounded trace equivalence.

The Python does not implement the required comparison. `convert_to_petri_net` is not unpacked into net and markings, soundness and enabled transitions are called with `None`, and trace generation is `pass`. It neither explores markings nor states a real exhaustive-versus-playout distinction, yet the prose claims that it derives and compares traces. Complexity code is likewise a stub, while the text falsely says exact values were derived. No node, depth, leaf, duplicate-label, or simplicity figure is reported. Footprints are mislabeled as stochastic; no footprint, stochastic-language, or EMD code is given, and no probabilities are declared.

The proposed “P1” tree deletes `Analyze Revision` and approval, puts `Close Case` in the loop’s redo child, and forces `Rework Case` inside the first child. Complete executions therefore need not end with close, and the listed regression traces are not complete traces of that tree or of A. The preservation table is blank or contradictory, and simplicity is asserted without a structural comparison. Recommending that this model replace B for P1 and P3 is not supported. The closing caution does not state the actual limit: equality inside the P3 bound would still not prove unbounded equivalence, whereas a valid in-bound counterexample does prove inequality.
