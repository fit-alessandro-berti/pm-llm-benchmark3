7.5

The case judgments are the strongest part of the answer, and they are not mechanical. Same-user cases are split across met, partially met, and failed, and the two formally conforming u12/u13 cases are correctly kept out of an automatic pass. AP-205 as loss prevented before completion, AP-202 and AP-208 as objective failures despite activity having occurred, and AP-209 as indeterminate are all well supported. The equivalence dimensions, the rejection of expected loss as the sole standard, the loss-horizon reading of pre-settlement ratification, and the alert-versus-proof monitoring rules are coherent and specific.

The score is held down by repeated overclaims and internal conflicts.

The answer’s own “fully equivalent” test requires every ED-1 condition to be verified, including an L3-or-above manager. It then calls AP-203 and AP-207 fully equivalent and says all seven conditions are evidenced, while the source facts never state anyone’s level. It flags the L3 gap for m15 and not for m02, even though both are described only as independent. For AP-208, “token presence” is treated as a satisfied condition, although the facts say only that a token was active, not that it was active before preparation.

Section 2 also sorts AP-204 and AP-209 into compensating-package tiers. Those cases are nominal four-eyes executions, not compensating packages. AP-204 is “merely mitigating” there and OBJECTIVE_FAILED in section 3; AP-209 is “merely mitigating” and INDETERMINATE. Those axes can differ, but the answer never reconciles them.

Recall timing is wrong in the case table. AP-201 and AP-207 are said to lose recall at settlement. The stated rule is a usual 24-hour bank recall, and section 4 itself treats a within-24-hour audit as still corrective. On the given times, AP-201’s audit is about 23 hours after the 10:00 settlement, so the claim that recall ended at settlement is the opposite of the answer’s own rule.

The code is labeled as evidence and handles several real PM4Py uncertainties, but it invents facts: a 5-day vendor age, a definite pre-prepare token for AP-208, and independence for m21. Shared service accounts are removed before the intersection test, so the same shared account on both steps would not be flagged as a violation. Violators are computed manually; the required filter is only probed for direction and is not used to produce the violation set.
