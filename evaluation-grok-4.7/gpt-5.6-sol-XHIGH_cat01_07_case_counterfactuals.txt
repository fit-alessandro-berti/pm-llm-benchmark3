The answer is strong on intervention typing, queue displacement, and decision-policy risk, but it is not nearly flawless. The formal feasibility model contradicts both the stated rules and the later case analysis, and one upside range is not supported by the queue evidence the answer itself relies on.

The partial order is the main failure. For cases that do not need originals, it asserts that Identity Check precedes Document Check. Rule 3 allows those activities to run concurrently, and K-411’s own feasibility check later says they may overlap. The join before Assessment is therefore inconsistent with the serial edge above it. For originals, Identity Check is also placed before Originals Receipt and Document Check. Rule 4 only requires originals before Document Check and both checks before Assessment; identity may start earlier, but it is not required to finish first. That spurious serialization is exactly the constraint the K-411 intervention is meant to relax.

K-219’s no-rework timing is too confident. The claimed Tuesday 02:30–09:00 completion, and the resulting 4–11 hour earlier readiness, need D2 to burn down the Monday 16:00 backlog overnight. The next snapshot shows D2’s queue falling only from 5 hours to 4 hours by Tuesday 08:00. The write-up never reconciles those snapshots. It also treats “no clarification” as uniformly faster. D2’s standard band is 4–8 hours against D1’s factual 4-hour service, so a later start plus slower service can finish after Tuesday 13:30 with no rework at all. The 0-hour floor only partially covers that, while the best case of 10–18 hours depends on an unstated after-hours shift and an earlier M1 window.

Smaller faults accumulate. K-104’s table says a usual saving of 4–20 hours and an adverse delay of about 6, while the derivation’s base is 6–14 hours and its worst case is only “a small delay.” The post-assessment wait is exactly 18 hours, not “almost 18.” The Friday 105–115 hour best case for K-337 leaves as little as 3.5 hours for a C1 queue, a 3–6 hour review, and M1, which is only barely possible. The K-337 path writes the approve/reject choice before Decision even though the prose correctly gives C1 a recommendation and M1 the decision. Assumption 7 bundles non-overlap and reuse into one claim.

The rest is well controlled. K-337 is correctly treated as a change in allowed decision policy rather than a pure time cut. Final compliance, four-eyes separation, and the ban on silent acceptance of the discrepancy are preserved. Readiness is consistently separated from notification. The D1 overlap between K-219 and K-337 is a real joint-schedule contradiction. The 100-case discussion covers C1 reservations, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment bunching, weekend effects, and false concurrency, and the ranking does not reward raw cycle time. Those strengths keep the answer well above a weak one; the control-flow error and the unsupported K-219 upside keep it far from 9.
