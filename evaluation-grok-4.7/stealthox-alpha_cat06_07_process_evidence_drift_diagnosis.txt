7.5

The month-by-month diagnosis is right and well separated. April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure with mature labels. June is a routing-mix shift with stable within-variant breach rates, so it need not be concept drift. August is supplier-driven performance and concept drift with Partner Z absent. September stacks that drift with a new population and slower maturation. October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. Falsifiers, stratified tests, maturity gating, and the rule that a label-delay alarm must not retrain are all in place.

The remediation is less reliable than the diagnosis. The interim V3 uplift treats overall mean predicted risk (~0.19–0.21) as if it were the V3 score (~0.2). That quantity is not in the data, and with baseline AUROC around 0.81 the model was likely already scoring V3 above the portfolio mean. A logged uplift built on 0.6/0.2 can badly overstate the correction. Calling that ratio a hazard ratio is also the wrong quantity. The short-term calibrator then pools Jan–Jul with matured Aug–Sep. Pre-drift volume dominates, so the fit is pulled back toward the old regime the calibrator is supposed to leave. The Stage 1 gate is safer than that fit: because calibration does not restore AUROC, Aug–Sep would still fail a ~0.78 discrimination bar and fall through to retraining. The monitoring gate is looser: an M4 calibration breach alone can open a retrain ticket, which is exactly the wrong response to a June-style mix shift.

Two smaller evidence slips: June V3 breach 0.35 and a 42 h parts edge are treated as unchanged versus ~0.31 and 28–38 h, and August’s missing 7% is asserted to be long-path breaches even though that backlog mechanism is documented for October, not August. September Brier matching \(0.31 \times 0.69\) is a fair Brier-skill claim, but “zero skill” overstates the case while AUROC is still 0.66 and only 82% of labels have matured.

The code sketch does the required work: label normalization before discovery, an audited duplicate drop, event-calendar versus case-start cohorts with the right interpretations, and pandas deltas instead of a fictional drift detector. It is not runnable across PM4Py versions. `discover_performance_dfg` often returns a float (default mean seconds); `_median_hours` iterates a dict and will throw, and it never passes `perf_aggregation_key="median"`. The calendar DFG is built on event-sliced traces, so directly-follows edges are distorted. V3 share is proxied by the presence of `Order Part` even though `get_variants` was already computed.
