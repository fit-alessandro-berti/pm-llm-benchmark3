5.5

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid and hits every required section, but it is not decision-ready. Several claims are wrong, internally inconsistent, or stronger than the evidence.

The recommendation contradicts itself. The opening limits coordinator ownership to low-risk segments; the close rolls that role out nationally. That is a different decision, and it ignores 93% utilization and untested absence risk while ranking the absence experiment near the bottom. The memo also calls M1 and M7 “full downstream cost tracking,” which they are not.

Causation is overstated. Aggregate median, tail, cost, and complaint shifts are treated as redesign effects, without the pilot’s heavier apartment and old-building mix, the shorter window, incomplete risk attributes before week 3, sparse start timestamps, or cost data that drops complaint handling after week 4. The sentence about accepting higher cost “in those segments” points at low-risk houses, which would keep the cheaper skip path. “No headcount increase” is unsupported while second-visit utilization is already 92%. Permit-check absence is collapsed into the survey-skip story and never becomes a control. Business and rural deterioration are left outside the routing rule.

Process-mining use is thin. Fitness and precision appear, but precision is explained loosely, and generalization, p95 (45 to 62 days), the four alignment moves, temporal-profile relations, and DFG shares are mostly unused. The evidence table has the minimum eight rows, only one assumption, and classifies stated findings as interpretations. Lower-ranked measurements often do not say what decision would change. Thresholds omit first-time-right, manual activation, second visits, and conformance.
