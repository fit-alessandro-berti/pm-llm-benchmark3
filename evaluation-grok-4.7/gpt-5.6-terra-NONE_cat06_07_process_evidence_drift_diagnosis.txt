8.3

The audit correctly separates the period into four regimes: April as a diagnosis-label and feature-pipeline failure, June as a remote-routing mix shift with stable within-path timing and breach rates, August–September as overlapping parts-delay, V3 mix, and Partner Z composition change, and October as path-dependent label censoring. The table, timeline, falsifying tests, training gates, alert ordering, and failure modes agree with each other and with the reported figures. Observed breach, mean risk, AUROC, ECE, variant shares, edge medians, and Partner Z facts are used in the right direction, and October’s 0.16 is treated as a biased incomplete label rather than a recovery.

The required code sketch has defects that would stop it from producing the audit it describes. `format_dataframe` renames `case_id` and `timestamp` to `case:concept:name` and `time:timestamp`, after which `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is called with the original names. On current PM4Py that is a missing-column error before any monthly row is built, and the temporal-feature frame is never attached to the returned summary. `discover_performance_dfg` is unpacked as a three-item tuple; the current high-level function returns one dictionary from arc to performance, so the unpack fails. The call also leaves the default aggregation untouched, so it does not extract the median durations the rest of the analysis uses. `filter_time_range(..., mode="events")` then discovers a DFG on truncated traces, which drops or rewires an `Order Part -> Part Received` pair that crosses a month boundary — the supplier edge under study. The event-calendar window is closed on the right, while the case-start window is half-open, so a timestamp exactly on the next month boundary falls into two event-calendar months.

The diagnosis table also over-bundles mechanisms. April is placed in the process-drift row and then described there as a representation incident. June’s routing change is filed at once as covariate drift, process drift, and population shift; that policy changes control flow for the existing population, while Partner Z from September is the population change. The narrative later separates those claims, but the table does not. Confidence that May is incomplete remediation is a step stronger than the audit record: the unfinished cache backfill is specified for April, and May’s own unknown-activity and missing-queue rates are only mildly above the January–March baseline.

The same-path test uses same-case supplier delay as a covariate that can account for the apparent concept shift. That is a valid mechanism comparison, and that delay is not known at the diagnosis-time scoring point. The leakage constraint appears only later, in the feature-repair list. A direct consistency check is also absent: August’s matured variant rates imply an aggregate breach near 0.31 against the reported 0.28, and September’s imply about 0.34 against 0.31. That gap is further evidence of differential maturity.

These faults are local. The overall attribution, the refusal to pool April with June with October, and the separation of operational action from retraining are sound, and they are what keep the score in this range.
