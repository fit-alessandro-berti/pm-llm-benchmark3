7.2

The essay is complete, constraint-compliant, and right about the main pattern: early slowness tracks a harder carryover mix, errors bottom near positions 16–30, and the later speed-up with rising errors lines up with the queue/WIP break rather than with elapsed time alone. Interventions 6 and 8 are handled correctly, and almost every cited percentage matches the tables. It is not nearly flawless.

The integrated account has two real slips. “Throughput being pulled down by congestion” contradicts the same sentence’s claim that work is getting faster: shorter service time raises processing rate; the queue grows because arrivals still outrun finishes. The high-WIP “respectively” sentence then lists 4.9%, 6.8%, 12.4%, and 8.2% against seniors, mid-level, and night staff, which attaches the junior rate (12.4%) to night staff (actually 8.2%). Section 1 has the correct pairing, so this is an internal error, not just a missing number. “In experience order” also treats night staff as an experience rank.

The proposed estimator would not recover the adjustment it describes. Direct standardization should weight cell means by the target mix. The code uses `w_target / w_obs` and clips `w_obs` at `1e-6`, which explodes rare cells and is a different, unstable functional. Separately, `case_error` / `case_rework` are case-level maxima merged onto every event, so the code does not reattach defects to the producing activity as the prose claims. `filter_time_range(..., mode="events_contained")` over a January–June span is not a day-shift slice (`mode` should be `"events"`), yet batches and the dotted chart are read as day-shift evidence. `filter_paths_performance` on Quality Check → Rework Correction measures one directly-follows delay, not 7-day rework. `resource_experience_months` is not the senior/mid/junior/night grouping, and that group variable is never built.

Evidence is occasionally cleaner than the tables. High-WIP later error rates are cited as support for fatigue even though that contrast is the congestion result; the limiting column then walks it back. The junior 5.1% → 4.4% contrast is called the clearest warm-up evidence, but the first-20 rate is not conditioned on low WIP. “Seniors look fast … partly because they take complex work” is backwards: a harder mix would slow them. Intervention 2 overstates the first hour as the quality-protecting window; the error low is positions 16–30 (2.5%), not positions 1–5 (3.4%). The handover warning treats every next shift’s first hour as the 48%/56% carryover problem; day and late first hours are 31% and 35%. The dashboard’s “early-shift rework baseline (~5%)” is not in the data, and alerting as the day-end queue “approaches 75” or the high/high cell “nears 10%” sets the alarm at the current bad level (already 75 and 10.9%).
