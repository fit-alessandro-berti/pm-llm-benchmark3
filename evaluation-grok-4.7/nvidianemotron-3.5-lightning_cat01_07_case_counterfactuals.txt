The write-up matches the required section, table, and paragraph structure, and it correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change, preserves four-eyes and final-versus-preliminary compliance in prose, and discusses C1 scarcity, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment congestion, weekends, and false concurrency. Those strengths do not rescue the counterfactual schedules.

Cycle time is internally contradictory. K-104 still notifies on Wed 18:30–18:40 and K-219 still notifies on Wed 08:50–09:00, yet both claim multi-hour cycle-time reductions. Section 3 states that K-104’s notification is unchanged and then claims a 6–12 h shorter cycle. Earlier readiness is repeatedly counted as earlier completion.

K-104 misses the intervention. Preliminary compliance is supposed to run during identity and document work; the path starts it only after assessment, as an infeasible Tue 19:10–Wed 13:10 C1 block, then repeats the original Wed 13:10–17:40 review. That violates single-resource capacity and ignores C1’s 16–20 h backlog and the urgent cases already in queue. The plausible gain is mainly the stated 1–3 h saved on a later final review, or an earlier post-assessment slot, not a new 18 h activity.

K-219 ignores the Mon 16:00 D2 queue of about 5 h and Tue 08:00 A2 queue of about 6 h, so D2 does not start at 17:30 and A2 does not start at 09:30. The stated 4–8 h service also does not end at Tue 06:00. Handwriting rework is recognized, but the no-rework path cannot save 8–16 h while M1 still decides at the original Wednesday time.

K-337’s Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00 wait is about 70.5 elapsed hours, not 38.5. Skipped calendar time through the original Wednesday decision is much larger than 40–50 h if a Friday or Monday decision is real, and “immediately” after assessment is not justified for C1. Rule 7 is also misapplied: after the first request, Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 already occurred. Prohibiting a second request avoids a new loop; it does not override the rework rule for a request that is not issued. Compliance before decision and the changed approve-with-conditions or reject outcome are handled correctly.

K-411 is the most coherent case, but a congested queue that moves assessment from Wed 09:30 to Wed 14:00 is treated as a 4–8 h reduction, which is the wrong direction. The partial order also makes identity precede document check when no originals are required, contradicting the allowed concurrency on which K-411 depends.

The pilot for K-411 measures four-eyes compliance on cases with no compliance review. The 100-case discussion is directionally right, but 300 h of displaced C1 time and a 20–30 appeal count are uncalibrated. The assumption audit does not surface the load-bearing errors: zero queues, unchanged terminal timestamps, and overnight C1 availability.
