The write-up has the required ten sections and a real three-way bias story, but the formal models do not implement that story and would not accept the log they claim to fit.

The conservative and permissive process trees drop `Release Production` and `Archive Case`, place `Expedite Review` after a subtree that already ends at `Approve Configuration`, and never generate the dominant pattern (supplier after both checks: 21 of 30 cases). The conservative tree also admits the unobserved order Engineering → Supplier → Credit, while Section 6 claims it admits only the seven observed variants. Those statements cannot both be true.

The balanced POWL is closer to the stated partial-order idea, but every activity, including Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review, is a mandatory node. A strict partial order then accepts only the two rare expedite traces and rejects the other 28. Section 4 also says supplier confirmation must follow at least one check, then describes a model that allows it before both checks. Reported fitness of 100% for all three candidates is therefore false.

The PM4Py code is not executable: invalid tree syntax, `parse_process_tree_string` instead of `parse_process_tree`, string edges instead of `Transition` objects, a 30-case log that is never actually built, fitness treated as a list of alignments, precision replaced by an imperfect-alignment ratio, and no `generalization_tbr` or `play_out`. Duplicate labels and XOR scalability are never discussed. The evidence ledger, DFG-versus-concurrency note, comparison criteria, and data-collection plan are usable; they do not rescue models whose languages contradict the evidence.
