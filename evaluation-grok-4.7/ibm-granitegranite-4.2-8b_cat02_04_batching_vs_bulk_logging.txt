4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and uses several real contextual clues well (fixture capacity, the group-start button, folder submission, the shared analyst login, the bulk-close form, quarter-hour rounding, and the safety hold). G2, G3, G6, and G7 are pointed at defensible primary mechanisms, and the G6 waiting-versus-service discussion is directionally right. Those strengths do not offset the process-mining errors below.

The two clearest clusters are misranked. G1 has one shared start and one shared end, a 3-minute cycle, 1-second PLC times, and exactly four items on a four-item fixture. That is simultaneous fixture processing; “start batching” predicts unequal ends and is the wrong first label. G4 is exact single-server handoff at 1-second resolution (12:05–12:09, 12:09–12:13, 12:13–12:17, 12:17–12:21, 12:21–12:25). Calling that start batching contradicts the data and the answer’s own counterevidence. The stated start range “12:05–12:13” is also false; starts run through 12:21. G8 has the same adjacency pattern after a ~30-minute gap, so “concurrent work” is not a serious second explanation. G3’s second label, start batching, fights the staggered starts. G5’s second label, end batching, fights four different completion times, and the four-way overlap against a stated cap of three analysts is missed.

`physical_concurrency_plausible` is “Yes” for almost every cluster, including G4, G6, and G8, where the timestamps show either strict non-overlap or zero-duration bulk stamps. That repeats the mistake the task warns against: identical or nested timestamps are treated as evidence that concurrent physical work is plausible.

Section 4 does not meet the analysis requirement. The log omits resource and does not use `time:timestamp` / `start_timestamp`. `format_dataframe` is called on `case_id` / `start` / `end` / `activity`, and the batch objects are then parsed as if they were event lists, so batch categories are never compared. `recorded_at` is parsed without the event date and with a seconds-only format that does not fit minute-valued rows, so the lag calculation cannot produce the lags that are reported. The “expected” overlap and backlog table contradicts both the code and the data: equal positive-length intervals (G1, G2, G3, G7) overlap; G5 overlaps throughout; G4 and G8 do not; arrivals before the first start are the whole group for G1, G2, G4, G5, G6, and G8, not the zeros and “about 3–4” printed. G7’s 14:00–14:15 service is 15 minutes, not 1 hour or 1.25 hours.

Bias coverage is not per cluster. G2/G3 should have been the main examples of overlap inflating service and utilization while pushing wait into the service interval; G4 should have been the low-bias control; G1 should have been one cycle counted four times if cases are summed. Robustness flips G5 to end batching and G7 to start batching under alternatives that the timestamps do not support. Final adjudication then treats clean scanner sequences (G4, G8 service) as interval-censor cases and says G6 needs no operational check, which conflicts with the earlier admission that 17:00 may be submission rather than execution.
