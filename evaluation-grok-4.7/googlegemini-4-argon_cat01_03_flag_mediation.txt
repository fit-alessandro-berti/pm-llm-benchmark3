6.4

The write-up is structurally complete and uses the right causal vocabulary, but the timestamp arithmetic that the argument depends on is systematically wrong, and P1 is not the intervention the problem defined.

The separable-effect and interventional-effect framing is appropriate. Exposure-induced confounding through triage completion time, treatment–confounder feedback, and shared-queue interference are named explicitly, and natural direct/indirect effects are rejected for a real cross-world reason. The seven estimands cover cycle time, triage, review occurrence, review waiting, escalation, and decision quality. E6’s collider warning is sound. The pilot is a real switchback with a spillover outcome, a shadow audit, and stopping rules. The PM4Py script parses timestamps, does not invent absent stages, keeps missing stage times as missing, and warns against reading DFG gaps as natural effects.

Those strengths do not survive the log. Conditional stage summaries are repeatedly incorrect, not just rounded. Flag 0 arrival-to-triage mean/median are 2.48/2.50, not 2.33/2.25. Flag 0 review-queue mean/median are 1.875/1.75, not 2.13/2.00. Flag 1 cycle time is 16.51/16.45, not 17.06/18.30. Flag 1 manual service is 2.70, not 2.60, and flagged escalation wait is 7.00/7.00, not 7.17/6.50. All-case cycle time is about 12.34/9.05, not 12.63/10.65. High-complexity arrival-to-triage median is 0.25, not 0.60. Flagged non-escalated cycle time is 9.15, not 10.28. The prose then reuses those errors: the 8.85-hour penalty, the 2.43-hour triage lead, the 6.55-versus-2.13 review gap, the 9.17-hour escalation add-on, unflagged high-complexity cycle time of 15.83, and flagged high-complexity cycle time of 21.75. The code would not reproduce Section 3, so the answer contradicts itself.

Several mechanism claims are inconsistent with the same clocks. M2’s intervals overlap, including R01, R09, and R03 together, so the R01→R03→R10 single-server chain does not “force” R09 to wait. High-complexity review is not exclusive to M2: R17 is M1. Escalation starts are not concentrated on 00:00, 00:42, and 02:42. R01, R06, and R08 do not wait exactly until 14:00. Both unflagged M2 high-complexity cases are rejected, so the stated 50% reject rate is false. The regression-discontinuity illustration compares medium cases above 0.64 with high-complexity cases below it.

P1 is redefined as turning off priority routing, mandatory review, and display together. The requested P1 only suppresses display and leaves the score and other information in place. The graph makes that worse: `risk_score` points only to `flag`, with no direct path into human review or escalation. E4 also restricts the population to cases already reviewed, which is post-treatment selection. Sections 3 and 9 exceed the word caps, and the pilot description is slightly over its cap.
