The write-up is complete and often careful, but the decision-relevant capacity result is not reliable, and the simulation does not implement the model it claims to compare.

The necessary daily balance is missed. With the stated uniform mixture, expected base service is about 6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50, and 15.74 minutes. Across 42 expected cases that is about 45.4 base-hours of Intake, diagnostics, and Validation. Regular staffed time, after breaks, is 42.0 person-hours. Every multiplier is at least 1, so clock time is at least 45.4 hours before handover, setup, the 8-minute parallel coordination, preemption restarts, and idle gaps. Maximum overtime is 3.0 hours and does not close that gap once slower electrical and software assignments are included. The base day is already infeasible. Section 2 instead treats per-skill ratios as if they were usable utilizations.

Those skill capacities count the same hour in every skill a person can perform. On 14:00–16:00 the five people present provide 9.5 hours, while the arrival cohort needs about 10.9 base-hours. On 16:00–18:00 Deepa and Emil provide about 3.5 hours against about 6.5 base-hours. Calling Intake and Validation “not bottlenecks,” and calling 52% electrical utilization comfortable, does not follow. A per-skill ratio below 1 is only an upper bound on dedicated capacity.

Two arithmetic errors feed that table. Validation’s mixture mean is 15.74, not 14.74, so every Validation offered-load cell is about 7% low. Intake on 14:00–16:00 is \(10 \times 6.94 / 60 \approx 1.16\) hours, not 0.98. Emil’s 16:00–16:30 break is omitted: software capacity in that block is about 1.50 hours, not 2.00, so the headline 1.54/2.00 = 77% is about 103% even inside the flawed skill-ratio method. The text also says overtime is in the capacity figures; the figures exclude it except the starred 18:00 mechanical cell. Morning software coverage is attributed to Farah, although Chen contributes more base-equivalent software time.

U1, U2, U3, and U5 scalings are mostly reproducible given that method. U2’s renormalization is a fair reading. The migration story is still pointed the wrong way: shocks deepen a base deficit. They do not create the first bottleneck.

Section 4 cannot be audited from Section 2. Base maximum utilization is shown as 0.85–0.90 while the same answer has an end-of-day mechanical ratio of 0.46/0.40 and, correctly calculated, an afternoon person-hour deficit. Resilience is defined as a cross-scenario SLA drop, then reported as a different number on every scenario row. Hybrid is recommended under a fairness constraint of max−min utilization ≤ 0.20 and a P3 SLA floor of 0.80, while the table’s fairness column is 0.25–0.48 and P3 SLA is never shown. P3 “starvation” also sits poorly beside p90 delays of roughly 350–800 minutes when the P3 limit is 1440. The 60-minute parallel rule is unexamined against the answer’s own means: base mechanical+electrical expectation is about 59 minutes, so ME almost never qualifies. Surge mode adds at most 30 minutes of overtime and cannot absorb U5 morning software load near 133–171% of the remaining qualified capacity.

The Python encodes the tables and names the right penalties, but it does not run as written and does not estimate the policies. `SKILL_MATRIX` stores unqualified skills as `None` while still containing the key, so `skill in multipliers` is true for every resource and `compute_capacity` divides by `None`. Pooled dispatch, and hybrid P1 dispatch, can therefore assign an unqualified resource. `Task` is constructed with multiplier 1, and assignment never replaces it, so Ada at 1.35 and Farah at 1.40 take the same time as a specialist at 1.00. The SERPT rule in Section 3 multiplies by the resource factor; the code does not. Parallel ME/ES creates Validation when the first branch finishes and creates it again when the second finishes, so the critical-path trade-off is not simulated. The extra 5 minutes is charged on every diagnostic-family change; electrical to software is inside one team under the stated rule. SLA uses only cases finished by the horizon, which drops the delayed cases that matter in U3 and U5. Utilization divides by availability across the full 36-hour array, including a second shift. Overtime is unconditional in the simulator and optional in the prose. Three seeds and an unidentified crash do not support the printed ranges.

Section 6 is relevant, but the claim that U2 can move the bottleneck to Validation conflicts with the model: U2 changes mix, not the one Validation per case. Sections 7 and 9 have the right attributes, PM4Py calls, shadow/backtest idea, and P1 protection. The stage-wait calculation can go negative when branches overlap, and case-level PM4Py waiting time is not separated from the SLA clock or from queue time.

The policy prose does state eligibility, preemption, overflow, and a real specialization–pooling trade-off. That is not enough to carry a recommendation whose load model misses the staff-hour identity and whose experiment does not apply speed, qualification, or a parallel join.
