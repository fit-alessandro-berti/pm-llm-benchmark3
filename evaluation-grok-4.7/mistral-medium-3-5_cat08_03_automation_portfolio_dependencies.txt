4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and reaches the right high-level conclusion that raw frequency is a poor guide, but it is not a reliable portfolio recommendation. Several claims misuse the process evidence, two hard constraints are broken, and the rollout contradicts the portfolio it says it is implementing.

The candidate matrix covers all twelve options and the required columns, yet multiple dependencies are overstated or misplaced. Entity-mismatch assistance does not require the KYC pre-classifier; it sits on the post-KYC loop (`KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch` 1750, with 1160 returning to KYC). Contract generation does not require committee-pack automation: 7600 contract preparations versus 3900 committee cases, and only 3650 contracts directly follow a committee decision. The control-remediation workbench is likewise treated as dependent on pack assembly and contract generation, although it is the module that can stand alone as a control strengthening. A10’s prerequisite is KYC clearance as a legal state, not implementation of A3. Loan activation is classified as light integration; posting to core banking is a deep integration. A8’s prerequisites omit collateral valuation and credit-analysis notes, both of which the case description says committee packaging waits for.

The dependency narrative repeats those errors and misstates four-eyes. Four-eyes is a segregation rule between credit analysis and committee packaging on High-exposure SME cases (5.8% violations in the month-end rush). It is not a contract-generation rule and is not satisfied by A9. Concurrency is also missed: KYC and financial-document review run in parallel, while the text later treats “KYC → financials → committee” as one chain. External valuation is mentioned only generically, without the 71% Equipment-finance / 18% Working-capital split or the 58-hour wait.

The three portfolios are distinguished, and the quick-win set correctly flags the missing control module. The recommended bottleneck set (A3, A4, A6, A8) does not actually meet that requirement. A3 adds false-negative risk; A8 is assembly, not a control. The set also leaves out the worst rework loop (missing-document requests, 52% repeated, 3300 out and 2800 back), the longest wait (external valuation, 58 hours), and the second-longest (customer signing, 44 hours). KYC’s own queue is only 4 hours, so calling A3 a dominant cycle-time lever is not supported. A8 remains exposed to garbage-in because missing documents, collateral, and credit notes are still unclean.

Section 4 replaces qualitative effects with invented precision. Cycle-time cuts of about 30% and 20% have no baseline. Wait reductions (KYC 4h to 1h, entity mismatch 31h to 10h, normalization 20h to 5h, contract preparation 22h to 8h) treat queue and calendar waits as if they were service time. The 82% figure applies only to entity-mismatch cases when that variant is present, not to KYC plus normalization. The 24% financial-review exception rate is used as if it were the missing-document repeat rate. The 1.1% figure is today’s share of provisional signings that needed remediation, not a forecast residual. The 14% contract-mismatch rate is the Credit-line-renewal rate, not the overall 8.5%. Four-eyes violations are projected to 0% by a portfolio that never enforces segregation of duties. A12 is credited with cutting remediation incidence even though a workbench handles exceptions; prevention would have to come from rule checks. Directly-follows edges are almost never cited.

The risk table covers false negatives, drift, audit overrides, and customer friction, but it never addresses KYC false positives hitting a three-person specialist pool concentrated on two seniors. Four-eyes is again attached to contracts. Exception load, month-end legal surge, and the weekly high-exposure committee cadence are not carried into the controls.

The rollout is the clearest failure. The stated portfolio has four modules. The year-one plan implements A3, A12, A4, A6, A8, A9, and A10 — seven modules against a four-module budget. Phase gates also disagree with earlier claims (packaging errors cut 60% versus an earlier 15% to 8%). The two deep integrations in the first six months (A3, A6) do respect the IT limit, which is the one constraint that is handled cleanly.

The closing trade-off, deferring registration because it would expose KYC and classification, is directionally sound, and rejecting an activation bot until controls are complete is correct. Those points are outweighed by an oversold synchronization story, a control constraint that is only waved through, statistic misuse, and a first-year plan that is not the recommended portfolio.
