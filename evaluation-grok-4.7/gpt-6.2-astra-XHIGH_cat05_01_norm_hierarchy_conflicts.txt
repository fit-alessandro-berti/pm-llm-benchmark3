The assessment is a serious, issue-specific compliance analysis: GPIP-4 is kept dominant on segregation, pre-commitment spend authority, and Compliance; ECD-2 is confined to PO sequence and register status; the CFO email is competence-limited; ON-44 is treated as evidence only; and late approval is not treated as a cure. All eight cases are classified under a stated burden, the timing of the binding act is used, and P-502, P-503, and P-506 are robust. The alternatives actually change premises, and the 24-hour backup rule plus nonwaivable integrity controls are a real anti-normalization safeguard.

The score is held down by internal breaks, not by missing sections. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is defined as an authorized departure with the remaining material controls satisfied, yet P-501 lists post-commitment Plant and Finance approvals as deviations and the justification never clears them. B5 keeps Finance in place, and section 1’s “no express B2 deadline” reading is not applied in that row. P-508’s satisfied-rules cell says C permits the early commitment and off-register supplier, while the same row leaves the C3 record unverified and the pseudo-logic makes that record part of `C_route`. P-504 places the eventual PO under satisfied rules even though B1 requires the PO before commitment. P-506 lists Finance/Controller capacity as satisfied and then asks for that same capacity as missing evidence. Section 1 applies emergency routes narrowly but never states a freestanding necessity rule, despite that being a required methodological point. The draft clause says any substantive relaxation needs a CRO exception and then makes two of those controls nonwaivable. “New” and “unapproved” are correctly split in the method and then blurred for P-502.

None of those defects overturns the strongest holdings, so this is not a failed analysis. It is also not nearly flawless: several rows do not follow from the author’s own rule catalogue.
