4.5

The write-up keeps the required seven-part shape and assigns a defensible primary label to G1 (fixture-sized simultaneous batch), G2 (common start), G3 (common folder completion), G4 (adjacent serial packing), G6 (bulk point logging), and G7 (quarter-hour censoring). Those labels are not enough to carry the analysis. Bias directions are reversed on the clusters where the mechanism matters, several signature cells contradict the timestamps, the required code cannot support the comparison it claims, and confidence claims conflict with each other.

**Diagnoses.** G4’s runner-up, concurrent work, is incompatible with zero-gap, non-overlapping 4-minute scanner intervals; confidence 50 also conflicts with the later “high confidence” adjudication. G3’s runner-up, a shared account, has no support: T-C is not described as shared, and spread starts are evidence for end-stamping, not counterevidence to it. G5 treats a known shared login as if it explained the timeline. Four intervals overlap from 13:12 to 13:20, while the prompt allows at most three analysts, so the residual is multitasking, bad times, or a violated capacity bound. G7’s runner-up, a genuine simultaneous batch, is the reading most damaged by the data: M02–M05 arrive at 14:01–14:11 after the shared 14:00 start, and `recorded_at` steps 14:16, 14:17, 14:18, 14:19, 14:20. G6’s primary call is sound, but “end batching” is a poor second label because both lifecycle fields are the same zero-duration instant. G8’s serial 7-minute chain is clear; “no counterevidence” ignores the explicit cross-clock warning. Many counterevidence cells are “None,” which avoids the required comparison.

**Signatures.** The matrix is cluster-by-feature. The requested matrix is hypothesis-by-signature, so tolerance, equality, and adjacency are never stated as predictions that could separate the nine explanations. Logged G2 overlap is total, not low: every case starts at 10:00 and the first completion is 10:08. G5 is overlapping, not adjacent. G6 points coincide and have no duration, so “high overlap” smuggles in concurrency. G8 arrivals span 15:00–15:08; the wide feature is the wait to 15:38, not the arrival process. “Smaller tolerance” does not collapse already-equal timestamps, and it does not create G6’s zero duration.

**Performance bias.** G2’s stated waiting bias runs the wrong way. Logged waits are 18, 14, 11, and 6 minutes. Under the sequential reading implied by one inspector and completion gaps of about 6–7 minutes, later waits are about 22, 25, and 27 minutes, so logged wait is too small and logged service (8, 14, 21, 27) is too large. Summed service is 70 minutes inside a 27-minute wall interval. G3 is also reversed: delaying every completion to 11:30 lengthens early service; the “30 minutes logged as 15” example, including 11:15, is not in the data. G5’s utilization claim is mechanically wrong: non-overlapping sequential work stays at or below 100 percent; summed overlap is what exceeds it (25+15+20+10 minutes inside 13:00–13:30). G4 is absent, so the four required metrics are not covered for every cluster. Bottleneck detection, throughput, and conformance are mostly missing. G7’s “true duration 20–25 minutes” is invented; the reportable fact is a 15-minute bin plus negative waits of about 1, 4, 7, and 11 minutes. G6 is the one solid mechanism: wait becomes arrival-to-17:00, service becomes zero, and T-F shows no busy time before a seven-case spike. G1’s 4× utilization warning from summing four 3-minute intervals is valid. G8 correctly suspects misattribution of the pre-15:38 gap, then still calls that gap ordinary waiting without separating a real safety hold from clock error.

**Code and PM4Py.** The frame is embedded, and G6 is not dropped. The interval log never sets `start_timestamp`; the activity column is left as `activity` rather than `concept:name`; and `format_dataframe` / `discover_batches` are not given a parameter dict that would reliably select merge distance and minimum batch size. The result stored is a global count, not batch category by cluster. Overlap rate, equality-group size, recording lag, granularity, and queue build-up are not computed. `max_queue_wait` would also hide G7’s negative waits by keeping only the maximum. The code therefore cannot compare independent diagnostics with PM4Py categories, which the task treats as temporal detections rather than physical proof.

**Robustness, redesign, adjudication.** Resource trust is discussed only for G5, and again as if sequential analysts produced utilization above 100 percent. Precision censoring is applied only to G7, not to the 1-minute sources whose exact equality is on the same grain as the clock. Of the three “same pattern, multiple causes” cases, G2 versus G8 is false (G2’s starts are equal), and G3 versus G5 pairs a folder-submit pattern with an overlapping shared login. The equal-timestamp trio G1/G6/G7 is the real example and is only partly stated. Redesign covers case-level execution times, batch IDs, badges, clocks, and precision. It omits workstation capacity and pause/resume or downtime events, which are exactly what makes G1 and G8 interpretable, and it does not keep recording time distinct from execution time. Section 7’s buckets are broadly usable, but G4 cannot be both 50 and high confidence, and G8’s serial service is much better identified than its safety-check cause. The closing assumption that the rows are the actual execution events contradicts the G6 and G7 conclusions.
