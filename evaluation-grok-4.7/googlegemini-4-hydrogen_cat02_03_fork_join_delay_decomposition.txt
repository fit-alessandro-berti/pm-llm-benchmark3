7.1

The fork-join decomposition is the right model and most of the case arithmetic holds, but several stated results contradict the same timestamps, so this is not a near-flawless analysis.

The additive identity is correct: total flow equals upstream + fork-join elapsed + downstream gap + final service, and fork-join elapsed is the max of the three branch times. Summing branch sync waits is correctly rejected, and the F07 illustration (25 + 52 = 77 against a 75-minute stage) is valid. The 12-row table, the F02/F04/F07/F11 walkthroughs, critical counts (A 2, B 8, C 2), service/queue/sync means, and the ranking of B over A despite A’s longer service all check out. Counterfactual 1 (15 minutes total; only F01 and F11 move; F01 capped at 5) and counterfactual 2 (92.5 minutes; four cases unchanged; F05 and F10 capped by A’s 50-minute floor) are also correct. The pooled two-server FCFS trace (only F11 waits 5 minutes; 171 minutes saved) is correct under its own assumption. Sections 5 and 7 cover the requested redesign dimensions and the main identification limits.

Material errors:

- B’s excess over the second-slowest branch on the eight B-critical cases is 10, 15, 10, 15, 25, 15, 5, and 25 minutes. That is 120 minutes, mean 15.0, or 10.0 per case overall. The reported 105 minutes, mean 13.1, and 8.75 minutes per case are inconsistent with the case table.
- “A sits only 5–15 minutes behind B in 4 of 8 B-critical cases” undercounts. F03 and F09 are also 15 minutes behind. The four named cases are the ones where a 50% B-queue cut meets or crosses A, which is a different claim from the one written.
- “The assigned server is already idle at `join_ready` in 8 of 12 cases” is false. F04 is busy on F2 until 10:00 against a 09:52 join, and F10 is busy until 11:25 against an 11:20 join. Strictly idle before join: F01, F02, F03, F07, and F12. F06 is free only at the join instant.
- “Only F05, F08, F09, and F11 have the assigned resource busy at `join_ready`” omits F04 and F10.
- The claim that those four still wait after the assigned server finishes is the reverse of the log. F05 starts at 10:20 when F1 frees, F08 at 11:05 when F2 frees, F09 at 11:30 when F1 frees, and F11 at 11:50 when F1 frees. Their downstream gaps are dedicated-server contention. Sub-scenario 3A’s “adding F3 saves 0 even on these cases” is therefore unsupported unless an immovable release clock is assumed, and the text cites the wrong evidence for that assumption.
- Maximum observed concurrency on C is 3 (F08, F09, and F10 overlap at 10:30), not 2.
- The script will not finish as written if `pm4py.get_service_time` returns the scalar mean documented for that call. `.items()` then raises, so the required PM4Py summary never prints. The printed decomposition also drops `join_ready`, which the case table is required to show.
- “~67% over ~180 minutes” mixes an arrival span (12 × 15 minutes) with a two-server occupancy ratio. The load 20/(15×2) ≈ 67% is the defensible figure; the 180-minute window is not.

The core max-based argument and the zero benefit of speeding a noncritical branch are sound. The downstream resource narrative, which the task explicitly asks for, is not reliable enough for a higher score.
