The answer has the right skeleton and catches the main pattern: Orion’s crude cycle time is longer (21.96 h vs 15.24 h), while within every complexity stratum Orion is shorter. It also refuses a blanket 50% move and proposes a small routing pilot plus a capacity change. Those are the load-bearing points. Under a strict reading, the rest has enough factual, logical, and reproducibility failures that it would mislead an operations committee.

The executive section never separates total, direct, and mediated effects, which the task requires. It treats a non-selective 50% reassignment as something that would improve Orion’s case mix. Randomly moving half the cases leaves the remaining mix unchanged in expectation; complexity travels with the case, so Nova’s average would rise and system cycle time would change only if group assignment itself changes waits, service, or rework. The within-stratum gap is mostly wait (about 1.6–3.3 h), with review service nearly the same or slightly shorter for Orion. The claim that review service “largely explains” the gap is the wrong decomposition. “Partly causal through mechanisms” at confidence 60 is an allowed verdict, but the success story is not identified by the tables.

The graph reverses selection (`case_completed → cycle_time` instead of completion as a selected collider). Complexity is upstream of routing, yet the role table calls it a mediator of the group effect. Compliance required is a pre-treatment case attribute, not a mediator of group. “Collider if you condition on group and shift” misuses the term. `shift → queue_wait` is labeled as supported, while within Low the only Orion day/night pair (C02 7.8 h vs C01 7.2 h) and the Nova Low night vs day contrast do not show night as slower. `quality_outcome` is unused. The graph note exceeds the 120-word cap; the executive section is well over 300 words.

Several descriptive figures are wrong or invented. Orion compliance-required mean cycle time is about 26.7 h, not ~30 h. There are no High cases without compliance, so “High wait 19.5 h vs ~0 for non-required” is a fabricated cell. Appeals are 2/30, one Low Nova (C24) and one High Orion (C14), not “~10%, mostly high complexity.” Night Nova cycles are 8.0, 9.8, 18.2, and 34.0, so a 9–11 h median is false. Joint overlap of complexity × shift × group is almost empty (several decisive cells have n = 1 or 0). The reversal in High and Low rests on n = 2. The write-up treats those stratified gaps as stable and never states the positivity failure.

The five hypotheses match the required types, but the predicted observations do not test them. A harmful Orion effect is not “shorter than historic Nova,” because Orion’s cases are harder. A beneficial Orion effect on High cases is not “Nova’s cycle does not rise versus Nova’s own High cases.” Request-info service times already contradict the mediation prediction (Medium 5.9 h vs 6.2 h; High roughly 9.2 h vs 9.4 h) and are not used. The final recommendation then reverses the action: if Orion adds time on complex night cases, it says to move Low-complexity cases instead.

The rollback rule compares reassigned hard cases with Nova’s overall baseline (~15 h). High cases already take ~30 h, so a 20% breach would fire even if Nova matched Orion. Phase 2 (“daytime reviewers during peak night shift” for two days) does not cleanly target compliance or queue capacity. Phase 3 is monitoring, not an intervention.
