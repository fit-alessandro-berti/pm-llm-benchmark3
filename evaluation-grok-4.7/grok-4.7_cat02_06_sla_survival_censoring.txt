8.4

The analysis is technically strong: estimands are fixed before estimators, open cases stay in the risk set, and the main Aalen–Johansen figures check out (resolution incidence about 0.304, rejection about 0.101, withdrawal about 0.140, transfer about 0.075, still undisposed about 0.380; cause-specific 1−S(72) about 0.361; restricted mean to 72 about 50.4 hours). The bounds [9/32, 14/32], the priority and cohort splits, the 9/13 completed-case contrast, the prefix counts, and the terminal edges are consistent with the extract. Censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches are separated cleanly, including the point that censoring an open case at 92 versus at any time at or after 72 does not change the 72-hour cumulative incidence. Transfer is given three real treatments, escalation and rework are kept out of baseline covariates, and the code implements the product-limit, the bounds, the strata, and a PM4Py fallback without inventing sojourn times.

Those merits do not make the write-up nearly flawless. In the sensitivity section, dropping only `Screen>Reject` does not remove P3’s rejection incidence: S14 is still a `Review>Reject` at 63 hours. “Half of P3’s pre-72 exits are reject or withdraw” is also wrong; 5 of the 7 pre-72 P3 exits are reject or withdraw (exactly half of the 10 P3 cases, which is a different denominator). “P1 is the worst priority” does not match the E1 numbers printed in the same sentence, where P3’s resolution incidence is 0.20 and P1’s is 0.25; the ranking reversal is real, but the slogan is attached to the wrong estimand. “Sign flip” for the cohort contrast is loose: A’s two censored P2 cases missing and B’s two resolving would create a B advantage, not reverse the already slightly higher B point estimate (0.316 versus 0.292). Assumption “A7” is used without A6 ever being defined. The lead-in to the four focal cases says follow-up through 72 is complete for the “first, second, and fourth” situations, which does not match the transfer-at-68 case that follows, and the survivor-bias paragraph still contains the drafting slip “removes S03, no.” The Kaplan–Meier-versus-CIF gap is also described as “the competing-event mass” being pushed into resolution, although that mass is about 0.32 and the gap is only about 0.056.

None of these errors overturns the primary coding or the verified life table, but they are concrete inaccuracies in a section whose job is to say what can reverse.
