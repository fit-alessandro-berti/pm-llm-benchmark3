The answer has the right skeleton and the right primary suspects, but it is not a reliable diagnosis. It misstates causal direction, contradicts its own evidence, and its most checkable quantitative claims conflict with the figures it was given.

The evidence ledger has twelve rows and the right column ideas, yet the support/weaken assignments are often inverted or empty. The causal block omits `reviewer_behavior`. It sends `measured_fitness` into `business_outcomes` and `business_outcomes` into high-value status, which reverses the attribute that should constrain sign-off. `normative_model --X alignment_moves` denies the mismatch that actually produces moves. The prose is about 170 words, short of the required 180–280, and it says an obsolete sign-off model amplified the collapse. June–July traces contain no sign-off. Stable outcomes are used as if they ruled out noncompliance. They do not: detection is incomplete, sign-off need not move the approval rate, and a logging failure can coexist with a real bypass. The counterfactuals are already inconsistent with the alignment table. Label mapping is predicted to push fitness above 90%; the given result is 58%.
