The required section skeleton is present, and the broad story (faster priority triage, longer cycle time through later routing and shared queues, case-mix confounding) points the right way. The deliverable is not reliable enough to carry that story.

Timestamp decomposition, the part that can be checked exactly, is badly wrong. Flagged mean cycle time is about 16.5 h, not 13 h 43 m, because R13 runs to the next evening (26.5 h), not 2.5 h. Unflagged triage service is about 57 minutes, not 105; flagged triage service is about 36 minutes, not 25. Residual-to-close is copied from total cycle time, but the post-last-stage gap is about 1–1.5 h for both groups. Absent manual and escalation stages are entered as 0 instead of NA. Support counts are false: high complexity is 8, not 6; flagged high load is 6 and normal load 4, not 5 and 5; flagged escalation is 6/10, not 10/10 with an empty non-escalation cell. Low and medium complexity rows are missing. The perfect staffing split (flagged T1/T2 only, unflagged T3/T4 only) and the perfect risk-score split (flag at about 0.64, no overlap) are never used.

Intervention definitions confuse “well-defined” with “identified,” map a post-triage policy (P2) onto triage service, and omit a decision-quality estimand. Natural direct and indirect effects are named without a serious cross-world argument, despite queue interference and treatment–confounder feedback. The graph omits escalation queue wait, lets case-level underlying risk cause system load, and lets cycle time cause the decision. “Exposure-induced confounding” is described as ordinary collinearity. R04 is called high-complexity; it is low, and unflagged high-complexity cases do exist (R09, R17, R20).

Identification proposals miss the threshold design’s immediate failure under non-overlap, treat a binary load label as a numeric shock, misuse a negative control as a quality endpoint, and call load stratification an interference design. P1’s expected cycle time is the raw unflagged average, which reintroduces the case-mix contrast the prompt warned against. The P3 pilot is too thin for shared queues, and the synthesis then mislabels it as encouragement that identifies a triage effect—an effect that occurs before manual capacity can act. Manual review is 4/10 unflagged, not 50%, and escalation is not mandatory. No outcome evidence supports a “higher-quality path.”

The Python block does not build interval events with real start timestamps and resources, calls `format_dataframe` with a missing resource field and the wrong start-timestamp argument, discovers one log rather than flagged and unflagged DFGs, and never prints stage-level support. Two reversal conditions do not reverse the stated P3 recommendation.
