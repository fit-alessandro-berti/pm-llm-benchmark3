They are not enough for a near-flawless score because several precise claims are wrong or stronger than the data.

- R2’s key payoff is miscalculated. Peak repeat re-entries are about 10.5% of assay entries versus about 5.4% normally, an excess of roughly 5 points. Closing that whole gap is what moves utilization from about 98% to about 93%. “Halfway back” frees about 2.5 points, not 5, and the ρ/(1−ρ) improvement is then about 2×, not 3–4×. The same paragraph says repeat reduction both removes technical-validation work and sends more work into technical validation. The DFG branches Assay → Repeat before technical validation, so a failed run does not create a technical-validation arrival; steady-state validation volume per completed case barely falls. Higher assay throughput can still flood technical validation later, but that is a different mechanism, and the two claims are left in contradiction. The further instruction that R2 must not go live without R5 therefore rests on a muddled load argument.
- The Urgent miss-rate extrapolation is not identified by the data. Normal p90 of 19h supports “more than about 10% miss 18h.” Peak median 17h and p90 33h do not support “probably 40–50%.” A median of 17h means the miss rate is strictly under 50% and could be far lower. The “eight- to ninefold” penalty exposure is arithmetic on that unsupported range.
- The 48h Hold → Medical edge is attributed largely to the every-second-Friday absence. That absence is structural in both periods. Normal queue is 5h against an 18h edge; peak queue is 36h against a 48h edge. The +30h movement tracks the ×5.6 hold surge, not the calendar. The Thursday-clearing policy can still be sensible, but the cause given for the peak edge is wrong.
- “Adding 5 points to a station at 93% pushes it into the unstable range” is false on the answer’s own formula: 98% is severe congestion; instability is at or above 100%.
- Internal repeat-assay loops and the Repeat-after-failure order type are treated as one self-feeding system, including a “2–5 days later” regeneration lag that the log does not contain. The prompt only says those orders consume assay capacity twice and cluster after night shifts and M2.
- Loop times are sums of medians, then used as if they were the median loop. The batch-path “36h” is an all-case median applied specifically to Urgent orders that fail the bypass.
- P3 says the partner is worthwhile when transport plus partner turnaround beats the local queue, then sets the trigger at 24h, which matches transport alone and omits partner turnaround. The 9h error-rise limit is stated for registration clerks and is later applied to validators. The dashboard’s repeat red band (>10%, or M2 > 1.5× M1) does not match P2’s “1.5× that machine’s baseline.”
- Normal completions also fail to close: 15,870 + 570 = 16,440 against 18,400 starts, about 10.7% unresolved. Peak censoring is real, but treating it as a peak-only bias overstates how much of the p90 gap is a measurement artifact. The claim that part of the historical 18%→29% Urgent rise is already label inflation is likewise unsupported; the winter mix shift is the given explanation. Label inflation is a valid future risk of the reserved lane, not a reading of this log.
