The macro-diagnosis is right and the required skeleton is complete: March is mainly a label/provenance break, mapped fitness does not restore the later process, May rework lines up with Supplier Z, queue growth is separated from the small Assess service-time change, and October and December are treated as seasonal and confounded. That is not enough to carry the paper. Several checkable claims are wrong, one causal claim is invented, and multiple explicit requirements are missed.

The DFG distances are simply miscalculated. On the six edges used, L1 from January is 3 (March), 40 (May), 59 (July), and 64 (October), not 4, 33, 44, and 58. The code would print the correct figures, so Part A contradicts §3. The cycle-time split into 3–4 h mix, 4–5 h rework, 0.6–1 h service, and 7–8 h queue is an unanchored identity: no month is specified, median is treated as if components add, rework and queueing are not independent, and `fast_variant_pct` (about 60 to 35) is never used. February’s mapped-fitness delta is 0.01, not 0, and a February rework-rate delta of 0 is imputed without a DFG row.

Temporal and causal claims slip in the same way. Median cycle time does not plateau from June to August (40.8 → 44.2 → 45.0). The first performance break is May, before the 15 June staffing cut; June and October are not sufficient performance change points, and logging is given only March despite the requirement of two per family. The July–August row attributes a rising high-complexity share to a priority rule that reorders the queue and cannot create that share. September’s partial reversal is buried inside a September–November “seasonal” block. Worst, §9 states as fact that the Request Info spike was localized to Supplier Z. The aggregates show only co-movement. The revision sentence later admits this is untested.

§5 does not reliably discriminate. Post-migration `Eligibility Screen` variants are logging, not behavioral. Missing resources breaking the handover network are logging, not behavioral. For the staffing cut, the population column states a null (“no change in mix”) rather than the pattern that would indicate population drift. The Senior Assessment jump is never tested against the stated history of inconsistent recording; October’s rate, 22 per 100 cases against a 41% high-complexity share, is about 54% of complex cases versus about 32% in July (9/28), so mix alone does not explain it.

Monitoring is broad and thresholds are not universal, but the required seasonality and multiple-testing discussion is absent. Mapped fitness never falls below 0.86, so an alert at 0.85 misses the residual conformance loss. Post-migration duplicate and missing-resource alerts fire forever with no rebaseline. Part A never validates the tables. Part B ignores `format_dataframe` renaming columns to PM4Py standard names, so the later `activity` / `case_id` handling would fail. The change-point rule uses a strict `> 5` hour cutoff and therefore skips May’s exact +5.0 h, while fitness recovery `> 0.2` flags every month from March onward rather than an onset.
