The write-up has the right skeleton—parallel join as a maximum, separate rework paths, weekend constraints, excursion dependence, and range forecasts—but the clock arithmetic and queue accounting are not reliable enough for the required forecast.

The primary incubation window is wrong by a day. Forty-six to fifty-four hours after Friday 14:00 is Sunday 12:00–20:00, so a Saturday readout is impossible and Saturday QA cannot see this batch. Authorization probability is also inverted in the decomposition: 0.35 is the middle of the on-call probability, not the probability of denial. Because readiness is already Sunday, on-call versus Monday 08:00 readout changes the join by a few hours at most, and often by nothing once chemistry or QA is later. Ranking that decision first and assigning it a 16–24 hour P50 shift is therefore not defensible. Section 6 repeats that magnitude.

Chemistry is too optimistic and internally inconsistent. Four samples are ahead, only about 2–3.5 analyst-hours remain before 18:00, and the urgent sample outranks B-417, so a Friday start by 16:30 at probability about 0.55, and a start by 18:00 at about 0.97, are not credible. Under the stated 55% FIFO share, B-417 is unlikely to start before Monday and can set the join. The smooth-path narrative also has chemistry finishing Friday 18:30–20:00 after the laboratory has closed. A rerun cannot start before 15:00, because that time is already past and the first assay has not finished; the central rerun finish (Monday 10:30) is only half an hour after the smooth chemistry finish, so the extra triangular service is missing.

The QA queue is inflated by treating 0.35 arrivals per open hour as if they accrue over ~62 calendar hours, producing ~21 arrivals and a two-day wait. From Friday 15:30 to Monday morning there are only about 10–11 QA open hours, and the five batches already waiting are being served Friday evening and Saturday. A multi-day wait is an upper scenario, not the center. That error pushes the smooth and P50 windows out by roughly one to two days.

The published ranges do not match their own clocks. Tuesday 22 September 13:00 is about 94 hours from Friday 15:30, not 72. Monday 28 September 12:00 is about 237 hours, not 195; Wednesday 30 September 18:00 is about 291 hours, not 267; 1–5 October are likewise short by one to two days. Within one smooth path, the table, the decomposition, and the summary give three different central windows, including impossible weekday–date pairs (“Wed 22 Sep,” “Thu 23 Sep”). The ledger’s max-versus-sum note is also confused: a near-tie makes the sum about twice the maximum, and the join remains the maximum in every case.

Scenario coverage, overlap warnings, release-versus-rejection, and the excursion as a tail driver are the parts that hold. They do not repair the elapsed-time error, the inverted on-call weight, the QA arrival window, or the remaining-hour column.
