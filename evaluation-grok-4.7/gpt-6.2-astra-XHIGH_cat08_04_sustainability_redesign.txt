The answer meets every required section, stays inside the evidence, and handles service, quality, and resilience as binding constraints. Boundary, indicators, path-level interventions, rebound, the redesign package, and SP1/SP2/Q1 governance are all present and mostly precise. The made-to-order energy share (34% of orders, about 87% of order-attributed kWh) is reproducible from the segment shares and means. Quoted benchmarks (18.5 vs 3.6 kgCO2e, 2.8 to 1.3 kWh/item, 4h vs 38h, 1.7× lateness, 6% unshipped at 14 days, 22% and 31% policy gaps, 52 kgCO2e, critical 15h/30h/4%) match the log. Dedicated vans, reinforced packs, and residual air are kept where downtime, damage, or slack requires them.

The score drops because the central batching trade-off is signed against the wrong baseline. Plan production already enters Batch wait on 3,020 of 3,780 cases, and the main made-to-order ground path is the batch-wait path. A waiting-time cap therefore often cuts a batch off before the 12–20 piece, 1.3 kWh/item regime. The effects cell still leads with “2.8 to 1.3” and “cap limits gain,” which is the saving versus single-piece production. Versus the observed batch-heavy flow, direct energy per item often rises, in exchange for less lateness, less rescue freight, and fewer unshipped units. Lateness in that same row is compared with both immediate and uncapped practice; energy is not.

Transport mass is also treated less carefully than energy. Segment means put roughly two-fifths of transport kgCO2e on critical orders, almost entirely the 52 kgCO2e dedicated-van path. The lead package is the stock-air correction, which is the cleaner SP1 breach and a much smaller freight share. That magnitude gap is never stated, so sequencing by implementation risk is easy to misread as sequencing by impact.

Smaller faults: intensity per original-promise delivery mixes lateness into the environmental KPI; the release rule is defined as a latest start and then called the earliest release deadline; SP1’s 85% is a mode-eligibility probability, not a segment service floor; `rework_flag` never becomes an indicator; several intervention rows bury the service, quality, and resilience effect in a safeguard clause. The 11,200 + 530 versus 12,000 gap is real, and the same caution applies to larger internal imbalances (ship-to-confirm arcs sum to 9,410 against 11,200 closes; cancel inflows are 470 against 530 ends). Those are omitted, not miscounted.
