The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and is strongly grounded in the event attributes, DFG arcs, path metrics, batching evidence, transport evidence, and policies SP1, SP2, Q1, and R1. It keeps energy, transport, scrap, packaging, rework, and obsolescence distinct; it does not ban air or dedicated vans indiscriminately; and the recommended lane design is conditional on promise slack, criticality, and backup routes. Rebound coverage is real, not generic, including batch-wait expediting, unshipped batch inventory, consolidation recovery, packaging material versus damage, machine-queue displacement, inspection bottlenecks, and customer expediting or cancellation.

The score is held down by local imprecision. The Standard-stock Service-contract median of 18h is treated as if it were the air-shipped subset’s cycle time, although that figure is the whole segment and the stated SP1 violation rate is 22%, not an air share. In the effects table, CNC batch waiting is reported as moving from 4h to 38h, which restates the two batch-size medians rather than the cycle-time change between them. The same table attaches the parcel-air 4–6× gap to van replacement, even though the dedicated-van path is separately given at about 52 kgCO2e per order. Several effect cells are also syntactically ambiguous, so direction and magnitude are harder to read than the underlying evidence allows. None of these errors reverses the package, but they are enough to keep it short of a near-flawless mark.
