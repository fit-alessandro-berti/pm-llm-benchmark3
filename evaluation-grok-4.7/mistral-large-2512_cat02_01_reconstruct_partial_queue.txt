3.5

The write-up has the required seven headings, a labeled assumption list, and correct arithmetic for several fully observed cases (P06, P08–P14, P16’s S1 times, P17’s S1 times and S2 wait, P21’s S1 wait). Those merits do not carry the reconstruction. The hard cases, the station totals, the bottleneck claim, and the required code are internally inconsistent and often incompatible with the timestamps.

**Case-level identities fail.** Whenever arrival and completion are both known, wait and service must sum to that fixed interval. They do not:
- P07’s S1 span is 75 minutes (08:45–10:00). The reported ranges, [15, 75] and [20, 80], cannot be paired.
- P15’s S1 span is 125 minutes (10:10–12:15). [0, 115] and [5, 120] do not conserve it.
- P05’s S2 span is 50 minutes (09:25–10:15). Wait [5, 50] with service fixed at 15 does not.
- P11’s S2 span is 80 minutes (11:05–12:25). Wait [5, 80] with service fixed at 15 does not.

A4’s own bounds are not what the table uses, and they ignore the resource each missing start must respect. I1 is busy with P05 until 09:25, so P07 cannot start near 08:45–09:00. I1 is busy with P13 until 11:40, so P15’s S1 start is not free over [10:10, 12:15]. C1 is busy with P03 until 09:45, so P05 cannot start at 09:30. C2 is down from 10:55 to 11:10 and then busy with P08 until 12:00, so P11 cannot start at 11:10. Those imputations create the overlaps A3 says are forbidden.

**Further case errors.** P15’s S2 wait is 13:30 − 12:15 = 75 minutes, not 15; only the 30-minute figure matches elapsed service to 14:00. The P01–P04 rollup is wrong on every field: S1 waits are 5, 5, 20, and 20; S1 services include 30; S2 waits are 5, 5, 10, and 15; S2 services are 30, 40, 40, and 40. P17 is flagged `OPEN_S1` even though S1 ends at 12:50. P16, and the omitted P18–P20, are still in the S2 queue at 14:00, so their S2 waits are censored lower bounds (about 85, 50, 35, and 15 minutes). A dash drops that queue. P21 and P22 are given point waits/services equal to time already elapsed; the prose says these are lower bounds, and the table drops the inequality. Elapsed service and final service are not distinguished numerically.

**Station table does not follow from the log.** Fully observed S1 waits run from 5 to 105 minutes, with median 75 among the 19 known starts, not 5–95 with median 50. External arrivals after 12:00 are one case (P22), not seven. S2 entries by 10:00 are the seven S1 completions through P07, not four, and three of those have left S2 by 10:00, not four. S2 completions after 12:00 include P10, P11, P12, P13, and P14 (P08 ends on the boundary), not four. Subperiod waiting summaries such as “95 (median 100)” and “45–5” are not summaries. Busy-time and utilization bands are not tied to a time integral, and the 15-minute C2 outage is too small to justify the two-hour capacity story built on those bands.

**Bottleneck reasoning contradicts the numbers it cites.** For 10:00–12:00 the text gives S1 utilization 80–90% and S2 65–80%, then calls S2 the bottleneck on that comparison. Early S2 is idle until the first S1 completion at 08:25/08:30, so “S2 never starved” is false in the window used to call S1 the constraint. Around the outage the S2 queue is on the order of P08, P10, and P11, not a peak of 7. A 15-minute loss of one C2 resource is real; it does not by itself define the 10:00–12:00 regime. No total sojourn is reported for completed cases, and no numerical Little’s Law comparison appears in the analysis. The code later prints that the law “holds approximately” as a fixed string.

**Sensitivity is not a sensitivity analysis.** The case table allows P07/P15 service to swing by roughly an hour, but optimistic/central/pessimistic utilization moves only five points (70/75/80 and 65/70/75). Continuing an OPEN activity after 14:00 does not change in-window busy time once the start is known. Rounding of ±2.5 minutes is named in A2 and then never applied, including at the C1 touch point where P07 ends and P09 starts at 11:00. Section 6’s “stable” claim that S1 is the primary bottleneck early and late is stronger than either the utilization gaps or the censored end-of-window snapshot (S1 WIP is two at 14:00: P21 in service, P22 waiting; S2 still holds four waiting cases plus P15 and P17).

**The Python block misses the required checks and mis-measures WIP.** `MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank are all parsed to `None`. The interval log keeps only rows with both endpoints, which drops open service and every missing start. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called. `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, and `pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals` are not; there is no code comment that names the last of these. The timeline treats an arrival as +1 WIP, a start as −1, and an end as −1 again, so the counter is neither queue length nor WIP and can go negative. S2 capacity depends on the loop variable `time` left over from that timeline. Busy time is the sum of fully observed completed services only. The printed mean WIP is an unweighted average of event instants, not a time average. No optimistic, central, or pessimistic scenario is computed. The script therefore cannot reproduce sections 3–6, despite the closing note that says it does.

Definitions in section 2 are directionally the right ones, and the assumption list shows the right topics. The delivered numbers do not implement those definitions, and several formulas are unreadable because the rate and inequality symbols are missing. The conclusions a reader would take away — imputation windows, late-morning arrival counts, a queue peak of 7, and a clean S1–S2–S1 bottleneck sequence — do not survive the event table.
