6.5

The fork-join identity is right: elapsed stage time is \(\max_i(queue_i+service_i)\), total flow adds upstream, that max, downstream queue, and final service once, and the algebra shows why summing the three synchronization waits is not elapsed time. The 12-case table, critical-branch counts (A 2, B 8, C 2), and the prose counterfactuals are largely correct, including clipping (A −20% saves only 5 and 10 minutes on F01 and F11; B queue −50% sums to 92.5 minutes, with F05 and F10 limited by A). Ranking B ahead of A, despite A’s longer service, is the right conclusion.

The aggregate table misstates C. Queue delays sum to 125 minutes, so the mean is 10.4, not 10.0, and mean queue-plus-service is about 30.6, not 30.2. “Within 10 minutes of critical” is also the wrong benefit rule: F04, F08, and F10 sit inside that window and still save nothing. Tail slack and near-ties (margins of 5–7 minutes) are not reported. Recommendation wording treats ~7–8 and ~14.7 as flow-time levels and cites a ~200-minute tail; observed flow times run from 93 to 130, and speeding B shortens A’s synchronization wait rather than lengthening it.

The required script reverses the savings subtraction, so printed savings are negative, truncates 12.5-minute effects with `int`, never prints per-case savings, and does not compute synchronization wait or fork-join elapsed time. The PM4Py comment names the directly-follows limitation but not the quantities it fails to recover.
