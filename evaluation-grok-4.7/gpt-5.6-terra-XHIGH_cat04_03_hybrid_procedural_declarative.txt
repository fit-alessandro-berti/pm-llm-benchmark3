The write-up is a serious hybrid design: R5 and R6 are kept apart, the strict profile is explicit, the process tree matches that profile, DECLARE template direction is mostly right, the temporal arithmetic is right, and the eight overall verdicts under the strict profile are defensible. It is still not executable as written, and several layer verdicts do not follow from the artifacts.

The log skeleton will not check what Section 5 claims. In PM4Py, `always_after (A, B)` means A occurs after B, so the stored pairs are reversed relative to the prose “when A occurs, B occurs later.” As encoded, `("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment")` requires activation after payment. `directly_follows` is an allowlist; `set()` does not mean “no direct-follows constraint.” A normal checker then rejects every multi-event trace. The T1–T7 skeleton cells of `pass` are therefore not what `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton` would return. Equivalence, `never_together`, and `activ_freq` are the parts that match the stated intent, including the correct refusal to pin `Verify Bank Account` to `{1}`.

Section 7 and Section 8 disagree on T6. `check_sanctions_policy(..., mode="strict")` fails activation at hour 13 before the opinion at hour 23, and the text says the deployed profile calls that mode. The matrix still marks the resource/data layer `pass`. Under the emergency profile the same function is `policy-dependent` only while authorization is missing; that part is right, and the 10-hour versus 17-hour distinction is right. The strict column is not.

T5’s “earliest” violation is the model’s extra assumption, not R3. R3 is violated when `Activate Vendor` at hour 9 becomes a final outcome with no `Receive Clarification`. Classification at hour 5 is still a viable prefix. DECLARE `altresponse` and skeleton equivalence fail on the completed trace, which the text notes, but the decisive-point claim is still wrong. The tree’s `(Request; Receive)*` only before `Classify Risk`, and only after all three checks, is a real restriction and is disclosed; it should not be reported as the policy violation point.

R11 is not actually compatible with the backbone. A later `Verify Bank Account` uses the same label as the initial check. Projecting only `Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` leaves the re-verification in the trace, and the tree has no place for it after the initial `AND`. That trace fails procedural conformance even though Section 7 would accept it. The skeleton discussion of frequency never states this label collision.

Smaller faults: T8 also violates `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Manager Approval)` and the skeleton `never_together` pair, not only reject/activate and the missing payment. The temporal header `mean ± 2` is the wrong formula label, though 0–96, 0–24, and 0–144 are the correct `zeta = 2` windows, and T7’s payment z-score of about 1.94 is correctly left unflagged. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is cited without saying the returned log is the compliant subset, and the snippet covers only the manager route. `int(joint_authorizer_count)` throws on `None`. The 24-hour clock starts at activation in code, but that anchor is not one of the listed assumptions.

What holds up: the tree’s `S || A` before activation is the right strict reading of T7; T6 fails that tree; T4 fails only four-eyes; T3 is a vacuous temporal and SoD pass; activation without either approval is admitted as a DECLARE/skeleton hole; response vacuity and `support`/`confidence` are not treated as legal authority. Those strengths are real. The required dictionaries are not reliable enough, and the T6 layer result contradicts the checker that is supposed to implement the chosen policy.
