7.5

The analysis is strong on regime structure, Little’s Law limits, and the refusal to treat outage recovery or pooled averages as a steady state. Most regime medians, ratios, and hourly dispersion figures check out, and uncertainty is usually labeled. It is not nearly flawless.

The burstiness section treats the Fano factor of raw hourly counts as stochastic overdispersion versus a Poisson benchmark. On 2026-04-07 the series is a smooth diurnal hump, so most of that variance is a non-stationary mean, not extra-Poisson noise. That directly weakens the required dispersion claim; only 2026-04-21 is clearly bursty beyond the time-of-day profile.

Required tables contain corrupted WIP figures: “WIP declining 4332” and “WIP rising 3750” (meant as 43 to 32 and 37 to 50). Read literally, those are false. R2 is also slightly inconsistent: arrivals median is 41.5, not 42, and the p90 median is 55.5, not 56, while \(\rho_{p90}\approx 1.28\) uses the unrounded inputs.

The Python is mostly faithful, but `pooled.round(3)` is applied to a mixed-type Series and should fail before any table prints. The change-point heuristic also misses 2026-04-29, which the narrative treats as a main boundary. Day-equal weighting of service medians is an unlabeled estimand, and the Simpson-style reversal is named rather than shown.
