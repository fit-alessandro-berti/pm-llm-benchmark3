The answer meets the structural bar and the three hallmark tests: I-03 is correctly ineligible after Diagnosis Complete, I-05 is suppressed as an unchanged repeat while I-07 re-enters because of the new log, and untreated-case spillover is quantified rather than ignored. The scenario arithmetic that can be reconstructed (gross ARR × value, €3,400 false-alarm total, €1,350 swarm cost, €900/€1,800/€2,700 per slot) is internally consistent, and fairness plus monitoring are concrete.

Decision-theoretic coherence is weaker than the write-up suggests. The lower-bound direct-benefit gate vetoes I-08 for a roughly €450 shortfall at the extreme low ARR, even though its midpoint net after the stated spillover charge is about €2,750 and would rank second. That same worst-case standard is not applied after spillover: I-02 is still selected though its low ARR minus a high spillover charge is clearly negative, and the portfolio is still recommended after the adverse row is interpreted as a loss. Worst-case gating, midpoint ranking, and acceptance of an adverse portfolio loss are three different risk preferences.

Calibration is described and then not used. Segment adjustments never enter the euro scores; Non-Native-Language Support is supposed to be treated conservatively, yet I-09 is Tier 1 on the unshrunk supplied ARR. The €15,000 untreated-case value fixes who is net-positive and is not sensitivity-tested. Optimistic effects are the top of each range, not the upper half. Time-to-deadline and the option to defer long-horizon triage cases are absent from the score. In the impact table, positive nets are signed and the adverse net is not, so the adverse conclusion is not cleanly auditable.
