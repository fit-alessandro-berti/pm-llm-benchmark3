6.5

The final write-up covers every required section, uses concrete object IDs and timestamps, keeps machine capacity last, and treats lot-to-job linkage as an integration problem. Most of the delay arithmetic is right, and the batch, rack, shipment, and calibration decompositions are usable. It is not nearly flawless. The central timing story contains a hard arithmetic error and a critical-path error, and several claims are stronger than the log.

The J2 calculation states that 08:25→12:05 is 3 h 40 min and then says J3’s 11:55 readiness plus 10 minutes of setup “accounts for the last 3 h 50 min.” That interval is longer than the wait it is supposed to explain. 08:25→11:55 is 3 h 30 min; the 10 minutes to 12:05 are already inside the 3 h 40 min. The same section is better on J1 (3 h 55 min, 3 h 40 min avoidable under a two-of-three start), J4’s fixture gap (09:50→11:45 = 115 min, about 110 min under a 5-minute setup), S1 (13:30→19:25 = 5 h 55 min), and J6 (80 min, of which 45 are CAL-3). C6 and C7 never state a counterfactual in the way the prompt requires.

Narrative A then misuses those numbers. OVEN-1’s idle window, 12:55–14:50, is after B1’s 12:05 start, so it does not show the oven was free while J1 and J2 waited from 08:10/08:25. The claim that FIX-A delay and the B1 delay “converged on S1” is inconsistent with the answer’s own slack. J1 was packed at 13:30; S1 left at 19:25 because of J4. J4 was batch-ready at 13:10, 90 minutes before J5 at 14:40, so the morning fixture hold did not move B2, J4’s 19:10 pack, or S1. J3 holding FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40 is occupancy, not a contention delay imposed on J3. The local J4/CNC-2 inference is the part that holds: release at 11:40, process at 11:45, and a 42-minute CNC idle inside the wait. That is marked, correctly, as an upper bound because no `Wait for Tool` event exists.

The rework sentence is unsupported. Inspect→Rework frequency 2 does not establish 2 of 6 jobs or a 33% rework rate. Summing the two shipment-edge medians to 1.1 h treats the median of a sum as the sum of medians. The 73-minute machine gap versus the 115-minute oven idle is a real inconsistency, but it is presented as proof of missing events; other event types could split the gap while the oven was still non-productive. “Produces C4” contradicts the given `requires` relation.

The ranking table is directionally sound and does not crown machine capacity. Its observability cells do not cover each flattening. Calling aggregated member waiting “~9 h” stacks overlapping J1 and J2 waits without saying so.

Intervention 2 repeats the critical-path miss and adds a false beneficiary: a second FIX-A does not make J3 release earlier, and it need not move J4’s delivery or S1 while J5 still binds B2. Intervention 4’s harmed party is a freight cost, not a job or object. The other interventions do hit synchronization, tools, shipment policy, and logging, and the perverse metrics are specific.

The PM4Py block names the required functions and contrasts interaction counts with objects-per-event, but it is not executable-looking in any strict sense. It uses `ocel.oel`, `ocel:eoid`, and `ocel:ooid`; `discover_ocdfg` is called with a positional `"activity"`; `discover_performance_dfg(..., measure=)` is not that API; `readiness_wait` contains `and None` and `if False` and is never called. The comment against reading waits from machine gaps is the right caution, but the code does not carry it out.

Section 8 is the strongest close: the falsifiers include oven occupancy before 12:05, an indivisible cure, timestamp provenance, and rack availability, and the extra fields would actually separate tool, batch, lot, and shipment waits. Those caveats do not retract the 3 h 50 min figure or the S1 convergence claim.
