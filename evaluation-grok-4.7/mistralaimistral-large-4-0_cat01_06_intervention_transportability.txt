6.1

The transport conclusion is right: conditional adoption, originals as a hard gate, and Site A’s −9 h median left unidentified. The write-up still fails the quantitative and model-fidelity bar in several places that the decision depends on.

Process comparison is largely accurate and covers the required features. “Parallelize identity with originals receipt for all cases” overstates eligibility. Originals receipt exists only where originals are required (62%).

The selection diagram includes the required nodes, but several arrows are misspecified. `originals_required -> treatment_parallelization` makes a statutory modifier a cause of treatment. `S_legal_requirement -> legal_capacity` collapses a 40% demand rule into staffing, and `S_legal_staffing` points at the same node. `legal_capacity -> rework` and `cycle_time -> appeal` are not justified mechanisms. Originals delay (median 18 h) never reaches cycle time. The 187-word gloss correctly says a matching control-flow diagram is not transportability, then overclaims that this graph separates selection from mediation.

The evidence table correctly refuses to treat 4,800→5,200, 41 h→32 h, and 96 h→78 h as treatment effects and lists the co-interventions. It misses a direct productivity check: cases per verification FTE are about 267 before and 260 after, so throughput did not rise per person. Confidence 80 on the complexity-mix row is confidence in a modifier, not in transport of that 8-point shift. Site B’s directly-follows counts are almost unused (only 360/3,400 ≈ 10.6% supplements).

Scenarios include a real tail-worsening case, but the arithmetic is inconsistent. Section 5 scales medians and p90s by the 38% eligible share, which is invalid for both statistics: the overall median need not move if the median case still waits on originals, and the overall p90 can sit entirely in the untreated tail. Under that same scaling, optimistic “−3 to −6 h overall” implies an eligible effect of roughly −8 to −16 h, while the digital-only row says −4 to −7 h eligible and −1.5 to −2.7 h overall. Section 9 then recommends “−3 to −6 h for eligible digital cases.” Those three claims cannot all be true. No scenario uses pool sizes, service times, or the DFG (2,108 originals→document, 1,160 identity→document, 1,360 assessments→legal) to derive a queueing range. The 15.2% “digital and legal” cell assumes independence of originals and legal review.

The segmented policy is the right intervention: immediate document check only without originals, assessment after both checks, legal review and supplement loop retained, role-specific work, immutable timestamps. The formal model contradicts that policy and standard POWL. Identity is concurrent with the whole document choice, so it may overlap document check on originals cases, while the prose forbids that. `DecisionChoice` and `Document Check` are reused under two parents. `Operator.ACTIVITY` and `Operator.XOR_CHOICE` are not the POWL activity/XOR constructors (`Transition`, `Operator.XOR`). A loop with four children is illegal in PM4Py (a loop is exactly do and redo); the redo also ends in another `Legal Review`, so legal review would run twice per supplement. WIP caps (document 56, assessment 30, legal 16) are too loose to prevent the bottleneck shift the scenarios warn about, and they are not derived from the six-task Site A rule.

The pilot’s overlap-versus-waiting-versus-queue-versus-rework split is the right way to separate mechanism failure, implementation failure, and bottleneck shift. The design underneath does not hold. Case-level 1:1 randomization is the primary plan on pools of 4, 7, 3, and 2, with cluster randomization only as a fallback; interference is the main threat, not a side case. “1:1” is also incompatible with T1, T2, and control. Rank effect size 0.55 for a 3 h shift is not credible against a 44 h median and 110 h p90; 400 per arm is therefore not an 80% power target. Intake is about 131 cases per week, so 12 weeks yields roughly 300 no-originals cases per arm, not 400, and not 2,100 overall. Expansion triggers drop the inequality on rework and appeals (“rework baseline +1 pp”).

Assumptions 1–8 are real and testable. The register omits the independence assumption used for 22.8% and 15.2%, and the touch-time reading of “service h” that any capacity claim needs. Assumption 6’s consequence (“congestion gets worse”) does not follow from the modeling assumption being false.

`conditional go` is the correct call, and the subgroup order (digital, non-legal first; originals gated; legal watched) matches the statute. The number attached to that recommendation is not stable across the answer, and the POWL that is supposed to carry the partial order would not convert or play out.
