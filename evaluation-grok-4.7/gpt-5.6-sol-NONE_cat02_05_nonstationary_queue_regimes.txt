The conceptual structure is strong, but the document is not numerically reliable and is internally inconsistent in several required tables. Under a strict standard, those defects keep it well below a high score.

What holds up. Regimes are separated by demand, mix, outage, recovery, backlog, and staffing, and recovery is not treated as ordinary high productivity. Little’s Law is defined as open-hour \(W=12L/X\), median service is not used as flow time, and outage, recovery, accumulation, transition, and the full 28-day pool are correctly marked not defensible. The factorial and backlog-state alternatives are real alternatives, with the circularity of the latter stated. The risky pooled policies (weekend cuts; targets taken from catch-up days) follow from the segmentation. The single code block builds both tables, computes regime summaries, Fano/CV/max-share, lag-1 correlation, a labeled non-definitive one-split heuristic, Little’s Law only on chosen windows, and a correct PM4Py caveat that these aggregates are not an event log. Many primary-regime figures check out: early weekdays 100.1/99.6 and net +4; 15 Apr net +47; 16–17 Apr 105.5/120.5; 20–24 Apr 111.8/98.6, WIP 42.2, net +66; 27–28 Apr 119.5/105.5, WIP 49.5.

Material failures.

- Headline totals are wrong. The 28 days sum to 2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions, net +110 (means about 90.0 and 86.0), not 2,780, 2,679, net +101, or 99.3 and 95.7. Pooled mean WIP is 836/28 ≈ 29.9, not 30.3; mean FTE is 144/28 ≈ 5.14, not 5.25; mean daily WIP/FTE is about 5.9, not 6.14.
- Section 4 leaves the weekday row unfinished (“121? No…”) and asserts weekday totals 2,421 and 2,333. Weekday arrivals are 2,160 (108.0/day) and completions 2,063. The text both publishes false precision and defers to unexecuted code.
- The 6–14 vs 20–28 contrast is not self-consistent. 20–28 Apr arrivals average 892/9 ≈ 99.1, not 97.0, so the “about 12%” rise is off (true rise ≈ 14%). Net +106 cannot coexist with 97.0 and 87.3 over nine days. The 6–14 net is +5, not +4.
- “Early weekend, 11–14 Apr, 4 days,” with means 41.8, 41.5, and WIP 13.8, is the set 11–12 plus 18–19 Apr. That overlaps the post-outage weekend row, so the narrative segmentation is not a partition, and the date label is false (11–14 contains two weekend days). The code labels only 11–12 Apr as `early_weekend`, so the script does not reproduce the prose or the Little’s Law cell \(L≈13.75\), \(X≈41.6\), \(W≈3.96\).
- Discharge nets are written as positive “30,” “5,” and “13” after defining net as \(A-D\). Actual balances are −30, −5, and −13.
- Required table cells are corrupted: “WIP falls 3632,” “WIP changes 5235,” and “WIP 2219” (36→32, 52→35, 22→19).
- The claim that representative hourly vectors do not sum to the daily totals is false. All four arrival and completion vectors match (101/99, 105/58, 115/101, 51/43). The audit sentence is also garbled (“105 rather than the daily 105”).
- Section 5 never reports the Fano factors, CVs, or lag-1 values it says were calculated. `peak_08_10_pct` is not turned into a peak-hour intensity, so burstiness stays qualitative despite a full-sample column that supports it.

Smaller issues: mean-of-daily-medians 31.1 vs about 31.0; weekend WIP/FTE 6.73 vs about 6.6; “high” confidence on two-day states; a one-split SSE heuristic that cannot represent three known breaks, which is admitted but not computed in the narrative.

The operating story a reader would take away is mostly the right one. The grade is for execution: wrong pooled evidence, a non-partition, prose/code disagreement, and broken cells in the sections that were supposed to carry the quantitative argument.
