6.2

The write-up is complete, multi-hypothesis, and mostly well calibrated on compliance, but it is not nearly flawless. Several process-mining claims are wrong or unchecked, including the central reconciliation.

The working-together account misstates the metric. Standard working-together counts case co-membership once, with no duration weight, so it does not “penalize brief touches.” Mira–Carla at 8% is about 330 cases if the denominator is the full log, which matches the 350 handover rather than proving non-overlap. Low weighted degree is better explained by a split star versus dense team cliques and by global normalization. Calling her involvement “non-overlapping,” and calling the pattern a metric consequence of short events, answers the main question with the wrong mechanism. Her case-count percentile is 0.91, so “small in case count” is also inconsistent with the table.

Listed handover volumes are used as real dispatch counts without a bound check. Mira has 960 events, yet the cited outgoing edges alone are 350+330+290+280+210+180 = 1640, and the listed inflows are 920+160+140+190+170 = 1580. Direct succession cannot exceed her event count. The return-edge total is also misadded as 520 (160+190+170, dropping Priya’s 140). Those sums are then reused as coordination load (~1220 cross-team handovers).

Inter-event times are treated as coordination gaps. Mira→Priya is 51 minutes against Priya’s 51-minute median service, and Mira→Omar is 42 against 37. If timestamps are completions, elapsed time already contains the target’s service. Section 3 still calls 16–19 and 42–51 minutes coordination overhead.

The absence reading is too strong. Evidence is a 6-day window in which low-value median cycle time was unchanged and exception p90 moved from 8.4 to 13.9 days. The answer turns that into “78% of claims, all low-value claims, unaffected,” and into “Hospital of Vendor_Verify/Pay not affected,” which is both garbled and unsupported. There is no cohort, censoring, or case-mix caveat, despite p90 cycle times of 8–14 days versus a 6-day absence. “19–31% routed to the desk” mixes Bot_Ingest/Bot_Rules exception shares with the separate ~22% Mira coverage.

Fairness is misread. Elise and Farid have higher exception shares (0.36 and 0.35) than Carla and Diego (0.33 and 0.29). The senior-versus-junior exception-burden claim points the wrong way. “Carla/D Jefe” is unreadable and reverses the team-lead → adjuster → team-lead subcontract.

Section 4 would not run as written. `filter_event_attribute_values` on `time:timestamp` does not implement a time window. `discover_organizational_roles(..., num_roles=5)`, Jaccard-versus-cosine working-together, and Burt constraint via `discover_network_analysis` are not the actual PM4Py contracts. `beta=0.5` is unexplained. Service-time dependence on start/complete lifecycle pairs is under-specified where the analyses are proposed.

What holds up: five distinct hypotheses with limits and predictions, segmented rather than global bottleneck language, no misconduct claim, bots and vendors kept in view, removal refused unless the routing function is replaced, and an executive summary inside 120–180 words. Those merits do not offset the metric error, the impossible handover arithmetic, or the non-executable analyses.
