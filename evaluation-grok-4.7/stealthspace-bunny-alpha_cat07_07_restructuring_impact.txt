6.5

The forecast itself is careful and complete: all seven required sections are present, East is treated differently from North and South, and the recommendation correctly refuses a full rollout. The score is held down by broken PM4Py code, a few claims that do not match the supplied numbers, and guardrails that contradict the baseline.

Sections 1–6 meet the brief. The mechanism map covers nominal handover reduction, central queues, utilization, rework, local expertise, role clarity, absence resilience, and fairness, and it correctly treats management’s frequencies and the “15% utilization” claim as hypotheses. The three scenarios each include handover pattern, waits, cycle-time distribution, rework, accountability, fairness, and early signals. Leading versus lagging indicators are separated and tied to event-log measures. Section 4 covers tail latency, mix shift, local knowledge, fairness, learning, accountability, resilience, and hidden coordination. The pilot has scope, a comparison group, windows, segmentation, guardrails, staff feedback, and rollback. The recommendation is conditional and lists what early averages cannot show. Management’s own waits (technical 3.2h to 4.0h, return 1.6h to 2.2h, approval 2.4h to 3.0h, legal/risk 9.6h to 10.5h) and higher referral counts are read correctly.

The quantitative link is still incomplete. On the published tables, technical handovers rise from 36+31 to 41+36 per 100 cases, and the listed handover types sum to about 343 versus 359 per 100. That increase, plus difficult quality check and legal/risk triage on an unchanged three-person technical pool, largely accounts for the “15% utilization” figure as extra load. Volume-weighted technical utilization is already near the mid-70s; about 14% more technical referrals, before added triage, lands near the mid-80s. The text describes saturation risk but never closes this arithmetic, so the main benefit claimed by management is left half-explained.

The optimistic scenario assumes “sufficient staffing and flexible skills” while Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91% and the proposal adds referrals (9 to 12 per 100) without new specialist headcount. That is a different operating design from the one being forecast. Calling the optimistic checks “early warning signs” also blurs the category used for the central and adverse cases.

The pilot guardrail “effective utilization above 85%” is already breached by Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) before any change, so it cannot govern the pilot as written. With only three teams, randomized rollout timing has almost no power, and a one- or two-team shared queue does not reproduce the variance reduction of pooling North, South, and East. Contamination and a stepped wedge are mentioned; power and external validity are not.

Section 7 names the requested algorithms, keeps originating team distinct from executing resource, and warns that closed-case comparisons hide backlog. The sketch as written does not run, and several failures are ordinary API mistakes rather than version adapters:

- `pd.to_pd.to_datetime` is invalid; the call is `pd.to_datetime`.
- `pm4py.format_dataframe.log(...)` is invalid. `format_dataframe` is a function and does not take `resource_key`.
- `pm4py.convert.log_to_dataframe` does not exist; the wrapper is `pm4py.convert_to_dataframe`.
- `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with a dict and `case_id_glue`. The signature is `(log, attribute_key, values, level=..., retain=...)`.
- `get_rework_cases_per_activity` returns an activity-to-cases mapping, then is passed through the log converter.
- `SEGMENTS` and the pooled network use `origin_team`, but `prepare_log` never builds it from `team`, so the segmentation loop raises `KeyError`.
- `case_interval_table` always raises `NotImplementedError`, so service-versus-waiting comparison never executes. `complete_case_ids` is never applied, and `.all()` drops a case unless every event’s outcome is a completed label. `filter_variants`’s result is discarded. No pre/post edge rates, waits, or rework deltas are computed.
- Sorting ties by resource name invents handover direction; it does not resolve simultaneous events.

The closing interpretation notes are methodologically sound, especially on subcontracting as an activity-association pattern and role discovery as descriptive clustering. They do not repair the sketch.
