8.3

The memo meets the required structure and the decision constraints. It selects a guarded, segment-limited path, refuses national scale and a blanket rollback, and keeps facts, interpretations, and uncertainties in separate columns. Counts, rates, DFG shares, model metrics, alignments, utilization, and segment deltas match the evidence. Fitness versus precision, median versus tail, handover counts versus absence risk, visible versus downstream cost, and house versus apartment/old-building effects are explained with the right mechanisms, including mix, censoring, and a permissive model.

The score is held down by several analytical gaps. The tested change is a package in a harder building mix, with incomplete risk labels before week 3 and coordinator load already at 93%. The recommendation still unbundles that package—keeping coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling, while banning survey skipping and post-billing activation for every segment—and labels this unmodified Alternative E. Simple-house gains were observed only under the full pilot, so those gains do not identify which component produced them. That extrapolation is named once, then treated as settled enough to authorize another tranche.

Section 4 quietly rewrites E toward that unbundled policy, which makes the comparison with C and D easier than a faithful reading of “deploy R” for low-risk houses. The rollout bars (2 days, 1 percentage point, €5) are stated as decision rules without a loss basis or any check that a feasible sample could resolve them. “Completion-to-completion intervals” is stated as a fact; the log evidence only says that starts are sparse and that those relations changed. Generalization is used only as a vague caution. M7 is ranked first even though the billing and permit gates are already adopted, so its remaining value is mostly detecting logging artifacts, while M5 bears more directly on the coordinator model being expanded. The executive memo also spends none of its unused word budget on the precision collapse or the normative-versus-discovered model distinction, which is the main process-mining reason the happy path cannot be treated as controlled.
