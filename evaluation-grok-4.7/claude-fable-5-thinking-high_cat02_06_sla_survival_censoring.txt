6.5

The document is a serious competing-risks analysis: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72 h, withdrawal/rejection kept as competing events, open cases retained, transfers given several explicit codings, and escalation/rework treated as post-baseline. Those choices match the operational question. The headline numerics and several data claims do not, and they contradict each other.

The published E1 interval is wrong for the definition the answer itself adopts. Section 7 tells governance to quote T-a (transfer is non-resolution) together with bounds [15/32, 23/32]. Under T-a the three transfers are known non-resolutions by 72 h, so the identified floor is 18/32, not 15/32. Even under T-d (“resolved at the transfer clock”), S32 transfers at 96 h and has already missed 72 h; Section 6 says S32 breaches under every treatment, while Section 3 and `bounds_E1` still omit S32 and treat all three transfers as possible on-time resolutions. The tight assumption-free range is about [16/32, 23/32], and the T-a range is about [18/32, 23/32]. The code prints the looser interval.

Section 6 then says that counting the 7 withdrawals/rejections as breaches raises the lower bound from 15/32 by adding those 7. The 15 already includes them (4 late resolutions + 4 open cases past 72 + 7 terminal non-resolutions). The same paragraph treats T-a, T-b, and T-c as a roughly 2/32 swing from S08 and S21 “flipping.” For a resolved-by-72 indicator, T-a and T-c agree on both cases; T-b censors them rather than flipping them to successes. The code comments that `breach` has the same risk-set math as `competing` and does not even run that mode. S08, censored or competing at 68 h, also cannot move CIF_resolve(72), because no resolution occurs in (68, 72].

Other counts are off. Cohort B has 5 open cases, not 6 (stated twice). All 8 P1 cases are escalated, not 5/8. Cohort×priority cells are size 3–7, not 1–3. S07 resolves at 54 h and is grouped with “slow” repeat-review cases. The Mermaid graph, claimed to contain only observed transitions, omits AutoCheck→Resolve (S01), AutoCheck→Review (S15, S17), and Rework→Resolve (S27), which the prose mentions.

E2 is written as CIF_resolve(∞) − CIF_resolve(72) = “eventually resolves.” With cases still open at the freeze, that difference is only the identified late-resolution mass; eventual resolution stays only partially identified. “Greenwood-type” standard errors are the wrong variance name for the Aalen–Johansen CIF, and no standard errors are computed. `lifelines` is named and never called.

The parts that hold up are the estimand-to-decision map, the open-past-72 versus open-before-72 distinction, the transfer profile (all three are escalated P1), the gap=0 separation warning, landmarking given no activity clocks, and the data wishlist. Those do not repair an SLA bound that is attached to the wrong transfer scenario.
