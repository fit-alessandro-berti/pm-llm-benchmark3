4.4

The headline diagnoses for G1, G3, G4, G6, and G8 sit in the right family, and the write-up keeps the required seven sections, uses the fixture, begin-group, folder, shared-login, rounding, and safety-check notes, and correctly shows that G6 produces a multi-hour wait and a zero service time. Those merits do not offset the factual and directional errors below.

The signature matrix treats logged time as if it were physical execution. G2 and G3 are nested overlaps (shared start or shared end); G5 is a four-way overlap from 13:12–13:20. All three are marked low/serial. G7 is the same 14:00–14:15 interval on every row, so overlap is total under the logged times, yet the matrix says low and also says a smaller tolerance would split it. Identical timestamps do not split when the tolerance shrinks. G6’s precision is given as 15 minutes; the source says 1 minute. G6 arrivals run from 14:05 to 16:25 and are not a clump. The matrix is cluster-by-signature rather than the requested hypothesis-by-signature comparison.

Concurrency flags contradict the evidence. G5 is marked physically non-concurrent because a shared login is treated as one person, against the stated cap of three analysts and against the overlap; four simultaneous intervals still exceed three analysts, which is never used. G8’s repairs are adjacent and non-overlapping, but physical concurrency is marked yes. G2’s staggered, separately recorded completions are weak evidence for concurrency, and the group start is marked as needing no correction even though later cases’ logged durations include earlier cases.

Bias directions are not reliable. For G3, a completion later than the true finish lengthens start-to-complete service; the answer calls that truncation and underestimation. G1’s simultaneous cycle and G2’s copied group start are given the same “inflated wait” mechanism, and the utilization double count from summing overlapping case intervals is missing (G2 sums to about 70 logged minutes inside a 27-minute wall span; G5 sums to about 70 person-minutes). G7 never uses the strongest defect: M02–M05 have queue arrival after the recorded start, so waiting time is negative and the single-server queue metric is invalid. “De-round to the true range” is not identified by an undocumented rule; interval censoring is the defensible conclusion, and it appears only later.

G4’s top and second explanations are the same claim. Perfect adjacency with a 4-minute service and second-level recording lag supports uninterrupted single-server flow; it does not by itself prove an operational batch. G6’s recommended repair, estimating service from arrival to completion or from `recorded_at`, is not supported: every G6 `recorded_at` is 17:01, so it contains no duration.

The Python block would not run and would not answer the question if it did. `IntervalLog.from_dataframe` and `EventLog.from_dataframe` are not PM4Py’s interval interface; completion is never mapped to `time:timestamp` or start to `start_timestamp`; `format_dataframe` is called on the wrong object; discovery is attempted on start instants only, so simultaneous, start, end, and sequential categories cannot appear. The overlap rate the comments promise is not computed. The exception handler records zero batches, and the “comparison” is a comment.

Section 5’s utilization split of G5 into 100% versus 33% does not follow from the overlap. Section 7 still offers `recorded_at` as a G6 duration source and labels G4 a batch with high confidence. The collection redesign covers the requested topics, but the performance conclusions are not stable enough to use.
