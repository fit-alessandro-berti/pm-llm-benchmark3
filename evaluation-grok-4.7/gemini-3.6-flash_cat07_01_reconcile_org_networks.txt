5.5

The write-up covers every required heading, keeps four competing hypotheses, and uses many of the given figures correctly (centralities, 960/905, the 8.4→13.9 absence shift, the ~65% relative increase). It is not a single careless narrative. It still fails the core validity test often enough that it cannot score higher.

The reconciliation of handover/subcontracting centrality with working-together periphery is the question, and the mechanism is wrong. Co-case rate is about shared cases, not events per case. One event in a case still counts as co-occurrence, so “~1.06 events per case dilutes pairwise overlap” does not follow. Partner spread or metric normalization might; event thinness does not. Calling Alice–Carla “27% of Team North’s cases” invents a denominator the table does not give. That error sits on the main contradiction the answer was asked to resolve.

Several claims are stronger than the log. Role discovery was not run, yet section 1 reports a unique activity-profile cluster. “No Approve Settlement” and “exclusively Alice and Bruno” treat a main-activity list as an exhaustive matrix. “Non-exception cases were completely unaffected” upgrades an unchanged median for ordinary low-value claims. “Unassigned queues,” “unnecessary” specialist return loops, and “unmonitored discretion” are interpretations stated as findings. Inbound Bot_Rules→Mira elapsed time is 3.5 minutes, and Mira→Omar / Mira→Priya elapsed times (42 and 51 minutes) match those specialists’ own service times. Section 1 still says exception delay is queue time before and after Mira.

Hypothesis logic is mismatched. Specialist returns are what a formal exception desk would do; they are used as evidence against that hypothesis. Low working-together with Alice and Bruno fits a broker who does not co-own cases; it is used as evidence against the broker hypothesis. The compliance hypothesis treats a reviewer’s worry as evidence that she is a governance gatekeeper. One six-day absence is treated as proof of undocumented tacit rules, with no confounders. Workload fairness calls senior handovers more “complex” even though the notes are missing-info reassignment versus rework, and the count gaps are modest. Vendors are effectively ignored, despite long Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay waits and the instruction not to ignore them. “External/specialist” also blurs internal specialists with vendors.

The PM4Py section would not discriminate the hypotheses. Filtered logs and service-time frames are built and never used. The absence window is given invented dates. `discover_*_network` is unpacked as `(matrix, graph)`, which is not the usual return. `filter_time_range` is called with `dt_start`/`dt_end`. Service time is proposed without `lifecycle:transition`, which is what makes service time distinct from sojourn time. The block mostly rediscovers tables already supplied.

Section 5 then locks in the contested story: specialist returns should be removed immediately as waste and sent “back to the assigned adjuster,” including on paths where Mira may be the first human and no adjuster exists yet. That both prejudges the hypotheses and can drop the coordination function it does not replace. The executive summary says the pattern “confirms” she is a transient triage broker, which collapses the multi-hypothesis requirement into a ground-truth label.
