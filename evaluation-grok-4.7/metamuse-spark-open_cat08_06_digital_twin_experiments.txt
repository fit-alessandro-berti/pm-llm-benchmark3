7.2

The design covers all eight required sections and meets the hard constraints: non-stationary arrivals, calendars, cross-case bundling, latent objections, and a service/wait split that is not silently collapsed. The experiment matrix, CRN plan, tail treatment, and decision rules are substantive. It is not nearly flawless.

The main reasoning error is capacity. About 57 cases per day, mandatory planning at a 3.5 h median, and 8 planner FTEs imply planner demand several times weekly capacity. Fire and environmental pools are similarly overloaded, so finite historical waits cannot be a steady state. The validation section misreads the Little’s Law gap (WIP ≈ 2,650 versus an asserted municipal WIP of 800–1,000) as applicant delay. Applicant time does not consume planner or inspector hours, and the “~46 d mean” is only a blend of variant medians, which Little’s Law cannot use. Warm-up WIP is also stated as both 600–900 and 800–1,000.

The skeleton does not stay on one PM4Py object model. `format_dataframe` returns a dataframe, but variant and case-duration calls use the EventLog APIs with no `convert_to_event_log`. `timestamp_key_2` is not the start-timestamp argument, the process-tree import is not the real parser, and `pm4py.conformance.fitness_token_based_replay` is the wrong path. The replication-size formula is garbled. No-show rate (~5%), excluded emergencies (<2%), and a 10–20% DFG bundling bias are point assumptions without a basis. Stress cases are not fully crossed with D1–D10 in the matrix, and several trade-off cells lose their direction.
