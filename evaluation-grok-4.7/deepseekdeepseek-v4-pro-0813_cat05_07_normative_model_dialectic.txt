7.6

The review is strong on structure, case coverage, and the refusal to treat raw M0 mismatch as automatic misconduct. It still has several concrete accuracy and model-semantics defects that keep it well short of nearly flawless.

The dialectical frame is the best part. Prosecution and defense each concede the other side’s real points, the burden of proof is usable, and the case calls are mostly right: SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-705, and SUP-714 as noncompliant; SUP-704 as a valid FD-7 exception; SUP-707, SUP-709, and SUP-710 as model or scope problems; SUP-706 and SUP-711 as data problems; SUP-701 and SUP-713 as conforming. The system judgment correctly separates “CS-9 was breached” from “M0 is not a valid sole norm,” and it rejects both overreach positions with case references rather than splitting every case.

The evidence handling is not equally careful. SUP-702 is dated 2026-06-15, while N4’s CRM move starts 2026-07-01, yet the defense and matrix explain the missing onboarding-log notification as the CRM migration. The case’s own stable `notification_id` can still support JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; the stated cause cannot. SUP-708’s matrix treats Quality_Review before Sanctions_Check as an M0 violation. M0 puts those two activities in a strict partial order with no edge, so that order is allowed. The real control-flow breach is activation before sanctions, plus manual approval after activation. The final label for SUP-708 is still right, but the conformance diagnosis is not. SUP-704 lists retrospective approval as missing even though Manual_Procurement_Approval is in the trace, and its risk is “low-medium” in the matrix and “low” in the judgment. The defense also never uses the strongest aggregate fact: 289 of 312 Auto_Approve cases appear PD-3-eligible.

The revised architecture is the right layered design, and the POWL mostly encodes the required routes: concurrent sanctions and quality on the standard branch, manual approval before activation, auto-approval with quality after activation, provisional emergency activation, rejection, and notification before close. Two implementation problems remain. `SilentTransition(label=None)` is not the PM4Py constructor (`SilentTransition()` takes no arguments), so the required executable block fails. Master_Data_Validation is added as a mandatory visible activity without saying that current pilot traces do not contain it and will fail the new model until that event is collected. Reactivation and withdrawal are discussed as scope rules, but the POWL still forces Register_Supplier on every path and the code section does not state that limitation. The policy table also drops the comparison operators, leaving “requester approver” and the EUR 20,000 cap unreadable.

Those are not cosmetic slips. They hit model semantics, evidence dates, and the executable artifact the task specifically required. The judgment itself would still be a sound review memorandum.
