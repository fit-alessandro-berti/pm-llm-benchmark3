7.4

The diagnoses are coherent and usually well supported. Identical timestamps are not treated as proof of simultaneous physical work, operational batches are separated from logging batches, and the bias directions for waiting, service, utilization, and bottlenecks are mostly right. G2’s point that a shared start understates later waiting while inflating later service, G3’s inflated early service, G5’s peak concurrency of four against a pool of three, G6’s long-wait/zero-service paradox, and G7’s negative waits are all sound.

The required PM4Py comparison is not actually delivered. `format_dataframe` standardizes keys to XES names, but discovery is then called with `case`, `activity`, and `resource`, so it should fail. The fallback also treats the formatted result as a list of events and will break when it is a dataframe. Because that failure happens before the pandas print, the script does not reliably produce the cluster table or the comparison. Expected PM4Py categories—simultaneous, start, end, and sequential, including zero-gap sequential detection for G4 and G8—are never stated in prose.

Conformance bias is missing, and throughput bias is only incidental, despite both being requested. Smaller defects include calling G1’s 2–3 second recording lag “1–2 seconds,” treating 17:00 as end-of-day without evidence, leaving precision censoring bounds unspecified, and not using G7’s strictly staggered insertion times as evidence against physical simultaneity.
