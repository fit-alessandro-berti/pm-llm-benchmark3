The answer has the right structure and clears the three headline traps: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed for an unchanged refresh while I-07 is reopened because of the new log, and spillovers are treated as a real cost. The base gross-benefit arithmetic (about €15,050 at midpoints) and the €1,350 labor cost are checkable. Beyond that, the decision does not follow the rule that is supposed to justify it, and the impact table is not internally consistent.

The deployable rule subtracts swarm cost, full false-alarm burden, and about €2,400 of spillover per slot, then says to take the top positive scores after a calibration shift. That rule is not what the allocation does. On those terms I-04 is the strongest case (midpoint effect × €60,000 still dominates after its €2,000 burden), while I-07 is negative once spillover is charged, and I-08’s lower-bound effect (€5,000) does not cover a €5,000 false-alarm burden plus €450. The chosen book is I-07, I-02, and I-08, with I-04 labeled Tier 3 for a two-hour difference in slack (8h vs 10h). Calibration adjustments (+0.065 SME, −0.085 Strategic) never enter the score or the euros, so the two Strategic selections are not robustified against the bias the rule itself names. There is no marginal hurdle against the reserve slot and no option value of waiting on long-horizon triage cases.

The impact table then splits from the effects. “Breaches plausibly avoided” of 1.8 / 1.1 / 0.4 cannot come from the effect ranges: even the upper bounds sum to 0.69, and the midpoints sum to about 0.51. The optimistic 1.8 matches 0.72+0.66+0.43, i.e. the sum of observational `p_now`, which is the failure mode the task called out. The euro range does use effects, so the same row mixes two incompatible causal assumptions. Base benefit is shown as both €12–15k and €15,050. False-alarm burden, especially I-08’s €5,000, is in the Section 1 formula and missing from Section 3, which is what makes the base case look comfortably positive (~€6,500). Optimistic spillover of €2,400 does not match the stated 0.01 × 6 × €20,000 × 3 slots formula (€3,600). The €20,000 × six diagnosis-stage cases is not tied to the live queue, which has only a few diagnosis-started cases. Adverse is directionally negative but not arithmetically tied to “one selected case gets little or no benefit,” and a negative adverse case does not change today’s three-slot commitment.

Section 4 gets the I-05 vs I-07 contrast right, then overreaches: new evidence should reopen consideration, not automatically buy a slot for a €5,000 case. Escalation is only a label. I-02 is a second alert with no stated new event and is still intervened on, which the persistence rule as written would not allow. The 1.5h case is treated as the best reason to swarm, without asking whether a 45-minute diagnosis intervention can still move resolution and customer validation before that deadline.

Fairness and monitoring name the right risks (value starvation, non-native uncertainty, segment miscalibration, label feedback, spillover incidence) and offer concrete devices: a weekly non-Strategic floor, manual review, a shadow log, uplift, and a two-week negative-net trigger. Those devices are loosely specified and partly at odds with the policy. A 10% holdout “where safe” estimates the effect on cases one is willing to neglect, not on I-08-type cases. A Strategic breach rate under 5% is as consistent with successful treatment as with overprediction, so the recalibration trigger can punish the policy for working. Non-monetary duties for public-sector cases, specialist sequencing (two seniors, four slots, a possible regulatory arrival before 15:00), and a fast repair of I-06’s imputed queue feature are not operationalized.
