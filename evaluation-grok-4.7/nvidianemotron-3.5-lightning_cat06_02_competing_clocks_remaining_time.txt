The write-up has the required six-part structure, uses ranges rather than a single timestamp, and correctly places primary incubation readiness in Saturday 12:00–Sunday 20:00. Those merits do not survive the calendar and parallel-path errors.

The decisive mistake is in the final update. Authorizing a weekend microbiologist cannot move release to Saturday 19 or Sunday 20 September. Chemistry has no weekend hours, and with four samples waiting plus analysts still occupied, B-417 almost certainly cannot finish on Friday. The join is a maximum, so a Saturday microbiology result still waits on chemistry until Monday. Section 6’s claim of a 1.5–2.5 day advance contradicts the answer’s own smooth path, where chemistry ends Monday 11:00 and microbiology Monday 09:00, so the weekend readout changes nothing.

That same error warps the ranking: weekend microbiology staffing is called the largest driver, while the binding constraint on the smooth path is the chemistry queue carried to Monday.

Other material failures:

- “Delayed microbiology” is only the ordinary no-on-call Monday readout, and its central finish (Monday 12:00–13:30) is earlier than the smooth path. The required 20–30 h contamination confirmation is never timed.
- QA arrivals are computed as 60 elapsed hours × 0.35 ≈ 21 batches. The 0.35 rate applies only during QA opening hours (Friday afternoon, Saturday morning, Monday), so arrivals before a Monday join are a handful, not 21. The later cut to “3–4 ahead” is unexplained.
- Under the more common FIFO discipline, Monday chemistry does not start at 08:30 after a 0.5 h handover. Four waiting samples remain; the sensitivity of “1–2 h” is far too small.
- Elapsed hours do not match the stated clocks. Monday 19:00 is about 75.5 h from Friday 15:30, not 72.5 h; Tuesday 10:30 is about 91 h, not 79 h. The P80 and P90 hour bands are likewise mis-mapped (Tuesday 08:00 is ~88.5 h, not 86; Wednesday 12:00 is ~116 h, not 100).
- The stated QA triangular does not have median 1.5 h or an 80th percentile of 3.5 h.
- Temperature-excursion dependence is named and then ignored: scenario probabilities are not updated jointly. The 3–7 day laboratory investigation is omitted from the severe tail, which stops on Wednesday.

The Monday-afternoon central window is only loosely plausible and is reached by treating the 45% priority rule as the base case. The forecast is not a reliable envelope.
