The write-up keeps the required nine-section shape, refuses to code rejects as non-defaults, and the cohort counts sum to 48,000. The estimand, censoring model, prediction time, and implementation nevertheless contradict each other, so the study as specified would not estimate a well-defined early-warning risk.

The primary target is labeled both “default among activated accounts” and a policy-specific counterfactual for all 48,000 starts. Those are different estimands. The displayed quantity conditions on “activation information,” which is post-decision relative to an early prefix, while the population is defined as every started application. Section 2 then restricts the primary analysis to the 21,600 already-labeled activated accounts, and Section 8 retreats to an associational claim. No identification argument is given for transporting that sample to would-be-activated applicants, and the July 2025 policy change means there is not one observed activation policy. Positivity is ignored: many applications are structurally never activated.

The alternative multi-state target mixes transition probabilities with a Fine–Gray model and measures rejection, withdrawal, transfer, and default on one “90-day” clock. Default is defined only on the post-activation clock; the other absorbing outcomes occur before that clock starts.

Cohort accounting misclassifies the mechanisms the design was supposed to separate. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, still-open, and immature activated cases are all called censored. Only the 3,600 immature activated accounts are ordinary delayed observations of `default_90d`. Rejection and withdrawal are competing terminal states under which post-activation default is undefined. Transfer is a loss-to-follow-up and identity problem; linkage for about 60% changes identifiability, not whether the event competed. The table also states that those 3,600 activations occurred on 15 February 2026. That is the extract date, not their activation time. Section 2 drops them from the primary sample while Section 3 treats them as included and censored.

The censoring strategy is not implementable as written. Delayed feed arrival is treated as interval censoring of the default time on `[activation+90, label_available_at]`. The feed delay is when a binary 90-day indicator becomes known; it does not place the event after day 90. The same 3,600 cases are also described as right-censored at extraction. Fine–Gray is then proposed for “activation without default versus activation with default,” with both right and interval censoring. Standard Fine–Gray does not take interval-censored event times, and a sample restricted to activated accounts no longer has non-activation as a competing event. The IPW sensitivity uses `P(activation | prefix)` to “approximate the full set of activated cases.” Weights of that form reweight activated cases toward the applicant pool; they do not recover unlabeled activated accounts. Label maturity would require a weight or inclusion rule based on time since activation and feed lag. Convergence of a weighted model does not test selection ignorability. Permanent partner missingness is called an unknown subset of the labeled 21,600, but that export is defined as labeled; those feeds are a different missingness mechanism, plausibly MNAR, and are never tied to `source_system`.

Prediction time is not stable. The prose picks an early point such as after KYC, then the prefix example sets the prediction point to `Account Activated`. Elapsed-time prefixes are replaced by timestamp differences, with no horizon Δ and no rule that the case must have been under observation for Δ. Short cases are padded with `NA` instead of being declared ineligible for a k-event prefix. Document loops are collapsed to the last event and to `documents_total`, which is the same-day snapshot already flagged as leaky. The October 2025 migration’s duplicate events are never removed, and the rename map invents a lookup table and a direction of renaming.

Several proposed features are not known at an early decision: experience at activation, queue time before activation, `label_available`, and days until the label. Resource experience at the February 2026 extract is look-ahead for 2024–2025 events, but the leakage table marks it Keep and calls it non-temporal. `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, and `collection_status` are correctly dropped, yet their “valid availability” rules describe the post-activation condition under which they become known. `documents_total` is rated low risk and is both allowed for same-day snapshots and repaired with a 24-hour lag. The audit discusses invented `label_30d` / `label_45d` fields and does not police `default_90d` repeated on event rows or `label_available_at`. Future events are excluded only after a prediction point defined as activation, which keeps the selection event itself.

The calendar split is only partly aligned with the process breaks. Training ends on 30 June 2025, so it cannot fit the post-policy regime that validation is supposed to represent, while the text also calls for separate pre/post models. The test window starts 1 October 2025 even though the migration is 15 October. There is no as-of maturity rule. An application started in late 2025 generally cannot have a matured 90-day outcome plus a 7–35 day feed by 15 February 2026; scoring those rows either waits for labels that were unavailable at the decision date or silently keeps the fast, early activations. Subgroup performance by channel, region, amount, partner origin, and policy regime is absent. C-index and interval Brier scores presuppose a survival time origin and censoring scheme that the target never fixes. Prevalence is about 12.5% among labeled activated accounts, so ranking metrics without precision-recall or calibration at an alert rate are poorly matched to an early-warning decision.

The skeleton does not implement the required pipeline. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`. It invents `prefix_view`, `filter_activity_univ`, `EventLog` construction from a frame, and several import paths, and it reads a nonexistent `static_attrs.csv` although the case attributes are on the event file. The cutoff filter uses the extract date, so collections and default events remain. `split_prefix_and_post` iterates a log as a frame and drops only the first activation row in the whole file. The temporal split calls `.dt.to_period.isin` without a frequency and will not run. The leakage audit is a handwritten table, not a check that post-prefix columns are absent, and cohort counts, activation time, terminal state, and label maturity are never computed. The placeholder comment does not state a time origin or a censoring indicator that a later survival model could use.

Taken together, the draft recognizes the business trap and then specifies an analysis that still trains on the convenient labeled activations, cuts prefixes at activation, and applies an incorrectly defined censoring model. That is a failed design with a usable outline, not a defensible study.
