7.5

The recommendation is the right one: do not scale the full redesign nationally, do not roll it back, unbundle it, keep coordinator ownership and merged scheduling for simple low-risk houses, suspend survey skipping and billing-before-activation, restore permit checks, and buy more evidence. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated, all five metric conflicts are addressed, alternatives A–E are judged with real conditions, and the process-mining evidence is used rather than ignored.

The memo is not nearly flawless. High fitness with low precision is explained incorrectly: many observed variants do not force low precision; low precision means the model permits unobserved combinations. The drop in generalization is then misread as week-to-week instability, which that metric does not measure. “Simpson’s paradox” overstates ordinary segment heterogeneity, since the median improved in the aggregate and in most segments. “Demonstrably works” and “captured nearly all of it” treat a bundled pilot as identified component effects; apartments, business, and rural cases also gained median time. The complaint-mix shift is weak evidence of better ownership and is partly the deliberate billing-before-activation design, not merely an execution problem. Section 6 contradicts itself by pausing all geographic expansion of any redesign component and simultaneously extending coordinator ownership to remaining regions. Rural cases are never explicitly assigned. Value-of-information ranking mixes decision value with operational urgency: conformance monitoring is ranked second while described as not changing the strategy, and the absence test that gates the thing they want to scale is ranked below measurements they say do not gate the decision.
