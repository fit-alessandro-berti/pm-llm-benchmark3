3.8

The write-up covers every required section and correctly treats R5/R6 as a real conflict, temporal deviations as warnings, and segregation of duties as external to activity-only models. The formal artifacts and the verdicts do not cohere, and several template semantics are simply wrong.

The process tree puts the payment/close choice after the route choice, so a rejection can still release a payment and an acceptance can close without payment. Clarification, sanctions escalation, and legal opinion are absent from that tree, yet T2, T6, and T7 are marked procedural passes. R11 is assigned to the procedural model in the mapping and then deliberately excluded from it.

The DECLARE dictionary is not the PM4Py shape requested: templates are packed into tuple keys, and unary `exactly_one` is given activity pairs. `exactly_one` is used where the requirement is at most once, so ordinary accepted and rejected cases would fail. `altprecedence(Reject, Approval)` requires rejection before approval. `precedence(Receive Clarification, Request Clarification)` forbids the first request. `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` demands an opinion before every activation, including cases with no escalation. Chain response is described as eventual response. Under these rules T1 cannot pass DECLARE, but the matrix says it does.

The log skeleton is unsatisfiable: `never_together` forbids reject with activate, while `activ_freq` requires each of them exactly once. `{1}` is not “at most once,” and the cap of three clarification cycles is invented. `directly_follows` is an allowed-successor whitelist, but most real direct successors are missing, so the happy path fails. `always_before` from legal opinion to activation has the same over-strong effect as the bad precedence rule. T1 and T4 have the same activity sequence and differ only by resource, yet they receive different DECLARE and log-skeleton verdicts.

T6’s elapsed time from escalation to opinion is 17 hours, inside \(12 + 2 \times 6 = 24\). The matrix calls that a warning, cites \(23 > 24\), and later says the same pair passes. The four-eyes helper inverts `filter_four_eyes_principle` and passes a list as `activity2`. The emergency check implements the policy that was rejected, and it treats a missing authorization attribute as success. Vacuity is misstated: a count of zero violates `exactly_one`; it is not a vacuous pass.
