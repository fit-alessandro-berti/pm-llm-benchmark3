The diagnosis is strong on mechanism separation, but it is not nearly flawless. Several concrete inaccuracies and one implementation error keep it well below a top score.

The timeline table mis-anchors comparisons. The August–September row treats `Assess→Request Info` as 29→20, but 29 is July’s rate; August is not in the DFG fragment. The “2026-10-01 to 2026-11” row mixes September baselines (670 cases, 26% high-complexity, median 41.5 h, senior rate 7) with an October senior jump and a November peak. Those are the right phenomena, attached to the wrong windows. Section 2’s “0.37-point” raw-fitness drop does not match 0.93→0.55 (0.38) or the later, correct −0.375 versus the January–February mean.

The mapped-fitness discussion overreaches. Mapping explains the March collapse, and the post-mapping L1 of 3 is calculated correctly. Calling those two artifacts “exactly” the deviations the old model cannot parse is too strong: mapped fitness still falls from about 0.925 to 0.90. The later claim that this residual “widens with rework and Senior Assessment” fits rework better than senior assessment. Mapped fitness is worst in July (0.86), while the senior edge peaks in October (22 per 100) at a better 0.88. Fitness is also not a frequency statistic. If `Request Info` is allowed, more rework need not reduce fitness; that link is asserted rather than shown. May is also labeled only as control-flow and case mix in the timeline, then correctly reused as a performance change point in §3.4.

The cycle-time split is directionally right and appropriately gives queueing the dominant share, but the hour ranges are not an additive partition. July’s stated bands can sum from 9.5 h to 17 h around a +13.1 h median. November’s service band of 0.5–1.5 h has no service observation beyond July–August. L1 on the six rates is arithmetically correct, including March = 3, May = 40, July = 59, and October = 64, and the omitted-edge caveat is sound. It still double-counts the near-lockstep rework pair (`Assess→Request Info` and `Request Info→Collect Docs` differ by 1 in every listed month) without saying so. Hypothesis rank is undefined, so the best-supported mechanism, March logging, appears as rank 5.

Part A’s baseline deltas match the monthly table. Part B does not implement the observed duplicate. The DFG shows `Open Case → Register` (26 events, about 4% of March cases), but the cleaner drops only consecutive `Open Case` rows and would leave that spurious start in place. The docstring also promises a timestamp check the code never makes. Discovering the reference Petri net from the mapped log makes mapped fitness high by construction and does not reproduce conformance against the old normative model. The one-hit flag is labeled “mixed/gradual,” and `recovered_share_of_jan_loss` is unstable on the baseline months. Section 9 is about 400 words, at the cap.

What holds the score up is the actual attribution: March as persistent semantic/provenance drift, May as supplier rework that composition alone cannot explain, summer delay as queueing with flat service time, July priority as unidentified in the aggregate, October–November as seasonal mix on a thin bench, and December as non-separable. Segmentation, discrimination artifacts, distinct thresholds, seasonality, and multiple-testing control are usable and restrained.
