7.3

The load accounting in §2 is the strongest part of the answer and largely checks out: mixture means, distribution-free range bounds, branch counts (18.09 / 24.30 / 12.85), 2,721 raw minutes versus 2,490 regular and 2,670 overtime minutes, block capacities (including breaks and Farah’s short shift), isolated skill capacities, the U2 renormalization delta to about 2,729, the harsher “add ~7 cases / ~467 minutes” reading, U4’s ~242 diagnostic minutes, and the U1/U3/U5 ratios. Parallel-versus-sequential critical paths on Deepa, Chen, and Emil are computed on the right multipliers, and the recommendation correctly treats base overload and U5 as capacity problems. Assumptions about Poisson arrivals, independent priority and type, uniform mixtures, non-staffed handover, and next-day clearance are labeled, and both required objective types are present.

The policy comparison does not meet the same audit standard. §4’s SLA, delay, CV, and handover bands are judgmental ranges with no derivation from the load model or from the simulator, yet they are precise enough (for example base hybrid P1 at 88–96%) to drive the recommendation. Calling hybrid “optimized” under \(J_B\) overstates what was done: the scalar objectives are never evaluated. \(J_A\) measures CVaR of \(T/60\) and \(J_B\) of \(T/120\), so the two scores are not on a common scale. Specialization is also specified harshly—Deepa, Ada, and Chen are barred from validation, and sequential ES is pinned to Chen only—so part of S’s collapse under U3 is an eligibility choice.

The simulator diverges from the stated policies in ways that would change the comparison. Pooling always parallelizes, with no projected-completion test. Preemption has no net-benefit test. The hybrid reserve stays capped after 90 low-priority minutes even when no P1 is waiting, which contradicts the stated soft release, and it counts clock minutes rather than available minutes. Stress mode is absent. Electrical and Software share one setup family, so E↔S switches cost nothing; that boundary is not stated in §1. Dispatch sees true remaining base work, while §6 treats remaining-time estimates as weak. U1’s proportional shrink of P2/P3 is only in code. Right-censored cases are entered into mean and p90 delay as if they finished at the horizon.

PM4Py use covers the required calls and correctly separates stage queue wait from case-level service/waiting. It still leaves two biases only partly flagged: overnight splits become extra activity instances, and summed parallel intervals can distort case service and waiting. Those gaps, plus the unauditable §4 table and the code/policy mismatches, keep the answer well short of nearly flawless despite a strong capacity argument and a serious monitoring and pilot design.
