5.5

The write-up hits every required section, uses real identifiers, and reaches a defensible Position B. It is not reliable on the mechanics that the comparison is supposed to test. Convergence and divergence are defined as object-graph fan-in and fan-out, then applied to the wrong side of the relation. In this flattening task, convergence means one event is copied into several cases of the chosen type, and divergence means one case is forced to serialize repeated events that belong to different related objects. Those two columns are wrong for most rows.

Item flattening is the clearest miss. I12, I21, and I31 co-occur on e16 and e24; I21 and I31 co-occur on e13; I32 and I41 co-occur on e14, e17, and e27. Duplication is material, not “None to Low.” Each item has one Add Item, one Pack Package, one Dispatch, and one Confirm Delivery, so item-level divergence is absent. I11 and I12 have the same activity sequence; only the package, delivery, and timestamps differ. Calling those “completely disparate execution paths” is false. Item traces also lose Create Order and Close Order, not only invoices and payments.

Package, delivery, and invoice rows repeat the same swap. P3 and P4 mix orders inside one package case; they do not copy pack events across packages. The only multi-package events are e16 and e24, shared by P2 and P3. No event touches more than one delivery or more than one invoice, so delivery and invoice convergence and duplication are none. The invoice row nevertheless reports moderate duplication of e19 and e22 “across V2’s linked orders,” which is order flattening. Invoice timing for V2 is the accurate one: e19 → e22 → e25. Each package has one pack, one dispatch, and one confirm, so package divergence is not moderate. The delivery duplication list also skips e24.

Order is directionally right and still sloppy. Every event e01–e31 touches an order, and the shared ones are e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, and e27. The matrix drops e25 and e27, then calls e16 a packing, billing, or payment step. Copied events do not create DFG edges between O2 and O3. They inflate frequencies and, inside one trace, impose a total order. The real order-divergence pattern is missed: O1 and O3 contain Add Item → Add Item, Pack → Pack, Dispatch → Dispatch, and Confirm → Confirm. O2 and O3 also produce both Record Payment → Confirm Delivery and Confirm Delivery → Record Payment, because e24 sits between e22 and e25.

Several causal claims are not in the log. Nothing shows that D2 waited on O3, or that P3 had a missing item. P2 and P3 are packed at 10:10 and 10:12 and dispatched together at 11:05; the next-day confirmation is D3/P4 via e27. Settlement qualifiers are O2O relations, and M3 is `completes_settlement`, not a second `partially_settles`. A payment-flattened case cannot answer how many installments an order took, because M2 and M3 become unrelated length-1 traces.

The partial-payment and singleton observations are sound. V2 really is Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment, while V1 and V3 pay once. D1–D3 are Dispatch → Confirm Delivery, and M1–M4 are single events. Treating copied events as both evidence and duplication is right, as is refusing to treat the longest trace as the true case notion. Orders are the only type present from create through close.

The sensitivity plan restates the right four checks and then specifies two of them incorrectly. e19 relates to two orders, not more than two; a “more than two orders” filter keeps e19 and removes only e16 and e24. Propagating item and package events up to orders adds nothing: those events already name the orders. The informative propagation is downward, onto items, packages, and deliveries that never see invoicing or payment.

The PM4Py block contains the requested discovery calls and does not pretend a picture settles the semantics. `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is reasonably omitted. `ocel_get_attribute_values` and `get_objects_by_type` are extra invented accessors, and the outline never flattens the four types whose ratings are already wrong.
