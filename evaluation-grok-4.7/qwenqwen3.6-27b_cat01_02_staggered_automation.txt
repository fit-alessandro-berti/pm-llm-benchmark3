The final answer is structurally complete and conceptually alert, but it is not quantitatively reliable enough to rate higher. It correctly refuses a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim, separates availability from intensity, flags staggered nonrandom rollout, small-cluster inference, survey bias, fraud-desk interference, and median-versus-tail divergence, and chooses embedded evaluation rather than immediate expansion. Those are the right scientific instincts.

The empirical core does not hold. Alpha’s post-period escalation average is 11.38, not 11.26. Gamma’s pre-period p90 average is about 85.8, not 87.4, so the reported −2.5 hour change is overstated. The never-treated comparison is garbled (“34.439.0”, “41.440.8”) and substantively wrong: Epsilon’s median ends lower than it starts, and both Delta and Epsilon have larger raw p90 increases than Alpha or Beta. A transparent pre/post contrast against those controls points toward relative improvement, or at least not toward automation-driven tail harm. Section 7 nevertheless calls p90 “likely worse,” using the adopters’ raw increases as the strongest evidence. Escalation is likewise over-attributed: much of the +2–3 point rise is the March fraud-desk jump and the May complexity shift, which also appear in untreated branches, yet that effect is given confidence 70.

The intensity and “adjusted OLS” figures, including p-values and standard errors, are presented as results even though they are not produced by the supplied code. Within branch, fast-path share rises almost lockstep with calendar time, so those dose slopes are not separable from the common summer decline; that collinearity is missed. The reproducible script calls nonexistent `pd.StringIO`, imports matplotlib without using it, builds broken `days // 30` relative-time codes (February and March collide), never uses those indicators, and labels pooled OLS without branch or month effects as a panel model. That regression confounds branch level with adoption.

The rollout write-up is directionally sensible but under the 250-word minimum and internally inconsistent: exposure ramps to 100% while a 10% manual holdout is retained. The p90 guardrail is an absolute change that common case-mix shocks would trip. “Conditionally causal: 1–2 hours above 40% fast-path use” is stronger than Alpha/Beta’s high-intensity months support.

Overall: strong causal checklist, weak and sometimes contradictory quantitative execution.
