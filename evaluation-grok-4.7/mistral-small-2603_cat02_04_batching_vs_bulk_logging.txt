3.5

The write-up keeps the seven required headings and gets a few primary labels into a defensible neighborhood (G3 bulk completion, G6 zero-duration bulk form, G7 quarter-hour rounding, G4/G8 contiguous chains, G5 shared `analyst_pool`). Those hits do not carry the analysis. Core diagnoses, bias directions, temporal facts, and the required code disagree with the data and with each other.

G2 is inverted. Every case starts at 10:00 and completes at 10:08, 10:14, 10:21, and 10:27, with a one-minute recording lag and a single “begin group” button plus separate completion buttons. That is start batching / group-start logging followed by staggered completion. The answer calls it end batching, cites “completes clustered at 10:00, 10:14, 10:21, 10:27,” and treats the begin-group control as counterevidence. Section 2 then says G2 has neither start equality nor end equality. Section 3’s 8–27 minute services only exist if start is 10:00 and the ends differ. The three sections cannot be true together.

G1 ranks common setup above a simultaneous fixture cycle even though start and complete are both identical and RBT-7 is stated to hold four items. Physical concurrency is marked “No,” and section 2 records overlap “None” for four identical [09:00, 09:03] intervals. The fixture sentence is used as counterevidence to multitasking rather than as support for a four-item cycle.

G3’s common 11:30 completion is the right top call, and the folder-submission remark supports a logging batch. The second call invents a shared login for T-C. The only shared account in the prompt is `analyst_pool`. Recorded services are 28, 20, 13, and 6 minutes, not “~12–50.” The four intervals overlap; section 2 says they do not.

G5 is the real concurrency / shared-identity case: completions are all different (so end batching is not available), and all four intervals overlap from 13:12 to 13:20. That is four-wide overlap against a stated cap of three analysts. Section 2 marks overlap “None,” services are given as “~20–30” when the durations are 25, 15, 20, and 10, and section 5 says the shared-account reading survives even if resource IDs are trusted.

G4 and G8 are contiguous, non-overlapping handoffs (4 minutes and 7 minutes). Sequential single-piece flow is a fair temporal label. Physical concurrency is still marked “Yes.” G4’s second explanation is start batching, which equal starts would show and these chains do not. G8’s queue sits from about 15:00–15:08 and service begins at 15:38, beside a 15:00–15:35 safety check and an explicit cross-system clock warning. Section 3 calls G8 waiting, service, utilization, and bottleneck detection accurate, and the safety-check / clock-skew evidence never appears.

G6 is correctly seen as a manual bulk stamp (start = complete = 17:00, recorded_at = 17:01) with arrivals spread from 14:05 to 16:25. The required dual effect is then misstated: waiting is inflated and service is driven to zero, but the table says “both inflated,” and utilization is “overestimate (apparent idle time),” which is an idle-time story and an overestimate story at once.

G7’s top label, coarsening, is right, and correction is correctly required. Service is then called accurate at 15 minutes. Under 15-minute binning, a recorded 15-minute width is the bin, not a measured duration. Arrivals at 14:01, 14:04, 14:07, and 14:11 fall after the stamped start of 14:00, so naive waits are negative. `recorded_at` still advances one minute per case (14:16 through 14:20). Those two facts are stronger than the counterevidence cell that was written, and they are unused.

Bias signs are systematically off for a logging batch. A completion stamp later than true completion lengthens measured service. A group start earlier than true individual starts lengthens measured service and shortens measured wait. The tables repeatedly say the opposite (service underestimated, waiting overestimated) for G2 and G3. Throughput and conformance bias, which the task asks for, are absent. Bottleneck cells mostly repeat “false bottleneck” without a capacity or cycle-time mechanism.

Section 2 is the wrong matrix. The request is hypothesis by signature (start equality, end equality, adjacency, overlap, recording lag, precision, identity reliability, arrival pattern, and what a tighter tolerance does). The delivered table is cluster by claimed observation, with equality, overlap, and tolerance cells wrong for the overlapping clusters, and “sequential” used as a level of start equality.

The Python block transcribes the rows, then cannot perform the analysis it claims. `format_dataframe` is called on an event log after conversion. `discover_batches` is called with `merge_distance` and `min_batch_size` as keywords; in PM4Py those belong in `parameters`, and the return is batch descriptors, not traces with `batch_type`. `recorded_at` and `queue_arrival` stay strings, so the lag and queue-build-up subtractions fail. Overlap is divided by case count, so it is not a rate and can exceed 1. Resource “reliability” is high whenever a cluster has one resource string, which is every cluster, including `analyst_pool`. No cluster-level comparison with PM4Py categories is produced. Suspect rows are retained only because nothing filters them.

Section 5 mostly says “no change.” Trusting IDs would reclassify G5 as one-worker multitasking or impossible load. Treating timestamps as exact versus ± precision would reclassify G7 and would leave G4/G8 adjacency intact only because the gaps are zero at one-second resolution. Several “same pattern, two explanations” pairs are different patterns (sequential versus start batching, shared account versus end batching).

The redesign list names the requested topics in a sensible order and stays generic: no raw-versus-rounded retention, no rejection rule for zero-duration bulk closes, no fixture or group-button event, no badge under the VDI login, no cross-clock offset for the safety system.

Adjudication then freezes the weak calls: G1 is “high confidence, no correction” while labeled as start batching; G8 is settled without the safety hold; G2 is sent to validation because it was misread, not because the group-start button leaves individual start times unidentified. Confidence values of 80–95 sit on those readings with no labeled assumptions.
