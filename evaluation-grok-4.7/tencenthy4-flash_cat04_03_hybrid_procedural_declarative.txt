The delivered write-up stops inside `check_legal_policy` and never produces sections 8–10. The policy split, DECLARE dictionary, and temporal profile are the strongest parts; the executable backbone and the required case-level analysis are not.

Section 1 correctly treats R5 and R6 as incompatible, states two resolutions, adopts strict legal precedence, and names the operational risk of rejecting emergencies that cannot be evidenced in the log. Section 2 covers R1–R12 and usually picks a sensible formalism, but several rows list multiple layers without one authoritative layer. The clarification and escalation placements are disclosed as approximations.

The accepting net does not implement the concurrency it describes. `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` all consume the single token in `p_received`, so they are in conflict. The silent AND-join then needs all three completion places, which is unreachable. The accepted language is empty, so every test trace fails procedural replay. The later choice, escalation gate, and post-activation `Change Bank Details` → `Suspend Vendor` → `Verify Bank Account` loop would be reasonable only after a real AND-split.

The DECLARE model is largely well formed: correct template names, tuple keys, `support` 1.0, and explicit confidence. `altresponse` versus `response`/`precedence`, and `noncoexistence` versus `nonsuccession`, are distinguished. Encoding both `response` and `altresponse` on clarification is redundant, and alternate response still does not by itself stop a final outcome while a request is open. Leaving strict R5 out of DECLARE is the right limitation.

The log skeleton has the six required keys, but `directly_follows: set()` is not an inert omission under PM4Py: that relation is the allowed directly-follows whitelist, so an empty set flags every successive pair. `always_before` also includes `Obtain Legal Opinion` before both `Activate Vendor` and `Close Onboarding` without saying whether this applies only when both activities occur. That is the same global overconstraint the DECLARE section correctly refuses, and it is especially damaging for ordinary closures that never have a legal opinion.

The temporal profile is accurate: the three pairs, hour-to-second conversion, and `zeta = 2.0` match R12, and deviations are correctly warnings. The 34-hour sanctions example is a real zeta breach (`22/6 > 2`).

Section 7’s finished checks for approval cardinality, clarification closure, four-eyes, and bank-detail rework are mostly coherent, including fail-closed behavior when `resource` is missing. The emergency function is syntactically cut off, so the required R6 check is not executable, and `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is only mentioned. There is no T1–T8 matrix, no second verdict for T6, no discussion of vacuity, loops, or support/confidence, and no deployment order. Those omissions, plus a Petri net that accepts nothing, keep the result well below a complete solution.
