7.0

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activation as non-default and chooses an activation-conditional 90-day default estimand with a real as-of, temporal backtest. It is not implementable as written: the primary label logic contradicts itself, and several cohort and code claims are inaccurate.

The central break is in the survival coding. Section 3 says \(y=0\) only after a matured feed, and that suspected-missing feeds stay censored and are handled with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights. In the skeleton, follow-up is capped with `fu = min(cutoff − activation, 90)`, `np.fmin` turns a missing default time into that cap, and `event` is driven only by `Default Recorded`. Any activated case with no default event and `cutoff − activation ≥ 90` — delayed feed or permanently missing feed — is stored as `(time=90, event=False)`. For a horizon of 90 days that record is the same as a confirmed non-default, so the promised weighting cannot undo it. `y90` and `(time, event)` also disagree whenever the feed and `Default Recorded` disagree. The comment states the required fallback (censor at `cutoff − 35` days) and does not implement it.

Section 1 then gives that conditional probability a counterfactual reading: every open case at \(t_k\) is scored as “risk if activated,” while the same section says the decision is essentially recorded in the prefix and that no counterfactual assumptions are required. Alternative B correctly says the prefix does not contain the reviewer’s information, so those two claims conflict. `decision_reason` is also misstated there as an input the reviewer sees; it is recorded at or after the decision.

The cohort table does not partition 48,000. Rejected, withdrawn, transfer, open, and matured-activated rows sum to 44,400, and the remaining 3,600 is listed three times as “part of” or “subset of,” so immature horizon, delayed feed, and permanently missing feed can be double-counted. “None activated after mid-October 2025” is stronger than the stated 7–35 day feed lag: a 7-day feed can label activations after 13 October 2025. That sentence also folds the July policy change into the maturity cutoff. “Open / no decision yet” assigns all 5,100 still-open cases to the pre-decision state. Accepted-but-not-yet-activated cases have a decision and have not started the 90-day clock; the code’s comment allows that state, the table does not.

Prefix rules, the leakage table, and the rolling-origin scheme are otherwise aligned with the process. Two repairs do not work. `documents_total` is replaced by a `Documents Received` activity that is not in the process or the alias map, so the replacement count is identically zero. `risk_score` uses `.last()` rather than the last non-null value, and a back-filled final score on early rows still passes the timestamp cut. At the primary \(k=3\) prefix (Started, KYC, first Credit Review), document-loop and rework features are structurally zero; that subpopulation change is stated for \(k≥4\) and still offered as a general feature list.

Smaller implementation errors: linked-transfer follow-up is specified and then absent, with no linkage field in the CSV; the July 2025 fold is the pre/post-policy test, but `c < POLICY_CHANGE <= c+91d` is false when `c` is the policy date and true for the April fold; adjacent 91-day test windows overlap by about a day; the univariate screen treats AUC above 0.9 as leakage, which also flags a legitimately strong as-of `risk_score`. The Manski interval is the right marginal bound only when \(P(\text{default}\mid A=1)\) is itself the censored-data estimate, which the sensitivity does not say.
