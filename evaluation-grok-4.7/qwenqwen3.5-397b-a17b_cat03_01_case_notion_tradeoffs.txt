5.5

The write-up is complete and often concrete, but it misapplies its own central definitions often enough that the comparison cannot be trusted as stated.

Section 1 correctly treats a copied shared event as both interaction evidence and frequency duplication, and it correctly limits base analysis to direct event–object links. Convergence and divergence are defined in the usual object-centric sense. Those definitions are then inverted in the matrix. Convergence is multiple objects of the chosen case type sharing one event. No event touches more than one delivery, invoice, or payment, so flattening on those types has no convergence and no cross-case copying. The Delivery, Invoice, and Payment rows still rate convergence High or Medium because one delivery, invoice, or payment gathers several orders. That is aggregation seen from the other side, and it is exactly the Order-flattening convergence of e16, e19, e22, e24, and e25. The same rows contradict themselves: Delivery is “High” convergence and “Low” duplication, and Invoice’s “Medium” duplication is explained by copying payment events across orders, which does not happen in an invoice log. Invoice divergence is also undersold. V2’s trace is e19 → e22 → e25, so Record Payment follows Record Payment.

Several timestamped claims run against the log. M3 (e25, 09:00 on Feb 4) cannot be caused by e27 (10:00). M1 (e21, 12:30) precedes e23 (14:00), so it is not a late payment explained by a late delivery. D1 is the earliest dispatch, not a late one. e22 and e25 already list O2 and O3, so the second payment does not appear on those orders only after propagating through V2. Pack events already list the orders, so a package log does not need propagation to see which orders were packed. Down-weighting shared events would not remove O3’s Pack → Pack loop: e13 and e14 are two real events, which is divergence, not a copied event. The payment trace for M1 is one event; there is no second “settlement” event.

The required distortion themes are present, and the order/item/package discussion of e13, O1’s two dispatches, and V2’s two installments is largely right. Effects are still described too loosely: false edges such as Dispatch → Dispatch and Confirm → Confirm are not named, and the 4 pack events versus 6 order-case occurrences are left uncounted until the last check. The lead-object rejection is a defensible position and the counterargument is structured, but its strongest causal example is temporally impossible. The code names the requested routines, then inspects frequencies through nonexistent accessors and never measures duplication, so it does not confront the semantic problem it is supposed to outline.
