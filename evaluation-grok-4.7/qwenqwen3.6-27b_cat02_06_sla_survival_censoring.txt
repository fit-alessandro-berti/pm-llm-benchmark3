4.5

The write-up has the required nine-section shape and shows real awareness of competing risks, immortal time, and non-randomized cohort change. Those strengths do not survive the definitions, the data claims, or the code.

The first estimand is mislabeled. \(S_{\text{resolve}}(72)=P(T_{\text{resolve}}>72\mid T_{\text{resolve}}<\infty)\) is not a survival function, and it is not identified for open or transferred cases. Calling \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{resolve}}(72)\) “tail risk” pools late resolution, withdrawal, rejection, transfer, and cases still open. The prompt asked for those to be separated. An open case aged 92 hours is an observed failure to resolve or receive any disposition by 72 hours; only its eventual resolution time is censored. An open case aged 35 hours is censored before the horizon. That contrast is never drawn, and S32 (transferred at 96 hours) is grouped with open cases.

The encoding table gives `transferred` a competing-risk code and a right-censoring rule at the same time. The “pessimistic” scenario then equates resolution after 72 hours with censoring at 72 hours and event = 1. Those are opposite treatments. S08 and S21 transfer before 72 hours, so their 72-hour resolution status is hypothetical; S32 is already an observed non-resolution by 72. All three transfers have `last_event_gap_h` = 0, so the claim that cohort B transfers carry long gaps is not in the data. The “~5–10%” inflation is not calculated.

Landmarking at 24 and 48 hours is recommended for these data even though activity times are not observed, so state at a landmark cannot be built. The diagram adds AutoCheck→Review and omits observed transitions, including Screen→Resolve, Screen→Escalate, and Rework→Resolve. `current_stage` and the full prefix include Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, and Transfer, so the proposed features leak the outcome the model is meant to predict. `duration_or_age_h` is not a proxy for time to escalation.

The code does not implement the prose. Its “Aalen–Johansen” update multiplies by \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{resolve}}\) rather than by the probability of remaining free of every cause. After an early withdrawal, a later resolution can be counted as if the competing event never removed probability mass. In the “transfer as competing” scenario, transfers are not coded as competing events and never leave the risk set. The breach scenario does not set an event indicator. `activity_key='prefix'` does not split activities; the variant merge would not match prefix strings; and a `format_dataframe` failure is not caught, so the summaries are not reached. Conditional-on-resolution is never computed. No interval accompanies cell sizes of a few cases, while section 7 proposes decile calibration and a 0.35 alert cut on n = 32. Staffing the stage with the highest resolution hazard points capacity at the flow that is already clearing, not at the bottleneck.

Section 9 does list the missing timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, start dates, workload, and rollout compliance. It still does not treat n = 32, or the fact that every terminal gap is zero, as a constraint on the Fine–Gray, IPCW, and calibration plan recommended above.
