The reconstruction is unusually careful and, on direct recomputation, numerically consistent: missing-start windows, negatively coupled wait/service splits, sojourns, completed-case mean (2050/14), WIP areas (2085 and 1340), busy-time ranges, resource utilizations, half-open snapshots, two-hour rates, subperiod queues (about 3.35 vs 0.71, then 3.29 vs 3.73), and the sample-path occupancy identity all check. Open and waiting cases are kept in exposure, service is not confused with sojourn, PM4Py case metrics are correctly limited to a completed two-activity projection and labeled as such, and the bottleneck claim is properly time-varying rather than a single utilization ranking.

Deductions are for smaller but real weaknesses. Station-table wait ranges and service ranges are both written low-to-high even though those endpoints move in opposite directions, so the primary table can be misread as paired scenarios. Censored durations are described as strictly exceeding elapsed time in the formulas and as “≥” in the case table. Two nonempty-queue idle gaps are never identified: C2 from 13:20 to 13:30 before P15, and I2 from 13:45 to 14:00 while P22 is still waiting. The repeated exact five-minute handoff gaps are absorbed by the rounding envelope but not stated. The system-WIP peak intervals are written as 11:30–11:40 and 11:50–12:00 even though, under the answer’s own post-event convention, WIP is 12 at those right endpoints. The code’s “following start” assert is tautological. None of these overturns the stable conclusions, but they keep the write-up short of nearly flawless.
