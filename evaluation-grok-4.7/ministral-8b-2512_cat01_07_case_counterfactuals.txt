3.5

The write-up keeps the required section and table skeleton, and it correctly flags K-337 as a decision-policy change whose main danger is compliance and appeal exposure. That is not enough to carry the analysis. The counterfactual timings, prerequisite orders, and cross-section numbers are internally inconsistent, and three of the four case paths miss the mechanism the intervention actually changes.

The partial order is not a usable feasibility model. With the precedence marks gone, several lines are unreadable, and the readable ones are wrong. Identity is placed before Document Check when no originals are required, which forbids the concurrency the rules allow. Rework is given a new Identity Check the rules do not require. Final compliance is ordered after assessment only “if preliminary work is done,” which reverses the rule: a preliminary screen may start early, and the final review must still follow the latest assessment. K-104’s feasibility check then cites “Rule 12” to let compliance start before assessment; rule 12 only says queue estimates are forecasts. The factual K-104 trace has no rework loop, yet the mechanism text says late escalation caused one.

K-104’s path never shows preliminary S1 or C1 work overlapping identity and document checks. Escalation is placed after Document Check, and compliance appears only once, after assessment. The claimed saving is not a range that survives contact with the snapshots. C1’s Monday queue is about 20 hours, with two urgent cases added by 16:00, so a reserved slot is displacement, not a free start. The derivation adds 16 h and 5 h to get 21 h, while its own endpoints (Wed 18:40 versus Wed 08:00) differ by about 11 h. The table’s 12–24 h band, an end window of Mon 18:00–Wed 18:00, and a “best” case of 12 h under a “base” of 21 h cannot be the same scenario. Best is also smaller than base.

K-219 misses the intervention. Originals arrive Mon 17:30 and D2’s 16:00 queue is about 5 h, so D2 can start Monday evening. The derivation instead starts D2 at Tue 09:30, the same instant as D1, which removes the only reason to switch resources. The table then reports 12–36 h slower, while best/base/worst are 0 / +4 / +36 h. Handwritten standard documents are later costed with D2’s complex band of 8–14 h. The text also says identity and document check cannot overlap under “Rule 2”; when originals are required, identity may still run earlier, and in this trace it had already finished before the originals arrived.

K-337 drops a mandatory control. After Assessment 2 the case is still high-risk and high-value, so C1 must review, or explicitly reaffirm, before M1 decides, and the unresolved discrepancy must be accepted as an approve-with-conditions or a reject. The path goes from Assessment 2 straight to Decision and Notify. The clock arithmetic does not remove the loop either: both the factual slice and the counterfactual are Fri–Wed, 09:10 versus 08:30, a 40-minute gap that is then called one day, three-to-five days, and five days versus four days. There are two information requests, not three. “Supplier responds within 24 h” is irrelevant to a policy that stops waiting for the supplier.

K-411’s table says the cycle is 6–12 h faster and then cites Tue 09:00–Wed 14:30 against the same Tue 09:00–Wed 14:30. The derivation holds the assessment start at Wed 09:30, which leaves the critical path unchanged, and still offers a best case of 6 h saved. Earlier readiness is not distinguished from earlier completion, even though A2 is already queued into Wednesday. Concurrency is justified as “Rule 10,” which is the preliminary-compliance rule, not the no-originals concurrency rule. The pilot then sets the primary outcome as a shorter assessment queue, while the capacity table says that queue grows.

The 100-case discussion names the right themes and is still short of the required 250–400 words (about 180). It treats “doubling C1 capacity” as the consequence of 100 reservations, applies complex service times to standard handwriting, and says supplier delay still occurs under a policy that forbids the second wait. Compliance risk is entered in a column that asks for a queue. K-104 is ranked most robust, with high benefit and manageable scarcity, in the same answer that says scaling it is impractical and that its compliance queue both shrinks and starves other urgent cases. K-411’s principal risk is called low while the same answer’s bottleneck story says the opposite. The synthesis is about 150 words against a 220–320 requirement, and it names K-411 as the least transportable result while the non-transportable object is the ceteris-paribus C1 reservation.

Six of the eight “assumptions” restate rules or published service ranges. The load-bearing uncertainties are missing: whether a Monday C1 hold survives the two urgent cases, D2’s handwriting clarification rate, M1’s availability after an earlier join, and whether the case-lock workaround is real concurrency or only backdated timestamps.
