6.8

The forecast is complete against the required headings and reaches the right decision: phased pilot, no full rollout, and no reading of early averages as success. East is treated differently from North and South, and fairness, learning, tails, and accountability are all in scope. The score is held down by contradictions with the given frequencies, one infeasible-capacity result that is never computed, a pilot that cannot identify the main losers, and a PM4Py sketch that would mis-measure the quantities the memo relies on.

**Directional claims conflict with the management table.** Today, caseworker → embedded tech is 36 per 100 cases and the return to the same caseworker is 31. The proposal’s own estimate raises those edges to 41 and 36, and raises approval edges from 42/39 to 49/43. Mechanism chain 1 still says specialist edge counts can fall. The optimistic scenario then says local tech loops drop from about 31 to under 10 per 100, while the same cell says caseworker → shared service → the same caseworker subcontracting edges dominate. In PM4Py, subcontracting is that A→B→A return, so those two claims cannot both be true, and neither matches the 36 return frequency in the estimate. Summing the published rows, listed handovers per 100 cases rise by roughly the mid-teens. “Nominal handover reduction” is a change in who sits on the edge, not a reduction in handover count.

**The tightest capacity check is missing.** Legal is already at 93% and Risk at 91%, while legal/risk handovers go from 9 to 12 per 100 (+33% arrivals) and the shared-service plan adds triage and difficult quality-check load. With unchanged headcount, utilization scales to about 1.24 and 1.21. That queue is unstable, so the management median of 10.5h versus today’s 9.6h is not a credible steady state. The same gap applies to supervision: one of three supervisors is removed while approval flow rises from 42 to 49 per 100, about 1.75× load on the remaining approvers. The memo gestures at saturation and sets a legal/risk guardrail of “not sustained above 92–95%,” but Legal is already at 93%, so the guardrail is failed before the pilot starts. The optimistic line that legal/risk waits stay stable “via better batching” is not available under the stated staffing.

**The pilot cannot answer the decision it is asked to gate.** Phase A treats East plus half of South and holds North as an embedded control. East is the overloaded outlier (E_Tech 82%, median 8.6d, p90 22.8d, rework 11.5%), so an East-versus-North difference-in-differences comparison does not have a parallel baseline. A green result on East is also the result most favored by the status quo; it does not estimate the treatment effect for North, where local expertise and CSAT are strongest. The sketch never says whether N_Tech is left in North or pulled into the pool. Pulling N_Tech contaminates the control; leaving N_Tech out means the pilot pool is not the proposed three-team service. Learning loss is described as quarterly, the window is 6–8 weeks, and skill-use or over-referral is not a hard rollback trigger, so Phase B can be authorized before the risk the memo itself flags is observable. The illustrative tech-shared-service p90 cap at 1.25× the 4.0h median (5.0h) is far tighter than the heavy tails already in the baseline (East p90 cycle time 22.8d versus median 8.6d) and would churn the pilot on ordinary variance.

**Section 7 would not support the comparisons in sections 1–3.** `pm4py.format_dataframe` does not take `resource_key`, so `prepare()` fails as written. Team filtering is `level="event"`, which drops shared-service events that are not tagged with North/South/East and truncates the handover, subcontracting, and DFG paths the pilot is supposed to compare. `handover_waits` uses the gap between consecutive timestamps on a resource change, which mixes the previous activity’s service time into “wait,” unlike `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` (the latter is only commented out). Case KPIs take `team` and complexity with `"first"` without sorting by time, so cross-team cases (today 6 per 100) are attributed arbitrarily. `weekly_ss_pressure` reads a `ts` column that `handover_waits` never creates. `guardrails()` expects raw `wait_h` rows, while `compare_segment` stores `wait_summary` output (`n`, `p50`, `p90` only). Role discovery is treated as an accountability measure; it clusters resources by activity mix and does not show who owns a waiting case. `reassignment_count`, `handover_reason`, and a named owner during the wait are the better log proxies, and those are only partly wired in.

**What still holds.** The central and adverse patterns (easy cases faster, high-complexity and legal/risk tails worse, East gaining while North/South and regional segments lose, rework lagging the cutover) follow from the baseline. Section 4 correctly separates tail latency, complexity shift, local-convention loss, fairness, skill atrophy, ownership diffusion, false resilience, utilization mixed with triage/QC, and delayed rework. The recommendation’s modifications — staff to tails, keep a named local owner, keep tier-0 local tech, delay broader legal/risk detection, publish segmented views — are the right conditions. The artifact is a serious operational argument with those corrections still required before the numbers or the pilot gate can be trusted.
