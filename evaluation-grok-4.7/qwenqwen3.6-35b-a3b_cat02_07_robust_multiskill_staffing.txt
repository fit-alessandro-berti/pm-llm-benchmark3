3.5

The write-up follows the nine-heading format and gives a defensible qualitative preference for a reserved-capacity hybrid over pure specialization or unrestricted pooling. That preference is not earned by the analysis that is supposed to support it.

The service model is mis-specified. Complex-tail cases are separate ranges, not an ordinary draw multiplied by 1.25; that factor is the meaning of a resource speed multiplier. Offered load is then treated as about 28 minutes per case and about 49% aggregate utilization. Every case consumes Intake, one or two diagnostic branches, and Validation, and parallel ME/ES work consumes both branches even when flow time follows the critical path. On the stated ranges and mix, diagnostic work alone is on the order of the full staffed day, before multipliers above 1.0, handovers, setups, and the fact that only Chen, Emil, and Farah can do software, with Farah gone at noon and Emil absent until noon. The 49% figure reverses the capacity picture. The later claim that doubling complex-tail probabilities raises mean service about 12–18% does not follow from the 1.25 model used in Section 1.

The time-block/skill audit never appears. Shift coverage is misstated: Chen is on from 08:00 and Deepa from 10:00; Emil, not Chen or Deepa, starts at 12:00. U2 renormalization, U4 tail inflation, and the U5 outage are described, not calculated. The comparison table is one collapsed row per policy, not policy-by-scenario, and its SLA and utilization figures are not traceable to Section 2. A claimed 15% mean-delay advantage for full pooling is not in that table.

The Python does not implement the process. Shifts, breaks, queues, Intake, Validation, preemption, and parallel versus sequential branches are absent; ME/ES lookups are not in the service table; U1/U2 leave the eligibility function undefined; U2, U4, and U5 are not encoded; policies B and C pick the same resource; “delay” is service time; timestamps for the stated blocks are invalid; and each case is a single event, so a handover network cannot exist. The PM4Py calls use nonexistent variant arguments, read `time:remaining`, and label case-level service/waiting output as an activity distribution. Omitted mechanisms are not identified, so this is not a valid scaffold. Section 9 is the most usable part, but it cannot rescue an unsupported robustness claim.
