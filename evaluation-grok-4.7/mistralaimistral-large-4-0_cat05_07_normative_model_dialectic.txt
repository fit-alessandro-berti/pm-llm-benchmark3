6.4

The case analysis is structured, traceable, and genuinely two-sided, but the required formal revision is invalid and several authority and semantics calls are asserted past the evidence.

Sections 1–5 are the stronger half. The burden split (rule in force, scope, breach, overlay, reliable linkage) is the right one, and it is mostly applied: SUP-701 is accepted, SUP-703 and SUP-708 are treated as real control failures, SUP-705 and SUP-714 are not saved by informal necessity or “approval was imminent,” SUP-706 stays indeterminate because the CRM link is mutable and late, and SUP-709 is left on the scope ambiguity rather than forced into noncompliance. Prosecution does admit model and logging defects; defense does admit that ineligible, post-expiry, sanctions-after-activation, and segregation failures are not cured by an obsolete diagram. The matrix has every case and a usable falsifier column. Arithmetic on the 312 auto-approvals is correct.

The authority treatment is less finished than the classification table suggests. Section 1 correctly leaves PD-3’s power to weaken CS-9 disputed, including the observer’s later disclaimer. Section 5 then marks SUP-702 a high-confidence justified exception because “PD-3 valid,” without deciding what the disclaimer does to that validity. FD-7 is a written, self-expiring directive; PD-3 is an unincorporated pilot. Those are not the same kind of authority, and equal high confidence is unwarranted. PD-3 also required automated master-data validation before activation. That condition is never checked for SUP-702, SUP-703, or SUP-714, and it disappears from the revised rules.

Case-level precision slips where the conclusion is otherwise fair. SUP-708’s trace also puts Manual_Procurement_Approval after activation; the matrix and judgment discuss only the sanctions inversion. SUP-713 is labeled COMPLIANT while the dominant reason calls it a dictionary defect, and the mapping was approved a week after the event. SUP-714’s matrix risk is medium/high and its judgment severity is high. SUP-704’s retrospective quality and approval fall on the next business day after a Saturday activation, which is outside FD-7’s expiry, and that timing is not examined. SUP-706 is undated, yet N4 is applied as if the migration window were established. “spend 20k” is used for a EUR 20,000 cap and an EUR 18,000 actual.

The revised architecture has the right layers: POWL for order, data checks for eligibility, amount, and time, separate identity and conflict rules, and overlay governance with expiry. Two control-design errors remain. Baseline CS-9 checks are written as if they always apply, with no override relation to PD-3 or FD-7. Auto and emergency routes turn “quality may follow provisional activation” into “quality must follow activation,” so a stricter, fully pre-activation order would fail the model.

The Python block does not construct a usable PM4Py POWL. LOOP is given three children and described as if exit were a child; PM4Py loop is exactly two children, do and redo, with exit implicit. The same Transition objects for sanctions, quality, approval, and activation are placed in multiple XOR branches. POWL nodes are single-parent, so this model is structurally illegal and will not build. The Request_More_Info approximation is also in the wrong place: it is confined to before route selection, while SUP-707’s requests occur after Sanctions_Check and before Quality_Review. The exemplar desirable trace does not fit the model offered to legalize it. The event schema itself is strong: stable notification ids, eligibility snapshots, canonical actors, delegation, overlay ids, and separate event and record times.

Net: dialectical coverage and evidence discipline are good enough to carry the answer well above a partial response. The normative artifact that the task makes central is not executable, and the authority judgment overclaims relative to the disputes the answer itself identified.
