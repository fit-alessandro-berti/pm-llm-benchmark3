7.4

The answer is structurally complete and conceptually strong, but it is not nearly flawless. Several timing claims are loosely anchored, and one bottleneck diagnosis is wrong.

Format compliance is excellent. All eight required sections, table schemas, labeled derivation paragraphs, and the 100-case discussion are present and within the stated length bounds. The partial order correctly encodes register-then-triage, originals-before-document-check, both checks before assessment, request-more-info rework, final compliance only after the latest assessment, and four-eyes separation. K-337 is correctly treated as a decision-policy change rather than a pure time cut: compliance remains, the unresolved discrepancy cannot be ignored, and the intervention is ranked last and called the most dangerous. Readiness is distinguished from final completion for K-219 and K-411. Queue snapshots are treated as forecasts. The pilot rows and assumption labels match the specification.

The main analytical error is K-337’s time range. Assessment 2 ends Friday 10:30, and the second supplier wait runs through Monday morning before another document check, assessment, compliance review, and a Wednesday decision. That weekend wait is removed by the intervention itself. Thursday’s compliance queue is about five hours and clearing, so C1 cannot plausibly push the outcome all the way back to Wednesday and produce “near-zero” savings. A Friday-evening decision saves on the order of four to five days; a Monday decision saves about two. “1–3 days, or near zero if C1 binds” therefore misidentifies the binding constraint. Section 3’s K-337 propagation never names that weekend, while the best case (“several days”) conflicts with the table.

K-219’s “0–8 hours shorter” clean branch is also under-derived. Originals arrive at 17:30, when D2’s forecast queue is far shorter than D1’s, so an earlier Monday-night start can move an accurate check to early Tuesday and can move A2 from 20:00 into Tuesday afternoon. The factual decision is Wednesday 08:30, after a Tuesday 22:30 assessment. The answer never tests whether that gap is an M1 calendar gate. If it is, savings can be about zero; if M1 can sign Tuesday, savings can exceed eight hours. Slower D2 service is mentioned, but the table puts every lengthening into the clarification branch.

Smaller defects accumulate. K-104’s path arrow places preliminary S1/C1 work after both checks while the same cell says the work occurs during verification. No counterfactual path carries approximate clock times, so the 0–12h, 0–8h, and 0–6h bands cannot be audited against service intervals. K-411 is called a routing change even though I2 and D2 stay the same; the change is task creation and removal of a lock. The closing partial-order line states final assessment before compliance without limiting it to high-risk or high-value cases. Rule 6 lets assessment or compliance issue Request More Info, but the K-219 branch has D2 issue it directly. At 100 cases, one certified reviewer cannot honor 100 provisional slots, yet that capacity arithmetic is only implied. Naming K-411 the least transportable local result is defensible because of synchronized assessment arrivals, but K-104’s ceteris-paribus reservation breaks more sharply once the same scarce reviewer is reserved at scale.

Constraint handling, interference awareness, and the policy-versus-time distinction are good enough to keep this well above a merely adequate answer. The unsupported near-zero K-337 scenario and the unreconciled cycle-time ranges keep it out of the high band.
