The diagnosis is the strongest part: it uses the frequency DFG, performance times, fitness versus fitting traces, alignment moves, rework rates, segment outcomes, and resource limits, and it treats the fast path as locally attractive and globally unsafe only for some claims. The three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms (segment gates, repair cells, evidence front door), each with a sacrifice, and the table correctly refuses exact ROI and strict dominance.

The evidence handling is not clean enough for a higher score. Redesign A targets “the 22% high-value population, especially” the 14% and 21% high-value segments. Those two shares are 35%; 22% is the unclear/missing-evidence cohort, which the same answer later uses correctly. That is a decision-relevant mis-sizing of the lane the redesign is built for. The conformance sentence pairs the three alignment moves with R1, R2, and R3 “respectively,” which does not match the rules: fast approval before evidence is not R1, reopen-after-close is not R2, and dispatch without risk review is not R3. R1 also allows manual eligibility instead of fraud/risk review, and R2 governs dispatch, not fast approval itself. Fitness 0.91 with 68% fitting traces is then glossed as a substantial minority of event behavior failing. That reads the 32% imperfect traces as if they were the event-level deviation; token fitness says the nonconforming share of behavior is much smaller.

Two further inferences are tighter than the artifacts. The 24-hour service-credit exposure is stated for Premium low-risk accessories and is then applied to Premium customers as a class, including as an aging rule and a go threshold. The 22-hour mean on Manual eligibility review → Repair quote is treated as part-price confirmation holding the case; that edge is only the directly-follows time, while the resource note does not quantify the price wait, and Repair quote → Customer approval (mean 30 hours) is a separate wait. Calling the 96-hour mean dispatch-to-complaint edge the “tail” also blurs mean and p90 (180 hours). Mid-risk devices are named in the process context and then disappear from every lane.

The experiment plan has the right controls and downstream checks, but the first pilot holds high-value routing fixed and therefore does not test the contradiction the diagnosis centers on: R1/R2 exposure and 18–22% fast-path complaints. The stop rule, a “small low-single-digit” rise in complaints, is too loose to falsify a pilot whose current fast-versus-manual gap is already about three points.
