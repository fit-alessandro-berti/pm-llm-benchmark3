The answer meets the required seven-section structure and is strongly grounded in the given segments, DFG paths, batching and transport evidence, and SP1/SP2/Q1/R1. It keeps a fulfillment gate-to-gate boundary, separates energy, transport, scrap, packaging, rework, and obsolescence, and refuses a blanket ban on air or dedicated vans. The twelve options each name a path or segment, a mechanism, service/quality/resilience effects, and monitoring evidence. The recommendation is a coherent conditional package, and rebound coverage includes batching, transport, packaging, rework, obsolescence, and customer behavior. Validation is specific: shadow mode, phased pilots, segment and path checks, and exception governance.

The effects table is the main defect. Its legend uses the same marker for lower, higher, and neutral, and many cells lose any readable direction. “4–6x higher” and “35–55%” sit in an effect column without a verb, so a reduction can be read as an increase. That weakens a required deliverable even though Sections 3 and 6 state many directions in prose.

Other gaps are smaller but real. CNC_B is described as slower only via queue, not as the faster machine in the evidence. Cumulative slack from batch wait, heat-treatment wait, and slower routing on the same made-to-order case is not stress-tested. Made-to-order service-contract orders, with higher energy, higher transport, and tighter cycle times than regular made-to-order, never get an explicit cap. Cancellation waste is monitored more than designed for. Some indicators depend on attributes the log does not clearly contain.

Substance is high and constraints are respected; the broken effect directions and the unstacked delay logic keep it short of an excellent score.
