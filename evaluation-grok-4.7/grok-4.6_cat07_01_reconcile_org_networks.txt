The answer meets every structural requirement and is unusually well calibrated on role ambiguity, segregation of duties, bots/vendors, and the absence window. It still has validity errors large enough to block a top score.

The central flaw is an unreconciled contradiction. Section 1 treats 960 events over 905 cases as about 1.06 events per case and a single short intervention. The same section, H2, and the executive summary then say she often returns and remains accountable after specialists. Those cannot both be straightforward facts: 960 − 905 leaves only 55 surplus events, while the Mira–Omar and Mira–Priya subcontracting frequencies alone are 126 and 98. “Typically once” and “often takes it back” are stated side by side with no arithmetic check. The summary then resolves the network paradox as a broker/desk signature and tells a process owner she often takes work back. That is stronger than the event totals support and flattens the multi-hypothesis body.

The same cross-foot was missed for handovers. Listed inbound edges to Mira sum to about 1580 and listed outbound edges to about 1640, against 960 events. Under ordinary direct-succession semantics each of her events yields at most one incoming and one outgoing handover. Those edge frequencies cannot all be raw counts. The interpretation still treats 920, 350, 210, and so on as literal volume. That should have been a primary data-quality limit on betweenness, “dispatcher,” and “key person,” not only the softer points about “Assign Specialist,” lifecycle, and a shared login.

A second consistency check is also absent. Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules cover essentially the same population (4100 vs 4090 cases) but show exception shares of 0.19 and 0.31. If `exception_flag` is a stable case attribute, those shares should nearly match. Later use of 0.48, 0.19, and “many flagged cases never reach her” inherits that unresolved metric problem.

Smaller reasoning and wording errors add up. “905 cases / 960 events ≈ 1.06” writes the ratio backwards; 960/905 is the events-per-case figure. Low working-together with Alice and Bruno is called odd for a formal desk, but a Bot_Rules split that sends exception cases to Mira and ordinary cases to the leads predicts exactly that separation, so it is weak limiting evidence against H1. “Juniors receive more Mira rework” is not what the edges show: Carla and Diego receive 350 and 330, Elise and Farid 290 and 280. The 42- and 51-minute Mira→specialist gaps are read as waiting for capacity, although they match specialist service times (37 and 51 minutes) and `lifecycle:transition` is unused, so the gap may contain service rather than queue. “Upper bounds on recorded routing” is then immediately qualified by possible unlogged advice, which pulls the other way.

What keeps the score here is the rest of the substance. Does/does-not implications are mostly right. Six hypotheses each have a mechanism, support, limits, and log predictions. The similarity trap that “Request Missing Information” could falsely cluster Mira with Elise and Farid is expert-level. Risks stay proportional: main-flow bottleneck low, exception key-person risk high, discretion framed as control design rather than misconduct. The PM4Py follow-ups are discriminating, especially absence-window handover recomputation and the wait-versus-service split. Recommendations staff a deputy, separate detection from destination, and refuse both removal and new approval rights until the function is replaced. The executive summary is about 168 words and inside the required range.
