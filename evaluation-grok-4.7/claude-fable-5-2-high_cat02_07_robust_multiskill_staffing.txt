7.8

The capacity arithmetic, objective definitions, and policy-level trade-off are unusually careful and mostly auditable. The submission still misses a near-flawless mark because the reproducible script does not parse, one shock is specified more severely than the prompt’s wording supports, and a few dispatch claims do not match the code that is supposed to justify the tables.

The deterministic core holds. Mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74; U4 diagnostics 29.60 / 37.14 / 44.50) are right. Day volume at those means is 2,721 base minutes against 2,490 net minutes. The lower/upper mixture bounds of 2,065 and 3,376, the block loads, U1–U5 fluid backlogs, the 12:00 P2 count of 13.6/22.4, and the empty-system load ratios 1.02, 1.11, 1.19, 1.24, 1.29, and 1.57 all recompute. Parallel-versus-sequential ME/ES critical paths, primary-minute gaps, and the evening structural hole are coherent, and the lower-bound evening loads of 1.41 and 1.21 correctly survive the uniform-mean assumption. Assumptions A1–A16 are labeled, including the direction of error, and O1 (weighted SLA shortfall) plus minimax regret is a real robustness objective. O1 cells match the printed attainments within rounding, including C’s maximum regret of 6 coming from U3 against the idle-reserve ablation.

The lead claim is stronger than those bounds. At the low end of every range, daily load is 0.83, so “the roster cannot carry base demand under any routing policy” is an estimate at the mixture mean, not a bound. Same-day P2 failure after 16:00 is the robust part; the prose itself switches from “after about 12:30” to a count that starts at 12:00.

U2 is the weakest scenario definition. The prompt doubles E and ES demand and then renormalizes proportions, which reads as a constant-volume mix shift. The analysis instead doubles E and ES counts, lifting 12:00–16:00 arrivals from 17 to 24 and adding about 467 base minutes. That reading is disclosed as the harsher one, but the milder reading is never quantified, so the U2 column is not clearly the specified shock.

Policy C is operationally specified, and the C versus C_edd contrast is the right ablation. The write-up then calls C_edd “oldest work first” and later “EDD ordering.” In the code, turning off salvage only stops negative-slack cases from falling into band 5; slack under 60 minutes, including already-missed cases, still outranks ownership and primary-skill work. That is not global earliest-due-date. Policy B is also speed-blind while C sorts on multiplier, so the specialization-versus-pooling contrast confounds eligibility with speed. The tables actually show the ordering rule moving P2/P3 far more than routing does, and under U5, B’s O1 (396) beats C_edd (416). “Neither A nor B is preferable” is tighter than that evidence. The 20-minute and queue-length overflow triggers in the policy table are also easy to misread against the sibling hold, which the code releases only when slack falls below 60 minutes.

Section 8 is a genuine discrete-event model with the required tables, handover, setup, team-cross, coordination, once-only preemption with a 10% remaining-time penalty, common random numbers, an interval log, and a correct warning that PM4Py `@@service_time` / `@@waiting_time` / `@@sojourn_time` miss pre-Intake delay and double-count parallel branches. As pasted, it is not runnable: the `met_{p}` assignment in `run_metrics` is missing a closing parenthesis. The PM4Py demo also skips the segment-merge the text says is required before `get_service_time`. Sections 4 and 5 therefore report 40-seed figures from a script that does not execute. Cross-checks make wholesale invention unlikely, but the numerical comparison is not reproducible from the submission. The overtime gate’s use of realized duration is an disclosed oracle and makes absolute SLA figures optimistic. Floating breaks in the simulator versus fixed break windows in the load table are a smaller inconsistency.

The recommendation itself is the right shape: hybrid routing, ordering switched by objective, interruptible rather than idle P1 reserve, reversible retiming, and extra electrical/software coverage, with explicit admission that two added shifts still leave people above the 90% fairness rule and that U4/U5 P1 loss is partly touch time. The pilot (trace replay, shadow mode, day-level switchback, P1 non-inferiority stop) matches the claim strength. Those merits support a high score; the broken script, the U2 specification, and the over-strong routing claims keep it below the top band.
