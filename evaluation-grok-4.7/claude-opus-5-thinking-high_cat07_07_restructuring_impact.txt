7.5

The forecast is unusually rigorous and covers every required heading, East’s asymmetric position, fairness, learning, tail risk, and a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The handover totals (343 → 359; 588 → 722 hours per 100 cases) and the main M/M/c waits (about 0.7, 1.5, 2.7, and 6.0 service times; Legal/Risk load about 2.45; approval load ×1.75) check out. Those strengths do not make the write-up nearly flawless.

The central “2–5 point headroom” claim is tighter than the author’s own model. Load-weighted status-quo wait is about 3.1 service times, and pooled M/M/3 crosses that near 91% utilization, not 90%. South’s crossover is about 88%, North’s about 89%. At 87% both are still better off, so the recommendation’s “below ~87% for North and South” line is conservative past the calculation. Organization-wide anchors are repeatedly South’s figures (7.4 d, 18.2 d, 9.0% rework, 7.2% SLA). Volume-weighted means of those medians are about 7.5 d, 18.6 d, 9.3%, and 7.6%. The 12% rework guardrail is called the midpoint of 9% and 18%; that midpoint is 13.5%. “Broadcast to every case” contradicts the stated 49 per 100. The ≈30% waiting-share baseline is invented and sits badly next to the claim that queueing, batching, and calendar time are most of the multi-day cycle time. The 20–30% high-complexity share used for power is not in the data. Erlang results are never tied back to the observed 3.2 h specialist wait, and Legal/Risk ρ>1 is stated as fact while assuming the 9→12 referral jump scales the whole job.

The pilot contradicts itself: steps are 10 weeks, while post-measurement is specified as 12–16 weeks (minimum 8) after a 4-week washout. A 4-week washout inside a 10-week step leaves an underpowered window.

The PM4Py sketch would not reliably test the essay’s own claims. `post_path` is unused. Queue WIP uses min and max timestamps of the queue activity, which collapses on a single-timestamp log — the failure mode the pilot itself flags. Arrival-cohort “queue wait” is time from case start to the activity, not handover wait. `reassignment_count`, outcome, and rework are taken from the first event, which wipes the end-state measures the text treats as decisive. The beta comment reverses PM4Py handover semantics (β=0 is direct succession; β=1 does not weight direct handovers more). `discover_subcontracting_network` has no `n` argument. Freeman centralization is not normalized on the standard denominator. SLA day cutoffs are fabricated. Variant keys are passed through with the usual string-versus-tuple footgun.

Conceptually this is a strong, decision-useful forecast. The numeric overprecision, a few internal contradictions, and an analysis sketch that mishandles the stability and ownership tests keep it well short of nearly flawless.
