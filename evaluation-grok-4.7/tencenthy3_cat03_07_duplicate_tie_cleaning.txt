6.5

The design is structurally complete and mostly conservative: the six duplicate classes are separated, e05 and e14 are kept, e03/e04 and e11/e16 are the only strong merges, abstraction events and the carrier-accept collision are retained, original timestamps are stored, ingestion time is not the business order, and a total order is not treated as physical truth. The required PM4Py calls, audit fields, and listed failure modes are present.

The ordering policy is not implemented. At 10:00 the prose requires both picks before e06 and e07 after e06, with e03 vs e05 left concurrent. The `sort_key` ranks every 10:00 event at 0 and then sorts by `source_seq`, which yields e07 (30), e03 (100), e06 (110), e05 (552): the ERP summary precedes the picks and the I2 pick follows packing. That contradicts the lifecycle rules the same answer calls semantic.

The same rules are applied inconsistently in prose. PSP `source_seq` 401 then 402 orders e13 before e14, and the scanner sequence orders the I1 scan before e05, yet both pairs are called equally concurrent. e08 < e09 is called mandatory, although the given facts only force e09 before e10. The “2-second gap” from synthetic microseconds is the wrong magnitude.

The verified-pair helper drops e04 and e16 without copying `source_event_key` onto the canonical row, so the claimed provenance is not actually retained. The pre-check assert compares array-valued hashes and would fail, and it does not group candidates. `have_common_object=True` would merge e05 with e03/e04 and e13 with e14 and e15 because they share O1/P1 or O1/V1; “may” and a 10–13 count understate a determinate collapse to 10 events.

Sensitivity counts are off (e06 has four objects, so raw E2O is 44, not ~45). “Issue Invoice → Authorize Payment” is not doubled in a total-order DFG. Lifecycle and performance effects, and OC-DFG frequencies per dataset, are barely covered. Section 1 also says the first two terminals merge, while the first terminal drops.
