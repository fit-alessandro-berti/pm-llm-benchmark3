8.5

The analysis is numerically reliable and methodologically strong: time origin, the 72-hour threshold, competing events, administrative censoring, and already-observed breaches are separated correctly, and the worked bounds match the table. Timely resolutions are 9/13 completed resolutions; known internal non-resolution is 18/32 to 23/32; no terminal disposition by 72 is 9/32 to 14/32; cohort partial-identification intervals (A 9/15–11/15, B 9/17–12/17) and the global-resolution bound 16/32–23/32 are arithmetically right. AutoCheck exposure (2/15 vs 13/17), P1 imbalance (3 vs 5), and the fact that S08, S21, and S32 are all escalated P1 cases are all accurate. Aalen–Johansen is appropriately preferred to a resolved-only rate and to Kaplan–Meier with competitors censored; immortal time, prefix leakage, and structural `last_event_gap_h = 0` on terminal rows are handled correctly. Observed transitions in the state list are real, and no timestamps are invented.

The score is held down by concrete defects. Defining \(T=\min(T_R,T_W,T_J,T_X)\) and then writing \(P(T_R>72)=1-F_R(72)\) overloads latent-time notation with the factual subdistribution; later prose rescues the intended meaning, but the equality is not true for latent times. In the code, `identification_summary` always labels counts as no-resolution and timely success, including when it is applied to the no-terminal estimand. The Aalen–Johansen print calls \(1-F_R\) “P(no resolution)” under transfer-censoring and transfer-as-resolution, where that functional is no longer the primary estimand. Full-prefix features, including terminal tokens and final variants, are built with no landmark truncation despite the leakage discussion. The state list is missing explicit arrows, so the “diagram” is only an implied adjacency list. `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` is called on a list and only an `AttributeError` falls back, so a signature mismatch aborts the required control-flow step. These are implementation and notation faults, not wrong case counts or a biased completed-case analysis.
