3.5

The write-up follows the required outline and the process tree is a recognizable block-structured sketch, but the answer contradicts itself, misstates its own artifacts, and presents an invalid POWL model as the more faithful one.

The process tree is only partly right. Initial parallelism, containment before planning, analysis before design, the risk XOR, optional supplier skip, and a do/redo trial loop are the right block shape for a conservative under-approximation. That is undone by concrete construction errors and false claims. `Operator.LOOP` takes exactly two children; the third `tau` exit is not process-tree semantics. The same `tau` object is then reused under the supplier XOR, so one node has two parents. The prose says supplier consultation cannot start until design finishes. In the code, design and supplier are parallel after analysis, so that claim is false. The real extra constraint is that supplier and the whole approval route must finish before the trial. “All other requirements are exact” is therefore false. Parent links are also left implicit despite the required constructor discipline.

The POWL model does not implement the narrative. A strict partial order executes every node once and cannot contain a cycle, an XOR, an optional skip, or a repeat-until. The code never uses `OperatorPOWL`, calls `add_edge` instead of `order.add_edge`, and leaves `tau` disconnected. `Inspect → Adjust → Execute → Inspect` is a cycle, and the comment that Adjust is “taken when inspection fails” treats a precedence edge as a choice. Because Supervisor, Quality, Regulatory, and Consult Supplier are all ordered before release, every one of them is mandatory. Section 1 says missing edges make the risk branches exclusive. Missing edges mean concurrency. Section 6 then calls this an under-specification in which all three reviews “could” occur. They must occur, so valid low-risk, high-risk, and no-supplier traces are lost and the illegal combined route is kept. Claiming the loop, supplier option, and release join are exact is not accurate. Preferring this POWL because it “keeps all intended interleavings” is the opposite of what the code does.

The witness table does not match the required five allowed traces followed by three forbidden ones: T1–T4 are marked allowed and T5–T8 forbidden. T4 takes both risk routes, so its intended verdict cannot be allow, and the tree’s XOR forbids it as well. T3 puts Consult Supplier and Supervisor Review after Inspect Trial; the tree forbids that, but the verdict says allow. T6 is an ellipsis, not a trace. Every POWL “allow” is inconsistent with a model that also demands the missing reviews, supplier consultation, and Adjust Parameters.

The dependency table and the validation checklist show that the intended control flow was broadly understood. They do not repair the reversed partial-order reasoning, the illegal cycle, the mislabeled witnesses, or a self-critique that still describes mandatory reviews as merely permitted by data outside the control flow.
