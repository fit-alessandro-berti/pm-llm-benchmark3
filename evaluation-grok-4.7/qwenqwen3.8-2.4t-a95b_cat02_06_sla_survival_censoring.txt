The analysis is strong: estimands are separated from estimators, open cases are retained, competing risks are not casually treated as non-informative censoring, and the case counts used to show completed-case bias check out (13 resolved, 4/13 after 72h, 18 known non-resolutions, 9 successes, 5 still unknown). Transfer is handled under three explicit assumptions, immortal time for escalation is identified, and the primary Aalen–Johansen recommendation fits the small competing-risk sample.

The main defects are statistical and representational. `P(T_R > 72)` is equated with `1 - CIF_R(72)`, which are different once withdrawal, rejection, or transfer can occur; later sections partly repair this, but the definition itself blurs the latent and crude targets. In the code, `km_net_resolve_72` stores Kaplan–Meier survival (probability not yet resolved), so the net-resolution sensitivity column is mislabeled and easy to compare in the wrong direction, even though `cif_resolve_72` itself is coded correctly. The proposed multi-state diagram also omits observed transitions, notably `AutoCheck → Review` and `Rework → Resolve`. Those gaps keep it short of a nearly flawless answer.
