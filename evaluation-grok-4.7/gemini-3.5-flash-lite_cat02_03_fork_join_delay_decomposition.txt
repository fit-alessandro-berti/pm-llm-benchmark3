4.5

The write-up has the right fork-join skeleton and then reports a critical-path result that contradicts that skeleton, the timestamps, and its own worked examples. That is the core of the task, and it is not reliable.

Section 1 is the strongest analytical part. Upstream, queue, service, sync wait, `join_ready = max_i end_{i,c}`, downstream queue, final service, and total flow are defined so that total flow is upstream + fork-join + downstream queue + final service. The double-count argument is right: `E_{i,c} + W^{sync}_{i,c}` equals fork-join for every branch, so summing sync waits across A, B, and C stacks the same clock interval. What is missing is a numeric identity on one case and an explicit statement that summing queue + service + sync over branches equals `3 ×` fork-join time.

The case table is only partly evidence-based. Upstream, the three queues, the three services, final service, and total flow match the log. `join_ready` and `critical_branch` do not. Branch ends make the binding branch:

- A binds only F01 and F11.
- B binds F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, and F12.
- C binds F04 (`09:52`) and F08 (`10:50`).

The table instead marks A on F02, F04, F08, and F10. For F04, F08, and F10 the printed `join_ready` is A’s end even though another branch ends later, which breaks the max rule in Section 1. Downstream queue is then wrong for F04 (printed 25, actual `10:10−09:52 = 18`) and F10 (printed 25, actual `11:40−11:20 = 20`). F08 lists join `10:45` with downstream 15, but `11:05−10:45 = 20`, so that row is inconsistent with itself.

The worked lines disagree with the table. F02’s narrative eventually calls B critical; the table says A. F04’s narrative correctly gets C, join `09:52`, and downstream 18; the table says A, `09:45`, and 25. F07 and F11 are correct. Those corrections are not carried forward.

Section 3 contains three incompatible frequency stories, all false: 6/5/1, then 5/6/1, while the ranking still says A controls 50%. The true split is A 2/12, B 8/12, C 2/12. There is no near-tie. Mean B queue is `230/12 = 19.17`, not 17.5, so mean B queue-plus-service is `56.67`, not 55. Mean C queue is `10.42`, not 11.7. Mean downstream queue is `176/12 = 14.67`, not 16.25, and 16.25 is not the mean of the table either (`188/12 = 15.67`). C’s reported mean sync wait of 76.5 is impossible: every fork-join length is between 50 and 75 minutes, and C’s actual mean sync wait, zeros included, is about 30.4. “Queue up to 30–35 minutes” overstates a maximum of 30.

The influence ranking follows A’s 50-minute service, which the task says not to treat as control. A does impose a 50-minute floor, and that observation is valid. Control is who binds the max and by how much. Excess of `join_ready` over A’s completion totals 132 minutes; B accounts for 120 of them and C for 12. A’s own lead over the runner-up is only 5 minutes on F01 and 10 on F11. B is first, then A, then C. No tail statistic is reported.

The counterfactuals use the wrong critical set and the wrong pass-through model. Cutting A’s service from 50 to 40 saves join time only on F01 (5 minutes, until B) and F11 (10 minutes). It saves nothing on F02, F08, or F10. The claim that every A-critical case moves 10 minutes, and the clock phrase “B finishes at ≥ 11:10,” are both false. Halving B’s queue does more than the cited F06, F07, and F12 cases: join time also falls on F02 (10), F03 (10), F05 (10), F09 (12.5), and F10 (5), for 92.5 minutes in total, while slack caps F05 and F10 below half the queue. The prose never reports these savings. Both branch counterfactuals subtract the full join reduction from flow time. That assumes the final start moves with the join. The log does not support that. F1’s F05, F09, and F11, and F2’s F08, start exactly when the previous case on that resource ends, so an earlier join is absorbed as downstream wait. Several other gaps remain after the assigned resource is already free, so the gap is not identified as capacity congestion. CF3’s “proves under-resourced” claim conflicts with Section 7 and with a simple load check: arrivals every 15 minutes and a 20-minute final service on two resources are about two-thirds utilization. The true downstream range is 8–20, not 8–25.

The redesigns inherit those numbers. Extra B capacity is a reasonable candidate, but “the critical path moves permanently to A” is false: with B’s queue removed, B’s service is still at most 45, while C’s elapsed time remains 57 on F04 and 55 on F08. An odd-versus-even case pilot does not measure a real operating factor. The final-capacity recommendation treats every `final_start − join_ready` minute as removable queue. Section 7 correctly says that quantity may be a release policy. Throughput, tails, utilization, and a successor bottleneck are discussed, but synchronization is thin on the second redesign, and the pilot does not separate policy lag from resource blocking.

The single Python block does encode the max join, a deterministic tie label, pandas durations, three counterfactual columns, `format_dataframe`, and a relevant comment that directly-follows performance does not recover sync wait. It is not under heading 6. It was not what produced the written tables: the critical-branch function would not emit the printed labels. The block also leaves a contradictory CF1 flow formula in place, assumes one-for-one flow reduction, prints only mean savings rather than the required per-case savings, and wraps `discover_performance_dfg` in an exception sink. `get_service_time` is unprotected if that symbol is absent. Inline “Wait” comments record an unresolved choice between fixed final timestamps and an earlier final start.

Section 7 correctly limits causality, branch capacity, preemption, release policy versus queue, and the unobserved upstream step. It still does not say that n = 12 on one morning cannot separate a deterministic 50-minute assay from a sample artifact, or that the counterfactuals assume a branch change does not move another branch’s queue or the final resource’s free time.
