The write-up keeps the required section skeleton, but the counterfactual timelines are not derived from the stated queues, service ranges, or intervention points, and several mandatory deliverables are incomplete or internally inconsistent.

The partial order is not a usable constraint model. `Identity Check  Document Check < Compliance Review` is syntactically broken. `Decision > Compliance Review` flips the relation direction used elsewhere. Originals, rework, preliminary-versus-final compliance, and four-eyes are missing from the order. The conditional constraints are an unfenced bullet list, so the required second fenced block is absent. The intervention labels are broadly right: K-219 is routing, K-337 is decision policy, K-411 is task creation and concurrency. K-104 is then described as timing-only in the derivation and as resource coordination in the model.

The case table does not give an explicit activity path for any case. K-104 places preliminary compliance at Tue 19:20, which is the factual S1 start, after assessment, rather than at Monday triage while identity and document checks are still open. The Monday 08:00 C1 backlog of 20 h is treated as the wait this case actually faced; the case reached C1 on Tuesday night, when the snapshot backlog was already falling. K-219 starts D2 at Mon 17:30 despite a D2 queue of about 5 h at 16:00, and it never carries the earlier document finish through A2’s backlog. K-411 starts both checks at Tue 09:35 despite Tue 08:00 queues of about 2 h for I2 and 4 h for D2. K-337’s outcome range of −48 h to −72 h is not tied to Friday C1 capacity or weekend coverage, and the assumption that the discrepancy “is not material” contradicts the requirement to approve with conditions or reject without ignoring it.

The derivations subtract hours instead of propagating readiness through queues. K-337’s best case (−48 h) saves less than its base (−50 h). K-411’s best and base are the same −4 h. Downstream assessment absorption is asserted, not calculated. Service-time bands are unused.

The capacity table conflicts with its own prose. Early compliance is said to shrink the identity queue. Parallel checks are said to shrink assessment and grow nothing, while the text claims an assessment rush. The 100-case discussion is about 150 words, below the required 250–400. It treats Wednesday assessment load as growing; the snapshots decline from Monday through Thursday. K-219’s standard handwriting is analyzed as complex-document load. D1/D2 substitution, avoided rework, weekend external delay, and timestamp-versus-true-concurrency are each named and then left undeveloped.

Robustness rank 1 is the intervention whose own row says robustness is low: suppressing the second information request. That rank follows cycle-time savings and conflicts with the quality, compliance, spillover, and reversibility criteria. The synthesis, also short of 220 words, calls that policy change the most robust result because it prioritizes throughput. The least transportable case is identified as K-411’s system lock. The single-case assumption that fails first at scale is K-104’s reserved C1 slot, which is slack only while other cases are held fixed.

The pilot rows exist, but eligibility for the rework policy is cases that already have more than one information-request loop, so the intervention point has passed. The compliance pilot is assigned by manual volume selection. Stopping thresholds have no baseline. The eight assumptions are labeled, yet several are generic or mislabeled, and the assumptions that actually drive the clocks—overnight staffing, snapshot queues as remaining work, and materiality of the K-337 discrepancy—are not the ones audited.
