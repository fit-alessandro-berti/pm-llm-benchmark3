5.5

The main partition is largely defensible: X1 and X2 are identified under renaming, activity-label traces correctly group X1/X2/X5/X6/X7 and separate X3, X4, and X8, and the object-level splits for consolidated versus separate dispatch, external logistics, external billing, replacement, and return/repack are the right distinctions. The three relations, the required table, the six borderline pairs, and the four use-case recommendations are all present.

Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors. The canonical routine references `otype` without defining it, sorts timestamp ties by `origin_id` despite claiming identifier invariance, and only encodes binary relations, so the ternary “P82 replaces P81 for item I82” is lost. The prose claim that any order of concurrent events yields the same signature is false once trails use absolute indexes. The X5 signature wrongly places the external item in Dispatch; it occurs only in Pack. In the X3–X4 comparison the text says “X2,” and “X2/R3” is used where R2/R3 is meant.

The robustness conclusions do not follow. Collapsing the two payments does not turn X4 into the baseline, because the two pack–dispatch pairs remain; those Pack events are also not consecutive. Treating payment multiplicity as an amount likewise does not move X4 into S1. Section 8 then contradicts Section 3 by compressing X1/X2/X3/X4/X7 onto one nominal activity path while omitting X5/X6, which actually share X1’s activity sequence. Typed and preferred partitions are identical on all eight cases, but that is never stated. The PM4Py discussion hedges the key’s contents and treats `max_objs` as splitting oversized executions rather than giving a precise, reproducible account.
