7.5

The fork-join accounting is mostly right, and the main conclusion holds: B controls completion through queue plus service, while A’s longer service is usually slack. The case table, the no-double-count identity, the worked cases, and the three counterfactual saving rows are numerically correct, including savings capped when another branch stays last. That is not enough for a high score, because several later figures and one mechanism claim contradict those same calculations.

The branch comparison table is internally inconsistent. B’s queues in the case table sum to 230, so the mean is 19.2 minutes, not 17.5. C’s queues sum to 125, so the mean is 10.4, not 10.0. Queue plus service must equal end minus fork release; the reported 55.0 and 30.2 therefore cannot sit beside the correct contribution means 56.7 and 30.6. The ranking prose repeats the wrong 55.

After halving B’s queue, the stated migration to A in about 8 of 12 cases is false under the answer’s own A-before-B tie rule. A is critical in 6 cases, B remains strictly critical in 4 (F03, F07, F09, F12), and C remains critical in 2. “Invert” overstates that shift.

The synchronization claim in the first redesign is reversed. Synchronization wait is join time minus branch end. Moving B earlier lowers join time in the cases where B is critical, so C’s mean synchronization wait falls by the same total as the join-time saving. It does not grow, and the redeployment argument built on growing C idle is unsupported.

The third-server utilization is also inconsistent with the answer’s own window: 240 busy minutes over 424 resource-minutes is about 57 percent; three resources over that same window is about 38 percent, not about 50 percent. In the limits section, idle time inside a start–end interval makes recorded duration an upper bound on busy time. Calling busy time “bounded below” by end minus start reverses that bound, and “optimistic” does not follow.

The Python matches the correct counterfactual arithmetic, but `join_ready` is built as fork-release datetime plus minutes since midnight, so the printed clock time is wrong even though the minute-based columns are not. The 60-versus-116 split of final waiting also misses partial resource blocking in F04 (8 of 18 minutes) and F10 (5 of 20). Exact alignment in F05, F08, F09, and F11 is real; treating the other eight waits as wholly non-capacity is not.

The definitions, binding-path counterfactuals, and most limits are strong. The contradictory aggregates, the wrong post-change critical counts, and the reversed synchronization effect are material, not cosmetic.
