The write-up covers every required section, keeps several live hypotheses with counters and predictions, and avoids treating centrality as inherently good or bad or recommending removal without a backup. Those strengths do not offset clear validity and calibration failures on the question’s core.

The handover reading reverses the metric: out-degree 0.82 is explained as “many resources send cases to her,” which is in-degree. Out-degree is her fan-out to others. That reversal is never corrected and is reused to argue that out-degree shows work queuing on her desk. A dispatcher bottleneck and a receiving bottleneck are different claims.

The working-together reconciliation is also off the metric. PM4Py-style working-together is case co-presence, not duration or joint intensity. Short routing events still count if she and another resource both appear on the case. Low pairwise rates are explained by her 22% case coverage spread across many targets, not by events lasting 4.2 minutes. The answer treats brevity as what the network measures.

The executive summary then misstates the absence result: 8.4 to 13.9 days is about 1.65×, not “more than doubled,” and the absence was 6 days, not a week. “All” exceptions, a “compulsory” passage point, and “no other resource” able to reroute overshoot 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints, while Bot_Rules still sends large volumes to Alice and Bruno. Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay are effectively ignored despite the constraint. “Does not imply” is explicit for handover and thin for subcontracting, time, and role discovery, and the extra hypotheses drop the required four-part structure. Role similarity “perhaps with temps” is a weak cluster: temps have low exception rates and a different activity mix.
