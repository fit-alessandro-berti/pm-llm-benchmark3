8.3

The memo is a strong, constraint-compliant decision piece: it chooses hybrid E, refuses both national scale and full rollback, and ties that choice to segment splits, tail/rework, and billing-before-activation. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated; all five metric conflicts are explained mechanistically; fitness, precision, generalization, alignments, DFG, temporal profiles, and handover vs. working-together networks are used rather than ignored. The VoI ranking is the best part, especially deferring a live survey-skip RCT until the risk rule is validated offline.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 6 scales “survey bypass” for simple low-risk houses, while the memo’s own action list does not, and the pilot skip rule was for medium-risk addresses; simple-house gains are tied in the evidence to merged scheduling and ownership. “Proven” and “doubled” (7% to 15% is about 2.1×) overstate identification and arithmetic. “Churn” and “regulatory” exposure are not in the data. The rollout gate “strictly lower than baseline (≤ EUR 190)” mixes the EUR 184 visible baseline and the EUR 198 loaded baseline. Model precision ≥ 0.75 is treated as an operational hard gate even though precision depends on miner settings and case mix. Segment NPS ≥ +21 is required although NPS is only reported overall and is not in the measurement plan (M8 is ranked last and is a different split). Immediate pod backup also sits awkwardly beside M5, which is supposed to decide the ownership model. One evidence-table “assumption” restates an interpretation of low precision. Rural harm is described as rescheduling difficulty, but the remedy emphasized for rural orders is pausing survey skips.
