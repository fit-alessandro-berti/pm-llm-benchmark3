5.5

The final diagnosis gets QA and QD directionally right, and the required seven-part structure is complete, but the decision sets that the task makes central are repeatedly wrong or incomplete, and those errors change the competing-rule tests.

QA’s headline (FIFO, with an appointment skip) is defensible, and A02 over A04 plus A06 over A08 really do knock out global priority and SPT. The 08:20 table still omits A04, who has been waiting since 08:13 with priority 1 and `est_min` 7. SPT and priority both select A04 there, so the claimed local equivalence of appointment and SPT does not exist. At 09:20 the appointment column is “None” even though A06’s 09:15 appointment is already due, so FIFO and appointment are observationally the same and the later claim that A06 over A07 isolates appointment is not earned. The clean 09:05 choice of A05 over A08 and shorter A07 is never used.

QB is the weakest label. “Preemptive priority, P1 only,” at confidence 70, does not survive the idle selections that were left out. At 09:48 B03 starts while B05 (priority 1, arrived 09:46) and shorter B04 are already waiting. At 10:28 B04 (priority 3) starts over B06 (priority 2) and over B07 (`est_min` 6, appointment 10:30). B07 is absent from the reconstructed sets, despite the explicit note that the appointment was not honored. Resume-versus-requeue is also blurred: 09:31 is a free-server choice, not a test of whether a priority-2 job can preempt a running job. Every QB service length matches `est_min`, so “SPT with noisy estimates” is not a live competitor.

QC correctly flags C02 and C03 as SPT-like and C04 over C06 as a violation. It then misses stronger SPT failures: at 10:54 C05 (`est_min` 40) is taken over C06 (`est_min` 6) and C07, and at 11:40 C01 (`est_min` 36) is taken over C07 (`est_min` 18), whose 12:00 deadline was still feasible. C01 running last among the initial backlog is listed as contradicting an SPT diagnosis; that fact cuts against FIFO. Realized QC durations also match the estimates exactly, so strategic inflation of C05 or deflation of C06 is closed by the given segments. Confidence 55 is still attached to SPT with “no secondary component,” which is too tidy.

QD’s within-resource conclusion is the best piece: D04 before D08 and D07 before D10 hit FIFO and miss both priority and SPT inside real skill sets. The 11:30 row reverses FIFO (D06 arrived at 11:10, D07 at 11:14, so FIFO selects D06) and drops D10, already present, priority 1, electrical, `est_min` 12. Cross-resource holes are untreated: D-X is idle at 11:20–11:30 while D08 would fit before the appointment, and D-F is idle at 11:36–12:00 while D05 no longer fits before the meeting and D10 would.

The scan-delay exception points the wrong way. A delayed scan makes true arrival earlier than the stamp, by at most two minutes, so it cannot push C06’s entry past 10:32. That story also contradicts the answer’s own assumption that a two-minute perturbation changes no decision. One counterfactual asks for C06’s realized duration, which the segments already give as 6 minutes.

Section 7 is more careful than the queue table: QA/QD FIFO, P1 preemption only as a QB ingredient, SPT merely possible in QC, and priority the dangerous policy to read into QA or QD. Those closing claims are the strongest part of the write-up. They do not repair the eligible-set mistakes, the inverted QD FIFO prediction, the duration-versus-estimate checks that were never done, or the over-identified QB rule.
