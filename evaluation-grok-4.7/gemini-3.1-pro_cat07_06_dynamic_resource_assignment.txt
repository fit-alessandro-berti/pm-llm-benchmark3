The write-up has the required seven sections and honors several hard rules (no unsupervised high-priority trainee, no new work for high overtime-risk people, skill 0 blocked, a 2/9 training count). The assignment logic and the audit sketch still fail in ways that would make the policy unsafe to run.

# 1. Objectives and constraints
Hard versus soft constraints are mostly right, and the four hard rules match the brief. Resilience is never stated as an objective, even though the brief requires it alongside throughput, development, fairness, continuity, and SLA risk. SLA compliance and throughput are merged into one objective, so the conflict between them is not set up. Skill 0 is treated as banned only for “regulated” tasks; the matrix defines 0 as not allowed on every activity.

# 2. Assignment policy
The procedure is not an executable policy. Urgency has a formula, then continuity, overtime, and training are described as “strong,” “massive,” or “a bonus,” with no weights, no combined score, and no case-ordering rule. Two required scoring terms are absent: skill-match (beyond a hard eligibility gate) and resilience or bottleneck avoidance. Workload is only an overtime penalty plus a tie-break on the initial open-case count. Hours remaining never act as a feasibility check against open work plus new work. Supervision is not an assignment type, despite rules 2, 6, and 7 depending on it. Intra-batch load is not updated, so the same lightly loaded names keep winning. Exception handling (no eligible resource, training quota unreachable, both security specialists absent) is missing. A deviation audit later cannot be run against this text.

# 3. Recommended assignment for the current queue
Several rows are sound: C002, C004, C005, C007, C008, and C016 respect skill floors, continuity, and overtime. C006 to Jonas is a fair shed from Iris. The 9 normal/low cases and 2/9 = 22.2% arithmetic are correct. The rest of the table has serious errors.

The table cites that 2.7h and still moves the case. Celine’s security skill is 1, so unsupervised execution is illegal, but rule 6 explicitly allows skill 1–2 under R7 or R8. Constraint 5 prefers continuity here unless supervision is infeasible. A supervised pair was feasible and is never considered. Hugo also already has 7 open cases and 5.5h, and he is given C015 as well. “Requires skill 3, owner ineligible” is an incomplete reading of the rules the answer itself listed.
- **C003 contradicts the policy.** David is the owner at diagnosis skill 3. The move sends the case to Farid at skill 1, raises reassignments from 2 to 3, and hits the stated 1.8× rework / 1.4× appeal region on a repeated diagnosis loop of complexity 0.66. Amal, Gita, and Jonas have approval skill 1, and rule 7 says skill 1 may prepare but not finalize. David and Hugo, whose training priority is approval, do have approval skill 0 and cannot perform it. # 4. Comparison with baseline policies
The seven comparison axes are the right ones, and the qualitative directions (first-available raises mismatch and rework; specialist-only queues Security and Approval) match the prompt. The section never builds either baseline assignment on this queue, so the comparison is not grounded in C001–C016. “Mathematically guarantees ≥ 20%” is false: the algorithm only adds a bonus. “Bus factor of 1” is false: security has two skill-3 resources, and approval has skill 2 as well as Farid. The rework claim says the policy limits reassignment count, while section 3 pushes C003 past 2. Farid’s four-case concentration is the bottleneck the resilience paragraph says the policy avoids.

# 5. Explanation to workers
The tone is non-punitive and mentions continuity, load, and learning. It would not tell a worker why C001 left Celine, why Farid received both urgent cases, why a high overtime-risk person received nothing, or what supervision requires. Contestability, team boundaries (A, B, Risk, Quality are unused everywhere), and supervisor time are absent.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators
Every requested indicator is named, and the >2 reassignment link to 1.8× rework is stated correctly. Operational definitions are weak. SLA miss is described as service time plus waiting time exceeding `sla_deadline`, which mixes durations with a timestamp. Skill mismatch covers only urgent/high work below skill 2, not weak-skill retention on normal work. Appeal is in the heading and not in the measure. `assignment_reason`, `handover_cost_band`, `workload_snapshot`, `required_skill`, and `reassignment_count` are the fields that make this policy auditable, and they are not part of the indicator definitions. No pass/fail thresholds are given, so “deviation” cannot be decided from section 2.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch
`format_dataframe`, handover network, `save_vis_sna`, performance DFG, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, both filters, `discover_batches`, and `discover_organizational_roles` are real, relevant calls. The sketch still does not show learning or a real audit. `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`, `get_service_time`, and `get_variants` are never used. `filter_case_performance(..., min_performance=86400)` flags cycle time over 24h, so a 3h-SLA case that finishes in 10h is missed and a 72h-SLA case that finishes in 25h is flagged. `len(rework_log)` counts events, not cases, and case-level activity filtering does not mean “rework after a handover.” The custom columns required to score skill, training share, overtime exposure, handover cost, and policy deviation are never read. Role discovery is not tied to the skill matrix or to specialist queues.

Overall, the skeleton matches the brief and a minority of the queue is assigned carefully. The urgent security case, the diagnosis handover, the approval skill reading, the non-operational score, and the SLA audit code are material failures, not polish issues.
