The write-up covers every required section, uses the given identifiers and timestamps, and keeps most causal claims explicitly counterfactual. The delay arithmetic and one counterfactual are wrong, and those errors sit in the section the task makes central.

The B1 “avoidable” calculation contradicts itself. J1’s observed wait is 3 h 55 min (08:10–12:05) and J2’s is 3 h 40 min (08:25–12:05). Starting the same batch at J3’s readiness (11:55) removes 10 minutes from each job, not 3 h 45 min and 3 h 30 min. Those two figures are the residual waits until 11:55 (3 h 45 min + 3 h 30 min = 7 h 15 min). The sentence says J1 “would wait 3 h 45 min less than it did,” then says that reduction “leaves” 7 h 15 min of synchronization wait. Under a start-at-11:55 counterfactual the remainder is about 10 minutes per early job; under a start-at-own-readiness counterfactual the savings are 3 h 55 min and 3 h 40 min (7 h 35 min combined), not 7 h 15 min. J3 “would wait zero” is the only clause in that sentence that matches a start at 11:55. The required avoidable-versus-synchronization split is therefore not actually computed.

The RACK-7 figure has the same problem. Oven idle 12:55–14:50 is 1 h 55 min, and 12:55–14:35 is 1 h 40 min, but the counterfactual that B2 was otherwise ready is false on the supplied times: J4 is ready only at 13:10 and J5 only at 14:40. From 13:10 to 14:40 the rack hold and J5’s unreadiness are co-present; before 13:10 J4 is not ready either. The 1 h 40 min cannot be attributed to RACK-7 on the stated assumption.
