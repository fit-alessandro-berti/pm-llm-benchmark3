5.8

The memo has the required seven-section structure, uses real log quantities in many places, and does not ban Critical-downtime vans outright. It still misstates several central trade-offs, so it is not a reliable decision memo.

The main logistics claim is directionally wrong on service. Stock-to-air is 17 h median and 3% late; stock-to-ground is 34 h and 8% late, and air removes about 24 h of transport time. Section 4 marks that shift as cycle-time neutral (S1) or an internal speedup with late delivery held flat (S4). Priority picking has no time evidence that could offset that gap. “About 80% less transport CO2 for this segment” applies the air-path pair 18.5 versus 3.6 kgCO2e to Standard-stock Service-contract as a whole, whose average is 7.8. S4 also drops the slack test; only the later package restores the SP1 85% rule.

Resilience is misread in the recommendation. R1 requires a remaining alternative for Service-contract customers during carrier disruption. The closing rationale treats “keeping ground” as that alternative. Ground is the primary lower-emission mode; shifting volume onto ground and twice-daily consolidation concentrates disruption risk. The table also uses “resilience” for smoother flow and fewer defects.

Rebound coverage misses the evidence the prompt makes mandatory. The six risks never take the packaging displacement: reinforced packs cut damage-related returns about 40% for heavy made-to-order parts and double package material, while Q1 ties that choice to parts over 8 kg. The obsolescence item is mislabeled: the body describes smaller batches raising setup energy, whereas the log’s obsolescence mechanism is large batches leaving about 6% of items unshipped within 14 days. Heat-treatment waits, with the lowest energy per item above batch size 10 and 1.7× late risk, are not carried into that risk list.

Other grounding problems are material. The material-yield formula needs total part weight, which is not an attribute; the resilience ratio needs disruption events that are not in the log. “Halve rework” generalizes the CNC_B 18% versus CNC_A 9% made-to-order rates, and “single-pass energy is slightly higher” on CNC_A is not in the paths (the CNC_B rework path is 32 kWh and 1.8 kg scrap largely because of the loop). S6 “eliminates” packaging rework and claims less material; incompatibility fixes can require more reinforced material. Missing SP2 justification on 31% of van cases is treated as evidence those orders are not true downtime. The 7% partial-shipment path (79 h, 24% late, second dispatch) and the cancel-due-to-delay endings never enter the redesign, despite hurting both service and duplicate transport.

Governance names SP1/SP2/Q1, then sets thresholds only for SP1 and SP2, and watches late-delivery drift for only one segment. Critical-downtime and made-to-order service levels, scrap, package mass, batch size versus wait, and obsolescence are not wired to the indicator set.
