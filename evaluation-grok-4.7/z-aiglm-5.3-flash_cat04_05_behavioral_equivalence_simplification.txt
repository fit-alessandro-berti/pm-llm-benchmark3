The verdicts and counterexamples are right, but the required comparison code is unsound and the footprint conclusion is factually wrong.

P1, P2, and P3 are judged correctly: A and B are not trace-equivalent, they agree only after the dashboard projection, and the split already appears inside the P3 bound. Loop scope, `|Check| = |Analyze|` versus exactly one check, Approve-after-both in A, and the free-floating check in B are all handled correctly. CA-1, CA-2, CB-1, and CB-2 are genuine complete visible traces, including a two-analysis witness in each direction. Tree size (12/12), depth (5/6), six visible leaves, one `tau`, and no duplicate labels are exact. The P2 simplification is a real reduction (7 nodes, depth 3), and the preservation table honestly marks per-iteration checks, approval, and check placement as lost or weakened.

The exhaustive enumerator does not enumerate the language. `best_budget` drops a marking once seen with a greater or equal remaining budget. Loop entry is the same marking on every iteration, so every second-and-later iteration is discarded, and equal-budget interleavings (Analyze/Check order) are discarded too. The written asserts, including the two-analysis traces, therefore fail, while the text calls the search a complete P3 enumeration. Budget dominance preserves reachable markings, not trace sets. `eps_closure` also calls undefined `fm_clean` / `fm_holder`. Section 8 then crashes on `dashboard(set)` before the re-enumeration, so it never checks the claimed image equality.

Section 6 expects empty or near-empty footprint differences. That is false: `(Approve Revision, Check Revision)` is a directly-follows pair only in B (CB-2) and is impossible in A, where Approve starts only after the Analyze/Check block. The general warning that equal footprints or a small EMD would not prove trace equivalence is fine; the claim that these footprints match is not. The Petri expectation of 9 transitions also drops the silent loop-exit, so the “9→7” simplicity comparison is off.

Conceptually this is strong; as a formal artifact that asserts an exhaustive proof, it is not.
