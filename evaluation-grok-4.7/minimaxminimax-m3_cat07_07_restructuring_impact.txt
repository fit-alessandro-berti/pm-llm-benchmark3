The forecast is complete and structurally compliant: all seven required sections are present, East is treated differently from North and South, fairness and learning are carried through the scenarios, indicators, risks, and pilot, and the recommendation correctly refuses an unmonitored full rollout. The qualitative warning that stable averages can hide tails, queue conversion, accountability loss, and slow skill atrophy is sound. It is not reliable as a quantitative forecast.

The central handover claim is wrong against the tables given. Current frequencies sum to about 343 handover events per 100 cases (about 3.4 per case). Management’s post-change frequencies sum to about 359 (about 3.6). The local tech loop (36+31) is replaced by a larger Technical Shared Service loop (41+36), and approval handovers also rise. The answer instead asserts a drop from ~3.1 to ~2.6, ~2.7, and ~2.5 in every scenario, and the mechanism map states a “lower nominal handover count.” That baseline matches no subset of the table, and the direction is the opposite of the supplied routing model. Overlap of cross-team edges would not reverse it. This matters because handover change is the question’s primary observable.

Related quantitative and causal errors compound that miss. Organization-wide anchors are South’s median (7.4d) and p90 (18.2d), which understate a volume-weighted tail pulled by East’s 22.8d. Embedded specialists are parallel team resources, not “three serial local queues,” so the pooling argument is built on the wrong queue structure. The claim that informal advice prevents ~30% of unnecessary referrals, “visible as” the 67/100 tech handovers, confuses the formal specialist loop with the advice that would avoid a handover, and the 30% is not in the data. In the optimistic case, Legal/Risk median wait falls to ~7h “because volume is detected earlier,” while those resources are already at 91–93% and detected volume rises from 9 to 12 per 100; management’s own estimate is 10.5h, and broader detection increases arrivals. The 0.5 CSAT gap is the North–East team gap, then labeled a customer-segment gap. The adverse “~4d” segment p90 gap is invented. “Median cycle 1.5d” and unsigned CSAT “0.1” are ambiguous.

The pilot does not test the intervention it is meant to gate. An East-only live phase that leaves North and South with embedded specialists does not pool N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech, and a go/no-go based on East cannot observe the North expertise loss the staff risk identifies. Phase 3 still withholds North.

The PM4Py sketch would not run as written. `format_dataframe` is applied to an XES event log; `get_variants` values are treated as case-id lists; `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with a dict plus `level` in a way that does not match its signature; a broken `groupby` expression is left in place; and the waiting-time helper returns service time with an unspecified `timeout`. Pseudocode was allowed, but these are incorrect APIs, not omissions.
