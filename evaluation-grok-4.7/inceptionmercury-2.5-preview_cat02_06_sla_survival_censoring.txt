4.5

The write-up keeps the required nine-section shape and a few defensible choices: time starts at `Submit`, the horizon is 72 hours, withdrawal is not treated as ordinary censoring, escalation is recognized as post-start information, and Aalen–Johansen is a sensible primary estimator for a resolution cumulative incidence. Those pieces do not survive the encoding, the case arithmetic, or the code.

Section 1 defines \(P(T_{resolve}>72)\) and the cumulative incidence of resolution by 72, then misstates how the requested cases enter the second estimand. S03 is an observed resolution at 76 hours: it is still unresolved at 72 and fails only later. It is not censored. S09 is observed open through 92 hours, so it is known to be unresolved at 72 and is censored at 92, not at 72. Assumption 1.1 says the opposite of the table in the same section. S08 at 68 hours is declared a breach under estimand 1 even in the censoring reading, which removes the case from the risk set before 72 and leaves its 72-hour status unknown. S32, transferred at 96, is never separated from S08, although S32 was still internal and unresolved at 72. The composite “not resolved” probability is also written as if it were a survival function after competing events have made resolution time improper.

Section 2 lists the five statuses and three transfer stories, but the stories are not operationalized. Transfer assumption B is described as right censoring and, in the same paragraph, as already not resolved by 72. Cause-specific encoding, where competing events are censored, is not distinguished from cumulative-incidence encoding, where they are events. Open cases censored before 72 and open cases already past 72 share one rule. “Imputed failure” is the wrong label for an open case whose age is already greater than 72: non-resolution at the horizon is observed.

Section 3 misreads the extract. S13 is resolved at 110 hours, yet the exclusion list “S09–S15” and the later open-case example both treat it as open. The resolved subset used for the “~30h” claim includes S20 at 74 hours; those five times average about 36 hours, and all 13 resolved cases average about 50 hours, with S03, S13, S20, and S28 already over 72. Completed-case bias here is exclusion of open, withdrawn, rejected, and transferred cases, not an assumption that open cases finish within an hour. Four of nine open cases are past 72; five are censored earlier (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31). The “30%+” cohort claim is also high: nine of 32 rows have time greater than 72.

Section 4 recommends Aalen–Johansen for the incidence estimand, which matches one decision. It does not compare restricted mean time, landmarking, or a multi-state model, all of which were required. Kaplan–Meier’s direction of bias is only roughly stated. Fine–Gray is the secondary method in the table and cause-specific Cox is the secondary method in the prose. There is no small-sample justification: 32 cases, 13 resolutions, and four late resolutions cannot support the covariate models being endorsed.

Section 5 flags escalation timing, then leaves the main leakage in place. Full prefixes end in `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, or `Transfer`, so “current stage” and raw variant are outcome labels for terminal cases. `rework_count` and `last_event_gap_h` are end-of-follow-up measurements; every terminal row has gap 0, so gap mostly restates openness at the cutoff. Immortal time is not actually analyzed. The Mermaid diagram is invalid (`Escalate]` versus an undefined `Esc`), omits rework and `Escalate → Transfer`, invents `Screen`/`Review → Transfer`, and misses the only clear policy footprint in the prefixes: many cohort-B cases start `Submit>AutoCheck`, while cohort A starts `Submit>Screen`, with several B cases still on the old order.

Section 6 does not vary withdrawal, rejection, long inactivity gaps, or non-random rollout. The cohort-reversal claim is conditional speculation. Transfer counts are available and unused: one in A (S08) and two in B (S21, S32). Treating open cases older than 72 as breaches is the primary fact for a 72-hour unresolved indicator, not a sensitivity; the unidentified cases are those censored before 72.

Section 7 sets compliance equal to resolution incidence after Section 1 assigned compliance to breach probability. Thresholds of 60% and 70% are unexplained. Calibration is absent. Priority and cohort are not given a non-causal case-mix check, despite B carrying more P1 cases (5/17 versus 3/15).

The code omits `escalated`, `rework_count`, and `last_event_gap_h`. Its three scenarios do not encode three transfer treatments: `treated` is unused, so “competing” and “censored” return the same count, 23/32. The open-breach branch subtracts the four open cases past 72, lowering the breach count. Cases censored before 72 are counted as breaches. No risk-set, Kaplan–Meier, or cumulative-incidence table is built. The stratified print is cohort-only. The PM4Py string is one row per case, with duration in place of event times and the whole prefix as one field, so variants cannot recover control flow; the result is never printed. No survival fallback is implemented.
