The required structure is complete, and every case classification is defensible. State is reconstructed in `(timestamp, event_index)` order, blanks carry forward, and event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are kept apart. TX-302 is correctly left compliant because the Enhanced_Review already satisfies R1 and the qualified signature separately supports EX-77. TX-305 correctly stays indeterminate and does not copy the next-day settlement amount back to release. TX-310 correctly treats the post-release certificate and the requester’s oral account as unresolved. Sections 6 and 7 cover the requested failure modes and the logging controls.

TX-311’s release state is wrong: risk remains LOW from creation, while the findings table says MEDIUM. That error does not flip R2. The alternative reading is also unsound. If the DE update were effective only after release, the release state would be US and 115,000, so R1 would apply, and the 16:20 Enhanced_Review would still follow the 16:10 amount increase and precede release. Reversing the two 16:20 events while leaving both before 16:30 would still yield DE at release, so R1 would stay inactive.

Severity does not follow the answer’s own definition. Compliant TX-306 and TX-308 are High, while compliant TX-302 and TX-311 are Low.

R2’s pseudo-logic requires the Enhanced_Review to survive R4. R4 invalidates a review only for amount-dependent obligations; R2 is a risk/PEP rule. R4 is also formalized as a hop-to-hop test against the immediately previous amount. Growth of more than 20 percent from the amount known at review, split across smaller hops, would not be caught. This log has no such pattern, so the case rows survive.

The script has the required skeleton: embedded CSV, `format_dataframe`, within-case forward fill, release snapshots, prior-event timestamps, exemption fields, and unknown-value flags. On this log its coarse flags agree with the narrative. Missing risk, jurisdiction, or customer type can make `==` return `pd.NA` and the trigger condition raise. A conflicted exemption forces R1 unknown even when a qualifying Enhanced_Review already discharges it. `r4_status` becomes `INVALIDATED` if any earlier review was invalidated, including when a later review is still valid. Materiality is global rather than obligation-specific. No DECLARE or trace-order check is included; that piece was optional, and section 6 supplies the conceptual account.
