7.4

The answer has the required six-section shape, exact conformance labels, and the right core judgment: conformant second review first, A and B last, rescreening not treated as a substitute for Rule 6, and P-901 not sacrificed by default. That is not enough for a high score. Several quantitative and internal-consistency defects remain.

The Rank 1 clock is not grounded. “~50–90 minutes if S4 is early” assumes a short post-arrival review. The only stated review window is S2’s 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record. If S4’s review is similar, an arrival at 35 minutes already yields about 75–125 minutes, so the deadline is usually missed even in the early case. That unsupported interval props up D’s operational appeal in the table and in Branch 1a.

The P-901 spillover is also under-specified. The +0.12–0.28 breach shift is tied to a 45-minute interruption, while S2’s review can run to 75 minutes. The €2,400–€9,800 figure is arithmetically fair only under the extra assumption that P-901’s deadline value matches P-884’s, and it is not adjusted for a longer interruption. Action C’s own bounds sum to at most 90 minutes, yet its harm cell treats deadline loss as an ordinary in-range outcome.

Section 6 conflicts with Section 4. The fallback can interrupt P-901 for S2 after a favourable screen, including when a fresh score below 70 and a resolved match would lift the Rule 6 trigger. Section 5’s claim that, if S4 arrives at 35 minutes, C could fall below E is not earned: C would then be dominated by D, but it is not shown to be worse than a 6–24 hour evidence cycle.

Smaller defects add up. Rule 10 is cited as if A and B violate an override rule they do not invoke. “Risk is 72 (40)” is opaque. S4 is called a guaranteed reviewer. The €15,000–€80,000 false-rejection/hold range is applied to rejections that may be true positives. The €480,000 principal and the 0.48–0.64 baseline breach band are barely used to calibrate D. G sits above F even though the text later calls standalone G a low-information time sink.

The regulatory dominance argument, four-eyes distinction between A and B, transportability caveat, SAR limits, and F/G validity conditions are sound. The recommendation is directionally right, but the timing model and fallback logic are not tight enough for a higher mark.
