The fork-join arithmetic is correct and internally consistent: every case row, synchronization wait, critical-branch label, additive identity \(T=U+L^{FJ}+D+R\), and the three headline counterfactuals (15, 92.5, and 176 minutes) check out. Slack clipping is handled properly (F01 saves 5 of a 10-minute A cut; F10 saves 5 of a 10-minute B-queue cut; F11 ties). B is ranked above A for the right reason, zero-benefit cases are identified, and the Python implements max-based joins, deterministic ties, the three counterfactuals, and a real interval log passed through `format_dataframe` and `get_service_time`.

The score is held down by defects that a strict reading should not ignore.

The required formulas are corrupted exactly where the argument is hardest. Synchronization wait is written \(W^{sync}*{c,i}=J_c-e*{c,i}\), fork-join time as \(L^{FJ}*c\), and the double-count derivation repeats those broken subscripts. The factor-of-three claim is the right idea, but the algebra as printed is not a clean proof.

Section 3 treats a “fixed downstream queue” and a fixed final start as the same masking mechanism. A constant lag passes join savings through to flow time; only a fixed start absorbs them into \(D_c\). Section 4 later separates those cases, so the earlier sentence is a real slip rather than the paper’s only view.

The largest analytical miss is the final station. F1 and F2 alternate strictly by case, final service is 20 minutes in every case, and in most cases at least one resource is idle at `join_ready` (F01, F02, F03, F04, F06, F07, F11, F12, among others). That evidence is in the supplied timestamps and cuts against reading all 176 minutes as removable congestion or as the biggest capacity prize. The text hedges that \(D_c\) may be a release policy, but it never uses the resource trace it has, while still leading Redesign 2 with the full 176-minute figure.

Smaller issues: n=12 “p90” values such as 24.0, 21.8, and 16.3 are undefined interpolations presented as tails, and 14.7 versus 14.67 is needlessly inconsistent. Cross-case queue coupling is appropriately labeled as out of the mechanical counterfactuals.
