3.5

The write-up keeps the required nine-part skeleton and a few headline facts, but the quantitative core, estimands, graph, and code are not reliable enough to use.

The only clean descriptive results are the flag split on triage start (about 0.2h versus 2.5h) and total cycle time (about 16.5h versus 8h). Almost every other stage number is wrong, and the rows fail a basic add-up check. For flagged cases the components sum to about 11.3h against a reported cycle of 16.5h. The missing time is the manual-review queue: triage-done to manual-start is about 6.5–7h for flagged cases, not 0.5h. That single error erases the main downstream mechanism. Escalation waits look zero-filled (flagged high-load manual-to-escalation of 4.5h matches putting non-escalated cases at 0), which the text claims not to do. Residual time is stably about 1h, not 0.5h or 1.5h. Support counts are wrong: low/medium/high are 4/8/8, not 3/10/7; flagged escalation is 6 versus 4, not 5 versus 5. Flagged normal-load cycle time is about 12–14h, not 7h; flagged cases without escalation average about 9h, not 6h. Unflagged triage service is about 1.0h, not 1.2h. The short commentary never reaches 100 words and never says why these contrasts are non-causal.

Several empirical sentences in later sections contradict the event log. Manual service is essentially the same for flagged and reviewed-unflagged cases (about 2.7h), so “flagged review takes longer” is false. Rejections are 2/10 in both flag groups, all high-complexity, so “more rejections for flagged” is false. Risk score completely separates the flag (flag 0 max 0.61, flag 1 min 0.64). The proposed RDD cutoff of 0.70 is not the policy threshold, and there is no within-score overlap to adjust over. High complexity is always reviewed, including three unflagged cases, so review is not flag-only.

Section 1 does not define the interventions it names. E1’s obstacle “flag is post-treatment” is the wrong way around. E3 and E5 are mediator contrasts with undefined outcomes when the stage is off, not controlled direct effects. E4 conditions on entering review and is not an interventional indirect effect. The prose says the analysis uses interventional indirect effects; the table mostly labels associations, controlled direct effects, and a separable effect. P1 (hide the flag, keep the score) is never separated from changing priority-lane or mandatory-review behavior. E6 calls a system-level P2-versus-P0 contrast ill-defined merely because of interference, while a system policy effect can be well-defined under interference if the unit is the system.

The graph omits `triage_wait`, `manual_review`, `escalation`, `decision_outcome`, and any `U_` node. Priority is wired to `triage_service` rather than to triage waiting, and the flag never points at review occurrence. The second diagram is a slogan, not a time-ordered mechanism. The interference paragraph conflates exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding with SUTVA.

Identification ideas are named and then misspecified. The negative-control row uses rejection, an outcome the flag policy can affect. The policy-change row assumes no spillovers, then treats a shorter flagged cycle as something that would overturn the conclusion rather than support it. The mediation row is an accounting split, not an interventional indirect effect. Case order is not defended as an instrument.

P2 is a reasonable pilot target, but case-level 50/50 randomization on a shared queue does not “minimize interference,” the guardrail that bypasses review changes the contrast, and rejection rate is not decision quality. Required process data stop at triage and review timestamps. Section 9 is under 180 words and repeats that interference claim.

The code does not run (`pd.compat.StringIO`) and does not do the required work: no event log, no `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp` / `start_timestamp` / resource, no `format_dataframe`, no flagged-versus-unflagged `discover_dfg` or `discover_performance_dfg`, and no support counts. Reversal item 1 has the hidden-risk inequality backward for the delay claim; item 5 is not about positivity, and decision quality never gets a real reversal condition.
