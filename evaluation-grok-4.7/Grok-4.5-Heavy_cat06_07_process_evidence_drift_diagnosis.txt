7.5

The month-by-month diagnosis is largely the right one: April is a schema and feature-lineage failure, June is a routing-mix change, July–September combine a parts-delay process shift with a V3 change in breach risk, Partner Z is a separate September population shift, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable at 55% maturity. The six required sections are present, the tests name competing outcomes, and the retrain gates are directionally proportionate.

The score is held down by concrete errors, not by tone.

The required process-evidence sketch misdefines the case-start cohort. `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_contained")` keeps only traces whose first and last events both fall inside the window. That drops open and long cases. The comment calls this “cases whose start timestamp falls inside the window.” That is the opposite cohort, and it recreates the short-path bias the October analysis is trying to detect. A case-start cohort has to be built from the first timestamp of each case, then retain the full trace. The calendar-event filter is also used for `discover_dfg` and `get_variants` without noting that cutting events mid-trace invents variants and breaks edges.

Performance-edge extraction is not the PM4Py return shape. `discover_performance_dfg` edge values are a float or a dict with `"mean"` / `"median"` keys, not a `(mean, ...)` tuple. `parts_edge[0]` fails or reads the wrong field. The audit evidence is medians in hours; the sketch stores a purported mean in seconds. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is only a commented ellipsis, so the lineage check the tests depend on is not shown.

Several claims are tighter than the evidence. April within-variant matured breach rates did move (V1 0.15 to 0.18, V2 0.11 to 0.13, V3 0.30 to 0.34) and reversed in May; “no material” change is too flat, and those rates are on the mapped subset only while 18% of April is unmapped. “Diagnosis-time features stayed essentially flat” is not shown. Flat mean predicted risk and low missingness do not imply stable covariates, and the covariate row simultaneously says August–October mix changes those distributions. V3-specific under-prediction is a sound inference from the aggregate gap plus stable V1/V2 rates, but sections 2 and 4 state it as measured fact. There is no variant-level predicted risk in the data. June is mostly mix, not “exactly” pure mix: the parts edge had already crept from 28 h to 42 h, and V2’s own breach rate ticked down.

Operational rules conflict: maturity cutoffs are 90% in the diagnosis table and 95% for provisional metrics, calibration, and training. May is quarantined in containment and then omitted from the explicit unsafe-month list. July’s overall ECE improved to 0.042 while AUROC fell, which matters if someone treats every post-June month as the same calibration failure.

The monitoring dependency order is sound, and the failure modes in section 6 match the real risks. The analytical spine would score higher; the cohort bug, the invented performance-DFG shape, the feature overclaim, and the inconsistent maturity rule are enough to keep it out of the top band.
