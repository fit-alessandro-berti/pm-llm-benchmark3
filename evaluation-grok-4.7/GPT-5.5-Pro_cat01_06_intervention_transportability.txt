The answer is structurally complete and substantively strong: it rejects a literal copy, gates Document Check on originals, treats Site A’s before/after change as confounded, joins Assessment on both branches, and specifies a segmented pilot with mechanism-versus-implementation checks. It is not nearly flawless. Several independent quantitative and causal errors keep it well below a top score.

The transportability graph misstates mechanisms. Queue huddles point to identity and document capacity, and digital documents point to document capacity. Huddles change discipline and waiting, not FTE; channel changes feasibility and service time, not headcount. The two added document reviewers, a central Site A confounder, are not a distinct cause. `cycle_time -> appeal` is an unsupported direct effect. Role specialization also flows into the capacity nodes that already have their own selection parents, so site differences and co-interventions are partly fused.

The transport-confidence column is not one estimand. Median cycle time is 25 because the effect does not transport, while “no negative control” is 90 and timestamp unreliability is 80. Those high scores measure certainty about a caveat, not transportability, so the column cannot be compared across rows.

Scenario arithmetic does not match the intervention that is actually recommended. No-original × non-legal ≈ 23% omits the low/mid-complexity cut used in sections 6, 7, and 9, and it treats the 38% complement of “originals required” as digital-at-registration. Under the same independence assumption, adding the 52% low/mid-complexity slice cuts the first wave to roughly 12%, before any digital-availability filter. p90 changes are not labeled as all-case versus stratum, while the median column is. The optimistic all-case median of −3h to −6h is only coherent if originals-required cases also gain several hours, which sits uneasily with the later claim that identity is often already off the critical path during the 18h originals delay.

The pilot does not close. About 3,400 cases in six months is roughly 131 a week; a 23% stratum yields about 30 eligible cases a week, and half of that treated is about 15. Reaching 300 treated and 300 control takes on the order of 20 weeks at a 50/50 split, longer under a stepped wedge, against a stated duration of 8–12 weeks. Legal expansion after only 50 legal-review cases is a weak bar for a 40% stratum whose failure mode is tail and supplement congestion. Team-by-week assignment also cannot block interference through pools of 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers; monitoring spillovers is not the same as controlling them.

The POWL candidate itself is the right partial order: Register precedes both branches, originals precede Document Check on that choice, Assessment waits for identity and the document path, and the supplement loop returns through Document Check and Assessment before another Legal Review. The code matches that structure and uses the expected constructors. The soundness call is not reliable: `check_soundness` is unpacked as a pair inside a bare `except`, so a boolean return becomes a swallowed failure rather than a reported check. `SilentTransition` is unused. Free XOR still stands in for statutory choice; that limitation is stated, but the model can still play out illegal combinations unless guards are applied outside the net.

Smaller slips accumulate. Section 4 equates the 38% no-original share with digital claims. Site A before is called a strict sequence despite 8% observed overlap. “Due legal congestion” is broken phrasing. None of these overturns the `conditional go`, which is the right decision and is tied to sensible expansion and stop rules. The deductions are for inconsistent quantities, a muddled causal graph, and a pilot whose sample size, duration, and interference controls do not fit Site B’s volume or shared specialized pools.
