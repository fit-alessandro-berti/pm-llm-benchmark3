The memo is numerically careful and covers every required section, but several interpretations stay soft where the logs and rates conflict, so it is not nearly flawless.

The phase metrics, fitness figures, and cited DFG frequencies check out, including triage 2,500 / 1,600 / 1,000, pack-to-release 1,650, pack-to-unscanned dispatch 1,800, release-to-unscanned dispatch 1,450, and the T4 counts 1,400 / 1,200 / 2,300. T3 is correctly labeled as a hypothesis in the absence of a DFG. Cycle-time, exception, rework, and cancellation paths are tracked through T4, and the audit tail is treated as open workload. The redesign does include triggers, triage, product-level minimum controls, point-of-work evidence, backlog rules, and a compliance close that is separate from invoice close. Hospital priority is not allowed to waive authorization, custody, temperature, or dual control.

The serious gap is abductive. Pattern F is described as producing fewer controlled-substance errors while the same table notes residual dual-control gaps. Those claims are never reconciled with the measured rise in missing dual control from 0.2% to 2.9%, or with the manual-pack split: 1,800 packs go straight to unscanned dispatch and only 1,650 enter temporary release. About 1,450 of those released orders still dispatch without a scan, so F mostly feeds G. The answer never states that relationship. Likewise, unscanned dispatch has 3,250 incoming traversals and only 2,600 outgoing phone proofs. That leaves a large displayed gap with neither a scan nor phone proof, which is stronger evidence of a missing custody record than the qualitative warning given for G.

The “evidence of resilience” cell for C and G records continued movement and dispatch. Under the stated standard, throughput with an eligibility or custody control missing is not evidence of resilience. The Retire decisions partly correct this, but the column still makes the error the constraint forbids. G’s Retire label is also inconsistent with the later model, which still dispatches without a system scan when a contemporaneous signed handoff exists. That is a modification of G. B, J, and K are marked Keep while their rationales add versioned sources, published rules, and lot-level replay. Those are material changes.

Section 3 never places all of A–L into the requested three groups. K is grouped with real-time resilience even though it is post-outage repair, and L is correctly denied equivalence to real-time compliance without being classified. Spreadsheet invoice hold is read as retention for follow-up, yet its only displayed outgoing edge is 2,100 transitions to END. Backlog prioritization cancels 450 of 1,500 orders in T4, and compliance holds rise from 700 on 42,000 baseline orders to 1,300 on 35,000 stabilization orders. Neither rate is used. The window-length warning is apt, but intake is not restated per day, so the raw case counts remain easy to misread. Hospital p90 moving from 15h to 21h, and a 91% fill rate, are also thinner in the T2 risk narrative than the median and fill figures.

These are interpretation and classification defects, not missing sections or invented rates. They are enough to keep the answer well below a near-flawless score.
