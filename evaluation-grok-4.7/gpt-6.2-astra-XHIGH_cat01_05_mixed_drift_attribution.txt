The diagnosis is numerically careful, temporally separated, and restrained, but several local slips keep it short of nearly flawless.

The baseline deltas, L1 fragment distances, 44 pp supplier-mix bound (11 / 0.25), and senior-edge bound (7 × 41/26 ≈ 11 versus 22) all check out. March is correctly treated as a semantic/provenance break: raw fitness 0.93 → 0.55, mapped recovery of 0.35, fragment distance 3, and almost unchanged cycle time. Later rework, waiting, and the senior branch are not collapsed into that logging event. Confidence levels, mediation, and revision conditions are explicit. Sections 5–7 and the seven decision rules are usable and do not share one threshold.

Deductions:

- April is a weak second logging break. Mapped-minus-raw fitness widened from 0.35 to 0.40 because raw fitness fell from 0.55 to 0.51 while mapped fitness rose only 0.01. That is a larger discrepancy, and a thin basis for “migration stabilization.” June’s missingness move, 5.5% to 6.2%, sits on a smooth post-March climb from 4.8% to 6.5%.
- The Part A rule encodes that weakness. An absolute missingness change of 0.6 pp flags December (6.0 → 5.4) as logging/provenance, and a fitness-gap change of 0.04 flags April. July → August, the pre-renewal tail peak (median +0.8 h, p90 +6 h), produces no performance flag. The printed caveat limits the damage; the screen still disagrees with the prose.
- The 13.05 h median anchor is July versus the January–February baseline, while the 4.8 h → 5.4 h service comparison is July–August. The component table stays qualitative, which is appropriate, but those windows are not the same contrast.
- “Percentage differences below are percentage points” does not describe the cases, hours, or 0–1 fitness rows. Row labels repair most of the ambiguity.
- “After May, both legs strengthen, then partially recede in September” compresses a May onset that keeps rising into July (12/11 → 24/23 → 29/28, then 20/19). “21 loop entries” in December names the request leg, not a case-level loop count.
- Part B sorts stably on `event_seq`, then calls `format_dataframe` and discovery routines that reorder by case and timestamp alone. Tied timestamps can still scramble. The duplicate-open and alias handling otherwise preserves the source frame, and the PM4Py call names match the simplified API.

Word counts, required columns, segment coverage, and the seven-rule format are satisfied. The attribution itself holds up.
