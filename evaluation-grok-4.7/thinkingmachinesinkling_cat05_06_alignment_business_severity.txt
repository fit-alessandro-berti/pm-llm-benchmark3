7.5

The business analysis is strong and mostly faithful to the controls, but it is not nearly flawless. Sections 1–6 correctly separate edit distance from exposure, treat `T(tau)` as a fit, and identify CL-609 and CL-610 as zero-cost policy failures. The P1/P2 split is right: CL-609 breaches only the auto-approve conditions, while CL-610’s bank change independently requires fraud screening and manual approval. Telemetry, retries, label drift, denial, and withdrawal are not treated as payment-control failures.

The main failures are in certainty, consistency, and the required code.

CL-602’s policy column states a severe P2 breach as fact. The log only shows that `Fraud Screen` is absent; the verbal check is uncorroborated, so this is missing evidence, not proof the screen never occurred. Section 4 is more careful than Section 2. CL-604 is similar: `Fraud Screen` appears only as `M`, so it is absent from the log entirely, not merely missing “at pay time.” CL-608’s “P4 likely satisfied” runs ahead of the mutable text link, which may fail the retained-linked-evidence condition. Section 1 calls pre-approval payment irreversible, while CL-604’s immediate action is to attempt reversal.

The Python block is not reliably executable. The dataframe has no timestamp and is never passed through `format_dataframe` or `parse_event_log_string`, so `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` should fail. The sample traces drop the log-only moves that create the stated costs. The summary table hardcodes CL-607’s amount as 0, labels CL-606 as a P2 gap even though fraud screening and manual approval are synchronous, and aggregates policy flags rather than move patterns. Raw cost and severity stay in separate columns, but that does not complete the reproduction checklist.
