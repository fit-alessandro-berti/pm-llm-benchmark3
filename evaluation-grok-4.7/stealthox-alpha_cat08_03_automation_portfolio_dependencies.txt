The recommended portfolio (A5, A6, A9, A10), the decision to keep A8/A11 behind upstream controls, and the treatment of analyst overload, audit trails, and the two-integration cap are coherent and mostly grounded in the DFG, resource network, and control findings. The required trade-off against automating the highest-frequency work first is not. Section 7 fuses Receive application (3 min, 0.2h wait, 72% already digital, portal/clerks) with Register/A1 (8 min, 1.5h wait, back office) and then uses both “28% of 3-minute tasks” and “~0.8 FTE” in the same argument. Those cannot describe one activity, and the 72% figure does not apply to registration.

That error sits on top of other breaks in the reasoning. P2 claims an earlier collateral trigger even though the dependency map says that trigger needs A2, which P2 does not include. P1’s integration strain omits A10 after A10 was assigned a deep core slot. A9 is said to need a committee-decision record, but only 3,650 of 7,600 contract preparations directly follow a committee decision. Expected effects state unsupported magnitudes (44h to the high 20s, 75 min to 25–30 min, 650 loops to under 300, 8.5% to under 4%) despite the qualitative-rating constraint. The candidate table is in ID order, not ranked. Deferring entity mismatch is disclosed, but the effect of that 31h, 82% critical-path block on the KYC gate inside A10 is never carried into the claimed signing-time gain.
