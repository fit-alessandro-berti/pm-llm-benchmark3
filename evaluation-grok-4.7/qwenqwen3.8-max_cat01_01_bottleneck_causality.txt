The answer is strong on the core pattern: the crude Orion penalty reverses within every complexity stratum, standardization to the pooled complexity mix flips the sign, and that reversal is not treated as identification. Queue time is separated from review service, completed-case selection is explicit, and the recommendation correctly blocks a blanket 50% reassignment in favor of a limited pilot plus a capacity change. Arithmetic is largely auditable and accurate.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 1 puts compliance on the total-effect and mediation paths of resource group, while the DAG and later sections correctly treat compliance requirement as a pre-treatment, policy-driven confounder. Shift and arrival load point directly at `resource_group`, bypassing `routing_rule`, which conflicts with assignment by the routing engine. Rank 1 is given to a harmful Orion effect that the same answer’s evidence mostly contradicts. Within-stratum service gaps of about 0.1–0.4 hours, including cells with two cases, are used as supportive evidence. The shift-adjustment test treats attenuation as proof of confounding and does not say the same result would occur if shift were a mediator. The day-only pilot cannot identify the night, high-complexity caseload the proposal would actually move, and the hypothesis-2 prediction is worded as an effect of zero rather than a negative Orion contrast.
