The answer covers every required section and respects most hard gates, but it is not a reliable, auditable policy. The scoring rule and the C001–C016 roster diverge, several mechanisms do not do what the text claims, and a few assignments are unsafe given the stated evidence.

The largest defect is that the published algorithm cannot produce the published roster. Urgency is identical for every eligible resource, so the 0.30 weight never changes who is chosen, yet section 4 says urgency weighting is what controls SLA risk. Training has weight 0.05 and there is no repair pass, so the rule cannot enforce the 20% normal/low training share. The 2/9 = 22% result is a manual annotation: C009 loses to R3 under the formula (skill 3 and a better workload score outweigh R1’s training bonus), and C015 is shadowing on a case R8 already wins as owner and skill-3 security. There is no case order, no update of open cases or hours as the batch fills, and no score for a supervised pair. “Subtract 0.2” never says whether it hits the resilience term or the total. Empty-feasible-set handling says to relax weights; an empty filter is unchanged by reweighting. Batching is used for C006/C012 and never specified. Continuity for a new case (`none`) and for `reassignment_count > 2` is undefined or case-constant, so it does not affect the choice. Complexity never enters the score, and no threshold defines “high complexity.”

Checked against that formula, C003 should go to R3 (only eligible diagnosis skill 3; R4 is correctly excluded as high overtime and non-urgent), not to R8 at skill 2. C014 should go to R3 or R8 (rework skill 3), not to R10 at skill 2. C010 follows the formula onto R6 and thereby breaks the stated resilience goal: R6 also receives urgent C002 (2h) and urgent C011 (4h) while holding 5 open cases and 6.5 hours. C001 is allowed by a literal reading of supervision rules and is still a bad decision. R3’s security skill is 1, the SLA is 3h, and the given evidence says weak-skill continuity adds about 3.3h of service time. The same R3, with 6 open cases and 6.0 hours, is also given high-priority C007 (6h, complexity 0.81, already 3 reassignments) and diagnosis supervision of C009. That stack is not feasible, and moving C003 to R8 is an ad-hoc patch (“R3 already loaded”) rather than a rule. R8 is then supervising the 3h enterprise review while also taking C003 and C015. The historical 2.7h handover penalty versus 3.3h weak-skill penalty is never applied numerically.

Constraint reading is mostly right: high overtime resources R2, R4, and R9 get no new work; approval is skill 2–3; no unsupervised skill-1 resource gets urgent or high work; the batch cap of 4 is met. Continuity is upgraded from “preferred” to a hard rule, which locks in C001. “No documented approval-trainee plan” is inaccurate: R4 and R8 have approval as training priority but skill 0, so they cannot prepare or finalize. C015 never states that R5 and R10 have security skill 0 and must not perform the review. Section 6’s security/approval bottleneck set includes R10, whose security skill is 0 and approval skill is 1. The handover indicator counts every resource change between events, which mixes ordinary activity handoff with ownership change even though `current_owner` and `reassignment_count` exist. Baseline comparison is directional only; specialist-only continuity is overstated as uniformly weak, because owners who are already specialists would be kept.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested discovery and performance functions, but it is not runnable as written. `format_dataframe` is given `resource_key`, which is not a parameter of that function. `filter_case_performance` is called with `min_case_duration` / `max_case_duration` and an ellipsis. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied after `convert_to_event_log`, although it expects a dataframe. The worker-facing explanation is clear and non-punitive, and it overstates the role of the deadline, which the scorer does not use to separate resources.
