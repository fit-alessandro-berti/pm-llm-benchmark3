The design is statistically serious and usually correct: rejected, withdrawn, and transferred cases are competing outcomes, missing labels are not coded as non-default, the 48,000-case partition and 12.5% versus 5.625% distinction are right, and the 13 October 2025 maturity bound is real (15 October 2025 plus 125 days is 17 February 2026, so no conservatively complete post-migration default cohort exists). The leakage table, temporal split, and refusal to invent service time from one timestamp are sound.

The primary estimand is not closed. Activation within 180 days and default within 90 days after activation are different clocks. At day 180 an account activated on day 100 is neither still in process nor resolved as default or non-default, but that residual state is absent from the reported probability list. Cause-specific hazards are also not shown to be turned into that joint cumulative incidence.

The skeleton then breaks rules the prose states. `cohort_status` is joined into `features` even though it encodes eventual default, competing outcomes, and censoring, while the leakage assert only checks prefix columns and is tautological (`timestamp <= max(timestamp)`). `risk_score` uses first and last rows, not the latest non-null score at or before prediction time. Prefixes shorter than k=3 stay in the primary table. Simultaneous terminal events are silently ordered, not quarantined. The train/validation cut compares a UTC timestamp with the string `"2025-01-01"`. Lineage splitting, origin-specific `label_available_at` gates, and the 180-day landmark are described and then not built.
