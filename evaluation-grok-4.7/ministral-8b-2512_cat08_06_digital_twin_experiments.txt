The write-up covers the required sections and names the right phenomena (seasonality, calendars, architect bursts, batching, tails, holdout). It is not a usable design: several quantities contradict the supplied log summary, waiting time is never identified, the simulation frame fights non-stationarity, and the PM4Py code does not follow PM4Py and would not run.

**Grounding errors.** Monday is described as 2.2× a baseline rate; the evidence is Monday versus Wednesday only. Public notice on about 30% of new-residential cases cannot sit beside an 18% variant inside a 28% case-type share (about 64% of that type). A 10% appeal chance on complex cases cannot sit beside an 8% appeal/rework variant inside a 10% case-type share. Planning review is given an 80% waiting share; median wait is 6 days and median service is 3.5 hours, so waiting is about 98% of that pair. `application_value` is treated as a stand-in for building area. Rework chances (5%, appeals only on complex cases) are not estimated from loop frequencies or variant shares. The objection rate of 5% is not calibrated so that a 14-day floor mixed with extended durations reproduces the public-notice distribution. Objection status is also placed on the case as `objection_flag` even though those events are absent and duration is only a proxy.

**Simulation design.** Warm-up as 30% of the run, plus an extra 6 months “for steady state,” assumes a steady state this seasonal system does not have. Finite-horizon replications aligned to weekday and season, initialized from observed WIP, are the matching setup. M/M/1 and M/G/1 are the wrong checks for multi-server, calendar-limited, batched, non-stationary queues. “Hippocratic modeling” is not a heavy-tail method. Pareto applicant delays are stated with a median and a p90 of 14 days that the log summary does not give, and the infinite-mean region of Pareto is ignored. Whether waiting is an input distribution or an output of queues and calendars is unresolved, so resource constraints can be double-counted or validated in a circle. The baseline resource table already district-batches inspections and points at D3, so the baseline contains the intervention. D7 is specified to reshape Monday arrivals, while the assumptions say D1–D10 leave base demand unchanged. Planners are one pool for review and decision drafting; that shared contention is missing. The process-tree order, the decision-drafting join, the redo labels `Completeness rework` and `Inspection rework` (the excerpt repeats the original activities), appeals, and attribute-conditioned XOR choices are never reconciled. Council weeks and holidays stay “external” with no estimation path.

**Experiments and decisions.** The matrix lists D1–D10, the D1–D4 bundle, and three stresses, but almost every “level” is one invented effect size (30% fewer missing-document loops, 30-day aging cutoff, +20% objections with an unclear base). There is no ablation strong enough to support the synergy claim, and no common random numbers. D5 is “more parallel legal work” rather than a control-flow move of the legal check. D8 is priority by district rather than triage. D9 can shorten a notice that has a 14-day legal floor. Fire review (2 FTE, Tuesday–Thursday only) never appears as a stress. SLA cutoffs sit at or above the reported baseline medians and p90s, so they do not separate policies. Simple-renovation rework under 10% conflicts with the 19% missing-document variant. Robustness, fairness, utilization, and backlog thresholds are undefined as selection rules (ratio versus Gini, case-mix adjustment, pass versus fail, peak versus off-peak). Applicant burden and D2 compliance risk are trade-offs in the matrix and then drop out of the criteria. Fifty replications are declared “sufficient” for a 95% median interval without a variance estimate; credible intervals are named with no posterior.

**Validation.** Replay, conformance, and generative simulation are collapsed into one row. A KS test is a weak tail check. Utilization is checked against HR calendars, which do not contain utilization, and start timestamps are already missing. The bottleneck example ties D3 to planning review. The held-out window is not specified as a seasonal temporal split.

**Code versus the stated PM4Py conventions.** `load_log_excerpt` builds columns of length 21, 20, 18, and 13, so the DataFrame constructor fails. P001 is given 14 case ids and 13 activities; P002’s attributes are repeated 5 times, not 7; `requires_fire` and `requires_public_notice` do not match the excerpt. `format_dataframe` is called with an `attributes` map it does not take, and the result is used as an event log (`.get_case_ids()`, `log[case]`). Imports and calls to `variant_discovery`, `performance_discovery`, and `dfg_discovery` do not match PM4Py; `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return a graph plus start and end sets, and `view_dfg` needs all three. The estimation section adds further non-APIs: `discover_dependencies`, `discover_performance_metrics`, `discover_loop_patterns`, `discover_variants`, and `filter_events_by_calendar`. Inter-event gaps are labeled `service_time`, so service and waiting stay collapsed. Arrival spacing is an unsorted `.diff().mean()` rather than `get_case_arrival_average` on timestamps ordered by case start. The process-tree string is rejected as unparsable and replaced with `None`; that notation is in range for `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, and the real limits (data-blind XOR, redo labels absent from the log, no resources or calendars) go unstated. The clerk calendar is a daily midnight range, so `hour >= 8` is never true. Environmental reviewers are omitted from the resource dict. There is no event calendar, so non-stationary arrivals, office hours, and cross-case batching have no execution semantics, and `simulate` takes no intervention argument.

Checklist coverage is real. The parameter estimates, control-flow semantics, experiment levels, uncertainty plan, and skeleton are not reliable enough to compare these interventions.
