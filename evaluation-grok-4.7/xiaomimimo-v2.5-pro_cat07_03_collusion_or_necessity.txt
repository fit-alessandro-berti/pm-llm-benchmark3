The prioritization is directionally sound: V-77x is correctly treated as the sharpest cluster, emergency channels are kept as plausible, routine pairs are not treated as suspicious merely because they co-occur, and the report does not assert fraud. Those strengths are undercut by repeated mixing of populations and by a formal wording error.

The 42 Create-PO / Approve-PO four-eyes cases (same resource) are assigned to Buyer_Carla → Approver_Gita and given the 88% emergency rate from the separate 126-case co-working subgroup. The scenario only says “many” of those 42 are emergency-flagged, and the resources also include Buyer_Dan. Section 3 then calls the whole 126-case Carla/Gita cluster a four-eyes violation. Section 6 states that 226 cases bypassed separation between PO creation and approval. That figure is not in the evidence (126 + 104 = 230), and those subgroups are mostly different-person handovers, which is the control operating, not a four-eyes breach. The same slippage appears when the 9 same-person bank/payment violations are described as “the same individual or tightly paired individuals,” and when the 17-minute median (16-case fast subgroup) is applied to all 44 Lena/Kim cases (pair median 19 minutes). The 11-minute Carla/Gita handover median is also used to lean the separate Gita → Carla → Gita loop toward rubber-stamping.

The claim that same-person retrospective justifications “likely” missed the 3-working-day limit by weeks treats quarter-close batch entry as proof of lateness. The recorded same-resource median is 9 minutes, and 12 of 17 timestamps are migration artifacts, so original timing is unknown. “Documented rush channel” is stronger than “known rush channel.”

Section 6 is also about 400 words against a 200–300 word requirement. The PM4Py sketches cover the requested analyses, but they do not implement the 3-day, different-resource justification rule they describe, and they invent source-system names (`standard_erp`, `automated_import`) not present in the log.
