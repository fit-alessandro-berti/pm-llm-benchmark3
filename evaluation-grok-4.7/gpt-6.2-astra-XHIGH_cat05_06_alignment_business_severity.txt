The analysis is complete, numerically careful, and unusually disciplined about evidence versus occurrence. All twelve cases are interpreted, zero-cost policy failures and the silent tau are handled correctly, and the code’s policy flags match the narratives, including the EUR 574,500 sum and the separation of CL-609 from any P2 trigger. The main defects are internal consistency and a few overprecise claims.

Severity bands overlap. Level 5 is defined as any release with strong evidence of a preventive-control failure, which also describes CL-609 and especially CL-610, yet both are scored 4 while CL-610 still receives a level-5 tracing response. The case reasoning is intelligible, but the written scale does not decide the boundary it creates. “P1 fails twice” is loose: two conditions of one rule failed. P3 is also stretched from payment-before-approval to “an earlier but inapplicable auto approval,” although the code comment distinguishes those mechanisms more carefully than the narrative.

“Including CL-609” treats a worked example as a member of the monthly set of four synchronous violations. That membership is not stated. UI telemetry and tau are grouped despite different mechanisms. CL-607 is scored 1 while denial validity is still an open check. The CL-604 test specifies the inculpatory timing result more clearly than the exculpatory one. Priority labels P0–P3 collide with policy names P1–P4, though context usually resolves it.

Section 6 and the hypotheses are strong: missing events are not treated as proof of omission, retries and external documents are not over-read, and raw cost is kept independent of severity. The PM4Py block reconstructs only log-consuming moves, preserves the supplied unit costs, avoids using synthetic timestamps for P2–P4, and does not turn severity into a formula. Residual execution risk is version-dependent diagnostics formatting, which the answer discloses rather than asserts away.
