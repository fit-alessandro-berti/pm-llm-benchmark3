The answer is strong on the central identification problem and is not close to flawless. The crude-versus-stratified reversal, the Kitagawa split (about +9.0 h composition and −2.3 h within stratum), the wait-versus-service split, overlap failure, collider/selection roles, and the refusal to treat complexity stratification as identification are all handled carefully, and the main stratum figures check out. Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors and a few places where the decision language outruns the identification claims.

The required decomposition table is not fully auditable. Night versus day is given as 22.4 h versus 13.9 h; the case sums are about 21.7 h (369.6/17) and 14.0 h (181.7/13). Compliance-required versus not is given as 26.3 h versus 12.8 h; the correct means are about 26.8 h (348.2/13) and 11.9 h (203.1/17). The directions survive, but these are presented as calculated results, not labeled approximations. Smaller slips sit nearby: Nova’s own-mix mean is written 15.25 rather than 15.24, and the within-stratum piece is alternately −2.32 and −2.31.

Section 1 overweights a mechanism the later sections correctly doubt. The certified-pool story is offered as the reading of a gap that “widens with complexity,” but Low and Medium are essentially flat (−2.14 h versus −2.21 h), and Low cases are faster at Orion even though nothing in the evidence says they require certification. Hypothesis 2 then states as the mechanism that only certified reviewers may take High or compliance cases; the prompt only says Orion has more certified reviewers and is usually given difficult work. The same section predicts roughly +2 to +3.5 h per moved case. That treats the stratified contrast as a transportable effect after the answer has already said shift, load, residual difficulty, and completion selection block that interpretation.

Hypothesis 3’s supporting column does not support a harmful Orion effect. Orion’s High wait of about 19.5 h is long, but Nova’s High wait is longer (22.75 h), and the higher crude rework rate is the case-mix pattern already attributed to confounding. Hypothesis 6’s “despite covering 25% nights” misreads `night_shift_pct`: four night cases is exactly 25% of Nova’s 16 cases, not a discrepancy. “Shift and load are inseparable” is too absolute; Nova’s day cases do vary in load, and the claimed 0.8 h High-versus-Normal contrast is an unspecified subset (Low day, including the rework case, is about 0.55 h).

The learning plan has a real design hole. Phase 1 randomizes Low and Medium cases to Nova while phase 2, overlapping from week 5, adds certified hours and compliance slots at Nova. The pilot then does not estimate group assignment under current staffing, and the write-up does not treat that interference as a threat. `cycle_time -> case_completed` is also labeled as supported by the completed-case sample; the sample shows conditioning, not that duration causes completion.

The graph, role table, falsification columns, code specification, and five data gaps are otherwise at a high level. The score is held down by wrong auditable margins, an executive prediction that contradicts the answer’s own identification limits, and a pilot that contaminates the comparison it is meant to learn.
