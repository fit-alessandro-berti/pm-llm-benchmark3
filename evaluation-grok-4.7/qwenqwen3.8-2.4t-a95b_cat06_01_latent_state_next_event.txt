The case states, obligation splits, and refusal to copy the directly-follows graph are strong. C6-A versus C6-B is correctly split by severity, injury, lot linkage, and duplicate similarity. C6-C does not treat Technical Investigation → Disposition as allowed while a reportability signal is open, and it does not force Supplier Analysis across different lots. C6-D uses the worklist and the source-system attachment instead of treating missing log events as proof the work did not occur. C6-E correctly blocks fast close and keeps Supplier Analysis open for a lot tied to six complaints. Intervals are broad, evidence for and against is separated, and the cross-case discussion is specific.

The main flaw is internal inconsistency on whether an activity is process-enabled. For C6-A, Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment are marked not enabled until Evidence Review completes, even though the reasoning says they may already be running concurrently and the historical log shows triage leading directly into technical investigation. That overstates a sequencing rule the model does not impose, and it conflicts with the required distinction between an enabled in-progress task and the next visible completion. C6-B then marks closure as not enabled while its own alternative state is disposition-ready, whereas C6-E marks Supplier Analysis as enabled on a similar “maybe already opened” premise.

Two rankings are also weaker than the rest. Fast Close is a poor third outcome for C6-B after the case has already left triage and entered an evidence cycle. Authority Notification as C6-C’s third next event skips two missing predecessors (assessment and disposition) and crowds out the stated “root cause not confirmed” branch. Skip-the-predecessor probabilities for normal-feed cases also sit above the stated 8–15% delay rate, and “Supplier Analysis is mandatory” for C6-A is a bit stronger than the evidence, because the three similar complaints are not explicitly tied to that lot.
