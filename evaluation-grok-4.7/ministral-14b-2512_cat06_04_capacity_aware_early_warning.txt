The answer has the required six-section shape and a few correct qualitative calls, but it is not a deployable policy. The scoring identity is wrong, the portfolio exceeds capacity, and several facts and safeguards point the wrong way.

The stage gate is right: I-03 is correctly ineligible because the swarm has almost no effect after Diagnosis Complete. I-05 is correctly suppressed as a repeated refresh with no new event, and I-07 is correctly treated as re-opened by the new customer log. Segment calibration is directionally right in the rule text (SME up, Strategic Enterprise down, Public Sector unadjusted). The monitoring section names operational, predictive, causal, capacity, and fairness checks, including an experiment and numeric recalibration triggers.

Those points do not rescue the decision math. The score uses adjusted breach probability times full breach value, minus costs. That is the observational expected loss, not the intervention benefit. The prompt’s own sketch is absolute risk reduction times value, minus swarm cost and false-alarm burden. Ranking on \(p\) is exactly the weak approach the task warns against. It is why I-07 (€5,000 value, midpoint reduction about 0.26, direct net benefit on the order of €800 before spillover) and I-01 are Tier 1, while higher midpoint-value cases I-04, I-08, and I-09 are told to wait. No numeric score is shown, and the displayed formula is missing its operators, so the €5,000 / €2,000 tier cuts are not auditable.

Capacity is infeasible. Four slots exist. The answer assigns I-01, I-02, I-06, and I-07 and also reserves one slot. That is five claims on four slots, with no rule for which case is dropped if the reserve is kept. “Wait” has no review time or trigger.

Factual and calibration errors are material. I-01 is Public Sector, but the table adds the SME offset (0.61 + 0.065). I-05’s false-alarm burden is €100, not €2,000. Non-Native-Language Support is given an invented effect band of 0.05–0.15; I-09’s stated band is 0.18–0.30. SME underprediction is described as causing over-intervention and overstated benefit. Unadjusted underprediction does the opposite. The persistence rule requires a +0.10 rise and new evidence, yet I-07’s probability falls from 0.75 to 0.72 and is still swarmed. I-06’s jump depends on an imputed live-queue field and is still given a scarce slot. Point midpoint offsets are then treated as known probabilities.

The impact table does not match the three required scenarios. “Breaches avoided” is written as 4, then 2–3, then 1. Those are case counts. Even the upper reductions on the four chosen cases sum to about 1.2 expected breaches, not 4. Optimistic benefit books €9k+€25k+€15k+€5k = €54k, which is full contract value conditional on certain avoidance, not reduction times value (roughly €15k before costs at the top of those ranges). Base €34k and adverse €9k are unexplained partial sums. False-alarm burdens are omitted. Spillover is not scaled by slots used: \(0.02 \times 6 \times €20k = €2.4k\), but the base cell says €6k–€12k. Adverse congestion of 15–30 minutes per slot never enters the arithmetic. A positive adverse net of about €10k–€27k is then described as spillover dominating. Optimistic confidence is labeled High even though the effects are explicitly non-causal and uncertain.

Temporal policy has a harmful rule: a case that breaches after a swarm becomes permanently ineligible. That denies later help to hard cases and worsens the label bias it claims to stop. Auto-escalation after four hours above 0.70 ignores remaining slots, value, and spillover.

Fairness covers the required topics, but several controls are incoherent. Capping “spillover harm” at 50% for Non-Native-Language Support does not identify an effect or a fairness constraint. A stricter SME score cutoff fights the upward calibration adjustment and is justified by the wrong bias direction. Prioritizing \(p \times\) time-to-deadline does not reduce spillover; spillover depends on how many seniors are pulled and how congested the queue is. One high-\(p\) slot per week still starves moderate-probability, low-value cases. Non-monetary duties for Public Sector and the possible regulatory arrival are not given a constraint separate from euros.

Monitoring is the best section, but the identification plan is weaker than it looks. A matched swarmed-versus-not comparison is not a difference-in-differences, and assignment also depends on value, stage, and overrides. A 20% randomization only inside Tier 2 does not estimate the effect of the Tier 1 cases actually treated, and it does not handle interference unless congestion is clustered. “Thirty percent of swarms show no breach reduction” is not observable case by case, because the counterfactual breach is not seen. The untreated/treated breach ratio of 1.5 mixes selection with harm: treated cases are chosen because they look worse. Queue-feature failure, the two-specialist constraint, and label contamination from logging post-swarm outcomes are thin or absent. Subtracting a bias and also raising the probability cutoff double-counts the same error.

Overall, the answer would withhold the post-diagnosis case and would not keep paging an unchanged alert, but it would spend today’s capacity on a high-probability, low-value set, overstate benefits by treating contract value as expected harm avoided, and leave the reserve slot unfunded. That is structurally responsive and analytically unsafe.
