The write-up has the right method and a complete required structure, but it is not reliable on the decision sets that the diagnosis depends on. Three material faults keep it well below a high score.

The QC reconstruction is wrong at 10:32. C06 arrived at 10:25 with `est_min` 6, so the waiting set is C01, C04, C05, and C06. Dynamic SPT/SEPT selects C06, not C04. The table omits C06, predicts C04, and then treats C02→C03→C04 as strong SPT evidence. That overstates the early SPT streak: 10:10 and 10:18 fit SPT, but 10:32 already breaks it if C06 was dispatchable. Section 4 inherits the error. “If C05 was dispatchable, SPT gains strong support” is false, because SPT would still have chosen C06. Choosing C04 rejects both pure SPT and priority/EDF. The same section also misses that by 10:18 C05’s slack was already negative (deadline 10:50, estimate 40), so an “override only once the SLA is endangered” story does not explain why C03 and C04 were still run first.

QA contradicts itself. The 09:32 table correctly says SPT selects A07, and A07 was chosen. The following paragraph says that 09:32 rejects SPT. It does not. It rejects priority (A08) and is tied between FIFO and SPT. The clean three-way split is the omitted 09:05 release: FIFO selects A05, SPT selects A07 (9 < 15), and priority selects A08. That is the best QA evidence and it is not in the decision table. A06’s 09:20 start is also treated too loosely as appointment tolerance. A05 occupied the resource from 09:05 through 09:20, so the five-minute lateness is exactly non-preemption.

QB calls the 09:31 SPT comparison ambiguous. It is not. B01 had already consumed 14 of its 45 minutes, so expected remaining is 31, and even the unreduced estimate of 45 loses to B03’s 12. SPT selects B03; resuming B01 is clear evidence against SPT, which the answer’s own “estimates match realized time” assumption makes sharper, not fuzzier. The table also skips 10:00, when B05 (priority 1) is taken over older and shorter B04 and over paused B01. That episode cuts against the claim that selection among waiting jobs is FIFO-like. Scan delay cannot repair 09:48: a delayed scan makes true arrival earlier than 09:46, so B05 is more present, not less. Only a separate, unobserved release delay can save FIFO there.

QD’s skill conclusion is directionally right, but the scarce-resource explanation of D-F’s 11:36–12:20 idle does not work. D05 needs 30 minutes and only 24 remain before the meeting, so the meeting already pushes D05 to 12:20. D10 needs 12 minutes, fits in that gap, and is priority 1. Idling does not protect D05. The FIFO column’s “start D05 at 11:36” ignores that same calendar. The strongest within-pool test is also missing: at 11:25 D-M takes D04 over D08, which is both priority 1 and shorter (8 < 10). D07-before-D10 does reject priority and SPT, but it does not separate FIFO from “do the job only D-X can do.”

What keeps this above a weak score is real process-mining work, not labeling. QA as FIFO plus a single appointment override at 08:20 is well supported, and A03 is correctly shown to be neither oldest, nor priority 1, nor shortest. QB’s urgent-interrupt versus later FIFO/resume split, the uselessness of B07’s appointment, and preemptive-resume rather than restart are all earned from the trace. Exact equality of every `est_min` with realized service is an important observation and is used honestly to down-rank estimate gaming. Priority-versus-EDF confounding, ranked data-quality alternatives, and the collection plan are specific to these ambiguities. The closing claim that strict priority is the most dangerous inference is the right cross-queue conclusion: priority loses inside QA, QB, QC, and QD once eligible sets are written down. Calling QD the most identifiable queue is defensible for skill constraints, though QA’s single-server FIFO tests are cleaner than the stated confidence gap (80 versus 70) implies.

Format, assumption labels, and uncertainty tone are in good shape. The grade is held down by a wrong eligible set on a core QC decision, a self-contradiction on SPT, an avoidable remaining-time error, and decision tables that omit the comparisons that would actually separate the competing rules.
