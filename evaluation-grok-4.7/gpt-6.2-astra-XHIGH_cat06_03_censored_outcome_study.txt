8.2

The design is statistically serious and gets the central traps right: default is not treated as observed for non-activated cases, activation is part of the estimand rather than a nuisance to delete, process time and label time are separated, and the cohort arithmetic accounts for all 48,000 starts. The six-way fixed-horizon target, the g-then-q factorization, MAR versus MNAR, and the decision boundary in section 8 are coherent and operationally usable. Date checks for 26 January 2025 and 13 September 2025 match the stated 155-day rule, and the primary as-of path in the code is unusually careful.

The validation write-up is the main weakness. “Train on starts before 1 November 2024 using information through 31 October” and “score validation starts through June 2025” read as larger labeled sets than the maturity rule allows. The code’s `buffered` mask corrects this, so prose and implementation disagree. Tuning on pre-policy validation and then refitting on that same pre-July data leaves no untouched pre-policy test; July–September degradation is therefore confounded with selection optimism and the policy change. Repeating origins only partly repairs that.

Smaller defects keep it short of excellent. At the exact 35-day boundary a case can be `buffered` while its missing feed is still “awaiting,” not overdue. `new_policy` is constant on the pre-July refit and constant the other way on the test, so it is not identified. The 48-hour branch’s `c.index[elig]` indexing is unsafe, k-event and 48-hour landmarks are only counted rather than passed through the same label panel, and merging `get_prefixes_from_log` back on timestamps will break if PM4Py alters timestamp precision. The IPW Brier formula is Horvitz–Thompson without stabilization, and receipt weights that use post-activation covariates are not tied to a correctly specified q. The 30-day cutoff is declared rather than justified.

Section 9 is still a real pipeline: temporal splits, prefix truncation, label isolation, and leakage asserts are in the right places, and it does not invent a survival API. Those remaining gaps are implementation and validation-specification errors, not a biased cohort story.
