7.6

The written study design is strong and mostly faithful to the problem: the primary estimand is activation-conditional 90-day default risk with right censoring, rejected/withdrawn/open cases are not treated as observed non-defaults, the 48,000-case accounting adds up, leakage rules for the named fields are sound, and the temporal split is aligned to the policy change and migration. The alternative policy and multi-state targets are distinguished from that estimand rather than blurred into it.

The implementation skeleton contradicts that design in ways that would recreate the biased outcome estimates the study is supposed to avoid. `survival_time_days` is always extraction minus activation, including when `event_indicator` is 1, so every matured default is timed at or beyond day 90 and a survival fit of \(P(T_D \le 90)\) collapses. Cases with a permanently missing feed are marked matured once `activation + 125 days` has passed and then receive `event_indicator = 0`. The line that was meant to fill only true non-events never runs, because the indicator is initialized to 0/1 rather than missing. `terminal_status` is joined into `feat` even though it is determined after the prefix, and the leakage check does not flag it. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is called with `prefix_length`, which is not the public parameter name (`length`).

A smaller prose defect matches that sloppiness: the delayed-label example has expected availability on 2026-01-19 and extraction on 2026-02-15, so it does not illustrate the censoring rule stated immediately afterward. The post-migration test window is also presented as a normal holdout despite matured binary labels in that window being a thin, early-activation subset; survival evaluation is mentioned, but the selection is understated.
