8.1

The organizational analysis is strong, accurate, and well aligned with the task. Roles are not assigned from raw frequency alone; complexity, authority, substitutions, and shadow work are separated carefully, and the six required sections and table columns are all present. Resource counts, similarity figures, substitution episodes, and complexity medians match the evidence. Shadow roles are treated as useful but risky rather than improper, and temporary cover is not treated as permanent competence. Chen versus Dina, the temp pool, Nora, Erik, and Mateo are interpreted with the right distinctions.

The main defects are in the code. `complexity_counts` keeps the column `org:resource`, then is merged on `member`, so that join fails. `validate="one_to_one"` will also fail if one resource lands in more than one mined role. `format_dataframe` is called with `activity` and `timestamp` rather than `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`, and `discover_organizational_roles` is given `similarity_threshold` and `frequency_threshold`, which are not the PM4Py parameters. The comment also puts `case_complexity_score` on resource metadata even though the code reads it from events.

There is one material overstatement: Giorgio’s 72 pricing events are large enough that “no other permanent resource performs comparable volumes of A7” is too strong. The early claim that Hana’s rechecks “confirm” exclusive final authority is tighter than 13 of 21 rechecks support, although later sections correct that.
