The capacity analysis is the strongest part of the answer and is largely auditable. Expected service times, daily branch counts, the 2,721 versus 2,490 aggregate comparison, and the block loads (about 445, 586, 455, 651, 392, and 192 against 480, 600, 510, 570, 240, and 120) are coherent. Multipliers are treated as a lower bound rather than ignored, and the evening and Electrical/Software bottlenecks, including Chen’s absence, follow from the staffing calendar. Assumptions, the two objectives, and the U2 count-doubling interpretation are labeled.

The policy designs are operational rather than nominal: eligibility, reservation, overflow, preemption limits, and parallel-versus-sequential rules are specified, and the hybrid recommendation is justified by shock behavior and fairness rather than base-case mean delay. Monitoring attributes and the pilot design match the requested process-mining questions.

The quantitative comparison and the code are not at that standard. The scenario table is a set of judgmental ranges, while the code that was supposed to produce seeded metrics was not run and would not reproduce those ranges. In the code, resource load accumulates all day but is divided by one block’s capacity, so the hybrid overflow rule trips almost immediately and the policy collapses toward pooling. Activity timestamps do not respect shifts or breaks, so off-shift work is recorded. Preemption is a random penalty on lower-priority cases; it is not tied to an actual P1 interruption and does not free capacity for that P1. The stated 15–20% P1 reservation and the 30-minute overtime limit are not implemented. Skill congestion excludes handover and setup, while utilization includes them. Those are material execution gaps, not omitted details that the scaffold identifies.
