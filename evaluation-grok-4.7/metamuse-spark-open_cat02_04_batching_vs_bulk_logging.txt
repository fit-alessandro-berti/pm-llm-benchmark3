6.5

The write-up meets the required seven-part structure and gets the main cluster rankings mostly right: G1 as a real fixture batch, G2 as grouped start plus serial completion, G3 as deferred folder submission, G4 as a clean adjacent chain, G6 as a logging batch, G7 as quarter-hour censoring, and G8 as a blocked queue then a serial drain. It also keeps operational batches distinct from logging batches and treats PM4Py labels as temporal patterns. Those strengths are undercut by repeated factual and definitional errors.

The most serious miss is G5. A01–A04 are all active together from 13:12 to 13:20, so peak concurrency is 4, not 3. That exceeds the stated pool of three analysts, so the claim that observed concurrency “matches documented capacity” is the opposite of what the intervals show. “Concurrency rate 75%” is also undefined and does not match the pairwise overlaps (all 6 pairs overlap).

G3 invents a result. Inter-start gaps of about 7–8 minutes are an estimate under a serial hypothesis. The context never reports a 7-minute or 7.5-minute ground truth, and the same sentence treats 7 and 7.5 as the same figure.

G7 reverses the waiting-time sign. Arrival at 13:58 against a 14:00 start is a +2 minute wait. Negative waits are M02–M05, whose arrivals fall after the rounded start. The “true measure likely 2–4 minutes” claim is not supported. The comparison note of a 60–240 second recording lag is also wrong; recorded times run from 14:16 to 14:20, so the lag is 60–300 seconds.

G6’s mechanism is right, but the numbers are not. Observed waits run from 35 to 175 minutes, not 99–176, and the seven-case mean is about 103 minutes, not ~120. “True waits maybe under 60 minutes” is unlabeled speculation.

The PM4Py discussion misstates batch typing. Exact adjacency already groups G4 as a sequential batch at `merge_distance` 0. A larger merge distance does not turn different start and end times into a G1-style simultaneous batch. The printed comparison also says G1 has “zero overlap pairs,” while the code’s own overlap test would count all 6 pairs. The comparison is hardcoded commentary, not a join to `discover_batches` output, and “queue build-up” is only an arrival span plus a gap, not a queue-length series.

Smaller defects add up: garbled cells (“100%  54%”, “W210”), a 6.5 ± 0.8 minute service figure that matches neither (8+6+7+6)/4 nor the last three gaps, and a G2 correction that interval-censors the one start the group button actually explains. The redesign and final include/exclude rule are still usable, but the numerical and concurrency mistakes are large enough that the analysis cannot be treated as nearly reliable.
