6.7

The design is thorough and covers every required section, with real simulation literacy: non-stationary arrivals, calendars, latent objections, service/wait separation, CRN, finite-horizon replication, and an explicit refusal to treat uncalibrated policies as optimal. It is not nearly flawless. Several structural and quantitative errors are material.

The baseline control flow is wrong. The supplied tree runs planning/fire/environment in parallel, then optional public notice, then inspection, then decision drafting. “Drafting waits for those results” is precedence, not concurrency. Section 1 puts public notice and site inspection in the same parallel token set as planning, and Section 3 presents that AND-join as a correction of the tree. That changes cycle time, resource overlap, and which interventions can help. Inspection rework and completeness rework are also misidentified: the excerpt’s second completeness check follows receive-missing-documents and is not the tree’s separate “Completeness rework” redo, and a 9% variant share is not a per-visit rework probability.

Arrival volume is not identified. \(\lambda_0\) times a seasonal factor of 1.7 for half the year and a Monday factor of 2.2 does not integrate to 31,200 cases; the mean multiplier is well above 1. The skeleton never renormalizes. The abstract hour clock is not anchored to March, holidays, or council weeks, so those calendars cannot fire correctly. Warm-up of 180 days is shorter than the stated complex-case p90 of 260 days, and there is no seeded in-flight backlog. Empty-system deletion will bias the tail the decision criteria optimize. Paired-\(t\) intervals on means sit awkwardly beside the heavy-tail quantile story; BCa on 40 replication summaries is thin.

Policy parameters are asserted as if measured. About 65% of simple renovations eligible for D2, travel cuts of 35–45%, completeness-rework \(\times 0.40\), a 1.5 h environmental fast track, and SLA caps of 21/60/90/180 days are not in the log or the brief, yet Section 7 calls them municipal SLAs. D2 depends on prior violations, which are absent from the log and from the estimation plan. The D2 “uninspected defect” gate is outside the stated boundary, which ends at issue/reject, so the model cannot produce that metric. “Minimax regret” is used for a weaker rule (stay better than baseline under S1–S3). Sensitivity does not cover the largest invented effect sizes. Eighteen months cannot cleanly separate the 1.7\(\times\) season from trend; year-over-year stationarity is assumed anyway. District effects on planning waits and the scheduler who actually performs inspection scheduling are omitted. Issue-permit’s 0.5 day wait is collapsed into an instantaneous system task.

The code matches the requested PM4Py surface (`read_csv` via pandas, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average, and an explicit refusal to split service and wait without a start timestamp). Implemented policy logic contradicts the prose: D4 adds a permanent inspector, not a peak-month one, and D7 drops the Monday multiplier for every applicant instead of shifting top-20 architect bursts onto later weekdays. Objection shocks and the drafting join are not in the engine the comments claim. Placeholders would have been safer than wrong mechanics.

Validation and the experiment list (B0, D1–D11, three stresses, trade-offs) are the strongest parts. Those do not offset the baseline flow error, the arrival/warm-up bias, or decision thresholds the twin cannot identify.
