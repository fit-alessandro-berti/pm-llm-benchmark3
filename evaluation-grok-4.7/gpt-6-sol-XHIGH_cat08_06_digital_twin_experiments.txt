7.5

The design is coherent, constraint-aware, and strong on calendars, latent objections, service-versus-wait identification, paired comparisons, censoring, and non-stationary demand. It is not nearly flawless: a few methodological ambiguities and one PM4Py API error are material under a strict reading.

The skeleton’s main path is not consistent with PM4Py’s return conventions. `pm4py.discover_dfg` returns `(dfg, starts, ends)`, but `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns a single edge dictionary of performance statistics. Unpacking it as `performance_dfg, _, _` raises `ValueError` on this excerpt, so the required performance DFG is never produced. Timezone-aware timestamps are a second fragility: several PM4Py statistics paths assume naive datetimes. The pool table also fixes fire, environment, inspector, and legal work to 09:00–17:00 and treats “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” as both 2.0 FTE and a three-day week, which changes weekly capacity. Monday portal-clerk dedication, simple-renovation-only planners, council blackout weeks, holidays, and travel never appear in that resource state.

Event-log replay is the wrong check under that heading. The text compares newly generated paths with variants and conformance. Replay validation would drive the resource model with historical arrivals and case attributes and compare predicted enablement, waiting, and completion times with the log. Those are different tests. The later rule that a simulation must not finish work while a pool is closed also fights the excerpt: clerk, planner, fire, inspection, and legal timestamps fall on weekends. That discrepancy is noted earlier, but validation treats the HR calendar as already decisive.

Control flow is underspecified at the public-notice gate. Drafting is correctly held until every applicable result exists, yet the tree and P001 put the notice period before inspection scheduling. A join only at drafting still allows a site visit during the statutory notice window and would shorten the cycle time that D3 and D4 are meant to change. Appeal is required in the narrative and absent from the executable tree.

D9 is specified as identifying a fraction of eventual objections earlier. That is an oracle. A deployable early-warning policy needs a prospective false-positive and false-negative rate; false alerts are only named in the trade-off. The 65–85% utilization band treats spare capacity as a failure, so D2 and D4 can be penalized for relieving inspectors. An unweighted overall p90, and district or architect SLA gaps without case-mix stratification, confound mix with performance. The outer uncertainty loop does not say how many parameter draws are used, nor that resampling must be clustered by architect, district, and week. An ordinary case bootstrap would look too precise given the dependence the rest of the design insists on. It is also unspecified whether each policy is active during the 12-month warm-up or switched on only in the measurement window; those setups answer different rollout questions and produce different backlogs.

The estimation plan, assumption split, illustrative multi-level matrix, common random numbers, heavy-tail handling, completed-case bias warning, and refusal to treat a performance DFG or an unobserved objection as ground truth are all sound. The provisional SLAs and “no exact optimum” rule match the evidence. Those strengths support a high score; the API break and the replay, notice-gate, oracle, and uncertainty-aggregation gaps keep it well below a near-flawless mark.
