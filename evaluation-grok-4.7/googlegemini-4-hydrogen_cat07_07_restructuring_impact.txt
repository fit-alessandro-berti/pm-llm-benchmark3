7.5

The forecast covers every required section, keeps East distinct from North and South, and correctly treats utilization, tails, fairness, learning, and accountability as separate from mean cycle time. The recommendation is a monitored pilot with modifications, and it states what early averages cannot show. Those strengths are real. The score is held down by quantitative contradictions, an internally inconsistent pilot, and a PM4Py sketch that would not run.

The handover totals 343 and 359 match the two tables, and the mix shift is the right object of analysis: tech 36→41, approval 42→49, legal/risk 9→12, cross-team 6→3. Calling that shift “inflated nominal handovers” overstates a net rise of 16 events per 100 cases (+4.7%). The scarce-queue mix is the problem; the grand total is not a structural flaw of the same size. The opening sentence also labels 343→359 as “specialist and approval handovers,” which those sums are not.

The central scenario is arithmetically inconsistent. It assumes legal/risk volume rises 33% with no triage deflection, from bases of 93% and 91%, and then says effective utilization “exceeds 96%” with waits of 14–18h. 0.93 × 1.33 is about 1.24, so the queue is unstable and waiting time does not settle near 18h. The adverse scenario later says the same volume pushes arrival rate above 100%. Both cannot be the central case. The M/M/1 form \(W_q \propto \rho/(1-\rho)\) is also the wrong formula after the text has already framed the pool as M/M/3. Supervisor capacity is stated as a 33% cut (3→2) without utilization data; the three supervisors are not shown to be full-time approval servers.

“~60% of cases are low/medium complexity” is not in the log. 41 technical routings per 100 cases does not identify the complexity mix, and many of the other cases still enter the shared approval queue (49/100). Scenario hour figures (tech 5.5–7h, approval 4.5–5.5h, legal 14–18h, adverse waits of 24–40h) are presented as forecasts without a capacity model that could produce them. The 14-day SLA in the sketch cannot reproduce North’s 6.5% miss rate when North’s p90 is already 16.4 days.

The pilot contradicts itself. Phase 1 puts 30% of `N_Tech` into the shared overflow, then Phase 2 calls North an intact embedded-specialist control. North is already treated, so South-versus-North differences are confounded. Legal/risk is correctly kept out of an immediate volume expansion, and guardrails, segmentation, censoring, and rollback triggers are otherwise specific.

The code sketch calls the requested families of techniques, then breaks on contact with the library. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` returns a case-level feature table, and the sketch uses that return value as the event log for every later step. `get_service_time` returns a dict of activity durations and needs a start timestamp; dividing it by 3600 and calling it case-level service time is wrong. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` writes `@@waiting_time` / `@@service_time`, while the KPI block reads `@@case_waiting_time`, so the waiting-time branch never fires. Current handover, working-together, and subcontracting wrappers return NetworkX graphs, which have no `.connections`. `discover_performance_dfg` supplies a mean arc time, not the median and p90 claimed in the indicator table. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` on `team` double-counts cross-team cases in every team segment. Handover, working-together, and resource-similarity results are computed for both periods and never differenced.
