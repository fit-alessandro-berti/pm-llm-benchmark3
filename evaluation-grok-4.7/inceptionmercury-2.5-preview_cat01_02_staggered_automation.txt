The write-up uses the required headings and correctly rejects a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, but it is not a reliable causal assessment. Arithmetic, windows, signs, and the required code are wrong in ways that change the conclusion.

Section 5 does not match the panel. Alpha’s April–August p90 average is about 108.1 hours, not 106.1. Beta’s median post average is about 47.8, not 47.6, and its p90 post average is about 102.7, not 102.3. Delta’s April–August median average is about 40.5 (+6.0 versus January–March), not 39.3 (+4.8). Epsilon’s comparable change is about +4.4 hours, not +1.4. Gamma’s “post” value of 37.2 is July–August only, while the column is labeled April–August; that window hides the April–June rise to the mid-40s before adoption and the later drop. The claimed within-Alpha correlation of about −0.6 is not shown and is too strong for the post-period path. The “+5% complexity adds +2 hours” adjustment is invented.

Those errors feed later claims. Section 4 says Beta’s p90 “drops slightly”; section 5 says it rose. August rework does not revert: Alpha stays at 14.5 and Beta at 12.7. Selection is given a “downward bias in gains” and, in the same row, said to overestimate gains. Controls’ medians and p90s deteriorated by more than the early adopters’ before-after changes, so “identical increases,” “automation may increase tail latency,” and the conditional claim that equal shocks imply no cycle-time reduction are not what these contrasts show. Absolute worsening and relative DiD are never separated.

The estimands are also poorly defined. Horizons of 12 months exceed the eight-month panel. Several rows are before-after contrasts labeled “not identifiable,” rather than effects with explicit versions. Interference through the shared fraud desk and routed demand, and treatment versions (availability, fast-path intensity, learning), are not clarified. Rework is misread as “error rates.”

The preferred design is named as a staggered event study, but as specified it is relative time plus month fixed effects, the two-way fixed-effects form the text claims to avoid. No cohort-robust estimator is named. Complexity and arrivals are conditioned on without saying how that changes the estimand. The synthetic-control row treats Gamma as both a weighted pool and a single comparison. The third design says it targets intensity while using a binary adoption change.

Two required mechanisms, tail amplification and survey selection, are absent. Fast-path “beneficial” and “harmful” signatures both say the median drops. Confidence of 60–70 for tight ranges is too high for five nonrandom branches and concurrent shocks. Escalation’s March step is common to untreated branches; confidence 70 that automation caused the increase overstates a partly mechanical routing change.

The recommendation is the most defensible choice, but the text is about 160 words against a required 250–400. A four-week ramp, a three-month baseline despite eight months already observed, and a two-month “2%” rule do not identify the effect or match the stated four-month follow-up. Staffing and the routing campaign are left free to change.

The code omits Gamma and Epsilon, does not validate, prints only two outcomes rather than trends for five, builds no relative-time indicators, fits neither panel model, and does not cluster standard errors or warn about aggregated medians. `pd.io.StringIO` is also not a reliable constructor. The bottom claim-ladder rung is right; the three above it overstate or mis-sign the evidence.
