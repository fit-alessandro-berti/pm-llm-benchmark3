The write-up keeps the required ten-part skeleton and the right headline split: March is mostly semantic/provenance drift, while May–December contain real rework, queueing, mix, and seasonal change. That is not enough to carry the grade. Several claims are factually wrong, the quantitative section manufactures precision the aggregates cannot support, and the code does not implement the method it describes.

**Factual and causal errors.** The September-to-October supplier-Z change is reported as −12 percentage points. The table moves from 24 to 26, so the change is +2 points. The large drop is August to September. October rework is called stable; the rework-variant share moves from 20 to 26 and `Assess -> Request Info` from 20 to 26 per 100 cases. The July priority rule is tied to a rise in `Senior Assessment`, but the normalized rate only moves from 7 in May to 9 in July. The jump to 22 per 100 cases is in October, together with the complexity jump from 26% to 41%. May 5 is listed as a logging change point. Incomplete bundles after the supplier API break are an input-quality and control-flow event; label cardinality and duplicate-`Open Case` rates step up in March and then stay in a narrow band. Section 9 states that rework doubled only for Supplier Z. No supplier-stratified rework rate is in the evidence. The same section then says the account should be revised if Supplier Z shows no May rework spike. Those two statements cannot both be findings. “The priority rule mediates seasonal complexity” misuses mediation: queue priority can reallocate waiting; it does not generate the complexity share. Alternative explanations are entered as “None” for the migration, despite a partial cutover (about 140 of 640 March cases still use `Validate Eligibility`) and no event-level proof that the business path was untouched.

**Quantitative decomposition.** The cycle-time split for January–July assigns 20/35/10/35 percent to mix, rework, service, and queueing and sums to 100. Median cycle time rises by about 12.7 hours, while recorded `Assess` service rises by about 0.6 hours, which is roughly 5 percent of that median gap, not 10. The other shares do not follow from the cited percentage-point changes, and rework, mix, and queueing are not an identified additive partition. The p90 rise is larger than the median rise and is unused. Mapped fitness is treated as stable after the label repair, but it still falls from about 0.93 to a low of 0.86. Because the normative model already allows `Request Info`, that residual decline is not explained by permitted rework and is left uninterpreted. Fast-path share, which falls from about 58% to the mid-30s, is essentially ignored.

**DFG distance.** Jaccard distance on the supplied relation sets is 0 in every comparison: all seven edges are present in every listed month, so the edge sets do not change. The reported distances 0.05, 0.22, and 0.18 are not Jaccard values, and they are not the pairs the code computes. The code always compares each month with January. “Omitted edges treated as 0” would matter for an L1 or similar numeric distance; it does not matter for set Jaccard. This violates the instruction not to manufacture precision.

**Discrimination and monitoring.** In the complexity row, longer service time is labeled behavioral and higher rework is labeled population; those assignments are swapped relative to what the columns ask. The pre/post-staffing row is empty in all three pattern columns. Historically inconsistent recording of `Senior Assessment` is never used as a logging alternative, even though the prompt states it. The primary segment is supplier by complexity and leaves out priority class, which is the only cut that can show the redistribution the monthly aggregate hides. The mapped-fitness alert fires below 0.85, under the observed drifted range of about 0.86–0.88, so it would miss the residual conformance decline. A three-month rolling reference recenters after a level shift and clears a persistent regime. Variant entropy is one-sided: an alert on an entropy drop can miss a rework shift that spreads mass across variants. The ordering monitor reuses the same Jaccard rule, so rate changes on a fixed edge set do not alert.

**Format and code.** Section 2 is about 120–140 words against a required 180–280. Section 9 is about 190–210 words against a required 250–400, and it omits September’s partial reversal and December’s confounded improvement. Part A embeds only part of the monthly summary. `fitness_gap` is raw minus mapped, hence negative, while the change-point rule requires `fitness_gap > 0.3`, so the logging break cannot flag. The rule also flags every later month that remains elevated versus January; that is a level screen, not a change point. Part B does not call `pm4py.format_dataframe`. Time filtering is invoked as `filter_time_range.apply` rather than `pm4py.filter_time_range(...)`. Variants are imported as `pm4py.statistics.get_variants` and then called as `get_variants.get_variants`, which is not `pm4py.get_variants(...)`. Label comparison depends on a nonexistent `insert_classifier.apply`; a classifier that did exist would still leave `concept:name` unchanged, so the raw and “mapped” variant calls would match. February windows built as `YYYY-02-31` are invalid.

The operational story is pointed in the right direction: separate artifacts have separate onsets, service time barely moves, waiting and rework do, and seasonality confounds December. The unsupported Supplier Z rework claim, the impossible DFG distances, the misdated senior-assessment jump, the non-identified percentage split, and code that neither runs nor matches PM4Py keep it well below a passable diagnostic.
