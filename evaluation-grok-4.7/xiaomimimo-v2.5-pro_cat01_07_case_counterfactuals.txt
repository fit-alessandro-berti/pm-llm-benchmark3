6.5

The required structure is complete and the control-flow reading is mostly right: prerequisites, originals, four-eyes, preliminary versus final compliance, and the fact that K-337 changes decision policy rather than only elapsed time are all handled. Interference, bottleneck migration, and reversibility are discussed in the right places. The grade is held down by temporal errors that change the counterfactuals.

K-337’s best case equates a Friday 18:30 decision with about 163.5 hours. From Monday 11:00 to that Friday 18:30 is about 103.5 hours, so the published 164–170 hour band collapses the Friday and Monday outcomes and understates the weekend boundary by roughly 60 hours. K-219 ignores the Monday 16:00 snapshot (D2 still at about 5 hours) and treats 9.5 hours of clock time since 08:00 as clearing the queue, then treats a midnight arrival as first in A2’s Tuesday 08:00 six-hour queue. The 26–30 hour base path and ~20 hour saving depend on that mistake. K-104 puts C1 at Wednesday 08:00 in every scenario despite an 8-hour Wednesday queue and the instruction to hold other behavior fixed; the 52–55 hour band does not include the much smaller gain if the reservation only shortens a from-scratch review. The 100-case analysis often counts total identity, document, and compliance load as new demand created by the policy. Weekly capacity of about 40 hours per person is load-bearing and unaudited, and the rollout and synthesis both run past the required word limits. Rank 1 is K-104 while the synthesis calls K-411 the most robust.
