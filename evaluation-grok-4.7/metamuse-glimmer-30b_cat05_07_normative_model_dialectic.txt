The final answer covers every required section and all fifteen cases, and several hard calls are defensible (in-window FD-7 versus post-expiry use, M0-fitting traces that still break the different-person rule, CRM notification versus a mutable link, and post-pilot auto-approval). It is not a split-the-difference review. It is still well short of a sound conformance judgment because the formal model is ill-formed, a few evidence claims are wrong, and the stated burden is not applied consistently.

The revised POWL does not meet the minimum it claims to meet. `Request_More_Info` is built and then left out of `M_REVISED`, so the model cannot replay the repeatable request behavior. The offered approximation is an XOR, not a loop, and `SilentTransition` is unused. The same `Transition` objects are placed in multiple parents (`sanctions`, `quality`, and `manual_approval` appear in more than one partial order), which is not a valid POWL. `Reject_Supplier` is an alternative to the whole routes, so rejection can skip sanctions. Reactivation and withdrawals are absent. The “normalized policy-overlay table” is only a one-line schema, and master-data validation, which PD-3 requires before activation, is never modeled. The limitation note correctly parks amount, risk, date, and identity outside POWL, but it hides these control-flow failures.

Evidence handling breaks on the severe case. The record states that no transaction occurred before sanctions clearance in SUP-708; the prosecution brief treats that as an unevidenced operations claim, and the matrix invents a logging-lag theory the file does not contain. SUP-702 is cleared with high confidence and “missing evidence: none” even though automated master-data validation is a PD-3 precondition and is not shown. SUP-704’s monitoring is stated as existing, then downgraded to “claimed.”

The classifications contradict the answer’s own burden rule. That rule says scope ambiguity makes a case indeterminate, but SUP-709 and SUP-710 are labeled model/scope defects. SUP-711 is determinate: both register rows share a source transaction ID, the business path otherwise fits, and residual risk is none, yet it is labeled data-quality indeterminate. SUP-701 is a complete compliant case and is given only medium confidence. PD-3’s authority is marked disputed, then SUP-702 is a high-confidence justified exception without explaining why a non-incorporated minute plus a later denial of any CS-9 weakening still excuses the deviation. SUP-715 is noncompliant only with the disclosure duty; the text never separates that from the CS-9 requester/approver rule, which this case does not violate. Severity for SUP-704 and SUP-715 also shifts between the matrix and the judgment.

Dialectically, the later rejection of each side is the fairest part. The briefs themselves are not. The prosecution does not concede the real model and logging defects, and the defense does not concede SUP-703 and SUP-708 as misconduct. “No defense” on SUP-703 abandons the required strongest-case defense. Garbled wording (“requester manual approver human,” “2% 29%”) obscures the resource rule the review depends on.
