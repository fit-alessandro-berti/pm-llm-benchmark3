6.5

The required structure is complete: partial order plus constraints, four-row counterfactual table, labeled derivations, capacity table, 100-case discussion, ranking, pilots, synthesis, and eight labeled assumptions. The conceptual distinctions the task requires are mostly right. K-337 is treated as a decision-policy truncation rather than a pure speedup, four-eyes is preserved (A1 assesses, C1 reviews), RMI1 is kept while only RMI2 is removed, and K-411 correctly separates earlier verification readiness from an Assessment-bound completion. Single-case ceteris paribus versus weekly interference is also the right method.

The quantitative temporal work is not reliable enough for that framework.

K-337’s headline range is arithmetically inconsistent with the endpoints given in the same answer. Register is Mon 06 Apr 11:00 and factual notify is Wed 15 Apr 09:10, about 214 hours later, not “~8 days.” Notify Fri 10 Apr 17:00 saves about 112 hours; a Mon 13 Apr morning decision saves about 45–50 hours. The stated −65 to −90 hours, with −90 paired to Fri 17:00 and −80 to Monday, matches neither endpoint. The interval is both mis-centered and too narrow, so the main outcome claim for the largest intervention is wrong even on the answer’s own timeline.

K-219 overstates the no-rework gain. D2’s Mon 16:00 queue of 5 hours and Tue 08:00 queue of 4 hours do not imply a Mon 21:00–Tue 01:00 start unless D2 works at night; D1’s overnight stint on K-104 does not establish that. A business-hours D2 start plus a 6–10 hour handwritten service, above the stated 4–8 hour standard band, can finish later than factual D1 at Tue 13:30 even with no clarification. That sign-flipping scenario is missing from the −8 to −16 hour “no rework” range. The 30% rework expected value is also miscalculated: \(0.7\times(-12)+0.3\times(+30)\approx +1\) hour, not “near −4 hours.”

K-104 is directionally sound but internally inconsistent. The path moves escalation and preliminary compliance up to Monday, while temporal propagation still holds compliance readiness at Tue 21:20 “after Assessment+Escalation,” reinserting the two-hour post-assessment escalation the path deleted. The table’s −3 to −11 hours also drops the derivation’s worst case of −1 to +2 hours.

Section 4 names the right mechanisms and then uses snapshot-scale numbers for a 100-case week. D2 is one person. One hundred handwritten checks at 6–10 hours is on the order of 600–1000 hours against roughly 40 hours of weekly capacity, not a queue moving from 2–5 hours to “15h+.” “D1 queue 13h lower” recycles Monday’s snapshot. “Two urgent cases wait 3–8 hours” is the single-day spillover, not the C1 collapse implied by 100 reserved high-risk reviews. The 25–40% and 60–75% splits are presented as operative probabilities without evidence.

Constraint handling, minimal-change paths, and readiness-versus-completion reasoning are strong. The required cycle-time ranges and the system-scale capacity arithmetic are not, and those are central to temporal consistency and interference.
