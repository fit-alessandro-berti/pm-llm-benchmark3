6.2

The final write-up is well structured and shows real queueing judgment: regimes are separated by day type, outage/recovery, mix, and the 2026-04-29 staffing change; outage clearance is not treated as a normal high-throughput state; median service is not used as mean flow time; and Little’s Law is mostly marked approximate or not defensible, with opening-hours units and carry-over stated. Many primary-regime means (R1–R5 arrivals, completions, service, WIP) and the R1, R2, and R5 flow times check out.

Those strengths are undercut by material errors. The series has 28 days, not 30, so pooled arrivals (~90, not ~84), completions (~86, not ~80), WIP (~30, not ~28), and staff (~5.14 FTE, not ~5.7) are wrong. The claim that the pooled ratio (~14.7) is higher than the weekend ratio (~17) contradicts those same figures. Using 31 minutes does not understate R4 service by ~10 minutes; the pooled-versus-R4 gap is about 4 minutes. R2 `complex_pct` is slightly higher than R1, not lower, so that Simpson-style reversal is backwards.

On 2026-04-21 the hourly arrival Fano factor is about 10, not ~6.2; the 2026-04-07 vector sums to 101, not 93. In the R4 weekday window, completions below arrivals must lengthen implied flow time: with L≈42.2 the completion proxy is ~308 minutes, not ~256, so the reported 16-minute gap is impossible under the stated rates. The 7.6 FTE / ~20% deficit figure is not derived cleanly and uses median service as work content after warning against that. The Python block does not run (unbalanced parenthesis in the R5 window) and that window includes Saturday–Sunday while labeled weekdays only.
