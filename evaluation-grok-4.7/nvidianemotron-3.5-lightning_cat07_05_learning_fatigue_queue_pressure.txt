6.5

The required six-part structure is present, the seven mechanisms are separated, all eight interventions are assessed, and the main constraints are observed: fatigue is not treated as the sole cause, early complexity and senior selection are acknowledged, late speed is tied to queue pressure, raw late-shift error rates are not endorsed as punishment, and quality checks are not removed merely to raise throughput. The integrated account is mostly multi-causal and uses the supplied rates coherently.

It is not nearly flawless. `minutes_since_shift_start` is used as the service-time outcome in the proposed regression and in one aggregation, although that column is elapsed shift time (about 459 minutes in the last bin versus 10.8 minutes of service). Waiting time on the dashboard is defined the same way, and its thresholds reuse queue counts (Night 29, Day 38) as if they were minutes. The service-time alert above 15 minutes against an 18.8-minute early baseline would fire under normal conditions.

The case-mix story contradicts the data and itself. Complexity falls from 0.72 to 0.43, yet the narrative says simpler work is done while complex work is left for later. The ethics section then says late error spikes may reflect an accumulation of simpler cases. The stratified table shows the opposite: lower complexity has lower errors, so falling complexity cannot explain the late rise in errors.

Several PM4Py calls are not usable as written. `filter_case_performance` does not take an arbitrary WIP lambda, `group_by_case_attribute` is not a real operation here, batch output is passed to an events-distribution view, and rework results are merged as if they were a case table. `discover_batches` on a plain dataframe and row-wise `shift(-1)` handovers are also unreliable. Hyphen and arrow stripping makes many terms harder to read, and “deliberately” overstates the evidence that seniors appear to take complex cases early.
