6.5

The write-up has the right causal skeleton: availability versus dose, staggered and targeted adoption, separate median and tail estimands, SUTVA at the fraud desk, small-cluster humility, and a rollout that tries to buy identification. The quantitative core is not reliable enough to carry that argument.

Section 5 contradicts itself. Alpha/Beta versus Delta/Epsilon is shown as −5.4 from components 0.75 and 5.2; those components subtract to about −4.5. Alpha’s “Post_late versus June” median of −10.2 and p90 of −18.7 are August alone versus June; July–August versus June is about −7.9h and −14.5h. Gamma’s row cites an April–June p90 mean of 94.2 and then a −15.6h change, which is a May–June contrast, while rework −2.0pp also drops April. Alpha post-period complexity is about +5.8pp, not +7.3; Delta’s is about +4pp, not +5. Early April–June Alpha/Beta median deterioration is about +2h versus pre, not +3h, so the −3h early difference-in-differences is off. Those figures are reused in the mechanism table and as Alpha’s “10h drop” in the rollout section.

The sensitivity bounds add invented pieces (0.15h per load point, +2h for two FTEs, ~0.4h per complexity point) onto a raw pre/post change even though the same shocks hit the controls used in the difference-in-differences. The resulting 0 to −6h interval is a compromise, not a bound. The dose slope of −0.12h per point (cluster SE ~0.08) is stated as a fitted result and is not produced by the code.

The event-study code builds relative time with day-counts floor-divided by 30. Negative floor division collapses Alpha/Beta February and March onto the same lead and shifts Gamma’s pre months; there is no −1 for the April cohort, despite the comment that −1 is the reference. Leads are built and then omitted, so the promised pre-trend test is not estimated. Post dummies for this calendar happen to land on the intended months, which limits the damage to the fitted post coefficients only.

The claim ladder is ordered by causal ambition. The supported ordering would put the observed path and escalation facts first. The lead “conditionally causal” bullet also tightens the median effect to 2–6h after Sections 5 and 7 had left the interval on 0 to −6h. June already has Alpha dose at 39% and the worst median (53.5h), so the policy story that gains appear once fast-path share crosses ~35–45% is contradicted by the same series.

Smaller defects add up: “No credibly” and “No more difficult than E1” are unclear; pre-period medians are printed as concatenated decimals; the primary design fuses event-study, stacked difference-in-differences, and synthetic control while calling staggered timing the clean variation the threat table says is confounded; assignment flips between case-level randomization and a branch-month lottery; the scale-up rule demands a significant wild bootstrap after the text has correctly said five clusters cannot support that inference. The pre-adoption fast-path share of 4–6% in every branch, including never-treated units, is never treated as a measurement problem. Section 8 runs past the 400-word limit.

Estimands, mechanism map, threat list, CSAT skepticism, and the preference for case-level randomization with queue and response-level measurement are the parts that hold.
