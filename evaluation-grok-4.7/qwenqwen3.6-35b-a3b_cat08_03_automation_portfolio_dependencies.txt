5.5

The write-up has the required seven sections, uses the case waits and directly-follows counts, and correctly rejects a frequency-first start on registration (1.5h wait, 4% rework, downstream shift). That core judgment is sound. It is not a reliable portfolio recommendation because the plan contradicts itself and several causal claims are wrong.

The year-one budget is four modules. Section 7 recommends A4, A6, A7, and A9. Section 3 defines the “bottleneck” portfolio as A4, A6, A7, and A8. Section 6 then rolls out eight modules (A5, A4, A6, A9, A7, A10, A8, A12), including A1 as an optional deep integration that is not in the plan. The two-deep-integration limit is mentioned, but the binding four-module limit is broken. A reader cannot tell which set would actually be funded.

Control attribution is wrong. Four-eyes applies to credit analysis versus committee packaging (5.8% of high-exposure cases, month-end). KYC clearance must precede signing (6.5% provisional; 1.1% remediated). A9 checks contract terms against the committee decision; it does not, as specified, enforce four-eyes or block provisional signing. The recommended set therefore does not implement the control findings the text claims it closes, and the phase-2 gate of “zero four-eyes violations” is attached to the wrong module. The quick-win set includes A11 with no control prerequisite, which the case and the task both rule out.

Resource logic is reversed. The working-together note says analysts overload when clean inflow rises unless normalization is improved. A6 is that improvement and frees the 75-minute normalization load on the same 9 FTE pool. The matrix and effects treat A6 as a likely cause of analyst saturation.

Process mechanism is incomplete. KYC and financial-document review run concurrently, so speeding one branch does not cut cycle time while the other remains longer; that variant is never used. High-exposure committee decision is a weekly cadence with low automation feasibility, so packaging or valuation triage cannot remove that wait. Collateral applies to 71% of equipment finance and 18% of working capital, and contract remediation is 14% on renewals; effects stay unsegmented. External valuation is described as “cut” from 58h in the recommendation after section 4 correctly says the wait stays external.

The matrix is not a ranking; it keeps A1–A12 order. Section 4 states unsupported precision (5–10%, 25–35%, waits falling to under 8h and under 5h) after the task asked for qualitative judgment. Dependency coverage hits the required topics, but A3 is treated as a packaging prerequisite and A8’s garbage-in risk is left inside the unsynchronized bottleneck set.

The anti-frequency argument and the instinct to pair a synchronization module with a real control module are the parts that hold. The delivered portfolio, rollout, control mapping, and capacity story do not.
