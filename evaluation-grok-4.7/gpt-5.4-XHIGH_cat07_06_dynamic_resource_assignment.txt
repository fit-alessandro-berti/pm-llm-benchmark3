6.2

The write-up is complete and often careful, but it is not a reliable policy. Several stated rules contradict the queue decision, and the scoring rule that is supposed to make the policy auditable is internally inconsistent.

**Constraint breaches in the recommended batch.** C001 is the clearest miss. Complexity is 0.88, handover cost is high, the owner’s security skill is 1, and supervision by R7 or R8 is feasible, so continuity under supervision is the required default. The median owner-change penalty given in the evidence is 2.7 hours against a 3-hour SLA. Assigning the lead to R8 still accepts that handover. Keeping R3 only as “context support” does not remove the owner change. C003 breaks the overtime rule: R4 is high overtime risk, the case is normal with 18 hours left, and R3 is an eligible diagnosis specialist with low overtime risk and more time today. Continuity at two prior reassignments is a real concern, but it is not the exception the constraint allows. The same rule is applied to R2 and R9 and then dropped for R4.

**The scoring procedure does not implement the objectives.** Candidate SLA score is defined as high when slack is small. That ranks a barely recoverable assignee above one who leaves more margin. Component scales are never normalized, so weights 0.35/0.20/0.20/0.15/0.05/0.05 are not operational. Training and resilience at 0.05 cannot enforce a 20% training share or bottleneck avoidance. The 20% quota never appears in the algorithm. In the batch it is claimed via C009 plus C015. C015 is shadowing by a skill-0 resource who cannot perform security even under supervision, so the training is close to cosmetic. C010 is the case explicitly marked for trainee preparation with a skill 2–3 finalizer, and that pattern is never used. No score components are shown, so the table cannot be audited back to the formula. “High complexity,” “critically low hours,” and the “8–10h” band are undefined.

**Comparison and audit sketch.** Baseline comparison stays qualitative. It never assigns these 16 cases under first-available or specialist-only rules, so claims about SLA, fairness, and bottlenecks are untested on the given queue. The PM4Py section names the right families of techniques, but the sketch would not audit this policy. The skill matrix is empty, so every skill check is vacant. Violation logic looks for the substring “supervis” and does not check that the supervisor is R7 or R8. Pair assignments used in the table do not fit the single-resource audit frame. `discover_batches` is called with keyword arguments that the usual PM4Py wrapper does not accept. Overtime overrides are flagged for every non-urgent high-risk assignment, including continuity cases the prose treats as justified.

**What holds up.** Hard skill gates for security and approval are respected in the table. High-priority work is not given to an unsupervised trainee. The four-assignment cap is respected on primary owners. C007 and C016 correctly keep a capable owner when handover cost and complexity are both high. Quality batching stays inside the normal/low and >24h rule. The worker text is plain and non-punitive, and the indicator list covers every requested audit question. Those strengths do not repair the inverted score, the two constraint breaks, or the gap between the formula and the queue.
