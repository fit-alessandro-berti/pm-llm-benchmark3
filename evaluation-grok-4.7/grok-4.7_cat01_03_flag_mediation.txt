7.5

The stage table is arithmetically reliable: conditional medians, support counts, and the decision to leave skipped stages as missing all match the timestamps, including the flag split (triage wait 0.20 h vs 2.50 h, cycle 16.45 h vs 5.75 h), the load and escalation slices, and the within-High manual-wait contrast (8.00 h vs 2.00 h, n=5 vs 3). The causal framing is also the right one. The flag is treated as a policy bundle, natural direct and indirect effects are rejected because a shared queue cannot host the required cross-world mediator, occurrence is separated from waiting, and interference, exposure-induced confounding, and treatment–confounder feedback are stated as regime problems rather than as an afterthought.

The resource pathway is factually wrong. R17 is High complexity, reviewed by M1, and not escalated, so M2 does not handle every High review. M2’s cases are all High, and no Medium case is on M2, so “move Medium work off M2” does not describe this log. That error sits in the observable-evidence column and distorts the side-effect claim.

Complexity is not a usable negative-control exposure. In the answer’s own graph it affects review, service, escalation, and the decision, and the cycle medians run from 4.45 h (Low) to 19.45 h (High). A pre-flag duration could serve; complexity cannot. The regression-discontinuity row also slips: the design is said to identify a total bundle effect without conditioning on review or escalation, while the result that would change the conclusion conditions on path occurrence.

The pilot is substantively aimed at the right slice (Medium, flag 1, score below 0.85; five approved cases here), with block assignment, a quality stop, and protected High review. A 4-hour switchback does not contain the outcome. Manual waits are about 4–8 h and escalated cycles are about 16–26 h, so treatment-block cases are reviewed under later blocks. Section 5 requires a buffer; section 6 does not, and a 2 h median guardrail on one or two High cases per block is an unstable trigger. E3 and E6 are both described as the total effect of P0 versus P2, so the occurrence pathway is not cleanly isolated. The adjacency list sends complexity into the flag, while the time-ordered diagram defines the flag as a hard score threshold in (0.61, 0.64]. “Near 19–22 h” understates escalated High cycles, several of which are 24–26 h. The code matches the PM4Py request and does not impute absent stages; the seconds-versus-hours cutoff is only a heuristic.

What is observed, what is a queue-and-mandate mechanism, and what stays unidentified are kept apart, and the numbers that carry that argument are right. The resource misread, the negative control, and the carryover gap are large enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
