The case-level authority judgments are mostly right and the two briefs are real arguments, but the revised model does not implement the control flow it claims to authorize, and several matrix conclusions are logically inconsistent with their own reasons.

Dialectical fairness is adequate rather than strong. Section 1 correctly separates controlled norms from time-bounded overlays and states a usable burden for noncompliance versus exception. The judgment correctly treats SUP-703 and SUP-708 as serious, refuses to extend PD-3 or FD-7 by inertia (SUP-705, SUP-714), and does not let M0’s silence excuse the same-person breach in SUP-712. The prosecution brief, however, never really concedes the genuine model and data defects it was required to concede; that correction appears only later. The defense invents “small spend” for SUP-714, for which no amount is given, and ties SUP-706 to the CRM migration without a case date.

Evidence handling slips in the matrix. SUP-711 is labeled `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` even though the stated reason is that the duplicate register shares a source transaction ID and the underlying trace conforms; the reversal cell then points the opposite way. SUP-706’s reversal (“stable immutable link → data defect”) would actually clear the indeterminacy toward compliance. SUP-703’s defense cell is “None,” which is not an interpretation. SUP-713 is called `COMPLIANT` at high confidence although the label mapping was approved a week after the case. SUP-702’s trace also omits `Notify_Requester`; the stable CRM id supports the result, but that second deviation is never identified.

The model revision is the largest failure. The prose says sanctions and quality are concurrent only on the standard route, and that pilot and emergency activation may precede quality. The code places one `checks` partial order, containing `Quality_Review`, before the XOR over all routes. That excludes SUP-702 and SUP-704, forces quality even on rejection, and adds a second retrospective quality only on the emergency branch. `Request_More_Info` is sequenced entirely before both checks, so it also excludes SUP-707, where sanctions occur before the requests. The same `activate` and `skip` objects are reused across operators, so the POWL is not a well-formed executable model. The data-overlay table and collection fields are much better, but they overstate FD-7 by imposing a two-business-day deadline on manual approval when the directive states that deadline for `Quality_Review`. Withdrawal scope is named and then left unspecified.
