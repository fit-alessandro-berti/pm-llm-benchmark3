8.3

The audit’s central diagnosis is right and well separated: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure with a still-healthy label channel, June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September stacks parts-performance drift, a V3-local change in breach risk, and (from September) Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is a maturity artifact rather than a recovery. The six required sections are all present, the falsifiers are concrete, and the monitoring gates correctly block retraining on data-quality or label-delay alarms. The score is held down by arithmetic, consistency, and code defects that a strict reading should not ignore.

The mix-implied rates are the spine of the argument and are not fully reliable as printed. February is 0.172 using that month’s variant rates (0.44×0.16 + 0.36×0.11 + 0.20×0.31), not 0.174. March is 0.166, not 0.168. Both misses match the previous month’s V3 rate. April’s 0.159 is the share-weighted sum with the unmapped 18% implicitly contributing zero; the rate on mapped cases is the rescaled 0.194. The table labels those two figures the other way around. “Within roughly one point” of the January–March band is also loose: April’s matured rates sit about two to three points above that band on every variant.

July is described two ways. The concept-drift row and the narrative correctly treat the V3 step from 0.35 to 0.46, with the parts edge moving from 42 h to 65 h, as the first real outcome-relationship change. The covariate-drift row then says June and July are “pure input-distribution change with stable per-variant outcomes.” July’s per-variant outcomes are not stable, so that cell over-claims composition and collides with the rest of the audit.

Two mechanisms are stated more strongly than the evidence. Shifting mass into a low-risk, homogeneous V2 segment does not mechanically depress AUROC; if those cases are well separated, the usual effect is the opposite, and June’s 0.81 to 0.79 move is too small to carry that story. The expected V3 observed-over-expected ratio “approaching 3 (0.64 against a predicted level near 0.22)” invents a segment score the tables do not contain. Pooled mean risk is 0.19–0.21, and a model with AUROC around 0.81 may already have been scoring parts-like cases nearer the old V3 base rate of about 0.30. The same over-precision appears in the censoring test: length-biased delay should pull the observed breach rate down, but it does not follow that January truncated to a 55% maturity profile would reproduce October’s AUROC of 0.64, or that the remaining cohort is simply “easier.” Calling the August–September mix-implied rates “where the month actually landed” also goes past what they identify. They correct pooled rates for differential variant maturity; within-V3 label delay can still bias 0.64 and 0.61 downward.

The short-term calibration window has the same blur. A single V3 map fit on June through August averages a pre-supplier V3 rate of 0.35 with August’s 0.64. June belongs in the prevalence check; it does not belong in the same isotonic fit as the post-10-July regime.

The Python sketch uses the requested PM4Py calls, normalizes the renamed diagnosis before extraction, distinguishes event-calendar from case-start cohorts, and refuses a fictional drift detector. It would still mis-implement the audit it supports. Exact-duplicate keys include `source_system`, so the same event landing in both sources during the migration survives, which is the failure mode under discussion. `discover_performance_dfg` is read as a mean, while every conclusion in the paper is about medians and a 90th percentile the sketch never computes. Timestamps are forced to UTC and then passed to `period_range` and `filter_time_range`, a combination that commonly breaks on timezone-aware comparisons. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is computed and then left out of the monthly summaries.

The decision staging, Partner Z caution, and “do not retrain on October or calibrate on April” warnings are sound and would survive the numerical cleanup. The grade reflects that the verdict is right and the supporting arithmetic and code are not yet at the same standard.
