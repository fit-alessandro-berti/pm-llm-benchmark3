6.5

The answer covers every required section, names all seven mechanisms, and gets the main pattern right: early slowness is tied to a harder case mix, later speedup with rising errors shows up with queue pressure, and raw late-shift error rates are a poor basis for punishment. It is not nearly flawless. Several claims are stronger than the tables, one intervention contradicts the answer’s own selection story, and the code as written would not support the tests it advertises.

The mechanism table is the strongest part. Low-WIP later error rates (seniors 2.2%, mid-level 2.9%, juniors 4.4%) are correctly used to limit a pure fatigue account, and the coarse WIP-by-complexity table is correctly used to limit a pure case-mix account. The integrated narrative then walks that back. It treats warm-up as shown by the early error drop from 3.4% to 2.8%, even though complexity falls from 0.72 to 0.61 and high-priority share from 41% to 34% over the same bins. It calls cognitive fatigue a phase-3 driver and says time-on-task impairs anomaly detection, while the same answer’s low-WIP split shows no such main effect. “Exponentially,” “proving,” and the specific claim that workers truncate validation and skip sanity checks state micro-behavior the log does not record. Bot_Risk is described as less accurate when volume spikes; the given clue is only that humans correct it more often when queue length exceeds 70.

The cleanest contrast in the data is underused. Early shift queue falls from 61 to 42 and last-two-hour error is 4.2% at 12.7 minutes; Day shift queue rises from 38 to 75 and last-two-hour error is 7.5% at 10.9 minutes. That same-hours, different-queue comparison does more to separate pressure from hours-worked and circadian time than the phase diagram does. The diagram also mislabels events 51+ as starting near 270 minutes, while those bins are centered at 378 and 459 minutes. Queue length and WIP are switched as if they were the same measure. Positions 51–75 already show 4.6% errors and 8.2% rework at a mean queue of 62, below the WIP>70 cut the answer treats as the break.

Intervention 4 says random assignment would stop senior “cherry-picking.” The clue, and section 5, say seniors take complex cases early. Cherry-picking is the opposite selection. The Gini fairness alert has the same problem: a higher senior share of complexity is the safer pattern, and the formula would flag it. Intervention 8’s monitoring list includes turnover intentions, which are not event-log indicators. Deferring quality checks is correctly marked high-risk.

The script is presented as runnable analysis and does not implement the identification strategy in section 1. `C(org:resource)` is invalid Patsy because `:` is the interaction operator. Cluster groups are built from a separate slice and will not match rows Patsy drops. `sequence_position_within_shift` and `minutes_since_shift_start` are nearly the same clock, so that service-time model cannot separate warm-up from elapsed time, and it has no WIP-by-time interaction. The error logit omits within-resource fixed effects. `filter_event_attribute_values` is the wrong tool for a numeric WIP range and, at case level, keeps other events from the same case; the filtered logs are then unused. `filter_paths_performance` does not select paths that involve Bot_Risk, and `get_service_time` is an aggregate, not an event-level outcome to regress. Clock hour is extracted and never entered, so the circadian overlap test (Day vs Late, 14:00–17:00) stays prose. Waiting time is a required dashboard indicator and is absent; queue length is not a substitute. Several alert cutoffs (−3.5 minutes, Gini 0.35, escape ratio 0.65, QTPI 1.4) are stated as operating rules with no link to the observed distributions.
