7.2

The design is conceptually strong: the primary estimand is explicitly conditional on eventual activation under the observed policy, rejected and withdrawn cases are not recoded as non-default, censoring mechanisms are separated, and the leakage table makes the right keep/repair/drop calls. It is not nearly flawless. Several date, cohort, and implementation errors would change who is labeled and what a computed metric means.

The validation windows contradict the stated maturity rule. With an as-of date of 15 February 2026 and a 90+35 day buffer, only activations on or before 13 October 2025 can be labeled. Every application that started on or after the 15 October 2025 migration has activation after that cutoff, so the migration “test” is empty for this target, not merely thin. The 1 July–14 October post-policy slice is increasingly restricted to fast activations, and that time-to-activation selection is never named. The later remark about incomplete ascertainment and an embargo does not repair the concrete split. The same snapshot rule also quietly drops slow-to-activate cases inside the earlier windows whenever activation plus 125 days falls after extraction. A random split is rightly rejected, and the metric set (PR AUC, Brier, calibration slope, subgroup calibration, cluster-aware intervals) fits a matured binary target. Those strengths do not offset an outcome test that the design’s own calendar makes impossible.

The cohort table adds to 48,000 and correctly refuses to put the 9,100 rejections in the non-default denominator. It then labels all 3,600 immature activated accounts “pending,” while Section 3 correctly treats a feed still missing after the buffer as unknown rather than administratively censored. The supplied aggregate cannot support that split, and the table should have said so. Linked transfers are discussed, but the flow never shows how a 60% linkage rate, or a second identifier, would move cases across the partition.

The first-Credit-Review landmark is a coherent early decision point, and fixed-length versus elapsed-time prefixes are specified without padding. At that landmark the process has not yet produced document loops or repeated reviews, so several proposed rework, loop, and handoff features are essentially degenerate. That is not flagged. Cross-platform consolidation is also left ambiguous: partner activation can count as the event, even though partner feeds are disproportionately missing and may not share the lender’s default definition.

The skeleton uses real PM4Py calls, cuts feature rows at the landmark, keeps the label out of the prefix frame, and splits on case start. It does not implement the prose. `between(..., "2025-06-30")` and `between(..., "2025-10-14")` end at midnight, so almost all of those end dates fall into no split; the migration slice is empty after the maturity filter. The audit assert checks `features`, then the code merges `activation_time`, terminal fields, and labels into `model_table` and splits that frame. Same-timestamp events can enter the prefix, and cases with a decision before the landmark are not quarantined. `handoffs` is a distinct-resource count. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and ignored. Placeholder rename rules and commented-out linkage are acceptable only as stubs.

Sections 3 and 8 are the most reliable: missingness, transfer, and permanent feed loss are not treated as negatives; MAR/IPW limits and untestable counterfactuals are stated plainly; deployment feedback through selection, reviewer behavior, applicant behavior, collections, and capture change is concrete. Those sections describe the study that Sections 2, 7, and 9 do not quite implement.
