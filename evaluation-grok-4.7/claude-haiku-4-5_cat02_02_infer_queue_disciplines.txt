The write-up follows the seven-part structure and sometimes builds real eligible sets, but the diagnoses rest on misread queues, reversed comparisons, and arithmetic that changes the predicted job. Those are core failures for this task, not presentation issues.

QA is the clearest miss. Service order is A01, A03, A02, calibration, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08. At 08:20, A04 (priority 1) is already waiting and the server starts A03. At about 08:32, A04 is still waiting and the server starts A02. From 08:58 onward the remaining jobs run in arrival order, so A08, also priority 1, starts only after A05–A07. FIFO plus an exact-time insertion of A03 fits; priority and SPT do not. The diagnosis instead names non-preemptive priority at confidence 78 and cites A04 “jumping ahead of A02 and A03” and A08 being “selected over” A05–A07. Both episodes are the opposite of the log. The 08:32 eligible set is given as empty, and the 09:05 set treats A06 and A08 as not yet arrived (they arrived at 08:29 and 08:40). The appendix repairs the A01–A03–A02 order and still concludes that priority is operating. Section 7 then calls QA the most identifiable queue and says it can be automated.

QC’s mechanism is built on impossible slack. C05 arrived at 10:11, so C05 is eligible at 10:18; the answer excludes C05 with the comparison “10:11 < 10:18.” C02 is also given C01’s arrival and deadline. At 10:54, C01’s slack to 13:00 is about 126 minutes, not 6, and C07’s 12:00 deadline is still in the future. At 11:34 and 11:40, C07 still has positive slack (about 26 and 20 minutes). Starting C01 at 11:40 is what makes C07 late. The “catastrophically overdue, so drop back to fairness” story therefore has no support in these timestamps. The appendix also reverses EDD: 10:50 is earlier than 12:00, so earliest-deadline selects C05, the job that was actually started. At 11:40, SPT selects C07 (18 versus C01’s 36); the answer says SPT allows C01. Serving C04 at 10:32, while C05 is priority 1 with deadline 10:50, is a direct counterexample to a live deadline rule, and the answer treats it as support.

QB is partly right: B02’s 09:16 stop is labeled urgent arrival, B07’s appointment is missed, and “dispatcher reassignment” is ambiguous. The reconstruction then breaks. Scan delay means the recorded arrival can be later than physical arrival, so it cannot remove B05 from the queue at 09:48 when the scan is already timestamped 09:46. That inverted mechanism is ranked as the most plausible exception at 85%. B01’s second segment ends at 09:48, after which B03 runs; B05 does not interrupt a 09:31–10:00 segment. The 10:28 row says priority predicts B06 and that priority predicts B04. “12 < 8” is stated as the SPT comparison between B03 and B04. B01’s resume at 09:31 over waiting B03 is noted and then folded into a “non-preemptive priority” label that does not describe a fresh dispatch.

QD’s main label, skill-based routing, matches the hard constraints: D05 can use only D-F, D07 can use only D-X, and D06 starts at the stated 11:30 appointment. The within-pool story does not. At 11:25 D08 is already waiting and D-M starts D04, which is FIFO and contradicts both priority and SPT; the text lists D08 and also says there is no competition and that D08 may not be in queue. At 11:48 D10 is eligible for D-X and is omitted, so D07 over D10 is not tested. D-F is not busy for the whole 11:36–12:00 gap; a 30-minute D05 would collide with the 12:00 meeting, which is a better hypothesis than “unavailable until 12:20.” SPT is named as the secondary rule in the same table that says SPT and FIFO cannot be separated. D10 is called a “third-priority” job although its priority is 1, and the appendix once gives D05 an estimate of 40 rather than 30.

Every realized duration in all four queues matches `est_min` exactly (B01 only after summing its three segments). The estimate discussion still treats noise as a 70% exception and presents exactness as a QD peculiarity. The data-collection section is the strongest part and asks for the right missing objects, but its worked examples repeat the bad slack and scan-delay reasoning, including a critical-ratio value of −0.004 for a ratio of about −0.1. Confidence and the final ranking point the wrong way: the policy the answer is most willing to automate is the one these logs most clearly reject.
