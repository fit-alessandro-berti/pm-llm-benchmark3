4.7

The write-up covers every required section and correctly frames a calendar-aware, non-stationary DES with architect bursts, district batching, and separate applicant delays. Those strengths are outweighed by quantitative and statistical errors that would bias intervention choice.

The cross-case travel formula \(T_{\text{base}}(1+\ln N/N)\) is at least \(T_{\text{base}}\) and rises for small batches, so it does the opposite of the claimed travel saving. Seasonal factors set January–February at 0.7 and peaks at 1.7, a ratio of about 2.43 rather than the stated 1.7× winter rate, and that NHPP is not normalized back to 31,200 cases. Adding a separate compound Poisson for the top 20 architects on top of the full NHPP double-counts about 31% of demand. The thinning routine returns only the last exponential draw after rejections, so accepted interarrival times are too short. System tasks are given zero delay even though issue/reject still have observed waiting. A 60-day warm-up is shorter than the complex-variance median (140d) and p90 (260d), with no length-bias correction. Welch steady-state detection is then applied to a seasonal process and described as a “non-stationary steady state.”

Public-notice durations beyond 14 days are treated as an unbiased observation of missing objections, despite holidays and council calendars also stretching that activity. Tail metrics are protected by truncating durations at the empirical 99.5th percentile, which systematically understates p90/p95 and SLA breach. Output t-intervals from 40 runs omit parameter uncertainty; the bootstrap is not re-simulated. CRN is named without separate streams for a policy-dependent event count. Validation demands 100% token-replay fitness and a two-sample KS \(p>0.05\) on tens of thousands of cases; neither is an appropriate equivalence check. Counterfactual policies have no holdout outcome, and waiting is not validated separately from service.

Decision gates fight the supplied summary. Simple-renovation 95% within 14 days and complex-variance median ≤75 days sit far inside current medians (clean simple p90 already 24d; complex median 140d). Environmental permit utilization cannot reach a 70–85% target when 40% of time is reserved. Rework ≤15% is already below the variant shares for missing documents, inspection rework, and appeals. District equity uses raw medians, so case mix is confounded with geography. D6’s single 40-day aging rule would capture nearly all complex cases. D1–D9 effects (70% fewer missing-doc loops, 35% fewer inspections, 25% less travel, 5–10 days, 30% queue cut) are stated as point facts with one level each. Stress rows are not crossed with policies, while the robustness index still requires ≤15% mean degradation under +30% demand or a 50% inspector loss. “Optimal” and “maximum synergistic” claims are made before calibration.

The skeleton builds the excerpt with pandas and calls `format_dataframe`, `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, and `discover_performance_dfg`, and it does separate mining from the engine. It never computes case arrival average. Performance-DFG edge values are divided as raw seconds although current PM4Py returns aggregation dicts. The supplied tree is replaced by a shorter one, and `->`, `X`, `+`, `*`, and `tau` are mislabeled as non-PM4Py syntax. `run_simulation` draws one independent lognormal per case, records it as service, and reports it as lead time. Calendars, the event heap, district pools, rework, public notice, and architect bursts are unused. D1 and D3 flags do not change outcomes. `has_prior_violations` defaults to false, so the D2 safety check is always passed. The demo horizon (180/30) also disagrees with the stated 540/60 design.
