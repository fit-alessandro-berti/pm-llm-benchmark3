The answer keeps the required eight-part structure, avoids treating the four protected unmodeled relations as violations, and correctly separates raw diagnostics from a semantic layer. Those strengths are outweighed by repeated overclaims, one invented norm ID, and several classifications that contradict the stated scopes or the supplied counts.

The closed-world definition is too narrow. Closed-world coverage is limited to forbidden relations and to mandatory ET–OT bindings, omitting mandatory OC-DFG and OTG relations that are equally closed within scope (N1, N4, T1, T2). The seven deviation classes are otherwise distinguished clearly enough.

The findings table covers most observations and correctly leaves reopen, codeath, customer, risk, and Validate Order → Issue Invoice unregulated. The reasoning around them is not reliable. E11 is invented; Manual Compliance Review → Customer has no such ID. The two orders missing Create Order → Validate Order are said to “directly correlate” with the three Manual Compliance Review orders. The context says the review may be unrecorded, and 3 does not equal 2, so that correlation is unsupported while confidence is marked High. Add Item weights 1450 and 1448 are treated as 1450 events of which exactly two lack an Order. Those are relation weights, not event counts. T2 is cited as the violated norm for two dispatches even though the OTG weight is 200 and is later called compliant. N4 is under-ranked: the two gaps are among packages already described as externally shipped, yet the interpretation says they may have been cancelled before dispatch. That contradicts the observation’s scope, and a mandatory in-scope gap is then scored Low. E8 is mandatory in this snapshot because the unapplied-cash exception requires explicit typing and none is present; later placing it in the typical bucket changes the norm’s status. Severity is also inconsistent: confirmed forbidden T6 is Medium, suspected-join E10 is High and containment priority two, and the N4 mandatory gap is Low.

Section 3 explains the directly-follows versus eventually-follows danger for N1, which is required, but then repeats the false CMR correlation. It never resolves the obvious cross-family case: Package–Delivery can look complete in the OTG (200) and incomplete in ET–OT (198). OC-DFG reasoning omits N4 and the typical N2/N5 counts. “1450 Add Item events” and “93 Record Payment events” again equate weights with events. Missing mandatory behavior is called an “E6 Violation,” collapsing it into forbidden behavior.

Section 4 handles the open-world cases and states some conditions, but it drops N4 entirely, marks T2 fully compliant, repeats E11, and derives “17 orders open” from an unstated denominator of 100 validated orders. E8’s conditional note does not repair putting a mandatory, untyped gap under typical patterns.

The code calls all six required functions and preserves raw results beside a semantic layer, with a comment that status metadata lives outside the graph. The layer does not implement that comment. `forbidden_otg` and `typical_ocdfg` are unused, so T6 is never detected; typical edges in the norm are ignored; unmodeled OC-DFG edges are blanket-labeled allowed; and the only mandatory gap computed is E6. The sketch therefore cannot reproduce the classification argued in the prose.

The six norm gaps are real, but the responses are uneven. E8’s clarification is assigned Typical, weakening a mandatory rule whose exception is a scope carve-out. The billing gap cites undetected revenue leakage and then proposes Typical status, which would not make that bypass nonconforming. N4’s DF-versus-eventually ambiguity, the undefined “standard Order,” tolerance bands, and the fact that a forbidden “after” rule can be undercounted by a directly-follows summary are not identified. N3’s two DF pairs are sufficient evidence of “after” for those orders, not proof that two is the full violation count.

Remediation is correctly separated into the five requested phases and does not escalate reopen, codeath, or customer links. It still over-commits: recall of “2 affected packages” when the count is two orders, pipeline freezing for a suspected key collision ahead of confirmed T6, and almost no investigation of the N4 gap. Section 8 then calls N3 unambiguous and T6 robust after the table itself offered timestamp and migration explanations, and it says the two validation gaps are “confirmed” abstraction artifacts. “Conform fully” is also too strong for 92/110 and for a DF approximation of 98/100.

The framework is right often enough to stay above a failing treatment. The concrete normative judgments are not trustworthy where counts, scopes, and cross-model abstractions matter.
