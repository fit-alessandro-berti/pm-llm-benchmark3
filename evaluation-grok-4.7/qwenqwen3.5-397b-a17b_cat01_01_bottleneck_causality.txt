5.5

The final assessment correctly spots the crude-versus-complexity reversal and correctly advises against moving half of Orion’s work, but it is not reliable enough for the decision it addresses.

The headline numbers match the supplied aggregates: Orion’s overall mean is about 22 hours against Nova’s 15, while the complexity-specific means reverse (about 7.5 vs 9.6, 17.2 vs 19.4, and 31.6 vs 35.0). The pilot, the capacity and compliance phases, the overlap warning, and the complexity-standardized contrast are the right kinds of responses. Confidence 85 in “mainly confounded,” and the later claim that the data strongly show Orion is more efficient, treat that reversal as identification. Within every complexity level Orion is still much more often on night work, several complexity–shift cells have one or zero cases, and no cell supports a stable group comparison. Stratification on complexity leaves shift and load unbalanced.

The decomposition table is not auditable and some figures are wrong. Night/Low is not Orion 7.5 versus Nova 9.8: the only Orion night/Low case is 7.2 hours, and the two Nova night/Low cases average 8.9. Medium compliance-required Orion is about 16.9 across three cases, not the single case at 17.0. Medium Nova service time averages 6.4, not 6.5. Day versus night is never actually calculated. Within strata, almost all of the cycle-time gap is `wait_before_review_h`; service gaps are only about 0.1–0.4 hours. The skill story overstates service. Compliance rates are balanced within complexity, so the claim that Orion triggers more compliance checks is already contradicted. Appeal rates are essentially the same and are barely analyzed. Cycle time also exceeds wait plus service by a trace-dependent residual that is never used.

The graph and estimands are inconsistent. `resource_group -> shift` makes shift a consequence of assignment, while the role table calls shift a confounder. `quality_outcome -> case_completed` reverses time. `review_service -> rework` conflicts with service time already including the repeated review. `compliance_performed` is unused. Total, direct, and mediated effects are named in passing but never separated. There is no hypothesis that Orion assignment itself is harmful. The shift/load hypothesis cites high load as support for a lower-load mechanism. The within-reviewer test assumes reviewers cross groups, and the front-door claim does not meet the front-door conditions.

The code loads the cases, reproduces the crude and complexity means, standardizes on the overall complexity mix, and warns on cells under three cases. It never computes directly-follows relations or calls `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` / `pm4py.discover_dfg`. The closing recommendation is slightly under the required 120 words and states the within-mix efficiency result more strongly than the support, positivity, and queue-versus-service split allow.
