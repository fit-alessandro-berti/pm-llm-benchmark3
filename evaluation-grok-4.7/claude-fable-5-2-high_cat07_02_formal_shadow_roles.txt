The role model is largely the right one, and most of the arithmetic holds: event totals (10,534), activity shares, complexity shares, and the split of Giorgio’s A7/A8 inside versus outside Fatima’s absence all check out. Specialization, complexity, certification, and substitution are kept apart, and the ambiguities in section 4 are real. The score is held down by over-certain claims, two bad evidence links, and code that does not quite do what it says.

The broker row treats all 44 of Mateo’s A9 events as “preliminary” and all 16 of his A6 events as “triage.” The log only supports that reading for the week-5 episode (21 approvals, 13 later rechecked) and the weeks 7–8 filtering episode. Section 3 then turns 13/21 into “his preliminary approvals had a 62% recheck rate,” as if that were the rate on the full set of 44. Section 4 correctly reopens both questions, so the structure asserts what the later section admits is undecided.

Fragment 2 is misused twice. It is Nora register → R01/R02 validate → R03/R04 diagnose. It does not show front-office A3, so it does not show that Amira’s and Ben’s 75 and 60 diagnoses are triage. It also does not show a second validation after Nora’s A2; “validate again” is not in that fragment. The pre-check label itself is supported by Nora’s note, not by that citation. A13 is also conflated with rework: fragment 6’s rework step is not the Reopen activity, and Amira and Ben have only 8 and 4 A13 events against Mateo’s 42.

Smaller factual slips: Lina’s high-plus-critical share is 4/364 ≈ 1.1%, not ≤ 1%. “Nobody substitutes for Erik” sits poorly next to 50 other A6 events and the later suggestion that Chen’s 24 may be late-shift cover. “The 17 reopenings landed on Amira, Ben, and Mateo” joins a count to a frequent fragment and states the join as fact. Calling the bot the other late-shift front-office resource stretches a resource whose home team is Automation.

The code is a coherent sketch and the closing cautions about authority versus frequency are right. It still has real defects. Reopen rate is attributed to the last Validate Entitlement on the case, which can be a validation after the reopen. `originator_importance` is stored and ratioed as `events` even though that field is a weight. Activity cosine is computed on the full log while the PM4Py similarity uses the no-substitution view. The network frame is built from graphs that need not share a node set, so the constructor can fail. `n=2` is not a subcontracting parameter, and `escalation_level` is averaged with no check that it is numeric. `roles_humans` and `roles_complex` are built and then never compared, despite the text saying the views should be compared.

What keeps this above a weaker score is the discipline elsewhere: Chen is not treated as interchangeable with Dina, temps are not treated as certified agents, Giorgio’s standing commercial work is separated from one absence, Erik is not discarded for low volume, and the events-versus-cases gap for Mateo (415 vs 429) and Hana (245 vs 274) is a genuine finding. The faults are local overclaims, not a wrong organization.
