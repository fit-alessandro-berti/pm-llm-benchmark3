4.4

The memo keeps the required section outline and names non-stationarity, calendars, and cross-case effects, but it does not specify a simulatable twin and it misses several explicit deliverables.

Scope stays generic. Case, resource, and queue state are listed, not defined so a clock can advance them. Missing are an arrival intensity state, review join conditions before decision drafting, latent objection status, architect burst clusters, inspection-bundle buffers, travel versus wait, part-time and council calendars, and the environmental 40% reservation. Cross-case behavior is a sentence, not a mechanism.

Parameter estimation invents PM4Py operations (`split_log_to_periods`, `calculate_service_time`, `calculate_waiting_time`) and never specifies conditional routing from case attributes, a start-event arrival series, or a performance DFG. Fitting rework from unstructured reasons overclaims data the brief says are unreliable. Service and waiting are confounded by “extrapolating” missing starts from inferred queue delay, without stating that one timestamp cannot identify both.

Assumptions include that error directly: objections are “inferred solely from duration deviations,” which treats an unobserved event as measured and ignores holidays, council weeks, and staffing as other causes of long public notice. Rework independence, daily-accurate HR calendars, and seasonal multipliers “accurate to ±5%” are stronger than the evidence. Applicant delay is at least separated from municipal service time.

The experiment table is the largest gap. It stops at D5, omits D6–D9 and the required D1+D2+D3+D4 package, and has one stress instead of three. Cells such as “Captured” and “Major” are not factors and levels. D4 changes peak demand at the same time as capacity, so the effect is confounded. Trade-offs named in the brief are absent: applicant burden for the pre-checker, safety and missing violation history for skipping inspection, and longer wait for the first case in a geographic batch.

Output analysis is thin. A one-year warm-up and three-year runs are not tied to discarding initial bias or to seasonal-cycle statistics. Twenty-five fixed seeds are not common random numbers, paired contrasts, or quantile intervals. Beta-PERT is a bounded distribution and is a poor model for the heavy tails the log summary emphasizes. Sensitivity is two phrases.

Validation names the right headings and then blurs them. “Event-log replay” is used for simulated-trace comparison rather than control-flow conformance. KS tests on a mixture of case types will not check tails. Utilization is pointed at HR records instead of observed waits and throughput. There is no temporal holdout design and no acceptance range.

Decision rules do not meet the request for thresholds. Median duration is collapsed into undefined “SLAs,” p95 is mislabeled as fairness, and a universal 90% utilization cap ignores part-time fire staff and reserved environmental time. District and architect disparity, rework, backlog, and robustness under stress have no metrics or ranges. Optimality is not falsely claimed.

The skeleton is not a coherent PM4Py front end. `pm4py` is never imported; legacy `factory` paths are mixed with `format_dataframe`; the tree argument is a placeholder, not the given string, and its missing data conditions, appeals, and resources are not discussed. Variants use a wrong module path. Performance DFG and case-arrival average are absent. Service and waiting subtract `Complete timestamp` and `Start timestamp`, which are not in the excerpt. Arrival generation, routing, and resource calendars have no placeholders; one empty `SimulationModel` does not separate a dependency-aware engine from log handling.
