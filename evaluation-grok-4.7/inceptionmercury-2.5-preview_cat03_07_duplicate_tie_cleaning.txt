3.5

The write-up keeps the required nine sections and a few sound instincts: e05 and e14 stay, ingestion time is not the business clock, handover stays after carrier acceptance, and M1/M2 are not treated as one payment. Those points are outweighed by contradictions, wrong object facts, a decision tree that does not partition the six required cases, and an implementation/sensitivity pair that misses the checks the design is supposed to rest on.

The decision tree is not a tree. “Objects differ” sits under “same objects.” Harmonization collisions are placed on a different-timestamp branch, while e08 and e09 share 10:15:00. Exact technical duplication, same physical event, summary-versus-detail, label collision, true concurrency, and quarantine are blurred, and the terminal list disagrees with the branches: e03/e04 is “retain both” in one place and “merge attributes” in another. No branch states a full action for every required outcome in a mutually exclusive way.

The event table misses e14/e15. It repeatedly calls event-to-object links O2O and drops objects that are actually present (P1 on the picks; O1 on the invoice and dispatch rows). e06/e07 is marked high confidence even though the source fact leaves abstraction-level versus one transition unresolved, and there is no second scenario. “Merge to e06” and “link e15 to M1 and M2” fight the “keep both” decision. Fact 8 says the ERP summary has no Payment object; adding M1 and M2 invents relations. e11/e16 “merge attributes, keep both” never says whether one event or two remain. e13/e14 is the one clearly correct row.

The tie-break list exists, and the microseconds warning is aimed at the right risk, but the example uses Pick-before-Pack, which the same section treats as a real lifecycle constraint. Within-source `source_seq` is labeled merely deterministic, then not applied where it is the evidence: carrier 1 then 2, PSP 400 then 401 then 402, ERP 40 then 41. The 10:00 policy never places e05 before the pack that contains I2, and it never gives a deterministic total order among e03, e04, and e05. e08 before e09 is stated as logic although the facts only order e10 after e09. At 12:00, invoice issuance and authorization of M1 are placed before both captures. Fact 7 orders e12 only before e13. M1 before M2 “via ID” is an arbitrary key presented as if it were object semantics. No admissible alternative orders are given. The text does say the total order is not physical truth.

The conservative path keeps 17 events, which is right if the only deletions are exact key-and-hash duplicates, and this log has none. It then suffixes the source onto every activity, including Create Order and the two distinct picks. That is a global alphabet change, not a justified repair of harmonized collisions, and it manufactures cross-system directly-follows edges. Section 2’s equivalence link and attribute union for e03/e04 never appear in the pipeline steps. Original timestamp preservation is asserted, then contradicted by the code.

Evidence-based merging of e03/e04 and e11/e16 to 15 events is a coherent aggressive alternative, and leaving e06/e07 and e08/e09 unmerged is the right restraint. The damage statement stops at provenance and the sentence on payments is cut off. The mechanical scenario cites shared P1 for e13/e14; those events share O1 and V1 only. A same-activity, same-timestamp, any-common-object rule also collapses e03/e04/e05 through O1 and P1, e06 with e07, e08 with e09, e11 with e16, and e13 with e14 and e15. That is on the order of 10 retained events, not 14, and it drops the distinct I2 pick the constraints forbid. Section 7’s claim that the aggressive log only removes e14 cannot be reconciled with that rule. `have_common_object=True` is correctly called insufficient for M1 versus M2, then weakened by the P1 error and by never enumerating the other components that flag would glue together.

The code deep-copies once and keeps `original_timestamp` and `original_activity`. The duplicate mask is computed and then ignored; rows are filtered with `index.duplicated`, which does not implement the stated key. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is absent. `ocel_merge_duplicates` sits under `if False`, uses an `activity_col` argument the function does not take, and never passes `have_common_object`. The verified-pair helper is `pass`, covers neither e03/e04 nor a real e11/e16 union, and is never called. Sort is by `timestamp`, so every tie stays in input order. `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` then rewrites the working timestamp, which is the synthetic-duration failure the later safeguard says to avoid. The integrity check only asserts that the copied timestamp column is non-null. Relations, object identifiers, and dangling event references are unchecked. One scenario is returned.

Sensitivity names the four logs and then mis-states the mechanisms. Flattened DFGs are single traces on O1, not “parallel branches.” Merging e03 and e04 changes I1 frequency and removes a Pick-to-Pick self-loop; it does not reduce activity nodes from 3 to 2. Retaining same-label ties creates apparent rework (Pick, Pack, Dispatch, Invoice, Capture). Merging those pairs removes those loops. The write-up says the aggressive merge creates a Payment-to-Payment loop, which reverses the effect, and also says it yields a single capture. Capture-to-Issue-Invoice is the opposite of the section 3 order. Handover does not share an activity with Dispatch, so the stated mechanical rule does not absorb it. Event-to-object counts are not calculated (the raw log has 44). Event-type/object-type weights, the object-type graph, directly-follows frequencies, and lifecycle or performance effects of zero-duration ties versus index deltas are not analyzed. Several of the six “edges” are not edges under the author’s own order.

The provenance schema has the right fields except an explicit rule version, and raw reconstruction via source identifiers is stated. It does not say how to restore event-to-object links after a union, or how to undo global relabeling when no rows were merged. Among the eight failure modes, the payment safeguard says a merge may proceed on exact `source_seq` or different object IDs, which allows the M1/M2 collapse. The relation safeguard, “one object id per event type,” would reject legitimate multi-object events such as e06. The microseconds safeguard prescribes a sort index that the code does not build.

Taken together, the design names the right dangers and then does not carry them through the tree, the orders, the counts, the library calls, or the metric comparison.
