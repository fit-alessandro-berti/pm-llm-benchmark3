The write-up has the requested section skeleton and a correct offset identity (`true = recorded − offset`), but it is not a reliable interval analysis. Decision-relevant limits, time zones, duration arithmetic, and classifications are wrong often enough that the findings cannot be used.

The 15-minute S1 limit is replaced throughout by the 45-minute S2 limit. T-401 and T-402 are then judged against that substituted limit, so a roughly 12–16 minute T-401 gap is called a certain S1 breach and a roughly 16–21 minute T-402 gap is called a certain S1 pass. Under the actual 15-minute rule those two results reverse or become boundary cases: T-401 overlaps 15 minutes, and T-402 lies wholly above it. Tolerance shifts of 2 and 5 minutes therefore change classifications the sensitivity table reports as unchanged.

Profile parameters are not the supplied pairs. Alert→start is treated with invented centers such as 2250±450 or 2100±300 instead of 600±180, and start→containment is repeatedly given a standard deviation of 300 instead of 600. An interval lying wholly below a two-sided profile band is labeled CERTAIN_PASS in T-403, T-404, T-406, and T-409. The stated rule makes that situation a certain violation. Section 3 and Section 4 also disagree with each other: T-401’s profile result is a pass in one section and a violation in the other, and the T-402 zeta=2 “fully inside” claim uses a band of 1500–2700 against a stated duration of 1080–1230.

UTC intervals do not follow from the stated rounding and offset bounds. OPS-BER 10:14 CEST is 08:14 UTC plus about one minute of flooring and an offset of [−90 s, +120 s], a few-minute window. The table instead gives hour-scale ranges such as 06:38–07:38 and 06:48–07:38, while the duration math silently uses yet another window near 08:47. VENDOR-NY ±180 s is rendered as ±20 minutes, producing 07:40–08:20 instead of about 07:57–08:04. HUB’s combined ±0.5 s resolution and ±1 s offset is sometimes ±0.5 s, sometimes ±10 s, and often one-sided. SENSOR nearest-10-second rounding plus ±5 s becomes a multi-minute interval. MOBILE-LON is floored to a minute even though the source rounds to the nearest minute, and the same cell offers 11:49–12:14, 11:48–12:14, 12:09–13:14, and 11:48–12:20.

T-405 misses Europe/Berlin entirely. On 2025-10-26 the 02:00–02:59 fold is UTC+2 then UTC+1, so 02:50 is 00:50 UTC or 01:50 UTC, not 02:50 or 03:50. Floor-to-minute and ±60 s are not applied. Ingestion order leaves one feasible fold combination (alert on the first fold, start and containment on the second). Same-fold ordering is impossible because 02:05 is earlier than 02:50 on one offset. The surviving alert→start duration is about 12–18 minutes, which straddles S1. The answer’s 5- and 7-minute certain passes do not describe that union.

T-404 subtracts the 18–25 minute work-record duration from Containment_Complete rather than from Investigation_Complete, then calls the resulting gap 30–600 seconds. Anchored at 10:35, the inferred start is about 10:10–10:17 UTC and S1 is a possible violation. T-406’s duration is computed as if the earliest start produced a +12 minute gap; interval subtraction also allows the start to precede the alert, and the upper end exceeds 15 minutes. T-407 sets Alert_Received ≤ Alert_Sent, drops the [0, 20] minute delivery bound, and scores S1 on Alert_Sent. Receipt is unobserved, the causal assumption is unlogged, and S1 is indeterminate or only conditionally possible. T-408’s close gap is about 38 h 25 min, a certain S3 breach of 36 hours and a profile violation at zeta 1 and 2; the answer reports 30 hours and a pass. T-409’s weak [−12 min, +12 min] offset is applied only backward, so the upper end stops near 16:04 instead of about 16:17. S1 for that case is omitted, and the shortened interval hides a zeta=1 profile overlap.

Most testable rows are absent. Cases with all endpoints, especially T-401 and T-408, need S1, S2, S3 and every supplied profile pair. ISO intervals are not used. Several tabulated durations are not the differences of the tabulated endpoints.

The code does not run. Time zones are parsed from fragments such as `"BER"` and `"NY"`, T-404 and T-405 indexing refers to columns that were never created, ambiguous Berlin times are localized without a fold, and the offset return order reverses the interval. Rounding, entry delay, and order constraints are not implemented. The midpoint frame is built, but `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` is never called; a token-based stand-in checks a fixed 900-second cut and ignores zeta. The required comparison with the interval conclusions is not produced.

Section 6 treats T-402 as the only possible violation and reports no indeterminate result. The minimum-evidence discussion therefore misses the missing receipt in T-407, the DST-fold and ingestion evidence in T-405, the duration anchor in T-404, the entry-delay semantics in T-406, and the weak NTP bound in T-409. Section 7 names several useful controls, but it does not state a crisp rule for whether overlap alone is enforceable, and it does not cover raw timestamp retention, start-versus-completion semantics, or causal identifiers in a way that would repair these cases.
