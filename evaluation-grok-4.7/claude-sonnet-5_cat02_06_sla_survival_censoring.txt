The write-up shows real competing-risks competence through section 7, then fails required deliverables and contradicts itself on several case-level facts.

Sections 1–7 correctly set the origin at `Submit`, separate already-observed breaches from right censoring and from competing exits, keep open cases in the analysis, and refuse to treat `Escalate` or rework as baseline covariates. The Aalen–Johansen primary choice, the n=32 warning against Fine–Gray, the three transfer codings, and the non-causal reading of cohort B are defensible. Case lists for opens (9/32), cohort sizes (15/17), AutoCheck non-compliance (S20, S23, S26, S30), and withdrawals/rejections (4/15 vs 3/17) match the table.

The document is incomplete against the required format. Heading 9 is absent, so the mandated data-gap priority list is never given as a section. The single code block ends mid-token inside `groupby(["coh`, so it is not executable. It never finishes the cohort-by-priority summary and never builds a PM4Py log or a control-flow feature (`parse_event_log_string`, `get_variants`, or an equivalent). Those are explicit checklist items. The hand-rolled Aalen–Johansen update that does appear is structurally right, and the case table is transcribed accurately, but a truncated script cannot satisfy the print-and-compare requirement.

Internal errors are concrete. Section 4 says there are 4 transfers; section 6 correctly counts 3 (S08, S21, S32). E1 is defined as \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\) and then treated as assumption-dependent for withdrawal and rejection; those assumptions change the estimand, rather than the contribution of S06 to a fixed CIF. E2 is written as \(\mathrm{CIF}(t)\) for \(t>72\), which still includes resolutions before 72; the late-only target is the increment. Minus signs are missing in several displayed formulas (`1 CIF`, `1CIF_Resolve(72)`).

Section 6.3’s best case, “every open case resolves within 72h,” is impossible for S09, S11, S25, and S26, whose ages already exceed 72. That contradicts sections 2–3, which correctly call those breaches deterministic, and it inflates the unidentified share from the five opens still under 72 to all nine opens. S26’s gap of 72h on an age of 104h places the last event near 32h, not “near the start.” The alert rule (age > 48, gap > 24, stage Review or Escalate) matches S09, S11, S25, and S26; the cited examples S10 (47h, gap 19) and S29 (gap 6) fail it. The state diagram omits observed edges Screen→Escalate, Screen→Resolve, AutoCheck→Escalate, AutoCheck→Review, AutoCheck→Resolve, and Rework→Resolve. Dropping non-AutoCheck rows is also labeled compliance adjustment even while selection bias is noted.

Taken together, the reasoning is well above a generic survival sketch, but missing closure, a non-running code block, a skipped process-mining step, and repeated conflicts with the same table keep it far from a high score.
