These issues keep it short of nearly flawless:

- The effects table says dynamic batching may lower scrap per good item. The log links scrap to rework and the CNC_B path (1.8 kg), and links larger batches to energy per item and to items left unshipped. Scrap reduction is not an evidenced batching mechanism.
- Intervention 2 ties the aggregate stock-to-air path (5% of orders, 18.5 kgCO2e) to Standard-stock Service-contract. That path is not segment-specific. The segment average is 7.8 kgCO2e, and the 22% SP1 gap is a policy failure that need not all be air. The “especially” clause narrows the claim, but the attribution is still loose.
- Twice-daily truck departures and a 12–24 h consolidation wait are both adopted without reconciliation. Evenly spaced twice-daily departures put the wait near 12 hours, so the top of that range is not a property of the design as stated.
- The consolidation option warns against extra shipments sent “to meet a batch cutoff,” which imports production-batch language into a transport rule.
- The availability option adds “pick/transfer,” a stock-transfer step the DFG does not contain.
- Section 5 covers batching, transport, packaging mass, rework, obsolescence, and customer response, but it leaves implicit the stated link between route_mode and packaging rework (260 standard-pack and 360 reinforced-pack rework arcs). A ground or consolidation shift can create those loops unless the compatibility check is binding. The later package pairs them; the risk section itself does not.
- The closing “largest burdens” list gives CNC_B (5% share) and partial shipments equal billing with the bigger pools: made-to-order holds most of the energy, and Critical-downtime dedicated vans hold most of the transport. The redesign still includes batching and van controls, so the package is sounder than that sentence.

No cited number is materially wrong, and none of these points reverses the recommendation. They are enough to withhold a higher score.
