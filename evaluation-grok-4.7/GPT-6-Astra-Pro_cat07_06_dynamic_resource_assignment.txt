7.5

The answer is unusually careful and mostly faithful to the scenario, but it is not nearly flawless. The central assignment plan conflicts with the stated continuity rule, overclaims necessity, and leaves the author’s own feasibility checks unresolved.

The strongest parts are real. Hard rules for skill 0, Security, Approval, overtime, the cap of four, and Quality batching are stated separately from preferences. Skill levels in the table match the matrix. Nine normal/low cases and a minimum of two training assignments (2/9) are counted correctly, as are 10 retained owners out of 15. Invalid training is refused where the matrix says skill 0 (Eva and Jonas on Security; David and Hugo on Approval) or overtime is high (Bruno on Quality). The first-available illustration is right: Jonas has the fewest open cases and nonzero Approval skill, but level 1 cannot finalize. Causation is not invented from the 1.8×, 2.7-hour, and 3.3-hour associations. The audit definitions avoid the usual process-mining traps (double-counting `reassignment_count`, treating DFG gaps as waiting, reading role discovery as authorization). The worker text is non-punitive, and the answer does not pretend the snapshot proves an optimum.

The main failure is C001. Constraint 5 prefers continuity on a high-complexity, high-handover case unless the owner is skill 0 or 1 **and** supervision is infeasible. Celine is Security skill 1, and constraint 6 explicitly allows skill 1–2 under Gita or Hugo. Supervised retention is therefore the option the rules prefer. The answer never compares it. It promotes the case note “needs specialist” into a hard rule and labels Hugo’s takeover “Necessary.” That word is false. Both options are dangerous: a high-complexity handover has a 2.7-hour median wait against a 3-hour SLA, while weak-skill execution has a 3.3-hour service penalty. The recommended plan then leaves C015 on the same specialist and only says “avoid delay,” instead of moving or deferring C015 so the urgent case is structurally protected.

The same pattern appears elsewhere. Celine is given C007 (high complexity, handover already at 3, 6 hours), lead of the repeated-loop C003, and supervision of C009, while the algorithm says optional training should be removed when the joint schedule is infeasible. C003 is also the assignment that pushes reassignments from 2 to 3, which the cited history treats as the danger zone, and it is used to clear the 20% training bar. Farid, the only Approval level 3, is given both sub-4-hour urgents, C010, and that learning task, without a direct comparison to Eva, who is independently allowed to finalize at level 2. Jonas is placed exactly on the cap of four, with hour feasibility deferred. Step 9 of the algorithm is not applied to the plan that is published. “Small swaps” and “material advantage” are also too vague for the strict deviation audit promised in section 6.

Lesser but real defects: the C004 and C012 trade-off cells describe alternatives the constraints have already removed; the worker explanation never says why Jonas and Farid absorb the batch; and a few PM4Py call signatures are more specific than a sketch can guarantee, even though the semantic warnings about those functions are largely right.
