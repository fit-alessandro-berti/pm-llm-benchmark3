The policy is complete, usable, and usually faithful to the queue, but it is not internally stable enough for a near-flawless score. Several rules contradict each other, one comparison claim is false, and the audit sketch mis-measures two indicators it defines.

The recommended queue is mostly coherent. Skill 0 is never assigned. Urgent and high work stays at skill 2–3. Security and Approval completers are skill 3. Bruno, David, and Iris receive no new work. New loads stay at or below 3. C009 is a real supervised training ownership. C015 correctly refuses to let Eva or Jonas perform Security at skill 0. The 10 kept owners and the six moves are counted correctly, and the historical 1.8×, 1.4×, 2.7h, and 3.3h figures are used as stated. The worker note is specific and non-punitive, and the answer does treat the result as a trade-off.

The comparison overstates a cost that the baselines share. Under both a static open-case reading and an urgency-ordered running tally, specialist-only also sends C003 from David to Celine, so the reassignment count still becomes 3. First-available does the same, because David has 14 open cases. The claim that the proposed policy is worse than both baselines on C003 is therefore false; that outcome is the same scar, not a distinctive one. The specialist map itself (C014 to Hugo, four cases each for Celine, Farid, and Hugo, and a C015 tie with Gita) is reproducible only if open cases are updated after every assignment and the queue follows the policy’s urgency order. The stated baseline says only “highest skill, then fewest current open cases, then more hours,” and “in this snapshot” reads as the table’s static counts. On those counts Celine, at 6 open cases, takes both rework cases and Hugo never ties Gita. Section 4 cannot be re-audited from the rule it prints.

The algorithm does not decide what the prose says it decides. Owner-keep fires whenever the owner is eligible and skill is at least 2, including low handover cost. Scoring, training, fairness, and resilience then never run for most of the queue. C005 and C016 are described as close calls; under the control flow they are automatic keeps. The later equal-points order (fewer new assignments, then overtime, then skill, then owner) conflicts with `close_call` (owner earlier, then same activity family). On C014 those rules can select different people: Celine and Jonas both score 7, family-continuity selects Celine, and fewer new assignments selects Jonas. High-overtime eligibility asks whether any other legal candidate exists from inside `eligible`, which is circular, while `relax_once` also implements that exception. The 0.60–0.69 “partial continuity” band is never added to `score`. Hours remaining are a per-case floor and are not consumed, so Farid can be given a 2h approval, a 4h negotiation, and C010 against 6.5 hours, and Celine can receive three cases plus a checkpoint and a brief against 6.0. Supervision is said to count as load and then omitted from `new_load`.

C001 is the sharpest constraint miss. The scenario allows Security at skill 1–2 under Gita or Hugo, and it prefers continuity on a high-complexity, high-handover case when supervision is feasible. The policy bans every critical Security performer below skill 3 and applies the 3.3h weak-skill penalty to that supervised option. The evidence given is about keeping a weak skill, not about a specialist supervising the current owner. Hugo-as-performer may still be reasonable, but the supervised-continuity alternative is removed before it is scored, on a 3h SLA where the 2.7h handover median is itself enough to breach.

The audit sketch has concrete faults. `get_variants` puts the activity tuple in the key and the count or case list in the value; the code tests the value, so the rework cohort does not form. `ends_with_rework` also tests containment, not the final activity. Training share checks only `assignment_reason == "TRAINING_SHADOW"`. C015 is stored as owner continuity plus `shadow_resource`, so the 2-of-9 figure the policy reports would not be reproduced. Counting that skill-0 shadow toward the 20% assignment target is disclosed, and performer-level training is honestly 1 of 9, but the metric and the log write-back still disagree.

Smaller slips sit on top of those. C010 has no reason code from the published list. C005’s backup is an “unnamed spare,” against the policy’s own named-valve rule. The worker note says the cases Bruno, David, and Iris already own stay with them, then says C003, C014, C010, and C006 moved. SLA urgency is a sort key and a one-point critical bonus, not a score component comparable to the others. Those would be nits in an otherwise fixed specification. Here the specification can still assign C014 either way, and the baseline comparison states a contrast the encodings do not support.
