The write-up has the right skeleton: parallel completion is treated as a maximum, weekend closures are applied, reruns are cut at 18:00, the temperature-excursion flag is used as a shared cause, and scenario overlap is declared. Those choices are not enough to carry the forecast. Several headline times contradict the stage arithmetic that is supposed to justify them, and two probability bands are not the events they name.

The smooth-path clock is internally inconsistent. Section 3 puts a central chemistry result at Monday 11:00–13:00 and QA acceptance at 14:00–18:00, so release falls in the late afternoon. The scenario table and the earliest-completion window still open at Monday 11:00 (about 68 h). That instant is only an extreme stack: minimum chemistry service started at 08:00 with nobody ahead, an empty QA queue, and a sub-median review. It is not a plausible lower edge of the same path whose chemistry result is still arriving at 11:00–13:00.

The Monday chemistry start (08:00–09:30, done by about 11:00–13:00) is also the wrong base case. FIFO is the more common discipline (about 55%), and one urgent plus three ordinary samples are already ahead. Both analysts are occupied past 16:00; the minimum chemistry service is 1.5 h, so a new sample started after about 16:30 cannot finish by 18:00. Under priority, B-417 might be started and paused; under FIFO it mostly does not start. Monday therefore opens with resumed work-in-process plus a residual queue. Centering the smooth path on a near-opening start, and calling a strict FIFO slip “several hours” and “modest,” understates a shift that can move the join from late Monday morning into the afternoon or Tuesday.

That error propagates into the rerun scenario. Conditional on a borderline result, the first-run finish-time distribution is the same as the smooth path. A mode rerun of 1.8 h (maximum 3 h) after a Monday 11:00–13:00 result finishes before 18:00, and QA is open until 20:00, so the central rerun release is Monday evening. The table instead gives Monday 20:00 as the earliest and Tuesday 14:00–18:00 as the center. Tuesday is the spill case (first result after about 15:00, or a long service that hits the closure), not the center. The favorable narrative in Section 3 (release about 19:00–20:30, and an even earlier stack near 17:00) does not match the table.

Microbiology timing is mostly right: Friday 14:00 plus 46–54 h is Sunday 12:00–20:00, and without on-call the readout waits until Monday 08:00. The contamination scenario then drifts late and gets an impossible probability. A Monday 08:30 indication plus 20 h is ready around Tuesday 04:30 and can be read at 08:00, with release around midday, but the earliest terminal time is Tuesday 12:00 and the center is 16:00–20:00. On-call plus contamination is dismissed as still landing on Tuesday “because chemistry and QA constrain earlier steps”; a Sunday readout plus 20–30 h often lands on Monday, when a smooth chemistry branch is already done. The scenario band 0.08–0.18 also exceeds the ledger’s own unconditional contamination band of about 0.08–0.12.

The coupled tail is mis-counted. Excursion (about 0.30) times elevated rerun risk, elevated contamination risk, investigation risk 0.30–0.55, and a 20–40% laboratory share is on the order of 1% or below if those factors must occur together. The stated 0.03–0.08 matches, at best, the marginal chance of some laboratory investigation, not the named four-way conjunction. Using 3–8% for that conjunction makes the multi-week tail look like a P90 driver when it is a far-tail driver. The P80 and P90 “ranges” also start near the center (Monday 15:00, then Wednesday) and run out to 120–340 h, so they are spans of the distribution rather than ranges for those quantiles.

Smaller defects add up. Remaining hours do not match the clocks: Tuesday 16:00–20:00 is about 96–101 h, not 99–103 h; Wednesday 10:00–18:00 is about 114–123 h, not 119–127 h. Documentation-only work of 8–20 QA hours from a Monday afternoon open generally finishes Tuesday–Wednesday (12 h QA days), not Thursday–Friday. QA “moderate congestion” does not follow from 0.35 arrivals per hour against roughly one batch per hour of two-reviewer capacity; steady-state wait is short. The five batches already queued, and the single reviewer only through Friday 20:00 and Saturday 09:00–13:00, are the real backlog, and that initial condition is never propagated to Monday. Saturday QA cannot be used anyway: primary readiness is Sunday at the earliest and the chemistry lab is closed all weekend.

The excursion flag is a reasonable primary update, and treating on-call authorization as secondary is right because a Sunday microbiology result still waits on chemistry. The forecast that sits on top of these calculations is systematically early on the smooth Monday chemistry start, about half a day to a day late on ordinary reruns, and too thick in the laboratory-investigation tail.
