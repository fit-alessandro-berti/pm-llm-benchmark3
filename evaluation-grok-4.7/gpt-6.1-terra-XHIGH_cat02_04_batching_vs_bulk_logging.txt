7.6

The diagnoses are numerically careful and usually epistemically disciplined, but the required program is not reliable and two primary rankings are stronger than the answer’s own evidence.

The wait, service, occupancy, and overlap calculations check out: G1 4.25 and 3; G2 12.25 and 17.5 against a 27-minute span; G3 19.5 and 16.75 over 28 minutes; G4 waits 7–19 with mean 13; G5 15.25 and 17.5, with all four intervals meeting over 13:12–13:20; G6 35–175 with mean about 103.43 and zero service; G7 mean wait −4.2; G8 mean wait 48.2 and a 35-minute run. G6’s inflated-wait/zero-service mechanism, the G5 three-analyst capacity contradiction, refusal to clamp negative waits, and separation of PM4Py labels from physical execution are sound. The signature matrix, metric definitions, and collection redesign cover the requested phenomena without inventing a process model.

The code would fail on standard PM4Py: `discover_batches` takes a `parameters` dictionary, not `merge_distance` and `min_batch_size` keywords, so it raises `TypeError` before printing the required table. “Tested implementation” and the claimed category map are therefore unsupported. Zero-duration G6 behavior is also version-dependent. The queue snapshot is weak because `start >= first_start` is true for every row once `first_start` is the minimum.

G4’s 85 for “sequential batching” conflicts with the stated counterevidence. Exact adjacency and a short pre-arrival queue fit ordinary single-server flow equally well; there is no batch identifier or operational batching statement. G8’s 75 attaches an unproven safety-related capacity loss despite the explicit unsynchronized-clock warning. G5’s 88 treats a known shared login as though it established that the overlap is an artifact. G3’s “one 11:31 insertion” overstates minute-resolution timestamps, and G6’s “end-of-shift” adds a boundary the data do not state. Bias is explained well for the preferred reading of each cluster, but not systematically for every competing interpretation.
