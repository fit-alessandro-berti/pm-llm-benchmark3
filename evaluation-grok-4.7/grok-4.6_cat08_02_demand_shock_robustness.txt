7.5

The redesign is operationally strong: mix-versus-volume math, conditional policies, and the rejection of standing I1/I2/I3/I9 are well grounded. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-mining claims are wrong or internally inconsistent, and those errors sit on the exact bottleneck logic the task asks for.

The weekly decomposition is right and important. Volume rises about 43% (2,300 to 3,300/week) while Urgent rises from about 414 to 957/week and Complex-panel from about 299 to 660/week, with Routine essentially flat. Branch rates at pre-analytical check (4.2% to 8.0%), batch wait (4.4% to 12.7%), assay repeat (6.7% to 14.8%), technical-validation to medical-validation (24.5% to 42.3%), and specialist hold (1.8% to 7.5%) match the DFGs. Scenario coverage, on/off triggers, the trade-off table, redesign-caused fragility, and the leading-versus-lagging split all meet the required form. Gating I8, refusing global Urgent-first, keeping Routine off a pure M2 path, and refusing I9 because `Medical validation -> Release result` is already 2h/5h are the right calls.

The serious miss is I5 in S1. Evening medical capacity acts on the medical-validator queue, which the table puts at 22h peak and which lines up with `Technical validation -> Medical validation` at 8h/24h. The answer instead says I5 shortens `Medical validation -> Release result`, the edge that is already 2h/5h, and treats the 24h wait as if it remains after that relief. S3 and the P-Evening policy later call I5 the direct medical-queue relief. Those passages contradict each other.

The recollection arithmetic is also wrong. Recollection inflows balance: normal 760+70=830 and peak 780+110=890, matching 540+290 and 520+370. Cancels are 35% then 42% of resolutions. The cited “38% → 47%” and “47% of the 780 cancel” divide cancel outflows by only the direct pre-analytical inflow and drop `Manual accession correction -> Request recollection`. That false rate is repeated in the dashboard.

S1’s bottleneck order does not match the evidence it cites. Under the mix shift, the longest queues are specialist review (36h), medical validation (22h), and assay (18h); technical validation is already at 101%. Leading with pre-analytical check (9.5h, 96%) as the first overload describes flow order more than constraint severity. Specialist volume, which the answer itself puts at roughly 5.6× weekly, is left out of that opening sequence.

R3 has the same migration blind spot. Shifting M1 toward Urgent and Complex-panel pushes a larger share of completed assays onto medical validation and specialist review. The trade-off row names technical validation as the next bottleneck. The P-Urgent row does mention the later medical queue, so the package is not consistent on this point.

Smaller faults: raw 11,700 versus 4,050 technical-release counts are compared across 8-week and 3-week windows even though weekly automatic releases only fall from about 1,460 to 1,350; “share collapses” needs the 74% to 50% denominators. End-to-end completion of 86% versus 81% is similarly unadjusted for unequal windows. “Urgent p90 back under 18h” is not a return to baseline: normal Urgent p90 is already 19h. R5’s failure mode misses the risk that moving failure-prone assay families onto M1 degrades the protected machine if the night/M2 cluster is case-mix rather than machine causation. “Still need local sign-out” for cases already in specialist hold is an added assumption, and those in-hold cases are the ones occupying the scarce specialist.

The control design would still be usable. The score stays at 7.5 because a bottleneck-migration analysis that mis-labels the edge I5 changes, mis-computes a DFG cancel rate, and mis-orders the main scenario’s constraints is not a near-exact reading of the log.
