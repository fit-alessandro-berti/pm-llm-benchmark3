5.5

The write-up covers every required heading and correctly refuses to treat raw approval gaps as proof of discrimination. It is not a reliable assessment: several load-bearing figures are invented or misread, the adjustment strategy mishandles the biased covariates the scenario flags, the PM4Py plan misuses core functions, and the verdict contradicts itself.

**Invented or misread evidence.** “7 bilingual caseworkers handling 57% of Group B cases” and “5 specialists handle 62% of Group C cases” swap need/request rates for caseload. “45 general caseworkers handle 88% of Group A cases” appears nowhere; Group A is spread across 52 caseworkers. “Handover adds 2+ days” is not in the log. V3’s 18.4 days and V5’s 21.6 days are full-path medians, not step waits; the fraud-review wait is about 2.0–2.8 days and barely differs by group. “Higher documentation_completeness” reverses the table: B, C, and D have higher incompleteness. Section 1’s “24% go through language support” and “31% through accommodation review” are single-variant shares. Those sit next to 57% and 62% need/request rates, and that gap is never examined. Handovers are generalized beyond the two regions where they are reported. Group C’s 24% rework rate, the highest, is left out of the appeal/rework claim.

**Adjustment logic.** The strategy lists `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested` as matching variables without saying that they nearly define Groups B and C and are the reason the rights-preserving steps exist. Conditioning on them absorbs the procedural difference under study. Overlap is thin (language support 2% vs 57%; accommodation 4% vs 62%) and is not mentioned. The requested sensitivity check on biased covariates is replaced by re-thresholding a band and by urban/rural splits the data do not define. Re-estimating with and without `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`, as mediators or bad controls, is absent. Section 8 then says support delays remain “after accounting for resource constraints,” but the preliminary model does not control for resource or workload.

**Mechanisms, mitigations, and the verdict.** Case complexity is applied to B and C through the fraud path that mainly fits D. Language support is described as delay “without clear benefit,” which undercuts its status as a rights-preserving step. Capacity, separate timeliness targets, fewer handovers, explanations, monitoring, and audits are all present. Side effects are thin or pointed the wrong way: monitoring never mentions over-control, and loosening a biased risk score is said to raise false positives. The closing section states both that the process “is not procedurally fair” and that the evidence is “indeterminate,” and it calls the burden “unnecessary” and “without clear justification” while still allowing legitimate complexity as an open cause. A calibrated reading is residual unequal burden, especially queues and handovers on support paths, with causation unresolved.

**PM4Py plan.** Filtering, variants, DFGs, performance DFGs, and handover networks are aimed at the right questions, but the plan would not answer them. `filter_variants` is called on a variant dict with a lambda; it filters a log to listed variants. `get_all_case_durations` is labeled waiting time. `discover_declare` is given a log skeleton. Resource similarity is treated as overload, and organizational roles as load. Waiting times are computed on the pooled log, not within the same variant and step across groups, so path mix and within-step delay stay entangled.
