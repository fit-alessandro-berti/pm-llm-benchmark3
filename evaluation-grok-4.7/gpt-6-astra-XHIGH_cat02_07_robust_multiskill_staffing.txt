The offered-load arithmetic, calendar accounting, and scenario definitions are largely auditable and internally consistent. Midpoint service means (6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50, 15.74), block loads, the 2,490-minute roster, the 2,241-minute 90% budget, the 479.6- and 532.9-minute gaps, U1’s 53.2 cases and 3,442 minutes, renormalized U2, Chen’s 450-minute removal, and the U4 diagnostic increment all check. Skill ceilings correctly refuse to add overlapping pools, and the 2,065–3,376 range correctly blocks any claim that every distribution inside the given ranges overloads the roster. SLA flow time, isolated-day drain, U2’s two readings, and \(E[\max(X,Y)]\neq\max(E[X],E[Y])\) are handled explicitly. The PM4Py section matches the library’s actual definitions: summed interval service, sojourn from first start to last completion, waiting as their difference, and a completion-order handover network that is not a causal branch log.

The policy experiment is weaker than that capacity analysis. Strict specialization is implemented as a pathological roster: Software is only Emil, so morning software capacity is zero and Chen/Farah are unused; Mechanical excludes Deepa, so the “late M” failure is built in; Intake excludes Ada despite a 1.00 multiplier. Because those diagnostic queues are single-skill, setup cost is identically zero for Specialist and is paid only by Pool and Hybrid. The reported specialization-versus-pooling gap therefore mixes stranded coverage with routing. That is disclosed as “deliberately strict,” but it is still the only specialization arm behind the recommendation.

Several implemented mechanisms diverge from the stated rules. During the 09:30–10:00 intake outage, workers already assigned to Intake stay locked and cannot move to diagnostic work, so U5 removes more capacity than the prose claims; U5 is the only scenario in which Hybrid’s P1 attainment clearly exceeds Pool’s. The P1 “reserve” protects one fastest on-shift worker per skill globally, not one per threatened P1. Sequential mode pins the case to a worker but does not reserve that worker, while the branch estimate assumes immediate continuation. Validation after a two-branch case emits two transfer edges even when both parents are the same resource. `expected_work` floors residual work at 5 minutes, which is undisclosed and can distort preemption and ETAs. The fairness penalty uses cumulative day fraction and is inert until late, and the four-hour P3 promotion rarely beats a true P2 because the original deadline remains the next key. Those gaps make the table illustrative in a stronger sense than the caveats already say.

The recommendation itself is appropriately conditional: routing is not treated as a substitute for the missing half-shift-plus, no row is said to meet 90%, and seed overlap is reported. Naming Hybrid as the pilot default still puts more weight on one noisy U5 contrast (about 71.7% versus 65.4%, on roughly 190 P1 cases) than the paired uncertainty supports. Pool is better on mean delay in every listed scenario and more even on the reported utilization spread.

Format, objectives, monitoring attributes, and the pilot/stopping design meet the brief. The score stays below 8 because the horse race that separates the three policies is biased by eligibility design and by at least one undisclosed U5 mechanic, even though the staffing infeasibility result does not depend on that horse race.
