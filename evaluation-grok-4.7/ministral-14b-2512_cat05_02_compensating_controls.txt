4.5

The write-up has the right overall shape and several defensible case calls, but it is not reliable. Required distinctions are missing or contradicted, case facts are misstated, and the mandatory checking code cannot run.

The classification column is the strongest part. AP-202, AP-204, AP-205, and AP-208 are correctly separated from raw four-eyes form. AP-203 and AP-207 are fairly treated as permitted emergency paths. AP-201 as only partly met, because the same-user release was convenience, is defensible. AP-209 as partly met is also defensible. AP-206 is the weak call: token timing is explicitly unresolved, so the honest class is indeterminate, not a settled partial pass.

Those calls are undercut by false or overstated cells. AP-206 is given AP-208’s defect (“no bank-ownership evidence”) even though AV-2 fully passed and there was no bank change; that error is repeated in the counterfactual. AP-201’s manager acted before the 10:00 settlement, and the audit is about 23 hours after settlement, yet corrective capacity is “none.” AP-209’s audit is about 16 hours after settlement, still inside the usual recall window, yet corrective capacity is also “none.” AP-203 and AP-207 are assigned residual risk and severity “none.” AP-201 lists the emergency-token status as missing when the file states there was none. AP-202’s late, conflicted ratification is listed as preventive.

Section 2 never defines “merely mitigating,” one of the four required sufficiency levels. The expected-loss paragraph confuses loss amount with likelihood and does not show why equal expected loss can still fail an independence, timing, or accountability objective. The “fully equivalent” test also conflicts with later semantics: pre-settlement manager review counts as preventive equivalence in §2 and as non-preventive hybrid detection in §4, while the case table calls it preventive for AP-201 and AP-207.

Section 4 answers the timing question, then misstates the mechanisms. An overridden AV-2 warning is a bypassed preventive check, not a detective control. A manager acting after release but before settlement can stop settlement; that is not a bank recall. Section 1 never maps AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 for each threat, treats ED-1 itself as prevention, and gives collusion no corrective path even when recall is still open.

Section 5 reads “which control breaks the causal chain” as “which control failed,” and the AP-206 counterfactual depends on the false missing bank-ownership premise. Section 6 covers the requested monitoring topics, then says AV-2 should block every recent bank change (it may warn and be overridden) and labels risk patterns as “proof of misconduct,” including a same-user condition that does not fit AP-204.

The code does not satisfy the executable check. `pm4py` is never imported; `from pm4py.format_dataframe import dataframe` and `format_event_log` are not the library API; `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called with nonexistent `start_activity`/`end_activity` keywords; the variant and attribute filters are invoked with the wrong signatures and their completed-payment result is unused. There is no dataframe join. `emergency_eligible` looks for an `Emergency_Token_Active` event that is not in the log, so it is always false. `same_user` is true for every preparer row, including distinct-user cases. Hardcoded manager timing marks AP-204 and AP-209 as `after_release` although neither has a manager event, and audit flags echo those labels instead of timestamps. The notes do not state the version-sensitive point that four-eyes filtering may keep compliant cases rather than violations.
