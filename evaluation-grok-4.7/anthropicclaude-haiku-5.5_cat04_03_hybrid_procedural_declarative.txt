The answer is incomplete and cannot score higher. Sections 4–10 are missing, so there is no `declare_model`, no `log_skeleton`, no `temporal_profile` (and no hour-to-second values), no resource/data checks, no T1–T8 verdict matrix, and no deployment guidance. The text also claims that policy B is implemented in §7 and applied to T6 in §8. Those sections are not there.

What is present is mostly coherent, but not tight enough to offset that gap.

Section 1 handles R5 versus R6 properly: the policies are incompatible, they are not silently merged, Resolution A is selected as fail-closed, and the false-positive and off-log risks are stated. Assumptions A1–A14 are explicit. A8’s choice to treat post-activation labels as procedurally “not evaluated” can hide later violations, and A5 does not match the net that was actually built.

Section 2 assigns the right families of constraints to the right formalisms: exclusivity and route choice to the partial order, eventual and alternate obligations toward DECLARE, at-most-once counts toward the log skeleton, durations toward a warning-only profile, and segregation of duties plus the emergency register toward a data layer. Several rows never mark the authoritative layer. `precedence(Activate, Change Bank Details)` forces every bank change to follow activation, which R11 does not say. `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend)` likewise ignores the “after activation” guard. `always_after(Request, Receive)` is asserted without the orientation argument the task requires. `succession` does not enforce “before the next request”; only `altresponse` does, and neither enforces “before the final outcome.”

The POWL in §3 is the strongest artifact. `procedural_model` has an explicit receive start, concurrent capture/screening/verification, a join into classification, `XOR` of reject versus accept, `XOR` of manager versus EDD-then-compliance, an optional `SE → LO` block ordered before activation, payment after activation, and close after both terminals. `LOOP(τ, Request → Receive)` correctly allows zero or more closed cycles. The accepted-path-only escalation is disclosed as conservative.

Two modeling gaps are understated. The clarification node ends before the whole outcome block, so a request cannot run during approval or EDD and still close before activation or rejection, which is stricter than A5. Legal opinion cannot occur without sanctions escalation, and a rejected case cannot contain escalation at all. R10 versus a later release under R11 is deferred to a missing §9. The formal partial-order notation is also damaged: the order symbol and the precedence arrows are blank, so §3.1 is not readable without the code.

The design judgment in the completed portion is real. The required hybrid model, dictionaries, executable checks, and layered verdicts were not delivered.
