7.3

The design is a serious activity-level discrete-event twin: municipal service, queue wait, and applicant/third-party delay are separated; calendars, seasonality, architect bursts, inspection batching, and latent objections are inside the model; D1–D10 plus three stresses are specified with levels and trade-offs; and optimality is correctly withheld. The deductions below are from concrete inconsistencies, not missing polish.

The experiment runner breaks the common-random-number claim. Section 5 requires the same arrival, service, and external-delay streams across policies. The skeleton sets `seed = base_seed + 10_000 * (hash(exp.name) % 10_000) + r`. That gives each policy its own stream, and Python’s salted `hash()` is not stable across processes, so paired differences and reruns are not reproducible. `ExperimentRunner` also never builds or calls `DigitalTwinSimulator`, so the placeholders are not one pipeline.

Waiting-time code contradicts the estimation plan. The plan defines wait as start minus prerequisite enablement. `enrich_service_wait` uses the previous completion in case order. Under the tree’s parallel planning/fire/environment block, that predecessor often does not enable the activity, and overlap makes the computed wait negative. With completion-only logs the NaNs are appropriate; the start-timestamp branch is not.

`pm4py.get_variant_statistics` is not a top-level PM4Py call. The list-returning helper lives at `pm4py.statistics.traces.generic.log.case_statistics.get_variant_statistics`; the supported wrapper is `pm4py.get_variants`, which returns a dict. `artifacts["variants"][:5]` only matches the old list shape. `format_dataframe`, `convert_to_event_log`, `discover_dfg`, and `discover_performance_dfg` are used coherently. The tree fallback is defensive, but it does not record the real semantic limits: the log repeats `Completeness check` and `Site inspection`, while the tree invents `Completeness rework` and `Inspection rework` and omits planning, legal, and appeal loops.

Cohort and censoring rules are missing. Complex-variance p90 is about 260 days. A 12-month warm-up plus an 18-month scoring window still needs an explicit rule for arrivals still open at the horizon. Completions-only scoring drops the longest cases and biases cycle time down; arrival cohorts need run-off after the window. That choice changes every SLA and tail comparison.

Calibration and validation share the same targets. Service quantiles are already an inferred split of sojourn, and capacities are then tuned so simulated waits match the published wait medians. Section 6 treats those medians, and the performance DFG, as validation. Fit to a calibration target is not confirmation. The 15/3 month holdout also leaves the last quarter’s season unidentified, so seasonal misspecification and model error stay mixed. Alignments of the historical log to the tree check control-flow fitness; they do not replay historical arrivals through the resource model.

The outer uncertainty loop conflates two designs. “Bootstrap the log by cases (block-bootstrap by week)” cannot both resample cases independently and keep week blocks, architect bursts, and cases that cross weeks. Those designs answer different questions and break different dependencies.

Several policy mechanisms are not identifiable from the stated data. D2 depends on “no prior violations,” which is absent from the attributes, the case state, and `needs_inspection`. D3 is specified as if batching starts at zero, while Section 1 and the estimation plan already put same-day district batches in the baseline; savings can be double-counted. D1’s harm is pre-submission delay and possible drop-off, outside a boundary and a cycle-time metric that start at `Submit application`, so D1 and D10 look better on the decision table than the applicant-facing trade-off allows. No-shows are a state field and a stated timing issue, then disappear from estimation and assumptions. Reject probabilities are never estimated, even though reject service is 30 minutes to 2 hours and is not the 5-minute system issue step. The planner pool serves both planning review and decision drafting with no allocation rule, and same-planner continuation visible in the excerpt is ignored. “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is left ambiguous between two people on three days and two full-time-equivalents compressed into three days. Public notice is a statutory timer that holidays can extend, but the design never says whether the 14-day minimum runs in calendar time. Arrival estimation is piecewise constant by month-group and weekday, while the assumptions claim sub-daily intensity; Monday’s 2.2× load and after-hours portal arrivals will not hit the clerk calendar correctly under a flat within-day rate. Removed emergency cases, which would thin the fast tail, are not in the missing-data assumptions.

The SLA cuts largely restate the variant table (Simple p90 45 days, residential p90 95, commercial 130, complex 260). Labeled as placeholders, they still do not separate case type from variant group, and they do not define an improvement bar beyond historical performance.
