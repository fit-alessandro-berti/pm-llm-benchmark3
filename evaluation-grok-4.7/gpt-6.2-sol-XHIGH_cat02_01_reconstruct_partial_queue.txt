8.5

The reconstruction is numerically coherent and unusually careful. Reported-clock waits, services, and sojourns match the log; missing-start bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 respect queue entry, predecessor completion, successor start, and the C2 outage; midpoint areas (S1 queue 1515, busy 570, WIP 2085; S2 queue 797.5, busy 542.5, WIP 1340; system 3425) match the case sums; subperiod flows, snapshots, resource busy times, and the noon-to-14:00 queue envelopes (about 3.15–3.58 versus 3.38–4.08) are internally consistent. Open and still-waiting cases are kept in WIP and busy time, active service is separated from sojourn, and the early/mid/late split correctly leaves entries, completions, and WIP unchanged while moving time between wait and service. The bottleneck claim is properly time-varying and does not over-rank stations by utilization.

The remaining problems are interpretive, not arithmetic. The S2 Little’s Law check on 08:20–08:50 is an accounting identity: with an empty boundary and both waits fully inside the window, \(\bar Q=\lambda\bar W_q\) cannot fail, so it does not test the reconstruction. The station waiting means are conditional on having started, so S2’s 38-minute figure excludes P16, P18, P19, and P20, whose elapsed waits are already long. The completed-case mean sojourn of about 146 minutes is likewise a finisher average; several open cases have already reached 150–230 minutes, and that selection effect is understated. The late scenario’s zero-service endpoint, and outer utilizations up to about 97–99%, are valid mathematical envelopes but wider than jointly attainable schedules, and they sit too close to the headline station metrics. Bare case-table durations also look exact even though the text assigns them an implicit ±5-minute band. The repeated five-minute resource gaps, exactly one rounding quantum, are not examined, although they affect how much apparent idle time is real.
