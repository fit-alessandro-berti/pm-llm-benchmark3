The write-up is complete, well structured, and often careful: mixture means, most block workloads, the U1 P1 algebra, the U4 tail means, multi-skill double-counting, and the case-level versus queue-wait distinction are handled correctly. It still misses a high score because a feasibility-changing arithmetic error, conflicting quantitative layers, and a policy comparison that does not implement the stated rules undermine the recommendation.

The 16:00–18:00 capacity row omits Emil’s 16:00–16:30 break. Software capacity is stated as 120 base minutes and load as 92/120 = 77%. Emil’s multiplier is 1.00, so available time is 90 minutes and offered software load is about 92/90, already above the independent upper bound before handovers, setup, or Electrical work on the same person. Electrical capacity in that block is about 189, not 212. The prose still names that block as tight, but it treats a hard staffing deficit as a 77% ratio and then says base individual utilization can sit in a 70–90% band. Moving Deepa to 12:00–20:00 covers the real evening Mechanical hole and adds no Software skill after 16:00, so the staffing suggestion does not address the tighter base deficit. The code does subtract breaks, so a run would contradict Section 2.

Section 4 and Section 8 are not the same analysis. The narrative bands give base mean delay of roughly 1–3 hours and hybrid P1 attainment of 90–97%. The code’s pressure proxy, using the corrected peak ratio near 1.03 and capping pressure at 0.95, produces base mean delays of about 4–5 hours and P1 proxies below 90% for every policy. Those proxies also drop infinite rho when Mechanical capacity is zero after 18:00, so the coverage hole emphasized in the prose never enters the score. U1/U5 raise P1 by 8 points and leave P2+P3 unchanged, so priority shares sum to 1.08 without that being stated. The 12:00–14:00 U2 shares are wrong: doubled E and ES weights renormalize to about 40.0% and 17.1%, not 45.9% and 19.7%. The 14:00–16:00 U2 figures and the code’s renormalization are right.

The three policies are described in operational language, but strict specialization never pins multi-skilled people to queues, so its capacity is undefined. The Python comparison does not dispatch those rules. It scales one optimistic peak rho by 1.00, 0.88, and 0.90 and adds flat SLA credits of 0–4 points. Section 4’s much larger P1 gaps are separate judgmental ranges, overlap heavily across Pool and Hybrid, and cannot audit the claim that Hybrid wins the robust objective. The event-log demo is labeled hybrid but schedules three cases in list order, ignores priority, reservation, breaks, overtime, and preemption, chooses parallelism from static best speeds rather than current distinct-resource ETAs, and emits a fictional `case-coordinator` resource.

Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest part: preemption, P3 aging, Validation spillover, auditability, interval semantics, and a P1-protected pilot are specified at a level the numerical core does not match. Format compliance is met. Under a strict reading, the correct qualitative instincts do not offset an internal contradiction on the main bottleneck and a recommendation table that the supplied model does not generate.
