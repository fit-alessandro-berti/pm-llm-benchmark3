The answer covers every required section, uses concrete object ids and timestamps, keeps machine capacity off the top rank, and usually separates observation from inference. ## Quantitative errors

These matter most because section 2 is the required delay decomposition.

- S1 timing is mishandled twice. 13:30 to 19:25 is 355 minutes, and the text first states 325 minutes before correcting itself. The rank-2 cell then says J1 “waits to 19:25 (340 min).” 340 minutes ends at J4’s pack time, 19:10, not at dispatch. Applying S2’s 25-minute post-pack norm to a still-consolidated S1 would dispatch at 19:35, 10 minutes later than 19:25. The claim that J1’s wait would be unchanged is false.
- The CAL-3 split of J6’s 80 minutes is wrong. J5 occupies TEST-1 from 16:10 to 16:35, which is 25 minutes while J6 has been ready since 16:05. Calling “~5–10 min” the queue behind J5 drops most of that occupancy. The 50-minute figure also folds in 17:20–17:25, after CAL-3 recalibration has already ended at 17:20.
- The FIX-A figure of 100 minutes (10:05–11:45) treats CNC-2 as free for the whole interval. The only documented idle window is 10:05–10:47, 42 minutes. The answer’s caveat does not state that boundary, so 100 minutes is an upper bound under an extra assumption, not the delay the log supports.
- The L7 sentence is internally inconsistent. “Added 50 min … if earliest otherwise 13:10” cannot be 50 minutes: 13:10 to 14:40 is 90 minutes, and the quarantine overlap from 13:10 to the 13:50 release is 40 minutes. Fifty minutes is only the post-release lag from 13:50 to 14:40.
- For B2, RACK-7 is free of cleaning/quarantine at 14:35, before J5 is ready at 14:40. An independent rack therefore does not explain the 14:40–14:50 gap, and it does not by itself save J4 those 10 minutes. The parenthetical “RACK free” at a 13:10 solo start is also easy to read as a claim about the actual rack, which is still in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35.
- J2’s 08:25 readiness is not invalidated by a quarantine that starts at 10:20. The hold can affect eligibility from 10:20 to 12:05; the wording “already quarantined” moves the event backward.

The B1 excesses themselves are right: raw waits 235, 220, and 10 minutes, and excesses 225 and 210 minutes if the batch cannot start before J3.

## Evidence the answer does not reconcile

OVEN-1 is described as idle from 12:55 to 14:50, about 115 minutes, while the machine summary’s longest gap is 73 minutes. TEST-1 is described as idle from 16:35 to 17:25, 50 minutes, while its longest gap is 39 minutes. CNC-2’s 42-minute idle fits under its 48-minute maximum. The answer cites both the long narrative idles and the shorter flattened maxima as if they were the same evidence. That tension changes the CAL-3 and RACK stories: either intermediate machine events exist, or “idle” means “not processing a job” while Calibrate/Load-class events still occupy the flattened log.

## Ranking

Putting batch synchronization first is justified by J1/J2’s multi-hour excess over J3. Folding RACK-7 into that same rank-1 mechanism is not. On the answer’s own B2 arithmetic, the rack is free before the last job is ready, so it does not account for the hours in B1 or for most of J4’s 100 minutes. Those 100 minutes are mostly waiting for J5, with L7 as a plausible upstream contributor to J5. Tool contention is real for FIX-A and CAL-3, but the rank-1 bundle overstates the rack. Shipment consolidation as a separate high rank is appropriate, and the lot-linkage and logging rows correctly treat missing Job relations as an observability problem.
