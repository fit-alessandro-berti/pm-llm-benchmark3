7.3

The audit is structurally complete and mostly right on the obvious leaks, the appeal-versus-impossible-order distinction, and the decision not to drop region or channel. It is not nearly flawless: several process and lineage claims are imprecise or internally inconsistent, and those errors sit in the checks the task specifically required.

The leakage table hits every top-12 feature, all five patterns, and the local-only fields. Duration, appeal-anywhere, full-trace variant, document-request totals, final decision, the prior-claim SQL (`claim_created_at <= extraction_date`), the 23:00 same-day snapshot, mutable `risk_score_current`, closure-time `last_resource_role`, and extraction-time employee experience are all correctly condemned. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is correctly treated as a legal post-appeal edge that is still future at `t0`, unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. Queue length, frozen complexity, and elapsed time are preserved, and Case Z is not given a fake precise story. The JSON shape is valid, and leaked outcomes are not retold as facts.

Those merits do not clear the following defects.

The enabled / future-possible / impossible split is wrong. After the first coverage check, damage assessment, a document request, and a triggered fraud investigation are enabled; document receipt, validation, settlement, appeal, and closure are not. The answer dumps all of those into “future-possible,” then also lists `Claim Closed` as impossible. Appeal is called impossible at `t0` even though it is later legal after Partial or Deny. That collapses the distinction the validation suite was required to make.

The availability rule “reject any feature whose extraction date is after `t0`” is false. A later extract is valid if the as-of boundary is `t0`. The actual prior-claim and employee-experience bugs are wrong time predicates, not the fact of a later warehouse run. That sentence would discard a correct point-in-time store. Several remediations also drop the comparison operator, so “source event timestamp is `t0`” reads as equality.

`queue_length_at_t0` is graded low and kept with the ±5 minute tolerance. A snapshot after `t0` is look-ahead; a snapshot before `t0` is stale. “Enforce the tolerance” preserves the defect. Severity should not be the same as a frozen registration field.

`initial_reserve` is not defensibly available: there is no timestamp or immutability evidence. It is still placed in Case Y’s `credible_drivers`. `claimant_channel` is asserted as known at registration with no lineage, while reserve is hedged. Section 4 was limited to information defensible at `t0`. The base value is not a removable driver; it has to be decomposed. Signs are copied from a model whose largest attributions are leaked, with no warning that refit can flip them.

`Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` cannot be repaired by “use it only if fraud opened at or before `t0`,” because this edge is a successor of the prediction event. Severity is high here and critical for the other future edges, with no stable rule. `last_resource_role = Special Investigation Unit` is treated as a generic end-of-case role and not as evidence that the fraud path occurred. `total_case_duration_hours` is not a restatement of a settlement-time label; closure time includes post-decision time, and appeal can extend final resolution only if the label is final settlement rather than the first decision. “Breach leads to appeal” mixes the timing label with Partial/Deny.

Section 3 does not cleanly separate predictive validity, causal identification, operational actionability, and fairness. “May not claim fairness” for complexity and elapsed time is not a coherent prohibition. Repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is both “defensible” and “unusable.” Queue length is the only place actionability is actually distinguished from association. Conformance testing is P2 even though impossible ordering contaminates training, not just text explanations.
