7.5

The business analysis is strong and mostly faithful to the alignments: all 12 cases are covered, P1–P6 are applied correctly, zero-cost auto-approve breaches (CL-609, CL-610) are separated from fitting tau (CL-605), and raw cost is not used as the ranking. Severity, confidence, hypotheses, and remediation are generally consistent and specific.

Deductions are for concrete defects, not tone.

The required PM4Py block is not reliable. `res["alignment"]` moves are `((log_label, model_label), cost)`, but the loop unpacks them as `(log_m, model_m)`. Visible-move and tau counts therefore stay 0, so CL-605’s silent move disappears from the diagnostics the code claims to report. CL-604’s 10:03/10:20 timestamps are attached only to Pay/Approve/Notify while Register, Validate, and Assess stay on a later clock (12:00+). `format_dataframe` sorts by timestamp, so that case is reordered into a payment-before-registration trace and cannot reproduce the supplied alignment. The `if False` branch is dead code left in the prioritization table.

Two prevalence figures are overstated. Only the 74 notification retries are explicitly “in the full month.” The nine adjuster gaps and four synchronous auto-approve violations are not. The write-up treats both as monthly rates and then uses those rates in owners, risk reduction, and validation metrics.

Smaller reasoning nits: CL-612 is filed under the P6 scope dispute, which names denied pre-screens and re-opened claims, not withdrawals; re-opened claims are never discussed. CL-604 is High confidence that payment preceded approval while clock skew is still an open competing hypothesis. P1 investigation language slides from control breach toward “confirmed fraud loss,” including the EUR 18,000 P1-only case. Pattern 2 is called systemic from two sample cases.
