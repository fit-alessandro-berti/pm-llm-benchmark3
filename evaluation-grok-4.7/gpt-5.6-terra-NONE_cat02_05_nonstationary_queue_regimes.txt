The regime narrative is the right kind of analysis: outage is separated from recovery, service quantiles are not used as flow time, carry-over and cohort mismatch are explicit, and the weekday/weekend and mix/capacity/backlog distinctions are operationally meaningful. The required headings, matrix, alternative segmentation, and Python checklist are all present. The score is held down because several load-bearing quantities are wrong, a few observations are simply false, and those errors contradict the code that is supposed to reproduce the essay.

Pooled totals are not 2,730 arrivals and 2,712 completions (97.5 and 96.9 per day). The table sums to 2,519 and 2,409, about 90.0 and 86.0 per day, a net surplus of arrivals of +110 rather than near balance. Mean open-hours WIP is about 29.9, not 30.5. That false balance is then reused in §§4 and 7 as the pooled result that hides internal swings. The swings are real; the claimed monthly identity is not.

Within regimes, baseline is 781 arrivals and 776 completions over 6–14 April (86.8 and 86.2 per day), not 85.4 and 84.6. Recovery arrivals over 16–19 April are 74.5 per day, not 76.5. The high-mix block, 20–28 April, is 892 arrivals and 786 completions (99.1 and 87.3 per day, net +106), not 88.0 and 76.2 per day and not “roughly +96.” Weekday arrivals in that block are 114.0, not 112.8, so the 12.2 cases/day gap is about 13.4. The Little’s Law inputs inherited from those figures are wrong: baseline is not 18.7/7.11, and high-mix WIP is 39.2, not 38.1. Post-29 April weekday rates (115 and 121 per day) and the 5.15 FTE average are in good shape; the broken numbers are the ones used for pooling and for the congested regime.

Two universal statements fail on the rows given. Completions do not exceed arrivals on every day of 16–19 April (18 April is 48 vs 46) or of 29 April–3 May (2 May is 53 vs 51). Threat 12 is also backwards: all four hourly vectors sum exactly to the daily arrivals and completions, including 7 April’s 99 completions.

The intervention rows in §6 are marked approximate despite poor boundaries, high carry-over, and WIP falling from 43 to 19. That is too permissive for a recovery window and inconsistent with the outage-recovery verdict. The alternative story says 25 April is high-complexity accumulation and that “normal” means WIP below about 25; the code labels that day `normal_low_wip` unless WIP is at least 35. Section 5 defines Fano factor and CV correctly but never reports them, and the essay’s hand totals would not match the printed code tables.
