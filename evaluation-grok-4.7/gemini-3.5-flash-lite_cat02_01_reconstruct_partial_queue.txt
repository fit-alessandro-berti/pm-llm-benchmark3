3.5

The write-up matches the required section layout and defines waiting, service, censoring, and sojourn in the right direction, but the reconstruction is not reliable enough to use.

The core missing-start cases are resource-infeasible. P05 on C1 cannot start at the S1 completion of 09:25 because P03 occupies C1 until 09:45; a central service of about 50 minutes overlaps that job, and a wait of [0, 10] does not describe the feasible window. P07 on I1 cannot start near 08:55–09:10 because P05 occupies I1 until 09:25; the feasible start is about [09:25, 10:00), so wait is about [40, 75] and service at most about 35 minutes, not wait [10, 25] and service [50, 75]. P11 on C2 cannot start at 11:05: P08 occupies C2 until 12:00, so service is at most about 25 minutes. The reported wait [70, 90] and service [70, 90] cannot hold together, because those midpoints put the start at the recorded end. The same error appears as a method: end minus queue-entry (wait plus service) is reported as service. P15 is only partly constrained; service up to 45 minutes still crosses P13’s occupancy of I1 through 11:40.

Fully observed arithmetic is also wrong in several rows: P06 S1 service is 30 minutes, not 25; P08 S2 service is 45, not 40; P14 S2 service is 45, not 50; P17 S2 wait is 65, not 70; P20 S1 wait is 105, not 100. P01’s wait band uses ±2.5 minutes while other durations use ±5, so the rounding model is not applied uniformly. A difference of two independently rounded timestamps has a ±5-minute envelope, and nonnegativity plus resource occupancy cut that envelope further. Those cuts are not applied.

Station totals do not follow from the log. S1 observed waits average about 65 minutes, not ~52. S2 has 20 queue entries (14 completed, 2 open, 4 waiting), not 21 entries and 5 waiting. Known S2 busy time inside the window is at most about 570 resource-minutes even with maximum feasible fills for P05 and P11 and elapsed time for the open jobs; ~630 busy minutes is above that cap, so the 85–92% utilization range is not attainable. Mean and peak WIP are stated with no timeline. Case sojourn, subperiod arrival and departure rates, and a numerical Little’s Law check are absent. Calling 09:00–12:00 a steady-state window is the wrong justification: queues are building, so only a finite-interval identity with an explicit WIP correction would be defensible.

The bottleneck narrative is only partly aligned with the evidence. S2 service is longer, S2 completions are fewer (14 versus 20), and end-of-window WIP sits mainly at S2 (about 6 versus about 2). That supports S2 as the tighter capacity constraint later in the window. It does not support “balanced” utilization of 60–70% before 10:00, “manageable” S1 queues (observed S1 waits reach about 100 minutes), or C2’s 15-minute outage as the tipping point. The outage can delay the next C2 start; it is too small to explain the afternoon S2 backlog. “Starvation of downstream capacity” is the wrong mechanism: S2 is the last station, and the visible effect is an upstream queue.

Section 6 names optimistic, central, and pessimistic scenarios but computes none of them. The claim that P07, P11, and P15 move by at most 15 minutes is smaller than the feasible windows. Stable and unstable conclusions are therefore asserted, not shown.

The Python does not reproduce the tables. `parse_time` maps blank, `MISSING`, and `OPEN` to the same missing timestamp. There is no WIP series, no imputation scenario, and no Little’s Law calculation. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are called on the full formatted log, not on a completed-case projection, their outputs are not printed, and a bare exception handler can skip them. The comment on `convert_log_to_time_intervals` meets that one requirement only. Reported means, busy time, and missing-start bounds cannot be regenerated from the code.
