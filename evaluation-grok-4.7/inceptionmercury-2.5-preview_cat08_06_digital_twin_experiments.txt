3.5

The write-up keeps the required eight-section outline and a few sound instincts (applicant delay as its own clock, objections kept latent, batching’s travel-versus-wait trade-off, no optimality claim). It is still a weak design: the twin is too thin for the dependencies it was asked to represent, the experiment plan drops half the policies, estimation and validation contain biased or invented quantities, and the skeleton is inconsistent with PM4Py and would not run.

**1. Model scope and state variables.** The boundary stops at “municipal workflow” and never fixes the level of detail (discrete-event cases versus work items, tokens versus resources). Case state is only “current activity, elapsed time, completed activities,” so routing attributes, parallel-branch completion, rework counters, appeal status, violation history, latent environmental sensitivity, and a latent objection flag are absent. Those are exactly the fields D2, D8, and D9 need. Queues are one bag per activity; calendar delay, applicant delay, batch hold, and join-wait at decision drafting are different mechanisms and are not separated. Resources in the entity list omit fire, environment, legal, and scheduling, while the narrative depends on their calendars. Cross-case state is only an inspection bundle. Neighborhood objections that hit several related cases, and architect-level submission clusters, are outside the state. Rework is described as “decision rejections return to activities based on variant,” which copies historical variant labels instead of the stated loop points (planning, inspection, legal, appeal). The process tree itself only loops completeness and inspection and has no appeal; that mismatch is never registered.

**2. Parameter-estimation plan.** “Variants of F” is undefined. Variant-group shares are treated as routing probabilities. Those shares overlap (inspection-rework cases also sit inside case-type groups), and fire, environment, and notice are attribute-conditional, with the area threshold and district sensitivity missing from the log. A share such as “18% fire + notice” cannot be dropped in as a branch probability. Service-time fitting from median and p90, then waiting as completion minus median service minus previous completion, is not identified: many activities have no start timestamp, weekends and holidays sit inside that gap, and the result can be negative. The plan never states whether waiting is an input draw or an output of queues and calendars. Both cannot be fitted. Architect bursts (top 20 architects, 31% of cases, same-week clusters) are not an estimation target; a month/weekday Poisson rate washes them out. Bundle-size “probabilities” mined from a flattened log treat an intervention (D3) as if it were already the data-generating process. Objection mass is correctly left inside public-notice duration, but the 14-day floor is not separated from the extension, so D9 has no estimable mechanism. PM4Py is named only as a generic calculator; alignments, conditional branching, sojourn versus service, and calendar checks against observed timestamps are not specified. Calendars come from HR and are accepted without a conformance check against when work actually occurs.

**3. Assumptions.** Arrivals are an independent non-stationary Poisson with the given 1.7× and 2.2× multipliers. Conditionally independent cases cannot generate architect bursts or correlated objections, which breaks the constraint that this not be an independent-case model. Routing is “static given case type and district,” which ignores missing area and sensitivity fields and the AND-join before decision drafting. Resource assumptions stop at binary skills and no multitasking; the Monday portal clerk, the 40% non-permit hold on environmental time, fire Tue–Thu, inspector field days, and legal blackouts except an undefined “urgent” class are not assumptions. Unstructured rework text is treated as a usable stochastic trigger even though it is not reliably coded. Prior violations, required to apply D2, are never given a missing-data assumption. Cross-case dependence is explicitly limited to inspection bundling, so objection shocks across related cases are assumed away. Seasonality is a rate multiplier only; capacity non-stationarity (holidays, council weeks) is not part of the demand/capacity story.

**4. Experiment matrix.** D5–D9 are absent, with no reason. The D10 definition in the prompt is only D1+D2+D3+D4, so the other five policies still needed their own rows. Factor cells for D1–D4 and D10 are blank, so the matrix does not encode levels. No numeric levels appear for checker effectiveness, false-negative rate on the skip-inspection rule, batch window, peak months for the extra inspector, or any other knob. Stress A is an inspector cut on the all-off row, while the trade-off text says it tests D3/D4. Stress C (“D2 rule error rate high”) has no level. One-at-a-time runs plus a single combo cannot support the “synergistic gains” claim. Monday smoothing (D7), aging reservation (D6), and objection early warning (D9) are the interventions that match the stated bottlenecks and data gaps, and they are the ones left out. Case-mix shift inside the peak season is not a factor.

**5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty.** A 3-month warm-up “to steady state” is the wrong frame: March–May and September–November are a 1.7× seasonal cycle, so the process is cyclo-stationary at best. Initial backlog is unspecified, and a single 18-month mean mixes unlike seasons. Thirty replications with ordinary 95% intervals are stated for “total duration and throughput” and do not cover quantile error on a 260-day tail. Seeds are fixed, but policies are not paired under common random numbers, so the comparison the study exists to make stays high-variance. Sensitivity swaps in p5/p95, which the summary does not provide, instead of the real ambiguity (many distributions share a median and a p90 and disagree on the mean and p99). The “p90 within 10%” check is an in-sample calibration target used as validation. Pareto tails are allowed without a moment or truncation rule, so mean throughput can be undefined.

**6. Validation strategy.** Replay is “feed the 18-month log and match the summary it was estimated from,” which reproduces case counts by construction and does not check the tree. The tree’s “Completeness rework” label does not match the repeated “Completeness check” events in the excerpt; that conformance gap is not a test. Planner utilization “70–85%” and a lower legal rate are presented as empirical expectations. The summary contains no utilization figures, and missing start times make utilization unidentified. Bottlenecks are named as legal check and inspection scheduling. The longest median waits in the table are environmental review and site inspection (8 days); public notice is a timer; inspection scheduling waits 3 days. District holdout is a poor out-of-sample design when staff are shared across districts. A temporal split, calendar conformance (no fire work outside Tue–Thu), burst-size checks, and conservation of parallel joins are not in the plan.

**7. Decision criteria.** The median-duration bullet sets a target of “<50% reduction in tail duration” for commercial cases, which mixes two metrics and, read literally, caps the gain. The complex-variance p90 target is “<260 days,” equal to the historical p90, so the baseline already passes. SLA compliance is “90% within statutory limit” with no limit by case type. Fairness is districts only; architect fairness is required and missing. Rework has no metric. Utilization 70–85% fights the seasonal staffing policy (idle inspectors off-peak are intended). D2’s compliance risk from skipped inspections is not a decision metric. There is no rule for conflicts across duration, fairness, backlog, and risk, and no statement that thresholds stay provisional until the model is calibrated.

**8. Python skeleton.** The frame is inconsistent and will not build. `case:concept:name` has 14+8 entries; activities, timestamps, resources, and attributes have 13+7. `pm4py.variants.FormatParameters` is not the `format_dataframe` interface (case id, activity key, and timestamp key are ordinary arguments). `pm4py.process_tree.ProcessTreeRoot`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg.discover`, `pm4py.algo.filtering.log.simple.filter_by_columns`, and `pm4py.get_performance` are not PM4Py’s DFG, performance-DFG, or filtering API. The tree string is native `parse_process_tree` syntax; the code skips the parse and says to convert it to a dict/JSON. `len(log)` after `format_dataframe` counts events, divides the two-case excerpt by an 18-month day count, and labels the result cases per month. Variants, a real performance DFG, case-arrival average, and sojourn columns are not computed, and there is no comment that a single timestamp cannot split service from wait. The engine is one `Simulator.run` with `pass`. Arrival generation, routing, calendars, and experiment execution are not separate placeholders, and nothing in that stub expresses non-stationary arrivals, calendars, or cross-case state.

The piece satisfies the constraint against pretending objections were logged and against claiming optimality. It misses the harder constraints: dependencies beyond a single bundle, calendars that actually drive capacity, an identified wait/service split, a complete intervention design, and PM4Py calls that match the library.
