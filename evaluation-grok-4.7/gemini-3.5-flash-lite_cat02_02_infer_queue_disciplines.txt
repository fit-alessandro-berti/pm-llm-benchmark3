3.5

The write-up follows the required section order and records some real facts, but the decision-set analysis that the task makes central is frequently wrong, and the headline diagnoses are more confident than the evidence allows. Those are not presentation issues. They reverse what several rules would have chosen and then build the synthesis on those reversals.

The recurring failure is the eligible set. Jobs are treated as waiting when they have not arrived, are already in service, or are already complete, and jobs that are actually waiting are left out. Predictions drawn from those sets are then used as if they discriminate disciplines.

**QA is not a priority hybrid.** At 08:20 the resource finishes A01 with A02 (08:04, priority 3, 20 min), A03 (appointment 08:20), and A04 (08:13, priority 1, 7 min) all waiting. FIFO selects A02, priority and SPT both select A04, and only the appointment rule selects A03. The 08:20 row omits A04, predicts priority as A02 and SPT as A03, and therefore manufactures an equivalence between SPT and the appointment that the data break. The same priority rule fails again when the server is free: A02 is started at 08:32 over A04, A05 is started at 09:05 over A08, and A07 is started at 09:32 over A08, which is priority 1 and still goes last. Non-preemption during A02, A03, and calibration does not explain choices made at idle decision epochs. After A03 is inserted, the service order is arrival order. A06 at 09:20 is what FIFO produces once A05 ends; it is not independent evidence of an appointment rule, and the claimed five-minute tolerance is not in the data. The contradicting episode is also misstated: A04 runs at 08:58–09:05, before A06 and A07, not “until the A06/A07 window.” Section 3 then treats that delay as support for non-preemptive priority, while the diagnosis table calls it the contradiction.

**QB is not identifiable preemptive priority at confidence 85.** B02’s interruption of B01, with the stated urgent-arrival reason, is the one clean preemption. It is not repeated. At 09:31 B01 is resumed while B03 (priority 2) waits. At 09:48 B03 is started while B05 (priority 1, arrived 09:46) waits, and B05 does not preempt B03; B05 merely follows B03’s completion at 10:00. Calling that a cut-in is incorrect. At 10:14 B01 is resumed over B06 (priority 2). At 10:28 B04 is chosen over B06. The 10:28 row says B01 is still paused, but B01’s third segment ends at 10:28, and it gives priority’s prediction as B04. Priority 2 outranks priority 3, so priority predicts B06. SPT predicts B07 (6 minutes), not B04 (8). The cited contradiction, B07’s missed appointment, challenges appointment scheduling, which was not the claimed primary rule. The resume-versus-reprioritize question and the explicit dispatcher reassignment are the actual identifiability problem, and they are not given a decision row.

**QC’s SPT-plus-SLA diagnosis is internally inconsistent and factually inverted.** At 10:10, among C01–C04, SPT does select C02. Priority would select C03, not C05, who is not in that eligible set; earliest deadline and least slack also select C03. C02’s deadline is 14:00, looser than C03’s 12:30 and C01’s 13:00, so the opening choice separates SPT from deadline urgency rather than confounding them. The table’s identifiability claim that short jobs are the endangered ones is false: C05 is the longest job (40), C06 has the loosest deadline (15:00), and C07 (deadline 12:00) is served last. C06 has been waiting since 10:25, yet C04 (22) is chosen at 10:32 and C05 (40) at 10:54. The 10:54 row omits C07, who arrived at 10:40. At 11:40 SPT predicts C07 because 18 is less than 36; the row predicts C01, which is the longer job. Starting C07 at 11:40 would finish about 11:58 and meet 12:00 without making C01 miss 13:00. Starting C01 instead makes C07 miss. C05 was already infeasible on arrival (40 minutes of work against a 10:50 deadline from a 10:11 arrival) and was still passed over at 10:18, when its slack was already negative, in favor of C03 and then C04. That record does not prove a deadline safeguard. Listing C01 as the strongest contradiction of SPT is also backwards: C01 being last is what SPT says about the opening backlog and what the narrative itself later calls anti-FIFO.

**QD gets the right family of constraint and the wrong decision chronology.** Parallel routing by skill is real: firmware work uses D-F, mechanical-only work uses D-M, and D05 cannot leave D-F. The 11:00 row nevertheless includes D03, D04, D05, and D06, who arrive at 11:03–11:10, and predicts D08, who arrives at 11:16. At 11:30, D03 and D04 are already in service; the row treats them as waiting and predicts FIFO as D03. D-X is idle from 11:20, when it becomes available, until D06’s 11:30 appointment, despite eligible work, including D08, which would fit before 11:30. That idle hold is the appointment evidence, and it is missed. On D-M at 11:25, D04 is chosen over waiting D08 (priority 1, 8 minutes versus 10), which is FIFO within skill and a priority violation. D-F is free from 11:36 until the 12:00 meeting while D10, an electrical job D-F can perform, waits; the meeting is not a reason for that earlier idle interval. The claim that D10 waits because D-F is unavailable is therefore wrong. The 12:20 row lists D07 and D09, already complete, and D10, already in service since 12:16, and assigns D05 an appointment prediction although D05 has no appointment.

Section 4 does not repair this. The QC comparison already has its answer in the log: later choices are not jointly consistent with SPT or with deadline urgency, and the two “next-job” predictions are not even about the same decision. The QA test says A03 was held while the resource was available; A1 was busy with A01 until 08:20. The in-sample discriminator was A04. Section 5 rightly names scan delay, calendars, estimate error, and ad hoc preemption, but the impacts are overstated. A two-minute scan delay cannot swap A02 and A03 across a four-minute gap, and every realized duration in these tables equals `est_min`, which cuts against using noisy estimates to excuse QC. Confidence values of 70–85 do not reflect the unresolved contradictions. The collection list covers the requested field types, yet it is generic relative to the actual forks: resume priority after preemption, whether D-F is reserved for firmware, appointment hold versus early start, and the objective used when slack turns negative.

The synthesis then ranks the wrong queue as most identifiable. QB’s single labeled preemption is clear; a standing preemptive-priority discipline is not. QA is comparatively legible once priority is dropped: one appointment insertion, then FIFO. Calling QC’s SPT reading the dangerous inference is reasonable only in outcome. The stated reason, that the short jobs are the deadline-critical ones, is the opposite of the slack ordering above. A manager who automated these four labels would not reproduce the observed choices.
