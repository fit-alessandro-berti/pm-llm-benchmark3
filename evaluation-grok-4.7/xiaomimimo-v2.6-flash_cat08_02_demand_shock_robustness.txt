The final answer is a strong, evidence-grounded redesign: required sections are complete, scenarios vary by mix and disruption rather than volume alone, and the flat Routine load versus the Urgent/Complex surge is used correctly to reject average-cycle-time optimization. Bottleneck migration, conditional triggers, downstream overload, and redesign-caused risks are handled explicitly.

It is not nearly flawless. The claim that a 22 h medical-validation queue makes the 18 h Urgent SLA arithmetically impossible is contradicted by the stated Urgent peak median of 17 h; that queue is pooled, and Urgent is already inside the window at the median. The “~20× sooner” cancellation argument also misapplies the 42–60 h recollection-to-receive edge to cancellations, including registration cancels that never enter that loop. The Kingman-style ρ/(1−ρ) comparison is a useful proxy but is applied to multi-server medians too literally, and “the few” leading indicators expands to fifteen. Those slips do not collapse the redesign, but they are material enough to keep this below a near-perfect score.
