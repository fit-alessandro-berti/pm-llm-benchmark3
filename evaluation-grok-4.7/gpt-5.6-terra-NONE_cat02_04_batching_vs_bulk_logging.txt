6.8

The diagnoses, signature matrix, G6 mechanism, redesign, and adjudication are strong, and most cluster math checks out. The score is held down by false performance numbers, a capacity contradiction, ranking inconsistencies, and a batch-comparison step that does not actually compare.

The G1–G5 and G8 wait and service figures are right, including G1’s 6/5/4/2 and 3-minute cycle, G4’s 7/10/13/16/19 with exact handoffs, and G8’s 38–58 minute waits and 7-minute cadence. G6’s zero service and inflated “arrival-to-closure” reading is the right mechanism. G7’s coarsening diagnosis is also right. Those conclusions are not matched by the cited evidence.

Under waiting time for G6, C01 is given as 12:55. From 14:05 to 17:00 is 2 hours 55 minutes (175 minutes). C07’s 35 minutes is correct, so the endpoints are not even in one unit system. “Almost two hours” for arrivals from 14:05 to 16:25 (2 hours 20 minutes) is also off.

G7’s waiting paragraph says waits run “from 1 minute (M02) to 2 minutes,” while also calling M02 negative. The literal waits are +2, −1, −4, −7, and −11 minutes. Only M01 is non-negative. Four inversions, growing to 11 minutes, are the pattern; the sentence both misstates the range and contradicts itself. Section 5 then discusses only M02. The coarsening conclusion survives, but the calculation does not.

G5 misses a binding constraint. A01–A04 are all open from 13:12 to 13:20. “Up to three” analysts cannot cover four simultaneous cases one-for-one. Calling pooled concurrency “plausible” or “ordinary parallel team work” without that excess overstates what the shared login explains. Multitasking, non-active interval time, or a bad “up to three” claim is required.

The ranking table fights the better later reading. G2 completions step by about 6–7 minutes and G3 starts by about 7–8 minutes; those cadences favor a logging boundary around serial work. The table’s second explanation for both is concurrent work. Section 3 and the multi-explanation list then prefer serial processing plus a group or submission timestamp. G1’s second place, start batching, fits poorly: ends are identical too. Shared cycle-boundary logging, already the counterevidence, is the better runner-up.

Section 4 builds the log, keeps G6 and G7, calls `format_dataframe` and `discover_batches`, and grids `merge_distance` in {0, 60} and `min_batch_size` in {2, 4}. The independent metrics are the right ones. The comparison the task asks for is a raw dump plus a reminder sentence, not a cluster-level join of batch category to equality, overlap, lag, precision, and queue build-up. The `inspect.signature` path can also call `discover_batches` with defaults if those arguments are only `**kwargs`, and the positional fallback would bind the distances to the wrong parameters. Strict overlap treats G6’s identical zero-duration instants as non-overlapping; equality counts save the diagnosis, but the overlap rate alone would hide the worst cluster.

Conformance bias is barely covered, and G7’s unidentified service duration is not given as a bound. Clock non-causality, precision versus accuracy, and PM4Py labels as patterns rather than physical proof are handled carefully, which is why the wrong figures do not sink an otherwise coherent adjudication.
