7.5

The assessment is unusually careful about act-time, window boundaries, clock skew, and non-curative later approvals, but it is not nearly flawless. Several stated rules are not applied as written, and a few scope distinctions are collapsed without being owned as assumptions.

The sharpest defect is internal. M10 says an unproven exception is NONCOMPLIANT and that no case is factually balanced enough for INDETERMINATE. P-505, P-507, and P-508 are nevertheless JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION even though elements of the very exception being used are missing: the four-hour authorization record in P-505 and P-508, and authorizer independence from requester and committer in P-505 and P-507. Section 6’s later split between “confirmatory” facts and burden-bearing facts does not repair that, because a required recording is not collateral. P-508 is further strained: D1’s sufficiency cap is EUR 150,000, the purchase is EUR 155,000, and the answer’s own alternative concedes that the more literal reading of D2 flips the case.

P-501’s confidence is miscalibrated. The catalogue correctly says B2 is silent on approval order and that A2’s pre-commitment rule starts at EUR 50,000, yet the case is NONCOMPLIANT at high confidence for a EUR 38,000 commitment. That depends on treating B4’s Plant Manager substitution as a rule about who may bind the company. Section 5 then calls the opposite reading genuinely strong and the closest call. High confidence cannot sit with that admission. A6 is also used to bar a “cure” of a pre-commitment duty the governing instruments do not clearly impose below EUR 50,000. The claim that Plant and Finance approvals were “by others” is not in the file.

Section 6 overreaches. It says no further evidence could change P-501, P-502, or P-504, while Section 4 lists evidence that would: a pre-08:50 Plant Manager authorization, an earlier compliance screen, a facilities delegation, or a supplier that is unrecorded rather than unapproved.

Two scope assumptions are used as if they were facts. “Unapproved” is equated with A3’s “new supplier,” despite the file using both terms, and that equation is load-bearing for P-504 at EUR 24,000. The cafeteria repair is run through the Aachen maintenance SOP without asking whether a freezer falls inside “maintenance and production-support.” B6’s 24-hour emergency form is not tested on the in-window cases.

Smaller defects accumulate. The hierarchy column “what it probably cannot override” is misused for A1 and A3. Pseudo-logic line 3 makes segregation “always” forbidden while the catalogue preserves an A7 defeater. The required 5–12 pseudo-logic lines are exceeded at 13. M1 says Section 5 tests the negation that ECD-2 is an A7 exception; it never squarely does. In P-502, an earlier real commitment under the outage lag is mislabeled “buyer-friendly”; that direction would hurt the buyer. The lag analysis itself, in P-501 and P-505, is otherwise right.

What keeps the score here is the issue-specific hierarchy, the correct expiry and email-timing cuts on P-502 and P-506, the P-503 over-determination, the external-artifact treatment of P-505’s 17:59:31 acknowledgment, the P-507 PDF-versus-ERP distinction, and a clarification clause that actually separates waivable sequence from non-waivable integrity controls. The method is more consistent than the classifications that are supposed to follow from it.
