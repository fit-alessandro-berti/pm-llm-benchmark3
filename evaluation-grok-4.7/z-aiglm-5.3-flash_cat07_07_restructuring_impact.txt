7.2

The final answer is complete against the required seven-section format and is analytically strong, but it is not nearly flawless. Several statistical, logical, and implementation errors are material under a strict reading.

The handover arithmetic is right and is the best part of the forecast. The tabulated frequencies do sum to 343 versus 359 per 100 cases, the specialist out-and-back medians do sum to 4.8h versus 6.2h, and the case-weighted rework and SLA rates (about 9.3% and 7.6%) are calculated correctly. The mechanism map hits every required node, East is treated differently from North and South, and the recommendation is a gated pilot rather than a full rollout. The risk section and the “do not infer this from early averages” list are concrete and tied to process-mining signals.

The main analytical problems:

- “Blended median ≈ 7.5d” is the weighted average of team medians, not the pooled median. The same shortcut appears when 3.2h+1.6h and 4.0h+2.2h are stated as the round-trip medians; the median of a sum is not the sum of medians.
- Pooled specialist utilization is weighted by case volume (72.6%). N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech are three people, so equal-FTE pooling is (71+68+82)/3 ≈ 73.7%. The error is small, but the ~83% target is built on the wrong base.
- Legal/Risk are already at 91–93% utilization, and referrals rise from 9 to 12 per 100 (+33%). That pushes offered load to roughly 1.22 if capacity is unchanged, so there is no steady-state wait. The central scenario still quotes a finite 12–15h median, which contradicts both that arithmetic and the later claim that queueing theory rules out management’s 10.5h figure. The adverse case (“broken”) is the consistent one; the central case is not.
- The optimistic legal/risk wait of ~11h holds only “because capacity was added.” That capacity is not in the proposal, so the optimistic scenario forecasts a modified program while the section presents it as a forecast of the restructuring.
- “Total logged handovers” overstates the 343/359 figures. Those sums cover only the published handover types and omit quality-check, rework, and customer-contact edges. The resilience line that today’s 18% absence-cover failure “becomes the structural norm” is also stronger than the map’s own 10–14% rework range.

The PM4Py sketch names every required function, but key parts would not produce the comparison it describes:

- `Series.fillna` is given an `np.where` ndarray, which current pandas rejects, so `with_org` fails before any metric runs.
- Handover, working-together, and subcontracting results are treated as a weight DataFrame. These APIs return a graph or edge dict, and the print label says “handovers/100” without dividing by case count.
- `edge_wait` looks up `aggMean`. Current performance DFGs use `mean` / `median`, so the watch-edge comparison returns `None`.
- Segment filters use `location == "north"` rather than `team` values such as Team North, and `required_specialty` is never filtered, despite that being an explicit requirement. Empty segments then make the fairness `max-min` fail.
- `get_rework_cases_per_activity`, resource similarity, role discovery, DFG counts, and the alert lambdas are computed or declared and then unused. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is described as a weekly KPI panel; it produces case-level temporal features, not that dashboard.

Section coverage and the handover-topology argument are good enough that this stays above a weak score. The queueing inconsistency, the mislabeled median and utilization bases, and a sketch that does not actually run the before/after comparison keep it well short of an excellent one.
