The decomposition is the right one, and the arithmetic behind it is almost entirely exact. Flow is partitioned as upstream + critical-branch queue + critical-branch service + downstream lag + final service, synchronization waits are kept off the sum, and the double-count identity (the three branch rows each reproduce the fork–join interval, so adding them counts that interval three times and overstates flow by twice the fork–join time) is stated and checked on F02. All 12 case rows, the four worked cases, the critical-branch counts (A 2, B 8, C 2, no ties), the 732 fork–join minutes, the 260/472 critical queue/service split, and the flow total 1383 (mean 115.25) match the timestamps. B is correctly ranked above A even though A’s mean service is larger, and the 50-minute A floor is the right reason.

The counterfactuals are also right where it matters. A −20% saves 15 minutes and only in F01 and F11. Halving B’s queue saves 92.5, with 7.5 of a notional 100 absorbed by A, and the tail moves from 130/124.5 to 115/114.5. The combined change saves 117.5, 10 more than the sum of the parts, for the right three cases (F01, F05, F10). A two-server FIFO replay of the final station really does produce only F11’s 5 minutes of wait against 176 observed, both servers are busy at join in only F05, F09 and F10, and the F1/F2 alternation is real. Pass-through, capacity replay, and a fixed release slot are the right bounds, and the code implements that distinction, including deterministic A < B < C ties and a custom max-based join rather than a directly-follows graph.

The deductions are for concrete mistakes, not taste.

The branch table gives C’s synchronization-wait median as 34.5. The twelve values sort to 0, 0, 22, 27, 30, 30, 35, 35, 40, 42, 52, 52, so the median is 32.5. Mean 30.42 and max 52 are fine. This is a wrong number in the main comparison table.

Section 7 says B has “more than 4” tests in progress. The maximum overlap is 4 (A also tops out at 4, C at 3). From “more than 4 concurrent while others wait” it does not follow that a work-conserving fixed-server queue cannot explain B. Four busy servers still queue. The observation that would support the conclusion is an idle gap during a wait: after F10 and F11 end at 11:20, B is empty until F12 starts at 11:25, despite a 30-minute queue. That fact is not stated, so the capacity claim is both numerically wrong and argued from the wrong mechanism.

Redesign 1 says that once B’s queue is halved, further B gains yield nothing until A changes. After that cut, B is still strictly last in F03, F07, F09 and F12, with 27.5 minutes of margin left before A. The earlier “stops at A’s floor” wording was right; the recommendation overwrites it.

The CF1 replay note overstates tie-break fragility. Putting F11 ahead of F10 moves 15 minutes from F10 onto F11; the aggregate saving stays 5. “Only F01’s 5 survive” is true only under the stated order.
