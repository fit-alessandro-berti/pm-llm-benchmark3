5.5

The periodization is mostly right: April is treated as a pipeline break, June as a V2 mix shift with stable discrimination, August–September as overlapping supplier, variant, and Partner Z effects, and October as too immature to train or compare. That spine is undermined by taxonomy errors, one invented result, an unsafe training call, and code that would not run or would mis-measure the process.

Section 1 assigns covariate drift to June–October at high confidence while its own contradicting cell says there is no direct evidence of feature-distribution shift on existing variants. The June V2 jump and Partner Z’s missing rural and device-age attributes are mix and population changes, and they are counted again in those rows. April’s 18% unmapped share is also filed under process drift even though that mass is the renamed diagnosis and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`. Concept drift is limited to August–September in the table, then started in July in the narrative. The immediate action to retrain on post-August data conflicts with September’s 82% maturity and with Section 4.

Section 2 uses the right business timeline and the right variant breach rates, but states July label maturity as 100% when it is 98%. The August–September close attributes population shift to both months; Partner Z is 0% through August. It also asserts that V3 AUROC drops sharply and points to Section 3, which only proposes tests and contains no stratified AUROC. Residual direction is not used as evidence: April’s mean prediction of 0.28 against an observed 0.19 is the opposite error from August–September, where predictions stay near 0.19–0.21 while observed breach rises to 0.28–0.31. June’s ECE of 0.060 and 0.20 versus 0.15 are reported and then left unexplained.

Section 3 names the requested checks, but several expected results belong to a different test. Fixed-vintage backtesting is described as training on January–March, then the pipeline hypothesis is that April improves after feature recomputation. The missingness test treats rural and device age as covariates that can be missing; they were never in the model. Reweighting is specified as training on reweighted September data, and the concept-drift alternative is that reweighting will not help at all. Informative censoring by long V3 paths is not operationalized. The lineage of the cached pre-diagnosis count and the 20 April duplicate cutoff is thin.

Section 4 correctly freezes October and repairs April–May before model changes, then marks June–September safe and proposes one recalibration over that window. June over-predicts after the mix shift; August–September under-predicts after the supplier shift, so one calibrator blends opposite errors. September is not safe until labels mature, especially for Partner Z. “Time since part order” is not known at the diagnosis-time prediction point. The decision never states that the AUROC drop cannot be repaired by calibration alone. July is omitted from the safe/unsafe list.

Section 5 covers the required monitor families and blocks a label-delay alarm from automatically retraining. The code sketch does not survive execution or the cohort definitions. `format_dataframe` renames `case_id`, `activity`, and `timestamp` to the standard PM4Py fields, while later steps still use the original names. `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` is nulled and dropped, after which unknown share is computed on that frame and is identically zero. The monthly `filter_time_range` keeps only in-month events, so the “case-start” DFG is a truncated trace, not cases started that month followed to completion. `get_variants` returns a dictionary, not a frame with a `variant` column. Performance DFG output is reduced with `mean` although the audit evidence is median sojourn, and the event-calendar cohort is never summarized.

Section 6 has the right failure modes, including immature October labels, calibrating on the April bug, ignoring the supplier shift, and treating Partner Z as unfairness without a stratified check. Those mitigations do not correct the earlier call to treat September as training-safe or the invented V3 AUROC.
