The business reading is strong and mostly faithful to the policies: telemetry and the silent routing move are treated as non-events, zero-cost auto-approvals are caught as real P1/P2 breaches, pay-before-approval is separated from cheap edit distance, and denial/withdrawal cost is attributed to scope. The deviation types, pattern evidence, competing hypotheses, and remediation portfolio are concrete and generally well owned.

Severity is applied unevenly. CL-602 is scored 5 on an unproven missing fraud screen even though manual approval is present and precedes payment, and that gap is stretched into P3. CL-610 is scored 4 despite a confirmed auto-approval after a one-hour bank change, which is a completed bypass of the required fraud-and-manual path. CL-604 is marked high confidence in the case table while a live timestamp-lag hypothesis remains open. Realized exposure on CL-602 and CL-604 is placed fourth in the portfolio, behind future control design.

The code meets the PM4Py calls and keeps raw cost visible, but the prioritization table works against the narrative. Alphabetical sorting places LOW ahead of MAJOR and MODERATE. The bucket formula labels the EUR 480k fraud-screen gap MODERATE and the Assess Damage label mismatch MODERATE. Narrative severity never appears as its own column, and there is no real cross-case aggregation of patterns, cost, amount, risk, and policy flags. The tau discussion also blurs a genuinely benign zero-cost fit with the separate zero-cost policy failures.
