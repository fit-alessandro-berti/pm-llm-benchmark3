7.4

The final write-up is complete, numerically careful, and rightly refuses to treat Little’s Law or the post-outage completion spike as steady state. The main regime rates, WIP levels, Fano factors, and pooled weekday/weekend contrasts match the table. It is not nearly flawless.

The primary outage block (15–17 Apr) reports one completion rate of 8.31/h, almost identical to the baseline 8.30/h. That blend hides the 58-completion outage day and the 129/112 recovery days, which is the pooling error the narrative itself warns against. Recovery is also not separated from the temporary rise to 7.0 and 6.5 FTE on 16–17 Apr, when WIP still peaked at 52.

“Pooled service median: 31.0” and “pooled p90: 67.1” are unweighted means of daily medians and daily p90s, not a pooled median or a pooled p90. That is a definition error on quantities the task specifically distinguishes. The “Simpson-like reversal” is only an average sitting between an outage day and a recovery day, not a reversal of an association.

`peak_08_10_pct` is never used, despite rising from about 30% to about 45–50% and directly supporting the burstiness claim. Service times are already elevated on 15–19 Apr while `complex_pct` is still in the mid-20s, so the mix shift on 20 Apr is not cleanly separated from the outage shock. Little’s Law boundaries omit whether 15-minute WIP snapshots cover opening hours only or the full day, which changes the meaning of every L/λ ratio. The code never prints the pooled service and mix summaries asserted in the prose, and the PM4Py comment names the functions without showing how an event log would be summarized.
