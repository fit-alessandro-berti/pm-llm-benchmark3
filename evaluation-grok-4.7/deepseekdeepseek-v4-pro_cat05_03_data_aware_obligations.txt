The write-up is organized and gets several straightforward cases right, but the central materiality rule is applied incorrectly, two planted edge cases are decided the wrong way with high confidence, the formalization is incomplete, and the checking code does not implement the checks it claims to perform.

**Case reasoning.** TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-307, TX-309, and TX-311 are classified correctly, including the unknown amount at release in TX-305, the standard-packet approval in TX-307, the >20% invalidation in TX-309, and DE evaluated at release after the same-timestamp `event_index` order in TX-311. Those are real strengths.

TX-306 and TX-308 are not. R4 states that only an increase of more than 20% invalidates a review for amount-dependent obligations, and that a decrease does not. The notes on those cases (“increase below 20 percent,” “decrease”) exist to draw that line against TX-309. Treating every attribute update as a “latest material data change” that forces a new Enhanced_Review makes R4’s safe harbor do no work, and both cases are then marked NONCOMPLIANT at high confidence. Under the rule text they are COMPLIANT: the reviews still stand, and R2 on TX-306 is already met. Section 4 records the reading that would flip them, then leaves the worse reading as the holding.

TX-302 is also mis-stated. The Enhanced_Review at 09:20 already satisfies R1 for the unchanged 180k GB state; EX-77 is additional, not the reason the case clears. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, and “R1 met via exemption,” would become NONCOMPLIANT if the exemption evidence were weaker, which is the wrong dependency. The exemption discussion itself (signature at 09:05 versus the 11:00 insert) is sound. TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT is defensible, but high confidence overstates a record the prompt marks as contradictory.

**Formalization.** Forward-fill, blanks, `event_index`, and the split among event, effective, record, and evidence time are stated clearly. The rule table covers the triggers, but “material data change” is defined as any update to amount, risk, jurisdiction, or customer type, which collides with R4. The pseudo-logic only really states R1–R3; R4–R7 are not given the same temporal form. As written, the predicates drop the comparison and connective symbols (`jur  DE`, `amount  100000`), so the formulas are not usable as logic.

**Code.** The script has the right skeleton: `StringIO`, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `event_index`, forward-fill, and a release-level frame. The material-change mask is computed after the fill, so carried-forward values look like new changes and the release row itself becomes the “latest material change.” `r1_review_ok` is then false for any earlier review. R4’s 20% test is absent. There is no exemption-register join, only a non-null `exemption_id` flag. The sanctions-screen timestamp is overwritten with the case’s final value, which is the final-state leakage section 6 warns about. The DECLARE “check” is two print lines, and the TX-301 line both says a review exists and that Enhanced_Review is missing.

**Coverage gaps.** Section 4 never discusses TX-311, which the prompt requires explicitly. Sections 6 and 7 do meet the brief: six distinct control-flow failure modes, and the requested logging changes (immutable release payload, signature versus record time, rule version, scope, provenance, stable order).

The result is a readable draft that would still mis-decide the decrease and sub-20% cases and would not compute those decisions from the log.
