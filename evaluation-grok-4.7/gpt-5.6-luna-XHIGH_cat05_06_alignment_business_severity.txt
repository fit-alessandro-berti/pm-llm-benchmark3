The business analysis is strong, consistent, and correctly refuses to treat raw alignment cost as risk. Severity, confidence, scope, and evidence quality are separated, and the case ordering is defensible: CL-602 and CL-604 are critical, CL-610 is a zero-cost policy failure, and CL-601, CL-605, CL-607, and CL-612 are low-severity noise or scope problems. Uncertainty is handled carefully, especially where a model move only proves that a core event is absent.

The main analytical miss is CL-609. P1 fails on two independent predicates: EUR 18,000 exceeds EUR 5,000, and MEDIUM risk is not LOW. The case write-up and the cost discussion cite only the amount ceiling. The later control design does include the risk predicate, so the conclusion that auto-approval was prohibited remains correct, but the case-level policy result is incomplete. CL-602 also slightly blends an absent Fraud Screen with P3’s payment-before-approval rule. The recorded order still has Manual Approve before Pay Claim; CL-604 is the case that actually shows bank acceptance before approval.

The code keeps raw cost and narrative severity in separate columns and the move inventory matches the supplied alignments, but the evidence flags contradict the narrative. CL-601 is marked as a policy-overlay issue even though P1 is satisfied and the events are telemetry; CL-605, with the same satisfied outcome, is marked false. CL-606 is flagged as a policy issue although the write-up says P2 appears satisfied. CL-607’s amount is invented as 0. The event log covers only four cases and drops CL-601’s deviant UI events, so the PM4Py call cannot reproduce the authoritative moves. Attaching the full claim amount to every move also makes pattern-level amount totals non-additive across patterns.
