The write-up covers every required section and gets the headline qualitative calls right: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed, I-07 is treated as new evidence, spillovers are quantified, and calibration, label contamination, and fairness are all discussed. It is not a deployable, decision-theoretically coherent policy. The scoring rule, the gates, the portfolio, and the later safeguards contradict one another, and the scenario math is not used to choose the slots.

The net-value formula does not generate the scores in the allocation table. The formula charges \((1-\hat p)\) times false-alarm burden and subtracts queue spillover. Every published NEV instead equals midpoint absolute risk reduction times breach value, minus €450, minus the full false-alarm burden, with spillover omitted. That identity is exact: I-01 is \(0.265\times 9000-450-250=1685\); I-02 is \(0.15\times 25000-450-600=2700\); I-04 is \(0.19\times 60000-450-2000=8950\); I-07 is \(0.26\times 5000-450-50=800\); I-09 is \(0.24\times 20000-450-800=3550\); I-10 is \(0.19\times 11000-450-150=1490\); I-03 is \(0.015\times 40000-450-400=-250\). Segment calibration never enters those scores. On this arithmetic, I-04 (about €8,950) and I-08 (about €4,550) outrank every selected case, while I-07 is last among positive scores and is still labeled Tier 1 and given a slot.

Under the answer’s own base spillover, that slot is value-destroying. One slot at \(6\times 0.02\times €18{,}000\) costs about €2,160. I-07’s direct net is about €800, so its marginal base-case contribution is negative; dropping it raises the reported base net from about €1,670 to roughly €3,100. Optimistic marginal value is about zero, and the adverse case is clearly worse. The three scenarios are computed after the portfolio is locked, and no weighting, hurdle, or regret rule converts them into the allocation. Capacity is simply filled with whoever survives the gates.

Those gates are not stable across sections. Section 1 re-admits a repeat alert only for a new artifact, a stage change, or a calibrated jump of at least 0.15. Section 4 adds a \(t_{\text{SLA}}\le 3\) hour path and draws I-02 and I-09 as first alerts. Both are second alerts with no stated new event; I-02’s move from 0.58 to 0.66 is only +0.08. Urgency cutoffs are 6 hours, 4 hours, and 3 hours in different places, with no rule for the gap, so I-05 at 5 hours is unclassified by the runway filter. Non-Native-Language Support is told to shrink effects to the lower bound, is then scored at the midpoint of 0.24, and is later told to use an upper confidence bound. The €30,000 value cap, the 0.15 efficacy hurdle, and the 25% segment floor appear only as safeguards; none of them is in the allocator that produced the table. An operator cannot execute one rule.

The runway filter also answers the wrong clock. The swarm dies at Diagnosis Complete, while the filter waits on time remaining until the contractual deadline. I-01, I-04, I-08, and I-10 are deferred because 7–12 hours of SLA slack remain, without an estimate of when diagnosis will finish or a scheduled re-entry before the intervention window closes. I-06 is held for a 10-minute imputation check, but all four slots are already assigned, so a confirmed jump has no contingent slot. “Exactly one” reserve is also called dynamic. Public-sector and regulatory duties beyond the euro table are essentially unaddressed; €9,000 is treated as the full stake for I-01.

The impact table’s chosen point estimates add up, and that part is auditable: optimistic \(0.20\times 25{,}000+0.28\times 20{,}000+0.30\times 5{,}000=12{,}100\); base \(3{,}750+4{,}800+1{,}300=9{,}850\); adverse \(2{,}000+1{,}200+500=3{,}700\); base spillover \(3\times 6\times 0.02\times 18{,}000=6{,}480\). The surrounding ranges are not. The optimistic benefit band of €10,500–€13,500 does not match the upper-half effect envelope, which is about €9,850–€13,100. False-alarm charges of €150, €350, and €550 are not derived; full false-alarm burdens on the three cases are €1,450, and probability-weighted burdens are about €500. Optimistic spillover is priced at €15,000 per breach after the text sets the queue value at €18,000. Adverse effects for I-09 (0.06) and I-07 (0.10) sit far below the supplied floors of 0.18 and 0.20, and the spillover probability 0.035 sits above the stated 0.01–0.03 band, while neither selected case is actually taken to little or no benefit. “Portfolio value at risk €50,000” is the sum of contractual values, and calling I-07’s breach deterministic at a calibrated probability near 0.7 is false. Optimistic confidence of “moderate to high” is too strong for the stated uncertainty.

The I-05 versus I-07 comparison is the cleanest piece of reasoning: unchanged 30-minute refresh versus a new diagnostic log, with very different slack. Monitoring is concrete on calibration error, imputation, spillover minutes, and label drift. It still retunes a value cap the live rule does not use, proposes regression discontinuity without a small-sample caveat, and does not track the failure mode that matters most for this policy: cases that were told to wait, reached Diagnosis Complete, and then breached. The staffing safeguard of a dedicated swarm rotation would also remove the interruption externality that the rest of the answer treats as binding, with no sequencing between today’s queue and that future design.
