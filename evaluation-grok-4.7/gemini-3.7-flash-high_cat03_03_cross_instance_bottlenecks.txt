5.5

The write-up covers every required section and gets several local facts right: B1 waits of 235 and 220 minutes, S1’s 355-minute dwell and 340-minute partner gap, the 45-minute CAL-3 window inside J6’s 80-minute test wait, the L7 override asymmetry, and the co-presence account of idle machines beside waiting jobs. Those strengths do not offset invented observations and a cascade that the same timestamps refute.

The FIX-A arithmetic treats CNC-2 as idle from 10:47 to 11:45 (58 minutes) and sums that with the documented 10:05–10:47 gap to get 100 minutes of starvation. The source gives only the 42-minute idle window, and the longest CNC-2 event gap is 48 minutes, so a 58-minute empty stretch is contradicted. That 100-minute figure is then reused in the ranking and in the tooling intervention. J3 is also placed on CNC-2 for the whole 09:00–11:40 FIX-A hold, which is not stated and sits badly with the machine being idle while the fixture is still held.

The rank-5 cascade (FIX-A → J4 → B2 → S1, with S1 moving to about 17:15 and J1 saving more than two hours) does not follow. J4 is already batch-ready at 13:10. B2 cannot start before RACK-7 is free at 14:35 and J5 is ready at 14:40, and it actually starts at 14:50. J4 has on the order of 100 minutes of slack, so an earlier CNC start is absorbed before the oven. Machining is also equated with “ready at 13:10,” which the log does not say. Motif 1 makes the same overstatement by treating rack cleaning as why B2 cannot start, even though the rack is free five minutes before J5.

Narrative 3 states as temporal precedence that J6 is packed at 18:25 and S3 dispatches at 19:00. Neither timestamp exists. Narrative 2 likewise compresses J5’s 16:00 test readiness into “tests, packs, and joins J6.” Those are conjectures written as a trace. The 13:45 independent-ship counterfactual is the same kind of slip: pack time is 13:30, and 340 minutes is asked to mean both wait-for-J4 and dispatch acceleration.

Smaller quantitative and design faults add up. Rank 1’s “565 job-minutes” is not 235+220+100. The B2 split accounts for 85+5 minutes and drops 14:40–14:50. “Completed curing before 10:00” and the 435 job-minute saving assume an unstated cure duration. Dynamic batching is credited with saving J4 85 minutes, but that 85 minutes is rack unavailability; de-batching J4 from J5 saves about 10–15 minutes unless a free rack is also assumed. Median load and dispatch times are added and called a 1.1-hour median. Batch synchronization is labeled very high confidence and deterministic, despite conditional readiness and no single provable bottleneck. The thermal-mass falsifier (need at least three parts) is inconsistent with B2 running as two jobs, and three of the six falsifiers attack lower-ranked mechanisms. The PM4Py sketch names the requested functions and does not derive waits from machine gaps, but one of its two “frequency” metrics is ordinary event counts, and it assumes a “Ready for Batch” activity the flattened log does not contain.
