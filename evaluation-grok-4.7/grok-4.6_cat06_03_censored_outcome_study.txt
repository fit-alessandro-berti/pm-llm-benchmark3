7.0

The design gets the central scientific point right: 90-day default is defined only after activation, the 21,600-row export is a selected matured subset, and rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are not non-defaults. The cohort arithmetic accounts for all 48,000 starts, the leakage table covers the required fields with defensible keep/repair/drop decisions, and the two alternatives are actually different questions. The write-up then breaks that standard in the estimand, the censoring classification, the backtest calendar, and the skeleton that is supposed to enforce the as-of rules.

The primary estimand is not stable. Section 1 defines \(\pi(x_\tau)=\Pr(Y=1\mid X_\tau,\text{activated},\text{label identifiable})\), which conditions on administrative label arrival. Section 3 then makes a censored survival likelihood on all 25,200 activations the primary analysis and treats the 21,600 complete cases as a biased sensitivity. Those are different functionals. The \(Y\) cases are also not mutually exclusive: an observed default before \(T_{\text{act}}+90\text{d}+\Delta_{\text{feed}}\) satisfies both \(Y=1\) and the censored clause.

Non-activation is mislabeled. Section 3 calls a still-open, never-activated file ordinary right censoring in the same bullet that says the default process is undefined for that file. Section 1 had already separated “undefined” from “censored.” Permanently missing partner feeds are diagnosed as closer to MNAR, then left inside the primary censored likelihood; bounds are only a sensitivity. Inverse-probability-of-activation weighting is listed as a check on \(\hat\pi\), but those weights target a different, transported estimand, which the assumptions list itself calls untestable.

The cohort table and the transfer rule disagree. The table removes all 2,900 transfers from the 25,200 risk set. The prose then uses follow-up for the linked 60%. Linked partner activations cannot both be outside the risk set and contribute outcomes. The unlinked 40% and the permanent-feed cases stay correctly unlabeled.

The temporal split is the right idea and the random-split critique is sound, but the fold calendar does not implement it. Under the stated 125-day rule, a case started on 14 October 2025 is not identifiable by 15 February 2026 even if activation is immediate (14 Oct + 125 days is 16 Feb 2026), and any realistic time-to-activation leaves much of Test-A immature. The table still marks all of Test-A identifiable. Validation labels are not available until 30 November 2025, after Test-A has already started, so this is not a prospective backtest. Requiring maturity by a fixed date also keeps fast activations and drops slow document-loop cases, and that selection is not stated. Prefix length has the same hole: a \(k=4\) model drops files that reach Accept before four events, so fast-track bookings leave the primary sample.

The skeleton does not implement the design it accompanies. `case_status` is run once at the 15 February 2026 extract, so training labels use feed arrivals the validation section forbids. Maturity is true when `label_available_at` has passed or when the buffer has elapsed and `default_90d` is merely non-empty on an event row; a backfilled label on an early event therefore counts as known before `label_available_at`. There is no time-to-event or censoring time, only a mature flag, and `mature=True` with a missing `y` can fall out of both the immature count and the \(y\in\{0,1\}\) counts. `assert ... or True` cannot fail. Stripping terminal activities and then calling `get_prefixes_from_log` keeps later non-listed events in the prefix whenever they are among the first \(k\) remaining events. The DFG and variant calls are fit on every prefix, including later folds. Elapsed-time prefixes, linkage, and the competing-exit model remain comments. The 125-day rule, the disabled assert, and the global cutoff are enough to keep the pipeline from being an honest as-of audit.
