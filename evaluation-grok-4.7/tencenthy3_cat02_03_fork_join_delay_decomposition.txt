The case table, fork-join identities, critical-branch counts, synchronization means, and the A-service and B-queue counterfactuals are arithmetically correct, and the answer correctly refuses to treat A’s longer service as the controlling path. The double-counting argument is also right: only the overlapping tail of synchronization is counted twice, and summing each branch’s padded queue, service, and wait reproduces the stage three times.

The serious miss is the final-station congestion analysis that the task specifically requires. With the observed F1/F2 alternation, the assigned resource is already busy at join for F04, F05, F08, F09, F10, and F11, about 73 minutes in total, not “F05, F08, and F10, maybe 30 minutes,” and not 8–9 idle cases. F09 and F11 are omitted despite full assigned-resource waits, so Counterfactual 3 and the “do not add capacity” recommendation overstate how little of the 176 minutes of downstream delay is resource waiting. The “~42% / ~570 minutes” utilization figure is also not derived from a stated window.

Smaller gaps: arrival spacing is summarized as 15 minutes in the recommendation after the first gap is 10, and average marginal contribution to `join_ready` is only indirect. Definitions, worked cases, ranking, masking caveat, Python structure, and inference limits are otherwise strong.
