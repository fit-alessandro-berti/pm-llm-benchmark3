7.4

The required seven-part structure is complete, and the recommendation is coherent: A1 is correctly rejected on process grounds, A11 is deferred until controls are reliable, capacity shifts and exception lanes are explicit, and the four-module, one-control, two-deep-integration limits are respected. The grade is held down by analytical overclaims that sit in the reasoning, not just the wording.

The selected directly-follows list is treated as a traceability audit. “~47% of contracts untraced” and the 380 `Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation` cases as a decision bypass overread a partial edge list. Committee volume (3,900) versus contract preparation (7,600) is also what delegated authority for non-high-exposure cases would look like. Phase 0 does allow that explanation, but the matrix, dependency map, and final recommendation still state the gap as fact.

Pre-activity waits are repeatedly converted into automation benefits. A6’s direct effect is up to 75 minutes of service time, with medium feasibility and residual human review. The 18 h and 20 h figures are waits before document review and normalization. Section 4 still says the document leg “18 h + 20 h” is compressed. Those waits can fall only to the extent they are analyst queues; relative to service time, much of them is batching, handoff, or customer delay.

Analyst arithmetic does not close. Logged analyst-grade touch time is given as ~29,700 h against a 9 FTE hub, while A6 is said to release ~6,500 h, “~40% of capacity,” and ~3,000–3,500 extra credit analyses. That treats gross normalization time as fully releasable spare capacity. 6,500 / (9 × 1,600 h) is about 45%, not 40%, and the same hours cannot both close an overload and fund thousands of new analyses. The ~2,000 h A5 relief is similarly unshown.

Portfolio C (A3, A8, A9, A12) is offered as control-optimizing even though the answer’s own rule is that A8 is no-go without A6 and clean upstream artifacts. Shipping A8 in that set can worsen control through bad committee packs. Overall funnel loss (12,000 → 7,100) is also tied too tightly to the missing-document loop.

What keeps this above a mid-range score is the variant and concurrency handling, the specialist key-person constraint, the refusal to automate activation or e-sign ahead of KYC clearance, honest admission that four-eyes is not solved by the recommended set, and a gated rollout with measurable stop rules.
