7.3

The response covers every required section, separates case types, and treats peak failure as a mix-and-disruption problem rather than a volume problem. Weekly mix arithmetic, the 1,320-order peak gap, queue and utilization figures, and the main performance edges are handled carefully, and each adopted intervention is tied to a downstream station that can saturate. Those strengths do not offset the process-mining and control-design defects below.

The technical-validation split is not a valid partition. Peak assay-to-technical-validation entries are 7,240, while the three outgoing edges sum to 8,080. Reporting 56% direct release, 47% medical validation, and 8.4% specialist hold “of entries” makes those shares sum to about 111%. On the outgoing total, medical validation is 42% and the two clinical routes together are 50%, so the claim that more than half of entries take a clinical route is an artifact of the denominator. The earlier censoring caveat is not applied to this calculation. The same looseness appears in the repeat and batch rates: 15.5% and 69 per 1,000 use preparation-to-assay counts (8,140 and 16,550) rather than assay outcomes, which are 1,260/8,500 = 14.8% at peak and 1,140/17,030 = 6.7% normally. Normal batching is stated as 4.6% of preparation exits; 760/(16,550+760) is 4.4%, while 4.6% is 760/16,550. Peak batching is then calculated with the other denominator.

The operating rules are also internally inconsistent. Policy P4 turns on when receipt-to-check median exceeds about 4 hours, while the dashboard warns above 2 hours. A 2-hour band sits on the normal receive-to-check edge and the 1.8-hour normal pre-analytical queue, so the “leading” alarm is on in the baseline the redesign is meant to distinguish from stress. Cross-training is called robust only with named technical-validation backfill, but technical validation is already at 101% and the package never identifies a backfill source that avoids overtime or lost validation throughput. Direct release is offered as general protection even though the observed release share shrinks as the mix shifts toward urgent and complex-panel work, which is exactly when that lever is smallest. The residual risk about WIP limits concealing congestion refers to a control the redesign does not actually install. The M2 scenario briefly treats “move routine work onto degraded M2” as the migration path; that is the failure mode of static I3, while the physical loss of M2 pushes work toward M1, preparation, and technical validation.
