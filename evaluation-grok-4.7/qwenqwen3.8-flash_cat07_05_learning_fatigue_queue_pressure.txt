The final answer meets every structural requirement: the six headings are present, all seven mechanisms are compared with signature, support, limits, and tests, and the eight interventions, ethics cautions, and dashboard indicators are covered. It also respects the hard constraints. Fatigue is treated as a modifier, early complexity and senior selection are kept in view, late speed is attributed mainly to queue pressure, raw late-shift error rates are rejected as a basis for punishment, and quality checks are not dropped to raise throughput.

The integrated account is accurate against the supplied tables. Service time falls while errors rise only in the high-WIP regime, especially for complex work and less experienced resources; the middle of the shift is the low-pressure window; batching and end-of-shift quality-check timing are correctly treated as both a real workaround and a detection-lag problem; Bot_Risk is handled as downstream verification load rather than free speed. Intervention judgments, including constrained randomization and rejection of penalties, are sound.

The score is held down by concrete defects in the proposed analyses. `get_service_time` is treated as an event table, so the later models use columns that pipeline never carries forward, including `tmp_hour`. The spline is assigned as one column from a basis matrix and cannot enter the formula as written. The propensity model predicts high complexity while including `case_complexity_score` itself, so the score is circular, and no matching step follows. Case-level results are grouped on continuous mean complexity, which does not produce usable strata. `filter_paths_performance` is used as a path-existence filter, and several PM4Py calls rely on argument names the libraries do not take, only partly caveated for batch discovery. Experience is also rebinned by months in a way that erases Night staff and Bot_Risk, which the scenario treats as distinct groups. Those are methodological errors, not styling issues, in a required section.
