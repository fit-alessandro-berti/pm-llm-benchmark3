The write-up keeps the required six-part structure, avoids naming fraud or collusion as fact, and ranks the clusters in a defensible order: Lena–Kim first, the emergency channels next, and the high-volume routine pairs as monitoring only. The central control is mishandled. Four-eyes means the same human performed both activities. The answer repeatedly treats different-person handoffs as those violations: Carla→Gita and Dan→Hugo are listed for Create PO + Approve PO, although the violating resources are Carla, Dan, and Gita, and Hugo is not in that row. Lena→Kim co-work (44 cases) is merged with the 9 same-person bank-detail/payment cases. That error carries into the priority rationale and the report wording.

The benign explanations do not repair it. Delegated approval by a second person would satisfy four-eyes, so it cannot explain same-person violations. The strongest artifact for the 42 PO cases—the legacy_erp mapping that can make Bot_PO and a human look like one `org:resource`—is not used there. Fast-approval figures are the co-case medians (11, 14, and 19 minutes) rather than the fast-subgroup medians (8, 10, and 17 minutes). Vendor-master and AP payment roles are blurred. Vendor concentration and manual source-system concentration are collapsed into one row.

Section 4 names the requested analyses, but the calls would not run as written: `filter_four_eyes_principle` takes two activities and `resource_key`, not an `activities` list and `resource_attribute`; attribute filters need a list of values; path-performance filtering needs thresholds.
