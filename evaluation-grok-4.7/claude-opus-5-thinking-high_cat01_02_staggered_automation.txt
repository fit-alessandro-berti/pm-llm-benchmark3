7.5

The memo is unusually strong: the estimands separate availability from intensity, median from tail, and direct escalation from unmeasured desk congestion; staggered-adoption logic correctly rejects a single two-way fixed-effects number; most checked contrasts reproduce the panel (adoption-month rework steps of −2.0/−2.4/−2.0, escalation steps of +1.0/+0.8/+1.0, the common March +2.0 escalation and May +5 complexity shifts, Alpha/Beta April arrival jumps of 14.4%, and cohort ATT(e) values such as −6.6 and −11.2 log points). Uncertainty, interference, and the refusal of an “X%” slogan are handled at a high level, and the code matches the requested descriptive design.

It is not nearly flawless. Section 3 attributes a favourable pre-trend to both Alpha and Beta, but the sensitivity table moves Beta from −9.3 to −11.4 log points, so Beta’s own pre-trend runs the other way; the +3.4/+3.6 placebo pair is also a level gap into March, not a steady −1.5 log-point monthly slope. “Adding Gamma moves Alpha by only 1.6 log points” compares the full post window with the early window, so it does not identify the control-group change. The top claim-ladder bullet is false as written: rework does not stay about 2 points lower in every post-adoption month (Alpha is 14.0–14.5 after a one-month drop to 11.2), and never-treated median increases are about 11% and 17%, not 13–17%. The intensity write-up never reconciles a −3.4 log-point effect per 10 points of use with the claim that doubling use buys only about 2 points and is therefore a step, not a dose response. Log points are sometimes mean-of-logs, sometimes treated as percent changes, including on rework and escalation shares whose common shocks are additive. The recommended within-Delta case randomization is a poor match to the congestion/capacity mechanism the memo itself treats as central, and calling it the only route to identification overstates the case.

Those are interpretation errors, not formatting nits, so the score stays well below excellent despite the overall rigor.
