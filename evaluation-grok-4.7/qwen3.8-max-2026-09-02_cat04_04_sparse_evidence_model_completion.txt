7.5

The three candidates are genuinely different hypotheses, the evidence ledger covers the variants, rules, and stakeholder claims, and the treatment of completion-only timestamps, bidirectional DFG arcs, missing versus optional Supplier Confirmation, and D5/D6 is careful and internally consistent. Conservative language is exactly the seven observed traces; the balanced partial orders do cover every observed variant while blocking S-before-both and X-before-both-checks; the permissive boundary, audit traces, matrix, recommendation, and alternative case-type interpretation are all defensible.

The required code is not reliable. `parse_process_tree` expects `X(...)`, not `XOR(...)`, so the conservative tree can fail to parse (`XOR` is easily misread as operator `X` plus leftover `OR`). POWL normally allows only XOR/LOOP as operators and encodes sequence as a strict partial order with `.order.add_edge`, which is the pattern the prompt itself shows. Both non-conservative models instead call `OperatorPOWL(Operator.SEQUENCE, ...)`, and the constructor helper only catches `TypeError`, then falls through to `StrictPartialOrder(order, children)` or an unguarded `StrictPartialOrder()`. If those signatures are wrong, `model_balanced` and `model_permissive` are not executable, so the later “perfect fitness by construction” claim and the validation block do not hold. The validation script is also not self-contained: it depends on names from earlier cells.

Smaller issues: the no-S rate is 7/30, well above the stated 5–10% missingness, and that comparison is never made; the conservative exclusion “after Engineering Review and Supplier Confirmation” is really a failure at S immediately after the initial E; and the permissive model relaxes D5 rather than staying inside the domain rules, even though that choice is disclosed.
