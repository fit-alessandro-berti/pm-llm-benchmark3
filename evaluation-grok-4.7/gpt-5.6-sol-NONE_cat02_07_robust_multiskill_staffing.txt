6.0

The write-up is complete, operationally specific, and often directionally sound, but it is not audit-clean. Headline counts contradict later arithmetic, several scenario deltas are wrong, and the simulator does not implement the policies it is supposed to compare.

The required nine-section structure is present. Mixture means are defined correctly (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74). Net shift capacity of 2,490 minutes, the 42-case base day, the U1 volume increase from 16 to 27.2 morning arrivals (53.2 for the day), and most block diagnostic loads are reproducible. The recommendation is tied to a worst-scenario fairness objective, and Sections 6, 7, and 9 treat starvation, gaming, case-level versus stage-level waiting, and pilot guardrails seriously.

The opening census is false and inconsistent with the author’s own workload table. From the demand weights, expected branch executions are about 18.1 mechanical, 24.3 electrical, and 12.9 software (55.2 executions), not 15.1, 20.9, and 10.4 (46.4). Those wrong counts cannot be reconciled with 475 mechanical minutes at 26.30 minutes per branch. The 16:00–18:00 electrical load is 79 because the ME electrical branch is omitted; it is about 108 minutes, so daily electrical work is about 798 rather than 766. The 14:00–16:00 cohort’s intake load is about 69 minutes, not 54. U4’s electrical mean at a doubled tail probability is 37.14, not 37.6.

U1’s P1 count rises from about 5.4 to about 9.1. The stated rise from 5.3 to 8.2 matches adding 8 points on the pre-surge morning volume. U2 is interpreted as doubling E and ES weights and renormalizing, which is defensible, but under that reading the 12:00–16:00 change is about +63 electrical minutes and essentially no software increase. The stated +110 electrical and +50 software minutes, and the claim that U2 then moves the bottleneck into software, do not follow.

The capacity table omits Chen from 08:00–12:00. Chen is on shift, electrical throughput is 1.00 base minute per wall minute, and software throughput is 0.80. The prose later adds Chen to morning software only. Morning electrical capacity remains understated, so “Farah only” and “software is acutely exposed” are stronger than the staffing matrix supports. Post-16:00 single-server mechanical and software coverage is a real structural hole and is identified correctly. Aggregate load near 108% of net regular capacity is a valid tightness warning; it is not a per-resource feasibility result, and per-resource offered load is never computed.

Section 4’s numeric bands are judgment, not outputs of the model, and they overlap so heavily that they do not demonstrate separation. The robust objective also leaves “normalized SLA loss” undefined, so the objective cannot be recomputed.

The Python encodes the tables and calls the requested PM4Py functions, but the dispatch model is not a valid comparison engine. Cases are fully booked in arrival order; the comment calls this urgency order, while priority is only a same-timestamp tie-break. Specialist scores prefer the primary resource whenever that person exists, so Ben receives every mechanical branch and, under Chen’s absence, Emil receives every software branch even while Farah is idle. That contradicts Policy A’s overflow rule. Preemption does not interrupt a victim: it starts the P1 earlier, double-books the resource, and adds the restart penalty to the P1. Parallelization does not test an 8-minute net saving; the hybrid gate `min(means) > 12` is true for every diagnostic mean. Coordination time is not written back to resource availability. Thirty minutes of overtime is always open. Electrical and software share one setup family in code, which is an unlabeled assumption. The q90 column rename keeps the second lambda’s raw name. A scaffold was allowed, but these are incorrect mechanisms presented as the three policies, so the printed metrics would not support Section 5.

6.0 reflects a serious policy argument and a usable monitoring design, discounted because the audited arithmetic and the reproducible comparison are materially unreliable.
