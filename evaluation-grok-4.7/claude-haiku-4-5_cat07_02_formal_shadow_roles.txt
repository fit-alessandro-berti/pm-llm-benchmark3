5.5

The role map is directionally right: front office is split from temps and from Nora, Chen is not collapsed into Dina, Fatima, Erik, Hana, Giorgio, and Mateo are separated by authority and complexity rather than raw volume, and shadow roles are given both benefits and risks. That is not enough to carry the answer. The required evidence is numerically unreliable, internally contradictory, and in several places misread.

Factual and arithmetic failures are repeated, not isolated. Mateo is given 504 events; the matrix sums to 415. Erik is given 144 events; the matrix sums to 170. Hana is given 226; the matrix sums to 245. “680 cases registered” for Amira and Ben is 430 A1 events. Giorgio’s “450 registrations + validations” is 280. Temp volume is claimed at about 1.3 times Amira+Ben on a stated base of 1340; those two permanent agents have far more events than Kai and Lina. Lina’s high-complexity count is 4, not “0–2.” The quality row’s “~100 medium and ~15 critical each” disagrees with the same answer’s later, correct ~205–210 medium and ~7–11 critical. A6 is not ~170 events or 56 percent Erik; the column sums to 146, and 96/146 is about 66 percent. Erik’s high-or-critical share is about 83 percent, not 68 percent; Hana’s is about 70 percent, not 49 percent. Mateo’s critical-case share is not “second after Erik,” and A9/A13 are not his concentration peaks: A12 (52), A2 (50), and A4 (45) are higher. Dina has the lowest permanent tenure, not the second-lowest. “96 is higher than 104” is simply false. “13% rechecked” and “62% rechecked” are both used for 13 of 21.

The duration error is worse than rounding. Median pricing time moves from 38 minutes to 74 minutes. The answer repeatedly turns that into “3874 min,” including in the role table and the staffing advice, while elsewhere stating 38 to 74. Weeks 3–4 also get the weeks 7–8 result: complaints did not fall during Giorgio’s cover; false-escalation complaints fell when Mateo filtered security referrals. That misattribution is used as evidence that the substitution succeeded.

Classification and causal claims slip. A11 is put in the technical core and called minor in the same table; 22 combined events are not core beside 515/475/435. Quality check is called mandatory for all cases, which sequence fragment 1 contradicts. Temps are given no peripheral work and “zero” A3/A8, despite 8 and 12 diagnoses and 3 and 4 negotiations. The same temp subsection says both “2–4 high” and “100% low or medium.” Own A13 counts are treated as proof of Tier-1 judgment, while the temp failure is correctly traced to cases those temps did not themselves reopen. Security “veto that Hana cannot override,” “senior architect,” and “design veto” are stronger than the log. Nora misclassification is invented (“if 5%… ~30”) and then tied to the temp note failures without evidence.

Section 6 converts 10-week totals into impossible weekly rates. Hana’s 138 approvals become targets of “~70/week”; Fatima’s 122 pricing reviews are supposed to yield “30–40 A7 events/week” automated away. Delegation is described as reducing Hana’s load to 70–85 events per week from a stated current load of about 20 per week. Headcount, “$50–100K,” and a “$120K” hire are presented as if derived. “1 additional permanent” and “0.5 FTE” are the same action. The four cautions match the brief, but they sit on top of recommendations a process owner should not execute.

The code covers `format_dataframe`, `discover_organizational_roles`, activity similarity, handover, and working-together, but it does not cleanly join discovered roles to complexity and `temporary_substitution`. It invents `discover_organizational_roles_activity_profile`. It aggregates case-level complexity and service time on events, so long cases are over-weighted and full case duration is charged to every resource who touched them. `case_complexity > 0.75` is treated as the critical category. Section 11 then assigns formal roles with Herfindahl and complexity cutoffs — the naïve frequency-to-authority step the brief says to avoid — and those cutoffs would not reproduce the roles argued in prose. An extra top-level `# Summary` also breaks the required heading set.

The qualitative story would be a strong draft. As an evidence-backed role analysis, too many cited counts, percentages, attributions, and operating targets are wrong.
