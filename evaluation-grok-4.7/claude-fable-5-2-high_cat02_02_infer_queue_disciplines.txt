7.5

The diagnosis is strong and mostly well evidenced, but it is not nearly flawless. Several claims are overstated or internally loose, including one false structural claim in a prominent identifiability cell.

The decision-set reconstructions are the best part. Eligible sets, skill filters, calendar blocks, and the one-sided two-minute scan window are handled carefully. QA’s 5/6 FIFO score, the A03-only appointment break, the A05 overrun of 09:15, QB’s split between the B02 preemption and the B03 beneficiary, the 09:35 refutation of shortest-remaining-time, QC’s 10:10/10:18 SPT choices, the 10:18 versus 10:54 deadline distinction, C07’s avoidable miss, and QD’s D04-over-D08 and D07-over-D10 tests all check out arithmetically. Service times really do equal `est_min` on all 32 jobs. Assumptions are labelled, and observational equivalences (SRPT versus P1 at 09:16, work-in-progress versus arrival FIFO for B01, skill feasibility versus a skill “policy”) are recognized rather than collapsed.

The clearest error is the repeated claim that QC priority rank and deadline rank are identical, and later “perfectly collinear.” They are not. C07 (priority 2, deadline 12:00) is due before C03 (priority 2, deadline 12:30), so earliest-due-date and priority-with-FIFO disagree on that pair. Those two jobs never share an eligible set, so the rules do coincide on every observed decision, but that is sample non-separability, not identical ranking. Stating the false identity as QC’s main identifiability problem overstates what the log identifies.

The aging explanation of C04 over C06 is also muddled. With recorded timestamps the response ratios are about 2.14 and 2.17, and highest-response-ratio-next selects C06, the same job as dynamic SPT. C04 wins only if C04 arrived early enough inside the scan-delay band. Calling this a tie that explains the observed choice buries the fact that the hypothesis misses on the recorded data.

Smaller overclaims accumulate. D05’s five overtakes are not “fully explained” by skill and the meeting: D10 is electrical, and D-F was idle long enough to finish it. The QD 11:20 cell says the idle fits “only” a hard hold or hidden downtime, while section 1 correctly treats early binding as equally viable. QA’s and QB’s “contradicting” episodes are the cases the hybrid component was built to absorb, so those cells do not actually contradict the stated diagnosis. “P1 may interrupt; otherwise FIFO” is also worded more narrowly than the rule being scored: at 10:00 the server is free, and literal otherwise-FIFO would resume B01, yet that choice is counted as a hit. The QB violation tally of one out of eight is right only under the broader rule “P1 always outranks, else FIFO.”

The counterfactuals, exception ranking, and collection plan are concrete and mostly well aimed. Ranking a logged “dispatcher reassignment” below speculative unrecorded downtime, and ranking the 32-for-32 estimate match seventh, is debatable but labelled as judgment. Confidence levels appropriately track ambiguity, especially QC at 35.

Main diagnoses survive these defects: QA is the cleanest queue, QC the least identifiable, and reading QB as ordinary preemptive priority is the dangerous inference. The score is held down because a hypercritical reading cannot ignore a false identifiability claim, a response-ratio argument that points the wrong way on the recorded times, and repeated only/fully wording around the QD anomalies.
