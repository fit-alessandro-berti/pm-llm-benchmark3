5.1

The write-up covers every required section and names every required scoring factor, but the worked policy is not internally consistent and is not safe to run on this queue.

The concrete assignment breaks the answer’s own hard overtime rule. R2, R4, and R9 are high overtime risk, yet they receive C010, C003, C014, and C006, all normal priority, with safer alternatives available (R5/R6 for approval, R3/R8 for diagnosis and rework, R8/R10 for quality). R4, already at 14 open cases and 3.0 hours, receives two of those. Several of those rows then record the trade-off as “None.”

Training claims do not match the skill matrix. Documented priorities are diagnosis for R1 and R6, quality for R2, negotiation for R3 and R9, approval for R4 and R8, and security for R5 and R10. C010 is justified as Farid’s approval coaching; his priority is diagnosis. C013 treats Eligibility as indirectly Diagnosis. C016 marks Eva’s diagnosis work as training; her priority is security. R4 and R8, the only approval-priority resources, both have approval skill 0, so they cannot prepare or finalize. The quota arithmetic conflicts with itself: the table marks five training rows, the summary says 4 of 11 normal/low cases (36%), and the comparison says 4 cases (20%). Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, and C015: nine cases. Only C009 is a clean priority match. C015 has Eva shadowing Security at skill 0; rule 6 allows skill 1–2 under R7 or R8, not skill 0.

Continuity is over-applied and then miscounted. Every existing owner is kept, so new handovers are 0, not the “1” claimed in the comparison. That choice also ignores the stated evidence that weak skill adds about 3.3 hours of service time: C001 has 3 hours of SLA, complexity 0.88, and an explicit specialist need, but it stays with Security skill 1. Constraint 5 permits that only because supervision exists; the SLA collision is not faced. Batching is allowed for normal/low quality work above 24 hours, but section 1 drops “low,” and C006 and C012 are not batched. C012’s trainee note is dismissed while invalid training rows are counted.

The scorer would not produce this table. SLA score is constant across candidates for a case, so the 0.35 weight never affects who is chosen, and cases are not ordered by urgency even though the worker text says urgent work goes first. `training_boost` of 0.15 is then multiplied by 0.10, a 0.015 effect; the resilience term is about 0.005. Handover bands are used as numbers, `required_min` is undefined, the eligibility line is missing a comparison operator, and `c.prioritynormal` is not a condition. Supervision pairs are added only when nobody is eligible, yet C009, C010, C013, and C016 are supervised pairs whose trainees were already eligible. The training swap is applied after the constraint re-check. The cap of 4 is not incremented in the loop, and supervisor load is exempt from it. The eligibility filter also lets any high-priority case onto high-overtime staff, which is looser than section 1.

Section 4 invents “+8%” rework and “<2%” throughput loss, then says overtime is balanced. It is not. Section 5 is clear and non-punitive, but it describes a different procedure from sections 2 and 3, including an invented two-hour cycle. Audit coverage is complete, yet skill mismatch only flags skill below 2, so unsupervised Security skill 2 passes; `get_variants` does not mine `assignment_reason`. The PM4Py sketch misses `filter_case_performance`, reads a `skill_level` column the log does not have, treats `get_service_time` as a row field, passes an activity name into `discover_batches` as if it were the activity column, and sends a performance DFG to `save_vis_dfg`. Role, similarity, and variant results are computed and then unused, so the sketch audits more than it learns.
