8.2

The final memo is a strong causal-policy assessment: availability is separated from dose, median is not collapsed into p90, the arithmetic behind the pre/post and difference-in-differences figures checks out, escalation is treated as its own result, and the refusal of a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is the right conclusion. The rollout choice (embedded evaluation, Epsilon first, Delta held back, queue and fraud-desk measurement, explicit stop rules) matches the identification problem.

The score stays well below excellent because of several concrete weaknesses.

The selection threat misstates the direction of bias. Regression to the mean among branches picked for bad cycle times would make cycle time fall absent automation and overstate a benefit. Labeling that bias “upward,” while the next row correctly says nonparallel trends “overstate benefit,” is inconsistent and wrong under the usual signed-effect reading.

The headline adopter table drops June, the worst post month for Alpha and Beta (Alpha median 53.5, p90 119.9). The windows are disclosed, and adding June does not flip the relative comparisons, but the raw “mature” median improvement is softer once the full post window is used. Alpha’s April–August median is about 48.5 versus a 48.9 pre-period, not the −6.8% suggested by July–August alone.

The preferred design is not the design that was actually quantified. A synthetic control with two donors and three pre-months for the April cohort is barely identified and, with equal weights, is the same comparison as the difference-in-differences that was computed. No pre-fit, donor-weight, or placebo-in-time diagnostic is shown.

The dose analysis mostly restates pre-versus-post co-movement. Alpha and Beta’s fast-path paths are nearly identical, so a branch-and-month fixed-effects intensity coefficient is weakly identified. The reported +2.2 hours of p90 per 10 points of fast-path share is a fair univariate association, but it is easy to read as a dose effect when it is largely the May–June shock lining up with the ramp.

The claim ladder then pools those branches into a conditionally causal 5–10% median reduction. Beta’s own median is flat to slightly worse in every post window; the relative gain is almost entirely controls deteriorating. That is a narrower and weaker claim than the ladder’s third bullet, and it sits above the memo’s own −8% to +2% sensitivity range and the 35/100 confidence assigned in the effect table.

The rollout design is also shorter than the dynamics the memo itself emphasizes. A 6–8 week holdout for Delta cannot separate learning from case-mix, and within-branch week-level phase-in shares the same specialist and fraud-desk queues the memo flags as interference.

What keeps this in the low 8s rather than the mid 7s is that the main decision, the five-outcome table, the threat list, and the verified contrasts are careful, and the code is a real descriptive panel and dose model with the small-cluster and aggregated-median warnings, not a fake causal proof.
