The framework is strong: time origin, 72-hour horizon, competing risks, and already-observed breaches are separated; E1–E4 are tied to decisions; transfers are not treated as ordinary censoring; immortal time and prefix leakage are handled correctly; and the main Aalen–Johansen figures check out (overall T1 CIF of resolution 30.4%, E1 69.6%, E2 38.0%, RMST 50.4h, E3 30.1%, S13 weight 15.8%; T2–T4 and the cohort/priority splits for those estimands also match).

The sensitivity table is not fully reliable. If all five open cases at or before 72 hours resolve, no one remains censored before 72, so the E1 lower bound is \(18/32 = 56.3\%\), not 53.1%. The naive cause-specific survivals are about 63.9% in A and 63.8% in B, not 64.9% and 63.0%, even though the pooled 63.9% is right. On-time resolved prefixes average 4.67 activities, not 4.4. Section 3’s 36.1% and Section 6’s 63.9% are complements, but that is never stated, so they read as conflicting naive results. “Already observed with certainty” for the four open cases past 72 hours is slightly too strong given the same document’s stale-status concern, and Cohort B’s extra early censoring is asserted as a consequence of later rollout rather than shown. Priority standardization is described and then not calculated.

The code covers the required estimands, keeps open cases, and does not invent timestamps, but `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` and `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` are not real PM4Py entry points. A successful import with the wrong API crashes instead of using the fallback, and `len` on `get_variants` values is version-fragile. Bootstrap intervals promised in the plan are also absent.
