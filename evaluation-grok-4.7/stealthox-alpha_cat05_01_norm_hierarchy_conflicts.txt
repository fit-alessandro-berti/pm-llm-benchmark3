The memo is a serious, issue-specific compliance analysis: time-of-act testing, one-way ERP skew, non-curative later approvals, the €150k D1 cap, and the P-505/P-507 timing reconstructions are handled with real discipline. It is not nearly flawless.

The hierarchy table reverses the “cannot override” column on the GPIP rows, so A3 appears unable to displace ECD-2 onboarding while the ECD-2 row and the P-502 holding say the opposite. A1 is simultaneously “never overridden,” waivable by A7, and waivable in the draft clause. The approval ordering “B2 > ECD-2” contradicts the claim that ECD-2 displaces the Plant leg. D1 is formalized as window-based, then withheld from out-of-scope P-503 without that conflict being stated.

P-503 omits a clear A3 breach: €140,000 with no Compliance_Clearance at all. That breach survives both scope readings under the memo’s own rule, and PROC-3’s Incident Command role is treated as unproven even though the case states it. P-501 overreads B4. Commit-before-PO and Plant-Manager substitution are separate permissions; a reachable manager and a later Plant_Approve do not, without more, make SHIFT-1’s commitment unauthorized. High-confidence NONCOMPLIANT is too strong, and the stated alternative is weaker than that textual one. Severity is also inconsistent: the same missing budget-owner leg is Low on P-505 (€88k) and Moderate on P-507 (€110k).
