3.5

The write-up follows the requested outline and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, but its arithmetic, identification, and rollout design are not reliable enough to brief leadership.

Section 5 does not implement the windows it announces. Alpha’s pre-period median mean of 48.9 hours is right, but the reported post median of 47.9 is July alone; April–July averages about 49.8. Alpha’s reported post p90 of 112.6 is May, not a four-month mean (about 109.9). Beta’s pre-period rework is 12.8%, not 10.8%; 10.8 is later escalation, and Gamma’s “pre rework” of 11.2% is Alpha’s April figure. Gamma’s stated pre median of 38.1 hours is not the three months before July (April–June average about 43.7). The same section then calls Gamma’s p90 change of +5.7 hours an improvement. Endpoint quotes for Delta (36.2 to 39.0) and Epsilon (40.2 to 40.8) are single months, so there is no aligned difference-in-differences. Absolute p90 increases are treated as harm even though never-treated tails rose by more over April–August after the shared May complexity shift.

The intensity result—about −1.5 hours per 10 percentage points, p < 0.05, and −2.0 for Gamma—is not computed from any reported specification. The sensitivity “90% CIs” are likewise unearned. Gamma’s pre-trend is described as stable at 36.4–36.5 hours with a “drop” to 38.4. Those early months omit the rise to 45.4 and 45.9, and 38.4 is higher than 36.4. That surge-and-partial-reversal is exactly the regression-to-the-mean pattern also visible in Delta and Epsilon.

Estimands are not sharply defined. Pre/post contrasts are marked identifiable; the use effect is given a case-level unit the file does not contain; “dynamic learning” is a two-month Gamma trend; and several “yes” identifiability marks ignore routing, staffing, fraud-desk interference, and the May case-mix shock. Baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is never treated as a measurement problem. The primary design is named as an event study with branch and month fixed effects, but heterogeneous-timing bias is not actually addressed. The justification is roughly 55 words against a required 150–250, and the two alternatives are not in the required table. Adjusting for arrivals, FTE, and complexity invites bad-control bias because those series move with the rollout and the product change.

Mechanism columns are inverted or directionless: specialist bottlenecks and tail amplification are placed on the beneficial side, and “CSAT if quality falls” is treated as a benefit. Section 4 says Alpha rework rose toward 14.5%; Sections 5 and 7 say it fell to 11.6%. Escalation is called unchanged at confidence 75 while Alpha moves from 7.8% to 11.6%, partly from the March fraud desk that also hits untreated branches. Bias directions contradict themselves (“positive (underestimate),” “negative (overestimate)”). Confidence 85 that automation lengthens p90 by 5–20 hours is the most consequential overclaim, and the “safe” median claim of up to 3 hours is not safe.

The embedded-evaluation choice is the right family of decision, but the plan expands first to Gamma, which has already been treated since July, forces a 40% fast-path target that changes the intervention, and scales on absolute hour thresholds a common shock would trip. The code does not reproduce the prose. `pd.compat.StringIO` is not a current pandas loader; adoption time is each branch’s first observed month, so every branch is “adopted” in January; the regressions are pooled OLS without the fixed effects promised in Section 3; escalation is omitted from the five-outcome loop; and the models are labeled ITT rather than left as assumption-laden descriptions. The claim ladder puts causal “effect” language under `Observed` and, under `Not supported`, writes the contorted sentence that a blanket X% claim “is defensible.”
