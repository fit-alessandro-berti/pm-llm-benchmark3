6.5

The write-up follows the required eight-part structure, uses the concrete object IDs and timestamps, ranks machine capacity last for the right reason, and keeps most clock arithmetic straight. It is still not a reliable delay decomposition: two of the derived “effects” measure the wrong interval, and the cross-job cascade the case is built around is denied in the narrative that should have established it.

The FIX-A figure is the clearest quantitative failure. CNC-2 is idle from 10:05 to 10:47 (42 minutes), while J3 holds FIX-A until 11:40 and J4’s process starts at 11:45. The 58 minutes from 10:47 to 11:45 begin when that idle window ends, so they are the period with no evidence that the machine was free. The counterfactual makes this worse: if FIX-A had been free at 09:50, the plausible start would be when the machine was actually idle (about 10:05), not at 10:47. The defensible overlap of “J4 present, FIX-A held, CNC-2 documented idle” is about 42 minutes, plus a 5-minute release-to-start lag. That 58-minute number is then reused as the benefit of buying another fixture.

The extra RACK-7 calculation is also unsupported. OVEN-1’s idle run from 12:55 to 14:50 is real (1 h 55 min), and RACK-7 is in cleaning until 14:35. B2 still contains J5, who is ready only at 14:40. A free rack at 12:55 would not let B2 start at J4’s 13:10 readiness. Rack release (14:35) precedes J5’s readiness (14:40), so cleaning is not the binding constraint on the 14:50 start. The claimed 1 h 25 min reduction treats a two-job batch as if it were J4 alone.

J1’s shipment wait is arithmetically right (13:30 to 19:25 is 5 h 55 min) but left gross. S1 dispatches 15 minutes after J4 is packed at 19:10; S2 and S3 leave 25 and 35 minutes after their last pack. The consolidation penalty is that gross wait minus a normal post-pack lag, and the counterfactual says so without changing the headline number. The same section treats J2’s full 3 h 40 min as batch synchronization. L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released only at 13:50, with J2 entering B1 at 12:05 under a supervisor override. J2’s “sync” wait is confounded with a lot hold; J1’s is much cleaner. “Same counterfactual” for J1 and J2 also fails: starting when the first job is ready (08:10) does not describe J2, who is ready at 08:25.

Narrative A then calls the S1 wait an independent consolidation policy. The coupling the case asks for is the opposite. J4, the partner J1 waits for, reaches CNC-2 at 09:50, is held off FIX-A until 11:45, is ready for B2 at 13:10, and is packed only at 19:10. B2 itself waits for J5, who does not move until after the 13:50 lot release. J1’s post-pack hours are the shipment rule transmitting upstream tool, lot, and batch lateness from jobs that never share J1’s oven cycle. Labeling that path independent drops the cascading-delay question. Policy lines in the narratives also state more than the log shows: “all three jobs required before start,” “only one FIX-A,” and “mandatory recalibration after every Final Test” are single-case inferences placed in the policy bucket.

The ranking table has the right mechanisms and a fair last place for capacity (54% / 44% / 58%, with idle time sitting next to tools and batches). It still discounts shipment relative to batch sync even though J1’s consolidation wait is the largest single interval, and it applies “might be intentional policy” more heavily to shipment than to batching. “High recurrence — every multi-job batch recreates the barrier” overstates severity: B2 and J3 show only about a 10-minute post-quorum lag; multi-hour waits need dispersed arrivals. The observability cells do not actually cover each flattening.

Flattening commentary is the strongest section: medians and short shipment DFG edges hide partner waits, lot events drop out of the job view, and low utilization can coexist with long waits because the machine is idle for a rack, fixture, calibrator, or incomplete batch. It never faces the mismatch between those narrative idles and the machine summary: OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 is 115 minutes against a 73-minute longest gap, and TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 is 50 minutes against a 39-minute longest gap. That gap is direct evidence that the machine flattening understates the idles just described. L8, which structurally ties C1/J1 to C3/J3 with no quarantine event, is listed and then ignored. The cure event at 12:05 is described as six objects while the evidence also binds C1, C2, and C3.

Interventions cover sync, tools, shipment policy, and logging, and each has a harmed party, a perverse metric, and an object-centric check. Two claims do not hold. Bidirectional lot logging does not remove the need for supervisor overrides; an override is an operational breach of quarantine, and logging only records it. Size-1 curing of B1 would more plausibly fill the morning oven idle and push B2, RACK-7, J4, and therefore S1/J1 later; “oven utilisation may fall” points the spillover the wrong way. The 58-minute tool benefit is inherited from the bad calculation.

The Python outline names every required PM4Py entry point and warns against reading waits only from machine gaps. The comparison of two object-centric frequency metrics is a comment under `extract_ocel_features`, not code that computes two frequencies and contrasts them. `networkx` is imported and unused.

Falsifiers and extra fields are specific and mostly on target. “Single-job curing is unacceptable” would show that the B1 barrier may be deliberate; it would not show that synchronization is absent. Parallel work during the B1 window would reclassify the wait, which is the right kind of test.

The required clocks for J1, J2, gross S1, and the CAL-3 split (1 h 20 min ready-to-start, 45 min recalibration, about 50 min if J6 could follow J5 at 16:35) are in good shape, and CAL-3 is properly kept as a counterfactual. Those correct pieces cannot carry the two wrong counterfactuals, the confounded J2 attribution, or the claim that J1’s shipment wait is independent of J4’s upstream holds.
