4.6

The write-up has the required eight-part skeleton and several correct clock differences, but it is not a reliable account of the coupling. Too many headline claims are arithmetically tidy and causally overstated, and several explicit deliverables are missed or malformed.

The B1 waits of 3 h 55 min and 3 h 40 min, and J1’s 13:30–19:25 gap of 5 h 55 min, match the timestamps. They are still labeled “root cause,” which the task forbids. The FIX-A figure of 1 h 55 min (09:50–11:45) treats the whole interval as tool delay even though CNC-2 is only recorded idle from 10:05 to 10:47. The CAL-3 figure of 1 h 20 min is wrong in substance: J6 cannot start at 16:05 while J5 holds TEST-1/CAL-3 until 16:35; the recalibration window is 16:35–17:20, about 45–50 minutes, not the full queue. The extra lot calculation calls 10:20 the “quarantine end.” That timestamp is the quarantine start; release is 13:50. Subtracting them does not yield J5’s avoidable wait. J5 is ready for B2 at 14:40, after release, and J2 is already in B1 at 12:05 under a side-system override.

The graph fabricates J1–FIX-A, leaves the “Observed?” column empty, and omits RACK-7 from the edge list even though both batches use it. Of the four motifs, tool contention and shipment consolidation involve only two object types, so the “three motifs of more than two types” requirement is not met. Ranking puts batch synchronization first and machines last, which is directionally defensible, but the rank principle is never stated against the larger S1 delay. “Evidence against” cells often restate other mechanisms or observability limits. Affected sets are too wide: J6 is not in a batch, and FIX-A reservation is said to help J6. “B2 idle 1 h 55 min due to RACK-7” misnames OVEN-1’s 12:55–14:50 gap and ignores that J5 is ready only at 14:40, after the rack is free at 14:35. The 1.8-SD fact is about jobs, not machines.

Flattening notes never treat duplication. Some “misleading conclusions” are inverted: a lot flattening would show a 3.5-hour quarantine, and Job–Tool interactions are the highest count in the given table (15), so tools are not “rarely used.” The low-utilization paragraph lists real upstream waits but does not use the supplied DFG medians. Pack→Dispatch at 1.1 h median cannot be the same clock as J1’s 5.9 h pack-to-S1 gap; that discrepancy is the actual proof that shipment waiting sits outside the job performance DFG.

The narratives do not carry the cascade the case is built on. J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40 and is batch-ready at 11:55; that is a candidate driver of B1, not an exogenous “supplier delay.” Narrative 1’s title promises tool and shipment coupling and then says RACK-7 has no conflict. Narrative 2 ties J5 to the CNC-2 idle gap without evidence and says an earlier release would let J5 “start at 14:40,” which is already its ready time. Narrative 3 names only Job, Tool, and Machine, short of four types, and its title asserts a shipment effect that the body never shows.

Interventions include synchronization, tools, shipment policy, and logging, but spillovers are unreliable: partial B1 loads are described both as shrinking waits and as growing batch size; quarantined C2/C5 are given “no harmed objects”; S1 relaxation is said to lengthen J4’s processing. Perverse incentives are often named without a metric. The PM4Py block overwrites `ocel`, never calls `discover_otg` or `ocel_flattening`, and filters batch membership with an object model OCEL does not have. Only one falsifier targets batch synchronization; utilization above 80% would not weaken that hypothesis.
