The diagnosis is strong and mostly faithful to the artifacts: fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces is interpreted correctly, the fast-path cost gap is kept as 31% before rework and 9% after, segment medians and complaint rates are not averaged away, and the 6–18 hour gate, 24-hour Premium credit line, and 72-hour Standard tolerance are used as design constraints rather than slogans. The three redesigns are mechanistically different, each states a sacrifice, and the experiment design watches complaint tails, utilization, and downstream shift instead of mean cycle time alone.

The score is held down by a real compliance error and several tighter faults. Redesign C treats advance exchange before evidence and review as preserving R2, but R2 binds replacement dispatch itself, with an exception only for Premium low-risk accessories. Shipping a high-value replacement early recreates the alignment deviation the diagnosis names; “review before final settlement” does not repair that. Loaners are a different control, and bundling them with advance exchange blurs the point.

Other issues are smaller but cumulative. “Clear mid-risk devices” are placed with Standard accessories although no mid-risk outcome row exists and the given shares already sum to 100%. Manual-capacity risk is repeatedly anchored to the 15–35% auto-rule side effect, which does not bound a structural move of high-value and unclear-defect volume onto seven reviewers. “Reduces the discovered model’s low precision” misstates precision: future controls can raise fitness to the normative net and may make a future discovered model more precise; they do not reduce precision of the historical model. The dominated column answers both “No” and “conditional.” Redesign C’s flow covers “Premium customers,” then later excludes routine accessories. Quote-to-approval “reaches 80 hours” without saying that figure is the p90. The express lane both requires complete evidence and claims the R2 pre-completeness exception. The 150–180 hour observation window stops at the complaint-edge p90, so it truncates the tail it is meant to capture. Customer-approval abandonment (70) and repair-quote complaints (80) never enter the redesign logic, and the Premium-specific slowness of manual review to repair quote is cited only as a generic tail.

Those gaps keep the answer short of nearly flawless, but the artifact use, segment trade-offs, and non-generic mechanisms are still well above a generic Lean rewrite.
