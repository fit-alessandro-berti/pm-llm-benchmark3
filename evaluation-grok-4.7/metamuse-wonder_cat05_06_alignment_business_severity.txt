The analysis is strong and complete: all seven required sections are present, all 12 cases are interpreted, severity is separated from raw cost and from confidence, silent `tau` is treated as fitting, and the zero-cost P1/P2 failures are recognized as the central trap. Remediation order, hypotheses, and the cost-versus-risk argument are generally well grounded in the supplied evidence.

The main policy error is CL-609. P1 allows Auto Approve only when amount ≤ EUR 5,000, risk is LOW, and there was no recent bank change. The case is EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM. The write-up, Pattern E, Section 6, and the code flag `P1:Amount>5000` cite only the amount. The same row correctly tests risk for P2 (not HIGH) and then ignores the LOW requirement in P1. That is an incomplete overlay result on one of the two showcase zero-cost failures, repeated rather than a one-line slip.

The reproduction block has two concrete defects. CL-604’s supplied alignment has five synchronous moves (Register, Validate, Assess, Manual Approve, Notify), one log move, and two model moves; the dataframe stores `n_sync = 4`, so 4+1+2 does not equal the eight alignment steps. `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` returns dicts with `cost` and `alignment`, not `case_id`, so `a["case_id"]` raises before either table is printed. The snippet therefore does not meet the executable-output requirement. Move-pattern aggregation also omits risk and policy flags, and the `(0 log, 0 model)` group pools compliant CL-605 with the policy failures.

Smaller problems: CL-601’s cost 3 is called jointly highest, although CL-607 and CL-612 are 4; the code flattens CL-602’s confidence to “Med-High” where the narrative correctly splits “evidence absent” from “activity may still have occurred off-log”; CL-607’s amount is hardcoded as 0; CL-612’s missing notification is folded entirely into model scope without considering P4. The CL-604 narrative itself is sound; “without either required gate” is only slightly loose, because manual approval exists and is late.

Severity choices elsewhere are defensible, counts other than CL-604 check out, and external evidence is not treated as proof that a missing log event means the activity never happened.
