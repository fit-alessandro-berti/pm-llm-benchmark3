7.5

The memo meets the required structure and lands on a defensible hybrid (E): keep the simple-house gains, stop survey-skipping and billing-before-activation, and demand more evidence before wider rollout. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated, all five metric conflicts are explained, A–E are compared, and M1–M8 are ranked with decision impact.

It is not nearly flawless. The executive memo reverses the precision finding (“the process is less controlled than the model suggests”); low precision means the model is too permissive relative to the log. Section 3 later states this correctly, but the lead conclusion remains wrong. Value-of-information ranks conformance monitoring first even though alignments already call those deviations frequent; full downstream cost and a stratified test of survey-skipping would change the scale decision more. Segment effects, the basis for the gate, are under-caveated given attributes missing before pilot week 3, a harder regional mix, and a shorter, smaller pilot—especially for p95. Thresholds (0.5%, +1pp, 85%, +2pp) are stated without a rationale, and SLA plus the generalization drop are thin relative to the other mining evidence.
