6.4

The write-up follows the required structure and gets the main qualitative distinctions right: prerequisites and four-eyes are preserved, K-337 is treated as a decision-policy change, earlier readiness is separated from earlier completion, and C1 reservation is recognized as zero-sum. Those strengths do not offset material temporal, calibration, and scale errors.

The counterfactual paths are not internally consistent. K-104’s compliance interval is not a readable timestamp (“Wed12:00-13:10 start; 2-5h”), and a 5-hour review from the later start finishes no earlier than the factual case, outside the stated 54–57h band. K-219’s table “base” (Mon 20:00–Tue 04:00, 0–16h saved) disagrees with §3’s base (start Tue 00:00, about 15h saved) and with its best case (start Mon 19:30). K-411’s table caps savings at 0–10h while §3’s best case claims about 17h, and the assessment notation “Tue19:00–Wed09:30 start” is ambiguous. Identity is also silently lengthened from 1h50 to 2h.

Several schedules fight the snapshots. On Mon 16:00, D2’s queue is about 5h, so a Mon 19:30–20:00 start is not a supported best case. The Mon 17:30–Tue 09:30 gap is attributed entirely to the D1 queue, although an 11h D1 backlog at 16:00 does not by itself explain a Tue 09:30 start if overnight work is allowed, as K-104’s own D1 interval assumes. K-411’s Tue 21:00 decision ignores the observed pattern that a late Tuesday finish waits for M1 until Wednesday morning.

The K-104 mechanism is mistimed. The preliminary S1 packet is placed Mon 09:00–11:00, before identity and before the complex documents exist, yet the base case assumes that packet stops C1 reading the file from scratch. Useful pre-reading can occur only after Document Check produces evidence. The expected 1.5–4.5h saving is therefore centered on the wrong information set, while §5 calls that same small effect a “high” benefit and §7 says K-411 creates no compliance risk after §5 flags audit failure.

The 100-case analysis names the right bottlenecks but misses their magnitude and is short of the 250-word requirement (about 240). One hundred D2 checks at 4–8h, or one hundred S1 packets at about 2h, are hundreds of hours against one person-week; “D2 may become the bottleneck” understates a backlog collapse. “About 100 weekend waits” generalizes one Friday–Monday response, and “one narrow arrival window” treats a week of K-411 arrivals as a single synchronized burst. Total assessment hours do not jump that way; at most there is a transient phase shift.

The K-411 pilot population is misspecified: requiring both verification tasks and excluding lock failures removes the cases the workaround is for. Sample size is supplied where support/overlap logic was required. Assumption 6 states as operative the optimistic packet effect that the worst case correctly says would erase the gain.
