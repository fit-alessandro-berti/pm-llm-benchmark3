6.5

The write-up covers every required section, stays conditional, and treats East differently from North and South. It is not a reliable forecast or a usable measurement plan. Several central claims conflict with the tables, and the PM4Py sketch would mis-measure the effects it is meant to detect.

The capacity arithmetic is missing, and that omission distorts the scenarios. Legal/Risk volume rises from 9 to 12 per 100 cases while utilization is already 91–93%. A one-third demand increase on that base pushes utilization past 1 unless headcount is added, so a move from 9.6h to 10.5h median wait is not a credible central case. Approval volume rises from 42 to 49 per 100 while one of three local supervisors is removed, about a 1.75× load per remaining approver. The mechanism map treats that change mainly as a role-clarity problem. Summing the handover table gives roughly 343 events per 100 cases now and 359 after the change, so the optimistic claim that nominal handover count falls “as in the management table” is unsupported. “15 percentage points” also misstates the stated 15% relative utilization gain. The tech pool is multi-server; an M/M/1 story overstates its queue and still never shows the Legal/Risk breaking point.

The pilot cannot identify the intervention. Moving one non-East team onto a service used only by that team does not create cross-team pooling, and East left unchanged is a non-equivalent control, not a natural experiment. A shared Legal/Risk queue already couples treatment and control, so spillover is unaddressed. An 85% utilization guardrail is already breached by today’s Legal/Risk baseline. The highest-risk load shift is outside the recommended pilot scope.

Section 7 has definite logic errors. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting results are graphs; `.items()` does not yield weighted edges. Passing every variant to `filter_variants(..., retain=False)` drops all variants. `rework_flag` and `reassignment_count` are averaged at event level, so long, reworked cases are overweighted. The team SLA split computes duration only on that team’s events. A 120-hour SLA cannot sit next to 6.8–8.6 day medians and 6.5–9.8% miss rates. `get_service_time` is used for post-handover wait, role discovery is cited as a utilization source, and `aggregation_measure="perc90"` is not a supported measure. DFG self-loops are equated with queue wait. `local_expertise_flag`, `handover_reason`, and `priority` are unused, and planned specialist touches are conflated with loss of ownership via `reassignment_count`.

Rework never appears as its own mechanism, despite the required map. There is no reinforcing loop from lost local practice to more referrals, longer queues, and further skill loss. The scenarios stay qualitative where the baselines support rough numeric ranges. Section 4 and the conditional “pilot first” recommendation are the strongest parts, but they rest on an untested central case and a pilot that would not reveal the failure modes the text itself describes.
