The case conclusions are largely sound and internally consistent: event-time roles are used, aliases are normalized before segregation checks, D-QM-01 is grounded in the pre-use signed PDF rather than the 12 August migration row, D-IC-04 is rejected as prohibited subdelegation, CHG-508 is caught as a same-person breach, CHG-509 does not treat the weekend as proof of Asha’s absence, and CHG-505’s 14 August review is correctly late against a Tuesday 11 August trigger (second business day ends Thursday 13 August). Repeated Clara–Ben pairing is treated as an indicator. Service-account handling differs appropriately between CHG-503 and CHG-509.

These gaps keep it short of a near-flawless analysis.

Delegation scope is incomplete for RR4. D-QM-01 is “QM approval” for production-equipment changes. Both retrospective reviews are a different activity on `cooling_system`. The write-up flags the equipment-versus-cooling question and leaves equivalence open, but it never tests the activity limitation. That omission matters: a timely CHG-510 review can still fail the delegate’s authority even if the cooling system is later mapped to production equipment. CHG-505 is therefore firmer as unauthorized than the review’s scope analysis shows, because lateness, sick-leave use of `u_bk` on 14 August 16:00, unproven CI-44 linkage, and unproven scope all stack; the primary label is defensible, the delegation analysis is thinner than the classification.

CHG-504 is resolved too cleanly. The 09:22 approval sits inside the 09:15–09:40 VPN window and matches Asha’s “one urgent case” statement, but security expressly cannot exclude credential sharing. `AUTHORIZED` as the primary label, even at medium confidence, treats an unresolved operator as the QM. The better primary label is indeterminate identity with authorized-if-personal as the leading alternative.

CHG-507’s control objective is under-examined. Eva, then the Independent Verifier, approves, and Grace Li verifies. RR1/RR2 resource separation holds, and that is what the finding says. It does not say that the designated verifier approved while an undesignated Site Director performed verification.

CHG-508’s secondary `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` blurs a resolved identity with a system weakness. `b.koch` and `u_bk` are Ben; RR1 fails. Alias masking explains why a raw-ID check would miss it. The actor is not unidentified.

The required table is one row per case. CHG-510 is split across two rows, and several SoD cells are only bare name pairs, so the pass/fail result is not stated in the column.

The Python meets the structural checklist: `StringIO`, timestamps, `format_dataframe`, alias normalization before four-eyes, both four-eyes directions plus an independent pandas check, interval tests, both organizational networks, a guarded optional filter, and an explicit refusal to infer collusion. Service attribution is event-specific and documented. The implementation still parts company with the prose. CHG-506 is emitted as `AUTHORIZED_QM_DELEGATE` because Asha’s whole leave interval is coded `absent=True`; the identity dispute is only a caveat. CHG-504 gets no caveat at all. IC matching ignores CI-44 scope. Retrospective events get a business-day timer and no interval test of reviewer role, delegation scope, or Ben’s sick leave, so CHG-510’s review prints as timely without an authority result. `serializable_network` handles a dict and otherwise uses `str(net)`. Current PM4Py returns a NetworkX graph, whose string form does not list weighted edges, so the required network evidence is not actually produced. Sick-leave and partial-return logic stay manual after the code has already printed an authority status.

Section 4 and the improvement list are specific and usable. The three named investigations are the right ones. The systemic split among confirmed breaches (502, 507, 508, 510), a supported substitution (501), and open identity or absence questions (503, 504, 506, 509) matches the log.
