The required structure is complete: partial orders, conditional constraints, one counterfactual row per case, the six derivation labels, interference and robustness tables, pilots for the top two interventions, and eight typed assumptions. The qualitative model is strong. Timing-only, routing, and decision-policy changes are separated correctly; K-337 is treated as a change in evidentiary tolerance; final compliance still follows final assessment; four-eyes is preserved; queue times are treated as forecasts; and earlier readiness is distinguished from earlier completion and from manager signing windows.

Temporal consistency is not reliable enough for a higher score. In K-337, Friday 10:30 to Monday 09:00 is 70.5 hours, but the write-up calls it both 46.5 hours and 68 hours. The first external wait is given as 19.5 hours and 20.5 hours; only 20.5 is right. Elapsed time is called about 9.5 days when it is about 8.9, and the claimed savings (80–96, 90–110, about 100, and 95 hours) do not line up with the stated Friday-evening versus Monday-morning endings. K-411’s best case is −19 hours in the derivation and −10 to −12 hours, then −3 to −5 hours, in the table. K-219’s best assessment start at 12:00 conflicts with A2’s 6-hour Tuesday 08:00 queue. Rework odds drift among roughly 20%, 25%, and 15–35%.

The 100-case discussion also softens K-104 into a 25% high-risk mix (20–30 cases) instead of 100 similar high-risk cases, which understates the C1 collision. Tuesday’s assessor-training note is unused. “Superlinear” throughput and “exponential” congestion overstate the queueing math, and the precedence symbol in the formal block is not reliably readable. The direction of each intervention is still credible; the hour arithmetic and cross-section ranges are not.
