The memo covers every required section and correctly resolves several clearer cases: CHG-501 is a valid D-QM-01 substitution backed by the pre-existing signed PDF; CHG-502 and CHG-508 breach drafter/approver separation, with `b.koch` correctly collapsed into Ben; CHG-507 is an unauthorized high-risk approval because Eva was still only an independent verifier and D-QM-02 did not exist; CHG-510 is unauthorized both as prohibited subdelegation and as same-person draft and approval; CHG-504 is properly left indeterminate. Those calls show real event-time role reasoning. The designed traps and the required code are handled poorly enough that the write-up is not reliable.

CHG-505 is wrongly cleared as a high-confidence valid substitution. Approval was Tuesday 11 August at 13:00 and the retrospective review was Friday 14 August at 16:00. Two business days after Tuesday 11 August end on Thursday 13 August, so the review is late under either an end-of-second-business-day rule or a `BusinessDay(2)` offset. The same row also accepts Ben as the QM reviewer while he was inside certified sick leave that ran through 14 August 23:59. That contradicts CHG-506, where Ben’s use of `u_bk` on 13 August at 13:00 is treated as indeterminate. D-QM-01’s scope is QM approval of production-equipment changes, and the row never tests whether that scope covers a cooling-system retrospective review. The emergency flag is treated as sufficient even though no safety rationale is in evidence, and the missing-evidence cell says none.

CHG-509 is the second false clearance. D-QM-02 requires Asha to be absent. Her recorded leave ends 14 August; 15 August is a Saturday with no formal unavailability record. Weekend is not that proof. Eva was a quality engineer, so without a valid delegation this high-risk approval fails RR3. The row also attributes `svc_qms` to Eva and still calls independent verification intact, without separating technical implementer/verifier difference from the control weakness that the approver and the attributed verifier are the same person.

CHG-503 labels the right family of problem, data or identity, and then analyzes the wrong segregation. Diego implemented; the session came from his usual shared station, WS-DM-04, with his badge and no re-authentication. The adverse reading is self-verification. The stated alternative, that Eva performed it, is the weaker story. The segregation cell only compares Clara with Ben.

CHG-506’s indeterminate label is defensible, but “Ben remote work” is already excluded: the IP was an office workstation, and Ben denies the action. A live benign reading is that he entered the office while certified sick. Section 4 repeats the remote-work explanation and asks for home-IP evidence that the file already answers.

Rule formalization is thinner than the case requires. RR4 as written omits that the retrospective reviewer must be the QM or a valid QM delegate, and omits the credible safety-rationale condition. “Mon–Fri” never defines whether the event day counts or whether the deadline is an exact business-day offset, which is how the 14 August review was waved through. A principal on leave is reduced to an identity anomaly; Asha’s QM appointment continues unless identity itself fails. The late database record is correctly denied retroactive force, while the matrix still presents Ben’s evidence as “signed PDF + DB record” across the whole 10–14 August window. CHG-501, on 11 August, depends on the PDF alone because the migration insert was 12 August at 09:15.

The code reconstructs the log, parses timestamps, normalizes `b.koch` to `u_bk`, and names the right PM4Py calls. It does not do the required pandas interval test of role, scope, absence, and delegation at each approval. The four-eyes, handover, working-together, and different-resource calls discard their results, so no flags or network evidence are produced. Leaving `svc_qms` unmapped also makes CHG-503 look four-eyes compliant against `u_dm`. `filter_activity_done_different_resources` is passed a list and aimed at an activity that does not repeat.

The closing split of 40/30/30 treats three nonconforming populations as a partition of cases the memo itself called valid. The priority list never opens the late CHG-505 review or Asha’s unproven absence on 15 August. Headings are bold-wrapped (`**## ...**`), so they are not the exact required heading lines.
