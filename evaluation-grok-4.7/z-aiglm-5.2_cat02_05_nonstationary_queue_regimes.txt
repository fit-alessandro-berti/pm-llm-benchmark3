The regime structure, Little’s Law cautions, and pooled-versus-segmented argument are substantively strong, but the final write-up is not numerically reliable and overstates one backlog link.

The primary segmentation is the right kind of story: pre-mix weekdays, weekends, a one-day outage, a recovery transient, a post-20 April mix shift, and a weakly identified staffing change from 29 April. Recovery is correctly refused as a steady-state high-throughput regime, median service is not substituted for mean flow time, and the Little’s Law matrix uses the required verdicts with sensible proxy and stationarity reasoning. The alternative WIP-per-FTE cut is genuinely different, and the “cut weekend staff because pooled demand looks low” policy is a real Simpson-style operational trap. Format, the single pandas script, the non-definitive change-point heuristic, and the PM4Py caveat all meet the specification. The code’s median summaries are more careful than the prose.

Those prose figures often do not match the table. R1 weekday arrivals are given as 98, then 101, then 99. R5 weekday median arrivals are 115, not 111; the stated 108–121 range drops Friday’s 106, and weekend completions are 39–43, not 39–46. R1 completion median is 100, not 101; R5 median p90 and complex share are 83 and 45, not 82 and 44. Pooled mean arrivals are about 90, not ~86; the pooled arrival median is 103.5, not ~99; all-weekend median arrivals are 44.5, not ~41; mean WIP is about 30, not ~27. Hourly means for 15 April, 21 April, and 25 April do not equal the vector sums (105, 115, and 51). The reported Fano factors are therefore off, especially 7 April (~3.2, not 2.2) and 21 April (~9–10, not 12.3). Raw lag-1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals is about 0.41, not 0.6–0.8. “WIP falls 5235” is garbled twice. Running the embedded code would contradict several of these stated results.

The flow–stock identity is missed. Arrivals exceed completions by about 110 over the month while start and end WIP are similar, and the post-mix week accumulates about +106 net cases while mean WIP only moves from the high 30s to about 50. That gap matters for every throughput proxy. The claim that the outage remainder persists into the post-mix regime is also too strong: by 19 April mean WIP is back to about 13, in line with the prior Sunday, so the later WIP jump is a new episode. Tail service “roughly doubled” (about 47 to about 82 minutes) is overstated, and the pooling discussion is a step-change mixture rather than a shown reversal. The ×24 Little’s Law conversion is asserted even though the prompt never says the 15-minute WIP snapshots cover closed hours.
