The quantitative core is accurate and the causal framing is disciplined: availability is separated from use, median and p90 are never collapsed, the single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is rejected, and the main pre/post and never-treated contrasts check out (Alpha/Beta/Gamma median DiDs about −5.6/−3.4/−3.9 h; escalation about +1.1/+0.7/+0.9; event-time median gaps and the −0.13/−0.19 dose slopes are reproducible). Threats, mechanisms, and the embedded-evaluation rollout are substantively strong.

Deductions under a strict reading:

- The primary design is a transparent windowed DiD, not a Callaway–Sant’Anna group-time ATT. The label overstates the estimator.
- The code’s event study dumps Gamma’s early leads (k≤−4) into the omitted k=−1 bin, so the reference period is contaminated. That regression is not the prose estimator, and the two intensity models are not reconciled.
- “Baseline-window” sensitivity is promised and not reported. The 0–0.5 h per extra case/FTE bound is asserted, not derived. The p90 single-control range (−4.5 to −9 h) and the joint “35–43” cases/FTE line are a bit loose (Beta tops out near 39; single-control p90 DiDs are about −4.8 to −8.2).
- “Gamma’s effect is separated in time from the May shock” is the wrong reason. Complexity stays high; identification comes from controls sharing that shift, not from the shock ending.
- Escalation at confidence 70 underweights the piecewise-constant series the answer itself flags. Alpha’s rework level rose (+0.5); the “likely beneficial” reading is almost entirely control deterioration.
- The experiment randomizes only low- and medium-risk cases, so it does not identify the tail and fraud-desk channel treated as the main harm. Calling Epsilon the “likely high-benefit” site also sits badly next to “heterogeneity not identified.”
