7.1

The design is careful and mostly correct, but it is not nearly flawless. Counts, focal-object reasoning, and the refusal to treat shared Order/Invoice links as identity are strong. The implementation then contradicts that design, and a few ordering and metric claims are tighter than the evidence.

The conservative counts are right: 17 events and 44 E2O rows; merging e03/e04 and e11/e16, whose object sets really match, yields 15 events and 39 relations. Keeping e05, e13, and e14, restoring release versus acceptance, and not inventing an M1/M2 link on e15 are the right conservative calls. The `have_common_object=True` warning is accurate: e13 and e14 share O1 and V1 while referring to different payments. Under activity/timestamp grouping plus any shared object, the aggressive collapse to about 10 events and 28 deduplicated relations (picks e03–e05, packs e06/e07, dispatches e08/e09, invoices e11/e16, captures e13–e15, with e10 left out) follows.

The blocking defect is in the verified-pair helper. It rejects the merge unless the two `(oid, type)` sets are equal, then the evidence scenario calls that helper on e06/e07. Those sets are `{O1,I1,I2,P1}` versus `{O1,P1}`. Section 2 says the evidence path should union a subset; section 5 expects 14 events and 37 relations; the code raises before either result exists. Because that call sits above the library steps, a straight run never reaches `ocel_drop_duplicates`, `ocel_merge_duplicates`, `ocel_sort_by_additional_column`, or `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`. The assert of 14 events is unreachable. Snapshots are also taken before label refinement, so an absorbed refined label would not be in the observation list even if the union were allowed.

Ordering is less conservative than the rest of the answer. e09 before e10 and e12 before e13 are given. e08 before e09 is not: fact 4 only separates warehouse release from carrier acceptance. Unlike the e03/e05 and e11/e12 cases, no admissible partial order is left for 10:15. The rank map then collapses semantic edges and display choices into one integer, reuses 10/20/30 across 10:00, 10:15, and 12:00, and parks e01/e02 at 10000+. That is safe only if the sorter always uses timestamp first. `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` then writes synthetic times into the primary timestamp, which is exactly the false-duration footgun sections 3 and 7 describe. `original_timestamp` is kept, but discovery on the ordered object would still use the altered clock. “Remove two canonical rows” is the wrong description of dropping e04 and e16.

In the sensitivity section, raw I1 `Pick→Pick` from e03/e04, the surviving order-level `Pick→Pick` for I1 then I2, payment flattening that leaves e15 off M1/M2, and the new M1–M2 co-occurrence after a union merge are right. “Duplicate removal lowers Issue→Authorize” is not. On one order trace the direct edge stays one; what disappears is `Issue→Issue`.

The decision tree, provenance schema, and failure list match the required cases. The grade is held down by the non-runnable evidence path, dead required PM4Py calls, primary-timestamp mutation, and over-committed 10:15 order.
