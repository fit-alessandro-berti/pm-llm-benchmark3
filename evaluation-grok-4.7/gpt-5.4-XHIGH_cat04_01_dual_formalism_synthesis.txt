8.4

The POWL model is the right canonical object and is essentially exact at the control-flow level: registration is the unique source, containment and analysis are unordered, planning depends only on containment, design/approval/supplier depend only on analysis, the trial is a do/redo loop gated by both plan and design, quality and regulatory review are an unordered pair under an exclusive high-risk branch, supplier is a silent skip versus one occurrence, and release is the real three-way join. The eight witness verdicts match those semantics, the early-sync loss of the tree is visible in A3 and A4, and F1 is a genuine cross-branch failure rather than a start/end failure.

The main semantic fault is the process-tree cut. Early serialization is a safe under-approximation, but it is not required in order to avoid unsafe release. A still-block-structured tree can join containment→plan with analysis→design, then run the trial loop in parallel with the risk XOR and the supplier XOR, and only then release. That tree also excludes intended behavior (approvals and supplier consultation can no longer overlap design or verification planning), but it preserves the late release join and the approval/trial overlap that make the dependency graph non-series-parallel. The write-up treats “serialize before the trial” versus “admit release without the obligations” as the available choice. That is a false dichotomy, and it drops the concurrency the narrative singles out.

Two smaller inaccuracies remain. F3’s “earliest” violation is earliest only for the intended partial order and the POWL model; the tree is already illegal at Execute Trial, because Supervisor Review has not occurred. The concurrency argument in the POWL section covers the important incomparable groups, not every incomparable pair it claims to cover. Loop orientation, label discipline, high-risk conjunction, and the release gate itself are handled correctly, and the validation plan tests the approximation instead of treating conversion as proof.
