The write-up follows the eight-part outline and reaches the right broad story: June’s fitness collapse is mostly logging and extraction, August’s extra drop is largely an unupdated model after the sign-off rule, and stable approval and appeal rates do not prove staff misconduct. The load-bearing facts, the causal graph, and the code do not hold up.

The alignment table is misread. Label remapping alone moves June–July fitting traces from 32% to 58%, not to about 81%. Removing `Queue Routed` after that remap is what reaches 81%. Source-sequence ordering then reaches 89%, not an invented 95%. Section 4 shifts every stage by one step. Section 3 then says the label change alone explains the fall from 93% to 32%. Those two claims cannot both be true, and neither matches the staged results. That table is the main evidence that separates the hypotheses, so the ranking’s posteriors (95 / 90 / 85 / 80 / 70 / 60) are not actually tied to the observed effect sizes. Residuals are also entered as “contradicting evidence,” which treats coexisting causes as mutually exclusive. The rank order is still sensible, but it is not split by period: model obsolescence is a weak explanation of the June collapse and the main explanation of the remaining August gap.

Chronology is crossed. The 25 enhanced-review traces without `Compliance Check` are June–July, before the 1 August sign-off rule, yet sections 3 and 7 treat that rule as their cause. “25/30” and later “30/30 August–September” misstate the counts: August–September has 30 such traces among a much larger enhanced-review volume (150 + 90 + 30), not 30 out of 30. The 10 July memo did not report a rise in violations; using it as support for noncompliance over-reads a reminder. The ledger’s “weakens” column is effectively unused, and the stable-KPI row puts the weakening claim in the “supports” cell. Flat outcomes are then reused in section 4 as a falsifier of real bypasses, which cuts against the requirement that unchanged appeals neither prove a data error nor clear noncompliance.

Several discriminating facts never enter the ledger or the ranking: the directly-follows counts, the 40-case UI audit versus the separate 11-of-70 screenshot sample, the 30 June–July traces still labeled `Classify`, `Queue Routed` on most but not all cases, the 12 August `Manual Override` logging patch, the rise in events per case and in missing resource attributes, and the perfect April–May `Enhanced Review` → `Compliance Check` relation (180/180). IT’s statement that `Manual Override` is a real user action is reassigned to `Queue Routed`.

Section 3 is not a directed adjacency list. The mermaid graph renames required nodes, adds self-loops, omits the technical event, and draws `measured_fitness` → `business_outcomes`, which reverses the measurement relationship the case is about. Section 4 has six rows, not the required eight, and its label-remap row predicts both an 81% fitness jump and a DFG that still contains `Risk Screen` after remapping. Precision is absent from the diagnostic program even though an optional sign-off model can buy fitness by overgeneralizing. Phase 2 bundles label mapping, technical-event removal, and reordering instead of isolating them. `pm4py.compare_models`, `DFGDiscoveryAlgorithm`, and `TokenReplayAlgorithm` are not real APIs.

The code would not run and would not answer the question if the import errors were patched. `convert_to_petri_net` is not unpacked. Variant tables are expanded by a local splitter instead of `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`. DFGs are not discovered with `pm4py.discover_dfg`. Conformance is token-based replay on a list of lists passed through a nonexistent dataframe converter, not alignment fitness, and precision is never computed. The normalization maps `Risk Screen` to `Receive`, not `Classify`, and “removing” `Queue Routed` drops every case that contains it. `O ('Supervisor Sign-off')` is not an optional activity; a skip is an XOR with `tau`. The EUR 50,000 caveat is printed, which is the one code requirement that is met. Section 8 covers the six governance topics, but calling the amount threshold a temporal constraint is the wrong constraint type.

Section 7 states a numeric confidence only for the residual compliance concern (60%) and calls model obsolescence an artifact. The actions for IT, process owners, and compliance are directionally right, and the “must not claim staff are noncompliant” close matches the evidence. The arithmetic, the June/August causal split, and the reproduction code are not reliable enough to carry that conclusion.
