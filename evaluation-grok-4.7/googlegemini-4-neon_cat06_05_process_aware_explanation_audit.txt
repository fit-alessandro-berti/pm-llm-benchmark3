The audit is strong on the hardest distinctions: post-`t0` leakage versus the normatively valid but still future `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` path, the impossible same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` ordering, and preservation of queue length and elapsed time as real prefix signals. Coverage of the top-12 features, sequence patterns, and local drivers is complete; predictive, causal, actionable, and fairness standards are separated; and the Case X/Y narratives do not retell leaked outcomes. The validation suite and remediation plan are concrete.

It is not nearly flawless. `final_handler_team`’s unmatched 35% is asserted as escalations that occur precisely when claims stall, including an SIU example borrowed from a different feature; that mechanism is not in the lineage. `total_case_duration_hours` is said to directly encode the 10-day breach label above 240 hours, collapsing time-to-settlement into post-closure duration. Local SHAP signs are treated as cohort facts (“Broker-submitted claims show higher historical breach rates”; region R4 is “statistically associated” with higher rates) even though those attributions come from a contaminated model. Admissibility is inconsistent: `claimant_channel` is a credible driver on an unstated registration assumption, while `initial_reserve` is suspended because reserves are “routinely revised,” which the notes never say. Two sequence rows also drop the negative sign (−0.12 and −0.09). Those are analytical overclaims, not missing leakage.
