The answer is structurally complete and substantively strong: it separates availability from uptake, rejects a single cycle-time percentage, keeps median and p90 apart, treats rollout as endogenous and staggered, flags fraud-desk interference, small-cluster uncertainty, and regression to the mean, and recommends a holdout-plus-within-branch evaluation rather than an estimator name. Those are the right conclusions.

Quantitative discipline is not reliable. Alpha’s post FTE is 32.4, not 32.8. Gamma’s Jan–Jun escalation mean is 8.7, not 9.0, so the +1.7 pp change and the +0.5 pp DiD are wrong (about +2.0 pp and +0.9 pp). Gamma’s pre p90 is 85.8, not 86.0. The Gamma rework control change is about +1.7 pp, not +2.0, so the −2.5 pp DiD is overstated. Those errors are repeated in the mechanism table.

The code does not match the stated design. Relative-time indicators are filled only after adoption, so every lead is zero, and the fitted model is the naïve two-way fixed effect the identification section rejects. Pre-period `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is never treated as a measurement contradiction. Adjusting for post-rollout arrivals, FTE, and complexity is not flagged as post-treatment. Section 1’s “learning” is a causal gloss, and the claim ladder calls a 0 to −5 hour interval conditionally causal while Section 7 calls the same result suggestive.

The policy recommendation and threat analysis would score higher alone. The arithmetic and the event-study implementation keep this well short of nearly flawless.
