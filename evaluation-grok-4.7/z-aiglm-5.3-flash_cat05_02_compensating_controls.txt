7.5

The case judgments are largely sound and non-mechanical: same-user emergencies are not auto-failed, distinct-user AP-204 is failed on substantive independence, AP-205 is stopped before completion, AP-208 is failed despite a legitimate outcome, and AP-209 is correctly left indeterminate. Timing versus settlement, the hybrid reading of post-release/pre-settlement approval, and alert-versus-proof monitoring are well reasoned.

The final answer is not nearly flawless. Section 3 treats AP-208’s 8-month vendor as an ED-1 breach, but 8 months exceeds the 180-day tenure condition; the code’s 240-day test correctly does not flag it, so the rationale and the checker contradict each other. The checker also labels manager timing as post-release hybrid whenever approval is after prepare and before settlement, so AP-203 and AP-206—both approved before release—are mislabeled, contrary to the prose. The EventLog fallback reads `case` instead of `concept:name`. AP-203 is said to have all seven ED-1 conditions evidenced while L3 level is still listed as missing, and AP-209’s Low confidence conflicts with the stated definition of confidence.
