The answer is a rigorous, largely accurate audit: POWL remains authoritative, loop and partial-order semantics are right, the N-shaped dependency is correctly identified, both pipelines are specified without inventing a POWL-to-BPMN API, and the bounded explorer’s exhaustiveness claim holds for its declared domain. The visible-language count of 45 is correct, witnesses match the poset, tau is hidden only when the label is `None`, and completion is exact marking equality rather than coverability or deadlock. Limitations are stated instead of being papered over with an unrun conversion.

The preservation table is still too confident. Petri→BPMN is marked `expected preserved` for the partial-order invariants (both assessment/evidence orders, legal review before evidence, legal/verification overlap, and the release join) even though the same document says that conversion can change acceptance, gateways, and tokens. Those cells should be `ambiguous` until a run; `expected preserved` is defensible for POWL→Petri, not for this BPMN step. Negative-witness cells also mix forbidden words with converter meta-failures, so the column is not one kind of evidence.

The validator does not fully implement the checks it treats as decisive. Reachability and playout are present but default off, including after a soundness pass. Enabled transitions at the final marking are recorded and listed as a rejection condition, yet they do not fail `equal_under_bound`. True-concurrency collapse into an exclusive choice of the same interleavings is a stated rejection criterion and is not detected by words or footprints; that limit is admitted later, but it is not enforced. “Skip review” in the visual section can be read as a new task, which would change the visible language the rest of the audit is trying to protect.
