The normative reading is strong and mostly well calibrated. Forbidden, mandatory, typical, and unspecified behavior are separated correctly; the four relations that must not be treated as violations merely for being unlisted (`Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review`–Customer, Package–Delivery `object_codeath`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`) are kept in the unregulated bucket; N3, T6, and E10 are tied to counts; and the directly-follows versus “eventually validate” mistake on N1 is explained clearly, including cross-family cases such as E5 holding at 200 while N4 is only 198 of 200.

Deductions are still required.

The semantic sketch does not implement the essay’s own distinctions. `semantic_class_for` treats any positive mandatory count, including 98 or 198, as “observed,” so the partial gaps that dominate the analysis never become a class. For forbidden rules, a missing count (`None`) is falsy and is labeled `forbidden_absent_ok`, which treats extraction failure as evidence that the prohibition was respected. The layer also never emits data-quality, abstraction-mismatch, or insufficient-evidence classes, and it only re-labels four hardcoded open-world edges rather than systematically reinterpreting PM4Py insertion diagnostics. Raw diagnostics are preserved, and the six required calls plus external status metadata are present, but the classifier would mis-bin the highest-stakes cases.

Severity is slightly off in a few rows. The suspected E10 join is ranked and scored above the in-scope N4 gap (198 of 200 externally shipped packages), even though the directly-follows warning in the source is attached to N1, not N4. E8 is likewise placed above N4 although finance already describes a legitimate unapplied-cash case and the defect is missing labeling. The four write-offs, which N5 explicitly allows, are Low-medium rather than benign. Section 8 then lumps N1, N4, E2, E6, and E8 under scope, cardinality, and abstraction together, although E6 is a representation question about carrier attributes and E2 is a weight-versus-event question.

Several norm-gap statuses are hedged (“mandatory or unspecified,” “forbidden with an exception,” “typical or unspecified”) instead of choosing one status. One OTG explanation suggests carrier attributes could themselves account for a Package–Delivery interaction edge. The write-up is also far past the 1,700–2,600-word target, excluding code: the same findings are restated in the table, the model-family discussion, and the four buckets.

Counts, norm IDs, open-world handling, and the containment-versus-rare-benign split are otherwise reliable. The score stays high because the audit reasoning is right; it is not higher because the required classifier contradicts that reasoning on partial obligations and unknown forbidden counts, and because calibration and length are imperfect.
