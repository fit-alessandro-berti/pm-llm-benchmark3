7.5

The reconstruction is quantitatively strong and mostly internally consistent, but it is not nearly flawless. Several claims are looser than the evidence, the sensitivity design is thinner than it looks, and a few methodological boundaries the task explicitly requires are only partly respected.

The case table, feasible windows, and station aggregates largely check out. For the missing starts, the nominal no-overlap windows are right: P05 on C1 is 09:45–10:15, P07 on I1 is 09:25–10:00, P11 on C2 is 12:00–12:25, and P15 on I1 is 11:40–12:15. The reported wait and service ranges match those windows, including central starts at 09:30, 11:45, 09:45, and 12:00. Completed sojourns, the 146 / 145 summary, subperiod counts and rates, end-of-window state (S1 queue 1 and WIP 2; S2 queue 4 and WIP 6), and the flow identities 22−20=2 and 20−14=6 are correct. Mean WIP of 5.79 and 3.72 is the in-window area of those sojourns over 360 minutes and is properly scenario-invariant. Busy time, 74–84% and 73–81% utilization, and the resource split, including C2 on 345 minutes, follow from the same service totals. The stated peaks, queue 7 and WIP 8 at S1 and queue 5 and WIP 7 at S2, agree with a completions-before-entries tie break. The Little’s Law arithmetic on 10:25–12:20 is also right: seven starts, mean wait 85.7 minutes, λW≈5.22 against a mean queue of about 5.87.

The bottleneck reading is the right one. Early S1 inflow of 6.5/h against 3.0/h completions builds WIP; 10:00–11:10 is balanced; after 11:10 S1 drains while S2 takes 3.18/h and completes 2.47/h. Longer S2 service and the C2 gap are correctly treated as amplifiers, and whole-window utilization is correctly rejected as a static bottleneck test. Open cases are kept in WIP and busy time, elapsed service is not called final service, and censored waits are not dropped from the narrative.

The defects are still material. “Monotone growth” of completed sojourn is false: the series falls from P08 at 180 to P09 at 155. The early claim that the 6.5 versus 3.0 gap is “exactly” the queue trajectory conflates queue change with WIP change; that gap fixes WIP, which goes from 0 to 7, while the queue ends at 6. The S2 “in-window” service mean of 32.2–35.6 mixes completed services with P17’s 5 elapsed minutes and P15’s 30, so it is not a service-time estimate; fully observed completed S2 work averages about 40. Section 4 still puts the implausible zero-service pessimistic end into the headline range.

“Central” is not an interior imputation for either missing S2 start. Both sit on the earliest feasible point, so central and optimistic S2 queue, service, and utilization are identical, and the three-scenario table overstates how much of S2 was probed. The pessimistic end is a bound, as labeled, but it is a weak plausibility check. The feasibility windows are also tighter than the prompt allows. They treat nominal resource precedence as exact, without widening for ±2.5-minute rounding or for the undocumented assistance the prompt explicitly allows. Those choices move P05’s and P11’s maximum service, not just their point imputations. Rounding is discussed only qualitatively; independent ±5 minutes on chained services is infeasible, and that joint constraint is never used to bound utilization.

A2’s verbal order, arrivals then starts, does not match the code’s departures-before-arrivals sort. Post-event integrals are unaffected, but the write-up and the implementation are not the same convention. The outage length of 15 minutes is assumed without discussing “through 11:10.” PM4Py is scoped to fully observed completed cases, which is appropriate, but `interval_df` is formatted and then unused, the Little’s Law and subperiod results are not produced by the code, and the `insert_case_*` calls label rate parameters as interarrival times. That case-level block does not audit the station-level numbers.

Stable under the stated imputations: the early-S1 to late-S2 shift, end-of-window WIP, scenario-invariant total WIP, and C2’s nominal consistency with the outage. Not stable, and not fully delimited: utilization ranking, S2 mean service, and any bound that depends on forbidding overlap or ignoring rounding.
