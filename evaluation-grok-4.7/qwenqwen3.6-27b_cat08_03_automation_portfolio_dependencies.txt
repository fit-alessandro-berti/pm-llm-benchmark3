The final recommendation correctly refuses a frequency-first start and is grounded in the entity-mismatch critical path, analyst overload, and the need for a control module. The required sections, matrix columns, and three portfolios are present, and several dependency points (A5 before normalization, specialist capacity, external valuers, audit trail, no A11) are real.

It is not reliable enough to score higher. The year-one rollout deploys about seven modules (A5, A9, A12, A4, A6, A8, plus A7 or A3), breaking the four-module budget, while section 7 recommends a different set, A4, A6, A8, A12. That set omits A5 even though the dependency map says A5 must precede A6, and it drops collateral triage without accounting for the 58-hour external wait or the equipment-finance variant. Phase 1 also puts contract generation ahead of entity-mismatch resolution despite the stated prerequisite.

Impact claims are over-precise and over-strong: invented reductions such as 10–15%, 25–35%, 52%→35%, and 5.8%→<1%, plus “cutting” the full 1.5h and 44h waits and “collapsing” the 31h mismatch wait, ignore A4’s limited coverage, residual customer delay, and the weekly committee cadence. The compliance portfolio is credited with fixing four-eyes even though none of its modules governs credit-analysis versus packaging assignment. The quick-win portfolio includes A9 but is then said to have minimal control effect. “Preserving concurrency” is not an effect of A4/A6/A7/A8. Back-office slack after 15:00, the KYC–mismatch return loop, and exposure-segment differences are largely unused.
