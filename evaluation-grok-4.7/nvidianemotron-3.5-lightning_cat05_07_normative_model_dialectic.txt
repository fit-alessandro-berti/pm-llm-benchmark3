The final opinion is organized and gets several easy calls right (SUP-701 compliant, SUP-703/705/708/714 noncompliant, SUP-707 a real model gap, SUP-704 inside the FD-7 window). It is not reliable on the points the task makes decisive: POWL semantics, authority, and the revised model.

The defense brief misstates the given model. In M0, `checks` has no edge between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review, so either order already conforms. Citing SUP-708 and SUP-713 as evidence that concurrency forbids sequencing confuses an activation-before-sanctions breach and a renamed activity with an ordering restriction. The defense also never really concedes SUP-708, the EUR 180,000 ineligible auto-approval, segregation of duties, or the post-expiry cases; several matrix rows just say “N/A.” That fails the requirement to steelman the defense while admitting genuine risk.

Authority is flattened. PD-3 was never incorporated into CS-9, and the compliance observer later denied approving a weakening. SUP-702 is still JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at high confidence. SUP-715 is treated as a spouse prohibition with a waiver regime; the record only establishes a missed disclosure where no rule forbids the relationship itself. SUP-713 is fully COMPLIANT at high confidence even though the label mapping was approved a week after the event.

The proposed POWL does not implement the routes it claims. Quality review still completes before the XOR, so neither the pilot nor FD-7 can put Quality_Review after provisional activation, and sanctions-only rejection cannot skip quality. The emergency path invents `Emergency_Provisional_Activate` instead of `Activate_Supplier`. The same transition objects are reused across submodels. `Request_More_Info` is a disconnected one-child `Operator.LOOP`, which is not valid PM4Py loop arity and is not in `M_revised`. `OperatorPOWL` is imported from `pm4py.objects.powl.operator` rather than `pm4py.objects.powl.obj`, so the block is not executable. The overlay then gives CS-9 an invented end date of 2026-05-31, contradicting the judgment that CS-9 still governs the June–August cases.
