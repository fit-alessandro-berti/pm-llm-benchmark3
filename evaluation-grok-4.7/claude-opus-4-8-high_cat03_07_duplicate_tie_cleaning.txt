The answer covers every required section and makes the right high-level keep/merge calls on the dangerous pairs (keep e05 and e14, do not treat e08/e09 as one dispatch, merge e03/e04 on the reconciliation report, keep pack and payment summaries in the base log). It is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and PM4Py-specific mistakes change the pipeline’s actual behavior.

## Decision tree and case table
The six terminal classes and the bias toward retention are clear, and the required pairs are all present. Two policy breaks matter. The preamble says the first unmet or ambiguous case is quarantined, but e06/e07 and e15 are explicitly “may be one transition or two abstraction levels” and are still given a default retain/merge treatment instead of Q6. e13/e15 is tagged as a summary with `derived_from={e12,e13,e14}`, while e14/e15 is “retain both unchanged.” e15’s status cannot depend on which capture it is paired with. Linking the summary to the authorization as well as both captures is stronger than fact 8 (“generated after payment messages”).

Merging e11/e16 inside the minimum-change pipeline is defensible from fact 6, but only if both authorities survive as separate attributes. The later code does not do that.

## Ordering
Layers 1–4 versus 5–6 are the right split, and the microseconds warning is correct: an offset applied before the partial order becomes a fake directly-follows edge and a fake duration, including between the concurrent picks.

The intended orders then contradict those layers. Layer 3 calls PSP `source_seq` a semantic order and states that e12 < e13 < e14 is trustworthy. The 12:00 schedule then treats e13 and e14 as concurrent and lists a swap as admissible. Those cannot both be true. Independent payments mean there is no cross-object lifecycle edge; they do not cancel a within-source sequence the answer already accepted as semantic.

e08 → e09 → e10 is over-closed. Fact 5 and carrier `source_seq` support only e09 < e10. Fact 4 distinguishes release from acceptance and does not time-order them. “No admissible alternative” turns a lifecycle assumption into a determined physical order, which the constraints forbid. Invoice-before-authorization is the same kind of slip: the text both emits `e11 → e12 → …` and admits the policy is unknown.

`build_order_key` is specified as a lexicographic tuple that includes `source_seq_if_same_source`. A global tuple cannot apply sequence numbers only inside one source. Raw `source_seq` would place ERP 30 against WMS 100 and scanner 552 and would order e03 before e05 by sequence, contradicting the claim that concurrent events differ only in `eid`.

## Counts and aggressive merges
Conservative 17−2=15 and evidence-based 15−2=13 match the stated deletions. The mechanical grouping the answer itself lists (three picks, two packs, two dispatches, two invoices, three captures) removes 7 events and leaves 10, not “roughly 9–10.” e10 is a different label and must survive.

The `have_common_object` argument is the strongest part of the answer: O1 and V1 make the guard true for e13/e14 while M1 and M2 are the distinguishing objects, and the same overlap (O1, P1) fails to separate I1 from I2. That point is then undone in the sensitivity section.

## Implementation
`snapshot_originals`, separate deep copies, a pre-check, verified merges for e03/e04 and e11/e16, sort-then-`ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, and an `ocel_raw` identity assertion match the requested shape. The block would not implement the conservative design.

Standard `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` collapses equal activity and timestamp. Calling it on the conservative copy is not an empty exact-key pass. It is exactly failure mode 8, and it would remove e05 and e14 before the verified merges. The comment that this dataset has no such duplicates is true only for `(source, source_event_key, payload_hash)`, which is not what that function keys on.

PM4Py’s OCEL attributes are `event_id_column` and `object_id_column`. `ocel.event_id` and `ocel.object_id` are wrong, so the helper does not run against a real OCEL. `verified_pair_merge` always rewrites relations and does not implement the stated checks: object-set equality, both payload hashes, both sources, both `source_seq`s. The authority example writes `invoice_amount`, a column this log does not have. `candidate_groups` claims object footprints and returns only event ids. The integrity check covers relation event ids only, not object ids. `build_order_key` is never defined.

## Sensitivity and reversibility
Event-count direction is right; relation counts are not. Raw E2O is 44. Merging e04 and e16 drops five duplicate relations (3+2), so conservative is 39, not “≈ raw.”

The Order-flattened claim that conservative cleaning removes the Pick self-loop is false. After e04 is absorbed, e03 and e05 are still both `Pick Item` on O1; any total order still emits Pick → Pick. That edge is a linearization of legitimate concurrency, unlike e03/e04 on I1, which is a true double observation of one scan. Section 7 calls the whole self-loop a raw artifact and says conservative “keeps legitimate concurrency” without suppressing equal-rank DFG edges. Section 4’s “not asserting a real edge” conflicts with section 3, which correctly says a forced micro-order becomes a DFG edge.

Aggressive merge damage is mis-stated. `ocel_merge_duplicates` remaps and unions relations. M2 and I2 stay attached to the surviving event, so Capture↔Payment does not fall from 2 to 1 by “losing the M2 link,” and Pick↔Item does not lose I2. The real damage is one event meaning two partial payments (and two picks). “Erasing M2” describes drop-without-union, which section 5 mixed together with merge. The raw Pick↔Item baseline of 2 is also off: e03, e04, and e05 contribute three item relations.

OTG is a type graph. Union does not remove the Payment–Invoice or Item–Order type edge, and OTG does not store “how many payments an order touches.” That is instance cardinality. “Evidence-based merge removes rework legitimately” is too strong for pairs the facts leave open.

The provenance field list matches the requested schema, and label/timestamp reversal is real. “Byte-for-byte the raw OCEL” is not. The illustrated record omits `ingested_at` and `source_seq`, the code never stores per-source relation rows or both hashes, and `abstraction_level` / `derived_from` are extra columns the reversal steps do not remove.

## Safeguards
The ten failure modes include the eight required ones, and the distinguishing-object rule, summary tagging, original-timestamp metrics, and authority map are the right controls. They are not what the code does: global `ocel_drop_duplicates` on the conservative copy, unconditional relation union, and a sort key that can still use cross-source sequence numbers.
