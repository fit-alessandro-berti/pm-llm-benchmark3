6.5

The write-up is complete, well labeled, and right on the main distinctions: K-337 changes decision policy, preliminary compliance does not replace final compliance, four-eyes stays intact, queue figures are forecasts, and earlier readiness is not the same thing as an earlier finish. Those strengths do not offset several timing and model errors.

The K-104 partial order is internally inconsistent with the intervention. `Assessment < Escalation <= PreliminaryCompliance` forces, by transitivity, preliminary compliance to follow assessment. The intervention’s whole point is that the S1 packet and provisional C1 work run with identity and document checks, before final assessment. The later narrative describes that early path, so the required relation set and the counterfactual disagree. The table’s “Identity ∥ Document (as in fact)” is also inaccurate: identity ended Mon 12:50 and document work started Mon 21:40, so the factual checks did not overlap.

K-219’s clock arithmetic does not close. A no-rework gain of 6–12 h assumes D2 starts Monday evening and that the saving passes through A2 and M1. Under the answer’s own daytime-shift assumption, D2 may only start Tuesday morning; D2’s 4–8 h standard range against D1’s factual 4 h finish at 13:30 can erase the head start. The factual assessment already waited from 13:30 to 20:00, and M1 signed only at Wed 08:30, so an earlier document finish can sit in those queues. The rework downside of 4–10 h is incompatible with the same answer’s 8–36 h external-response range plus a new document check and assessment.

K-104’s upper saving of about 10 h has the same problem. Final compliance cannot start before assessment ends Tue 19:10. With a daytime C1, the earliest realistic start is Wed morning, against a factual Wed 13:10–17:40 review and 18:40 notify, which supports a gain nearer 5–8 h only if the reserved slot and a 1–3 h pre-read both materialize. C1’s Mon–Tue backlog of 16–20 h makes that pre-read a strong assumption, not a base case.

K-411 misstates the factual assessment slot. Assessment already began Wed 09:30. Parallel checks can bring document completion forward from 19:30, but the overnight boundary and A2’s Wednesday queue make a 3–6 h cycle-time gain the optimistic tail. The Tue 08:00 snapshot is I2 at 2 h and D2 at 4 h, not 2–3 h and 4–5 h. “Quality slightly improved” is not supported by any defect in the factual case.

The 100-case discussion names the right mechanisms—C1 reservations, D1/D2 substitution, extra versus avoided rework, assessment migration, weekends, and false concurrency—but misses the scale. One C1 cannot absorb 100 final reviews, let alone preliminary reads; one D2 cannot absorb 100 handwritten checks. “Lengthen” and “hard bottleneck” are too mild, and calling the D2 routing “more scalable” repeats that miss. The pilot also drops the worst handwriting, which is the population that created the D1 routing in the first place.

Assumption 3 (daytime shifts for every pool) is already in tension with D1’s observed overnight work and with the evening starts used to generate the larger savings. The ranking correctly puts the policy cut last and does not rank by speed alone; the transportability claim is weaker because it treats K-337’s risk as the least portable result and underplays how quickly K-411’s local hours disappear once assessment is binding.
