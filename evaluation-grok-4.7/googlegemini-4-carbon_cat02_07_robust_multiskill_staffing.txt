6.4

The write-up is complete, operationally specific, and often insightful, but it is not internally consistent. The capacity finding and the reported policy results cannot both be true, and the recommendation’s main elapsed-time claim contradicts the earlier critical-path arithmetic. Those are central failures, not presentation nits.

The service-time mixture means are correct (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74). Shift nets (2,490 regular minutes; 30-minute breaks; Farah 240; evening Emil-only) and the U1/U2/U4/U5 scenario mechanics are mostly right. The evening mechanical hole is real: Emil has no Mechanical skill, Deepa ends at 18:00 plus at most 30 minutes of overtime, and a P1 mechanical arrival after that cannot meet 120 minutes. Morning software before Emil, Chen’s absence under strict 1.0× rules, Ben’s uselessness on an E/ES surge, the 1.35–1.40× pooling tax, lunch-time Validation, and day-level rather than case-level piloting are all sound.

The offered-load arithmetic is not stable. Block mechanical/electrical/software minutes imply about 18.09, 24.30, and 12.85 tasks. The daily text uses 18.19, 24.51, and 13.38. Block software sums to about 495 minutes; the daily text says 515.1. The 18:00–20:00 mechanical cell is 30.2 minutes; \((0.25+0.10)\times 3\times 26.30=27.6\). U1’s narrative split (18/51/31 and 23/50/27) does not match proportional renormalization (about 18/50.1/31.9 and 23/49.8/27.2), although the code does renormalize correctly. The structural deficit survives these errors: even corrected 1.0× work is still above 2,490 minutes, and multipliers, setup, and handover make it worse. That conclusion is then mis-scaled. Weighted 08:00–16:00 load over the block table is about 2,137/2,160 ≈ 0.99 before speed penalties, so “88–94% daytime utilization” is too low. The block ratios are also skill-blind. At 08:00–10:00 the 0.93 ratio hides Chen facing roughly 126 electrical plus 67 software base minutes in 120 minutes, with Emil off shift.

Section 1’s parallel-versus-sequential comparison is coherent: \(\mathbb{E}[\max(T_M,T_E)]\approx 36.5\) is a plausible approximation, and Deepa sequential at \(1.25\times 26.30+5+32.82\approx 70.7\) is slower in elapsed time by about 19 minutes while using one resource. Section 5 then says sequential saves 20 minutes of coordination and handover at only a 6.5–8.2 minute speed cost. That 6.5–8.2 figure is only Deepa’s \(0.25\times 26.3\) multiplier wedge. It omits the second branch moving onto the critical path. The same document therefore treats sequential execution as both substantially slower and essentially free.

Section 4 is the largest break. It presents 25-seed Monte Carlo ranges, while Section 8 runs 15 seeds and never shows output. More importantly, the ranges contradict Section 2. About 2,750 base minutes versus 2,490 staff minutes, or 2,670 with full overtime, means material end-of-day backlog is mandatory once multipliers are included. The code imputes unfinished cycle time as \((750-t_{\text{arrival}})+690+45\). Those cases miss P1 and P2 and contribute hundreds of minutes of delay. A base mean delay of 34–44 minutes, P1 SLA of 92–96%, and maximum utilization of 86–89% cannot occur together under that model. Expected P1 volume is only about 5.4 cases, and the evening mechanical gap alone makes a mid-90s full-day P1 SLA an upper-edge story, not a demonstrated result. Quantitative claims such as “~280 minutes recovered,” “55–65% fewer preemptions,” and the whole policy-by-scenario grid are unsupported precision.

The simulator encodes the tables, breaks, overtime cap, single 10% preemption penalty, U5 outage, and the requested PM4Py calls, but it does not implement the stated policies. `locked_res` is unused, so “sequential on Deepa/Chen/Emil” is only an ordering, not retention. Handover is charged for a chosen resource, `prev_resources` is then cleared, and the resource is not held; later assignment can avoid the delay that was just purchased. Handover also overlaps queue wait once wait already exceeds 7 or 12 minutes. P1 parallelization in code requires one idle resource, not two Tier-1/2 resources. `surge_mode` is hard-wired to `P3_Hybrid`, and any absence turns the whole day into surge mode. The prose delay is non-service wait; the code subtracts expected 1.0× service, and for ME/ES it subtracts the max of the two means even when the policy ran them sequentially, so the second branch is counted as delay. Section 7 correctly warns that preempted intervals pollute service time; the code then averages `segment_type==COMPLETE`, which keeps the short resumed fragment.

Assumptions A1–A5, both objective functions, the four dispatch rules, the monitoring schema, and the pilot guardrails are stronger than the numbers attached to them. The robust qualitative ranking — strict 1.0× queues strand morning software and idle Ben; unrestricted pooling burns 1.35–1.40× capacity; controlled overflow is the relevant tradeoff; no dispatch rule creates an evening mechanical resource — follows from the roster and the shocks. It does not follow from the Section 4 table.
