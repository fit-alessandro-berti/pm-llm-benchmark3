7.5

The final answer is a strong causal-policy memo, but it is not nearly flawless. Estimands, identification, mechanisms, and the rollout choice are the best parts; the quantitative signs as displayed and the required Python script are the main failures.

What holds up: availability is separated from fast-path intensity, endogenous selection and co-occurring shocks are kept in view, and median versus p90 are never collapsed into one cycle-time effect. The estimand table covers ITT, dose, tail, rework, escalation, CSAT, dynamics, and transport to Delta/Epsilon, with interference and treatment-version issues stated. CITS as the primary design, with synthetic control and heterogeneity-robust staggered DiD as alternatives, fits a 5-branch, 8-month panel, and the rejection of naïve TWFE is explicit. Mechanism and threat tables are specific, and the claim ladder correctly refuses “automation reduces cycle time by X%.” Choosing embedded evaluation, starting with Delta and holding Epsilon, plus case-level logs, fraud-desk waits, guardrails, and a scale-up rule, matches the evidence. Hand-calculated magnitudes that can be checked (Alpha median change of about 0.4 h, p90 about +9.7 h, control median change about +5.2 h, relative gap about 4.5 h) are arithmetically sound.

What does not: negative signs are missing throughout the prose and result tables (`0.4`, `6.5`, `4.5`, `6h to 0h`, “Alpha 2” FTE, “adopter control”), while plus signs remain. As printed, Gamma’s improvement and the DIDs can be read with the wrong sign even though later qualitative sentences say “less deterioration.” That is a serious quantitative-discipline failure in the sections that carry the result. The script also does not run: the sparkline character set is an empty string and will raise on the first non-constant series, and `(Period - Period).n` fails where pandas returns an int. Those crashes occur before either clustered model. The code then fits the TWFE specification the identification section warns against, only as a labeled description. Section 8 never states a minimum pre-period length. Secondary nits: Beta’s pre-period fast-path share is 6%, not “about 4%”; the intensity split is confounded and not within-branch; branch averages are unweighted; escalation confidence (45) is a bit high given a relative gap of only about 1 pp and the March fraud desk.

Substance is well above a generic DiD write-up. Broken reproducibility and unsigned result tables keep it well short of excellent.
