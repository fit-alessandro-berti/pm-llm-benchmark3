5.5

The write-up has the right skeleton: parallel join as a maximum, calendar pauses, FIFO versus risk-priority, excursion-driven dependence, and overlapping path bands rather than one summed mean. That structure is undermined by a load-bearing clock error and by several places where the narrative range does not follow from the answer’s own premises.

The incubation window is internally impossible. Friday 14:00 plus 46 h is Sunday 12:00, and plus 54 h is Sunday 20:00. The ledger and §3 instead use Sunday 12:00–Monday 20:00, a 32-hour span for an 8-hour interval. Primary readiness therefore always falls on Sunday afternoon or evening. Absent on-call readout, the microbiologist starts Monday 08:00 and finishes about 08:30–09:30. There is no Monday-evening readiness and no Tuesday-morning primary readout. S3 (probability about 0.04–0.10, dominant clock “micro staffing,” terminal time Tuesday–Wednesday) is an artifact of that extra day. Even inside the stated window, a uniform draw is ready before Monday 08:00 more than half the time, so median readout is Monday morning, not a Monday 14:00–18:30 join.

That error drives the smooth-path story. After the weekend gate, chemistry is the branch that actually moves the maximum: risk-priority can finish mid-Monday morning after a short Friday start, while FIFO (stated as about 55%) often runs into Monday afternoon and sometimes Tuesday. Microbiology staffing sets a Monday-morning floor; it does not usually remain the long pole through Monday evening. On-call authorization (0.25–0.45) barely moves the smooth join, because chemistry is closed Saturday and Sunday and QA has no Sunday hours. A Sunday readout cannot be used until chemistry is also usable. Ranking “technical-ready time plus staffing cutoffs” first treats a nearly non-informative Sunday window as the main uncertainty. The central claim of Monday 18:00–Tuesday 14:00 is correspondingly late, and “compress toward S1/S3 (Mon night–Tue)” in §6 preserves the same bad slice.

Other timing choices are softer than the arithmetic can support. B-417 cannot finish chemistry on Friday under the answer’s own priority rule: the early analyst takes the urgent sample, the second analyst is free only around 16:42–17:30, and minimum service is 1.5 h against at most about 1.3 h before 18:00. A priority rerun after a Monday-morning result can still finish Monday and reach QA the same day; S2’s earliest terminal time of Tuesday ~12:00 drops that path. The five-batch QA queue is never run through the remaining Friday 15:30–20:00 reviewer-hours plus Saturday 09:00–13:00, so the Monday “0–6 h, sometimes 8–12 h” wait is asserted rather than tied to the given 0.35/h arrivals, two Monday reviewers, or weekend depletion. S6’s tail to 1–5 October outruns a 3–7 calendar-day lab investigation unless extra, unnamed delays are stacked. Contamination “indication” is also treated as usually being a confirmed critical rejection; the prompt only forces a 20–30 h confirmatory incubation.

Dependence is the strongest quantitative piece. The constructed conditional rerun and contamination bands, mixed at an excursion probability near 0.3, land near the stated marginals, and a confirmed excursion correctly raises investigation probability to 0.30–0.55 even when both assays look normal. Scenario overlap is acknowledged, though not for S1 versus S3 or for queries stacked on otherwise smooth tests. Hour-to-clock conversions of the reported ranges are arithmetically consistent with those ranges. The forecast is still a coherent scenario envelope built on an incubation endpoint that is off by a day, a spurious Tuesday micro path, and a dominant clock that does not match either the corrected calendar or the answer’s own uniform window.
