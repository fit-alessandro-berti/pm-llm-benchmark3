The audit is complete, well structured, and right on the distinctions that matter: post-cutoff leakage versus a legal-but-future appeal path, the tied `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` ordering failure, point-in-time defects that are not obvious full-trace fields, and the split among predictive validity, causation, actionability, and fairness. Suffix-invariance tests, `available_at` versus event time, partial orders, and retraining rather than explanation masking are all handled correctly.

The score is held down by a handful of concrete slips. `total_case_duration_hours` is treated as the settlement-breach label itself, and the remediation says to use closure duration to construct that label. Closure time and time-to-settlement can diverge once appeals and post-decision closure lag exist, so that recommendation mis-specifies the target even though removing the feature is correct. Severity is also inconsistent: `appeal_seen_anywhere` is critical, while the equivalent future pattern `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is only high, with the same gap between total document requests and `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`.

In the revised explanations, evidentiary standards drift. Broker channel is listed as a credible driver although its as-of storage is undocumented, while initial reserve is withheld for the same reason. Queue length is likewise presented as a current driver even though the stated ±5-minute join can see past `t0`. Directions are taken from a model whose attributions are contaminated by leaked features; the recompute status and caveats limit the damage, but those signs are not yet a repaired explanation. The 1.2-hour elapsed time is also narrated as meaningful slack against a 10-day settlement clock, which over-reads a negligible share of the budget. The extra “fundamentally inadmissible” class then blurs its own boundary by allowing repaired prefix counterparts.

None of these errors overturn the audit. Coverage of the required items is essentially complete, and the process-state, conformance, and fairness reasoning is credible.
