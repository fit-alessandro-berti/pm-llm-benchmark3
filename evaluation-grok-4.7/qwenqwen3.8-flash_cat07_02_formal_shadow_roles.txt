The role model is largely well supported: all 14 resources are placed, complexity and substitution are used, shadow roles are treated as useful but risky, and the required sections and decision cautions are present. Most cited counts, medians, similarities, and episode facts match the evidence.

The score drops for concrete errors. Kai and Lina are described as low-complexity only, with effectively no peripheral work. They also have 76 and 70 medium-complexity cases, plus Diagnose and Negotiate events. Hana’s Close activity is labeled core even though Negotiate and Quality Check are more frequent and Close is not her authority activity. Several non-trivial peripheral counts are omitted, including Erik’s Quality Check and Reopen work.

The PM4Py section would not carry out the analysis it describes. `format_dataframe` and `discover_organizational_roles` are called with parameters those functions do not take, and role output is not handled as the usual list of role objects with originators. The similarity result is treated as pair rows, so the risk-gap check never runs on a normal resource-by-resource matrix. Graph edges are read without edge data, so handover weights collapse to 1. `get_service_time` is reshaped as if it were a two-column table rather than a case-to-time mapping, so the substitution-duration comparison breaks. Shadow labels are then hardcoded to named resources, and “critical” is defined with unsupported 0.6 and 0.8 score cutoffs instead of the given complexity categories.
