The answer covers every required section and is often anchored in the case data (waits, FTE pools, the 82% critical-path rate, four-eyes, provisional signing, contract-term loops). The case against automating registration first is the strongest part: short service time, a 1.5h wait, post-15:00 slack, and the risk of pushing work into the 3-specialist and analyst queues. That is not enough to hold the recommendation together.

The first-year cap is four modules. Portfolio 2 plus A9 is five, and the rollout then deploys A2, A5, A6, A4, A9, A1, A10, and A8 (with A3/A11 still open). That abandons the constraint the portfolio section is supposed to respect. Phase 1 (A2 and A5) also leaves the required control-strengthening module until Phase 2.

Section 4 contradicts Section 3. Portfolio 1 includes A5, yet its effects write-up says the missing-document loop is untouched. Portfolio 2 (A2, A4, A5, A6) is credited with signing gates and high control strength it does not contain. Portfolio 3 is credited with removing four-eyes breaches and provisional signing, although none of A2, A3, A9, or A12 enforces segregation of duties or a KYC-before-signature gate. Four-eyes is later attached to A8, which is outside every named portfolio.

Process claims overreach the artifacts. A5 is treated as cutting the 18.0h wait before financial-document review; that wait is not the 8.0h wait before the request activity, and customer delay still dominates. A4’s “limited coverage” is incompatible with a gate of 31.0h to under 4.0h. A6’s full 6,500 hours are treated as freed despite medium feasibility, a 16% rework rate, and weak PDFs. The Phase 4 gate of committee-pack wait under 2.0h cannot survive a 58.0h external valuation that packaging must wait for, especially on 71% of equipment-finance cases. No portfolio includes valuation triage, and the weekly high-exposure committee cadence is never treated as a cycle-time floor. The dependency sketch also sends entity mismatch into credit analysis, while the directly-follows graph mostly loops it back to KYC (1,160) rather than onward to contract preparation (380).

The matrix is A1–A12 list order, not a ranking. Concurrency of KYC and document review, legal ops’ month-end spike, relationship-manager resistance to A5 friction, and residual judgment work on high-exposure credit analysis are thin or absent. Numeric gates (98% accuracy, 92% extraction, 45% cycle-time cut, 0% breaches) are presented as criteria without a basis in the mining evidence.

Directionally right on registration versus mismatch and normalization; internally inconsistent, over-precise, and over the budget once the recommendation is made concrete.
