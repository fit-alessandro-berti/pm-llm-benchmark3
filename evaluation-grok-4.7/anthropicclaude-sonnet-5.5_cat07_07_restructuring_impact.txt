7.5

The answer covers every required section and is quantitatively serious, but it is not nearly flawless. The lead claim, the queueing-to-forecast link, several internal thresholds, and the PM4Py sketch all contain concrete errors.

The opening audit misstates the routing table. Total handovers do rise from 343 to 359 per 100 cases, and the weighted median-wait index does rise from about 588 to about 722. The sentence “Only cross-team handovers fall” is false: local intake to local caseworker also falls, from 88 to 83. “3641” and “912” are unreadable stand-ins for 36 to 41 and 9 to 12, and the supervisor factor is printed as “1.17 / 0.67  1.75,” with the operator missing. Those are defects in the headline arithmetic, not cosmetic typos.

The 23% figure is then treated as if handover waiting time itself rises by 23%. It is a sum of frequency times median, medians do not add, and most cases do not traverse every edge. That overclaim is repeated in section 4. The M/M/3 calculations are otherwise accurate (about 1.5 service times at 84% pooled utilization, about 3.5 at 92%, about 6 at 95%). They conflict with the central scenario, which still puts technical median wait at 4.0 hours at 85–90% pooled utilization. At that load, the same model says pooling should shorten mean technical wait relative to today’s separate queues, unless service time itself inflates. That bridge is never made, yet the scenario still quotes multi-day P90 shifts of +10–15% and +30–40% from an index change of roughly 1.3 hours per case.

Section 3 and section 5 disagree on the same guardrail: pool P90 wait is “> 1.5× baseline” as a leading warning and “> 2× baseline” as a rollback trigger. Synthetic control is proposed with three teams and is not identified. The 20–30% high-complexity share is assumed even though the log summary gives a 36% technical-referral rate, which is a different quantity.

The sketch would not run as written. `graph_metrics` calls `in_degree` on the undirected working-together graph, which NetworkX rejects. `get_variants` is treated as if its values were counts; on an event log they are trace lists, so the coverage and entropy math breaks. The learning metric counts non-pool “Technical Assessment” events, which the restructuring removes by definition, so the series drops at go-live whether or not skills erode. Queue length uses `resample().max()` on event points and misses the level carried across empty bins. The utilization proxy takes the gap until the next event of the same case, so it folds queue wait into busy time. Difference-in-differences calls `summarize` on all cases after the text has correctly warned that closed-case averages are censored. Resource parsing in the network metrics (`split("_")[0]`) does not use the `unit` labels built in `prep`, so shared-service nodes are grouped differently in the two analyses.

What keeps the score here is real substance. Required mechanisms, three scenarios, leading versus lagging measures, tail and fairness risks, an unbundled stepped-wedge pilot, and a conditional “modify and pilot” recommendation are all present. East is treated separately. The weighted baselines (about 7.5 days, P90 about 18.6 days, rework about 9.3%, SLA miss about 7.6%) check out. Legal/Risk overload at roughly 121–124% and the supervisor stability bound near 57% are the right calculations under the stated assumptions. The censoring, mix-shift, and utilization-is-not-efficiency warnings are the correct ones.
