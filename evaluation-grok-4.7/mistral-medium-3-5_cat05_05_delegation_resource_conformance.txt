The write-up has the required headings and gets a few plain segregation cases right, but the authority analysis contradicts itself and misclassifies most of the hard cases. The checking code would not run, and it does not evaluate delegations.

## Rule contradictions
Section 2 treats the 9 August signed PDF as controlling and then uses the 12 August migration row to deny CHG-501. That row was created during the delegation window, not after it. The same section says Asha’s VPN does not count as availability and later says the VPN means she was not absent, then applies that conclusion to CHG-504, which she approved as principal. Sick leave is treated as the principal-absence condition. Those are different facts: Asha’s availability limits Ben’s delegation; Ben’s incapacity and denial go to whether he was the actor.

The business-day note lists 11, 12, and 13 August while calling that two business days. Ending the CHG-505 window on 13 August is a possible reading. It is not applied consistently, and it is stated as if no other reading exists.

## Case errors
CHG-501 is inside D-QM-01 (10–14 August), production-equipment scope, with Asha on leave and a prior signed instrument. That is a valid substitution. The finding calls it an RR1 violation and unauthorized. Clara and Ben are different people, so RR1 holds; the cited defect is authority, and that authority was present.

CHG-502 and CHG-508 are correctly unauthorized same-person approvals, including the `b.koch` / `u_bk` merge. CHG-507 is correctly unauthorized: Eva had no QM delegation on 9 August. The “apparent violation” column still labels several of these RR1.

CHG-503 attributes `svc_qms` to Diego strongly enough to declare an RR2 breach. The terminal was shared, there was no re-authentication, and CCTV is missing, so identity is unresolved. The SoD cell also says Eva verified. Ben’s 12 August approval is not assessed, although D-QM-01 covered it. CHG-506 has the same identity problem in stronger form: Ben denies the 13:00 act, he was on sick leave, and the IP was an office machine. Calling that a failed delegation, rather than unresolved identity, overclaims.

CHG-504 is the principal QM acting inside her own VPN window, or an unknown person using her credentials. It is not a substitution, and confidence should be limited because security cannot exclude sharing.

CHG-509 requires Asha’s absence. Her leave ended 14 August; 15 August is only a weekend. Scope matches training documents, but absence is unproven, so “valid substitution” at high confidence is wrong. Attribution of `svc_qms` to Eva is comparatively strong and is separated from the weaker CHG-503 session only in passing.

CHG-510 misses a direct RR1 breach: Clara drafted and approved. D-IC-04 is invalid, so the emergency path never opens. Ben’s 12 August retrospective review is called valid even though D-QM-01 is limited to production-equipment approval, not a cooling-system QM review. CHG-505 has the same scope error, plus the review falls on 14 August while Ben was on sick leave. No safety rationale is in the log, despite RR4 requiring one. Farid’s own CI-44 window is the part that can be valid, and the matrix instead says the absence condition was “Diego present.” Diego’s IC role from 1 July 2026 is standing authority, not a delegation. Eva’s 12 August change to Quality Engineer is only a caveat, not part of the standing-role history.

Technical four-eyes results are not separated from the control objective. Repeated Clara–Ben handoffs, approver-as-verifier on CHG-509, and verifier-role approval on CHG-507 needed that distinction.

## Collusion, code, and conclusion
Section 4 does give benign and adverse readings and discriminators, and it does not treat the 18-of-23 pattern as proof. “Strongest collusion signal” still couples that pattern to CHG-506 more tightly than the evidence allows. CHG-505’s review during sick leave and the CHG-509 absence gap are not in that analysis.

The code loads the CSV, calls `format_dataframe`, and normalizes aliases, but `filter_four_eyes_principle` is invoked with `activity_1` / `activity_2` and without `keep_violations=True`, so conforming cases would be printed as violations if the call worked at all. The interval index is built from three-element tuples, is not closed over UTC, and is never applied; `delegate_valid` is hardcoded false. `view_*(..., format='dataframe')` is not a real return path. Service-account attribution is correctly left manual. Role windows, business days, scope, absence, and prohibited subdelegation are not checked.

Section 7’s 60/20/20 split is false precision built on those misclassifications. Suspending Ben and Clara treats repeated collaboration and disputed logins as established misconduct. The better priorities are CHG-506’s credential use, CHG-504’s VPN actor, and CHG-503’s unattributed session, followed by scope and absence for the two retrospective reviews and D-QM-02.
