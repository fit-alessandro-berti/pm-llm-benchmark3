The answer is well structured, arithmetically careful, and aligned with the requested headings, but it is not nearly flawless. Several queueing interpretations are softer or less consistent than the clean tables suggest.

The regime rates, pooled contrasts, Fano factors, CVs, and Little’s-law ratios check out. Outage recovery is correctly kept separate from sustainable capacity. The `peak_08_10_pct` conflict with the hourly vectors is real: on 2026-04-21, bins 08:00–09:59 are 11/115 ≈ 9.6%, not 47%, and the other three vectors disagree as well. The main analytical miss is conservation. Arrivals exceed completions by 110 cases (2519 versus 2409) while mean WIP starts at 18 and ends at 19. That cannot be dismissed merely by saying one day’s net flow is not that day’s WIP change. It implies abandonment, a WIP definition that is not system inventory, or broken aggregates, and it directly limits any Little’s-law claim. A second miss is causal separation. Cumulative net flow is still about 17 cases behind by 2026-04-19, so Monday 2026-04-20 WIP of 37 is compatible with residual outage backlog on top of a normal weekday level near 20. Service p90 also jumps to 68–72 on 2026-04-15/16 while `complex_pct` is still 25–27, then jumps again after 2026-04-20. Labeling 2026-04-20–28 a high-confidence mix regime therefore over-attributes one shift to case mix. The earlier 7.0/6.5 FTE bump on 2026-04-16/17, later withdrawn, is reported in the recovery row and then left unused.

Little’s-law judgments are inconsistent. Regime E is “not defensible” because WIP trends, but regime G is “approximate only” even though the same matrix says WIP falls from 43 to 32 and steady state has not been reached. Regime F is absent from the matrix. “Boundary stability” is sometimes just restated flow balance. Hourly Fano factors are treated as evidence against a Poisson process, without separating a deterministic diurnal profile from residual overdispersion; that distinction matters for 2026-04-07 and matters less for the genuinely bursty 2026-04-21 vector. The rolling three-day net flags a long imbalance stretch rather than the three narrative change points, so calling it a change-point heuristic oversells it. Averaged daily medians are also presented as regime service behavior without saying that they are unweighted means of daily medians.

Section 4.3 says the pooled before/after comparison “shows little change” while its own net rates already change sign, from about −0.46/h to +0.30/h. The sharper reversal is the high-mix weekday contrast, and that Simpson-like point should have been stated more tightly. Assumptions are embedded rather than labeled, and stripped comparison symbols leave some matrix entries ambiguous.
