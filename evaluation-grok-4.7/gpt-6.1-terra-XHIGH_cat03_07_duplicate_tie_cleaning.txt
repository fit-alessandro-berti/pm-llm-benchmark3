8.2

The design is conservative, internally consistent, and unusually careful about provenance, partial orders, and PM4Py damage. Counts, pair decisions, and the stated tie orders agree with the rules the answer adopts. Remaining gaps keep it short of a near-flawless reference.

The decision tree separates technical copies, reconciled physical duplicates, derived summaries, harmonization collisions, true concurrency, and unresolved cases, and it does not chain deletion methods. Event decisions match the known facts: merge e03/e04 and e11/e16, keep e05 and e14, qualify e06/e07 and e15, and split the harmonized dispatch label so e08, e09, and e10 stay distinct. Refusing capture-to-summary edges and release-before-acceptance edges is the right bias. The 17/44, 15/39, and conditional 14/37 counts follow from those choices. The 10/28 mechanical outcome is the right connected-component reading of equal activity, equal timestamp, and shared objects, including the required point that O1 and V1 do not protect M1 versus M2. Ordering is a real precedence graph, ingestion time is not business time, original timestamps stay intact, and index offsets are applied only after the order and are rejected as durations. The audit schema and failure list cover every requested field and hazard.

The implementation does not do the attribute merge it claims. `verified_merge` keeps the canonical row and archives conflicts; no per-field authority matrix is applied, so scanner and billing values survive only as nested history. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is confined to e17 and is a deliberate no-op, so the required use is explained rather than shown on a real duplicate group. Within-source sequence is described as conditional on business meaning, then applied unconditionally; that forces e13 before e14 even though the facts only require e12 before e13. Admissible orders are understated: e07 can lead the 10:00 bucket, and e08 can follow e10. Section 7 names the right edges and correctly says unweighted OTG type pairs need not change, but it mostly instructs how to define ET–OT, OC-DFG, and OTG instead of giving concrete weight changes. “Reporting latency disappears” overstates e06/e07: their business timestamps are already equal, and the delay exists only in ingestion time, which the answer elsewhere refuses to treat as process time. Release → Accept is presented as a cleaned-log pattern although, under the answer’s own rules, it is a canonical-ID tie-break.
