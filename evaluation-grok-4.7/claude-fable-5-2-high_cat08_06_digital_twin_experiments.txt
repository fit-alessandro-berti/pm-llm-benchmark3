7.7

The design is strong on boundary, calendars, latent objections, non-stationary warm-up, and the separation of PM4Py from the engine. It is not nearly flawless: several quantitative and coherence errors would bias the comparisons the twin is supposed to support.

The arrival model misses its own target. Seed cases are labeled top-architect with probability 0.31, then each of those seeds adds Poisson(1.5) further top-architect cases. Volume is deflated by `1 + 0.31 × 1.5`, so total demand is right in expectation, but the top-architect share becomes `0.31 × 2.5 / 1.465 ≈ 53%`, not 31%. Fairness, D7, and S2 are then run on the wrong mix.

Common random numbers are oversold. Section 4 says every experiment, including stress cases, shares the same demand paths. S1 changes the Poisson mean, so the arrival path changes, and attribute draws sit on that same stream. Divergent routes also desynchronize the single service stream. Paired differences are only partly paired.

Section 5’s runoff is not what the code does. The text keeps arrivals going for 12 months and right-censors with Kaplan–Meier. The engine stops arrivals at the horizon, drains the queue, and drops unfinished cases in `kpis`. Late cohorts finish in an emptying system, so tails are biased down. The demo warmup (empty start, 60 days) is also far shorter than the stated Complex-variance tail.

D1’s cost is dead code. `presubmission_delay_d` is set and never applied, while Section 7 treats a ≥30% missing-document reduction as a result. That reduction is the scenario input (`missing_doc_multiplier`), so the gate is circular. The printed D1 comparison shows the clerk benefit only.

Timing instrumentation undoes the paper’s best distinction. Applicant waits, statutory notice, and municipal work are all logged as start–complete intervals. `add_timing_columns` then calls the whole interval service. Notice and document response dominate that median. External delay, queue time, join-wait, and effort are not separable in the simulated log.

Capacity arithmetic is directionally right and quantitatively loose. Median × volume is not expected load. For these skews the mean is well above the median (planning is about 1.8× the median under the two-quantile lognormal), so the gap is larger than 2,200 h versus 320 h. Multiplying by medians is a valid “even this already breaks” check only if that is stated. The clerk figure of about 230 h is not derived. `effort_scale` and environmental `availability=0.6` both shrink the same work and can be double-counted. “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday” is implemented as two people on three days (about 1.2 five-day FTE) without noting the ambiguity.

Baseline inspection bundling is described in Section 1 and absent in the baseline engine. D3 is a per-case wait until a district weekday plus an unconditional travel discount, including single-case “batches,” and D1 shares Tuesday with D5. As-is batching is never a parameter, so D3 is credited against a no-batching world.

Smaller gaps: planning rework is in scope and has no code path; appeals are only a TODO; Issue/Reject consume no time; council days default to empty, so the legal calendar story does not run; the 70–85% utilization band and “gain per unit cost” have no cost model and may be jointly unsatisfiable; residual month and weekday factors are hardcoded while Section 3 says they are estimated. PM4Py usage itself (`format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average, token replay) is consistent and appropriately limited. The narrative correctly refuses optimality and does not treat objections as observed events.
