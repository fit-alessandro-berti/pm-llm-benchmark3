The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and stays attached to real segments, paths, and policies, but it is not reliable enough to score higher. Several headline numbers contradict the log, the same intervention is described in incompatible ways in different sections, and the package-level savings are not share-weighted.

The system boundary is usable, yet outbound transport is mislabeled as Scope 3 upstream, and post-delivery damage returns sit outside a boundary that ends at delivery even though later quality claims depend on them. Facility energy is excluded without saying whether dedicated vans are own-fleet energy. The flattened-order caveat is stated and then ignored when per-order savings are treated as exact.

The indicator set covers the requested dimensions, but several targets fight the evidence and each other. An MTO energy cap of ≤16 kWh is the batched CNC_A path, which already has 21% late delivery, so it collides with the on-time guardrail. A rework ceiling under 5% is infeasible if CNC_A itself reworks 9% of MTO cases. Standard-stock scrap under 0.10 kg is looser than the observed 0.05–0.06 kg, so the guardrail would miss deterioration. Packaging “returns” are defined as the internal rework-packaging arc, which the log separates from damage returns. Carbon intensity is an average by mode, while the stated target is air’s share. Part-weight normalization is not an event attribute.

Intervention mechanisms are often directionally right and correctly refuse a blanket air or van ban, but the magnitudes are invented despite the instruction not to do that. S1’s cycle-time penalty of +2 to +6 hours contradicts both the 24-hour air-versus-ground gap and the 17-hour versus 34-hour path medians. S4 then calls that same shift cycle-time neutral, as if priority picking could absorb a full day, and applies the saving to the whole 16% service-contract stock segment rather than the 22% SP1-violation subset. S1 and S4 are the same modal shift counted twice. S2 promises batch sizes of at least 12, shorter waits, negligible extra energy, and a late-rate drop from 18% to under 12%; the batch curve shows energy per item falling from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh only as wait rises from 4 to 38 hours, and no result supports that late-rate cut. S3 treats 1.8 kg as energy-and-scrap saved per shifted order. That figure is average scrap on the 5% CNC_B rework path, not a saving, and CNC_B is the faster machine for the cases that do not rework. S5 claims a 35–55% transport cut in prose and −25% in the table. S6 and S7 again equate packaging-compatibility failures with damage returns, and a stricter inspection gate can lengthen the queue the log already links to missed defects. S8 rightly uses the missing SP2 justification, then assigns an unsupported −15% and a neutral service effect to shared couriers without the true-downtime versus precautionary split.

The effects table therefore looks quantitative while violating the evidence. Share-weighting makes the recommendation’s “30–50% transport” and “20–35% scrap” claims untenable: unjustified stock air is only about 3.5% of orders, whereas the 8% dedicated-van path at about 52 kgCO2e dominates transport. Partial shipments, at roughly 7% of orders with 24% lateness and a second dispatch, are absent, as are the 1.7× late risk from heat-treatment waits and the cancellation arcs.

The six rebound items do cover batching, transport, packaging, rework, obsolescence, and customer gaming, and the CNC_A concentration risk is real. They do not repair the contradictions: section 5 admits that wait caps shrink batches and raise energy, while sections 3, 4, and 6 still claim the ≥12 sweet spot and negligible energy change. Consolidation-induced air re-ships and critical-flag gaming are plausible, but resilience is still scored “low” after removing van and air options that policy R1 needs during disruption.

Governance names SP1, SP2, Q1, segment service, and an exception log, which the prompt requires. It never confronts the log’s low precision on emergency and partial-shipment variants, does not define late delivery from promised hours versus actual timestamps, and sets a sub-2% SP1 violation target without a service stop-rule tied to the 24-hour modal gap. The result is a coherent-looking package whose environmental and service conclusions are over-specified relative to the paths given.
