The three candidates are genuinely different, nested hypotheses, and the observed-language claims check out: all 30 cases fit, the conservative XOR is exactly the seven variants, the balanced XOR-of-partial-orders encodes “S after at least one check completion” plus “X after both,” and the permissive model only additionally frees S. DFG counts, variant arithmetic, D7/S7, and the missing-versus-skipped supplier distinction are handled carefully, and the ledger, audit, matrix, recommendation, and data plan meet the required structure.

Deductions are for real, not cosmetic, slips. Overfitting and underfitting are muddled in the sections that were supposed to analyze them: too-loose behavior is listed as an underfitting risk for the balanced model, and the permissive model’s overgeneralization is misnamed “overfitting the hypothesis space.” “V5 and D5 support completion after either check” overstates V5, which only shows S after C and before E; E-then-S-before-C is an unobserved symmetry inferred from D5 alone. “Approval without either check” does not clearly state that approval after only one check is forbidden. The shared assumptions describe an optional-S branch as common to all three models, but the conservative model only memorizes the observed omissions. Calling alignment fitness and precision “sample-based” is incorrect; those metrics are computed on the full 30-case log, unlike bounded playout. The D5 ledger cell is also garbled. None of these break the models, but they keep the answer short of nearly flawless.
