The design meets the required structure and constraints: hard rules are separated from trade-offs, all seven scoring concerns appear in the policy, every case is assigned with a reason and residual risk, and the baseline comparison stays qualitative rather than claiming a false optimum. Constraint handling is generally sound, including no unsupervised high-priority trainee work, no new work for high-overtime resources, the four-assignment cap, security/approval rules, the quality-batching conditions, and a stated 2/9 documented-training share.

The score is held down by several concrete flaws. The Python-style pseudocode is internally inconsistent: candidates are stored as tuples, then accessed as objects (`x.total`, `chosen.resource`). The urgent weight table can sum above 1 when training is 0.03. The recommended queue is not actually scored, so it is not shown to be the output of the stated algorithm. The C001 override treats the 3.3-hour weak-skill penalty as if it applied equally to supervised execution. In the audit sketch, training share can be inflated by mixing case membership with activity-level decisions, rework and appeal use the first event rather than a later event, workload Gini double-counts repeated rows, and supervisor rows are marked compliant without a qualification check.
