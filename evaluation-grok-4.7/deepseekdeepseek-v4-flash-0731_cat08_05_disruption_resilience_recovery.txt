The final answer meets the required seven-part structure, uses the period metrics and DFG counts accurately in most places, and draws the right core distinction: hospital protection and logged manual checks can be resilience, while skipped authorization, unscanned dispatch, and after-the-fact audit closure are not real-time compliance. The control matrix, evidence fields, and complete/reconstructed/residual-risk split are concrete and usable.

It is not nearly flawless. Cycle-time recovery is underused: hospital median/p90 nearly returns by stabilization, while pharmacy and wholesale tails stay far above baseline, yet the timeline and trade-off mostly cite exception rates and one wholesale p90. Pattern F treats 2.9% dual-control misses as “well below” the 9.5% traceability rate, which is the wrong comparator (baseline dual-control misses were 0.2%) and ignores that most temporary-release cases still dispatch without a scan. Section 3 never classifies deferral or manual-reservation reconciliation, and it does not use the stated temporal-profile deviations (backlog duration beyond baseline p95; highly variable phone-POD delay). “Slightly” and “moderate” soften a roughly twelvefold traceability-exception increase and a pharmacy fill rate of 61%. Yellow/partial-recovery mode is named but not operationalized, despite T3 being a distinct false-recovery phase.
