The answer is a strong, decision-ready causal and process analysis: the Simpson reversal is computed correctly, standardization is explicit and audited, queueing is separated from service, stratification is not treated as identification, and the 50% reassignment is rejected for positivity and transportability reasons. The required hypotheses, pilot-versus-capacity design, and diagnostic code are all present and mostly well specified.

It is not nearly flawless. Orion is repeatedly described as 100% high arrival load (executive assessment, variable table, and the night/load hypothesis). C02 is Orion, Day, and Normal, so the share is 13/14 (92.9%). The Low-stratum note that both Orion cases are high load is therefore false, and it erases the only normal-load Orion case. The claimed within-Nova-Low load contrast of about +0.24 h is also wrong; those five-versus-five cycle times differ by about 0.04 h. The 0.24 h figure matches a confounded all-Nova contrast that was mislabeled as within-stratum.

The negative-control claim is conceptually off. The roughly 0.8 h residual is `cycle − wait − service`, which bundles Register, Classify, and Close, plus any unallocated Request Info time. It is not an isolated pre-assignment duration, so group constancy does not show that a pre-treatment negative control is balanced. The supplied Orion median of 18.15 h is also inconsistent with the case data (17.7 h) and is repeated without reconciliation, although the code would recompute the median.

Those defects do not overturn the verdict, the sign reversal, or the recommendation, but they are repeated factual and identification errors in an otherwise highly disciplined answer.
