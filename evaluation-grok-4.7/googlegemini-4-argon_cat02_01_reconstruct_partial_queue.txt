5.6

The reconstruction is conceptually strong and format-complete, but it is not numerically reliable. Too many quantities labeled exact contradict the case table, the stated formulas, or both.

What holds up is the framework. Waiting, active service, and sojourn are separated. Open and still-waiting cases stay in WIP and busy time instead of being dropped. Missing starts are bounded from resource precedence, queue entry, and nonnegative service rather than replaced by one silent value. Station WIP and completed sojourn are correctly treated as invariant to those missing starts. The C2 outage, the P16/P17 overtake, and I2 sitting idle while P22 waits are real features of the log, and the evidence-versus-hypothesis split on those anomalies is sound. The PM4Py caveat is also right: case-level inter-activity waiting omits the external S1 queue. The qualitative bottleneck story survives: an early S1 arrival surge, then a slower S2 that is tight after late morning, with the 10:55–11:10 outage cascading through P08, P09, P10, and P11.

The arithmetic does not. Fully observed P01–P14 sojourns sum to 2050 minutes, so the mean is 146.4 and the sorted median is 145, not the stated exact mean 150 and median 165. Central S1 wait for the 21 started cases is 1380/21 = 65.7, not 66.7. Central S2 service for 14 completions is 530/14 = 37.9, not 38.9. Central S2 wait for 16 starts is 590/16 = 36.9, not 39.1, and the sorted median is 35, not 45. I2’s fully observed busy time is 290/360 = 80.6%, not 79.2%.

Time averages and peaks are also off, despite being called exact. S1 presence sums to 2085 minutes (mean WIP 5.79); S2 sums to 1340 (mean 3.72). Subperiod means are farther off: S1 is about 4.88, 7.58, and 4.92 over the three 120-minute blocks, not 4.58, 8.08, and 4.67. A cadlag sweep never puts S1 WIP at 9 or S1 queue at 7; the peak is 8 in system and 6 waiting. S2 peaks at 7 between 13:45 and 13:50, not 6. At 12:15 the levels are about WIP1 = 7 and WIP2 = 5; S2 does not pass S1 at 12:35. In-window S2 busy time is about 200 minutes in 10:00–12:00 (available utilization near 89%, not 93%) and 210 minutes in 12:00–14:00 (87.5%, not 93.8%). The Little’s Law cohort P09–P15 has mean wait 80 and mean sojourn 108.6, not 77.1 and 105.7, and full sojourns are not an in-window Little’s Law identity. The claim that unconditional mean sojourn is already >180 is not identified; elapsed time only forces a mean above about 156.

Internal contradictions make the tables hard to use. A4 says every fully observed S1 service is 25 or 30 minutes, but P01 is 20 and section 5 later uses 20–30. That false floor makes the S1 “plausible” bounds too tight. Section 4’s bracket is described as optimistic, central, pessimistic, yet busy time and utilization run from pessimistic to optimistic, while wait runs the other way. P11 is pinned at 12:00 in the text and in scenario 3, then given a plausible wait of 55–60 in the case table. The prose was not produced by the Python: the overlap logic in `evaluate_scenario` would not reproduce these station totals. The “9 of 10” S2 handover claim is also wrong; on the nominal grid, 9 of 12 observed queued handovers are 5 minutes, with exceptions of 0, 10, and the outage gap.

So the operating picture is directionally defensible, and the uncertainty design is the right kind of design. The reported means, medians, peaks, subperiod WIP, and late utilization are not safe to cite without recomputation.
