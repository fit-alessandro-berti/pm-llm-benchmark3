The forecast is complete, balanced, and unusually precise where it counts: the handover arithmetic (67→77 technical, 81→92 approval, 343→359 total) correctly falsifies the “fewer handovers” claim, East is treated differently from North/South, and the recommendation is conditional on tails, fairness, rework, and learning rather than mean cycle time. All seven required sections and the required mechanism, scenario, and risk elements are present.

That quality is undercut by operational and technical errors that would bias the very comparison the answer warns about. The pilot both pools specialists and keeps North/South embedded support “unchanged,” so the control is either contaminated or the pooling mechanism is not actually tested. The within-East low-complexity comparator is confounded with case mix. Rollback triggers stay qualitative (“materially,” “meaningfully”) despite known baselines (rework 8.2–11.5%, SLA miss 6.5–9.8%, p90 16.4–22.8d), and 8–12 weeks is not adjusted for open long cases.

The PM4Py sketch repeats those risks in code. Splitting on event timestamp tears in-flight cases and truncates cycle time. Filtering `team` at event level drops shared-service events from the cases under study. Inter-event gaps are labeled post-handover wait, while `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` results and `key_edges` are unused. Team is taken from the last event, SLA logic is an empty stub whose merge keys omit specialty, and `filter_variants` never appears. Scenarios also stay directional: no ranges anchored to the published medians, tails, or rework rates, and the +15% utilization claim is too loosely pinned on Legal/Risk, who are already at 91–93%.
