3.5

The write-up hits the required section list and repeats much of the brief (case mix, calendars, D1–D10, three stresses), but it is not a usable twin design. Several intervention mechanisms contradict the problem, the estimation plan invents PM4Py APIs, and the skeleton fails the explicit log-handling checklist.

**Process-mining grounding is largely fabricated.** `pm4py.formulate_dataframe`, `event_attribute` / `case_attribute` / `activity_attribute`, `pm4py.discover.dfg`, `pm4py.discover.process_tree(..., activity_generalization=True)`, `pm4py.statistical_analysis.service_time`, `pm4py.distributions.estimate_parameters`, `pm4py.summarize_arrivals`, `pm4py.analyze_rework`, `pm4py.replay.simulate_log`, `pm4py.analyze_bottlenecks`, and `pm4py.util.defaults.default_policies` are not PM4Py. Real entry points (`format_dataframe` with `case_id` / `activity_key` / `timestamp_key`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `discover_process_tree_inductive`, `parse_process_tree`, `get_variants`, `get_case_arrival_average`) are unused. The skeleton renames away `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, and `time:timestamp`, imports a nonexistent CSV `view`, never parses the given tree, and never computes variants, a performance DFG, case-arrival average, or service/waiting columns. `run_experiment` calls `simulate(n_replications=..., warm_up_days=..., run_days=..., seed=...)` while the method signature is `(replications, warm_up, run_duration, seed_offset)`.

**Service versus wait is mishandled.** With completion-only stamps there is no logged service time to subtract from sojourn. The plan still does “arrival-to-completion minus logged service time,” then fits a three-parameter shifted lognormal from median and p90. Applicant delay is named once and absent from the engine. Rework is treated as coded activities (`Planning review rework`) even though reasons are unstructured; the tree only loops completeness and inspection.

**Intervention logic is wrong in ways that would reverse a decision.** D2 is specified as overriding `requires_fire`, not skipping site inspection under value and violation rules (prior violations are not even a state variable). D4 places the temporary inspector in Jan–Feb and Aug–Sep, which are not the stated peaks (Mar–May and Sep–Nov). D5 is modeled as +0.5 legal FTE rather than pulling legal earlier. D8 is a 30% cut in environmental reviews rather than triage. Monday staffing is an extra clerk, not one of five FTE moved to portal support. “40% off-shift” misstates time reserved for non-permit work. Architect bursts never enter the arrival process; district batching is conflated with architect bundling and assigned an invented 0.7 travel factor, while the extra wait for the first case in a bundle disappears from the trade-off table. Objection extensions are given an “empirical” distribution although objections are unobserved; a flat ±30% band does not fix that. A district-wide flag that stretches every open case is stronger than the stated neighborhood effect.

**The arrival estimator double-counts demand.** Daily counts are event counts, not case starts. A mean taken over all months is then multiplied again by the 1.7 seasonal factors. Poisson draws with weekday multipliers omit overdispersion from the top-20 architects (31% of cases). The scope excludes pre-submission behavior while D1 and D7 only make sense as changes to that behavior. Parallel join rules (decision drafting waits on whichever of planning, fire, environment, notice, and inspection apply) are not state.

**Steady-state experiment design fights the non-stationary problem.** A 1000-day warmup is justified as “3× baseline median cycle,” which is false for the ~12–140 day medians cited. One long-run median washes out peak-season SLA failure. There are no common random numbers, no seasonal slices, and no sensitivity on the dominant gaps (timestamp split, latent objections, uncoded rework, incomplete fire/environment rules). Claimed effects (−8d, −12d, −25%, and similar) are pseudo-precision. Baseline targets (median ≤42d, p90 ≤70d, 90% within 45d, district gap ≤5d) contradict the variant table, where large shares already have medians of 48–140 days and p90s of 95–260. Cost/benefit >1.5 has no cost model. Pass rules (KS p>0.05, RMSE<5%, utilization ±10%, correlation ≥0.85) are incompatible with n=31,200, missing starts, HR calendars, and a flattened log that cannot identify cross-case dependence.

The outline shows the right topic list. It does not yet specify a calibratable, comparable, non-stationary twin.
