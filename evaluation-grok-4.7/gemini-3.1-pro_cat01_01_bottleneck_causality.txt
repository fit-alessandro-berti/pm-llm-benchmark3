5.5

The write-up is complete and format-faithful, and it correctly sees that Orion’s higher crude mean (21.96h vs 15.24h) reverses inside every complexity stratum. That is not enough. Confidence 85, the efficiency story, and the claim that a 50% move will backfire are stronger than the 30 completed cases can support, and several mechanism claims are contradicted by those same cases.

What holds up is the shell of the task. All eight sections, the required tables, five hypothesis types, a randomized pilot, a capacity phase, and five uncertainties are present. The stratum means match the aggregates. Calling the crude gap `mainly confounded` is fair. Resource group is described as a bundle of skill, shift, and routing policy, and completion is flagged as selection. The Python embeds the CSV, standardizes on the sample complexity distribution, prints complexity×shift overlap, warns on cells under 3, and attempts group-wise variants and DFGs.

The identification argument does not hold. “True efficiency” and “will likely backfire” treat the stratified means as a causal effect of group. The same text says within-stratum risk may remain. Medium and high Orion cases are all night; medium Nova is mostly day; medium-day and high-day contain no Orion cases. Every complexity×shift cell has fewer than three cases on at least one side, so shift and load stay open after complexity stratification. That positivity failure is never interpreted in the prose.

The within-stratum cycle-time gap is almost entirely `wait_before_review_h`. Review service differs by about 0.1–0.4h (high: roughly 9.17 vs 9.3; medium: 6.0 vs 6.4; low: 3.25 vs 3.64), while the cycle-time gaps are about 2–3.5h and line up with the wait gaps. The answer never makes this comparison, then attributes the gap to certified reviewers, predicts longer service and more rework after reassignment, and recommends cross-training. Rework is also not higher for Orion inside strata (high is 50% in both; medium is 1/6 vs 1/4). A 50% transfer is a shared-queue intervention: Nova’s queue would lengthen and Orion’s would shorten together. Case-level contrasts do not identify that system effect, and interference is never stated.

Two mechanism claims fail simple checks. Within complexity, night waits are not longer than day waits, so the crude night-versus-day gap does not show that night work structurally adds wait. Hypothesis 2’s counterexample is C15, a low-complexity night case with a 3.8h wait. Within medium, the only band where compliance varies, compliance-required cases are slightly faster (about 17.8h vs 18.3h), with lower wait and the same service. “Compliance adds significant wait and service” and the closing claim that night compliance is the primary bottleneck are not supported. Section 4 also says Orion handles 92.9% of night cases and Nova 25%. Those are P(night|group), they sum to more than 100%, and Orion’s share of night cases is 13/17. The day/night and compliance rows give no arithmetic. Their “allowed” cells state causal readings the crude patterns do not license.

The graph and the role table disagree. Queue wait is called a mediator of resource group, but there is no `resource_group → queue_wait` arrow and no `complexity → review_service` arrow, even though service rises from about 3h to 6h to 9h with complexity. `case_completed` is called a collider while the graph has only `cycle_time → case_completed`. `quality_outcome` is unused. `shift → resource_group` is marked as fact, although “Orion covers nights” does not fix that direction versus the routing rule. Total, direct, and mediated estimands are never separated; every estimand cell is only `resource_group → cycle_time`.

Hypothesis 5 reads Nova’s two high-complexity rows as abandonment. That count is what the routing rule predicts. Selecting only Nova’s fast completions would bias Nova’s observed times downward, which does not explain Nova being slower inside strata. The compliance test is labeled front-door analysis, but compliance follows complexity and policy and does not intercept paths from group. The predicted column mostly states the confirming result.

The pilot randomizes low/medium day cases, a different margin from the night and high-complexity work a 50% Orion move would transfer, and calls that a clean group effect. The compliance phase stops on an unmeasured failure rate, not on whether wait actually falls. “Degrade quality” is unsupported: the only appeals are C14 (Orion, high) and C24 (Nova, low).

Smaller defects: `pm4py` is imported outside the fallback, so a missing library blocks the pandas results; the `>` separator is not passed; a missing stratum would be standardized as zero; uncertainties 2 and 3 both ask for timestamps; “significantly higher” has no test. The right headline is the reversal and the refusal to act on the crude mean. The mechanism, the leftover shift confounding, and the system effect of moving half the work are wrong.
