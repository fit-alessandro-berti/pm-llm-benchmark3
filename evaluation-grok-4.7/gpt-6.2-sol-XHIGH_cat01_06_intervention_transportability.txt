The answer is strong and largely compliant: it keeps Site A’s nine-hour drop confounded, refuses blanket parallelization where originals are mandatory, stages a digital no-legal cohort, and builds a real partial order in which Assessment waits on both checks. It is not nearly flawless.

The quantitative section is the main weakness. Eligible share and the weights used to move quantiles do not match: about 30% eligible versus 40% “near the median,” and 45–55% eligible versus a 60% median weight. Those gaps change the reported hour ranges by roughly a fifth to a third. Interval endpoints are also subtracted as if independent, which widens the bottleneck p90 band beyond the paired assumptions. The local-share quantile shortcut is especially fragile because ready digital, no-originals cases are likely already left of the sitewide median, so a population-median shift is not a constant fraction of the case-level gain. The critical-path identity, gain = min(D, max(0, I−R)), is correct and rightly gives about 3.4 h for a ready case and 0 h at the 18 h receipt median, but the “optimistic structural” 4–8 h band exceeds that identity and imports Site A’s confounded queue movement. The mean-improves / p90-worsens case is present, yet one sentence mixes the 30/10 mean illustration with a different p90 calculation.

The PM4Py block has the right trees, POWL partial order, originals XOR, and legal supplement loop, and it states the data, capacity, lock, and duration limits. `sound, _ = pm4py.check_soundness(...)` is not safe: standard `check_soundness` returns a bool, so the script raises before any check is printed. Playout was optional.

Smaller issues: individual randomization in shared pools does not identify a clean “direct” effect; 8–12 weeks is tight for 200 per arm in the proposed intersection; appeal is reached only through rework. The DFG outgoing-register excess, co-intervention split, WIP/lock failure modes, and conditional-go kill criteria are sound.
