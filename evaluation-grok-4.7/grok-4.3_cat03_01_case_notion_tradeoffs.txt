The write-up keeps the required section structure, a usable case-notion definition, a correct account of convergence as same-type event copying, and a defensible rejection of one lead object. Those merits do not survive the factual and conceptual errors that run through the comparison, the distortion list, and the decision criteria.

Divergence is defined as a short lifecycle. In this setting it is the collapse of distinct related objects into repeated activities of one case, such as O1’s two pack and two dispatch events for P1/D1 and P2/D2, or O3’s separate P3/D2 and P4/D3 paths. That inversion makes the matrix’s divergence column unreliable: Order is rated Low where split fulfilment creates the strongest divergence, while Item is rated High on I11’s four-event trace Add Item → Pack Package → Dispatch → Confirm Delivery, which has no repeated activity. The Invoice cell then cites V2’s M2/M3 split, which contradicts the truncation definition just given.

Convergence is repeatedly confused with “this object touches several objects of another type.” Under delivery flattening, e15/e23, e16/e24, and e17/e27 each name one delivery, so convergence and duplication are absent; D2’s three orders distort the Order log, not the Delivery log. The same mistake marks e13 as duplicated for Package, although P3 is its only package, and marks e19, e22, and e25 as duplicated for Invoice, although each names only V2. Item convergence is rated Low despite e16 and e24 each relating to I12, I21, and I31, and e13/e14 relating to two items.

Specific claims are contradicted by the log. No order trace has a directly-follows edge Create Order → Dispatch: Add Item and Pack Package intervene. e19 relates to O2 and O3, not three orders. O3 appears in two deliveries, D2 and D3; e14 is Pack Package for P4, so it cannot make a third delivery. D2’s dispatch-to-confirmation interval is e16 at 11:05 to e24 at 16:00, about 4 hours 55 minutes, not five minutes. Order flattening does not lose e13, e14, or D2; those events are directly related to orders and are copied. “OrderDispatch” and “PackageDispatch” are not activities. The claim that e13, e16, e19, and e24 guarantee convergence for every lead type is false: package, delivery, invoice, and payment flattenings do not duplicate all of them.

Section 3 therefore misses the main order-level trap, a Dispatch → Dispatch directly-follows pair on O1 that looks like rework and is actually P1 then P2. The P3 and V2 examples mislabel which flattening copies the events. The M1 singleton and the I21 set {e07, e13, e16, e24} are sound, but too little of the required distortion analysis is.

The portfolio’s safe KPIs are narrower than the reasoning admits. Create-to-close can be measured per order because e01–e04 and e28–e31 are one-to-one, while intermediate frequencies cannot. V2’s two Record Payment events do answer the instalment question. Delivery and package flattenings are the clean lifecycles for their own questions and are dropped without a valid convergence argument. The artefact rule “edge support exceeds the number of distinct objects” is not an operational test. Sensitivity covers the four requested themes, but deleting every multi-object event would remove O2’s only dispatch, propagation is not tied to concrete O2O paths, and the 30% OC-DFG cutoff is arbitrary.

The code names the requested functions, then flattens Item rather than the recommended Invoice log, calls non-existent `object_type_freq` and `activity_freq` attributes, and still centers `discover_dfg` and `discover_log_skeleton` on Order after rejecting a lead object. `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is included with a comment that the decision section never justifies. The essay is also far below the requested 1,800-word depth, and the missing traces are exactly where the counts go wrong.
