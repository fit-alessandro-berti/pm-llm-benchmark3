4.6

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and reaches a pilot-first conclusion, but it does not hold up as a forecast grounded in the given log, baselines, or PM4Py measures.

The mechanism map treats “nominal handover reduction” as an effect. The management routing table itself raises handover intensity: tech out-and-back moves from 36+31 to 41+36 per 100 cases, approval from 42+39 to 49+43, and legal/risk from 9 to 12, while only cross-team and intake-to-caseworker fall. Summed handover events rise from 343 to 359 per 100 cases. The same map treats a central pool as more resilient to absence, without the concentration failure mode already visible in the 8.9h / 18% rework cross-team path and in near-saturated Legal (93%) and Risk (91%). Learning is absent from the map. Role clarity is conflated with “less specialized” local jobs. Effects are named as slogans rather than as log measures (edge frequency, wait after the edge, rework after the edge, `reassignment_count`, `local_expertise_flag`, stratified cycle time).

The scenarios are not calibrated to the tables. The central case calls a 4–6h median wait a slight uptick, below today’s legal/risk median of 9.6h and management’s own 10.5h estimate. A +33% referral increase onto resources already at 91–93% utilization is a queueing break, not a one-hour slip; the 15% utilization claim is repeated with no capacity check against North 71%, South 68%, and East 82%, or against extra triage and difficult quality-check load that the management handover table omits. The optimistic case sets early warning signs to “None,” so that required field is empty. Team baselines (East 8.6d / 22.8d / 11.5% rework / 9.8% SLA miss versus North 6.8d / 16.4d / 8.2% / 6.5%) never enter the cycle-time or fairness forecasts. The adverse case asserts that the average still improves while the 90th percentile “worsens drastically,” with no distribution.

The indicator table mis-assigns functions. Activity-based resource similarity is a profile-similarity matrix, not utilization. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` is rework, not SLA miss rate. `get_service_time` is service time, not queue wait, and this log has a single `time:timestamp` and no lifecycle, so service/waiting insertion is not directly defined. A performance DFG summarizes edge times; it is not a case-level 90th percentile cycle time. Team handover frequency is observable in days and is labeled lagging. “SLA miss likely increase” takes a direction before measurement. Rework-after-handover, `reassignment_count`, `handover_reason`, `local_expertise_flag`, segment- and team-stratified sojourn, and role drift are missing.

Section 4 lists six one-line risks and drops exception handling (rework activity, absence cover, language or regional accommodation, dropped handovers). Tail delay, complexity, local knowledge, fairness, learning, and accountability are named and not tied to a measure or a baseline.

The pilot keeps North and South unchanged and centralizes only East. That does not create the proposed multi-team technical queue, does not load the shared pool with North and South demand, does not remove a supervisor, and does not stand up the shared approval queue. East is the team already most likely to look better, so an East-only gain cannot identify the North/South expertise loss the forecast is supposed to test. “SLA +10%” does not say whether that is points or relative, against an East baseline of 9.8%. Legal/risk capacity is not a guardrail.

The recommendation correctly withholds full rollout and warns against early averages. It still treats East as a sufficient testbed and does not state the non-inferences that matter: an early East mean is selected on the overloaded team; a short window cannot show skill atrophy; a stable mean is compatible with a worse 90th percentile, more rework on high-complexity and legal paths, and uneven customer-segment delay.

The sketch calls handover, variants, performance DFG, waiting-time insertion, resource similarity, and a complexity filter, then stops. Working-together, subcontracting, organizational role discovery, rework, SLA, and filters on team, `required_specialty`, and `customer_segment` are absent. `new_log` is never formatted. Nothing diffs edge weights, waits, or rework before and after. The similarity result is stored as if it were roles.
