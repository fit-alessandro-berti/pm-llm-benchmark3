5.2

The answer is complete and directionally right — conditional go, no blanket copy, Site A’s −9h is confounded — but its formal model, stratum arithmetic, and pilot math are unreliable.

The transport decision is the right one. Parallel identity and document checks are confined to cases that already have digital documents, originals stay a hard gate, the −9h before/after change is treated as confounded, and bottleneck shift, locks, and rework are named as risks. Format, required columns, node list, and word-count bounds are met.

Those strengths do not survive the details the task asks to be right.

**Stratum error, repeated.** “38%” is the complement of originals required (100 − 62). It is not the digital, non-public, non-high-value intersection. Legal-review cases are a separate 40%. The overlap is unidentified, so eligible volume can be far below 38%. Sections 6, 7, and 9 treat that intersection as 38% and also fold high-complexity (48%) into the legal-review exclusion. The pilot population is therefore unidentified.

**Partial order does not match the prose or the statute.** The written eligibility rule is selective concurrency. The POWL is one order that always inserts Originals Receipt, always runs Legal Review, and always takes Request Supplement. `Request Supplement → Document Check` cycles with `Document Check → Assessment → Legal Review → Request Supplement`. A strict partial order cannot contain that cycle; a supplement path has to be a loop operator with an exit, and legal review has to be a choice. Notify is absent. Assessment waiting on both checks is the one dependency that is represented. Identity concurrent with Originals Receipt — the concurrency Site B’s rules already allow, and the one that can hide a ~3.4h identity service inside an 18h originals delay — is never specified as the physical-case design.

**The PM4Py block would not implement the required model.** It never calls `pm4py.parse_process_tree` or `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`. XOR nodes never get `.children`, so Approve/Reject are not in the trees. `StrictPartialOrder(..., order=...)`, `OperatorPOWL.XOR`, two-argument `Transition`, `workflow_lexical_analysis`, and `simulator.apply(net, variant_parameter=10)` do not match the library’s constructors or signatures. The legal branch is not optional, and the supplement edge is the same illegal cycle. Soundness checks omit markings. The commentary correctly lists data guards, capacity, locks, and durations as outside the net, while “free-choice constructs” misstates how data-dependent routing is encoded.

**Scenarios are not derived from the hours given.** No critical-path or capacity arithmetic ties Site A’s 6h wait change, Site B’s 18h originals delay, service times (3.4h / 8.1h), or pools (4 / 7 / 3 / 2) to the stated percentages. Scenario 1 is both “optimistic transfer” and an illegal start that raises rework. Scenario 2’s −10% to −18% is labeled an overall median while only 38% are eligible; a median does not move by the eligible-share fraction. Scenario 3’s eligible share is “38% + 62%” under a digital-only assumption. Scenario 5 predicts a rework drop to 14–16% and assigns confidence 80, above the narrower digital-only case, even though Site A rework rose from 11.8% to 12.4%. “Starve” is used for legal-queue overload.

**The pilot cannot generate the evidence it names.** About 3,400 cases in six months is ~131 a week, ~1,050 in eight weeks, and ~400 if 38% are digital — not 1,200 eligible completions. A 90-day appeal rate cannot fire an eight-week stop rule. Randomization is described as 50/50 treatment versus control and as two treatment versions. Shared reviewer pools violate individual-level randomization; “cross-trained floating reviewers” and “dedicated pilot FTEs” are different interference designs, and cross-training contradicts separate roles. The same impossible reallocation appears as the assessment-bottleneck mitigation.

**The transport graph mis-wires the confounders.** `API_latency → identity_capacity` treats latency as headcount. `S_legal_mandate → legal_capacity` treats a statutory branch as a staffing selection node. `role_specialization → *_capacity` and `queue_huddles → rework` miss substitutability and the huddle path into waiting and cycle time. The added verification FTEs, a main Site A co-intervention, are not a confounder of `cycle_time`. `originals_required` and `IT_case_lock` never point at `true_overlap`, which is the mechanism they block. Section 3 embeds some co-interventions in other rows and still attributes the identity service-time drop partly to cross-training that already existed before the change. Several “transferable components” restate confounders (extra FTEs, API latency) as if they were the treatment.

The recommendation’s triggers inherit the bad stratum, the 10–18% effect size, and the unobservable appeal rule. Assumption 2’s failure mode (premature document checks) describes the policy the design says it will not run. The register never states the missing overlap between the 38% and 40% shares.

Use the conditional-go direction only. Do not use the eligible share, the effect ranges, the POWL, the code, or the pilot sample size as specified.
