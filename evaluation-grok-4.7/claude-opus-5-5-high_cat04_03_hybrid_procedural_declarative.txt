7.5

The hybrid design is coherent and the T1–T8 matrix is right under the stated rules, including the splits that matter: T4 fails only on resources, T5 fails on the open clarification, T6’s skeleton cannot see R5/R6, T7 is a temporal warning, and T8’s first hard break is Reject after Activate. R5/R6 is treated as a real conflict, Option A is not silently mixed with Option B, and the DECLARE direction table (response vs precedence, alternate vs chain, noncoexistence vs nonsuccession) is accurate. The score is not higher because several encodings and explanations are wrong or over-strong.

The closing constraints are the largest defect. `chainresponse(Release First Payment, Close Onboarding)`, the same rule for Reject, and log-skeleton `directly_follows` on those pairs require the next event to be Close. The backbone only says Close follows rejection or first payment. R10 allows one release, and R11 still requires Suspend after a post-activation bank change even when no later payment exists. `Activate → Release → Change Bank Details → Suspend → Close` meets that reading and is a hard DECLARE and skeleton failure here. Section 4.1 calls that immediacy part of the backbone. It is not. The same text correctly refuses `directly_follows(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` because a clarification may sit in between, so the closing chains are an internal inconsistency, not a harmless projection of the tree.

The log skeleton contradicts its own commentary. Equivalence of escalation and opinion is described as a stricter per-event rule than DECLARE `response`, which one opinion after two escalations would satisfy. `activ_freq` already limits both activities to `{0, 1}`, so that case is impossible inside a skeleton-conformant trace. Equivalence at that cap is only co-occurrence. The cap itself is also an extra norm: R10 bounds Activate, Reject, and Release only, and A14’s arbitrary bounds do not list escalation or opinion.

R2 is a control-flow XOR (exactly one of the two approvals before activation, none on the reject path). The process tree already expresses it. Making the resource/data layer authoritative is justified only as an alignment-free reimplementation, not because the constraint is resource- or data-dependent. The code then drifts from §7.1: `check_approval_cardinality` never returns `not evaluated` when timestamps are missing. `clarification_cycles` is not called from `run_resource_data_layer`, and a second request overwrites the open one instead of matching the i-th cycle. `check_segregation` returns `not evaluated` as soon as any generic account appears, before the intersection test, so a real same-person pair is hidden if `Vendor` is also on one side. `check_opinion_before_activation` returns `not evaluated` if any escalation is at or after activation, and does not check earlier escalations. Joint authorization assumes the roles Operations and Legal with no assumption id.

Smaller presentation issues: the parent T6 row is `policy-dependent` after Option A was selected (the sub-rows repair this), and absent temporal pairs are `not evaluated` while vacuous resource rules are `pass`. `keep_violations` on `filter_four_eyes_principle` is not a stable PM4Py parameter; the usual switch is `positive`.

Z-scores, seconds conversion, zeta bands, and the warning-only reading of R12 are correct. T6’s 17 h / 10 h clocks, T7’s z ≈ 3.67 versus z ≈ −1.94, and the separation of the 24 h emergency deadline from the ±2σ band are correct. Those strengths are why this stays in the mid-7s rather than lower.
