The write-up has the required sections and a usable dependency sketch, but both models are semantically wrong, the witness table does not match either the narrative or the code, and several conclusions contradict the artifacts.

The process tree is not a coherent approximation. `LOOP(sequence(Execute Trial, Inspect Trial), Adjust Parameters)` is the right repeat-until shape, and the initial contain/analyze split plus the risk XOR are recognizable. The rest breaks the specification. `Plan Verification` sits only in the parallel containment branch, so `Execute Trial` does not wait for it and can even precede `Contain Material`. Approvals and supplier consultation are forced to finish before design and the trial, so legal approval–trial overlap is excluded. One silent node is inserted under two parents, so the tree is not a tree, and passing `children=` never sets parent links despite that being required. The fenced expression does not match this code: it duplicates activities, nests `Plan Verification` twice, and gives the loop the wrong shape. Calling this a permissive over-approximation is also inconsistent with the prose, which simultaneously claims excluded interleavings, premature release, and “under-constraint” for behavior the tree actually forbids.

The POWL model misses the point of POWL. A `StrictPartialOrder` executes every node exactly once and must be acyclic. This model puts `Execute Trial → Inspect Trial → Adjust Parameters → Execute Trial` in the order, so it is not a partial order and cannot represent a second iteration. It also makes supervisor, quality, and regulatory review, supplier consultation, and adjustment all mandatory, so low risk, high risk, and “supplier absent” are all inexpressible. The claim that consultation “can be skipped” is false for this API. `OperatorPOWL` and `SilentTransition` are never used, even though XOR and LOOP are the supported way to encode the risk choice, the optional supplier, and the trial loop, and partial-order edges are how the cross-branch joins should have been stated. The later claim that POWL lacks XOR and loop support is therefore false.

The eight-row table fails its own rules. Process-tree and POWL verdicts are blank. T3, T4, and T7 are marked allowed while omitting every approval activity, so they violate release. T6 and T8 are the same trace. T5 does not expose the tree approximation: that loop already refuses an adjustment after the exiting inspection. No witness shows the intended case the tree actually drops, an approval overlapping the trial. The comparison table then disagrees with the models: the tree does enforce the risk XOR; the POWL does not “admit mixes,” it demands all three reviews; release is not both over-constrained and able to fire early; unbounded retries are intended, not an under-constraint.

The self-critique repeats those mistakes. The tree does enforce the risk XOR. A single supplier XOR fires at most once. The residual semantic gap that matters is the one not stated cleanly: the tree never joins plan and design at the trial, and the POWL never becomes a partial order with choice and loop operators.
