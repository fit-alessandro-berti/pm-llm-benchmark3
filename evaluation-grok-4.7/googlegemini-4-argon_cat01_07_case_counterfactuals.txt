6.5

The write-up is structurally complete and often conceptually sharp, but it is not numerically or temporally reliable enough to score higher. Format, constraint handling, and interference reasoning are strong. The case-level clock times contradict the snapshots, the author’s own scenarios, and in one place a single sentence.

The feasibility model correctly separates timing (K-411), routing (K-219), preparatory control-flow (K-104), and decision policy (K-337). Four-eyes, final-versus-preliminary compliance, originals-before-document-check, and the ban on silently dropping K-337’s discrepancy are preserved. The unconditional base relation `Assessment -> Decision` is still false for both high-risk cases and conflicts with the later compliance precedence.

K-104 is the weakest derivation. Tuesday 08:00 shows C1 at about 16 hours and Wednesday 08:00 still at about 8 hours; factually the case was ready Tuesday 21:20 and C1 started only Wednesday 13:10. The base case nevertheless starts final review Tuesday 20:00 or Wednesday 06:00 and invents a Tuesday-morning C1 “gap.” A Wednesday 02:10 completion needs an unobserved night decision by M1. Even the stated worst case (done Wednesday 13:10, −5.5 h) finishes before factual compliance starts, so the range excludes the realistic no-preemption outcome of about zero. Reservation is treated as both held-fixed capacity and free preemption.

K-219’s arithmetic is internally split. The table’s no-rework band is only −11 to −14 hours (Tuesday 19:00–22:00). The derivation then says a day-shift M1 cuts that to about −0.5 hour (Wednesday 08:30). D2 starting Monday 22:00 and finishing Tuesday 04:00–06:00 assumes overnight work. The only observed night document interval is D1’s; D2 is observed in the daytime (Tuesday 15:00–19:30). Citing an A2 queue of 6 hours at Tuesday 08:00 and then starting A2 at 11:00 is also inconsistent with that queue.

K-337 gets the policy point right and then breaks the clock. Factual end is Wednesday 09:10; Monday 09:10 is −48 hours, Friday 17:30 is about −112 hours. The table’s “−92 to −114 hours (Friday 17:30 to Sat/Mon 11:00)” matches neither endpoint. The base-case paragraph says both “−96.0 h, completes Mon 09:10” and “saving exactly 48 hours.” The worst case repeats Monday 09:10 while labeling it −72 hours. “9 days, 22.2 h” for a Mon 11:00 → next Wed 09:10 trace is about 8 days 22 hours. Calling the remaining discrepancy “minor” is not in the trace.

K-411 is the best single-case analysis: earlier task creation is not treated as an earlier D2 start, FIFO and pre-09:35 work are considered, and false concurrency is explicit. The table still omits the derivation’s 0 to +1 hour worst case and sells −18.5 hours, which needs a same-evening M1 sign-off.

Section 4’s nonlinearities are the right ones: C1 reservation starvation, D1/D2 substitution with rework feedback, Friday compliance surge and appeal bounce-back, and assessment-bottleneck migration under batch-logged “concurrency.” The 100-case discussion covers every requested mechanism. Ranking K-337 last and K-411 first is defensible on quality, reversibility, and spillovers rather than cycle time alone. The pilots have eligibility, randomization, path and quality outcomes, spillover measures, timestamp validation, and stopping rules.

Assumption 1 never states the D2 overnight mapping on which K-219’s headline gain depends, and the outcome ranges do not span the best/base/worst text. The synthesis is within length and answers the four questions, but it repeats the optimistic “saves 12 hours” local result that the same answer elsewhere retracts.
