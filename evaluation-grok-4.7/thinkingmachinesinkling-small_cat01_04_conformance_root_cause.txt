6.5

The analytical core is strong: the staged fitness path (32% → 58% → 81% → 89%, then Aug–Sep 66% → 86%) is used correctly, employees are not blamed for log moves, label change, technical events, ordering, and model obsolescence are separated, and the optional sign-off model is flagged as overgeneralizing past the EUR 50,000 rule. The ledger, ranking, counterfactuals, four-phase program, and six governance rules are specific and mostly well calibrated.

That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading. The required code is not executable or logically coherent. The “raw” builder drops `Queue Routed` before comparison, so raw versus normalized cannot show the technical-event effect. CSV separators are mixed (`sep=";"` against comma-separated fields), timestamps include day `00`, and fitness is `1 - mean_cost / 3`, which is not alignment fitness. August–September is never loaded, and fitness and precision are never computed for the candidate net—only promised in comments. `?(...)` is not valid PM4Py process-tree syntax (optional is an XOR with `tau`). The supplied frequency tables are replaced by three capped toy variants.

Other defects: Section 3 is about 315 words against a 180–280 limit, and Section 7 is about 470 against 250–400. The conclusion’s opening treats the Jun–Jul drop to 32% as “data and model changes,” but the sign-off rule is August; that 32% is labels, `Queue Routed`, and ingestion time. “Largely timestamp errors” overclaims an 11-of-70 audit that the ledger itself leaves unresolved. The causal list includes a non-effect (`UI_release → true_process [no direct change]`), a duplicated edge, and `measured_fitness → business_outcomes`, which reverses the actual relationship. Several “weakens” cells are loose (label mapping does not weaken the technical-event hypothesis). The ranking never explicitly splits “cause of the conformance drop” from “remaining operational concern,” despite that being required for the table.
