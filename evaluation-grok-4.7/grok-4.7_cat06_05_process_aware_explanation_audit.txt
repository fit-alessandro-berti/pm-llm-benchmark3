8.2

The audit is complete, well structured, and right on the central traps: label leakage via `total_case_duration_hours`, closure-time and full-trace fields, the extraction-date prior-claim predicate, the nightly document snapshot, mutable `risk_score_current`, and `last_resource_role`. It keeps queue length and elapsed time as real `t0` signals, refuses to treat region or week as causes, and correctly separates the later-legal `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` succession from the norm-violating, unordered `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pair. The four standards and the validation suite, including alignment-cost, DFG, and out-of-log snapshot limits, are credible. Sections 4 and 5 match the required schema and do not recycle leaked futures as reasons.

The main conceptual miss is in the enabled / future-possible / impossible split. Appeal and a second settlement decision are reachable after Partial or Deny, so they are guarded future-possible activities, not impossible in the way a post-closure event is. Binning them with dead transitions undercuts a distinction the audit otherwise handles well. The Case X narrative also puts evidence collection “still ahead,” which erases the fast-track branch. `initial_reserve` and `claimant_channel` are listed as credible drivers while their immutability is still unverified. The residual “other features” mass is never treated as unaudited leakage, and a few claims are a step too strong: duration fully restating the settlement label, week 52 specifically carrying the policy change, prefix control-flow having no variance beyond these three cases, and SIU appearing only after a later fraud assignment.
