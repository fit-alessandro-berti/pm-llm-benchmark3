7.5

The final JSON is a strong, process-aware analysis: states, alternatives, rule-based obligations, queue timing, feed delay, and the C6-A/C6-B split are mostly handled correctly, and missing events are not treated as proof that work did not occur. It is not nearly flawless.

The main problem is that the intervals do not have one stable meaning. Several rank-2 and rank-3 items are events that normally follow the rank-1 completion (closure after review, disposition after assessment, a new evidence request after review). Their probabilities are too high if they are the next log line, and in C6-B too low if they are the eventual outcome. C6-D is the clearest timing error: Regulatory Assessment was opened at 12:40, typical completion is 2–14 h, and disposition is a further median 2.2 h later, so a 0.12–0.28 chance that disposition is next is not supported, even with migration delay.

Other inaccuracies: C6-E’s completeness is stated as 0.72 after the second receipt, but the answer twice says it “fell” or “dropped,” which invents a prior score. Rule 2 is the Fast Close gate, yet it is cited as authorizing Close — No Safety Issue. Evidence-review severity overtaking is not in the operating conditions. “Explicit reportability signal” overstates “can be reportable.” C6-A’s support says Supplier Analysis is probably triggered, while the unknowns correctly say the shared lot is unproven. C6-D’s third prediction bundles two different activities into one slot, and `process_enabled: false` is applied to that disjunction rather than to one event. The 101/342 re-request rate also treats a partial directly-follows table as an exhaustive successor distribution.
