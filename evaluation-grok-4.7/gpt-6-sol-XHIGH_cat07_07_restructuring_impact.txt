The answer covers every required heading, and the core quantitative checks are right: the listed handover edges sum to 343 per 100 cases now and 359 after, the specialist, return, approval, and legal/risk median waits all rise in management’s own table, and East is kept distinct from North and South. It is not nearly flawless.

The scale of the waits is never checked. Frequency-weighted median handover waits are on the order of six hours per case, while median cycle time is 6.8–8.6 days. Management’s hour-level medians therefore cannot move cycle time unless tails, rework loops, or non-handover delay do. The answer stresses tails but never makes that comparison, so the forecast stays looser than the data allow.

Legal/risk load is also left soft. Referrals rise from 9 to 12 per 100 cases, about one third, against utilization already at 93% and 91%. Unchanged service time and capacity imply load above 120%, which is an unstable queue, not merely a warning. The optimistic scenario still allows steady tails through “reserved skill capacity” without stating extra headcount as a precondition.

Baseline rework after handover (14% on legal/risk, 18% on cross-team cover) is barely used, so the quality forecast is directional only. Removing one local supervisor is also treated as if the approval path were fully centralized, which is what the routing table shows and not what “one position” plus a medium/high-value queue necessarily means.

The PM4Py sketch names the right miners and correctly refuses to treat adjacent completion timestamps as waiting time. The cohort logic is still fragile. The code is a before/after split and does not implement the concurrent within-team control the pilot depends on. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is unused, and the service-time calls omit the parameters those functions require.
