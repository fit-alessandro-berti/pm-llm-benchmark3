5.5

The role map is directionally usable, but the evidence layer is not reliable enough for a process owner. Many headline shares are wrong, several figures are not in the log, and authority claims contradict the sequences the answer itself relies on.

The six required sections and the three named shadow roles are present, temps are separated from permanent front office, and Nora is treated as an automation boundary. Complexity is used, and the staffing cautions match the brief. That is the ceiling.

Arithmetic and invented precision pull it down. Erik’s security work is 96/170 of his events (57%) and 96/146 of all A6 (66%), not 80%. High-plus-critical cases are 137/166 (83%), not 90%. Hana performs 138/218 approvals (63%) and 138/245 of her own events (56%), not “nearly all” and not 75%; high-plus-critical is 192/274 (70%), not 90%. Fatima’s pricing-plus-negotiation share is 212/291 (73%), not 85%, and quality’s core pair is about 75–78% of each analyst’s work, not 90%. Ivan and Julia do 608/684 quality checks (89%), not 96%. Mateo’s reopens are 42/140 (30%), not 50%, and critical cases are 76/429 (18%), not 30% and not second only to the supervisor: Erik’s critical rate is higher, and Mateo’s absolute critical count exceeds Hana’s. Nora is 760/1668 registrations (46%) and 610/2251 validations (27%), not 63% and 40%. Those last figures, and Giorgio’s “37% of A7 / 49% of A8,” only appear if the denominator is a convenient subset while the prose says “of total.”

Unsupported numbers are stated as facts: a 60% coordination core for Mateo (A13 is 42/415, about 10%), “95% of normal volume,” a 30% cut in security referrals, temps as “20% of simple cases,” and a median of 3874 minutes. The log says 38 minutes rose to 74. That is a misread, then repeated in the staffing section.

Authority is over-claimed. Fragment 4 ends at Hana’s approval, so Fatima does not have final pricing authority. Hana does not handle all escalations; Mateo has more reopens (42 versus 24). Erik does not have “absolute” security authority when Mateo filters referrals. Week 5’s 21 preliminary approvals are treated as Mateo’s core role even though 13 were rechecked. That uses a temporary substitution as if it were standing competence.

Giorgio, the clearest shadow resource, is absent from the role table. His own mix is about 68% front-office closure work and 20% A7+A8, not 60% and 30%. Naïve-clustering rationales for front office and technical analysts are inverted: frequency clustering would separate those groups and would instead wrongly merge Chen with Dina, Amira/Ben with the temps, and Giorgio with front office. The code hard-codes Giorgio into Commercial and the temps into Front Office, never joins `discover_organizational_roles` output to complexity, and flags every resource on a substituted case. Section 6 then recommends formalizing Giorgio and letting technical analysts absorb quality checks, which cuts against the doubled exception time and against an independent quality gate.
