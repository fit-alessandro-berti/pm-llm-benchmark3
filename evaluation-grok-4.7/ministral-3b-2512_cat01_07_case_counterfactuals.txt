3.5

The write-up keeps the required eight-part skeleton and a few right intuitions, but the counterfactual paths and timings are not operationally usable. Mandatory control-flow is broken in the two highest-stakes cases, four-eyes is misstated throughout, and several numeric claims are internally inconsistent or physically implausible.

The partial order is not a usable precedence model. Identity Check is placed before Document Check, Assessment is treated as always branching into compliance or a further-information request, and the declarative text waives the Assessment prerequisite when originals arrive. Originals Receipt gates Document Check only; both checks still have to finish before Assessment. Four-eyes is repeatedly described as Decision versus Compliance Review. The rule is that the same person may not do the final Assessment and the final Compliance Review. Escalation advice is also written as if it were a required predecessor of compliance.

K-104 drops Assessment entirely, so final compliance has nothing legal to follow. Identity and document work are treated as the new parallelism, although originals are not required and the factual critical path was already the long D1 queue, not a serial identity gate. Claimed cycle time of 12–24 hours, or a 12–24 hour reduction, is not reconciled with D1’s complex service range, the Monday queues, or C1’s backlog. Preliminary versus final compliance is never separated, and the reversal test invents a supplier that this case does not have. The real local effect is overlapping S1/C1 preparation with the document wait, with a later service saving on the order of the stated 1–3 hours, against the cost of holding the only compliance reviewer.

K-219’s activity list is the best of the four, but the derivation then starts D2 at Tuesday 09:30, the same instant as factual D1. That erases the intervention. Originals are in at Monday 17:30 and D2’s late-Monday queue is short, so the live question is whether earlier readiness meets an M1 signing window or dies overnight. Rework is given an unsupported 20–50% probability, and the capacity table still says the effect is positive if D2 is faster, despite D2 being the slower resource.

K-337 deletes both rework loops and Compliance Review. The intervention only suppresses the second information request, after Assessment 2, and this high-risk, high-value case still needs a final compliance review before Decision, by someone other than A1. “Reject with conditions” collapses the allowed choice of approve-with-conditions or reject. Cutting the Friday-to-Monday external wait and the following document, assessment, and compliance cycle is far more than 12–36 hours, and the derivation reuses the factual next-Tuesday compliance timestamp. The weekend mechanism is later reversed: avoiding a weekend supplier wait is described as delaying weekend responses.

K-411’s path is only a total order, and both checks are said to start immediately at triage despite Tuesday queue snapshots. Factual document work was already D2, so “if D2 is slower” is not a counterfactual. False concurrency is noted, but the claimed 4–12 hour gain is not derived from the lock time actually removed.

The 100-case discussion names the right themes and correctly treats C1 reservation as a bottleneck shift, then weakens that with invented 20–30% and 30–50% effects, the wrong queue directions for K-104 and K-337, and no demand reduction from avoided rework. Ranking K-337 first, with “high robustness” and “no new risks,” inverts the decision-policy hazard the intervention was built to expose. Calling K-411 the most dangerous case overstates a standard, non-compliance file and understates forced disposition of unresolved high-risk evidence. The K-337 pilot assignment fires when a second information request is not issued, which is the complement of the policy, and its quality outcome says defensibility and appeal risk improve. The K-104 pilot path restores Assessment, contradicting section 2, and eligibility drifts onto originals. The synthesis is under the required 220 words and repeats those inversions. The assumption audit mostly relabels given rules; four-eyes is called behavioral, queue uncertainty is called interference, and the load-bearing uncertainties—M1 hours, night coverage, handwriting error rates, whether a C1 “reservation” is a hard hold, and whether preliminary reading is reusable—are absent.
