5.4

The write-up covers every required section and the base offered-load arithmetic is largely auditable, but the recommendation rests on a comparison table the supplied program cannot produce and on a simulator that does not implement the stated policies. Under a strict reading, that breaks the result half of the argument.

**Load math that does check out.** Block calendars, net minutes (2,490), mixture means (6.94 / 26.30 / 32.82 / 38.50 / 15.74), and Table 2.3 skill totals are reproducible from the given ranges and mix. The 16:00 staffing cliff and the post-18:00 mechanical blackout (Emil has no mechanical skill) are real and important. U1’s 1,030 → ~1,750 versus 1,080 morning minutes is the right scaling. Priority mix ≈ 13% / 53% / 34% is right.

**Arithmetic and scenario errors.** Table 1.1 standard deviations do not follow the variance formula printed above them (intake ≈ 1.74 not 1.91; software ≈ 13.4 not 12.38; validation ≈ 5.35 not 5.01). The parallel-versus-sequential comparison uses unmultiplied means for parallel (max(26.3, 32.8)+8 = 40.8) and a specific multiplied pair for sequential (75.6), so the P1 rule is biased toward parallel. The “≈1.15× → ≈3,250 min → ≈130%” load is not derived. “Morning ρ ≈ 85%” contradicts the same section’s 92.6% and 97.7% block ratios. U4 is defined as diagnostic tails only; the code doubles Intake and Validation tails as well. Assumption 1.1 specifies a block-wise Poisson process; arrivals are a fixed rounded count placed uniformly in the block.

**Policies are not the policies that were scored.** SS-FQ both “dedicates Ada exclusively to mechanical” and puts Ada in the electrical silo; Chen is in two silos while setups are claimed to be zero. FDP’s written rule is shortest-remaining preemption; the code always preempts P3 before P2. TR-CO-AB’s μ≤1.25 sticky gate, “no P1 waiting” retention, 10/45-minute overflow triggers, and virtual P1 reservation are absent from the code. Team +5 handover never fires for Deepa or Farah because of the invented team labels. Section 4 says 10 replications and prints delay standard deviations; the script runs 5 seeds and never computes a standard deviation. J_SLA and J_Robust are never evaluated.

**The event engine does not measure the claims.** Preemption leaves the original finish event on the heap, so the interrupted activity later completes anyway and clears the preempting task. `ready_time` is ignored, so the 8-minute coordination delay is not enforced. Break end and shift start are not events, so idle capacity is not reliably woken. Breaks and the 30-minute overtime cap do not constrain a task once it has started. Unfinished cases are dropped, so SLA and mean delay are computed only on completions while active time is still charged the full pre-preemption duration and utilization is capped at 100%. That censoring is the plausible reconciliation between a claimed ~109–130% load and base P2 SLA of 100% with mean sojourn near 140 minutes. Completion count is tracked and never reported. Handover falling from ~614 (base) to ~497 (U5) under a larger arrival volume is a warning light the text does not use.

**The recommendation does not follow the table.** No policy hits the 90% P1 target in any scenario; that infeasibility is never treated as the decision. Several gaps are smaller than the reported delay noise (±12 to ±40) and, with about 5–6 P1 cases a day, smaller than sampling error on the SLA rate. FDP is better on U2 (mean, p90, and P1 SLA 93% vs 86%) and on U5 mean, p90, and P2 SLA (90.5% vs 77.7%), yet every TR row is labeled Superior. “Lowest σρ ≈ 0.084–0.098” is false for U1, where TR is the worst of the three. “Sticky retention eliminates up to 35% of handovers” is false against 701 → 654. Section 5.3 does not name the shocks where the table already favors pooling.

**What remains usable.** The control-flow, preemption-once, and handover/setup rules are stated clearly. Sections 6–7 and 9 cover gaming, P3 starvation, skill decay, validation clumping, interpretability, the required log fields, and the case-level-seconds limitation of PM4Py service/waiting time. The pilot’s case-level 50/50 split on a shared roster is contaminated; day- or shift-level assignment would be the design that can actually separate the policies. The qualitative specialization-versus-pooling tension is the right one. The quantitative ranking is not evidence for it.
