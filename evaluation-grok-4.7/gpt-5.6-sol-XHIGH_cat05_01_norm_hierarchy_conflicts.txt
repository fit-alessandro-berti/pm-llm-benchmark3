7.6

The assessment is a serious, mostly coherent compliance analysis: issue-specific authority, time-of-act application, and the hard timing calls are largely right. It is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions sit on the exact points the task makes dispositive — evidential category, burden of proof, and formal rule precision.

The strongest work is the time-and-evidence handling. P-501 is kept outside ECD-2 because an ON-44 lag would move 08:50 earlier, not across 09:00. P-505 correctly prefers the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment and the fast-clock direction over the 18:06 ERP stamp. P-507 correctly treats the 12:40 PDF as inside the four-hour recording window and the 14:30 ERP stamp as a logging delay. P-506 correctly refuses an unwritten overnight extension. Thresholds are applied accurately: 24,000 is below both 25,000 and 50,000; 62,000 triggers A2 and new-supplier A3 but not the amount limb of A3; 140,000 triggers A3 but not the email’s 150,000 Legal add-on; 155,000 triggers Legal under D2. B4’s sequencing permission is kept separate from the Shift Lead substitution. A1 is analyzed as a real fork on P-503. Post-commitment Finance and Compliance are not treated as cures. The hierarchy is issue-specific, the required tables and headings are present, and section 5 gives real, premise-specific ways to flip classifications.

The central flaw is evidential. Section 1 defines `INDETERMINATE` as the result when a dispositive fact is unresolved, and says a missing qualification is not to be presumed into existence. Section 6 then defines “policy breach” as an act that was absent, late, or done by an ineligible actor, and “inability to prove compliance” as a control that might have been satisfied. The same A2 role gaps for P-505, P-507, and P-508 are placed in both rows. Those definitions exclude each other. The primary labels then collapse all three into `NONCOMPLIANT`, so the unused `INDETERMINATE` class and the section 6 distinction do no classificatory work. Confidence ratings and section 5 alternatives only partly repair that. P-502 and P-503 are proven failures; P-507 and P-508 are, on the answer’s own evidence, unproven role mappings. Those are different findings.

The formalization is looser than the narrative. The defeasible line `D_satisfies_local_finance_execution` has no EUR 150,000 cap, so it covers P-508, which D1 does not. Several catalogue triggers are written as “Amount EUR 50,000 / 25,000 / 150,000” with no operator, while A2 and B2 are “at least,” A3 is “above,” and D1 is “up to.” The prose usually gets this right; the catalogue does not. Later-in-time treatment is shown in the cases but never stated as a method, despite section 1 requiring it. The new-versus-unapproved supplier point is substantively sound and then misfiled as a fact.

Case-table discipline slips in smaller ways. P-506 lists the Finance event as satisfied while the missing-evidence cell still questions whether that actor was a Finance Controller. P-504 lists a conditional new-supplier A3 breach as an apparent deviation. Plant approval is an “apparent deviation” for P-508 after D is said to explain the missing local event, and it is simply omitted for P-505 and P-507. Section 5 never tests the alternative that would most plausibly flip P-504: cafeteria repair outside the SOP’s maintenance scope, which would drop B1 and B3 and leave no GPIP threshold engaged. High confidence on P-504 therefore overstates how closed that case is.

P-501 as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` is a permitted primary reading, because B2 has no express pre-commitment text and the contrary reading is disclosed. The other primary labels are defensible under the stated hierarchy. The score is held down by the breach-versus-proof contradiction, the overbroad D rule, and those inconsistencies, not by the outcome pattern itself.
