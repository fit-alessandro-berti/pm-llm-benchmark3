7.5

The case analysis is largely coherent and often precise, but it is not nearly flawless. Several identity and code defects are material under a strict reading.

The strongest reasoning is the event-time authority model. Alias normalization of `u_bk` and `b.koch`, refusal to treat `svc_qms` as a person, the signed-PDF versus 2026-08-12 migration-row distinction for D-QM-01, the CI-44 limit on D-IC-03, the prohibited Farid-to-Clara subdelegation, and the production-equipment limit on Ben’s QM delegation are all handled correctly. RR1 and RR2 are applied to the right activity pairs. CHG-501 as a valid substitution, CHG-502 and CHG-508 as same-human draft/approval, CHG-507 as approval without QM authority, and CHG-510 as self-approval plus an invalid emergency delegation are well supported. The business-day convention is explicit and applied consistently: Tuesday 2026-08-11 implementation due by end of Thursday 2026-08-13, so CHG-505’s Friday review is late and CHG-510’s Wednesday review is timely. Section 4 does not treat repeated collaboration as collusion, and the control recommendations track the actual failure modes.

The clearest analytical miss is CHG-505’s retrospective review. `u_bk` at 2026-08-14 16:00 falls inside Ben’s certified sick leave (2026-08-13 12:00 through 2026-08-14 23:59). The write-up treats Ben there as a resolved human, while correctly refusing to resolve the same account for CHG-506. Scope and lateness still support UNAUTHORIZED, but the identity cell is inconsistent with the answer’s own standard. CHG-504 is also classified AUTHORIZED despite an unresolved credential-sharing possibility; the 09:22 approval does fall inside the 09:15–09:40 VPN window, which is corroborative, but that overlap is never stated, and the primary label is firmer than the evidence. CHG-509’s failure is lack of proof that Asha was absent, not proof that she was available. UNAUTHORIZED is an auditor-conservative choice, but the executive conclusion presents it as established authority failure rather than an unproven delegation condition.

The required script has a real pandas defect. `read_csv` will leave `true`/`false` as strings, and both `.astype(bool)` and later `bool(row["emergency"])` treat the nonempty string `"false"` as True, so every case becomes an emergency. Outcomes on this particular log mostly survive only because QM and low-risk branches return first. The code also emits a clean valid QM delegation for CHG-506, with no sick-leave or denial flag, and an unknown CI-44 link collapses to `NO_VALID_HIGH_RISK_AUTHORITY` instead of the indeterminate status argued in prose. Retrospective QM authority and the two-business-day test are absent from the flags entirely. Those gaps make the executable check a weaker instrument than the narrative.

Format, alternatives, and network caveats are strong. The grade is held down by the sick-leave inconsistency, over-firm labels on ambiguous authority, and checking code that can silently misstate emergency status and clear a disputed approval.
