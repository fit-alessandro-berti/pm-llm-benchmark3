5.5

The write-up is complete and well organized: assumptions, dependency table, approximation policy, partial-order edges, witness design, comparison, and validation plan all match the requested structure. The POWL cross-branch skeleton (containment before planning, analysis before design/approvals/supplier, trial after both plan and design, release as the join, no order between approvals and the trial) is the right shape for this process.

Both models still get the central repeat-until behavior wrong, and the process-tree code does not run.

PM4Py `Operator.LOOP` is `do (redo do)*`: the first child runs at least once, and the second child is only the redo between repetitions. It is not `body* exit`. The code puts the failing cycle first and the passing cycle second, and then calls that “the common PM4Py unfolding.” Under the real operator the trial always contains at least one `Adjust Parameters` and always ends on the failing cycle. Single-pass traces are excluded, and release after a trailing adjustment is admitted. That breaks requirements 8 and 9 in both the tree and the POWL model. T1–T5 are therefore not “Allowed” by the code that was written, even though the table says they are. The suggested repair `XOR(pass, fail -> LOOP(fail, pass))` has the same inversion and still ends the failing branch on `Adjust Parameters`.

The process-tree constructor is also invalid: `ProcessTree(label("Design Corrective Action"))` calls an undefined `label`, so `tree` cannot be built. The duplicate `Execute Trial` / `Inspect Trial` nodes are an artifact of that inverted loop; a correct `LOOP(→(Execute, Inspect), Adjust)` would repeat one subtree and keep `Adjust Parameters` strictly between cycles.

The approximation discussion is internally consistent only with the stated, incorrect loop law. As executable PM4Py, the models are not a conservative under-approximation.
