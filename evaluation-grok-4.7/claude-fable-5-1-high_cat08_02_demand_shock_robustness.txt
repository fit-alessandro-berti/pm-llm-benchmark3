7.0

The redesign is a serious, scenario-based operations argument with the right structure, real use of the logs, and explicit priority conflicts. It is not nearly flawless. Several load-bearing figures are wrong, a few of its own controls contradict its volume math, and some causal claims are stronger than the DFG supports.

**What holds up.** Weeklyization is right: 2,300 vs 3,300 cases, Routine essentially flat (~1,403 to ~1,386), Urgent ~+131%, Complex ~+121%, Repeat-after-failure ~+61%. Assay-run totals (18,260 and 10,250; 2,283 and 3,417 per week), repeat incidence (6.2% to 12.3% of runs), MV entries (~530 to ~1,367 per week), Hold entries (~36 to ~203 per week), recollection (4.6% to 9.1%; cancel share ~35% to ~42%), and accession-correction volume are correct. Missing END mass (10.7% and 13.3%) and the TV imbalance (8,080 exits vs 7,240 entries) are real. Release and cancel paths conserve; the answer’s flow checks are mostly careful. The six scenarios vary by mix and disruption, not just volume. Migration stories for I1, I3, I4, I8, and I10 follow the conflict hints. Slack dispatch, guarded cross-training, rejection of fixed I1/I2/I3/I9, on/off triggers, and redesign-caused failures (deadline bunching, TV as the sink, triage raising cancellations) are the right kind of reasoning. The answer does not “add capacity everywhere,” and it does not optimize the historical mean.

**Material numerical error.** “TV → MV rose from 24.5% to 47% of TV output” is false on the output definition. Peak TV exits are 3,420 + 4,050 + 610 = 8,080, so MV is 42.3% and Hold is 7.6%, not 47% and 8.4%. Those higher figures are shares of the 7,240 assay → TV entries. The same swap produces “direct release falls to 56%” (4,050/7,240) rather than 50% of exits (4,050/8,080). The answer separately computes the 840-case gap, then still labels the inflow ratios as output. That inflates the mix-adjusted story: 42.3/24.5 is 1.73× against a 1.58× Urgent+Complex mix shift, not 1.9×. Hold remains the real excess (about 12% of Complex cases to about 31%, or ~5.6× weekly entries against ~2.2× Complex volume). “Almost certainly behavioural” is too strong for the MV edge; abnormal winter results are still a live alternative, which R4 later admits must be checked.

**Internal contradictions.** Section 2 says peak repeats “double” and batch re-entries “triple.” The same answer’s table shows repeats +194% (~143 to 420 per week, about 3×). Batch-wait → assay is ~90 to ~343 per week, about 3.8×. S2 is defined as normal or mildly elevated demand, then analyzed with peak edge times (8h+26h, machines moving through 98%).

**Policy math that fights the evidence.** One MV sign-off slot per hour cannot cover ~140 Urgent cases/day. The P3 partner cap of “20 orders/day” equals the entire peak accession workload (420 corrections / 3 weeks ≈ 20/day) on a two-person pool already at 91% and a 14h queue, so the cap saturates the station it is meant to protect. R1’s table says Complex out-ranks Routine “as slack shrinks”; under the stated slack rule, an aging Routine case out-ranks a fresh Complex case, and Complex wins only on a tie-break. “Urgent p90 should return under 18h” is stronger than the evidence: normal Urgent p90 is already 19h, peak assay median queue is 18h, and Urgent-versus-Urgent contention rises with a +131% Urgent volume. Residual vulnerability 1 walks this back; the table does not.

**Missed mechanism the source states outright.** Urgent bypass “fragments machine schedules.” R1 forbids Urgent batching and adds express runs at least every 2 hours, then rates the rework effect as slightly positive. The fragmentation path back into repeats and M2 night-shift failures is not analyzed. Path times are sums of medians (14+22=36h, 8+26=34h); medians do not add, and those edges are not Urgent-specific. “Specialist capacity halves” is not in the log: one absence every second Friday could be a total loss if that person is the pool. Consolidated one-review-per-Complex-order assumes per-assay MV events the flattened log does not show. “Fewest recollections are Routine” is not supported. The 40–50 “vanished” Batch-wait/Hold cases fit the normal batch gap (40) and the peak Hold gap (50), but the peak Batch-wait gap is 150.

**Format and dashboard.** The required output is exactly seven sections. The preliminary evidence section is useful and is also an extra top-level section. Section 7 asks for a few leading indicators; sixteen red/amber/green rows is a second control system, and several cut-points (35% escalation, 20 partner orders, the 9h-shift parenthetical on “>2h overtime”) are not tied cleanly to the measured queues.

The architecture would still be a credible peak design after those corrections. The grade is held down because the wrong TV-output rates, the repeat contradiction, the partner cap, the MV slot, and the unexamined express-run fragmentation are errors in the reasoning itself, not presentation nits.
