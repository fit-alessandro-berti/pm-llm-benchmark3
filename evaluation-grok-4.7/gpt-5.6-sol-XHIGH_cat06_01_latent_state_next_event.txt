7.4

The answer is a disciplined process reading with the right state skeleton, but several next-event rankings are hotter than the enablement and timing evidence the answer itself cites.

C6-A is the strongest case. S3, with S4 held as a concurrent or delayed-review alternative, matches a high-severity injured complaint whose evidence arrived at 12:10. Technical investigation and regulatory assessment are correctly treated as mandatory and still open, and supplier analysis is correctly left conditional: one complaint on two devices from one lot, plus three undescribed similar complaints, does not yet meet the same-lot or same-component rule. Evidence Review Completed as the leading log event is coherent at 1 h 50 min into a 1–5 h review window. The combined 0.10–0.24 and 0.08–0.20 mass on the two investigation completions is still rich. Those activities are obligatory, but the case took the evidence path, technical median time from triage is about 9 h, only about 5 h have elapsed, and engineering capacity is reduced. Review is the activity already inside its ordinary service window.

C6-B correctly refuses to copy C6-A. Low severity, no injury, one device, a 0.93 duplicate match, completeness 0.98, and no 30-day cluster remove the mandatory-investigation triggers, so Evidence Review Completed, then a non-investigation close, is the right qualitative story. Two problems remain. The alternative state S5 DISPOSITION_READY mislabels a path the normative model and the directly-follows summary both allow from evidence review itself: Close — No Safety Issue, without a disposition. The rank-2 interval 0.08–0.21 is too high for an event that can be next only if review has already finished near the fast edge of 1–5 h and that completion is missing from a feed flagged normal.

C6-C correctly keeps disposition behind regulatory assessment. Medium severity and no injury would not force that assessment; the country rule on repeated malfunction, applied to four same-failure-mode complaints, does. That is a proper refusal of the frequent Technical Investigation Completed → Disposition Recorded adjacency. The third prediction is the weak slot. Supplier Analysis Completed is marked not process-enabled, the complaints are on different lots, and no common component is given, yet it still receives 0.06–0.17. The explicit “root cause plausible but not confirmed” finding is a more material threat to the ranking and never becomes a predicted activity. Disposition at 0.08–0.20 again prices an out-of-order hidden regulatory completion under a normal feed.

C6-D uses the migration flag, the source-system attachment, and the R-R2 worklist entry without treating a missing evidence cycle as proof that evidence work happened. Regulatory Assessment Completed is a sound primary prediction. The timestamp detail is underused: the worklist opening at 12:40 precedes Technical Investigation Completed at 13:05, which is direct evidence that the regulatory fork was already open. Rank 2 is the clearer miss. Disposition Recorded at 0.11–0.23 requires an assessment that has had only 1 h 20 min against a stated 2–14 h post-assignment range, plus ingestion of the disposition before the assessment event. A delayed evidence-cycle completion fits the migration window of up to 6 h better than that compound path, and it is only rank 3.

C6-E has the sharpest ranking inconsistency. Primary S3 is right at 40 minutes after the second receipt, and making supplier analysis mandatory for a lot linked to six complaints is right. Customer Evidence Requested is then ranked second at 0.09–0.22 while marked not process-enabled. At 40 minutes, below the 1 h floor of ordinary review time, with a normal feed, that event is next only if review has already finished unusually fast and its completion is absent. Supplier Analysis Completed is marked process-enabled, the case is already about a day old, and supplier service is 6–36 h, so a yesterday start can finish before this newly queued review. That prediction belongs ahead of the third evidence request. The rank-1 lower bound of 0.60 is also firm given an unknown position in a four-item review queue.

The cross-case discussion is the best part of the answer. It separates C6-A from C6-B for the right attribute reasons, treats directly-follows counts as confounded observational adjacencies, and refuses to turn the 8–15% migration figure into a transition probability. The observation caveats repeatedly say that a downstream event is next only when a predecessor completion is hidden. The intervals do not carry that discount. Across B, C, D, and E, probability that belongs to an eventual successor leaks into the immediate next-log-event distribution, and in E the rank order conflicts with the answer’s own `process_enabled` flags. That is a systematic uncertainty flaw, not a local slip. The control-flow reading is otherwise careful enough that the answer stays well above a generic frequency baseline.
