**5.0**

The write-up is well organized and the POWL topology is the right idea, but both executable models are invalid and the process tree hides extra loss of behavior.

The loop encoding is wrong in both artifacts. PM4Py `Operator.LOOP` has exactly two children: do, then redo, with language A(BA)*. Exit is the choice not to redo. The code builds `LOOP(SEQ(ET, IT), AP, τ)` in the tree and the same three-child `OperatorPOWL` in the POWL model. Current POWL construction raises on a loop that does not have two children, so `powl` is not a constructible model. The compact `*(…, …, τ)` expression repeats that mistake. The repeat-until claim is therefore not what the code denotes.

The process tree also reuses one `tau` node as a child of both the supplier XOR and the loop. A process-tree node cannot have two parents; `set_parents` overwrites `tau.parent` and the result is not a tree. The skip branch and the loop exit are aliased.

The stated under-approximation is incomplete, which violates the requirement not to disguise representational loss. `SEQUENCE(RD, PAR(CM, ARC), PAR(PV, DCA, risk, CS), loop, RM, AC)` inserts a full barrier: both containment and root-cause analysis must finish before planning, corrective design, approvals, or supplier consultation can start. That forbids intended overlap of plan verification with root-cause analysis, and of design, approvals, and supplier consultation with containment. Section 3 says no other requirements are violated. Section 6 marks the two initial streams and the split into planning/design as exact (“None”). Both claims are false. A still-simple tree, `PAR(SEQ(CM, PV), SEQ(ARC, …))` joined only before the trial, would keep those overlaps while retaining the same approval-before-trial restriction. The tree is not as faithful as the chosen policy allows.

The POWL edges, if the loop were binary, would be much closer: CM∥ARC, PV after CM only, DCA and the two XORs after ARC only, the trial enabled by both PV and DCA, and release as the join of the loop, the risk XOR, and the supplier XOR. Unordered approval versus trial, and supplier versus trial, is the right partial-order reading. That design is undermined by the three-child loop and is not matched by an exhaustive account of every unordered pair.

The eight witnesses are in the required shape, and A5 and F3 do the jobs asked of them. Several verdicts assume the intended loop language rather than the invalid three-child operator. The second self-critique is also wrong: the risk XOR does not allow a low-risk branch and later Quality/Regulatory Review in the same run.

Format, dependency inventory, and the decision to prefer POWL are sound. The grade is held down because the two required models do not implement PM4Py’s operators, and the comparison table reports lost concurrency as exact.
