The write-up covers every required heading and lands on a conditional pilot, but several core forecasts contradict the supplied numbers, and the measurement plan would not identify the effects it claims to track.

The mechanism map treats Legal/Risk utilization as likely to fall from 93% and 91%. Referral volume in the management table rises from 9 to 12 per 100 cases, with no added Legal/Risk headcount, so load moves up, not down. The bottleneck is also misstated as central capacity falling below the sum of local capacity. The three embedded techs are merged, not cut; demand rises (technical referrals 36 to 41 per 100, plus difficult quality checks and legal/risk triage) on the same headcount, while one supervisor is removed as approval volume rises from 42 to 49. Summing the handover table gives about 343 handovers per 100 cases now and about 359 after the change, so the “nominal handover reduction” is not established. Mix shift is ignored: more volume moves onto the legal/risk path, which already has a 14% post-handover rework rate.

Scenario figures such as a 10% cycle-time cut and a 15% rework drop are not derived from waits that are measured in hours against cycle times of 6.8–8.6 days. Pooling, Kingman-style variability effects, and the fact that management’s own central waits are longer than today’s local specialist waits (3.2h to 4.0h, 9.6h to 10.5h) are not reconciled. Team volumes and East’s 82% specialist utilization never enter a capacity calculation.

The East-only pilot does not test the proposal. Keeping North and South local either leaves the pooled service unstaffed by their specialists, or pulls N_Tech and S_Tech into the center and contaminates the controls. The `local_expertise_flag > 0.5` guardrail would trip because hard work is routed away by design. East’s rework ceiling of 12% sits above its 11.5% baseline, and the “+10% versus baseline” rollback is a different number. Thresholds such as a P95 wait of 8h sit below today’s 9.6h legal median.

The PM4Py sketch calls `format_dataframe` on networks and DFGs; that function standardizes an event dataframe. It also calls nonexistent helpers (`get_p90_cycle_time`, `get_rework_rate`, `get_attribute_value_frequency`), never splits before versus after, and omits a working SLA-miss calculation and customer-segment comparison. Filtering on `team == East` after centralization drops specialist events whose team attribute is no longer East, which biases the comparison the code is meant to make.
