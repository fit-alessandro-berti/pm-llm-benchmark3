6.5

The answer is a complete, case-by-case review with real dialectical structure, a usable matrix, and a layered redesign whose POWL roughly matches the requested routes. Under a strict reading it still misstates controlling rules, resolves disputed authority too confidently, and contradicts itself on several holdings.

The central authority holding is over-resolved. PD-3 is a steering-committee minute and email, expressly not folded into CS-9, and the compliance observer both allowed a monitored exception and later denied weakening CS-9. Treating in-window eligible auto-approvals as high-confidence justified exceptions, and grounding that in board approval and a “Disaster Command,” asserts an authority the record does not establish. FD-7 is on firmer ground; equating the two overlays flattens the record. Invented instrument IDs (BOD-RES-2024-88, BOD-RES-2026-92, SC-MIN-2026-05-28, FD-EXEC-2026-07-09) and a CS-9-V2 effective 2026-09-01 are written as overlay records. That blurs proposal with evidence in the section that is supposed to restore auditability.

Several source rules are dropped or rewritten. CS-9’s abbreviated pre-screen clause never enters the stipulated facts, yet SUP-709 is closed at high confidence. PD-3’s pre-activation automated master-data validation is omitted, while SUP-702 is held to have met every substantive pilot criterion even though the trace contains no such event. FD-7’s two-business-day duty attaches to quality review and daily monitoring; the answer repeatedly folds manual approval into that SLA. Section 6 then implements the SLA as “<= 48h (business days),” which is a different rule. The resource rule is requester ≠ manual approver; the proposed control tests the Register_Supplier actor instead.

Case logic is uneven. SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are pointed in a defensible direction, with expiry correctly defeating “still factually an emergency” and “approval was imminent.” SUP-711 is labeled data-quality indeterminate at high confidence while the same paragraph finds that the real execution conformed. SUP-706 is correctly left indeterminate, but the defense brief states that the CRM message was dispatched, contradicting the answer’s own unproved-linkage finding, and it lumps that weak mutable link with SUP-702’s stable notification_id. SUP-710 treats a still-valid prior certificate as satisfying CS-9’s quality-before-activation rule. SUP-715 correctly notes a missing disclosure, then finds that independence was fatally compromised, which the facts do not prove. SUP-708’s sanctions-before-activation breach is real; calling it OFAC/EU strict criminal liability, and saying nothing could reverse the finding, goes beyond the record. The 289/312 in-window facially eligible auto-approvals are stipulated and then largely disappear from the system verdict.

The briefs are forceful, but each omits the concession the task required of it. The prosecution never really grants the early-reject overconstraint, the CRM break, or the duplicate-register artifact. The defense concedes SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-712, and still defends expired-mandate practice as lawful necessity. Section 5 repairs some of this after the fact.

The POWL is substantively aimed at the right routes: concurrent standard checks, manual approval before activation on that route, auto-approval with post-activation quality review, emergency activation after sanctions only, early rejection, reactivation, notification before close, and a tau/Request_More_Info loop whose visible language is zero or more. Limits on amount, identity, and dates are stated. The implementation is looser than the prose. Request_More_Info is concurrent with the checks, not a redo of an incomplete check, and on the auto route it is unordered relative to activation; the enforcement matrix claims a blocking loop the code does not build. Layer 4 already contains the overlay registry that Layers 5 and 6 then reassign. Activity aliasing for QA_Clearance and the master-data-validation event are absent from the collection design.
