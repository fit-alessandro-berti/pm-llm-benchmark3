7.2

The case results are mostly right, and the event-time model is real, but the write-up is not nearly flawless. Two internal contradictions and a few code/formalization gaps keep it well below a 9.

The strongest part is the release-time reconstruction. Blanks are non-updates, later rows are not copied backward, and `(timestamp, event_index)` is applied to TX-311 so the review is under US and the release is under DE. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are correctly NONCOMPLIANT for the right reasons: no enhanced review after the 56% increase, no compliance approval after HIGH, no rescreen after XZ is listed, a standard packet is not an enhanced review, and 118k→145k is about +22.9% and kills the 15:25 review. TX-305 is correctly INDETERMINATE; the next-day 150,000 is not a release payload. TX-302 is correctly COMPLIANT on the 09:20 enhanced review, with EX-77’s 09:05 qualified signature treated as corroboration rather than as the 11:00 insert time. TX-308 correctly treats “at least 100,000” as inclusive and a decrease as non-invalidating. TX-306’s +4.5% reading is coherent, and the stricter alternative is disclosed. TX-310’s NONCOMPLIANT (medium, provisional) is a defensible burden-of-proof choice: displayed 08:50 and a requester’s oral claim do not meet R5 once the signing certificate exists only at 10:30.

Section 6.1 contradicts that analysis. It says judging TX-311 by DE and TX-308 by 100,000 is final-state leakage and “ignores the state at release.” Those are the release states. Final-state leakage is TX-305’s settlement amount, or stamping 125,000 onto TX-301’s earlier review. As written, the example implies the DE result is an artifact, while Section 3 correctly treats DE at release as turning R1 off.

The formalization slips. The rule table correctly says unknown jurisdiction or amount yields INDETERMINATE only when the other conjunct does not already decide the trigger. The pseudo-logic then says unknown jurisdiction or amount makes Trig1 indeterminate in general. That is false: DE decides R1 off with an unknown amount, and amount under 100,000 decides it off with unknown jurisdiction. The code’s `tri_r1` implements the table, not the pseudo-logic. Many temporal connectives are also blank in the formulas, so the “direction of every temporal relation” is only partly recoverable from the notation sentence.

Materiality is wider than the policy. Threshold crossing in either direction is added as a material change and implemented in `last_material`, while R4 says a decrease does not by itself invalidate an amount-dependent review. No supplied case flips, but the extra rule is not identified as an extension in Section 4. “More than 20%” via peak versus basis is the right reading, and exact equality is correctly left valid (`>`).

The code meets the structural brief: in-memory CSV, timestamps, `event_index`, `pm4py.format_dataframe` with a fallback, forward-fill only, release snapshots, review/screen/list trackers, coarse R1–R4 flags, exemption join, unknown flags, and a trace-order check that is explicitly not the real policy. It does not use a classification lookup. Residual defects: R2 is only “some enhanced review, then some approval,” so it would accept an enhanced review that predates the HIGH/PEP change and cannot see that TX-307’s approval was a standard packet (saved here only because no enhanced review exists). Waiver scope is not data; any `VALID` exemption satisfies R1. EX-88’s evidence time is hardcoded to 10:30, so the code fails the exemption instead of leaving the oral-versus-certificate conflict unresolved except for a `needs_human_review` bit. `invalid_flag` mixes strings and booleans, and diagnostic times drop `event_index`, which is the tie-breaker the narrative depends on. TX-310 becomes `DEVIATION_FLAG` whenever `devs` is non-empty, even though human evidence review is still required.

Section 4 is useful on TX-302, TX-305, and TX-311, but TX-310 never takes INDETERMINATE seriously. Contradictory approval evidence was the case most entitled to that classification; the answer only moves it to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or a firmer NONCOMPLIANT. Section 7 covers the requested controls and ties them to the failures.

Smaller clarity issues: “LOW125,000,” an undefined and inconsistent severity scale, and a TX-305 alternative that first treats a pre-11:15 settlement inference as enough for COMPLIANT and then says settlement cannot establish the release amount.
