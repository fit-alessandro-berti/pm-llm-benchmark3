6.5

The final recommendation correctly keeps the case on a conformant second-officer path, ranks immediate execution and R17 self-certification last, and does not treat rescreening, a suspicious-activity report, or self-certification as a substitute for independent review. The regulatory asymmetry and the four-eyes rule are applied in the right direction.

The top plan is not operationally reliable. S4’s review is repeatedly costed at 5–15 minutes, which is the stated recording/execution window, not the 35–75 minute review time given for the second-officer step. That compression produces the 40–80 and 50–80 minute claims. The stated sum 15–30 + 5–15 + 5–15 is 25–60, not 50–80. The fallback is also inconsistent: a checkpoint at 50 minutes plus a 35–75 minute review cannot yield a 55–90 minute path, and interrupting P-901 then returns S2 around 95 minutes, after P-901’s 70-minute deadline. The table nevertheless still prices that delayed interruption at only +0.12–0.28. In the primary continuation, the S4 branch is then decided by “S2 Approves/Rejects/holds,” so the required decision-maker is wrong. Section 6 adds a second, conflicting fallback to further evidence, while the ranked plan still defaults to the damaging S2 interrupt.

Evidence-request benefits also include €0–€35k of on-deadline value even though that path almost certainly misses the deadline. “Strictly dominated” overstates detection certainty, and Action A is only loosely tied to the four-eyes rule. The compliance ordering is sound; the timing, spillover, and continuation used to justify the winning plan are not.
