The write-up correctly splits the year into an April pipeline break, a June routing-mix change, a later parts-driven deterioration, and an October label-maturity problem, and it hits every required section. It is not safe to act on. Month boundaries are wrong, two quantitative claims are false, the repair advice contradicts itself, and the code never produces the process evidence it is supposed to audit.

**Diagnosis table and timeline.** April-as-pipeline, June-as-mix, and October-as-censoring are the right calls. The label-delay row says observed breach falls while parts time rises across August–October. Aggregate breach rose from 0.19 in July to 0.28 in August and 0.31 in September; only October falls. The August V3 rate of 0.64 versus October’s 0.16 is listed as contradicting censoring, when that gap is what length-biased censoring predicts. “V2 share doubled (35% to 52%)” is false (about 1.5×). The parts edge does not merely double: 28 h in January versus 130 h in August is about 4.6×, then 148 h and 155 h. Concept drift is pinned only to August, but mature V3 breach already moved from 0.35 in June to 0.46 in July and stayed 0.61 in September, while V1 and V2 stayed near 0.16–0.19 and 0.09–0.12. Process drift is cut off at August, so the worst edges are omitted. September is told mainly as mix, despite predicted risk of 0.21 against an observed rate of 0.31. AUROC 0.79 is treated as evidence against covariate drift; June kept a mean prediction of 0.20 against an observed rate of 0.15 and ECE of 0.060, which is a calibration shift with ranking intact. “Partner Z uses the same variants” does not contradict population shift. August is the control the answer never uses: Partner Z was still 0% after V3 breach and parts wait had already broken.

**Tests.** The maturity test says August should stabilize at 0.64. That is the mature V3 rate, not the cohort rate, which is already 0.28 at 93% maturity. Reweighting September–October back to January mixes the supplier regime into the Partner Z question. Same-duration and transport tests announce one expected result before the competing hypotheses, and several alternatives are straw men.

**Remediation.** Section 4 fits Platt scaling on January–June, which includes the broken April pipeline and June’s lower base rate. That calibrator moves probabilities down, the wrong direction for August–September under-prediction. Section 6 then says to drop April–May from calibration. August–September are marked unsafe to train on because the concept changed; once labels mature, those are the months that contain the new regime. July is also not a clean pre-shift month. A variant-specific model is recommended without noting that the V1/V2/V3 branch is after the prediction point unless routing is fixed at diagnosis.

**Code and monitors.** The sketch never calls `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`. `log[pm4py.filter_time_range(log, month, month)]` treats a returned log as a mask and passes a month string as both bounds. Timestamps are never parsed. There is no real event-calendar cohort versus case-start cohort, and no note on how those cohorts differ. Breach rate is an event-level mean of an invented outcome column. `format_dataframe` renames the activity column, so later `cohort['activity']` lookups do not match. Monitors exist and the maturity alarm blocks retraining, but a data-quality or mix alarm is not wired the same way, and June’s ECE of 0.060 would not cross the 0.10 calibration cut.

**Risks.** The four failure modes match the brief. The immature-label mechanism is backward: the early-matured slice is enriched for short non-breaches, so the model understates breach risk. The Partner Z fairness note misses the August no-Z comparison and Z’s slower label maturity, so 0.45 versus 0.27 is not yet a fair gap.

The narrative would point a reader at the right episodes and the wrong repair. That is a 6.0.
