The write-up has the required structure, real segment trade-offs, and enough correct log evidence to be usable, but several process-mining interpretations are wrong or overclaimed, so it is not close to flawless.

The diagnosis states the speed/cost versus rework/compliance contradiction with the right anchors (14.1% vs 3.3% complaint/reopen, 31% cheaper before rework and 9% after, fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces, 26% repeat evidence requests, 320 abandons, 890 evidence-to-manual handoffs, 34h and 96h edges, EUR 410). The three redesigns are genuinely different control-flow choices, and each names a sacrifice. The pilot plan has metrics, thresholds, sequencing, and a downstream cost check.

These errors keep the score down:

- The fast path is called a median of about 5 hours by adding the mean edge times 0.8, 1.0, and 3.0. Those medians are 0.5, 0.4, and 1.8, and the sum still omits dispatch-to-close. Segment fast-path medians are 8–28 hours, so the headline cycle time is inconsistent with the outcome data.
- Rule R1 (review before fast approval above EUR 400) is treated as violated by “Fast approval before evidence completeness,” which is the evidence rule, not R1. Rule R3, reopen-after-close, and low model precision are unused.
- Reviewers are said to be already near the 82% queue threshold. The data only says queues worsen above 82%; current utilization is not given.
- “Erasing” the fast-path advantage contradicts the correctly cited residual 9% cost gap. The evidence portal is said to eliminate the 34-hour loop and cut the 320 abandons, against a stated 30–45% repeat-request reduction and a higher abandonment risk.
- High-value cycle time “will” move from 20 hours to about 96 hours. That pair is only the premium fast versus reviewed medians; standard high-value is 28 versus 120, and the current mix is unknown.
- The 96-hour dispatch-to-complaint edge is later called a complaint-resolution cycle. The quality-gate workload is sized from the 260 complaint cases rather than the much larger dispatch population that would be inspected. Premium is both included in the gate and exempted as low-risk.
- Marking the current process Pareto-dominated is not supported and conflicts with the instruction not to claim dominance from this data. Each redesign is worse on at least one listed dimension.
- The designs mostly restate the three levers already in the brief. They do not deal with premium slowness on manual review to repair quote, the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate, part-price wait, or escalation after reopen.

Trade-off language and format are strong. Evidence handling and conformance reasoning are not strict enough for a high score.
