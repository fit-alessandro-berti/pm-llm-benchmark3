6.5

The audit covers every required feature and pattern, uses the mandated structure, and correctly drops the obvious full-trace and post-closure fields. It also keeps queue length and elapsed time, and it treats the closed-then-assessment pattern as more suspect than appeal-then-decision. Those are the right core moves.

The severity scheme is inconsistent. Full-trace fields such as `appeal_seen_anywhere` and `full_trace_variant_id` are critical leakage, but the sequence patterns that encode the same future events are only low or medium “legitimate future signals.” At this `t0`, appeal is not enabled: it can occur only after Partial or Deny. Fraud opening, document cycles, and damage-to-decision are likewise unobserved. Normative legality later does not make them admissible explanations of a prediction made immediately after coverage check. Remediation for those patterns mostly annotates them instead of removing them from the `t0` explainer.

`total_case_duration_hours` is identified as post-closure, but not as near-label leakage. Breach is defined by duration past 10 days, so “long cases breach” is circular, not merely late. The ±5-minute queue join is accepted as valid even though a snapshot after `t0` leaks. `num_document_requests_total` is correctly rejected, yet the repair is incomplete: immediately after coverage check that count is normally zero, so it cannot be an early warning of rework. The negative document-cycle weight and the negative damage-to-decision weight are never contrasted with the positive impossible closure pattern or with the team’s rework story.

Case revisions violate the availability rule. Case Y lists `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` as a credible driver while the SQL is known to use extraction date rather than `t0`. `initial_reserve` is treated as established `t0` state without a timestamp. Both narratives still present the original high-risk conclusion after the dominant leaked drivers are removed. Region R4 is given a repair-network mechanism the evidence does not isolate from language, channel, and socioeconomic correlation. Case Z’s recomputation list treats sequence-index repair as if it could salvage `Claim Closed`, which remains future regardless of order, and it omits the same-day document snapshot and week-52 policy/holiday confounding.

Section 2 is directionally right about prefixes, timestamps, and DFG edges not being rules, but it overstates a direct closed-after-assessment footprint and skips fraud parallelism. Section 3 names the right classes, then calls congestion operationally actionable and does not cleanly separate predictive validity, causal interpretation, actionability, and fairness. Section 5 hits the required topics, while rejecting any feature without an event timestamp would also drop immutable registration attributes.
