3.5

The required sections and tables are present, and two holdings are broadly defensible: P-503 is noncompliant on segregation, missing finance, and missing compliance, and P-504 is outside both the local emergency definition and the sterilization-line scope. The method section also correctly recites that rules are applied at the time of the act, that later approval is not automatically curative, and that an ordinary email is not a controlled instrument. Those strengths do not survive contact with the cases.

The hierarchy conflates sources. GPIP-4 is called the non-waivable floor, yet ECD-2 is credited with the CFO email’s EUR 150,000 finance rule. D1 is repeatedly described as ECD-2’s threshold. The rule catalogue then contradicts itself on whether compliance clearance is waived, omits the stricter Aachen EUR 25,000 plant-and-finance rule that the hierarchy itself recognizes, and invents a defeater under which an Incident Command record replaces the emergency form. Comparison operators are missing from the normalized rules, so several triggers do not state the threshold they purport to apply.

Case application is outcome-determinative and often internally inconsistent:
- P-501 is justified under ECD-2 even though the commitment at 08:50 preceded the directive’s 09:00 start. The local EUR 25,000 controls are ignored, the unauthorized Shift Lead substitution is reduced to a logging defect, and a supplier call log is cited that the file does not contain.
- P-502 is held compliant with high confidence. Finance at 13:05 and compliance at 18:30 both follow the 10:15 commitment; there is no plant approval and no emergency form, yet the row claims the form was recorded and finance was timely.
- P-505 treats the 18:06 ERP stamp as decisive despite the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment and the possibly fast ERP clock. It then says later finance “cures” the gap under A6, which states the opposite. Section 6 separately calls the same case a timely act that should be accepted as compliant.
- P-506 lists finance-after-commitment as a deviation while its own evidence shows finance at 06:05 and commitment at 06:20. The post-expiry purchase-order sequence is missed.
- P-507’s noncompliance theory is that compliance followed commitment. The same row gives compliance at 08:50 and commitment at 09:00. The signed PDF at 12:40 and the complete absence of finance and plant approval are not analyzed.
- P-508 is held compliant at high confidence. EUR 155,000 is above the email’s EUR 150,000 sufficiency cap; “legal clearance as well” is treated as a substitute for the missing finance approval.

The alternatives invert credibility on P-502, P-507, and P-508, and they omit the evidentially strongest classification-changing reading of P-505. No case is classified indeterminate despite contested scope, authority, and timestamps. The draft clause resolves several topics, but it writes the email into board policy and says a failure to record does not invalidate the act, undermining the burden of proof stated in the method.
