The final policy is complete, constraint-aware, and mostly operationally sound, but it is not nearly flawless. Several justification errors, an unexecuted scoring model, and internal inconsistencies keep it well below a top score.

The assignment plan respects the hard safety rules that matter most. No high-priority case goes to a skill below 2. Bruno, David, and Iris receive no new work. Security and final approval stay inside the legal floors. The batch cap of 4 is met. Keeping Eva on C016 and Celine on C007 follows the continuity rule, and moving David off C003 and C014 is justified by his overtime flag, 14 open cases, and 3 hours left. The skill-0 conflict on C015 is correctly caught: Eva and Jonas cannot perform Security even under supervision. The training shortfall is disclosed rather than hidden, and the seven required sections, comparison axes, worker explanation, and named PM4Py functions are all present.

The highest-stakes justification is wrong. Section 1 defines H2 as level 3 or supervised level 1–2, but the C001 row calls level 3 “mandatory (H2)” and the handover “unavoidable.” Celine is Security level 1, and supervision by Gita or Hugo is allowed. Constraint 5 therefore still favors continuity unless supervision is infeasible. A 3-hour SLA, complexity 0.88, the enterprise note, and the stated 3.3-hour weak-owner penalty can still support sending C001 to Hugo, but that argument is not the one given. The worker note repeats the overstatement that security “must” go to a specialist.

The training summary contradicts the table. C009 is counted as the training assignment, yet the write-up calls C012–Bruno “the only perfectly matching trainee slot.” Farid’s documented diagnosis priority is never closed out as a second candidate. Development is also only a 0.05 term, and the algorithm has no repair pass if the 20% quota is missed, so the stated target is not actually enforced.

The scorer is never run. Weights are published, but no component scores are shown, so the 16 assignments are expert judgment presented as the output of the formula. That gap matters because urgency plus skill is 55% of the score while resilience is 5%, which may not reproduce the narrative choices. The code tie-break uses the case-level handover band for every candidate, so it does not distinguish resources, and the coded overtime gate drops the “no safer alternative” test required in the prose.

Feasibility is asserted but not checked where the day is tight. Hugo has 5.5 hours and receives urgent C001, retains C015, and gains C014. Eva has 5.0 hours for C016, C005, and new C010. Farid holds two urgents due in 2 and 4 hours. Celine must finish a 6-hour-SLA rework and also absorb C003. No service-time estimate is applied, and the supervised-Celine option for relieving the two-person security pool is not examined. Baseline comparison stays directional and never shows the concrete conflicts, such as first-available giving urgent C001 to the lightest security-capable person.

The audit list covers the required indicators, but several target cells are blank or unreadable, and the sketch returns a placeholder for mismatch rate while referencing a nonexistent `hard_gates.reason_codes`. Those are sketch-level defects, not fatal ones. The plan is usable and explainable; the grade is capped by incorrect rule citations, an unproven optimizer, and unquantified load on the critical resources.
