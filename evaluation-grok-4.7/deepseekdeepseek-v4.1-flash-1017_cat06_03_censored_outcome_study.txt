The study design is statistically serious and meets the required structure. The primary estimand is an observed-policy, cause-specific cumulative incidence of activation followed by 90-day default. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are competing exits or censored cases. The 48,000-case flow adds up, prediction time is stopped before the decision gate, and the leakage table correctly drops or as-of-repairs `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, `risk_score`, extraction-time resource experience, and the label fields. Temporal validation by start date, with separate policy and migration windows, is the right split.

The skeleton undermines that design. `feat` is the feature table, yet it is joined to `default_label`, `state`, and `event_time_from_tau_days`. The audit only forbids the raw names `default_90d` and `event_time_raw`, so it passes while the outcome is in the frame. The survival comment also never says mature non-defaults are censored at activation plus 90 days, which is what the estimand requires. The block is about 200 lines, past the requested 80–160, and the prefix, DFG, and variant objects are computed and then unused. `risk_score` uses `last`, so a missing score on the final prefix event erases an earlier valid score.

Two design gaps remain. The main split does not require training labels to have been available before the validation origin, so it is not a full as-of backtest. Post-migration performance is also entangled with immature 90-day outcomes, and the compound-event score can rank applications mostly by who gets approved.
