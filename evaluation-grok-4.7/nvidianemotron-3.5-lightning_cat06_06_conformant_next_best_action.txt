The write-up keeps the required six-part structure, correctly bars immediate execution and self-certification from the recommendation, and treats regulatory exposure as larger than the deadline benefit. Those strengths do not offset several material errors in the ranking itself.

The primary plan’s continuation treats a rescreen that pushes the score under 70 as enough for auto-clear. Auto-clear is available only below 40 and with no alert. A score of at least 70, or an unresolved fuzzy match, still requires an independent second-officer review before approval or execution. Rescreening is described as a possible substitute for that review, which the case forbids while those conditions remain. That error sits in the ranked table and in the top continuation, so it is part of the conclusion.

Rejection is labeled currently nonconformant and placed below both unlawful execution and self-certification. The case only makes second-officer review mandatory before approval or execution, and it states that rejection is conformant only if an evidence threshold is met, which is not established. The matching label is conditional conformance. Ranking a fast, possibly valid refusal under clearly unlawful payment also contradicts the later claim that those unlawful shortcuts are ranked last.

Deadline arithmetic is inconsistent with the recommendation. Sequential screening, a wait for S4, and review are added to roughly 85–160 minutes against a 90-minute deadline, yet the same plan is called the only conformant path with a realistic chance of meeting it. The expedited S2 review is the tighter clock. Parallel screening while waiting for S4 is never specified with an explicit sequence, concurrency rule, and fallback, even though that is the natural composite. “S4 within 65 minutes” covers the entire given window, so it does not decide anything.

Section 5 never assesses the risk of burning the deadline while waiting for a director and an incident identifier. Prediction ranges, the 12-minute-old score, transport under intervention, and a conformant hold-plus-report branch are barely used. Suspicious Activity Report is repeatedly written as SAF, and several continuation lines are garbled where symbols dropped out.

The closing recommendation (alternate screening, then a second review, with S2 as fallback) is the right family of action, at medium confidence, with sensible reranking triggers. It is not reliable as written, because the path still contains the auto-clear mistake, an inverted deadline claim, and a ranking that prefers unlawful execution to a conditional refusal.
