The interval arithmetic, time-zone conversions, DST-fold pruning, SLA bands, and zeta sensitivity are largely right, including several one-second boundaries. That is not enough for a high score. The write-up contradicts itself, a required computation is hardcoded, and several stated intervals or “minimum evidence” claims do not match the argument attached to them.

The OPS-BER upper-bound algebra is internally inconsistent. The simplified formula gives displayed UTC plus 1 min 30 s, while the stated 270 s width and the table’s `08:16:30` / `09:20:30` endpoints are the correct plus 2 min 30 s. The published durations follow the table, so later verdicts survive, but the derivation as written is not reliable.

T-409 is classified three different ways: POSSIBLE_VIOLATION in §1.4, INDETERMINATE in §3, and a starred hybrid in §4. The supplied ±12 min estimate is weak but bounded. Either accept it and classify the computed interval, or reject it as too weak and stop presenting that interval as the feasible duration. Doing both without a single primary semantics is not a careful distinction between possible and certain.

T-407 has the same problem in smaller form. Capping receipt at the investigation maximum and then taking an independent Minkowski difference reintroduces the infeasible pair and yields `[-3, 1023]` s; under that causal rule the duration floor is 0. The Alert→Containment interval `[2277, 3303]` s is the capped window, not the full 20-minute latency window, whose floor is 2097 s. The classification stays CERTAIN_PASS either way, but the rationale cites the window the numbers do not represent.

§5 does not do what it claims. `ZoneInfo` is imported and never used; the raw frame is not normalized; DST folds are not built as alternative intervals and then pruned. The bounds are pasted constants. The script also classifies T-407 and T-409 as ordinary overlap results, omits SLA checks, drops fractional seconds, and assumes diagnostic columns and a non-strict zeta cutoff. The hand-checked midpoint examples (855 s, 2625 s, 2490 s, 3300 s) are arithmetically right, and the false-certainty point is valid, but “PM4Py returns no deviations” is asserted rather than produced. The profile is defined as eventually-following, while this PM4Py routine is direct-follows; that mismatch is never stated. Calling T-401 a “false alarm” overstates a case whose feasible interval still includes real violations.

§6 misses T-405 entirely. For T-406, separating entry delay from occurrence time cannot settle S1: both models still straddle 900 s (`[208.5, 1171.5]` and `[508.5, 1171.5]`). Clock or timestamp evidence is required, and it is not identified. The SLA acceptance region is silently `[0, limit]` rather than the stated one-sided “no later than” constraint; that choice does not flip these cases, but it is an unexamined semantic change. Shared-clock correlation is also ignored, though the close T-408 zeta=2 straddle remains possible even if the common offset cancels.
