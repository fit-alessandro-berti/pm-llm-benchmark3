The write-up follows the required structure and often reasons from eligible sets, but it is not reliable enough on the queues where the discipline is actually ambiguous. Several predictions are wrong, one empirical claim is invented, and the two weakest diagnoses are protected by omitted decisions.

The clearest factual error is the claim that realized service times “sometimes” fail to match `est_min`. Every job’s segment length equals `est_min` exactly, including B01’s three segments summing to 45. That invention props up the noisy-estimate exception and undercuts the later claim that SPT is hard to test. Ex-post error is not present in these data; only the untested possibility that the dispatcher saw a different number remains.

QB is the weakest reconstruction. The primary label “preemptive priority” is carried by B02’s preemption and by B05 at 10:00, while the table skips the decisions that cut against that label. At 09:48 B01 is stopped for a documented dispatcher reassignment and B03 (priority 2) is started while B05 (priority 1, arrived 09:46) is within both the one-minute timestamp grain and the two-minute scan bound; B05 also does not preempt B03. At 10:14 the server resumes residual B01 (priority 3) with B06 (priority 2) already waiting. Those two episodes belong in the decision table. The SPT cells are also wrong: at 10:00 remaining B01 is 14, B05 is 14, and B04 is 8, so SPT selects B04, not “residual or B04”; at 10:28 B07 (estimate 6) is listed as eligible and is shorter than B04 (8), so SPT cannot select B04. “Priority” is also left ambiguous at 09:16, where non-preemptive priority would continue B01 and only preemptive priority would switch. Resume-FIFO plus a manual override fits the trace better than the stated primary rule, and confidence 60 does not repair the missed tests.

QC is directionally reasonable through 10:54 and correctly refuses to hide the 10:32 choice of C04 behind a two-minute scan lag (C06 has been present about seven minutes). The diagnosis then cherry-picks. At 11:40 C01 (priority 3, estimate 36, deadline 13:00) is taken over C07 (priority 2, estimate 18, deadline 12:00). Started then, C07 would finish at 11:58 and still meet its deadline; starting C01 makes that miss certain. That choice contradicts SPT, priority, and deadline urgency together, so it is not absorbed by an “override once the SLA is already missed” rider aimed at C05. Citing C06 as later support for SPT, without this next decision, makes the secondary component look more coherent than the trace allows. Confidence 50 and “least identifiable” are therefore better than the supporting argument.

QA is the strongest part: A03 at 08:20, A02 over A04, A05 over A08, and A07 over A08 really do support non-preemptive FIFO with an appointment jump only once the appointment time has arrived. The 09:05 cell still says the appointment rule predicts A06 “near.” A03 was held until its exact time rather than started early, so a near-appointment prediction is inconsistent with the same queue’s own evidence. The stated identifiability problem, a weak priority rule that only breaks ties, is also misplaced: these conflicts are not ties, and priority loses them.

QD correctly treats skill as a hard eligibility constraint, uses D-M’s 11:25 choice of D04 over shorter, higher-priority D08, and flags D-F’s 11:36–12:00 idle. Smaller faults remain. At 11:36 FIFO selects D05 alone, not “D05 or D10.” The meeting explains not starting 30-minute D05; it does not explain refusing 12-minute D10, and “reserve D-F for exclusive work” sits poorly with D-F having taken ordinary electrical D03 at 11:21 while D05 was already waiting and D-X was free. The 11:30 row has FIFO and the appointment rule agreeing on D06; the discriminating fact is D-X staying idle from 11:20, and that decision is not tabulated.

The discrimination tests are relevant but only partly ambiguous: each pair already predicts different jobs, and the extra observable is mostly eligibility or visibility. Section 5 is ranked and includes data quality, yet it depends on the false estimate mismatch. Section 6 is a real collection plan. The synthesis is right that QA is the most identifiable and that a center-wide priority rule would be an unsafe inference, but “priority is subordinated to SPT in QC” repeats the over-reading above.

Overall: competent decision-set reasoning and a correct QA conclusion, pulled down by an invented observation, incorrect SPT predictions, and omitted QB and QC decisions that contradict the stated primaries.
