6.6

The answer is complete, well structured, and right in its decision: Site A’s before/after contrast is confounded, the statutory originals constraint blocks a literal copy, and a segmented conditional pilot is the defensible response. Format, required nodes, column schemas, scenario count, and the tail-worsens case are all met. The score is held down by repeated quantitative and causal errors.

The central mistake is treating Site B’s 20h median waiting-before-assessment as evidence that three assessors are already saturated. That interval is the sojourn until assessment, and Site A’s own drop from 18h to 12h shows it moves when upstream verification is shortened. At B it also contains the 18h originals delay and 3.4h + 8.1h of verification service. Assessor queue time is not observed, and no assessment service time is given, so “utilization is high” is unsupported. That misreading is then built into the transport graph’s downstream story, the assessment-bottleneck row, the bottleneck-shift magnitudes (+5h to +15h at p90), the pilot’s interpretation of a rising pre-assessment wait, and the recommendation.

The causal graph has a related specification error. `originals_required` points only at `true_overlap`. The receipt delay lengthens cycle time even if overlap is held fixed; `case_complexity` is given a direct edge to `cycle_time`, so the omission is inconsistent. `role_specialization` likewise affects queue delay, not only the feasibility of overlap.

Overall-median arithmetic is wrong. A 2–3.5h gain inside a 25–38% stratum is reported as about −0.7h to −1.3h, and then “about 1 hour,” on the overall median. The overall median is not the eligibility-weighted average of stratum medians. The same looseness appears in the unexplained 2–4h mix-shift subtraction, the 0.5–1.5h originals increment, and a power claim that assumes an IQR of about 10h beside a p90 that sits 66h above the median.

Directly-follows counts are read as case-level ordering. Register→Identity (2,050) plus Register→Originals (1,860) already exceeds 3,400 cases, so those frequencies are not a partition of traces. The qualitative claim that much of the identity–originals overlap already exists is plausible; the numerical gain taken from those counts is not. Separately, “62% originals required” is treated as “38% digital and available at registration.” The complement is only the set of cases without that statutory prerequisite.

The pilot leaves a confound the answer itself flags at Site A. Huddles are applied to both arms, but the new per-reviewer WIP cap is specified as part of the intervention and is absent from the treatment-version and balance rows. On shared pools that cap also spills onto control cases. There is no pre-period, so the huddle effect the transfer table rates at 70 is not identified. Stop rules and expansion thresholds are coherent, and the manipulation check via system-generated overlap is the right separation of implementation failure from mechanism failure.

The POWL candidate does the hard part correctly: identity and document check are unordered, assessment has edges from both, originals are a skippable predecessor of document check, and legal review is an optional loop. Two weaknesses remain. `soundness[0]` assumes a sequence return that the public `check_soundness` API does not stably provide. The single nondeterministic XOR also cannot enforce the wave rules the prose depends on; the comments admit the data-condition limit but the net is still looser than the proposed policy.

Assumption 5 records only the benign direction of failure. The WIP limit is moved from 6 to 5 without a basis. Several section 1 rows place the before→after delta in the “before” cell and leave “after” blank.

The recommendation’s direction — conditional go, digital non-legal cases first, do not import the 9h figure, keep statutory order — is sound. The hour ranges attached to that recommendation are not identified effects.
