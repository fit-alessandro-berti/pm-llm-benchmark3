7.4

The memo is a serious causal analysis: availability is separated from uptake, the single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is rejected, median and tail are not collapsed into one effect, CSAT is treated as unidentified, staggered-adoption bias is recognized, and the rollout choice is an embedded evaluation rather than an immediate scale-up. The primary pre/post table is largely arithmetically right, and the claim ladder is ordered in the right direction.

Those strengths do not make the quantitative conclusions reliable. The late-window table shows Gamma’s raw p90 change at about −0.9h, yet §§4–5, §7, and the observed bullet still say every adopter’s tail unambiguously worsened by about +7 to +10h. Alpha’s full post-period median is about −0.4h versus Jan–Mar, not +1.9h; +1.9h is Beta’s full-post change, misattributed to Alpha. That error props up the false summary that adopters’ raw medians did not improve: Alpha’s late median fell about 3.3h and Gamma’s about 3.4h. “Controls fell 0.4–1.3h” from April to August is also wrong (Delta rose about 0.4h). The sensitivity claim that at least ~3h (~6%) survives every scenario sits above the memo’s own Beta figure of −2.8h and treats three branch contrasts as if they were a range for one common effect. The not-yet-treated Alpha change is about +1.5h, not +1.47h.

Interpretation slips in the same places. Excess arrivals line up with the stated routing shifts (about +10pp at Alpha/Beta and +5pp at Gamma), so “13–29% more arrivals per FTE” is mostly routing plus Alpha’s FTE loss, not demonstrated automation throughput. The decision rule nevertheless treats that ratio as a capacity benefit. Epsilon’s complexity rise matches Alpha’s, so “adopters were hit harder by case mix” is selective. Branch-month p90s are not unstable because monthly n is small; arrivals are roughly 600–1,400. Rework and escalation are defined in the brief.

The design section contradicts itself: Epsilon is ordered first, then the order is said to be randomized, and a six-month clean pre-period cannot exist for a 2026-11-01 start. Callaway–Sant’Anna is named as primary, while the code fits a descriptive event-time OLS and does not implement that estimator. Conditioning on load and complexity also changes the estimand relative to the bundled rollout. The code’s validations, windows, clustered-SE caveat, and non-causal comments are otherwise sound.
