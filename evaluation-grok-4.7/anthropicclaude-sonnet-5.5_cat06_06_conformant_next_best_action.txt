The answer covers all six required sections, uses the four conformance labels correctly, ranks immediate execution and self-certification last, and keeps second-officer review mandatory. The main recommendation is a conformant plan, and the S4 timing split, stale score, SAR non-disclosure, and observational-forecast caveat are all addressed. Under a strict reading it still has several material reasoning errors.

The recommended plan’s harm is understated. C*’s harm cell is about €1k–€15k of residual hold cost plus a small P-901 spillover. A conformant approval of an unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match on a €480k payment to a sanctioned-border supplier still leaves a sanctions tail. That tail belongs in the same €100k–€500k-plus non-monetary scale used against Actions A and B. Time pressure and a context switch make a shallow clearance more plausible, and the answer itself says the approval forecast may not transport. Leaving that tail out of the table makes C* look cheap relative to rejection and hold.

The decision rule contradicts the break-even math. Section 3 puts C*’s advantage over D* at about €13k–€66k and the break-even loss on P-901 at about €50k–€550k. Section 3 and the reranking trigger then switch to D* once P-901’s expected added loss exceeds about €15k–€25k. That threshold sits at the bottom of the stated advantage, so the operational rule abandons C* in cases the same section still counts as net-positive. The €15k–€25k figure is also out of line with the table’s own spillover illustration of about €2k–€10k under a €20k–€35k P-901 loss.

The numerical case against A and B conflates two different probabilities. Ultimate non-approval of 0.25–0.45 is not the probability that execution would be unlawful. Non-approval includes ordinary risk and evidence decisions. The interval also does not sit above the stated 4–35% break-even band: at a 25% non-approval rate and a 35% break-even, the claimed dominance fails. A and B are correctly excluded because rules 6, 7, and 9 are mandatory, but the forecast arithmetic does not independently establish that.

Ranking B above A because a non-independent look is better than no look underrates the harm. Self-certification writes a false four-eyes record. Section 4 correctly calls that a fabricated control, which cuts against the Section 1 ordering.

C* interrupts P-901 at T+0, while alternate screening returns only at T+15–30 and is the event that might drop the score below 70 and resolve the match, releasing S2. The option value of waiting for that result before imposing a 0.12–0.28 breach increase on a case with a 70-minute deadline is acknowledged only as an early-release branch, not as a reason to delay the interrupt. The “two-minute stakes check” is an assumed capability the case file does not provide.

Other gaps are smaller but real. E and F are given the same €19k–€86k expected harm, while E is ranked higher and simultaneously described as not a first move. The E cell then adds the €20k–€35k deadline loss again after that loss is already inside the €19k–€86k product. Deadline loss and customer harm are added with no overlap check. “Earliest plausible continuation” is filled with full ranges. A and B show gross exposures while C*–E show probability-weighted figures. The 80–90% chance that the decision lands by T+90 is not supported by a review time of 35–75 minutes, which already ends before T+90; that haircut flows into the 0.45–0.65 execution probability. Re-screening is cited to rule 8, which is the SAR rule. The long preamble before the six sections also departs from the required output shape.

The bones of the recommendation are sound. The inconsistencies in the spillover trigger, the missing residual sanctions tail, and the non-approval/unlawfulness conflation are large enough that the answer is not near-flawless.
