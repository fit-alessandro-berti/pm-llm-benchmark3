The answer has the right overall control posture: A and B are ranked last and treated as prohibited, re-screening and a SAR are not treated as substitutes for independent review, P-901’s spillover is carried through the ranking, and the 35- versus 65-minute S4 split is explicit. The live prefix ends at Evidence Validated. Rule 5 places Analyst Review after that step, and the only Analyst Review Completed event is before the evidence cycle. Ranks 1 and 2 nevertheless send the case straight to S4 or S2 and mark that path `CONFORMANT NOW`. That skips the review that would make the later review a true second review under the four-eyes rule. The rank-4 trace states the correct order, Validate evidence → Analyst Review → Second Officer Review, and then fails to apply it to the current prefix. Preparation of a packet for S4 is not that missing analyst review.

The timing used to justify D over C is unsupported. The only stated review duration is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute. With S4 arriving in 35–65 minutes, a central path is on the order of 90–140 minutes, so the 90-minute deadline is missed except in a narrow fast tail. The table’s “45–80 min best-to-central” figure invents a shorter review and is placed in the earliest-continuation column. That rosy window is part of why D is ranked first. The given baseline SLA-breach range of 0.48–0.64 is never used, so D is not compared with ordinary processing on the quantity that matters for the deadline.

Harm categories are crossed. The €15,000–€80,000 range is defined for false rejection or a prolonged hold. Rank 1 applies it to a deadline miss. Missing the deadline forgoes the separate €20,000–€35,000 timing benefit; it is not automatically the larger customer-harm range. Section 3 states the categories correctly and the table does not.

G is ranked above two more substantive conformant options even though no incident facts are present, the path is not enabled, and the stated confidence is low. The composite is also an add-on to ordinary review, not an alternative to D or C, while section 6 drops it entirely. The 12-minute-old score of 72 is never treated as uncertainty or as a reranking trigger, despite being the fact that keeps the case over the second-review threshold.
