6.8

The policy clears the main traps: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed as a no-new-evidence refresh, I-07 is reopened on the new log but not given a swarm, and spillovers are priced rather than ignored. The scenario arithmetic that is fully specified mostly checks out, and the monitoring plan is concrete. It is not internally reliable enough to score higher.

The decision rule conflicts with the allocation table. Rule A.2 makes \(T_{\text{SLA}}<2\text{h}\) ineligible, but I-07 is marked eligible. Rule A.3 puts imputed critical telemetry in data-hold, but I-06 is marked eligible. Rule D.4 says wait in triage when \(T_{\text{SLA}}\ge 7\text{h}\) and \(p_{\text{cal}}<0.40\); I-04 is triage, 10h, and \(p_{\text{cal}}=0.395\). The later override is doing the real work and is not applied transparently.

False-alarm accounting switches between \((1-p)B\) and full \(B\) without a stated rule, so several headline figures are not reproducible. I-04’s \(\sim€7{,}700\) matches haircut midpoint ARR and expected burden, but the cited \(+€3{,}550\) floor is \(0.10\times 60{,}000-450-2{,}000\), with no haircut. I-02’s \(\sim€2{,}950\) matches neither midpoint treatment (about \(€2{,}560\) with the stated haircut, about \(€3{,}050\) without it). I-08’s write-up is arithmetically wrong: at low ARR, \(0.05\times 100{,}000-450-0.655\times 5{,}000=+€1{,}275\), not \(−€450\). The negative figure appears only if the full \(€5{,}000\) burden is subtracted while the 65% probability is merely mentioned.

Calibration is counted twice. Shifting \(p\) and also multiplying the supplied causal ARR by \(p_{\text{cal}}/p_{\text{now}}\) treats an observational bias as if it were a proportional causal effect. Combined with a hard \(p_{\text{cal}}\ge 0.38\) gate, that double penalty is what sidelines I-08, the \(€100{,}000\) case, even though midpoint value can clear a \(€2{,}000\) shadow price. I-04 is likewise knife-edge: overprediction of 0.12, inside the stated range, puts \(p_{\text{cal}}\) at 0.36 and below the gate. No endpoint sensitivity is shown for the case they rank first.

I-02 does not survive its own lower bound. Haircut low ARR net of burden and about \(€2{,}000\) spillover is negative, yet it is selected because the diagnosis window is closing. That urgency is real, but the rule says intervene only if \(\text{NEV}>0\), and the answer never states whether that test is midpoint, lower bound, or base-rate expected value. The adverse row then shows the chosen three-slot portfolio can increase breaches and lose money, without a risk preference that justifies committing anyway or a pre-committed scale-down if congestion is already high.

The shadow price is also under-tested. \(€15{,}000\text{–}€20{,}000\) per untreated diagnosis case is anchored partly on cases being treated. Section 5 says spillover falls on lower-value untreated groups; if those values are nearer SME levels, the shadow price is closer to \(€1{,}000\) than \(€2{,}000\) and several Wait decisions reverse. Adverse spillover of \(0.025\text{–}0.04\) is asserted from the 15–30 minute delay, and 0.04 exceeds the problem’s 0.01–0.03 model. The same \(€3{,}060\text{–}€4{,}750\) cost band is pasted into the optimistic row, where charging the maximum false-alarm burden contradicts upper-half risk reduction. The unexplained \(€2{,}100\) adverse burden is not auditable.

Non-monetary obligations are thin: the reserve slot is sensible, but public-sector duty is still just euro value. Fairness safeguards (concave value, segment floors, UCB) are not used in today’s linear ranking and are not reconciled with a portfolio of two Strategic Enterprise cases and no SME case. A regression discontinuity at \(\text{NEV}\approx 0\) is not credible at four slots a day. The constants 0.38, 2.0h, 3.5h, and \(€2{,}500\) are undeclared policy choices, not costs derived from the loss function.
