8.1

The quantitative core is exact and the fork–join accounting is sound. Case components, the flow identity \(T^{up}+T^{fj}+T^{down}+S_F\), sync totals (132/52/365), critical frequencies (2/8/2), margins (147 minutes, 120 of them on B), and the Model P cascades (join savings 15 and 92.5; flow savings 5, 122, 73, 171, 176; stacked 268.5) all reconstruct from the log. The double-count argument is the right one: \(Q_i+S_i+W_i=T^{fj}\) on every branch, so summing branches yields \(3T^{fj}\). Influence is correctly separated from mean service, ties are defined properly, and non-critical acceleration is treated as zero at the join. Assumptions A1–A8 and H-U are labeled, and the PM4Py comment correctly refuses to read synchronization out of a completion-time directly-follows graph.

Those strengths do not make the write-up nearly flawless.

The worked F04 example reverses the critical path it has just computed. C is critical with a 7-minute lead over A, so a further delay of C moves `join_ready` one-for-one. The text says that delay would leave the join unchanged. That is true of a delay of A up to 7 minutes, and of a speedup of A, which the same sentence gets right. As written, the claim is false, and it is false in the exact place the task is testing.

The dual-idle claim for F07 is also false and is repeated as evidence. F06 releases F2 at 11:05 and F08 starts F2 at 11:05, so 11:05–11:10 is not a window in which both final servers are idle. F1 is idle while F07 waits; F2 is not. The same interval is cited again in the redesign and in the limits section. The F02 window 09:28–09:40 is real, and F07’s residual of 15 minutes on a free F1 is real, but the stronger “both idle” observation is not in the timestamps.

Two smaller numerical statements do not match the arithmetic beside them. Model P’s mean flow reduction of 10.17 exceeds the mean join reduction of 7.71 by about 2.5 minutes; 29.5 is the difference of the totals (122 − 92.5), not of the means. “Flipping all three” 5-minute margins does not move B’s critical count through {7, 8, 9}: F01 would add one B case and F10 would remove one, so all three flips leave B at 8. The range 7–9 is the range over subsets. B would still rank first after reassignment, but the sensitivity sentence is not the calculation.

The counterfactual-1 row “F02–F10 except none of these are A-critical” is not a readable case specification, and the redesign’s “roughly 8–10 if the final station tracks readiness, 7.7 if only the join is counted” does not line up with the three readings already computed (one-for-one 7.71, Model P 10.17, fixed appointments 0). The identity assertion in the Python is only accidentally correct, because `not x == 0` is parsed as `(not x) == 0` and `False == 0` is true in Python.

The conclusions that survive are still the right ones: B controls the join and the tail through queue plus service, A is the 50-minute floor and usual runner-up, C controls two near-ties, and a large share of the downstream gap is residual lag rather than busy time of the assigned server. The grade is held down by the reversed critical-path sentence, the repeated observation that is not in the log, and the mis-attached aggregates.
