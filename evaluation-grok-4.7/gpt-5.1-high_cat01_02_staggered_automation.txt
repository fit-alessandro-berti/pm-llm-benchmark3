6.6

The write-up is complete, numerically careful on its chosen windows, and appropriately refuses a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It still has material failures of quantitative discipline, internal consistency, and design logic.

The headline Gamma result is an artifact of the window. Gamma’s April–June median (~43.7 h) already embeds the May product-change jump; July–August (~37.2 h) only returns Gamma to its January–March level (~37.5 h). Delta and Epsilon also improve from the May–June peak to July–August. Section 7 and the claim ladder still treat the ~6.5 h drop as the strongest support for a benefit. That overstates both the level change and how specific it is to automation.

Escalation is scored too confidently and on the wrong contrast. Alpha/Beta rise about 2.6–3.1 points versus a January–March baseline that mixes in the pre-automation fraud-desk step, while Delta and Epsilon rise about 1.8–2.1 points over the same calendar windows. The adopter-minus-control gap is roughly 1 point, not the “+2 to +4” interval given confidence 60. The April rework drop (Alpha 13.2→11.2, Beta 12.8→10.4) is also erased by starting the post window in June, so the rework narrative is window-dependent in the opposite direction.

The claim ladder contradicts the stated uncertainty. Section 5/7 put the median effect in about −15% to +5% and call the sign unclear after confounding; section 10 then says automation “likely reduces” median cycle time by single-digit percentages. Those bounds are also not calculated from explicit alternative scenarios.

The code does not implement the primary strategy. Relative time uses day-counts with floor division, so negative lags collide (March is coded like February for the April cohort). The regressions are static two-way fixed effects in `automation` and in `automated_fast_path_pct`, not the relative-month event study, and they condition on arrivals per FTE without treating that as a post-treatment routing outcome. The intensity specification does not hold availability fixed, despite estimand E2.

The rollout choice is directionally right, but within-branch randomization shares specialist and fraud queues, so it cannot identify the tail and congestion effects the mechanism section treats as first-order. Workload is never quantified even though Alpha’s arrivals per FTE rise on the order of 30% while the median stays flat—the central capacity-versus-substitution comparison.
