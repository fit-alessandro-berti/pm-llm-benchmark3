5.5

The write-up has the required sections, rejects a fatigue-only story, and correctly blocks punishment and dropping quality checks. The central causal reading is still wrong, and several analyses would not identify the mechanisms they name.

The sequence table does not show service time falling because the queue rises. From positions 1–5 through 31–50, mean service time falls from 18.8 to 12.9 minutes while the queue falls from 46 to 31, complexity and high-priority share fall, and error and rework stay flat or improve. Most of the speed-up is in that window. Only after position 50 does the queue jump (31 to 62 to 88) while service time falls a further 2.1 minutes and error/rework jump to 7.9% and 13.7%. Queue pressure explains the late tail, not the continuous decline. Section 2 asserts the opposite and then dismisses learning, even though later low-WIP error is essentially unchanged for seniors and mid-level staff and slightly better for juniors. That within-experience contrast is the cleanest evidence that speed can rise without a quality loss, and the narrative does not use it.

The same section says late errors are mainly leftover complex cases. Late bins have the lowest complexity (0.43). High WIP raises error in both complexity bands (low: 2.1% to 5.8%; high: 4.2% to 10.9%). It also says early selection creates an illusion that service time is stable. Early service time is not stable. Batching, priority mix, and bot corrections are absent from the integrated account. The mechanism table is more careful than the narrative, but several “against” cells are cautions rather than limiting evidence, and the circadian signature treats shift differences as clock-time effects.

The proposed code measures the wrong outcome. Grouping and correlating `minutes since shift start` by sequence position is nearly tautological and is not service time. `DataFrame.corr` is called incorrectly, and a negative correlation with elapsed time would describe when hard cases arrive, not what drives duration. `filter_time_range` is used as a time-of-day filter, the bot rework slice is chained incorrectly, and `view_events_distribution_graph` is treated as an arbitrary scatter. There is no within-resource model of service time and error on position with complexity, priority, queue, and shift held fixed, and no experience-by-WIP interaction, despite those being the listed identification checks. Arrival versus finish rate is never used, so the day-shift queue build (38 to 75) is not separated from early-shift clearance (61 to 42).

Intervention 2 is a Strong Yes aimed at an “early error spike.” Early error is 3.4%, then about 2.5%, against 7.9% at the end. Moving complex work out of the first hour risks putting it into the high-WIP cell that already has a 10.9% error rate; seniors taking hard cases early looks protective. Random assignment is rejected for the wrong operational risk. Micro-breaks are called low risk in the same row that says they increase throughput pressure.

The dashboard omits waiting time, one of the required indicators, and does not define denominators, shift and experience slices, or how to read service time jointly with WIP and complexity so the early warm-up is not relabeled as pressure. Ethics coverage is the strongest part, but it does not fix the misread of the event table.
