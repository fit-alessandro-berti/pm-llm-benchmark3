The analysis is thorough and often well judged, but it is not nearly flawless. Several conclusions contradict the answer’s own rules, and the checking code mis-states authority for events after the delegation was registered.

Material defects:

- D-QM-01’s QMS row exists from 12 August 2026 09:15. CHG-501 at 11 August 09:00 is before that row; CHG-503 at 12 August 11:30 is after it. Calling CHG-503 “conditional, same as CHG-501” breaks the answer’s own rule that a later registry row fails to prove earlier authority. The code makes this worse by storing `registered_pre_use=False` on the whole delegation and labeling every later in-scope Ben approval as “registry entry post-dates use.”

- Section 3 leaves CHG-509 indeterminate because Asha’s absence on 15 August is unevidenced. Section 4 then says Eva approved CHG-509 outside her authority.

Code quality is below the narrative. `cooling_system` is described as unresolved and then returned as `INVALID`. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is used, but its keep-versus-drop semantics are only assumed. After CHG-503’s verifier is renamed to an `UNRESOLVED...` label, four-eyes treats that label as a different resource and can report separation as satisfied. `discover_handover_of_work_network` and `discover_working_together_network` are called with direct resource and case keywords that the usual PM4Py wrappers do not accept; the exceptions are swallowed and pandas is used instead. The four reciprocal training approvals are background pattern evidence, not a contradiction of this extract. Eva’s Independent Verifier role ends 11 August 23:59, and later verifications are scored only as different-human RR2 passes. Control recommendations cover the requested topics and the priority investigations are sensible. The score is held down by contradictory holdings and by authority logic the code does not actually implement.
