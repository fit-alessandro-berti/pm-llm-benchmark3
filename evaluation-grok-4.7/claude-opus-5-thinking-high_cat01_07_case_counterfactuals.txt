7.2

The write-up meets the required structure: partial orders, declarative constraints, one counterfactual row per case, the four derivation subsections with the requested labels, a capacity table plus a rollout discussion inside the word bounds, a full ranking, pilots for the top two interventions, a synthesis inside the word bounds, and exactly eight labeled assumptions. It also gets the main qualitative distinctions right. K-104 and K-411 are treated as timing changes, K-219 as a resource substitution, and K-337 as a change of decision policy. Final compliance still follows the last assessment, four-eyes is kept (A1 assesses, C1 reviews), originals still gate document checks, and earlier readiness is repeatedly separated from earlier completion at A2, C1, and M1. Interference on the single certified reviewer, D2’s slower service, induced rework, assessment displacement, the Friday–weekend effect, and false concurrency are all identified.

The quantitative layer is not reliable enough for a higher score.

K-104’s best case is temporally impossible on the answer’s own clock. Register starts Mon 08:10 and factual completion is Wed 18:40 (58.5 h). A decision at Wed 08:30 cannot finish before about 48.5–49 h, yet the best case is given as 44–47 h while M1 is said to sign at Wed 08:30. The lower end of the 47–51 h base band has the same problem. Those figures are hour-subtractions that were not checked against the propagated timestamps.

K-219 repeats the failure. Section 3’s base case is 38–44 h with a 4–10 h saving while the decision “waits for Wed 08:30.” From Mon 09:00, that window closes the case at about 48 h, a saving of roughly 0–1 h. The case table states that window-binding outcome correctly as a 0–2 h saving, so the derivation contradicts the table. The assessment interval is also inconsistent: the table shows Tue 10:00–14:00, while the prose has a start in that range and completion as late as 16:30. A Tue 10:00 assessment start is also aggressive against the Tue 08:00 A2 backlog of about 6 h.

K-411’s best case puts A2 on Tue 20:00–22:30 because that is when A2 worked K-219. Under a single-case change with other behavior held fixed, that interval is already occupied, and the same Wed 08:30 M1 slot is already used by K-219. The realistic 0–2 h base case is coherent; the 24 h best case is not. The claimed naive gain of 5.7 h does not match identity-plus-handoff on this trace (about 3.5–5.4 h depending on the anchor).

The 100-case compliance argument conflicts with itself. Capacity of 35–40 h and 25–30 final reviews implies utilization above 1 even if review time falls from 4.5 h to 3.5 h (30 × 3.5 h is still far above 40 h). The text first says C1 saturates regardless of scheduling, then says a 20% service cut makes the policy positive-sum through an M/M/1 ρ/(1−ρ) effect. That formula does not stabilize an overloaded server. Packet-completeness thresholds also wander: reversal below 60%, table break-even 60–70%, assumption 7 and the ranking note at 80%.

K-337’s decision ranges of 50–65%, 15–30%, and 10–20% are not a coherent distribution. “Unchanged clean approval” sits poorly with the requirement to record the discrepancy. “Evidence accepted” on loop 3 does not show that the extra confirmation changed the decision; that overclaim is then used in the ranking and the synthesis. The partial order allows the preliminary C1 screen to overlap the final C1 review, and the symbol for non-substitution is undefined. The tail in which S1 “issues” Request More Info goes beyond rule 6, which limits that action to Assessment or Compliance Review. “D1 idles” is a poor side effect while D1’s own snapshot queue is 11–13 h. Discrete M1 windows at 08:30, 14:00, and 18:00 are treated as a hard constraint; K-104’s own trace, with compliance ending 17:40 and the decision at 18:00, is better read as prompt signing once work is ready.

The ranking, pilots, and assumption audit are still substantively strong: K-411 is preferred for reversibility and log integrity, K-337 is last because it changes admissibility of the decision, and the pilots measure readiness and compliance-segment time rather than only end-to-end cycle time. Those strengths do not repair the impossible timestamps or the capacity contradiction.
