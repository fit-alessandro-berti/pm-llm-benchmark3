The design is complete and largely constraint-aware, but several numerical, accounting, and consistency errors keep it well short of a tight solution.

Hard safety choices in the roster are mostly right: security stays at skill 3, final approval is not signed at skill 1, no urgent or high case goes to an unsupervised trainee, quality batching respects the 24h rule, and the skill-0 block on Eva and Jonas for C015 is correctly enforced. The seven required sections, the scoring factors, the worker-facing note, and the audit indicator list are all present, and the slack order C002 through C013 matches remaining SLA time. Weights sum to 1.

The comparison section mis-counts and mis-simulates the baselines. Under the specialist-only rule as the answer itself applies it (highest skill, ties by the initial open-case snapshot), owner changes are C001, C003, C005, C006, C008, C009, C010, C014, and C016 — nine, not eight. The omitted case is C001, the urgent high-complexity, high-handover move. On first-available, a static lowest-open-cases reading does not give “Farid and Celine the rest”: Farid’s security skill is 0, and every activity he can do, Jonas can also do with fewer open cases, so Farid receives nothing and Celine receives the three security cases. That reading also ignores the baseline’s own “enough hours” clause; Jonas’s 6.5 hours cannot absorb the non-security queue. “Roughly 13 of 15” owner changes is likewise off (14 under that static reading). The later claim that the proposed policy has the lowest SLA risk is hard to sustain: Farid still holds both C002 (2h) and C011 (4h), the same sequencing exposure the answer treats as a specialist-only weakness, and C010’s finalization is added on top.

C010 also cuts against the answer’s own scarcity rule. Approval level 3 is Farid alone, complexity is 0.38, and skill 2 is allowed to finalize. Bruno (owner, skill 2, 20h) or Eva (skill 2) fits “save the specialist for cases that need level 3” better than a prepare/finalize split onto Farid. The 20% training claim is similarly soft. Of the nine normal/low cases, the only hands-on documented match is C009. C015 is observation by people at security skill 0, which the scoring function never marks as training (`train_score` requires a performing trainee in a non-solo mode). C010 is not Jonas’s documented priority. Counted strictly, the share is 1/9, below the stated floor; the 22% figure is produced by counting shadowing. The batch checker that was supposed to repair a share under 20% is not actually applied.

Smaller internal breaks add up. Component scores are described as lying in [0, 1], but continuity and fairness can go negative. The 0.05 tie band is stated and then not implemented. H5’s half-count for a supervisor disagrees with the batch summary, which charges Farid a full assignment for finalizing C010. H7 is declared satisfied with no service-time estimates. The “~45 min” C001 buffer does not follow from a 2.7h median handover penalty on a 3h SLA, and “≈4 of 5 hours” for Eva is an unshown figure. C014 keeps new work with high-overtime David even though Hugo is skill 3 on rework, handover cost is only medium, and complexity is 0.52; the write-up logs this as an exception rather than showing that no safer alternative exists.

The PM4Py sketch names the right functions, but the audit path would not measure the policy as specified. `assignment_reason` is filtered on codes the policy never writes (`sla_override`, `overload`, `loop_break`). `level="event"` drops non-matching events, so the rework rate on that filtered frame cannot see Rework Correction. The skill-matrix join ignores `valid_from` / `valid_to`. Shifting the previous event timestamp into `start_timestamp` mixes waiting and service under the answer’s own two-event assumption. `min_batch_size=3` would also miss the recommended C006/C012 pair.

Section 5 is clear and non-punitive, and the indicator set in section 6 covers the requested outcomes. Those strengths are real. They do not repair the baseline arithmetic, the training-share inflation, or the gap between the stated scoring rules and the Farid/C010/C001 choices.
