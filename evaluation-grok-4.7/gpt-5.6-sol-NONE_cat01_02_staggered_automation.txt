The answer is conceptually strong and structurally complete, but it is not nearly flawless. Quantitative mistakes and one factual rollout error are large enough to block a high score.

What holds up: availability is separated from uptake, selection, and co-occurring shocks; no causal claim appears in the descriptive section. The estimands cover intention-to-treat, intensity, median versus tail, rework/escalation, satisfaction, dynamics, heterogeneity, and fraud-desk interference, and they correctly treat the automated fast path as an outcome-defined route. The preferred design avoids already-treated controls, rejects a single two-way fixed-effects coefficient, and treats five branches as a sensitivity problem. Mechanisms, the threat list, the refusal of an “X%” claim, and the phased Delta-versus-Epsilon evaluation are well matched to the decision.

What does not: several reported contrasts are arithmetically wrong. Equal-branch Delta/Epsilon escalation is about 8.07 to 9.89 (+1.82 pp), not 8.23 to 9.77 (+1.53). Rework’s untreated post mean is about 14.52, not 14.63. Gamma versus Delta/Epsilon is about −3.8 median hours, −7.1 p90 hours, and −2.1 rework points, not roughly −4.4, −4.7, and −0.8. August versus March is about −3.8 hours for Alpha/Beta and +1.7 for the controls, a difference-in-differences near −5.5 hours; “6.25 versus 1.45, again about 4.8” does not match those components. Delta also does not have more productive capacity than Epsilon: August staffing is 23 versus 27 FTE.

The code would reproduce the main early-cohort median, p90, and satisfaction arithmetic, but its relative-time regression is still the naive two-way specification the prose rejects, and Gamma’s distant leads are dumped into the reference period. Sticky repeated rework and escalation values, and the cleaner April escalation break before the May complexity shock, are underused. The scenario intervals are judgmental rather than calculated. Directional conclusions mostly survive, which is why the score stays in the 7s; the evidence section is not reliable enough to go higher.
