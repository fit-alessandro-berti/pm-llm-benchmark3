5.5

The conclusions specify a coherent OCEL design: singular types (`Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`), timezone-aware timestamps, payment amounts on events, shared event IDs for the joint stock reservation and for packing P1, and O2O links that match the stated containment, packing, carriage, billing, and payment facts. The intended integrity checks, the choice to keep propagated links out of source E2O rows, and the flattening predictions for Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment are largely consistent with that design. O200 closing before O100, and the identification of the shared reservation, P1 packing, and V1 as the consequential convergence points, also match the narrative.

The required deliverable is not actually produced. There is no single executable code block and no finished sections 1–8. The semantic-check list stops around the third assertion, short of the six required.

Remaining technical problems in the settled choices:
- M2 is labeled `completes_settlement` only by assuming an unstated invoice total of 250. The narrative calls both M1 and M2 partial payments that together settle V1.
- The source-versus-inference policy is applied unevenly. Packing P2 is given a direct `for_order` link to O100, and delivery confirmation is given a direct package link, while dispatch is refused the same kind of inferred order link.
- The OC-DFG and OTG discussion treats P1, I1, I3, O100, and O200 as nodes of those graphs. Those artifacts are type-level; the instance convergence belongs to the relations and the interaction summary.
- The Order DFG is reduced to one chain. Under the chosen relations, O100 also yields Add Item → Add Item and Pack Package → Pack Package, which that chain omits.
- The commented object-centric Petri-net view indexes a discovery result as a `(net, im, fm)` tuple without a reliable basis for that shape.

The alternative relation policies and the eight failure modes are the right topics, but they stay at the level of a plan rather than a finished analysis.
