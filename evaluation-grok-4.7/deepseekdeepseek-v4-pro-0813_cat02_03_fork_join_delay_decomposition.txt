8.2

The case arithmetic, fork-join identity, critical-branch counts, and the three counterfactuals are internally consistent and match the timestamps. B is correctly ranked ahead of A despite A’s larger mean service, partial savings when the next branch becomes critical are handled, and downstream waiting is treated as something that can absorb branch gains. Required sections and the minimum case table are present.

Points come off for issues that a strict reading should not ignore. Summing branch synchronization waits is explained mainly by showing that queue + service + sync across three branches equals three times the stage; the F02 “double-count” illustration then just adds slack onto an already complete stage, so the required overlap argument stays loose. Synchronization waits are defined and averaged, but they are not shown case by case, including in the four worked examples, so that part of the decomposition is not directly auditable from the table. The PM4Py block calls `discover_performance_dfg` with `case_id` rather than `case_id_key` and hides failure in `try/except`, so the required performance call is not reliably executable. The ~57% final-station utilization depends on a 09:08–12:40 window that is stated but not tested against other availability windows, and half-minute counterfactual savings are not reconciled with one-minute timestamps. Observations, assumptions, and recommendations are separated in practice, but not labeled as systematically as the task asked.
