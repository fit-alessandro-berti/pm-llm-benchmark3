7.5

The final recommendation is substantively strong: unlawful shortcuts are ranked last, second-officer review is treated as mandatory, rescreening is not treated as a substitute, P-901 spillover is priced in, and the S4-at-35 versus S4-at-65 reranking is directionally right. It is not nearly flawless.

The repeated timing model is wrong. Action C gives 35–75 minutes for review, then 5–15 minutes to record the decision and, if approved, execute. The answer adds Action A’s separate 10–15 minute execution time on top of that 5–15 minute step. That error is carried into the table (C earliest t50 and latest t105; composite earliest t80–85; S4-at-65 earliest t115) and into the continuation traces. Under the stated ranges, C’s worst case is 90 minutes, so the claim that C’s on-time result is materially uncertain is overstated.

The abandon-review cutoff is also inconsistent with the deadline. Even on the answer’s own minimum path, a review must start by about t40 to finish by t90; on the correct reading, by about t50. Section 6 still waits until t60–70 before moving to rejection or hold, which is already too late to preserve the deadline, and that shift is not explained.

The dominance claim is slightly loose: a €100k regulatory lower bound does not by itself dominate unless unlawful execution is sufficiently likely. The later hard-constraint treatment of A and B repairs the conclusion. The t30 S2 trigger is reasonable but not derived, and “medium-high” speed confidence for C sits awkwardly next to the answer’s own t105 upper bound.

Conformance mapping, four-eyes, SAR limits, the conditional status of F and G, and the resource analysis are otherwise careful and complete.
