4.5

The write-up follows the required section order and states the fast-path paradox, but it is not a defensible synthesis. Several load-bearing figures are misread, improvement mechanisms are attached to the wrong artifacts, and the comparison table does not actually compare directions.

Diagnosis errors are material. Auto-screen cost is EUR 0.20; EUR 34/hour is manual-review labor, so “EUR 0.20 + EUR 34 for auto screen” is false. The 30-hour mean is Repair quote → Customer approval, not Repair quote → complaint (80 cases, no duration given). “5–13 hours Premium vs 10–16 hours Standard” is not in the manual-review or segment data. Non-fitting traces are 32% because fitting traces are 68% at log fitness 0.91; that is not a measured R1/R2/R3 violation rate. Precision being low is omitted. The residual fact that fast approval is still 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits is quoted and then dropped.

Redesign A targets the right speed-tolerant segments, then breaks its own logic. Premium plus Standard low-risk share is 43%, but the EUR 200/EUR 100 rules do not imply that 43% is newly auto-approved, and most of that volume is already on the auto path (3600 to auto screen, 2550 to fast approval). The 30–45% figure is the evidence portal’s effect on repeated evidence requests, not wrong-part detection. A part-compatibility gate does not implement R2, and Premium low-risk accessories are the explicit R2 exception. Adding 6–18 hours to an 8-hour median can cross the 24-hour service-credit cliff; that trade-off is absent. The portal abandonment risk is assigned to a design that has no portal. “AI” also contradicts the table’s “Low” complexity.

Redesign B is a different architecture, but the mechanism does not match the constraint. A 30–50% cycle-time cut is unsupported. Parallel manual and fraud review consumes the two scarce pools (7 reviewers at an 82% queue cliff; 2 fraud specialists, Monday/campaign bottleneck) without creating capacity. The fast-approval branch can still pass a >EUR 400 claim on the auto screen, so R1 is not enforced. Repair-quote delay is attributed in the evidence to part-price confirmation, which this flow does not change, while 48- and 72-hour SLAs are asserted against medians of 96 and 120 hours. A post-fulfillment audit cannot prevent the Dispatch replacement → complaint tail (mean 96h, median 70h, p90 180h) or the EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost.

Redesign C overlaps A’s gate and B’s risk routing and adds generic predictive scoring. The 40–60% complaint reduction and “>85%” model accuracy are invented. Status visibility is the tolerance condition for Standard low-risk customers up to 72 hours; the design spends it on Premium customers, whose binding problem is the 24-hour credit cliff and, for high-value devices, 22% fast-path complaints versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path. Complaint edges are long-tailed, so detection “within 24 hours” does not match the observed timing and does not by itself cut complaint incidence or escalation-board load. R3 (reopen before close or denial must pass manual eligibility) is never operationalized. Path outcomes are also treated as causal despite segment and defect mix.

The Pareto table has the requested columns, but the directional signs are blank, so cycle time, cost, quality, compliance, experience, and resource pressure cannot be read. “Dominated? No” for A and C overclaims resolvability. Failure modes meet the two-plus-one count, yet A’s critical credit-cliff failure and the 15–35% manual-inflow risk from rule changes are missed; an 85% gate threshold is fabricated beside the given 82% cliff.

The experiment plan is sequenced, but it is not a valid learning design. Phase 5 calls 3600 cases the fast-approval path; 3600 is Intake → Auto eligibility screen, while fast approval is 2550 + 240. Phase 3 tracks high-value cycle time and compliance and omits the 22% vs 5% complaint contrast that justifies the pilot. Phase 4’s EUR 140/150/160 cost gates have no baseline. Thresholds for abandonment, service-credit incidence, and fraud utilization are likewise unsupported. Observation windows ignore p90 complaint lags of 150–180 hours, there is no holdout or segment-stratified end-to-end cost (reshipment, credit, conformance replay), and stacked pilots on overlapping volume confound attribution. “Shifted downstream” is named once and not measured.
