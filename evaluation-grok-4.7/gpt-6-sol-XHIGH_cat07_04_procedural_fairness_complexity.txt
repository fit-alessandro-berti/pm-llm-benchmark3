The answer is constraint-compliant, numerically careful, and well calibrated: it separates raw gaps from adjusted residuals, treats support steps as possible burdens without recommending their removal, and refuses to treat risk scores or case mix as a full justification. The bottom line matches the evidence.

It is not nearly flawless. Need indicators are never reconciled with path shares: about 57% of Group B needs language support but only 24% sit on V4, and about 62% of Group C request accommodation but only 31% sit on V5. Those ratios are similar to Group A’s (~50%), so the omission does not overturn the conclusion, but a fairness reading should have tested under-delivery versus compositional mix. Appeal rates also exceed V6 shares (for example, 13% vs 8% for B and 10% vs 5% for C) without comment. The timeliness discussion never uses the 90th percentiles (21.4, 34.2, 38.8, and 29.5 days), which are much larger than the adjusted average gaps. Identity-verification waits and the in-person note, plus the modestly longer fraud-review wait for D, are unused. “Verified risk” overstates `risk_score_band`, and “specialists doubling as supervisors” overgeneralizes two of five specialists. “Only part of the log” is misleading for B and C, whose listed variants cover about 99% and 98%.

The PM4Py plan names the right techniques and correctly warns that one timestamp cannot separate service from queue time. The sketch would not run: groups are filtered as `"A"` rather than `"Group A"`, result dictionaries are never initialized, and several calls rely on undefined placeholders. Section 2 covers disparity, legitimate routing, and uncertainty, but it never cleanly separates those from discrimination as distinct inferential claims. Rework rates are mentioned once and then dropped.
