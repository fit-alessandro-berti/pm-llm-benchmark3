6.5

The final write-up is a real survival/process-mining analysis: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72, competing risks kept separate from right censoring, E1 (`1 - F_Resolve(72)`) tied to SLA reporting and `S(72)` tied to workload, withdrawals not treated as harmless censoring, and `Escalate` / final rework / full prefixes correctly marked as leakage. Aalen–Johansen as primary, with landmarking and a multi-state view as secondary, fits n = 32. The code keeps open cases, stratifies by cohort × priority, and the CIF update order is right.

It is not nearly flawless. Counts are wrong: AutoCheck-immediately-after-Submit is 12/17 in B and 0/17 in A, not “9/17” and “S01 in A.” S01 is `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck`. “Cohort B has three transfers (S21, S32, plus S08 in A)” is false; B has two. The state diagram omits the observed `Rework→Resolve` edge (S27).

Assumptions B and C are the same encoding (`cause = 4` at `min(t, 72)`), so C is not the upper bound the text claims. S32 transferred at 96 h is written as a transfer event at 72 h. At 72 h that case was still internal; the cap uses post-horizon information and pulls `S(72)` down. Resolved-after-72 is called “not a censored observation” and then censored at 72. Those match only for the value of the CIF exactly at 72, which is never stated.

The long-gap sensitivity (S09, S11, S25, S26) does lower `S(72)`. It does not automatically raise E1. Those four cases are already known to be unresolved at 72; recoding them as earlier competing exits shrinks the later risk set and can increase `F_Resolve`. “Raises the breach estimate” is not justified. Fast exits (S05 at 9 h, S12 at 15 h, S23 at 11 h) are also not “slow” cases removed by completed-case analysis.

`parse_event_log_string` is used, but case ids are prepended onto the activity list, so variants are not the true prefixes. Landmark alerts are recommended in §§4 and 7 and then, correctly, called infeasible without event times in §9.
