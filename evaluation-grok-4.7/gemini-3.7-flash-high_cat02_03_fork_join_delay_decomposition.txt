6.8

The fork-join accounting is mostly right, and the case table, worked examples, critical-branch counts, and per-case counterfactual savings check out. The write-up still has concrete numerical errors, one internal contradiction, and an overstated downstream-queue conclusion.

Definitions are sound. Upstream, branch queue, service, elapsed time, `join_ready = max(end_i)`, synchronization slack, downstream wait, final service, and flow time are specified correctly, and the identity `flow = upstream + max(queue_i + service_i) + downstream + final service` holds on every case. The double-counting argument is the right one: each branch’s queue + service + sync equals the same stage length, so summing branches multiplies wall-clock time by three, and synchronization slack is concurrent idle time.

Sections 2 and the counterfactual case rows are accurate. All 12 decompositions match the timestamps, including F02, F04, F07, and F11. Critical branches are A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, and B on the other eight, with no exact ties. A 20% cut in A service saves 5 minutes on F01 and 10 on F11 (15 total). Halving B’s queue saves 92.5 minutes in total. Zeroing downstream wait saves 176 minutes, or 14.67 per case. The code implements those definitions, breaks ties deterministically, and correctly treats synchronization as a custom max calculation rather than a directly-follows delay.

The aggregate layer is not reliable. Several reported standard deviations do not match the series: B elapsed is about 13.71 (sample), not 13.54; C elapsed about 12.34, not 11.96; A sync about 8.38, not 8.74; C sync about 16.88, not 14.83. The near-tie rule is applied inconsistently: F04’s 7-minute margin and F02’s 10-minute margin meet the stated ≤10-minute cutoff and are omitted, while other 10-minute cases are included. The CF2 summary says 7 of 12 cases benefit; the same table’s nonzero rows are F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, and F12, which is 8.

The downstream story is the largest reasoning flaw. F04 is still on F2 at 09:52, and F10 is still on F2 at 11:20, so those cases were not idle at `join_ready`. F02, whose server is free for the whole wait, is missing from that list. Under the observed F1/F2 assignment, about 73 of the 176 downstream minutes are residual service of the previous job; about 103 minutes remain after the assigned server is already free. That supports a mix of dedicated-server contention and extra lag. It does not prove that downstream delay is not a queue, and it does not justify treating every downstream minute as procedural waste. The same section also equates stage-time savings with flow-time savings. If `final_start` is held by the assigned server, F11’s 10-minute A improvement is absorbed into downstream wait. That masking check was requested and was not run, which conflicts with the later claim that final start times are policy-driven.

The redesigns inherit those overclaims. Branch B already overlaps on three cases, so prescribing a second fixture as the way to create parallel capacity is not what the intervals show. Cutting B cannot pull the stage tail to 50 minutes while C still finishes at 55 and 57. Pooling F1 and F2 does not by itself deliver 14.67 minutes per case when servers are already idle and the case still waits. SMED is a hypothesized mechanism, not something these timestamps identify.
