7.2

The forecast has the right skeleton: weekend release is impossible, the join is a maximum of the two branches, Friday chemistry time is too short for a normal completion, and the temperature-excursion flag is treated as a common cause. The remaining-hour arithmetic for the stated clock times is consistent (Monday 10:00–12:00 is about 66–69 h; Tuesday midday is about 93 h). Those strengths do not offset several calibration errors.

The unconditional investigation weights are too low. A confirmed temperature excursion alone, probability 0.20–0.40, moves the batch into the 0.30–0.55 investigation regime even with clean assays. Mixed with the baseline 0.04–0.10 rate, an unconditional investigation probability around 0.15–0.25 is defensible, so documentation-only investigations are roughly 0.10–0.18 and laboratory investigations roughly 0.04–0.08. The stated 4–12% and 1–5% bands count mainly the clean-path rate and drop the excursion mass. That makes the P80 (Wednesday–Thursday) and the severe tail too light relative to the process rules used elsewhere.

The P50 is not coherent with the scenario probabilities. The smooth path is given only 35–50%, and its own center is already Monday evening–Tuesday midday, with a tail to Wednesday. The mixture median therefore sits later than that smooth center, around Tuesday afternoon to Wednesday morning once queries, reruns, and excursion-triggered deviations are folded in. Section 4 instead reports the smooth-path center as the overall P50.

The no-authorization microbiology band is wrong. Authorization 0.25–0.45 implies no weekend readout with probability 0.55–0.75, not 45–60%.

The contamination envelope is late at the early end. A 46 h incubation is ready Sunday 12:00; with on-call authorization, a 1 h readout, a 20 h confirmation, and a 0.5 h second readout, a usable result can exist Monday about 09:307.1

The forecast is structurally strong: it uses the parallel join as a maximum, respects laboratory and QA calendars, separates queue time from service, and treats the temperature excursion as a common cause. Several numerical and consistency problems keep it well short of nearly flawless.

The microbiology clock is right at the start. Friday 14:00 plus 46–54 h is Sunday 12:00–20:00, and a Sunday result still cannot be released because QA does not work Sunday and chemistry is closed. Partial Friday chemistry and the Monday 08:00 resume are also handled correctly. Saturday QA capacity is correctly applied to the future queue rather than to B-417 itself.

The clearest arithmetic error is the contamination clock. A Monday 08:30–09:30 readout plus 20–30 h is technically ready about Tuesday 04:30–15:30, not “Tuesday morning–early Wednesday.” The Sunday-start confirmation window is similarly shifted late. The central Tuesday-afternoon to Wednesday-afternoon terminal band is only partly rescued by later QA time.

The ledger misreads weekend staffing. The scenario says authorized dispatch and readout together take 1–3 h. The ledger adds 0.5–1.5 h of readout on top of that 1–3 h. Section 3’s Sunday 14:00–23:00 window matches the combined 1–3 h, so the ledger and the later calculation disagree.

Scenario probabilities are not coherent with the inputs. No weekend authorization should be about 55–75%, but S3a is given as 45–60%. S3a’s dominant clock is listed as microbiology staffing, while the decomposition correctly says chemistry spillover often sets the Monday join. Those two claims conflict. The illustrative joint-anomaly band of 0.03–0.09 is higher than the product mixture implied by the ledger’s own conditional rates. Documentation-only and laboratory investigation bands of roughly 4–12% and 1–5% look low once a 0.20–0.40 excursion chance is allowed to raise investigation probability to 0.30–0.55 by itself. Calling a confirmed excursion “immaterial” inside the smooth path understates that direct effect.

The smooth-path envelope is also a little tight. Under FIFO, long queued services can leave B-417 unfinished at Monday 18:00 and paused until Tuesday 08:00. The S1 join is capped around Monday 17:00, and its tail is blamed only on QA. Friday progress of “0.2–1.3 h” does not match a 16:40–17:40 start, which leaves about 0.3–1.3 h before 18:00. With a smooth path at only 35–50%, a mixture P50 is more naturally Tuesday morning–afternoon than Monday evening–Tuesday midday; the stated central band is early relative to the answer’s own scenario weights.

The ranked drivers, overlap warning, working-hour versus calendar-day distinction, and rejection caveat are sound. The excursion flag is a defensible primary update, and Friday chemistry start is the right secondary trigger. The answer does not add unconditional means or collapse the forecast to one timestamp, but the readiness error, double-counted weekend readout, and probability inconsistencies are material.
