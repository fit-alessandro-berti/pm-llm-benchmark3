4.5

The high-level split is right: A and B are not visibly equivalent, A is the compliance model, and P3 non-equivalence is witnessed inside the bound. The write-up does not hold up as a formal comparison.

Equivalence definitions are not safe to use. P1 is formalized so that every `Analyze Revision` needs a later `Check Revision`. Model A puts those two labels in parallel, so it accepts `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case`, which the stated OC predicate rejects. P1 also keeps approval counts and positions; the OC rules then allow “all other visible behaviour” arbitrarily often. P2 is defined with multisets of `(#Analyze, hasRework, hasCheck)`. Those multisets differ, because approval placement and check interleaving change the preimage size (for one analysis, A has four visible completions and B five, including `Analyze, Approve, Check`). Section 3 nevertheless calls P2 equivalent under that abstraction. Open/Close never enter the tuple. Duplicate labels are said to be positional, but the illustrating pair is syntactically broken.

The structural table is mostly aimed at the right features, and min/max checks are right (A: one check per iteration; B: exactly one). “Approve = Equal” is not. In A the XOR sits after the parallel join, so approval cannot precede that iteration’s check; in B it can. The A-2 row calls a 10-event trace a 12-event trace. Admission cells in the counterexample table are empty. The four traces themselves are valid both-direction witnesses, including two analysis attempts, and the skip-branch reading of `tau` is fair.

The bounded search is not executable as written. `pm4py.execute_transition(net, marking, t)` is not the PM4Py firing API; classic `execute` is `(transition, net, marking)` and returns a new marking, so this BFS would not move. Soundness is called and ignored. Unused imports and a visited key that includes the whole trace are secondary. The bound logic (≤12 visible events, ≤3 analyses, silent steps free, accept only on the final marking) would be exhaustive if firing were fixed. Section 6 gives no code. It also claims the footprints are essentially the same; they are not: B has the directly-follows edge `Approve Revision → Check Revision`, A does not. Equal footprints or a low EMD would still not prove language equality, and that caution is right. Invented probabilities are correctly refused.

Section 7 reports exact figures “from scripts omitted for brevity,” which the task forbids unless derived. The trees are both 12 nodes and 6 visible leaves, not 15/13 and 6/5. B’s longest operator chain is deeper (`-> > + > * > -> > X`). No label is duplicated in either tree. The 0.61 and 0.55 simplicity scores are unsupported, and they conflict with the claim that B is the simpler net.

The P2 simplification does not preserve P2. `*(Analyze, X(Rework, tau))` uses standard loop semantics: the XOR is the redo, and `tau` continues the loop, so `Open, Analyze, Analyze, Check, Close` is accepted. Neither original can produce two analyses without `Rework Case`, so `(#Analyze≥2, hasRework=0, hasCheck=1)` is new. The preservation table still marks `hasRework` exact, and “soundness verified” is not in the code. `Open, Check, Analyze, Close` is listed as a negative trace, but both originals accept it and P2 deliberately forgets that interleaving. Node count 9 versus the originals is a real reduction; the baseline numbers it is compared with are wrong.

Keep A for the compliance view. Do not replace either original with this simplified tree for the dashboard. Bounded disagreement inside 12 events and 3 analyses proves P3 non-equivalence only; it does not by itself decide the unbounded languages, though the one-check versus one-check-per-iteration gap already does.
