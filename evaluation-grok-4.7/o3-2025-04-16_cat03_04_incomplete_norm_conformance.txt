The answer has the right skeleton and the right top priorities, but it is not close to precise. Several classifications contradict the norm’s own wording, the model families are not reconciled, and at least one observation the task specially protected is simply missing.

The strongest material is the treatment of N3 and T6. Two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` paths are correctly kept as the leading forbidden-behavior finding, with carrier-level containment and a demand for timestamps. The single `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge is correctly kept second, as a real T6 prohibition rather than an unmodeled curiosity. N6, T4, T5, the seven `object_codeath` edges, and Manual Compliance Review are not promoted into violations merely because they are rare. The 4 write-offs plus 14 invoices with no successor are arithmetically aligned with 110 − 92, and N5 is rightly treated as typical rather than mandatory. Section 1’s closed-world statement is broadly right: only forbidden relations, and mandatory relations inside scope, license a noncompliance inference. The N1 discussion correctly warns that an “eventually validate” rule is not a directly-follows rule. The remediation plan has the five requested tracks and does not escalate reopen, codeath, or compliance review.

Those successes are undercut by inconsistent application of the same principles.

The table omits `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 orders). That is the clearest unmodeled-but-legitimate OC-DFG path: N2 expressly allows an intermediate invoice. It also omits `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3 orders) and the OTG edge `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` (weight 100). The three review counts line up across OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT and are the obvious recorded explanation for part of the N1 directly-follows gap. The context’s “unrecorded review” hypothesis is weaker once those three recorded successors are noticed. The answer never makes that link.

`Validate Order -> Close Order` is reported as “83/100 (17% deviation).” The 100 is the creation count, not a validated-order denominator, and a missing directly-follows arc is not a failure to close. N2 expects invoice, review, or fulfilment events in between. Calling the shortfall a deviation rate turns a typical pattern into a quota. The same false precision reappears as “within five business events” and a “>2%” monitoring threshold. Two of 200 packages is 1%, so that monitor would not fire on the N4 gap the plan itself cares about.

T1 and T2 are marked fulfilled because the aggregate edges exist at weights 1450 and 200. That is the wrong reading of a mandatory edge. T1 is required for an Add Item interaction, yet Add Item–Order is 1448 against Add Item–Item at 1450. T2 is required for a Dispatch Package interaction, yet Dispatch Package–Delivery is 198 against 200 dispatch events. Edge presence at type level does not establish that every in-scope event carries the relation. Section 3 states that an OTG cannot see multiplicity within cases, then Section 4 ignores that limit. The 200-versus-198 cross-family contradiction is never explained, despite the task asking how a relation can conform in one abstraction and fail in another.

E8 is mis-weighted. The exception applies only to events explicitly typed as unapplied cash, and the snapshot says none are so typed. Under the norm as written, the one Payment without an Invoice is a missing mandatory relation with high normative confidence. Business legitimacy depends on a future label or a norm change. The answer inverts this: deviation class “missing mandatory OR unapplied cash,” confidence Low. Finance’s comment is a completeness issue, not a current exception.

The Sensor Heartbeat row contradicts itself. Deviation class is data-quality error, while the business interpretation says the link violates segregation. Both cannot be the primary conclusion. If the reused identifier produced a false join, no forbidden business event occurred and severity should be the integrity risk of that join. If the link is real, E10 is forbidden behavior. Section 8 then calls the join error “almost certainly” true, although the source only says it is suspected.

N4 is classified in the table as straightforward missing mandatory behavior, “dispatched without packing,” but Section 8 treats the same two packages as possibly compliant under an eventually-follows reading. The norm text attaches the directly-follows approximation warning to N1, not N4, so equating them needs an argument the answer never supplies. The two missing deliveries are likewise forced into missing mandatory behavior even though the carrier reference is already the competing explanation; that is a modeling gap, not a scan failure.

Section 1 says the seven labels are mutually exclusive. The table then uses compound classes, and Section 4 adds condition-dependent buckets. The conditions that are stated cover only N1 and E8. They omit the conditions that actually change the class of N4, E6, E10, and T6.

The code meets the named-call and raw-output requirements only on the surface. Conforming mandatory edges, present typical edges, and absent forbidden edges all fall through to `"unregulated"`. `norm_ocdfg`, `norm_otg`, and `norm_etot` are both the graphs passed to conformance and the status dictionaries, so the comment about keeping status metadata outside the bare graph is not implemented. Categories the prose considers essential — scope, abstraction mismatch, data quality, insufficient evidence — are absent. Only edges already inside `"checked_edges"` are classified, so an unexpected observed edge never becomes “unregulated” unless the diagnostic already named it.

The six norm gaps are real enough to satisfy the count, but several are weak. E10 is already forbidden and T5 is already unspecified; restating them crowds out sharper holes: what “standard Order” means, how “intended for external shipment” is decided, whether N3’s order-lifecycle arc establishes a package dispatch, snapshot right-censoring of the 14 open invoices, and the explicit absence of frequency bands.

Uncertainty is sorted into the requested headings and then misfiled. That the two N1 directly-follows gaps are not automatic violations is robust on the norm text plus the stated abstraction warning. What remains undecidable is whether those orders are eventually validated. Cobirth is called a robust violation after the table gave it medium confidence because of a possible key error. The data-quality bucket absorbs missing deliveries, missing order links, and scanner omissions that the source does not identify as suspected errors. The only suspected join error is the sensor–invoice link.

The result shows a sound priority order and a usable open-world vocabulary, but not stable severity, confidence, or separation of behavior, data quality, and model incompleteness.
