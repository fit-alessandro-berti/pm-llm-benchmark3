The required structure is largely met, and the main story is right: March is mostly label and provenance drift, later mapped-fitness loss, rework, and cycle time are real, queueing dominates service time, and priority, seasonality, and December are confounded. The baseline deltas and L1 distances on the seven-edge fragment are arithmetically correct, and the monitors are differentiated rather than one threshold.

Those strengths do not make the attribution reliable. Section 9 is about 460 words against a 250–400 limit, and its strongest claims overreach the monthly aggregates. Supplier Z moving from 18% to 46% is not a doubling, and the monthly table cannot place that shift “almost exactly” on 5 May. Mapped fitness returns to 0.90, not the whole 0.92-to-0.55 drop. “June and July show the steepest cycle-time acceleration” is false: May is +4.5 h, June +4.3 h, July +3.4 h, while October is +6.3 h. August is skipped even though cycle time peaks locally at 45.0 h / p90 110 h while Supplier Z and rework are already easing. The pre-October complexity rise from 21% to 29%, and the warning that senior assessment was recorded inconsistently, are not treated as serious alternatives to the October population story.

The median decomposition assigns roughly 40–60% to queueing without accounting for composition. Fast-variant share falls from about 58% to below 50%, so the median case changes identity; extra rework also adds service, not only waiting. The performance “change point” rule is nearly redundant because baseline (p90−median)/median is already about 1.19. Rule 3 requires service growth under 10%, but 4.8 h to 5.4 h is about 12.5%, so it misses the stated queueing case. Rule 6 targets teams, whereas missing resources are concentrated in migrated activities. In the discrimination table, several “logging drift” cells describe equality or stability, which is evidence against logging drift. Part B’s duplicate removal drops every repeated activity per case and would erase legitimate rework; `resource_key` and `discover_skeleton` are also unlikely PM4Py names.
