3.5

The write-up has the required eight sections and a few defensible cautions, but the regime map, pooled arithmetic, Little’s Law units, and code are wrong in ways that reverse the operating story.

The open interval is 12 hours (07:00–19:00, 12 bins), not 14. `mean_wip` is cases in system, not an in-queue-only count that might be “inflated” by that distinction. Late arrivals before 19:00 are in the daily totals; unfinished work carries overnight with service stopped. Those audit mistakes sit next to real limitations (missing 07:00 backlog, service quantiles versus sojourn, ambiguous `complex_pct`, non-independent days) that are named but then not used.

The primary segmentation does not match the series. 2026-04-06–14 is labeled weekdays while it contains 11–12 Apr, and “steady arrivals 100–112” is false (Fri 92; weekend 44 and 36). 15 Apr is an outage day with `complex_pct` 25, not a high-complex regime; the mix break is 20 Apr (about 23–25% to about 41–47%) with staff still about 6 FTE, completions below arrivals, and WIP rising into the high 30s–50. 16–28 Apr is not “post-intervention” or “normalized”: the prompt places the intervention during 29 Apr, 16 Apr (`completed` 129, WIP 52, 7.0 FTE) and 17 Apr are backlog flush, and 20–28 Apr is mix-driven accumulation. Calling 29 Apr–1 May a low-complex weekend is a date/type error (Wed–Fri, arrivals 116/120/109, complex about 43–45%, staff 7.0/7.0/6.5). 2–3 May is a slightly better-staffed high-mix weekend, not outage recovery. Recovery is explicitly dismissed (“normal flow resumes immediately”), which folds the 129-completion day into ordinary throughput.

Pooled figures are not small rounding errors. Over 28 days, arrivals total 2519 (mean about 90) and completions 2409 (mean about 86): a net deficit of 110. The text’s 99 and 101 reverses that sign. The unweighted mean of daily medians is about 31, not a pooled median of 32; the mean of p90s is about 67, not 78; mean `complex_pct` is about 33, not 40; mean WIP is about 30, not 28. Weekend complex is not “39–42” if early weekends (about 21–24) are included, and weekday peak share is not “27–33” after 20 Apr (peaks about 40–50). The mix jump is dated to the staffing change, so the Simpson contrast the task asks for—the pre-intervention high-mix weekdays run a large completion deficit, while recovery and 29 Apr–1 May run a surplus—is missed. No offered-load check (cases × service minutes versus FTE × 12 × 60) is given; “WIP versus staff × service” is not a utilization identity.

Burstiness numbers look invented. Within-day arrival Fano on 7 Apr is about 3, not 0.7; the morning peak is overdispersed, not nearly Poisson. The 22 on 15 Apr is the fourth bin (10:00–11:00 under the stated labels), not 08:00–09:00; arrival shape that day is ordinary, while completions are the zero block in 11:00–14:00. 21 Apr arrival Fano is about 9–10, not about 5. Pooling all representative hours gives an arrival Fano near 5.5 and a completion Fano near 2, not 2.1 and 1.3. The daily peak percentages are not reconciled with those vectors (index 2+3 matches several days; 21 Apr still does not match 47%). Lag-1 correlations and the change-point date are “typical” or “likely” results, not computed. Four days are correctly called insufficient, but that does not repair the indicators.

Little’s Law is not in one unit system. Narrative uses `L×60/λ` (a day treated as 60 minutes) and “corrects” toward median service; the code uses the first day’s WIP, not the window mean, multiplies by 24×60, and the supposed weekend window 25 Apr–3 May includes the busiest weekdays. Early-regime `L/λ` on a 12-hour open day is on the order of a couple of hours, not 20–23 minutes. Several reported sojourns fall below median service, which is impossible if service is inside sojourn, and that contradiction is explained away instead of used to catch the unit error. Baseline and weekends are marked `applicable`; with unknown boundary WIP, snapshot WIP, and within-day peaks, `approximate only` is the strongest honest verdict even on the clean early weekdays where arrivals ≈ completions. Outage and fully pooled windows as `not defensible` are the right calls. Throughput should switch to completions when the day is draining backlog; arrivals are used anyway.

Operational advice inherits the reversed timeline: complex mix is treated as rising after the 7 FTE change, whereas WIP was already climbing under about 6 FTE and the intervention days are when completions exceed arrivals and WIP falls 43→32. The “pooled averages say cut staff toward 7 FTE” policy contradicts itself (uniform 7 FTE would not overload weekends that now run at 2.5–3). Data-collection ideas (opening backlog, arrival-time complex flag) are sound and are the strongest part of the paper.

The single code block does not do what it claims. The markdown parser keeps only the first header, so the frame will not build; reported “script output” therefore cannot have been produced. Regime comparison tables are absent. Fano is pooled across regimes. The change-point prints an index, not a date, and a 3-day median deviation will not cleanly flag 15 Apr or 29 Apr. Little’s Law windows and the 24-hour factor are wrong. The PM4Py note names only `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, with non-runnable imports, and omits `get_case_arrival_average` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`, though it does say these aggregates are not an event log.

Structure and a few correct refusals (do not pool the outage into steady throughput; do not treat the whole sample as one stationary queue; do not pretend four days are a stochastic model) keep this above a blank failure. The segmentation, balance of arrivals and completions, and flow-time arithmetic do not.
