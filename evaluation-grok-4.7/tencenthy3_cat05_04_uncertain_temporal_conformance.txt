6.5

The interval analysis is largely careful and numerically reliable, but the answer is not nearly flawless. Several required pieces are incomplete or wrong, and a few stated conclusions do not follow from the intervals.

The normalization rules are sound: resolution is expanded before clock correction, `true = recorded − offset` with offset in `[a, b]` is applied in the right direction, June offsets (CEST +2, BST +1, EDT −4) are right, and the 2025-10-26 Berlin fold is resolved correctly. Ingestion order forces Alert onto the first fold and Investigation/Containment onto the second, and the surviving windows still keep minute-floor and ±60 s uncertainty. HUB ±1.5 s, OPS-BER, SENSOR ±10 s, and the weak T-409 ±12 min window are constructed correctly. SLA is treated as one-sided and the temporal profile as two-sided, including low-side deviations.

Most classifications match those intervals, including the easy-to-miss boundaries: T-402 S1 is a certain violation (minimum about 958.5 s), T-404 S2 and T-408 P2 at zeta 2 stay possible violations by about 3 s, T-403 P1 stays outside the zeta-3 band by 1.5 s, and T-408 S2/S3 and P4 at zeta 1–2 are certain violations. Tolerance and zeta changes are tracked correctly, and unchanged certain violations are not relaxed into passes.

The main failures are these.

The required program does not do what section 5 claims. Only T-401 is encoded; the other cases are explicitly omitted. DST alternatives are mentioned in a comment and never built. Duration classification is demonstrated for one pair only, and that call compares `timedelta` with `int`, so it raises `TypeError`. The midpoint log is therefore incomplete, and there is no actual comparison with the interval results. `pm4py.format_dataframe` and a zeta loop are sketched and the version caveat is fair, but the block is not a working reconstruction of the supplied evidence.

Section 6 misstates what would resolve T-401 S1. The excess over 900 s is about 91.5 s, while the slow-clock side of OPS-BER is only 90 s. Removing that entire side still leaves a maximum near 901.5 s because of the open minute and HUB uncertainty, so it does not produce a certain pass. “An exact start” would make the result certain, but not necessarily a pass. The same section also treats T-408’s containment–close profile as already settled, which contradicts the earlier, correct finding that zeta 2 is still a possible violation (`[3297, 3303]` against an upper bound of 3300).

The mobile entry-delay assumption is stated, but its effect is not. Dropping the extra five minutes leaves S1 a possible violation and flips T-406 S2, and the zeta-1 investigation–containment profile, from possible violation to certain pass. That is a material assumption and should have been shown. T-407’s hard causal cap is also applied only as an independent upper bound, leaving a −3 s duration that the constraint itself rules out. A few rationales say an interval falls outside “all” zeta bands when zeta 3 is breached on only one side.

Those gaps keep the answer well below a near-flawless score despite the strong case-by-case arithmetic.
