The answer is a serious, case-specific reading: states follow the normative abstraction, A and B are split on obligations rather than prefix shape, rule 4 and rule 5 are not collapsed, migration delay is not treated as proof a task never happened, and the intervals are wide enough to avoid false precision. That is not enough for a high score. Several required distinctions are contradicted inside the same case.

The recurring failure is the next-event distribution. For C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E, rank 2 or 3 is an activity the text itself says cannot be logged until Evidence Review Completed is logged first (a second or third evidence request, Close — No Safety Issue, Fast Close). Those entries are still given roughly 0.07–0.25 and marked process-enabled. That mass is far above the 8–15% feed-delay mechanism that could reorder an already-finished review ahead of its completion event. C6-A rank 3 states that Technical Investigation completion is “not the next event” while listing it as a next-event prediction. C6-A also says review is the only enabled next activity, then marks two other activities process-enabled.

C6-C and C6-D repeat the boolean error in a sharper form. Disposition is described as “not enabled at the current instant” and “currently blocked,” yet both predictions set `process_enabled` to true. C6-C’s third slot is therefore a blocked successor, not a live next completion. A further evidence cycle, which the unconfirmed root cause actually leaves open, is only mentioned as an obligation.

C6-D is the best observation-model case, and the backfill row correctly uses `process_enabled: false`. The rest is looser than the evidence. “Can take 2–14 h” is turned into a hard claim that Regulatory Assessment cannot have finished before 14:40 and that an earlier timestamp would be a data defect. The case is already assigned to R-R2 at 12:40, but the rank-1 note still has it earning a place in the unassigned risk queue. Smaller overreads remain. The directly-follows edge from triage completion to Technical Investigation completion is taken to suggest that investigation “often” starts at triage; that edge only shows no intervening logged completion, and it is a minority path. C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E are timed as if the same two-reviewer queue were independent. The cross-case discussion is the strongest part: attribute-blind DFG frequencies, completion-only logging, non-FIFO queues, lot relatedness, and the 16:00 batch are named as real ambiguity. Those caveats are not carried back into the flags and rankings, so the final predictions are less coherent than the commentary.
