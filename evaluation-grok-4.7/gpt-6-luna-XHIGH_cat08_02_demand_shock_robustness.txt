7.2

The response covers every required section, stays inside the given evidence, and treats mix, tails, rework, and downstream overload as design constraints. The deductions below are for analytical and control-logic defects, not missing headings.

The peak-mix narrative calls pre-analytical check the first hard constraint because it is at 96% with a 9.5h queue. In the same snapshot, assay is already at 98% with an 18h queue, technical validation is at 101% with a 16h queue, medical validation has a 22h queue, and specialist review has a 36h queue at only 87% utilization. Those are concurrent constraints. A linear story that starts at pre-analytical check and then “moves” the backlog downstream understates a system that is already multi-bottlenecked, and it never explains why the specialist queue is extreme while utilization is still below the machine pools.

Frequency comparisons are not put on a common time base. “610 peak holds versus 290 normal” uses an 3-week log against an 8-week log, so the stated contrast is about 2× while the weekly rate is about 5–6×. The same window problem sits under the repeat-assay counts. Within-period shares such as 1,200 of 9,730 are fine; the cross-period count contrast is not.

The bypass sentence is internally tangled. The variant evidence says bypassing batch wait accelerates urgent work and fragments machines, and that urgent bypass falls from 74% to 58% in the peak. The write-up treats “only 58%” as the fragmentation fact. A lower bypass share means more urgent cases are entering batch wait, which is the opposite mechanism from fragmentation-by-bypass. The dashboard then sets an investigation floor at that same 58% peak rate, so the degraded peak becomes the control limit.

The two-hour defect triage is adopted without a capacity path. Pre-analytical check is the step that would have to make that decision, and at peak it sits at 96% utilization with a 9.5h median queue. Nothing specifies a separate fast screen, a pull-ahead rule, or extra people, so the two-hour target is infeasible in the scenario where it is most needed. The recollection-overload warning is valid only after that screen actually happens.

Bounded cross-training is placed in the robust package, then disarmed by its own interlock: technical validators may move to accession correction only when technical WIP is below its cap, while that pool is already at 101% in the peak the policy is meant to survive. The residual section admits the risk of moving labor at the wrong time; the package still presents the intervention as generally robust.

Triggers ratify the bad state. Mix policy turns on as urgent share “approaches 29%” and complex-panel share “approaches 20%,” which are the observed peak shares, and can turn off once shares fall below those bands for two days, while normal shares are 18% and 13%. Rework coverage turns on above the 11.8% peak rate, leaving a dead band between the 7.5% normal rate and that crisis rate. Several queue warnings (correction 12h against a 14h peak median, specialist 24h against 36h, pre-analytical 8h against 9.5h) sit next to the failure medians rather than ahead of them.

Referral rules conflict. The machine-outage policy sends any case that will miss its deadline to the partner path. The validator policy correctly treats the partner’s 12h transport as unsuitable as an urgent shortcut and limits referral to complex-panel work with chain-of-custody. Both cannot be operated as written.

The central warning in the evidence is also underused. Peak urgent median cycle time is 17h, just inside the 18h penalty, while urgent p90 is 33h and normal urgent p90 is already 19h. Routine median at peak is 62h against a 72h target, with p90 at 120h. The redesign monitors p90 as a lagging outcome, but the reasoning never says that those medians are the misleading averages the policies must not chase. Direct technical-validation release also falls from roughly three-quarters of that step’s exits in the normal log to about half in the peak log; that routing shift is what loads medical validation and is only implied by the raw hold counts.

The trade-off table, on/off hysteresis for staffing, refusal of a permanent M1/M2 split and of batch size 24, and the self-inflicted risk of stranded capacity from WIP caps and reserved slots are sound. Those strengths keep the answer well above a merely complete template. They do not make it nearly flawless.
