3.5

The write-up keeps the eight required sections, two bundles, confidences, and a few sound instincts (scanner outage on ev08, N-421 as a hint for DEL-Y, unapplied cash, arithmetic is not decisive). Those pieces do not form a coherent repair. The selected bundle contradicts itself, several “additions” are already in the log, and the validation code would not check the log it claims to repair.

**Rules are misclassified and the evidence model is thin.** Domain rules 3–7 are explicitly defeasible (item-only pack rows, bulk dispatch, portal confirmation with no carrier id, fees/tax, unapplied cash). Section 1 files them as hard invariants, then immediately notes the exceptions that show they are not invariant. Timestamp order and identifier prefixes, which the brief treats as informative but not decisive, are upgraded to strong defaults. There is no account of how identifier similarity, time, amount, customer, route, lifecycle order, cardinality, and existing O2O structure are weighed together. The strongest local cues are barely used: `scanner_ref=PK8-2` on ev08, `external_ref=N-421?` against `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421`, and the fact that PAY-1 (100, already settled to INV-44) matches ORD-B while PAY-2 (130, unlinked) matches ORD-A.

**The relation table repairs the wrong endpoints.** ev08 already relates IT-B1; the missing E2O is a package. ev10 already relates PK-8; the missing E2O is a delivery. R01 and R02 propose those existing links, at 0.3 and 0.7, with consequences that refer to other objects (“DEL-Y may not exist” — DEL-Y is already an object). R07 puts the 130-versus-230 mismatch in “Evidence for” and rejects settlement for that mismatch, which ignores partial payment and the 100+130 alignment. R03 gives ev11→DEL-X 0.8 because `N-42*` “matches” N-420; the same prefix matches N-421. The table never proposes the relations a repair would actually turn on: ev08→PK-8, ev10→DEL-Y or DEL-X, DEL-Y `carries` PK-8, ev12→ORD-B, ev14→INV-44, plus explicit non-additions of those same candidates.

**Bundle A, the audit, and the JSON describe three different repairs.** The prose leaves ev08 unresolved and assigns ev10 to DEL-Y. The JSON, labeled Bundle A, adds ev10→PK-8 (already observed) with a justification about DEL-Y, adds PK-8 `packs` IT-B1, and also lists that pack link as unresolved. DEL-Y appears in no addition. Section 5 then says both orders have complete lifecycles, that every item is packed, and that IT-B1 is unresolved, and that PK-8 is resolved to DEL-Y. ORD-B is not complete under the conservative story: its item’s package is open, it is uninvoiced, and ev11 names only ORD-A. The circularity paragraph denies circular reasoning without examining the actual pattern the brief asks about — an O2O edge inferred from an assumed E2O and then treated as independent support. Here the O2O pack edge is added with no corresponding ev08→PK-8 E2O at all.

**Bundle B is not a coherent lifecycle.** It sends ev10 to DEL-X as a bulk dispatch and still has ev11 confirm DEL-Y. DEL-Y then has a confirmation and no package, no dispatch event, and no `carries` edge, while DEL-X would carry both packages only if an O2O edge the bundle never states is added. “ORD-A = 130 + 100 = 230” is false: ORD-A is 130, ORD-B is 100, and the sum equals the invoice total. The same paragraph then says the total does not match. That sum is evidence, not proof (fees and taxes remain possible on ORD-A alone); the text both miscomputes it and treats it as a reason to link. Cross-order packing is the hinge of the log — PK-8 already packs IT-A2 (ORD-A), and PK8-2 makes IT-B1 (ORD-B) a serious candidate — and neither bundle works through what that does to dispatch, confirmation, and invoicing together. ev11 is closed onto DEL-X alone in Bundle A without a real comparison to order-only confirmation, DEL-Y only, or both deliveries. ORD-A’s items sit in PK-7 and PK-8, so a DEL-X-only confirmation leaves the PK-8 line of ORD-A open.

**Downstream effects stay generic and some claims are the wrong mechanism.** No concrete OC-DFG edge is named. In the unrepaired log, DEL-Y has no events, so it is absent from the delivery DFG; PK-8 already has Pack Item → Dispatch Package via ev07 and ev10; DEL-X has Dispatch and no Confirm; Confirm Delivery is tied to Order only, so Confirm Delivery–Delivery is missing from the ET–OT graph until an E2O is added. Adding another Package–Item or Invoice–Order fact does not create a new object-type pair; the OTG topology can stay the same while multiplicity changes. Flattening does not merge ORD-A and ORD-B into one case when both are related to ev12; it puts Issue Invoice on two order cases. Pack and Dispatch never touch the order objects unless new E2O rows or object-to-object propagation are introduced, so order flattening is mostly insensitive to the package repairs these bundles discuss. Event frequency versus unique-object frequency versus total-object frequency is not analyzed. Equivalent-execution clustering is described as clustering “under INV-44,” which is not how order lifecycles become equivalent.

**The PM4Py block does not implement the requested checks.** It reads CSVs instead of using `events`, `objects`, `relations`, and `o2o`, then appends E2O rows onto the event table. Referential integrity is inverted: `assert not series.isin(...).all()` succeeds when some identifiers are missing. There is no duplicate check on (event, object, qualifier) or (object, object, qualifier). `pm4py.convert_to_ocel(..., version="2.0")` and `ocel_flattening(..., flattening_attribute=..., flattening_value=...)` are not a marked, usable constructor-and-flatten path. Discovery calls are present in name only.

**Over-repair warnings are five in number and mostly off-target.** Warning 3 treats reconciling ev08 to PK-8 as a violation of the pack rule; the domain statement presents that reconciliation as the ordinary follow-up to a scanner outage. Warning 5 says a two-delivery confirmation “splits” the event; one shared event synchronizes two delivery lifecycles. Missing are the failures that would actually invent behavior: hanging Pack/Dispatch off Order so the order flattening grows a fulfillment path, giving PK-8 two `carries` parents so one package is dispatched twice, letting object-to-object enrichment multiply Pack Item across orders and inflate total-object counts, and closing DEL-Y and PAY-2 solely so every lifecycle looks finished.

Prose length is also short of the requested 1,700–2,700 words outside code and JSON, and the missing length is the missing argument: bundle-level lifecycles, independent E2O/O2O support, and named graph fragments. The skeleton shows what a repair memo should cover; the repair it selects cannot be executed without contradicting the memo.
