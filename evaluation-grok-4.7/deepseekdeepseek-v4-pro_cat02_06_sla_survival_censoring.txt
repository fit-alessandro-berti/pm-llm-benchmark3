The required structure is complete and the core framing is sound: time origin at Submit, horizon 72h, Resolve as the event of interest, withdrawal/rejection/transfer as competing events rather than default censoring, open cases retained, two distinct estimands (failure to resolve by 72h versus still having no terminal disposition), Aalen–Johansen as the primary estimator, and escalation/rework kept out of baseline covariates. Those choices match the decision problem and avoid the main process-mining traps.

The write-up then misreads its own table and breaks several claims that depend on that reading. AutoCheck is not cohort-B-only: S01 and S15 are cohort A, and the visible policy difference is order (B often places AutoCheck immediately after Submit). S05 is a rejection at 9h, not a fast resolution. The state diagram also drops observed transitions, including Screen→Resolve, AutoCheck→Resolve, and Rework→Resolve. Sensitivity text treats S08, S21, and S32 as if all three change the 72h risk set; S32 is transferred at 96h, so competing-event versus censoring versus “resolved” coding at 96h does not change either estimand at 72h. The further claim that imputing transfer as resolution could make P1 look SLA-compliant is not supported by the eight P1 rows.

The operational formula is wrong and is incorrectly called equivalent to a correct one. With \(B_A=1-F_R(72)\), \((B_A-F_R(t))/S(t)\) is not \(1-[F_R(72)-F_R(t)]/S(t)\). Cause-specific hazards are also misdescribed as requiring independence between causes; the cause-specific hazard is identifiable without that assumption, which is needed only for a hypothetical world without competing events. Already-observed breaches are blurred with censoring: open cases older than 72h are known non-resolutions at the horizon, while only open cases younger than 72h are censored for that binary. Long inactivity gaps (S11, S25, S26, S09) are never given a real sensitivity, despite being the other informative-censoring mechanism in the extract.

No estimate is actually produced from the 32 cases. The code builds the table, keeps open cases, and names lifelines plus a manual outline, but it fabricates hourly timestamps against the stated constraint, and the Aalen–Johansen extraction calls `cumulative_incidence_` / `cumulative_incidence_at_times`, which do not match lifelines’ `cumulative_density_` API, so the stratified comparison as written would not run.
