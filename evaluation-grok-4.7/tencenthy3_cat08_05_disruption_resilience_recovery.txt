The final answer meets the required structure and is strongest on the resilience-versus-noncompliance distinction: A is kept only with authorization, C is retired, G is restricted by risk, and K/L are treated as visible after-the-fact work rather than real-time compliance. Most cited counts and phase metrics match the logs, and the prose recovery design covers triggers, triage, tiered controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, and reconciliation.

It is not nearly flawless. The optional process notation contradicts the prose: snapshot checking and picking are modeled as alternatives although they are sequential; normal processing still falls into emergency controls; retired skip-authorization remains a branch; and audit remediation is an alternative to invoice close rather than the observed post-close tail. Phase-level exception and rework rates are attributed too directly to individual patterns. “Prevented formal conformance collapse” and “possible illegal shipment” are stronger than the evidence. The fast lane structurally bypasses authorization, and backlog prioritization directly cancels 450 orders, but neither fact is used. Indicator targets around fitness 0.90 and fill rate 90% would treat the still-degraded stabilization state as acceptable. Phone proof-of-delivery is marked for modification but omitted from the recommendations, and “large outbreaks” misstates the cyber scenario.
