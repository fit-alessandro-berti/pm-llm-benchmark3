The diagnosis is right in the ways that matter. April is treated as a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June as a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September as overlapping supplier-driven process and concept change plus the September Partner Z shift, and October as label censoring that makes the 0.16 breach rate incomparable to mature months. The six-row table, month narrative, paired hypothesis tests, staged train/recalibrate rules, alert suppression, and failure modes all match the requested structure and stay proportionate.

The score is held down by concrete defects. The code would not run as written: `format_dataframe` is called with `activity_col` and `timestamp_col` rather than `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`, normalization then reads `activity` after formatting would have renamed that column, and `pm4py.filter_dataframe` is not a real PM4Py function. `discover_dfg` and `discover_performance_dfg` return three-element tuples, so `len(...)` does not count edges. The sketch never extracts the actual audit quantities—edge durations or variant shares—and the event-calendar performance DFG would bias waiting times that cross month boundaries. “Raise decision thresholds” for V3/Partner Z points the wrong way if it means a risk-score cutoff, because those segments are under-predicted. Smaller slips include calling April V1/V2 rates “unchanged” (they tick up), saying a mature October rate near 0.30+ “aligns” with a mean prediction of 0.23, listing August as training-safe while imposing a 95% maturity gate (August is 93%), and barely using April’s 18% unmapped-variant share as process corroboration of the pipeline break.
