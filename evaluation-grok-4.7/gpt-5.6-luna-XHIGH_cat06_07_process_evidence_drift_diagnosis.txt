The diagnosis is strong and mostly well calibrated: April is treated as a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June as a routing-mix change whose stable within-variant breach rates do not establish concept drift, August–September as overlapping parts-delay, mix, and Partner Z shift, and October as label-maturity confounding. The 0.16 October breach rate is correctly treated as incomplete ascertainment. Training gates, the refusal to give Partner Z its own model from an unstratified gap, the leakage warning on post-diagnosis parts time, and the alert order (lineage, then maturity, then stratified process evidence, then a human retraining decision) are all sound. Cited figures match the tables.

The required code does not run, and that is a material defect in a mandatory deliverable. `pm4py.discover_dfg` and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` return `(graph, start_activities, end_activities)`, but the sketch calls `.items()` on that tuple. On a dataframe, `pm4py.get_variants` returns variant-to-count integers, so `len(v)` raises. `feature_missingness` is the missingness of the full formatted log, copied unchanged into every month, so it cannot reproduce April’s queue-feature failure. Exact-row deduplication misses migration duplicates that differ by source or event id; the ambiguous-key count still leaves those rows in the DFG. The performance graph is never reduced to the `Order Part -> Part Received` median that is the actual process evidence.

Category boundaries are also looser than the evidence. June is a policy and path-mix change, yet it is listed as population shift. April is placed on the process-drift row even though the same row says the business process was not shown to change. Rural distance and device age were never model inputs, so they are an omitted-variable and transport problem, not covariate drift of the deployed feature set. The write-up never states the cleanest localization result: matured V1 and V2 breach rates stay near the winter baseline through September while V3 moves from about 0.30 to 0.64. Aggregate mean predicted risk near 0.19 is compared with that V3 rate without variant-specific scores. The 20 April duplicate-removal cutoff is absent from the discontinuity tests. June’s AUROC move from about 0.81 to 0.79 is overstated as a ranking change; the real June signal is calibration under a new mix.

These are avoidable errors in an otherwise careful audit. The judgment would be in the low 9s if the code executed and the V1/V2 localization and drift-type boundaries were tightened. As written, the non-running process extraction and those analytical slips keep it at 7.5.
