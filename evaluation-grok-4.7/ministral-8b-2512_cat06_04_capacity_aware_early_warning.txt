**3.5**

The write-up has the required six-part shape and gets three qualitative calls right: I-03 is ineligible because Diagnosis Complete makes the swarm ineffective; I-05 (repeat alert, no new event) is treated differently from I-07 (new customer log); spillover, segment miscalibration, and label feedback are named. The policy that is supposed to be deployed does not survive its own arithmetic, its own ranking, or the scenario definitions. Those are central failures, not presentation nits.

The decision score is not a usable rule. The displayed formula has no operators between the benefit term, the cost term, and the segment term, so it cannot be executed. The euro figures do not equal the expressions printed beside them. A faithful reading of those expressions is `p_now × midpoint risk reduction × value − false-alarm burden − €450`, and that product is the wrong benefit identity: absolute risk reduction is already a change in breach probability, so multiplying by `p_now` again double-counts probability and shrinks every benefit. Checked rows include I-01 (about €755, printed as €1,120), I-04 (about €2,450 at the stated 0.17 factor, printed as €3,800), and I-08 (`0.43 × 0.10 × €100,000 − €5,000 − €450 = −€1,150`, printed as €3,500). The €5,000 false-alarm burden on I-08 is effectively dropped. I-04’s reduction range is 0.10–0.28, so the midpoint is 0.19, not the 0.17 used. Subtracting `0.02 × 6 = 0.12` from a euro score is dimensionally meaningless and identical for every case, so it never changes priority. Segment multipliers in the rule (SME ×0.8, Strategic ×0.9, non-native ×0.7) do not appear in any printed score.

The SME factor also points the wrong way. The model underpredicts SME breach risk by about 0.04–0.09, which calls for a higher probability, not a lower score. Section 5 later says to add 0.10 to SME `p_now`, which reverses Section 1. Strategic Enterprise overprediction (0.05–0.12) is a small 0.9 haircut on the whole score rather than a shift in probability, and I-04 is then described as suffering “SME underprediction” even though I-04 is Strategic Enterprise. I-01 (€9,000) is called high value. Non-native uncertainty is a ×0.7 penalty in the rule and a +30% bonus in the safeguards. None of these adjustments are actually applied to the portfolio.

Capacity allocation contradicts itself. The decision column marks six cases Intervene (I-01, I-02, I-04, I-06, I-08, I-09). Only four slots exist. The named portfolio is I-01, I-02, I-04, and I-06, which is not the top four of the printed scores (those would be I-04, I-08, I-09, I-02). The text calls that set the highest scores. Section 1 reserves one regulatory slot; the summary uses four and reserves zero; I-10’s rationale says the reserve is being kept. I-06’s jump depends on an imputed live-queue feature, and the rule has no data-quality downgrade or wait-for-repair step before spending a slot. I-07 has 1.5 hours to the deadline, a new log, SME underprediction, and a high effect range, but the stated 50% evidence boost is not in the €700 score, and the 2-hour cool-down is longer than the remaining SLA. The 0.65–0.75 “senior confirmation” gate covers I-02 (intervened) and I-07 (deferred) without a consistent application. Option value from long horizons (I-04 at 10h, I-08 at 8h, I-10 at 12h) is ignored. Public-sector and regulatory harm beyond the euro values is never scored, despite being required.

The impact table is not auditable and does not implement the three scenarios. “Breaches avoided” is 4, then 3, then 1, which counts treated cases rather than expected risk reduction. Even upper-bound effects on the named quartet sum to about 1.15 expected breaches, not 4; midpoints sum to about 0.83. Optimistic benefit is written as `(9+25+60+15)k × 0.75 = €105k`; the sum is €109k and 0.75 of that is €81.75k, while 0.75 is far above every case’s effect range. Upper-bound gross benefit is about €30k before false-alarm burdens and the €1,800 labor cost. Base benefit is written as `(9+25+60)k × 0.5 = €57k`; that product is €47k, the 0.5 factor is not a midpoint effect, I-06 is omitted from benefits while its €450 is still charged, and false-alarm burdens (€3,150 on the quartet) are omitted in every scenario. Adverse uses `€60k × 0.25` for I-04; the lower end of I-04’s range is 0.10, and the scenario definition is lower-bound effects plus one near-zero case, not a single case at 0.25. Spillover euros (€0–€300, €600–€1,800, €3k–€9k) are not derived from 0.01–0.03 on six diagnosis-stage cases per slot, and `6 × 0.02` is not a euro quantity. Base is labeled high confidence with no justification. The three scenarios are computed after the portfolio is chosen, so uncertainty never changes the allocation.

Repeated-alert logic splits into conflicting gates: suppress unless `p` rises by 0.10, unless it rises by 0.15, unless `p > 0.70`, unless a new event occurs, with a 50% weight that is never applied. I-07’s probability fell, so a delta rule and an evidence rule disagree, and Section 4’s “defer below 0.70” does not explain a wait at 0.72. Alert suppression is specified more clearly than the actual constraint, which is a lockout on repeated swarms unless a materially new event occurred. I-05 versus I-07 is distinguished in words, then not carried into the score.

Fairness covers the required topics and then proposes controls that fight the decision rule or cannot be operated: a weekly low-value slot that does not protect today’s 1.5-hour case, a 0.75 probability trigger for non-native cases, and a “cap spillover at 0.01” as if a queue externality could be set by declaration. Spillover is pinned on “I-03’s peers,” but I-03 is already past diagnosis; the externality falls on untreated diagnosis-stage work because the two specialists stop ordinary diagnosis. Training-label feedback is named, without a logging design that would separate treatment from outcome.

Monitoring lists operational, predictive, causal, capacity, and fairness checks, then estimates causal effect with difference-in-differences on pre/post `p_now`. That is a change in a model score, not in breach outcomes. Monthly A/B language is better and is not reconciled with that design, with who is eligible, or with congestion spillovers that violate independent assignment. Recalibrating on recent breaches every two weeks, including intervened cases, is exactly how policy-induced drift gets written back into the model. The ±0.10 calibration trigger is already met by the stated SME and Strategic biases. The reserve alert (“flag if fewer than one slot is reserved”) contradicts the allocation just chosen. No record is specified for score, rank, congestion, assignment reason, or the cases that were eligible but untreated, which is the minimum needed to estimate effects and detect drift later.

The result is a policy memo that names the right constraints and then allocates scarce slots with a non-executable score, inconsistent decisions, reversed SME calibration, and scenario economics that overstate benefit by several times. That is not a deployable early-warning rule.
