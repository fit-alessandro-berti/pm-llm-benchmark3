The write-up has the right schema and often the right primary state label, but it repeatedly confuses an eventually allowed outcome with the next logged completion, misreads the frequency table, and misstates case facts.

C6-A is the least damaged case. S3 is right, fast close is correctly excluded, and Evidence Review Completed is a defensible rank-1. The alternative is a future transition, not a latent state. Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment completions are treated as serious immediate successors even though evidence arrived at 12:10, review usually finishes in 1–5 h, and those investigations have much longer queues. The 2.8 h figure is Evidence Received → Evidence Review, not a generic “Evidence Review next step.” A further evidence request after a 0.86 score is ignored. Rule 5 is asserted in the evidence list and then dropped from the predictions.

C6-B is the central failure, and it is exactly the contrast the cross-case section is supposed to explain. The case is already past S1: triage, a request, and receipt have all occurred, so the alternative “S1 if review fast-closes” is incoherent. Rule 3 makes Evidence Review the next completion; Fast Close in this log follows triage (286), not evidence receipt. The answer calls 68/356 a fast-close rate after receipt, then separately calls 286/1000 that same rate. Both are wrong: 68/356 is Evidence Review → Close — No Safety Issue. Fast Close is ranked first at 0.40–0.80 and marked process-enabled, while Evidence Review Completed is demoted to 0.10–0.40. That inverts control flow and then uses the bad forecast as the reason A and B differ.

C6-C’s primary state is reasonable, and the country rule is a real reportability hook. The rest slips. Rank 3 says the case is migration-affected; the caveat repeats that; the flag is normal. Four complaints on different lots do not satisfy Rule 5’s same-lot/component test, yet Supplier Analysis Completed is given up to 0.30 as the next log line despite a 6–36 h service time. Disposition is marked enabled while Regulatory Assessment is still the open obligation.

C6-D uses the 12:40 worklist well, so Regulatory Assessment in progress is the right primary reading. S5 is implausible: assessment has been open about 80 minutes against a 2–14 h range. The source-system attachment with no evidence events, on a migration-affected feed, is the designed ambiguity and never becomes a predicted backfill. Disposition up to 0.50 and Authority Notification up to 0.30 skip an unfinished assessment, an unrecorded reportability decision, and the 16:00 batch.

C6-E again gets S3, second cycle, as the primary state, then misses the obligation the facts were written to force. Six complaints on one lot make Supplier Analysis mandatory and block disposition until it is opened; that obligation is absent from the open-obligation list. Close — No Safety Issue is still rank 3 and process_enabled. “Score dropped” invents a prior score. Technical Investigation completion is again ranked as if it could beat a review that has had the new package for only 40 minutes.

Every `process_enabled` flag is true, so the required distinction between an enabled task and the next visible event is never encoded. Intervals are wide, but they overlap so much that the ranks are not identified, and several midpoints put downstream completions on the same horizon as the immediate control-flow successor. The cross-case discussion is the strongest prose in the answer and still ratifies the C6-B error.
