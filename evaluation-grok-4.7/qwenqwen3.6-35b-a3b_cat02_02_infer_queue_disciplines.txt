4.5

The write-up has the required seven sections and a few sound local readings, but the decision-set reconstruction—the core of the task—is wrong often enough that the queue diagnoses are not reliable.

The appointment reading on QA is directionally defensible: A03 starts at 08:20 over the earlier A02. The reconstructed sets are not. At 08:20, A04 is already waiting (priority 1, `est_min` 7). Shortest-expected-time therefore selects A04, while the table says it selects A03. The 08:58 set drops A07 and A08. The 09:41 row treats A05, A06, and A07 as waiting after they have already finished; A08 is the only remaining job, so that row cannot discriminate FIFO, priority, or shortest-expected-time. The clean FIFO test, A02 starting at 08:32 while A04 is waiting, is never analyzed. A08 is also listed as contradicting an appointment-plus-FIFO diagnosis, even though A08 had no appointment and being last is what FIFO predicts. The prose then says that same start “aligns with FIFO” after the table shows FIFO predicting A05.

QB is the largest error. B01’s segments are 09:02–09:16, 09:31–09:48, and 10:14–10:28; B05 runs 10:00–10:14. Those intervals do not overlap, and no missing break is required. Calling 10:00 a preemption of a running B01 is false: B03 ends at 10:00 and B01 is already suspended. Strict preemptive priority is violated by evidence the answer does not use: at 09:31 B01 resumes over waiting B03 (priority 2); at 09:48 B03 is chosen over waiting B05 (priority 1); at 10:14 B01 resumes over waiting B06 (priority 2). Confidence 85, plus a primary label of preemptive priority, is far too strong. The later B04–B06–B07 order is FIFO, and B07’s missed appointment is read correctly, but that does not repair the mechanism.

On QC, the 10:10 choice of C02 is a real shortest-expected-time point against FIFO, priority, and earliest deadline. The claim that C02–C03–C04–C06 “exactly matches” estimate order is false: the observed order is C02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C01, C07, and 8, 14, 22, 6 is not sorted. At 10:32, C06 (`est_min` 6, arrived 10:25) is eligible, so shortest-expected-time selects C06, not the stated C04. Choosing C01 over C07 at 11:40 also rejects the proposed deadline override: C07 has the tighter deadline and, if started then, can still finish near 12:00, whereas doing C01 first makes C07 late. Priority and earliest-deadline agree with each other there and both lose to C01; the answer treats that episode as deadline/age support. Every QC realized duration equals `est_min`, so estimate noise cannot explain these inversions. That check is absent.

QD correctly notes that nobody is served by an unqualified resource, and D05 can only use D-F. The intra-resource tables then erase the discriminating choices. At 11:25, D08 (priority 1, estimate 8) and D09 are waiting, and D-M selects D04, which rejects both priority and shortest-expected-time; the table lists only D04 and marks all three rules as agreeing. At 11:30, D06’s estimate is 18 and D07’s is 28, so shortest-expected-time selects D06; the table says D07. The informative facts are elsewhere: D-X idles from 11:20 until the 11:30 appointment, D-F idles from 11:36 to 12:00 while D05 needs 30 minutes and the meeting starts at 12:00, and D10 (priority 1, shorter) waits behind D07. “No contradicting episode” and confidence 90 are not earned. The D10 note that D-F is unavailable until 12:20 also conflicts with the stated meeting window, and that conflict is not discussed.

The two counterfactuals meet the format, and the QC 10:10 pair is the right comparison, but both discriminators are hypothetical estimate or arrival edits rather than observables targeted at the actual hard cases (QB at 09:48, QC at 11:40, QD at 11:25). Section 5 invents a logging overlap and an unsupported ±15-minute appointment buffer, and it credits calibration for A04’s wait even though A04 was skipped from 08:30 to 08:52, before calibration. The data-collection list is the strongest section and is properly prioritized, though it is generic relative to those episodes. The synthesis is right to distrust a blanket priority rule and to call QC poorly identified; it is wrong to call QD nearly unambiguous and to attribute a “uniform priority” claim to management that the scenario does not make.

Under a strict reading, complete structure and several correct episode calls do not offset invented timeline facts, repeated ineligible sets, and two overconfident mechanism labels.
