The write-up keeps the required seven-part shape and correctly stops short of a full rollout, but it is a generic template laid over data it barely uses. Several claims contradict the tables, the scenarios are not forecasts, and the pilot thresholds are already impossible under the baseline.

**Mechanism map.** Nominal handover reduction is stated backwards. Management’s own routing table raises specialist and approval handovers (tech 36→41, return 31→36, approval 42→49, legal/risk 9→12) and cuts only cross-team transfers (6→3). A shared queue is a handover, not an internal flow that removes one. Utilization is treated as a free gain from volume. Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91%, and the same pool is also asked to absorb technical work, triage, and difficult quality checks, so the “15% higher utilization” claim is capacity-infeasible. East (E_Tech 82%, median 8.6d, p90 22.8d, rework 11.5%, SLA miss 9.8%) is not separated from North/South, who still have slack and stronger local loops. Rework is not tied to the observed post-handover rates (local loops 8–9% versus legal/risk 14% and cross-team 18%). Resilience is described as improved load-spreading, which ignores the new single queue and the removed local supervisor.

**Scenarios.** Optimistic, central, and adverse never use those frequencies, waits, or team baselines. Optimistic asserts a large handover drop the proposal itself does not claim, and its “early warning signs” are success metrics. The central case pairs congestion with low utilization, which is the opposite queueing relationship, and it is not anchored on the management estimate plus partial staff risks. Learning and resilience are essentially absent. East versus North/South never appears as a distributional result.

**Indicators.** Leading and lagging columns repeat the same utilization and complexity measures. Several entries are not event-log measures (role queries, training completion, unspecified fairness indices). Missing are per-100-case handover edges and median waits, within-team versus cross-team working-together density, subcontracting (local → shared → same caseworker), service versus waiting time, variant shift, `rework_flag` and `reassignment_count` by segment, `local_expertise_flag` decay, and p50/p90 cycle time by team, complexity, specialty, and customer segment.

**Hidden risks.** Tail delay and high-complexity delay overlap, and exception handling is missing despite being required. The section never explains the mix shift that makes averages misleading: simpler cases speed up (intake→caseworker 88→83, wait 1.1h→1.0h) while high-complexity, legal/risk, language, and regional cases wait longer, so the mean can fall as the tail and fairness gaps worsen. Early completions are also right-censored because p90 cycle time is already 16–23 days.

**Pilot.** North plus East versus South confounds pooling with spillover onto the already shared legal/risk queue, and a nonexistent West team is invented. Restricting the pilot to medium-complexity cases avoids the rules that matter (all high-complexity to the technical pool; legal/risk/security to the specialist queue). Two-week pre/post windows cannot observe censored cycle times, rare specialties (~9–12 per 100), or expertise loss. Guardrails fight the baseline: a 4-hour maximum wait is below today’s 9.6-hour legal/risk median; SLA “below 90%” is already about East’s 90.2% adherence; a +15% utilization “guardrail” pushes saturated specialists harder; a +20% wait trigger fires even if management’s own +25% to +37% waits occur exactly.

**Recommendation.** Pilot-first is the right posture, but it does not require splitting the bundle, adding legal/risk capacity before broadening detection, or protecting local advisory time. The warning about early averages misses censoring, Simpson mix shift, and East gains masking North/South losses.

**PM4Py sketch.** Most named discoverers are called, then printed. `format_dataframe` is never used on the stated columns. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `get_service_time` are absent, so service versus wait is not measured. `get_variants_with_attribute` is not a PM4Py function. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., 'North')` should take a list, filters events rather than whole origin-team cases (shared-service events drop out), and the filtered logs are unused. There is no per-100 normalization, no segment loop, and no before/after diff of edges, waits, rework, or SLA.
