4.5

The write-up has the required six sections, the seven mechanisms, and all eight interventions, and it correctly treats the pattern as multi-causal. It is not reliable as an analysis. Several mechanism rows cite the tables incorrectly, the code would not run, and required monitoring content is missing or mis-specified.

**Mechanism table.** Bin 16–30 has mean service time 13.6 min; 12.9 min is bin 31–50, so the learning row attaches the wrong number to the wrong bin. Early error is 3.4%, below the later 4.6% and 7.9%, so “high error rates in first positions” overstates that cell. The fatigue row’s expected signature includes slowing, but the supporting evidence never confronts the main contrary fact: service time keeps falling through position 76+ (10.8 min). Night staff are faster later in the shift (17.5 to 14.9 min); 15.2 min in the night’s last two hours is a cross-shift comparison, largely with a high first-hour complexity share (56%), so it does not show within-shift fatigue slowing. Juniors’ later error under low WIP is 4.4%, down from 5.1% in the first 20 events; the claim that their errors rise under low WIP reverses the table. The 4.4% versus 12.4% contrast is low WIP versus high WIP, and that experience table does not hold complexity fixed, so “outside complexity-related bands” is unsupported. The clue says seniors take complex cases early; attributing juniors’ early 5.1% error to first exposure to complex work runs against that selection pattern. “WIP > 70” is applied to the 51–75 bin, whose mean queue is 62. Fewer night batches, and juniors or night staff rarely correcting Bot_Risk, are not in the evidence. More frequent correction of Bot_Risk above queue 70 is also treated as human classification error.

**Integrated account.** Early complexity, backlog, senior resilience, and late WIP are all mentioned, and fatigue is not offered as the sole cause. The timing is flattened. The large speed gains and the error decline occur while complexity and queue fall (positions 1–30: 18.8 to 13.6 min, error 3.4% to 2.5%, queue 46 to 33). The error jump is later (4.6%, then 7.9%) when queue rebounds to 62 and 88, with only a further move from 12.9 to 10.8 min. Calling that late change a sharp drop, and placing complexity 0.43 in mid-shift, merges two different regimes. Bot corrections are then tied to end-of-shift quality-check bursts without evidence that those are the same events.

**Analyses.** The headings match the request; the implementations do not identify the mechanisms. `pd.cut` is given string labels as bin edges and will fail. The OLS formula uses `service_time`, `complexity`, and `queue_length`, which are not columns, treats `shift_id` as numeric, omits error as an outcome, and omits sequence position, experience, and the WIP interactions the table itself proposes. `get_service_time(..., aggregation="resource")`, `discover_batches(..., case_id=, resource_id=)`, `cluster_log(..., clustering_attribute=)`, `view_events_distribution_graph(..., attribute=)`, and `get_rework_cases_per_activity(..., attribute_filter=)` do not match these functions’ parameters. `cluster_log` clusters trace profiles; it does not split on `resource_experience_months`. Filtering three named people and aggregating service time is not a within-resource early-versus-late comparison. Return values of the insert functions are discarded. There is no within-person sequence model, no performance DFG or path filter from error to Rework Correction, and no batch versus non-batch comparison of service time and error.

**Interventions.** Benefits, risks, and monitors are present but thin. Random assignment is described as equalizing workload; it equalizes case mix and, given junior error rates, can move hard cases onto the resources who already fail most under pressure. Deferring quality checks is given a throughput-side benefit even though end-of-shift quality-check bursts are already a documented workaround; extending that deferral is not connected to the batching evidence. Penalizing late errors is still given a positive incentive benefit and a “before penalization” monitoring step. Capping shifts and moving complex work off the first hour are not tied tightly to night and early shifts, where the first hour is disproportionately complex carryover.

**Ethics and dashboard.** The five caution topics are named in one sentence each. Selection is illustrated only as complex work inflating strong workers’ errors, while seniors remain the lower-error group; measurement error is limited to changing definitions and does not cover short batch service times, bot faults attributed to humans, or error versus seven-day rework. The dashboard has eight items and omits worker-load fairness. “Handover” is defined as an activity-family change, which is ordinary flow, rather than shift-boundary queue ownership (the start-versus-end queue columns). Calculations, thresholds, bot-correction rate, and within-resource views are unspecified.
