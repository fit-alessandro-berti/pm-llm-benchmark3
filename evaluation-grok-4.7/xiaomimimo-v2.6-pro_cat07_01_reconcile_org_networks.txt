6.5

The write-up meets the required structure and stays multi-hypothesis: all six headings are present, each network is given both an implication and a limit, bots and vendors are kept in view, centrality is not treated as inherently good or bad, and removal is refused unless the routing function is replaced. The touch-and-go reading (about 1.06 events per case), the coverage cap on working-together, the separation of case-mix from “caused exceptions,” and the segregation-of-duties limit (no settlement approval and no payment release) are valid.

Those strengths are undercut by quantitative contradictions that the same text both relies on and fails to reconcile.

The subcontracting story is infeasible on the counts given. Mira has 960 events and 905 distinct cases, so at most 55 events are repeats and at most 55 cases can contain a second Mira event. The n=2 table then cites 126 Mira→Omar returns and 98 Mira→Priya returns. One of those rows already exceeds every surplus event she has. The answer still treats those loops as evidence of retain-and-delegate control and of a tight-loop versus fire-and-forget split.

The working-together arithmetic is internally inconsistent. The text correctly notes that a 27% Alice–Carla co-case rate cannot be a share of all ~4,100 cases when Alice appears in only ~16%. It then computes lift as if 27% were that impossible probability, and it writes the formula as `(P(both)/P(A)·P(B))`, which is `(P(both)/P(A))·P(B)`, not lift. Section 3 later drops the conditional and calls this a pipeline error.

Handover elapsed times are misread. Mira→Omar is 42 minutes against Omar’s 37-minute median service, and Mira→Priya is 51 against 51. Those edges are repeatedly called time “until the specialist starts” and therefore dispatch delay. Completion-to-completion gaps of that size are at least as consistent with the successor’s own service. The same pattern appears on Alice/Bruno→Vendor_Pay (1.2–1.4 hours versus Vendor_Pay’s 1.3-hour service). That mistake is used to locate the tail bottleneck downstream of Mira.

Absence from a top-frequency edge list is treated as proof that no Alice→Bruno or cross-team edge exists, and that claim is repeated in the hypothesis, the node-removal prediction, and the recommendation. A top-k table cannot establish structural disconnection.

Smaller overclaims add up: a “30–50%” recovery from delegate rights and a “6–10 week” centrality horizon are invented; the executive summary settles on one role label (“exception router rather than a team lead”) after the body was required not to pick a ground truth. The proposed PM4Py checks are mostly the right ones, including service-versus-wait, but they do not repair the invalid readings already stated as findings.
