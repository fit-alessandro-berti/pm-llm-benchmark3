6.2

The write-up covers every required section and many case-level choices are reasonable, but it is not operationally reliable. Several assignments contradict the stated rules, the baseline comparison mis-simulates both policies, and the scoring model cannot actually produce the table.

The queue plan breaks its own constraints. C012 gives a low-priority quality check to R2_Bruno, whose overtime risk is high, while safer skill-2/3 alternatives with spare capacity exist (including the current owner, R10_Jonas). The override condition allows that only for urgent SLA risk with no safer alternative. The fairness note then calls this assignment justified. C010 is counted as priority training even though R1_Amal’s documented training priority is diagnosis, and the SLA is 20h against the policy’s own “>20h” cutoff. C015 trains R3_Celine in security, while her documented priority is negotiation. R5_Eva, R10_Jonas, R4_David, and R8_Hugo all have training priorities on skills where their level is 0, so those priorities are impossible under the skill-0 ban; that conflict is only noted for Eva/Jonas on security. The claimed 4/9 = 44% training share therefore does not measure documented training priorities. On a correct reading, C009 is the only clean hit, which is about 11% of the nine normal/low cases and below the 20% target. C001 (complexity 0.88, high handover cost, owner skill 1, supervision by R7/R8 feasible, 3h SLA) never resolves the clash between continuity-with-supervision and the “needs specialist” note. A handover whose historical median wait is 2.7h consumes almost the entire remaining SLA, and that risk is only labeled, not weighed against a supervised continuity option.

Workload accounting is inconsistent. R6_Farid receives both urgent cases (C002 at 2h and C011 at 4h) plus C010 final sign-off, and that contention is absent from the risk column. R3_Celine receives C003, C007 (already 3 reassignments, 6h, complexity 0.81), C009 supervision, and C015 as trainee; the summary “R3: 3” does not show this arithmetic. Supervision is defined as 0.5 of an assignment and then not applied. C006 and C012 are described as one quality batch while being given to different executors, so the batch is not a batch under the rule or under batch discovery. C003 already has 2 reassignments; moving it creates the >2 band tied to 1.8× rework, which is recorded as a follow-up check rather than a cost of the decision.

The selector itself is not executable as written. Urgency U is constant across candidates for a case, so the 0.30 weight never changes the ranking. The continuity formula gives no base score for a non-owner. Fairness can exceed 10 when open cases are below 5. Eligible-set pseudocode is missing the union operator. Case order and within-batch updates to open cases, hours, and the cap of 4 are unspecified, so the procedure is not deterministic. No sub-scores are computed, and several rows (C012, C010, C001) disagree with Step 3. The worker explanation says high-overtime staff get new work only for true urgency with no safe alternative, which is the opposite of the Bruno assignment.

Section 4’s counterfactuals are wrong. First-available, ignoring only skill 0 and picking the lowest open-case count, gives C001 to R3_Celine (6 open, skill 1), not to Jonas or Iris. It also serves R1_Amal (9) before R2_Bruno (12); the “R2 before R1??” remark is left unresolved and is backwards. Specialist-only, with open-case tie-breaks, sends diagnosis to R3_Celine (skill 3, 6 open), not to R4_David (14). “Hugo (712)” is an undefined figure. The “+~1–2h” and “+~0.5–1h” deltas are not derived from the 2.7h / 3.3h evidence. Calling the policy best on SLA, continuity, fairness, and resilience at once is stronger than the assignment errors support.

The audit sketch names the requested PM4Py functions, but several measures do not mean what they claim. `filter_case_performance(..., max_performance=4*3600)` filters short case duration, not proximity to `sla_deadline`. Mean `workload_snapshot` on security rows is resource load, not security queue length. Event-level `.mean()` on `reassignment_count` and skill mismatch overweights long cases. `get_service_time(df, aggregation="mean")` does not match the activity-level service-time API. `score_candidate` is a stub, `dataframe_utils` is unused, and appeals have no defined event type in the given columns. The 15% handover alert and the Gini cutoff are unexplained.
