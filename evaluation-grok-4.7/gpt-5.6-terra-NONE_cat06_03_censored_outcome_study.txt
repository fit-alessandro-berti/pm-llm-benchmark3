7.5

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activation as non-default, separates selection into activation from ordinary censoring and delayed feeds, and keeps an as-of prefix discipline with a temporal split. Those choices match the decision the study has to make. The write-up is still not reliable enough to implement or to defend as specified: the cohort definition, the maturity rule, and the code disagree, and one central date calculation is never followed through.

The primary estimand is unstable. Section 1 defines \(P(Y=1\mid X_t,A=1,R=1)\), the complete-case association. Section 3 then treats that fit as an approximation to \(P(Y=1\mid X_t,A=1)\) under a MAR-like assumption. Those are different parameters. The population sentence also mixes the scoreable set (still in underwriting at \(t\)) with the labeled set (eventually activated). Pre-decision use is endorsed before that train/serve gap is pinned down.

The 48,000 reconciliation is sloppy. The same sum is first called 48,300 and then 48,000. Permanently missing partner feeds have no row and no residual in a partition that already sums exactly, so they are silently absorbed into the 21,600 or the 3,600. The flow table then assigns all 21,600 to the supervised cohort, while the text’s conservative rule (activation + 90 + 35 days \(\le\) 15 February 2026) only keeps activations on or before 13 October 2025. The code implements the stricter rule; the table does not. The preferred rule in the text (90-day horizon elapsed and `label_available_at` \(\le C\)) is a third cohort.

That date collision is the main design miss. Migration is 15 October 2025, two days after the conservative maturity boundary. Under the rule the answer itself uses, no mature labeled case activated after migration, and no prefix of such a case can contain migrated activities. Test 2, as an outcome backtest, is empty. Under the looser `label_available_at` rule it might not be. The validation plan never states which is true. It also splits on application start, so June starts decided after 1 July 2025 cross the policy break, and later folds keep only fast activations. That time-to-activation filter can change both the risk mix and the apparent policy-shift result. Sample-size caveats do not name this bias.

The censoring taxonomy in Section 3 is the right one, including IPW limits, tipping-point bounds, linkage sensitivity, and what is untestable. The skeleton undoes it: every unlabeled activation becomes `activated_not_mature_or_feed_missing`, so right censoring, feed delay, and permanent missingness are not operationalized. Deduplication is described as migration-artifact-only and implemented as a global exact drop on normalized activity.

The leakage table’s decisions are sound. The audit code is not runnable. `features` is an inner merge of `primary_cases`, so `activation_time` and the other terminal timestamps are present, and the assert against those names fails. `default_90d_label` is the real label column and is not in the forbidden set. `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` run on the full multi-era log, including post-outcome activities, despite the training-only warning. Fixed-length prefixes are demonstrated, then rebuilt manually; short-prefix attrition among mature activations is not counted. The block is also well beyond the requested length, without covering elapsed-time prefixes or the selection model the text calls part of the primary strategy.

Sections 5 and 8 are the most stable: as-of control-flow, rework, queue, and reference-window features are distinguished, and the three deployment feedback loops (acceptance, intervention, routing) are concrete. The scientific stance is right. The study as written would not reproduce one cohort, one maturity rule, or a passing leakage check.
