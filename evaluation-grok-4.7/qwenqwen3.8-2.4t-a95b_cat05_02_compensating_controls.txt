The final assessment is complete, well structured, and generally well reasoned. It separates nominal four-eyes form from the control objective, applies ED-1 as a conditional permission, and avoids classifying by amount or by “a control occurred.” AP-203 and AP-207 as objective met, AP-202 and AP-208 as failed, AP-205 as stopped before completion, and AP-201 as only partial are all defensible. The hybrid treatment of a manager action after release but before settlement is clear, and the code covers the required reconstruction, flags, and verdict disclaimer.

Deductions are for accumulated defects, not a wrong overall framework. Section 1 calls the same post-release, pre-settlement manager action both preventive and detective, which conflicts with the later hybrid conclusion on the exact timing question. AP-206 is the source’s clearest logging-delay versus precondition-failure ambiguity, yet it is resolved as partially met while the similar evidence gap in AP-209 is left indeterminate. In the code, AP-208’s emergency token is treated as active before preparation although the facts only say it was active, vendor ages are invented (especially for the merely “established” vendor), and the repeated-pair flag counts only the two logged u12/u13 cases rather than the stated 37 payments in 30 days. “Merely mitigating” is defined and then not tied to the required case labels. None of these invert the main case judgments, but they are concrete inaccuracies under a strict reading.
