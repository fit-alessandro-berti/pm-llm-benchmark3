6.5

The final judgment is competent and mostly well-discriminating, but it is not nearly flawless. Several classifications are internally inconsistent, a few control mappings are factually loose, and the checking code would mis-flag material evidence.

The strongest results are the non-mechanical ones. AP-202 and AP-208 are rightly `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` despite very different surfaces (override plus conflicted late ratification versus a payment that later proved legitimate). AP-204 is rightly failed even though prepare and release were different users, because a fabricated receipt passed both people and audit was too late to recover. AP-205 is rightly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-209 is rightly not auto-passed merely because the user pair differed. The hybrid reading of manager action after release but before settlement is clear and usable. ED-1 is treated as a conditional permission rather than a waiver of the objective.

Those strengths are undercut by classification and logic errors.

AP-207 is the clearest miss. The write-up says ED-1 is met, the manager was independent, the review was substantive, ratification occurred before settlement, and audit fell inside one business day. Under the stated rule, approval before settlement satisfies MG-4 and ED-1 condition 6. That is the same authorized package as AP-203, yet AP-203 is `OBJECTIVE_MET` and AP-207 is only `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`. The partial label is not explained. AP-206 has the stronger sequencing — independent approval before release — and a clean pass, but is left `INDETERMINATE` with medium residual risk solely because the token timestamp is ambiguous. Procedure doubt is real; equating it with medium loss exposure overstates the case and conflicts with the AP-207 treatment. The manager-before-validation order (13:04 versus 13:05) is also missed.

AP-205 is labeled a nominal four-eyes violation. FEP-1 applies to completed payments. This one was blocked and cancelled, so the nominal result is not a completed-payment violation; the prevention classification itself is right. AP-201 as partially met is defensible, but the 25-hour audit versus the 24-hour recall window is never used, even though that is why the timing fact was given.

Section 1 over-assigns AV-2. Vendor tenure is an ED-1 condition, not an AV-2 check. AV-2 also does not establish who controls an account and can be overridden; describing it as able to prevent an employee-controlled destination is too strong. Section 5 says retrospective audit “breaks the causal chain” in AP-204, then admits recovery was already unlikely. Detection after irrevocability does not break the loss path. AP-208’s counterfactual treats outage exploitation as the operative mechanism even though the payment was legitimate; the exposed failure is control failure, not a demonstrated fraud. Section 2’s “not only expected loss” point is directionally right but never uses the best illustration: AP-208 had no realized loss and still failed the objective. Section 6 covers the requested monitors, yet the 5% and 20% cutoffs are presented as rules rather than illustrative triggers, and alert versus proof is one sentence.

The code reconstructs the log, calls the four-eyes filter, correctly treats that filter as keeping different-resource cases, separates AP-205, and labels the output as evidence rather than a verdict. The auxiliary flags are not reliable. `Emergency_Eligible` is only token presence, so AP-206 and AP-208 look eligible although amount, bank change, validation outage, or token timing defeat ED-1. Manager timing cannot see AP-201’s 10:00 settlement because that fact is absent from the log and the case-facts frame, so the important before-settlement case becomes `After_Release_No_Settlement`. Audit “within one business day” is implemented as elapsed days under 2, which swallows the 25-hour case and conflates recall with a business day. AP-205’s vendor age is invented. On any filter exception, every completed case is marked violating. Repeated pairs are hardcoded rather than counted.
