6.4

The write-up follows the required eight-part shape, keeps arithmetic from being treated as proof, refuses a unique assignment of ev11, and separates a smaller repair from a broader commercial story. Those strengths do not offset several reasoning errors that matter for this task.

The evidence model never actually uses the required class “evidence against a repair.” Hard constraints are mostly referential integrity, while the combination rule for identifiers, time, amounts, customer, route, cardinality, and existing O2O structure stays a short ordering slogan. Route is then used as support for ev10→DEL-Y even though both deliveries and both packages are NORTH, so route does not discriminate. The same table lists “IT-B1 needs a package” as evidence for an accepted edge, which is completeness pressure rather than evidence. Section 8 later warns against that pressure.

Bundle A is not a minimum-change repair. The domain explicitly allows an item-only Pack Item row after a scanner outage, yet ev08→PK-8 and the derived packs edge are accepted at 0.75 and 0.70. ev10→DEL-Y is accepted at 0.92 despite the question mark in `N-421?`. Derived O2O confidence barely falls (0.90 versus 0.92). Calibration is inconsistent elsewhere: ev14→INV-44 is scored 0.85 and still excluded from the conservative bundle, while the weaker ev08 link is accepted. Bundle B changes only the commercial reading. It does not offer a second physical hypothesis, such as leaving ev08 unresolved, or treating the shared 10:00 timestamp and failed carrier reference as a bulk attachment of PK-8 to DEL-X while DEL-Y stays unused.

Inference direction contradicts itself. Section 1 says O2O edges are added only after an E2O hypothesis and are not independent confirmation. P9 is justified as “if P8 is correct,” which derives the event–object link from the object–object link. Section 5 then describes P8 as an O2O edge inferred after an E2O edge. That is the circular pattern the audit claims to avoid. The audit also calls ORD-A’s Bundle A lifecycle paid and mostly direct. Under Bundle A, only PAY-1 (100) settles INV-44 (230), and packing and dispatch reach the order only through items and packages.

The amount pattern is under-analyzed. PAY-1 equals ORD-B’s declared value but is already observed settling an invoice billed only to ORD-A; PAY-2 equals both ORD-A’s declared value and the residual 230−100. That double reading is why the financial extension is tempting, and it is not laid out. Gross weight is unused.

Two downstream claims are wrong or incomplete. For PK-8, inserting ev08 between ev07 and ev10 creates Pack Item→Pack Item; it does not strengthen Pack Item→Dispatch Package, which remains one directly-follows step for that package. Bundle A also joins ORD-A and ORD-B through PK-8 (IT-A2 and IT-B1), so object-graph or equivalent-execution clustering can pull the orders together. The claim that Bundle A leaves them far apart is only safe for flattened order traces. The required contrast among event counts, unique objects, and total related objects is missing. Bundle B’s invoice trace becoming Issue Invoice→Record Payment→Record Payment, and the duplication of ev11 and ev12 across two order cases, is likewise thin.

The validation sketch does the requested copy, append, referential and duplicate checks, version-sensitive construction, three discovery calls, Order and Delivery flattening, and an explicit refusal to treat O2O enrichment as semantic proof. Duplicate checking is full-row only, so a logical duplicate with extra columns can pass. The over-repair warnings are specific and usable. The table also has one extra separator cell, and “reject” is never used for tempting bad links such as reversing qualifier direction.

Overall, the artifact is careful and well structured, but the conservative bundle over-repairs, the alternative does not vary the real physical ambiguity, confidence and inference direction conflict, and two process-mining consequences are misstated.
