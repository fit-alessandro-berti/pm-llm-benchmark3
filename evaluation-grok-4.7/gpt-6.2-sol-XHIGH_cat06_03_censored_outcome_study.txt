7.5

The design is statistically serious and avoids the main failure modes: rejects are not coded as non-defaults, activation is selection rather than ordinary censoring, the estimand is explicitly conditional on later lender activation, and validation is temporal across the policy change. The remaining problems are concrete enough that the package is not nearly flawless.

The primary estimand is coherent, but several claims are looser than the notation suggests. \(D\) is never defined when no default occurs, and nothing reconciles `default_90d` with `Default Recorded`. The 24-hour landmark and 60-day activation cap are arbitrary without an operational SLA, and activations after day 60 are simply dropped rather than shown as a sensitivity against “eventually activated.” The multi-state alternative is only sketched: time origin, horizon, and censoring are not formally stated. Transfer re-entry is acknowledged, but the unit of analysis is not closed. With only 60% linkage, a new identifier can appear as another start among the 48,000, and the write-up never gives an exclusion rule.

Cohort accounting is right at snapshot level (the seven groups sum to 48,000, and 21,600 + 3,600 = 25,200 activations). It stops there. Landmark eligibility, the 60-day window, and label maturity have no numeric flow, and the code’s status field cannot support one. Resolved non-activation, acceptance without activation, ordinary administrative censoring, feed delay, and permanent missingness collapse into `open_or_other_unresolved` or `activated_report_pending_or_missing`. `timeline_conflict` is also wrong: any activation at or after a competing event is flagged, so those competing dispositions disappear from the tally.

The missingness strategy is the right family—fixed opportunity set, immature cases excluded, IPW plus bounds and partner-stratum tipping—but the identifying assumption is under-restricted. Allowing unspecified post-landmark variables into the observation model contradicts the leakage section. `collection_status` or any field affected by default can separate “missing” almost perfectly and bias the weights. Bounds \(d/N\) to \((d+u)/N\) are valid only if recorded labels are already correct; that is assumed, not checked.

Prefix rules in prose are sound, including ties, renaming, and preserved document loops. The skeleton does not implement them. `ALIASES` is empty while intake events are hard-coded, so renamed or differently worded activities are silently dropped. The fixed-length path does not omit a \(k\)-th event that merely shares a batch with earlier events. Duplicate rows are counted and then kept, which inflates prefix counts.

The leakage table itself is strong and correctly drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, and raw labels. The code’s audit is weaker than the table. `y` becomes available once `activation + 90 days` has passed, without requiring `label_available_at` to fall after the horizon for a non-default or after a verified default time for a positive. An early placeholder 0 is then a matured negative. There is no check that the warehouse flag agrees with `Default Recorded`.

Temporal folds are dated correctly and the maturity gaps are real, but the design is thinner than it looks. Train, validation, and test cover only short start windows; most of 2024–2025 is unused. The test is about six summer weeks in one regime. The June–July policy bridge, where scoring is pre-change and activation may be post-change, is excluded and barely discussed. Post-migration prefixes cannot be scored for the primary endpoint in this extract; that limit should be stated as a power and transport problem, not only as a future extract.

The skeleton is a real pipeline, uses a case-start split, and keeps the modeling frame to pre-landmark intake events. It is not a faithful implementation of sections 2–4. IPW is a comment, not a calculation; observation weights are easy to omit and train complete-case. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then assumed to return a frame with the original columns. Cohort counts printed from `status` would not match the paper taxonomy. Those are implementation errors, not stylistic nits.
