The answer is a strong, case-specific process reading: obligations, queue timing, completion-only logging, and the migration flag are handled carefully, A is not copied onto B, and the probabilities are ranges rather than false point estimates. It is not nearly flawless. Several control-flow and calibration decisions are internally inconsistent, and those are material under a strict reading.

C6-C’s alternative state is not an alternative. Primary and alternative are both S4; the alternative only restates that regulatory work may already be in progress, which the primary and the observation caveat already say. The unknowns admit that the country rule may not apply at this count and failure mode, which would remove the regulatory gate and leave the case disposition-ready after a completed technical investigation. That world is missing from the state field, and it is barely present in the numbers: Disposition Recorded is held to 0.07–0.15 with `process_enabled` false. The same answer treats “can be reportable” as a firm obligation while listing applicability as ranking-changing. Those cannot both be right at that probability mass. Calling a new Customer Evidence Requested after technical completion a “permissible” evidence-review loop also stretches rule 3, which attaches that loop to Evidence Review.

C6-B mis-locates Fast Close. Eligibility under the severity, injury, and device tests is real, but this case already left the triage branch at 09:30, when evidence was requested. In the normative map and in the directly-follows summary, Fast Close is an Initial Triage outcome; the post-review non-investigation ending is Close — No Safety Issue. Marking Fast Close `process_enabled` true, and spending the third prediction on it, treats an expired shortcut as a live transition. The top three then contain two closure labels and omit the branch that would actually change the ranking: the 0.93 similarity is not accepted and an investigation is opened. Close — No Safety Issue is a fair S3 outcome, but it is grouped with Fast Close under the same enabled-now flag even though the write-up says a review completion should appear first.

C6-E’s second interval is too high for its own evidence. Supplier Analysis Completed at 0.15–0.30 can precede a review that has been queued for about 40 minutes only if the analysis was opened many hours earlier. Typical supplier completion is 6–36 hours, three items are already waiting, and “apparently related” is not established. The text cites those limits and then assigns a range that would make a slow, uncertain completion a common next log event. Customer Evidence Requested as the next event has the same ordering problem as B’s closures: in a normal feed the second-cycle review completion should be logged before a third request.

`process_enabled` is used strictly on C6-C and C6-D, where blocked disposition and a delayed Evidence Received are false, and loosely on C6-B, where an out-of-state Fast Close is true. That split should have been consistent.

Smaller gaps accumulate. Case-level ranges barely use the supplied counts (Evidence Received → Evidence Review Completed 356, Technical Investigation → Disposition 119 versus Technical Investigation → Regulatory Assessment 74, Initial Triage → Fast Close 286, Supplier Analysis → Disposition 39). The cross-case warning about those frequencies is accurate and is the right restraint, but the per-case intervals are not visibly shifted from those base rates. Every case was created inside 48 hours, so the stated 8–15% delay still applies when the flag is normal; only D is treated as feed-uncertain. A, B, and E also share one four-item, two-reviewer queue, and that coupling is never identified.

C6-A and C6-D are the best of the set. A correctly blocks fast close, keeps both investigations mandatory, and leaves supplier analysis conditional. D correctly reads the 12:40 worklist line as an unlogged start, keeps disposition disabled until regulatory completion, and separates a delayed evidence event from an enabled next action. The cross-case discussion, especially the directly-follows limitation and the next-event versus eventual-event distinction, matches the task. Those parts are why the answer remains clearly competent rather than generic. The C6-B control-flow error, the empty C6-C alternative, and the C6-E timing calibration are why it is not a high score.
