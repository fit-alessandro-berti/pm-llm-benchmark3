6.5

The settled conclusions are careful and mostly faithful to the evidence, but they are not a complete, required-format assessment, and several smaller defects keep them well short of nearly flawless.

The final position correctly refuses to treat raw approval gaps (15, 9, and 20 points) as discrimination, refuses to wave away residuals because of case mix, and does not treat `risk_score_band` as neutral. It separates disparity, adjusted residual, legitimate routing, and unidentified discrimination. B and C are tied to support queues (3.8 and 4.7 days), specialist concentration, handovers, and adjusted cycle-time gaps (+2.6 and +4.1 days); D’s fraud path is treated as more plausibly risk-related while appeals and score bias stay open. Support steps are preserved, endpoints such as V6 are not treated as comparable, n/a waits are not treated as zero, and appeals are not read as automatic proof of error. Limitations and mitigation trade-offs cover the required topics.

The deductions are for the answer that remains. It never becomes the required eight-heading assessment; the later text is still an outline plus a bottom line. The PM4Py sketch is not a reliable plan: public PM4Py calls take a `parameters` dict, not loose `case_id_key` / `resource_key` kwargs, and the variant-normalization loop is left as `pass`. “Explanation problems” are asserted in the close even though explanation quality is unmeasured. The important non-inference that Group B’s 57% language-support need versus 24% V4 share does not show missing support is dropped from the path conclusion. “Warrant remediation now” is a bit stronger than an explicitly indeterminate, interval-free adjusted model can support.
