7.5

The stage arithmetic is careful and mostly right, and the mediation framework is unusually serious. The answer still misstates a core intervention and several case counts.

The time decomposition holds up. Conditional means (absent stages left missing, not zeroed), the 8.3 h gap split into about −2.6 h triage, +7.4 h manual, and +3.6 h escalation, the 10/10 versus 4/10 review rates, the 6/10 versus 2/10 escalation rates, the 0.61–0.64 score separation, T1/T2 versus T3/T4 separation, and the M2 overlap of R01 and R09 are all consistent with the log. Occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation. Cross-world natural effects are rejected for the right structural reasons: the flag is a bundle, triage completion induces the queue state, and queues interfere. P0–P4 are not treated as the same contrast, and the P3 pilot measures spillover and decision quality instead of optimizing cycle time alone. The PM4Py block parses timestamps, emits no events for absent stages, calls `format_dataframe` with a start key, builds both DFGs by flag, reports support, and warns against causal reading of performance edges.

The estimand table then undoes some of that care. E6 labels a threshold contrast as P1 versus P0. Crossing the cut changes the whole observed bundle (display, lane, and mandatory review) while the score itself jumps; it does not identify “hide the flag and leave the score visible.” That contradicts E1, which correctly says P1 is not `flag := 0`. E3 has the same kind of mismatch: column 2 is the full P2 policy, which would change queue waits, while column 5 holds `manual_queue_wait` at its P0 law. Those are different interventions. E4 is closer to a real separable capacity effect; calling it an interventional indirect effect is less damaging.

Countable claims are wrong. There are eight High-complexity cases, not seven, and R17 (High, unflagged) is reviewed by M1, so “M2 carries all High-complexity work” is false. It is repeated in the synthesis. Reviewed cases number 14, not “8 flagged/unflagged reviews.” Medium flagged cases number five (R02, R06, R08, R12, R15), not four. Escalation starts are split four overnight and four in daytime hours (09:42, 10:54, 15:12, 16:36); “mostly overnight” and “about 9 h of waiting” fold in roughly 2 h of senior service and overstate the night batch. The direct `complexity -> flag` edge is unsupported once the score threshold perfectly separates the flag. Reversal condition 1 treats long queue waits as appropriate handling of hidden risk and would drop P3; extra risk would justify occurrence or service, not a longer wait, so it threatens P2 more than protected capacity. The audit stopping rule (a 2-point miss-rate change on a 10% sample) is not a detectable trigger.

These are localized errors in an otherwise strong, process-aware answer. They sit on the exact points the task stresses: which intervention is defined, and what the timestamps actually show.
