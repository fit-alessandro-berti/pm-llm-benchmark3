7.4

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activated applications as non-defaults, reconciles all 48,000 cases, and keeps activation-conditional risk, competing outcomes, and counterfactual “would default if activated” as different estimands. Leakage handling for post-decision fields is mostly right, and the deployment feedback discussion is concrete. The score is held down by inconsistent prediction-time semantics, a misstated coverage figure, censoring categories that are collapsed, an as-of backtest that the code does not actually implement, and an incorrect repair for `risk_score`.

**Target.** The primary estimand is the right family: 90-day default given eventual activation under the observed policy, with multi-state and counterfactual targets clearly separated. The prediction time is not stable. Section 1 simultaneously specifies a fixed early prefix and a cut immediately before Accept/Reject. Those are different decision points. A cut defined from the future decision timestamp is not an online early-warning time, and rework features in section 5 are informative only if the prefix runs through later document loops. “Eventually activated” in the estimand formula is also wider than the matured-by-cutoff population actually used for labels. Gating an accept decision and pricing an already accepted account are left as one use case.

**Cohort.** The flow table adds to 48,000, 2,700/21,600 = 12.5% is right, and 60% of 2,900 is 1,740. The sentence that 21,600 is “88% of activated-matured accounts” is wrong. Activated accounts are 18,900 + 2,700 + 3,600 = 25,200, so 21,600 is 85.7% of activated accounts; the matured labeled set is the 21,600 itself. Permanently missing partner feeds never receive their own row, so they stay buried inside the 21,600 or the 3,600. Inclusion rule (iii) can drop matured activations that lack a valid prefix, but the table still assigns all 21,600 to labeled training. Accepted-but-not-yet-activated cases are absorbed into “still open.”

**Censoring.** Delayed labels, activation selection, transfer linkage, and MNAR feeds are distinguished, and ignorability is correctly called untestable. The opening move still files the 5,100 still-open applications and the 3,600 immature activations under one “ordinary right censoring” heading. On a time-to-default-after-activation clock, only the 3,600 are in the risk set; still-open cases have not started that clock and may later be rejected, withdrawn, or transferred. The primary text later fits survival only on activated cases, so the strategy is better than the taxonomy. IPW/Heckman is proposed without positivity: a hard credit threshold leaves regions with no activated counterparts, where inverse-probability weights are undefined. “Heckman-style” is also the wrong default name for a binary outcome; a bivariate probit is the actual selection model. Requiring activation + 90 + 35 days even when `label_available_at` is already known discards labels that have in fact arrived. No rule separates “feed not yet arrived” from “feed never arrives,” so the survival arm’s non-informative-censoring claim is doing too much work for partner-originated accounts.

**Prefixes and features.** Fixed-k prefixes, elapsed-time prefixes, short traces, loops, and the October 2025 rename are all addressed in prose. The as-of rule for `risk_score` is the wrong direction. Updates at or before the prediction time are legitimate; the value to keep is the latest one with timestamp at or before the cut. Taking the earliest value, which the code does with `iloc[0]`, throws away observed pre-decision updates. Case attributes repeated on every event (`case:requested_amount` in particular) are treated as known at start; in a flat export they are often the final snapshot copied backward. Historical base rates are correctly required to use only labels already available at prediction time. That safeguard is stated and not implemented.

**Validation.** A random case split is correctly rejected, and AUROC plus AUPRC, calibration, decision utility, concordance, and integrated Brier score match the binary and censored versions of the target. The calendar plan does not include a maturity embargo. With extraction on 15 February 2026 and a 90 + 35 day lag, an activation after about 13 October 2025 cannot be mature. A test fold of “later starts,” restricted to cases that already have labels, over-represents fast activations. The code then ignores the prose: one global cutoff of 15 February 2026, plus 60/20/20 quantiles of the pooled labeled start times, rather than predeclared regime windows and a rolling as-of label freeze. Quantiles of the full labeled span also use the test period to place the cutpoints.

**Code versus the design.** The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points, drops post-decision activities and leaky columns, asserts that those exclusions held, splits on case start, and does not invent a PM4Py survival API. It does not implement the as-of rule it documents. Terminal state, activation time, and prefixes are computed from events after `AS_OF_CUTOFF`, so an earlier backtest cutoff still sees future activations and rejections. For cases with no decision activity, `timestamp < max(timestamp)` drops the last event. `get_prefixes_from_log(..., length=3)` will discard short traces, while the text says to keep them with an explicit not-yet-occurred flag. `documents_total` is only a comment. Variant and temporal outputs are computed and never joined. Conflicting `default_90d` values become `unique()[0]`. Partner accounts with a missing feed are not removed. At the default extraction date some of this is latent; as a configurable backtest it is incorrect.

**What holds up.** Honest label semantics, a reconciling cohort, a usable leakage table, regime shift as a first-class validation problem, and explicit limits on counterfactual default risk are all in place. The remaining gaps are internal contradictions and a few operationally important biases, concentrated in prediction time, late-cohort selection, censoring of non-activated cases, and the as-of path in the code.
