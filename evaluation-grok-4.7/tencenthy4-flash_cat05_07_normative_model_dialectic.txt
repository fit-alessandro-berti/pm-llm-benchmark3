7.5

The final answer is dialectically strong, factually careful, and structurally complete, but the revised POWL has serious semantic holes that the write-up does not disclose.

What holds up: all seven required sections are present; both briefs are genuinely argued and each concedes the other side’s real points; case facts, dates, amounts, and aggregates match the record; the matrix covers all 15 cases, including control-flow-fitting resource and conflict cases; classifications are mostly reasoned rather than split down the middle; scope, expiry, and authority are handled with a real burden of proof; and the overlay table plus collection proposal include the required identifiers, snapshots, aliases, delegation, and timestamps.

What keeps it well below excellent:

- Rejection is XOR-exclusive with screening. `Reject_Supplier` sits in a branch that contains no sanctions or quality, so the model cannot express sanctions-then-reject, ordinary post-check rejection, or SUP-709. That is worse than M0 on a required path.
- `Request_More_Info` is placed entirely before route selection. SUP-707’s observed and desired order (sanctions, then repeated requests, then quality) is not a trace of this model. The “0..n loop” note does not state that failure.
- `Automated_Master_Data_Validation` is a mandatory visible transition on the auto route, so the same answer’s justified pilot cases fail the model whenever that event was never logged. The defense itself treats that gap as a data issue.
- The emergency branch forces quality and manual approval after activation. FD-7 only permits activation to precede them.
- SUP-711 is called indeterminate even though the same source transaction ID makes the duplicate a determined logging artifact. SUP-702 is called a justified exception while a mandatory PD-3 precondition is unevidenced. SUP-703 is dumped at the end of the judgment table.

The control-flow revision was a scored requirement, and these are model-semantics errors, not presentation nits.
