7.3

The dispatch reconstructions are largely careful and numerically reliable, but the write-up is not nearly flawless. Several required claims are factually wrong or internally inconsistent, and the scan-lag discussion mishandles a constraint the task specifically flags.

The decision tables are the strongest part. Segment sums really do equal `est_min` for all 32 jobs, including B01 = 14+17+14. QA/QB/QC/QD eligible sets, non-preemption checks, and the FIFO / priority / SPT / appointment / EDD predictions are mostly right. Slack arithmetic checks out (C05 is −8 at 10:18 and −22 at 10:32; C07 is +2 at 11:40; D05 started at 11:21 would finish at 11:51). The QC counterfactual correctly sees that a frozen 10:10 list and dynamic SPT make the same 10:32 prediction and then split at 11:34 and 11:40. QD correctly treats D07-before-D10 as FIFO/exclusive-skill equivalence, and the D-F idle while D10 fits is a real stickiness test. Assumptions A1–A3 are labeled. Confidence is ordered plausibly, and QC is appropriately left unstable.

Those merits do not cancel the following errors.

The synthesis overclaims QA. Section 2 correctly says that at 09:32 FIFO and SPT both select A07, while only priority selects A08. Section 7 then says three releases select the FIFO job when priority and SPT each name someone else, and includes A07 at 09:32. Only 08:30 (A02 vs A04) and 09:05 (A05 vs A08 and A07) are triple separations. That inflates the reason QA is called the most identifiable queue, and it contradicts the author’s own table.

The QC diagnosis cell mislabels its own best episode. C02 is the shortest initial job, and FIFO, priority, EDD, and least slack do miss it. C02 is not the longest-deadline job: C04’s deadline is 14:30, C02’s is 14:00. C02 is also not second-newest: among C01–C04 it is second-oldest. Those are false superlatives in the required diagnosis table.

Scan lag is applied in the wrong direction on the main QB ambiguity. Recorded arrivals are scan times, so a job is known at its scan and only uncertain earlier. B05 is scanned at 09:46; the stop is at 09:48. B05 is in the visible set at the stop. The lag cannot “move B05’s visibility” onto the preemption minute, nor can it put B02, scanned at 09:14, in doubt at 09:16. A decision committed before the recorded stop is a separate, unlabeled hypothesis. Section 5 repeats the slip by blaming that same lag for the D-X 11:20–11:30 insertion being too tight. That tightness is processing time versus 11:30, not arrival-scan delay. D08’s estimate of 8 minutes would finish at 11:28 if started at 11:20; the two-minute setup used to make D08 “collide” with the appointment is conjectural. This matters because 09:48 is QB’s strongest contradiction: if B05 was visible, the dispatcher interrupted B01 and still chose B03 over priority-1 B05, which weakens the priority-1 clause more than a scan-lag confound would.

The QD identifiability cell says FIFO, SPT, and priority “tie on D03 vs D05.” They do not. All three predict D03; scarce-skill and meeting-fit predict D05. The rules are equivalent to each other, not indifferent between the jobs. Section 2 already has this right.

Smaller defects accumulate. “Times below are minutes after 10:00” is false; the QC table uses clock time. At 08:58, SPT is described as a tie-break between A04 and A08, but the estimates are 7 and 25, so SPT uniquely picks A04. D09, arriving at 11:20, is omitted from D-X’s opening eligible set. “Gap rule” is undefined. “Six-minute appointment” means a six-minute job. “Scan bound already known to be active” overstates a stated maximum delay.

The operative conclusions — QA as myopic FIFO plus one idle-time appointment jump, QB as FIFO with a narrow priority-1 exception and a labeled 09:48 override, QC as an unstable sequence, QD as sticky eligibility rather than a skill optimizer, and center-wide priority as the dangerous overgeneralization — remain defensible. The grade is held down because those conclusions are carried by a diagnosis table, a scan-lag argument, and a final synthesis that each contain a clear mistake.
