5.5

The write-up has the required structure, a sensible six-regime chronology, and mostly correct within-regime means. It correctly refuses to treat 2026-04-16 catch-up as steady-state capacity. Those strengths are offset by arithmetic errors, unit confusion, and several queueing claims that the aggregates do not support.

Pooled headlines in §4 are wrong. Total arrivals are 2519 (mean 90.0, not 92.5) and completions 2409 (mean 86.0, not 89.5), so the pooled imbalance is about +3.9 cases/day, not +3.0. Mean WIP is 29.9, not 30.7; mean FTE is 5.14, not 5.05. Mean daily p90 is about 67 minutes (median of daily p90s is 72), not 73. The “understates weekday load by 19%” line is computed from the wrong pooled arrival mean. Regime means for R1–R6 are largely right, and the code would have printed the correct pooled means, so §4 and §8 contradict each other.

Hourly dispersion is also miscalculated. For every representative day the reported variance sits between the n and n−1 estimators and matches neither (2026-04-07 sample variance is 27.17, not 26.63; 2026-04-21 is 97.0, not 92.6). Fano factors inherited those errors. More importantly, a Fano factor across 12 hours mostly reflects a diurnal profile. D>1 on 2026-04-07 does not show a clustered non-Poisson process, and it is not evidence that M/M/c “severely” understates waits. Only 2026-04-21 looks genuinely bursty, and even there n=12 bins from one day cannot separate a time-varying rate from extra-Poisson noise.

λ is defined as cases per hour in §1 and then reported as cases per day. `mean_wip` is treated as if it were opening and closing inventory (“18 → 23”, “47 → 52 → 35”). Snapshot means are not boundaries. Cumulative net flow and WIP also fail to line up: from 2026-04-18 through 2026-04-28, arrivals minus completions are about +106 cases, while mean WIP only moves from 16 to 50. Equating R4’s +13.4 net flow with “WIP growing by 13.4 cases/day,” and calling the build “continuous,” ignores the weekend drop (43 → 23 → 20) and the non-monotonic weekday path. “Drained over 48 hours” is likewise false: mean WIP rose from 47 on the outage day to 52 on the high-completion day.

Little’s Law arithmetic for the stated inputs is fine, and outage, growth, and pooled windows are rightly marked not defensible. The R1 verdict `applicable` is too strong for non-contiguous days, unknown 07:00 stocks, and overnight closure, and the result is never specified as open-hours sojourn rather than clock time. Subtracting 30 minutes of service from the 146-minute implied flow is invalid; R1’s median is 25, the mean is unknown, and a median cannot be removed from a mean sojourn. Workload math repeats that mistake: 114 × 35 minutes uses the median as a mean. With p90 near 83, the distribution is right-skewed, so 66.5 touch-hours is more likely a lower sketch than a load. The 80% efficiency factor is an unlabeled assumption, and the jump to “~98 cases/day,” “+16/day,” and “>150 cases” / “>12 hours” contradicts observed WIP, which peaked near 50.

The audit misses a direct conflict in the supplied tables. `peak_08_10_pct` does not match arrivals in 08:00–09:59 on the hourly vectors (2026-04-07: 17 cases, about 17%, versus 31% in the daily column). On three days the percentage is closer to 09:00–11:00; on 2026-04-21 no two-hour slice equals 47%. Calling that window “the first two operating hours” is also wrong (open time starts at 07:00). Staffing advice aimed at 08:00–10:00 follows the column name rather than the vectors, whose weekday mode is 10:00–11:00. “Completions per FTE were lower” on 2026-04-16 is unsupported: 129/7.0 exceeds the R1 completions-per-FTE rate. Express-lane times of ~20 and ~40 minutes are invented; service time is not split by variant. The coding-change explanation for `complex_pct` is dropped in favor of a causal mix story. The alternative “matrix” mostly relabels the same blocks and omits weekends. The change-point code builds a CUSUM and then ignores it.

Coverage and regime design are above a superficial pass. Numerical hygiene, the WIP-versus-net-flow identity, dispersion, and several policy numbers are not reliable enough for a high score.
