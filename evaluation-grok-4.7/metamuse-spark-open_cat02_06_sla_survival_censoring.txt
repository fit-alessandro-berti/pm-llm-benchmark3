6.3

The estimand design is the strongest part of the answer: time origin, horizon, right censoring versus competing events versus already-observed breaches, and the E1/E2/E3 split are defined before estimators and mapped to governance versus workload. Transfer is given three labeled treatments, immortal time for `Escalate` is handled correctly, and Aalen–Johansen plus landmarking is a justified primary/secondary plan. The code keeps open cases, stratifies, and builds a timestamp-free PM4Py-style log.

The numerical layer is not reliable, and several of those errors are conceptual rather than rounding. Under transfer-as-censor, \(S(72)\approx0.435\) and \(\mathrm{CIF}_1(72)\approx0.313\); under transfer-as-competing, \(S(72)\approx0.380\) and \(\mathrm{CIF}_1(72)\approx0.304\). Claiming those CIFs are identical is wrong: an early transfer changes \(S(t-)\) and therefore later resolution increments. Cohort CIFs are about 0.29 (A) and 0.32 (B), not 0.31 and 0.30, and Gray’s \(p\approx0.9\) is not computed. Naive \(1-\mathrm{KM}(72)\) with non-resolution censored is about 0.36, not 0.45. Resolved-only means are 54.7 h (A) and 45.6 h (B), not 44.3 h and a 10 h gap.

Supplied traces are also miscounted. AutoCheck-first is 12/17 in B and 0/15 in A, not 7/17 and 1/15. S15 is cohort A, but it is cited as B non-compliance. S21, S25, and S32 are Screen→Escalate, not Review→Escalate. Escalated non-transfers are 9/29, not 8/29. “2/8 P1 opens” and “2/5 P3 stalled” use the wrong denominators. B has one transfer by 72 h (S21), not two.

Bias direction is reversed for pre-horizon censoring: dropping S10 shrinks the risk set and raises, rather than lowers, \(\mathrm{CIF}_1\). The “Manski” band adds an AJ estimate that already assumes independent censoring to a stalled-open probability; that is not a bound. RMST is given as both ~38 h and ~42 h. The third transfer scenario in code duplicates the censor encoding, and `len(v)` will break if `get_variants` returns counts. Assumptions and limitations are otherwise well prioritized, but the invented precision and trace errors keep the analysis from being dependable.
