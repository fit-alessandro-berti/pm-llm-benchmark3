The six-section structure and the main timeline are right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing-mix change, August–September stack process, concept, and population effects, and October is label-censored. That is not enough to carry the memo, because several operational conclusions contradict that story or the data.

The training decision is unsafe. January–June are called safe with a “stable pipeline” and “steady” variant shares, but April is the corrupted month (renamed diagnosis, 17% unknown activity, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped variants, stale cached feature, duplicates only partly removed) and June’s V2 share jumps to 52%. Short-term recalibration that fits V3 to January–June labels would pull risk back toward ~0.33 when the matured V3 breach rate is 0.46 in July and 0.64 in August, which hides the real shift. The containment rule is also inconsistent: maturity under 80% does not cover July (98%), August (93%), or September (82%), yet those months are listed as suppressed. October’s matured subset is still a biased sample, because the backlog hits longer paths, and that selection problem is not carried into the recalibration gate.

Taxonomy and evidence slip in the same places. June is both the primary covariate-drift month and, later, a pure mix shift whose “signals still align.” They do not: mean risk stays 0.20 against a 0.15 breach rate, with ECE 0.060. V2 did not triple; it rose from about 34–35% to 52%. January–June part-wait is not a flat 35 h; it moved from 28 h to 42 h before the July break. April is implicated under label delay while the same row says April was fully mature. Partner Z’s 0.45 versus 0.27 is treated as high-confidence population evidence without the stated slower maturation of Partner Z labels. The same-path test treats a higher V3 breach, which coincides with a much longer order-to-receipt edge, as proof that the conditional outcome relationship changed.

The code sketch misses the process-mining point. Dropping duplicates on case id keeps one event per case and destroys traces, so the DFG, variants, and edge times are not meaningful. `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` is never mapped, so the unknown-activity rate stays wrong. Variant shares call `.count()` on counts and will fail. Event-calendar and case-start cohorts are named, not built. `discover_performance_dfg` is given an invented `performance_key`, and the monthly return never actually summarizes the order-to-receipt edge that drives the audit.
