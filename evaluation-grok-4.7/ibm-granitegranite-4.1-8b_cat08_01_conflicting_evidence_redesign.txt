The diagnosis names the right tension, and a few anchors match the evidence (fast-approval complaints 14.1% versus 3.3% on the repair path, 31% cheaper before rework and 9% after, premium high-value 22% versus 5% and 20h versus 96h, R1, and the stated resource pools). The required sections are present. The quantitative layer and the redesign logic are not reliable enough to defend a change.

Edge counts are treated as case counts, including “25,500 cases (51%)” for the auto-screen → fast-approval edge of 2,550. “36%” for that same move does not match 2,550/5,000 or 3,600/5,000. The directly-follows graph does not balance at manual review, fraud/risk, dispatch, or repair, so those frequencies are not a closed census. That limit is never used.

Activity-edge time is treated as cycle time. Auto-screen → fast approval has median 0.4h; segment fast-path medians are 8h, 13h, 20h, and 28h. The same write-up later cites 20h versus 96h and still uses ≤0.5–1h as a pilot go-threshold. Performance figures are also misread as the duration of the destination activity (the 5.2h / 11h edges).

Other evidence is misassigned. The 390 abandonments are not all evidence failures (320 from missing evidence, 70 after customer approval). The 22% figure is the unclear-defect segment share, not the repeated-request rate or the abandonment rate; repeated requests are at least two in 26% of cases that enter that activity. Premium manual cycle time is not a median of 20h. Repair quote is not described as underused; quality check is. Fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces, low precision, R3, and the complaint lag (median 50–70h, p90 150–180h) are unused. The long-tail link between dispatch and wrong-part selection is only lightly touched.

The three redesigns are one split (fast lane versus reviewed lane) plus an evidence portal and a pre-dispatch gate, with the classifier changed. That is not three operating designs. Redesign A conflicts with itself: premium low-risk may skip evidence, then a later gate requires evidence before dispatch. It applies the 15–35% manual-inflow warning as a labor saving and claims an unsupported drop to 8% complaints and a 5–10h shorter manual path while sending more work into a pool that already queues above 82% utilization. Redesign B replaces the EUR 400 rule with an unexplained EUR 300 rule, skips the evidence check for standard low-risk accessories (R2 allows that exception only for premium low-risk accessories), and still rates compliance “very low,” while parking volume on a single 45-minute escalation slot. Redesign C is a generic learning screen. It keeps the 0.4h error, promises about 3% complaints and about 20% less labor, and adds only 3–6h on the compliance lane, against reviewed-path medians of 46–140h. Stricter routing would pressure fraud/risk (two specialists) and manual review; the design claims the manual lane shrinks.

The comparison table’s direction symbols are blank, the current process is marked best-fit for premium and standard high-value devices (the segments where the fast path is worst), and the added-hour claims are off by roughly an order of magnitude. Failure modes repeat the 22% abandonment baseline error. The experiment plan’s 54% scope does not equal the segments named, Phase 1 and Phase 2 sequencing mix those segments, and stop/go levels sit far from the observed base rates. Downstream learning is a single counterfactual sentence and does not wait out the known complaint lag, so a short pilot would count shifted failure as speed.
