The answer meets the required structure and reaches the right decision: conditional go, limited to legally enabled work, with Site A’s −9 h median treated as confounded. It still has material analytical defects.

The transport graph mis-specifies causation. `case_complexity → originals_required` and `case_complexity → digital_documents` reverse or invent influence: originals and channel are legal and intake constraints that may shape complexity, not effects of it. `cycle_time → appeal` is likewise weakly motivated; appeals track decision quality and rework more directly than duration. The leading space on `document_capacity` is a real adjacency-list defect. The prose is better than the graph: shared join-versus-critical-path reasoning, selection nodes, and the warning that similar control flow does not identify a transport effect are sound, and the explanation stays inside the word limit.

Section 5 is not faithful enough to Site B’s constraints. For 62% of cases the median originals delay is 18 h and is external; identity service is only 3.4 h and may already start after registration. A population median change of −3 to −6 h under “optimistic structural transfer,” justified by “earlier identity and receipt,” therefore overstates what parallelization can move, especially if the median case sits in the originals majority. The digital-only dilution, the ban on multiplying a subgroup effect by 38%, and the bottleneck-shift case where the center improves while p90 worsens are much stronger. Those ranges remain judgmental rather than mapped from the service, wait, and delay figures.

Section 4’s originals row treats identity-during-receipt as a benefit of the new intervention even though Site B’s rules already allow both to start after registration. The incremental gain exists only where the single-owner lock, or local practice, currently serializes them. That mechanism is discussed elsewhere and not tied cleanly to this row. The exact DFG identity `2108 / 3400 = 62%` for Originals Receipt → Document Check is also left as mere “consistency.”

The recommendation locally contradicts itself. After correctly refusing to treat the nine-hour drop as causal, it says the mix shift, API upgrade, added reviewers, and huddles make that improvement “inseparable from parallelization.” Those co-interventions make the improvement inseparable from the bundle, not from parallelization. “A legal reviewer must determine whether legal review is required” also risks spending the two-person legal pool on triage. Case-level randomization is appropriately hedged, but with pools of 4, 7, 3, and 2 it should have been the fallback, not the default.

The remaining sections are strong: statutory document gating, WIP not copied from Site A, intention-to-treat versus post-hoc overlap, implementation failure versus mechanism failure, eight well-formed assumptions, and a non-sequential POWL candidate with an assessment join and a supplement loop. The soundness-unpacking and `validate_partial_orders` calls are only as credible as the unshown local PM4Py API; they are not an independent reason to reject the model.
