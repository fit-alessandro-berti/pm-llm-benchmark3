7.5

The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and stays tied to the log: segments, DFG paths, SP1/SP2/Q1/R1, batch-size and wait effects, air versus ground, dedicated vans, rework, and obsolescence. It correctly refuses a blanket ban on air or vans, keeps true downtime and short-slack cases fast, and treats rework, returns, packaging, and 14-day obsolescence as environmental effects. The intervention set, rebound list, segment playbook, and conformance checks are specific enough to implement and monitor.

The effects table is the main flaw. Stock-to-air is already about 3% late, while stock-to-ground is about 8% late, and SP1 still allows anything better than 85% on-time. Shifting Standard-stock service-contract air to ground can therefore raise lateness while remaining SP1-compliant. S1 and S4 are marked as reducing late-delivery risk, and the combined package is also marked down. That overstates the service result the rest of the answer is trying to protect. Several component rows show late risk rising, so the package-level improvement is not earned.

Smaller problems remain. Critical-downtime is treated as a customer segment in the assumptions, although it is a case type. “Critical paths” are described as very high-transport even though the stock/ground exception is low-emission. Rework is marked down for promise negotiation and partial-shipment reduction without path evidence. Inspection congestion is filed under resilience. Kilograms of scrap and packaging are left incomparable with transport kgCO2e, and excluding upstream material burdens is not flagged as biasing transport above scrap and rework. Obsolescence is covered inside batching, but not developed as its own displacement path.
