7.8

The design is strong where it matters most: it refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, it accounts for all 48,000 applications, and the primary estimand is an observed-policy decomposition (pathway competing risks × conditional 90-day default given activation) with an explicitly stronger counterfactual alternative. The leakage table, as-of reasoning, temporal split rationale, and claim boundaries are largely right. The score is held down by estimand slips and by a skeleton that contradicts the design.

**Target.** The product \(\Pr(\text{activate}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_p})\times\Pr(T_{\text{def}}\le 90\mid\text{activate},\mathcal{F}_{t_p})\) is a coherent joint only if both pieces use the same activation definition. Section 1 then offers an unspecified “or 60-day resolution window,” which would make that product incoherent. Cause-specific Cox and Fine–Gray are treated as interchangeable, but the stated estimand is a cumulative incidence, not a cause-specific hazard. The joint labeling rule is missing: failure to activate is an observed zero for the joint incidence endpoint, which is different from imputing `default_90d = 0`.

**Cohort and censoring.** The flow table sums to 48,000 and separates competing terminals from administrative censoring. It then places permanently missing partner feeds inside the 3,600 “not yet matured” accounts. Those 3,600 are defined as immature horizon or delayed feed; the snapshot identity leaves no residual bin, and that ambiguity is not flagged. Weights are called stabilized IPCW while the formula is unstabilized \(1/\hat\pi\). The 35-day buffer is a blunt rule, and the sentence that both excludes immature activations and censors them at \(C_i=0\) is unclear. Informative censoring from long-running open cases is asserted away rather than tied to conditioning on start time.

**Validation.** Expanding windows by case start, with policy and migration stress tests, are the right structure, and the rejection of `pm4py.split_train_test` is correct. The post-15 Oct 2025 fold cannot support mature 90-day default metrics by a 15 Feb 2026 extract once a 90-day horizon and a 7–35 day feed lag are taken seriously. That limit is understated. A regime indicator is proposed as a feature even though a pre-July training window makes it constant and non-estimable.

**Code versus prose.** Several required pieces are present and used appropriately: `read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, start-time splits, `get_prefixes_from_log`, train-prefix-only `discover_dfg` / `get_variants`, dropping post-decision fields, and a survival handoff without a fake PM4Py survival API. The implementation still breaks the design. Still-open cases are labeled `Selectively_Unobserved_Open`. One global extract date supplies labels for every fold, so the Section 7 as-of label cutoff is not what the code does. `default_90d` is not blanked when `label_available_at` is after the cutoff, which leaks if labels are repeated on earlier events. Resource experience uses `cumcount` on a frame sorted by case id, so it is neither time-causal nor leakage-safe. The main leakage assert compares each prefix’s max timestamp with itself. Timezone-aware timestamps are compared with naive date strings. Elapsed-time prefixes are specified in prose and absent in code.
