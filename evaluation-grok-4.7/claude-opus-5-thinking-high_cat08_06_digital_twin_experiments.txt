The design is strong on the decisions that matter: it is a prospective discrete-event twin, waiting is an output of calendars and queues, objections stay latent, gross and net lead time are separated, and optimality is explicitly disclaimed. The deductions below are for internal contradictions and identification gaps, not for missing engine code.

The arrival model can double-count. Section 2.1 writes intensity as a background non-homogeneous Poisson process plus a top-20 cluster process, while the Poisson GLM is fit to binned counts that already include those architects (31% of volume). A superposition only works if the background is fit on the complement, or if the cluster mean is subtracted from the GLM mean. As written, mean load and Monday/peak congestion are inflated, which biases every capacity comparison.

Interval censoring in §2.2 is not valid under multitasking. Bounding service by the gap between consecutive completions of the same resource is an upper bound only if that person does one case at a time. Planners, legal officers, and environmental reviewers, with multi-hour tasks and multi-day queues, are exactly the pools most likely to juggle work. Those are also the pools whose service times are confounded with the HR calendars that D4 and D6 depend on. The utilization identity constrains the ratio of work to capacity; it does not by itself identify the effect of adding one inspector.

Empirical “waiting” is treated as a queue target more often than the identification argument allows. Section 2.3 correctly says only sojourn between completions is assumption-free, and that applicant delay and the statutory notice are exogenous. Section 6.5 then ranks bottlenecks against the published waiting column. For decision drafting that column mixes the AND-join with resource queueing; for fire review and inspection it mixes calendar closure, travel, batch hold, and queueing. Interventions aimed at the wrong component will look successful in the twin and fail in the office.

Public-notice timing is operationally ambiguous. The excerpt’s notice stamp falls about one day after fire review and about fifteen days before inspection scheduling, so the fourteen-day statutory delay sits in the next activity’s sojourn if every stamp is a completion. `add_timing_columns` does that, then marks the notice row itself as the exogenous gap. Council meetings are said to gate the notice period, but unlike the legal blackout there is no rule for whether the clock pauses, a meeting must fall inside the window, or issuance simply waits. Complex-variance tails are described as council-dependent, so this is not a side detail.

Control flow for rework is thinner than the state vector implies. Completeness and inspection loops have hazards and caps. Planning rework, legal rework, and appeal do not say whether the case repeats one activity or re-enters the whole parallel block, which changes fire, environmental, and inspector load. The tree’s redo labels (`Completeness rework`, `Inspection rework`) also disagree with the log pattern the estimator uses (the same activity repeated). Variant and alignment checks will fail until one representation is chosen. The ≥0.95 fitness target is coherent only against models discovered from both logs, not against the simplified tree, and that distinction should be explicit in the acceptance rule.

Baseline batching and D3 are collapsed. The known coupling is same architect and district; D3 is district-wide batching. The engine only opens bundles by district and week, so it cannot represent today’s narrower practice and therefore cannot estimate the incremental travel saving versus extra wait. `discover_batches` will not recover that distinction unless it is applied to architect–district slices and interpreted against a geographic travel model.

Common random numbers are over-claimed. Entity-indexed streams are the right design for D2 or D6, where the same cases lose or reorder activities. They do not give identical arrival streams once S1 changes the demand multiplier or S5 changes cluster size: case i is no longer the same entity. Paired regret across those stresses needs thinning on shared uniforms, or it should be described as unpaired.

The decision rule does not match the risk register. D2’s missed-defect and compliance risk is declared unmodelled, but the guardrails only limit rework, utilization, backlog, and a 5% worsening of complex-variance lead time. Skipping inspection can improve every modeled metric while increasing an unmeasured safety failure. D9 is appropriately conditional on the latent objection rate; D2 needs the same treatment, or an explicit veto, before it can win on regret.

Smaller inconsistencies: the 180-day cooldown is short relative to a 260-day p90, so the default tail estimator still censors the cases that drive the decision; S2’s temporary peak-season absences and extra council weeks are encoded as a permanent cut to 3 inspectors and 1 legal officer; `ExperimentConfig.n_parameter_draws` is never used; no-shows have a random stream and no estimator; WIP initialization via traces-intersecting time filters does not recover phase, queue, or in-service state when starts are missing. Calibration to variant shares, medians, p90s, and utilization, followed by acceptance tests on those same statistics, is in-sample fit. The temporal holdout is the real check and should be the gate, with the ±10%/±20% bands applied out of sample.

PM4Py usage is largely faithful: pandas ingestion, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, and case-arrival average are separated from the event engine, and the tree’s missing data conditions, calendars, and appeal paths are stated. The skeleton is coherent as a skeleton. It is not yet a specification that would keep estimation, validation, and the stress matrix aligned.
