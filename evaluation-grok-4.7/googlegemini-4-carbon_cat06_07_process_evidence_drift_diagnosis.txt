7.4

The audit’s main chronology is right: April is a schema and prefix-pipeline failure, June is a post-decision routing-mix change, August is a supplier performance shock with concept drift and no Partner Z, September stacks population shift on that shock, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The six required sections, falsifiers, stratified tests, leakage warning, and retrain gates are all present. The score is held down by repeated places where monthly aggregates are rewritten into sharper facts than the table supports.

The May split is the clearest logical error. Unknown-activity share of 2%, missing-queue share of 3%, and ECE of 0.050 are May totals. The answer treats them as measurements of 1–5 May, then marks 6–31 May safe to train. The patch date makes that a hypothesis. It is not a cohort you can cut from these data, and “not all derived features were recomputed” can still taint post-patch rows.

The concept-drift and covariate rows mis-state the series. V3 matured breach is 0.33 in May, 0.35 in June, 0.46 in July, and 0.64 in August. “Jul–Aug … doubles (0.35 → 0.46 → 0.64)” assigns June’s rate to July and calls a 0.46-to-0.64 move a doubling. July’s overall breach is 0.19 against a prediction of 0.18, with ECE 0.042, so “Jul–Oct (severe upward)” overstates July. Predicted risk is 0.21 in May and 0.20 in June, not an 0.18–0.19 plateau from January through August. August’s mean score stays 0.19 while V3 share rises. That flat score is evidence against pre-diagnosis covariate drift. V3 share is a downstream path mix, and listing August as genuine covariate drift conflates it with process drift.

Section 6 states that October breaches are already “exceeding 30%+.” Elsewhere the same quantity is an IPCW hypothesis that is falsified if the mature rate stays near 0.16. The diagnosis-to-remote-fix median of 3.8–4.6 h is an edge time, not full cycle time. Stage 2 fits a recalibrator on August at 93% maturity, while the safe-month rule wants August at or above 99% and the text itself says 93% understates August breaches.

The monitoring gates are the right design: data-quality and label-maturity alarms block retraining, and June-style calibration shift is separated from August-style feature failure. The code uses real PM4Py calls, normalizes the renamed diagnosis before features, deduplicates, and distinguishes event-calendar from case-start cohorts. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is reduced to a column count, so the April cached event-count defect would not show up in the summary. `v3_complete_share` and case-start performance DFGs drop unfinished paths, which is the same censoring the narrative warns about. Predictive and calibration checks are merged into one monitor row.

Brier is unused. Variant-weighted matured rates in August (about 0.31) sit above the headline 0.28, which would have strengthened the censoring point and is never reconciled. Core remediation—freeze October, repair labels, add supplier and Partner Z features, refuse realized-variant models—is proportionate and correctly argued.
