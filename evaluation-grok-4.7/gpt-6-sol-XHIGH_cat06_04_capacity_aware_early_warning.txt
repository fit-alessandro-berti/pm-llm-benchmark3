Defects that cap the score:

The live decision does not implement the stated rule. Section 1 dispatches only when a credible path to resolution exists and expected benefit plus any service duty covers the marginal shadow cost. Section 2 then marks I-07 “Eligible now? Yes” and puts it first, while the same row lists “ability to finish resolution in time” as the main uncertainty. I-07 is still in Triage with 1.5 hours left; a 45-minute swarm leaves about 45 minutes for diagnosis completion, resolution, and customer validation. Its pre-spillover margin is only about €800. One slot at the low spillover rate is already 0.06 expected extra breaches among six untreated diagnosis-stage cases, which clears €800 at modest untreated values. The service override is named, and the beneficiary-and-exposed-cases log is a real safeguard, but the write-up never shows that this override survives the shadow-cost test the rule itself requires.

Priority scores are pre-spillover midpoints, so the ranking metric omits the externality the rule says to subtract. That inflates I-07 and I-09 relative to later, higher-margin cases and makes the €0.8k / €3.6k / €9.0k ordering look more decisive than the deployable rule is.

The scenario table does not keep the three scenarios apart on delay. Optimistic spillover is defined as the low end, but the optimistic row still lists 24–60 aggregate minutes, which is the full 3×(8–20) base range and is identical to the base row. Adverse delay (45–90 minutes) is right; adverse breach spillover is only “at least 0.54,” i.e. the top of the base band, so the adverse probability harm is not actually separated from base.

Net sign depends on an illustrative W = €20,000. At that W, base net is about +€2.5k to +€9.7k and adverse is negative by at least about €5.4k. There is no break-even W. Base high-spillover net crosses zero near W ≈ €13.3k / 0.54 ≈ €25k, inside the value range of cases already on the board. “Revalue before dispatch” is the right caveat, but the planned three-slot portfolio is still presented as the action while the audited numbers are only conditionally positive.

The allocation also has an operational hole. I-06 is correctly frozen pending source verification, yet the only precommitted swap is I-02 replacing I-04. A verified I-06 would be a Public Sector diagnosis-stage case with a 4-hour deadline and a ~€2.6k midpoint margin, and the plan has no slot for that result. I-04’s table action (“Intervene”) is also firmer than the later conditional deferral.

Smaller faults: “clinical/operational severity” imports a severity construct this incident process does not supply. Full false-alarm burden is charged inside an expected-impact table; the text admits this is conservative, and it matches the prompt’s screening formula, but it is still not an expected cost. The one-hour duplicate cool-down, reserve-release rule, and post-swarm re-entry bar are operationally usable.

Coverage of the six required sections is complete, the hard exclusions are correct, and the displayed arithmetic checks. The score stays below the top band because the first dispatch contradicts the eligibility and shadow-cost rule, the optimistic harm row misstates delay, and the portfolio’s net claim is not robust to the untreated value the answer itself treats as unknown.
