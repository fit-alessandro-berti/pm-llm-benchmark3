7.5

The final design is internally consistent and technically strong, but it is not nearly flawless.

The POWL model is the right canonical choice and is essentially exact: `R` forks into `C` and `A`; `C→P` and `A→D` stay independent; the trial loop is an AND-join of `P` and `D`; risk and supplier stay unordered relative to the trial; release joins the loop, the risk choice, and the optional supplier; archive follows release. Risk as `XOR(Supervisor, concurrent(Quality, Regulatory))`, supplier as `XOR(Consult, τ)`, and `LOOP(Execute→Inspect, Adjust)` correctly give `(Execute, Inspect)(Adjust, Execute, Inspect)*`, so Adjust cannot follow the exiting inspection. The eight witnesses match those models, and the approximation witness (Design before Contain finishes) really does separate the tree from the intended partial order.

The process tree is a safe under-approximation, not an unsafe one: the early barrier after `*(Contain, Analyze)` adds false precedence instead of allowing trial-before-plan or release-before-approval. That policy is explicit. It is not shown to be as faithful as possible. A different series-parallel cut — join `(Contain→Plan)` with `(Analyze→Design)`, then run risk, supplier, and the loop concurrently — preserves the two-stream cross-join and approval/trial overlap, and only delays approval and supplier until planning and design finish. The chosen cut instead destroys `Contain || Design` and `Analyze || Plan`. Those are alternative Pareto compromises forced by the N-shaped dependency; presenting the early barrier as the considered encoding, without that comparison, overstates what process trees must lose.

Lesser problems remain. The compact tree expression is not valid PM4Py syntax for the silent supplier skip. “Every unconnected pair” is covered only by a short list plus “etc.” Token-based replay is a weak conformance check for a model whose optionality and loop exit are silent; alignments are the check that matches that risk. The construction was never converted or played out, so the API-plausible code is still an unverified claim.
