5.5

The answer has the right skeleton and several locally correct reconstructions, but it is not reliable on the station-level quantities that drive the bottleneck, Little’s Law, and “stable conclusions.” Under a strict reading those are core failures, not cosmetic ones.

Case-level fully observed waits, services, and completed sojourns are mostly right, and the hard missing-start bounds are the best part of the write-up. For P11 the physical window (wait/service splitting 80 minutes) and the strict C2 window after P08 (start only in 12:00–12:25, so wait 55–80 and service 0–25) are correct, and the answer properly refuses a single typical-service point. P07 and P15 resource windows are also essentially right, open cases are kept as elapsed lower bounds, and the C2 outage is correctly judged small relative to the queues. Definitions of queue wait, active service, and sojourn are not confused in the formulas.

The aggregate arithmetic then breaks. S1 completions are 20 (P01–P20), not 21; “21 comp + 1 OPEN + 1 waiting” sums to 23. The 08:00–10:00 / 10:00–12:00 / 12:00–14:00 completion bins sum to 21, so a boundary case is double-counted, and arrival versus completion endpoints are not the same rule. S2 “arrivals” are likewise off: about 7, 7, and 6 S1 completions across those bins, not ~5, ~8, and ~8, and late S2 completions are 5, not 4. Overall “21 arrivals to S2” repeats the off-by-one.

S1 utilization is the most serious process-mining error. Clipped busy time is about 193/240 (≈80%) in 08:00–10:00, ≈205/240 (≈85%) in 10:00–12:00, and ≈195/240 (≈81%) in 12:00–14:00, with a full-horizon total near 595/720 (≈83%). The reported 62% then 94% then 96% path is not a defensible range. The early figure matches summing only services that both start and end inside the bin (~150–155), which drops P08’s carry-in and P07’s in-progress work — exactly “utilization from completed cases alone.” I1/I2 are on a steady 5-minute-gap chain all morning, so the claimed jump through 90% after 10:00 is false. Calling “>88% after 10:00” a conclusion that survives imputation is therefore not stable; it is not true in the central scenario. Overall S2 busy near 570/705 (≈81%) is reasonable, but the early S2 arrival/completion counts and the garbled “240+225” availability expression are not.

Those bad rates contaminate the Little’s Law check. Late S2 inflow is about 3–3.5/h, not 4/h, so λW near 6.9 versus L≈5 is mostly the counting error. Non-stationarity is real; the numerical “failure” as written is not. Completed-only S2 wait is about 32 minutes for the 14 finished cases, not ~36, and the censored-adjusted mean of elapsed waits is about 38–39, so “≥45” is not a demonstrated lower bound. “Monotone” completed sojourn is false (P08 180, P09 155, P10 175). Local false notes: P06’s S2 wait is 25 against an S1 service of 30, so it does not exceed it; P16’s elapsed S2 wait of 85 exceeds P14 and P17 (both 65), not “all except” them.

P05 is mishandled relative to the answer’s own rule. Strict C1 feasibility caps service at 30 minutes (start 09:45–10:15). Typical S2 service is ~40 and is infeasible, so the central value is the resource upper bound, not a typical-service imputation. The text still calls it the “only fit” and “typical.” The optimistic paragraph first defines late starts, then reverses itself; the pessimistic scenario uses service 0, which is a degenerate bound for an activity that occurred, and the busy-time remark does not parse.

The single code block does not reproduce the claimed metrics. MISSING, OPEN, and blank all become NaT. The WIP service test `s1_end > t` drops P21 for its whole open service. Only central imputations are hardcoded; the three scenarios are not computed. The PM4Py helpers are wrapped in `try/except`, aimed at a synthetic ARRIVE/FINISH log rather than the fully observed S1/S2 intervals, and their outputs are never printed. There is no code comment that `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is the wrong tool for station queues; that remark is only a runtime string. Station rates, busy time, and uncertainty tables are not produced.

The directional story that can survive is narrower than the one written: S1 service is stably ~25–30 minutes, S2 is longer and more variable, both resources are tight, S1’s queue appears first and then flattens near 100 minutes, and by 14:00 the residual WIP is mostly S2 (4 waiting, 2 in service, plus P21/P22 still at S1). That bottleneck shift is plausible. It is not established by the utilization path, the subperiod rates, or the Little’s Law arithmetic in this answer.
