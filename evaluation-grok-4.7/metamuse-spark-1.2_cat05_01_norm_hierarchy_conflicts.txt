6.8

The assessment is structurally complete and often precise, but it is not nearly flawless. Several stated rules are misapplied, one primary classification is poorly supported, and the burden and time rules are relaxed in the cases where they matter most.

The method, issue-specific hierarchy, and defeasible catalogue are the strongest parts. Authority is split by control type, silence is generally refused as a waiver, A6 is treated as non-curative, and ECD-2 is confined to timing and the supplier register. The pseudo-logic uses the requested defeasible form, and the draft clause covers hierarchy, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, records, expiry, and anti-normalization. P-503, P-504, and P-506 are substantively well decided: self-approval and missing Compliance on a €140k purchase, the cafeteria purchase outside both B4 and ECD scope, and the post-expiry chemical purchase are all handled with the right time and scope cuts.

The rule catalogue then overreaches. R-B2 imposes a before-commitment sequence the SOP does not state. B5 keeps Finance obligatory; it does not place it before commitment. A6 concerns missing GPIP pre-commitment approvals and cannot supply that missing sequence for a €38k local approval. R-A1 is also written as a triple identity, while A1 separately bars the requester or the committer from being the final approver. R-D2 states a single reading of “as well” as if it were settled.

Those choices distort the cases. P-501 is the clearest miss. A four-hour stoppage supports B4’s permission to commit before the PO; the supplier was approved; the emergency form falls inside 24 hours; and €38k triggers neither A2 nor A3. B4’s delegation sentence substitutes for an absent Plant Manager’s approval. It does not make SHIFT-1 an unauthorized committer, and the record does not show that SHIFT-1 issued Plant_Approve. Same-day Plant and Finance approvals therefore leave, at most, a debatable sequencing question. NONCOMPLIANT at high confidence is too strong, and the section 5 alternative does not test the stronger reading that B2 is silent on sequence.

P-502’s Compliance result can stand, but the Finance discussion cannot. The CFO email is 15 July 07:20, after the 14 July 10:15 commitment. Applying D1 at that commitment breaks the stated time-of-act rule, and calling it a “cure” breaks A6. R-B6 is marked satisfied although no emergency form is in evidence. P-503 wrongly says there is no authorization record: Emergency_Authorize is timestamped 10:55, before commitment. The real defects are SoD, absent Compliance, and doubtful backup-line scope. P-505 treats R-A3 as applicable and satisfied for an €88k approved-supplier purchase; A3 does not trigger. The nine-minute-fast clock is a case fact, not the ON-44 outage note, and the alternative invents a B1 breach without a PO time.

P-505, P-507, and P-508 expose a method split. The stated burden resolves ambiguity against the party claiming an exception, and the hierarchy itself says IC members are not shown to be budget owners. D1 can at most be argued to cover a Finance operational step. The residual A2 budget-owner gap is nevertheless classified as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION rather than INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT. The closing note adopts that permissive view openly, but it does not revise the burden rule. Emergency-form handling is also inconsistent across P-501, P-502, P-506, and P-507. Remediation is practical, yet it repeats the P-501 delegation theory and the P-503 missing-record claim.

The answer distinguishes sources, windows, and evidence types better than a flat compliant/noncompliant review. The grade is held down by the invalid B2 timing inference, the retroactive use of D1, the A3 trigger error, misstated evidence, and primary labels that are more permissive or more certain than the answer’s own rules allow.
