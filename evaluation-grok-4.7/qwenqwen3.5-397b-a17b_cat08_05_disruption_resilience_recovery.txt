The answer covers all seven required sections and lands the central thesis: hospital protection can be resilience, throughput without authorization or custody controls is not, and post-close audit repair is hidden workload rather than real-time compliance. The recovery design includes triggers, triage, product controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, and a remediation gate, and the recommendations carry effort, benefit, and residual risk.

Those strengths are undercut by material misreadings. Pattern C (ambient standing-authorization skip) is blamed for the 2.9% controlled-substance dual-control gap, which is a different control and a different product class. Pattern F already required a check of every controlled-substance order plus a sample of others; its stated miss was cold-chain temperature documentation, yet the table says sampling is inadequate and that controlled substances need a 100% check. The 4.8% and 9.5% phase rates are treated as if they were the effects of F and G alone.

Temporal reasoning is thin and, in one place, wrong. Stabilization medians are not “near baseline” except for hospitals (8h versus 8h). Pharmacy median 28h versus 23h and p90 74h versus 49h, and wholesale median 58h versus 36h and p90 120h versus 72h, are still badly elevated. The timeline barely uses cycle time, cold-chain rates, cancellations, fitting-trace percentages (44% / 59% / 76%), or the stated temporal deviations (slow backlog release, variable phone proof-of-delivery delay, long compliance tail). The direct-follow graph fact that about 1,450 of 1,650 temporary-release cases still flowed to dispatch-without-scan is missed, so the control that was supposed to compensate is not shown to have failed at the handoff.

Other logic problems: L is grouped with C and G as a shortcut even though it is the cleanup of earlier gaps; G is retired globally but forbidden explicitly only for controlled substances; ambient “customer history” checks reopen C; retiring D ignores that the snapshot was reasonable early in T2 and only became unsafe as it aged. The fairness indicator sets wholesale cancellation above 20% as a success target, and the indicator set never clearly defines throughput or order-backlog age. Partial recovery, when intake was back but inventory sync and scanning were unreliable, has no distinct mode in the redesigned process.
