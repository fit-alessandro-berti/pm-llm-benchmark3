5.5

The policy analysis is the strongest part of the document: R5 and R6 are treated as incompatible, three resolutions are distinguished, Resolution C is chosen explicitly, and its compliance risk is stated. The requirement mapping uses the right kinds of formalisms, temporal deviations are correctly kept as warnings, and the overall column for T1–T5 and T8 matches a sensible reading of the scenario. That is not enough to carry the model artifacts or the layer verdicts.

The DECLARE model, as written, cannot accept a normal case. `exactly_one` on both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor`, together with `noncoexistence` of those two activities, is unsatisfiable: R10 is “at most once,” and an accepted trace has no rejection while a rejected trace has no activation. The global `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` additionally rejects every accepted trace that never escalates. Section 2 admits that `exactly_one` is too strong and that the legal-opinion precedence is only a non-authoritative approximation, but both rules remain in `declare_model` at confidence 1.0. Section 8 then marks T1, T2, T3, T4, and T7 as DECLARE `pass`. Section 9 never states that the rule set is unsatisfiable. The T8 claim that “R10 individually passes (each terminal exactly once)” is false: `Release First Payment` does not occur, so `exactly_one` fails there too.

The dictionary shape also misses the required encoding. Unary rules need activity keys and binary rules need pair keys, each mapping to `{support, confidence}`. The lists of `(key, payload)` tuples are not that structure and are not what `conformance_declare` consumes.

The process tree reuses the same `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, and `Close Onboarding` node objects on more than one XOR branch. A PM4Py process tree is a tree; those leaves are reparented, so `procedural_model` does not actually contain three independent branches. Traces that contain clarification, escalation, or legal opinion are still scored as procedural passes without saying that this means projection onto the backbone rather than language membership.

The log skeleton has the same execution problem. `directly_follows` is treated as a set of mandatory immediate edges, with only `Reject Vendor → Close Onboarding` included because other orders vary. In conformance, that set is the allow-list of consecutive pairs, so every ordinary trace would be flagged. `equivalence` is explained as co-occurrence; it is equal per-trace frequency, and several activities constrained to `{1}` are equivalent. The caps `{0,1,2,3}` and `{0,1,2}` are invented.

Temporal arithmetic is inconsistent. T2, T6, and T7 use elapsed time, and the T7 boundary check is right (`|2−72|=70`, which is inside `2×36`). T1 and T4 instead compare the release timestamp itself (80 h and 70 h) with the 72±72 h band. T5’s temporal cell is `warning` because the clarification response is missing; a missing pair is not a temporal-profile deviation, and the activation-to-payment gap is inside the band, so that cell should be `pass`. T8’s absent payment pair should be `not evaluated`, not `warning`. The T6 DECLARE cell is not one of the allowed values and contradicts the later statement that the global precedence fails under every reading.

The resource-layer sketch covers both segregation rules and the external emergency flag, but it does not implement the missing-attribute policy it states. The branch that claims to fail a missing approval or verification resource is unreachable, and a missing `org:resource` column raises `KeyError` before that check.
