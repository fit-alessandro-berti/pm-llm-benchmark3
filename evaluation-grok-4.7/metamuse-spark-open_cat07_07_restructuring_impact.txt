The answer covers every required section, keeps East distinct from North/South, and does not treat average cycle time or an unmonitored full rollout as sufficient. The scenario split, indicator table, hidden-risk discussion, and conditional recommendation are substantively aligned with the brief.

The handover arithmetic is wrong and internally inconsistent. Summing the given frequencies yields about 343 handovers per 100 cases now and about 359 under management’s own post-change table, so the aggregate count rises. The optimistic and central scenarios instead use an unexplained 230 to 195 (then 215) decline. The same optimistic paragraph says local specialist loops “drop” from 36+31=67 to 41+36=77, which is an increase. That error sits on the main mechanism the forecast is supposed to test. Missing comparison arrows throughout make many of those claims harder to parse.

The pilot is underspecified on capacity versus demand. Routing only East’s volume into a shared service that may or may not contain N_Tech and S_Tech would not reproduce full-scale queue pressure, yet the write-up treats an East bottleneck as a sufficient signal that a full rollout would be worse. Section 5 pilots the proposed central model; Section 6 recommends a hybrid with local liaisons and split queues. Those are not the same intervention.

Smaller problems: blended p90 is stated as 19.1 days versus a case-weighted figure near 18.6; rework and SLA baselines are slightly off; several satisfaction and “60% faster” figures are false precision; and the PM4Py sketch leaves the required stratified comparison as `pass`, while `get_case_duration` and the per-activity reading of `get_service_time` are not reliable API uses.
