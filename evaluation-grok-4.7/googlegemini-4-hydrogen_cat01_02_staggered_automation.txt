The answer is strong on estimands, process mechanisms, staggered-adoption logic, and refusal of a single “X%” claim, but it is not nearly flawless. Quantitative discipline breaks in the sensitivity write-up, and the estimator that is described is not the estimator that is coded.

Section 5.5 is the main failure. The Gamma full-baseline cell still contains draft deliberation (“Wait:”) and inconsistent figures: a window-mismatched “−17.0h,” then “controls ~+4.5h” and “DiD ~−5.4h,” plus “−7.90h vs full 01–06.” Those control changes are wrong for the stated windows. From 2026-01–06 to 2026-07–08, Delta/Epsilon p90 rises by about +7.9h and +4.6h (pooled about +6.3h), so Gamma’s −0.88h p90 change is a DiD of about −7h, not −5.4h or −17h. The median DiD on that same window is about −4h, not −7.90h. That −7.90h is what you get by subtracting the same loose +4.5h from Gamma’s −3.38h. This is the section that was supposed to bound the result, and a reader cannot tell which number is the conclusion.

Most other arithmetic checks. Alpha/Beta/Gamma pre/post means, the March +2.0 pp escalation step in every branch, the adoption-month rework drops, the Apr–Aug DiDs versus Delta and Epsilon (−5.62h / −3.36h median; −6.40h / −6.24h p90), the May–Jun versus Jul–Aug Gamma contrasts, and the Jun–Aug intensity comparison are correct. The flat post-May rework series and the separation of median versus p90 are handled well. Smaller slips remain: arrivals did not fall ~9.7% “across all branches” (Gamma fell about 5% from June to August), and the period-average rework DiD (−1.4 to −2.5 pp) is described as if it were the immediate step.

Identification is sophisticated but oversold. The preferred design is described as cohort-by-event-time Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham ATTs. The code instead pools cohorts into common `et_pos_k` dummies, with month and branch fixed effects, on 21 parameters and G=5. A saturated pooled event study avoids the worst static two-way fixed-effects comparison, but it is not cohort-specific and it is not CS. The only lead, `et_pre_m2`, is tested against a mixed reference of other pre-periods plus never-treated months, so it is not a clean k=−1 pre-trend. Cluster-robust standard errors with more regressors than clusters are singular; the warning is right, but the fitted model still presents those standard errors as the uncertainty.

The rollout choice (`expand with an embedded evaluation`, Epsilon only, Delta held back, case-level randomization, fraud-desk guardrails) matches the decision problem. It still misses the partial- versus general-equilibrium point: a 50/50 split inside one branch shares specialists and the fraud desk, so it does not identify the effect of turning the branch fully on. Pre-adoption `automated_fast_path_pct` stuck at exactly 4 or 6 is also never treated as a possible measurement floor.

Estimands, mechanism table, threat list, confidence levels, and the claim ladder are otherwise careful and well matched to the data. The grade is held down by the unfinished, incorrect sensitivity numbers and by the gap between the named design and the regression actually specified.
