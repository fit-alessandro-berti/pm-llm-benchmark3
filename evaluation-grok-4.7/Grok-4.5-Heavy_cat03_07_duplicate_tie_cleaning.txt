6.4

The write-up covers every required section, keeps e05 and e14, preserves original timestamps, and correctly treats `have_common_object=True` as too weak to separate M1 from M2. Those strengths sit next to contradictory rules, an over-strong total order, and several sensitivity claims that do not follow from the pipeline.

The decision procedure is a flat catalog, not an exclusive tree. Same-physical events are defined as merge cases, yet e11/e16 is labeled the same physical invoice issuance and then retained as two refined activities at high confidence. Fact 6 supports one issuance with split attribute authority, which is an argument for a provenance-preserving merge. Fact 3 is explicitly unresolved between one transition and two abstraction levels, but the quarantine list is declared empty. e15 is split into two pairwise “summaries” of e13 and of e14 even though it is one invoice-level balance update over the payment messages collectively.

Ordering rules 1–4 are all marked semantic. A blanket “operational source before summary source” rule is a tie-break, and it duplicates the derivation constraints already stated. Fact 5 only forces e09 before e10. e08 is a different source and is not ordered by carrier `source_seq` or by the stated package lifecycle, which collapses both warehouse release and carrier acceptance under Dispatch. The claim of a unique admissible order e08 ≺ e09 ≺ e10 is therefore stronger than the evidence and the rule set. Within PSP, `source_seq` is also used as if it were business precedence between concurrent captures M1 and M2. The microsecond warning itself is sound.

The conservative count of 16 is arithmetically consistent with merging only e04 into e03. Calling that merge irreversible and then fully reversible is an internal contradiction. Evidence-based retention is left as 14–15 because dropping e07 is optional, so the sensitivity comparison has no fixed dataset. A standard activity-and-timestamp merge with object overlap, connected across shared O1, removes seven events (e04, e05, e07, e09, e14, e15, e16) and leaves 10. The stated ≈9–11 range is only roughly right. Union-merge attaches I1 with I2 and M1 with M2 to the surviving events; it does not orphan those objects. Absolute E2O row counts fall by deduplication, while false same-event co-occurrence rises. End-to-end order cycle time from e01 to e17 does not shrink merely because intermediate events disappear.

Sensitivity misstates the resulting graphs. Relabeling removes same-label successions for pack, dispatch, invoice, and the ERP payment summary. It does not remove Capture Payment → Capture Payment, because e13 and e14 keep that label. Under a total order, distinct concurrent picks also produce Pick → Pick on the order DFG, which is apparent rework the analysis misses. Issue Invoice → Authorize Payment still exists after an invoice merge; what disappears is the invoice self-succession. Index-based timedelta after sorting destroys timestamp ties, so any concurrency test based on equal timestamps no longer sees e03 ∥ e05 or e13 ∥ e14, even though both events were retained. That damage is largely uncounted. ET–OT and OTG comments stay qualitative and point the wrong way on object co-location.

The implementation sketch uses deep copies, a candidate pre-check, verified-pair merges, `ocel_merge_duplicates` with a real warning, sort-then-timedelta, and a referential check. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is only commented out, so the required justified call is absent. `verified_pair_merge` implements fill-if-null, not WMS-preference plus an alternate scanner hash, and not the source-prefixed union described for e11/e16. Provenance is a single overwritten `merged_from` scalar, so the audit schema’s per-source attributes, conflicts, and source-event lists are not actually recoverable from the transformed log. `order_rank` is a hardcoded map, including the non-entailed placement of e08 before e09, rather than the stated constraint solver. Relation union has no compatibility test despite that safeguard being promised.
