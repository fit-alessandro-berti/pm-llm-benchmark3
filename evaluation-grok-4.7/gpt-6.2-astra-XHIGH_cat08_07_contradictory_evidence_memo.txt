The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and keeps segment gains, tail risk, rework, and early billing separate. Facts, mechanisms, and open assumptions are split cleanly, and the five metric conflicts are explained with real process-mining distinctions, including fitness versus precision and move-on-log versus move-on-model. Value-of-information rankings are tied to go/no-go choices, and the numbers checked (cycle times, costs, edges, model scores, segment deltas) are accurate.

Deductions are for accumulated unclarity, not a wrong decision. The €37 “could absorb” sentence is a break-even hypothetical placed next to an already breached €46 downstream estimate, so the cost position is harder to read than it should be. Aggregate cost and duration are still stated a shade too causally before the harder building mix and incomplete post-week-4 costs. Lower generalization is glossed as weaker unseen-case confidence even though precision of 0.51 implies an overly permissive model. M7 is ranked first partly for immediate operational control, which blurs value of information with value of action. The release rule stacks one-sided 95% bounds on p90/p95 and several 1-point non-inferiority tests without power, multiplicity, or estimability, while cost is held to a point estimate. “Restore surveys elsewhere” and “pause predictive skipping” are reconcilable, but the initial low-risk wave is not explicit that contemporaneous control teams remain.
