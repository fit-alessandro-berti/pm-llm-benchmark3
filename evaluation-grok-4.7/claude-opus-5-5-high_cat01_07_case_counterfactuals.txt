The answer is strong on control-flow, four-eyes, and the difference between earlier readiness and earlier completion, but several internal contradictions and over-precise probabilities keep it short of nearly flawless.

The feasibility model matches the normative rules: Register then Triage, originals before document check, both checks before assessment, final compliance after the latest assessment for high-risk or high-value work, rework forcing a new document check and assessment, and a preliminary screen not substituting for final compliance. Intervention typing is right: K-411 is timing, K-104 is timing plus queue priority, K-219 is routing, and K-337 is a decision-policy change. Case paths keep mandatory compliance. The derivations correctly hold other work fixed, treat snapshots as forecasts, and show how M1’s day window and C1’s calendar turn large readiness gains into smaller cycle-time gains. The 100-case discussion covers reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment congestion, weekends, and false concurrency. Ranking K-411 first and K-337 last follows feasibility, quality, spillover, and reversibility rather than speed alone. Pilots include assignment, spillovers, logging, and stop rules. Format, row counts, and word limits are met.

The defects are real. In K-219 the base derivation leaves about 6 h of A2 work ahead at Tue 08:00, but the best case starts A2 at 08:00 without explaining how that queue disappears. Difficult handwriting is kept inside D2’s standard 5–8 h band; the complex 8–14 h band is not run as a scenario, even though that band can erase the gain with evening coverage and no rework. K-337’s 55–75% and 20–35% decision shares are not a coherent partition, and other percentages are sharper than the evidence. Section 5 says this intervention is always faster, while section 3’s appeal path can finish later than the factual case. The Friday saving of −105 to −111 h includes times after the same answer’s M1 cutoff, which elsewhere roll to Monday. The K-104 best case runs C1 until 21:30, past the ~19:30 window inferred from the logs. Clarification rates drift from 15–35% in the case analysis to about 5%→25% in the rollout. The synthesis says a soft reservation removes most of the 16 h compliance wait; the careful base case only replaces it with an overnight wait until Wed 08:00 and saves about 6–8 h. K-411’s “slip ~4.5 h” treats a same-day D2 reorder as a full-service persistent delay.

Main conclusions still hold: K-411 is the robust pilot, K-337 buys speed by changing the decision, and a local C1 reservation does not transport to a scarce single reviewer. The arithmetic and rule checks are mostly careful. The grade is held down by contradictory bounds, one-sided service-time scenarios, and uncertainty that is labeled but not actually calibrated.
