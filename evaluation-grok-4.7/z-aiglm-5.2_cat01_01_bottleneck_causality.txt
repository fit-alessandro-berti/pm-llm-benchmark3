The core verdict is disciplined and largely right: the crude Orion penalty reverses inside every complexity stratum, that reversal is not identification, and a blanket 50% reassignment is the wrong operational bet. Format, estimand language, and the learning plan are mostly in place. The write-up is not reliable enough on the comparisons it asks the reader to audit.

Material errors:
- Quality is miscounted. There is one Nova appeal (C24), 1/16 ≈ 6.3%, not two appeals and 12.5%.
- The compliance comparison is confounded. C09 (high, 18.4 h wait) versus C08 (medium, 11.1 h) mixes complexity with compliance. Inside medium, compliance-required cases do not have higher waits (Orion about 8.6 h versus 9.9 h; Nova about 10.4 h versus 11.9 h). “Required inflates wait” is not what these rows show.
- The wait parenthetical “9.4 vs 5.1 Low-Nova” does not match any actual contrast. Overall waits are about 12.8 h versus 8.8 h, and Orion’s wait is lower in every complexity stratum. The executive claim that Orion’s higher waits reflect compliance therefore repeats the crude confounding the answer elsewhere rejects.
- “Night associated with longer cycles” is not an allowed reading of one Orion day case (low complexity, 7.8 h) against a night range that runs up to high-complexity cases. Inside low complexity, night is not slower.
- The DAG treats `routing_rule → shift` as fact-supported and puts shift downstream of routing, while the role table calls shift a confounder. Arrival shift is more plausibly upstream of routing. That changes whether conditioning on shift blocks a mechanism or removes confounding.
- Phase 2 calls medium, day, non-compliance “positivity-rich” and says positivity holds. In this log that cell has no Orion cases.

The standardized contrast (about −2.5 h under the pooled complexity weights), the overlap problem, selection on completed cases, queue-versus-service, and the capacity-plus-narrow-pilot recommendation are the parts that hold.
