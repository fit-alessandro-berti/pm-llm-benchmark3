The redesign is complete, scenario-based, and operationally coherent. All seven required sections are present, the four required stress types are distinct, and the package refuses blanket capacity adds, average-cycle-time optimization, and simultaneous priority for every case class. Downstream overload is stated for each main intervention, on/off triggers are explicit, redesign-caused risks are included, and the dashboard separates leading signals from lagging SLA outcomes. Most cited figures match the logs (weekly volume 2,300 to 3,300, Urgent+Complex 31% to 49%, pre-check departure shares about 7.0% and 12.3%, Urgent p90 already 19h against an 18h limit, peak queues and bypass rates).

The score is held down by several concrete analytical faults. Accession correction is folded into a “defect rate” even though most corrections continue to preparation (430/500 normal, 310/420 peak), so the metric mixes repairable data errors with true recollection failures. Repeat-after-failure, an incoming case type, is treated as the same object as the in-process Repeat assay loop; the DFG does not attribute that loop to the case type, so the causal link is overclaimed. Activity counts are compared raw across an 8-week and a 3-week window; only some metrics are normalized, which softens how large the batch-wait and repeat intensification actually is. Two constraints in the resource notes never enter the policies: technical validators need four weeks to cross-train, so I4 cannot be a same-peak switch, and registration error rates rise after 9-hour shifts, which extended receiving in the courier protocol would aggravate. M2’s faster runtime is also left out of the machine-allocation trade-off. I9 is placed inside the robust package while the text itself treats it as non-robust. These are not fatal, but they are more than cosmetic.
