7.5

The memo is a strong, decision-grade synthesis: it rejects both blanket scale and blanket rollback, separates segment effects, and uses fitness, precision, alignments, DFG edges, utilization, and the handover versus working-together networks. The evidence table actually keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions apart, and the five metric conflicts are explained as mechanisms rather than restated. That is the right decision shape (modified E).

It is not nearly flawless. Several claims are wrong or internally inconsistent, and those errors sit in the reasoning the decision depends on.

The executive memo treats the pilot as causal proof. “Proves,” “directly shifts,” and “~35% of the speed benefits” overreach. Simple houses are 34% of pilot volume in a sample already skewed toward apartments and older buildings; volume share is not a share of the median gain, and medians do not add. Sparse start timestamps, the pre-week-3 attribute gap, and the post-week-4 cost truncation are underweighted in the recommendation even though they threaten the cycle-time and segment results used to scale.

Section 3 misnames Little’s Law. \(L=\lambda W\) is Little’s Law; \(W_q \propto \rho/(1-\rho)\) is an M/M/1 waiting-time formula, then applied as if it dictates the p95. “19% manual activations” conflates the installation-to-manual-activation DFG share with the 15% case rate. Survey skipping, missing permits, and post-billing activation are bundled as one cost mechanism even though the redesign gives them different causes. Business and rural harm is then attributed to the survey-skip rule, which the segment notes do not support (billing disputes and rescheduling). Generalization falling from 0.70 to 0.56 is cited and never reconciled with the fitness-precision story.

The value-of-information section is complete but uneven. M6 says expanding survey skipping would unlock the survey team’s idle capacity; more skipping idles that team further. M7 is monitoring, yet it is scored as if it were the control that blocks billing and permits. Pods are mandated in week 1 and then scheduled as an M5 A/B test. Surveyors are cross-assigned to second visits in the same plan that restores mandatory surveys for most volume. “Resolving the pre-week-3 data gap” by capturing attributes going forward does not repair historical labels. A 60-day cost audit of the new design cannot be finished by week 6, so exit criterion 5 cannot be observed on the stated clock. Several gates (0.5%, 75–82% utilization, NPS ≥ +22, apartment FTR ≥ 80% with no segment base rate) are false precision.

The strategic call is still defensible. The score is held down by causal overclaim, metric conflation, backwards capacity logic, and a rollout calendar that contradicts its own experiments.
