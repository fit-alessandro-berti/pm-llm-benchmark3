The final answer meets the required structure and is strongly grounded in the timeline, DFGs, conformance results, and segment/product risks. It correctly separates throughput from control, treats post-recovery audit work as a residual burden, and gives a concrete recovery model with triggers, triage, minimum controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, and reconciliation.

The main weakness is classification clarity. A, H, I, and L are placed in more than one of “true resilience,” “risky shortcut,” and “ambiguous,” so the central distinction is restated conditionally rather than cleanly resolved. G is marked “Retire” while its rationale permits a controlled replacement, and K is marked “Modify” while the rationale says to keep it. Fitness is also slightly over-read: low conformance to the normative model shows deviation, not by itself loss of control. T3 “partial scanning” and “early reconciliation” go modestly beyond the supplied evidence, and several indicator thresholds are arbitrary relative to the baseline cycle times.
