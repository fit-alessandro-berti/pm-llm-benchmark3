7.0

The analysis is detailed, numerically careful on most cluster diagnostics, and correctly refuses to treat equal timestamps as physical simultaneity. Several concrete technical errors still make it unreliable as an adjudication.

The G3 correction interval is the wrong one. Under bulk submission with staggered, plausible starts, a defensible window for each completion is (own start, 11:30], tightened to (own start, next start] only if non-preemptive serial work is assumed. The stated window [next start, 11:30] excludes that serial window, forces the previous item to still be open after the next start, and is undefined for E04. Section 7 repeats it.

Waiting-time censoring for G6 is named backwards. Arrival→17:00 is an upper bound on true wait if 17:00 is entry time, so the true wait is left-censored (true value at most the logged value). The text correctly says “bounded above” and “inflated,” then labels that fact right-censoring in the diagnosis table, the G6 discussion, and the final adjudication. A right-censored estimator would push the bias the other way. The mechanism story itself (one entry instant creates both a long wait and a zero service) is sound, including the 2 h 55 min example for C01.

Section 2’s tolerance test does not discriminate coarsening from genuine simultaneity. Quarter-hour stamps that are exactly equal remain equal when merge tolerance shrinks; G7 would still match the “equalities persist” row given for a real simultaneous batch. The actual separators are boundary clustering, negative waits, and finer `recorded_at` / `sequence_no`. The matrix asserts the opposite for coarsening.

The G7 robustness claim overreaches. Precision of 15 min with an undocumented down/up rule is not ±7.5 min nearest-rounding. If 14:00 is a rounded-up value, M02–M05 starts stay before their arrivals and the negative waits are confirmed. “Censoring resolves the contradiction” holds only for some preimages of the rounding rule. Item-level quarter-hour throughput is likewise not fully recoverable, because true completions can leave the displayed bucket.

G8’s “waits of 38–65 min” mixes definitions. Arrival-to-start waits are 38, 43, 48, 54, and 58 min. 65 min is Q05’s arrival-to-complete sojourn (16:13−15:08). G4’s “7–20 min” waits are actually 7–19. The G5 pool-utilization bound of “33% to 100%” is not derived: the four cases contain 70 person-minutes inside a 30-minute span (13:00–13:30). Section 2 also says concurrency of 4 favors a shared account, while the documented cap is three, so four simultaneous intervals still require multitasking or timestamp error. That caveat is stated in §1 and dropped in §2.

G4 at confidence 95 is too high. Perfect minute-level adjacency on a 1-second scanner is observationally the same under physical back-to-back work and under a protocol that stamps the next start from the previous complete. Those are the top and second explanations, and the data do not separate them. G2’s second rank is the same mechanism as its first, only reworded, so it is not a real alternative. G1’s fan-out and genuine four-item fixture cycle largely agree on a 3-minute wall-clock service; the ranking treats them as more conflicting than the performance consequences justify. The utilization warning (12 item-minutes in 3 wall-clock minutes) is the right one.

The code builds the log, keeps G6 and G7, varies `merge_distance` in {60, 900} and `min_batch_size` in {2, 4}, and the printed pandas table matches the data (overlaps, equality counts, lags, medians, four negative G7 waits, G6 zero services, buildup). The comparison is then a hardcoded sentence, not a reduction of `discover_batches` output. Under usual PM4Py semantics, `merge_distance` relaxes equality. G5’s starts span 12 min and its ends 10 min, so 60 s and 900 s need not yield the same category; “Concurrent” at 60 s can become “Simultaneous” at 900 s. The claim that all four runs are stable is therefore unjustified, and a wrong return shape would throw before the diagnostic table. Conformance bias, required in the analytical task, is never discussed; throughput is discussed only for G1 and G7. Bottleneck bias is covered and is mostly directionally right.

The diagnosis table, signature inventory, assumption list (A1–A4), four same-pattern/different-physics cases, and the collection redesign are strong. High-confidence use of G4 as-is, exclusion of G6 service times, and refusal to split G8 waits until the safety-clock offset is known are the right practical calls. The grade stays at 7.0 because the censoring direction, the G3 correction window, the tolerance test, and the uncomputed PM4Py comparison are errors in the parts of the task that are specifically about not over-reading temporal patterns.
