8.3

The design is strong on the criteria that matter: shared-resource DES rather than independent cases, calendars, latent objections, service/wait separation, non-stationary and cross-case dependence, paired experiments, censoring, and no claim of optimality. The capacity inconsistency check (planning median 3.5 h vs ~25,000 staffed hours) and the weekend timestamp audit are correct and well used. PM4Py usage matches the requested conventions (`read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average) and does not invent service times from completion gaps.

Deductions for remaining flaws: baseline queue discipline and the issue/reject mechanism are still underspecified relative to the metrics that depend on them; “six can serve the remaining eligible work” is ambiguous for D6 and planner capacity; S3’s “normal Legal closure” is unclear. Decision gates (±5% p90, ±2 pp SLA) sit at the same scale as the stated interval half-widths, so those rules are not reliably resolvable. The skeleton’s `run_experiments` uses one horizon and refuses WIP, against the stated follow-up and initialization design; `case_arrival_average_s` is not clearly in seconds; `requires_fire` is unsafe if log strings `"yes"`/`"no"` are passed through. None of these break the design, but they keep it short of nearly flawless.
