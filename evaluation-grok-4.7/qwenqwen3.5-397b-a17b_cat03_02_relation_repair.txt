The write-up follows the required eight-part structure, proposes the right candidate edges, keeps O2O direction consistent with the observed qualifiers, and leaves `ev11` unresolved in the selected bundle. Those merits do not offset several errors in the final conclusions.

The conservative bundle is not conservative. `INV-44 bills ORD-B` and `ev12–ORD-B` are accepted at 0.95, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` is taken at 0.90, from one numeric coincidence. The domain treats amount equality as defeasible evidence, and it explicitly allows unapplied cash. Calling that arithmetic an “invariant,” then treating the inferred E2O and O2O edges as independent confirmation, is circular: both edges are projections of the same sum. The table’s claim that `PAY-1` covers the ORD-B portion and `PAY-2` the ORD-A portion adds an allocation the log does not state.

The `ev11` asymmetry is poorly supported. `N-42*` is compatible with both `N-420` and `N-421`. ORD-A is already split: IT-A1 is in PK-7, while IT-A2 is in PK-8, so a confirmation of ORD-A implicates both deliveries if the package repairs are kept. Rejecting DEL-Y because “ORD-B invoice late” is invalid; invoicing after confirmation is a normal order. Section 5 then says DEL-X has a transitive confirmation anyway, which contradicts the unresolved status in the selected bundle.

Cardinality and lifecycle claims fail. PK-8 packing two items is one-to-many, not many-to-many. The weight remark adds the two package gross weights and compares that sum with PK-8; item weights are not in the data, and the glass-versus-cable split is unused. In the unrepaired log, ORD-B participates only in Create Order and Add Item. It does not end at Dispatch Package. The repaired orders are not equivalent executions: item counts differ, and only ORD-A has Confirm Delivery.

Downstream effects inherit that conflation. The OC-DFG path drawn from Add Item through Pack and Dispatch into Invoice mixes object types and events that are not directly related to the order. Instance-level repeats of Package–Item, Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, and Payment–Invoice do not by themselves add object-type edges, so the claimed OTG change is overstated. Event-versus-unique-object-versus-total-object frequencies are only sketched.

The code outline hits the requested discovery and flattening calls and flags the constructor as version-dependent, but it omits the required duplicate check. The integrity predicate also closes over a global `o2o` rather than taking it as input. The five over-repair warnings are specific, yet they describe risks that Bundle A itself accepts.
