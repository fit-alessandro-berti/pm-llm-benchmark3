The normative core is sound. A and B are ranked last and are refused any rescue by a favorable forecast. Second-officer review stays mandatory while the score is at least 70 or the fuzzy match is unresolved. A suspicious-activity report is not treated as permission to pay. Alternate screening is not treated as a substitute for independent review. The 35-minute versus 65-minute arithmetic for S4 is correct, and the required labels and six-section structure are intact.

The operational claims are looser than that core.

The 0.12–0.28 rise in P-901’s breach probability is the given effect of a 45-minute interruption. The recommended plan occupies S2 for a 35–75 minute review plus 5–15 minutes to record and, if approved, execute, and it may reserve S2 before R17 finishes the required post-evidence review. Using the 45-minute increment as the plan’s spillover is more precise than the facts, and it understates P-901 harm if S2 is pulled early and then waits.

Section 5 says a confirmed 35-minute S4 arrival can outrank immediate interruption when P-901’s loss is large. Section 6 nevertheless interrupts S2 at once and names S4’s timing as the reranking trigger. That trigger arrives after the costly commitment. The memo never sets a pre-interrupt checkpoint, nor does it show why none is available once R17’s unmeasured handling time is admitted to an already tight 90-minute window.

The claim that immediate S2 is the only path whose full 40–90 minute duration fits the deadline overreaches. The slow end equals 90 minutes, and that interval still omits R17. Timely completion across the whole range holds only if recording ends at the deadline and R17 consumes no material time.

Benefit and harm cells are blurred. On-time value is €20,000–€35,000 if execution meets the deadline, and that timing benefit is absent otherwise; it is not a filled interval from €0 to €35,000. For F, stacking false-rejection harm on “lost deadline value” mixes a non-execution loss with a lateness loss. The column asks for expected harm; the cells are unweighted scenario ranges.

Smaller slips remain. On the evidence path, second review “may” cease once the score is below 70 and the match is resolved; under rule 6 it then simply is not required. The rule 3 auto-clear branch, no alert and score below 40, is missing. “Attach the alternate result before S2 decides” sits awkwardly beside “do not postpone S2,” although a 15–30 minute screen inside a 35–75 minute review can reconcile them. The observational 0.55–0.75 approval range is used to disfavor rejection after the memo has correctly warned that the range is not an effect of the intervention.

The conformance order still stands. These gaps in spillover, commitment timing, and ranges are why the mark stays well below a near-flawless score.
