7.0

The case analysis is strong and mostly faithful to event-time state, but the required checking code does not run, and its R1 flag would miss the clearest stale-review violation even if it did.

Temporal semantics are handled correctly: blanks carry forward, final state is not copied backward, `event_index` orders ties, and event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are distinguished. That reading is applied consistently to TX-311 (jurisdiction becomes DE after the same-timestamp review, so R1 is off at release) and to TX-305 (settlement amount is not an immutable release amount).

Formalization covers R1–R7, defines `≺`, and keeps exemption scope, packet type, and unknown values from collapsing into safe defaults. “Material change” is still softer than the rest: R1’s `m` is described with “at minimum” and “conservative variants,” and the extra `m ≺ Enhanced_Review` constraint on R2 is stricter than the rule text without being marked as an interpretation. It does match the later case results.

Case conclusions are defensible. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant for the right missing or stale controls. TX-306 and TX-308 correctly keep sub-20% increases and decreases from invalidating a review, and both alternatives are stated. TX-302 is compliant on the enhanced review alone, with EX-77’s qualified signature correctly separated from the late insert. TX-305 and TX-310 are appropriately indeterminate; section 4 gives the evidence that would move each one. Calling R1 “triggered” for TX-305 is slightly loose, since the trigger itself cannot be established.

The code is not executable. `exemption` repeats `jurisdiction` and `customer_type`, so the merge suffixes those columns and `exemption_validity` raises `KeyError` on `row["jurisdiction"]` for EX-77. `r1_needs_review_flag` then reads `has_enhanced_before_release`, which was dropped when `diagnostics` was built. Separately, that flag is only “R1 trigger and no enhanced review at all,” so TX-309 (review present, then a >20% increase) would not be flagged. Ordering checks use raw timestamps rather than `event_index`. R2 is computed and then omitted. `approved_by` is never tested. Unknown-value flags cover amount and jurisdiction only. The pipeline shape is otherwise right: `StringIO`, `format_dataframe`, within-case forward fill, no settlement leakage into the release row, R4’s baseline reset, and EX-77/EX-88 kept as evidence rather than a classification lookup.

Sections 6 and 7 are complete: final-state leakage, conditional activation, post-review amount changes, post-hoc exemptions, missing data, same-timestamp order, sanctions context, and scope, plus the requested logging fixes.
