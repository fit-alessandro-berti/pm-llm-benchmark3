6.1

The assessment is organized, time-aware, and often precise, but it is not consistent enough to carry its own classifications. Several verdicts contradict the method stated in §1, and one case treats a stated fact as a proof gap.

The load-bearing finance theory is applied unevenly. §1 treats joint Incident Command authorization, whenever ECD-2 applies, as the independent budget-owner gate, and treats the CFO email as satisfying the Finance Controller limb only from 2026-07-15 07:20 and only up to EUR 150,000. P-505 and P-507 are then marked COMPLIANT on those assumptions. P-502 is also inside ECD-2, with two Incident Command authorizations before commitment, yet the same budget-owner limb is listed as an unmet deviation. P-503 is under EUR 150,000, inside the email’s time window, and has joint authorization, but the row never explains why that theory fails there and succeeds for P-507. The SoD taint in P-503 can support a distinction, yet the text does not draw it. D1’s “operational approvals,” D3’s power to interpret finance controls, and D4’s reservation of essential integrity controls are collapsed into a board-level Finance Controller approval. That inference is labeled defeasible, then used as if it were established.

The burden of proof does not govern the labels. §1 requires affirmative evidence for COMPLIANT. P-505 and P-507 still receive that label while the committer’s identity is unknown, so A1 is untested, and while budget-owner status is expressly an assumption. The charter says Incident Command members are not stated to be budget owners. INDETERMINATE is never used. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is also undefined: P-501 uses it for a partially failed local emergency path, and P-508 uses it for operational necessity plus a threshold ambiguity. The second use conflicts with the A7/A6 position that necessity and later paperwork do not cure a missing pre-commitment finance approval. On the author’s stricter reading of the EUR 150,000 line, P-508 has no Finance Controller event at all.

Case handling has concrete misses. For P-501, the record states that the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1. The assessment calls that missing evidence and a “potential” delegation defect. The failed B4 condition is established on the case facts; what remains open is which act the substitution clause covers. P-504 is NONCOMPLIANT at high confidence without deciding whether a cafeteria freezer is inside a maintenance and production-support SOP. Below EUR 25,000, GPIP-4’s amount gates do not apply, and “unapproved” is not shown to mean “new supplier.” P-505 never addresses Plant_Approve. P-508’s satisfied-rule list omits the clean A1 fact that BUY-8 was neither authorizer, and the absent four-hour recording is parked as missing evidence without affecting the label.

§5 does not test the premises that actually decide the close cases. The P-505 and P-507 alternatives attack the ERP clock and the signed-PDF medium. The stronger challenge is the D1/budget-owner equation itself. The P-502 alternative, equating ECD-2 with an A7 waiver of A3, still leaves A2’s finance limb open: commitment was on 07-14, before the CFO email, and Finance_Approve was after commitment. The P-508 alternative offers only the move toward COMPLIANT, not the equally available move to NONCOMPLIANT if the email stops at EUR 150,000 and no A7 exception exists.

The hierarchy table, the refusal to let the 07-14 ERP outage rewrite obligations, the P-505 acknowledgment-over-ERP-clock analysis, the P-507 PDF timing, the P-506 rejection of a verbal extension, and the P-502/P-503 integrity findings are sound. §7 covers authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, recording, expiry, and normalization, although it titles controls “non-waivable” and then permits an explicit waiver. Those merits support a competent framework. They do not offset contradictory application of that framework across the portfolio.
