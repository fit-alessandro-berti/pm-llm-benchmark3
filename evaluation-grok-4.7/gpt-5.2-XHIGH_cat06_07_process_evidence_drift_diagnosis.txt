7.5

The month-by-month story matches the evidence: April is a schema/pipeline incident (breach stays ~0.19 while mean risk jumps to 0.28, with unknown-activity 17%, missing queue feature 14%, and 18% unmapped), June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September combine parts-delay, V3 risk, and Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable at 55% maturity. Tests, retraining gates, and failure modes are concrete and mostly well hedged. The score is held down by mechanism errors in the required diagnosis table and by a code sketch that does not implement the process evidence it claims to compute.

The diagnosis table misfiles two months. June is implicated as covariate drift, but nothing shows that diagnosis-time P(X) changed. V2 rising from 34% to 52% is a post-diagnosis routing change; V1/V2/V3 matured breach rates stay near May (0.16 / 0.09 / 0.35). The 0.20 vs 0.15 gap is a base-rate/calibration shift from that mix, which the prose later treats correctly as process drift, not a feature-distribution shift. April is also listed under process drift as “logging/schema.” The task separates process drift from pipeline failure, and the April signature (wrong prefixes, duplicates, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, unmapped variants, over-prediction with a stable breach rate) is measurement corruption. Putting April in both rows blurs that distinction even though the contradicting cell hedges it.

A few claims are stronger than the data. May’s ECE of 0.050 and mean risk of 0.21 are tied to the cached “events before diagnosis” backfill, which the audit limits to April; May’s own residual is the 2% unknown-activity and 3% missing-queue rates, including days before the 6 May patch. July’s global AUROC drop from 0.79 to 0.76 is treated as loss of ranking power inside V3, while July ECE actually improves to 0.042 and global mean risk (0.18) matches breach (0.19). The supplier break starts 10 July, so July is a partial month; that is not stated. The October narrative calls the month primarily instrumentation and omits the parts edge at 155 h, which is the worst value in the series and is still real process deterioration under the censored labels. The table’s Jul–Oct process-drift row and the October write-up therefore disagree. Weighted matured variant rates are also left unreconciled with headline breach (August about 0.31 vs 0.28 observed; September about 0.34 vs 0.31), which is exactly the maturity-bias check this audit needs. The mixture formula is credited with adapting to August’s V3 inflation, but that inflation sits in P(breach | x, V3) and will not move unless that conditional model is refit.

The code has the right ingredients (alias normalization before extraction, exact-duplicate drop, both cohorts, and the requested pm4py calls) and then fails the metric it exists to produce. `discover_dfg` is unpacked as a triple, but `discover_performance_dfg(..., aggregation_measure="median")` is not: that keyword is not on the public function, so the call raises, and the real return is `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`, not an edge-to-seconds map. As written, the parts median is either an exception or silently missing. `start + MonthEnd(1)` is midnight on the last day, so both the event-calendar filter and the case-start window drop almost all of that day. Case-start performance DFGs for recent months also use open traces with no right-censoring rule, which biases `Order Part -> Part Received` in the same months the answer says not to trust. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is computed and discarded, so it never enters the auditable summary.

Section 3’s competing expectations, the maturity gate on retraining, and the six failure modes are the strongest parts and would have supported a higher score if the table’s drift assignments and the process-evidence code had been equally careful.
