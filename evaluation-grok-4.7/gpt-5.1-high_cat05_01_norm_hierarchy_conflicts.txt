6.5

The assessment is structurally complete and often precise on timing, but it does not reliably apply its own hierarchy, burden of proof, or classification rules. Several primary calls are defensible; the reasoning around them is not stable enough to treat the result as nearly rigorous.

The strongest work is the refusal to let ECD-2 or the CFO email silently repeal GPIP-4, the correct non-cure of P-502’s post-commitment Compliance_Clearance, the P-503 segregation breach, the P-505 use of the 17:59:31 acknowledgment rather than the 18:06 ERP stamp, and the P-507 treatment of the 12:40 PDF as a timely record rather than the 14:30 ERP entry. Section 7 also covers authority, thresholds, segregation, expiry, and emergency normalization.

Those merits are undercut by direct errors and internal contradictions.

P-504 is EUR 24,000. Local rule B2 starts at EUR 25,000. The row nevertheless records a breach of R6 for a purchase “≥25k.” That is a false trigger. The same row places an alleged segregation violation in “Satisfied rules,” and it says commit-before-PO is not explicitly prohibited outside the emergency route. B1 states the opposite: the PO must precede commitment unless B4 applies. The cafeteria purpose still supports NONCOMPLIANT through B1, B3, and a failed B4 emergency, but the deviation inventory and MAJOR severity are not earned. “Unapproved” is also treated as “new” for A3 without argument, which matters only at this amount.

The formal system contradicts the case results. The pseudo-logic withdraws the CFO/Incident Command override above EUR 150,000. R11 and P-508 do the reverse: EUR 155,000 is COMPLIANT at HIGH confidence, with “no material deviations,” because joint Incident Command authorization plus Legal_Clearance is said to satisfy both the budget-owner and Finance Controller requirements. D1’s sufficiency cap is EUR 150,000; D2 adds Legal clearance “as well”; D3 limits the CFO to Finance controls; the charter does not make Incident Command budget owners. Legal clearance is not a Finance Controller approval. HIGH confidence is not available on these facts.

P-506 repeats the role collapse. Finance_Approve at 06:05 is treated as satisfying both R2 and R3, while Plant_Approve occurs at 09:10, after commitment. A2 requires two independent pre-commitment approvals. The later plant approval is not curative under A6. The verbal-extension rejection is sound; the claim that core pre-commitment controls were intact is not.

P-501 never uses a fact the record supplies: the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1. B4’s substitution conditions are therefore unmet. Confidence is still HIGH. P-505 lists R4 as satisfied for an EUR 88,000 approved-supplier purchase, although A3 is not triggered. The same row calls the purchase COMPLIANT while the stated method says unclear budget-owner status should be INDETERMINATE when it could change the result.

Assumption 9 defines JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION for a real emergency overlay in tension with GPIP-4, then never uses it. P-505, P-507, and P-508 are exactly that category: compliance depends on reading an informal email as a substitute for board-level pre-commitment approvals. The binary COMPLIANT/NONCOMPLIANT split hides the ambiguity the method itself identified. Burden of proof is also uneven. Missing Finance or Compliance events are breaches; missing proof that the approver is independent, or is a budget owner, is treated as compliance.

The P-503 alternative does not change the classification. Even if Emergency_Authorize were merely operational and some unrecorded budget approval existed, the absent Compliance_Clearance on EUR 140,000 would remain an A3 breach. Section 5 also leaves untested the P-508 reading that would actually flip COMPLIANT to NONCOMPLIANT: D1’s cap does not survive above EUR 150,000.

R1 incorrectly adds a pre-commitment timing requirement to a segregation rule. R7 restates A3 as applying to new suppliers above EUR 100,000, although A3 is disjunctive. The emergency-route logic makes the 24-hour form a condition of the route rather than a later duty, which conflicts with judging the act at commitment time. P-502’s date is not in the record, yet “CFO email not yet in effect” is stated as controlling; the A3 result happens to survive that assumption, the discipline does not.

The hierarchy and remediation discussion show real process awareness. The case table and rule logic do not consistently follow them. Under a strict reading, that is a competent but materially flawed assessment, not an excellent one.
