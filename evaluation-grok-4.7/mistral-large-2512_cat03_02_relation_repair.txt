4.3

The write-up follows the required eight-part shape, keeps both bundles on the five mandated decision points, and sometimes hedges arithmetic. Those merits do not offset a misreading of the observed log that spreads through the repair, the audit, and the mining effects.

The central fact error is ev10. Its observed objects already include PK-8, and ev07 plus the O2O edge already pack IT-A2 into PK-8. Proposal P3 still “adds” ev10–PK-8 at confidence 1.0, with evidence against listed as none, and the JSON treats that duplicate as a repair. The same mistake reappears as “PK-8 is the only package not dispatched,” “PK-8 would have no items,” an orphan package in the unrepaired OC-DFG, and a missing Pack Item → Dispatch Package edge. That edge is already there on PK-8 via ev07 → ev10. IT-B1 is likewise not an orphan: ev05 and ev08 already touch it. What is missing is a package on the item-only pack row.

Bundle A is not a minimum-change repair. It accepts the scanner link because an orphan would otherwise remain, then also commits DEL-Y on ev10, DEL-X on ev11, INV-44 bills ORD-B, and PAY-2 settles INV-44. That is completeness pressure, which the task forbids and which section 8 later warns against. DEL-Y is described as both complete and unconfirmed. ORD-A is called complete even though IT-A2 sits in PK-8: under Bundle A that package is the one whose delivery is not confirmed, so the order is a split shipment, not a finished DEL-X lifecycle.

ev11 is assigned to DEL-X despite the explicit constraint. `N-42*` matches N-420 and N-421 equally, both deliveries are route NORTH, and both orders are customer C17, so route and customer do not discriminate. The stated reason, “ORD-A is delivered via DEL-X,” is circular and ignores IT-A2. Confidence 0.70 is then written into the selected additions while the table still says provisional.

Identifier and amount confidence is poorly calibrated. Rule 9 says prefixes are informative, not decisive; section 1 calls identifier similarity strongest and nearly definitive. Both deliveries share NORTH, so “route matches DEL-Y” is not evidence for DEL-Y. P10 accepts ev14–INV-44 at 0.90 because 130 equals ORD-A’s declared value. That equates an order attribute with invoice settlement. The coherent arithmetic observation is 100+130=230, and even that is not proof. PAY-1 equals ORD-B’s value and PAY-2 equals ORD-A’s, which cuts across a simple “this payment is ORD-A’s share” story and is never discussed. Bundle B then says INV-44 is “already settled” by PAY-1. A payment of 100 does not settle a total of 230.

The invoice bundle is internally inconsistent. O2O `INV-44 bills ORD-B` is accepted while E2O ev12–ORD-B is rejected at confidence 0.00 and also listed as unresolved. Issue Invoice is the event that would relate the invoice to the billed orders. Keeping the object edge and dropping the event edge is the reverse of the dependency, and it is mislabeled as avoiding circularity. Bundle B’s “accept P13 (ev12 → ORD-A only)” points at the wrong proposal: P13 is ev12–ORD-B, and ev12–ORD-A is already observed.

True circularity is missed. PK-8 packs IT-B1 and DEL-Y carries PK-8 are inferred from proposed event links, then section 5 treats most E2O and O2O pairs as independent confirmation and only carves out the invoice and payment. ev08 does not currently relate PK-8; citing that event as support for the O2O edge assumes the repair under test. The same pattern holds for the payment.

Cardinality and type defaults drift from the given rules. Dispatch is written as 1:N:1 and payment as at least one invoice, against the stated one-package default, the bulk-event exception, and unapplied cash. “No circular O2O” and lifecycle completeness are elevated to hard or positive evidence even though the domain rules are defeasible. The important new cardinality, PK-8 packing IT-A2 and IT-B1 from two orders, is never treated as a suspicious bridge, though it is exactly what would glue the two orders together in an order flattening or in equivalent-execution clustering.

Bundle B has a coherent-looking shared DEL-X story and then discards the main identifier evidence: ev10’s `N-421?` points away from DEL-X’s N-420. A second dispatch event is also not the domain’s “one bulk event, several packages” pattern, because PK-7 remains on ev09. DEL-Y becomes an unused object with no account of why its carrier reference is present.

Section 6 answers the wrong frequency question. Adding relations does not change event counts. The required contrast among event frequency, unique objects, and total object–event pairs is replaced by claims that Pack Item, Dispatch, and Record Payment become “included” once a missing object is attached. Those events already exist. Concrete consequences that should have been named are mostly absent: a Record Payment → Record Payment self-loop on INV-44 if ev14 is attached; a new Dispatch → Confirm edge only on whatever delivery is actually linked from ev11; order traces that still skip pack and dispatch unless relations are propagated; and two orders collapsing toward one cluster if the cross-order package is enriched. Several unrepaired “missing edges” are already present on PK-8 and on INV-44 via ev13.

The code copies the frames, checks referential integrity and duplicates, calls the three requested discovery functions, flattens Order and Delivery, and does not treat O2O enrichment as semantic proof. It also appends the already-observed ev10–PK-8 row, so its own duplicate check passes only when the qualifier differs. `ocel_consistency.check_ocel_consistency` and `.edges` / `.nodes` on the discovery results are not justified APIs, and a dataframe length is labeled as a trace count. The version note is a comment beside the constructor rather than a clearly isolated line.

Section 8 does list several real over-repair paths, including bulk dispatch, invoice scope, double confirmation, and forced completeness. Warning 6 then says IT-B1 might not have been packed at all. ev08 is already a Pack Item event; the outage rule is about a missing package link, not a missing pack. The selected bundle commits several of the repairs this section warns against.

The result is a complete template with some usable alternative branches, built on an incorrect observed graph, over-certain scores, one non-conservative “conservative” bundle, and downstream effects that would mislead an OC-DFG or flattening comparison.
