8.4

The answer is a careful, numerically disciplined causal-process memo, and almost all of the arithmetic that can be checked against the panel is right. It still has enough local inaccuracies, unrun diagnostics, and one policy inconsistency that it is not nearly flawless.

The descriptive core holds. Adopter pre/post means, the April-cohort event-time gaps (median about −2.6 to −5.8 h, mean −4.5 h; p90 mean about −6.3 h), the exact escalation pre-gap of +0.25 pp and post-gap of +1.15 pp, the go-live rework steps, Gamma’s short- versus long-window sensitivity, and the common May rise in the p90/median ratio are correctly computed and correctly kept separate from causal claims. Median and p90 are never collapsed into one cycle-time effect. The March fraud-desk step and the May complexity step are not mistaken for adoption effects. Estimands distinguish availability, fast-path intensity, tail versus center, interference, and transport to Delta/Epsilon. The refusal of “reduces cycle time by X%,” the absolute-versus-relative tail split, and `expand with an embedded evaluation` are the right conclusions.

The gaps are real. The primary design names an April fake break and a covariate-adjusted contrast, then does not report either. Gamma, still untreated, improves by about 2 hours relative to Delta and Epsilon from March to April, which is the same size as Beta’s first-month median gap and close to Alpha’s. That placebo belongs next to the −4.5 h figure. The pre-trend write-up leans on Alpha minus Delta (16.0, 14.1, 13.2) while the contrast actually used, Alpha+Beta versus Delta+Epsilon, has a nearly flat pre-gap near +10 h. Both facts matter; presenting only the pair makes the DiD look more pre-trended than its own comparison group is. The median scenario interval of 0 to −6 h excludes harm even though Beta’s absolute median rose and the answer’s own counterevidence puts zero, and worse, in play. “Escalation rises and then stays flat” is true of the relative gap, not of Alpha or Beta levels, which keep climbing into June. Beta’s April worsening is cited as occurring despite easier mix and an added FTE, without the same sentence noting arrivals per FTE still rose. The 4–6% automated-fast-path floor at never-treated branches is reported and then never treated as a measurement problem. A pure mix calculation is missing: shifting cases onto a low-rework fast path can mechanically produce a drop on the order of the observed 2 points, which is the process question the rework claim most needs. The strategy table still lists Gamma as an April–June control after the calculations have dropped it. In the rollout fallback, sending Epsilon first because it is the weaker branch reintroduces selection on the outcome and the regression-to-the-mean problem the rest of the answer is built to avoid. Guardrail cutoffs of 15%, 20%, and 0.15 are unexplained. The code matches the requested descriptive specification and its caveats, but the regression it fits is the pooled two-way fixed-effects model the prose correctly distrusts, and the relative-time column is swept into the indicator totals by the `rel_` prefix.

None of this flips the decision or invents a percentage effect. It does keep the answer short of a near-flawless identification memo.
