7.6

The regime split is right and well supported: April is a schema and pipeline failure, June is a routing mix shift with stable within-variant breach rates, July–September are a real V3 parts-delay deterioration with population shift only from September, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The six-way table, month narrative, alert gating, and failure modes track the evidence without collapsing the year into one drift label. That core is strong. The score is held down by test logic, decision staging, an incomplete October calibration reading, and code that would mis-count the event-calendar cohort.

The diagnosis table uses the right months and the right numbers. Confidence is properly uneven: pipeline and October censoring high, V3 concept drift only moderate, Partner Z’s existence high and its metric effect moderate. Two soft spots: “Jan–Jul maturity 98%” is wrong for January–June, which are fully mature, and “stable within-variant rates, so not concept drift” overstates what marginal P(breach|variant) can prove about P(breach|X) at the diagnosis prefix.

Section 2 correctly refuses a single failure mode. May is slightly muddled: the unfixed cache is an April feature-store defect, while May’s own residual (unknown activity 2%, missing queue 3%, AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050) is a separate, smaller data-quality remainder. Missing queue never returns to the 1% baseline after April; that residual is left outside the pipeline row. August–September headline rates are treated as the deterioration itself. Variant-weighted matured rates sit higher (about 0.31 in August and about 0.34 in September versus observed 0.28 and 0.31), so the published monthly rates are still downward-biased. The direction is acknowledged later, but the triangulation is not completed.

October is correctly called an underestimate, and retraining on it is rejected. The missing step is the sign of the error. A backlog that withholds longer paths makes 0.23 versus 0.16 look like over-prediction. A rough mix using September variant rates and October shares is nearer 0.33, so the model can still be low while AUROC, Brier, and ECE on the 55% labeled slice look like the opposite failure. “Not model failure alone” leaves that reversal half-said.

The seven required tests are present and usually paired with competing expectations. Test 1 then breaks the contrast it needs. A mature cohort defined as “90 days of follow-up or a known outcome” re-admits October’s early labels, which are the short-path sample the backlog creates. Fixed-vintage evaluation has to freeze the cohort by start time and horizon, then score labels that exist only after the horizon. Test 6 says reweighting improves performance on Partner Z. Reweighting changes the reference distribution; it does not repair Partner Z cases. Adding rural distance and device age is a different intervention from a transport check. Test 5 is the right concept-versus-process separator and is clearly stated.

The containment rules are proportionate: freeze training, quarantine April until recompute, keep October out until maturity, review long V3 and Partner Z manually, and do not give Partner Z its own model on two immature months. The short-term block then mixes three different actions. Platt or isotonic scaling, variant and supplier features, and a new fit are listed as recalibration. New features require a refit. Pooling January–March with matured August–September fits one calibrator to two outcome regimes; a segment intercept is named, but it is not shown that an intercept cannot restore the AUROC drop from about 0.81 to 0.66–0.68. “Add variant features” also ignores prefix timing: V1/V2/V3 occur after the prediction point, so the realized variant is a leakage risk unless the routing decision is already known at diagnosis. June–July as “safe with caution” is too vague for months that are mature but distributionally different.

The monitor set matches the request, and label-delay blocks training rather than firing a retrain. Dependencies on data-quality and population alerts are sound. Thresholds are a bit blunt: AUROC below 0.75 for two months would miss July’s drop to 0.76, and unknown-activity above 2% misses May at 2% and the still-elevated post-migration floor. The calibration monitor is not explicitly suppressed by the maturity alarm, so October can still page a misleading ECE.

The code sketch includes both cohorts, label normalization, a dedup rule, and the requested PM4Py calls, and it does not invent a drift detector. The event-calendar window is wrong: `MonthEnd(0)` yields midnight on the last day, and an inclusive `filter_time_range` then drops almost all of that day. That second filter is also redundant with the period cut and, under default event mode, still runs DFG and variant discovery on traces already sliced at the month boundary. `format_dataframe` adds `concept:name` before the rename is applied, so any call that uses the standard column rather than `activity_key="activity"` sees the unnormalized April label until the inner format. The returned object stores full variant maps and DFGs rather than the auditable edge medians the rest of the audit uses, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`.

Section 6 covers the four required failure modes and adds the June over-retrain and the false October improvement. The weak line is that Partner Z’s 0.45 versus 0.27 “may be driven by slower label maturity.” Under the stated mechanism, slower maturity hides long paths and pushes the observed breach rate down, so it does not explain a higher rate. Higher V3 share can explain a large part of that gap; slower labels would more likely widen the true gap than create the observed one.
