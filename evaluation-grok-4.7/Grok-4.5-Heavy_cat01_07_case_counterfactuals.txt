6.4

The write-up follows the required section order, preserves the control-flow and four-eyes rules, and correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change. Those strengths are outweighed by repeated temporal impossibilities, internal contradictions, and mis-scaled interference.

The partial order is usable. Register before Triage, both checks before Assessment, originals before Document Check, final compliance after the latest Assessment, and rework reopening Document Check and Assessment are all represented. Calling K-104 “lightly routing” is inaccurate: the intervention inserts preliminary S1/C1 work and changes compliance timing; it does not reroute an existing activity. Several later “assumptions” merely restate given rules (sole C1, non-overlap, preliminary work not replacing final review) and omit the assumptions that actually drive the ranges: whether M1 decides outside morning hours, whether queues are worked overnight, and whether a Monday C1 reservation can preempt a 16-hour Tuesday backlog.

The case table’s cycle-time ranges are not feasible under the answer’s own precedence. For K-104, Assessment is held at Tue 19:10, yet the table claims −0.5 to −1.5 days and the base case claims a full day. One day before Wed 18:40 is Tue 18:40, before Assessment finishes; 1.5 days is Tue morning, before Assessment starts. The parenthetical “Tue night–Wed midday” is a different, smaller interval and still does not match −0.5 to −1.5 days. The Tue 08:00 C1 backlog of 16 hours makes an immediate post-Assessment start a strong preemption scenario, but that scenario is used as the base case. For K-219, originals arrive Mon 17:30 and D2 already has about 5 hours of queue, so −1.5 days (decision around Mon 20:50) is impossible; Section 3’s own best case is only 6–14 hours. The table’s rework band of +0 to +2 days also conflicts with Section 3’s +1 to +3 days and with an 8–36 hour external wait plus a new check and assessment. For K-337, −5 days places the decision before Fri 10:30, when Assessment 2 ends. The same case is given three ranges: −3 to −5 days, 2.5–3.5 days, and 3.5–4.5 days. K-411 resamples D2 service up to 8 hours and a 22:00 finish, later than the factual 19:30, while still claiming readiness advances by 0–4 hours. The same resource and documents should stay near the observed 4.5 hours. The table range of −2 to −8 hours also drops the zero-gain case that the derivation itself treats as plausible once the assessment queue and overnight gap bind.

The 100-case discussion covers C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment congestion, weekends, and false concurrency, but it runs past the 400-word limit. “20–40 extra loops” converts “20–40% higher than D1” into an absolute 20–40 point failure rate. The capacity table says K-104 shrinks the assessment queue even though assessment work is unchanged, and says K-411 shrinks the identity and document queues while the same row says instantaneous load rises and total work is unchanged.

The ranking, pilots, and synthesis are the strongest part: K-411 is preferred for reversibility and learning, K-337 is identified as dangerous because it changes risk appetite, and K-104 is correctly the least transportable because C1 is a singleton. Stopping rules and spillover measures are concrete. That judgment cannot repair summary ranges that violate the finish times the answer itself established.
