The final answer meets the required 10-part structure and reaches the right policy posture: no single “X%” effect, separate median and tail conclusions, endogenous staggered adoption, intensity versus availability, small-cluster uncertainty, fraud-desk interference, and expansion only with an embedded design. Those strengths do not offset several material failures under a strict reading.

The mechanism table’s beneficial/harmful signature cells are largely unreadable (“Median , variation , …”, “Intensity rises 2548%”). The adopter intensity column is likewise false as written (438.0, 640.0, 447.0, 428.5, 44.0). Those look like stripped arrows, but the delivered numbers are wrong and a required table does not communicate its contrasts.

Arithmetic is uneven. Adopter pre/post medians, most p90 changes, and the Alpha/Beta never-treated median DiDs are broadly right. The Gamma p90 comparison is not: controls’ Jan–Jun to Jul–Aug p90 change is about +6 hours, not +1.5, so the reported −2.4 hour relative effect is badly off. Late DiDs subtract Apr–Aug control changes from Jul–Aug adopter changes. March medians are said to move together by +2.2 to +3.1 hours; Beta and Epsilon moved about +0.5 and +0.9. Delta rework “+2.2” does not match the stated Apr–Aug average window. The pooled FE intensity coefficient (−0.08 to −0.15 h per point) is asserted, not calculated.

Identification is sophisticated but overclaims. Month fixed effects do not absorb a heterogeneous May complexity shock, and an event study does not make fraud-desk SUTVA violations “visible.” The code’s negative relative-time indicators use floor division of day counts, so March is not −1. “Doubles effective clusters” for within-Delta shift randomization is incorrect, and that design leaves shared-queue interference unaddressed. Pre-period fast-path rates of 4–6% while `automation=0` are not treated as a measurement problem.

Estimands, threat coverage, effect caveats, claim ladder, and the holdout-plus-randomization recommendation are otherwise strong and appropriately low-confidence. The score is held down by quantitative and clarity failures in the sections that were supposed to carry the evidence.
