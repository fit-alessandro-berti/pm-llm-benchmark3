4.5

The headline choice is a conformant second-officer plan, and Actions A and B are correctly left at the bottom because regulatory exposure dominates the deadline value. The supporting analysis is not reliable enough to act on.

The control model is wrong in places that change the decision. Auto-clear is treated as available once the score falls below 70. The rule allows it only with no alert and a score below 40; a score of at least 70 or any unresolved fuzzy match still requires an independent second-officer review before approval or execution. That error is repeated as a reranking trigger, so a resolved name match with a score still at or above 40 could be read as permission to skip mandatory review. In the conformance map, execution without a second review is described as mandatory. The actual obligation runs the other way: second-officer review is mandatory, and execution is lawful only on a valid approval path. Action A is tied to four-eyes and the override rule, while the approval-path rule is omitted. Action F is tied to the prohibition on disclosing a suspicious-activity report, which that rejection action does not do.

The counterfactuals invent facts. Approval is treated as meeting the 90-minute deadline even though S4 at 65 minutes plus review and execution can finish at about 75–95 minutes. The 6–24 hour evidence cycle is given a branch in which the deadline is saved. Match persistence is called a 30% false negative. Branch probabilities such as 60/20/20 and 20/80 are stated without using the given 0.55–0.75 approval range, and the warning that those predictions may not survive intervention is ignored. The 12-minute-old score of 72 is never used.

The ranges are not expected ranges. Deadline value of €20k–€35k is entered as the benefit of plans that can still miss the deadline, including a lower harm bound of €0. P-901’s harm is given as €10k–€28k, a figure the case does not support; only a 0.12–0.28 increase in breach probability is given. Action G is charged the €100k–€500k unlawful-execution loss for seeking an override that is not yet enabled.

The ranking itself is muddled. Action C appears twice, and the S4 plan appears twice, once across 35–65 minutes and again fixed at 65. The primary plan is labeled conditionally conformant although independent review by S4 is already part of the plan. A rejection whose evidentiary threshold is unsettled is placed above a second copy of a conformant S2 review. “None” is used as a confidence level, while the preferred plan is given 85% confidence despite the officer-arrival window, the stale score, and the scarce API call.

Section 5 does not say how the order changes at the two S4 arrival times. At 35 minutes, screening can finish in parallel and S4 can still complete inside 90 minutes with no P-901 impact, so that plan stays first. At 65 minutes, S4 can miss the deadline, and pulling S2 becomes a live trade against P-901’s 70-minute deadline and a 45-minute interruption. The required analysis of time lost waiting for an override is also missing. The fallback threshold of 60 minutes is arbitrary, and recording the spillover does not reduce it.
