5.5

The write-up keeps rejected, withdrawn, and open cases from being coded as non-default, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and prefers a start-time split over a random split. Those choices fit the problem. The design still does not hold together as an uncertainty-aware study, and the skeleton does not implement the as-of rules it describes.

The primary estimand is \(P(\text{default}_{90d}\mid\text{activated}, X)\) on the full set of 48,000 applications. That quantity is defined only on the activated subpopulation. The primary analysis then reweights activated cases by \(1/\hat P(\text{activated}\mid X)\). That weighting targets a transported or counterfactual mean under conditional ignorability, not the conditional-on-activation probability, and it does not estimate loss “among approved portfolios.” Approved-book prediction would use the activated covariate distribution and inverse-weight only label missingness. The counterfactual reading is also the formulation Section 1 sets aside as requiring stronger assumptions. Ignorability is correctly called only partly testable, but it is still made load-bearing for the primary estimator in a prediction study.

Censoring is mislabeled for the chosen endpoint. Default time starts at Account Activated. Still-open, rejected, and withdrawn applications have not started that clock, so their default outcome is undefined under the observed policy. Treating them as ordinary right censoring or as events competing with default collapses two different processes. Transfer is likewise a mixture of non-activation and missing follow-up on another platform; calling all transfers right-censored at transfer erases that split. The immature activated cases are the group that actually matches administrative censoring plus delayed feeds. Permanently missing partner feeds are discussed only as a bound, with no place in the cohort flow.

Prediction time is also unstable across sections. Section 1 uses the third event or 14 days, whichever is earlier. Section 4 runs those rules in parallel and allows a `<TERMINAL>` pad, which can put Accept, Reject, or Activated inside the prefix. Prefix control-flow features drawn from a DFG or variant list are not restricted to past training prefixes. The maturity rule does not line up with extraction on 15 February 2026: a test window of starts from October through December 2025 leaves many activations inside the 90-day horizon plus the 7–35 day feed lag, so a “mature labels only” test set is a fast-activation subset. The migration date is 15 October 2025, while the test fold starts on 1 October. Calibration “per fold” can be fit on the same cases used to report calibration.

The code is about 70 lines and does not carry the design. `get_prefixes_from_log(..., length=3)` is stamped with one global `prediction_time` of 30 September 2025, while features are taken from every event up to that date, so early cases receive post-prefix history. Label maturity uses that same date, and the unused 120-day constant never enters. `default_90d` is aggregated with `"first"`, which is typically missing on early events. Cohort flags for open, transferred, rejected, and withdrawn are absent. The leakage assert checks that prohibited columns are absent from a dataframe that still contains them, so the check fails instead of showing that those columns were removed. Terminal state and activation time are taken from the full extract, not as of a cutoff. The split dates also differ from Section 7.

Section 6 correctly drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-cutoff events, and the label fields, and it gives reasonable repairs for `documents_total`, updated `risk_score`, and extraction-time resource experience. Section 8 gives three real deployment feedback loops. Those parts do not repair the estimand, the censoring classification, or the as-of pipeline.
