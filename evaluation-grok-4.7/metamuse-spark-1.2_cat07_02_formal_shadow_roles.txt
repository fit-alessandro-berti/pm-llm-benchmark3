The role structure is substantively strong and covers every required section, but it is not reliable as written. Several quantitative claims contradict the tables, the core/periphery rule is applied inconsistently, and the PM4Py snippet would not run.

**Quantitative errors.** Nora does not originate more than 60% of A1/A2. She accounts for 760/1668 registers (46%) and 610/2251 validations (27%), about 35% combined. Amira’s A2+A4+A12 share is 960/1375 (70%), so the claim that those three activities exceed 75% for the permanent front-office pair is false for Amira. Giorgio’s 72 pricing events are 59% of Fatima’s 122 and 37% of their combined 194; section 2 calls 37% “of Fatima’s volume,” and section 4 then says 59%. Only 31 of Giorgio’s 72 A7 events are tied to the absence, which is 43%, not the stated 72%. The pricing duration is given as rising from 38 to 74 minutes; the answer repeatedly writes 3874 minutes, including as the size of the gap, which also contradicts its own “almost doubled.” Chen’s A3+A5+A10 share is 81%, not about 85%. Chen’s critical-case count is 42 versus Dina’s 9 (4.7 times), not 5 times.

**Logic and evidence beyond the log.** The stated core test requires more than 15% of a resource’s events, yet Fatima’s 16 approvals are 5.5% of her events and are still listed as core. Section 2 treats Giorgio’s pricing work as substitution-only, while 41 A7 events sit outside the documented 31-event absence; section 4 then correctly treats that split as ambiguous. Resource-level medians are used as if they described specific activities. Unsupported details include Amira’s 18 pricing events being medium-complexity notes drafted ahead of Fatima or Giorgio, Giorgio being an “authorized” shadow when the notes call him informal, and Mateo “spawning child cases” via the subcontracting metric. Many sequence claims are unreadable as printed (“Norahuman,” “CloseReopenrework,” “Similar profiles similar risk authorization”), so key comparisons never actually get stated.

**Code.** `timest_key` is not a `format_dataframe` parameter. Handover, working-together, and subcontracting wrappers return graphs, so `from_pandas_edgelist` on those results is incoherent. `get_service_time(..., agg_by_activity=True)` is an invented argument. The complexity join is event-level, so long cases are overweighted, and `case_complexity > 0.8` is an undefined stand-in for the critical category. The comment that critical volume is dominated by Erik, Hana, Mateo, and Chen omits Giorgio (36) and Fatima (25).

The separation of automation, contingent front office, Chen versus Dina, Erik’s rarity, Mateo versus Hana, and shadow benefits versus risks is the right analysis, and the ambiguity section genuinely offers deciding evidence. Those merits do not offset repeated false shares in the staffing recommendation.
