6.4

The baseline fork-join decomposition is largely correct and the main conclusion—that B, not the longest service (A), controls completion—is supported. The definitions telescope properly, the 12-case table and the F02/F04/F07/F11 walkthroughs match the timestamps, critical-branch counts (A 2, B 8, C 2) are right, and the CF2 and CF3 minute totals (92.5 and 88) are arithmetically right under a full pass-through assumption. The triple-counting argument for queue+service+sync is sound.

Those strengths do not offset several definite errors.

CF1 is wrong on F01. Cutting A from 50 to 40 minutes moves A’s end from 09:00 to 08:50, but B already ends at 08:55, so the join moves only 5 minutes and the critical branch flips to B. The table and text give a 10-minute saving and a new total of 83. That misses the slack limit the task is built around. Aggregate CF1 savings are therefore 15 minutes, not 20.

CF1 is also internally inconsistent: it says `final_start` is unchanged and downstream queue is unchanged, while total flow still falls by the join reduction. If `final_start` is fixed, downstream wait rises and `final_end − arrival` does not change. The same pass-through is used for CF2 without estimating how much earlier readiness is absorbed by F1/F2. The prompt asks for that masking check; the numbers assume it away.

CF2’s prose says 9 of 12 cases benefit and that B is non-critical in “two” cases, then lists F01, F04, F08, and F11. Eight cases benefit, and B is non-critical in four. The claim that A is critical because the other branches are “slower,” or because of B/C lateness, is backwards: in both F01 and F11, A would remain critical even if B’s queue were zero, because A’s 50-minute service exceeds B’s service.

B’s service standard deviation is given as 6.2; from the listed services it is about 5.6 (population) or 5.8 (sample). The “tail” column lists every critical case, including totals of 93 and 110, so it does not show the ≥120-minute tail. `final_resource` is never used. Same-resource occupancy shows a large share of `final_start − join_ready` occurs while that resource is already free, so treating all of it as capacity queue, and recommending a third server on that basis, overstates what the log identifies. Section 7 says this distinction cannot be made; the resource column supports a partial test.

The Python would not reproduce the reported savings. After `B_start` is reduced, `B_end` is rebuilt from the new start and the old end, so B’s completion does not move. `total_flow_time` stays `final_end − arrival`, and CF3 moves `final_start` without moving `final_end`, so computed savings are zero. Per-case savings are not printed. Throughput is discussed as a flow-time average.

Correct core table and a right ranking of B, but a slack error on a highlighted counterfactual, contradictory delay accounting, miscounts, and counterfactual code that does not implement its own results.
