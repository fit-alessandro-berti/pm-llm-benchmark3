The answer meets the required nine-section structure, uses interventional and separable effects for the right reasons, and treats the flag as a policy bundle. Queue-versus-service and occurrence-versus-waiting are separated, interference and exposure-induced confounding are explicit, and the pilot, falsification tests, and reversal conditions are concrete.

The stage table is not reliable. Medium-complexity arrival-to-triage is about 1.19h with median 0.40h, not 1.06h [0.35h]. Its cycle-time median is about 8.05h, not 7.70h. Flagged high-load escalation service has median 2.0h, not 1.75h. Flagged-with-escalation cycle time has median 21.9h, not 20.40h. Flagged rejects are 2/10, not 3/10.

The preferred P2 forecast is inconsistent with the same log: every high-complexity case is manually reviewed even when unflagged, and those cases dominate the long tail. A drop from 100% to 30–40% review is therefore too large, so the claimed cycle-time gain is overstated. `underlying_risk` is left unmarked as unobserved, E4 lumps P2 with P3, and the pilot mixes individual 1:1 assignment with day-level clustering. The headline flag contrast and the causal framework remain sound.
