The policy reading is precise and the case narratives are disciplined: P1 is separated from P2 on CL-609, zero-cost synchronous traces are treated as real overlay breaches on CL-609 and CL-610, the silent tau on CL-605 is treated as a fit, missing events are not treated as proof of nonperformance, and pay-before-approval on CL-604 is ranked by control consequence rather than by edit distance. The severity scale still folds confirmation into the score. Section 1 defines severity as the consequence if the condition is real and confidence as how well the evidence establishes it, but level 4 is illustrated by “lacking reliable evidence” and level 5 by a “confirmed” failure. CL-602, the largest exposure, is therefore scored 4 because the fraud screen is unevidenced, which is the fact confidence was meant to carry. If that screen truly did not occur, the conditional consequence is at least as serious as CL-604.

The code meets the requested PM4Py steps and keeps raw cost and severity in separate columns, but the move aggregation sums each case’s full raw cost onto every pattern that case exhibits. CL-604’s cost of 3 is attached separately to the early payment, the fraud-screen model move, and the later payment move, so the pattern table is not a cost of those moves. CL-607’s amount is entered as 0 although no amount was supplied. CL-612’s code confidence is “Low-medium,” dropping the narrative’s high confidence in the withdrawal and no-payment facts. CL-610’s newly changed beneficiary account is contained in the case row, while the portfolio’s top corrective line is aimed at CL-604 and future gating.
