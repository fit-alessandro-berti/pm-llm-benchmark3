The audit correctly rejects the main full-trace leaks, keeps queue, complexity, elapsed time, region, and calendar week in play, and separates some predictive associations from causal claims. The required five-part structure and JSON shape are present, and Case Z is properly treated as unusable.

Those strengths do not make the final conclusions reliable. `total_case_duration_hours` is ranked first and is nearly collinear with a 10-day breach label, but it is described only as a post-closure measurement. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is labeled a definite process impossibility even though the same timestamp and missing sequence index make the order uninterpretable; `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is future leakage, yet it is also a normatively allowed reconsideration path, and the suggested substitute (`risk_score_at_t0`) is itself tainted. The fraud-investigation timing is confused: at this `t0` that transition is future, not “before t0.”

Required coverage is incomplete: `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` never appear in the findings table. Reserve is later asserted as known at intake without lineage, and the broker effect is given an invented documentation-delay mechanism. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is classed as unusable until the SQL is fixed, then still used as a Case Y driver. Case X is marked `retain` although leaked appeal and decision features dominate the local attribution; the leaked model’s SHAP directions are treated as stable, and the large residual is ignored. Case Y drops the legitimate low-queue effect from the structured drivers while still recommending region for prioritization despite the fairness proxy problem.

The validation suite misses a clear enabled / future-possible / impossible split at `t0` and muddles empirical footprints with normative conformance. The extraction-date join for employee experience is never picked up.
