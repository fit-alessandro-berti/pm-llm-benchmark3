The write-up meets the required seven-part structure, covers all twelve candidates, and correctly rejects a frequency-first bet on registration. The A5–A6 pairing, the exclusion of activation until controls exist, and the use of contract rules to satisfy the “one control module” constraint are sound. It is not nearly flawless: several ratings contradict the event log, the “synchronization” portfolio ignores the longest waits, and expected effects are stated far more strongly than the evidence allows.

The candidate matrix underweights the two dominant delays. External valuation is a 58.0h synchronization point, late 19% of the time, on the path for 71% of equipment-finance cases, and the brief explicitly says it can be triaged or parallelized. Rating A7’s process-level benefit as Low because “the wait remains 58h” treats an immovable outside clock as a fact and then drops A7 from every portfolio, including the one named for synchronization. Customer signing is a 44.0h wait on about 7,300 cases with a 21% reminder loop; A10 as only a Medium process benefit, and only inside the quick-win set, leaves the second-largest wait out of the cycle-time strategy. A4’s exception/control risk as Low is too soft for identity resolution on a 38% exception activity that can clear the wrong entity. A9’s dependency cell cites clean committee decisions and omits the stated KYC dependency. “Saves 75m/case for 9 FTE” conflates normalization touch time on 5,200 cases with the analysts’ full load (document review and 110-minute credit analysis as well).

The dependency map misreads the log. Wait for external valuation → Committee packaging is 1,980, not 2,350, and 2,700 packs come straight from credit analysis, so packaging does not generally sit on valuation. Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation (380) versus back to KYC (1,160) is a control bypass and is never discussed. A10 is treated as if it were the KYC-before-signing gate; e-sign adoption is not that gate and can worsen the 6.5% provisional-signing path. A1 does not create more applications; it releases WIP sooner. “Completely overwhelmed” overstates a steady-state volume effect that the 3 FTE / two-senior-specialist network only partly supports.

Section 4 overclaims and misses required variant reasoning. “Collapses” the 31h, 20h, and 8h waits and “eliminates” the 8.5% contract loop conflict with A4’s limited coverage, A6’s medium feasibility on bad PDFs, and A9’s own outdated-rule risk. The 8.0h figure is wait before the request activity; customer delay sits mainly on the return arc into document review. “Perfectly balances” workloads and “inherently fixes” mismatches are not earned. Concurrency of KYC and financial-document review, the 82% critical-path rate when mismatch occurs, the weekly high-exposure committee cadence, the 28.0h credit-analysis queue on 8,900 cases, equipment-finance versus working-capital collateral, and the 14% credit-line-renewal remediation rate are thin or absent. Four-eyes (5.8% at month-end) is assigned to A8 as if pack assembly enforced it, then left unmeasured in the recommended portfolio.

The rollout misuses the integration cap. The limit is two deep core-banking integrations in six months, not two deep integrations of any kind. Portal nudging is a CRM/portal change and is a weak use of a core-banking slot, while Phase 1 uses only one deep slot and still delays A4, which the answer itself calls a non-core overlay, until month 7. A gate of zero control violations and >90% generation will stall on non-standard and renewal terms. Monitoring omits provisional signing, four-eyes, override/audit completeness, analyst and specialist utilization, and RM/customer friction, despite an explicit constraint that relationship managers resist added friction and that they trigger incomplete document instructions.

The final trade-off against automating registration first is the strongest part and is grounded in back-office utilization after 15:00 and downstream queues. It still does not defend leaving 58h and 44h off a synchronization portfolio, and it describes A9 as not automating a broken upstream process even though KYC hit resolution stays manual and the 380-case mismatch-to-contract arc is untouched.
