7.5

The design is unusually careful about selection, label timing, and leakage, and most of the calendar arithmetic is right. It is still short of a near-flawless study: one censoring category is misstated, the identification strategy is underspecified where it matters, and the skeleton neither implements the stated primary analysis nor runs as claimed.

The primary estimand is the right one. `D90` is defined only after activation, the clock starts at Account Activated, rejected and withdrawn cases stay unlabeled, and formulations B–D are separated by the question each can answer. Conditioning on activation within a predeclared `A_max`, and requiring start on or before 29 August 2025 so that every such activation could have matured by 15 February 2026, correctly blocks the fast-activator bias that a “label is non-null” rule would create. The maturity cutoff of 13 October 2025, the 170-day label gap, the 1 July 2025 training vintage, and the 5 May 2026 re-extract date all follow from the 90+35-day rule. The cohort partition adds to 48,000, and 2,700/25,200 versus 6,300/25,200 is the correct crude bound.

Those bounds are then asked to do too much. They bound the marginal rate among all 25,200 activated accounts. They do not bound `pi(x, r)`, and they ignore partial follow-up that a survival analysis could use to narrow them. The IPCW proposal is the larger hole. A model of “label observed” that includes recent activations and activation month mixes administrative immaturity with permanently missing feeds. Recent months have observation probability zero by the calendar, so the weights are not identified there; truncation only hides that. The weight model has to be fit on design-mature activations, with administrative censoring handled by the date rule alone. That restriction is absent.

Section 3 then mislabels the 5,100 still-open applications. They are inside the stated 22,800 “non-activated” rows whose default is called undefined. For starts after 29 August 2025 their activation is still censored; for earlier starts, failure to activate within `A_max` removes them from the estimand, which is a different fact. The cohort-flow table treats them as right-censored, so the two sections disagree. “Several hundred” overdue or feed-missing accounts is also loose: uniform arrivals leave about 900 of the 3,600 beyond 13 October 2025, and “close to the 21,600” is not a justified expectation once `A_max` and the start-date cap remove slow and late activators.

Prefix rules, the leakage table, temporal validation, and the decision boundary are strong. Fixed-k versus elapsed-time prefixes, canonical renaming before counting, no padding, loop retention, and same-case split membership are the right controls. The audit correctly drops or repairs `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-decision events, same-day `documents_total`, extraction-time experience, and `label_available_at`. Random splitting is rejected for the right dependence and regime reasons, and the deployment feedback list is concrete. Two audit holes remain. A warehouse export can stamp the final `risk_score` onto early events; “last value at or before `t0`” does not detect that. `res_load_asof` is a cumulative count from 1 January 2024, so it recreates the calendar-time confounding the text attributes to extraction-time experience. A trailing window was the feature actually justified.

The skeleton does not substantiate “ran end-to-end.” `pm4py.format_dataframe` does not create `@@index`, but the next sort and the resource cumcount both require it, so execution stops before any cohort count. Even after that fix, the code diverges from the design. Activation before start satisfies `activated_at - start <= 45 days` and can enter `primary`, despite the stated exclusion. `event_90d` is derived only from `Default Recorded`, while `y` comes from `default_90d`, so the two outcomes can disagree, and an early default that exists only in the label is discarded for immature accounts. Unknown activities do not stop the pipeline. `data_extract_time` is never checked for a single snapshot. Stage-2 training is an unweighted complete case; IPCW and the rolling-origin backtest appear only as comments. `get_variants` is sorted as if every value were a count, which is version-dependent. The whitelist, prefix assertions, and start-time split are the right shape, but this is not yet an executable audit pipeline.
