**7.2**

The policy hits the required shape and the three load-bearing judgments: I-03 is refused because the swarm is past its effective stage, I-05 and I-07 are not treated as the same kind of repeat, and untreated-case harm is carried into selection, scenarios, fairness, and monitoring. Most case-level nets that can be recomputed (I-01, I-02, I-04, I-06, I-09, I-10, and the three-scenario gross figures) are internally consistent, and calibration is not ignored. It is not nearly flawless. Several defects are decision-relevant, not cosmetic.

The deployable rule is not actually deployable as written. Delta and approximation operators are dropped throughout (“p 0.10”, “true risk p_now + 0.04”, “0.03×40000850=€350”), so a reviewer has to guess the inequalities. The ranking formula adds an undefined urgency multiplier and subtracts spillover, but the table never computes either. False-alarm burden is correctly described as \((1-p)\times\) burden, then every net charges the full burden. Tier 1 is defined as a 2–4 hour window, then I-01 at 7.0 hours is labeled Tier 1 and given a slot, while I-08 at 8.0 hours is deferred. That cutoff is arbitrary relative to the written rule.

I-08’s lower bound is wrong, not merely garbled. \(0.05\times 100{,}000-450-5{,}000=-450\), not “+€450 to positive.” Waiting is still reasonable, so the portfolio does not flip, but the memo reports the wrong sign. The optimistic “0.66–1.0 breaches” tops out at \(0.35+0.22+0.30=0.87\). The adverse row’s €3,620–€5,220 band is not derived cleanly, and stretching spillover from the stated \(3\times 6\times 0.03=0.54\) up to 0.80 is unexplained. Treating 8–20 minutes of delay as harm still to be subtracted after the 0.01–0.03 breach uplift double-counts one congestion mechanism. The benefit column mixes gross and net, so the cost column can be subtracted twice.

I-05 is mishandled. There has been no prior swarm, so the repeated-swarm ban does not apply. The third alert is a duplicate page, which justifies suppressing the page, not declaring the case ineligible. Its lower-bound net is about \(0.25\times 7{,}000-550=1{,}200\), above I-01 (~€920) and I-02 (~€950), with the widest effect range and an SME underprediction the rule itself says should raise concern. Blocking it “on policy” while claiming a lane for lower-value, high-effect cases is an internal contradiction. I-02 and I-09 are also repeat alerts with no documented new event and are still swarmed.

Allocation under-argues the scarce-slot choice. I-04’s own lower bound (~€3,550) dominates the selected cases, and absolute risk reduction was not checked against Strategic-adjusted probability (true \(p\) roughly 0.36–0.43, so an effect near 0.28 is barely feasible). Deferral can be right because the case is still in triage, but the option value of waiting, the chance Diagnosis Complete arrives before the SLA, and the value of the regulatory reserve are asserted rather than compared. I-09 is given a firm slot on a 0.18–0.30 range the brief says is poorly identified; the adverse scenario then assumes that same pick fails. I-06 is correctly frozen for imputation, with no swap rule if the queue feature checks out inside 30 minutes. Two specialists “deliver the swarm,” yet three time-critical assignments are treated as immediately available slots. If both specialists are required per swarm, I-09 (2.5 h) and I-02 (3.0 h) cannot both be served first, and that sequencing is never scheduled.

Sections 4–6 are the strongest part: cool-down versus re-entry, the I-07 log versus I-05 refresh contrast, segment caps, label logging, and concrete tripwires for bias, spillover, and rule changes. Those sections do not repair the arithmetic error, the non-operational score, or the I-05 eligibility mistake.
