6.4

The primary state calls and the rank-1 next events are credible for all five cases, and the C6-A versus C6-B contrast is grounded in severity, injury, lot structure, and duplicate similarity. The score is held down by a repeated failure to keep “next logged event” distinct from a later enabled step, by probability mass that is too large for those later steps, and by a soft misreading of the supplier-analysis rule on the case that most clearly triggers it.

The next-event error is systematic. In C6-B, Close — No Safety Issue (0.15–0.30) and Disposition Recorded (0.08–0.20) are marked not process-enabled, and the text says they assume review is skipped or collapsed. Under a normal feed, Evidence Received is followed by a logged Evidence Review in the historical directly-follows relation (356 cases). Those two outcomes are competing post-review logging patterns, so together they consume most of the residual probability that should have stayed on other immediate successors. In C6-E the same move ranks a third Customer Evidence Requested at 0.15–0.30 while stating that it is downstream of the second review and not cleanly enabled at 14:00. In C6-C, Corrective Action Opened at 0.08–0.20 requires both Regulatory Assessment Completed and Disposition Recorded to be absent from the log; that is two skips, and the directly-follows summary places corrective action after disposition (53 cases, median 3.0 h). The intervals still present these as ordered alternatives to the true frontier event.

`process_enabled` is applied as “on the primary-path frontier,” which fights the requested distinction. In C6-A, Regulatory Assessment Completed and Technical Investigation Completed are predicted as the next log events precisely because work may already be underway, yet both are marked not enabled. If that parallel work is real, completion is enabled and the log is merely late; if it is not enabled, combined midpoint mass near 0.40 is far above a normal-feed omission rate. C6-D uses the flag correctly for a backfilled Evidence Review versus an in-progress regulatory assessment, so the distinction was available and not applied consistently. C6-B’s alternative S5 would make closure enabled, but the prediction marks it false.

Rule 5 is uneven. C6-E’s lot is already linked to six complaints, which is the mandatory supplier-analysis condition, and the second package contains a supplier certificate and photographs. The obligation is described only as “likely,” Regulatory Assessment is left out of the open set despite a cluster-level safety signal, and Supplier Analysis Completed is absent from the three predictions while Technical Investigation Completed is included. C6-A, where same-lot linkage of the three similar complaints is unconfirmed and the two devices belong to one complaint event, calls supplier analysis “likely mandatory.” The unknowns later soften that claim; the obligation list does not.

Other calibration problems are smaller but real. Rank-2 and rank-3 intervals overlap in every case, and in C6-D they are effectively the same (0.12–0.28 versus 0.12–0.26), so the published order is not identified. C6-D’s disposition range is high given a worklist that still shows R-R2’s assessment open and only about 1 h 20 min elapsed, below the stated 2–14 h completion band. C6-A’s hidden investigation mass is hard to reconcile with calling S3 primary on a normal feed. Queue facts are cited loosely: the regulatory queue of 12 and reportability priority, the loss of one of three engineers, and the 16:00 notification batch never change a numeric ranking. C6-B’s S5 alternative sits ahead of any logged review, so it does not match the disposition-ready definition.

What holds up is specific. S3 for C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E, S4 for C6-C and C6-D, and the rank-1 activities (review completion, review completion, regulatory completion, regulatory completion, review completion) follow the visible prefixes. Fast close is correctly withheld. C6-C treats different-lot failure-mode clustering as a reportability issue rather than an automatic supplier case. C6-D respects the migration flag, the source-system attachment, and the separate worklist, and it refuses to treat missing evidence events as proof that evidence work never occurred. Supporting and contradicting points are case-specific, the caveats about completion-only logging are apt, and the directly-follows discussion correctly refuses to treat pooled frequencies as compliance probabilities.
