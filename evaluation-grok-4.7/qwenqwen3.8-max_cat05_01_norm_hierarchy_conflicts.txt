7.5

The assessment is structured, case-complete, and strong on time, scope, and non-curative later approval. ECD start and expiry are applied at commitment, P-503’s segregation conflict and weak three-day backup scope are correctly treated as independent failures, P-506’s unwritten extension fails, and P-508 correctly keeps GPIP finance/budget controls above the EUR 150,000 email line while recognizing that compliance, legal clearance, and segregation were met. P-507’s signed PDF is rightly preferred to the later ERP time, and P-505’s supplier acknowledgment is rightly preferred to the 18:06 ERP stamp.

The central flaw is that the primary labels for P-505 and P-507 treat an ordinary CFO email as a successful waiver of GPIP’s pre-commitment budget-owner and Finance Controller requirements. That conflicts with the assessment’s own premises: Incident Command membership is not budget-owner status, A4/A7 leave non-tender controls in place absent a CRO exception, and the email says essential integrity controls remain. Those two cases should have been INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT. The same missing-role rule that makes P-501 indeterminate is not applied to the unknown authorizer identities in P-505 and P-507.

ON-44 is also misused. That outage was 14 July, 08:30–13:00; it does not rebut P-505’s 18 July ERP time. The clock note and acknowledgment do. P-504 is classified NONCOMPLIANT at medium-high confidence even though cafeteria work may fall outside the local SOP and “unapproved” is not shown to mean “new.” Section 6 also invents ERP-recovery defects for P-502. The hierarchy, alternatives, and replacement clause are otherwise careful and usable.
