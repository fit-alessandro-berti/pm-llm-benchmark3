7.5

The stage table is numerically exact, absent stages are not coded as zero, and the estimand framework correctly rejects cross-world natural effects in favor of policy-level interventional contrasts under shared queues. P1 is properly separated from “set the flag to zero.” Those are the strongest parts of the answer.

The same log, however, shows two deterministic separations that the write-up never states. `risk_score` has no overlap: every unflagged case is at most 0.61 and every flagged case is at least 0.64. Triage resource is likewise split cleanly, T1/T2 for flagged cases and T3/T4 for unflagged cases. Any resource-adjusted flag contrast therefore has no positivity, and a local cutoff comparison is essentially R15 versus R20, which also differ in complexity. Section 5 proposes a threshold design and names lack of overlap as a failure mode, but it does not apply that test to this sample.

The policy choice overweights manual waiting. Conditional escalation waits are similar (median 420 versus 390) and escalation service medians match at 120; the large contrasts are review occurrence (10/10 versus 4/10) and escalation occurrence (6/10 versus 2/10). Escalation is almost confined to high complexity (7/8), and all four Reject outcomes are high complexity, with equal reject rates by flag (2/10). P3 leaves occurrence and the senior queue in place, so it cannot account for most of the 987-versus-345 cycle gap. Calling protected review capacity the main downstream bottleneck is stronger than the decomposition supports.

The mechanism graph adds unsupported structure: unobserved risk pointing into `complexity`, the triage priority lane pointing into the shared manual queue, and waiting time pointing into service time. `senior_resource` is unobserved but not marked as such.

The Python logic for stage times matches the table and correctly keeps missing stages as missing. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with `resource_key`, which is not a parameter of the standard function, so the script fails before discovery. The performance DFG is built on completion timestamps only, so its edges mix the next wait with the next service, and no arrival event is emitted, so the DFG cannot represent the triage-wait contrast at all.
