7.5

The final recommendation is substantively right: a conformant, independent second-officer review is required, parallel rescreening does not replace it, and immediate execution and self-certification are correctly ranked as currently nonconformant despite their deadline benefit. All six required sections are present, every action is ranked with the specified conformance labels, and the asymmetric regulatory harm is treated as dominating the €20k–€35k deadline saving.

The score is held down by concrete analytical errors. S4 timelines repeatedly omit the 5–15 minutes needed to record the decision and execute, so the “70–110” and “100–140” ranges are too short and make waiting for S4 look more viable than the stated times allow. The P-901 analysis then uses the 45-minute interruption figure to claim that an immediate interruption “ends before” P-901’s 70-minute deadline and “leaves some buffer,” while the same answer uses a 35–75 minute review. At the upper end, the interruption itself overruns P-901’s deadline, so that buffer claim is not generally true and it props up the primary plan’s spillover acceptability.

Benefit figures are also the conditional upside of a timely approval, not an expected range. Approval is only 0.55–0.75, the S2 path can consume the full 90 minutes, and the regulatory range is “plausibly” €100k–€500k, yet the text treats €100k as a hard lower bound and says the parallel plan “dominates” immediate S2 review. Prediction non-transportability and the 12-minute-old risk score are barely carried into the recommendation. The fallback also says P-901 may be non-interruptible and then says to interrupt it anyway, and the rejection trace mixes rejection with a hold/SAR outcome.
