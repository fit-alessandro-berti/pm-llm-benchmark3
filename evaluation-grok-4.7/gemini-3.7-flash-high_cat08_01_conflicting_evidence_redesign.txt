5.5

The write-up follows the required section structure, separates three genuinely different operating logics, and cites many real figures correctly (fitness 0.91, 68% fitting traces, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen rates, the 31%→9% cost gap, segment shares and complaint rates, evidence-loop and complaint tail times, abandonment 320+70=390, the 82% utilization threshold). Those strengths do not offset material misreads, invented quantities, and one redesign that breaks the rules it claims to satisfy.

**Diagnosis errors.** The dominant variant is counted as 2,550 cases. That number is only the Auto eligibility → Fast approval edge. Fast approval has 2,790 inflows and only 2,300 go to dispatch, of which some arrive via fraud review, so the full path Intake → auto → fast approval → dispatch → close cannot be 2,550 traces. “Median ~5 hours intake to dispatch” is the sum of the three edge means (0.8+1.0+3.0), mislabeled as a median; the median edges sum to 2.7h, and edge times do not add to a path cycle time anyway. The dispatch graphic is arithmetically impossible: 2,320 closes plus 260 complaints cannot be 98% and 11% of a 2,300 inflow. Complaint “510 total” sums directly-follows edges, while 9.2% of 5,000 cases is 460 cases. Fraud outflows in the same diagram (24%+32%+21%) leave about 220 edge occurrences unexplained. “Legally exempt,” “> EUR 400 = high-value device,” and “accessory = under EUR 400” collapse three different attributes. Alignment findings are mapped too cleanly onto R3: the stated deviation is reopen after close, not a measured bypass of manual review. Fitness 0.91 with 32% imperfect traces is described as critical non-compliance; many of those traces can be slight deviations. Mid-risk devices, named in the process scope, never appear.

**Redesign A.** Segment split (keep accessories fast, force review on high-value) matches the complaint gap and R1. The workload claim does not. Manual review already has about 2,600 observed touches; accessories are the population most likely to be on today’s auto-fast path, so “divert 43% and cut touches from 2,600 to ~1,100 / ~65% load” is unsupported and may run the wrong direction. Blocking fast approval for ~35% of volume can raise manual inflow by far more than the stated 15–35% rule-update risk. The 48–72h high-value median, fitness “>0.98,” “100% compliance,” complaints “to ~5%,” and a 50% cut in evidence loops (above the given 30–45% portal range) are presented as expected results. The 22h quote delay is assigned to eligibility reviewers; the cost driver sits with the three quote technicians waiting on part price.

**Redesign B.** A pre-dispatch gate is a real option in the brief, but the brief’s delay is 6–18h, narrowed here to 6–12h and a “planned 6-hour window.” “Rework falls from 12.5% to <3%” misreads the metric: 12.5% is already the quality-check → rework rate on high-value repairs, so more inspection is what produces that statistic, not a missing control that will shrink it. All 260 dispatch→complaint edges are treated as wrong-part escapes the gate will drive to ~0; the log only links that tail to incorrect part selection. Two new senior resolvers are added while the table still shows a 15–20% cost reduction. A barcode match and a new resolver role do not implement R1 (manual or fraud review before fast approval), R2 (evidence completeness), or R3 (manual eligibility on reopen). The premium accessory path, median 8h with service-credit exposure past 24h, is not tested against a gate that can add up to 18h.

**Redesign C.** The portal range and the low-literacy abandonment risk are recognized, then over-converted: repeats “reduced by 45%,” unclear-defect cycle time from 140h to <24h, and all 320 evidence abandonments “prevented.” A card pre-authorization is treated as satisfying R1/R2. It does not. Those rules require specific preceding activities; skipping them for premium high-value — the segment with a 22% fast-path reopen rate and explicit R1/R2 exposure — recreates the failure mode in the diagnosis. Dock inspection of returned units also does not catch the forward-logistics wrong-part error. “75% of claims leave manual review” and “<18h” premium advance replacement are not derivable. Repair physics (quote, approval, repair, and check medians already stack well past 24h) makes “end-to-end average <24h on standard paths” and a 50–65% cycle-time cut untenable.

**Table and constraint breach.** The required comparison invents cost effects (−18–24%, −15–20%, −20–28%), overall cycle-time percentages, and utilization. The brief states that exact ROI and exact Pareto dominance cannot be known from these data. The table marks the baseline strictly dominated and all three redesigns Pareto-optimal. That claim is especially weak for C, whose control design can worsen conformance and high-value escapes. “Conditional” was the defensible cell.

**Experiment.** Stage 1 applies A’s guardrail to 100% of premium high-value and B’s scan to a 50% dispatch sample at the same time, so complaint movement cannot be attributed, despite the “A/B” label. “~700 cases annualized” assumes the 5,000-case log is a year. Abandonment is 390/5,000 = 7.8%, then the red threshold is written as “above baseline (9.8%).” The 60-day hold is justified by a 180-hour p90 (7.5 days). Stage 3 “advance exchange for Premium” can undo Stage 1’s high-value guardrail. Downstream counter-metrics and a delayed complaint window are the right idea; the design as specified would not identify which mechanism moved them.
