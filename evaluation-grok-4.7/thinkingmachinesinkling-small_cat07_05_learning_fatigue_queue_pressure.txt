The required six-part structure is complete, the mechanism split is real, and the constraints are respected: fatigue is not treated as the sole cause, early complexity and selection are kept in view, late speed is tied to WIP rather than learning, raw late-shift penalties are rejected, and deferring quality checks is not endorsed as a throughput tactic. The integrated account, intervention risks, ethics section, and ten-indicator dashboard are the strongest parts.

The grade is held down by material defects in the evidence and the required analyses. Arrow-less figures such as queue “6142,” “3875,” “0.720.43,” “3331,” and “15.911.8” make the core table and narrative literally wrong or unreadable. The claim that the error rise is almost entirely confined to WIP > 70 overstates the data: the 51–75 bin is already at 4.6% error with mean queue 62. Several PM4Py calls would not run or do the wrong thing: `get_service_time` is invoked without the required activity and with a nonexistent `aggregation` argument; service time is aggregated from `start_timestamp` rather than duration; `filter_time_range` is given clock strings, including an invalid overnight span; `discover_batches` uses `batch_size` instead of `min_batch_size`; `discover_performance_dfg` is passed the wrong variant; and `filter_case_performance` is used as a case-id filter. Complexity adjustment and the promised regressions stay at the level of `qcut` and `lambda x: ...`. `filter_paths_performance` never appears, and `cluster_log` is only named. The dashboard’s service-time definition (“start_timestamp difference”) is imprecise, and “handoverequality” is a typo.
