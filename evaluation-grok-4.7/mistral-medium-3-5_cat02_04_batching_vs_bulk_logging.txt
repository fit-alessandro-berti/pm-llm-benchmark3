4.5

The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and assigns several defensible primary labels (G1 fixture batch, G3 folder submission, G6 bulk close, G7 quarter-hour censoring). It is not reliable as an analysis. Bias directions contradict the mechanisms given in the same paragraphs, the signature matrix misstates overlap, G8 misses the capacity-loss evidence planted in the scenario, and the required code would not produce the comparison it claims.

**Diagnoses.** G2’s second place, end batching, is incompatible with completes stepped at about 6–7 minutes; those gaps match serial work after one group start. G4 and G8 place concurrent work second even though 1-second scanner timestamps form a strict chain with no overlap. G5 stops at “shared account” and never checks depth: A01–A04 are all open together around 13:12–13:20, which exceeds the stated cap of three analysts, so shared logins alone cannot explain the peak. G7 treats a copied 14:00–14:15 bin as the whole story and ignores two stronger data facts: M02–M05 arrive after the nominal start (negative wait if the start is trusted), and `recorded_at` advances one minute per case from 14:16 to 14:20. G8’s distinctive evidence is the safety-check window, arrivals packed into 15:00–15:08, and service only from 15:38 as a 7-minute chain. That is queue build-up during lost capacity, then ordinary serial service. Clock skew weakens the link; it does not make concurrency the runner-up. Confidences of 90–99 overstate how cleanly timestamps identify physical execution. “Precise” is treated as “correct” for G1, which the field definitions explicitly separate.

**Signatures.** Identical simultaneous intervals are full overlaps. Common-start/staggered-end intervals and staggered-start/common-end intervals also overlap. The matrix marks all three as overlap “None,” and reserves “High” for concurrent work and shared accounts, so the column cannot separate the hypotheses it is meant to separate. Exact ties produced by coarsening stay merged at a smaller `merge_distance`; they do not split once the source has already rounded them onto one timestamp. The queue-accumulation row (shared end, high recording lag) describes bulk submission, not G8-style accumulation followed by serial service.

**Performance bias.** For G2, a logged group start with later real starts makes `complete − start` too long for every item after the first, and `start − arrival` too short. The text says service is underestimated and waiting overestimated. For G3 the parenthetical correctly says true completion is earlier than 11:30, which lengthens logged service; the label then says service is underestimated. G1 calls a 3-minute cycle “accurate” service while four cases would contribute 12 resource-minutes if intervals are summed. Per-case waits of 2–6 minutes are observable from the distinct arrivals, so they are not hidden or systematically underestimated. G4 and G8 are declared accurate bottlenecks from a single activity fragment, with no second resource or downstream stage to compare. Throughput and conformance are absent: copied timestamps invent parallel resource use, distort directly-follows relations, and, for G6, dump seven completions onto one instant while contributing zero busy time. G6’s inflated-wait / zero-service point is the part of this section that holds.

**Code.** The 38 rows match the extract, and lifecycle fields are retained, including zero-duration G6. The PM4Py portion does not do the required comparison. `discover_batches` returns activity/resource batch groupings with a batch type, not a DataFrame with `case` and `batch_id`. `end_timestamp_key` is not part of `format_dataframe`, the resource column is never mapped to `org:resource`, and `merge_distance` is passed as a `timedelta` where the algorithm expects a numeric gap. The pandas “overlap rate” is a mean count, and strict inequalities miss G6 because every interval is a single point at 17:00. `groupby.apply(...).unstack()` does not yield a stable cluster table. The printed “comparison” is one sentence and never places PM4Py categories (simultaneous, start, end, sequential) next to the diagnostics.

**Robustness, redesign, adjudication.** Section 5 names three ambiguities and then withdraws two of them (1-second coarsening for G1; 1-minute coarsening for G3, which cannot pull only the completes onto 11:30). It never stress-tests the serial scanner chains, the 4-versus-3 analyst peak, or G6’s real fork: a true zero-duration administrative close versus backfilled timestamps for work done earlier. Those two readings share every timestamp and push service and waiting in opposite directions. The collection list touches every requested topic, but the first priority is sub-second execution time, which G1 and the scanners already have. The damage comes from bulk forms, undocumented rounding, shared `analyst_pool`, group-start buttons, and unsynchronized clocks. Section 7 then calls G1, G4, and G8 robust enough for performance conclusions. G8 still depends on an unaligned safety clock; G1 still double-counts if case intervals are summed; G7’s rounding rule is stated to be undocumented.
