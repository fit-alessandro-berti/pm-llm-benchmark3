The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and correctly refuses to lead with registration frequency. That is not enough to carry the recommendation. The final portfolio does not match the evidence it cites, and several load-bearing claims are factually wrong.

The compliance-first set (A12, A9, A11, A3) is justified as tackling the “biggest” waits: KYC at 4.0h, entity mismatch at 31h, and external valuation at 58h. KYC’s 4.0h median is not among the long waits; signing (44h), credit analysis (28h), committee packaging (26h), contract preparation (22h), and normalization (20h) all exceed it. Entity mismatch and external valuation are also not what this portfolio automates. A3 is a KYC-hit pre-classifier, not the entity-mismatch assistant (A4). A12 is the contract-control remediation loop (Contract preparation → Remediate control issue, 650; back, 510), not Resolve entity mismatch. A7 is absent, yet effects still claim about a 40% cut in the 58h valuation wait. The recommendation therefore says the portfolio “directly tackles” bottlenecks it does not include.

A11 is recommended in the first wave even though activation is unsafe until upstream controls are complete. The set never fixes the prerequisites that make activation and contract generation reliable: entity-mismatch resolution (critical path in 82% of cases where it appears; 1160 returns to KYC versus 380 skips to contract preparation), statement normalization, missing-document loops (3300/2800), collateral status, or classification (8% misroutes that choose the control path). A9/A11 will still run on dirty KYC, decision, and collateral data. A late activation check also does not enforce four-eyes where it is violated—credit analysis versus committee packaging in 5.8% of high-exposure cases at month-end. Calling the baseline “<0.1%” contradicts the given finding.

Resource effects are mis-assigned. Phase 1 is said to remove registration clerking although A1 is not deployed. A12 is said to produce cleaner normalized statements and fewer specialist entity-mismatch cases; those are A6 and A4, on different pools (credit analysts and 3 compliance-specialist FTE, not back office or compliance ops). Gate metrics include nudge/e-sign CSAT for modules that are not in the rollout. Waiting-time cuts (4h→1.5h, 31h→8h, 58h→30h, and similar percentage drops) are unsupported precision. False positives are essentially omitted. Variants, the KYC/document-review concurrency split (7250 vs 3940), product-specific collateral rates, and the weekly high-exposure committee cadence are barely used.

The matrix and the three-way portfolio contrast show real process awareness, and the decision not to automate A1 first is directionally right. The conclusion undoes that work by recommending a downstream activation bot on an upstream-incomplete path and by crediting it with synchronization gains it cannot produce.
