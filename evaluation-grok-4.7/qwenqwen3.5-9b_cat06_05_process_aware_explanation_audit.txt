4.6

The audit correctly flags the obvious full-trace and label leaks (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, mutable `risk_score_current`, the nightly document snapshot, and the `prior_claim_count` SQL predicate). The five-section shape, JSON skeleton, and recommendation list also match the request. Those strengths do not survive the required distinctions.

Section 1 does not cover every global top-12 or local feature. Missing rows include `queue_length_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, `calendar_week`, `claimant_region`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and `claimant_channel`. It also invents a Case X driver, `final_handler_team`, that the case explanation does not contain. The ±5-minute queue tolerance, the 65% closure-team coincidence, and the fact that a repaired document-request count is structurally empty immediately after coverage check are never examined.

The sequence audit reverses the main process distinction. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is normatively possible only later, after Partial or Deny, so at `t0` it is future-trace leakage. `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`, `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`, and `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` are likewise post-`t0`. Marking all four “Valid / Low” and saying “Retain as valid explanation” treats lifecycle feasibility as prediction-time admissibility. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is rightly rejected, but it is both future and an ordering artifact: the Case Z events share a timestamp and have no sequence index. That tie is not stated in the Case Z ambiguities.

Section 2 misdefines DFG as a “Declarative Finite Graph” and treats it as a normative reachability model. A directly-follows graph is descriptive; a frequent edge is not a rule. Alignment, prefix alignment, footprint, and declarative checks are not given roles. Enabled, impossible, and merely future-possible activities at `t0` are not separated. Appeal is impossible before a Partial or Deny; settlement and closure are future-possible, not enabled, on the given prefixes.

Section 3 is the best part: congestion, frozen complexity, region/channel proxies, and calendar confounding are mostly separated from leaked fields, and causal language is partly constrained. It then contradicts itself. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is “unusable until repaired,” but Case Y treats the value 3 as a credible driver. Leaked features are called “not predictive signal,” although retrospective predictiveness and `t0` admissibility are different standards.

The revised explanations are not reliable. Case X should be recomputed, not retained: appeal (+0.26) and `final_decision` contaminate 0.82. Elapsed time of 1.2 hours is a small negative contribution because a young claim has more slack, not less; the narrative says the opposite and implies that short elapsed raises risk. `claimant_channel` is dropped without a reason. Case Y invents `initial_damage_complexity`, omits admissible `claimant_region` and low `queue_length`, and keeps the broken prior-claim count. That silently deletes the correlated features the task said to review rather than discard. Case Z’s “invalidate” status is reasonable, but the note misses the timestamp tie, week-52 confounding, and same-day document leakage.

Section 5 names the requested controls, yet it still recommends a DFG validator and does not require point-in-time repair before any local narrative is reused. The result identifies gross leakage while failing the temporal, process, and explanation-revision judgments the audit was meant to make.
