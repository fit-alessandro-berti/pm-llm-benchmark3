The submission does not answer the policy question. Sections 6–10 are absent, and section 5 collapses into an unfinished, corrupted table (`-[[Testing,4Direction`). Availability versus use, endogenous targeting, and co-occurring shocks are recognized in broad terms, but the required estimands, identification argument, mechanisms, arithmetic, uncertainty, and rollout rule are not delivered at a usable standard.

Section 1 is the least weak part, yet it still misstates the design. Gamma is grouped with Alpha and Beta as a historically weak target; the prompt assigns Gamma to a readiness pilot, and its pre-period medians sit near Delta’s, not Alpha’s. Staffing changes at Gamma and Delta are omitted. The March fraud desk is described as shifting cases away from branches, whereas it is a shared queue that all branches began using, with automated triage sending a larger share of borderline cases into it. The pre-adoption `automated_fast_path_pct` of about 4–6% while `automation=0` is never flagged. Response rates of roughly 20–45% are reduced to “counts unavailable.”

Section 2 does not define identifiable contrasts. Every row is given a flat “4 months,” which fits neither Alpha/Beta (five post months) nor Gamma (two). “Partial, but confounded” treats confounding as partial identification. E4 mixes availability and use in one contrast. E6 claims a high- versus low-complexity effect on `median_cycle_h` at “branch-month subgroups.” Those subgroup outcomes are not in the panel; only `high_complexity_pct` is. That cell is not partially identifiable. Interference through the shared fraud desk and the routing campaign, and treatment-version differences between availability, fast-path share, and automated escalation, are never stated.

Section 3 names an event study and then specifies a single binary post indicator. That is the staggered two-way fixed-effects contrast the task said not to use without a heterogeneous-timing discussion. There is no relative-time definition, no already-treated contamination point, and no Callaway–Sant’Anna or equivalent. Adjustment includes “fraud desk usage,” which was not recorded, plus arrivals and FTE, which are concurrent with the routing campaign and staffing shocks and are not justified as pre-treatment covariates. Alternative 2 assumes a “valid instrument” for `automated_fast_path_pct` and lists instrument tests, but no instrument is named; omitted-variable bias is then given as the failure mode, which does not match an IV claim. The required 150–250 word justification is a short paragraph of roughly 55 words. “See above” does not fill the adjustment column.

Section 4 covers five mechanisms and drops three that were mandatory: case-mix or routing, rework displacement, and survey selection. Congestion is given the wrong beneficial signature (escalation should fall). Escalation jumps by about 2 points in March at every branch, including never-treated Delta and Epsilon, so the later “slight increase” is not evidence of an automation queue effect. “No collapse” at Alpha ignores the May–June median rise to 53.5 hours and the p90 rise from about 99 to 119.9 hours. Evidence cells mostly restate uptake rather than an outcome signature.

Section 5 defines no window, reports no pre/post figures, and does not compare adopters with not-yet-treated or never-treated branches. There is no intensity contrast, no pre-trend or regression-to-the-mean discussion, and no bounded conclusion for two outcomes. Median and p90 are not kept separate because neither is computed. The later mandatory pieces are simply not there: no ten-row threat table, no five-outcome assessment, no single rollout choice with phase-in, measurement, spillover, guardrail, and scale-up rules, no executable panel analysis, and no four-rung claim ladder.

What remains is an outline that correctly refuses a single “reduces cycle time by X%” slogan only by never reaching the decision. It does not establish what is identified, what the branch-month arithmetic shows, or what leadership should do next.
