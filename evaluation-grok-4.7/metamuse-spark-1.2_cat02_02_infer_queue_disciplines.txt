6.5

The required structure is complete and the decision tables are the real substance of the answer: most eligibility sets, remaining-time arithmetic, and FIFO/priority/SPT/EDF predictions are reconstructed carefully and are factually right. QA is the cleanest diagnosis and is largely earned. That is not enough to carry the write-up, because several claims that the required sections treat as conclusions are logically wrong or contradict the author’s own tables.

The QB argument reverses its own key test. At 09:48 the eligible set is B01 (remaining about 14, priority 3), B03 (priority 2), B04 (estimate 8), and possibly B05. If the two-minute scan delay removes B05, priority selects B03 and FIFO/resume selects B01. The observed B03 therefore supports priority and cuts against FIFO. The answer states the opposite more than once: section 1 says the delay “rescues” the episode, section 4 says the delay favors strict priority while also defining FIFO so that it predicts B03, and section 5 says the delay would raise confidence in a FIFO secondary rule. Those statements cannot be true together. The FIFO column is also redefined between 09:31 (B01 counts) and 09:48 (the preempted job is excluded). Separately, “only preemptive priority predicts the 09:16 interruption” is false on the answer’s own later arithmetic: B02’s 15 minutes is shorter than B01’s remaining 31, so preemptive shortest-remaining-time interrupts too. The 10:36 choice of B06 over overdue B07 is omitted, and the later claim that the resource was simply busy, so the missed appointment is not a violation, is false: B07 was eligible at 10:28 and 10:36. It is also false that FIFO, priority, and SPT all predict an earlier B07.

QC’s table is better than its diagnosis. Priority and earliest-deadline agree at every decision the table lists (C03, then C05, then C05, then C05, then C07, then C07). Section 1 nevertheless says that until 10:54 those rules “make opposite predictions” and that this allows discrimination. It does not. The proposed SLA switch is also not checked against its own trigger. C05’s slack was already negative at arrival (deadline 10:50, estimate 40), yet SPT skipped it at 10:18 and 10:32. At 11:40, C07 has about two minutes of slack and every urgency rule selects it, but C01 is served instead. Starvation and an SLA override therefore conflict at the same decision; the answer lists both exceptions without noticing that conflict. The “~90 minute” aging threshold is a one-point fit.

QD correctly treats skill as a hard partition, and D04 over D08 and D07 over D10 are genuine within-pool evidence against priority and SPT. The identifiability claim is then overstated. D-X is free at 11:20 and idle until D06’s 11:30 appointment, while D07 is waiting and only D-X can perform it. That idle separates an appointment hold from work-conserving FIFO; the answer calls those rules indistinguishable because, once the idle has already happened, D06 is the oldest job left. The D-F idle has the same problem in reverse. Rejecting D05 because 30 minutes does not fit before 12:00 does not explain refusing D10, whose 12 minutes fits in the 11:36–12:00 window and for which D-F is qualified. “D-M idle gaps” during D05’s wait are also not in the trace: D-M runs continuously from 11:00 to 12:03.

Assumptions are labeled, confidence is not theatrical, and the collection plan is aimed at the right ambiguities. The synthesis correctly refuses to read every FIFO breach as priority. Those virtues do not repair the reversed QB implication, the QC equivalence error in the required diagnosis table, or the QD idle being argued away.
