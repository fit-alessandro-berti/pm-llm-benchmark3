6.7

The design is complete and often careful, but several factual, arithmetic, and internal-consistency failures keep it from being reliable.

The required seven sections are present, hard rules are mostly separated from preferences, and the queue table covers C001–C016 with reasons and trade-offs. High-overtime resources R2, R4, and R9 receive no new work. No urgent or high-priority case is given to an unsupervised trainee. The cap of four is respected. C007 and C016 correctly keep a competent owner when complexity and handover cost are both high. C015 correctly refuses to let R5 or R10 perform Security Review: both have security skill 0, so supervision cannot make them eligible. Batching C006 with C012 meets the normal/low and SLA>24h rule. The worker text is non-punitive, and the audit list covers every requested indicator.

The assignment logic is not consistent with the stated rules. Constraint 5 says continuity should be preferred on a high-complexity, high-handover case unless the owner is skill 0 or 1 and supervision is impossible. C001’s owner, R3, has security skill 1, and R7 or R8 can supervise, yet the case is moved to R8. The justification cites the 3.3-hour weak-skill service penalty and ignores the stated 2.7-hour handover-wait penalty, which itself consumes almost the entire 3-hour SLA. The scoring rule also awards a continuity bonus only when the owner’s skill is already at least 2, which reverses the constraint. C009 keeps a skill-1 owner under supervision, so the two cases are treated inconsistently without a reproducible score.

The training claim is wrong. R10’s documented training priority is security, not approval, and nobody with approval as a priority has approval skill above 0. C010 therefore does not support a documented training priority, but it is counted as one. Of nine normal/low cases, only C009 is a clean match; C015 is shadowing only. The stated 22–33% share is inflated. “7/11 owned cases” is also wrong: fifteen cases have owners, and C009 and C012 are kept as well. Section 4 says approval is split between R6 and R5, but R5 receives no approval work.

Baseline comparisons contain concrete mistakes. First-available excludes skill 0, so C001 cannot go to R10 or R6; the least-loaded legal resource is R3. Specialist-only tie-breaks by open cases, so diagnosis goes to R3, not to R3 and R4. The urgency formula is not calibrated to the claimed 0–5 bands, weights are undefined, and a random tie-break is allowed, so an auditor cannot recompute the “top scorer” the audit section assumes. Hours remaining are declared a hard filter and then never checked for R3, R5, R6, or R10. The worker explanation incorrectly says that anyone below the required level may do the work under supervision; skill 0 is forbidden, and approval skill 1 may prepare but not finalize. In the PM4Py sketch, `timest_key` is not a valid `format_dataframe` argument, and the SLA filter is an admitted non-functional placeholder.
