7.5

The arithmetic is accurate, the required sections and mechanisms are all present, and causality is usually separated from precedence. The grade is held down by several analytical inconsistencies that affect the ranking and the intervention claims.

The delay arithmetic itself checks out: J1’s 235/225/10 split, J2’s 220/210/10 split, the 465 and 435 job-minute sums, J4’s 115 minutes at CNC-2 with a 110-minute FIX-A overlap and a 42-minute proven idle overlap, S1’s 355/340/15 split, J6’s 80/30/45/5 split, the 210-minute L7 hold, and the B2 oven-idle split of 100 + 5 + 10. Counterfactuals are stated, and the text correctly refuses to treat those quantities as causal proof. Provenance for the lot–job links, the warning against summing the two Job–ComponentLot rows, and the point that interaction counts are not severity are all handled well. Low utilization coexisting with long waits is explained as a conjunction of machine, tool, material, membership, and policy conditions.

The main flaw is that later sections do not use the best calculations already made. Section 2 shows that, with the other gates fixed, removing RACK-7 unavailability saves nothing and advancing J5 to 13:10 saves about five minutes, because the rack is free only at 14:35. Section 6 nevertheless says flexible membership would advance J4 when J5 is late, without applying that rack bound. The same gap appears in the fixture narrative: J4 is already batch-ready at 13:10, before J1 is packed at 13:30, and B2 still cannot start before J5 at 14:40 and the rack at 14:35. On the observed timestamps, shortening J3’s FIX-A hold does not move S1. Calling that propagation something that “might” happen and “could” be absorbed leaves open a path the clock times close.

J1’s 340-minute wait for J4 is also left unsplit. Only 80 minutes fall before B2 starts at 14:50; 260 minutes fall afterward, between the cure start and J4’s pack at 19:10. That post-start interval is most of the consolidation penalty, and the two rework cycles in the job DFG are never carried into the hypothesis table as an alternative account of it. Batch synchronization remains a strong explanation of the morning B1 exposure, but the ranking does not show how little of the S1 cascade it can explain.

J2’s headline figure of 210 synchronization minutes is not divided at the 10:20 quarantine. About 95 of those minutes sit inside the L7 hold, while the override is what let J2 enter B1 anyway. The prose caveat is right; the unrepaired headline number still conflates batch skew with lot eligibility.

Smaller problems accumulate. Falsifier 6 is explicitly not a weakening of the leading explanation, so the required set of six is only partially met. Rank 2 merges FIX-A contention, CAL-3 recalibration, and RACK-7 cleaning even though the quantified rack effect on B2 is zero, and the interaction graph never adds the CAL-3–TEST-1–J5–J6 coupling that this rank depends on. The table’s claim that the job flattening shows these waits sits uneasily with the median DFG and the six-job “normal duration” summary. Finally, OVEN-1’s stated 115-minute idle exceeds its longest event gap of 73 minutes, and TEST-1’s 50-minute idle exceeds its longest gap of 39 minutes. Those clashes are left unreconciled even though they bear directly on the idle-time counterfactuals.
