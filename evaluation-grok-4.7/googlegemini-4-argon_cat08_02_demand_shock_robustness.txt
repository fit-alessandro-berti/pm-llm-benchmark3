6.5

The redesign is structured, scenario-aware, and often numerically careful, but several claims misuse the logs or contradict the cycle-time evidence. That keeps it well short of a near-flawless analysis.

The four required scenarios are present and are not volume-only. Many core figures check out: 2,300 versus 3,300 cases per week, assay-to-repeat transition shares of 1,140/17,030 (6.7%) and 1,260/8,500 (14.8%), pre-analytical exception shares of 6.9% and 12.3%, cancellation rates of 3.1% and 5.5%, and recollection-to-cancel shares of about 35% and 41.5%. Queue levels, the I9 rejection, case-type conflict handling, and explicit on/off triggers are substantive. Downstream overload is discussed for most bundles, and two residual risks are actually created by the redesign.

The bottleneck story and several performance claims do not hold up:

- Scenario A is the observed peak, yet its “initial” bottlenecks are named as pre-analytical check and preparation. In that same peak, the longest queues are specialist review (36h), medical validation (22h), assay (18h), and technical validation (16h). Registration is only 2.8h. The always-on package still centers courier smoothing, registration automation, and a 9-hour clerk cap.
- “Nearly half of Urgent orders breach 18h” does not follow from a 17h median. Fewer than half exceed 18h. Normal p90 of 19h is then turned into “~12% breach,” and peak p90 of 33h into “>45% breach.” A p90 does not identify the share above a different threshold.
- Urgent cases are said to clear assay in 10 hours and then miss the SLA in the 22h medical-validation queue. That 10h figure is not in the data, and 10h plus a 22h queue cannot coexist with a 17h Urgent median. Station medians are being applied to a prioritized case type.
- The 370 peak cancels are `Request recollection -> Cancel order`. There is no `Manual accession correction -> Cancel` path. The table credits P1 with preventing that nonexistent flow. Manual correction goes to preparation (310) or recollection (110).
- Technical-validation outflow to medical validation or specialist review is 4,030/8,080 = 49.9%, not 50.8%.
- The ~80h Complex-panel saving adds assay, technical, specialist, and medical queue medians and subtracts 12h transport. Those medians are not one path, specialist review is not certain, and the partner’s own processing time is omitted. Complex-panel peak median cycle time is 111h, so the implied saving is not credible.
- `Repeat assay` is treated as conversion into the Repeat-after-failure case type, which is then said to demand priority. The log does not say that.
- The 6.7% and 14.8% rates are system-wide. The dashboard treats them as M2-specific. Scenario B also offers M1 outage or M2 drift, but the migration analysis only develops the M2 path.
- The batch-wait cap and dashboard disagree with the baseline they cite. Normal `Test preparation -> Batch wait` is 6h, while warning is >3.5h and critical is 4h, so normal operation is already critical. The larger peak edge, `Batch wait -> Assay run` at 22h, is unused. A 2h Urgent micro-slot also cannot “guarantee” a 2h wait at 98% assay utilization.
- P1 cross-trains “4 Technical Validators,” while the table says 2, and I4 only supports two. Training the two accession specialists to perform technical validation is not supported. The pull window is 10:00–13:00 despite a second courier spike at 17:00.
- I7+I8 are described as eliminating internally generated rework. Peak recollection growth is largely sample defects, clerk shifts over 9h are inferred rather than shown, and the package leaves technical validation at 101% and assay at 98% untouched. Lagging targets such as Urgent p90 <16.5h and Repeat-after-failure p90 <96h are tighter than the normal baselines of 19h and 132h, with no capacity path that would produce that.
