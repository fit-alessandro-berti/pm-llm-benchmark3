The final design is a strong, usable digital-twin plan: discrete-event scope, shared resources, calendars, non-stationary arrivals, latent objections, rework, and cross-case batching are all treated as first-class, and the eight required sections are actually filled rather than sketched. Service time is not silently merged with waiting, missing objections stay latent, PM4Py is limited to log/model handling, and the skeleton loads the excerpt with pandas, calls `format_dataframe` with the right keys, and keeps parse/DFG/variant logic behind version-safe fallbacks.

The score stays well below excellent because several final claims do not hold together. Common random numbers are recommended, then both the seed formula and `abs(hash(scenario.name)) % 100_000 + replication` make each policy’s demand stream different; `hash()` is also not stable across processes, so the reproducibility claim is false. Council-week blocking checks `"legal" in self.pattern` while legal officers use `"mon_fri_09_17"`, so that rule never fires. Several p90 “targets” (new residential 100, commercial 140, complex 280) are looser than the stated historical p90s (95, 130, 260), so status quo already beats them. “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is encoded as 2.0 FTE on a three-day calendar without resolving whether the FTE figure already includes the short week. Right-censoring of open cases is missing from estimation, Welch warm-up sits uneasily with a seasonal transient, and Monday clerk reservation, simple-only planners, peak-only inspectors, and the decision join are described but not carried into the executable placeholders.
