The write-up covers every required section and makes several defensible calls (protecting high-overtime staff, keeping C002/C005/C007/C008/C011/C016 with capable owners, batching C006 with C012, and using C009 as a real diagnosis-training case). Those strengths do not offset broken hard rules and internal contradictions.

Security Review may be done at skill 3, or at skill 1–2 only under R7 or R8. R5’s security score is 0, so C015 cannot be given to R5 even with R8 supervising. Final Approval may be finalized only at skill 2–3, and only skill 1 may prepare. R8’s approval score is 0, so C010 cannot use R8 as a trainee. Both choices also contradict the answer’s own eligibility pseudocode. After those two illegal “training” slots are removed, only C009 remains, which is 1 of 9 normal/low cases (about 11%), below the 20% training requirement the answer claims to meet at 33%. C012 is explicitly marked suitable for a trainee and is not used.

C001 is worse than a preference call. Changing owner on a high-complexity case adds a median 2.7 hours of waiting, and the SLA is 3 hours. Direct assignment to R8 is likely to consume the deadline, while R3 (skill 1) plus R7/R8 supervision was feasible and is what the continuity rule requires. Section 1 states that rule; section 2 then invents an urgency exception that overrides it.

The algorithm is not actually applied. Security and approval branches are not exclusive, so a skill-1 security resource can be added as both supervised and direct. Supervised pairs are not scored for dual workload. Case order is unspecified even though the four-assignment cap depends on it. The 20% quota is a portfolio constraint with no repair step. R6 is given two urgent specialist cases plus C010 supervision inside 6.5 hours and five already-open cases; that capacity conflict is waved through because the count is under four.

Section 4’s resilience claim—that security and approval are spread via R5 and R8—depends on those illegal assignments. Baselines are discussed abstractly and never run on this queue. The worker explanation presents the forbidden training pairs as normal practice.

The audit list includes the required indicators, but the PM4Py sketch misuses the APIs: `filter_case_performance` does not filter on complexity, `get_service_time` does not return a per-case frame for `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, and `discover_performance_dfg` / `view_sna` / `save_vis_sna` are called with the wrong arguments. `discover_batches` is omitted despite batching being part of the policy, and the historical handover and rework multipliers are never used to learn the scores.
