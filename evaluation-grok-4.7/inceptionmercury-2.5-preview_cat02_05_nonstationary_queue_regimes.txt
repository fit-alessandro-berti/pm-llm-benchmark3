The write-up names the right breaks (outage on 2026-04-15, mix shift on 2026-04-20, extra staff from 2026-04-29) and correctly refuses to treat the outage as steady state. The quantities attached to those regimes, and the code that is supposed to support them, do not hold up.

**Regime table does not match its own date windows.** “Baseline Weekday” is dated 04-06 through 04-14, which includes Saturday and Sunday, but arrivals 101 and completions 100 are the weekday-only means (about 100.1 and 99.6). The nine-day window averages about 87 arrivals and 86 completions. Recovery (04-16 to 04-19) is worse: the four-day means are about 74.5 arrivals and 82 completions, not 100 and 107. Sunday 04-19 is staffed at 2.5 FTE, not 7.0. That day’s p90 is 56; reporting recovery service as 30/68 copies the outage p90. High-complexity arrivals are about 99 over the stated nine days and about 114 on weekdays, not 110. Intervention weekdays complete about 121 cases against 115 arrivals, so “115 and 115, stabilized” erases a real backlog drain. Mean WIP on 04-16 is 52, the sample maximum, so “high FTE reduces WIP” is not what that day shows. Confidence is “High” on every row despite the unlogged intervention time, unknown 07:00 backlog, and weekend contamination.

**Pooled comparisons use the wrong pooled baseline and one reversed mechanism.** Unweighted means over all 28 days are about 33% complex, WIP 29.9, staff 5.14 FTE, and 86 completions per day. The reported pooled figures (32.5%, WIP 32, 5.95 FTE, 96.7 completions) do not describe that pool. “Complex cases move faster because of higher FTE” equates similar daily counts with shorter sojourn. Median service rises from about 25 to about 35 minutes and p90 from the high 40s to the low 80s, while WIP roughly doubles at nearly unchanged weekday headcount. Similar throughput with higher WIP is slower flow, not faster flow. Staffing 6.33 FTE is also assigned to “high complexity,” which in section 2 is still the pre-intervention 6.0 period. No Simpson-style sign reversal is shown; the table is a set of mismatched averages. Mean service remains unidentified from median and p90, so offered load is not estimated.

**Dispersion is the wrong side of Poisson, and the burst hour is misread.** For 04-06 to 04-10, sample variance of arrivals is about 21 against a mean near 98, so the Fano factor is about 0.22, not 1.2. The 04-20 to 04-28 weekdays are also underdispersed (Fano near 0.3), not 1.4. Daily totals in those short windows are smoother than Poisson; the burstiness is intraday and across weekends and regimes. The 04-21 vector sums to 115, and 34 sits in the 10:00–11:00 bin. 08:00–09:00 is 3. Later bins still show 24 and 14. Recommending 08:00–09:00 coverage from that vector targets the quiet hour. Those four vectors are never scored for an index of dispersion or coefficient of variation, and they are not reconciled with `peak_08_10_pct` (08:00–09:59 on 04-21 is 3+8=11 arrivals, not 47%). Lag-1 autocorrelation near 0.45 is plausible, but it is largely the weekend pair plus the level shift, not a demonstrated AR(1) “high day follows high day” mechanism. The alternative segmentation is three fragments, not a partition; its arrival standard deviation of 6 for 04-06 to 04-10 is about 4.1–4.6.

**Little’s Law arithmetic disagrees with the stated formula.** \(W = L/\lambda\) with \(\lambda\) in cases per opening day is already in opening days. Multiplying by \(24/12\) is a second, unjustified conversion, and the code does that while the table does not. Outage “0.45 days” equals \(47/105\) (arrivals), not \(47/58\) (completions ≈ 0.81), contradicting the note that the ratio uses completions. Baseline 0.25 days is not \(20/100\). The 04-06 to 04-14 window is marked applicable with low carry-over even though it mixes weekend and weekday operations and the opening backlog is unknown. The intervention window is absent. Flow time is never checked against service time, and arrival-based versus departure-based \(W\) is not derived when the net backlog changes.

**The Python block does not reproduce the document and misses the required checklist.** `baseline` is the mean of every row. `loc["2026-04-20":"2026-04-28"]` indexes a RangeIndex, so the script fails before any regime mean. There is no hourly table, no second dispersion statistic, no searched change-point rule (the dates are hardcoded in a comment), no PM4Py comment, and no statement that these aggregates are not an event log. Printed scalars cannot match sections 4–6. `pd.io.StringIO` is also an unreliable constructor.

What remains is a usable outline: outage separated from catch-up, mix shift separated from the later FTE increase, and a warning that a single pooled staff level hides the post-20 April WIP build. That outline is not yet a defensible queueing analysis.
