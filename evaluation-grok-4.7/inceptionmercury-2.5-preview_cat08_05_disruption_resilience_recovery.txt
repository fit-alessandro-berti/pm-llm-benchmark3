5.5

The write-up has the required seven sections, a complete A–L table, and the right headline metrics (fill rates, exception rates, fitness, the 2,300-case remediation arc). The decision line is only partly sound: C and uncontrolled G should go, B and explicit deferral should stay, and post-close remediation is not real-time compliance. Several claims are factually or logically wrong, and those errors would steer management.

The directly-follows graph makes Emergency triage a three-way split that sums exactly to its inflow: manual authorization 2,500, skip authorization 1,600, hospital fast lane 1,000. Fast lane then goes to the inventory snapshot. Section 3’s claim that A was paired with B is false. Treating A and F as unqualified resilience also breaks the rule that preserved flow is not resilience when the control is gone. Fast lane is the path that skips the authorization activity. F still sits on a log where dual-control gaps rose from 0.2% to 2.9% and cold-chain documentation gaps from 0.4% to 4.8%. If F really covered every controlled-substance order, that 2.9% means either those orders were not identified offline or the manual check was not a recorded dual control. More packs bypassed temporary release (1,800) than received it (1,650), and 1,450 “controlled” releases still went to dispatch without scan. E, H, and K are never placed in the resilience / shortcut / ambiguous classification. “Patient dissatisfaction” misstates the harmed party; the harm shown is to pharmacy customers and bulk buyers.

T0–T4 is too coarse and too rosy. T1 is folded into T2, so onset is lost. T2’s dominant variants are not grounded in counts: spreadsheet invoice hold (2,200), the ~650 dispatches with no phone proof of delivery (3,250 without scan versus 2,600 phone confirmations), and backlog dwell. Move-on-log versus move-on-model is barely used. “Cycle times nearly normalized” in T4 is true for hospital median (8h) and false for the rest: pharmacy median 28h versus 23h and p90 74h versus 49h; wholesale median 58h versus 36h and p90 120h versus 72h. Traceability is still 2.2% versus 0.8%, with fitting traces at 76% versus 91%. The long Invoice close → Audit remediation tail is recognized; the other temporal findings (backlog → pick beyond baseline p95, highly variable dispatch → phone-proof delay) are not. Snapshot rules conflict internally: under 1 hour in the table, under 30 minutes in the indicators.

The recovery model hits every requested heading and correctly refuses invoice close before evidence exists. It still misses the actual failure mode in T3: intake is back while inventory sync and scanning are untrustworthy, so a “WMS down more than 15 minutes” trigger never fires. There is no exit criterion, no starvation guard so deferred pharmacy and wholesale orders cannot age out, and no path when evidence cannot be reconstructed (quarantine, customer notice, regulator notice). Cold-chain “control” is a photograph of a logger, which does not show an in-range release decision. Ambient hospital product falls through to a handwritten pick sheet. Customer risk and product risk are written as separate bullets, so they do not cross.

Indicators omit backlog age, which was required. Throughput is replaced by an ungrounded rule that more than 80% of capacity go to hospital-critical orders; segment mix is unknown, so that target can be infeasible. The fairness target is written as a bare 1.5. Hospital fill 91% over pharmacy fill 61% is already about 1.49, so the target codifies the incident. Wholesale is left out. Authorization completeness, dispatch-evidence completeness, cold-chain documentation, dual-control, cancellation, and segment cycle time are not measured, despite being the series that actually moved.

Recommendations overclaim and mis-rate residual risk. A hard stop inside the system that is down cannot prevent C or G. Clearing 2,300 remediation cases in 48 hours is not low-effort or low-risk; temperature and chain-of-custody that were never captured cannot be rebuilt. A shadow WMS and daily authorization lists are proposed after a cyber incident with no check on whether the morning snapshot or customer master was altered, and with hospital-only lists that leave pharmacy controlled-substance buyers uncovered. Section 7 then narrows the prohibition to controlled and cold-chain traceability, which drops the authorization gap on ambient orders that the table itself retires.
