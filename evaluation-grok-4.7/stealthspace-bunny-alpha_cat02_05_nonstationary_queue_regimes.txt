5.5

The regime logic is the strongest part of the answer: outage, recovery, mix shift, and the staffing intervention are separated; medians are not substituted for flow time; and several baseline Little’s Law windows are calculated correctly. That competence is outweighed by numerical and reproducibility failures that change comparative conclusions.

Section 2’s primary rates are mostly right, but the high-mix weekday p90 range is given as 72–88 when the observed values are 78–88. Section 4 then breaks down. The median of the 28 daily p90s is 72, not 80, so the claim that the pooled p90 “looks like” the high-mix regime is false. Unweighted mean FTE is 144/28 = 5.14, not 5.16. Weekend FTE is 2.625, not 2.50, because 2–3 May are staffed at 3.0; weekday FTE is 6.15, not 6.225. With those corrections, arrivals per FTE-hour are about 1.46 on weekdays and 1.42 on weekends. The reported weekday-versus-weekend reversal is an artifact of the bad FTE averages.

The matched-weekday table is not reproducible from the stated dates. Baseline weekdays on 6–14 April are 7 days, with about 100.1 arrivals, 99.6 completions, and WIP 20.3, not 9 days, 103.0, 99.7, and 25.4. High-mix weekday completions average 100.6, not 106.1. The true contrasts are roughly +14% arrivals and +1% completions, not +11% and +7%. The post-period completion increase versus that baseline is about +20%, not +14%. The “22% demand growth” illustration uses 72.6 and 63.9 arrivals/completions per day for 20–28 April; those are week totals divided by the wrong day count. The correct all-day means are about 99 and 87, so the pooled contrast runs in the opposite direction from the one argued.

In the Little’s Law section, the displayed identity writes \(L \approx N/(HT)\) rather than \(L \approx \lambda T\). Worked examples usually use \(T=L/\lambda\), but 15–17 April arrivals are 316, not 326, and that total contradicts the 105 and 211 subtotals given just above it. For 29 April–3 May, \(152/5=30.4\), not 25.33; the implied flow times 206 and 198 minutes should be about 247 and 237. “WIP 475235” is unreadable, and “AC=66” is undefined notation.

Hourly alignment is inconsistent. Bins start at 07:00, so 08:00–09:59 is the second and third bins. April 21’s 08:00–09:59 share is 11/115, not 42/115; the 34-arrival bin is 10:00–11:00, not 09:00–10:00; the later burst of 14 is 16:00–17:00, not 17:00–18:00. April 25 is indexed differently from April 21 in the same audit. April 7 completion dispersion is wrong: population CV is about 0.43 and Fano about 1.56, not 1.033 and 8.81, which reverses the arrival-versus-completion comparison. Daily “population” SD 29.67 and Fano 9.78 are sample (\(n-1\)) figures, unlike the hourly population variances.

The Python would not run. The segmentation step selects `wip_per_fte`, a column that is never created, and the Little’s Law frame calls `set_index("name")` without a `name` field. The printed narrative also does not match the summaries that corrected code would produce. The weekend-cut example scales an index of 11.14 from 2.5 to 2.0 FTE and gets 11.60; the factor 2.5/2.0 produces about 13.9.

The qualitative warnings about carry-over, non-stationarity, and not treating catch-up output as normal capacity are sound. The submitted numbers and the required script are not reliable enough for a higher score.
