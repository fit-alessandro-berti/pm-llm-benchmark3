3.5

The write-up has the required seven-part shape and a few defensible calls (AP-202 failed, AP-205 stopped before completion, AP-203 and AP-207 broadly fit ED-1). It is not a reliable conformance assessment. Nominal segregation, control timing, and several case facts are misstated, and the required checking code cannot produce the evidence it claims.

**Nominal four-eyes is wrong on most rows.** AP-202, AP-203, AP-206, AP-207, and AP-208 are labeled “Different users” while the same resource both prepares and releases. That corrupts the column the review is built on. AP-201 and AP-205 are labeled correctly, so the mistake is inconsistent rather than a single misread.

**Controls are invented or inverted.** AP-204 and AP-209 have no manager event; both are given a post-release ratification. AP-204’s fabricated receipt passed a full AV-2 pass, yet the rationale says AV-2 prevented fraud. AP-206’s manager approved before release (13:04 vs 13:08); §5 treats that approval as having occurred after release. AP-208’s emergency token was active; the later code marks it inactive. AP-202’s conflict is stated in the facts (m09 rates u23); credential sharing is the open point, and the write-up swaps those.

**Classifications do not follow the control objective.** AP-206 is OBJECTIVE_FAILED even though the write-up itself says the late token may be a logging delay; that is INDETERMINATE. AP-208 is only OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET despite same-user release, amount far above EUR 100,000, a bank change inside seven days, AV-2 down, an unsigned checklist, ratification after settlement, and audit outside the recall window. Legitimacy of the payment does not make that package sufficient. AP-204 completed a fraudulent payment and was audited after the recall window; PARTIALLY_MET understates a failed objective. AP-201’s audit at 25 hours is treated as clearly corrective.

**Control logic is unreliable.** ED-1 is described as bypassing AV-2. ED-1 requires a full AV-2 pass with no override. MG-4 is treated as if it enforced preparer/releaser separation; it is a separate approver, not the second payment actor. §2 answers “why expected loss is not enough” by returning to likelihood × impact, and “90% reduction with 5% residual exposure” does not parse. The sufficiency grid leaves cells empty and cites m02 on AP-204; m02 belongs to AP-203. §4 says a pre-settlement act is preventive and a post-release act is detective, which leaves the required hybrid case (after release, before settlement) unresolved. The illustrative 95% loss on AP-208 contradicts the finding that the payment was legitimate.

**§7 does not meet the spec.** The CSV header and rows do not match the four source columns; one activity is `Prepare_ Payment`. The code never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`. Prepare and release are joined on equal timestamps, so the pair set is empty. The blocked-release filter requires one row to be both a block and an attempt. The printed sample includes non-violators, contradicts the case-fact table (AP-202 coverage, AP-205 manager timing, AP-209 bank change), and is not output the script could emit. Unused imports do not repair that.

**Headings and monitoring.** Two required headings drop the hyphen (“CaseLevel”, “ResidualRisk”). §6 does cover the requested indicators and separates alert from proof, but the 12-hour “late manager” rule is not the settlement/recall standard, and several thresholds are untethered from ED-1.
