5.5

The write-up follows the required outline and is directionally coherent, but it is not reliable as a process-mining interpretation. Several claims misstate the tables, over-read social-network metrics, and then harden those readings into a single role.

The clearest factual error is the exception rate. “Share of cases with exception_flag=True = 0.48” means 48% of Mira’s own cases are flagged. It does not mean she handles 48% of exception cases. Bot_Ingest touches essentially every claim and has a share of 0.19, so that column is a within-resource rate and the base rate is about 19%. The answer never uses the figures that actually scope her reach: 22% of all claims, 64% of reopened claims, and 71% of complaints. Those omissions make her look like a general hub. “Most” Bot_Rules exceptions go to her is also unsupported: the Mira edge is 920, against 610 and 590 to the two team leads and about 4,090 Bot_Rules cases.

Handover betweenness of 0.77 is treated as proof that most complex paths must pass through her. That statistic is a share of shortest paths on an aggregated resource graph. It does not count case paths. Subcontracting is likewise converted into retained case ownership. The n=2 pattern only shows an A→B→A return. Low working-together is then used to limit the broker hypothesis, even though high betweenness plus low joint-work closure is the usual brokerage signature. The real reconciliation is only partly stated. Short routing touches matter, and so does the denominator: a few hundred handovers spread over roughly 4,100 cases produce co-case rates around 6–8% without requiring a separate social story. Section 1 also drops the required “does not imply” boundary for subcontracting, time, and role similarity.

The absence reading is better: ordinary claims were stable, exception p90 moved from 8.4 to 13.9 days, and her 4.2-minute median service time argues against a processing-capacity bottleneck. The executive summary then undoes that calibration by describing unqualified “major delays,” “unchecked discretion,” and a settled identity as the informal primary triage broker. One six-day absence, with no stated controls for mix or concurrent change, supports an exception-path dependency. It does not establish organization-wide dependence or uncontrolled authority. No wrongdoing is invented, which is appropriate, but “Critical,” “unchecked,” and “High” data-quality risk outrun the evidence.

The proposed checks contain validity errors. Extending `discover_subcontracting_network` to n=3 or n=4 does not reconstruct Bot_Rules → Mira → specialist → Mira → adjuster lifecycles. Filtering variants that end in Escalate Complaint cannot show whether she prevents escalation, because prevented cases are excluded. A singleton from `discover_organizational_roles` would show an unusual activity profile, not that the role is informal. Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules (fraud-screen return), Temp_1 → Vendor_Verify → Temp_1, and the team-lead-only path into Vendor_Pay are ignored, so the automated and vendor boundary is missing. Immediate reason codes and a trained backup are proportionate, and removal is correctly deferred, but those actions are justified by one already-chosen role rather than by tests that could still distinguish the hypotheses.
