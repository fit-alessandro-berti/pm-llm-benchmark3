4.2

The write-up correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, or open cases as non-defaults, the 48,000-case arithmetic balances, and the obvious post-outcome fields are dropped. The design still does not hold together as an estimand, an as-of cohort, or an implementable pipeline.

The primary target is stated as \(P(D_{90}\mid A)\) on applications that eventually activate, while the prediction time is the first event or the moment just after KYC. Activation is then a future selection event, so the training population is not the population that would be scored. The phrase “if activated” also turns a selected-sample association into a counterfactual without stating the extra assumptions. The same target is later treated as a Kaplan–Meier hazard and as a classifier. Those are different estimands. The alternative Fine–Gray model of default versus rejection puts events from different stages on one competing-risk scale: default occurs only after activation, so rejection is not a competing first event for default.

Cohort rules contain a concrete error: excluding applications started after 1 January 2024 drops the observation window. The flow table does not separate ordinary right censoring, delayed feeds, permanently missing partner outcomes, transfer with partial linkage, and still-open cases. All 3,600 immature activated accounts are pooled as ordinary right censoring, and all 22,800 non-activated cases are simply “missing / N/A.” `label_available_at` is never used; maturity is a blanket activation-plus-125-days rule. The cutoff is not an as-of rule for open and not-yet-mature cases.

The primary analysis then treats that pooled censoring as independent in a Kaplan–Meier or survival classifier. Permanent partner missingness and transfer non-linkage are informative, and the text does not say which assumptions are testable, partly testable, or untestable. Calling every transfer a default changes the population rather than bounding censoring. Prefix rules conflict with the stated prediction time: stop at the first event, stop after credit review, or keep the shorter of five events and three days. Fixed-length and elapsed-time prefixes are not separate landmarks, short traces that never reach length 5 are not handled, and migration duplicates are not handled. `time_to_decision` can lie beyond the prefix.

The leakage table drops `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and future events, but it keeps `label_available_at` as a usable field even though label timing can track the outcome. Same-calendar-day `documents_total` leakage remains. Resource experience is marked “repair” although the extract stores experience at extraction time and cannot be mapped backward without an external history. Updated `risk_score` is reduced to the first value even when later values precede the prediction instant. Calendar and policy-era features are absent despite the 1 July 2025 shift.

Validation is one hindsight start-time cut at the policy date, with no validation fold and no requirement that labels be mature as of the backtest date. Post-July cases, and any October cases retained, include unresolved 90-day windows. Dropping October from the test set avoids the migration regime the model would face. AUC and the C-index are named without a censored-data rule, IPCW, or restriction to matured labels. Random splitting is rightly rejected because of the policy break.

The limitations section overstates what can be claimed (“accounts that will be activated,” “activation failure”) and one of the three deployment effects is an extract schedule rather than a change in the data-generating process. Feedback through who becomes activated is only partly stated.

The skeleton is about 76 lines and does not implement the design. `has_activation` is true only on activation rows, so the cohort keeps those rows and then deletes `Account Activated`, leaving an empty prefix log. The start time is therefore the activation timestamp. `CUTOFF` is unused. There is no case-level terminal state, censoring flag, or label-maturity flag, and no validation split. `format_dataframe` is called with `case`, `activity`, and `timestamp` rather than `case_id`, `activity_key`, and `timestamp_key`. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called with a dataframe and `prefix_length`, then indexed as a dataframe; it expects an event log and a length and returns a log. The audit selects a column named `Account Activated`, which is not created, and `leakage_audit_df` is never built.
