The memo is complete, well-structured, and generally careful: all seven required sections are present, classifications are not mechanical copies of four-eyes status or amount, and the code actually calls `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`, separates AP-205, joins case facts, and labels its output as evidence rather than a verdict. The score is held down by internal contradictions and a few over-precise claims.

The main logical break is AP-207. Section 2 defines a substantive manager review after release and before settlement, without evidence of a hard hold, as only partially equivalent. Section 4 then says an approval timestamp does not prove stop authority. Section 5 says a truly independent second releaser would still be a moderate improvement. Despite that, Section 3 assigns **OBJECTIVE_MET**. The rationale’s condition, “if settlement was genuinely stoppable,” is doing the work the classification itself does not. ED-1 allows approval before settlement, but the answer never reconciles that permission standard with its own stricter equivalence test.

AP-203 has a related overclaim. Approval is before release and the review is substantive, so this is the strongest package, but MG-4/ED-1 still require an evidenced L3 manager. The row records that the level is missing and then classifies the objective as met. The code is more disciplined (`CONDITIONAL_PASS__MANAGER_L3_NOT_EVIDENCED`) than the prose verdict.

AP-201’s corrective-capacity statement is too strong. Release was 08:06, settlement 10:00, and audit began at 09:00 the next day: about 25 hours after release, but 23 hours after settlement. On a 24-hour-from-transfer reading, the recall window had not expired when the audit began; at most, about an hour remained. “Effectively expired” blurs those clocks. The same-user action was convenience, so partial conformance is reasonable, but the row simultaneously calls the exception unjustified without cleanly separating an unauthorized deviation from residual loss risk.

AP-204 is well reasoned on independence, yet **INDETERMINATE** understates a known failure. A fabricated receipt was released and found only after ordinary recovery was uncertain. Actor separation is unresolved; prevention and timely correction of that fraud are not. AP-209 is a better use of **INDETERMINATE**, because no defect was found and the repeated u12/u13 pairing may be a designed cell.

Smaller defects: several ED-1 cells in Section 1 describe conditions without an explicit prevent/detect/correct/fail conclusion; “the realized legitimate-vendor assumption was false” is unclear; and the code sets AP-208 `emergency_token_before_prepare=True` although the facts say only that a token was active. That error does not flip AP-208’s eligibility result. The one-business-day test uses `release + BusinessDay(1)` at the same clock time, so a next-morning audit can fail by minutes, and unknown settlement times are filled as a failed pre-settlement check rather than left unknown. Both limitations are only partly disclosed.
