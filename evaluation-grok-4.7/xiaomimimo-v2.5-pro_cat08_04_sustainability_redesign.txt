6.3

The response meets the required seven-section structure, covers eight interventions and six rebound types, and correctly protects true critical-downtime van service while treating Standard-stock air as a slack-gated issue. That architecture is directionally sound, but several claims contradict the event-log evidence.

The Standard-stock Service-contract average of 7.8 kgCO2e is described as mostly air. Against ground near 3.6–4.2 and air at 18.5, that average implies roughly a quarter of the segment on air, in line with the 22% SP1 violation rate, not most of it. The air-to-ground cycle-time effect is given as +12–24 h, which is the consolidated-truck wait, not the air-versus-ground gap. Observed stock paths differ by about 17 h (17 h air versus 34 h ground), and air is stated to save about 24 h of transport time.

Dynamic batching is inconsistent with the batch evidence. Energy “approaches 1.3 kWh/item” only at batch size 12–20, where median batch wait is 38 h. A 24 h cap would often prevent that batch size, so both the energy claim and the cycle-time claim cannot hold together.

The CNC_B-to-CNC_A effect directions are reversed relative to the paths given. The CNC_B rework path is 160 h, 32% late, 32 kWh, and 1.8 kg scrap; the CNC_A path is 118 h, 21% late, 16 kWh, and 0.70 kg scrap. Moving that work toward CNC_A would be expected to shorten cycle time and reduce lateness, not increase them. “CNC_A queue is longer” is not in the evidence. “CNC_B is faster” is stated, but the observed rework path is slower.

Emergency production is called high-energy. The critical emergency-van path is 7.0 kWh and 52 kgCO2e; the hotspot is transport, not energy. Splitting critical orders is then credited with a significant transport cut and neutral cycle time even though the precautionary share is unknown and leaving dedicated van should add time. Missing SP2 justification events are treated as proof that vans were unnecessary.

The validation baseline of “830 reworks” does not match the log. Rework production is 470, with 390 returning to CNC; packaging rework inflows are 260 and 360. 830 is the consolidated-truck confirmation count. The SP1 control is also reduced to a flat “>24 h slack” rule instead of the stated >85% on-time probability, despite p90 cycle times far above the medians.

Other gaps: the order-level flattened log and multi-line attribution are not treated as boundary uncertainty; energy “per on-time order” biases the indicator; the partial-shipment path (7%, 79 h, 24% late, second shipment) is absent from the redesign; obsolescence is named as a risk but not controlled; packaging compatibility is said to eliminate all 620 packaging-rework cases and only reduce material, although reinforced packaging doubles package mass; and the customer rebound is generic hoarding rather than a promise-date or segment displacement effect grounded in the paths.
