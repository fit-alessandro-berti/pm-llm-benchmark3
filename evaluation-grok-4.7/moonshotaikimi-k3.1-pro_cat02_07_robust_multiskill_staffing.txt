6.5

The analytical core is strong and mostly well audited, but the required deliverable is incomplete, so this cannot score in the high range.

Sections 1–7 show real process-mining and capacity competence. Service means (6.94 / 26.30 / 32.82 / 38.50 / 15.74), shift capacity (2,490; 2,040 without Chen), block loads, dedicated base-equivalent capacities, and the central day ratio \(2720.6/2490 \approx 1.093\) are coherent. Multipliers, breaks, the 16:00 cliff, the post-18:00 mechanical gap, Emil’s late Software monopoly, and Farah’s 1.40 diagnostic penalty are used rather than mentioned. U1–U5 pressures, the fixed-N versus count-doubling U2 split, the uniform-versus-endpoint envelope (about 2,065–3,376), and censoring when next-day capacity is unknown are the right uncertainty treatment. Three policies have eligibility, scores, reserve, preemption, parallelization, and overflow rules. HYBRID plus explicit surge staffing is justified by \(J_{\rm RF}\), not by base mean delay, and POOL/SPEC are left as better under other objectives. Section 7 covers the requested event attributes and correctly separates PM4Py case service/waiting from stage queues and handover discovery.

The failures are structural. There is no `# 9. Limits and validation plan`: no pilot or backtest, no P1 protection rule, and no unsafe-rollout stop design, beyond scattered caveats. The single Python block is truncated inside `dedicated_capacity` and is not runnable. It never finishes an approximation or LP for three policies, never prints base/U1–U5 metrics, never emits an interval log, and never calls `format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, or `discover_handover_of_work_network`. Handover, setup, coordination, and preemption are named constants only. The section 8 prose claims an LP and a log that the code does not contain. The scenario table is an allowed qualitative scale, but `U+`/`G?`/`B/R` cells are repeated judgments, not calculations tied to the load model, and SPEC’s Intake/Validation roster is still not a dispatch rule.
