7.5

The memo is a strong, usable decision document: it picks a segment-gated hybrid, refuses both national scale and a blanket rollback, separates facts from interpretations and assumptions, explains all five metric conflicts with the right mechanisms, and ranks M1–M8 in a defensible order. Numbers that are cited match the brief, and fitness versus precision is handled correctly.

It is not nearly flawless. The recommendation is coarser than its own evidence. Survey skipping was aimed at medium-risk addresses and is explicitly implicated in apartment and permit failures, yet the memo keeps skipping for simple low-risk houses even while ranking a randomized skip test as the highest-value measurement. Coordinator ownership, which is not the identified source of rework, is withheld from the complex segments that most need a single owner. SLA (worse overall, better for simple houses) and NPS never enter the decision or the rollout bar. Known mix shift and missing risk attributes before week 3 are logged, then set aside by assuming confounders are minor, even though the whole recommendation rests on those segment cuts. Several value-of-information rows say what a measure “informs” without stating the result that would change the choice, and the scale threshold is left as an “agreed” tolerance rather than a proposed gate. Low precision is also treated a bit too directly as proof that the operation itself is uncontrolled.
