8.4

The memo is a strong, usable decision document: it refuses both national rollout and blanket rollback, unbundles Redesign R, and treats segment results as associations rather than identified effects. Facts, interpretations, and open assumptions are separated with unusual care, and the five metric conflicts are explained at the right level, including the non-equivalence of fitness with compliance and of precision with a failure rate. The value-of-information ranking is decision-linked, and the immediate pause on survey skipping and billing-before-activation is grounded in rework, complaints, and alignment evidence rather than in a single worsened KPI.

The main weakness is false precision in the rollout gates. Ruling out a complaint increase above 0.5 percentage points, alongside tight bounds on p90, p95, first-time-right, and SLA, under a multiple-comparison plan, implies sample sizes a regional experiment is unlikely to deliver. The memo never checks that feasibility, so the gates can freeze a decision indefinitely. The 85% utilization ceiling is similarly unanchored. These are labeled as proposals, but they are still operationally inconsistent with the answer’s own warning that the supplied volumes support no sample-size claim.

Smaller problems keep it short of nearly flawless. The executive memo reads discovered-model precision of 0.51 as evidence that the operation is uncontrolled; the later section corrects this, but the decision page overstates what a discovery metric means. The EUR 14 versus EUR 46 “downstream” split treats non-identical cost definitions as a clean incremental cost, even with a caveat. Aggregate mix bias is noted only symmetrically: a harder apartment and old-building mix would push aggregate quality and tail metrics against R, while within-segment harm is the stronger evidence. SLA (13% to 16%, better for simple houses) is largely absent from the memo and evidence table. The resilience row places a causal “because” in the fact column, and the two-week mobilization clock is arbitrary. The absolute rule that missing activation or permit records block expansion can also confuse logging gaps with real bypasses.

None of that overturns the recommendation or misstates the core baseline-versus-pilot figures. The judgment is narrower than the letter of alternative E, and that narrowing is explicit and defensible.
