The answer is a serious causal analysis with the right headline judgment: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not defensible, median and p90 must stay separate, and rollout should be an evaluation design rather than an immediate expansion. Most pre/post arithmetic checks out (Alpha/Beta/Gamma level changes, log-style DiDs near −13%/−9%/−9.5% for the median, March escalation exactly +2.0 pp in every branch, specialist-load increases near +73%/+55% vs about +35%). Availability vs use, the package of co-interventions, staggered timing, and TWFE’s already-treated contamination are handled at a high level. The score is held down by identification mistakes, inconsistent bounds, a broken required script, and a rollout rule that contradicts the panel.

Section 3 overclaims identification. Routing, staffing, and differential complexity are not common calendar shocks, so month fixed effects do not difference them out. Alpha/Beta arrivals rise about 23% against about 12% in Delta/Epsilon, Alpha’s load per FTE rises about 29% against about 10%, and the May complexity shift is +8 pp in Alpha and Epsilon but only about +3 pp in Beta. The threats table later admits these biases, which contradicts the primary-strategy justification. The fraud-desk bias row is also internally inconsistent: control cycle times pushed upward would make a DiD benefit look larger, not pull it toward zero.

Regression to the mean is misstated. Alpha’s flat absolute median (48.9 h to 48.5 h) does not argue against RTM. Alpha was selected for poor historical times, the pre-period gap versus controls is about 31% on the median, and that gap narrows because controls rise. That is what RTM plus a common upward shock looks like. Gamma’s May–June spike and July–August reversal are treated more carefully.

The sensitivity “bounds” mix different estimands. The median interval is labeled as a DiD range of −15% to +4%, but +4% is Beta’s naive absolute change, while the cited August DiD of about −17% falls outside the stated interval. p90 bounds similarly mix a naive +10% with an August-only contrast. Escalation’s +0.7 to +1.1 pp band is more coherent, yet confidence 55 is too high for piecewise-constant rates and co-timed routing. Rework DiDs are slightly off (about −1.2 and −2.3 pp, not −1.4 and −2.5). “15–28 percentage points more” specialist load in the strongest ladder claim is the wrong unit: the growth-rate gaps are roughly 20–38 pp, or about 15–28% in multiplicative terms. Beta’s pre-period fast-path share is 6%, not 4%, and the stable 4% automated-fast-path rate in never-treated branches is never treated as a measurement problem. Several table cells lose their minus signs or arrows, so magnitudes such as “2548%” and “2.082.26” are not reliably readable.

The script does not run end to end. Relative-time dummies are created on `es`, but `fit_and_show` always estimates `df`, so the event-study formula fails before the intensity models. Even if repaired, early Gamma months are dropped and cluster groups stay tied to the full panel. Assumptions are labeled as assumptions, and the TWFE models are correctly demoted to description, but the primary Callaway–Sant’Anna-style estimand is never computed.

The recommendation picks a reasonable policy option and asks for case-level logs rather than discovery on aggregates. The design itself is weaker. “Randomize” is then specified as deterministic weekly alternation, which is a poor switchback for multi-day cycle times and persistent queues. The scale-up rule then expands to Alpha and Beta, which already adopted in April, and the “exclude zero or a 5% reduction” rule would accept a precisely estimated trivial effect. Delta-only success is also allowed to green-light higher-complexity Epsilon.

The claim ladder’s bottom rung is the right leadership statement. Above it, p90 is “suggestive” of a 5–12% improvement after section 7 says no tail improvement can be claimed, and the conditional escalation claim omits routing and staffing from its conditioning set.
