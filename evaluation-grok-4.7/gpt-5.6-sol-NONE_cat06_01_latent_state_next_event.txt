7.4

The forecast is process-aware and usually well gated, but it is not nearly flawless. Primary states for C6-A through C6-E are defensible, fast close is correctly withheld, and supplier analysis is not mechanically copied onto every lot mention. The cross-case discussion correctly keeps C6-A and C6-B on the same visible prefix while separating injury, multi-device, and lot-cluster obligations from a low-severity near-duplicate. Those strengths do not offset several concrete reasoning and calibration failures.

The clearest error is C6-E’s third prediction. Customer Evidence Requested is ranked at 0.10–0.25 even though the reasoning itself says a third request occurs only after the second review and is not enabled until that review is performed. With a normal feed and only about 40 minutes since receipt, a hidden completed review is a thin tail, not a double-digit next-event mass. The same conflation of “likely after review” with “next logged event” appears, more mildly, in C6-B’s third prediction. Lowering C6-E’s chance that Evidence Review Completed is next because completeness is 0.72 repeats the mistake: incompleteness changes the post-review branch, not the identity of the event that review itself logs.

C6-C and C6-D are assigned the same disposition interval, 0.16–0.34, although the facts differ. C6-C is medium severity, has no injury, and has only a “can be reportable” country rule, so disposition without regulatory assessment is a real normative branch; the directly-follows counts also show disposition after technical investigation more often than regulatory assessment. C6-D is high severity with injury, regulatory work was opened at 12:40, and only about 80 minutes have elapsed against a 2–14 hour assessment range, so a next disposition is a much thinner missing-event tail. Cloning the interval erases that distinction. C6-D also overclaims: “opened” on a worklist is not “demonstrably in progress,” and uncertain evidence handling is placed under completed obligations. A late-arriving request or receipt is at least as plausible as Evidence Review Completed if the missing cycle lands in order.

C6-A’s second and third intervals overlap almost completely, so the rank of regulatory completion over technical completion is sharper than the evidence. Elapsed time versus the supplied medians is barely used: review at roughly 1.8 hours for A/B, 0.7 hours for E, technical investigation 2.3 hours before a 5-hour regulatory median for C, and regulatory work open for 1.3 hours on D. Queue narrative is present, but it is not turned into a race among already-open work and the enabled completion.

The calibration note says residual mass remains outside the top three. Case midpoints sum to about 0.94–1.00, so that claim is not what the numbers say. C6-B’s alternative is still S3 with a routing comment, not an alternative state. C6-E says the six-complaint lot “explicitly” makes supplier analysis mandatory and then lists “apparently related” as unresolved. Some contradicting points, including the 16:00 batch on C6-D, do not bear on the ranked next events.

Obligation reading, migration caution, and the enabled-versus-observed distinction are otherwise strong. The remaining gaps are internal inconsistencies in next-event logic and probability, not a failure to understand the process.
