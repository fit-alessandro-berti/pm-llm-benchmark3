3.5

The write-up keeps the required nine-section shape and a few sound cautions (open cases belong in the risk set, escalation is not a baseline covariate, transfer is not a harmless exit, risk scores are not causal effects). The SLA analysis itself is not reliable.

**Estimands.** Time zero at `Submit` and a 72-hour horizon are right. The estimands are not. Estimand B defines a “time to not resolve” and then folds competing exits and cases still open at 72 into one cumulative incidence. Those are different probabilities; the open-at-horizon share is the residual of the cumulative incidences, not part of one CIF. Estimand C conditions on avoiding transfer and withdrawal (and omits rejection), which is the hypothetical quantity you get by censoring competing events, not the operational probability of resolution. Case roles at the horizon are wrong: S03 resolved at 76 was still without a terminal outcome at 72 and is an observed late resolution, not a failure of \(S(72)\). S09, open at 92, is known to have passed 72; it is censored at 92 for time-to-resolution, and it is a known non-resolution for a 72-hour binary endpoint. S32 transferred at 96 was still internal at 72, unlike S08 at 68 and S21 at 26.

**Encoding.** The status table sets every open case’s censoring time to 72, including S31 at 23, S15 at 35, S10 at 47, and S29 at 51. That pretends follow-up continued to the horizon. Cases already older than 72 (S09, S11, S25, S26) are labeled “assumed open” and censored at 72 even though age and status are observed. Late resolutions (S03, S13, S20, S28) are typed only as “Breach,” which drops the fact that `Resolve` occurred. Transfer is given three assumptions, but “resolved immediately” does not state the event time, and pre-horizon and post-horizon transfers share one code. Withdrawal and rejection have no sensitivity coding in the table.

**Evidence.** Cohort A does not have three transfers, and “3 transfers (0%)” is self-contradictory. Transfers are S08 (A), S21 (B), and S32 (B), and all three are P1 and escalated — the clearest informative-censoring pattern in the extract, and it is unused. Cohort A does not have four reworks; `rework_count` is 1 only for S07, S13, and S27. The claimed resolved-case mean of about 45 hours is about 50. Section 7’s P1 list includes S01, which is P2. Section 3 never separates open cases still younger than 72 from open cases already past 72.

**Methods.** Kaplan–Meier, Aalen–Johansen, Fine–Gray, and restricted mean time are named. Cause-specific hazards, landmarking, and a multi-state model are not compared. The primary recommendation then equates Aalen–Johansen with “KM with competing-risk labeling,” which is the cause-specific survival calculation, not the cumulative incidence. With \(n=32\), that choice needed a precise estimand, a tie/censoring convention, and a statement of what 1 − CIF\(_\text{resolve}(72)\) includes (still open, withdrawn, rejected, transferred).

**Process model.** `AutoCheck` is treated as a baseline covariate “at start,” but it sits after `Screen` in S01, S15, and S17, so the same leakage warning applied to `Escalate` applies here. Rework, repeated review, current stage, and `last_event_gap_h` are not classified. The diagram has no `Submit → AutoCheck` path, no `Screen → Reject/Withdraw/Escalate`, no `AutoCheck → Escalate`, and sends `Rework` only back to `Review`, while S27 goes `Rework → Resolve`. Long gaps (S11 55h, S25 48h, S26 72h, S09 31h) never enter the censoring discussion.

**Sensitivity and operations.** The three scenarios misstate transfer timing as “censored at 68h,” do not refit withdrawal or rejection, and do not bound informative censoring from those gaps. The reversal claim is a conditional with no calculation. The 60-hour alert, the 24-hour gap rule, and the “2×” rework risk are unestimated. S27’s rework is omitted, and S04 escalates without a prior `Review`.

**Code.** The block does not implement the required analysis. Priorities are wrong for S18, S25, S26, S27, S28, S29, and S30. `escalated`, `rework_count`, and `last_event_gap_h` are dropped. Both “estimands” use the same resolved indicator; the non-censoring branch marks transfers as neither event nor censored; transfer-as-success is absent; and both calls mutate one frame. Step-index timestamps are invented. `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` is not a PM4Py constructor, and `variants[0]` does not match `get_variants`. Lifelines has no `survival_function_72`; the fitted object’s survival value is \(P(T>72)\), and the print labels it as the probability resolved by 72. `CoxPHFitter` is unused. The fallback proportion treats cases still open before 72 as failures and does not say so.

Limitations cover timestamps, external transfer outcomes, cutoff dates, workload, assignment, and incomplete rollout. That section cannot repair the horizon coding, the counts, or the non-running estimator.
