The required seven-part structure is complete, the four scenarios match the requested disruption types, and most cited utilizations, queues, mix shifts, and conflict hints are real. The package also avoids blanket hiring and simultaneous priority for every case type. Those merits do not make the redesign reliable.

The main scenario’s bottleneck sequence is incomplete. Under the winter mix shift, the longest peak queues are specialist review (36h), medical validation (22h), and assay (18h). The analysis instead stops at pre-analytical, accession, and technical validation, so the migration that actually produces the Complex-panel and Urgent failures is missed. The direct-to-release share after technical validation falls from about 74% to about 50% in the peak DFG, and assay-to-repeat incidence roughly doubles, from about 7% to about 15%. Neither structural change is computed.

The robust package does not keep the stated service levels acceptable. Urgent p90 is already 19h in the normal period against an 18h penalty and 33h in the peak; adding the peak edge medians exceeds 18h before any new shock. No critical path, case-type target, or coping rule at the 101% technical-validation pool shows how Urgent stays acceptable while Routine and Complex-panel are not all treated as first priority. Medical validation’s ordinary peak queue of 22h is left out of the always-on package and is covered mainly by a second-Friday or specialist-queue trigger.

Two policies fail in the conditions that should activate them. Tiered dispatch and larger Routine batches turn on only when the assay queue is under 15h and batch expansion is withdrawn once that queue exceeds 18h, so the rule is off at the observed peak. If M2 is actually down, Routine—still 42% of peak volume—has no disposition once Complex-panel overflow is outsourced. Repeat-after-failure, the slowest and double-consuming stream, never receives a control.

Evidence is also misused. Automation is said to “reduce” the registration queue while citing the historical rise from 0.7h to 2.8h. The weekly recollection count is called a 2.7× rate, although the per-case rate rises by about 1.9×. Several comparisons are unreadable (“18%29%”, “1.07.5h”). The daily recollection warning at 100 cases cannot fire: peak recollection is roughly 40 a day. The dashboard also omits assay, medical-validation, and specialist queues, which are both the worst delays and the triggers for the proposed policies, and the pre-analytical threshold conflicts between the table (6h) and the dashboard (8h).
