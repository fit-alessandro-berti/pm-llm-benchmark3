The three candidates are genuinely different, internally consistent hypotheses, and the language counts (7 / 12 / 14, with a strict inclusion chain) match the stated partial orders. Evidence handling is strong: S1 is correctly treated as refuted at completion time, V5 is used as a real falsifier of an AND-join before supplier confirmation, missingness is separated from true optionality, and the DFG-bidirectionality discussion does not treat both orders as proof of concurrency. The recommendation, two-tier severity, and falsification plan are concrete and well calibrated.

The required validation harness does not run. In `soundness_by_reachability`, the first loop evaluates `k(m)` before `m` exists, so it raises `UnboundLocalError`. That call is not guarded, so enumeration, metrics, inclusion checks, and the manual suite never execute. The closing note that those lines “have no effect” is false. Section 3’s claim that the language is “verified exhaustively in §8” is therefore not delivered.

Smaller inaccuracies: balanced precision is described as having a single escaping edge, but log-visited states also enable activities the log never takes there (for example EX after `R ER CC SC`, and AC after `R ER CC EX`). The S1 refutation is slightly overstated for equal timestamps under the ingestion-index rule. Dead code (`VARIANTS` assigned twice, unused `MIDDLES`) adds noise but is secondary to the crash.
