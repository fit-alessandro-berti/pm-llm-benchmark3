5.5

The write-up has the required skeleton and a usable balanced partial order, but it contradicts its own evidence standard, misreads a domain rule, misstates complexity, and ships validation that does not check the models it proposes.

The evidence ledger covers the rule set only in aggregate. All seven variants share one row, and S3 is merged with S4, so the trace that actually separates the hypotheses is easy to miss: the two cases of Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review are the only evidence that Supplier Confirmation can precede Engineering Review while still following Credit Check. The directly-follows discussion correctly says both directions are compatible with an exclusive choice of sequences, then the same row’s modeling implication says the checks are “likely concurrent,” and section 4 upgrades that to “the DFG explicitly supports” parallelism. S2 is treated as confirmation. It is one stakeholder claim against S1 and against D7; completion order plus a contested statement does not confirm concurrency. S1 is not refuted as a policy. The log only refutes “Credit Check always finishes first.”

Section 2 says every model represents Supplier Confirmation as an XOR with a silent transition. The conservative tree does not. It is an exclusive choice of the seven observed sequences. The missingness analysis never uses the counts. Supplier Confirmation is absent in 7 of 30 cases, about 23%, while S5 puts integration loss at 5–10%. Missingness alone cannot account for the skip rate, so legitimate stocked-component skips and dropped events are not behaviorally indistinguishable at the log level. Which individual cases are the bad ones remains unknown.

The conservative tree does encode the seven observed strings, and an XOR of those sequences is a permitted overfitting baseline. Duplicate labels are mentioned. The complexity claim is wrong: an exclusive choice of sequences has a state space linear in total length. Hundreds of variants remain easy to analyze. Concurrency, not this XOR, is what produces interleaving blow-up. The “underfitting risks” list is an overfitting description. The real underfitting risk is that any new linearization of the same activities is rejected even when it satisfies D1–D6.

The balanced POWL is the strongest piece. Register Order precedes both checks; the checks are unordered; both precede an optional Expedite Review; that optional node and an optional Supplier Confirmation precede approval; approval, release, and archive stay in sequence; Supplier Confirmation is additionally after Credit Check and unordered relative to Engineering Review and Expedite Review. Because the optional nodes are XOR-with-skip nodes, the skip still synchronizes the partial order, so both checks precede approval even when Expedite Review is absent. All seven observed traces fit, and the three unobserved balanced traces in the audit are real members of that language. Anchoring Supplier Confirmation to Credit Check is an empirical restriction stronger than D5, and that underfitting risk is stated honestly. Two further admitted traces are omitted: Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case, and the same pattern with Engineering Review before Credit Check. The language is finite, about a dozen visible traces, so this could have been enumerated exactly.

The permissive model drops the wrong constraint. D5 says Supplier Confirmation may start after one of the two checks, not after neither. Register Order then Supplier Confirmation then the checks violates D5, yet the audit cites “D5 (loose)” as the reason it is admitted. A strict partial order cannot express “after Credit Check or Engineering Review” by deleting the Credit Check edge. That needs an explicit choice between “after Credit Check” and “after Engineering Review,” with the other check left concurrent. The stated boundary therefore admits behavior the domain rules still forbid, while the underfitting list again describes overgeneralization.

The matrix is directionally sensible and the 100% fitness claims follow from language inclusion, not from a measurement. “Very low precision risk” for the conservative language is fair. Calling the balanced model the compliance model conflicts with section 10’s own point that a silent skip treats a lost Supplier Confirmation as conforming.

Section 8 is not a validation of the candidates. It rebuilds the 30-case log correctly, then replaces the models with a three-transition chain, scores only that chain, and never calls `generalization_tbr`. `play_out(..., max_traces=100)` does not match the playout parameter `no_traces`. The only negative trace is Register Order, Approve Configuration, Archive Case, which that dummy net accepts, so the printed expectation that fitness is below 1 is false for the code as written. No positive suite is checked against the three languages. The acyclic models have finite languages; sampled playout is an unnecessary substitute for enumeration, and the script never runs the enumeration either.

The recommendation of balanced now, permissive as a stress test, is reasonable only after the permissive net is repaired. The decisive future trace is not Supplier Confirmation before both checks. It is Supplier Confirmation immediately after Engineering Review and before Credit Check: that trace is still inside D5 and distinguishes “Credit Check must precede Supplier Confirmation” from “either check is enough.” Start and complete timestamps, plus the external-versus-stocked attribute, are the right data to collect. Section 10’s alternative, that some missing supplier events are violations and should not be normalized by a silent transition, is the best-calibrated paragraph in the document and would indeed change a compliance recommendation. Concurrency of the two checks is not high confidence.
