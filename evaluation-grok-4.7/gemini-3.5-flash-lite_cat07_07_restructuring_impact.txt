6.2

The write-up follows the required section structure and reaches a conditional pilot recommendation that separates East from North/South and warns against reading early average cycle time as success. Those are real strengths. Under a strict reading it is still analytically unreliable in several places that matter.

The mechanism map does not actually cover the required links for nominal handover reduction, role clarity, or resilience to absence. It treats a rise in rework as an expected consequence of centralization, and it cites `filter_variants` as if that function measured East throughput. The central scenario says total handovers stay stable and that a 0.1h intake improvement offsets longer specialist, approval, and legal waits. Adding the management frequencies gives about 343 handovers per 100 cases now and about 359 after, and the new waits are longer on the edges that grow. Legal/Risk are already at 91–93% and part-time, with volume moving from 9 to 12 per 100 cases and extra triage and difficult quality-check work pushed onto the same pool; none of the scenarios uses that capacity fact. The optimistic case then puts central waits under 3 hours, which cannot describe the legal/risk queue whose current median is already 9.6h. “Heavily driven by East” is also volume-wrong: East is the smallest team, about a quarter of monthly cases. Security is in the routing rule and has no named resource; Local Technical Assessment has no remaining owner once `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, and `E_Tech` are removed. Both gaps are unexamined. “50 relative percentage points” is not a usable rollback threshold.

The indicator table mis-assigns measures: a DFG is not a 4-hour inflow/outflow balance, `format_dataframe` does not measure reassignment, and SLA miss cannot be computed from `case:concept:name` because the log has no due date. The overall SLA cap of 8% sits almost on today’s volume-weighted miss rate of about 7.6% and hides East’s existing 9.8%. The pilot states two incompatible designs, then uses East versus North/South as a control comparison that neither design identifies. That contrast is confounded, contaminated if legal/risk change globally, and biased toward the team most likely to benefit. A 6-hour median wait guardrail is already broken by today’s 9.6-hour legal/risk wait. A 4-week post window also cannot support 90th-percentile cycle time when current tails are 16–23 days, and it cannot observe learning loss. The code sketch calls `discover_handover_of_work_network` with `activity_key`, uses `level="cases"`, never filters `customer_segment`, and labels a block as SLA analysis without calculating SLA or any before/after delta.
