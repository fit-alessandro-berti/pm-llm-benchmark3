The final answer is a strong, well-structured conformance review: both briefs are genuinely argued, concessions are explicit, authority is separated from control-flow fit, and the case matrix plus layered revision are mostly traceable. It is not nearly flawless.

The revised POWL misses a pattern the answer itself treats as central. SUP-709 is Register → Sanctions_Check → Reject_Supplier, with no Quality_Review. Route E is only Reject_Supplier, and Route D requires both checks before rejection, so neither branch accepts the observed trace. Section 6’s intake sketch also drops the sanctions pre-screen that caused the rejection. That is a model-semantics error, not a stated limitation.

M1 also forces post-activation Quality_Review to finish before Notify_Requester. SUP-702’s own evidence is notification at +2 hours and quality the next business day, so the enriched chronology is Notify before Quality. The answer still treats that case as fitting the revised control flow.

Quantitative claims in the judgment do not match the cited evidence. 47+1+1+1+1+1 is 52, not 56. 289/312 is about 92.6%, not 91%. The “20 / 6.4%” and “9.3%” figures put the six August cases in a numerator over the June–July denominator of 312. Section 5.2 says notification observability “fell 2% → 29%”; those figures are the missingness rates, which rose, while observability fell from about 98% to about 71%.

SUP-711 is labeled DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE while the same row says the duplicate is identified, severity is none, confidence is high, and the trace conforms after deduplication. That classification contradicts the analysis. The FD-7 ratification date is also inconsistent with the answer’s own five-business-day rule.

Case dispositions for SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, SUP-712, SUP-715, SUP-704, and SUP-706 are well supported, and the exception-versus-amendment reading of PD-3 is careful. The score is held down by the model gap, the notification/quality ordering conflict, and the arithmetic and labeling errors in the final judgment.
