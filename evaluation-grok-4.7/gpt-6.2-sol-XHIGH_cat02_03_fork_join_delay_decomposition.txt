The delay decomposition is correctly defined and internally consistent. Upstream time, branch queue, service, synchronization, fork-join elapsed time, the post-join gap, final service, and total flow time reconcile case by case: \(Y_c=U_c+T_c+D_c+P_c\), with \(T_c=\max_i L_{ci}\) used once. The double-counting argument is right: each branch satisfies \(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}=T_c\), so summing branches yields \(3T_c\). The case table, worked examples for F02, F04, F07, and F11, critical labels, and aggregate means match the log, including flow 115.25, fork-join 61, B critical on 8/12, and \(H\) means 1.25/10/1.00.

The lead \(H_{ci}\) correctly limits one-branch acceleration, which is why a 10-minute B-queue cut in F10 moves the join by only 5 minutes, and why a 20% A-service cut helps only F01 and F11. The fixed-gap, fixed-appointment, and two-server FCFS readings are distinguished, zero-benefit cases are identified, and the indirect F11 gain under the B replay is real.
