6.5

The write-up is organized, covers every required section, and is often careful about ties, fallbacks, scan lag, and observational equivalence. The QA label (FIFO plus a soft appointment hold), the QC hedge, and the refusal to treat skill labels as an ordering rule are directionally sound. Those merits do not offset repeated errors in the decision sets the task makes central.

The pivotal QA error is at 08:20. A04 arrived at 08:13, so the waiting set is A02, A03, and A04, not A02 and A03. Priority 1 and SPT both select A04 (estimate 7 versus A03’s 10 and A02’s 20). Only an appointment rule selects A03. The claim that this episode is also SPT, repeated in the diagnosis table, §3, and Test 1, is false, and it is exactly the identifiability problem the answer hangs on QA. Test 1 then ranks A07 and A08, which have not arrived by 08:30, and drops A02 from the SPT order. Later QA rows (08:32, 09:05, 09:20, 09:32) are right and do support FIFO against priority, so the primary label survives, but the stated ambiguity does not.

At QB 10:14 the waiting set omits B06 (arrived 10:05, priority 2) and B07 (arrived 10:08, estimate 6). Priority therefore selects B06, and SPT selects B07. The text’s claim that B01 and B04 tie on priority is wrong and hides another priority failure. Combined with the correctly noted violations at 09:31 (resume B01 over B03), 09:48 (B03 over B05), and 10:28 (B04 over B06), “preemptive priority” at confidence 70 is too strong. The better-supported hybrid is FIFO/resume, preempt only for priority 1, plus the logged reassignment. B02’s preemption is also compatible with preemptive SPT, since B01 had about 31 minutes left against B02’s 15; the “urgent arrival” note is what breaks that tie, and it is used, but not enough to carry the primary label.

QD routing is the best section: D05 only on D-F, D07 only on D-X, and D-M limited to mechanical work are read correctly, and D04 over D08 and D07 over D10 really are FIFO and not priority or SPT. Two material gaps remain. D-X is free from 11:20 and idle until D06’s 11:30 appointment, which is the discriminator §4 says is missing, because a pure FIFO start would not require the resource to sit idle if earlier eligible work was allowed. D-F is free from 11:36 to 12:00 while D10 (12 minutes) fits before the meeting and is not taken; “D-F unavailable until 12:20” is true at 12:16, not for D10’s whole wait. Eligibility is also applied inconsistently: D-X has mechanical skill, but mechanical-only jobs are dropped from D-X’s sets without stating that assumption. If they are eligible, priority/SPT at 11:30 pick D08, not D10.

Smaller faults: §5 leans on a two-minute scan delay for C04 versus C06, but C06 has been recorded since 10:25; the batch/early-commitment story is the one that actually fits. Every realized duration equals `est_min`, which is never noted, even though B01’s remaining work is therefore 31 and then 14 ex post. The QA “contradicting” episode is the same appointment case, not a violation of the hybrid. Preemption cost is deferred to the data plan; the segments themselves show no lost time.

QC is the strongest analysis. C02 and C03 match SPT and reject FIFO and priority; C04 rejects dynamic SPT; C05 is priority or an already-missed deadline rather than SPT; C06 returns to SPT; C01 over C07 rejects SPT, priority, and earliest deadline and blows C07’s 12:00 deadline. Confidence 55 matches that. §6 is specific and correctly prioritized. The synthesis is right that QC is least identifiable and that strict preemptive priority is unsafe to institutionalize. QD as most identifiable is only fair for eligibility, not for the appointment-versus-FIFO confound or the idle-resource gaps.

The grade stays at 6.5 because the required method is decision-set comparison, and the two headline comparisons are wrong in a way that changes the competing prediction.
