7.5

The required structure is complete and the core audit is real: the handover totals (343 → 359), exposure index (588 h → 722 h), Legal/Risk volume (189 → 252 per month), approver load (~294 → ~515), and the M/M/c multiples (about 4.6×, 2.4×, 2.1×, 0.7×, 1.6×, 3.5×, 6×) all check out. East is treated separately, fairness and learning are not sidelined, and the recommendation correctly refuses a full rollout. The score is held down by internal contradictions and measurement errors, not by missing sections.

The optimistic scenario is not the scenario it claims to be. It is assigned a 20–25% probability “under the proposal as written,” but its conditions include added Legal/Risk capacity, difficult quality check kept local or capped, affinity routing, and named-owner return. Those are modifications. The section intro also says the probabilities are not predictions of the unmodified proposal, which conflicts with that parenthetical. The stated ranges can sum past 100%.

Cycle-time forecasts are not tied to the wait arithmetic. Listed handover waits are hours; weighted exposure is about 6 hours per case against medians of 7–9 days. In the central scenario the extra waits are a few hours (tech 4–5 h, approval 3.5–5 h, Legal/Risk 14–20 h), yet North/South medians move +3–8% and high-complexity P90s +15–30% (roughly 0.2–0.6 days on the median, and several days on the tail). That gap is not bridged by rework or by an unstable queue; unbounded WIP is reserved for the adverse case. “The pooling advantage disappears as extra work is loaded” is also stronger than the same table: at ~85% the pooled wait (1.6×) is still below North and South today. “15% higher utilization” is taken as relative (74% → 85%) without noting that +15 percentage points would put the pool near 89%, where the M/M/3 wait is about 2.4× and the North/South gain disappears. Cross-team rows are summed as if they cannot overlap other handover types.

The sketch has definite logic bugs. Ping-pong counts every caseworker→tech or caseworker→approval step, so an ordinary tech visit plus an approval is already “ping-pong.” “Rework after handover” joins case-level `rework_flag` onto every handover in the case. Cross-team is only intake/caseworker→intake/caseworker, so specialist absence cover is missed. The Phase 1 difference-in-differences pools East and South into one arm even though the text says those effects can move in opposite directions. Working-together team labels are taken from the full log, so post-change labels leak into the pre period. `discover_subcontracting_network(..., n=2)` is not a parameter of that PM4Py call and would fail; implementation and quality-check resources fall through to `"other"`, so the group handover network cannot reproduce the scenario table. Main SLA rates treat still-open, not-yet-breached cases as successes.

The recommendation itself is conditional, segmented, and appropriately skeptical of early averages. The unsupported cycle-time magnitudes and the optimistic-scenario contradiction are what keep it well short of nearly flawless.
