The answer is complete, arithmetically careful, and decision-theoretically serious. It refuses I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, treats observational risk as distinct from treatment effect, prices spillovers, and keeps calibration, label feedback, and capacity explicit. The direct-net ranges and the scenario arithmetic check out, including the midpoint identity 0.15×€25,000 + 0.19×€60,000 + 0.24×€20,000 = €19,950 and the adverse endpoints produced by zeroing one case.

Those strengths do not make the allocation coherent with the rule the answer itself states. The rule says to rank portfolios on spillover-adjusted value, weight the lower tail, and test the segment corrections. The slate does not do that. I-02’s lower-bound direct net is about €950, while one slot’s illustrative spillover is roughly €600–€1,800, so I-02 fails the answer’s own robust marginal test, yet it is still tier A. The prescribed SME uplift and Strategic Enterprise haircut are never applied: adjusted risk puts I-05 near or above I-02 and makes I-07 the most time-critical calibrated case, but both stay on “wait” while two over-predicted Strategic Enterprise cases are booked. I-09 is scheduled first even though its €2,350 floor is not a reliable bound in a sparse-calibration segment.

Two further operational gaps matter. I-07’s SLA is about 13:30, and a 45-minute swarm is useful only if it starts almost immediately; “reconsider before I-04” is the same moment the deadline expires. The net lines say “after the full €3,400 burden” while the figures also subtract €1,350 of labor and spillover, so an auditor cannot reproduce €13,400 or −€550 from the sentence that introduces them. The adverse 0.03–0.06 spillover and the €10,000 recipient value are invented; disclosure keeps them from being false precision, but the jointly adverse corner is softer than the table’s −€550 edge, which pairs the best adverse benefit with the mildest spillover. Fairness safeguards stay prospective and do not correct today’s concentration away from the under-predicted SME cases.
