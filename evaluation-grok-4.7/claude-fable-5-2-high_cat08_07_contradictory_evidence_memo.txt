The memo is a strong, decision-ready synthesis: it refuses both national scale and blanket rollback, separates fact from interpretation in a real table, and uses fitness, precision, alignments, DFG edges, segments, and the resource network as mechanisms rather than decoration. The weighted segment check (−4.2 pp first-time-right, +2.9 pp complaints), the EUR 14→46 downstream residual, and the 640 / 340 case counts are arithmetically right. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The rollout gate treats baseline precision as “about 0.70.” Baseline precision is 0.79; 0.70 is baseline generalization. That is a concrete error in the threshold that is supposed to govern scale.

Resource advice contradicts itself. Survey utilization fell to 52% because surveys were skipped. The same plan reinstates surveys for apartments and old buildings and redeploys that slack onto second visits. Much of the slack disappears once the skip is reversed.

The value-of-information clock does not fit the outcomes it invokes. A week-12 decision cannot yet see 60-day cost, or complaint and tail results, for an M1 that starts in weeks 3–12. Backfilled M4 also measures original Redesign R, not the hybrid being scaled. M7 is oversold: a weak link to complaints would not justify resuming billing before activation, and option C already suspends that step.

The executive gate (“no worse than baseline”) does not match section 6 (concurrent control, with allowed slippage of −1 pp first-time-right, +0.5 pp complaints, and +2 days at p95). “Everything improved” for simple houses goes beyond the reported segment metrics. “Two specific components” omits the permit-check deviation the memo itself treats as causal. “No plausible result would reverse” simple-house scaling is too strong given four weeks of incomplete attributes and ownership never tested alone.
