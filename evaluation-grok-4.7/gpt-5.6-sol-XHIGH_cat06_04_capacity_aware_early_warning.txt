The answer is a serious portfolio policy: it separates untreated risk from an uncertain treatment effect, applies the stated calibration shifts with correct arithmetic, bars I-03 on stage grounds, treats I-05’s unchanged refresh differently from I-07’s new log, prices spillovers, and backs the choice with stop rules, fairness controls, and a real identification plan. The case-level direct-value figures and the optimistic, base, and adverse arithmetic check out. It is still short of a tight decision rule.

The allocation procedure does not govern the allocation. The lexicographic list puts every case with a positive lower-bound direct value ahead of deferral, which would fill the slots with I-01, I-02, I-04, I-05, I-07, I-09, and I-10. The write-up then defers most of those cases for time and option value. “Last safe start,” the gate that is supposed to justify that override, is never defined in hours of post-swarm resolution and validation, so I-07 can be ranked A1 while still in triage with 1.5 hours left. Three sequential 45-minute swarms are ordered without a clock: I-07’s deadline is about 13:30, I-09’s about 14:30, and I-02’s about 15:00, and serving the lowest-value case first compresses the other two. The portfolio utility \(U(A,s)\) is never computed for any alternative, including swapping I-07 for I-05 or releasing I-04 now. I-04 is simply named as the 15:00 beneficiary, by which time I-05 has about two hours left.

Several of the answer’s own safeguards are not used on this book. Ranking uses uncapped contractual value, while the fairness rule caps the ranking contribution at €50,000. The duty lane is not applied to either public-sector case. I-09 is tiered on its nominal effect range even though the calibration rule says that segment’s effect prior must include a near-zero branch. Full false-alarm burden is deducted in every score; for I-08 that choice flips the lower bound from positive to about −€450, and the \((1-p)B\) sensitivity that would show the flip is only mentioned. The section 1 spillover charge adds expected breach harm and a separate cost of the same 8–20 queue minutes, while section 3 subtracts only the breach term. I-06’s cell calls the treatment effect “near zero” when the actual problem is that the probability jump may be an imputation artifact.

Section 3 is auditable and appropriately low-confidence, including the €13k base-case break-even on \(\bar V_U\), but it evaluates only the chosen trio and leaves spillover harm symbolic. Sections 4–6 are concrete and cover the required failure modes; the 1.25 disparity trigger and the €50k cap are still unexplained parameters, and the experimentation quota for Non-Native-Language Support is not limited against the same congestion cap that is supposed to stop harmful slots.
