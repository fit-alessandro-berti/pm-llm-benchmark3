7.5

The write-up is complete, well structured, and strong on the hard qualitative points: flow is not conserved (+110 arrivals minus completions, with Friday–Saturday WIP drops), the outage and the recovery surge are separated from steady capacity, weekday/weekend mix and the 2026-04-20 case-mix break are not pooled away, and Little’s law is refused on the non-stationary windows. The alternative backlog-state segmentation is genuinely different, and the flat-FTE policy trap is the right Simpson-style warning. The Python block meets the requested calculations and correctly treats the aggregates as not an event log.

It is not nearly flawless. Several figures in the narrative do not match the data or the code that is supposed to reproduce them.

- R5 service summaries are wrong: median service is 35.0, not 34.9, and mean p90 is 83.0, not 83.3. Those figures are reused when R5 is compared with the intervention.
- Completion Fano factors disagree with sample variance. For the four hourly vectors they are about 1.70, 2.25, 0.98, and 0.78, not 1.65, 2.25, 1.07, and 0.90. Arrival Fanos are fine, so the burst-versus-smooth-completion story survives, but the printed numbers would not.
- Daily arrival dispersion is overstated: Fano is about 10.03 and CV about 0.334, not ≈10.4 and 0.34. Lag-1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals is about 0.41, not ≈0.48. The code would not print the prose values.
- “25% day lost (~4.5 effective FTE-days)” on 2026-04-15 inverts the arithmetic. A 180-minute stop on 6.0 FTE removes 1.5 FTE-days; 4.5 is the capacity still present.
- State A weekends are 36–53 arrivals, not 36–49. Post-shift weekday `peak_08_10_pct` reaches 39, not only 40–50.
- “ρ > 1” and “~95% utilization” are not computed. Section 1 correctly says mean service is unknown, then Section 7 states a utilization anyway.
- The one window marked `applicable` (Mon–Thu of week 1, W ≈ 2.36 open hours) is not stress-tested. With median service near 25 minutes and 6 FTE, face-value utilization is only moderate, while L ≈ 19.5 implies on the order of two hours of wait. That gap — heavy tail, shrinkage, multi-step occupancy, or WIP that is not a work-conserving queue — is left unresolved, so the positive Little’s-law claim is stronger than the aggregates support. The same magnitude problem is larger on the weekend window (W ≈ 3.97 open hours at 2.5 FTE).

Those are local errors, not a wrong operating story. Depth, regime design, and the refusal to treat recovery throughput as steady state keep this well above a merely adequate answer. The numerical and utilization inconsistencies are too numerous for a near-flawless score.
