The answer meets the required structure and is unusually careful on the hard parts: the flag is treated as a policy bundle, occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation, natural effects are rejected for the right reasons, and the stage table is numerically consistent with the timestamps, including `NA` for stages that did not occur.

The main weaknesses are in identification and the pilot, not in the arithmetic. E3 still defines the manual-review mediator distribution as “that under P1” while holding other flag effects at P0, which reintroduces the cross-world bundle problem the text claims to avoid. Separable effects, the framework best matched to splitting priority triage from mandatory review, are never addressed. The complexity rows are strongly flag-confounded (every low-complexity case is unflagged) without that being stated next to the table. The P2 pilot leaves scores between 0.85 and 0.90 outside both randomization and the mandatory-review exclusion, and it adds protected manual capacity, so the tested policy is no longer cleanly P2. “Compliance-adjusted ITT” conflates ITT with a compliance-adjusted estimand. In the code, the small-subgroup warning uses `n < 10` and therefore never fires for these flag groups or for the actually small cells (unflagged manual review n=4, escalation n=2), and the performance DFG is built from completion timestamps, so its edges mix queue and service time.
