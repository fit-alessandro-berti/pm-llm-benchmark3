6.5

The answer is structurally complete and often process-aware, but it is not nearly flawless. Several obligation, timing, and “next event” claims are overstated or internally inconsistent, so the probabilities look more disciplined than they are.

C6-A misapplies Rule 5. Two devices inside one complaint are not “two or more complaints,” and “similar” complaints are not stated to share a lot or supplier component. Supplier Analysis is therefore suspected, not mandatory. That error is repeated in the cross-case discussion. The primary state S3 and rank-1 Evidence Review Completed are sound, and concurrency with Technical Investigation or Regulatory Assessment is a fair alternative. “Normal feed quality guarantees” no delay is too absolute for a case created within 48 hours.

C6-B’s primary state and rank-1 review completion are right, and the contrast with C6-A is real. The alternative S5 is not supported: the answer itself says closure is blocked until review completes. Rank 2 then predicts that blocked closure via an invented auto-close step. Rank 3 marks Customer Evidence Requested as process-enabled even though it also cannot be logged before Evidence Review Completed. The same boolean is used in opposite ways for two events with the same predecessor. 0.80–0.94 is narrow given a shared four-item queue.

C6-C correctly treats the country rule as a reportability signal and correctly keeps disposition normatively disabled. The rank-1 interval 0.58–0.75 does not match the timing argument. “Approaching the minimum” of a 2–14 hour post-assignment range means completion now is early, not due, and 12 items are waiting. The alternative S5 describes a handler’s mistake, not a latent state in which mandatory Regulatory Assessment is already satisfied. “119 cases without regulatory assessment” overreads a direct TI-to-disposition edge; Regulatory Assessment may already have occurred before TI.

C6-D has the right core picture: high severity, injury, R-R2 in progress, migration delay. It then puts “evidence review completed or bypassed” under completed obligations because an attachment exists. Rule 7 forbids reading a missing event as non-occurrence; it does not establish completion. “Expected” Regulatory Assessment completion between 14:40 and 16:00 compresses a 2–14 hour range to its floor so the case can hit the 16:00 batch. Disposition still follows assessment (historical median about 2.2 hours) and notification follows disposition (median about 5.5 hours), so today’s batch is a weak reason for that forecast. Disposition is marked process-enabled here and process-disabled in C6-C on essentially the same Rule 4 logic. Rank 1 at 0.55–0.72 also underweights delayed log appearance on a migration-affected case.

C6-E is the cleanest case: S3 after the second receipt, lot-level Supplier Analysis correctly mandatory, fast close correctly excluded, and a low probability that an already-running supplier analysis finishes first. Rank 2 again treats a post-review evidence request as the next logged event and marks it process-enabled. Regulatory Assessment is omitted despite a six-complaint lot that is at least a plausible reportability signal.

Across cases, the directly-follows critique is strong: case-mix is not compliance, and TI/RA alternation is concurrency. Intervals sum coherently and are not fake point estimates. They are still overconfident where queue position, unobserved starts, and feed delay dominate, and ranks 2–3 often mix the immediate logged successor with a later enabled activity. Contradicting-evidence lists sometimes contain constraints that do not bear on the predicted events.
