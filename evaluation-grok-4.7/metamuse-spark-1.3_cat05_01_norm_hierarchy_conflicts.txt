6.4

The assessment is structurally complete and often careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Several classifications rest on unstated facts, over-read rules, or a decision method the cases themselves do not apply consistently.

The required architecture is present: assumptions are separated from facts, the hierarchy is issue-specific, the rule catalogue and defeasible logic are usable, all eight cases receive a permitted primary label, alternatives explain the premise that would flip them, and remediation is distinguished from logging defects and late proof of a timely act. Time-of-act analysis is strong on P-503, P-504, P-506, the P-505 acknowledgment, and the P-507 PDF. A6 is not treated as an automatic cure, and ECD-2 is not silently converted into an A7 exception.

The method then slips. Section 1 defines COMPLIANT to include a valid advance override and JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION as a covered deviation from baseline, without a stable boundary between them. P-505 is COMPLIANT, while P-507 and P-508 are JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, although all three depend on the same moves: CFO email D1/D2 standing in for a separate Finance approval, and B7/C4 treating Plant approval as an ordinary step. That email is simultaneously “low as norm” and sufficient to make an EUR 88,000 purchase cleanly compliant. A2 also requires an independent budget owner, and the stated facts say Incident Command members are not identified as budget owners. P-505 additionally has no shown four-hour authorization record and no PO time, yet those gaps are not treated as obstacles to COMPLIANT.

P-501 is the clearest misreading. B4 permits commitment before the PO in a qualifying emergency and, separately, permits an actively delegated Shift Lead to replace an absent Plant Manager. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate, so SHIFT-1 could not satisfy Plant approval. That does not make the 08:50 commitment itself unauthorized. B2 requires Plant and Finance approval at EUR 25,000 but does not state that either must precede commitment, and A2 does not apply at EUR 38,000. NONCOMPLIANT at high confidence therefore depends on an inference the catalogue admits is only purposive. The offered alternative, JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, is weaker than the textually available COMPLIANT reading that rejects that inference.

P-502 is assigned to 14 July so that the 15 July CFO email is inapplicable. No case date is given; “the same day” describes urgency, not a calendar date. The late Compliance clearance can still support NONCOMPLIANT, but the controlling interpretation and medium-high confidence treat a missing fact as established, and the missing-evidence cell omits the date.

P-507 assumes the seals are within “critical sterilization-line lots” without testing that scope. The eight-hour need also fails B4’s six-hour test, so the unapproved supplier and commit-before-PO result depend entirely on ECD-2. Scope failure would remove C1/C2 and make the absent Finance event much more serious. That reversing premise is not the alternative offered.

P-508 lists the four-hour recording rule as satisfied without a recording time, and D4’s reservation of essential integrity controls is underweighted for an EUR 155,000 purchase with no Finance Controller approval. P-503 asks for proof that PROC-3 was an Incident Command authorizer even though the case states that fact. The hierarchy table also reverses its own columns in places: cells headed “what it can override” sometimes describe what can defeat that source.

The result is a serious process analysis with visible uncertainty, but with too many internal conflicts and evidentiary overclaims for a high mark.
