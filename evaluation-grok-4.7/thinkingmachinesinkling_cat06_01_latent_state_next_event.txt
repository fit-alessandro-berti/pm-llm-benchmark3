6.5

The final JSON is structurally complete and often process-aware: primary states for C6-A–E are mostly right, A and B are correctly split by severity, injury, lot linkage, and duplicate similarity, and queues, concurrency, migration delay, and the worklist-versus-completion distinction are used rather than raw DFG successors. Intervals are ordered and not falsely precise.

Those strengths are undercut by material errors. On C6-D, Evidence Review is treated as an almost certain normative predecessor of Technical Investigation and is ranked as the next log event (0.30–0.55). The rules do not require an evidence cycle before investigation, and the DFG has a direct Triage → Technical Investigation path (126 cases). A source-system attachment does not prove Requested/Received/Review occurred; the 8–15% delay rate does not justify concentrating that much probability on one backfilled review event ahead of the Regulatory Assessment already opened at 12:40. Backfill order is also incoherent: the later review event outranks the missing earlier evidence events.

On C6-E, the contradicting evidence invents “four other complaints… across different lots.” The case is one device in a lot linked to six complaints, which is a same-lot Rule 5 trigger. That false fact contradicts the case’s own supporting evidence and weakens the Supplier Analysis obligation.

Ranks 2–3 repeatedly describe what follows Evidence Review rather than the next logged completion, with delay used as a patch. C6-B’s Close — No Safety Issue range (0.10–0.30) stacks an unfinished review, a missing completion, and a fast close. C6-C’s Supplier Analysis rank is speculative: different lots, no opened analysis, and a 6–36 h clock. `process_enabled` is true on every row, so the required split between an enabled in-progress task and the next visible event is only partly made in prose.
