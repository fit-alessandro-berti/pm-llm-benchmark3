The answer is a strong, decision-usable conformance analysis with the right normative skeleton: second-officer review stays mandatory, A and B are excluded as trace preconditions, re-screening and a SAR are not treated as substitutes, and P-901 is priced as a real spillover. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative slips sit exactly where the top-three ordering is decided.

The required six-part structure is complete, labels are valid, and the uniform-sum timing table in section 3 is internally correct (start at T+0/15/30/35/45 maps to about 1.0/0.75/0.38/0.25/0.03). The ~5% average for an S4 arrival uniform on T+35–65 matches that distribution. Section 4 is the best part: rules 6, 7, and 9 are treated as feasibility constraints, the observational 0.55–0.75 approval forecast is correctly refused as a stand-in for the review itself, and the evidence tests for F and G are specific.

The decision-relevant problems are these.

Section 5 equates unlike probabilities. P(decision by T+90 | start at T+35) ≈ 0.25 is then called an on-time probability and subtracted from rank 1’s 0.40–0.70 on-time execution probability to get an advantage of +0.15–0.45. On-time execution at a T+35 start is about 0.25 × 0.55–0.75 ≈ 0.14–0.19, so the advantage is larger. The same paragraph assigns a 0.4–0.6 execution probability to S4 alone if a clean re-screen shortens the review, with no shortened duration and no matching uplift for the S2 path, which also receives the re-screen. That biased sensitivity is what justifies “narrowly” flipping D-with-S4 above the interruption plan. The flip is not supported by the answer’s own timing model.

The stated SLA breach of 0.48–0.64 under continued normal processing is cited only as a transportability caveat. It is never reconciled with the claim that waiting for S4 misses T+90 about 95% of the time. Those figures can be reconciled only by an argument the answer does not make: either SLA is not the 90-minute customer deadline, or the 0.48–0.64 is a population forecast that is not conditional on this staffing state. As written, the baseline prediction and the structural ranking model disagree, and the ranking follows the model silently.

F’s tree equates “ultimate approval after conformant review” with “the payment was legitimate.” The table’s harm cell then adds the full €20k–€35k deadline value on top of an expectation that is already multiplied by 0.55–0.75. Forgone deadline value in the false-clear counterfactual has to stay inside that probability, and approval is not legitimacy. The proxy is flagged in the table and then dropped in the branch.

Section 4 says R17’s post-evidence Analyst Review must be recorded before S2 reviews. The rank-1 plan starts S2 at T+0 in parallel. A mandatory T+10 gate would move P(decision by T+90) off the T+0 row the benefit numbers use. The 0.75–0.95 friction discount may absorb it, but the sequence is self-contradictory.

Section 5’s best contingent policy — ask how much P-901 work remains, and if it is under about 15–20 minutes let S2 finish while the re-screen runs — is never promoted. Section 6 still opens by interrupting immediately. The answer therefore recommends a plan it has already argued is dominated under a cheap, immediately available observation.

Smaller faults accumulate. Benefit and harm cells mix euros, probabilities, and scenario ranges. Rank 2’s “€1k–€6k expected” matches the optimistic 0.05–0.25 scenario band, while the averaged hit rate is about 5% and the averaged deadline value is nearer €1k. The 1–3 percentage-point wrongful-approval reduction used to value the API call is stipulated. Branch probabilities for clean, inconclusive, and strengthened matches are illustrative and only loosely jointly consistent with 0.55–0.75. Rank 1’s harm cell leaves residual false-clear exposure qualitative beside monetized benefits, so the columns are not comparable across rows.

What keeps the score in the 7s is the part the task marks as decisive. A and B stay last and are never fallbacks. G stays disabled until both rule-10 conditions exist. E stays conformant and is correctly placed below a second review because it almost surely misses the deadline while preserving a later approval. The primary plan is still a conformant independent review by S2, with alternate screening in parallel, execution only after a recorded approval, and an explicit S4 fallback. Confidence is appropriately split: medium on the S2-versus-S4 implementation, high on excluding the unlawful shortcuts. The rerank triggers are observable. The normative conclusion is sound; the arithmetic that orders the conformant implementations is not reliable enough to treat the ranking as tight.
