The reconstruction is unusually careful and mostly numerically reliable: missing-start bounds, nominal WIP integrals (2085 and 1340 case-minutes), censoring, subperiod rates, busy-time ranges, and the four imputation scenarios all check out, and service is kept separate from sojourn and elapsed time. The bottleneck account correctly separates the arrival surge at S1 from S2’s longer service and later backlog, and the required case rows, formulas, and PM4Py projection are present.

It is not nearly flawless. The claim that 5-minute gaps occur in 19 of 21 observed same-resource transitions is false: 29 such gaps are observed, 26 of them are 5 minutes, and the exceptions are 0, 10, and 25 minutes. Post-warm-up S2 utilization is treated as robustly higher than S1, but the imputation ranges overlap. “Cohort wait ≥ 45–55 min” misuses a lower bound for an unproven projection (the defensible floor is about 39). Section 4 collapses S2’s central completed service mean of 37.5 into “central 38 [34–38].” Per-resource busy time is never reported, and several capacity and phase-boundary figures are rounded narratives rather than matched calculations. Those are enough to keep this below a near-perfect score.
