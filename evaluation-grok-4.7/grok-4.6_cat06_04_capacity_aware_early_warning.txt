6.5

The memo meets the assignment’s structural bar and the three headline tests: I-03 is ineligible because the swarm has no effect after Diagnosis Complete, I-05 is suppressed as an unchanged refresh while I-07 re-enters on a new log, and spillovers are carried into the scenario ledger. Calibration shifts point the right way, the one-slot reserve is explicit, and sections 5–6 name concrete safeguards and metrics. Those merits do not make the policy internally consistent.

The decision rule does not generate the allocation. \(S\) is denominated in euros, yet the published scores are an unauditable 40–81 index. On the rule’s own robust payoff, I-09 (lower-bound ARR \(0.18 \times €20{,}000 - 450 - 800 \approx €2{,}350\)) beats I-02 (\(\approx €950\) at the ARR floor, still below I-09 through the lower half) and both beat I-07 (\(\approx €500\)). The table ranks I-07 / I-02 / I-09 at 81 / 76 / 73. \(U\), \(C\), and \(\lambda Q\) have no magnitudes, so that order cannot be checked. The same scores are then ignored for the reserve: I-04 is scored 62 and I-01 58, but I-01 is next in line and I-04 is barred.

Eligibility is applied against the written gates. Gate 4 requires \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}}>0\) unless a “narrow urgency override” fires; that override is never defined. I-08’s floor is \(0.05 \times €100{,}000 - 450 - 5{,}000 = -€450\), with 8 h left in Triage, yet the row says “Eligible now? Yes.” I-09 is a second alert with \(\Delta p = 0.07 < 0.08\) and no stated new event, which section 4’s suppression rule blocks; it is still one of the three selected swarms. I-02 clears the 0.08 bar only by equality and is not examined. “Act now if Diagnosis Started” is waived for I-06 without being written into the rule.

The fairness claim that uncorrected calibration bias is “the opposite” of value bias is wrong. Both push the same way: Strategic Enterprise is over-predicted and high-value; SME is under-predicted and low-value. They compound. The ECE \(> 0.05\) rule-change trigger is also already inside the stated Strategic Enterprise bias band of 0.05–0.12, so it is not an operational tripwire unless it is defined on post-correction residuals, which it is not.

Section 3 is the best arithmetic, and several identities check: \(3 \times €450 = €1{,}350\), false-alarm sum \(€1{,}450\), base \(0.26+0.15+0.24=0.65\), and \(€1.3\mathrm{k}+€3.75\mathrm{k}+€4.8\mathrm{k}=€9.85\mathrm{k}\). The stress range does not. If a different case receives zero effect, \(0.20+0.18=0.38\), not “0.20–0.36.” Spillover euros assume an unstated €8–25k mix, then the base (€2–9k) and adverse (€6–13k) bands are not the corresponding products (\(0.54 \times €8\mathrm{k} \approx €4.3\mathrm{k}\), not €6k). Optimistic net breaches are \(0.47\)–\(0.66\), not \(0.5\)–\(0.7\).

Economically, one slot’s base spillover is about \(6 \times 0.02 = 0.12\) expected breaches. At the answer’s own €8–25k mix that is roughly €1.0–3.0k, against I-07’s midpoint direct remainder of about €800 after cost and false-alarm burden. Ranking that case first, and holding the three-case book when the base euro result is “breakeven to +€3k” at medium-low confidence and the adverse book is clearly negative, needs an explicit regret or fairness constraint with a cost. The memo asserts the constraint and does not price it. The per-case gate also omits the externality that the later table shows can wipe out the gains.

The result is a serious, directionally sound portfolio memo whose formal rule, scores, eligibility flags, and selected set do not describe the same policy.
