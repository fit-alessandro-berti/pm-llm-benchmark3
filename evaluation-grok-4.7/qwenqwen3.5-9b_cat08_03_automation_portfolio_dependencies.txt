4.5

The required seven-part structure is present, and the directional call not to lead with auto-registration is sound. The reasoning that supports that call is not. Several load-bearing claims are factually or logically wrong, and they survive into the final recommendation.

The matrix is not a ranking; it follows A1–A12 order. Dependencies are often reversed or impossible in the process order. Normalization is placed as a prerequisite of product/exposure classification, even though classification precedes document review and normalization. Collateral triage is made to depend on contract generation, which sits after the committee. The missing-document checklist is shown as depending on normalization in the matrix, then the dependency is flipped later. Entity-mismatch resolution is treated as depending on, and later as covered by, the contract-stage remediation workbench. Those are different activities, pools, and directly-follows loops.

Queueing is mishandled throughout. Case volume is exogenous, so auto-registration cannot “flood” the bank with extra work. Registration is itself back-office work, so automating it removes load from that pool and reveals later queues. The afternoon drop below 65% utilization is slack, not a capacity ceiling. Frequency times service time also shows normalization and contract preparation contain far more labor than registration, so the claim that the bottleneck portfolio saves less labor than auto-registration is false. That misses the point of the frequency warning.

The final portfolio (missing-document nudges, remediation workbench, statement normalization, contract generation) can be defended on rework, analyst time, rule checks, the four-module cap, and the two-deep-integration limit. The write-up does not defend it on those terms. It says this set reduces the longest waits, including valuation, while neither external valuation nor entity mismatch nor customer signing is in the portfolio. It credits the remediation workbench with the four-eyes violations, which occur between credit analysis and committee packaging, and it cites a packaging-module control that is not being implemented. Concurrency of KYC and financial review, the weekly high-exposure committee cadence, provisional signing, and the directly-follows loops are essentially unused. Cycle-time cuts of about 20%, 35%, and 15%, and an over-80% drop in control violations, are unsupported precision.

Exception handling and shifted demand stay thin: three compliance specialists, month-end legal peaks, relationship-manager resistance, and the likely move of the bottleneck onto judgment-heavy credit analysis are not seriously worked through.
