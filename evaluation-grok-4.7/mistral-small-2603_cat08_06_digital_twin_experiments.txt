4.5

The write-up covers every required section and restates the given calendars, case mix, and intervention list, but it is not a reliable simulation design. Control-flow semantics, PM4Py usage, waiting-versus-service identification, and output analysis are wrong in ways that would change the comparison.

The skeleton does not follow PM4Py. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe` or `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. Imports such as `from pm4py.algo.discovery import discover_variants, discover_dfg, discover_performance_dfg` are not the public API; variant counts are `get_variants`. The hand-rolled parser maps `+` to XOR and `X` to OR, uses a nonexistent `Operator.TAU`, and treats n-ary trees as `left_child`/`right_child`. The string actually executed drops the parallel block, so planning, fire, and environment become sequential and public notice is no longer optional. That contradicts the prose and removes the join that decision drafting is supposed to wait on. `Queue` is undefined, `sim_engine` is referenced but never bound, the resource check ignores `shift_pattern`, and experiments reuse one engine without a reset.

Parameter estimation is not grounded. `get_start_activities` does not estimate arrivals. Little’s law is written as `W = L /` with the arrival rate missing, and it is the wrong tool for splitting sojourn time. Missing start times are “inferred from queue lengths,” which are not in the log, and the code then fills service and waiting columns with one shared random lognormal and exponential. The lognormal comment is arithmetically wrong (`median = exp(2)`, not 3.5 hours), and the same draw is applied to every activity. Waiting time, when a start column exists, is the difference of successive starts, not start minus previous completion. Case arrival spacing is `diff()` on case-id order, not time order. “Simple with missing docs = 19%” is treated as the missing-document probability; that row is only one variant group. Performance DFGs are handled as if `len` were an edge count; `discover_dfg` returns a graph plus start and end maps.

Cross-case behavior and non-stationarity stay mostly narrative. The arrival class is an independent piecewise Poisson of timestamps, with no case type, architect burst, district, or related-case attributes. Objection handling alternates between “detected” and “proxy.” Building-area thresholds are silently inferred from case type, while prior violations (required for D2), environmental sensitivity, and the unobserved objection process are not uncertainty factors. Rejection contagion across an architect’s cases is invented. Truncation at p99 directly biases the tail KPIs used later. A 3-month steady-state warm-up is inappropriate for a seasonal, non-stationary horizon. “30 replications give a ±5% margin” cannot be known before the variance is seen. A random 20% case holdout leaks across time and across dependent cases; a temporal holdout is the relevant check. KS tests at this sample size will reject trivial differences.

The experiment matrix names D1–D10 and three stresses, but mechanisms are thin: D5’s “50% parallel” is undefined, D9 has no latent-objection measurement model, D2 omits the violation screen, and D10 cannot separate interactions. Stress-3 is another policy pair, and the capacity stress hits clerks and inspectors rather than the part-time fire, legal, and environmental pools. Decision rows are missing their inequalities, SLAs such as 60 days are unsupported, architect fairness is absent, and utilization of 70–85% can reject a policy that cuts delay. Matched streams and season-stratified effects are not part of the comparison rule. No optimality is claimed, but the design is still too incoherent to rank interventions.
