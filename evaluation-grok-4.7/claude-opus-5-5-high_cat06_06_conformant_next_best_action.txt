7.5

The answer is a serious conformance-first analysis: A and B are correctly excluded, screening is not treated as a substitute for independent review, SAR non-disclosure is respected, and P-901 is given a real spillover analysis with an explicit switch. It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative claims are internally inconsistent or stronger than the stated evidence, and those claims are what the top-rank comparison depends on.

The post-evidence reading of rule 5 is sound. The prefix stops at Evidence Validated, so the evidence cycle is not closed, and a fresh analyst review before Approve or Payment Executed follows the stated cycle. Rule 6 is also read correctly: score ≥ 70 and the unresolved fuzzy match are independent triggers, a 12-minute-old 72 does not clear the second trigger, and an internal override does not make a sanctions breach lawful. The lexicographic order — permitted set first, irreversible and asymmetric harm second, money third — matches the problem. Labels match the allowed set. F and G are properly conditional, and the reasons a favorable forecast cannot rehabilitate A or B are the right ones: the approval rate is conditional on a review that A skips, 25–45% of reviewed cases are not approved, the forecasts are observational, and B also falsifies rule 7.

The ranking arithmetic does not hold together.

- In the rank-1 trace, a clear-match execution at T+45–85 is described as on time in “most” sub-branches. The deadline is T+90, so that entire window is on time. The table’s execution window is T+50–100. Those cannot both be the continuation.
- The inconclusive branch executes at T+60–100 and is called on time about half the time. On a uniform reading of that interval, (90−60)/(100−60) = 0.75, not 0.5.
- Screening branches of 0.45–0.6, 0.3–0.4, and 0.05–0.15 sum to anywhere from 0.80 to 1.15. None of those probabilities is in the case. They are then multiplied into euro figures.
- The P1-over-P2 on-time gap is given as 0.2–0.45. The stated probabilities, 0.30–0.55 versus 0.03–0.15, differ by 0.15–0.52. The €4k–€16k gain is computed from the narrowed gap.
- “Remaining” P-884 deadline loss of €2k–€12k is not recoverable from P(approval) 0.55–0.7, P(on-time execution) 0.30–0.55, and a €20k–€35k value. P(approved and late) runs from about 0 to about 0.40, so the loss runs from about €0 to about €14k.
- F’s cell says the deadline value is lost with certainty. That value exists only if a legitimate execution would have occurred. A true rejection does not lose it, and the false-rejection customer harm is already counted as €8k–€60k.
- P2’s table harm includes part of the €15k–€80k prolonged-hold band. Section 3 then says a 30–60 minute overrun is a different and smaller harm. The table and the dominance argument are not using the same loss.

The P1-versus-P2 comparison is therefore weaker than the rank order suggests. Action C’s 35–75 minute review is the expedited interrupted review, not a measured duration for S4. Applying it to S4 produces a miss probability of about 0.75–0.97, which is then used to discard the case forecast of 0.48–0.64. That forecast is the observational baseline for “continue normal processing”; the prompt’s transport warning attaches to interventions. Treating the disagreement as evidence that the forecast does not transport, rather than as evidence that the handmade S4 clock is too pessimistic, inverts the caution. The spillover cap of about 0.35 has a related problem. A review that starts at T+10 and runs 75 minutes returns S2 at T+85, after P-901’s T+70 deadline. The stated +0.12–0.28 is for a 45-minute interruption only. The long tail can be much worse than 0.35 unless S4 actually takes P-901 over, which is not guaranteed inside the given 35–65 minute ETA. Section 3’s net advantage of about −€8k to +€13k already includes a negative region, and it rests on assuming P-901’s deadline value equals P-884’s. Calling the centre modestly positive, then ranking unconditional S2 interruption first, overstates how decisive that comparison is. The gate and the 0.55–0.65 confidence in section 6 partly repair this, but the rank-1 row does not.

Two dominance claims are too clean. Parallel screening does not have “no time cost”: the same trace lets an inconclusive result send the case into an evidence hold after P-901 has already been interrupted. P1/P2 do not dominate F in every state where the documented rejection threshold is actually met; they dominate F only while that threshold remains unlikely, which is the right factual judgment but not dominance.

Smaller conformance nits do not change the bottom of the table. E is marked conformant now, while every other decision is barred until R17 closes the evidence cycle. The 5–10 minutes assigned to that closure is taken from Action B’s timing, not from the case. “Release S2 early if both triggers clear” and “let S2 finish because 78/72 already fired” are both offered; rule 6 is about the current score and the current match, not a latch.

What remains reliable is the qualitative control conclusion: do not execute, do not self-certify, do not spend the window on an override that is not enabled, keep one alternate screen in parallel with an independent second review, and do not interrupt P-901 until its stake, pause point, and S4’s ETA are checked. The implementation ranking of that second review is less reliable than the answer’s table presents, because the probabilities, clocks, and spillover tail do not support a stable euro advantage for interrupting S2.
