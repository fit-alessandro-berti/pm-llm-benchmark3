6.2

The report is broad and often numerically careful, but it is not a reliable advisory. Fabricated measurements, contradictory classifications, and a recovery model that does not actually enforce its own rules keep it well short of a high score.

The timeline uses the real phase metrics, fitness scores, and most DFG counts correctly, including the hospital-versus-pharmacy split, the T4 median-versus-p90 divergence, and the audit tail. That spine is then corrupted. The comparison table invents hospital and pharmacy fill rates for T0 (~98% and ~96%), T3 (94.0% and 78.0%), and T4 (97.5% and 92.0%), then computes “relative shifts” from those inventions. Only the T2 fill rates were given. T3 is also narrated as fact—inventory collisions, paper-versus-screen work—without a DFG or other evidence. “Corrected 1,400” reservations is false: 1,400 entered reconciliation, 900 continued to pick, and 300 were cancelled. Manual pick-list volume is understated as 3,920 by dropping the 420 backlog releases. Fold-change and percent-change are paired carelessly (traceability “+1,088% (11.9x)”).

The resilience distinction is directionally right for C and G, and for refusing to treat throughput or later remediation as compliance. It then breaks. A is “true resilience” in the taxonomy and only “modify” in the table, even though the observed fast lane included authorization bypass. D is placed in the high-integrity resilience cell and later called highly noncompliant after an invented 12-hour mark. F is both a cold-chain bypass and an ambiguous control. L is put in the high-throughput quadrant and treated as rubber-stamping or fabrication; the log supports a post-close remediation queue, not fabricated temperatures or signatures. Phase-level 9.5% traceability exceptions are attributed to single activities, and later claimed to be eliminated by dock scanners, although unscanned dispatch inflow is 3,250 of 5,600 cases, not 9.5%.

The redesign names every required element and correctly splits administrative sync from quality deviations. The formal process tree still sequences deferred orders into fulfillment, so load-shedding does not actually stop the flow. The priority index has four weights, but the stated coefficients are 0.45, 0.30, and 0.10 and do not define those weights. Recall-sensitive product is a named risk class and then disappears from the control matrix. “Air-gapped” laptops that “automatically synchronize daily” are mutually exclusive, and the cyber event is redesigned as a generic warehouse outage: snapshot trust, clean recovery, and cutover integrity are unexamined. Targets of 100% cold-chain and dual-control ignore a baseline that already missed 0.4% and 0.2%. Fill rate is defined as on-time delivery. The fairness ratio demands near-parity between pharmacy and hospital fill, which contradicts the hospital-first policy, and wholesale is omitted. Backlog age is labeled lagging. Throughput and cycle time, the clearest recovery signals in the data, are not indicators.

Recommendations are concrete and include effort and residual risk, but several benefits are unsupported (“15 min to 10 sec”; scanners “eliminating the 9.5%”), and dock interlocks are scored as immediate and low effort. The management statement does say what to accept and what not to accept. The only evidence allowed to change it is an external legal waiver, and it again assumes retrospective fabrication rather than stating what operational evidence would justify a narrower control.
