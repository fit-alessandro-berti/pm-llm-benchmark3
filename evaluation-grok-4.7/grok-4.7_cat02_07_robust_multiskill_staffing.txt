6.5

The write-up is complete, auditable on the fluid load math, and specific about dispatch rules, but load-bearing contradictions and a simulation that cannot run keep it well short of a high score.

Section 2’s central arithmetic holds. Mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74), U4 diagnostic means (29.60, 37.14, 44.50), 42 arrivals, touch counts (18.09 / 24.30 / 12.85), 2720.6 base-minutes against 2490 contracted minutes, the ordinary-low corner near 1811, the ordinary-high corner near 2963, and the six block loads (445, 586, 455, 651, 392, 192) all match the tables. The multiplier-1 gap of about 230 minutes is a real lower bound, because every listed multiplier is at least 1. U2’s renormalized electrical touches (4.20→5.00 and 6.20→7.32), Farah’s morning software ceiling (240/1.40≈171 versus about 154), the 16:00–16:30 software hole, and the post-18:00 mechanical hole are also right. The feasible-low versus infeasible-midpoint split is the correct uncertainty to lead with.

The recommendation does not stay consistent with that evidence or with its own policy text.

- Policy A bars Chen from software and sends the whole morning software queue to Farah at 1.40. Policy C then says its homes match A, so Farah is again the software home whenever Emil is off shift. Emil and Farah barely overlap, so “P1 software prefers Emil, then Chen, then Farah when all three are free” almost never applies. Chen reaches software only through a 20-minute overflow gate, and that gate closes if his electrical head has waited more than 10 minutes. The stated reason C beats A is therefore largely the defect C reintroduces.
- Section 5 says C is the only base policy whose P1 band still touches 90%. Section 4 gives pooling 82–93% and hybrid 88–96%. Both touch 90%, and the author’s own ±10–15 point judgment band makes every stressed comparison overlap. Those SLA ranges are not outputs of the model; they are the inputs to the ranking.
- The 10-minute speed-loss rule does not do what Section 3 says. Extra electrical time on Emil is (1.30−1.00)×32.82≈9.8 minutes, so he sits inside the band. Ada at 1.35 and Farah at 1.40 sit outside. The prose lists Emil with the people the band stops.
- A5 treats “P1 may preempt only a P3” and “new overtime work only if P1” as process facts. The prompt allows preemption of any lower-priority diagnostic. Policy C’s 16:00 bridge then assigns Chen’s overtime to open P1 or P2 work. Mode 1 later preempts P2 as well.
- “Morning P1 volume roughly doubles” under U1 is the wrong factor. Morning P1 expectation goes from about 2.05 to about 5.66, roughly 2.8×. Full-day P1 goes to about 9.1, roughly 1.7×.
- The block table is headed as a bound. It is arrival-tagged fluid load. The U1 “morning deficit of about 670 minutes” counts diagnostics and validation that will not all occur before noon.

Section 8 encodes the right tables, shared service draws, explicit handover, cross-team, setup, coordination, preemption, breaks, and the U5 blackout, and the PM4Py discussion of parallel overlap is correct: summed service can exceed sojourn, so case-level waiting can be zero or negative and must not be used as stage queue time. The discrete-event run never reaches those prints. `Item` stores priority only on `item.case`, while `priority_rank`, `try_preempt`, `overflow_ok`, `donor_ok`, and `reserved_hold` read `item.priority`. The first dispatch raises `AttributeError`. Seeds also use salted `hash(scenario)`, `ot_day0` is incremented by zero, and parallel partners silently skip Farah. A scaffold would have been acceptable if those mechanisms were named as omitted. This is presented as the estimator that replaces Section 4.

The monitoring attributes, handover-versus-service split, shock-specific bottleneck signatures, shadow-then-day-randomized pilot, and P1 abort rule are concrete. Section 9 then compares `planned_service_min` with `actual_service_min` as a mixture check; the former is the pre-multiplier draw and the latter is split, post-multiplier occupancy, so that ratio mixes speed, routing, and the service distribution.

Highest-weight strengths are the auditable offered-load bounds, shift-level skill holes, and the refusal to crown a policy on base mean delay. Highest-weight defects are the Farah/Chen contradiction, SLA ranges that do not separate the policies under the stated uncertainty, and required comparison code that crashes before producing any policy metric.
