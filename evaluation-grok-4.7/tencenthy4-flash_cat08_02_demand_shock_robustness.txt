7.5

The redesign is well structured and meets the required seven-section format. It uses the mix shift correctly: routine volume is essentially flat at about 1,403 versus 1,386 cases per week, while urgent rises from 414 to 957 and complex-panel from 299 to 660. Scenarios cover mix, assay failure, validator absence, courier disruption, and front-end defects. Downstream overload is discussed for each bundle, case types are not treated as simultaneously prioritizable, and the dashboard separates leading signals from lagging outcomes.

Several analytical problems keep it well short of nearly flawless. “Urgent roughly doubles” is inaccurate: 957/414 is 2.3 times. Repeat-after-failure is a separate case class with 70h/145h medians, but the priority rule ties it to an “original Urgent SLA,” which misassigns a non-urgent class to the 18-hour contract. “Technical validation to release falls to 4,050” compares an 8-week count with a 3-week count; weekly direct releases are about 1,460 versus 1,350, so the real change is share, not a fall. The same unequal-window comparison weakens the hold and recollection-cancel claims.

The observed urgent-bypass drop from 74% to 58% is barely used, despite being direct evidence that peak load crowds out the bypass. Reserving urgent work for M1 does not confront M2’s faster runtime. Warning bands are miscalibrated to the given baseline: normal recollection is already about 4.2%, and normal urgent p90 is 19h, so the stated green bands would flag the stable period. The median of 17h is also left grammatically attached to the claim of being above the 18-hour threshold.
