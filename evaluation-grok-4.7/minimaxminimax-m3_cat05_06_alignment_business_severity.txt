6.4

The case narratives correctly separate noise, scope mismatch, missing evidence, and true control failure, and they rightly treat zero-cost auto-approvals and silent tau moves as different things. That core is undermined by factual and implementation errors.

CL-604 misreads the alignment. `Pay Claim` is an `L` move followed by `M(Pay Claim)`, not a synchronous move, and `Fraud Screen` is model-only. The claim that fraud screen and manual approval were both present is false; manual approval is in the log after payment, and fraud screen is absent. CL-610 calls P2 a violation “in spirit,” but a recent bank change literally requires fraud screen and manual approval. CL-606 is scored 4 while the same answer says a valid external report would make it a logging gap and not a control breach. Pattern E folds CL-602 and CL-604 into synchronous auto-approval failures and asserts that two of the four fully synchronous bypasses are large high-risk profiles; the evidence only identifies CL-609 and CL-610 inside that set of four.

The required code does not run: synchronous moves are 2-tuples, then unpacked as 3-tuples. Policy flags mark P1/P2 from amount, risk, and bank-change conditions alone, so compliant manual-path cases such as CL-606, CL-608, and CL-611 are flagged as violations. Amount, risk, and business severity never appear as columns, and the `risk_signal` sort therefore mis-orders the portfolio. The severity scale also has factors but no anchors for levels 1–5, and the remediation table does not give investigation and recovery of the irreversible payments a real priority.
