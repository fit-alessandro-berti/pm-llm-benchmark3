The memo hits every required section, stays inside the four-module and two-integration constraints, and correctly refuses to lead with registration. Rankings, DFG loop counts, critical-path share, control rates, and resource pools match the evidence, and the A5–A6 pairing is the right answer to analyst overload. The recommendation is coherent: A4 for the specialist/critical-path exception, A5 for the document loop, A6 so cleaner inflow does not swamp the nine analysts, and A12 so the wave strengthens controls before any signing or activation bot.

It is not nearly flawless. Downstream bottleneck risk for A1 is rated “Very high” even though registration is an 8-minute task with a 1.5-hour wait, so automating it cannot flood KYC or credit the way the rating implies. A4 is treated as a major cycle-time lever because of the 31-hour wait, without separating specialist service time from handover latency, registry delay, or batching. With only 1,750 cases at 55 minutes, the three specialists’ touch time is too small to explain that wait, so an assistive tool can easily miss the delay it is credited with removing. The same service-versus-queue slip appears when A6 is said to cut the 20-hour pre-normalization wait and when the compliance-first portfolio is said to cut the 4-hour KYC queue.

Classification is described as determining KYC, collateral, committee, and four-eyes paths, then dropped from both the balanced portfolio’s sacrifice statement and the final tradeoff. Leaving an 8% misroute rate untouched while automating later activities is a real portfolio hole, not a minor omission. Selected edges that matter for controls are also underused: classify splits 7,250/3,940 toward KYC versus document review, and 380 mismatch cases go straight to contract preparation. Committee volume (3,900) versus contract preparation (7,600) is never reconciled, so decision-to-contract control design stays too generic for non-committee products.

Rollout order is thoughtful but sits awkwardly against the matrix. A5 is ranked first and called a prerequisite for A6, yet broad A5 production is last, and the two deep core slots go to A12 and A4 while A6, which needs credit-model interfaces, stays in shadow until after month six. A12’s stated feeds (KYC, committee, contract, role, signing, remediation) also look wider than one integration slot. Gates are mostly “meet risk-owner thresholds,” so several phase exits are not falsifiable. None of this breaks the constraints or invents metrics; it keeps an otherwise strong process argument short of being tight.
