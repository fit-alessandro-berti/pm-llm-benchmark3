4.2

The regime story is directionally right, but the numerical layer is not trustworthy, and several queueing definitions are wrong.

Section 2’s fine split is mostly faithful to the table: baseline weekdays near balance, 15 Apr as an outage (105 in, 58 out, WIP 47), 16–17 Apr as recovery overshoot rather than a high-demand steady state, the 20 Apr mix/service/WIP shift, weekends as a separate load, and 29 Apr onward as extra staff with completions above arrivals. The rollback-staffing example is the right kind of pooled-versus-segmented policy. That is the part that holds.

The aggregates do not. All 28 days sum to 2519 arrivals and 2409 completions (means about 90.0 and 86.0), not 104.1 and 101.8. There are 20 weekdays and 8 weekend days, not 21 and 7. Mean WIP is about 29.9, not 31.6; mean staff is about 5.14 FTE, not 5.7. Pooled arrival variance is several times 245, so the Fano factor is not 2.35. The unweighted “typical day ≈ 70 arrivals” figure is not the mean of the series. Section 4’s overload block (n=6, 113.4 arrivals, WIP 45.8 for “20–28 Apr”) does not match either the six-day or the seven-day weekday slice; the seven weekdays average 114.0 arrivals and about 44.3 WIP. The low-complex and high-complex rows in the alternative segmentation do not match the date ranges printed next to them. Pre/post figures then appear as 113.4 vs 101, later as 105.7 vs 100.9 and 108.6 vs 114.2. Those cannot all be the same windows.

The pasted “program output” is not what the script would print. `DataFrame.mean()` on the embedded series cannot yield 104.1 or Fano 2.35. Little’s law in code does `W_days * 24` after dividing by a per-opening-day rate, which doubles the operating-hour sojourn relative to the 2.4 h / 5.4 h / 3.6 h narrative. Baseline “weekend” indices include Friday 17 Apr. `scipy.stats` is imported and never used, yet the text reports p-values. “PELT would likely identify…” is not the rolling-mean heuristic in the code. The outage hourly vector is both in the prompt and in the code, then section 5.5 says hourly outage detail is unavailable.

Definitions slip in ways the prompt forbids. Arrivals are not “cases starting service.” Active handling excludes waiting, so the claim that queue delay inflated the service median is outside the given measurement. Mean sojourn minus median service is not mean wait; p90 values (about 80–88 min in the overload week versus medians of 34–36) show how large that substitution is. The +10.4 min versus “only +4 min from mix” split states no simple-versus-complex gap and is not identified.

The largest process-level miss is conservation. From 20–28 Apr, arrivals exceed completions by about 106 cases, and the weekday-only gap is still on the order of 90, while snapshot WIP only moves from the high 30s into the 50s (day-to-day WIP changes of a few cases against daily nets of +10 to +16). Net flow is not “12.4 cases of backlog per day,” and “≈74 cases of backlog” cannot sit beside a mean WIP of about 45. Post-intervention is the window where the identity roughly closes (nets of about −5, −6, −7 versus WIP 43→36→32). Using the overload gap as capacity shortfall, then as Little’s-law throughput, overstates what these aggregates identify. Hour labels are shifted (the 20 and the 34 are the 10:00–11:00 bins; “peak 04–05” is not an open hour). The representative days also disagree with `peak_08_10_pct` (7 Apr: 5+12 is 17% of 101, not 31%), which is never flagged.

Uncertainty is labeled “very high” on conflicting point estimates. Dollar figures ($60/hour, $100–140k, SLA fines) and appointment quotas (30%, 50%) are not in the data and are not treated as assumptions. The extra summary then upgrades the matrix’s “approximate only” baseline verdict and calls the mix shift the established root cause, despite the stated coding-change ambiguity.
