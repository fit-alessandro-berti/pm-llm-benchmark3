The submission covers every required heading and all 15 cases, and several primary calls are sound: SUP-701 compliant, SUP-702 and SUP-704 justified in-window exceptions, SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-714 real noncompliance, SUP-712 a segregation failure despite an M0-fitting trace, SUP-709 a scope problem, and SUP-711 a duplicate log. Those merits do not offset decisive failures of model semantics, authority, evidence, and the required revision.

Stipulated facts are mixed with conclusions, and some “facts” are wrong or overstated. CS-9’s quality rule is tied to onboarding, not restated as an unconditional rule for every activation. FD-7 caps spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; the brief instead says there is “no sanction” on spend above that amount. PD-3’s automated master-data validation requirement never appears. The compliance observer’s split position—monitored exception versus no approval to weaken CS-9—is not treated as a live authority dispute. Next-business-day quality review is plainly inside “up to two business days,” yet it is listed as borderline. SUP-708’s intent is treated as the dispute, although absence of any pilot or emergency is already established. The burden section collapses written, time-bounded exceptions into a test of whether a deviation was “minor,” and it never separates control-flow fitness, CS-9 compliance, exception authority, and logging sufficiency.

The prosecution is not the strongest available case. It calls SUP-703 an ineligible low-risk German supplier; the trace is a high-risk new foreign supplier, falsely flagged as pilot, with a EUR 180,000 first purchase. It barely argues SUP-705, misses the 2% to 29% notification-missingness jump, and explains SUP-708 as M0 failing to require both checks. In a strict partial order, sanctions and quality are both members and therefore both required; the edge from `checks` to `decision` is the join. SUP-708 is a reordering and early activation, not a skipped node that the model permits. SUP-712 and SUP-715 are merged. CS-9 requires different humans; disclosure is the spouse case, and a disclosure record would not cure one person approving through two aliases. The matrix then has prosecution call the in-window FD-7 case noncompliant, which is a straw claim. Prosecution also does not clearly concede the genuine defects: no RMI loop, no reactivation or rejected-lead scope, no resource constraint in M0, CRM observability, duplicate register events, and retroactive label mapping.

The defense does not hold the required line on real risk. It calls SUP-708 a model artifact, attributes SUP-703 to the operator being unable to see eligibility in M0, treats post-expiry SUP-705 as legally permissible, and invents a reactivation annex that no source contains. Same-person approval is redescribed as delegation. SUP-702’s missing `Notify_Requester`, contrasted with a stable `notification_id`, is never used; SUP-706’s mutable link entered two days later is waved through as having no compliance impact.

The matrix and judgment then contradict themselves. SUP-705 is classified `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` while the reversal condition says only a written extension would legitimize it. The directive expired automatically on 2026-07-12; the 2026-07-13 case is unauthorized continuation. SUP-706 is a collection failure, so `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE`, not a model or scope defect. SUP-707’s dominant fact is that M0 cannot express a loop while checks and approval still preceded activation; `MIXED` avoids that conclusion. System percentages are false on the answer’s own labels: 5 of 15 are compliant or justified and 5 of 15 are noncompliant, which is not 57% and 43%. Fifteen curated cases are also not a compliance rate. The stated dominant cause—that M0’s missing overlays drove noncompliance—erases operator misconduct in SUP-703 and SUP-708. Confidence values such as 1.0 and 0.98 have no method, and SUP-713’s mapping was approved a week after the case.

The revision recognizes that amount, identity, eligibility, and authority do not belong only in a process model, then puts those conditions back into invalid POWL operators (`Operator.AND`, `Operator.IF`, `EligibilityCondition`, `TimeCondition`). The loop is a cycle on a strict partial order, which is acyclic by definition, and `Request_More_Info` is made silent. Withdrawals, required in the scope rules, are absent. The conflict expression is garbled and would treat disclosure as a substitute for distinct humans.

The Python block is not executable and does not meet the minimum model. `directed_petrify` is not a PM4Py module; `Operator` and `SilentTransition` are used without imports; string nodes and `Transition` objects are mixed; `Transition.add_edge` is not an API; emergency and pilot nodes are wired to activities that are not their children; the pilot condition sits after the decision. `checks.add_edge(sanctions, quality)` removes the concurrency the task required. Labels are arbitrarily renamed. The policy table omits jurisdiction, master-data validation, critical-relief scope, daily monitoring, and bitemporal effective versus record time. Actor-alias snapshots and immutable notification linkage are named piecemeal, not specified tightly enough to prevent a repeat of SUP-706, SUP-711, and SUP-712.
