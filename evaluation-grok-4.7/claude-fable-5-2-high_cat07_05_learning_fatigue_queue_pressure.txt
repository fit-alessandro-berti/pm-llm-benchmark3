The answer meets every structural constraint, respects the causal cautions, and is numerically careful on most direct calculations (WIP gradients, shift-end rank order, experience splits, ~1.8 rework-to-error ratio, cumulative recorded time). The two-regime account is the right shape. It is not nearly flawless.

The central quantitative claim is biased. Volume-weighting both high-WIP cells yields ~7% only because that mix is ~22% high-complexity. The 76+ bin has mean complexity 0.43, so the relevant cell is high-WIP/low-complexity at 5.8% and 9.4 min, not ~7% and ~11 min. The residual versus 7.9% and 10.8 min is material; calling it small overstates how fully queue pressure closes the late-shift gap. “8–10 minutes at every WIP level” is also high: the high-WIP gap is 7.4 min.

The equal proportional speed-up (26/28/30%) drops Night staff, whose later-versus-first-20 drop is ~15%, and “later” service time pools low and high WIP. That weakens the claim that the speed-up is one shared non-learning effect.

The analysis section would mislead if run as specified. `batched` is defined from short `svc_min` and then used to explain `svc_min`. The readout asserts a 7-day rework effect the models never estimate. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` is not `rework_flag`. The “low WIP” DFG keeps every case that is not high-WIP and selects at case level, so medium-WIP and mixed cases contaminate it. `early` and `pos10` are entered together, but the readout treats `early` as the warm-up effect. The error model omits the resource fixed effects the design section says are essential. The direct-standardization multiply will not align cleanly on those indexes.

Smaller slips: Day-shift overlap is described as hours 6–8 rather than about 5–8, and any later human Risk Classification is coded as a bot “correction.”
