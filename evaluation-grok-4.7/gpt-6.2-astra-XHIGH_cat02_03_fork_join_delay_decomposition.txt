8.2

The delay algebra, case table, margins, and counterfactual arithmetic are coherent and, on the quantities checked, correct. The answer uses max-based join logic, refuses to treat A’s 50-minute service as dominance, and shows that a noncritical cut has zero case-level effect. It is not nearly flawless.

Section 1 is the right decomposition. \(T_c=U_c+L_c+D_c+V_c\) with \(L_c=\max_i X_{ci}\) avoids double counting, and the F02 overlap (C’s 35 minutes and A’s 10 minutes share the last 10, while their union already sits inside the 60-minute stage) is the right demonstration that summed synchronization waits are not elapsed time. The identity \(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}=L_c\) for every branch, hence \(3L_c\) if all three paths are added, is also correct. Service is properly labeled as start-to-end elapsed time, not proven touch time.

Section 2 matches the log. Spot checks of F02, F04, F07, and F11, plus the column totals, agree: upstream mean \(235/12=19.58\), fork–join sum \(732\) so mean \(61\), downstream sum \(176\) so mean \(14.\overline{6}\), flow sum \(1383\) so mean \(115.25\). Critical labels are right (A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, B on the other eight). No ties exist in the raw ends. The worked minutes follow one critical path rather than summing synchronization.

Section 3’s ranking is justified. B’s completion-offset mean \(56.67\), P90 \(75\), \(8/12\) last finishes, and margin sum \(120\) (mean \(10\), max \(25\)) beat A’s constant offset of \(50\) and margin sum \(15\), and C’s margin sum \(12\). Nearest-rank P90 as the 11th of 12 is stated and applied consistently. The 5-minute leads on F01, F08, and F10, and A being second in all ten non-A-critical cases, are real. Calling A a 50-minute floor is the right caveat.

Section 4’s direct gains are right: A−20% yields only \(5+10=15\) join minutes; B queue −50% yields \(92.5\), with zeros on F01, F04, F08, and F11; F05 is correctly clipped at 10 by A; F02 and F06 become ties. The two-server replay also checks out, including the single 5-minute wait for F11 and \(171\) minutes versus the log. The fixed-start extreme (zero flow gain) versus preserved downstream delay versus re-dispatch is the right uncertainty range. The spillover claim is real: under B plus the same dispatcher, F11 still gains 5 minutes with an unchanged join, and \(171+97.5=268.5\) is the incremental total, not a double count. P90 flow of 100 under that joint case matches the resulting case flows.

The main analytical miss is `final_resource`. Assignment is strict alternation, and several starts equal the previous same-resource completion (F05=F03, F09=F07, F11=F09, F08=F06). About 73 of the 176 downstream minutes are consistent with waiting for that dedicated server; the rest is slack after that server is already free. The pooled model is labeled as a counterfactual, and F01’s unexplained 8 minutes is noted, but the \(171\)-minute and “only five minutes of contention” results are never split into removing dedicated routing versus deleting post-availability slack. That is supplied evidence on the exact question of queue versus release policy.

Section 6’s pandas decomposition would reproduce the tables, and tie handling is deterministic. The required PM4Py tail is not runnable as written: `pm4py.get_service_time` requires an activity and returns one scalar, while the call omits the activity and passes the result to `pd.Series` as if it were a per-activity map. The crash is after the printed pandas results, so it does not poison the reported numbers, but it fails an explicit reproducibility requirement. The P90 helper is correct for \(n=12\) and not a reliable nearest-rank implementation in general. “Fractional minutes come from the percentage interventions” is slightly loose: A−20% of 50 is an integer; only the B cut produces halves.

Section 5’s two pilots follow the evidence, and the limits section correctly blocks causal, preemption, and steady-state claims. “Machine counts cannot be recovered” is a little absolute: overlaps identify a concurrency lower bound, not the capacity. Upstream \(r_c-a_c\) is also left as one block, which the timestamps force, but that non-identifiability is not stated as sharply as the others.
