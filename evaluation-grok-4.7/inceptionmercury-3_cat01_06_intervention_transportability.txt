6.0

The recommendation is the right one, and the main transport claim is sound: Site A’s before/after gap is confounded, Document Check cannot start before statutory originals, and only a no-originals slice can carry identity/document overlap. That core is undermined by incorrect table cells, incoherent effect signs, a median calculation that does not match the case mix, a pilot that cannot be run as specified, and POWL code that does not implement the required model.

Process comparison. Several required before/after cells are false. Case mix, tail, rework, overlap, and capacity put both periods in “Site A before” and mark “Site A after” as “Same.” After is not the same: high-complexity is 40%, p90 is 78h, rework is 12.4%, overlap is 71%, and volume/FTE are 5,200 and 20. Queue discipline places the huddles in the before state; they were a co-intervention with the change. The design consequences in those rows are still mostly right.

Transport graph. The required nodes and `S_` selection nodes are present, and the prose correctly treats huddles, the API upgrade, mix shift, and added FTE as co-interventions. The explanation is about 130 words, short of the required 180–280. `API_latency -> identity_capacity` misstates the mechanism: latency changes identity service time, not reviewer capacity. Direct treatment arrows to cycle time, rework, and appeal sit beside the overlap path without separating mediation from residual confounding.

Evidence and modifiers. The transfer table correctly refuses to treat the 9h median drop as an effect and bounds a pure service-time overlap by about 3.4h. Modifier rows cover the required constraints and are operationally usable. Originals-required cases are a weak negative control: identity capacity and the assessment queue are shared, so a C1 speedup can spill into them.

Scenarios. The sign of “change” is not defined and is not consistent. Optimistic and digital-only cells are unsigned improvements; bottleneck p90, IT-lock, and legal cells use “+” for worsening. The legal median “0h to +2h” conflicts with the closing claim that the median improves while the tail worsens. `0.38 × 3.4h ≈ 1.3h` is a mean-style product applied to the median. No-originals cases are the faster 38% because they avoid the 18h originals delay, so they sit left of the median; speeding them up can leave the all-case median nearly unmoved. Section 5’s 1–2h digital-only figure and Section 9’s 1–2h C1 plus 0.5–1.5h all-case figure do not describe the same estimand. The 26.5% supplement share (360/1,360) is correct. Identity-with-originals overlap is already permitted at B, so part of the “optimistic transfer” is not Site A’s intervention. IT-lock confidence 70 on a +5h to +12h p90 range is high for a speculative no-op-plus-overhead story.

Intervention specification. Cohort rules, statutory gates, role separation, and logging are coherent. The partial-order list is not a partial order: `LegalReview < RequestSupplement < DocumentCheck < Assessment < LegalReview` is a cycle. The concurrency marks in that list are blank. Copying Site A’s WIP cap of six onto pools of 4, 7, 3, and 2 is untested.

Pilot. Week-level assignment, a fidelity-versus-mechanism contrast, and pre-specified harm limits are the right ideas. The design contradicts itself: four 3-week blocks are 12 weeks, while the duration row says an 8-week experiment. At about 131 cases per week, no-originals × low-complexity × non-legal volume is on the order of 15 cases per week, so eight weeks yield roughly 120 C1 cases, not 400 per version and not 400–600 available. A single shared staff pool cannot randomize stepped-wedge order. The case-lock fix is site-wide, so V2 cannot be turned on for some weeks and off for others. “Protect operations and patients” invents a patient population. The claim that a null result can be assigned cleanly to mechanism or implementation is stronger than this design supports.

PM4Py. The Site A process trees and `convert_to_petri_net` calls match the stated models. The Site B code does not follow the POWL API. The class is `OperatorPOWL`, not `OperatorPowl`. `Transition` takes a label, not `(name, label=...)`. `Operator` has `XOR` and `LOOP`, not `SEQUENCE`, `PARALLEL`, or `REDO`. There is no `add_child` or `add_order`. No `StrictPartialOrder` is constructed, so the required order edges are only a Python list assigned to `seq.order`, which a converter will not enforce. The skip is not a `SilentTransition`. Nested parallel-plus-XOR would have been a reasonable control flow, and the comments on data guards, capacity, locks, and durations are apt, but this code does not build that net.

Recommendation and assumptions. Conditional go is justified. The text correctly limits transfer to independence where both checks can legally start, keeps the first wave to no-originals, low-complexity, non-legal work, and separates expansion, modification, and stop rules. The eight assumptions are testable. Thresholds still disagree with the pilot: expansion at 200–300 C1 cases versus a stated minimum of 400 per version.

The answer would be a strong advisory memo if the comparison table, effect signs, median math, pilot arithmetic, and executable POWL model were repaired. As written, those are material failures on the criteria that ask for quantitative transport and model fidelity.
