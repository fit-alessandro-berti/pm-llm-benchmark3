6.4

The final write-up is a real regime analysis with the required eight sections, a usable Little’s Law matrix, and the right refusal to treat 15 April or 16–17 April throughput as a steady high-capacity regime. It is not reliable enough to score higher: several headline quantities contradict the dates they are attached to, the code does not reproduce the prose, and one burstiness claim has the wrong sign.

The primary segmentation leaves 11–12 April and 18–19 April in no regime. Regime D is bounded as Mon–Sun 20–28 April, but the reported ~113 arrivals/day, ~100 completions/day, ~43 WIP, and 6.0 FTE are weekday-only figures. Over those nine days the means are about 99 arrivals, 87 completions, 39 WIP, and 5.2 FTE. Regime E is split by weekday/weekend; D is not.

Pooled comparisons use a bad arrival total. Mean arrivals are 2519/28 = 90.0, not 90.7 or 91; weekday arrivals are 108, not 109; mean staff is 144/28 = 5.14, not 5.3. The case-weighted complex share is about 33.8%, not 37–38%, so weighting does not move the pooled mix by ~5 points. The 22% versus ~44% regime contrast is still valid; the weighting claim is not.

The hourly reading is wrong in a way that matters. On 21 April the 34-arrival bin is 10:00–11:00, and 08:00–09:59 is 3+8 arrivals (about 10%), not a dominant 08–10 peak. That vector also cannot produce the stated 47% `peak_08_10_pct`. For the other representative days, that column matches 09:00–11:00 rather than 08:00–09:59. Section 5 never audits this, and it describes 21 April lag-1 dependence as high and positive (“spike carries”). The spikes revert: lag-1 autocorrelation is near zero to slightly negative. Overdispersion was mistaken for persistence. The outage-day Fano/CV pair (~3.0 / ~0.58) is also low; sample moments are about 4.1 and 0.69. The 16–17 April flow-time illustration uses only 16 April (WIP 52, 112 arrivals) while labeling a two-day window.

The Python heuristic is a different segmentation from sections 2 and 6. Days with `complex_pct` ≤ 35 before 29 April, including the recovery days and early weekends, become `"baseline"`, and the Little’s Law printout for that bucket will not be the stated ~145 minutes. The rolling-difference detector peaks after 20 April (around 22 April), while the printed text says it finds 20 April. Baseline sojourn of ~2.4 h versus a ~25 min service median is also hard to reconcile with calling that regime a “moderate” queue.

Format, outage-versus-recovery handling, and the pooled staffing policy that fails once mix and backlog are separated are the parts that hold.
