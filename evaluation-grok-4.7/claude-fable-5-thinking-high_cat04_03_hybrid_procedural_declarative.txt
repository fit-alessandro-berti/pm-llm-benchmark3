6.5

The hybrid design is coherent on the R5/R6 conflict, DECLARE template direction, temporal arithmetic, and segregation of duties, but the log skeleton is not executable as written and several verdicts and authority claims do not follow from the model.

The empty `directly_follows` set is a whitelist error. In PM4Py log-skeleton conformance, that set is the permitted direct-succession relation: a consecutive pair absent from the set is a deviation, and the check is on by default. An empty set therefore rejects every trace with two or more events. T1–T4, T6, and T7 are not log-skeleton passes, and using this skeleton as the first deployment screen would fail the conforming cases along with T8. The other skeleton relations are mostly well oriented — `(a, b)` in `always_before` as “`a` is preceded by `b`”, `(a, b)` in `always_after` as “`a` is followed by `b`” — but they never get a chance to be the result.

T5’s temporal cell is wrong. `Activate Vendor@9` → `Release First Payment@80` is 71 hours, \(z \approx 0.03\), the same conforming pair scored as `pass` on T1. An unanswered clarification produces no interval; it does not suppress a realized pair. The cell should be `pass`. The case still fails overall on R3.

The procedural model and R11 disagree, and the write-up does not treat that as a contradiction. `Verify Bank Account` stays in the backbone alphabet, and the tree allows it once inside the initial parallel block. A second verification, which R11 requires before a later payment, survives projection and fails alignment. The skeleton simultaneously allows frequencies `{1, 2, 3}`. Alphabet projection cannot drop the re-verification without dropping the original check.

Section 2 correctly makes the process tree authoritative for the “accepted ⇒ exactly one of Manager Approval and Compliance Approval” half of R2, which the DECLARE rules never encode. Section 10 then names DECLARE authoritative for R1–R5, which would pass an approval-free activation. Those two deployment rules cannot both be followed.

Smaller defects: the T7 overall cell is `pass with warning`, outside the required vocabulary (`warning` belongs in the temporal cell, or the overall cell stays `pass` under the stated “flag, do not reject” rule). Under Resolution A the emergency checker is described as inapplicable, yet T6’s resource/data cell is still `policy-dependent`. The R4/R6 routine treats “first opinion timestamp ≤ activation” as compliant even when that opinion precedes `Sanctions Escalation`, so a later opinion is never required. Assumption 7 breaks timestamp ties by log order; the code compares timestamps only. R3’s “response before any final outcome” is not what `altsuccession` checks; the tree’s fixed loop position is what actually enforces it, so calling DECLARE authoritative for R3 overstates that layer.

What holds up: Resolution A versus B is explicit, with T6 reported both ways and the missing `emergency_authorized` attribute refusing to become a pass. `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is the right strict approximation and is correctly distinguished from `noncoexistence`; T7 is a pass of that rule and a temporal warning. The z-scores that are computed (71 h, 26 h, 17 h, 34 h, 2 h) match \(z = |\Delta - \mu| / \sigma\) and \(\zeta = 2\), including the near-miss at \(z \approx 1.94\). T4 is correctly invisible to every activity-only layer. The seconds conversion, vacuity discussion, and the warning that support/confidence are not policy are sound. The process-tree construction itself (parallel checks, `*(τ, request→receive)`, XOR of reject versus one approval route, close after either terminal) matches the backbone for the eight given projections.
