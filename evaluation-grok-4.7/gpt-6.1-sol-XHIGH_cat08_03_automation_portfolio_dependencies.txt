The answer is a strong process-level portfolio argument, and it stays faithful to the artifacts: frequencies are not treated as annual volumes or as a ranking key, waiting-time medians are not summed, KYC and financial review are kept concurrent, and the credit-analysis → packaging (2,700) and valuation-wait → packaging (1,980) edges are correctly refused as disjoint case counts. A1 is deferred because an 8-minute, 1.5-hour queue would accelerate inflow onto analysts and specialists; A11 is refused until controls are reliable; A5 is paired with A6 so cleaner documents do not simply move the constraint onto the nine analysts. Resource limits, month-end four-eyes breaches, provisional signing, audit trails, and harder residual work are all carried into the rollout gates.

The main flaw is scope inflation of A12. A remediation workbench for a roughly 650-case contract loop is turned into the preventive hard gate for KYC-before-signing, High-exposure four-eyes at analysis versus packaging, and approval-to-contract matching, and is then credited with cutting all of those violation rates. Those controls sit on different roles and at different points in the flow. Detection-and-hold is justified; treating one medium-feasibility module as the whole control architecture is not.

Two related weaknesses follow. “Deep integration with core banking” is redefined so that A12 and A6 consume the two first-half slots, even though registration, contract, signing status, and activation are the modules that actually write core-banking state. The numeric cap is respected only under that broader definition. In the matrix, A7 is rated high because it “addresses” the 58-hour external wait, while the recommendation later, and more accurately, says routing cannot control that wait. A4’s “high” process benefit is similarly stronger than an assistant with limited coverage can deliver when the 31-hour delay is a queue on three specialists, two of whom already concentrate the handoffs.

The directly-follows anomaly Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation (380), plus the gap between 1,750 mismatch cases and the 1,160 + 380 outgoing edges, is never examined, despite being the path most relevant to clearance before contract. A2’s 8% misroute rate, which selects collateral, committee, and four-eyes, stays ninth without a capacity argument for leaving it unassisted for a year. None of these are invented figures or a frequency-only ranking, but they are enough to keep the recommendation short of nearly flawless.
