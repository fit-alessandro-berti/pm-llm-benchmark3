5.0

The policy covers the required decisions and gets several qualitative calls right, but its central value calculation is wrong, the impact table does not audit, and those errors change both the ranking story and the adverse-case conclusion.

The deployable rule multiplies adjusted breach probability by the stated absolute risk reduction and by value. Absolute risk reduction is already the change in breach probability, so expected direct benefit is risk reduction times value of avoiding breach, minus swarm cost, minus false-alarm burden. Multiplying by \(p\) again treats the effect as a relative reduction. That identity is applied in the I-04, I-07, I-08, and scenario calculations, and it is what turns I-08’s midpoint from a clearly positive value into the reported −€1,950. False-alarm burden is also subtracted inside the value term and again inside cost, while the spillover proxy is \(0.02\) times the treated case’s own value rather than the harm to untreated diagnosis-stage cases. The stated score is never computed; the tiers are a separate judgment. The uncertainty trigger is reversed: effect ranges narrower than \(0.10\) are labeled high uncertainty.

Stage eligibility is handled correctly. I-03 is refused because Diagnosis Complete is outside the swarm’s effective window. I-05 and I-07 are separated on new evidence, which is the right distinction. The I-05 write-up then invents a prior swarm decision and a 6.2-hour deadline; the case has had no swarm, and the deadline is 5.0 hours. I-10 is SME and is analyzed as Public Sector, so the SME upward adjustment is omitted. In the spillover cohort, I-06 is placed in Triage although it is Diagnosis Started.

Capacity accounting does not match the rule. Four slots, one regulatory reserve, and a top-three allocation become two interventions, a reserve called Slot 3, and a possible Slot 4. I-01 and I-04 are economically eligible on a corrected midpoint, so the selected pair is not absurd, but the timing is. I-04 has 10 hours of slack. I-02 is already in Diagnosis Started with 3 hours and a robust lower-bound benefit, and the text’s own lower-bound figure of €450 is not the arithmetic of either formula (\(0.08 \times 25{,}000 - 1{,}050 = 950\)). I-07 has 1.5 hours, so “wait” expires the intervention window. No order is set for two specialists whose ordinary diagnosis work stops during a swarm.

The scenario table contradicts itself. Upper bounds \(0.35 + 0.28 = 0.63\), so “1.0–1.3 breaches avoided” is impossible, and the euro lines imply roughly \(0.3\) expected breaches under the \(p \times\) effect formula. Optimistic I-01 does not equal \(0.61 \times 9{,}000 \times 0.32 - 250\). Adverse I-04 is reported as −€400 from \(0.40 \times 60{,}000 \times 0.10 - 2{,}000\), which is \(+400\) before the separate €450 labor cost. Net ranges do not subtract the stated cost and spillover. Spillover uses made-up average values and unexplained 60% and 50% factors, and the optimistic row introduces a positive knowledge spillover the problem does not support. Optimistic, base, and adverse “credences” of 60–70%, 75–85%, and 40–55% cannot be scenario probabilities. The adverse requirement that at least one selected case gets little or no benefit is narrated, then priced at the ordinary lower bound.

Later sections are more useful and still loose. Cool-down, evidence, and re-entry rules are concrete. Fairness covers value starvation, non-native uncertainty, segment bias, label feedback, and spillover incidence, with quotas, manual review, and a holdout. The holdout contrast between cases that actually received a swarm and randomized controls is not an intention-to-treat effect, and a deterministic score rule leaves the inverse-probability scheme without overlap. Monitoring is broad, but the value-quartile targets disagree across tables (15% versus 12% for the lowest quartile), utilization targets condemn the recommended reserve rate, and biweekly segment AUC movement is not identifiable at this case volume. Two extra closing sections also break the six-section format.

The memo identifies the right constraints. Its numbers and the portfolio timing built on them are not reliable enough to act on.
