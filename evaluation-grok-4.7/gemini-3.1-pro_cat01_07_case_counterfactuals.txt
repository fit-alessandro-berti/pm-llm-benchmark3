The write-up matches the required skeleton, but several propagation claims contradict the snapshots, and the summary ranges contradict the case derivations.

The partial order is only a sketch. Conditionals sit inside the poset, and the constraint block never states that a preliminary compliance screen cannot discharge the final review, that a post-rework compliance step may be reaffirmed rather than repeated, or how escalation advice is ordered. Classifying K-104 as routing understates it: the intervention adds work and reserves the single compliance reviewer. K-411 is not timing-only in a harmless sense; it changes when the assessment queue is joined.

K-104 is the clearest temporal failure. A 1–3 hour saving on the final review does not let C1 start when assessment ends on Tuesday evening. At Monday 08:00, C1 already has about 20 hours of work, urgent cases are added by 16:00, and the document check still gates assessment at roughly Tuesday 15:10–19:10. A slot reserved at triage is likely to fall before final assessment, so it can support only an incomplete preliminary pass. The Monday backlog is treated as a hypothetical that would reverse the result, even though it is observed. The table’s 6–24 hour gain excludes the derivation’s own no-gain case. Four-eyes is asserted, not checked against A1 versus C1.

K-219 is more plausible and still loose. “Standard but handwritten” is given D2’s complex band of 8–14 hours after D1 finished the factual check in 4 hours. The path is a single happy-path sequence, while the table stops at +10 hours and the derivation at +10–24 hours. Earlier document readiness is not traced through A2’s Tuesday queue or M1’s next-morning signing, which is what actually separates Tuesday 22:30 from Wednesday 08:30.

K-337 correctly treats the intervention as a decision-policy change and keeps compliance before decision. “Immediately to C1 on Friday, saving 5 days” still ignores Thursday’s remaining compliance queue and the fact that the evidence is not complete, so the 3–6 hour guidance does not apply. Most of the calendar gain is an external weekend, yet the scale-up calls the policy a net capacity injection. Rule 6 is unresolved: compliance can normally issue another information request, and the forced-decision rule has to override that explicitly.

K-411 copies I2’s 11:30 start onto D2 even though Tuesday 08:00 shows D2 at about 4 hours and I2 at about 1 hour. A document task opened at 09:35 would start nearer noon and finish around 16:30, not 16:00. The table then says 2–4 hours saved while the best case says about 18. Those cannot both be the expected range. The reversal test that I2 and D2 might be the same person contradicts the resource model.

The capacity table says K-104 shrinks the assessment queue because final compliance is faster. Assessment is upstream of C1, so that mechanism is backwards. At weekly scale, C1 reservation is rightly called harmful, but 100 handwritten cases do not “balance” D1 and D2: one generalist cannot absorb that load. Suppressing rework on 100 high-risk cases still leaves 100 final reviews for the same single reviewer. K-411’s D2 queue is said to shrink even though the same job is inserted earlier.

The robustness ranking is a judgment, but “high” robustness for K-411 overstates a benefit the answer itself says the assessment bottleneck can erase. The pilots have the required rows; the sample sizes and 30% stop rule are unsupported, and an uncapped 50% assignment to D2 can recreate the spillover being measured. The synthesis is inside the word limit and correctly flags K-337 as the dangerous speed-up and K-104 as poorly transportable. It still claims the missing queue-discipline and rework data would “definitively prove” the end-to-end effect.
