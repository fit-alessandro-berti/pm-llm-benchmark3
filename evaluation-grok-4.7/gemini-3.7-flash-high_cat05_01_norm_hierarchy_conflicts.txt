6.4

The assessment is organized, covers every case, and reaches several defensible outcomes, but it is not reliable on the dimensions this exercise tests: time, scope, evidence, and consistency with its own rules. Confidence is uniformly High or Very High, and INDETERMINATE is never used.

The method overstates the chosen hierarchy. GPIP-4 A1 and A3 are treated as absolute, with no defeater, while A7 leaves open whether a COO and Incident Commander directive can count as an exception and while the same answer later lets an uncontrolled CFO email displace A2. The source table then says GPIP-4 “cannot override” A1, A3, and A7, which is a category error: those clauses are GPIP-4. Numeric weights (1.0 / 0.85 / 0.75 / 0.6) reintroduce a total order that conflicts with the issue-specific ranking. Assumption 3 converts the CFO’s authority to interpret finance controls into a power to replace both the Finance Controller and the independent budget owner, including Plant approval, despite D4 and the email’s status outside the controlled repository.

The rule catalogue does not match the case table. R-B2 and R-B3 are cited in the cases and are not rows. R-B6 is formalized with no defeater, then ignored for every case except P-501. The pseudo-logic requires compliance clearance as a condition of any unapproved supplier, collapsing the approved-register rule into A3’s “new supplier” trigger, and the `unless` clause has no clear scope. R-A1 also treats any Incident Command authorizer as “the final business approver,” which A1 does not say and which the charter does not support.

Case conclusions diverge from the facts and from the stated rules.

- P-501 is a plausible NONCOMPLIANT call on delegation, because the Plant Manager was reachable and did not formally delegate. Calling B2 a missed pre-commitment control is an added timing requirement. The same rule, R-B4, is both satisfied and breached. The facts already state that formal delegation did not occur, yet the missing-evidence cell still seeks a delegation record.
- P-502’s NONCOMPLIANT result is justified by post-commitment compliance clearance for a new supplier. The A2 discussion depends on an invented date: the case gives no calendar day, but the answer asserts the CFO email had not yet been sent. Actor identity is unknown, yet R-A1 is marked satisfied.
- P-503 should be noncompliant on the amount-based clearance gap and on PROC-3 approving a purchase PROC-3 requested and committed. The stated controlling ground is instead that three days of stock makes the purchase out of scope, which is one contested reading of an undefined phrase, held at Very High confidence. The row still credits R-C1, an ECD-2 permission that cannot apply if the purchase is out of scope. D1 is withheld here and applied to later in-window purchases.
- P-504 is plainly outside sterilization scope and outside the six-hour emergency definition. Listing R-A3 and R-C3 as breaches is wrong: EUR 24,000 and “unapproved” do not establish a new supplier, and an out-of-scope purchase does not fail an in-scope Incident Command rule.
- P-505 correctly prefers the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment over the 18:06 ERP stamp. COMPLIANT with High confidence does not follow. There is no budget-owner or Plant approval; finance approval is at 19:00, after expiry; and A3 is marked satisfied although EUR 88,000 from an approved supplier does not trigger A3. The section 5 alternative, “ERP is the only record,” is weaker than the live A2 objection, and it imports the July 14 recovery lag into July 18.
- P-506 is a sound NONCOMPLIANT result: the directive had expired, the extension is unwritten, and Plant approval follows commitment. SoD is still marked satisfied without actor evidence.
- P-507’s signed PDF at 12:40 can meet the four-hour recording rule, but the answer assigns that delay to ERP outage ON-44. ON-44 was the July 14 ERP outage; this is a July 17 document-system queue. “Missing evidence: none” is untenable. The committer is unidentified, and R-B6 was never checked.
- P-508 has clean segregation, timely compliance clearance, and timely legal clearance, and it fits the affected line. High-confidence JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION still skips the cap in D1. Above EUR 150,000, joint Incident Command authorization is no longer “sufficient”; “legal as well” can mean legal in addition to the ordinary A2 approvals. That reading is acknowledged and then called practically untenable.

P-505 is COMPLIANT, while P-507 and P-508 are JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, although all three depend on the same CFO waiver and on imperfect records. The opening facts also upgrade “ERP clock may have been nine minutes fast” into a documented skew.

Section 6 mis-sorts the cases. P-502’s late compliance clearance is a substantive pre-commitment failure, but it is used as the example of late evidence of a timely act. P-501 is treated as an inability to prove a delegation the scenario says did not occur. Remediation for P-507 again cites ON-44. Ratification is recommended for P-506 without keeping A6’s limit on cure distinct from cleanup.

The draft A8 clause covers authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, records, expiry, and a post-incident audit. It contradicts itself: clause 1.b.ii forbids any pre-commitment waiver of compliance clearance, and clause 4 allows commitment before screening. The matrix still fails to say what happens to the independent budget owner above and below EUR 150,000, which is the ambiguity that drives P-505 and P-508.
