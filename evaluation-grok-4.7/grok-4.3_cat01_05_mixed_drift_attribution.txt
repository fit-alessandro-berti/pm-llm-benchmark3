5.5

The write-up keeps the right multi-mechanism outline: March is mainly semantic, later rework and delay are real, and logging, supplier behavior, staffing, priority, and seasonality should not be collapsed into one change point. That outline is undermined by unmet format constraints, unreliable quantities, over-strong causal claims, and a monitoring/code design that would not reproduce or detect the series as written.

Section 2 is about 150 words and section 9 about 195, both below the required floors. Section 3 names one logging change point where two are required. Part A never embeds the monthly or DFG tables, so it is not reproducible. Validation is a comment. The change-point rule marks every post-February month as logging because the raw-to-mapped gap stays above 0.3, and it never flags May, June, or October. `conformance_diagnostics_token_based_replay` is called without a net, markings, or a discovered model. The distance in code is an unscaled Euclidean norm, which contradicts the min-max method claimed in prose.

The baseline-change table does not match a January baseline, a February baseline, or a January–February mean. Against January, March median cycle time moves by −0.3 hour, not +0.3; July rework is +17 percentage points, not +16; case-count deltas match February for March through October and January for December. The DFG section says six relations after min-max scaling; the fragment has seven, the constant edge makes min-max undefined, and “omitted edges are zero” conflicts with “omitted edges are excluded.” Reported distances (March 0.04, May 0.19, July 0.26, October 0.31, December 0.18) do not follow from that method. Unscaled Euclidean distance divided by 100 is about 0.017, 0.191, 0.277, 0.292, and 0.171. The cycle-time split into 55–65% queueing, 20–25% rework, 10–15% mix, and 5–8% service has no window and no arithmetic trace. Only Assess service time is observed.

Supplier-Z concentration of rework is stated as fact in sections 2 and 9. The supplied evidence is only a simultaneous jump in supplier share and aggregate rework; supplier cases were already more complex, and the revision clause admits stratification was never seen. June–August is called the performance peak, while November has the higher median (49.0 versus 45.0) and p90 (121 versus 110). October’s +12 complexity points and +13 senior-assessment points do not match September to October (+15 and +15). Senior assessment stays near 4–9 per 100 cases from January through September, then jumps to 22 in October, so the July priority rule does not account for that jump. The historically inconsistent Senior Assessment label is never used as a logging alternative. September’s supplier recovery is absent from the timeline and the attribution, and June’s missing-resource increase is treated as a staffing signature.

The discrimination table places migrated-activity missingness under behavioral drift for the resource cut, and the staffing row gives no positive signatures. The primary “month × supplier” population is a diagnostic cut of a non-random, region-confounded subset. Service time has no monitor. Several thresholds (`distinct labels > 10`, missingness `> 3%`, duplicates `> 2%`, rework `> 18`, cycle time `> 38`) remain breached for the entire post-change regime, while mapped fitness `< 0.85` never occurs in these data (the low is 0.86). A centered 12-month baseline uses future observations and therefore cannot be an online reference. The two-month confirmation rule also delays detection of a sudden schema break.

The useful core is the separation of the March fitness cliff from post-May mapped rework and queue growth, plus rules that sometimes require stratum-specific or rework-stable evidence. The numbers, the PM4Py plan, and several mechanism timings are not reliable enough to act on without rebuilding.
