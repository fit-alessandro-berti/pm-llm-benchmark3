5.5

The write-up has the required skeleton and a sound strategic split: keep a fast path for low-risk claims, and do not treat the slow reviewed path as universally better. Redesigns A–C are different in intent (segment routing, evidence capture, parallel risk checks), sacrifices and failure modes are stated, and the comparison and pilot sections exist. That is not enough to carry the grade.

Evidence handling is unreliable. Auto-eligibility volume (3,600; 72%) is treated as the fast-approval path; fast approval is 2,550 from the screen plus 240 from fraud review. The R3 claim is inverted and mis-counted: `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` is 310 at mean 40h, while 890 and mean 34h belong to `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`. Sending reopened cases into manual review is what R3 requires, not proof that R3 failed. The portal effect is overstated: 30–45% applies to repeated evidence requests, not to all evidence-request occurrences. “26%” is the share of evidence cases with at least two requests, not 26% of cases.

The main trade-off is also misstated. Premium and standard high-value reviewed paths are much slower (median about 96h and 120h versus about 20h and 28h on the fast path). A rough complaint adjustment does not reverse that. Calling Redesign A faster for high-value contradicts the performance data and the segment outcomes. The given capacity constraints are largely unused: manual-review inflow up 15–35%, the queue cliff above 82% utilization, two fraud specialists, and a pre-dispatch gate that adds 6–18 hours. A and C both add a part-match gate, and A explicitly leans on B, so the designs are less independent than claimed. Fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces, low discovered-model precision, and the larger `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` edge (260; long tail tied to wrong parts) are not used with process-mining care. Edge counts are treated as case counts.

The experiment plan has order, metrics, thresholds, and a downstream-shift check, but several stop/go numbers are unanchored, and piloting parallel risk control only on low-risk claims does not test the compliance mechanism. Dominance language is firmer than the data allow.
