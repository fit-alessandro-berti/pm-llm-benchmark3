5.5

The required eight-part structure is present, the selected conservative bundle is appropriately non-unique, and the main constraints are respected: no invented objects or events, no claim that 130+100 proves the invoice or payment links, and ev11 is not forced onto one delivery. The candidate table, unresolved list, and over-repair warnings are usable.

The reasoning is still not reliable enough for a high score. ORD-A is a split shipment (IT-A1 via PK-7/DEL-X and IT-A2 via PK-8), so a confirmation attached only to ORD-A cannot identify one delivery; that structural fact is never used. PAY-1 is 100, matching ORD-B, while it is already tied to an invoice that officially bills only ORD-A; that tension is missed. The invoice residual is exactly 100, not merely “numerically close,” and the fee alternative is therefore weaker than the write-up says. Several O2O rows are justified by “supports” the E2O inference they were copied from, which is the circular confirmation the audit later claims to avoid. The parallel O2O `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is never accepted, left unresolved, or rejected, even though Bundle A creates an E2O/O2O asymmetry with DEL-X.

Section 6 has concrete process-mining errors. Pack Item and Dispatch Package are not on the Order OC-DFG or an Order flattening unless those events actually relate to an order; they do not. `ocel_flattening` yields one trace per object and duplicates shared events; it does not merge ORD-A and ORD-B. An E2O-only addition does not create a new object-type edge, and DEL-Y is a delivery, not a package. Package, invoice, and payment flattenings are largely absent, and event frequency, unique-object frequency, and total-object frequency are not separated. The validation block only comments the required discovery and flattening calls and does not check O2O referential integrity.
