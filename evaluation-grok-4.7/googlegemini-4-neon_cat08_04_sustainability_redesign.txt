The answer meets the required structure and is unusually well grounded in the DFG, segment table, path variants, and SP1/SP2/Q1/R1. Aggregate checks such as MTO’s roughly 87% energy share, Critical-downtime’s roughly 41% transport share, 620 packaging-rework entries, and the 4.4% cancellation rate are sound, and the package correctly refuses blanket air/van bans while retaining rework, returns, obsolescence, slack, and resilience.

It is not nearly flawless. The effects table treats non-equivalent path averages as intervention results: 1.8→0.7 kg scrap and 32 kWh “avoided” confound machine choice with rework and heat treatment; 0.25→0.07 kg compares emergency production with the stock/ground exception; 18.5→3.6 is a stock-path contrast, not the Standard-stock service-contract segment effect (segment average 7.8). “Halves the MTO rework rate” overgeneralizes the CNC_B 18% versus CNC_A 9% evidence. S5’s 6–12 h and S8’s 1–4 h are invented precision. S2’s energy effect is shown only as a decrease even though wait caps can shrink batches, and S10’s large late-rate gain is partly created by loosening the promise. The 8% critical-van path (about 960 orders) is used beside 620 dedicated-van transitions without reconciliation, the 270 unclosed cases are illustrated with an unrelated rework imbalance (470 versus 390), and the validation gate ambiguously attaches the 4.4% cancellation baseline to MTO lateness while allowing Regular lateness to rise from 7% to 8%.
