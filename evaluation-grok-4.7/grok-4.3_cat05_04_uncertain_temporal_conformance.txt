The write-up keeps the seven required headings and a few robust calls, but the interval arithmetic, several classifications, the sensitivity results, and the code are not reliable enough to treat the findings as an analysis.

## 1. Normalization Assumptions
The offset definition is unusable as written (“true time = recorded time offset”). The later phrase “bounds are subtracted” points the right way, and HUB’s combined ±1.5 s is the one bound that is actually derived: ±0.5 s resolution, then offset in [−1 s, +1 s]. Elsewhere the rules are not applied. Floor-to-minute windows are collapsed toward the displayed label, OPS-BER’s asymmetric [−90 s, +120 s] is treated like ±120 s, SENSOR’s ±5 s clock bound is dropped after rounding, and “EDT = UTC4” omits the sign even though the table later uses UTC−4. T-404 is anchored to the wrong event: the 18–25 minute record is investigation duration ending at Investigation_Complete (10:35), not time before Containment_Complete. The mobile note adds [0, +300 s] to the recorded clock and never states whether that delay moves occurrence time or only entry time. Late entry, read naturally, places occurrence earlier than the typed value; that choice changes T-406’s containment gap. Ingestion order is announced for the DST fold and then not used.

## 2. UTC Event-Time Intervals
HUB rows are acceptable closed windows of ±1.5 s, though they are not written as explicit ISO ranges. The other rows are short or non-explicit.

- OPS-BER 10:14 CEST is clock [08:14:00, 08:15:00) UTC, then true time [08:12:00, 08:16:30), not [08:12:00, 08:16:00]. The same 30 s shortfall hits T-402 (upper end 09:20:30, not 09:20:00) and the weak-offset T-409 window (upper end 16:17:00, not 16:16:00).
- SENSOR 08:51:10 is rounding [08:51:05, 08:51:15] and then offset ±5 s, so about [08:51:00, 08:51:20], not [08:51:05, 08:51:15].
- VENDOR-NY 04:00 EDT floors to [08:00:00, 08:01:00) UTC and offset ±180 s, so about [07:57:00, 08:04:00), not [08:00:00, 08:03:00].
- T-404’s start is Investigation_Complete minus [18, 25] min, about [10:10:01.5, 10:16:58.5]. The printed [10:30:00, 10:37:00] is 18–25 minutes before containment. Investigation_Complete is missing from the table.
- T-405 is not an ISO union. Each ambiguous local minute is two folds, each widened by the minute floor and ±60 s. The printed ranges are the raw folded minutes with the offset removed, and the date is truncated to “2025-10-”.
- MOBILE-LON 13:14 BST is 12:14 UTC, nearest-minute preimage ±30 s, then ±300 s. The printed [12:09:00, 12:24:00] is the point stamp plus a +0–5 min shift and ±5 min, with rounding omitted.
- T-407 never constructs an Alert_Received window from sent time plus latency [0, 20] min.

## 3. Certain/Possible/Indeterminate Findings
The table covers a small slice of the testable pairs. S2/S3 and the four profile pairs are mostly absent. Two retained rows contradict their own endpoints.

T-401 S1 is given as 720–960 s against a 900 s maximum and labeled CERTAIN_PASS, “fully inside.” 960 > 900, so that row is POSSIBLE_VIOLATION on the answer’s own interval. The correct OPS window pushes the upper end still higher (about 16.5 min). S2’s pass is directionally right; S3 (about 24 h) is omitted but would pass.

The zeta = 1 band for (600, 180) is written [180, 1020]. Average ± 1 standard deviation is [420, 780]. The printed band is what you get by using (mean − sd) as the half-width. Under [420, 780], the answer’s own 720–960 s only partially overlaps, so that profile row is not a certain pass.

T-402 S1, about 16–20.5 min, is a genuine CERTAIN_VIOLATION. S2 is omitted and is a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION: containment at 10:02 minus a start near 09:16–09:20:30 crosses 45 min. T-403 S1 is a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION, but 780–1080 s does not follow from the printed alert window [08:00, 08:03] and an 08:16 start (that difference is about 13–16 min). Correct vendor bounds give roughly 12–19 min, same class, different interval. S2 there is about 42 min and a certain pass, and is missing.

T-404 S1 as 1800–2220 s and CERTAIN_VIOLATION is the containment-anchor error. Against 10:35, the start-to-alert gap is about 10–17 min, which straddles 15 min: POSSIBLE_VIOLATION. S2 lands on the 45 min boundary and is not reported.

T-405 is classified INDETERMINATE. Ingestion order leaves one fold assignment: alert on the first fold (CEST, ~00:49–00:52 UTC), investigation and containment on the second (CET, ~01:04–01:07 and ~01:41–01:44). Those windows are disjoint, ordered, and testable. S1 is then about 12–18 min, a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION; S2 is about 34–40 min, a CERTAIN_PASS. Calling the case untestable drops the constraint the assumptions section said it would apply.

T-406 S1 as POSSIBLE_VIOLATION is fair under either entry-delay direction, because both still cross 15 min. S2 is not. Shifting occurrence earlier by up to 5 min can push containment-minus-start past 45 min; shifting it later keeps S2 inside. That is the assumption the task said was material, and it is not carried into a classification.

T-407’s 1020–1170 s CERTAIN_VIOLATION inverts latency. Delivery delay moves receipt later and shortens start − receipt. With latency in [0, 20] min, the long S1 case is near-zero latency (~17 min, over the SLA) and the short case is a several-minute delay (under the SLA). That is POSSIBLE_VIOLATION if the latency bound is accepted, or INDETERMINATE if sent-plus-latency is refused as a receipt time. It is not a certain breach, and 1020–1170 s is not the feasible set.

T-408 S1, about 597–603 s, is a CERTAIN_PASS, but printing “600 s” replaces the interval with the midpoint the task forbade. The same case contains the clearest breaches in the set, both missing: S2 is about 55 min, CERTAIN_VIOLATION of 45 min, and S3 from 2026-06-01 16:05 to 2026-06-03 06:30 is about 38 h 25 min, CERTAIN_VIOLATION of 36 h. T-409 S1 as POSSIBLE_VIOLATION matches a weak ±12 min offset that crosses both zero and 15 min; S2 stays under 45 min and is omitted.

## 4. Sensitivity Analysis
The matrix is not recomputed from the intervals, and the changes that are claimed do not hold.

On the printed T-402 band [960, 1200], +2 min (limit 1020 s) does yield POSSIBLE_VIOLATION and +5 min touches 1200 s. The corrected upper end is about 1232 s, so +5 min (limit 1200 s) is still POSSIBLE_VIOLATION, not CERTAIN_PASS. T-407 is already only a possible breach at tolerance 0, so it cannot “move” from certain violation. T-404’s “still certain until ~15 min” describes the wrong 30–37 min gap; the real 10–17 min gap is possible at 0 and can become a certain pass once the allowance covers the upper end (around +2 min).

For zeta, (600 ± 180) at zeta = 2 is [240, 960]. The printed T-403 S1 upper value 1080 and T-406 upper value 1440 both stick out of that band, so they do not become CERTAIN_PASS at zeta = 2. T-408’s containment and close pairs change across zeta = 1, 2, and 3 (certain profile violations at zeta = 1, and the close pair still wholly above the band at zeta = 2). None of those moves are discussed. T-405 does not stay indeterminate once the surviving fold is kept.

## 5. PM4Py Temporal-Conformance Code
The block is not an executable reconstruction. The event list is a comment (“all other events”), interval normalization is a comment, DST is not represented as alternative intervals, and no duration interval or classification is computed. `pytz` is imported and unused; the requested timezone support is the standard library (`zoneinfo`). The midpoint frame adds a zero timedelta and is labeled lossy only in a trailing comment. `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` is called as `(log, zeta=…)`. In current pm4py that function requires a temporal-profile argument (the supplied mean/sd pairs) and takes zeta beside it; this call raises `TypeError` before any diagnostic. There is no comparison between midpoint output and interval classes, which is the point of the section. Version-dependent diagnostic columns are not isolated because nothing is run.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency
T-406 (entry-delay semantics versus server receipt of the typed time) and T-409 (real NTP for that hour) are the right kind of missing evidence. T-405 does not need a fold bit to become testable: ingestion order already selects the folds. What would make S1 certain is a tighter clock or second-resolution stamp that pulls the ~12–18 min gap entirely under or entirely over 15 min. T-403 is attributed to a missing receipt acknowledgment; its uncertainty is vendor floor-minute plus ±180 s, so the missing evidence is synchronization or a finer timestamp. T-401, T-402’s S2, corrected T-404, and T-408 are possible or certain results that this section never reaches. The four evidence types requested (synchronization, missing activity, duration, semantics) are not separated case by case.

## 7. Governance Recommendation
UTC storage, raw-value retention, offset telemetry, resolution metadata, start/complete flags, fold logging, and causal ids are the right control set. The enforcement sentence then swaps the predicate: it says overlap is enough only when the whole interval lies inside the SLA, and it ties that inside condition to “declaring violation.” Containment of the feasible interval inside the limit is the test for a certain pass. A certain violation is the whole interval lying outside the limit. Overlap is the possible case, and the policy still needs an explicit choice that overlap is not, by itself, an enforceable breach.

The net result is an outline of interval reasoning with HUB handled, T-402’s S1 breach preserved, and a usable control list, undermined by shortened windows, one inverted latency argument, one wrong duration anchor, a self-contradictory pass, the missed T-408 breaches, and code that does not implement the method.
