7.4

The design is substantively strong: counts, pair classifications, and the refusal to treat shared higher-level objects as identity are mostly right. It is not nearly flawless. Several safeguards described in the prose are not what the code does, one table cell invents a source key, and a frequency claim contradicts the labeling policy.

The arithmetic that matters is correct. Raw E2O is 44. Merging e04 into e03 and e16 into e11 drops 3 and 2 duplicate relations, leaving 15 events and 39 relations. Folding e07 into e06 then leaves 14 and 37. A same-activity, same-timestamp, common-object component merge really does yield the five clusters named ({e03,e04,e05}, {e06,e07}, {e08,e09}, {e11,e16}, {e13,e14,e15}) plus five singletons, 10 events, and 28 relations if unions are deduplicated. e05 and e14 are retained as events in the working log. Original timestamps are kept, ingestion is not the business clock, and a total order is explicitly denied the status of physical truth. The `have_common_object=True` discussion is the right one: O1 and V1 put e13 and e14 in one component, and transitivity through O1/P1 also pulls e05 into the I1 pick. `False` does not split those groups on this file.

Section 2 misstates provenance for the best-supported merge. e03/e04 are W-77 and S-901. “ER/W keys” points at an ERP identifier those rows do not have. That is a small cell, but it is the cell that says what an auditor keeps.

Section 7 contradicts itself on capture frequency. After e15 is relabeled, V1 does not still have three `Capture Payment` events; the same section later says the qualified summary is a different type. The table’s conservative “capture-like = 3” only works if qualifiers are ignored. The ingest-order caution also lists e13 before e14, although both have `ingested_at` 12:00:03, so ingestion does not produce that sequence. The soft edge e11 ≺ e12 does not determine the published 12:00 order either: hard edges plus ascending eid already yield e11 before e12. The write-up notices the coincidence and still presents the soft lifecycle edge as the reason for the intended total order.

The implementation would not run, and several checks are hollow.

- `merge_verified_pair` reads `contributing_seqs` before that column exists.
- On a later call, `pd.isna` on a list cell is not a scalar condition.
- `attr_authority` is never applied. A non-null value that exists only on the dropped row stays out of the canonical cell, so “SCANNER owns scan id” and “billing owns its own attributes” are unenforced. Canonical e03/e11 silently win the primary columns.
- The line `if False else ...` is dead. `assert ... or True` cannot fail.
- `justified_drop` builds a side log of events whose activity and timestamp are already unique, drops nothing, and returns the untouched log. Exact technical duplicates share an activity and timestamp, so they are excluded from the only subset the library function sees. The routine cannot do the job section 4 assigns it.
- The cross-second guard never reads timestamps after `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`. It sorts by `order_rank` and compares that to `(original_timestamp, order_rank)`, which was already true before the shift. Sections 3, 4, and 9 treat this as a reorder abort. It is not.
- `order_basis` stores one incoming reason, soft edges overwrite hard ones, and a chain root is labeled `eid-fallback`.
- Quarantine rows and relation-level `source_eid` are required by the audit story and absent from the code. Reconstruction still works only because `ocel_raw` is left intact, which is the right primary mechanism, but the lineage schema is over-claimed.

Ordering judgment is otherwise careful: e09 ≺ e10 is hard, e08 ≺ e09 and e11 ≺ e12 are weak, e06/e07 and e15 stay unmerged in the working log, and premature microsecond encoding is given a concrete false path through e16. Those are the right calls. The length is also past the stated 1,900–2,900 words of prose; the extra length is mostly repetition of the same policy in sections 4, 6, 8, and 9, not additional cases.

Taken together, the pipeline a careful analyst would adopt is the conservative 15-event log, and the damage account of the library merge is accurate. The score is held down by a wrong provenance label, an internal frequency contradiction, and executable-looking code whose merge, drop scope, authority rule, and timestamp guard do not implement the safeguards the text relies on.
