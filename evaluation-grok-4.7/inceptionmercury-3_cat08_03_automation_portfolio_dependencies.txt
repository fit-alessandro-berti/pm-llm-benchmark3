It is not nearly flawless. The recommendation contradicts its own dependency analysis. Section 2 states that A6 is starved or unsafe without A5 or an equivalent document-completeness control, and section 7 cites the 52% repeated missing-document loop as central process evidence. There is a factual slip. Section 1 and section 7 correctly say entity mismatch is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it is present. Section 4 reassigns that 82% to high-exposure cases, which the evidence does not say.

The integration plan is internally inconsistent. Section 2 names A1, A3, A9, and A11 as the deep core-banking candidates and does not include A6. The rollout then spends the two first-half slots on A3 and A6, while A9, whose own matrix row requires core contract integration, is deferred to a lighter interface. Section 4 names activities, waits, and variants, but it barely uses directly-follows relations, despite that being an explicit requirement.

The portfolio logic is therefore stronger than a frequency ranking and weaker than a closed dependency argument.
