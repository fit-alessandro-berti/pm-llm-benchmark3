7.3

The final JSON is a strong, process-aware analysis: states track obligations rather than prefixes, C6-A and C6-B are genuinely differentiated, rule 4/5/7 and the queues are used, absent events are not treated as proof of non-occurrence, and the DFG critique is specific. It is not nearly flawless.

The main defect is a repeated observation-model error. Several rank-2/3 events are downstream of an unlogged predecessor (C6-B closure and fast close, C6-A second evidence request, C6-C and C6-D disposition). The text says those become next only if the predecessor completion is delayed or flushed out of order, but a delayed completion is itself the next logged event when the feed catches up. Only omission or true reordering lets the successor appear first. The stated 8–15% lag, and the normal feed flags, cannot support upper bounds such as 0.32 for Close — No Safety Issue or 0.26 for Technical Investigation Completed as the immediate next log line. Midpoints for C6-A and C6-B then sit near 1.0, leaving no residual for that mechanism.

C6-A also contradicts itself: Technical Investigation Completed is `process_enabled: true`, while contradicting evidence says ranks 2 and 3 are not enabled in the primary state. The same boolean is applied inconsistently elsewhere (supplier analysis “maybe already open” is true for C6-E and false for C6-C). Fast Close after an evidence cycle is correctly called deviant, but it is still a weak third prediction. C6-C’s supplier-analysis rank is speculative: different lots, no opening trace, and a 6–36 h queue.
