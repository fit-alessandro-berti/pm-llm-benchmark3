8.4

The final answer is a strong, conservative design: the duplicate taxonomy matches the facts, e05 and e14 stay distinct, e08/e09 are treated as a harmonization collision, e15 stays an invoice-level summary, and ingestion time is not used as business order. Counts check out (17/44 raw, 15/39 conservative, 14/37 evidence-based, 10 and about 28 under union-style aggressive merging). The `have_common_object=True` failure on e13/e14 is explained correctly, and the ordering section separates semantic constraints from a deterministic projection.

The main miss is in the required PM4Py block. `ocel_drop_duplicates` sits in a loop over an empty list, so it is never applied to any justified exact-replay fixture. The verified-pair helper also does not carry out the stated attribute policy: e11/e16 gets no field-authority map, scanner-side fields are not materialized on e03, and absorbed E2O rows are deleted without being stored in the audit payload. Section 8 describes those snapshots, confidence, and reviewer fields; the code does not record them. Reversibility therefore depends entirely on keeping `ocel_raw`.

Smaller weaknesses: pick-before-pack is stated as the intended 10:00 order more firmly than the known facts support, even though the policy text says lifecycle rules need validation. The ET–OT and OTG discussion is thinner and less concrete than the flattened-DFG patterns. The `delta` argument to `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` is only plausible, not clearly matched to the library signature.
