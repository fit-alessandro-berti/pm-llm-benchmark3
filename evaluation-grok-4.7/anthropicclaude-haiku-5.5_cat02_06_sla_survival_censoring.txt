7.2

The primary design is sound: time zero at `Submit`, horizon 72 h, E1 as the resolution CIF with reject/withdraw/transfer competing, E2 as any-terminal survival, open cases retained, and Aalen–Johansen chosen before the estimator is applied. The hand-checked scenario values match a correct product-limit calculation (resolution CIF about 0.304, 0.313, and 0.379; exits-censored 1−KM about 0.361; any-terminal survival about 0.380; 9/13 = 0.692; bounds 9/32 and 14/32). Case counts, AutoCheck 13/17 vs 2/15, P1 composition, and the on-time resolution IDs are right. Leakage and immortal time for `escalated` are handled correctly, and the code keeps all 32 rows, stratifies, and does not invent timestamps.

Those strengths do not make the write-up reliable.

The 6-point gap between S1 and S5 is treated as proof that exits are informative. That gap is the ordinary CIF versus censored-KM difference and appears even when competing events are independent. Informativeness is a separate claim. Section 1 defines this correctly, then Section 6 collapses it.

The escalation contrasts (2/12 and 0/4 vs 5/10) put still-open cases in the failure count. S10 (age 47) and S29 (age 51) are unresolved at the cutoff. That is the censored-as-failed analysis Section 3 argues against. S29 is also called slow or stalled despite a 6 h gap and age under 72. The same imputation sits inside the crude priority rates (2/7, 3/7, 1/3, 1/5).

The Mermaid diagram is labeled as observed transitions only. `Review → Transfer` never occurs; every transfer is `Escalate → Transfer`. `Rework → Resolve` does occur (S27) and is missing. S29 is cited as a repeated-review loop with only one `Review`.

E2 is mishandled for S32. A transfer at 96 h is still non-terminal at 72 h whether that later exit is coded as an event or as a censor, so S2 does not change S32’s horizon contribution. The Section 6 S2 column still prints P(no terminal) = 0.38, while the supplied AJ function, with transfers coded as censoring, returns survival about 0.435. S4 is the same encoding as S1.

The alert rule (gap above 20 h or age above 60 h) flags S24 (age 67) and does not flag S10 (gap 19, age 47). The text does the opposite. Marginal P(still non-terminal at 72) ≈ 0.38 is then equated to a sum of conditional risks over the current open queue.

Secondary recommendations are uneven. A counting-process cause-specific model needs the same transition times that correctly make the multi-state model infeasible here. E4’s integral is typographically broken (the limit and minus sign are gone), as are several inequalities, though the prose often repairs them. Cohort is called intention-to-treat under non-random, partially compliant assignment. “Nine events” and “four breaches beyond 72 h” undercount terminal events by 72 h (18) and known non-resolutions by 72 h (late resolutions plus open cases past 72 plus S32). The open-case bounds are said to cover every scenario; under S3 the identified sample bounds shift because S08 and S21 become successes.

Format, uncertainty language, and the primary estimand–estimator link are strong. The local factual and conceptual errors above are too numerous for a near-flawless score.
