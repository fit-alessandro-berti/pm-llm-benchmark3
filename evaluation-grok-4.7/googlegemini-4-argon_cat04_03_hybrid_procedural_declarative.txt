6.3

The submission is a serious hybrid design with the right layer split, a real R5/R6 conflict analysis, usable DECLARE directionality, correct temporal arithmetic, and largely correct verdicts for T1–T5, T7, and T8. It is not reliable as an implemented model. Several normative artifacts contradict each other or the stated policy, and a default PM4Py log-skeleton check would not reproduce the matrix.

The procedural backbone is internally inconsistent. The compact expression repeats the three checks inside a clarification loop and inserts a post-activation bank-change loop. The Python POWL does neither: clarification is a `LOOP(τ, Request→Receive)` only before `Classify Risk`, escalation exists only inside the high-risk branch, and bank changes are omitted. Nesting does put an executed escalation/opinion before activation, so the coded net hard-fails T6, but there is no explicit edge from screening to escalation or from opinion to activation. Low-risk or pre-classification escalation is excluded without being treated as a modeling restriction.

The log skeleton is the weakest artifact. Under PM4Py conformance, `directly_follows` is the whitelist of permitted adjacencies; `set()` rejects every multi-event trace, so the published dictionary does not pass T1–T3 or T7. `always_after(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` does not encode R5: it forces activation after every opinion and false-fails opinion-then-reject. `always_after(Receive Clarification, Activate Vendor)` is the same error and is left in the dictionary after the text says to remove it. `always_after(Change Bank Details, Release First Payment)` plus payment frequency `{0,1}` cannot represent a bank change after the only payment. Clarification frequencies capped at `{0..5}` forbid a compliant sixth cycle with no disclosure. The orientation notes for `always_before` / `always_after` are otherwise coherent, and the Req/Rec equivalence-versus-alternation point is right.

DECLARE is closer to sound. `altresponse`/`altprecedence` on `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`, `noncoexistence` versus `nonsuccession`, and `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` for Resolution A are the right templates, and T5 fails `altresponse` on its own. The mapping then incorrectly says an unanswered request cannot be caught without skeleton equivalence. Keys are `Templates` enum objects rather than the string keys `conformance_declare` looks up. R5 is described as precedence/response between escalation and opinion; those rules do not state “opinion before activation.” `response(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)` correctly demands a later verification, but nothing stops that verification from landing after payment. Section 9 criticizes a `precedence(Verify, Payment)` rule that was never encoded.

R12 is handled correctly: seconds conversion, `zeta = 2.0`, and two-sided \(z\) make the three lower bounds 0 only because each mean equals \(2\sigma\). T7’s 34 h opinion delay is \(z \approx 3.67\) (warning); its 2 h payment delay is \(z \approx 1.94\) and correctly stays inside the band. T1, T2, T4, and T6 payment/clarification deltas are also calculated correctly. Temporal vacuity on T5 is properly identified.

The resource layer targets the right gaps (R9, conditional R6, the R11 window) and fail-closes missing `org:resource`. The commented `filter_four_eyes_principle` calls are labeled as violators even though that filter keeps four-eyes-compliant cases. The prose allows either 24 h clock; the code requires both. Default `allow_r6_emergency_override=False` yields `fail` for T6, while the matrix cell says `policy-dependent`. Timestamp math assumes timedeltas, which the `@hour` notation does not provide. Set intersection of every bank verification with the approver can also false-fail a later re-verification.

T6’s explanation does distinguish Resolution A (control-flow fail) from Resolution B (relaxed flow, missing `joint_authorization`). That meets the conflict requirement in prose, but it is not what the coded POWL, DECLARE model, skeleton, or default validator return. Those implemented objects fail T6 outright.
