8.5

The final answer meets every required section and constraint, and most of its quantitative claims match the logs. It is not nearly flawless: a few process-mining presentations overstate what the edges and frequencies can support.

**What holds up**

- All four required disruption types are present, plus a compound case, and they differ by mix and failure mode rather than by volume alone. Weekly class volumes are calculated correctly (Routine roughly flat at about 1,400/week; Urgent about 414 to 957; Complex-panel about 299 to 660).
- Direct-to-preparation shares (93.0% to 87.7%), non-direct paths (about 7.0% to 12.3%), repeat-arc shares (6.7% and 14.8%), queue and utilization figures, bypass rates (74% to 58%), and the cited single-edge medians are accurate.
- The redesign does not staff every station, does not chase mean cycle time, and does not rank every class first. I1, I2, and I3 are modified; I4 and I5 are triggered; I9 is rejected; I10 stays dormant until a time comparison beats local delay. Each bundle names the station it can overload.
- On/off triggers, hysteresis, the required trade-off columns, a redesign-caused failure mode (reserve/aging oscillation and validation bow waves), and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard are all present and internally consistent.

**Deductions**

- Median edge times are added and treated as path durations: batch edges “sum to 15 versus 36 hours,” and specialist-hold edges “sum to 24 versus 60 hours.” Median(A) + median(B) is not the median path time. Both component edges did worsen, so the direction is fair, but the summed figures are not evidence of path length.
- Hold arcs are converted to about 36/week versus 203/week without the censoring caveat at that point. Peak technical-validation exits (3,420 + 4,050 + 610 = 8,080) exceed Assay run → Technical validation (7,240), so weekly exit rates are not a clean demand series. The later WIP note uses Batch wait (1,180 versus 1,030) and does not correct this multiplier.
- Repeat-assay loops and Repeat-after-failure orders are blended. The 6.7%/14.8% figures are shares of assay exits entering the repeat loop; the night-shift/M2 clustering statement in the source applies to the Repeat-after-failure case type. Those are related but not the same object.
- The technical-validation backup block stretches “cross-trainable in 4 weeks,” which sits on the technical-validator row and is operationalized by I4 as validators learning accession correction. The answer hedges this as an assessment, but it is still an extra capacity path the log does not identify.
- Several bands (85% forecast load, 120%/150% arrival load, 45% mix, 1.5× run-rate) are round operating choices. They are labeled as starting bands and many queue cutoffs do sit between normal and peak medians, so this is a smaller issue.
- S3 and S5 migration stories are coherent but thinner than S1: they rely on the same peak edges rather than a distinct flow account for absence or for simultaneous failures.

The service logic, scarcity rules, and downstream-overload checks are strong enough that these issues do not undermine the package. They do keep it short of a near-flawless process-mining argument.
