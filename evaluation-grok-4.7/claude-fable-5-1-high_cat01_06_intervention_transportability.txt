7.2

The transport judgment is right and the statutory constraint is respected, but the quantitative layer and the pilot are not reliable enough for a higher score.

The structural comparison, confounding discussion, and refusal to open Document Check before originals are the strongest parts. Site A’s −9 h median is treated as a mixture of parallelization, mix shift, added reviewers, huddles, API latency, and timestamp change, and the copied “both tasks at Register for every case” proposal is correctly rejected. The partial order (identity concurrent with a document branch that still waits on originals; assessment after both; legal review optional and looped) matches the legal constraints. `conditional go`, with digital cases first, is the right decision.

The scenario arithmetic does not estimate medians. A 4–7 h gain on the digital 38% changes the pooled mean by about 1.5–2.7 h. It moves the pooled median by that amount only if the median case is in the treated group. Originals add an 18 h delay on 62% of volume, so the median case is likely an originals case. Speeding the already-faster digital slice can leave the overall median nearly unchanged. S2’s “pooled −1.5 to −3 h”, S1’s “−2.5 to −4.5 h”, S6, and the recommendation’s pooled 2.5–4.5 h all use share × subgroup effect and label it a median. S5’s “digital gains dominate the median” has the same defect. Ranges such as a 1–4 h handoff, a 25–40% supplement reduction, and p90 +5 to +15 h are stipulated and then repeated as expected effects. The 1.5:1 workload ratio multiplies medians by volume. “4 h (≈11%)” is 9% of 44 h. A log-scale SD of 0.6 is too small for a p90/median ratio of 2.5 (a lognormal would need σ ≈ 0.72), so the 450-per-arm figure is optimistic. Rework +0.6 pp is about one standard error and is still used as the quality prior. “60% decided directly” is just 100% − 40%; recorded assessment decisions are 1,940/3,400 ≈ 57%, and legal approve/reject edges are 980, not 1,360.

The pilot contradicts itself. Stratum D is a 50/50 case randomization, but the versions are control, T1, and T2, and the text says T2 − T1 prices huddles and the WIP cap. Those are reviewer-level policies; one reviewer cannot run a six-task cap and a daily huddle for some of their cases only. A two-team stepped wedge has essentially no degrees of freedom. A +3 pp rework stop over four weeks (~50 cases per arm) sits inside ordinary binomial noise. Case-level assignment in shared pools also means the 50% pilot estimand is not the full-rollout effect the transport claim needs. Section 9 then starts non-legal digital cases only, while section 7 treats all of stratum D in stage 1.

Smaller faults: I → O = 1,020 is not a clean “other half on the critical path”; decision and document-check counts do not reconcile to 3,400 (the register excess is noted, this deficit is not); several graph edges have an unsigned or ambiguous sign; the soundness call unpacks a pair that standard `check_soundness` does not return, and the transitive-order asserts are skipped unless optional methods exist. The code’s partial order itself is the right model, and the free-choice, capacity, lock, and duration limits are stated honestly.

Conditional go is justified. The benefit numbers and the design that is supposed to identify them are not.
