3.1

The write-up keeps the required six-part shape and names the right mechanisms (parallel branches, reruns, contamination, queries, the temperature-excursion dependence, weekend staffing). The forecast built from those pieces is not temporally reliable.

The incubation clock is wrong by about a day. Friday 14:00 plus 46–54 h is Sunday 12:00–20:00, not Monday 14:00–20:00. The parenthetical “Friday 15:30 + 46 h + 0.5 h readout = Monday 15:30” does not equal that timestamp: 46 h after Friday 15:30 is Sunday 13:30. Remaining hours are then inconsistent with the stated clocks. Monday 15:30 is 72 h after Friday 15:30, not 36 h; Monday 17:00 is about 73.5 h, not 40–42 h. The P90 hour column (70–78 h) also does not match Tuesday 00:30–01:30 (about 81–82 h).

Chemistry ignores the queue that actually governs it. One urgent and three ordinary samples are ahead, service is at least 1.5 h, and the lab closes at 18:00, so B-417 cannot finish on Friday. The ledger and decomposition instead start B-417 around 16:20–16:40 and finish it at 19:10–19:30 “within lab hours.” 19:10 is after the 18:00 close, analyst B’s residual 0.4–0.9 h ends around 15:54–16:24 rather than 16:40–17:00, and a rerun cannot start at 18:00 or 19:30. The triangular mode 2.5 h is also treated as the median; the mean is about 2.8 h.

The parallel join is not held as a maximum. Section 2 makes microbiology the smooth-path clock and puts the terminal event on Monday afternoon. Section 3 compares Monday 14:30 microbiology with Friday 19:30 chemistry and joins at Friday 19:30, dropping the date. In the rerun case, chemistry is said to finish Monday 11:00 and microbiology Monday 15:30, yet the join and release are placed at 11:00–11:30. That is the earlier branch, not the slower one. The same reversal appears when contamination is said to move the join to Tuesday while the release math does not follow the later branch plus QA.

Calendar constraints on QA and on the tail are missed. Documentation queries and documentation-only investigations consume QA working hours (weekdays to 20:00, Saturday 09:00–13:00, no Sunday), not overnight clock time, so Monday 23:00 and Tuesday 00:30 are outside the review calendar. The 3–7 calendar-day laboratory investigation is absent from every scenario and from the P90 band, which stops near Tuesday midnight. A confirmed excursion, rerun, or contamination also lifts investigation probability to about 0.30–0.55; treating that as a 6–12 h shift understates the tail by days. Batch Rejected is never a terminal event. The pre-existing QA queue of five, the 0.35/h overdispersed arrivals, and Saturday’s four review hours are mentioned and then replaced by “immediate start.”

Scenario accounting conflicts with itself. The smooth path is defined as no query, then its tail includes a documentation query. Smooth and rerun are described as overlapping executions even though rerun was excluded from smooth. Delayed microbiology and the QA tail are called largely independent, while contamination and a confirmed temperature excursion are exactly what raise investigation probability. The weekend on-call driver is placed on Saturday morning; primary readiness is Sunday afternoon, and a Sunday readout still cannot release the batch while chemistry is closed.

The assumption ledger mostly repeats given numbers. It does not choose an interpretation for urgent versus high priority, preemption, whether Friday starts are paused, how the QA queue drains before the join, or how excursion probability rescales the two lab risks. Sensitivities are off scale (“±2 h moves the ready window by a day”; a higher rerun rate “adds ~1 h”).

What remains is a correctly labeled skeleton with a Monday-ish central clock that sometimes lands near a plausible smooth release only by cancelling arithmetic errors. The decomposition, the hour forecast, the join, and the severe tail are not defensible as stated.
