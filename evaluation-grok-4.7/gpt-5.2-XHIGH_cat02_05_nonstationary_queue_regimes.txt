7.0

The write-up is complete, numerically careful, and right on the main operating story: weekday versus weekend, the 15 April outage as a shock, 16–17 April as burn-down rather than a high-productivity regime, a mix/load break on 20 April, and a staffing change from 29 April whose start time is unknown. Medians, quartiles, open-hour rates, lognormal pressure under the stated assumption, hourly Fano/CV figures, the pooled staff–WIP correlation of about +0.65, the within-window correlation of about −0.43, and the baseline Little’s-law arithmetic all check out. The code matches the required seven obligations, and the outage/recovery refusal is the correct one.

It is not nearly flawless, because three interpretations are materially over-strong relative to the same tables.

Stock versus flow is not reconciled. From 20–28 April, arrivals exceed completions by about +106, and over all 28 days the cumulative gap is about +110, while mean WIP starts near 18 and ends near 19 and only drifts from the high 30s to about 50 inside the “high-complex” weekdays. Day-level \(A-C\) also fails to track day-to-day WIP changes, including weekends where mean WIP falls while net inflow is positive. Calling median net inflow of +14 “backlog growth” and treating that regime as clearly unstable therefore overstates what the WIP path shows: a higher level and a mild drift, not a +14 case/day stock build. Unobserved exits, a WIP definition that is not the arrival–completion stock, or incompatible aggregates are all still open, and none is stated. That weakens the regime labels, the Little’s-law rejections that cite this drift, and the burn-down targets.

The two arrival pictures are not cross-checked. On 21 April, 08:00–09:59 in the hourly vector is \(3+8=11\) of 115 arrivals (about 10%), while `peak_08_10_pct` is 47. The ordinary days line up much better with 09:00–10:59 than with the stated 08:00–09:59 window (7 April: \(12+20\) gives about 32% versus 31). Section 7 then treats the peak-share jump and the 21 April spikes as one morning-peak problem. They are not the same evidence. The vector does show real later spikes (34, then 24, then 14); the daily peak field does not locate them.

Implied sojourn is never set next to active handling or offered load. Baseline \(W \approx 2.44\) h against roughly 0.5 h mean service leaves on the order of 2 h of wait and, at \(\lambda \approx 8.3\)/h, only about 4 cases in service versus mean WIP near 20. A single pool with 6 FTE at \(\rho \approx 0.7\) does not produce that queue under ordinary multi-server formulas, and the 7 April hourly profile drains too quickly to average WIP of 20. That gap is exactly why one pooled \(c\)-server model is misleading (multi-step waits, WIP that is not this desk’s queue, or service times that are not full case work content). It is also why “applicable” is a step too confident: carry-over is rated medium, the snapshot window is ambiguous, and \(W\) is an open-hours accounting ratio, not a checked steady-state sojourn. The intervention value \(W \approx 3.67\) h has the same unexamined gap and is computed on a declining stock.

Smaller faults: the pooled staff–WIP correlation is mostly weekday/weekend composition (about 95% of staff variance), not reactive staffing; the −0.43 figure is a short, time-confounded contrast with tiny staff variation. Hourly Fano on 7 April mostly reflects a smooth diurnal wave, not stochastic bursts; 21 April is the actual bursty day, and completions there are smoother. Autocorrelation is still mixed with the weekly cycle. Lognormal \(\rho \approx 1.18\) is labeled, but Section 7 uses “overloaded” more flatly than an unidentified mean allows; \(A>C\) plus a completion plateau near 100/day is the more robust evidence, and it does not by itself prove the stock is exploding. The change-point scan cannot place 29 April (five-day minimum) and the prose does not say so. Median rates in Section 2 and means in Section 6 are not tied together.

Format, arithmetic, outage handling, and the decision not to treat recovery days as the steady regime are solid. The grade is held down by the stock–flow contradiction, the unreproducible morning-peak claim, and the missing sojourn-versus-service check.
