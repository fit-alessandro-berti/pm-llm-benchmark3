The write-up is complete and directionally right—logging and extraction explain most of the measured drop, staff blame is not the default, and the August sign-off gap is separated from June—but the central arithmetic is incoherent and the code would not run.

The staged alignment table is misread throughout. Label mapping is 32%→58% (cost 0.74→0.44). Removing `Queue Routed` is 58%→81% (0.44→0.21). Source-sequence ordering is only 81%→89% (0.21→0.12), about eight points, not the ~27 points assigned to timestamps. Sections 2, 3, 6, and 7 still credit ordering with the `Queue Routed` step, then also count that step again as a technical-event effect. The conclusion adds those pieces to the August model change and claims they explain “~85 of the observed 61-point” June collapse (93%→32%). An August 1 sign-off rule cannot cause a June–July drop, and 85 cannot fit inside 61. The same section gives the residual compliance concern both 65% and 35% confidence.

The 11-of-70 screenshot note is treated as the size of the order defect. It is a partial check. The +8 point ordering gain is the better estimate of how many reversals are artifacts. The ledger also says June–July has “only 25” enhanced-review traces with a compliance check; 145 traces still contain that check. “600 cases affected” by the rename does not match the June log. The business-outcome row is marked as supporting noncompliance while its own rationale points the other way.

The label counterfactual says a gain under 40 points would falsify that hypothesis, while the same row reports the known +26 point gain. Phase 1 can stop once labels recover 20 points even though 42% of traces remain unfit. The anachronism test expects an optional sign-off to lower April–May fitness; an optional activity should not. The claimed drop of ~100–120 log moves for `Queue Routed` is far below the ~530–560 occurrences in the DFG.

Required headers say reliability and plausibility on a 0–100 scale; both tables are labeled 0–10. Section 7 is far past the 250–400 word limit. The causal block is annotated prose, not an adjacency list. The code never calls `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` or `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, never computes precision, uses nonexistent imports such as `conformance_diagn_alignments`, and models optionality with a one-child `*`, which is not a valid process-tree loop. Its printed interpretation repeats the impossible +27 point ordering claim that the script does not measure.

Governance rules and the audit/stratification tests are the strongest part. They do not offset a decision narrative that mis-sizes the three repairs the evidence was built to separate.
