5.5

The write-up is structurally complete and gets the main qualitative judgments right, but it is not temporally reliable. Several required quantities contradict the case logs, the queue snapshots, or the answer’s own timestamps, and those errors sit on the criteria that matter most here: temporal consistency, earlier readiness versus earlier completion, and calibrated ranges.

What holds up: all eight required sections, columns, and derivation labels are present. Activity order is generally respected (register then triage, originals before document check, compliance after the latest assessment and before decision on the high-risk cases, four-eyes kept as A1 versus C1, preliminary compliance not treated as a substitute). K-337 is correctly classified as a decision-policy change rather than a pure time cut, and the interference discussion does cover C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment migration, weekends, and false concurrency. Ranking K-337 last on robustness, despite the largest claimed speed gain, is the right call. The pilot rows are complete and usable.

What fails:

- K-337’s clock is wrong by about a factor of two to three. Assessment 2 ends Friday; the factual decision is Wednesday of the following week (after Monday document check 3 and Tuesday assessment and compliance). A Friday-evening counterfactual finish is on the order of 100–110 hours earlier, not 36–48. The parenthetical “Wed vs Fri of the same week” misreads the log. The base case is also wrong: a Monday 11:00 decision versus Wednesday 09:00 is about 46 hours, not 24. Section 4’s “~60h” and section 7’s “36–48 hours” do not match each other or the path.
- K-104 calls the wait from Tuesday 19:10 to Wednesday 13:10 a “5h queue gap.” It is 18 hours. The same subsection then offers a Tuesday 20:00 C1 start, a Wednesday 08:30 start, a Wednesday 11:00 decision, a 7-hour story, and a 12-hour best case. Wednesday 08:00 still shows an 8-hour C1 queue, so an 08:30 start is full preemption, not a small reserve. Claiming that preemption costs other cases only 3–5 hours understates the displacement required by the start time.
- K-219’s table says −8 to −14 hours and “decision Tue 11:00.” Tuesday 11:00 versus Wednesday 09:00 is about 22 hours. The derivation’s best case (−21h, D2 starting Monday 18:00) ignores the Monday 16:00 D2 queue of 5 hours; the path also starts A2 at Tuesday 08:00 against a 6-hour A2 queue. Earlier document readiness is treated as an immediate assessment start.
- K-411 attributes the whole Tuesday 09:35–15:00 gap to the case lock and calls it ~5.5 hours of artificial wait. At Tuesday 08:00, D2 already had about a 4-hour queue, and the factual document task started only ~1.5 hours after identity finished. The table’s −4 to −8 hours also conflicts with the derivation’s −20-hour best case.
- The partial-order block is not a clean relation set: the four-eyes line is syntactically broken, and compliance is placed on the universal path even though K-219 and K-411 do not require it.
- The “100 similar cases” rollout silently becomes 40 cases for K-104 and 30 for K-219, so the scarce-resource arithmetic is not the policy that was specified. Section 4 runs to about 460 words against a 250–400 limit; section 7 is slightly over 320. Assumption 3 restates a given fact, and assumption 8 (supplier responses do not cluster on weekends) is in tension with K-337’s own Friday-to-Monday wait.

The conclusions about which intervention is most robust and which is most dangerous are directionally sound, but they rest on cycle-time ranges that the answer itself does not compute consistently. That keeps the result well below a near-flawless score.
