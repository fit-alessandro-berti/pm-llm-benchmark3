7.4

The write-up meets the required structure and respects the hard constraints: no invented events or objects, ev11 is not forced onto one delivery, arithmetic is not treated as proof, circular E2O/O2O confirmation is named, and unrepaired vs Bundle A vs Bundle B effects on OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, flattening, and event-versus-object frequencies are mostly right. It is still over-confident and internally uneven on the judgments the task makes central.

The evidence model says a selected addition needs a strong semantic reason plus an independent signal, and that arithmetic or broad customer/route overlap should stay unresolved. The selected bundle then accepts six edges at 0.78–0.88. For ev08, `PK8-2` is compatible both with package `PK-8` and with the domain’s own scanner-outage story; ev07 already shows `PK8-1` together with an explicit `PK-8`, so the prefix does not discriminate the outage case the rules flag. Citing “PK-8 later dispatched” and “IT-B1 needs a package lifecycle” as evidence for that link is completeness pressure, not independent support. Route `NORTH` is listed in favor of `ev10 → DEL-Y` even though it fits `DEL-X` equally. The real discriminator is `N-421?`, and the question mark is too lightly reflected by status `accept` at 0.80.

`PAY-2 → INV-44` is accepted in every bundle. Unapplied cash is mentioned and then never adopted. Complementary amounts 100+130=230 are doing more work than the stated “not proof” rule allows, especially with no customer on the payment. Bundle A then fully settles a 230 invoice that still bills only `ORD-A` (declared value 130). Section 5 notes the suspicion; section 6 does not trace the false overpayment / unexplained-total behavior that choice creates in the invoice flattening.

Dependent O2O edges (`PK-8 packs IT-B1`, `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, `PAY-2 settles INV-44`) are marked `accept` at nearly the parent confidence. Section 5 correctly says they are not independent confirmation; the table and JSON still present them as accepted repairs. P7 and P8 are 0.45 and 0.43 despite the text admitting that `DEL-Y` depends on the ev10 repair while `DEL-X` is tied to `ORD-A` by observed pack/dispatch structure. Status labels drift: P5 and P10 are `provisional` in the table, unresolved in Bundle A, and only partly absorbed by Bundle B. Bundle B is also soft on `ev11 → ORD-B` (“a stronger version could add”), so that bundle is not one coherent lifecycle.

The “Delivery OC-DFG gains edges for DEL-X and DEL-Y” wording treats an object-type graph as if it stored per-object edges. The underlying claim is right: Bundle B introduces `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` with support from both deliveries, and Bundle A does not. Order flattening really is unchanged under A. Self-loops `Pack Item → Pack Item` and `Record Payment → Record Payment`, and double-counting ev11 across two deliveries, are correctly identified.

The validation sketch copies the tables, checks referential integrity and duplicate keys, avoids `ocel_o2o_enrichment`, flattens Order and Delivery, and marks the constructor as version-dependent. `append_without_duplicates` raises rather than appends, and `reindex` onto the base columns leaves `ocel:activity`, `ocel:timestamp`, and `ocel:type` null on new rows whenever the source relations table has those columns. Discovery can then invent a null activity. The import path is outside the `TypeError` handler.

These are calibration and consistency failures on the ambiguous repairs, not missing sections. They are large enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
