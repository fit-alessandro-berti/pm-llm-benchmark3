7.8

The answer is a strong, structured conformance review with genuine two-sided argument, mostly correct POWL reading, a complete 15-case matrix, and a layered revision that puts control flow, data, identity, and authority in different places. It is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions and one formal-semantics error are material under a strict reading.

The best part is the opening semantics. Unordered `checks` nodes are concurrent, so quality-before-sanctions is not an M0 violation. A partial-order edge between submodels requires every source activity before any target activity, so both checks precede approval, activation, and rejection. M0 is mandatory, single-occurrence, control-flow only. The consequence is right: M0-fit is neither necessary nor sufficient for CS-9 (SUP-712 versus SUP-709). Stipulated facts, disputes, and unproved claims are separated carefully, including the CRM missingness jump, the absence of any ex-ante eligibility snapshot, and master-data validation appearing in no trace. Both briefs concede what they must: iteration, label drift, migration duplicates, and post-migration notification gaps on one side; SUP-703, SUP-708, the 14 ineligible auto-approvals, post-expiry continuation, and unevidenced master-data validation on the other.

The judgment then breaks its own rules. Section 1.5 says an exception is accepted only if the operation proves a written instrument from a body empowered to vary the control, and it states that authority to run a pilot is not authority to disapply CS-9. D1 leaves PD-3’s authority unresolved, and the overlay table marks PD-3 `authority_basis` as NULL. SUP-702 is nevertheless `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. The dominant-reason cell also says “eligibility proven ex ante,” which section 1.4 and the matrix correctly deny: complete eligibility fields are not a sealed pre-decision snapshot. That is not a labeling quibble. It accepts the exception the stated burden says must fail, and it states as found a fact the same answer lists as missing. FD-7 is handled more generously still: D2 calls its authority probable but unproven, while SUP-704 is a high-confidence justified exception. Reliance and instrument quality can support that outcome, but the answer never reconciles them with the burden it announced.

The system count is wrong. The case table has six `NONCOMPLIANT` results (703, 705, 708, 712, 714, and 715). Section 5.2 says five and enumerates only the first five, dropping the disclosure breach. SUP-712 is also over-charged relative to the answer’s own standard: D5 treats same-person identity as unproven, severe findings require clear and convincing evidence that survives identity challenge, and the case is still high-severity noncompliance. Medium confidence only partly repairs that. A smaller factual slip appears in the prosecution concessions: SUP-702 is cited as a quality-before-sanctions pattern, but its trace is sanctions, then auto-approval, then activation, then quality.

The revised architecture is the right design: POWL for routes and ordering, DECLARE-style rules for cross-route obligations, tabular checks for amount, time, and eligibility, and organizational controls for authority and expiry. The required attributes are all specified. The Python is real PM4Py-style POWL, reads no files, keeps sanctions and quality concurrent on the standard route, separates manual, auto, and emergency routes, makes notification mandatory before closure, and states the request-more-info over-approximation. Loop orientation is right: `LOOP(τ, a)` is zero-or-more and `LOOP(a, τ)` is one-or-more. Sanctions-before-activation is preserved on every activating route.

The formal layer still contains a serious operator error. DECLARE `precedence(A, B)` means B may occur only if A has already occurred, so A is mandatory whenever B occurs. `precedence(Request_More_Info, {Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval})` therefore makes a request mandatory before every approval. The text says the rule is only meant to stop a request after the decision. The correct shape is a negative response from approval to `Request_More_Info`. As written, the constraint would flag the compliant baseline, SUP-701. A second modeling gap is unstated: `Reuse_Valid_Certification` is an alternative to quality review on every standard route, including brand-new registrations, not only reactivation. That silently weakens CS-9’s quality gate. Section 7.5 then overclaims that SUP-702 and SUP-704 fit M1 once provisional-activation labels exist. The auto route also requires `Master_Data_Validation`, and the emergency route requires at least one monitoring event; neither is in those traces. “Identical alignment cost” for SUP-704 and SUP-708 is asserted without an alignment and is implausible: only SUP-708 places activation before sanctions.

These are localized faults in an otherwise unusually careful review, but they affect the burden, a case verdict, the system tally, and the proposed constraint layer. That keeps the work well above a merely competent split-the-difference memo and clearly below near-flawless.
