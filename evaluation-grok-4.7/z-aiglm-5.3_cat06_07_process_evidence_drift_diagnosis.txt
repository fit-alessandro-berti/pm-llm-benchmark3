The final answer is a strong, correctly triangulated audit. It separates April as a schema/pipeline failure (healthy mature labels, corrupted prefixes, over-prediction), June as a routing mix shift with stable within-variant breach rates, July–September as supplier-driven within-V3 concept/process drift that aggregate calibration hides, September–October as a Partner Z population shift, and October as informative censoring rather than a real drop to a 0.16 breach rate. Mix-weighted reconstructions (about 0.16 / 0.31 / 0.34) match the tables, falsifiers are stated, remediation is staged and proportionate, and label-delay alerts are explicitly blocked from triggering retraining. All seven required tests and all eight monitor types are present.

It is not nearly flawless. Numeric sequences are often written without separators (`0.350.460.640.61`, `4265130148`, `V1 4330%`, `17%2%`), so core evidence is harder to read than it should be. “Support extension” is the wrong term for features that were never in the model (omitted context, not out-of-support covariates). Mix-implied rates are used as if precise, without stating that they weight case-start shares by matured variant rates. July is called aggregate-calibrated because means match, despite ECE roughly double the baseline. In the sketch, case-start variant shares and parts-edge medians are right-censored for open recent cases, and event-window DFGs can invent directly-follows pairs; both biases sit on the months under audit and are not flagged. Duplicate counts are computed and then dropped from the returned summary.
