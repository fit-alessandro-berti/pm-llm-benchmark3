The diagnosis is substantively right: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change with stable remote timing, August–September stack parts-path deterioration with mix and (from September) population change, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The six-row table, staged train/calibrate decision, alert gating, and leakage warning about post-diagnosis paths are all in the right direction. The score is held down by scoping errors, one internal contradiction on the point the case most depends on, underused discriminators, and a monitoring sketch that mis-reports its own audit trail.

The covariate row is the weakest cell. Queue missingness of 2–3% is a data-quality residue that is already 3% in May, before the June policy, so it is not evidence of June-onward covariate drift. Partner Z’s rural distance and device age begin in September, but they are written into the same supporting-evidence sentence as a period described as strongest in August–October. August has a real V3 mix shift and no Partner Z; the cell blurs those mechanisms. Confidence is split carefully, which limits the damage, but the evidence column still mis-attributes.

Label delay is correctly timed and correctly blocked from explaining April–June, then overstated. The table says the direction of October bias is not established. The case states that the backlog hits longer resolution paths, and those paths (especially V3) have much higher mature breach rates, so the expected direction is downward. Section 2 then says the 0.16 rate “could be strongly downward-biased.” Both the agnosticism and the later hedge cannot be the final claim. The useful next sentence is missing: predicted risk 0.23 versus observed 0.16 is the miscalibration signature censoring would manufacture, so October ECE must not trigger recalibration.

Other discriminators are left on the table. June keeps AUROC near 0.79 while mean risk stays 0.20 against a mature breach rate of 0.15 — base-rate shift with ranking largely intact. August–September flip the sign (about 0.19–0.21 predicted versus 0.28–0.31 observed). July’s aggregate looks almost calibrated (0.18 versus 0.19) while matured V3 breach has already moved to 0.46. September’s non-Partner-Z observed rate is already 0.27, so Partner Z cannot be the whole September gap, and that comparison is itself maturity-biased. The narrative reaches the right months without using these contrasts to separate the mechanisms.

The transport test has the same hole. Reweighting on the legacy pre-score features can look acceptable precisely because rural distance and device age were never in the model. The write-up says to add those features later and to refuse transport when support is poor; it does not say a legacy-feature transport can miss the shift the population evidence is about.

The code meets the requested library calls, canonicalizes the renamed diagnosis before extraction, separates event-calendar from case-start cohorts, and does not invent a drift detector. The audit metric does not. Deduping is applied globally, then every case-start row is given `duplicate_rows_removed_from_month = 0`, so one of the two required cohorts reports a clean extract that was not clean. Preferring `event_id` when present will keep the incident’s duplicates if re-ingestion assigned new ids; there is no timestamp sort before `keep="first"`. `grouper_freq="M"` and a performance-DFG value that is sometimes a stats dict are fragile relative to current pandas and PM4Py, and dumping `temporal_features` as full records fights the “monthly summary” requirement. “Existing operational fallback” is not in the evidence.

Section 6 and the retrain gates are proportionate: immature long paths, April calibrators, ignored supplier delay, unstratified Partner Z fairness claims, June-as-concept-drift, and post-score wait leakage are the right failure modes, each with a mitigation that matches the prediction timestamp. That is why this stays a high score. It is not near-flawless, because the central table mis-scopes covariates, the October bias direction is stated two ways, and the process-evidence code would audit the case-start cohort incorrectly.
