The final answer covers the required six sections, ranks every action, uses the conformance labels correctly, and keeps Actions A and B at the bottom as currently nonconformant. It does not treat re-screening, a SAR, or self-certification as a substitute for independent review, and it accounts for P-901, the API quota, and the S4 timing split.

The headline ranking is not well supported. The only stated review duration is 35–75 minutes, yet the top plan assumes an S4 review of 20–45 minutes and a favorable completion window of 60–90 minutes. That unsupported shortening makes waiting for S4 look more deadline-feasible than the case data allow. The final recommendation also blurs S4 becoming available in 35–65 minutes with the review itself finishing in 35–45 minutes.

The time-boxed fallback is logically soft. If S4 is not confirmed until about 40 minutes and the plan then interrupts P-901, P-884 still absorbs most of a 40–90 minute review and P-901 still takes the 0.12–0.28 spillover. That branch is generally worse than starting with S2, and the answer never weighs it against the limited chance that S4 is early. Section 5 notes the 35-versus-65 sensitivity, but the primary recommendation still rests on the optimistic timing.

A lesser looseness is the suggestion that refreshed screening can by itself clear the way to execution. Even if a new screen removes the risk-or-fuzzy-match trigger, execution still needs a valid approval path, and auto-clear is limited to no alert and a score below 40. Benefit figures in the table are mostly gross €0–€35k ranges rather than values discounted by the 0.55–0.75 approval chance, although later prose partly corrects that.

The conformance analysis, unlawful-shortcut rejection, and spillover discussion are strong. The unsupported review time and the unresolved dominance of immediate S2 review over a late fallback keep it short of a near-flawless answer.
