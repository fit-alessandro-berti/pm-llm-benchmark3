5.0

The temporal model is mostly sound, and 10 of 11 release verdicts are defensible, but the formalization is not well-formed, the required checker is only a sketch, and two of the four mandated edge cases are reasoned incorrectly.

Section 1 correctly forward-fills blanks, refuses to project final state backward, and separates event, effective, record, and evidence time. It never states the required default that an attribute update is effective at its own timestamp unless evidence says otherwise, and “material change” is left as a vague comparison with the previous review rather than the >20% amount-increase test.

Section 2 has the right columns, but as written the trigger conditions are missing their operators (`jurisdiction DE`, `amount 100000`, `amount(C,t,A)100000`). R4 is mis-modeled as an independent duty to perform a new Enhanced_Review; it only invalidates a prior review for amount-dependent obligations. The Datalog is not executable: R3’s negation does not bind the screen time or match the updated jurisdiction, R1 relies on an undefined `last_material_change`, and R6–R7 are absent. R2 does not require the Enhanced_Review to post-date the HIGH/PEP change, nor that a standard-packet approval is insufficient. R5 omits the actual evidential rule that a displayed backdate is not conclusive.

Case results: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 are classified correctly, and TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT is an acceptable reading of weak, interested-party evidence. TX-302 is not. Enhanced_Review at 09:20 is before release, with no later material change, so R1 is satisfied directly; EX-77 is unnecessary. “Deviations: None” plus JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is internally contradictory, and the high confidence is unwarranted. TX-306 is compliant, but R1 is also triggered and satisfied; only R2 is listed, and the sub-20% increase is not shown as the reason the earlier review survives. “Satisfied obligations” is used for events that satisfy nothing (TX-305’s amount-less review, TX-307’s Standard_Review, TX-311 when no rule fires).

Section 4 covers the required cases, but the TX-302 sensitivity is false: disproving EX-77 would leave the case COMPLIANT. The TX-311 counterfactual is also false. Reversing event_index 3 and 4 still applies DE before release, so R1 still does not trigger; order changes only the jurisdiction visible to the review. TX-305 is handled correctly.

Section 5 meets only the ingest steps: `StringIO`, timestamps, `event_index`, `format_dataframe`, sort, and `ffill`. It does not track the latest reviews, screens, list updates, or material changes; it does not compute R1–R4 flags, join the exemption register, or emit a case-level diagnostics frame with evidence times and unknown-value flags. `head()` does not even show every release. No DECLARE or trace-order check appears.

Section 6 names the six required failure modes and a few more, but only as a list, without showing how each one misfires on a specific case. Section 7 does include immutable release payloads, signature/effective/record time, rule version, exemption scope, change provenance, and a monotonic sequence number.
