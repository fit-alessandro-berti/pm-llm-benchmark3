7.4

The forecast is complete, balanced, and aligned with the required structure: all seven sections are present, East is treated differently from North and South, averages are not the decision metric, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The mechanism map, three scenarios, and indicator split are coherent and usable.

It is not nearly flawless. Several analytical and design errors are material.

The before/after sketch splits the log on event time. Cases that cross the cut are truncated, so cycle time, variants, handovers, and SLA comparisons are biased. That is the wrong unit for a pre/post process comparison; cases should be assigned by start or completion and in-flight cases excluded. The sketch also executes a placeholder `filter_event_attribute_values` on `time:timestamp` with an empty value list, then passes `resource_key` and `activity_key` into organizational-mining calls whose usual public signatures do not take those arguments. `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are used without a start timestamp, which this log does not have. The fairness loop claims priority control and then filters only on high complexity.

The pilot does not identify the pooling effect it is meant to test. An East-only Technical Shared Service does not give East access to North/South slack, while moving all three specialists and still treating North/South as controls contaminates the controls. Stepped-wedge language does not fix that interference. Learning erosion is a stated risk and is missing from the stop-the-line guardrails.

Capacity arithmetic in the source tables is underused. Legal/Risk at 91–93% utilization cannot take a 9→12 referral increase and stay near a 10.5h wait unless capacity is added; the optimistic scenario waves this through with “well-staffed.” Removing one of three supervisors while approval handovers rise from 42 to 49 is a large load increase per remaining approver, not a small standardization effect. Summing the management handover table gives roughly 343 to 359 handovers per 100 cases, so the proposal’s own numbers do not show a net handover reduction. The text notes offsetting queue handovers but never checks that total.

Guardrail wording (“tolerance,” “preset margin,” “safe operating band”) is too vague to operate.
