7.1

The reconstruction is competent and mostly coherent: missing-start bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are feasible, open and censored cases are retained, central WIP and queue integrals check out (S1 queue ≈ 4.14, peak 7; S2 queue ≈ 2.14, peak 5; total WIP ≈ 9.51, peak 13, 8 left at 14:00), central utilizations match the busy-time arithmetic, and the S1-then-S2 bottleneck handoff is supported by the rate imbalance. The Python meets the required semantics and correctly refuses to treat PM4Py case metrics as station metrics.

Those strengths do not make the write-up reliable. A6 calls the central imputation work-conserving, but P07 at 09:30 and P15 at 11:45 each leave a server idle for 5 minutes with that case already queued; only the early bounds are work-conserving. The same standard is then overused in §6. I2 is idle from 13:45 to 14:00 while P22 has been waiting since 12:10, so “every observed handover is ≤ 10 minutes, therefore idling with a queue is implausible” is false. That missed anomaly is exactly the evidence that should have qualified the preference for the early/central scenarios.

Several published figures are wrong or internally inconsistent. Fully observed S1 services are 20–30 minutes, not 20–35. Completed S2 waits average about 32 minutes and top out at 65 (P14); 75 is P15, who is still open, so “completed cases (5 → 75)” and “largest completed wait (75)” are mislabeled. The 12:00–14:00 S2 service mean is about 39 minutes for the six completions, not ≈ 46. Subperiod WIP is oversimplified: S2 is about 1–3 then 2–5, not 1–2 then 3–4, and S1’s peak of 8 is intermittent from 10:10, not a block at 11:30–12:10. Section 4’s utilization floors (I1 245 min / 68%, C1 75%, C2 71%) are the zero-service bounds that A7 explicitly excluded; the late scenario in §6 is about 71%, 76%, and 72%. The Little’s Law counterexample (0.05/min × 137 min) matches neither 14 departures in 360 minutes nor the stated mean completed sojourn of ≈ 146. “Monotonically rising” waits and “25–50 minute” late idles (actual extra idles are about 20–30 minutes) are further overstatements.

The main congestion and handoff conclusions survive these errors. The secondary table, the work-conservation weighting, and the claimed precision do not.
