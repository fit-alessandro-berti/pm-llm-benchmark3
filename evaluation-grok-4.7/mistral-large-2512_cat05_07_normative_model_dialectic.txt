The answer covers every required section and reaches several defensible case calls, but it is not a sound conformance judgment. Authority is misstated, both briefs fail their assigned duties, matrix logic is repeatedly inverted, and the revised POWL does not express the routes it claims to add.

## 1. Stipulated facts and burden
Concurrency is treated as a CS-9 duty. CS-9 requires both checks before activation; only M0 leaves them unordered. Notification-before-close is likewise treated as a CS-9 rule. CS-9 never mentions notification or closure, yet that invented duty drives the SUP-706 charge and the burden table.

The burden standard is unstable. One passage lets prosecution treat M0’s silence as inclusion of rejected leads; another correctly requires an affirmative rule. Harm is made an element of noncompliance, which collapses rule breach into loss. The Compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 is omitted from the stipulated record and then omitted again when the defense calls the pilot authorized.

## 2. Dialectical fairness
The prosecution does not acknowledge genuine model or data defects. It charges all 47 `Request_More_Info` cases, the in-window FD-7 case, eligible PD-3 cases, the label rename, and abbreviated rejection as if they were the same kind of breach. That is not the strongest prosecution. The winnable case concedes SUP-701, SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, and SUP-713, then concentrates on ineligible auto-approval, post-expiry continuation, sanctions-after-activation, separation of duties, and undisclosed conflict.

The defense fabricates or inflates facts. It says SUP-703 had no transaction before quality review and was “corrected”; the record shows a EUR 180,000 purchase and no correction. It says about 70% of missing notifications have stable CRM IDs; 70% is the mirrored share, so the missing share is the rest. “93% eligible or indeterminate” and “7% ineligible” do not match 289, 14, and 9 out of 312. The six August cases are folded into the June–July 312. SUP-708 is called procedural despite Internal Audit’s severe finding, and the post-activation manual approval is ignored. Belief that a permanent waiver was imminent is treated as authority. SUP-715 is absent from both briefs.

## 3. Case conclusions
SUP-701, SUP-703, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are directionally right. Several others are not:

- SUP-705 is labeled MIXED while the text says the dominant result is NONCOMPLIANT. The primary label should be the dominant one. “FD-7 (implicit)” is not an overlay; the directive expired by its own terms.
- SUP-706 is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE and, in the same cell, dominantly COMPLIANT. A mutable text link written two days later does not establish case linkage. The CS-9 notification theory is unsupported; the real issue is M0 logging under N4.
- SUP-708’s matrix names only activation-before-sanctions. Manual approval is also after activation, a second CS-9 breach.
- SUP-709 ignores CS-9’s abbreviated pre-screen sentence. Scope is unproved; “both checks are mandatory even without activation” is not established.
- SUP-711 is a proved duplicate with one source transaction ID. After deduplication the trace fits M0. INDETERMINATE overstates uncertainty.
- SUP-712’s reversal test is “proof of harm.” Alias evidence that the actors are different humans, or a recorded delegation, would reverse it.
- SUP-715 breaches the disclosure duty between quality reviewer and approver. CS-9’s distinct-human rule concerns requester and manual approver. The system judgment merges those rules.
- SUP-702’s log omits `Notify_Requester`; CRM evidence is a stable `notification_id`. The matrix says nothing is missing. PD-3’s automated master-data validation is never modeled or tested in any case.

The “what would reverse” column is logically backward for SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-714: the stated test would strengthen the adverse finding. SUP-712 and SUP-715 repeat the harm error.

## 4. Revised model
The layering idea is the right one: POWL for control flow, data checks for eligibility, amount, and time, separate constraints for actors, and an overlay register for temporary authority. The implementation does not carry that design.

The LOOP uses `[Request_More_Info, checks]`. In PM4Py that executes `Request_More_Info` at least once and uses the checks block as the redo child. SUP-701 is therefore nonconforming to the proposed normative model. `SilentTransition` is imported and unused; the usual zero-or-more encoding, LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info), is absent. The same `checks` object is also a predecessor of that loop, so sanctions and quality must finish before any information request. SUP-707 cannot be replayed.

Because those checks sit before the XOR, quality is mandatory before auto-approval, provisional activation, and rejection. The model therefore cannot represent PD-3’s post-activation quality review, FD-7’s provisional-then-retrospective path, or an early sanctions rejection. The emergency branch is a lone provisional activation, with no retrospective approval or later quality review. The limitation note does not state this. It says the routes were added.

Section 6 then introduces `FD-7-EXT` for 2026-07-13 through 2026-07-31. No extension exists. Publishing it as normative architecture would ratify SUP-705. Section 7 drops that overlay, so the two proposals disagree. PD-3’s table also invents a monthly review cadence and omits master-data validation and the two-business-day quality limit. The event schema does include stable notification IDs, rule and overlay IDs, aliases, delegation, and dual timestamps. It still lacks an immutable eligibility snapshot, a deduplication key, a COI-disclosure flag, and a ban on late mutable case links.

## 5. Bottom line
Structure, coverage, and several high-stakes classifications are real merits. They do not offset the CS-9 misreads, one-sided briefs, invented numbers and authority, contradictory labels, and a POWL whose standard path forces the extra activity while its exception paths still require the control they were meant to relax.
