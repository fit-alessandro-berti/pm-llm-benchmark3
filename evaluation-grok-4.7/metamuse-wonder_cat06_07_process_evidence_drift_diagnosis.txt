The diagnosis is strong and correctly non-homogeneous: April is a schema/pipeline failure, June is a V2 mix shift with stable within-variant risk, August–September stacks supplier performance, V3 concept change, and Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is censored rather than a recovery. The table, falsifiers, staged train/calibrate decision, alert gates, and failure modes all match that reading. The score is held down by code that would not run and by one mismatched falsifier.

The required PM4Py sketch misuses two of the APIs it is asked to call. On a dataframe, `pm4py.get_variants` maps each variant to an integer count, so `len(kv[1])` raises `TypeError`. `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` maps edges to a float duration in seconds (default mean), not a dict, so `pdfg.get(edge).get("median")` raises `AttributeError` and would not be a median even if the access were fixed. The duplicate rate is computed once on the whole file, including `event_id` in the key, then copied onto every month, so it cannot flag the April duplication incident and can miss copies that received new ids. The event-calendar cohort is only a row count, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is reduced to a swallowed exception plus a length.

The covariate-drift falsifier is wrong relative to the row’s own evidence. Stable feature distributions within variant would not falsify the June mix shift; that shift is covariate drift even when within-variant features are unchanged. Smaller slips: baseline ECE is given as 0.025→0.110 (January, not the March 0.024 comparator), and July’s 0.18 vs 0.19 mean gap is called under-prediction when the real signals are the V3 rate jump and the AUROC drop. There is no explicit mix-versus-rate standardization, though the qualitative split of June versus August is right.
