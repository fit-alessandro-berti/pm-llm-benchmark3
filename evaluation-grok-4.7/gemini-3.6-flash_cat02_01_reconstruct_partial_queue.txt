4.9

The case-level reconstruction is the only part that is mostly reliable. Fully observed waits, services, and completed sojourns check out, the required cases are present, and the resource-precedence windows for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are essentially right. Open and waiting cases are kept and marked with elapsed-time inequalities. That is not enough to carry the rest of the document.

Station aggregates contradict both the raw log and the case table. With the answer’s own central starts, mean S1 wait over the 20 S1 completions is 65 minutes, not 61.5; mean S2 wait over the 14 S2 completions is about 33.6 minutes, not 36.4. Fully observed S1 service averages about 28 minutes with a maximum of 30, so “observed 20–35” treats an imputation bound as data. Fully observed S2 service averages 40 minutes, not 39.2. The reported central service means (26.8 and 34.6) do not equal the means implied by section 3. Busy-time ranges of 560–630 and 535–585 minutes are likewise too high: in-window S1 occupancy is fixed at 535 minutes plus P07 and P15 in [0, 35] each, and S2 occupancy is fixed at 515 minutes plus P05 in [0, 30] and P11 in [0, 25]. The printed utilizations are arithmetic transforms of those unsupported busy times.

WIP is wrong where it is most checkable. S1 occupancy depends only on arrival and S1 completion, so missing starts do not affect it. The maximum is 8, and the time average over 08:00–14:00 is about 5.8, not a peak of 9 at 11:50–12:00 and a mean of 5.3. S2 reaches 7 from 13:45 to 13:50, while P14 is still in service and P20 has already arrived; the terminal state of 6 is not the peak. Section 4 says the S1 queue builds to 6, while section 5 says it peaks at 7. Those are different imputation regimes, but they are both stated as facts. “S1 fully cleared its queue by 13:30” is false: P22 is still waiting at 14:00, and P21 is still in service.

The Little’s Law paragraph is the clearest failure of uncertainty handling. The 08:30–12:30 window is transient. The arrival count is off (P22 falls inside it). The sojourn plugged into \(L=\lambda W\) is not the sojourn of that arrival cohort, and it uses 65 minutes after section 4 used 61.5. Time-average S1 WIP in that window is about 6.8, not 6.10. The claimed 1% agreement is not evidence that the reconstruction is consistent.

Imputation is internally inconsistent. Section 2 sets a latest start at end minus 5 minutes and says the midpoint matches historical medians of roughly 25–30 and 35–40 minutes. Section 3 allows 0-minute service. The resulting central services of 15–20 minutes cannot be those medians, and for P05 and P11 a 35–40 minute service is outside the feasible window. Section 6 then switches to a 5-minute floor and reports scenario means that fall outside the feasible averages implied by the same bounds. Total sojourn being invariant to a missing start is correct; calling peak WIP, service means, and the bottleneck diagnosis “100% invariant” and “completely solid” overstates a four-point imputation exercise. Rounding of ±2.5 minutes is assumed and then never applied. The 15-minute C2 outage is treated as a cause of the S2 shift without comparing 15 resource-minutes with hour-scale waits.

The code does not reproduce the analysis. `parse_time` collapses blank, `MISSING`, and `OPEN` to null; later branches only partly separate them. Missing starts are silently replaced by one midpoint inside the PM4Py log. There is no uncertainty scenario, no WIP series, and no C2 availability calculation, despite the comment claiming those are why pandas is safer. `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is called and then omitted from the printed table. The comment alternative to `convert_log_to_time_intervals` satisfies the wording of that requirement only.

Format and the deterministic case table are real strengths. The aggregate claims, the false Little’s Law confirmation, and the non-reproducing code are large enough errors that the write-up is not a dependable reconstruction.
