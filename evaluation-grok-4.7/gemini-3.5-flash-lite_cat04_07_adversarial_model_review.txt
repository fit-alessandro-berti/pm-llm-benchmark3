4.8

The memo is complete, well structured, and right in its broad governance posture: neither submitted model is fit for enforcement, positive-log precision is not a policy test, and a block-structured backbone plus external constraints is the right family of alternative. Those merits do not survive contact with the formal languages. Several accept/reject claims are false, the third model’s audit does not match its own code, and two required review topics are missing. A board that acted on this document would misclassify both candidates and would approve a replacement that does not implement the policy it claims.

Model S is a sequence of Register Dossier, a parallel (`+`) of four `X(activity, τ)` options, an exclusive approval, and Release Order. That language allows any subset of the four checks, each at most once, in any order. It does not contain `Correct Dossier`, a loop, or a repeated check. The review misnames `+` as inclusive choice and then asserts infinite rechecks and acceptance of B6 and B8. Those traces are outside the language under either the parallel reading or an inclusive-choice reading. The same memo later says Model S enforces exactly one approval, then marks Adv-3 (both approvals) as accepted. Skip traces B3 and B4, and the free interleaving of Expedite Review with the checks, are genuine defects; the repetition claims are not. Intentional optionality of Supplier Review and Expedite Review is also not separated from the accidental optionalizing of the two mandatory checks.

Model C is an exclusive union of eight concrete sequences. Training fitness 0.973 already falsifies the claim that it covers every observed positive trace. B2 (Technical Review before Credit Check, then Auto Approval) is not among the eight branches; the matrix marks it accepted. Auto Approval occurs on only one fixed check order and never with Supplier Review or Expedite Review. Expedite-before-Supplier is absent. The single correction branch always replays both checks. High training precision is therefore memorization of a thin whitelist, which the narrative gets directionally right while overstating “absolute” precision and zero ambiguity. The reported duplicate-label count of 33 is undocumented and is not reconciled with the tree; the later claim that duplicates bypass alignment state-space checks is false. Duplicates enlarge and ambiguate the alignment space.

The third model gets the mandatory pair, exactly-one approval, and “no repeat without correction” structurally right, and B1, B3, B4, B5, B7, and B8 are plausible under the code. It does not do what section 8 says. The loop body is the full partial order of both checks, so B6 (recheck Technical Review only) is rejected, as is Adv-2, which also skips approval. P4 allows Expedite Review on either side of Supplier Review; the backbone forces Supplier then Expedite. The same silent transition is shared by two operators, and `operator="loop"` / `"xor"` is not the PM4Py `Operator` enum, so the required self-contained construction is unlikely to convert. The declarative dictionaries are comments, not a conformance check. P6 is the stakeholder gap both original models miss, and this design neither represents “only Credit Check,” “only Technical Review,” or “both,” nor flags that limitation honestly.

Metric interpretation is uneven. The table covers every reported number and correctly refuses a single weighted score. The top pathology then says Model S’s precision is artificially inflated; the reported value is 0.584, and alignment precision does punish unobserved model behavior. The actual gap is that the measure never sees a negative suite and cannot tell a forbidden skip from an unseen legal combination. Token-based generalization is treated as a permissiveness score; it mainly rewards small models whose transitions fire often and punishes enumerated branches. Silent-transition moves of cost zero, which make mandatory skips fitness-free, and alignment-cost sensitivity are required topics and are not analyzed as such. “Leakage” is the wrong name for an overfitting drop. Holdout fitness of 0.981 is presented as generalization rather than as evidence that the model’s language is large.

The validation section names seeds, a negative suite, perturbations, and separate quality dimensions, and it does not invent new scores. It still omits a documented comprehension protocol and a reachability check. “Enforcement precision = 1.0” is the wrong metric name for a true-rejection rule, and Adv-1 confounds a supplier-on-internal violation with skipped checks and skipped approval. The dissent is a real counterargument, but at about 143 words it misses the required 150–250, and its reversal conditions would endorse Model S for reasons that do not repair mandatory-check skipping.
