6.2

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and treats activation as selection rather than ordinary censoring. The leakage table and the conditional-versus-competing-versus-counterfactual distinction are the strongest parts. It is not a reliable study protocol, because the maturity clock, the validation window, and the prediction-time rule contradict each other.

The maturity rule is wrong and inconsistent. `label_available_at` is already the time the feed arrived, so adding another 35 days double-counts the delay. The sensitivity cutoff is also too late: with extraction on 15 February 2026, activation plus 90 days plus a 35-day feed must fall on or before about 13 October 2025, not 31 October or 8 November. The primary test slice, case starts on or after 15 October 2025, is therefore almost empty of mature 90-day labels. December starts cannot have a matured horizon at all. Conditioning that slice on the few fast activations with short feeds selects on speed and feed timing, so it does not test the migration. The quarterly backtest has the same hole: no embargo between prediction time and label availability, so training uses outcomes that would not yet exist.

The prediction point is not one rule. Section 1 uses the first credit review or the third event, before contract or activation. Section 4 switches to the first three events, or seven days from start, without stopping at the decision boundary. A three-event or seven-day prefix can already contain acceptance, rejection, contract, or activation. Truncation bias from dropping fast activations is never discussed. Transfer “bounds” are mislabeled: setting unlinked transfers equal to the linked rate is not a worst case, and the activated default rate is not a bound for them. Inverse-probability weighting does not recover default risk for the other 26,400 applications unless positivity and ignorability hold; calling that an approximation to all 48,000 overstates what is identified. Only one of the three “deployment” shifts is actually caused by deployment. The policy and migration items are external changes that already occurred.

The skeleton does not implement the audit it describes. `pm4py.filter_log` is not a PM4Py API. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then ignored; the feature table is built from case start, region, and the label, not from prefix events. Post-prefix events are not removed. Cohort output never reconstructs the 48,000-way flow, and the leakage “check” only prints a drop list. Label maturity is applied once at the final extract date, then intersected with the time split, so the code repeats the embargo error.
