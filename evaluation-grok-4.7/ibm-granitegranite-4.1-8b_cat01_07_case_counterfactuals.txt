The write-up covers every required section, but its counterfactuals are not temporally or procedurally reliable enough to use. The feasibility model, the case paths, and the rollout numbers contradict the rules, the snapshots, and each other.

The partial order is not a valid reading of the normative rules. It forces Identity Check before Document Check both with and without originals, so it forbids the concurrency Rule 3 already allows and that K-411 is trying to restore. It also places Compliance Review before every Decision, which would make the factual K-219 and K-411 paths illegal. Originals Receipt is downgraded to a “preference.” The post-request rule — new Document Check and new Assessment, then a new or reaffirmed compliance review on high-risk or high-value cases — is missing. Several constraint lines are syntactically broken, so the precedence they were meant to state is not actually stated.

K-337 is the clearest feasibility failure. The case is high-risk and high-value, so Compliance Review must follow the latest Assessment and precede Decision. The table path does the reverse: Decision, then Compliance, then a second Decision. Section 3 then says compliance follows Assessment 2. The same case cannot have both paths. The 12–24 hour saving counts only a sliver of service time and drops the dominant delay, Request More Info 2 from Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00, plus the later queue delay into Wednesday. Section 4 makes this worse by “cutting two rework cycles” and claiming about 15 hours and 1,500 hours a week. The intervention stops only the second loop. A clean approve is also treated as the best case, even though the unresolved discrepancy may not be ignored; the factual approve followed resolution of that discrepancy.

K-219 reverses the local mechanism. Originals arrived Mon 17:30 and D1 did not start until Tue 09:30, while D2’s Monday-evening queue was much shorter. That is roughly an overnight queue gap, not “about 2 hours,” and D2 is not faster on difficult handwriting. The factual check had no rework. The table says the intervention avoids rework and lengthens cycle time by 2–6 hours; the same row and Section 3 then say D2 may create a clarification loop; Section 5 says the likely result is a 1-hour gain. Rework of 4–6 hours is also below the stated 8–36 hour external-response range before any new check or assessment. “D2 works while I2 completes” is false: I2 finished at 16:20.

K-104 misplaces the intervention. Preliminary compliance is allowed while identity and document checks run, and it can shorten the later final review by about 1–3 hours; it does not replace that review and it does not run as a step sequenced after Assessment. The path lists it after Assessment while calling it parallel. D1’s complex-document occupancy and A1’s Tuesday queue still bind, so a 4–8 hour case-level saving is asserted rather than derived. “No extra processing time” is wrong: a preliminary reading is extra C1 work. The worst case is a spillover onto other files, not an incomplete early packet on K-104 itself.

K-411 confuses clocks. Document Check started at 15:00 and finished at 19:30; it did not finish at 15:00, and a 4.5-hour check opened at triage cannot finish by 12:30. Earlier readiness is treated as earlier completion even though Assessment is the likely next constraint. A2’s factual Tuesday-evening occupancy, including K-219 at 20:00–22:30, is never used, despite the instruction to hold other behavior fixed.

The 100-case analysis names interference but does not quantify it. One hundred provisional reservations cannot delay other compliance work by 4–8 hours: C1 is a single reviewer with a 3–6 hour service time, so the policy swamps the week. Shifting work to D2 is described as lengthening later D1 waits. Earlier assessment arrival is said to free assessment slots and raise throughput; on a staffed bottleneck it reorders and queues work. The required contrast between timestamp behavior and true concurrency is absent. The 30–40% rework rate is invented.

The ranking weights speed over the criteria it was told to use. K-337 is called highly robust and insulated from external response time, although its only large saving is external response time, and it changes the decision, appeal, loss, and compliance exposure on high-risk, high-value files. That is the dangerous fast intervention. K-104’s reserved single reviewer is the local result least transportable to a standing policy; K-411 is a weaker choice for that role. K-219’s routing rule is more reversible than a decision already issued, not less. Assumption 8 says a single-person C1 reservation does not affect other work because C1 is a single person, which reverses the interference claim made earlier.
