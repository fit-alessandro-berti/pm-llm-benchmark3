The answer covers all seven required sections, stays mostly faithful to the given counts and rates, and draws the right central line: protecting hospital flow is not resilience when authorization, temperature, dual-control, or chain-of-custody evidence is dropped, and the stabilization arc `Invoice close -> Audit remediation` (2,300) is a compliance tail rather than proof of recovery. Temporal reading is incomplete. T1 (morning outage) is given “dominant variants” of manual improvisation that the timeline places in T2. T3 is described as already doing manual reconciliation, but that activity appears in the stabilization DFG, and T3’s own cycle times are omitted (medians 9h / 38h / 84h; p90 18h / 90h / 160h). The p90 path is the clearer non-recovery: pharmacy 49h → 118h → 90h → 74h and bulk 72h → 180h → 160h → 120h. Calling traceability at 2.2% “~3×” baseline (2.2 / 0.8 = 2.75) is a small looseness on top of that.

Abduction is softer than the logs. The hospital fast lane is treated as a bypass only “if” urgency is allowed to skip checks. In the manual DFG, fast lane → snapshot is 920 of 1,000, with no authorization activity on that variant; the narrative’s “sometimes” and the graph conflict, and the answer does not resolve it. Pharmacist release and dual-control are not shown to be the same control. Classification drifts. Section 3 puts E under true, control-preserving resilience even though the observed pattern was later scan reconstruction and a 12.5% rework rate. H is a risky shortcut there and a low-risk keep in the table. F is “true resilience” in the prose and “modify” in the table because it missed cold-chain records. The recovery model has the required pieces: triggers, triage, minimum controls, evidence packets, backlog split, and reconciliation versus audit exit. It is still partly schematic. The closing evidence test is mis-aimed: an offline capture system that restores near-baseline completeness would reduce the need to accept delay, not justify dropping authorization, temperature logs, dual-control, or handoff evidence.
