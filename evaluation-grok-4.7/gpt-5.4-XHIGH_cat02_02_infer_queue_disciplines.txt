6.5

The write-up is a real decision-point analysis with the required structure, labeled assumptions, and mostly correct FIFO, priority, and SPT predictions. It is not close to flawless: several pivotal choice sets and explanations are wrong or incomplete, and two confidences are materially higher than the evidence supports.

The clearest factual miss is QC at 10:54. C07 arrived at 10:40 and was waiting, but the eligible set is given as only C01, C05, and C06. Priority still points at C05 and SPT at C06, so the row does not reverse, yet the omitted job changes the SLA story: C07’s 12:00 deadline was still feasible, and serving C05 first helps make C07 late. The same counterfactual is missed again at 11:40. Starting C07 then would finish about 11:58, inside its deadline, and C01 would still finish before 13:00. Calling C01 an anti-starvation override is possible, but it is an override that sacrifices a still-savable deadline, not a harmless aging tie-break.

C05 was already unsavable on arrival: 10:11 plus an estimated 40 minutes is 10:51, one minute past 10:50. There is no moment in this trace at which C05 “becomes” endangered. It was skipped at 10:18 and again at 10:32, then taken at 10:54, when it was already late. That is weak evidence for an SLA-triggered policy switch. C04 at 10:32 matches none of FIFO, priority, SPT, or earliest deadline, and the answer says so, but a primary SPT diagnosis at 68 still treats a half-contradicted trace as fairly well identified. A myopic SPT rule fails three of the six free decisions. One alternative the reconstruction never tests is that the successor was fixed when the previous job started: at 10:18, C06 did not yet exist and C04 was the shortest remaining job, which would explain 10:32 without an unlogged readiness state.

On QD, assumption A3 does not explain D-F’s idle period. D05’s 30-minute estimate does not fit before the 12:00 meeting. D10’s 12-minute estimate does: D-F is free and electrical-qualified from 11:36 until 12:00, and D10 is priority 1. Section 5 repeats the overclaim by saying overlap avoidance “directly” explains the idle through 12:20. The 12:16 assignment of D10 to D-X is also less structural than claimed, because D-F could have taken D10 earlier. Separately, D-X’s 11:20–11:30 idle is not fully explained by holding the 11:30 appointment if pure mechanical work is eligible: D08 is estimated at 8 minutes and would finish before 11:30. That gap needed an explicit buffer, hard-hold, or “mechanical stays on D-M” argument. Confidence 90 sits poorly beside the answer’s own point that a global skill queue and per-resource FIFO subqueues are hard to separate. At 11:48, D07 over D10 is treated as local FIFO, but it is equally “do the job only D-X can do.” That equivalence is exactly the kind this trace requires.

On QB, the headline contradiction at 09:48 depends on B05 being visible. B05 arrived at 09:46, and the prompt allows arrival scans to lag by up to two minutes. If B05 was not yet scanned, priority among the visible waiting jobs also selects B03, so this episode does not cleanly kill priority. Section 5 then says scan delay is too small to overturn the major patterns, which is not true of this pattern. The table also drops B01 from the eligible set at the moment B01 is stopped, which makes FIFO look cleaner than a preemption decision is. The stronger, underused fact is that B05 is priority 1 and does not preempt B03 during 09:48–10:00, so the earlier B02 preemption does not generalize to priority-1 preemption. The episode that actually cuts against the stated resume/FIFO hybrid is 10:00, when B05 is taken over B01; 09:48 is aimed more at a pure-priority theory the answer has already rejected. Section 7 then calls QB non-stationary, while Section 1 calls it a hybrid. Those are different claims.

Assumption A1 is not earned. A06 starts at 09:20 because A05 occupies the server through its appointment, not because QA uses a soft window. B07 is the actual non-honored appointment, and it is in another queue. Cross-queue appointment behavior is therefore being assumed rather than shown.

Every realized service duration in these tables equals `est_min`, including B01’s three segments summing to 45. The answer never checks that, despite being told estimates may be noisy. The match supports the remaining-time arithmetic and cuts against rank 6’s noise explanation; leaving the check undone is a direct miss of supplied evidence.

What keeps the score from falling further is the QA reconstruction: 08:20, 08:30, and 09:05 are correctly read, and FIFO-plus-appointment-on-time is a disciplined conclusion rather than a relabeling of start order. QB’s low confidence, the QC warning that a priority rule would have moved C05 earlier, and the collection plan are also sound. The grade stops at 6.5 because the best-looking queue confidences, the D-F idle explanation, the 10:54 choice set, and the scan-delay treatment of 09:48 are substantive errors, not presentation nits.
