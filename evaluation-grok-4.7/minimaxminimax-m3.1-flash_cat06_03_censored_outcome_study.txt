7.8

The design is statistically serious and meets the required structure: activation-conditional \(D_{90}\), no coding of rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, a full 48,000-row cohort account, separate censoring mechanisms, a real leakage table, temporal splits around the July 2025 policy change and October 2025 migration, and an explicit decision-use boundary. The two-stage distinction between \(P(D_{90}=1\mid A=1,X)\) and the joint activation-and-default probability is correct.

It is not nearly flawless. The maturity dates in the validation section are wrong and internally inconsistent. By 15 February 2026, a 90-day horizon is mature for activations on or before about 17 November 2025, not “after approximately 17 October.” Full 90+35-day feed ascertainment by about 13 October is right, so the two clauses contradict each other on both the calendar and the inequality direction. That is a material error in a design whose main risk is immature labels. Start-date splits also do not isolate policy or migration regime for cases still in process on 1 July or 15 October.

The skeleton has execution and target bugs. `len(raw) - len(events)` is not a duplicate count, because the observation-window filter has already dropped rows. After `format_dataframe` / event-log conversion, the case id is a trace attribute, not `case:concept:name` on the first event, so prefix assembly would fail. Any non-null `default_90d` is treated as labeled even when the 90-day horizon has not matured, so a premature 0 can enter the training label set. Conflicting labels are also dropped into censoring statuses. The stated primary prefix is all events through first Credit Review, while the code only builds exact \(K=3\) prefixes.
