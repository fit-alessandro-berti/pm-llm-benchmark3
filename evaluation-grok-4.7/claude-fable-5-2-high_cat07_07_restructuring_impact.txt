7.8

The forecast is unusually rigorous: handover totals, volume weights, Legal/Risk overload, approval load, and the M/M/1–M/M/c table all check out, East is treated differently from North/South, and the recommendation is a conditional pilot rather than a full rollout. The score is held down by execution errors, an over-strong validation claim, and a few places where the quantitative stories do not lock together.

The listed-handover sums (343 vs 359), the 588 vs 722 frequency×median index, the 9.3% and 7.6% volume-weighted baselines, the 882/3 vs 1,029/2 approval loads, and the Erlang waits (2.1S, 2.4S, 4.6S, and 0.7S through 6.0S) are correct. The mechanism map covers every requested link, and the censoring, mix, and tail points in §4 are the right ones. Same-owner return (31/36) and approval pass-through (39/42) are reasonable proxies, with the gap left partly unexplained.

The script does not run as written, despite the claim that it was executed on PM4Py 2.7. `discover_subcontracting_network` has no `n` argument, so `n=2` raises. `edges()` builds a Series of tuples, not a MultiIndex, so `concentration()`’s `groupby(level=1)` raises. Those two faults sit on the main path. `astype(bool)` on a string `rework_flag` marks every non-empty value true. Resources renamed into the shared service fall through `role_of` into `"shared_queue"` and then disappear from the unassisted-learning metric. Busy time is zero when no start timestamp exists, which is the schema as given.

§1 and §2 are not fully reconciled. At 85–88% the Erlang model puts pool wait below North/South’s current wait in units of S, while the central scenario puts the technical median at 4–6h against today’s 3.2h and management’s 4.0h. Extra duties, a longer S, and pick-up delay can bridge that gap, but they are not quantified. North’s break-even is about 89%, not 88%; South crosses first. The central scenario’s “flat overall median” treats a volume-weighted blend of medians as the overall median. The 23% wait-index is later stated in §6 as a rise in handover waiting, which is stronger than the §1 caveat that medians do not add. The queue table assumes FCFS exponential service even though the log has priorities.

The pilot under-tests the failure mode the queue math identifies. South+East at today’s load is about 75% on two servers, where M/M/2 wait is about 1.3S, better than South’s current 2.1S. Step 1 also leaves Legal/Risk routing, Quality Check, and the referral increase unchanged. A clean Step 1 is what the model already predicts and does not validate the 85–90% regime, the added duties, or the three-team pool. Expand criteria can be met inside 12 weeks, while the same answer says local-skill loss shows up over quarters.
