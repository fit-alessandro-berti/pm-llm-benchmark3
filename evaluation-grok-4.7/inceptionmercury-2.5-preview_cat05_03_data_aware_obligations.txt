The write-up keeps the required seven-part shape and reaches a defensible label on most straightforward cases, but the formalization, several release-time reconstructions, and the checking code contradict the stated policy and each other. Those are central failures, not polish issues.

Temporal semantics are only a short paragraph. Blanks, no backward fill, and `event_index` as a tie-break are named. “Material change” is then defined as any update to amount, jurisdiction, risk, or customer type, with no 20% threshold, no decrease rule, and no unknown-baseline rule. Event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are collapsed into one vague sentence, so the EX-77 outage insert versus the EX-88 certificate time never gets a usable clock model.

The rule table does not match the policy. R1’s trigger is written “JurisdictionDE, Amount100k,” which does not state `jurisdiction ≠ DE` and `amount ≥ 100000`. The later pseudo-logic does state `!= "DE"`, so the table and the formula disagree. R3 is garbled into “Screen added to sanctions.” R5 omits approver, `effective_from`, scope, expiry, and the rule that a displayed backdate is not enough. R6 never says missing values must not be treated as low-risk. Formal logic is supplied only for R1, and that formula excuses the obligation on `Exemption_Visible` at release. Visibility is the wrong test: TX-310 references EX-88 and still lacks a proven approval. Ordering is raw timestamp inequality, so same-timestamp `event_index` is undefined. R2–R7 are not formalized.

Case results:

- TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are substantively right: missing enhanced review after the 80k→125k jump, missing `Compliance_Approve` after the HIGH reclass, no rescreen after XZ is listed, a standard packet does not satisfy R2, and 118k→145k is about 22.9% and invalidates the enhanced review.
- TX-306 and TX-308 are labeled COMPLIANT for the right practical reason (about 4.5% is not more than 20%; a decrease does not invalidate), but that contradicts the section 1 definition under which those later amount updates are material and the reviews are already stale. TX-306 also lists R4 as triggered when its predicate is false, and omits R1, which does apply at 230k / SG and is satisfied only because of the 20% rule.
- TX-302 is the clear misclassification. State at release is 180k, MEDIUM, GB, CORPORATE. The enhanced review at 09:20 is after creation and before release, with no intervening attribute change, so R1 is already met. EX-77 is strong surplus evidence, not the basis of the result. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, while also marking the obligation satisfied, is inconsistent. The section 4 counterfactual (lose the outage log and the case becomes noncompliant) is false for the same reason.
- TX-305 is correctly INDETERMINATE, then reconstructed as “150k (settlement).” That copies the next day’s settlement amount into the release snapshot. The trigger is therefore not established. The review also contains no amount, and an increase from an unknown baseline is not classifiable.
- TX-310 may be INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT; the write-up never states that only a standard review exists. The certificate created at 10:30, after the 10:00 release, is adverse evidence, and the only witness is the requester. Section 4 only discusses a path toward accepting the exemption.
- TX-311’s label COMPLIANT is right and the trigger is wrong. At release the jurisdiction is already DE, so R1’s foreign condition is false; amount 115k, LOW, CORPORATE does not trigger R2. `event_index` shows the review still saw US, but both same-timestamp events are before release and the jurisdiction update is what carries DE forward, so reversing them does not make the release state indeterminate. The row both records DE and lists R1 as triggered.

The findings table drops the required decisive-evidence column. Release states routinely omit risk and customer type. Satisfied facts are repeatedly written in the deviations column.

The Python block would not run: `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, df, event_log=None)` passes a DataFrame as `case_id`. The embedded CSV omits TX-305’s settlement row. Attributes are not forward-filled on the case log. The release snapshot does not sort by time and `event_index`. A missing amount is truthy as `NaN` and then fails `>= 100000`, so R1 becomes false instead of unknown. The exemption is taken from `iloc[0]`, the create row, so EX-77 and EX-88 are dropped. There is no last-review, approval, screen, list-update, or material-change tracking, no R4 delta, no exemption-register join, no evidence timestamps, no unknown flags, and no DECLARE or trace-order check.

Sections 6 and 7 name the six requested failure modes and the six requested logging controls, at checklist depth. The final-state example uses TX-311, which is the same-timestamp case, so two of the six modes are not actually distinguished.
