5.5

The headline split is right: April is a schema and pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change, August–September stack supplier performance, concept, and Partner Z shifts, and October is not comparable because labels are immature. That is the core of the task, and the six-way table, month narrative, test list, alert suppression, and failure modes all exist. The write-up is not reliable enough to act on. Figures, bias direction, prediction-time logic, training cohorts, and the required code repeatedly contradict the evidence or each other.

The report is full of broken quantities. Dropped arrows turn real changes into false levels: June “3552%,” V3 “223542%,” label delay “34384252 d” called stable, and the summary table’s April AUROC of 0.14. Actual April AUROC is 0.67. V3 breach from 0.46 to 0.64 is about a 39% relative rise, not 76%. September ECE is 0.140 versus August 0.120, not unchanged. A mix of the published variant rates and variant-specific breach rates is about 0.34, not 0.38–0.40. January–July volume is about 25,000 cases, not 28,000. At 93% maturity and an observed rate of 0.28, August cannot converge to 0.35–0.40; if every unlabeled case breached, the ceiling would be about 0.33.

Label bias is argued in opposite directions. October’s fall to 0.16, with the backlog hitting long paths, is correctly read as a downward-biased observed rate. Section 1 then says September’s 0.31 is “inflated” because unresolved cases are excluded. Test 1 and Risk 5 say that same rate should rise as labels mature. Those cannot all be true. The Brier comment is also wrong: Brier is a mean, so fewer scored cases do not improve it, and censoring easy paths can worsen it.

Prediction timing is inconsistent. The score is fixed immediately after diagnosis, before remote fix, parts order, and parts receipt. July’s claim that the model is already capturing the supplier shock through the case’s own wait-time features does not fit that prefix. Aggregate July calibration can look fine while the V3 shock is mostly invisible, because V3 is only about 22% of volume. The July falsifier is inverted: an AUROC drop is what blindness to a post-diagnosis cause looks like, not evidence the model is discriminating it. Test 5 then calls a stable coefficient on post-diagnosis wait “covariate drift.” That is process or concept structure, not a shift in P(X) at scoring time. Realized variant as a training feature would leak the post-decision path.

The remediation undoes the diagnosis. January–May is labeled a clean training window even though April prefixes and the cached pre-diagnosis count are corrupted and May is only partly repaired. January–February is both inside that training window and a “never used” hold-out. September, at 82% maturity with slower Partner Z and V3 labels, is still used as a calibration set. The invented 30 June model snapshot is not in the evidence. Partner Z “single-digit day” maturity, full-case resolution moving from 3–4 days to 5–6 days, and a 1–2 day SLA are not in the evidence; the 130–155 hour figure is only the parts edge.

The IPTW step estimates the wrong quantity. Weighting non-Partner-Z cases by 1/(1−e) makes them resemble Partner Z. It does not transport September back to the pre-Z mix. The non-Partner-Z rate is already given as 0.27, so the useful check is variant-standardized breach, which is not computed. The within-variant “remove Partner Z and the model degrades less on Partner Z than on everyone else” expectation is also reversed.

The code does not meet the spec and would not run. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `filter_time_range`, or `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, and it reaches DFG discovery through unofficial module paths while discarding the performance DFG. `duplicated(subset=...)` is passed a Series, which is invalid. The month filter keeps events inside the calendar window, defines case start as the first of those events, and then applies a tautological filter. That is a truncated event-calendar extract described as a case-start cohort. Both cohorts and their different interpretations are missing. `eval` on variant keys is an unnecessary failure point. There is no invented drift detector, and activity renaming is present, but the sketch is not an auditable extractor.

Monitoring covers the requested families and correctly blocks retraining on a maturity alarm. The eighth monitor is a business-outcome check, not an intervention or deployment monitor, so a policy or model change is not an explicit alert dependency. Thresholds are arbitrary but acceptable in a sketch.

Section 6 names the right harms, including immature-label retraining, calibration on the April bug, ignored V3 concept drift, and a fairness retraining story without stratification. It then repeats the same percent and censoring inconsistencies and offers a “after 10 July” flag that would not travel to the next regime.
