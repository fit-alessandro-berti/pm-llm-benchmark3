The answer has the right skeleton and a few sound headline judgments, but it is not a reliable conformance interpretation. It misses required observations, contradicts itself, collapses data quality into forbidden behavior, and sketches conformance against the wrong model.

The best parts are real. N3 (2 cancel-then-dispatch orders) is correctly the clearest forbidden finding. Those merits do not survive the errors.

Other calibration and counting problems are material. “N3 is 0% complete” does not mean anything. “N2 allows 17% divergence” assumes a denominator of 100 that the 83-order count does not support. The code preserves raw output, but it discovers a model from `ocel` and then runs conformance against that discovered model. `norm_ocdfg`, `norm_otg`, and `norm_etot` are not the reference models. Self-conformance cannot expose normative deviations. The semantic layer is an empty dictionary plus a pseudocode comment, so mandatory, typical, forbidden, and unspecified metadata are named but not applied.

The six completeness items meet the count, not the standard. The central gap—the norm’s own admission that N1 is an eventual rule approximated by directly-follows—is not one of them. Section 8 asks for robust conclusions, norm incompleteness, suspected data quality, and limits of graph summaries.
