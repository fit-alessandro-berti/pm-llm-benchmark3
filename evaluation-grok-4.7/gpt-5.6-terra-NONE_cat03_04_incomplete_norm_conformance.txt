7.5

The analysis is coherent on open-world semantics, on N3/T6/E10 as the forbidden patterns, and on refusing to treat `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, Manual Compliance Review–Customer, Package–Delivery codeath, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` as violations. It is short of nearly flawless because the findings table over-claims several counts, one mandatory exception is softened, and the code layer does not implement the distinctions the prose depends on.

Section 1 correctly separates forbidden behavior, missing mandatory behavior, unregulated behavior, typical-pattern absence, data-quality error, abstraction mismatch, and insufficient evidence, and it states the closed-world limit accurately: explicit prohibitions and in-scope mandatory relations only.

The severity table then drifts from that standard.

- Rank 7 states that two Add Item events lack an Order. The supplied evidence is an edge-weight difference, 1450 Item links versus 1448 Order links. Section 3 correctly limits that inference to a one-link-per-event assumption the summary does not give. The table states the stronger claim anyway.
- Rank 4 centers the business reading on external shipment without packing. Pack Package–Package and Dispatch Package–Package are both weight 200, while the directly-follows count is 198 of 200. Those figures support an intervening event or a projection artifact as seriously as a missing pack. The same row also treats N4 scope as unsettled, although the summary already labels the 200 packages externally shipped. Severity High rests on the unpacked-shipment reading.
- Ranks 5 and 8 are the same two missing Delivery links, scored High and then Medium. Ranks 9 and 18 are the same unmatched payment, scored Medium and then Low-Medium. Duplicate rows with different severities make the ranking internally unstable.
- Rank 5 assigns that Delivery gap to T2 and E6 together. The OTG weight for `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` is 200. The demonstrated miss is the E6 event–object link on two Dispatch Package events. A per-dispatch reading of T2 is arguable; the table records it as established.
- `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3 Orders) is an observed, unmodeled directly-follows relation and is relevant to the N1 abstraction problem. It is absent from the table and from the section 4 lists, despite the requirement to include every unusual observation, including benign ones.
- Rank 9 classifies the Invoice gap as mandatory “unless genuinely unapplied cash.” E8’s exception is explicit typing as unapplied cash, and the norm states that none are so typed in this snapshot. Section 4 is closer when it requires the event to be established and typed. The table replaces that condition with economic genuineness.
- E10’s severity cell mixes “high for data integrity” with “low evidence of business misconduct,” so the rank is not a single calibrated severity.

Section 3 is the strongest part. It states what OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT can and cannot establish, uses the 98/100, 198/200, 1448/1450, and 92/93 counts, and explains the false nonconformance created by reading N1’s eventual-validation rule as a directly-follows edge. The cross-abstraction point is real: an OTG edge of weight 200 can coexist with two E6 misses, and a carrier attribute can exist without the mandatory Delivery object. The claim that the 1450 Add Item–Item links “satisfy” E3 is still stronger than aggregate weights alone.

Section 4 places the protected benign relations in the unregulated bucket and gives conditions for N1, N4, E6, E8, and E10. The positive T1 observation of 1450 is omitted. “Expected aggregate levels” for E4, E5, and E7 sits poorly beside the explicit absence of frequency bands.

Section 5 calls `discover_ocdfg`, `discover_otg`, `discover_etot`, `conformance_ocdfg`, `conformance_otg`, and `conformance_etot`, retains the raw diagnostics, and stores mandatory/typical/forbidden metadata outside the graphs. The classifier then undoes that design. It walks only metadata keys, tests binary presence, never reads N1’s stored eventual-versus-direct flag, and never uses `context`. A relation seen 98 times is simply present, so the N1 two-order gap, the N4 two-package gap, the E2/E6/E8 partial gaps, and the N5 partial pattern are not classified. Unmodeled edges never enter the function, so the open-world rule is not applied in the sketch.

Section 6 supplies nine gaps with risks, clarifications, and statuses. The N1, N3 timing/recovery, T6 event-identity, codeath, and invoice-window items are operationally sound. The N4 item is a fair clarification, with the caveat that the directly-follows approximation sentence is attached to N1 specifically. The unapplied-cash item largely restates the typing rule E8 already gives. Calling scope determination for “standard” Order impossible overstates an ambiguity.

Section 7 keeps containment, data repair, process investigation, norm revision, and monitoring apart, and it prioritizes the two N3 cases and the one T6 edge without escalating the single reopen, the seven codeath edges, or the three reviews. The N4 investigation is still framed mainly as packing skipped versus packing unrecorded, which reintroduces the directly-follows conflation.

Section 8 names the robust pattern matches, the open-world non-findings, the identifier-join dependence of E10, and the chronology, scope, identity, eventual-validation, and unapplied-cash questions that graph summaries cannot settle. It also calls the E10 observation a robust forbidden conclusion and then says the E10 conclusion strongly depends on the suspected join, so one word covers both the edge’s existence and its business meaning. The N3 paragraph has the same split, and the final sentence does correct it for chronology.

The write-up also runs well past the 1,700–2,600 word aim, inflated by the duplicated rows. The normative core is sound and the benign rare behavior is not escalated. The over-sharp table, the loosened E8 condition, the missing review edge, and a semantic layer that cannot see partial or unmodeled relations are material defects under a strict reading.
