6.5

The memo reaches a defensible hybrid decision and hits every required section, but it is not reliable enough for an executive decision record.

The headline numbers are wrong or over-specific. Complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%, about 61% relatively, not 50%. Apartments, old buildings, and business customers are 55% of the pilot, not 47%. First-time-right losses for apartments and old buildings are 8 and 11 points, not “7–11.” The 34% simple-house share is the pilot mix, which was skewed toward harder buildings, yet it is treated as the national volume. Full downstream cost is an incomplete estimate, while the memo still calls the result a cost overrun.

The decision itself is inconsistent. The memo credits speed to coordinator ownership, then later to the merged task and survey skip, but scales only coordinators. It says the experiment will test coordinator absence, yet neither the funded list nor the final measurement set includes that comparison. The executive list (M1, M2, M4, M6, M8, 10 weeks) does not match the final list (M1, M4, M6, M7, 8 weeks). National coordinator rollout also has no staffing, absence backup, or requirement for reliable risk classification, despite 93% utilization, stalled cases when a coordinator is absent, and incomplete building-risk data.

Process-mining reasoning is only partial. The low-precision explanation correctly says the model allows unobserved paths, then incorrectly attributes low precision to many unique paths in the log. Alignments, the temporal profile, and the directly-follows graph are mostly unused, including missed permit checks and repeated installation without survey. Value of information is mis-ranked: survey-skip studies outrank the absence test that could overturn the immediate scaling decision, and conformance monitoring is treated as decision-relevant even though the memo says it is needed regardless.

The fact, interpretation, and assumption table is structurally sound, alternatives A through E are covered, and full national rollout and total rollback are appropriately rejected. Those strengths do not offset the arithmetic errors, shifting action lists, and gaps in the operating plan.
