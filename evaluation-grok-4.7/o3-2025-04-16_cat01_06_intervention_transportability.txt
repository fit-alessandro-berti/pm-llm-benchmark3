4.5

The write-up reaches the right decision class and names the real statutory, confounding, and capacity obstacles, but the quantitative layer, pilot arithmetic, and executable model are materially wrong. Those are central to the task, so the overall result stays well below a competent pass.

Process comparison covers the twelve required features and correctly keeps a hard originals gate. It mis-states Site A’s queue discipline: daily huddles are a co-intervention of the after period, yet the table puts them in both periods. “Toward 94%” digital is invented; the source only supports about 90% digital plus an unquantified mix shift. The originals row treats overlap as zero and therefore drops the legal concurrency that does exist: identity review can run during the originals delay (median 18 h versus identity service 3.4 h). The directly-follows counts are never used.

The transport graph contains the required nodes and selection nodes, but it is not a consistent adjacency list (`-|->`, a reversed `appeal <- cycle_time`). Cycle time is a poor direct parent of appeals; rework and decision quality are the coherent parents. The required explanation is about 80 words against a 180–280 word requirement, and “digital_documents share” is unfinished. Shared AND-gating before assessment is identified; effect modification is listed rather than argued.

The evidence table correctly refuses to treat the 9-hour median drop as an identified effect and records FTE, API latency, huddles, and timestamp risk. High-complexity share (48% to 40%) is not its own row. Several “transport confidence” scores are unsupported, the API row says no further Site B evidence is needed while the identity row requires latency logs, and huddle portability is scored above the core concurrency mechanism.

Modifier coverage hits the required topics. Beneficial and harmful signatures for the assessment bottleneck and the heavy tail are blank, so the later tail-worsens scenario is not specified here. “Identity reviewers starved” is the wrong specialization failure: document work is the longer verification load, and assessment (3) and legal (2) are the scarce downstream pools. Service times are treated as processing times even though 3,400 cases times 3.4 h cannot fit in 4 identity reviewers over six months; that elapsed-versus-touch ambiguity is never raised.

Scenario signs are inconsistent, so “6 h to 10 h” is not clearly a reduction. The one-sentence derivation does not map waits, service times, or directly-follows counts to the ranges. Diluting a subgroup median by 38% to get a portfolio median is invalid. Digital-only gains of 7–11 hours exceed Site A’s confounded 6-hour drop in wait-to-assessment and assume no IT patch, which contradicts the lock analysis used everywhere else. The optimistic case parallelizes originals-required document checks, which the statute forbids. Scenario 3 does supply a median-improves, tail-worsens case.

The candidate intervention correctly starts identity at registration, holds document check for originals, and keeps assessment behind both. It invents a “€100 k” legal threshold. Public-sector or high-value work is 40%, and originals are required for 62%; phase 1 is their intersection, not 38%. The supplement rule reopens document check only, while the process requires document check and assessment to be repeatable. The textual order is not a partial order: choice and the loop are written as ordinary precedence, and several operators are missing.

The pilot’s overlap-versus-wait diagnostic is the best causal idea in the answer. The design around it fails. Case-level 1:1 randomization on a shared reviewer pool does not “eliminate spill-over”; it is the interference mechanism, and team-level huddles in version 2 contaminate the control arm. Blinding is impossible once task creation differs. Baseline volume is about 131 cases a week, so six to eight weeks cannot produce 1,100–1,200 eligible cases, or 550 pairs inside the digital stratum. The stop rule “p90 cycle > 15 h” fires immediately against a baseline p90 of 110 h. “Rework > 4 pp” is ambiguous against a 17% baseline.

The code does not call `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. `Operator.SEQ` is not a PM4Py operator. The POWL import (`POWLNode`, `AND`, `XOR`, `LOOP`, `SEQ`) does not match `pm4py.objects.powl.obj`, and `add_node` / `add_order` are not the library’s partial-order API. Semantically the net is wrong even under a forgiving reading: edges from assessment to approve, reject, and legal review force all three; a back-edge is not a loop; `Identity Check → Document Check` serializes the tasks the prose says may run in either order; originals are unconditional. `check_is_workflow_net` is printed as soundness. The closing comment correctly says capacities, locks, durations, and data-dependent originals are outside the net.

`conditional go` is the right choice, and wholesale copying is correctly rejected. The recommendation then reinstates the weak numbers: phase 1 as 38%, a 7–11 hour median and 6–10 hour p90 gain, huddles as “30%” of Site A’s improvement, and benefit “below 20%” without a patch. The 4-hour expansion threshold sits above a cautious reading of the transportable wait reduction. Expansion when originals delay falls below identity service time misses the available design, which is to run identity during the originals wait. Assumption 5 introduces an unmentioned union constraint and another invented 20–30% effect share.

The skeleton is usable. The figures, sample-size claim, stop rule, and model are not safe to follow.
