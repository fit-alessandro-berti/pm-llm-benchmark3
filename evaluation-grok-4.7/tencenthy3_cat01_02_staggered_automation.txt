5.5

The required structure is complete and the headline caution is right: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not identified, and expansion only with an embedded evaluation is the sensible policy. The pre/post arithmetic for the chosen Jan–Feb versus Jul–Aug window is also correct. Those strengths do not offset the quantitative and identification failures that drive the conclusions.

The effect numbers are not used consistently. The same table shows adopters’ p90 and rework rising less than Delta and Epsilon, and escalation rising only about 1 percentage point more than the controls, yet the effect table treats p90 as null-to-harmful (0% to +15%), rework as no improvement, and escalation as +3 to +4 points with confidence 60. That escalation jump includes the March fraud-desk increase, which predates automation. The uniform window also buries the cleanest contrast: Gamma’s May–June medians and p90s, already after the complexity and staffing shocks, fall sharply in July–August by more than the never-treated branches.

The dose-response claim is wrong by an order of magnitude. Section 5’s slopes are about −0.10 to −0.34 hours per percentage point, which is −1 to −3.4 hours per 10 points; the claim ladder reports 0.1 to 0.3 hours per 10 points and drops the sign. Calling that “conditionally causal” is also unjustified: use is endogenous, and parallel trends do not identify an intensity effect. The primary event-study is never estimated; relative time is built and unused; the regressions condition on arrivals, FTE, and complexity without treating them as post-treatment. The scale-up rule then demands a cluster-robust interval after warning that five clusters cannot support one.
