The write-up covers all seven required sections, uses the case evidence, and correctly rejects a pure frequency ranking. That structure does not hold up under the constraints and the process data.

The recommendation and the rollout contradict each other. Section 7 selects the bottleneck set (A4, A6, A7, A8) and says high-frequency automation should wait. Section 6 does the opposite: Phase 1 is the quick-win set (A1, A2, A10, A5), spends both deep core-banking slots on A1 and A10, and leaves A7 and A8 optional in Phase 3. Across the year the plan also deploys roughly ten modules, breaking the hard limit of four. The first wave has no module whose purpose is to strengthen controls, despite the compliance requirement.

Several process claims are wrong or overstated. Median waits are added to “about 106 hours per case,” which ignores path mix and the explicit fact that KYC and financial-document review often run concurrently. The 20-hour figure is the wait before normalization, not the wait before credit analysis. Registration service time is 8 minutes, not 3. Claimed cycle-time cuts of 10–15% and 20–30% are not derived from the frequencies or the directly-follows graph.

Control and resource reasoning is also loose. Better statement normalization does not cause, or cure, four-eyes breaches; segregation of duties is a role control. The resource note says cleaner inflow overloads analysts unless normalization improves, yet A6 is treated as the overload source. A8 is put in the preferred portfolio even though KYC, missing documents, and credit notes are still dirty, while A11 is correctly gated for the same garbage-in problem. The largest rework loop—missing documents, 3300 out and 2800 back, 52% repeated—is left out of the recommended four, as is any real control module (A3, A9, or A12). The candidate table is also listed A1–A12 rather than ranked.

Compensating controls, exception capacity, and the entity-mismatch and collateral facts are handled more carefully, but the portfolio that is actually advised is not the portfolio the rollout implements, and it is not consistent with the budget, control, or dependency constraints.
