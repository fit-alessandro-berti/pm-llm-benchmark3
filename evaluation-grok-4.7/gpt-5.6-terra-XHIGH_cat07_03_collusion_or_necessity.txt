7.5

The assessment is careful and well calibrated: it treats the log as a mixture of emergency handling, informal specialization, control exceptions, and data-quality limits, keeps routine Aline–Elena and Boris–Felix volume as a baseline, and puts the V-77x bank-detail/payment cluster first without accusing anyone. Required sections, counts, benign tests, and the ~220-word report wording are in place. It is not nearly flawless.

The required PM4Py calls are written as executable code and would fail. `filter_four_eyes_principle` takes the two activities and a `parameters` dict; `resource_key` and `keep_violations` are not top-level arguments, and without `keep_violations=True` inside that dict the filter keeps compliant cases. `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, and `discover_subcontracting_network` likewise take the resource key in `parameters` (`n=2` itself is valid). `filter_paths_performance` takes the path list and `parameters`; thresholds and the keep/drop flag belong there, and the usual flag is `positive`, not `keep`. The single note that keyword names vary does not fix the parameters-dict convention and does not cover the network or path-performance calls.

The justification check is logically loose. `verified_emergency_compliance` is used before it is defined. A case passes if any in-window justification actor differs, so one different actor masks a same-person justification. An empty set falls through to `same_actor_or_missing_independent_review`, merging missing, late, and same-person results. A full Declare model or log skeleton discovered from compliant emergencies will also flag ordinary trace variants; confirmatory use should be limited to an imposed response constraint plus the working-day and actor test. That limit is only partly stated.

Smaller evidence slips: VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim are called role-labelled accounts, which the scenario supports for shared Approver_Gita and the legacy_erp bot mapping, not for those two resources. The report names the Lena–Kim loop a “correction loop” and the migration “documented,” both tighter than the open facts. “Under 30 minutes” never states which timestamp pair is measured, and identical quarter-close timestamps are not tied to unreliable handover or subcontracting order.

Judgment and prioritization stay sound, including the split of 17 same-person justifications into 12 migration rows and 5 unexplained rows, and the warning not to treat the 9 four-eyes cases and 16 fast cases as one set. The implementation section is not reliable enough for a higher score.
