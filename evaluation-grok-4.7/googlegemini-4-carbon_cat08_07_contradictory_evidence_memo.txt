7.5

The memo is decision-ready and lands on a defensible hybrid (E): keep the simple-house gains, stop post-billing activation and unsafe survey skipping, and buy information before any wider rollout. Format, fact/interpretation separation in the table, and the M1–M8 ranking are strong. It is not nearly flawless.

The median-versus-tail explanation is analytically wrong in an important way. Baseline cycle time is already heavy-tailed (median 14.2, p90 31, p95 45), so “unimodal became strongly bimodal” is unsupported. Four of five segments still have faster medians; apartments are −2 days at the median and +12 at p90. The “50–60% happy path versus 24% stuck in recovery loops” story equates 100%−76% first-time-right with second-visit congestion (second visits are 18%) and does not match the segment pattern. Section 3.5 later tells the better segment story, but it never reconciles the two mechanisms.

Several claims are tighter than the evidence. “Improved on every dimension” and “proven … cost wins” / “hard savings” for simple houses use only duration, first-time-right, and complaints; there is no segment cost or NPS. The +3.7 pp figure is the overall complaint rate (6.1% to 9.8%), not post-billing complaint escalation (edge share 3% to 8%). Aggregate failed-visit share, revisit utilization, and p95 are narrated inside the apartment/old-building failure paragraph. The simple-house first-time-right gain is pinned on fewer reschedules with no supporting evidence. “40%+ of field surveys” mixes a ~42% relative drop in edge share with a utilization drop from 78% to 52%.

Operationally, “coordinator ownership exclusively for simple houses” is not reconciled with an immediate 80–85% utilization cap that shifts unspecified intake back to standard scheduling. Thresholds such as EUR 192, precision ≥ 0.75, and <0.5% conformance violations are usable management gates, but they are presented with false precision. A 62-day p95 inside a 6-week pilot, sparse start timestamps, and week-4 cost truncation are only partly treated; right-censoring of the tail is missed. Temporal-profile deviations and the generalization drop (0.70 to 0.56) are thin relative to the fitness/precision discussion. SLA (13% to 16%, better only for simple houses) barely enters the decision.
