The write-up follows the required seven-part structure and stays balanced on East, fairness, learning, and a conditional pilot. It is not a reliable forecast.

The mechanism map names the required effects, but it does not use the supplied frequencies. Summing the handover tables gives about 343 events per 100 cases now and about 359 after the change, so the optimistic claim of a 15% handover reduction contradicts the management estimate. Several new paths are also slower in that same estimate (tech 3.2h to 4.0h, return 1.6h to 2.2h, approval 2.4h to 3.0h, legal/risk 9.6h to 10.5h).

The optimistic scenario treats Legal/Risk as absorbable slack even though they are already at 91–93% utilization and would receive more volume, plus security and difficult quality checks. It also calls East’s 8.6-day median cycle time a wait time. No scenario computes pooled utilization or weighted waiting time from the given volumes.

The pilot does not test a shared service. Moving only E_Tech into a queue, with North and South unchanged, creates no pooling, and East is a confounded control (lower volume, worse baseline). The design also leaves the saturated legal/risk queue, the main bottleneck risk, outside the trial. The “rework +5%” guardrail does not say whether it is absolute or relative.

Section 4 covers six average-hiding risks but never treats exception handling: absence cover, regional or language accommodation, and the current cross-team path (6 per 100, 8.9h, 18% rework).

The PM4Py sketch misses required comparisons: working-together network, subcontracting network, specialty and customer-segment filters, and SLA misses. Several calls are not real APIs: `get_service_time` does not take an activity and return `['median']`, rework results have no `['rate']`, and role discovery does not emit a consistency score. Service time is also used where waiting time or queue depth is meant. The final comparison would not run, and edge weights, `local_expertise_flag`, priority, and `reassignment_count` are never measured.
