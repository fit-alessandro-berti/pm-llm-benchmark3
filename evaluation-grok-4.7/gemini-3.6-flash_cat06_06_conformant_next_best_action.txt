The answer has the right compliance skeleton—independent second review first, A and B last, P-901 treated as a real cost—but the operational ranking that skeleton supports is not reliable. Several claims that carry the recommendation contradict the time ranges and uncertainty the case actually gives.

The primary plan depends on review lengths the case does not provide. S2’s review is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and, if approved, execute. The rank-1 path instead uses 20–30 minutes for S4 and, in the cleared-match branch, 10–15 minutes. Nothing in the case distinguishes S4’s speed from S2’s. With those invented durations removed, “decision and execution at t+50–75” and “review done by t+55–60 if S4 arrives at 35” are not supported. The same compression produces the false continuation label “[SLA Kept]” for the t+45 S2 fallback: interrupt at 45, review at least 35, then 5–15 to execute, so the earliest execution is about t+85 and the ordinary range runs past 90. Section 6 then says that fallback “ensures” the deadline. It does not. At the gate, about 45 minutes remain and the stated work needs 40–90. Only the extreme fast corner fits, while P-901 still absorbs a large breach-probability increase. The rerank trigger later moves that same gate from t+45 to t+50.

Benefit and harm figures are conditional upsides written as expectations. Rank 1 books €20,000–€35,000 and “elimination” of the fuzzy-match ambiguity, with harm of €0 if the payment is approved on time. Alternate screening may resolve the 0.46 match or may confirm it; it does not substitute for second review while the score stays at or above 70 or the match stays open, and it is not shown to move the separate risk score. Approval after a conformant review is only 0.55–0.75, those probabilities are observational and may not transport under this intervention, and a conformant false clear of a sanctioned-border payment still carries regulatory and non-monetary exposure. None of that is in the €0 cell. The 12-minute-old score is never used.

Other ranking and rule problems are smaller but real. Standalone D is a sequential strawman of the same screening-plus-review idea, so D is both rank 1 and rank 3. Action F’s benefit is “fully mitigates” €100,000–€500,000 even though the rejection threshold is explicitly unsettled, and the answer never faces rule 8’s placement of rejection after the required reviews. Section 4 does not map C, D, and E onto the rules that make them conformant; it mostly restates why A, B, and G fail. “System-generated” incident ticket and a 100% / 85% confidence split are sharper than the record. The t+45 interrupt also ignores that S4’s arrival window runs to 65 minutes, so the gate will often damage P-901 just before S4 frees, and it applies the “interrupt now for 45 minutes” spillover without adjusting for a delayed interrupt when P-901 has only about 25 minutes left.

What keeps this above a weak answer is the normative order: A and B stay currently nonconformant, forecasts are refused as waivers of rules 6, 7, and 9, screening and a SAR are not treated as the second pair of eyes, and the preferred family is parallel rescreening plus an independent officer, with P-901’s spillover explicit. The format, labels, and top-five traces are present. The recommendation itself, however, asserts a deadline-preserving conformant plan and an “ensuring” fallback that the case’s own clock does not support.
