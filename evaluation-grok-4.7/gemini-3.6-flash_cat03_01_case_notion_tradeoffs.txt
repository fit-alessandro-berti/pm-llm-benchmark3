The write-up follows the required seven-part structure, stays anchored to identifiers, and reaches a defensible rejection of a single lead object. It is not reliable at the level of definitions, directly-follows topology, or arithmetic. Those are load-bearing failures, not presentation nits.

**Assumptions.** The prose case notion is usable: a case is the time-ordered sequence of events directly tied to one object. The formal map \(\pi_T: \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O}_T)\) is the event’s object set, not that case notion. Convergence and divergence are defined correctly in §1 (shared event copied across cases of type \(T\); several child lifecycles forced into one parent trace). Copied events are treated as both coupling evidence and frequency distortion, and direct E2O, propagated O2O, and fractional weights are declared as transformations. The propagation path is reversed: the log has `O1 contains I11`, `V1 bills O1`, and `M1 settles V1`, not `I11 contains O1 bills V1`. `e18` and `e21` are already related to `O1`, so the invoice hop is unnecessary for that example.

**Matrix.** Several cells are exact: payment singletons (`M1`–`M4` in `e21`, `e22`, `e25`, `e26` only); delivery traces `D1=[e15,e23]`, `D2=[e16,e24]`, `D3=[e17,e27]`; package traces of three activities; item traces omitting `e18`–`e22`, `e25`, `e26`; order divergence for `O1` (`P1`/`P2`, `D1`/`D2`) and `O3` (`P3`/`P4`, `D2`/`D3`). The convergence column then abandons the §1 definition. Item convergence is rated Low while item duplication is rated High, even though `e13` (`I21`,`I31`), `e14` (`I32`,`I41`), `e16`/`e24` (`I12`,`I21`,`I31`), and `e17`/`e27` (`I32`,`I41`) are convergence. Delivery convergence is rated Moderate while duplication is None; no event touches two deliveries. Invoice convergence is rated High, but `e18`–`e26` each name one invoice; `V2` billing `O2` and `O3` is order-convergence. The invoice duplication cell describes that order-side copying. Package “convergence” mixes the real `P2`/`P3` share of `e16`/`e24` with `P3`/`P4` merely containing other orders’ items. “Merge `O1`,`O2`,`O3`,`O4`” is also too strong: the widest order share is three (`e16`, `e24`).

**Distortions.** The required topics are all present, and several mechanisms are right: `e13` counted twice on orders, `e16`/`e24` counted three times, `V2` issued once in `e19` and copied to `O2` and `O3`, four payment references from two real payments, and `I11=[e05,e11,e15,e23]`. Against that:

- `e23` is 14:00. It is cited as 12:30, which is `e21`.
- Order flattening does not put a direct `Record Payment → Record Payment` edge on `O2` or `O3`. Both run `e22` → `e24` (Confirm Delivery) → `e25`. That false loop is repeated as decisive evidence in §5. The direct loop does exist on invoice `V2` (`e19`→`e22`→`e25`).
- `e08 → e13` is not a direct-follows edge on `O3`; `e09` sits between them. On `O2`, `e07 → e13` is the real item-then-pack sequence; the defect is the duplicated count.
- `I32` is added at 09:23 and `I41` at 09:25, so `I41` did not sit waiting for `I32` to arrive. `Add Item → Pack Package` has frequency 6 in the item DFG, not 2, and two separately packed items would also contribute 2.
- Creation-to-invoice gaps of 3h 03m (`O2`) and 3h 01m (`O3`) do not show `O2` held until `O3` was ready. The doubled `Issue Invoice` count is the actual distortion.
- “Picking” is not an activity in this log.

**Portfolio and decision.** Restricting safe order questions to milestone duration is sound for `e01`→`e28`, because those events are order-specific. “Proportion of orders meeting delivery deadlines” is not safe: `O1` has `e23` and `e24`, `O3` has `e24` and `e27`. The package log is called ideal for parcel cycle time and then dropped from the portfolio. Fractional repair of `e16` to \(1/3+1/3+1/3=1\) is correct, as is the point that a classical DFG cannot see `D2`’s batch. Position C is still justified by truncation (`Payment`, `Delivery`, direct-E2O `Item`) plus real order divergence (`Dispatch → Dispatch` on `O1` and `O3`). The rebuttal that `e16` cannot be copied onto `O1`–`O3` or assigned to one order without loss is the strongest passage. It is undercut by the nonexistent payment self-loop and by “inflates by up to 300%” in §5 versus “overcounts by 200%” for the same 3-versus-1 count in §7. The 1–1–1 condition for switching to A is coherent; it skips when B would already be enough.

It never measures the duplications the essay depends on, and `ocel_get_object_types_summary` is not a demonstrated API. Check 2 repeats the reversed qualifiers (`Item contains Order bills Invoice settles Payment`). Check 3 asks for invoice transit time although `V1`–`V3` have no dispatch or confirm, treats non-edges `e15→e24` and `e16→e23` as DFG pairings, and recomputes the wrong 1h 25m from 12:30. The skeleton of the argument holds: no one object type both covers fulfillment and finance and keeps frequencies and precedence honest. Too many of the cited edges, timestamps, percentages, and convergence ratings are false for the score to go higher.
