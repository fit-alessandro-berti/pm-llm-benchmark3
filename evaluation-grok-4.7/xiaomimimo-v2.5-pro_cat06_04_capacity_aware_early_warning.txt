6.5

The final policy gets the main qualitative calls right, but it is not numerically or internally reliable enough to score higher. I-03 is correctly left ineligible after Diagnosis Complete; I-05 is suppressed as an unchanged refresh while I-07 is kept eligible because of the new log; segment calibration, effect uncertainty, a reserve slot, and queue spillovers are all treated explicitly. The allocation itself (swarm I-04, I-02, and I-01; hold one slot) is a defensible risk-averse portfolio. The machinery that is supposed to justify it does not audit.

The written priority rule is not the rule that was applied. `p_adj` is used both for the calibration-adjusted breach probability and for the discounted risk reduction, and the displayed formula, \(PS = p_{adj}V - 450 - (1-p_{adj})B\), cannot reproduce the worked examples. Those examples actually use discounted \(\Delta p \times V - 450 - (1-p_{breach})B\), which is a coherent incremental value, but it is not what the rule says. The same section then says to rank by adjusted score and allocate greedily. That ranking is I-04 (€6,890), I-08 (€3,275), I-02 (€2,108), I-06 (€1,485), then I-01 (€1,478). The recommended set skips I-08 and I-06. Robustness and the imputed queue feature are reasonable reasons to do so, but they are not in the rule, so the rule is not deployable as written.

Several headline scores are arithmetically wrong even on the formula the calculations actually use:

- I-08: \(0.075\times100{,}000 - 450 - 0.655\times5{,}000 = 3{,}775\), not €3,275.
- I-09: \(0.156\times20{,}000 - 450 - 0.31\times800 = 2{,}422\), not €1,882 (adjusted €1,211, not €941).
- I-06: \(0.191\times15{,}000 - 450 - 0.48\times300 = 2{,}271\), not €2,121 (adjusted about €1,590, not €1,485).
- I-04 is €6,890 in the scoring table and €6,920 in the base impact row; I-02 is €2,108 and then €2,120.

Section 3 then subtracts the swarm cost twice. Each case’s “direct economic benefit” already nets out €450 plus expected false-alarm burden, and the net line subtracts another \(3\times€450 = €1{,}350\). Base \(10{,}518 - 1{,}350 - 4{,}320 = 4{,}848\) is therefore low by €1,350; the same €1,350 is embedded in the optimistic net (€9,961) and the adverse net (−€4,866). The closing range “+€4,800–€6,300” mixes the double-counted and single-counted base cases. The benefit and spillover columns are also point values, not the ranges the table asks for. Zeroing I-02 on top of putting every case on its lower bound stacks the two adverse clauses into a harsher scenario than the one specified.

The I-02 opportunity-cost comparison is decision-theoretically wrong. Incremental value is about \(\Delta p \times €25{,}000\), roughly €2,800 before costs. Comparing that with \(0.575\times€25{,}000 = €14{,}375\) treats breach cost the swarm cannot remove as a reason to intervene. The later value-cap illustration is also wrong: capping I-08 at €60,000 does not turn €3,275 into about €1,960, because the €450 and the expected €3,275 false-alarm term do not scale with value. On the paper’s own arithmetic the capped score is about €775.

Sections 4–6 are the strongest part of the answer: the I-05/I-07 contrast, cool-downs, an escalation override, label contamination, segment spillover tracking, and threshold-review triggers are concrete. Two of those proposals still fail. Queue length is not a valid instrument for the swarm’s direct effect, because congestion changes breach risk directly. Applying the SME under-prediction correction to Non-Native-Language Support invents a direction the evidence does not give. Non-monetary duties, especially public-sector and regulatory exposure beyond the reserve slot, stay thin relative to the task. The euro-level scores, built from unsupported causal factors 0.65–0.85, also claim more precision than the inputs support.
