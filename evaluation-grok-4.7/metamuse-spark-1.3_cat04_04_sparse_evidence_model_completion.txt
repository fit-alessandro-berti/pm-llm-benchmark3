6.5

The three candidates are genuinely different, the conservative language matches the seven observed variants, and the balanced partial order is the right middle hypothesis: both checks before approval, credit check before supplier confirmation, engineering review unordered with supplier confirmation, expedite review after both checks and unordered with supplier confirmation. That design replays all 30 cases. The permissive net is a real relaxation, assumptions about completion timestamps and the stocked-versus-missing supplier abstraction are stated, and the data-collection plan is concrete. The write-up is not reliable enough to score higher.

The eventual-order count is wrong in the ledger and again in the balanced justification. Supplier confirmation occurs in 23 cases; engineering review completes before it in 21, not 20. V5 is the only exception (2 cases). “20/23, violated by 2” does not add up. The modeling choice still follows from V5, but a core evidence claim is false.

Section 6 does not meet the audit requirement cleanly. The third balanced row is unfinished scratch (“corrected”, “Let’s give”, “Supplier Confirmation?”). The extra permissive row lists `Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, …` as admitted, then says it violates `Engineering Review` before expedite review and is not admitted. A required cell cannot both admit and reject the same trace.

Missing supplier events are treated as if they justify reordering. S5 removes events; it does not hide supplier-first traces or make the permissive net more robust to that loss than the balanced net. Both use the same skip choice. Supplier-first also goes past D5, which allows supplier confirmation after one check, not before both. The matrix row that ranks permissive as most robust to missing supplier confirmation is therefore wrong. “Sequence would halve fitness” is also loose: a credit-check-before-engineering-review sequence still fits the 19 credit-check-first cases, not half the log. Duplicate labels hurt simplicity, not alignment precision. These languages are finite (no loops, each activity at most once); the validation note that they may be infinite is incorrect. “Bounded playout confirms” is claimed without a run.

The validation script is not self-contained: it tells the reader to paste the models. The positive suite drops the observed `Engineering Review, Credit Check` skip (3 cases) and the comment says the conservative model accepts the first 7 positives, whose 7th trace is the unobserved expedited skip that the XOR model rejects. The shared negative list marks supplier-first as negative for every model, including the permissive model that is built to accept it. Printed fitness without per-model expectations does not check the suite.

Balanced remains the right immediate model and the uncertainty section names a real alternative (V5 as ingestion order, both checks before supplier confirmation) that would change that recommendation. The arithmetic error, the broken audit rows, the missingness/order confusion, and the non-checking validation harness keep this well short of a nearly flawless answer.
