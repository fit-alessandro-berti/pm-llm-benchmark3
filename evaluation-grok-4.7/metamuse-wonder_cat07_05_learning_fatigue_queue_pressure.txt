The required six-section structure is complete, every mechanism and intervention is covered, and the central argument correctly refuses a single-cause story. Early slowness is tied to carryover complexity and warm-up, mid-shift gains are treated as mixed learning and easier work, and the late speed-up with rising errors is tied to WIP, with fatigue, batching, and bot corrections as partial contributors. The constraints on punishment and quality-check removal are respected.

The score is held down by concrete defects. Intervention 7 states a Day queue of 3875; the supplied figures are 38 at the start and 75 at the end, so the magnitude is wrong. Many comparisons also lose their direction marker, leaving values such as “18.8  13.6” ambiguous. The PM4Py snippets do not do what their comments claim: default `filter_event_attribute_values` drops cases rather than Bot_Risk events, the year-spanning `filter_time_range` does not isolate late-day hours, and `filter_paths_performance` is called without duration bounds and without restricting Risk Classification to the bot. “U-shaped” mislabels a shallow dip followed by a steep rise. “Later under low WIP” is also used too strongly against end-of-shift fatigue, because those low-WIP events are mostly mid-shift; the later falsification test is better than that claim.
