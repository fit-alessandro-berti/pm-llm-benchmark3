The answer matches the requested outline and makes several defensible case calls, but it is not a reliable design. The decision procedure, the stated orders, the counts, and the code contradict each other, and several of those contradictions reproduce the semantic damage the task is meant to avoid.

The decision tree is not executable. Its root test is identical `eid` plus payload, yet every candidate already has a distinct `eid`. Exact technical duplicates are repeated source identities or re-ingestions, not shared event IDs. The next test, shared timestamp, activity, and related objects, then lists three incompatible outcomes with no predicate separating them. e03/e05 is placed on that branch even though I1 and I2 differ. e11/e16 does share timestamp, activity, and {O1, V1}, but the tree handles invoices on the negative branch. Summary-versus-detail, harmonization collision, and unresolved cases are names in a list, not terminal tests with one action each.

The event table misses required pair e14/e15. That omission matters: e15 is an invoice-level ERP balance update after payment messages and has no Payment object, so it is not a one-to-one derivative of e13. Treating e13 as its canonical parent leaves M2 out of the summary relationship. Several rows also say “retain both” while filling a canonical eid and an attribute-merge policy. e09/e10 are distinct events, so a canonical eid is unjustified. e11/e16 are the same issuance, but dropping billing relations and keeping only ERP attributes contradicts the fact that each system is authoritative for different attributes. Those two events already have the same object set, so the relation policy changes nothing. e03/e04 is the right merge, but copying scanner `source_seq` onto the WMS event is meaningless because sequence is source-local. e03/e05 and e13/e14 are correctly retained.

The ordering section mislabels the rule types. Within-source `source_seq` is semantic inside one system. Source-specific meaning is labeled “deterministic.” Global comparison of `source_seq` is invalid: ERP 30, WMS 100, scanner 551, and carrier 1 are not one sequence. The 10:00 order still lists e04 after declaring it merged. The 12:00 order both merges e11/e16 and places e16 after the captures, which invents a second invoice issuance. e13 before e14 is only a PSP log order, not proof that the partial payments were physically sequential. e08 before e09 is a plausible lifecycle, not a stated constraint; only e09 before e10 is given. The microseconds paragraph equates synthetic deltas with `ingested_at` and discusses lost concurrency. It does not explain the actual failure: offsets written into the timestamp before semantic ordering become indistinguishable from real durations and can freeze a false causal story. The suggested ingestion example does not even break e04 versus e05, which share `10:00:03`.

The conservative count is arithmetically inconsistent. The stated operations remove only e04 and e16, which leaves 15 events, not 12–14. The prose also never stores `original_timestamp`, `original_activity`, `source`, and `source_event_key`, despite that being a required property of the minimum-change pipeline. Evidence-based merging claims e06/e07 can be merged because their related objects are identical; e06 has items and e07 does not. The 8–10 and 5–7 retained-event ranges are not derived. Activity-plus-timestamp collapse of this log yields about 10 events, not 5–7: the tied groups are the three picks, the two packs, the two dispatches, the two invoices, and the three capture-labeled rows. The `have_common_object` warning itself is right: O1 and V1 intersect while M1 and M2 differ.

The implementation would not perform the design. It treats the OCEL as a flat dataframe, so the PM4Py calls are aimed at the wrong object. `duplicated(..., keep=False)` drops every member of an exact-duplicate group. Boolean `drop(mask)` is invalid, and the verified-pair “merge” keeps the canonical row without copying any other source attribute. The lifecycle block rewrites `timestamp` rather than a sort key, and the equality at 10:15 means it does not order e09 and e10 anyway. A cross-system `source_seq` tie-break places e07 before the picks and e15 before authorization and capture, the opposite of the stated order. The calls invent `key_columns`, `merge_attributes`, and `merge_relations`. The block never calls `ocel_drop_duplicates` or `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, never preserves the required original fields, never pre-checks candidate groups, and never validates referential integrity.

Sensitivity figures are wrong at the baseline. The raw log has 17 events and 44 event-to-object relations, not 25. Conservative retention of one shared object set for each true pair leaves 39 relations, not 18–20. Item DFGs, payment DFGs, OC-DFG frequencies, event-type/object-type weights, object-to-object relations, and lifecycle or performance effects are essentially absent. The six “edges” are vague, and some are reversed: keeping two `Pack Package` events creates the rework loop; merging them removes it. Ingestion times `10:15:01`, `10:15:20`, and `10:15:21` do not reverse e08 before e10. Issue Invoice followed by Capture Payment is not rework.

The provenance record covers most required fields, but reconstruction is only “keep the raw log and an audit row.” It does not say how to restore per-source attributes and relations or how to discard adjusted timestamps. The eight failure modes are present. The safeguard for shared objects is inverted: requiring the same objects and different activities would miss e03/e04 and e11/e16 and would treat different activities as duplicate evidence. Referential integrity does not detect an unlawful union of item or payment relations. The attribute safeguard stores hashes rather than authoritative values, while the conservative policy drops billing attributes.

The usable core is narrow: merge the reconciled I1 scan, keep I2 and M2, qualify release versus acceptance, keep summaries distinct, and refuse object intersection as identity. The delivered pipeline does not consistently do those things.
