5.5

The write-up follows the required eight-section structure and correctly keeps machine capacity last, but several required calculations and causal claims are wrong or internally inconsistent.

The J1 and J2 batch waits (3 h 55 min and 3 h 40 min) and J1’s S1 dwell (13:30 to 19:25 = 5 h 55 min) are arithmetically correct and properly labeled as counterfactuals. The other two required delays are not. For FIX-A, 11:45 − 10:47 = 58 min treats an unexplained post-idle interval as tool delay: CNC-2 is recorded idle only from 10:05 to 10:47, while FIX-A is released at 11:40 and processing starts at 11:45. The counterfactual is also self-contradictory: a release at 11:00 cannot produce a start at 10:47. For CAL-3, the 1 h 20 min from 16:05 to 17:25 is J6’s total wait, not the recalibration delay. J5 occupies TEST-1 and CAL-3 from 16:10 to 16:35, so J6 cannot start at 16:10 even with no recalibration; the idle window attributable to recalibration is 16:35–17:25. The claimed saving of 1 h 15 min does not match the stated 1 h 20 min wait.

That same confusion reappears in narrative 3, which assigns the CAL-3 recalibration to J5 even though J5 has already finished testing. L8 is inserted into narrative 1 with no L8 timing evidence. Motif 1 asserts that a component lot batches a batch, which is not a relation in the data. The ranking also misses the main coupling: J3 holds FIX-A until 11:40 and is batch-ready at 11:55, so the FIX-A hold is a plausible gate on B1 and therefore on J1 and J2; J4’s later FIX-A delay likewise feeds B2 and S1. Those paths are listed separately rather than shown as one cascade.

Two of the six supposed falsifiers of batch synchronization actually concern the lot override and the carrier schedule, so they do not weaken the top-ranked claim. The PM4Py block names the requested APIs but mixes invalid imports with `.apply` calls, never uses the discovered graphs, and compares the same `event_count` feature on two object types rather than two frequency metrics.
