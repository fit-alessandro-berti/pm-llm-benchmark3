7.5

The final answer meets the required seven-section structure and is strong on the resilience-versus-noncompliance distinction, control substitution versus suspension, and a concrete recovery redesign. Most DFG frequencies, rates, and conformance figures are used accurately, and it correctly refuses to treat throughput or later remediation as real-time compliance.

It is not nearly flawless. The repeated claim that about 90% of dispatched cases left unscanned is miscalculated: 3,250/3,600 is the share of packed orders that reached dispatch, while every observed dispatch in that phase was already “dispatch without scan,” so the unscanned share of dispatches is 100%. The headline comparison of roughly 1,150 T2–T3 traceability exceptions with 2,300 T4 audit-remediation events, and the derived “about 29% of 17,000 incident cases,” mixes different periods and activity counts. In the stabilization log, audit remediation follows invoice close; those 2,300 events are not shown to be the same population, and adding reconciliation and proof-of-delivery remediation counts items rather than unique cases. Smaller overstatements include saying the fast lane can merge into the authorization-skip path, that the manual release gate “stopped” about 200 cases, that the sub-two-hour activation target was met “the same morning,” and that the baseline’s 9% non-fitting traces were latent informal behavior.
