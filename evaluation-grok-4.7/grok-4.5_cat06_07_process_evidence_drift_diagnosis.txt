The diagnosis is substantively right and well triangulated: April is a schema/pipeline failure, June is a V1→V2 routing mix with calibration lag rather than concept drift, July–September combine a parts-path performance shock with a V3 outcome shift and (from September) Partner Z, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is censored. Evidence, contradictions, staged remediation, alert gating, and the six failure modes are all present and mostly proportionate. It is not nearly flawless.

The population-shift row implicates “volume growth Jun+,” which conflicts with the later narrative and with the data (Partner Z is 0% through August; June’s change is routing, not a new population). Test A calls immature-label calibration “optimistic,” which reverses the direction: early labels depress observed breach, so scores look too high (October 0.23 vs 0.16), while the label itself is the optimistic object. The claim that June’s 0.20 mean prediction is the expected residue of a “higher-V3” history is loose: V3 share barely moved (about 20% to 18%), and a frozen pre-June mix would sit near 0.18–0.19, not 0.20. May’s residual (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050, 2% unknown, 3% missing queue) is pinned too cleanly on the April feature-backfill gap; the audit only states that April’s cached diagnosis-count feature was not fully recomputed, while 1–5 May predates the patch. “Rise toward Aug-like levels” understates September, whose observed breach is already 0.31 at only 82% maturity and with a slower Partner Z tail.

The required sketch has concrete defects. `top_variants` is the first five `dict` items, not the most frequent variants. `parts_wait_sec = pdfg.get(...)` does not extract the median the audit actually uses (and may store a stats dict or a mean). `MonthEnd(1)` yields midnight on the last day, so `filter_time_range` can drop that day’s events, and the filter is redundant after the calendar-month groupby. A bare `except` hides a failed temporal-feature audit. An extra closing summary also breaks the “exactly six sections” constraint. Falsifiers are mostly implicit in the tests rather than stated per drift type.
