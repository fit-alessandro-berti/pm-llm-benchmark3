The final write-up is complete and gets the central result right: raw alignment cost is not business risk. Zero-cost CL-609 and CL-610 are treated as data-aware policy failures, high-cost CL-607 and CL-612 as scope mismatch, CL-604 as a severe pay-before-approval failure, and CL-605’s silent `tau` as a fitting artifact. All seven required sections, the case table, the named patterns, and the required remediation types are present. The code follows the requested PM4Py path and keeps raw cost separate from business severity.

Those strengths do not make the analysis tight. CL-604 is EUR 90,000, so P2 requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve before payment; the alignment’s `M(Fraud Screen)` is never carried into the policy result, which cites only P3. CL-609 fails P1 on both amount and MEDIUM risk, but the narrative and Section 6 reduce P1 to the EUR 5,000 cap. P6 is an unresolved scope dispute, yet CL-607 is stated as out of scope. Population counts are inflated: the month has 74 retry cases and nine external-adjuster gaps, not CL-603 plus 74 others and CL-606 plus nine. Section 6 then invents a “missing EUR 800 notification,” which is not in the evidence. CL-608 is called likely P4-compliant and still scored 4, the same band as completed auto-approval bypasses, while the 1–5 scale never defines bands 2–4. The CL-609/CL-610 hypotheses are nearly the same claim, and the portfolio audits CL-602 without a preventive in-system fraud-screen gate. The code fabricates amount and risk for CL-607 and never aggregates risk level, which the specification required.

The judgment is therefore directionally sound and usable only after correction of policy overlays, counts, and the evidence table.
