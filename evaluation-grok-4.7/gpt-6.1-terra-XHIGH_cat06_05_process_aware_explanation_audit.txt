The audit is largely precise: it separates future-outcome leakage from mutable as-of defects, keeps queue and prefix age as conditional signals, refuses the 65% team coincidence, and treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as later-but-legal while treating `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as an unresolved tie rather than a proven reversal. The four standards (predictive, causal, actionable, fairness) are explicit, and the plan correctly requires a rebuild, perturbation tests, and no reuse of the leaked SHAP ranks.

The required enabled / impossible / merely future-possible split is still blurred. Immediate post-coverage steps (fast-track assessment or a document request) are the enabled XOR successors, but they are labeled only “future-possible,” the same term used for an appeal. Nothing in that check is explicitly marked impossible, so the trichotomy is named rather than applied.

Section 4 is the other material weakness. `prediction_status` is `recompute`, yet `credible_drivers` still reports increase/decrease from the contaminated model. The in-field wording admits those are original associations, but feature attributions are not transportable once appeal, final decision, and full-trace variant are removed; those directions are not yet credible explanations. Channel is withheld in Case X and then reappears in that case’s causal boundary. The residual “base and other features” bundles, where extraction-time employee experience could sit, are never audited.

Smaller gaps: a prefix document-request count at this milestone is structurally zero on conforming traces, not merely zero “here”; the nightly document snapshot’s training-extraction leak versus online staleness is collapsed into one “can include” claim; and the section 5 plan never pins down whether the 10-day clock starts at registration and whether closure duration is the settlement target.
