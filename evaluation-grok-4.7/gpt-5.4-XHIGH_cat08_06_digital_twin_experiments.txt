6.6

The design is complete, constraint-aware, and often realistic, but it is not nearly flawless. Several modeling choices are underspecified or inconsistent with the log and the tree, and parts of the skeleton implement the wrong rule.

The scope correctly treats this as a calendar-aware discrete-event model with non-stationary, clustered arrivals, external delays, latent objections, and cross-case batching. The join is misplaced. The tree synchronizes Planning, Fire, and Environmental before public notice, then runs notice and inspection in sequence before drafting. “AND-join before Decision drafting” allows inspection or notice to overlap reviews. Appeal, planning rework, and legal rework have counts but no return arcs. Service samples are not defined as interruptible working time, even though Planning, Environmental, and Legal p90 effort exceeds an 8-hour shift. Schedulers are collapsed into the inspector pool despite `scheduler_1` and a Tuesday–Friday field calendar.

Parameter estimation correctly keeps performance-DFG times out of service distributions, separates applicant delay, and treats long public notice as a latent objection proxy. It never handles right-censoring of open cases, which matters for a 260-day tail inside an 18-month log. It also misses that the tree’s `Completeness rework` and `Inspection rework` labels are not in the excerpt: P001 repeats `Completeness check`. Replaying that tree will look unfit for a labeling reason, not a routing reason. Seasonality is taken as a stable annual cycle from roughly one observed cycle.

Assumptions are separated cleanly and usually conservative. Service-time independence conflicts with shared bundle travel. The cap of three loops can suppress the tail the study is meant to measure.

The experiment matrix covers B0, D1–D10, extra packages, and three stresses, with sensible trade-offs. D4’s “off-peak utilization falls” is wrong for a peak-only temporary inspector. Low/base/high is not defined as one-factor versus full factorial, and the design is not tied to a compute budget. Decision rules then undo some of that care: thresholds are post-submission, so D1/D7 delay and attrition improve the headline metrics by shifting work outside the clock. There is no access or permits-issued guardrail. The 10%, 15%, 70–85%, and SLA day cutoffs are arbitrary, though marked provisional.

Replication practice is mostly sound: backlog initialization, long runs, separate streams, common random numbers, percentile intervals, and sensitivity to latent parameters. Paired differences under common random numbers are not the reported estimand. A 12/6 holdout is season-confounded; rolling-origin is only optional. Epistemic error from missing starts, missing objections, and one seasonal cycle will dominate Monte Carlo error.

The skeleton follows the requested PM4Py path (`pandas.read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, DFG, performance DFG, variants, arrival rate, no fake `read_csv`) and keeps discovery separate from the event engine. Working logic still has bugs: legal review is limited to Commercial and Complex, but P001 is New residential and has Legal check; external-delay matching cross-joins repeated request/receive pairs; `get_case_arrival_average` is labeled in hours while PM4Py returns seconds; waiting time uses the previous flattened event, which is not the predecessor under parallelism. Monday clerk loss, simple-only planners, and council blackouts are described and then omitted from the calendars.
