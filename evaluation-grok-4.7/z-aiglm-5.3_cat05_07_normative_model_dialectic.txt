8.5

The final answer is a high-quality dialectical conformance review: both briefs are genuinely argued, concessions are real, classifications are case-specific rather than hedged, and the layered revision matches the control-flow, data, resource, and authority problems in the record. It is not nearly flawless.

The strongest result is the separation of norm, model, and log. CS-9 is correctly treated as controlling where M0 is stricter, especially the requirement that both checks finish before the exclusive choice, which forces Quality_Review even on rejection. PD-3 is held to be a time-bounded exception rather than an amendment, so SUP-702 and SUP-704 are justified while SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714 are not. SUP-708 is correctly treated as a sanctions-control failure despite no transactions. SUP-706 and SUP-715 are distinguished on burden of proof in a way that is explicit and consistent. The matrix covers all fifteen cases, and the POWL construction is syntactically aligned with the given API: concurrent sanctions and quality on the standard route, manual, auto, and emergency branches, a zero-or-more Request_More_Info loop that avoids the odd-count loop quirk, rejection, and notification before closure.

The model still has an unstated regression. Because each route is a whole exclusive branch, Quality_Review followed by Reject_Supplier fits neither the exit branch nor the standard route. M0 could express rejection after the checks; M1 cannot, and the limitations list does not say so. “No Quality_Review required” on exit also understates the code, which makes quality on that path impossible rather than optional. The same exclusivity leaves Request_More_Info unavailable before rejection.

The validation paragraph overclaims. As written, the auto route requires Master_Data_Validation and reactivation requires Reactivation_Assessment, so SUP-702, SUP-703, SUP-714, and SUP-710 do not fit those branches. The legacy allowances are discussed earlier, but the validation sentence still says they fit. The auto route also turns PD-3’s permissive “may” for post-activation quality into a mandatory after-activation edge; a quality-first auto-approval trace fits no branch. That caveat is stated for the emergency route and not carried through to the auto route.

There is one clear logical slip in the system judgment: the nine cases with missing eligibility attributes are included in the clause describing where hard controls “were violated,” although they are unresolved. The approximate count of about twenty-five noncompliant cases is otherwise defensible only if SUP-703 is inside the fourteen and SUP-714 is inside the six; that arithmetic is not shown. Several overlay rows also remain compound predicates rather than fully atomic rules.

These are localized defects in an otherwise rigorous answer, but they are real inaccuracies in model semantics, validation, and the system-level count.
