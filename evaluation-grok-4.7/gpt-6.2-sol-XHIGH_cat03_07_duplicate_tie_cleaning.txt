8.2

The pipeline is conservative and numerically careful, but it is not nearly flawless. Several claims are softer than the write-up presents, and the implementation does not fully carry the policy it specifies.

The classification is mostly right. Exact replays are the only drops. e03/e04 and e11/e16 are identity merges. e05 and e14 stay distinct. e08/e09 are a harmonization collision, not one dispatch. e09 stays before e10. e06/e07 and e15 stay at separate abstraction levels unless a stricter scenario says otherwise. e13 is not treated as the cause of e15. Shared O1/V1 is correctly rejected as proof that M1 and M2 are the same capture. Counts check out: 17/44, 15/39, 14/37, and 13/35, including O1–P1 support 9→8→7→7 and O1–V1 support 6→5→5→4. The 22h45m e10→e17 interval is right. Ingestion is not used as business time, original timestamps are kept, and a total order is not sold as physical truth.

The decision procedure is a flat catalog, not a tree. It never says which test wins when e06/e07 can be read as one occurrence, a derived summary, or an unresolved pair. Fact 6 is also overstated as “reconciled” invoice evidence. Only e03/e04 is tied to a reconciliation report. V1 shows same invoice identity, not equal attribute authority.

Section 3 is the right partial order, then it overcommits. e08 before e09 and e11 before the PSP events are smallest-id linearizations, not domain results. The text says to “use” those orders and only afterward admits e09–e08–e10 and a payment-before-invoice order. The code cannot emit those alternatives: picks-before-this-pack, e06→e07, and every within-source sequence are hard edges in every scenario. Source sequence was supposed to be deterministic unless the producer guarantees transaction order. PSP sequence therefore freezes e13 before e14 without a lifecycle fact.

Section 7 is strong on the chosen linearization and weak on the uncertainty it names. Dispatch→Dispatch disappears in an admissible 10:15 order. Raw capture self-edge count and invoice/payment adjacency also move. Those flips are mentioned, not measured. “e03 replaces e04 as I1’s pack predecessor” is true only in the chosen raw order.

The code is PM4Py-aware in structure and not reliable as the specified pipeline. `contract` keeps the survivor row. `authority` is empty, so billing and scanner values are not field-merged or conflict-logged. The audit object stores canonical id, removed id, and rule, not the section 8 schema. `ocel_drop_duplicates` never runs. `ocel_merge_duplicates` is only a shape check. Its merged event is discarded and replaced by `contract`. `tie_rank` restarts inside each timestamp, so it is not a total sort key unless the library sorts by timestamp first. The per-bucket rank plus the assert is a fragile reading of `ocel_sort_by_additional_column`. The evidence path always merges e07, while the prose says that merge is conditional on one-to-one message lineage.

Provenance, failure modes, and the `have_common_object` warning are substantively good. The grade stops at 8.2 because admissible orders are not actually analyzed, attribute merge is specified and then not done, and the required PM4Py duplicate operations do not produce the logs being interpreted.
