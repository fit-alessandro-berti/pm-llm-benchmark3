4.5

The memo looks complete and reaches the right high-level decision — do not publish an “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, and do not expand blindly — but it is not reliable enough to brief leadership. Several headline numbers are wrong, the preferred design does not fit these data, and the required code does not run.

**Quantitative discipline fails where it matters.** The adopter table defines a 3-month pre and 3-month post window, then does not compute it. Alpha’s April–June median average is about 50.4, not 49.4 (change about +1.5 hours, not +0.5). Alpha’s three-month post rework average is about 13.2, not 14.1. Beta’s April–June p90 average is about 103, not 104, and the reported +11 hours does not match that window. Gamma has only two post months, so the stated 3-month post window is impossible, and the pre rework figure of 13.5 does not match April–June. The control comparison is a January-to-August endpoint, not an aligned contrast, and Gamma is never used as a not-yet-treated control for April–June. That omission reverses the story: over the early post window, never-treated and not-yet-treated medians and p90s deteriorated by more than Alpha’s and Beta’s, so raw “no median gain, tail harm” is not the comparative result. Escalation of +3.3 to +3.8 points is mostly the March fraud-desk jump, which appears in every branch before Alpha and Beta adopt. The post-March residual is closer to 1 point. Calling a +3.5-point automation increase “observed,” and a safe claim at confidence 80, is not defensible.

**Intensity, mechanisms, and sensitivity are selective.** Within Alpha, fast-path share rises smoothly while median and p90 worsen through June and then improve by August, coincident with the FTE loss and complexity jump. Gamma’s p90 falls as intensity rises, so “more fast path, worse tail” is an Alpha endpoint comparison, not a pattern. Gamma’s −6.5 hours uses a pre window that sits on the May–June spike; August median is about 36, essentially Gamma’s January level. The sensitivity claims (“robust to dropping June/May”) do not re-estimate against the common shock and do not report the alternative arithmetic. Arrivals per FTE rise sharply, especially at Alpha and Beta, yet cycle time is never normalized by load, so the memo misses the main operational question: flat cycle time under a much heavier queue is not the same fact as “no performance change.” Mechanism cells cite March-to-trough or peak-to-trough spans and label them as adoption windows.

**Estimands and identification are softer than the format suggests.** Availability versus use is distinguished, which is right, but “not identifiable” is applied inconsistently (E1 partial, E4–E5 simply “No”), and E5’s reason — escalation is substitution, hence not causal — contradicts the later claim that automation caused escalation. E8’s contrast (`automation=1` vs `0` on branches that stay at 0) does not define a spillover. There is no estimand that separates the automation effect holding arrivals, FTE, and case mix fixed from the effect of the whole rollout bundle. Synthetic control is called preferable because Delta and Epsilon may be non-comparable, which is exactly why two donors cannot build a convex counterfactual for branches selected for worse cycle times and only three pre-months. SCM still needs a no-unmeasured-time-varying-confounding assumption; it does not avoid parallel trends. The justification is also under the required 150–250 words. The best available contrast — Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon still untreated in April–June — is not the primary design.

**The recommendation is the right class and the wrong experiment.** Embedded evaluation beats immediate expansion. Piloting the easiest branch (Delta: lowest complexity, best cycle times) at 50% case-level randomization does not identify effects for Alpha-like load and case mix, and shared specialist and fraud-desk queues violate the case-level SUTVA the design relies on. Phase length is stated as both 3 and 6 months. Stop rules are absolute percent changes on series that already move by more than that from the May shock, with no concurrent control. Several decision-rule inequalities are missing, so the green-light threshold is not readable.

**The code does not satisfy §9.** `pd.compat.StringIO` will not load the panel on current pandas. Trends are drawn with `plt.subplot`, which the prompt forbids. `days // 30` collides February with March and skips a step into April, so relative time is not event time; never-treated rows collapse to −1; the column is never used. Branch fixed effects are not created (`branch` is not in `X`, so `get_dummies` raises). The intensity model reads `df['month_num']`, which was never assigned. Clustered standard errors and the small-cluster warning are therefore unreachable. Assumptions are print statements after a crash, not a specification that could be inspected.

What keeps this above a failing memo is coverage: availability versus intensity, staggered and endogenous adoption, fraud-desk interference, CSAT nonresponse, aggregation, small-cluster uncertainty, a refusal of a pooled percent effect, and a phased go/no-go rather than a rollout. Those are the right topics. They are implemented with arithmetic the panel does not support and with uncertainty numbers that are too high for the design.
