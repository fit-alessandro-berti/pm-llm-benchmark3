The answer covers all seven required sections, uses the timeline, DFGs, conformance results, and segment risks, and draws the right core distinction: hospital-first load-shedding with a contemporaneous control can be resilience, while skipped authorization, unscanned dispatch, and post-invoice reconstruction cannot. The recovery model includes triggers, triage, product-level minimum controls, evidence capture, backlog rules, and a split between reservation reconciliation and regulated-evidence work. Indicators cover throughput, service, traceability, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness.

Those strengths sit next to concrete errors.

T3 “defect rates halved” is wrong for the bundle. Traceability 9.5%→5.4%, cold-chain 4.8%→2.3%, and dual control 2.9%→1.4% are about half. Rework 12.5%→9.0% and cancellations 7.8%→5.2% are not.

The adaptation table contradicts itself and the case. Row E calls manual pick lists the largest contributor to the 9.5% traceability-exception rate. The case states that dispatch without scan created the largest traceability gap, and row G repeats that. Row D treats the 580 snapshot→defer arcs as snapshot conflict; that arc is labeled deferral of noncritical orders, so it does not show stock error. Row C says the 1,600 authorization skips likely supported hospital fill, but the hospital fast lane is a separate 1,000-case fork that never enters the skip activity. Row F says missing dual control “rose far less than a no-gate scenario would imply,” which is unevidenced; the observed rise is 0.2%→2.9%. The same row does not reconcile the fact that 1,450 of 1,650 temporary releases then take Dispatch without scan. Row L ties the 24% nonfitting T4 traces to audit remediation; 24% of 35,000 is about 8,400 traces, against 2,300 remediation cases.

Open-queue counts are overstated. Section 1 says 2,300 cases “still sat” in audit repair. Recommendation P7 says those 2,300 and the 1,400 reconciliations were “still open.” The stabilization DFG shows 2,100 of 2,300 audit cases reached end, and 1,200 of 1,400 reconciliations moved to pick or cancel. Gross activity volume is treated as a remaining backlog. That weakens the otherwise correct hidden-workload argument.

The backlog-queue→manual-pick temporal deviation (exceeded baseline p95) is the conformance finding least used. Hospital-ambient priority is left as “P0/P1,” and allowing pick to start before eligibility sits uneasily beside the rule that controlled product never moves without authorization. Section 3 also attributes the whole fitness drop (0.96→0.71; 91%→44%) to skip-authorization and unscanned dispatch, although missing release, phone proof of delivery, and spreadsheet invoice hold are separate move deviations.

The judgment and redesign are strong. The numerical and causal slips are frequent enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
