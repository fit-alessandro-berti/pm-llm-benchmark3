4.5

The write-up reaches the right headline — a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not identified — and it hits the required section skeleton. It is not a usable expert assessment. Several reported contrasts contradict the stated windows, the code cannot run, and a few causal labels are wrong.

**Format.** Section 1 is about 140 words (minimum 150). The identification rationale is about 80 words and the rollout section about 125 (minimums 150 and 250). Section 5 asks for a compact pre/post table and gives prose. Those are compliance failures, not style nits.

**Arithmetic does not match the windows given.** Alpha/Beta are defined as March versus May–August. On that window Alpha’s median change is about −0.9 h (49.4 → ~48.6), not +0.9 h. The parenthetical “49.4…48.8” already shows a decline, so the “+” sign is internally inconsistent; +0.9 only appears if August, the highest-intensity month, is dropped. Alpha p90 on that window is about +11 h, not +8.9. Beta rework goes from 12.8 to 12.7 (−0.1), not +0.4; April actually fell to 10.4. Gamma “June versus August” is then scored as median −4.9 h, p90 −13.3 h, and rework −0.7 pp. June→July/August is about −8.7 h, −16 h, and −2.0 pp. Delta “August versus April–June average” is about −2.3 h, not −1.3; Epsilon is about −5.2 h, not −3.3. “Approximate” does not cover sign errors or factor-of-two misses. The sensitivity sentence bounds only median cycle time, not two outcomes.

**Pre-trends are misstated.** Alpha and Epsilon medians and p90s are lower in March than in January; February is a dip, and March rises with the fraud desk. “All five branches show rising medians and p90s from January–March” is false. Regression to the mean is then blurred with the May complexity shock, which pushes cycle time the other way.

**Escalation is over-attributed.** Every branch, treated or not, jumps about +2 pp in March when the fraud desk opens. Later adoption-timed increments are closer to +1–2 pp (controls also rise ~0.5–0.8 pp in May). A “suggestive +2 pp” with interval +1.5 to +3.5 and confidence 60 folds a common shock into the treatment contrast. The same conflation appears in the “Observed” ladder bullet (2–4 pp).

**Mechanisms cherry-pick.** Alpha/Beta rework falls in the first automation month and rebounds with the May mix shift; Gamma’s rework falls from 14.6 to 12.6 at adoption. The table reports only “rework rose slightly in Alpha.” Gamma’s median and p90 fall immediately, so “median rose or stable in the first 2–3 months” is not the panel pattern. Arrivals at Alpha/Beta jump ~14% in April, not “+5–10%.”

**Identification.** Availability versus intensity, staggered timing, never-treated Delta/Epsilon, interference via the fraud desk, and small-cluster fragility are the right concerns. Naming the fast-path contrast a LATE is incorrect; nothing here is an instrumented complier effect. The preferred event study adjusts for arrivals, FTE, and complexity without flagging post-treatment / bad-control bias (routing, staffing, and the May mix shift are contemporaneous with treatment). SUTVA is listed as an assumption in the same answer that says the fraud desk interferes. Heterogeneous-timing bias is never actually discussed, despite the ban on naïve two-way fixed effects. The “conditionally causal” ladder claim (−3 h to +4 h under conditional parallel trends) is an informal bound, not an estimate under those assumptions, and section 7 already calls the median effect unidentified. The p90 interval (0 to +12 h, “possible worsening”) excludes the Gamma improvement cited in the same row as counterevidence.

**Bias directions are not coherent.** Staffing losses and a harder case mix would push treated cycle times up and hide benefits; fraud-desk spillovers that also hit controls would hide harm. The table calls both “downward bias.” Case mix is not even treated-only: Epsilon’s complexity rise matches Alpha’s.

**Code.** The CSV is replaced by an ellipsis, so it is not self-contained. `pd.compat.StringIO` is not a current pandas API. There is no panel validation, no trend print for the five outcomes (only two branch-level means), and no adopter-specific pre/post. Relative time codes every pre-period and every never-treated month as −99, so leads cannot be estimated, and the indicator is never used. The “relative time” model is binary post + branch + month fixed effects, i.e. the naïve TWFE the comment claims it is not, and it is not clustered. Clustered uncertainty appears on only one model. The warning tells the reader that assumptions are in comments; those comments are absent. The section 5 results (−0.8 h per +10 pp, clustered SE 1.4, +0.4 pp escalation) are not produced by this script.

**Rollout.** “Expand with an embedded evaluation” is a reasonable choice, then the design undercuts it. “Randomized phase-in” is immediately replaced by a deterministic Delta-first assignment, with no reason to prefer the easier branch over Epsilon for external validity. A 95th percentile of eight monthly p90s is not a weekly guardrail. With one treated and one control branch, “90% CI excludes +2 h” is not a feasible decision statistic. The OR branch (fast-path share >35% and no p90 worsening) can scale up with no median benefit. The experiment does not freeze the routing campaign that already confounds adoption.

The cautious refusal of an X% claim and the threat inventory are real merits. They do not offset incorrect contrasts, a non-replicating analysis, and an evaluation rule that cannot be computed.
