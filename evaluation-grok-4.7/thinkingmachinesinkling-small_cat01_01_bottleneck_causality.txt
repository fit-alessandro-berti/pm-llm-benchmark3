The final write-up gets the central pattern right and is usable, but it is not close to the required standard of causal and format discipline.

What holds up: the crude Orion mean (21.96 h) versus Nova (15.24 h) is correctly reversed inside every complexity band, and the pooled-complexity standardization (~17.1 h vs ~19.7 h) is arithmetically sound. The text explicitly says that reversal does not identify an effect. Queue time is separated from review service with correct component means, completed-case selection is treated as a real bias, and the 50% reassignment is rejected in favor of a small randomized routing pilot plus a capacity/compliance change. The five hypothesis types and the variable-role coverage (confounder, mediator, collider, proxy) are present. Most stratum, shift, compliance, and rework figures check out.

What keeps the score down:

- Section 8 is missing. The required five residual uncertainties, each with one data field or design change, are not there.
- Section 1 is roughly 450–480 words against a hard 180–300 limit.
- Mediation and confounding are mixed. The graph has no path from `resource_group` to compliance or rework, and the text admits compliance is set by complexity, yet compliance/rework are still called mediators of an Orion effect. Controlling for them is not a discriminating test, because that same result is predicted by case-mix confounding.
- Two factual errors: Orion does not “host 93% of night work” (13/17 night cases ≈ 76%; 92.9% is the share of Orion cases that are night). High-complexity Nova is not “both night/high”; C30 is Day/Normal.
- “Unbiased ATE” from the routing pilot ignores queue interference. Moving a case changes other cases’ waits, so the pilot is not an ordinary no-interference experiment.
- `resource_group → shift` treats shift as a downstream mediator. Shift is at least as plausibly a routing input or a bundled operating condition; that choice changes what adjustment identifies.
- The compliance-only contrast is overread as “group adds little.” Those slices are still complexity-imbalanced.
- “Ecological fallacy” is the wrong name for this Simpson reversal.
- The code never uses `pm4py.parse_event_log_string`, builds artificial timestamps, and mislabels `discover_dfg` output (`dfg[0]` is edges, not nodes; `dfg[1]` is start activities, not edges). Structural zeros in the complexity–shift–group overlap are also dropped rather than warned on.
- Confidence is inconsistent: 60/100 in the verdict, “almost certainly confounded” in the recommendation.

The decision-relevant conclusion—do not move half of Orion’s work on the crude mean—is the right operational call and is supported by the reversal. The missing section, the mediation slip, and the two misstated facts are enough to keep this well below a high score.
