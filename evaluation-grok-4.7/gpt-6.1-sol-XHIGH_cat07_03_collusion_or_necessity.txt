The assessment is carefully calibrated and structurally complete. It treats the log as a mixture, separates routine routing, emergency specialization, bank/payment exposure, and data-quality defects, and does not turn co-working frequency into suspicion or name collusion as a finding. Counts are not invented, overlapping populations are not added, and the Lena–Kim cluster is correctly ranked highest because separation-of-duties exposure, vendor concentration, value, manual upload, and speed coincide while emergency status explains little of it. Section 6 is within the word range and preserves the required distinctions.

The technical section is not equally reliable. The log skeleton is discovered from an emergency reference population and then checked against the full resolved log, so ordinary non-emergency variants would appear as deviations. The unused discovered DECLARE model invites the same scope error. The “verified” four-eyes call still depends on `org:resource` unless the unseen resolver rewrites that field; that is the contaminated identifier the scenario warns about. The independent actor-intersection test is stated afterward, but the executable path does not pass a resolved resource key. The 15- and 20-minute path filters never state that PM4Py expects seconds.

Smaller judgment issues remain. Published denominators for the same named pairs disagree (Carla–Gita 126/118/84 and Dan–Hugo 104/96/62; Lena–Kim’s 19-minute median on 44 cases versus a 17-minute “fast” median on 16), yet that inconsistency is only covered by a general overlap warning. Section 6 says the routine pairs are consistent with assigned teams, although the evidence labels approver teams, not buyer-to-team assignments. Two hundred ten six-minute Bot_Match-to-Kim payment-exception approvals on a higher-risk portfolio are placed at monitoring level despite deserving a defined sample.
