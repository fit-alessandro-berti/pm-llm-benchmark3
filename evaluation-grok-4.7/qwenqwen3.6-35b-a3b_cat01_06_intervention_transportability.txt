The final answer is structurally complete and lands on the right decision, conditional go, while correctly refusing to treat Site A’s nine-hour median drop as an identified effect. That is not enough to carry the analysis. Several required pieces are factually or formally wrong, and the quantitative and model sections do not do the work the question demands.

Section 1 has twelve features and the right columns, but tail and rework copy the same before/after figures into both Site A columns, so those rows do not compare anything. Section 3 lists the co-interventions and marks the cycle-time change as confounded, which is the right causal posture, yet the cells render as concatenated numbers (4132, 9678, 8%71%), and the waiting-time drop is still credited to parallelization without subtracting huddles, added document FTE, mix shift, and the API change. Confidence scores are unexplained.

The transport graph names the requested nodes and uses some `S_` selection nodes, but signs are parenthetical annotations rather than structure, interactions are missing, and there is no selection node for digital share, API latency, or huddles. The explanation is about 146 words against a required 180–280, and it never uses the graph to bound a transported effect. The directly-follows counts are unused, including the exact identity `2108 = 0.62 × 3400`.

Scenarios are labeled and one tail range moves the wrong way, but the bottleneck case’s median is −1 to +1 hours, so it does not actually state a case where average performance improves while the tail worsens. No arithmetic maps Site A’s six-hour wait reduction, the 18-hour originals delay, pool sizes, or the supplement count (360) into the ranges. The optimistic −4 to −6 hours on 100% of cases ignores that originals dominate the critical path for 62% of volume.

The intervention prose correctly gates document checking on originals, then contradicts itself by also starting legal review in parallel with verification. The relation set is not a partial order: it contains a supplement cycle, forces legal review on every case, and always orders originals before document check, erasing the digital path. The pilot then reintroduces a “full parallel” arm that the problem forbids. Assignment is simultaneously the case and the team; shared pools of 4/7/3/2 make team randomization interfere; 500 cases per arm does not fit three arms and strata inside roughly 1,570 cases in twelve weeks; and 90-day appeals cannot be observed in a twelve-week window.

The code calls the requested top-level names and the limitation comment is apt, but the Site B model is not a POWL strict partial order plus an optional legal branch and loop. It invents `POWLNode` and `POWLRelation`, encodes the loop as a back-edge, and makes legal review mandatory, so soundness and playout are not meaningful.

Section 9 is about 220 words, short of 250–400, and claims a 15–25% median reduction that contradicts both the “not an identified effect” warning and the −2 to −3 hour digital scenario. Assumption 3 (legal review concurrent with document checking) conflicts with the stated statutory order, and assumption 4 exists only to support cross-training that the recommendation says not to copy. The safe transferable piece—identity work overlapping the originals wait, with document check released only on receipt—is never cleanly specified as the first slice.
