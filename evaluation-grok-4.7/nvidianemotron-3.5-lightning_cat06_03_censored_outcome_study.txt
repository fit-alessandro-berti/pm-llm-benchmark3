5.5

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-default, accounts for all 48,000 applications, and prefers a temporal split. Those are the right instincts. The estimand, censoring scheme, validation timeline, and implementation do not hold together.

The primary target mixes incompatible quantities. It calls a prefix-conditional probability “marginal,” then conditions on eventual activation while predicting from a pre-activation prefix. At that landmark, activation is still future, so \(P(\text{default}_{90d}\mid\text{activation},\text{prefix})\) is not an operational early-warning estimand unless scoring is delayed until activation or activation is modeled explicitly. Section 3 then treats cause-specific Cox and Fine–Gray as interchangeable and puts non-activated outcomes into the risk set of a clock that starts at activation. Those are different estimands, and non-activation cannot be a competing event on a post-activation time scale. Still-open cases are ordinary right-censoring only if the default clock has started; here it has not. The 3,600 immature activations are called “left-truncated,” which is the wrong missingness mechanism: they are delayed labels and/or administrative censoring, not left truncation.

The cohort table repeats that confusion by describing the 21,600 already-mature labeled rows as right-censored when activation + 90 days passes 15 February 2026. Inclusion and exclusion rules stay too vague to implement, and transfers are not checked for double-counting under the 60% linkage table.

Validation contradicts itself. Training is defined as starts through 30 June 2025, before the 1 July 2025 policy change, but the text says that window includes early post-change cases. The second test fold uses starts from 1 January 2026 through 15 February 2026, which is outside the stated event window ending 31 December 2025. Subgroup performance is named as a requirement and then omitted; distribution shift is asserted rather than designed. Metrics are also restricted to activated accounts in one sentence and extended to competing-risk incidence in another.

Feature rules leak or mis-repair data. `first_activation_activity` is post-prediction if the prefix is early. Section 4 says events after the prefix are not used for the label, which would make the 90-day outcome unusable. “Experience at event” is defined as extraction time minus event time, which is staleness, not historical experience. Target-style encoding of similar prefixes is proposed without an out-of-fold, as-of constraint.

The skeleton does not implement the study. `get_trace_statistics_log`, `get_case_attributes`, and `filter_event_log` are not appropriate PM4Py calls. Activation time is stored as the case’s last timestamp, `default_90d` is read from the first row, `label_available_at` is never parsed, leaky columns are not removed, and the DFG/variants are fit on all periods before the temporal split. Cohort counts in code do not reproduce the status table, and the manual waiting-time feature is taken from the unfiltered log.
