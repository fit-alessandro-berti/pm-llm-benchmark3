5.5

The final recommendation is directionally sound: A and B are correctly last and labeled nonconformant, the primary path is a conformant second-officer review, and parallel rescreening is a legitimate way to reduce match uncertainty. The required labels, table, and six-section structure are present. The reasoning that supports that ranking is not reliable enough to carry the recommendation.

The largest error is in the Action D continuation. Clearing the fuzzy match and pushing the score under 70 does not reopen Auto Clear. Auto Clear requires no alert and a score below 40, and it is a path from screening to execution that this case has already passed. Analyst review has occurred, and a later rescreen still does not create that shortcut. Calling that branch a fast success misstates the normative process.

The S2-versus-S4 comparison is also misclassified. Both officers can deliver a conformant review. Waiting for S4 is another second-review plan, not a sanctions violation. The answer treats interrupting P-901 as the lesser of “SLA breach versus sanctions violation,” then uses a pessimistic bound for S4 (65 plus 35 minutes) while saying S2 “secures” P-884’s deadline. S2’s own stated range is 35–75 minutes of review plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute, so it can miss 90 minutes. The review can also run longer than the 45-minute interruption used to price P-901’s spillover. The required 35-minute versus 65-minute reranking is only described as “competitive,” without an actual reordered plan.

The fallback has the same category problem. If the deadline becomes unreachable during a lawful review, the process still permits late approval, rejection on the merits, or a hold while a report is filed. Immediate rejection is not required by the clock. Deadline loss is about €20,000–€35,000, whereas false-rejection or prolonged-hold harm is about €15,000–€80,000, and approval after conformant review is only 0.55–0.75. Rejecting because the SLA will fail can therefore be dominated by finishing the review.

Several required comparisons are incomplete. Section 4 never maps C, D, and E to the mandatory rules. Benefit figures are added as if they were expected value, without the approval probability, and P-901’s unquantified loss is simply declared smaller. The 12-minute-old score, non-transportability of the observational predictions, report non-disclosure, and the option to screen first and interrupt S2 only if S4 slips are not used. The reranking trigger, S4 free in under 20 minutes, sits outside the stated 35–65 minute arrival range.
