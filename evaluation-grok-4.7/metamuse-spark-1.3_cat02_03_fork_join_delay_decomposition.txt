7.4

The fork-join accounting is conceptually sound, and most case figures, critical-branch counts, and counterfactual savings check out. The write-up is not nearly flawless: the required decomposition is internally inconsistent on a spotlight case, one aggregate is wrong, one counterfactual tie is misstated, and the Python does not cleanly match the prose.

**What holds.** `join_ready = max(end_i)`, sync as `join_ready - end_i`, and total flow as upstream + one fork-join interval + downstream queue + final service are the right definitions. The F01 double-count illustration is correct: sync waits of 0, 5, and 22 overlap inside a 50-minute fork-join, so summing them does not measure elapsed time. Critical counts are right (A 2, B 8, C 2). Service and queue means are right (A 50/0, B 37.5/19.2, C 20.2/10.4), as are mean syncs (11.0, 4.3, 30.4) and mean fork-join (61.0). Ranking B above A, with A as a 50-minute floor and C as a two-case spike, follows from completion time and critical frequency rather than from mean service. CF1 savings of 15 minutes (F01 5, F11 10, else 0) and CF2 savings of 92.5 are correct, including the four zero-benefit cases. The same-resource split is largely right: F05, F08, F09, and F11 are fully busy-blocked; F04 is only partly blocked (8 of 18). Sections 5 and 7 cover masking, policy-versus-queue, capacity, and pilot measures without over-claiming causality.

**Defects that block a high score.**

- F02 upstream is 20 minutes (`08:25 − 08:05`), not 15. The master table and the worked example both say 15. The same paragraph asserts `15 + 60 + 15 + 20 = 115`. That sum is 110, while timestamp flow time is 115. The conservation identity used to validate the decomposition fails on one of the four required worked cases. The code would print 20, so Section 2 and Section 6 disagree.
- Mean total flow is 115.25 (`1383 / 12`), not 115.8.
- B queue SD is about 9.1 (population) or 9.5 (sample), not about 8.7. B service SD near 5.6 is acceptable as “about 5.5.”
- CF2 on F05 is not a tie. Half of the 25-minute B queue moves `B_end` from 10:10 to 09:57:30; `A_end` stays 10:00, so the join saving is 10 and A is solely critical. The later phrase “joint or sole” covers F05 only if that earlier “tie” is ignored.
- “Benefit where A is critical or within 10 minutes of critical” overstates CF1. F04, F08, and F10 have A within 10 minutes of `join_ready` and still save nothing, because another branch remains later. The zero-benefit list corrects this; the lead sentence does not.
- Average and tail contribution to `join_ready` is only partly measured. Mean completion and the two 75-minute cases are reported. The unique excess over the runner-up is not: A 15, B 120, C 12 minutes in total (per-case means about 1.3, 10.0, and 1.0). That is the direct “minutes this branch adds to the join” figure, and it is absent.
- F10’s downstream 20 is 5 minutes of F2 busy-wait plus 15 of residual idle. The text records the idle 15 and omits the busy 5. F12’s “idle 20” is the F10-end to F12-start gap; only 10 minutes of that is after `join_ready`.
- The script’s surviving CF1 expression is positive and correct, but only after a discarded assignment that multiplies the saving by −1 while commenting “positive = saved,” then an `if False` ternary. Tie handling returns a sorted joint label; the comment claims an alphabetical primary pick that is never stored. `get_service_time` and `discover_performance_dfg` are called, and the fork-join comment is appropriate, but the block is not a clean reproducible statement of the table in Section 2.

The influence conclusion survives these errors: B still dominates, speeding A rarely moves the join, and downstream waiting can absorb branch gains. The arithmetic and the code hygiene do not meet the bar the decomposition itself sets.
