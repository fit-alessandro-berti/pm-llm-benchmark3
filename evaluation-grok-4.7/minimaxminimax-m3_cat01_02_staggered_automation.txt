The answer’s causal frame is strong: availability is separated from ramp-up intensity, rollout is treated as selected on poor baseline cycle time, and the March fraud desk, May case-mix shift, routing, and FTE changes are kept in view. Median and p90 are not collapsed, SUTVA is flagged, staggered timing is preferred to naïve two-way fixed effects, and “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is correctly rejected. The embedded-evaluation recommendation (Delta first, within-branch holdout, queue and fraud-desk data, explicit stop rules) matches the identification problem.

Quantitative discipline is not at that standard. Several published contrasts are wrong or mislabeled. Delta’s April–August p90 mean is about 89.3, not 92.9, so the p90 difference-in-differences versus Delta is overstated. Gamma’s pre-period complexity, CSAT, and FTE do not match the stated January–June window; Delta and Epsilon rework (and some complexity and arrival figures) mix April–August with May–August. The escalation footnote says January–February, but the cells are full pre-window means that already include the March fraud-desk step. Tail-amplification evidence mixes late-post medians with full-post p90s. The effect table’s “Gamma ≈ −8% vs Delta” is Gamma’s own change, not the roughly −22 ratio-point contrast computed above it. Difference-in-differences in the comparison table are unsigned.

The final claim ladder then flips a sign the body had right: Beta’s July–August median is about +0.9 h versus January–March, not −0.9 h, and Gamma’s p90 is flat, not 5–10% higher. Intensity results of “1–2 hours” and a p90 coefficient that “stays positive or zero” are asserted without a computed fit and sit uneasily with the answer’s own control comparison, in which never-treated p90 worsened more. Relative time is off by one (g = 1…5 versus month minus adoption month, which is 0…4). The rollout section also runs past the 250–400 word limit, and the within-branch holdout is not reconciled with the branch-level event study it is supposed to extend.
