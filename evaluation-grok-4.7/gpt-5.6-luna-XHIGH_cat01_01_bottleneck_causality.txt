7.5

The answer is strong on the central pattern and mostly disciplined, but it is not nearly flawless. Several causal slips are material under a strict reading.

The crude-versus-stratified reversal is correctly detected and not treated as identification. Checked figures match the data: crude gap 6.72 hours; within-complexity Orion−Nova gaps about −2.14, −2.21, and −3.43 hours; waits 12.80 versus 8.79 and the within-stratum wait gaps; service 7.04 versus 5.04 and the within-stratum service means; compliance-conditional means 26.69 versus 27.00 and 13.44 versus 11.33; rework 4/14 versus 3/16; one appeal in each group. Queue time is separated from service time, completed-case selection is treated as a collider, and resource group is correctly described as a bundle of treatment, routing policy, and staffing. Required sections, hypothesis types, pilot-plus-capacity design, and the five residual uncertainties are all present. Word limits are met. The standardization target is explicit, and overlap warnings are implemented.

The verdict “partly causal through mechanisms” is sign-ambiguous. The body says the crude Orion penalty is confounded and that Orion is faster inside every complexity stratum. That narrative is closer to “mainly confounded” or “not identifiable,” with only a fragile association in Orion’s favor. Confidence 58 does not remove the mismatch. The expertise story is also overweighted: within-stratum service gaps are only about 0.1–0.4 hours, while the cycle-time reversal is mostly wait.

Compliance is mishandled. The DAG correctly has `complexity -> compliance_required` and no `resource_group -> compliance_*` path, so requirement is a pre-assignment branch and confounder. Hypothesis 4 still calls compliance a mediator of the group effect. Its own contradicting evidence—nearly identical means among required cases—supports composition, not mediation. Rework can be a mediator; compliance requirement cannot, on the stated process.

The final decision rule repeats a related mistake. If the group contrast disappears after conditioning on queue state, that is consistent with queue mediation, not with “there is no group effect, so change capacity instead.” Queue adjustment and reviewer adjustment are different estimands and are collapsed into one rollback rule. Hypothesis 3 has the same softness: shrinkage after queue adjustment would also occur if group worked through queues.

The graph note says group affects reviewer skill, but the adjacency list has `unobserved_reviewer_skill -> resource_group`. `cycle_time -> case_completed` is ill-defined because cycle time is observed only for completed cases. `complexity -> queue_wait` is missing even though wait rises with complexity beyond the compliance path. The static-DAG feedback remark itself is good.

A sharp positivity fact is underused. All 17 night cases are high load, so night and load are not separable, and Orion has no night-normal or day-high support. Hypothesis 3 is exactly about that separation. The code’s overlap table is only complexity × shift × group, so this empty cell is never flagged.

Section 7 meets the diagnostic checklist, but it does not parse the `trace` strings with the specified call. It builds XES and hopes `parse_event_log_string` accepts that document. Negative controls and sensitivity analysis are named without a result that would favor or weaken the hypothesis. Ranking a harmful Orion effect first is unexplained; the stratified evidence cuts against it. Treatment-on-the-treated is proposed beside safety overrides without noting that selective overrides reintroduce confounding.

Arithmetic, process semantics, and the refusal to bless a 50% reassignment are the answer’s strengths. The mediator/confounder confusion and the post-treatment adjustment rule are the reasons it stays well below a near-flawless score.
