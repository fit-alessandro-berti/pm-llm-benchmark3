7.4

The required structure is complete: both fenced blocks, the case table, all four derivation labels, the capacity and ranking tables, the pilot rows, a synthesis within the word bounds, and exactly eight labeled assumptions. The main causal claims are also right. Earlier readiness is repeatedly separated from earlier completion, K-337 is treated as a decision-policy change, four-eyes and final-compliance placement survive, and the 100-case discussion covers C1 reservations, D1/D2 substitution, rework tails, assessment congestion, weekends, and false concurrency.

The partial-order block does not state an explicit relation. As written, “Register  Triage” and the later chains have no operator, so the fenced block is not by itself a relation set; direction has to be inferred from the prose constraints. The model also places optional escalation only after Triage, while the factual K-104 escalation occurred after Assessment. Post-assessment escalation therefore is not in the feasible set being declared. The concurrency and originals constraints in the second block are substantively sound.

K-104 contains the sharpest inconsistency. The table path finishes identity and document checking before the preliminary C1 screen. The intervention and the K-104 derivation allow that screen to run during those checks. Those are different schedules, different C1 occupancy, and different opportunities to shorten the final review. The base saving of 8–18 hours also assumes a protected final slot begins essentially when A1 finishes at Tue 19:10. A reservation made at Mon 08:50 cannot know that completion time; a fixed reserved clock time will usually fall before readiness or after the factual Wed 13:10 start. That alignment problem is acknowledged only indirectly through preemption. The table’s downside, “roughly 8 hours slower,” is unsupported. The same answer says an early screen can trigger Request More Info, and the supplied external-response range is 8–36 hours plus another document check, assessment, and compliance review. The downside is potentially measured in days, so the published range is over-narrow.

K-219 is directionally careful about A2 and M1 absorbing an earlier document completion. It underweights a pure service-time reversal. D2’s standard range is 4–8 hours and difficult handwriting can behave like complex work at 8–14 hours. If D2 cannot start Monday evening and takes the long end, the document check can finish after the factual D1 completion at Tue 13:30 without any clarification loop. The published worst case is dominated by rework, so that no-rework reversal is missing. The Monday 16:00 D2 forecast of five hours is also applied too directly to an arrival at 17:30.

K-337’s policy reasoning is strong: the discrepancy remains explicit, C1 follows Assessment 2, A1 does not review compliance, and Friday readiness does not imply a Friday decision. The best-case magnitude is wrong. Assessment 2 ends Fri 2026-04-10 10:30 and factual notification is Wed 2026-04-15 09:10. A genuine Friday decision, after a multi-hour compliance review, saves approximately 4.5 days, not “three to four calendar days.” The base band of 1–3 days is more defensible if completion slips to Monday or Tuesday.

K-411 is the best-controlled counterfactual. A noon D2 start is a plausible FIFO consequence of the Tue 08:00 four-hour forecast, the resulting 16:30 join is not converted one-for-one into cycle time, and audit failure is allowed to erase the benefit. Classifying this with K-104 as primarily a timing change still understates it: the intervention removes a case-lock constraint and changes legal task concurrency.

The capacity table and rollout discussion are coherent, including the point that preliminary reading helps only when it reduces final C1 work by more than pre-read time plus reservation slack. The ranking is defensible because it privileges feasibility, reversibility, and learning over raw speed. Calling K-411 “high” robustness remains conditional on an unproven lock workaround; data corruption is reversible as a policy but not automatically reversible case by case. The pilots match the top two ranks and include spillover and integrity stops, though “worsens materially” is not an operational threshold and 25-per-arm block randomization across day, shift, and queue band will be sparse.

The synthesis correctly identifies K-411 as the robust candidate, K-337 as the dangerous fast option, and K-104 as the least transportable scale-up. The assumption audit contains the assumptions that actually carry those conclusions. The score is held down by the K-104 schedule contradiction, the unsupported eight-hour downside, the missed no-rework D2 reversal, and the K-337 calendar-day error.
