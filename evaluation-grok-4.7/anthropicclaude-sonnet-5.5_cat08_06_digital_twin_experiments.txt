7.4

The design is complete, modular, and unusually careful about latent objections, calendar constraints, emergent queues, and non-stationary cross-case effects. The score stays well below excellent because several concrete technical inconsistencies would mislead implementation or decision-making.

The strongest parts are the boundary between PM4Py and the simulator, the refusal to sample historical waiting times as inputs, the treatment of objections and prior violations as latent, common-random-number stream alignment, and the conditional status of D1, D2, D5, D8, and D9. The eight required sections are all present, and the skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points (`format_dataframe`, `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `get_case_arrival_average`, `parse_process_tree`, `convert_to_petri_net`, `fitness_token_based_replay`) rather than `pm4py.read_csv`.

The main defects:

- Warm-up is internally inconsistent. The text correctly says a seasonal process has no single steady state, then prescribes Welch’s procedure, which looks for a flattened mean. Seeding a dated WIP snapshot and then discarding 12 months also drops the seasonal phase of that snapshot, with no rule for realigning it.
- The runner contradicts that design. `horizon_h=24*30*24` and `warmup_h=12*30*24` leave about 12 months of measurement, no drain, and a 360-day year. Weekdays, month peaks, holidays, and council weeks then drift apart. Section 5 asks for 12 months warm-up, 24 months of measurement, and 12 months of drain on a real calendar.
- The inferred service split is biased. `service_h_inferred = min(elapsed, median prior)` makes service unable to exceed its own median, so it contradicts the stated p90 service times and the simulation-based calibration plan in Section 2. Capping at the median dumps excess elapsed time into waiting.
- The performance DFG is computed and described as edge durations without a warning that those values are elapsed completion-to-completion times, including queueing and closed hours. That is the easiest way for this skeleton to be misused as a service-time source.
- The process-tree critique overstates a gap. The sequence already finishes the parallel review block before public notice, inspection, and decision drafting. The real mismatch is over-serialization versus a join that could allow overlap, plus missing planning, legal, and appeal rework. Replacing those redo labels with `tau` also makes token replay permissive; the fitness check is weaker than the text implies.
- Decision thresholds fight each other. SLAs set near variant-group p90s, then a 90% compliance target, mostly restate current performance, and those rows are variant groups rather than case-type distributions. A 70–85% utilization band also penalizes D4 for the utilization drop that added inspector capacity is supposed to produce. “2 factorial (16 cells)” means a 2⁴ design; the written notation is wrong. The prose promises a t interval, while `compare` uses 1.96.

PM4Py call shapes, the Saturday/Sunday timestamp conflict with the HR calendars, architect bursts, district travel, and the separation of applicant delay from municipal service are handled soundly. The result is a strong design memo with implementation-level contradictions, not a nearly flawless specification.
