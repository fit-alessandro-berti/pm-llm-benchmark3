6.5

The required sections are all present, the hard qualification rules are mostly applied correctly, and the worker-facing and audit sections are careful. The recommended queue and the implementation sketch nevertheless contradict the policy’s own capacity, continuity, and training rules, so the answer is not nearly flawless.

The strongest material is the constraint split, the baseline comparison, the worker explanation, and the audit design. Safety rules are separated from preferences. Security skill 0 is kept ineligible, including the C015 Eva/Jonas suggestion. Final Approval is kept at skill 2–3. Urgent and high-priority work stays at skill 2 or above. Quality batching is limited to C006 and C012, both normal/low with more than 24 hours remaining. Bruno and Iris, both high overtime risk, receive no added work. The normal/low count is right: C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, and C015 are nine cases, and 2/9 is 22.2%. The comparison correctly treats first-available and specialist-only routing as trade-offs, including the compliance failure of an unwrapped first-available rule. The audit list covers every requested indicator and appropriately treats rework and appeals as associations.

The assignment table breaks the decision procedure in Section 2. That procedure reserves urgent work first and protects scarce Approval and Negotiation capacity. R6_Farid already retains C002, an urgent approval with 2 hours remaining, and C011, an urgent VIP negotiation with 4 hours remaining, inside a 6.5-hour day. The table then adds C010 and the C003 trainee task, putting Farid on the four-assignment cap. C010 is discretionary: it is normal priority, low handover cost, 20 hours from its deadline, and already owned by R2_Bruno at Approval skill 2. Moving it concentrates another approval on the same person who must clear two urgent cases. C003 is also a poor use of that slot.

C003 is internally inconsistent. The case is a repeated diagnosis loop with reassignment count 2, medium handover cost, and a high-overtime owner who has 14 open cases and 3 hours left. The table says R4_David remains owner so a third handover is avoided, while R6_Farid performs the diagnosis. A performer change is a handover in the same handover-of-work sense Section 6 later audits. It also adds supervision to a high-overtime resource on a non-urgent case when R3_Celine is a Diagnosis skill-3, low-overtime alternative. The training quota is met by counting this fragile arrangement. The later stop-rule does not repair the initial recommendation.

C013 is allocated to R10_Jonas because Jonas had the lowest starting open-case count. By the policy’s own urgency order, Jonas would already be holding the C006/C012 batch and C014, so the starting count is stale. Jonas then sits on the four-assignment cap with a quality batch, an eligibility case, and a medium-cost rework handover. C001 correctly moves an urgent, complexity-0.88 Security case from skill 1 to R8_Hugo, but the stated historical handover delay of 2.7 hours is left unresolved against a 3-hour SLA. “Structured handover” names the risk without a timing rule.

Two broader design gaps remain. The answer never states whether hours remaining are residual after the current open cases; with 4–14 open cases already on every resource, that assumption decides feasibility. Four documented training priorities sit at skill 0: David and Hugo for Approval, Eva and Jonas for Security. The 20% rule and Security/Approval resilience are therefore structurally narrow, beyond the local C015 remark.

Section 7 does not implement the policy it accompanies. `legal_option` attaches a supervisor only for Security skill 1–2 or for urgent/high work below skill 2, while the training score requires a supervisor. C003 and C009, the two assignments used to meet the training target, cannot be represented as supervised training. The hard continuity override from Section 2 is only a score penalty. Skill-1 Approval preparation is rejected outright. The skill dictionary is a small subset, so every omitted legal pair is treated as skill 0. Lateness, handover hours, skill points, and workload are added on incompatible scales, which makes the published weights uninterpretable. `live_state` is undefined, `short_cases` is unused, and “reassigments” is misspelled in Section 6. The PM4Py calls match the requested function names and are appropriately caveated, but the sketch cannot audit or reproduce the recommended decisions.
