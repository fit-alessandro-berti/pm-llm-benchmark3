7.7

The final write-up is a strong process-mining analysis: the eight required sections are present, regimes separate demand, mix, capacity, and backlog, the outage is not treated as a normal high-throughput state, median service is not used as mean flow time, and uncertainty is usually labeled. It is not nearly flawless.

The most serious problems are internal contradictions and wrong headline numbers, not missing structure.

- Section 4.5 and the Little’s Law matrix say pooled flow time is unchanged, 3.95 h before 04-29 versus 3.96 h after. The “before” WIP mean is wrong: weekend WIP on 04-25 and 04-26 is omitted while those days stay in the 23-day count and in completions. The correct before figure is about 29.7 cases and about 4.21 h, not 27.87 and 3.95 h. The Simpson contrast with weekday windows still exists, but the repeated “no change” claim is false.
- Section 5 says the 95% intervals for 04-07, 04-15, and 04-25 include 1, so Poisson cannot be rejected. The same table gives 1.52–8.74 and 2.07–11.92. Only 04-25 includes 1. That is a direct contradiction of the section’s main dispersion conclusion.
- Fano factors are not internally defined. Quoted variances are sample variances, while the reported indices use population variance. For 04-07 the sum of squares is also wrong (about 299, not 281), so I is about 3.0–3.2 rather than 2.78. The code uses sample variance throughout, so it will not reproduce the prose.
- Smaller arithmetic misses: pooled median arrivals are 103.5, not 105.5; week-1 staff-hours are 414, not 420; R0 mean p90 is 47, not 46.9; the 04-15 “08–10” share is 18%, not the stated 19% from “20+22”.

Judgment elsewhere is good: the 04-20 mix-versus-coding ambiguity, the WIP-versus-balance gap, the unlogged 04-29 start, carry-over censoring, and the refusal to fit a full queueing law are all defensible. Those strengths do not offset a false pooled Little’s Law result and a hypothesis-test claim that the author’s own intervals refute.
