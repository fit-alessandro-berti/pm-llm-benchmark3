4.2

The primary estimand is the right family: 90-day default only among matured activations, with rejects, withdrawals, and open cases left unlabeled, and with reject-inference kept off the primary path. That choice is undermined by an incoherent prediction time, a cohort that does not partition 48,000, invalid maturity dates, and a validation window that cannot observe the outcome it claims to test.

The prediction point is “the first Credit Review exit,” including the accept, reject, or document-request decision, while the population is cases that later activate. At that instant activation is still future, so the fitted probability is conditional on a post-decision event. By activation, document loops, contract signing, and limit-setting are already known, but the prefix rule cuts them off. The model is therefore late for underwriting and early for servicing, and it is not the early-warning estimand stated in the business question. “Still-open” is treated as an absorbing state in the multistate alternative; it is administrative censoring. “Paid” is substituted for “no default within 90 days.” The population is extended to starts on 15 Feb 2026 even though the event window ends 31 Dec 2025.

The cohort table invents figures and does not add up. Subtracting 2,700 starts after 15 Nov 2025 is not implied by the extract, and 15 Nov 2025 is about 93 days before extraction, not 90+35. Labeled maturity is set at activation on 23 Oct 2025, but 23 Oct 2025 plus 90 days plus a 35-day feed falls on about 25 Feb 2026, after the 15 Feb 2026 extract. The conservative maturity bound is about 13 Oct 2025. Still-open is rewritten from 5,100 to 1,600, rejected and withdrawn counts are left at their unfiltered totals, and the displayed pieces land near 47,200 rather than 48,000. The garbled non-activated row makes the flow unusable. Permanently missing feeds are given a fabricated 2% rate.

Censoring types are named, then collapsed. Immature binary labels are called ordinary right censoring and also dropped, which is complete-case restriction to an older, mostly pre-migration book, not a censored-data likelihood. Worst-case relabeling is claimed to lower-bound AUC; it can bound prevalence, not AUC. IPCW is used as if it addressed the MNAR partner-feed problem it cannot identify. Testable versus untestable assumptions are only sketched. Linkage for 60% of transfers is treated as if it supplied default outcomes.

Prefix rules contradict the features. Stopping at the first invented activity in {Credit Review – Accept/Reject/Request Docs} leaves KYC–review loops and repeated document cycles outside the prefix, yet those loop counts are later used as features. Empty-token padding invents a fake activity. A two-day prefix is stated without a minimum-information rule for cases that have not reached the decision point. The code instead clips at the first activity containing “Credit Review,” and `.index[0]` after `sort_values` follows index order, not time order.

The historical feature is leaked. The rolling default rate of the last 100 activations before prefix time uses outcomes that are not knowable until 90 days plus feed delay after activation. The experience repair also disagrees with the “pre-collected snapshot” wording, and a pre/post policy indicator is unidentified because training is restricted to the pre-policy side.

The leakage table’s drop decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-prefix events, and `label_available_at` are directionally right. The rationale still conflicts with a prefix that includes the decision activity. “First risk score” is not the as-of value at prediction time. `documents_total` is declared safe at 00:00 of the prediction day without a snapshot history that would make that slice computable.

The temporal split is the right idea and is implemented so that it tests nothing. An eight-day test of starts from 15 Oct 2025 through 23 Oct 2025 is after the maturity boundary once activation lag and a 7–35 day feed are included, so post-migration 90-day labels are largely unobserved. Splitting on start date does not keep June starts under the pre-1 Jul 2025 policy, because decision and activation can fall after the change. There is no embargo for delayed labels. AUROC, Brier, ECE, and subgroup AUROC ignore the prevalence and the alert-rate utility of a 2,700/21,600 outcome, and IPCW metrics do not match the complete-case primary analysis. In the code, the test mask runs from 15 Oct 2025 to about 13 Oct 2025 and is empty; the masks are never applied.

Limitations correctly refuse a counterfactual default probability for rejected applicants and name two real deployment feedback loops: collection intervention changes the observed default, and visible scores change officer thresholds. A future migration is a data shift, not a consequence of deployment. The third feedback path is therefore missing, as are limit changes, applicant gaming, and recalibration when the activation policy moves.

The skeleton covers the requested shape and would not run. `format_dataframe`’s returned frame is discarded. The activation-time aggregation calls `get_group` on a timestamp series whose `.name` is not the case id. Terminal status is collapsed to “never activated,” so the required cohort accounting is absent. Duplicate-activity names from the migration are placeholders. Prefixing keeps `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, and `collection_status`, and the audit then raises instead of removing them. `default_90d` is not in that check. DFG and variant results are discarded. The survival comment points at a binary complete-case target rather than a defined censoring or competing-risk likelihood.
