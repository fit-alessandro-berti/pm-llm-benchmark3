The write-up is complete and often points the right way, but it is not reliable as an audit judgment. Factual overclaims, a split the materials do not support, and a revised POWL that does not accept the behavior it is meant to legitimize keep it well below a strong score.

The briefs do not do the work the assignment requires. Prosecution never concedes the real model and data defects inside its own case, misstates SUP-704 as if FD-7 had expired or been over-applied on 11 July 2026, and treats SUP-703’s €180,000 purchase as a breach of a spend cap. That cap belongs to FD-7, not PD-3. Defense invents a later permanent amendment (“subsequently formalized”), invokes force majeure that the file does not contain, and explains SUP-708 as a posting artifact. Forty minutes before sanctions clearance, with no pilot or emergency, is a control failure even though no transaction occurred. Neither brief uses the aggregate counts that are the actual systemic evidence: 312 auto-approvals (289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 incomplete), 47 information requests, notification missingness moving from 2% to 29%, and six post-expiry auto-approvals.

Case calls are uneven. SUP-701, 703, 705, 706, 707, 708, and 714 are classified defensibly. SUP-702 is accepted as a justified exception without applying the answer’s own burden to the unresolved point that PD-3 never entered CS-9 and that the compliance observer denied weakening it. SUP-712 is the clearest split-the-difference error: the trace fits M0, but the same human approved through two aliases, which CS-9 forbids. That is noncompliance M0 cannot see, not a mixed technicality. SUP-715 is genuinely mixed only on a narrower ground: the forbidden pair is requester and approver, whereas the recorded defect is an undisclosed spousal conflict between reviewer and approver. SUP-711 is more determinate than “indeterminate”; identical source transaction IDs support a duplicate log row. SUP-713’s rename is cited to N3/N5, which do not say that, and the mapping was approved a week after the case. System confidence is stated as high across authority, scope, and logging disputes that the answer itself lists as unresolved. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation is never examined.

The revision misses required scope rules for rejected leads, reactivations, and withdrawals. The code is not a sound PM4Py model of the stated minimum. `Request_More_Info` is a one-shot XOR after both checks, so it cannot repeat and cannot produce SUP-707 (requests between sanctions and quality). `Operator.LOOP` is named and not used. The same `sanctions`, `quality`, and `manual_approval` objects are placed in more than one parent, so the result is not a tree. Rejection sits beside the branches and contains no sanctions check, so sanctions-then-reject is outside the language and unchecked rejection is inside it. Auto-approval and emergency activation are permanent XOR children, so ineligible, post-expiry, and post-directive traces fit the new “normative” flow. The overlay table covers identifiers, snapshots, aliases, and timestamps, but it does not encode amount, country, group, window, or authority predicates that would actually reject SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714.
