8.4

The analysis is numerically careful, regime-aware, and appropriately cautious about Little’s Law, recovery throughput, and pooling. Checked totals and regime means (2,519 arrivals, 2,409 completions, net +110, weekday/weekend splits, arrival-weighted mix shares, Fano/CV values, the Apr 6–10 balance of 491/491, and the open-hour ratios 2.40 and 3.95) are correct, and the peak-field conflict with the hourly vectors is a real data-quality finding.

The score is held down by concrete defects. In §4, “Apr 20–28 … arrivals 114.0, completions 100.6, FTE 5.93, net +13.4” reuses the weekday-only cell while that calendar window has nine days, two of them weekend, and is elsewhere given as 99.1 arrivals/day. Calling Apr 30–May 1 a matched comparison with that cell is also too strong: the later window is only Thursday–Friday. The PM4Py remarks name the required functions and correctly refuse to treat the daily table as an event log, but they do not show how an event log would be structured or what those calls would estimate. Two-day regimes are still summarized by means where ranges were the safer estimand. Outage-day service quantiles are reported without the selection problem created by 58 completions against 105 arrivals, and the weekday/weekend arrivals-per-FTE comparison (17.6 vs 17.1) is left unqualified by the longer weekend service times. The Apr 16–17 FTE rise to 7.0/6.5 and the return to about 6.0 during the main accumulation are visible in the tables but are not carried through as a capacity-timing result distinct from the Apr 29 intervention.

Conceptually the piece meets the task: demand, mix, capacity, and backlog are separated; recovery is not treated as steady capacity; and the pooled Little’s-Law ratio is refused. Those strengths are why the defects lower the grade rather than collapse it.
