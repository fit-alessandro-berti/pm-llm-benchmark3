The diagnoses themselves are strong and mostly consistent: G1 as a capacity-4 fixture batch, G2 as a group start with later serial completions, G3 as folder submission, G4 as a single-server drain, G5 as a shared login, G6 as end-of-shift entry, G7 as quarter-hour rounding, and G8 as a blocked queue followed by a serial drain. The final text still has concrete errors. G4 waiting is given as 7–17 minutes; the starts and arrivals produce 7, 10, 13, 16, and 19 minutes. G3 is described as 4-deep from 11:17–11:24 and then tied to the 2.4× ratio; that interval is only 3-deep (E01–E03), 4-deep is 11:24–11:30, and 67/28 ≈ 2.4 is average concurrency, not a consequence of that window. “Up to ~5×” service bias for G7 is a serial-fill scenario, not an upper bound. G4’s “equality to the second” is also stronger than the minute-displayed lifecycle stamps, while G1 correctly treats those `:00` seconds as counterevidence. The “12:02→12:05 lead gap” is 3 minutes; the 7-minute gap is 11:58→12:05.

The code constructs the log, keeps suspect rows, grids `merge_distance` and `min_batch_size`, and the pandas overlap, equality, lag, granularity, and backlog logic is largely right. It is fragile: it hard-codes `case:batch_type`, computes diagnostics only after discovery, and can exit before the comparison table if PM4Py returns a list or `@@batch_type`.
