The review is unusually strong: the language-versus-alignment distinction is held consistently, the probe matrix is correct, and the third model is a real control-flow improvement rather than a restatement of S or C. It is not nearly flawless.

The S/C readings match ordinary process-tree semantics. S is the interleaving of four independent skips, then exactly one approval. C is exactly those eight sequences, with no loop. B1–B8 are therefore decided correctly: S admits B3 and B4; C rejects B2 and B5; neither admits B6 or B8. The metric table refuses a bogus aggregate, and the pathology ranking correctly puts optionalized mandatory work and positive-log precision ahead of runtime and the informal 1–5 score. The absolute holdout drop of 0.229 is arithmetic, not a fabricated new metric. The dissent is a genuine alternative (controlled catalogue, noisy capture, unapproved timing), and at about 210 words it stays inside the required range.

The third tree is also the right shape. `*(tau, redo)` is zero or more correction iterations, not one or more; the initial `+(Credit, Technical)` really forces both checks; expedite cannot precede them; approval is exclusive. A1, A2, and A4 actually separate this language from both submissions. The post-hoc checker correctly refuses to treat a missing `source_type` or malformed `affected_checks` as a pass, and it does not walk a string as if it were a list.

The main defect is in that checker’s supplier rule. For a known external case it appends a hard failure whenever the visible supplier count is not 1. The same section says a missing supplier event cannot be judged skipped rather than unlogged without independent evidence, and section 9 says a deleted event must not be treated as non-execution. The surrounding prose mentions a completeness precondition, but the function that is supposed to implement the semantics has no such input and will return `fail` anyway. That collapses the exact missing-event ambiguity the review is otherwise careful about. A second status error is coarser: every structural miss is `outside_profile`, so a settled violation such as B3 is labeled like A5, which the text itself says must not be called a violation until timing policy is settled.

Smaller gaps keep it below 9. Credit Check and Technical Review are duplicated in the initial block and again in the redo, after duplicate labels were treated as an ownership and alignment problem for C; that residual is never discussed. The P4 cell “enforces ordering” is tighter than P2–P4: supplier may still sit before or between the checks, and a correction is not shown to be illegal after expedite. Those limits are disclosed nearby, but the audit table over-compresses them. The evaluation skeleton covers the named PM4Py metrics and warns about version-dependent soundness and costs; it does not show exact visible-move membership or the negative-suite harness, which are left as instructions.
