7.7

The forecast meets every required section and is grounded in the given handover rates, team volumes, utilizations, and rework figures. East versus North/South, tail delay, learning, fairness, and a conditional pilot are all treated seriously. The score is held down by quantitative contradictions and analysis-sketch errors.

The central Legal/Risk claim does not follow from the data or from the Kingman relation cited in the mechanism map. Volume moves from 9 to 12 per 100 cases, about +33%, on resources already at 91% and 93%. With unchanged service rate, utilization scales to about 1.21–1.24, so the queue is unstable. The scenario instead puts utilization at 97–99% and waiting time at 18–28h. Under \(W_q \propto \rho/(1-\rho)\), 9.6h at 93% becomes roughly 23h at 97% and about 70h at 99%, so 18–28h matches neither 97–99% nor a one-third demand increase. The adverse paragraph has the same slip: waits of 3–5× fit near 98%, while “cross 95%” fits only about 1.4×. The recommendation correctly treats added Legal/Risk load as dangerous, but the scenario numbers understate that mechanism.

Utilization arithmetic is also loose. A case-weighted tech baseline is about 73%. “15% higher” is either about 84% or 88 percentage points; the text uses ~85%+ and ~85–88% as if those were the same result. “Two-thirds of customers” for North plus South is actually about 75% of monthly volume (1,580/2,100). The claim that 55–60% of cases are simple and bypass scarce specialists is not recoverable from the handover table (tech 36, approval 42, legal/risk 9 per 100, with unknown overlap).

Scenario waits such as approval moving from 2.4h to 5.0–6.5h are stated like derived results. Supervisor utilization is not in the log, and a 17% volume increase on two-thirds of the previous supervisor capacity can range from moderate delay to an unstable queue.

Two guardrails are not operational: cross-location rework “must stay 10.5%,” and the A→B→A return rate “must remain 80%,” with no ceiling or floor. The indicator table also says “Operational Operationalization.”

The PM4Py sketch names every requested function, but several comparisons would mislead or fail. Handover and subcontracting discovery return graphs, so `len(...)` counts resources, not handover pairs. `list(variants.keys())[:10]` is not the ten most frequent variants. `perf_aggregation_key="all"` is not a valid performance aggregation. `discover_organizational_roles` is called with `activity_key`, which the public signature does not take. Cycle time is first-to-last timestamp with no completion filter, despite the prose warning about right-censoring. Case-level `team` filters double-count the cross-team cases the analysis is meant to isolate. The log has only `time:timestamp`, and the sketch never states how service versus waiting time is identified.
