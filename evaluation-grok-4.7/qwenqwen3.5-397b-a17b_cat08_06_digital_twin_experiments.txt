The write-up covers all eight required sections and recognizes seasonality, calendars, rework, applicant delay, and the D1–D10 set, but several design choices would mis-specify the twin or the comparison.

The largest simulation flaw is that waiting is never clearly made an output of queues, calendars, and contention. Service times are fit from the published median and p90 and then treated as independent draws. Empirical waits already embed historical congestion, so sampling them, or leaving the mechanism unspecified, makes capacity changes such as D4, D5, and D6 unable to move delay. Public notice and applicant response are not separated from staffed work as calendar timers or infinite-server delays. The `+` join is missing: decision drafting must wait only for the reviews that apply, and appeal returns are omitted. Cross-case structure is thin: a flat NHPP contradicts architect bursts, neighborhood objections that hit several cases are absent, and the “20% less travel per extra bundled case” figure is invented. Resource rules that were actually supplied (Monday clerk, fire Tue–Thu, environmental 40% holdback, inspector field days, council blackouts, holidays) are not encoded; the skeleton calendars return true.

Uncertainty treatment fights the non-stationary premise. A three-month deletion “until steady state” is inappropriate when demand has no steady state, and it does not say that WIP is retained when statistics reset. Complex cases (median about 140 days, p90 about 260) will still be censored. Common random numbers are cited instead of a heavy-tail method; case-level means, replication-level medians and p90s, and percentile intervals are conflated. Thirty runs and a global seed do not give valid paired intervals once interventions change the number of random draws. Stress runs are applied only to the baseline, while the decision rule requires the chosen policy to survive S1 and S2.

Process-mining grounding is partly wrong. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` does accept this `->` / `X` / `+` / `*` / `tau` string; the real limits are missing probabilities, data guards, and appeals, which are not stated. `pm4py.get_variant_statistics` and `pm4py.get_performance_summary(log, dfg)` are not the conventional calls, and `discover_dfg` returns a triple. Case-arrival average and service-versus-waiting columns are required and absent, and the frame is a three-row stub rather than the excerpt. Replay that compares stochastic completion timestamps with historical ones will reject a correct model. Fitting and then “validating” against the same median/p90 table is circular. SLA cuts (simple median under 30 days, complex p90 under 90 days) and a district Gini under 0.2 are unsupported, “optimal” is used for an uncalibrated design, and quality risk from skipping inspections has no decision metric.
