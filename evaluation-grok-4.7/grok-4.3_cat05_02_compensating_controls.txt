The case judgments are mostly in a defensible range, but the answer is not reliable enough to score higher. It misstates control rules, contradicts its own timing semantics, and the required code would identify the wrong four-eyes cases.

Section 1 names five real threats, yet the control mapping is incomplete and sometimes wrong. ED-1 does not allow an override that is later cured by MG-4; a warning override means ED-1 is unavailable. MG-4 does not itself override AV-2. Several rows never say whether each of AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 prevents, detects, corrects, or fails. Goods-not-received and compromised-account control are only partly covered.

Section 2 hits the requested dimensions and correctly rejects expected loss as the only standard. The full-equivalence test then requires every threat, including collusion, to be closed by one pre-settlement independent control. That standard is stricter than FEP-1, so the later OBJECTIVE_MET marks for AP-203 and AP-207 fail the test just defined. “Merely mitigating” is also tied to low loss probability, which reintroduces magnitude as a sufficiency criterion.

Section 3 uses the allowed labels and avoids a mechanical same-user failure rule. AP-202, AP-203, AP-205, and AP-208 are sound. The other rows are weaker. AP-201 calls corrective capacity low even though m04 acted before the 10:00 settlement and the audit started inside a 24-hour window measured from settlement; “missing evidence: none” overstates a review whose depth is not specified. AP-204’s rationale omits the fabricated receipt and treats an unproven pair pattern as the failure. AP-206’s late token is explicitly unresolved, but the row treats it as established. AP-209 assigns only partial objective achievement and low corrective capacity to a distinct-user payment with a clean AV-2 pass, no defect, and an audit inside the recall window. AV-2 is counted as preventive in some rows and omitted in others.

Section 4’s hybrid reading of post-release, pre-settlement MG-4 is coherent. Section 3 does not apply it: the same act is parked under detective controls and paired with low corrective capacity.

Section 5 covers five cases but not the requested structure. AP-204 says the overpayment remains undetected after the audit found the fabricated receipt. AP-202 again treats the override as MG-4. AP-206 states a retroactive token as fact. Ranges are mostly missing.

Section 6 lists the required indicators and eventually separates review from proof of misconduct. It still says to alert on audit completion before the recall window, which is the compliant condition. The parenthetical “(alert  proof)” is unreadable. A post-release but pre-settlement ratification is also treated as an alert even where the answer elsewhere accepts that timing under ED-1.

Section 7 reconstructs the CSV, parses UTC timestamps, calls `format_dataframe`, and labels the output as evidence. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called without `keep_violations=True`, so the default result is the cases that respect four-eyes, while the code prints them as violations. AP-205 is hardcoded rather than discovered. The case-facts frame is never joined. Flags omit emergency eligibility, AV coverage, manager timing, and repeated pairs, and AP-205 is marked audited within 24 hours although no audit occurred. The version caveat does not identify the `keep_violations` inversion.
