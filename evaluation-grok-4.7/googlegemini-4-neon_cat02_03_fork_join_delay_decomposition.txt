The core fork-join accounting is exact and the main conclusion is right: B controls completion through queue plus service, while A’s longer service is mostly a floor. The case table, worked examples (F02, F04, F07, F11), additive identity \(T = U + FJ + D + S_F\), critical frequencies (B 8/12, A 2, C 2), exclusive excess (120 / 15 / 12 of 147), CF1 (15 min, 2 cases), CF2 (92.5 min, 8 cases), and the +10 min CF1+CF2 synergy all check out. The score is held down by concrete calculation and consistency failures, not by the overall story.

- Both-busy downstream time is undercounted. F11’s wait includes 11:40–11:50, when F1 (F09) and F2 (F10) are both busy. Dual-busy wait is 35 min across four cases, not 25 min across F05, F09, and F10. The code only tests occupancy at `join_ready`, so it misses contention that starts during the wait. CF3a (25 min, 3 cases, 9 with zero benefit, 14.2%) is therefore wrong; the capacity-only bound is 35 min and 4 cases.
- Several reported tails do not match the linear P90 used for the baseline (124.5). Recomputed new flow-time P90s are CF3b 119.25 (reported 117.5), CF3c 114 (reported 110), CF3d 114 (reported 110), and CF1+CF2 114.5 (reported 112.25). Those cells would not match the code’s `.quantile(0.9)`.
- Under CF2, mean B synchronization wait is 6.21 min (sum 74.5), not 7.96. A (3.29) and C (22.71) are right. The wrong B figure is repeated in the recommendation.
- The PM4Py print label is false: `get_all_case_durations` uses completion timestamps, so it is earliest completion to `final_end`, not first branch start to `final_end`.

Those errors do not flip the ranking or the “do not hire F3 before measuring idle gaps” recommendation, because the correct 5-minute FCFS simulation already shows that logged F1/F2 capacity is not the downstream problem. They do break the required coherence between definitions, tables, and the reproducible script.
