The diagnosis is unusually careful and the three redesigns are genuinely different, segment-specific, and tied to the log rather than to a generic improvement list. The score stays well below the top because a few analytical slips and one over-strong comparison claim are real.

What holds up. Transition counts, medians, p90s, the 14.1% versus 3.3% reopen gap, the 31% to 9% cost shrinkage, fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces, R1’s either/or structure, the EUR 400 versus “high-value” distinction, the R2 premium-accessory exception, 7×36 hours and the 82% threshold, and the terminal-edge total 3,625 are all used accurately. The answer correctly refuses to treat the directly-follows graph as a set of case routes, to add edge times into an end-to-end cycle time, or to read the fast path as causal. Sacrifices are explicit: A leaves the evidence and repair loops in place, B does not promise the observed 3.3% complaint rate to newly routed cases, and C separates time-to-a-working-device from final closure. The experiment order is coherent, compliance breaches are stop conditions, and follow-up is tied to the 180-hour dispatch-to-complaint tail.

Main deductions.

The quote-path mechanism is aimed at the wrong wait. Manual review → Repair quote is median 15h / p90 60h. The slower Repair quote → Customer approval edge, median 21h / p90 80h, is never used. Redesign B nevertheless says a shared price source and coordination “attack the 60-hour p90,” while the resource note places part-price delay on the quote activity itself. Customer decision time on that 80-hour tail can remain after prices are fixed, so B’s cycle-time mechanism is only partly specified.

Redesign C’s mechanism says the loaner decouples the customer from diagnosis and repair, while its own flow keeps evidence completion, eligibility, and triage on the serial path before either workstream. The later caveat that evidence delays still block dispatch does not remove that overstatement.

The Pareto column marks A and B “No” under dominated. The footnote then says non-dominance has merely not been demonstrated. Those are different claims. With this evidence, “conditional” was the cell the constraint allows; “No” plus “plausible speed/cost advantage” is stronger than the log can support.

Smaller issues. A’s table applies the full 6–18 hour gate to cycle time even though the design itself avoids a blanket inspector gate for clear accessories. “Underused” inspectors are treated as available capacity for a new dispatch gate that could touch a large share of roughly 2,300 replacement transitions, with only a later caveat. Several go/no-go figures (4–6% complaints, 0–5% cost, 95% within 24 hours, +2 abandonment points, 20–30% p90) are decision choices, and they are labeled as such, but they sit close to unsupported precision.

No numerical invention, no single-objective optimization, and no claim that the reviewed path is uniformly better were found. Those strengths are why the grade remains in the low 8s rather than falling further.
