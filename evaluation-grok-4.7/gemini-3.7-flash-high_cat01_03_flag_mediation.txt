6.2

The stage table is arithmetically reliable, and the rejection of natural direct/indirect effects is the right framework for shared queues, exposure-induced timing, and a bundled flag. The answer still misses the prompt’s central identification problem often enough that it is not a strong causal analysis.

The flag is repeatedly treated as one manipulable cause. P1 is defined as hiding the flag while leaving the score and other information unchanged, but E1 and the policy table turn P1 into suppression of automated routing and a +2.2 h triage loss. Those are different interventions. E3–E5 are total contrasts of routing, capacity, and escalation policy, including contrasts conditional on post-treatment review, yet they are labeled interventional indirect effects. E2’s outcome is arrival-to-triage-done, not triage completion or service, and it is called a separable direct effect without a separable decomposition. E1 is marked well-defined at the individual level while the same row invokes a SUTVA failure.

Several empirical claims are wrong or overstated. Flag rates are 0/4, 5/8, and 5/8 for low, medium, and high complexity; “100% of high-complexity versus 0% of low-complexity” is the review rate and does not justify the sentence about flag assignment. Conditional on review, escalation is 6/10 versus 2/4, so the flag-to-escalation pathway is much weaker than the crude 60% figure. R09 and R20 are unflagged rejects, so they are not examples of harm from dropping mandatory review of flagged cases. The claimed “up to 3.12 h service / 6.5 h wait” for those two cases does not match their timestamps. A threshold near 0.60 is incompatible with R20 at 0.61 unflagged and R15 at 0.64 flagged, and a cycle-time jump would not show that the effect is “purely” queue wait. T1/T2 versus T3/T4 is a perfect lane split and flagged triage service is shorter (0.60 h versus 0.95 h), but the graph sends priority only into triage wait.

Cross-case congestion is mislabeled as treatment-confounder feedback. Elapsed clock time is treated as queue time despite overnight gaps. The P2 effects, “20–40%” load reduction, and “strong” evidence are false precision on n=20. The pilot guardrail sheds low-complexity reviews, which are not the congested flow, and it offers both shift randomization and case-level assignment without resolving within-shift interference. The performance DFG is called without the start-timestamp key, and `stats['mean']` will fail if PM4Py returns floats, stopping the script before the stage summaries.
