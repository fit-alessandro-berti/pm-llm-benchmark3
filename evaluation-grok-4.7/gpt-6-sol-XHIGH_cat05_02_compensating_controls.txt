The memo is a strong, non-mechanical controls assessment: FEP-1 is decomposed into real threat paths, compensating packages are judged by timing, independence, coverage, override, recoverability, and collusion resistance, and same-user versus split-user cases are not mapped automatically to failure or success. AP-202 and AP-208 are failed for defeated or missing pre-settlement controls despite later paperwork; AP-204 is failed on a fabricated receipt despite a formal split; AP-205 is correctly kept outside completed-payment conformance; AP-209 is left indeterminate because the u12/u13 pattern is compatible with both a payment cell and a captured pair. The post-release, pre-settlement manager action is correctly treated as hybrid and only preventive if a hold and a real veto exist. The code stays in memory, calls the required PM4Py filter, separates AP-205, and labels flags as evidence.

The main logical flaw is internal. Section 2 says full equivalence requires every ED-1 condition to be proven and an enforceable veto. Section 4 says a post-release manager act prevents loss only when a settlement hold and veto are shown. Section 3 nevertheless labels AP-203 and AP-207 OBJECTIVE_MET while L3 is unproven for both and AP-207’s hold/veto is explicitly missing. The row text hedges, but the required classification does not.

Smaller defects: AP-206’s manager approval at 13:04 is one minute before AV-2 and is never discussed. “New vendor” is coded as zero days after the code itself says unknown facts should stay null. With no settlement timestamp, the 24-hour audit flag stays unknown for AP-202 and AP-204 even though those audits are plainly outside any recall window measured from release. AP-204’s phrase that the record does not show the payment was fraudulent softens a stated fabricated receipt. m21’s independence is simply unstated and should have been listed as missing. The business-day cutoff is disclosed, but it is generous and fragile on timezone-aware timestamps.

Those gaps keep the answer short of nearly flawless. The case judgments themselves remain within a defensible range.
