7.3

The final answer is complete and structurally strong: it rejects a blind copy, treats Site A’s nine-hour drop as confounded, keeps the originals gate, segments by channel, and designs a dose-aware pilot. It is not nearly flawless. The quantitative core contradicts itself, and several precise claims are stronger than the evidence.

The clearest error is in the scenario table. The derivation says a 1.5–2.5 hour gain on 38% of cases is only a 0.6–1.0 hour aggregate effect, but the digital-only row states a 1–2 hour aggregate median change. Those cannot both be right. Medians are also not case-share-weighted averages. Because the overall median and p90 are likely to sit in the slower originals/legal mass, treating only digital cases can move stratum medians while leaving the overall median and p90 almost unchanged. The claimed −2 to −6 hour p90 shift in that scenario is therefore poorly justified. The 3–5 hour “structural share” is also fitted: an assumed 8–14 hour complexity premium, a 1–2 hour huddle effect, and a 50–75% realization rate are chosen so the pieces bracket 9 hours.

Directly-follows counts are treated as case subsets. `Identity → Document` = 1,160 is not shown to be 90% of digital cases, and `Identity → Originals Receipt` = 1,020 is not shown to be 48% of the 2,108 originals-required cases. Those fractions are repeated as facts in the modifier table, the scenarios, and the recommendation. Tier 1 at 23% and Tier 2 at 15% likewise assume digital status and legal review are independent, which is never stated, and that split drives the pilot’s support calculation.

In the transport graph, `case_complexity → true_overlap` and `timestamp_reliability → true_overlap` misstate an effect modifier and a measurement problem as causes of true overlap. The comments notice this, but the adjacency list itself is wrong. Scenario “confidence” also changes meaning: sometimes it is confidence in a range given the assumptions, and for the IT-lock row it is confidence that the failure occurs.

The process comparison, co-intervention accounting, statutory segmentation, bottleneck-shift scenario, POWL dependencies, and mechanism-versus-implementation pilot are all at a high level. Those merits keep this well above a merely adequate answer. The inconsistent headline numbers and over-precise DFG inferences keep it well below a near-flawless one.
