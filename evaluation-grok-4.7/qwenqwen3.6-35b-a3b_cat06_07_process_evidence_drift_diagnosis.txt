The audit correctly refuses a single drift label and roughly separates April (pipeline), June (routing mix), late summer (parts and Partner Z), and October (immature labels). That skeleton is the main reason the score is not lower. It is not nearly flawless: several claims blur the exact distinction the task is built on, the concept-drift conclusion is not well identified, and the required process-evidence code is incomplete and would not run.

**Label maturity is mishandled in the conclusions that matter.** Partner Z’s September rate of 0.45 and the other-source rate of 0.27 are observed rates, and Partner Z matures more slowly. The table and the September narrative both call 0.45 a matured breach rate. The table also treats the overall September rate of 0.31 as matured, while the timeline correctly says 82% maturity pulls that rate down. August’s overall 0.28 is likewise called matured at 93% maturity. A variant-mix check of the matured variant rates is about 0.31 in August and about 0.34 in September, so the published overall rates are still censored; that cross-check is absent. October is handled better, but Test 1 then expects a mature October breach of about 0.25–0.30 and performance like June. Part-wait times are still rising in October (155 h) and Partner Z is 27% of volume, so label completion should not restore the June baseline.

**Concept drift is dismissed for the wrong reason.** July–August V3 matured breach moves from 0.35 to 0.46 to 0.64 before Partner Z exists, while V1 and V2 stay flat and the model is scored at diagnosis, before part receipt. That is a change in P(breach | diagnosis-time features), overlapping process drift. Conditioning on realized part delay uses information the model does not have, and “add part-delay features” would leak if those times are within-case outcomes. The stable diagnosis-to-remote-fix edge (3.8–4.6 h) is never used, so June’s mix shift is not pinned down against a remote-performance change. June is also misfiled as population shift. The incomplete April backfill is placed on May records; the finding is that April derived features were not fully recomputed. “Perfect” correlation overstates September, when delay rose and matured V3 breach edged down. July’s overall mean prediction of 0.18 versus an observed rate of 0.19 is aggregate calibration, not proof of severe V3 under-prediction.

**Remediation is only partly proportionate.** Quarantining raw April and blocking October retraining is right. Excluding August–September until a future within-case delay feature exists would also throw away the mature evidence of the new V3 risk, which conflicts with the later warning not to ignore the supplier shift. Recalibration is not clearly separated from discrimination loss (August–September AUROC 0.68 and 0.66). A variant-specific model is barely decided. The April 20 duplicate cutoff and the lack of an outcome-definition change are unused.

**The code misses a required control and misuses the library.** It never builds both an event-calendar cohort and a case-start cohort, or says how those interpretations differ. `get_variants` is not a series with `value_counts()`; `discover_dfg` and `discover_performance_dfg` are unpacked incorrectly; performance values are statistic maps, not seconds; there is no `variant` column to filter; duplicate dropping is global on `(case, activity)` rather than migration duplicates; and the temporal-feature frame is discarded. The monitor list and the maturity gate on retraining are sound.

The four requested failure modes are present and the mitigations point the right way, but the part-delay fix repeats the leakage problem and the maturity rule based on median delay still keeps long open paths.
