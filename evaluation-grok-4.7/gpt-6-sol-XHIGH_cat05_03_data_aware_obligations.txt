8.5

The case conclusions are largely the right ones, and the temporal model is careful. The score is held down by a real rule-formalization error, a contradictory classification, and checker logic that does not implement the semantics the prose states.

The R4 baseline is wrong in both the pseudo-logic and the code. An increase invalidates a review when it exceeds 120% of the amount at that review. The answer also invalidates on a hop of more than 20% from the immediately preceding amount. After a decrease, a partial recovery can trip that test while the release amount is still within 20% of the reviewed amount, which R4 does not treat as invalidating. None of the eleven cases hit this pattern, so the table survives, but the stated rule does not.

TX-302 is internally inconsistent. The row says the 09:20 Enhanced_Review independently satisfies R1, deviations are none, and the qualified signature also makes EX-77 valid, yet the classification is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at high confidence. That label means the waiver is what makes an otherwise unmet control acceptable. Here the primary control was met and the waiver is redundant. Section 4 admits COMPLIANT is defensible, which makes the high confidence in the chosen label too strong. The facts about the 09:05 signature versus the 11:00 outage insert are handled correctly.

The checker does not implement the three-valued and tie rules given in sections 1–2. An unknown jurisdiction with no screen sets the R3 trigger to false. Equal list-update and screen timestamps are treated as no trigger rather than unresolved, and `event_index` is not used for that comparison. `iloc[-1]` is not a time-latest sanctions update once more than one case has a list event, because the frame is sorted by case id first. `r3_rescreen_ok = not r3_trigger` becomes true when the trigger is unknown. The R4 flag is a hard false when the baseline is unknown, with the unknown kept only in a side column. TX-311’s post-review change to DE makes `r1_review_ok` false even though the release-time R1 trigger is false. Status on this particular log still lands on NONCOMPLIANT, COMPLIANT, JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, or REVIEW_REQUIRED in the right places; the defects are latent or diagnostic.

Smaller issues: TX-305 is the cleanest INDETERMINATE result in the set, so Medium confidence in that classification fights the answer’s own definition of confidence. The standard-packet test is a substring match on a note. Item 8’s control-flow demonstration is absent; the surrounding explanation is not.

What keeps this above a mid-8 is the casework. Release state is not backfilled. TX-305 is not given the next-day 150,000. TX-306’s +4.55% is correctly kept inside R4, so both R1 and R2 still hold. TX-307’s standard-packet approval does not satisfy R2. TX-308 uses an inclusive 100,000 and does not let a decrease kill the review. TX-309’s second increase is measured from 118,000 (+22.88%), not from 95,000. TX-310 stays open for the right reason: a displayed backdate and an interested witness against a certificate created at 10:30. TX-311 orders index 3 before index 4 and correctly says a failed DE update would still leave a review of the US 115,000 state. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and logging fixes with concrete cases.
