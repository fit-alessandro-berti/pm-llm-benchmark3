The write-up names the right survival and process-mining ideas, then computes the wrong quantities from the supplied table and treats those quantities as cumulative incidence. That gap is large enough to make the operational conclusions unreliable.

**Estimands are not the probabilities that are written down.** \(P(T_{\text{resolve}}>72)\), the cumulative incidence of any terminal event, the cumulative incidence of resolution, and the probability of still being undisposed at 72 hours are all reduced to events divided by 32. Open cases younger than 72 hours (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are thereby counted as known non-events. That is a complete-cohort proportion, not a risk-set estimand. For the CIF of resolution, Reject, Withdraw, and Transfer are encoded as “censored,” which is the cause-specific 1−KM coding and not Aalen–Johansen coding. Assumption A is titled informative censoring and then implemented as a competing event; those are different mechanisms. An open case aged 92 hours has already missed resolution by 72 hours; calling S09 a “likely” or “potential” breach treats an observed milestone failure as a future prediction. Standard right-censoring is also described as assuming that open cases resolve on time, which it does not.

**The case table is miscounted, and those errors drive the results.** Resolved after 72 hours are S03 (76), S13 (110), S20 (74), and S28 (88): four cases, 4/32, not S03 and S13 alone and not 6.3%. Open cases are nine (A: S09, S10, S11, S15; B: S24, S25, S26, S29, S31), of which four are already at least 72 hours old (S09, S11, S25, S26), not three and not 9.4%. Withdrawals are four, including S12 at 15 hours; the reported withdrawal median of 44 hours drops S12. Any terminal event by 72 hours is 18/32, not 17/32. Escalation is 6/15 in A and 6/17 in B, not about 7/15 versus 9/17, so the claim that Cohort B escalates more is not in the data. S32 is transferred at 96 hours, after the SLA horizon; the text says all three transfers occur by 72 hours. The A2 arithmetic both excludes and includes S08 at 68 hours while stating 11/32. Scenario B3 is the same calculation as B1. The D3 claim that escalated cases resolve 7/7 and 9/9 is not true (A escalations include S08, S10, and S11).

**Priority and cohort headlines in the governance report are wrong.** Resolved by 72 hours is 4/15 in A and 5/17 in B, not 6/15 and 7/17. P1/P2/P3 sample sizes are 8/14/10, not 8/12/12. Every P1 case is escalated, and no P3 case is. The reported medians, the 68-versus-88-hour variant contrast, the 14-hour Escalate sojourn, and the “adds 15 hours” rework effect are not recoverable from these fields. The calibration deciles are invented numbers presented as a check. `Submit→Screen` is not mandatory: S18 and S29 never pass through Screen, and Cohort A also uses AutoCheck.

**The code does not implement the plan.** Status codes are mapped as `resolve` / `transfer` / `reject` / `withdraw` while the rows contain `resolved` / `transferred` / `rejected` / `withdrawn`, so the event type used for the “Aalen–Johansen” routine is missing for every case. That routine is still a count of rows with time ≤ 72 over n, not a risk set. No survival library is named. Prefixes are grouped as strings; there is no PM4Py log, `parse_event_log_string`, `get_variants`, or `get_prefixes_from_log`. The same full-prefix `escalated` flag that Section 5 calls high-leakage is then used as an effect modifier. Several extra fenced blocks and a tenth top-level summary violate the required single code block and exact heading list.

Section 4’s method menu and Section 9’s missing-data list are the competent parts: competing risks, landmarking, multi-state paths, and leakage are at least discussed, and external transfer outcomes, event times, cutoff dates, and non-random rollout are correctly prioritized. The numerical layer contradicts that discussion often enough that the piece cannot be used as an SLA analysis.
