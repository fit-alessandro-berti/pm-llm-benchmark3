The analysis is strong, numerically careful, and conceptually right where it matters most: identical timestamps are not treated as physical simultaneity, logging batches are separated from execution, G6’s zero service and inflated wait are derived from the same stamp, and G1/G6/G7 are kept in one temporal class with three different physical readings. The checked quantities (waits, services, unions, peaks, G8’s exact 38/43/48/54/58 split, G7’s negative waits and 15/14/11/8/4 bounds under A6) match the extract. Assumptions are labeled, and the bias directions are generally coherent.

The deductions are for local inaccuracies and a few places where the write-up is looser than its own definitions.

- In the G3 diagnosis cell, a last duration of 6 minutes is said to “match” start steps of 8, 7, and 7. Section 3 later states 8, 7, 7, and 6 correctly, so the table overclaims. That 1–2 minute shortfall is small relative to 1-minute precision, but it is not a match.
- The G1 costing sentence is muddled. Four logged 3-minute intervals sum to 12. Parallel robot occupancy is the 3-minute union. A serial partition of that same cycle sums to at most 3. The 12-minute total matches neither, so it cannot “resemble total touch time under a serial partition.”
- The stated rule is that endpoint contact is adjacency and a point mass has no duration. `adjacency_count` still counts G6 as six adjacencies, because every start equals every end at 17:00. Run as written, the pandas table partially describes the bulk point mass as a sequential chain, which the narrative correctly rejects. Overlap rate 0 and the reference label “Simultaneous” limit the damage, but the diagnostic contradicts A4.
- Section 4’s comparison is with a hand-built gap-chainer, not with output from `discover_batches`. That surrogate is appropriate and, on this log, likely to match a standard end-to-start rule. The prose nonetheless states as fact that membership is unchanged “between merge_distance 0 and 300 seconds,” while the code’s own probe exists because the unit is not established. For this particular log the grouping claim survives a millisecond reading, since every within-cluster service gap is already ≤ 0. The unit wording does not.
- G4 at confidence 93 as “sequential batching” fights the counterevidence in the same row: exact back-to-back scanner intervals are ordinary single-server flow, and the label “batch” is the risk. The startup gap before 12:05 is correctly called unexplained, then section 3 smooths the whole pattern into “ordinary transient overload.” The +3 minute growth per position is transient overload. The first item’s 7 minutes is not.
- G3’s single insert at 11:31 means the staggered starts were not committed as separate execution events. Section 3 is conditional (“if starts are true”), and later sections demand a shadowed folder, but “usable queue measure” still gives those starts more authority than the insert pattern supports. G2 is better evidenced, because each completion has its own insert.

No plant-level ranking is invented, G7 is censored rather than imputed, and G8’s hole is kept on the scanner clock instead of being pinned on the unsynchronized safety interval. Those are the right calls. The remaining defects are local, but they are real, and they keep the answer short of a nearly flawless mark.
