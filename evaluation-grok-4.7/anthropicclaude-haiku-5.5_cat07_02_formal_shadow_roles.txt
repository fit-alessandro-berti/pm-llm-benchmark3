The role model is usable and often well judged, but several load-bearing figures and one authority claim are wrong, so a process owner could not rely on the write-up as it stands.

The serious miss is R07_Giorgio’s A9 count. The matrix gives him 20 Supervisor Approval events, more than R06_Fatima’s 16. The role table records this as 0, and Section 2 treats that zero as proof that he does not approve. That understates his authority and weakens the shadow-role and ambiguity analysis. His commercial shadow status still holds on A7 (72) and A8 (88), including the Weeks 3–4 cover (31 A7, 18 A8, duration 38 to 74 minutes), but the approval facts are part of the same question.

Section 2’s core-versus-peripheral percentages are systematically off. Front-office A1+A2+A4+A12 is about 86% of R01_Amira’s 1,375 events and about 91% of R02_Ben’s 1,366, not about 75%. Technical A3+A5+A10 is about 81% for R03_Chen (715/879) and about 84% for R04_Dina (710/849), not 60–65%. Fatima’s A7+A8 share is about 73% (212/291), not about 60%. For the quality pair, A14 is about 18% of Ivan’s events and about 20% of Julia’s, not about 10%; A11 is about 57% of each person’s events. The correctly computed figures, such as Nora’s 27% of A2 (610/2,251) and the pair’s 89% of A11 (608/684), show the other percentages were not checked.

Severity is misranked. Erik’s high-or-critical share is about 83% (137/166 cases), and Hana’s is about 70% (192/274). Mateo’s 52% (224/429) is high for a coordinator and is not the highest share. Erik’s volume is 170 events, not about 210, and he touched 166 cases, not 152. Hana’s median 0.79 is not the highest median; Erik’s 0.86 is. Calling both Hana and Mateo “highest” contradicts the Erik finding in the same answer.

Smaller problems accumulate. “Four resources” then names Giorgio, Mateo, the Chen–Dina pair, and Ivan–Julia’s remediation. Mateo’s A2 (50) and A4 (45) are at least as frequent as the activities labeled core. The 13/21 recheck rate is a Week 5 episode, while Section 3 states it as a general rate. Amira’s greater dependence on Hana and Mateo, and a split in informal authority between Ivan and Julia, are not in the evidence. Training Ivan and Julia to cover security and technical gaps pulls against the independence risk the answer itself raises. Giorgio is placed in the commercial role and also described as having no clean cluster.

The code calls the requested PM4Py functions and states the right guardrails, but it would mislead if run. NetworkX betweenness treats `weight` as distance, so raw handover counts invert closeness. The working-together conversion is an opaque `items() and {...}` expression. Overlapping roles multiply events on the merge. The complexity share is event-weighted, which the answer’s own caution says is the wrong unit, and the title-mismatch flag is left hardcoded false. Similarity is computed and then unused.

What holds up is the separation of permanent front office, temps, Nora, tiered technical work, quality, Erik’s rare critical role, and Mateo’s brokerage; the benefits and risks of shadow work; the deciding-evidence lists; and the staffing cautions on routing, substitution, low-volume authority, profile similarity, exposure versus execution, and reopen attribution. Those parts are the reason the answer stays above a failing grade. The arithmetic and the Giorgio approval claim are why it stays far from a high one.
