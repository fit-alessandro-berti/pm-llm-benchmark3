The write-up is complete and format-compliant, and most pre/post and 2×2 difference-in-differences arithmetic checks out. It is not a reliable causal assessment. Several central claims contradict the series, the author’s own tables, and the identification limits the author elsewhere acknowledges.

The rework conclusion is wrong. Adoption-month changes are about −2.0, −2.4, and −2.0 points, but they are not a sustained step. Alpha moves 13.2 → 11.2 in April, then 14.0 and 14.5, so the April–August mean rises by +0.54, which Section 5 itself reports. Beta returns to 12.7, only 0.1 below baseline. Gamma’s −2.0 is from the May–June complexity peak, not from its 12.3 pre-shock level. Sections 2, 4, 7, and 10 still call this an immediate, sustained, shock-proof drop of −2.0 to −2.4 points, with confidence 90 and “no counterevidence.” That is the strongest result in the claim ladder, and it is false. The −1.9 point DiD is mostly controls’ rework rising with the May case-mix shift.

Cycle time is similarly over-aggregated. The −4.5 hour / −6.3 hour Cohort 1 contrasts average a non-monotonic path: Alpha and Beta do not show a clean adoption-month median drop (Beta’s median rises in April), June is worse in levels, and August improves for untreated Epsilon as well. Gamma’s headline −4.2 / −7.7 hour contrast uses May–June, the most adverse pre-window. Labeling these “causal findings” and “identical signatures” ignores targeted +10% routing, unequal FTE shocks, and Beta’s smaller complexity change. Parallel pre-trends are asserted from three noisy months and do not “strongly refute” regression to the mean.

Section 5’s intensity coefficients (−0.1304, cluster SE 0.0321, and the analogues) and the “controlled” −2.19 hour / −6.41 hour effects are not produced by the Section 9 code. Scaled by 35 points, they recreate the simple DiD, so they are not an independent dose-response. No specification, event-study fit, or monotonicity check is shown, yet the text says the regression confirms that use, not availability, drove outcomes. With five clusters those standard errors are not evidence.

Identification is internally inconsistent. Rework is “strongly identified,” while p90 is “not identifiable,” after which Sections 5 and 7 report precise p90 ranges. Binary Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham is recommended and then never estimated; the code fits the naïve two-way fixed-effects models Section 3 rejects and does not use the relative-time indicators. Fraud-desk interference is treated as a fact rather than a hypothesis. The intensity-versus-version problem is named, then the preferred estimator still assumes an absorbing binary treatment.

The rollout choice, `expand with an embedded evaluation`, is directionally sensible, but the decision rule demands statistical significance on two branches, two weeks of within-branch randomization ignores shared-queue interference, and an eight-week window cannot capture the multi-month learning curve the answer itself emphasizes.

Epistemic labels are miscalibrated: a violated parallel-trends/SUTVA contrast is placed on the “conditionally causal” rung, and the false sustained rework claim is placed on the “observed” rung.
