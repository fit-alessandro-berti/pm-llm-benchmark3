The later conclusions are a strong, skeptical review: Model S’s fitness is bought by optionalizing mandatory checks, Model C is a memorized variant table that rejects valid recombinations (B2, A1–A3), positive-only precision and the unreproducible “33” duplicate count are correctly discounted, and the third design (strict pass-1 checks, tri-state Supplier Review, non-empty recheck subsets, 4-valued checker that overrides alignment stories) is safer than S and more general than C. Costs and P1–P7 readings are mostly consistent after the revisions.

It is not nearly flawless. The required 11-heading document is never actually produced; the dissent is only an outline, not the required 150–250 words; the validation code ends truncated. In the final checker, `affected_checks` as a list of activity names (what the combination suite emits) is treated as rounds of strings, so `set("Credit Check")` splits into characters and P6 never matches. `model_third` reintroduces duplicate `Credit Check` / `Technical Review` labels without treating that as a limitation of its own metrics, and `_er_inside_round` is unused and unreliable. Those gaps keep the result well short of a governance-ready deliverable.
