6.5

The final write-up is complete and often numerically careful, but it is not internally consistent enough for a high score. The capacity arithmetic is mostly right; the decision table and recommendation are not reconciled with that arithmetic, and the simulation has metric and dispatch bugs that the limits section does not identify.

What holds up:
- All nine required sections are present, assumptions are labeled, and both a tail/SLA objective and a fairness/robustness objective are defined.
- Mixture means are correct (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74).
- Shift/break capacity is correct: block clocks 480, 600, 510, 570, 210, 120 and a 2490-minute day. Deepa correctly ends at 18:00.
- Reconstructed block loads (about 444.6 through 191.9, total 2720.6) and skill totals match the stated figures. U1, U3, U4, and the renormalized U2 electrical shift are arithmetically coherent, and the U2/U4 interpretation choices are stated.
- The specialization-versus-pooling tension, evening staffing cliff, Chen/Emil software bottleneck, handover/setup/preemption, and U5 fallback are real and well framed.
- Policies are operational, monitoring attributes match the brief, and the single Python block encodes the tables, runs base plus U1–U5 for four policies, emits an interval log, and calls the requested PM4Py functions.

Material problems:
- Section 2 shows standard work already above shift capacity (2720 vs 2490; still above 2670 even with full overtime at multiplier 1.0, and worse once multipliers exceed 1). Section 4 then gives Robust/base a mean end-to-end delay of 30–60 minutes and max utilization 0.85–0.95. Zero-queue elapsed service is already about 50–70 minutes by type, and average resource-minutes per case are about 65 before slowness and penalties. A 30–60 minute cycle time is below that floor, and a sub-0.95 max utilization cannot coexist with aggregate overload. Pool/Hybrid base means (40–80 and 35–70) have the same problem.
- Those figures drive the recommendation. P2 attainment of 90–96% in base is also hard to defend: 16:00–20:00 generates about 584 standard minutes against about 330 clock minutes, and there is no overnight coverage before a P2 deadline. The stated ±30–50% band does not repair a central estimate that violates the answer’s own capacity result.
- Section 4 is a hand-set pattern, not the output of Section 8. The two analyses are never compared.
- In the code, unfinished cases are closed at minute 1440, so `delay <= 1440` treats almost every censored case as a P3 success. That bias is not disclosed.
- “At most one preemption per case” is implemented per activity, so an ME/ES case can be preempted twice.
- The robust rule skips a busy resource if any other idle resource can take any of its eligible activities. Emil can therefore refuse software that only he can do.
- Handover and setup are added to activity duration, so PM4Py service time absorbs coordination penalties while the separate queue-wait table does not. The required service/wait labeling is therefore mixed.
- The code does not implement the stated SERPT, slack, or threshold-parallel rules. Section 9 mentions simplifications but not these omissions, the P3 censoring, or the dispatch bug.
- Strict software eligibility drops Farah, the only 08:00–12:00 software resource besides Chen, which tilts the comparison against specialization.
- Block “utilization” equates standard minutes to clock minutes inside the arrival block and ignores cross-block spill and skill mismatch. The evening cliff is real; the 162% and 209% morning ratios are offered-load ratios, not window utilization.
- Policy D’s priority rule still contains a struck-through contradiction (“otherwise P3 before slackless P2? No…”).

The ranking strict < pooling < reserved hybrid < dynamic hybrid, with a P1 fallback under U5, is a defensible operations conclusion. The quantitative support for how well that policy meets SLA, delay, and the 90% fairness cap is not.
