7.5

The final verdicts are right: A and B are not equivalent under P1 or P3, and they match under the P2 abstraction. The loop reading is correct (one Check per iteration in A, exactly one Check concurrent with the whole loop in B), the four counterexamples are valid complete traces, and the P2 simplification (Open, one Check, then `*(Analyze, Rework)`, Close) really does preserve analyze-count, rework presence, and “a check occurred” while dropping pairing, interleaving, and approval.

The grade is held down by a false final claim and a weaker-than-advertised enumerator. Section 9 says the P3 languages are disjoint. They are not: single-iteration traces such as Open, Check, Analyze, Approve, Close are in both models. For one pass, A’s visible language is a proper subset of B’s (B adds Check after Approve); for two or more analyses the sets are disjoint because Check counts differ (k vs 1). The right conclusion is overlap without either inclusion. The bounded explorer is also not reliably exhaustive: the silent-step cap is not part of the visited key, so a first arrival with a spent silent budget can block a shorter silent path, and the place/arc firing code is too fragile to treat the printed set difference as proof. Node counts, depth (4 vs 5), and the leaf inventory are exact; simplicity is correctly left uncomputed.
