The case holdings are mostly the right ones, and the temporal model is careful: forward-only state, `event_index` as the same-timestamp tie-break, settlement not copied back onto TX-305, EX-77 distinguished from EX-88, and TX-311’s US-then-DE order stated correctly. Sections 4, 6, and 7 are strong. The score is held down by contradictions between that narrative and both the formalization and the checker.

The material-change predicate is inconsistent. Section 1 says an R1 material change includes amount updates. R1 then requires the Enhanced Review to fall after the latest such change. Under that wording, TX-306 (220,000 to 230,000 after review and approval) and TX-308 (140,000 to 100,000 after review) both fail R1. The case table instead marks both COMPLIANT because R4 does not invalidate a decrease or an increase of at most 20 percent. That second reading is the better one, but it is never defined as `LatestRelevantMaterialChange`, which remains an undefined predicate. TX-306 is also missing from the ambiguity discussion, unlike TX-308.

R4 is then applied too widely. The policy invalidates a review only for amount-dependent obligations. The pseudo-logic and the code both treat amount invalidation as defeating R2, which is a risk/PEP rule. `RelevantToCurrentR2State` is named and never defined. `InvalidatedForAmount` also puts “no later qualifying review” inside the existential, so a review that was invalidated and later replaced can come out as not invalidated.

R3’s trigger is the violation pattern (latest screen still before the list update), so a proper rescreen makes the trigger false. The code is stricter and wrong: updates are filtered to those after the latest prior screen, and the rescreen search looks only in that same prior-screen set, so a satisfied rescreen is unreachable.

The checker hard-fails the two cases the narrative gets right. Every `Update_Amount`, including TX-306’s +4.55 percent and TX-308’s decrease, is a material change, and `R1_status` requires the Enhanced Review to be after that change. Both become `FAIL` / `NONCOMPLIANT_COARSE`, not `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. A later `Standard_Review` also opens a new `review_generation` and clears an earlier Enhanced Review’s invalidation via `cummax`. Evidence quality for EX-77 and EX-88 is hardcoded. `format_dataframe` is called and the resulting log is never used. TX-302 is labeled `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` even though the 09:20 Enhanced Review independently meets R1; that alternative is disclosed, but the primary label still describes an exception where the ordinary control was satisfied.
