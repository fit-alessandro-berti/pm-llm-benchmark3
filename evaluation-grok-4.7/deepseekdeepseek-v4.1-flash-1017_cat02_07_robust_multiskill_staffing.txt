The base capacity arithmetic is coherent and auditable: mixture means (6.94 / 26.3 / 32.82 / 38.5 / 15.74), case-mix totals (42), skill workloads (~292 / 476 / 798 / 495 / 661), and 2722 minutes versus 2490 minutes of shift capacity (~109% before handover, setup, coordination, and multipliers). Assumptions, two objectives, three operational policies, shock-wise bottleneck migration, and the monitoring/pilot sections are concrete and use the supplied rules.

That load result is not carried into the decision. Section 4 still gives Hybrid base max utilization of 88–94%, mean delay of 80–125 minutes, and P1 SLA of 92–96%. Resource-minutes above calendar capacity cannot finish inside shifts at under 95% utilization; handover (on the order of two changes per case) pushes the gap wider. The recommendation then treats those SLA and fairness figures as evidence that Hybrid meets the targets. Routing cannot create the missing capacity, and the 90%/95% utilization constraints in O1 are infeasible on the author’s own totals. U1–U5 stay qualitative, so the bottleneck claims for the shocks are not auditable the way the base case is.

The Python scaffold does not test the policies in the prose. Cases are booked from intake through validation at arrival, so later P1 work cannot reclaim capacity. Strict selection is minimum-cost over broad backup lists, not the stated fixed order. The 10% penalty is applied to the P1 diagnostic itself, not to an interrupted lower-priority activity’s remaining time. Setup is added both to ready time and to busy duration. `TEAM_HANDOVER` is unused. Unschedulable activities are skipped, which drops the worst cases from the delay metrics. Hybrid’s U5 fallback is not implemented. PM4Py is called, but case-level service/waiting output is not labeled and no stage- or queue-specific metrics are computed, despite requirement 7 and the correct warning in Section 7.

The specialization-versus-pooling tension, event-attribute design, and guarded pilot are strong. The score is held down because the decision table contradicts the capacity result and the executable model does not implement the dispatch rules being recommended.
