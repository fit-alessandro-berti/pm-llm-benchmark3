The control analysis is strong and case-specific, but the required checking code encodes false case facts and would emit the wrong evidence on the two cases that most need to be distinguished. That is enough to keep the answer well short of nearly flawless.

Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6 do the work the task asks for. FEP-1 is split into unilateral authorization, duplicates, bank-account integrity, receipt and commercial substance, collusion and credential misuse, recoverability, attribution, and normalization of emergency processing. Each control is tied to prevent, detect, correct, or fail. The sufficiency test uses timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, and collusion resistance, and it correctly treats equal expected loss as an incomplete standard. Manager action after `Release_Payment` but before settlement is explicitly hybrid, with internal authorization, stop-authority, and economic irrevocability separated. Counterfactuals cover every case, stay qualitative, and do not treat a second username as automatically effective. Monitoring rules cover the requested signals and separate an alert from proof of misconduct.

Section 3 is mostly well judged. AP-202 and AP-208 are failed and unjustified. AP-205 is stopped before completion. AP-204’s formal separation is not treated as success after a fabricated receipt. AP-209 is left indeterminate because the repeated u12/u13 pair and missing cell design cut against the clean result. AP-203 and AP-207 are reasonably treated as ED-1 paths, with AP-207 weaker because approval follows release. AP-201 is a tolerable low-exposure deviation rather than a mechanical failure. AP-206’s token-timing ambiguity is real.

The factual and calibration problems are these.

The AP-201 audit is said to fall outside the usual recall window because it began about 25 hours after release. Settlement was at 10:00 UTC and the audit event is 09:00 UTC the next day, about 23 hours after settlement. The recall clock attaches to the transfer. The audit may still be too late once completion, rather than start, is considered, but “outside the window” is not established. Confidence is also high for AP-203 while m02’s level is unconfirmed, and high for AP-204 while recovery and whether the pair is an authorized cell remain open. AP-206 is labeled indeterminate while the same row also treats the log, absent a proven clock defect, as technical nonconformance that was materially mitigated. m21’s independence is never raised for AP-208. The “fully equivalent” test also requires recoverability even when prevention succeeded before loss.

The code meets the structural checklist: embedded CSV, UTC parsing, `format_dataframe`, a four-eyes call with a version cross-check, AP-205 separated from completed releases, joined facts, preliminary flags, and an explicit non-verdict label. The fact table is wrong. `bank_change_lt_7d` is false for AP-202, whose bank account changed two hours earlier, and true for AP-203, which had no bank change. `bank_change_proximity_flag` is therefore reversed on both cases. AP-203 also fails `emergency_basic_eligibility` and the ED-1 composite because of that flag. AP-203’s stated 12:00 settlement is not encoded, while AP-201’s 10:00 settlement is hardcoded, so `manager_before_settlement` is false for the strongest pre-release approval. Executed output would contradict the narrative on bank-change proximity and on whether AP-203 even looks ED-1-eligible.
