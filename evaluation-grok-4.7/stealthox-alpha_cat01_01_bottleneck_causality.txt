The arithmetic core is solid and the decision is disciplined: the crude +6.72 h gap, the within-complexity reversals (−2.14/−2.21/−3.43 h), the pooled-weight standardized −2.51 h, the wait-versus-service split, and the refusal to treat stratification as identification all check out. The verdict, graph, role table, tests, pilot-plus-capacity plan, and diagnostics cover the required structure, and most cell counts are auditable.

The score is held down by concrete errors and one causal-role mistake. Nova’s high-complexity appeal count is wrong: both Nova high cases are “No appeal,” so the quality row’s “1/6 vs 1/2” is false (the Nova appeal is low-complexity C24). “Group×shift cells ≤2” is also false and is repeated; Orion–night is 13 and Nova–day is 12. “Night is 13/14 Orion” misstates 13/17.

Compliance is mis-specified as a mediator and front-door candidate of resource group. Within complexity it is balanced (0/50/100 percent in both groups), and it is fixed by policy before or independently of the group label, so the crude +14.83 h is complexity confounding. Hypothesis 3 then predicts a mediation share of an adjusted gap whose sign favors Orion, which does not match a harmful compliance path. Hypothesis 5’s claim that censoring “must hurt Nova’s record” does not follow: dropping Orion’s longest cases would manufacture an Orion advantage. Hypothesis 2 treats the confounded night gap (+7.76 h) as support even though, within complexity, night is not slower. Reviewer skill is labeled a confounder although certification is part of the group bundle.

The learning plan overlaps the routing pilot with the capacity wedge and calls a one-shot reassignment a crossover, so the proposed ITT is not cleanly identified. These are local failures in an otherwise careful analysis, not a collapse of the main conclusion.
