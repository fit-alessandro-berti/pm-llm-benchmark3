The memo meets the required structure and is substantively stronger than a generic Lean list: it names the fast-path cost illusion, separates low-risk from high-value treatment, states sacrifices, and sequences pilots so downstream complaints and queues can falsify a local win. Those parts are usable.

It is not nearly flawless, because several claims fight the artifacts. “~EUR 30 per case” is invented; only the 31% / 9% cost gap is given. Manual “inflow of 1,400” counts only Intake → Manual eligibility review and drops the 890 evidence returns and 310 reopen returns, so the 15–35% capacity warning is built on the wrong base. Redesign A also understates the two-person fraud bottleneck while saying pressure moves away from fraud. Redesign B treats 45 rework events versus 260 dispatch complaints as comparable, ignores that fast approval is still about 9% cheaper after complaints, and sells repair as eliminating the EUR 410 error while skipping the stated 12.5% rework rate on repaired high-value devices. “All ~1,050 standard high-value cases triple repair load” assumes a segment-to-path split the DFG does not contain. R1 is implemented as the high-value segment rather than the EUR 400 rule, mid-risk devices are uncovered, and R3 (reopen before close or denial) is never redesigned. Premium high-value times (20h / 96h) are applied to high-value generally. Redesign A’s evidence-portal branch also blurs the required material difference from C. The terminal DFG does not conserve (about 3,625 ends versus 5,000 starts), and the volume extrapolations never flag that incompleteness.

Trade-offs, segment fit, and the experiment logic keep it clearly above a weak pass. The unsupported number, unnormalized counts, and capacity/conformance gaps keep it well below a high score.
