7.2

The write-up is a real capacity-and-policy analysis: labelled assumptions, auditable offered-load math, explicit dispatch rules, a multi-objective trade-off that is not “lowest base mean delay,” and a PM4Py section that correctly separates case-level sojourn/service from stage queue time. It is not nearly flawless. Several defects change how hard the recommendation can be trusted.

The static load work checks. Base means (Intake 6.9, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.8, Software 38.5, Validation 15.7), block loads, and the ρ path through U1–U5 are internally consistent, including the harsh U2 reading (E and ES counts doubled, 12:00–14:00 electrical load ≈ 230 minutes, block ρ ≈ 1.42 / 1.84). The structural claim is right: pure service is already ≈ 2,720 minutes against ≈ 2,490 available, overtime of 180 minutes does not close the gap once handovers are included, Mechanical has no qualified server after 18:00, and Software before noon is only Chen at 1.25 and Farah at 1.40. Routing cannot create a feasible day or a plan in which every resource stays under 90%.

The simulation evidence is weaker than the prose.

Delay is censored. `mean_wait` and `p90_flow` are computed only on cases that finish (`cdf[~cdf.carry]`), while the text says every policy carries 16–20 of about 42 cases. In a day with ρ > 1, the 90th percentile of completers is not the process p90. SRPT, which finishes short cases and rolls long ones, is favoured. P1/P2 SLA do count non-completion as a miss, so those columns are on a better definition than the delay columns. The table never says the delay figures are conditional on completion.

`mean_delay` is waiting and `p90_delay` is flow. Parallel branches add both durations into `service_sum`, so wait = flow − service can be largely negative, not “slightly” understated, and the bias is larger for policies that parallelise more. Handover burden divides all handover/setup/coordination minutes by completers’ service only.

SPEC is not a fair specialisation policy. Home queues are fastest-only and have no overflow: Mechanical is {Ada, Ben}, so from 16:00–18:00 Deepa is on shift and Mechanical-qualified and still ineligible. Validation excludes Ada, Chen, and Farah, forcing handovers the authors later say should be avoided. SPEC’s loss mixes “fixed queues cannot cover skill holes” with “this roster ignores the only person still at work.” That overstates how cleanly pooling dominates specialisation.

Code and text disagree. The preemption comment and §3 say the victim with the most remaining work is chosen; the sort key `-(r.svc_end - t)` keeps the shortest remainder. §1.2 says sequential execution avoids the 8-minute coordination delay, which is what the prompt says; the code charges those 8 minutes whenever two resources touched the branches, including sequential handoffs. A5 quietly redefines the rule. O2 is described as the worst case of one scalar; the code adds the worst P1, the worst P2, and the worst p90 regret across possibly different scenarios. The 50% P3 aging rule almost cannot fire inside a single 08:00–20:30 day, so it does not explain the reported type-spread gap. Tie-break “alphabetical” is not implemented. The 20-seed threshold/guard/preemption variants, and the surge-mode pass-over cap, are not in the script. Those effect sizes (±15 minutes, +4–6 P1 points, −8 P2 points, “most of” the SRPT gain) are not reproducible from the delivered program.

The recommendation over-reads noise the authors themselves define (ties inside ≈ 0.05 SLA or ≈ 20 minutes). Pooled P1 in base is 0.86–0.87. Worst-case P1 is 0.79 vs 0.77 vs 0.76. That is not a demonstrated HYBRID win. The scalar O2 score ranks POOL_SRPT first (0.55 vs 0.28). Preferring HYBRID because ES/S starvation violates a 0.30 type-spread constraint is a legitimate different objective, but U3’s p90 gap (266 vs 219) is called “within noise” while U1’s similar gap is called real. The fallback that is supposed to take SRPT’s throughput without its starvation is explicitly unsimulated, then given numeric benefits.

Utilisation prose contradicts the table. “3–4 of 6 resources above 90% under every policy” and “Farah 0.79–0.85 under every policy” cannot sit with SPEC base `max_util` 1.02, spread 0.64 (implied minimum ≈ 0.38), and 2.6 resources over 90%.

§7 and §9 are strong: attribute list, interval-log caveats, handover network, drift vs. bottleneck migration, and a stepped-wedge pilot with P1 stop rules. The MDE of 0.05 P2 attainment at 30 days is optimistic given the stated day-level SD of 0.12–0.21.

Score reflects a complete, mostly coherent argument whose load analysis and objective framing are good, and whose policy ranking rests on a censored delay metric, a handicapped specialist arm, untested switching rules, and a handful of internal contradictions.
