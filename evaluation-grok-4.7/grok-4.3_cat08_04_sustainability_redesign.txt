5.5

The write-up hits the required seven sections, uses real log quantities, and correctly refuses to strip Critical-downtime of dedicated vans. It is still analytically unreliable: several effects point the wrong way, two “options” are the same lever, and the recommended package never closes the hotspots and side-effects it creates.

Boundary and indicators. Batch size is an observed attribute, so “batch sizes are not recorded per case” is false. The 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days is in-process obsolescence, yet the boundary places obsolescence outside the log. Order-level flattening is never treated as an attribution assumption, and low discovered-model precision is ignored. Quality is not a separate indicator: Q1 (mass threshold, reinforced pack, inspection waiver) is absent, and part weight is not in the attribute list, so later “Q1 conformance” cannot be measured. The resilience indicator rewrites R1 as “a non-air, non-van alternative,” which conflicts with justified Critical service. `expedite_flag` is unused.

Interventions. S1 and S4 are the same Standard-stock Service-contract air-to-ground rule, then both are recommended. S1 assigns the 5% “stock → standard pack → air” path to that segment; that 5% is all stock-air, while the 22% SP1 breach is a subset of the 16% Service-contract stock volume. S4 inverts SP1: policy is ground/consolidated truck when on-time probability is above 85%, not when “risk exceeds 85%.” S2’s floor of batch size 8–12 sits below the stated efficient region (CNC_A 12–20; heat treatment above 10) and does not encode the 1.7× lateness from heat-treatment waits. S5 attaches 12–24 h consolidation to the partial-shipment path, which is already 24% late and median 79 h. S3 never states that CNC_B is faster, so the service trade-off is missing. S8 asserts the 3% lateness rate will hold under shared couriers. S9 is never analyzed, so true downtime and precautionary urgent stay collapsed. S12 is recommended with no analysis.

Effects table. Cycle time is neutral for S4 and S3 and “neutral to slight” for S1. Air is about 24 h faster than ground (stock-air median 17 h versus stock-ground 34 h), and CNC_B is faster than CNC_A, so those signs are wrong; lateness can stay acceptable only when slack is real, which is not the same as unchanged cycle time. S2 is given a material improvement even though larger batches raise the 6% obsolescence loss. S7 cannot both lengthen inspection and reduce lateness without a net argument. S5’s “low” lateness increase contradicts its own partial-shipment target. S1 versus S4 transport ratings differ for the same switch.

Rebounds. Batching, packaging mass versus damage, and CNC_A queue growth are grounded. Mode shift does not cause partial shipments; those start at availability check. Twice-daily trucks do not, on this evidence, make customers order earlier; the 6% obsolescence is a forecast/batch fact. The SP1 gaming risk is aimed at Regular customers, while the cited 22% breach is Standard-stock Service-contract. The likely behavior response is expedite or Critical misclassification, which is not stated.

Recommendation and governance. The bundle double-counts S1/S4, batches MTO without an obsolescence cap or promise-date lever (S10), and leaves partial shipments (7%, 24% late, second shipment, 7.2 kgCO2e) untouched even though they hurt service and emissions together. Critical-downtime is about 11% of orders at 38 kgCO2e average, and the emergency-van path is 52 kgCO2e, so it dominates transport; 31% of van cases lack SP2 justification. Logging overrides does not create the missing justification, and S9 is omitted, so the constraint to separate justified from unjustified urgent transport is not met. Validation replays SP1/SP2/Q1 and customer type only. Service-contract versus Regular mixes Critical-downtime with stock and MTO contract work and can hide a Critical service failure. Path frequencies, batch size, 14-day unshipped share, route mix, and R1 alternate-route availability are not in the control plan, and no rollback threshold is tied to the stated baselines (Critical late 4%, MTO Regular late 18%).
