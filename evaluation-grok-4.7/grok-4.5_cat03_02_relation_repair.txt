The repair stance is directionally sound: Bundle A correctly treats the scanner and carrier pointers as local evidence, refuses a unique delivery confirmation, and does not treat 130+100=230 as proof. The required sections, non-additions, and anti-circularity discussion are present. The score is held down by misclassified rules, non-discriminative evidence, and several concrete process-mining mistakes.

The evidence model overstates invariants. “Create Order relates to exactly one order” is a strong default the domain marks as normal, not a hard invariant. “An item belongs to at most one order” is an observation from this sample, not a given rule. Customer C17 and route NORTH cannot corroborate anything here: every order, the invoice, the portal, both packages, and both deliveries share those values. Using them as positive support inflates confidence. The same problem appears in calibration. `N-421?` is explicitly uncertain, yet ev10–DEL-Y is accepted at 0.83; the derived `DEL-Y carries PK-8` edge is accepted at 0.80 even though it has no independent observation. Competing payment hypotheses at 0.62 and 0.50 are not a coherent distribution. The crossed amounts are also missed: 100 matches ORD-B and 130 matches ORD-A, which is a different story from a pooled 230 invoice.

Lifecycle and downstream claims conflate object participation with neighborhood. After Bundle A, ORD-B’s own events are still only Create Order and Add Item; flattening it does not contain Pack. “Add/Pack ambiguity” and shared Add/Pack motifs are therefore wrong for order flattening and for equivalent-execution clustering on Order. The important new fragment is also missing: PK-8 participates in both ev07 and ev08, so the package OC-DFG gains Pack Item → Pack Item before Dispatch. At type level, Package–Item and Delivery–Package already exist, so Bundle A need not add an OTG edge at all; “OTG adds packs(IT-B1)” describes an instance graph, not the type graph. Bundle B’s ET–OT count changes are not actually specified. Bundle B also fails its own coherence test: accepting confirmations of both deliveries means DEL-Y’s confirmation covers IT-B1 while ORD-B remains absent from ev11.

Warning 5 is factually false on time. ev08 is 09:05 and ev09 is 10:00, so putting IT-B1 in PK-7 does not create pack-after-dispatch. The validation block meets the required calls, but `getattr(..., "graph") and "ok"` does not inspect the discovered graphs. The main abductive choices are defensible; the audit of their mining consequences is not reliable enough for a higher score.
