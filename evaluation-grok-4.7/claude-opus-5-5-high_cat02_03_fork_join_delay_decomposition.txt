8.5

The decomposition is arithmetically solid and the fork-join logic is right: flow time is \(U + \max_i D_i + DQ + P^F\), synchronization waits partition the same window and must not be summed, and the case table, F02/F04/F07/F11 workings, critical counts (A2/B8/C2), margins, and 1383-minute identity all check out. B is correctly ranked first on critical frequency, tail, queue-plus-service, and counterfactual effect, and the 35/73/103 split of downstream wait is a real finding, not a restatement of mean service.

The score is held down by several concrete defects. The Python path is not reliably reproducible: `discover_performance_dfg` is called on completion timestamps without a start-timestamp key, then indexed as `stats['mean']`, while the prose asserts start-order edge means (−43, 16.1, 21.0) as if the script printed them. “Reproduces every number in Sections 2–4” is also too strong; runner-up counts, critical-minute shares, and the excess-over-floor split are not produced by the code. Assumption A1 overstates the consequence of a derived `fork_release` (“no branch queue can be measured”); B and C waits would still be numbers, only their interpretation would change. Calling A and C a tie on present criticality uses case counts (2 vs 2) and margins (15 vs 12) while omitting that C already controls more critical fork-join time (112 vs 100). Smaller looseness: “20-minute” upstream lead time (F01 is 15), and “30-minute tests had the shortest queues” (F01’s 40-minute test also waits only 5). CF3a’s rise of the maximum to 140 is an artifact of holding idle lag on a reserved server; the caveat is present, but the figure is still tabulated as a peer result.
