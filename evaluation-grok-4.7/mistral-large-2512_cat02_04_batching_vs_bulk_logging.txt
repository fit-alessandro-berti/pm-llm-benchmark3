4.5

The write-up has the right skeleton and gets several easy patterns, but it mislabels a textbook case, reverses core bias directions, and supplies analysis code that would not run or measure the claimed quantities.

The G2 row is internally contradictory. The evidence cited is a shared 10:00 start and staggered completions (10:08, 10:14, 10:21, 10:27), which is start batching / “begin group,” matching the single begin-group button. The top label is nevertheless “end batching.” The proposed fix, moving starts to queue arrival, is also wrong: arrivals are upstream times, while the completion gaps of about 6–8 minutes are the sequential-service signal. Under standard definitions (queue wait = start − arrival, service = complete − start), a too-early common start understates later waits and inflates service and logged overlap. Section 3 says both wait and service are overestimated.

G3 is the mirror error. Staggered starts with a shared 11:30 complete and a shared 11:31 insert, plus the folder-submission note, imply an end-logging batch. That lengthens measured service; it does not shorten it. “Performed throughout the half-hour” means serial work over the interval, not physical concurrency. Section 3’s wait-up / service-down / utilization-down account is the wrong direction on all three. The signature matrix repeats the confusion: start batching and end batching are both given low overlap, whereas equal starts or equal ends with staggered opposite endpoints produce nested logged overlap. Timestamp coarsening is also given low overlap, but G7’s rounded intervals overlap completely. Smaller `merge_distance` does not recover true times from already-rounded stamps.

Other diagnoses are only partly sound. G1 as a four-item fixture batch is fair, but “waiting underestimated” is undefined, and identical start and complete is not explained by start batching alone. G6 correctly gets inflated wait and zero service; utilization is then described as continuous work, which contradicts zero-length intervals (measured busy time collapses toward zero). G7 correctly prefers coarsening, yet misses the impossible facts: M02–M05 arrive after the shared 14:00 start, and `recorded_at` steps one minute per case. The closing claim that G7, like G6, produces near-zero service is false. G5 never notes a four-way overlap against a stated cap of three analysts. G8’s safety-check story ignores the explicit cross-system clock warning, and “no batch id” is weak counterevidence. Throughput and conformance bias are essentially absent. Assumptions are not labeled, and the counterevidence column often restates support or says “none.”

The Python block includes every row and calls `format_dataframe` and batch discovery at two merge distances and two minimum sizes, but it is not a valid comparison. `recorded_at` mixes minute and second strings while the format is chosen from the first value with a test that still selects `%H:%M`, so parsing fails. `discover_batches` is treated as a dataframe with a `cluster` column and batch categories are never read. Consecutive `shift` is not an overlap rate; the coarseness indicator equals 1 when all timestamps agree and when all differ; “max queue size” is just the case count. The diagnostic concat/melt cannot yield the required cluster table.
