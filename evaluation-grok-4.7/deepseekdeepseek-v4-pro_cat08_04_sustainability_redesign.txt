6.5

The final answer covers all seven required sections, stays mostly tied to the given paths and policies, and correctly refuses a blanket cut of air and dedicated vans. It is not nearly flawless.

The system boundary is usable, but calling it cradle-to-gate while excluding upstream materials is the wrong lifecycle term. Indicators match the requested dimensions and attributes. Resilience is weakly defined as historical route variety rather than an available backup during disruption. Returns are used as a quality metric even though they are not events in the log.

Nine interventions are structured as required and generally hit the right segments: SP1 air on standard-stock service-contract work, CNC_A versus CNC_B, batch-wait caps, critical vans, and packaging loops. Several claims are not in the evidence. Consolidated truck is about 850 of 12,000 cases (roughly 7%), not under 1%. CNC_B’s “rougher finish” is invented; Q1 is a weight rule. The 22% SP1 violation rate is treated as proven air use. Shared-lane savings of 30–50%, a 5–10% CNC energy cut, a 30–50% scrap cut, and a 60% packaging-rework cut “matching the proportion” are precise figures the source does not support. Partial shipments (about 7% of cases, 24% late, a second dispatch) never get a full option, and the inspection-queue finding is not used when a stricter inspection gate is proposed.

The effects table has the right columns and some defensible ranges (air at 4–6×, consolidation at 35–55%). Other cells invent magnitudes, and many directions are blank or only implied, so the table does not reliably show trade-offs. I1 and I4 also overlap on the same air-to-ground shift.

Rebound coverage hits batching, mode substitution, packaging mass, obsolescence, congestion, and customer reclassification. Rework itself is thin: the list does not develop the evidence that rushed inspection queues create later rework, or that catching more defects can raise recorded rework before it cuts returns. The later obsolescence warning does correct the softer treatment of carbon-aware promises.

The package is coherent: slack-based mode choice, segment wait caps, CNC_A only with slack, true downtime versus precautionary urgency, and exception logging. It protects service-contract and critical work and keeps an alternate route. Validation covers SP1/SP2/Q1, segment lateness, and drift, with a 5% baseline used correctly, though several conformance targets are arbitrary and obsolescence and empty van returns are not explicit control metrics.
