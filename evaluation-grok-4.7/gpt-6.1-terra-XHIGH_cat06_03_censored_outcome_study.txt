7.8

The design is statistically serious and honest about selection, but it is not nearly flawless. The estimand, censoring taxonomy, leakage rules, and refusal to code non-activated cases as non-defaults are strong. Several concrete design and skeleton errors keep it well short of a top score.

The primary estimand is coherent: \(P(\text{default\_90d}=1 \mid \text{prefix}, \text{activation})\) under the historical policy, at a single pre-activation prefix, with the 90-day clock starting at Account Activated. Multi-state and counterfactual alternatives are correctly separated, and the counterfactual is given the right assumption burden. The 48,000-row snapshot adds up and is labeled as labeled, competing, censored, selectively unobserved, or immature. MAR versus MNAR, ordinary censoring versus delayed feeds, activation selection, and unlinked transfer are distinguished, and the testable versus untestable claims are mostly right.

The fixed prefix is not a process state. With the stated path Application Started → KYC → Credit Review, \(k=3\) is often the first review, but Accept and Contract Signed are not terminals in the skeleton. Those events can sit inside the three-event prefix whenever they occur before Account Activated. The early-warning population then mixes pre-decision and post-decision histories. That changes both the available information and the activation mixture.

The temporal split is by application start, while the policy break is 1 July 2025 and the migration is 15 October 2025. A case can start in the training window and have its third event, credit decision, and outcome after the policy change. The same straddle hits migration and activity renaming. Era should be fixed by prediction time, with crossing cases removed or isolated. The September–December 2025 “test” window is also mostly immature at the 15 February 2026 extract once a 90-day horizon and a 7–35 day feed lag are required. The text admits this, then still specifies that split and a full calibration suite for it.

Label timing is underspecified. Defaults can be known from collections activity before day 90, while non-defaults often wait on the external feed. Inclusion as soon as day 90 has elapsed, without a common administrative lag, enriches the labeled set with early positives. The prose later requires horizon plus lag; the code’s `usable_label` does not. `lag_eligible` is only stored. The IPW proposal is a reasonable primary analysis for residual missingness after that restriction, but it is not implemented, and the text hedges between weighting, survival censoring, and a mature-vintage restriction.

The skeleton would not run. `prefix_eligible` opens a parenthesis that is never closed, and the following assignment sits inside that expression, which is a syntax error. `get_prefixes_from_log` is then ignored when features are built. `risk_score` is copied into the model table even though the audit says repair or drop, so an overwritten case-level score still leaks. The rename map is empty, and duplicate rows are printed and then kept, so they still shift event number. `censor_time` is the 31 December 2025 event cutoff for every case, which is the wrong follow-up clock for post-activation default. The split comment promises build-date label freezing; labels are taken from the final extract only. One case with both an activation and a terminal aborts the whole cohort instead of being quarantined. `Request Documents` is an invented activity name.

Leakage decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, experience, and label fields are right, and the allowlist is the right shape. Those strengths do not offset an unexecutable pipeline and a prediction-time definition that can see the credit decision.
