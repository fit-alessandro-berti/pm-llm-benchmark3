6.5

The stage medians, occurrence rates, score separation (flag 0 max 0.61, flag 1 min 0.64), and 20% reject rate in each flag group are arithmetically correct, and absent stages are kept as missing. The choice of interventional policy contrasts over natural direct and indirect effects is well justified by interference, the flag-as-bundle problem, and exposure-induced confounding. Required sections, estimands, and pilot elements are present and mostly concrete.

The central quantitative claim is wrong. Median cycle time is 16.45 h versus 5.75 h, a ratio of about 2.9. Sections 3, 6, and 9 still call this “roughly doubles” or “~2×.”

The mechanism reading misses a pattern already in the stage table. Manual review occurs for all 8 high-complexity cases, including all 3 unflagged ones, and for none of the 4 low-complexity cases. The flag’s incremental review effect is concentrated in medium complexity (5/5 flagged versus 1/3 unflagged). Treating “100% versus 40%” as the effect of mandatory flag routing, and preferring P2 on that basis, overstates the flag pathway relative to case mix. No within-complexity flag contrast is reported.

Reversal condition 2 has the mediation logic backwards. If flag status lengthens waits by filling a shared queue, conditioning on concurrent queue length should shrink the flag–wait association. That shrinkage would support congestion mediation. The write-up treats it as evidence that interference is weak, which contradicts the correctly stated queue diagnostic in section 5.

The mechanism graph adds `manual_queue_wait → manual_service` as a causal edge and omits `risk_score → manual_review` / escalation / decision, even though P1 is defined by leaving the score visible. E3 calls the total effect of P2 an isolation of review occurrence, while that policy also changes queue equilibrium and later escalation. “Mostly M2” is 5/10, and “clustered morning arrivals” does not describe the high-load flagged set. Clock-time waits that cross the night are not separated from in-shift queueing.

The Python path correctly avoids zero-filling missing stages and warns against causal DFG readings. `discover_performance_dfg` is called with `start_timestamp_key`, which the standard PM4Py signature does not accept, so the performance DFG fails inside the shared `try`. No arrival/register event is emitted, so the DFG cannot show the triage-wait acceleration, and completion-to-completion performance mixes waiting with service.
