**5.5**

The required seven-part structure is present, including differentiated case-type effects, activation triggers, a complete trade-off table, and a split between leading and lagging indicators. The operational stance is also directionally sound: urgent priority is capped, defect removal and arrival smoothing are preferred to blanket capacity, and several proposals name a downstream station. Under a strict reading, the reasoning still misses or mishandles the evidence that should drive the redesign.

The mix-shift scenario treats registration as the stress point. At the observed peak mix, registration is only a 2.8-hour queue at 85% utilization. The long queues are specialist review (36h), medical validation (22h), assay (18h), technical validation (16h), and accession correction (14h, two specialists). The migration story therefore starts at the wrong constraint and then cites 96% and 101% utilization as if those peak facts were consequences of the proposed rules.

Scenario C contradicts itself. Moving technical validators into accession correction reduces flow into medical validation; it does not both starve that step of input and leave cases piled up there. The same policy treats cross-training as a queue-triggered switch, despite the stated four-week training time. In the outage policy, “hold routine releases” addresses the release step, while the saturated resource is the assay line, so the action does not protect M2.

The package omits the main peak instability in the log. Assay-to-repeat incidence rises from about 6.7% (1,140/17,030) to about 14.8% (1,260/8,500), aligned with M2 and night-shift clustering. No standing rule keeps repeat-prone or complex work off degraded M2. Early triage is also scored neutral for urgent cases, even though recollection returns in 42 hours normally and 60 hours at peak, against an 18-hour urgent limit. Triage load on the pre-analytical station, already at 96% and a 9.5-hour queue, is not counted. The table then says the same intervention produces a large rework reduction while leaving every case type unchanged, and it rates registration automation as highly beneficial for all types after the text has already said it exposes pre-analytical and assay capacity.

Process-mining evidence is thin. Urgent batch bypass falling from 74% to 58%, the 3.1× medical-validation and 2.4× specialist-review rates for complex panels, and the performance edges are not used. The dashboard correctly sets several bands from the queue table, but it leaves out specialist-review delay, the worst measured queue, and the age of in-process urgent work relative to 18 hours. The redesign-caused vulnerability is mostly a management misread, while the concrete new fragility is urgent bypass further fragmenting machine schedules and evening medical validation feeding an unstaffed specialist queue.
