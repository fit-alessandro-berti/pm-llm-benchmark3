The role model is strong and mostly faithful to the evidence: it separates automation, temporary intake, front office, technical tiers, commercial work, security, quality, coordination, and approval; it uses complexity, certifications, handoffs, and substitution episodes rather than raw frequency; and the required shadow-role, ambiguity, and staffing cautions are substantively addressed. Many counts check out, including Chen 879 vs. Dina 849, Erik 170 and 96/146 A6 events, Fatima 291, Giorgio’s 72−31 and 88−18 non-substitution split, Mateo’s 23 non-substitution A9 events, and the 10,534-event denominator behind the ~1.6% claim.

It is not nearly flawless. Hana’s 138 A9 events are 138/218 = 63.3% of approvals, not 58%. The same table gives Kai/Lina 6 high-complexity cases and then calls their exposure zero. Mateo’s A9 is core in the role table and peripheral in section 2. The PM4Py sketch is directionally coherent, but the A7 diagnostic reads case-level `@@case_service_time` / `@@case_waiting_time` while claiming to measure pricing-activity duration, and the write-up says that pipeline produces the 38-to-74-minute result. “3874 min” is not a usable statement of that gap. Section 4 correctly leaves the Chen/Dina tier-versus-shift question open, while section 6 treats the tier interpretation as settled for training and staffing.
