The normative core is strong: open-world versus closed-world is applied correctly, N3, T6, and E10 stay distinct from benign absences, N1 is not treated as a proved violation, and E8 is not waived merely because unapplied cash can exist. The protected relations are not turned into violations, mandatory gaps are not dismissed, and PM4Py output is not treated as the business verdict. Priorities in containment are also right.

The score is held down by concrete flaws. The semantic layer labels N1 `(98, 100)` as `candidate_missing_mandatory` and ignores the directly-follows caveat the prose itself treats as decisive. Write-off is a legitimate N5 exception in the table and in section 4, but the code marks it `unexpected_but_unregulated`. `semantic_label` also reports a mandatory edge as present when the count is 0 and the scope total is missing. Splatting `norm_otg` and `norm_etot` is inconsistent with passing `norm_ocdfg` as one model argument.

`Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` is called unexpected even though N2 expressly contemplates intermediate invoice events. E2 is stated as two missing Order links with high confidence, although only a weight gap of 1450 versus 1448 supports that; unlike E6 and E8, the narrative never confirms event cardinality. The same directly-follows danger is developed for N1 false positives and not for N3 false negatives: non-adjacent dispatch after cancellation would not appear in the observed edge. The Create Order counts 98 and 3 are never checked against the denominator of 100. Ranks 9–10 place conforming edges with a counting mismatch at Medium, above the 14 invoices with no successor, and “High data-integrity” is not a severity level.
