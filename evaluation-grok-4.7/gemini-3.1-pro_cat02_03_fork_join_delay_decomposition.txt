The case table, critical-branch labels, means, and the three headline counterfactual magnitudes that can be checked directly (A: +5 and +10 only on F01 and F11; B: 92.5 minutes of `join_ready` reduction; downstream sum 176) are arithmetically right, and the ranking correctly treats B’s queue-plus-service, not A’s 50-minute service, as what sets `join_ready`. That core is sound. The write-up is still short of the standard it aims at, because several required distinctions are asserted rather than measured, and one central claim is internally inconsistent.

The fork-join definitions are the right ones: `join_ready = max` of the three ends, sync is slack to that max, and downstream is `final_start - join_ready`. The double-count paragraph explains the right object only indirectly. What triples clock time is summing, over branches, `queue + service + sync`, since that sum equals `elapsed_fork_join` for every branch. Summing synchronization waits alone does not do that (on F01 the syncs total 27 minutes against a 50-minute fork-join stage). The algebraic identity is never written, and the case table never shows `elapsed_fork_join` or per-case sync, so the “no double counting” claim is not closed numerically. The worked lines for F04, F07, and F11 skip queue and service and only restate the max, the downstream gap, and total flow.

Section 3 ranks B, then A, then C for the right reason, with exact frequencies 8/2/2 and no exact ties. It does not supply the tail or the contribution-to-`join_ready` view the task asked for. B’s mean sojourn is about 56.7 minutes against A’s fixed 50, which is why B wins; that comparison is only narrative. “Very close runner-up in most others” overstates the margins: A’s slack is 5–7 minutes on F04, F08, and F10, and 10–25 minutes on the other non-critical cases. F10 (5 minutes) is the near-tie that should have been named.

The counterfactuals are the weakest part relative to the question that was actually posed. CF2’s 92.5 is the correct capped `join_ready` effect, including the cases where A binds before the full half-queue is realized (F05 saves 10 of 12.5; F10 saves 5 of 10; F02 and F06 land on a tie). The prose then mislabels the four zero-benefit cases as ones where another branch “becomes critical immediately.” Those four were already strictly critical. The partial-cap cases are the ones where the runner-up becomes critical, and they are not separated. CF1 never states the 15-minute aggregate. Both CF1 and CF2 are `join_ready` shifts, but the code prints them as “Total savings” and does not print the required per-case savings. Downstream masking is left at “may” / “often.” It is checkable. F11’s 10-minute earlier join does not move `final_start` on F1, because F09 occupies F1 until 11:50. Several other downstream gaps occur while the assigned F resource is already idle (F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F12), so those waits are not F1/F2 occupancy.

CF3’s 176 treats every downstream minute as flow time removed by “adding capacity.” That figure is the sum of independent gaps only if final capacity is unconstrained and `final_end` moves one-for-one. Under the observed F1 assignment it is already infeasible: if F09 starts at its 11:15 join, it runs until 11:35 and F11, ready at 11:30, still waits. Section 7 later says this gap may be a release policy rather than a queue, which contradicts Section 4’s certain 176-minute capacity saving and Section 5’s “strictly improves” / “guaranteeing” language. The ceteris-paribus assumption that each B queue can be halved without reordering other B work is also unlabeled. Interval overlap shows B is not a single server (three-way overlap around 10:10–10:30 and again around 10:50–11:15), so a capacity story needs at least that lower bound; Section 7 says branch capacity cannot be profiled at all.

The Python constructs the log, parses the day, computes the decomposition, breaks ties by listing every branch that meets `join_ready`, and calls `format_dataframe` plus `convert_log_to_time_intervals`, with a comment that directly-follows performance does not recover sync. It never prints per-case counterfactual savings, never passes `start_timestamp_key`, and the interval conversion is not shown to return service times. Assumptions are not labeled where they are used.
