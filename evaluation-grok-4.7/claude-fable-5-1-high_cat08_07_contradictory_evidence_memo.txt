6.5

The memo reaches a defensible staged decision and is strong on structure: facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated; the fitness/precision, median/tail, handover/resilience, visible-cost/rework, and segment conflicts are explained with real process-mining content; A–E and M1–M8 are tied to the decision.

It is not clean enough for a high score.

The final recommendation contradicts itself. It pauses any geographic expansion beyond the four pilot regions, then scales the simple-house path to all regions where classification exists. The 12-week gate cannot produce the evidence it requires: M1 is specified as 8–12 weeks after M6, and M4 needs 60 days of maturity after instrumentation, so the gate arrives before those results exist.

Several claims are stronger than the record. Simple houses did not improve on every metric; only duration, first-time-right, complaints, and overall SLA are segmented or noted. The merge applied to simple cases, while missing permit checks are reported for older apartments, so blaming the merge is an unsupported mechanism. Baseline data do not show that every medium-risk case was surveyed. Complaints are not shown to be “rising,” the rule is not shown to have been calibrated on simple houses, and national coordinator utilization is not shown to exceed 93%. “Cost-negative” is ambiguous, and the memo calls the EUR 23 visible-cost drop a scope artifact while later calling it genuine.

The survey-skip rule targeted medium-risk addresses, yet the action keeps skipping for low-risk houses and is inconsistent about business and rural cases. A pooled precision floor of 0.70 is a poor gate for a deliberately two-track process. Apartment installation-to-activation delay and the generalization drop are left largely unused. Thresholds such as 0.5 pp, 5% false negatives, 98%, and 80% are stated without a noise or cost basis.
