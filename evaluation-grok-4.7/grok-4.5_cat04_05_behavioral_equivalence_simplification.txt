6.0

The verdict table is right: the visible languages are incomparable, P1 rejects B, P2 equates them only after the stated collapse, and the P3 bound already separates them. A1, A2, and B1 are genuine separators, the loop reading in §2 is right, and the P2 image \(\{(n,\mathrm{rework}=(n\ge 2),\mathrm{check}=\top)\mid n\ge 1\}\) is correct. The rest of the formal apparatus has several hard errors.

**P1 is formalized incorrectly.** The regular expression \((A\mid C)^*A(A\mid C)^*C(\mathrm{Rework}\,(A\mid C)^*A(A\mid C)^*C)^*\) admits `Analyze, Analyze, Check` and `Analyze, Check, Analyze, Check` with no Rework. Those are exactly the patterns P1 forbids (one check covering two analyses, and a second analysis attempt with no Rework between them). The prose claim that this expression gives each analysis its own check and that Rework separates attempts is false of the expression itself. Approve placement is only an informal side constraint, and it imports A’s “after the parallel pair” order rather than the stated business rule. The parenthetical that P1 rejects any model able to emit two analyses under one check is a different relation from equality of the P1-legal subsets; both happen to make these two models inequivalent, but the definition does not state that.

**One required counterexample is not a trace of either model.** B2 (`Open, Analyze, Approve, Analyze, Check, Close`) has two analyses and no Rework. Under the binary-loop semantics used in §2, B’s body is `Analyze · (Approve xor τ)` and the only redo is `Rework` then the body again, so B rejects B2 just as A does. §4 states that two analysis attempts are two Analyses separated by Rework, then marks B2 as B-only. That leaves a single valid B-only witness (B1), short of the required pair. A1, A2, and B1 do establish that neither language contains the other, and the remark that hiding `τ` only deletes Approve is right.

**The structural counts are wrong, and they are presented as derived.** Both trees have 5 operators and 7 leaves, so 12 nodes, not 11. With root depth 1, A’s deepest leaf (Approve under `→/* /→ /X`) is at depth 5; B’s path `→ /+ /* /→ /X /Approve` is at depth 6. The trees are the same size and are not the same shape. The simplification deletes the inner parallel only: 4 operators + 7 leaves = 11 nodes, and Approve is still at depth 5, so depth does not fall from 5 to 4. “Strictly simpler by depth” is false against A. Duplicate-label count 0 and one silent leaf are right. Petri-net simplicity is left as an expectation; that is appropriate only because it was not computed.

**§6 misstates both auxiliary equivalences.** Language equality always yields the same directly-follows relation, so equal footprints are necessary for trace equivalence in general, and here they are still not sufficient because they ignore check cardinality. The binary relation that actually differs is direct succession `Approve Revision` then `Check Revision` (possible in B, impossible in A, where Rework sits between an approval and the next check). Check versus Rework runs in both orders in both models. Earth Mover’s Distance is not total variation: a small EMD can coexist with a large \(L_1\) gap when the ground distance treats the differing words as near, and the converse also occurs. The separate point that a faithful distribution puts positive mass on A1 and B1, hence gives EMD \(> 0\), is right, as is the warning that down-weighting those words can make EMD small while the supports stay different.

**The bounded search is the strongest artifact.** The trees are parenthesized correctly, `τ` is hidden, the state key keeps the marking and the visible word, silent steps do not advance the visible bound, cycles are cut by the seen-set, and length 12 and three analyses are treated as limits on complete words rather than as a random playout. Dead import of Woflan and no executed output are minor beside that. §7’s walker would have produced the true sizes; the prose numbers contradict that walker.

**The simplified tree is a reasonable P1-oriented subset of A, with an overstated preservation claim.** Sequential `Analyze` then `Check`, optional Approve, and redo only through Rework keeps one check per analysis, keeps Rework as the separator, and keeps approval count and placement. It drops A’s intra-iteration `Check`-before-`Analyze` words, which the §1 pattern treats as legal, so “exact (P1)” is stronger than the language relationship. P2 observations survive because every complete word still has \(n\ge 1\) analyses, \(n-1\) reworks, and at least one check. The three regression words and two negative words are accepted and rejected as claimed. Replacing A with this tree is justified for a compliance oracle; replacing B is a change of business purpose. The unbounded-loop warning is the right limit on P3.
