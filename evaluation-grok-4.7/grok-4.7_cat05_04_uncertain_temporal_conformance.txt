8.5

The primary interval arithmetic, timezone and DST-fold resolution, SLA and zeta classifications, and tolerance matrices are internally consistent and, under the stated semantics, correct. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The severity column breaks the rubric defined in the same section. “Low ≤ 2 min” and “Moderate ≤ 15 min” are violated by T-404 S1 (exit 123 s, labeled Low), T-409 S1 (exit 121.5 s, labeled Low), and T-409 Alert→Start at zeta 1 (lower exit 901.5 s, labeled Moderate). These are small threshold misses, but they are mislabels against the answer’s own scale.

The shared-clock check is too narrow. Independence is stated, and the HUB-only cancellation claim is true for HUB’s ±1 s. It is not the important case. T-405’s three timestamps come from one legacy clock with a ±60 s bound. A common offset cancels in the durations and collapses Alert→Start from about [720, 1080] s to about [840, 960] s. Zeta 1 then becomes a certain profile violation, zeta 2 a certain pass, and S1 at +2 minutes a certain pass. None of that is in the sensitivity section, even though the write-up implies correlation was checked and does not matter.

Smaller defects: “about 24 h 9 min” for a set whose lower end is 24 h 8 min 39 s; the midpoint discussion omits that the T-406 Alert→Start center sits inside the zeta-1 band while both tails exit, and that the T-409 profile center fails on the fast side; the DST order predicate tests separation of endpoints rather than existence of an ordered triple (saved here only by the one-hour fold gap and the hard assert). The T-409 certain pass on a ±12 min engineering estimate is disclosed, but it sits awkwardly against the problem’s “too weak → indeterminate” category.

The required structure, code path, T-404 inference, T-406 semantic fork, T-407 refusal to equate send with receipt, and governance rule are all sound. The deductions are for rubric errors and a material untested dependence, not for the main feasible-set results.
