5.5

The narrative correctly refuses to code rejections, withdrawals, and open cases as observed non-defaults, and the 48,000-row accounting, leakage inventory, and deployment caveats are real strengths. The design still fails the central requirement: one primary estimand, one matching estimator, and an as-of pipeline that preserves that semantics. Section 1, Section 3, Section 7, and the code implement different analyses.

## Target
The conditional estimand \(\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X_{\le T_{\mathrm{pred}}}, A=1)\) is a defensible primary choice, and the three alternatives are distinguished by question rather than by slogan. The target population is then described as all applications with matured outcomes, which is a different set from accounts that actually activate. Prediction time is left as \(k=3\), \(k=5\), or 24 hours, so the unit of prediction is not fixed. “Accepted and activated” is also wider than \(A=\) Account Activated; accepted-but-not-yet-activated cases remain in the open or withdrawn pools.

## Estimator contradiction
Section 3’s primary weight \(1/\mathbb{P}(A=1\mid X)\) identifies \(\mathbb{E}[Y(1)]\) under conditional exchangeability, consistency, and positivity. That is the counterfactual target Section 1 and Section 8 say is not identified. It is not \(\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X,A=1)\). For the conditional estimand, activated cases are the analysis population; the selection weight redefines the population toward all applicants and explodes where the prefix makes activation nearly impossible. Positivity, overlap, and the fact that underwriting uses post-prefix information are unstated. Cause-specific hazards for activation, rejection, withdrawal, and transfer already model selection; stacking \(1/\mathbb{P}(A=1\mid X)\) on top double-counts it. Calendar censoring at a known extraction date is a deterministic function of activation time. A single logistic factor \(\mathbb{P}(C=0\mid X,A=1,\mathrm{channel})\) does not estimate IPCW, and conditioning on channel does not correct partner feeds that the same section treats as MNAR. The pattern-mixture sensitivity is the part that matches that MNAR claim. Manski bounds apply to the counterfactual population risk and will be extremely wide; they are not a sensitivity analysis of \(Y\mid A=1\).

Withdrawal is given both a competing-event treatment and a right-censoring treatment in the same cohort row. In-flight applications are called ordinary right censoring of default. Their default clock has not started. Little’s test is the wrong diagnostic for a single outcome-missingness indicator.

## Cohort
The six buckets sum to 48,000 and the \(2025\textrm{-}10\textrm{-}13\) maturity date is the correct \(125\)-day offset from \(2026\textrm{-}02\textrm{-}15\). Linked transfers are described as appended child cases, while the table leaves all 2,900 transfers as competing and unlabeled, with no rule against double-counting a child identifier. Permanently missing partner feeds have no row, so an old unlabeled partner account can pass the calendar rule and fall out of both “labeled” and “censored.” Data-quality exclusions are declared and then omitted from the flow.

## Prefixes, features, leakage
A fixed \(k\) is not tied to a pre-decision landmark, so a short accept path can put Contract Signed or Account Activated inside a \(k=5\) prefix. Padding with `[CASE_TERMINATED]` and zero durations writes the terminal state and a false time into \(X\). The code does something else again: it deletes those activity names and then renumbers the first \(k\) survivors, which pulls later events into the prefix. Section 4’s truncation rule and that implementation disagree. Renaming and one-second dedup are reasonable, with an arbitrary window.

Control-flow, timing, rework, and static attributes are concrete. Cohort and calendar features are absent despite the July 2025 policy break. The DFG-fitness feature has no train-prefix-only discovery rule. Queue depth and historical approval rate need a true as-of cross-case snapshot; hire date is not in the schema.

The leakage table’s keep/repair/drop decisions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-prefix events, `documents_total`, `risk_score`, experience, and `label_available_at` are the right ones. `label_available_at` is still required for maturity even though it is forbidden as a feature. The code drops `source_system`, which is not shown to be post-outcome.

## Validation
Random splitting is rejected for sound reasons: policy drift and shared calendar shocks. The written split (train through April 2025, validation May–June, test from 1 July through 13 October) contradicts the diagram, which places validation after the policy change and prints 15 October before 13 October. Maturity is enforced on case start, not on activation time, so late activation inside the test window is still immature at extraction. No application that starts on or after the 15 October 2025 migration can finish 90 days plus a 35-day feed before 15 February 2026, so the “migration test” cannot score outcomes. The sizes \(\approx 32{,}000/5{,}000/7{,}500\) are not identified by the snapshot. Metrics fit a matured conditional binary label (PR-AUC, Brier, ECE, channel and era slices) only if evaluation is restricted to activated, label-mature cases. That restriction is not stated, and there is no as-of backtest using `label_available_at`.

## Code
`pandas.read_csv`, timestamp parsing, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, case metadata, and a start-time split are present. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is not. Censoring time is not built. The skeleton is far past the requested length.

Two implementation errors reverse the written design. `default_90d` aggregated with `max`, then `== 1` versus everything else, codes a calendar-mature activated account with a null label as `ACTIVATED_NON_DEFAULT`. Duplicate Account Activated rows survive the one-second dedup and duplicate the case on the merge. The leakage assert only checks that blacklisted columns are absent after they were dropped; it does not check prefix time, label nulls, or maturity. The closing note points survival modeling at `elapsed_time_sec`, which is prefix duration, not time from activation to default or censoring.

## Boundary
Section 8 draws the right use line for a conditional-on-activation score and names three genuine deployment shifts: tighter screening changes who activates, applicants can game early prefixes, and underwriter reallocation changes the timing features. That section is incompatible with Section 3 presenting the selection-weighted functional as the primary analysis. “Accurately predicts” overstates what the study has identified.

The answer looks complete and avoids the crude failure of training on the 21,600 rows and calling every omission a non-default. It does not hold one estimand, one censoring story, and one leakage-safe as-of sample at the same time, and the skeleton mislabels missing defaults.
