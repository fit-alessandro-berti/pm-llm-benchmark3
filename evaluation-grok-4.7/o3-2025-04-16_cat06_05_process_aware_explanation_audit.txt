5.5

The audit covers the required inventory and correctly quarantines the clearest leakages (closed-case duration, anywhere-appeal, full-trace variant, closure team, final decision, untimestamped risk score, last role, extraction-time prior-claim SQL, and the same-day snapshot). It also keeps queue length and elapsed time, and it asks for recomputation rather than treating the published probabilities as usable. Those are the right structural calls. The write-up is still not reliable on the distinctions this audit exists to make, and the revised narratives add unsupported facts.

The sequence-pattern rulings are the main process error. At this prediction point the prefix is only registration followed by the first coverage check. `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is a later evidence cycle, so calling it a low-severity prefix signal and telling the reader to keep it is wrong; a pre-`t0` occurrence would be non-conforming, and the reported negative contribution comes from future trace behavior. `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is likewise a subsequent event. Fraud may run in parallel with later evidence or assessment work, but that opening is not known at `t0`, so “valid prefix signal / no action” over-accepts it. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is correctly excluded as unavailable, yet it is not identified as a normatively legal second decision after Partial or Deny. That is a different defect from `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, which is order-impossible and, in Case Z, tied to a missing sequence index. Both of those events are also after `t0` under either ordering; the note treats the problem as a tie that created an illegal edge. `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is future and close to the settlement-time label, but it is a normal transition, not an illegal one. Severity is also uneven: complete-trace document-request counts and the closure team are rated high while equivalent future fields are critical.

Section 2 never operationalizes the required three-way status of an explained activity: enabled now, impossible, or only future-possible. “Not enabled yet” collapses the last two. The declarative checks omit document order, appeal-only-after-Partial-or-Deny, and legal parallelism of fraud. A keyword scan for “final/last/total/anywhere” is stated without its false-positive limit. The duplicate-decision rule is present, which is useful, but “at most two” is stronger than the stated process.

Section 3 mixes the standards it was asked to separate. Calendar week is both a fairness proxy and a drift proxy; the supplied issue is holiday and policy confounding, hence non-transportability. Initial reserve is placed under inflation drift without any lineage. The document-cycle pattern is listed as a defensible signal while “patterns containing future tasks” are listed as unusable. Queue length is both an ordinary predictive signal and a congestion signal. Employee experience joined as of extraction date is never mentioned. `total_case_duration_hours` is not called out as nearly the 10-day label itself. The claim that document requests can simply be counted before `t0` misses that, on a conforming prefix, that count is structurally empty and cannot explain case differences; the same is true of a prefix variant at this fixed milestone.

The case rewrites do the most damage to credibility. Case X says high complexity “leads to” longer assessments, invents extra broker communication steps, and asserts that similar cases breach “about 4 times as often,” a figure with no source. Case Y states that region R4 has lower repair-network availability and that the claim needs extended repair-partner coordination; the source only says region is correlated with coverage, language, channel, and socioeconomic factors. `prior_claim_count_before_t0` remains a credible driver despite the known `extraction_date` bug, so the rewrite does not stay within information available at `t0`. Initial reserve and broker channel are treated as established registration facts although no lineage was given. Old local directions are reused even though those attributions came from a model that also saw leaked fields; “recompute” correctly withholds the probability, but the narratives still conclude that breach risk is elevated. Case Z properly invalidates the impossible pattern, mutable score, and last role, then omits week 52 and the post-`t0` same-day document count that are actually in the local explanation.

Section 5 names every requested control and puts lineage ahead of retraining. It still frames calendar week as a disparate-impact test, and its stability check is generic drift rather than explanation change once future events and leaked columns are removed.
