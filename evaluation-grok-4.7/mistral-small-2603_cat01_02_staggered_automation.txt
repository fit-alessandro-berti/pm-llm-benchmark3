3.5

The memo follows the required outline and names the right problems—endogenous targeting, staggered timing, intensity versus availability, median versus tail, fraud-desk interference, and small-cluster fragility—but its quantitative core is unreliable, and its causal conclusions contradict its own caveats.

The adopter table does not compute the 3-month windows it announces. Alpha’s “+1.2h (48.3 vs 49.4)” has the wrong sign and the wrong contrast: April minus March is −1.1h, while the actual Jan–Mar versus Apr–Jun mean change is about +1.5h. Parentheticals switch months across metrics. Beta’s rework baseline of 15.1 does not exist; the footnote’s average of 7.5 and 10.3 is 8.9, not the stated 9.5, and March escalation is already 9.5 in the file. Gamma’s “−1.6h (38.4 vs 45.9)” is actually −7.5, and 105.6 is Beta’s July p90, not Gamma’s. The never-treated difference-in-differences figures, confidence intervals, placebo “insignificance,” and the −0.28 intensity coefficient are not reproducible from the data or from the code. The 15% cycle-time bound invokes path-specific durations the panel does not contain.

Identification is overclaimed. Marking intention-to-treat, intensity, dynamic, and complexity-interaction estimands as identifiable is incompatible with selection on poor cycle time, two adoption dates, two never-treated donors, and concurrent routing, staffing, fraud-desk, and case-mix shocks. Alpha and Beta were not an exogenous stagger. Arrival, FTE, and complexity controls are partly post-treatment. Baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6%, so a contrast “versus 0%” is misspecified. The policy estimand—the effect at Delta and Epsilon—is never defined.

Every branch’s escalation rises by 2 points in March, before any go-live. Rework and complexity jump again in May at never-treated branches. Alpha’s rework ends at 14.5 versus a 13.2 pre-period, so “automation reduces rework 1–3 points” is false. Epsilon’s June-to-August p90 fall is similar in size to Gamma’s, which the Gamma success story ignores. The tail row swaps beneficial and harmful signatures. Section 7 then assigns confidence 70–80 and “conditionally causal” labels, and allows a p90 worsening that sits outside those claims’ own sensitivity language.

The code does not implement the stated event study. `cumcount(ascending=False)` is calendar position from August, not time since adoption. `treated * post` is collinear with branch fixed effects. `np.log` on a datetime and `pd.compat.StringIO` make the script fail before estimation. Statsmodels can cluster on branch; the comment that it cannot is wrong, and HC1 is used instead. The intensity regression is logged and includes pre-adoption rows, so it cannot yield the linear −0.28 result reported above it. Assumptions are not stated as comments.

The rollout label is plausible, but the design treats both remaining controls, “stratifies” two branches, shortens an already long pre-period to two months, and sets guardrails near harms already observed. The claim ladder leads with causal statements the arithmetic does not support and never records the strongest true claims as observed facts.
