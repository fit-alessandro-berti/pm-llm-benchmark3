4.0

The write-up follows the required nine-section outline and names the right mechanisms (speed multipliers, handover, setup, one-shot preemption, parallel coordination, SLA targets). Those merits do not survive audit. Central numerical claims contradict each other, the capacity analysis is not reproducible and mis-locates the bottleneck, and the mandatory program does not implement the model it cites.

**Objectives are not well-formed.** Objective A adds a probability to a quantity in minutes with unspecified P1/P2/P3 weights, so the 0.6/0.4 mix has no defined scale. Objective B takes a maximum over mean delay, p95 delay, and utilization. Delay is tens or hundreds of minutes and utilization is at most 1, so the maximum is always the delay term and the utilization term never binds. The fairness and 0.90 constraints are missing operators, as are the later “≤ / ≥” claims, so eligibility and feasibility cannot be executed from the text. Decision variables (reservation fraction, parallel/sequential rule, preemption trigger, overflow threshold) are not separated from narrative description. Uniform draws are assumed in §1, while §2 switches to midpoints with no link between the two.

**Offered load is not auditable and the peak is mislabeled.** No block-by-skill table shows expected activity counts, base minutes, multipliers, or available minutes after shifts and breaks. The single figure, mechanical load peaking at 14:00–16:00 around 210 person-minutes, does not match the demand table. In that block, mechanical exposure is about 0.40 × 10 activities. Ordinary/complex midpoints give roughly 26 base minutes, so about 105 base person-minutes before multipliers. About 210 is the electrical load in that same block (E+ME+ES ≈ 0.62 × 10 × ~33 minutes). Mechanical base load is higher at 10:00–12:00 than at 14:00–16:00. The base bottleneck is therefore already electrical/software, and U2 does not “shift” it from mechanical to electrical.

The late-day roster is missed. From 16:00–18:00 only Deepa and Emil are on duty, Emil breaks 16:00–16:30, and only Deepa can do mechanical. From 18:00–20:00 only Emil remains, and he is the only software resource after 16:00. Even ordinary-range lower bounds for the 16:00–18:00 arrival block exceed those two people’s clock minutes, before complex tails, multipliers, handover, and setup. A reported base maximum utilization of 0.88–0.93 is inconsistent with that roster. Break placement, Farah ending at 12:00, overtime of 30 minutes, and the end-of-horizon fate of unfinished work are unused. U3’s “after 12:00” electrical bottleneck ignores that Chen’s absence is most severe at 08:00–10:00, when Emil and Deepa are not yet in and only slow electrical cover remains. U4’s 12–18% inflation is an unshown approximation (diagnostic means rise closer to the low-to-mid teens). U5’s “migrates to Validation after 12:00” is a precise causal claim with no queueing arithmetic.

**Policies are not implementable as specified.** Specialization’s cutoff is written as “multiplier 1.10” with no operator. Read as “at most 1.10,” Chen (software 1.25) and Farah (1.40) are excluded, so 08:00–12:00 has no software resource, and Emil (electrical 1.30) cannot cover 18:00–20:00. That hole is not identified. Priority order, preemption yes/no, and handover minimization are incomplete for S and H. Hybrid’s 30% reservation is not derived from P1’s roughly 5–18% arrival share, and “30% of shift capacity” is not defined as time, tokens, or expected service. Section 6 then changes reservation to a 20% floor and switches parallelization to a 12-minute critical-path test, while §3 uses “both branches free within 10 minutes.”

**The scenario table does not support the recommendation.** S and P appear only for base and U1. U2 and U4 are absent. U3 and U5 show H only, so dominance cannot be checked. Metric definitions are missing: “mean_delay” is not identified as sojourn, queue wait, or wait beyond service. Base S reports mean delay about 48–55 and p95 about 95–110 together with P1 attainment 0.87–0.91. Unavoidable processing for an average mix is already on the order of 65 minutes before multipliers and handovers, and a p95 elapsed time under 120 minutes cannot coexist with more than about 10% of prioritized P1 cases missing a 120-minute clock. Ranges are presented as results. Section 8 shows they are not produced by the program.

Section 5 states that H keeps P1 attainment at or above 0.91 in all five uncertainty scenarios, holds maximum utilization to 0.94, and holds the fairness gap to 0.15. The same document’s table shows H at 0.88–0.92 on U3, 0.82–0.87 and utilization 0.96 with fairness 0.18 on U5, and no U2/U4 evidence. The fallback fires toward full pooling when the P1 arrival rate exceeds 1.5× base, which is exactly the stress under which the table shows pooling has worse P1 attainment and “preemption thrashing.” Robustness is never defined as a cross-scenario functional (worst-case SLA, regret, or constrained feasibility). It is inferred from the base and U1 rows.

**Required side effects and mining design are incomplete.** Gaming of planned service estimates is absent, even though the parallelization rule depends on an estimate. Validation as a new bottleneck is a failure mode in the table and is not analyzed. The pilot falls back to specialization in any block whose P1 attainment drops below 0.90, which is the weaker P1 policy in the author’s own U1 row. A one-shift, 10-day pilot misses the 16:00–20:00 staffing cliff. The backtest does not say how historical assignments would identify counterfactual paths.

Event attributes are listed. `discover_bottlenecks` is not a real PM4Py operation. Case-level `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is not distinguished from stage queue time. Drift is reduced to grouped service times, with no time-split or drift procedure. Eligible set, policy version, and SLA clock are not mapped to columns an analysis can actually group on.

**The Python block fails the reproducibility checklist.** Dict literals containing a bare `...` are syntax errors, so the script does not run. Service, skill, shift, demand, and scenario tables are not encoded. `simulate_policy` ignores policy and scenario, emits twelve Intake events all on Ada, and returns a hardcoded metric dict. Handover, setup, parallel/sequential choice, and the 10% restart exist only in comments. The closing prose claims those penalties are applied and that a 300-line implementation is represented by this scaffold. `get_service_time` is never called. U2 and U4 are never looped. `seeds=3` is unused. The log cannot show a handover network or stage-level delay. A scaffold was allowed only if omitted mechanisms were identified; this one asserts that the omitted mechanisms are present.

The hybrid-versus-pooling tension is the right question, and the section list matches the requested format. The grade stays at 4.0 because the load arithmetic, the comparison table, the robustness claim, and the required program are mutually inconsistent, and the evening capacity collapse that drives any serious recommendation is never calculated.
