The write-up is a serious capacity-and-policy analysis, and most of the auditable load math is right. It still has enough internal contradictions, undisclosed modeling choices, and policy-to-code gaps that it is not close to flawless.

The service means, daily task counts, and block loads check out (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74; 42 cases; 2,720.6 nominal minutes; 2,490 regular minutes). U1’s 53.2 arrivals and about 3,442 minutes, U2’s near-zero net load change after renormalization (Electrical about 861, Mechanical about 423), U4’s roughly +242 diagnostic minutes, the 16:00–18:00 fluid ratio of 1.93, and the pre-noon Software figure of 154 minutes are coherent. The specialization-versus-pooling tension, shift cuts after 16:00, Validation as a downstream bottleneck, and the conclusion that routing cannot create the missing capacity are all well supported. Sections 6, 7, and 9 are specific, and the case-level versus stage-level PM4Py distinction is correct.

The comparison and recommendation are weaker than that foundation. The U3/U5 “minimum max utilization” figures do not follow the same work/capacity arithmetic used elsewhere: 2,721/2,040 is 133.4%, not 136.4%, and 3,442/2,040 is 168.7%, not 172.6%. Section 5 then uses 2,040 and 3,442 directly, so those scenario-table percentages are unexplained. The daily 109.3% figure is a multiplier-free load ratio, not the skill-constrained min-max defined earlier. The risk and robust objectives are never evaluated; λ and γ are undefined, the positive-part notation is corrupted, and hybrid-over-pool is asserted over SLA bands that overlap in every scenario.

Policy H as specified is not the policy simulated. The code has no hard P1 reserve, no Validation guard, no protected-resource or Validation-queue parallelization test, no strict-lane sequential fallback, and a different overflow rule. Dispatch uses the true sampled service times, which favors the score-based policies and is not disclosed. Unfinished cases are inserted into means and p90s at the horizon, so those tail metrics are biased low; U3/U5 are further helped because Chen returns on the clearance day. Setup is described as a skill-family change, while Electrical and Software are grouped as one family in the transfer logic but charged setup as different activities in the code. The six-arrivals-per-hour stress trigger would fire often under the base Poisson rates. Two replications and a Wilson interval on dependent same-day cases overstate how much the scaffold identifies the ranking.
