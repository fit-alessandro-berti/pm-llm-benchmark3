6.5

The answer is complete, numerically careful, and explicit about counterfactuals, but it is not nearly flawless. Several attributions that organize the argument conflict with the same timestamps the answer itself calculates.

The central flaw is the featured cascade from `FIX-A` through `J4` to `S1`. Motif 3, Narrative A, and intervention I2 treat `J3`’s fixture hold as a path that delays `J4`’s pack and therefore holds `J1` until 19:25. The timestamps break that critical path. `J4` is already batch-ready at 13:10, twenty minutes before `J1` is packed at 13:30, and `B2` still cannot start until `RACK-7` clears at 14:35 and `J5` is ready at 14:40. Morning CNC delay is absorbed as slack before the oven. Advancing `J4`’s 11:45 process start does not move `B2`’s 14:50 start or `J4`’s 19:10 pack. Calling this “speculation” does not repair it: the feasibility section was supposed to test exactly this, and the speculation is inconsistent with the slack the answer already computed in C6. I2’s claimed spillover onto `S1` has the same defect.

C3 overstates the fixture effect. The lead counterfactual is a 1 h 40 min delay from a 10:05 start to 11:45. The only documented `CNC-2` idle is 10:05–10:47 (42 min). Occupancy from 10:47 to 11:45 is unknown, yet that 58 min is folded into the fixture number. The later remark that the machine is non-binding for “at least 42 min” is the supportable claim; the 1 h 40 min figure is not.

I1 then contradicts C6. C6 shows that, with the rack held until 14:35, moving only the peer-job constraint saves `J4` about 15 min. I1 still says a smaller-batch or start-when-ready policy lets `B2` start near 13:10 and removes a substantial part of the 1 h 40 min. That start time also requires a free rack. The “extra rack” option is named in the heading and then dropped from the benefit arithmetic.

The top rank is under-tested against oven availability. The 3 h 55 min and 3 h 40 min `B1` waits are synchronization waits only if `OVEN-1` could have started earlier. The supplied oven idle interval is 12:55–14:50, after `B1`. Nothing shows the oven was free from 08:10 to 12:05. The machine summary’s longest `OVEN-1` gap is 73 min, which is difficult to reconcile with a four-hour morning idle. The answer uses 115 min versus 73 min only to say the machine view under-reports slack, and uses that apparent slack to push machine capacity to rank 6. The same discrepancy also cuts against a multi-hour oven wait for `J3`. Section 8’s falsifiers include a noon schedule slot, an all-morning rack hold, and shifts, and omit a direct “oven busy with other work until about 12:00” observation.

Median DFG statistics are misread twice. `Pack → Dispatch` median 1.1 h and `Stage → Machine` median 1.1 h are order statistics. They do not “average away” `J1`’s 5 h 55 min, and they are not mixtures of `J1`/`J2` and `S2`/`S3`. `S2` and `S3` are also misused as near-zero job waits. The 25 and 35 min figures are last-pack-to-dispatch only. Partner wait for the earlier job in those shipments is unknown. On `S1` itself, last-pack-to-dispatch is 19:25 − 19:10 = 15 min, shorter than `S2`/`S3`. C4’s arithmetic (5 h 55 min; excess about 5 h 25–5 h 30 min versus a 25–35 min handling allowance) is correct for a ship-the-early-job counterfactual, but the prose calls that allowance “the same last-job slack.”

Smaller inaccuracies accumulate. “No job exceeds 1.8 SD of the mean” is not the stated rule, which is mean + 1.8 SD. “Possible only if” the other jobs are equally stretched is stronger than that rule supports. “`J3` holds `FIX-A` on `CNC-2`” is not in the evidence; `CNC-2` is idle during part of the hold. The `B2` edge list asserts a `Batch Cure` event with the same status as `B1`’s explicitly multi-object 12:05 event. Rework is floated as possibly lot-driven with no supporting relation.

Section 7 names every required function and correctly refuses to treat machine gaps as waits. The performance comparison would not run: `discover_performance_dfg` returns a performance DFG plus start and end activities, not `(freq_dfg, perf_dfg)`. The `try/except` would skip every flattening. Metric A and metric B are printed, not compared.

The length is far above the 1,800–2,800 word aim, on the order of twice the cap. The observability cells also do not cover each flattening for each mechanism.
