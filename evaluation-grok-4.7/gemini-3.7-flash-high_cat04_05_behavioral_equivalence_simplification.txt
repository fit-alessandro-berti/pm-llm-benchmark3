6.4

The verdicts are right, and the bounded language sizes are right, but the write-up is not reliable as a formal or executable argument. P1 is defined incorrectly, the required net exploration does not run, and several reported measurements are false.

The comparison of the two languages is the strongest part. Visible-trace equivalence after hiding `tau`, the P3 bound (length ≤ 12 and at most three `Analyze Revision` events), and the P2 map to `(Open, k, rework?, check?, Close)` are stated precisely enough to use. Duplicate labels are treated as positional symbols in a word, which is the right observation model. Loop scope, exactly-k checks versus exactly one check, approval after the local join versus approval beside an external check, and the AND-join before `Close Case` are correct. Neither language contains the other. The five witnesses are real complete traces: two checks are possible only in A; approval before the single check, rework before any check, and a second analysis with no second check are possible only in B. Optional approval does not repair k ≥ 2, because A still emits k checks. Under P3 the sets really do have sizes 76 and 85 with intersection 4 (the four one-iteration traces in which the check is not after approval). P2’s images really are `{(Open, k, k>1, true, Close) | k ≥ 1}` for both models, so “equivalent only under stated abstraction” is the right cell. The closing warning correctly treats a finite bound as a falsifier, not a proof.

Those results are undercut by the definitions and the numbers that surround them.

P1 is not an observation boundary. Equality of the traces that already satisfy the four invariants ignores illegal traces, which is the opposite of a compliance view: extra unsound behavior would not count as a difference. The invariants are also not the stated business rules. Global `|Check| = |Analyze|` does not enforce “this analysis’s own check before the next rework or close,” and “check before approve” is not one of the P1 bullets. Calling that list temporal logic overstates it. The later verdict is still “not equivalent,” but it is justified by counterexamples, not by the definition in §1. The P2 guard that drops any trace with no check also makes the check bit constantly true, so the abstraction cannot even represent the boolean it claims to record. That does not change this pair, because both models always check at least once.

The §5 procedure is not executable and does not do what it says. `pm4py` markings are counters and are unhashable, so `(marking, visible_trace)` cannot be stored in a set; exploration dies on the first state. Soundness is never checked, despite the required `check_soundness` step; the script only prints place and transition counts and asserts soundness in a comment. Unique traces are sampled, not printed. The 76/85/4 figures in §3 are correct, but they are not results of this script.

§6 and §7 present measurements that do not follow from the models. `discover_footprints` can separate these trees, and a pairwise footprint or a small Earth Mover’s Distance would still not prove equal languages; that discussion is sound. The value `~0.1387` is not derived. The snippet never installs the declared 0.5/0.5 XOR and loop probabilities, and it never states a finite unrolling, so the printed distance is an invented result. Tree size is right: 12 nodes each, depths 5 and 6, six visible leaves, one `tau`, no duplicate labels. The Petri-net columns are not. Under the standard process-tree encoding (sequence places, AND split/join, silent loop exit), A and B have the same operator multiset, hence the same size: 10 places, 10 transitions, 22 arcs, not 9/9/20 versus 7/7/16. The simplicity scores 0.818 and 0.778 are therefore not `simplicity_petri_net` values. The same counting error reappears for the simplification: removing the parallel operator yields 11 tree nodes, depth 5, and a net with 7 places, 8 transitions, and 16 arcs, not 6/7/14 and not simplicity 0.867.

The simplified tree is a coherent normative refinement of A for a stricter audit order: `Analyze`, then `Check`, then optional `Approve`, once per iteration. R1–R3 are accepted and N1–N3 are rejected. It is smaller than both originals. It does not preserve A under the paper’s own P1 definition: `⟨Open, Check, Analyze, Close⟩` meets those four invariants and is in A, but the serialized tree rejects it. §8 marks that change as strengthened, then §9 still says the new model preserves P1 exactly and should replace A. It should replace B only as a compliance redesign. It should not be described as an equivalent simplification of A, and P2 survival is only survival of the dashboard image. `check_soundness(...)[0]` is also an unjustified return-type assumption.

The operational conclusion — A for compliance, either model only after the P2 projection, neither as a stand-in for the other on bounded traces — is the right one. The grade stops there because a reader cannot trust the equivalence definition, the claimed exhaustive run, or the complexity and stochastic figures.
