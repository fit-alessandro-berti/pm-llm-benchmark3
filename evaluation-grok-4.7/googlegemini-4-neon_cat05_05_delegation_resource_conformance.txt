7.4

The case narrative is strong and mostly faithful to the event-time rules, but it is not nearly flawless. Several classifications are more certain than the evidence, and the checking code silently changes the delegation record.

The best work is the event-time reading itself. Alias normalization correctly turns CHG-508 into an RR1 breach. CHG-502 and CHG-510 are correctly unauthorized on drafter/approver separation, with CHG-510 also void under the D-IC-03 subdelegation ban. CHG-507 correctly refuses to treat Eva’s later Quality Engineer master as QM authority on 9 August. CHG-503 keeps `svc_qms` unattributed and separates a possible Diego self-verification from a proven one. CHG-504 stays indeterminate. CHG-505’s Friday retrospective is correctly outside two business days, and both cooling-system retrospectives are correctly outside D-QM-01’s production-equipment scope. RR6 is applied as an indicator, with benign and adverse explanations and discriminating evidence. The seven required sections and the case-table columns are present.

CHG-506 is the main reasoning failure. D-QM-01 still covers 13 August, production equipment, and Asha’s recorded absence. Sick leave does not, by the stated rules, void a delegate’s own act. Ben’s denial and the office IP make the actor ambiguous. That is the same kind of ambiguity treated as indeterminate for Asha in CHG-504, but here the primary label is UNAUTHORIZED, while the same row still offers a benign account in which Ben approved it himself. If that account is true, the approval is a valid substitution. The label decides the fact the analysis says is missing.

CHG-509 is too clean. D-QM-02 requires the principal to be absent, and Asha’s recorded leave ended on 14 August. A Saturday delegation is suggestive, not formal unavailability. VALID_SUBSTITUTION should have been indeterminate on that condition. The strong FIDO attribution and the separate approver/verifier weakness are handled well.

CHG-501 is directionally right, but the secondary label DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM misnames a migration-timing conflict. Identity is clear. Validity still depends on an untested PDF.

The code does not fully implement the narrative. It invents `2026-08-14T12:00:00Z` for D-QM-02 and `2026-08-11T11:30:00Z` for D-IC-03. It widens D-IC-03 from CI-44 to every `cooling_system` case, so Farid’s CHG-505 approval passes in code without the incident constraint. `signed_ts` is stored and never compared with the event; the database-lag check only appends a flag and still returns a valid delegation. Weekend absence is downgraded to a warning that still returns `DELEGATED_AUTHORITY_VALID_CONDITIONAL`. The four-eyes wrapper is a reasonable hedge around PM4Py’s polarity, and service-account handling is explicit, but those gaps mean the script can bless approvals the prose correctly doubts.

Smaller defects: the executive “4 cases” of identity failure does not match the cases then listed; CHG-508 is counted as both a confirmed authority failure and a data failure. CHG-502 is Medium while the alias-masked LOW-risk breach in CHG-508 is High. The retrospective activity is never tested against the narrower wording “QM approval,” though both retrospectives already fail on scope or time. “48 business-day hours” muddles clock hours and business days, even though 14 August still falls outside either reading.
