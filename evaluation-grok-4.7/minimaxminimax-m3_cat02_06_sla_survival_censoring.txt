The write-up is a real survival/process analysis: time origin at `Submit`, a 72-hour horizon, competing terminal states, transfer treated under more than one assumption, immortal time called out for `Escalate`/`Rework`, and Aalen–Johansen preferred to a single-event Kaplan–Meier. That framing is the right one. It is not nearly flawless. Several claims contradict the table or standard survival definitions.

The open-case count is wrong in the bias section. The text says six active cases, then names nine (S09, S10, S11, S15, S24, S25, S26, S29, S31). Nine is correct (32 − 23 closed). The same paragraph’s “23 closed cases” only matches the list, not the word “six.” `rework_count > 0` is not limited to S07 and S27; S13 is also 1 and its prefix contains `Rework`. Cohort B does not all contain `AutoCheck`: S20, S23, S26, and S30 do not, so “all Cohort B” and “near-perfect collinearity” with cohort are false. `autocheck_first` is strongly associated with B (12/17 vs 0/15), which is the claim that should have been made.

The sensitivity number “CIF ≈ 0.19 by 24 h” for pooled withdrawal/rejection cannot be right. Only S05 (9 h), S23 (11 h), and S12 (15 h) are those causes by 24 h, so that CIF is at most 3/32 ≈ 0.09. Calling rejection uniformly faster than withdrawal by citing S05/S23 against S30 ignores S12 at 15 h and S14 at 63 h.

Methodologically, cases observed still open after 72 h (S09, S11, S25, S26) were under observation from time 0. They belong in the risk set through 72 h. Delayed entry / left truncation is the wrong fix and would drop the clearest tail evidence. The encoding table’s instruction to set late resolutions (S03 at 76 h, S13 at 110 h, S20 at 74 h, S28 at 88 h) to event 0 for “resolved by 72” is not a survival encoding; those are resolution events at t > 72, and P(T ≤ 72) is read from the curve. The code later marks every resolution as event 1, so the table and the program disagree.

The competing-risk bias is misstated as “overestimate the median time to resolution.” Coding withdrawal/rejection as censoring makes 1 − KM larger than the cumulative incidence of resolution; that is the bias that matters. The IPCW sketch “weight = 1/(t)” is not an inverse-probability-of-censoring weight.

Estimand C in code is not the probability of no terminal disposition by 72 h. A binary flag that is 1 only for `open` (and optionally `transferred`) with age ≥ 72 marks late resolutions as non-events even though they were still active at 72 h, and marks opens censored before 72 h as non-events even though their status at 72 h is unknown. Comparing that mean with 1 − Σ CIF treats two different quantities as one check. The third transfer scenario (`ipcw_proxy`) is coded identically to `competing`, so the program never actually runs three treatments. S03 is also said to be “still in Review” at 72 h; only the resolution time is known, not which prefix step was current.

What holds up is the primary design: multi-state CIFs, transfer as competing versus censored versus an external-outcome weight, landmarking so post-`Submit` activities are not baseline covariates, cohort confounding from non-random incomplete rollout, and the missing-data list (timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff dates, assignment, workload). Those parts are the reason this stays above a failing analysis rather than anywhere near a 9.
