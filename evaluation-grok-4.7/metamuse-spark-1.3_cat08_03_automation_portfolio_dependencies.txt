The answer covers every required section, ranks all twelve candidates on process leverage rather than volume, and uses the waits, loops, resource pools, and control findings with real specificity. The case against leading with registration is grounded in afternoon slack, downstream overload, and misrouting. Those strengths are undercut by contradictions in the portfolio itself.

The year-one plan does not obey the four-module cap it claims to meet. The recommended set is A5, A6, A4, and A9, but the rollout also stands up a classification recommender, collateral triage, an e-sign pilot, and either the remediation workbench or the KYC pre-classifier. Section 6 puts collateral routing live in months 7–9; section 7 treats that same module as optional or a year-two item. A reader cannot tell which program would actually be funded.

The dependency map says classification errors flow into statement normalization and contract generation, and phase 0 only shadows the classifier. Nothing later promotes it, yet contract generation still goes live. Contract logic is also aimed at a committee decision that many contracts never have: packaging and decision cover about 3,900 cases, while contract preparation covers 7,600, and the listed predecessors do not close that gap. Four-eyes is enforced at contract generation even though the violation occurs when the same person completes credit analysis and committee packaging.

One causal sentence runs the process backward: faster contracts are said to hit the weekly committee, which sits upstream of contracting. Labor “savings” are written as full elimination of mean service time (75 minutes times 5,200; 15 minutes times 3,300) despite residual review, which is the unsupported precision the format asked to avoid. Gates such as “agreed threshold” and “pass rate met” do not define a stop. The 58-hour external valuation, the longest wait and the main equipment-finance synchronization point, is dropped from the recommended set and then quietly restored, so the trade against that delay is never scored in the process-effects section.
