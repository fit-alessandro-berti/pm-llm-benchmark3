5.5

The incubation clock is wrong by a full day, and that error is repeated often enough to move the smooth-path mechanism, the staffing driver, and the later scenario ranges. Primary readiness is 46–54 h after Friday 14:00, which is Sunday 20 September 12:00–20:00. The answer stretches that 8-hour window to “Sunday 12:00 through Monday 20:00,” then treats a primary result ready after Monday 18:00, with readout on Tuesday, as a real case. That case does not exist. Every primary incubation is technically ready on Sunday.

That mistake collides with an internal contradiction. The ledger correctly says weekend on-call barely changes an uncomplicated release because QA is closed Sunday. Section 5 then ranks microbiology readiness and readout staffing first and says the on-call decision can move the usable result by many elapsed hours. On the true clock, no on-call means readout from Monday 08:00 for 0.5–1.5 h, so a usable microbiology result around Monday 08:30–09:30. On-call can produce a Sunday result, but it cannot produce a Sunday terminal event. The delayed-readout scenario is therefore not materially different from the smooth path, and its Tuesday–Wednesday tail is largely invented.

The same gap shows up in the queue arithmetic the forecast needs. Analyst release times (about 15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30) are right, and four samples ahead of a high-but-not-critical batch is the right queue. The answer never checks those facts against the close. Only about 2.5 h remain; minimum chemistry service is 1.5 h and the mode is 2.5 h, so Friday completion is rare, the second analyst cannot finish a new sample, and under FIFO B-417 is unlikely even to start. A defensible join is often chemistry on Monday afternoon, with microbiology as a Monday-morning floor, not a microbiology clock running to Monday night. “Usable chemistry Monday 08:30–18:00” is stated as a reasonable window without a FIFO-versus-priority start time. The QA wait of roughly 0–8 working hours is likewise uncalculated: five batches, 4.5 h of Friday capacity, 4 h on Saturday, 0.35 arrivals per open hour, and two Monday reviewers never become a residual-work estimate.

The headline bands are only partly rescued by width. Earliest Monday about 09:30–13:30 is a fair extreme. The overall P50 (Monday 16:30–Tuesday 12:30) sits on top of the smooth-path center even though that path is given only a 35–55% band and the rerun, query, and contamination bands are later. At the low end of the smooth-path probability, the mixture median should fall in a mild exception path. The P80 and severe-tail intervals are so broad that they are hard to falsify; they are not tied back to the scenario weights. Shortcuts are used throughout and not stated.

The frame is otherwise the right one: join as a maximum, weekend pause from Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00 (62 h), overlapping probabilities, temperature excursion as a common cause, no invented rejection rate, and release versus rejection kept distinct. Those points keep the answer above a mean-adding, weekend-blind forecast. They do not repair a fourfold readiness window or an unquantified queue on the branch that actually competes with microbiology.
