7.4

The assessment is disciplined and largely well-structured, but it is not nearly flawless. A selective evidence rule and two classification choices are internally inconsistent, and those flaws sit on the cases where the label actually turns.

The sharpest contradiction is P-503. Section 1 says missing entries do not prove that an act did not occur, and the same row later repeats that absence of a separate event does not prove there was no timely clearance or qualified approval. The primary finding nonetheless treats the joint authorization as the sole final business approval and rates the A1 breach high-confidence. “Sole” is itself an inference from silence. If silence cannot prove that no other approval exists, A1 is not established and the primary classification should be INDETERMINATE under the paper’s own method. If the case file is complete enough to treat unlisted approvals as absent, then P-507 and P-508 should not remain INDETERMINATE merely because no Finance event is listed. The paper uses both standards.

P-501 has the mirror-image problem. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is a positive whole-case finding, but it depends on provisionally inferring from silence that the unnamed Plant approver was neither ENG-1 nor SHIFT-1. The section 4 preamble even says a justified exception should be a secondary label where the rest of the purchase is not yet shown to be compliant, then uses it as the primary label anyway. B4 does justify the late PO; that does not justify closing A1.

P-505 overclaims in a related way. Finance_Approve is the local B2 event, and B2 states no pre-commitment deadline, so a 19:00 finance approval can satisfy B2 without proving that the A2 Finance Controller approval occurred then, or at all. Leading the confidence cell with “High if…” does not make the unconditional NONCOMPLIANT match the burden rule used for P-507 and P-508.

Smaller defects add up. Unproven items are repeatedly placed under “Apparent deviations” (Plant approval, budget-owner approval, B6), which blurs the distinction section 6 draws carefully. B6 is defined as attaching to the local emergency procedure, then treated as a gap in ECD-pathway cases without showing that procedure was used. P-506’s NONCOMPLIANT result depends on recovery chemicals falling inside “maintenance and production-support,” which is asserted more firmly than the cafeteria scope question and is not tested in section 5. D1 is narrowed to sterilization-lot purchases when the email is written for the ECD-2 window; the narrowing is labeled a working reading but not really defended. The A1 pseudo-logic is broader than the prose “only authorizing decision” condition, and P-508 still treats the Incident Command pair as supporting A1 even though Compliance and Legal decisions also exist.

The hierarchy, timing, and evidence work are otherwise strong: GPIP is a floor rather than an automatic trump; ECD-2 is confined to local timing and supplier-register issues; P-501 is kept outside the directive window; P-505’s email, ERP stamp, and fast clock are not collapsed into one commitment time; P-507 prefers the signed PDF to the late ERP entry; late approval is not treated as a cure; new supplier is not equated with unapproved supplier; thresholds are applied on the correct side of EUR 50,000 and EUR 100,000; and the outcomes are not homogenized. Sections 5 through 7 are substantive. Those strengths keep the paper well above mid-scale, but the contradictory burden of proof is too central for a higher score.
