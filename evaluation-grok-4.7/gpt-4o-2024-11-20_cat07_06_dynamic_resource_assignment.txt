The write-up has the required sections and a usable constraint inventory, but it is not a deployable or auditable policy. Core formulas are not executable, several queue decisions break the stated rules, training is claimed where the skill matrix does not support it, and the baseline comparison asserts superiority without working either baseline.

**Policy is not operational.** Urgency multiplies by `case_priority`, which is a label, not a number, and then multiplies by an urgency factor again. Skill score uses `resource_skill - required_skill`, but required skill is an activity name. Handover cost is subtracted as if high/medium/low were numbers. Those scores are never combined into a decision rule. The tie-break (open cases, skill, training priority, overtime) ignores continuity, supervision, hours remaining, and the 20% training quota. Workload is not updated as the batch is filled. Hard rules for security, approval, overtime, and high-complexity handovers are only loosely echoed.

**The queue table is frequently wrong or unjustified.**
- C001 goes to R7 with trade-off “None.” Complexity 0.88, handover cost high, and SLA of 3h sit against the stated 2.7h median wait from an owner change. R3 has security skill 1, so supervision by R7 or R8 is feasible and continuity should be preferred. R8 is also the lighter specialist (7 open cases, low overtime, 5.5h versus R7’s 10, medium, 4.5h).
- C005 is marked as training. R5 is already negotiation skill 3 and the owner; R5’s training priority is security. This is a high-priority case.
- C006 is a one-case “batch” given to R9, who is high overtime, on a normal case with 36h left. R10 is a quality specialist with low overtime. C012 was the other batch-eligible quality case and was split away.
- C008 is called supervised training. R8 is security skill 3; the owner, R7, is also skill 3. Handover cost is low, so a load shift is arguable, but the reason given is not.
- C010 treats R2 as a trainee preparing approval. R2 is approval skill 2 and may finalize. R2’s training priority is quality, and overtime risk is high on a normal, 20h case.
- C012 calls R10 a trainee. R10 is quality skill 3; the training priority is security. The owner match itself is reasonable.
- C014 gives more work to R4 (14 open cases, 3.0h, high overtime) on a normal rework case. Skill-3, lower-overtime alternatives exist. The overtime rule is not applied.
- C015 says only “Supervised by R8.” No performer is named. The note’s candidates, Eva and Jonas, have security skill 0 and are ineligible even under supervision. Eligible skill 1–2 options are R3, R4, and R9, and none of them has security as a documented training priority.
- C003’s owner, R4, is already diagnosis skill 3. Moving the case creates a third reassignment, which the evidence ties to 1.8x rework. Overtime can justify the move; “needs a specialist” cannot.
- C009 to R1 is the only clear documented training match (diagnosis skill 1, normal priority, 22h). Skill 1 still needs a named supervisor. Nine normal/low cases need about two such assignments to meet 20%. The table marks six as training; only C009 qualifies, so the quota is missed while being reported as met.
- R1 receives three cases and R6 two urgent ones, with no hours-versus-complexity check. C002’s “no batching” trade-off does not apply to approval. Several risk notes describe a handover that the chosen assignment does not create.

**Comparison overclaims.** Specialist-only is scored “High” on continuity, but that rule sends work to the top skill, not the owner. First-available and specialist-only are not actually run on C001–C016. The proposed policy is then marked best or near-best on throughput, SLA, error, fairness, development, continuity, and resilience. That is the single-optimum claim the question forbids. The 1.8x rework, 1.4x appeal, 2.7h handover, and 3.3h weak-skill figures are not used.

**Worker text and audit measures are too thin to use.** The worker paragraph is generic and would mislabel specialist work as training. It does not state the security, approval, overtime, four-case, or continuity rules a worker could check. The ten indicators are names only: no threshold, no log-column mapping, no distinction between `reassignment_count` and a handover network, and no definition of mismatch or policy deviation.

**The PM4Py sketch would not audit this policy.** `filter_case_performance` filters case duration via min/max performance; it does not take `attribute="sla_deadline"`, and a value of 3 is not a 3-hour SLA test. `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with the attribute name and value list in the wrong positions. `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as handover statistics. Role discovery, activity-based resource similarity, batches, waiting-time insertion, and variants are absent, as is any estimate of weights from historical rework or handover cost. `org:resource` is never bound for the SNA calls.
