8.5

The answer is a strong, evidence-disciplined redesign, but it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative interpretations are left unresolved, and one control rule is miscalibrated against the answer’s own baseline.

The weekly normalization is correct: 2,300 to 3,300 orders (+43%), with Routine essentially flat (−1%) and Urgent/Complex-panel roughly doubling (+131% and +121%). That correctly stops a raw 18,400-versus-9,900 comparison. Cited edges, queues, and transition rates check out, including pre-analytical exception shares (~7.0% to ~12.3%), assay-repeat exit shares (~6.7% to ~14.8%), medical releases (~521 to ~1,330 per week, 2.55×), specialist holds (~36 to ~203 per week, 5.6×), and the 16,440 versus 18,400 end-event gap. Refusing to treat that gap as backlog, refusing to add medians across edges, separating order-level rework from assay-repeat transitions, and not treating the M2/night-shift cluster as proven causation are all sound. Required sections, scenario types, downstream overload, non-simultaneous priority, and leading-versus-lagging indicators are present. Rejecting blanket I1–I3 and I9 is well supported.

The main analytical miss is that the headline multipliers are not checked against utilization or mix. Medical completions rise 2.55× while medical utilization rises only from 58% to 93% (~1.60×). Specialist entries rise 5.6× while utilization rises from 43% to 87% (~2.0×), even as hold-to-medical time lengthens from 18h to 48h. Those cannot all describe the same fixed-capacity throughput. Hold incidence also rises from about 1.6% to 6.2% of orders; mix shift plus the stated 2.4× complex-panel propensity explains only on the order of 1.5×, not 5.6×. Placing the 2.4× fact immediately before the 5.6× count implies an explanation the arithmetic does not support. Assay repeats (~2.9× weekly) versus the repeat-order class (+61%) has the same undecomposed gap.

The surge rule is internally inconsistent. Urgent p90 is already 19h against an 18h contract in the normal period, so “any urgent forecast exceeds its deadline” is nearly always true at peak and often true normally. The matching switch-off, “no unaddressed deadline risk,” may therefore never clear. Triggers needed a rate or count, not a single order. Smaller issues: bundle B is said to help every scenario although courier smoothing is mainly an arrival-shock control; “≤2h buffer before the deadline” can be read as wall-clock time remaining rather than slack; and the 12-to-24 batch baseline is inferred from I2 rather than observed.
