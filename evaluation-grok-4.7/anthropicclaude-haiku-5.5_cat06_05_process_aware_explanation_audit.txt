7.6

The audit is thorough and often precise, but it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions land on the exact distinctions the task requires, and the revised case write-ups cite values that are not defensibly known at `t0`.

Section 1 is the strongest part. Every global top-12 feature, every sequence pattern, and every local-only driver is covered. Post-closure duration, complete-trace appeal and variant IDs, closure-time handler, total document-request counts, extraction-date prior-claim SQL, and the nightly document snapshot are correctly treated as inadmissible. Queue length and elapsed time are kept, with the ±5-minute join risk and the Case X sign conflict against the “less slack” story both noticed. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is correctly separated from `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`: the first is a normatively valid later pattern, the second is an ordering violation compounded by a missing sequence index. Severity labels sometimes leave the required scale (`critical as stored; repairable`, `high (fairness)`), and `total_case_duration_hours` is overstated as the numerator of the 10-day settlement label. Closure duration and time-to-settlement are different clocks; the feature is still critical leakage, but the “tautology” claim is too strong. Calling fraud investigation “a response to risk, not a cause” is an unsupported causal negation in the other direction. The stated as-of-extraction employee-experience join is never audited.

Section 2 proposes a real suite (point-in-time replay, alignment, footprint, DECLARE-style rules, tie handling, repeated-decision instance checks) and correctly says a frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule. It then breaks the enabled / future-possible / impossible split. Documents Received and Claim Closed are listed as both future-possible and impossible. Appeal is called impossible here, after Section 1 said it is not impossible and only not yet possible. That is a taxonomy error on the task’s central process distinction, even though the practical rule “do not explain `t0` with unseen activities” remains sound. “Any replay mismatch is a leak” is too absolute. “Response(Partial or Deny, Appeal) as an optional constraint” mixes a mandatory DECLARE response with an optional business path. Comparison operators are missing in several temporal remedies (`snapshot_ts  t0`, `availability  t0`), which is material in a leakage audit. Footprint relation symbols are also blank.

Section 3 states the four standards — predictive validity, causation, actionability, and fairness — and does not delete region, channel, or calendar week merely for being correlated. The class boundaries then conflict. Repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is a “defensible predictive signal” and a fairness proxy. `initial_reserve` both “may be claimed” as predictive and “cannot be cited until repaired.” The prefix variant `REG-COV` is recommended as a process-state signal after Section 1 correctly notes that, at this fixed `t0`, that prefix is constant and therefore has no discriminating power.

Section 4 has the right JSON shape, does not retell leaked appeal, partial, or full-variant outcomes, and rightly refuses to explain Case Z. Status choices are only partly coherent: Case X is `recompute`, while Case Y is `invalidate` even though its removal text demands recomputation. Case X puts `claimant_channel` in both credible and removed lists although registration-time capture was never established. Case Y’s credible drivers include `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` and `initial_reserve = €25,000`, despite a known bad SQL predicate and no reserve timestamp. Those values are not information defensibly available at `t0`. The X narrative also says “the prediction reflects” the surviving drivers while the published 0.82 still contains large leaked mass (`appeal_seen_anywhere` +0.26 and `final_decision`).

Section 5 is prioritized and hits feature-store bitemporality, point-in-time repair, prefix replay, conformance, recalibration, explanation stability, proxy review, and schema/process monitoring. It still omits the employee-experience as-of join and inherits the constant-prefix recommendation.

Net: obvious leakage, subtler lineage bugs, congestion, prefix age, and the appeal-versus-tie distinction are handled well, but the process taxonomy contradicts itself and the revised explanations do not stay inside `t0`.
