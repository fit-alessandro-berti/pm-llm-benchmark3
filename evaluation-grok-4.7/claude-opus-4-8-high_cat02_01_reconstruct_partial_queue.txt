5.5

The case-level skeleton is mostly competent, but several required syntheses are numerically infeasible or contradict the answer’s own evidence. Under a strict reading, that caps the result well below a strong score.

The feasible-start bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are correct, as are nearly all fully observed waits, services, and completed sojourns (P01–P14: 60 through 230). Wait/service/sojourn are defined separately, open cases are kept, and missing starts are given as intervals rather than a silent point. System WIP is in the right neighborhood (S1 mean about 5.8 and peak 8; S2 mean about 3.7 and peak 7). The C2 outage discussion is directionally reasonable.

Material errors:

- S2 utilization is not 0.85–0.95, and section 6’s optimistic/pessimistic pair of about 0.87 and 0.95 is outside the feasible set. Central busy fractions are about I1 0.69, I2 0.81, C1 0.78, C2 0.66, so full-window ρ is about 0.75 at S1 and about 0.72 at S2. Adding the in-window OPEN elapsed time and the longest feasible missing services moves S2 only to about 0.81. Optimistic imputation must lower busy time, so it cannot be 0.87 while the central value is about 0.72. Section 5 then treats “S2 ρ 0.87–0.95 > S1 ρ 0.78–0.85” as an observation. In-window busy time of an OPEN job is the elapsed overlap, not a lower bound on utilization.
- The Little’s Law check does not hold. In 08:00–09:30 there are 11 S1 arrivals (P01–P11), not 6, and P07’s start is missing, so the “fully observed” premise is false. Time-average S1 WIP over that window is about 4.3, with 5–6 cases present after 09:00, not 2–3. λW ≈ 2.4 is therefore not consistent with the path, and using only early completed sojourns while the queue is still filling is the wrong comparison. The prose also switches between defining λ as throughput and applying it as an arrival count.
- P16’s censored S2 wait is 14:00−12:35 = 85 minutes. The case table and the section 4 range say ≥95, while the note says ≥85.
- “Monotone” sojourn growth is false on the answer’s own list: P08 is 180, P09 is 155, P10 is 175. Listing that claim as stable does not survive the table.
- The early window is misread. By 09:30 S1 waits are already 5–40 (P05 = 35, P06 = 40), not 5–20, and S1 WIP has risen to 6. “Neither queue grows” and “near-idle early” are incompatible with that path; S1 is already near 0.8 busy by 09:30.
- S2 queue arrivals are the 20 S1 completions (P01–P20), not 16. The 16 count is service starts. “20 recorded S1 starts” is also wrong: P07, P15, and P22 have no start, so 19 starts are recorded. “S2 waits dominate the end” conflates end-of-day queue location with wait magnitude; completed late cases still have larger S1 waits (P14 95 vs 65, P13 80 vs 50, P12 85 vs 45).

The code meets the structural checklist (in-memory table, flags, interval log, `format_dataframe`, both PM4Py inserts on complete cases only, and a stated reason to prefer pandas for station WIP). It does not reproduce the claims. OPEN handling is a `pass`, censored waits are left as null, optimistic/pessimistic columns are never aggregated, and `if False` discards the explicit column names. PM4Py sojourn on that projection runs from S1 start to S2 end, so it drops the external-arrival wait and its “waiting” term is only the S2 gap; calling that whole-case overstates the function. Subperiod rates are defined and only printed as counts, not used in the bottleneck argument. Queue length is defined and then not reported separately from system WIP.

What does survive is narrower than section 6 states: S2’s four blank waits at 14:00 do not depend on imputation; S2 service is longer than S1 service on the same nominal capacity; congestion increases through the day. The single-station and the precise utilization ranking do not.
