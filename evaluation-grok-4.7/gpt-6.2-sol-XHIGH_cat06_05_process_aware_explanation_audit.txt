The audit is strong, precise, and process-aware. It covers every required feature and pattern, separates feasible future behavior from the impossible closure-to-assessment ordering, and keeps admissible complexity, age, congestion, region, and calendar signals under the right claim limits. The point-in-time tests, suffix-invariance check, appeal-linked decision semantics, and four-way split among prediction, causation, action, and fairness are credible. The score is held down by internal contradictions and a few overconfident attributions.

The main flaw is in the revised case explanations. `queue_length_at_t0` is treated as temporally unsafe in the leakage table, then listed for both X and Y as a credible driver with a definite direction, while the same text says to omit it unless an as-of replay succeeds. Those cannot be true together. The stated directions also come from the contaminated local attributions. That is especially unsupported for region R4: eligibility at registration is established, but the positive sign is only an artifact of the leaked model. Complexity and elapsed time have a domain reading; R4 does not. The narratives also never state that the reported 0.82 and 0.71 are dominated by inadmissible mass, including the hidden base bucket, so a reader can still take the short admissible list as an explanation of those probabilities.

Severity is slightly flattened. The prior-claim SQL, keyed to extraction date, systematically admits post-cutoff claims in historical extracts and can include the index claim; that is more severe than a queue snapshot that is normally inside five minutes. The headline causal stories are only partly dismantled: total duration is nearly tautological with the breach label, and an appeal can add time and is selected by Partial or Deny, so “appeals cause breaches” would remain unsupported even at a later checkpoint. “Right branch” is also unclear. The validation and monitoring plan itself is concrete and correctly ordered.
