7.5

The design is careful and avoids the fatal failures this task is meant to catch: rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and immature cases are not recoded as non-defaults; the 48,000-case arithmetic is exact; activation is treated as selection; and validation is temporal. The remaining problems are in the estimand, the prefix rule, and the skeleton, and they are large enough that the study is not yet implementable as written.

The primary estimand is not operationally closed. \(F_j(t\mid H_\tau)\) is written as a cumulative incidence, but no \(t\) is chosen, and states (a) and (b) are defined on a second clock that starts at activation and then waits out a 90-day window plus a 7–35 day feed lag. “Confirmed non-default” has no event time. It is the complement of default at a deterministic follow-up point, so it is not a competing hazard in the same sense as rejection or withdrawal. The five states are not exhaustive: activated-but-not-yet-mature and still-open cases carry residual probability, so the reported vector does not sum to 1. \(P(\text{activated}\mid H_\tau)\) is therefore not recoverable by adding (a) and (b). The sentence that only 25,200 of 48,000 cases “ever activate” also overstates a quantity that is censored by the 15 February 2026 extraction and by transfer off-platform.

Landmark handling contradicts itself. Section 2 correctly drops cases that never reach the third event from the risk set. Section 4 then calls those same cases censored. Inclusion is “terminal or activation before the third event,” and the code implements this as `terminal_time >= tau` or missing. A trace whose third event is Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, or Account Activated stays in the cohort, and that event stays in the prefix. The leakage assert only checks `max_prefix_time == tau`, which is true by construction and does not inspect activity names. Section 6’s ban on decision and post-activation events is therefore not enforced. That hole matters once migration duplicates and shortened traces can move a terminal activity into position 3.

The validation write-up and the code describe different studies. The prose uses rolling origins, a `label_available_at` purge, and a post-15 October 2025 slice that is monitored but not scored for default. The code uses one cut on start time, puts every start after 30 June 2025 into `post_policy_test`, and never applies the purge. The July–August 2025 evaluation window can still contain immature default labels: a late August start that activates after roughly 40 days is not mature by 15 February 2026 once the 90-day horizon and the feed lag are included. Multiclass log loss on maturity-known cases then drops censored cases and treats non-exhaustive cumulative incidences as class probabilities.

The skeleton is concrete and inside the requested length, and `format_dataframe`, the as-of case table, the start-time split, and the refusal to invent a PM4Py survival routine are right. Two API uses are not. `pm4py.discover_dfg` returns `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`, so `len(dfg)` is 3 rather than an edge count. `get_prefixes_from_log` is called with `variant="positive"` and `min_prefix_length`, then ignored; the feature table is built from the rank filter instead. Baseline IPCW on start time, source, channel, and region is also thin for label censoring that depends on post-landmark activation time. The sensitivity list and the testability classification are otherwise sound, and the deployment section correctly limits the claim to prediction under the observed activation policy.
