7.5

The firing analysis is accurate and the selected repair is valid, but the minimality argument is false and several smaller claims are loose.

The workflow-net versus soundness split is right: unique source and sink, every node on a source-to-sink path, and an unsound waiver route. Both counterexamples are correct, including enabled sets, premature `t_authorize`, `{sink}` plus a residual token versus exact `fm`, and why `p_analysis_done` can never be consumed once `p_review` was taken by `t_waive_review`. The standard path really does reach `fm`, so there is no dead transition. R1 (one arc `p_analysis_done` → `t_join_waived`) is sound, keeps analysis mandatory, and preserves waiver as a distinct state. R2 and R3 are genuinely different, and the policy change in R3 is stated. The standalone net and the enabledness-checked replay match R1. Soundness is correctly treated as insufficient for the business rule.

The main error is the claim that this is the unique cost-1 arc and that there is no tie. `p_analysis_done` → `t_waive_review` is another single added arc. It does not break the standard route, keeps analysis mandatory, and reaches exactly `fm`, but it serializes waiver after analysis because the waiver transition consumes the analysis token. Under the stated cost model that is also cost 1, so the “no tie / no other single-arc repair” sentence is wrong. The parentheticals for R2 and R3 do not add up to the bold costs unless the reader re-applies the undisplayed ×2 node weight (1+3+2=6, not 8; 4+1=5, not 6). `fm` is already unreachable at step 3 of counterexample 1, not only from step 4. The two silent joins already coexist before R1, so that is not a new risk. The alignment remark that either silent join may be taken at zero cost is not true for a trace that records one review outcome. The structural bypass test is worded more broadly than the two joins it actually applies to.
