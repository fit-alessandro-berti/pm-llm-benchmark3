The reconstruction is unusually careful and mostly arithmetically right, but it is not nearly flawless: two published S2 inventory figures are wrong, the maintenance narrative contradicts the handoff rule used to impute starts, the Little’s-law “confirmation” pairs incompatible quantities, and one utilization bound is stated backwards.

What holds up. Station sojourn is correctly separated from the wait/service split. Missing S1 starts (P07, P15) and missing S2 starts (P05, P11) keep fixed sojourns (75, 125, 50, and 80) while only the split moves, with hard windows that respect prior resource completions: P11’s service cannot exceed 25 minutes on the reported clock. OPEN work is elapsed service, not a completed duration, and the four S2 queue waits plus P22 are lower bounds. Imputation-free inventory checks out: S1 case-time 2085 minutes, mean WIP 5.79, peak 8, 175 minutes at 7–8, census 2; S2 case-time 1340, mean WIP 3.72, peak 7 for the five minutes 13:45–13:50, census 6; system peak 13 and end inventory 8. Central busy time 595 and 560, the 80–84% and 78–81% bands, the I1/I2 and C1/C2 split, the 14 completed sojourns (mean 146, median 145), and the S1 hourly WIP path are consistent with the log. The bottleneck reading is the right one and does not rest on the bad cells: external arrivals near 5.3/h exceed an S1 cycle near 3.4/h, which itself exceeds an S2 cycle near 2.7/h, so S1 builds and then drains after intake stops while S2’s WIP rises all day. The C2 outage is correctly treated as too small to be that accumulation. Scenario tables, censoring, and the PM4Py projection (complete pairs only, case-level labels, external arrival excluded from the PM4Py clock) match the requested semantics.

Errors that should not have survived.
- S2 hourly mean WIP is given as 1.1, 2.8, 3.6, 4.2, 4.9, 5.8. The integral of the same path is 65, 165, 200, 265, 295, and 350 case-minutes, i.e. 1.1, 2.8, 3.3, 4.4, 4.9, 5.8. Hours 10:00 and 11:00 are simply wrong, and the rounded series does not add back to 1340. The code’s own hour loop would print the correct values, so the prose and the reproducible script disagree. The monotone rise survives, which is why this is not fatal.
- Assumption A6 says the +5-minute rule reproduces 12 of 14 observed S2 starts, with only P09 (gap 0) and P15 (gap 10) as exceptions. That count is true only if P08 is a success. Once C2 is unavailable on [10:55, 11:10), the same rule predicts 11:15 exactly, which is the recorded start. Sections 3 and 5 instead say the rule would have started P08 at 10:55 and treat 11:15 as a deviation. The 15-minute hole and the roughly 20-minute displacement relative to a no-outage 10:55 start are real; the claim that the observed start breaks the rule is not.
- On [10:00, 12:00), S1 \(\bar L=7.58\) from 910 case-minutes is correct, and the P15–P21 full sojourns do average at least 129 minutes. \(\lambda W\approx 7.54\) is not a Little’s check. Those sojourns mostly fall after 12:00; time actually spent inside the window totals about 425 minutes, so this cohort contributes about 3.5 to \(\bar L\), not 7.5. Inventory in that window is still largely P08–P14. The near-equality is an artifact. Calling it supportive, even “fragile,” overstates what the comparison identifies. The full-window identity and the rejection of a stationary S2 check are handled correctly, as is the completion-only gap (980 vs 1340 case-minutes).
- The S1 hard busy-time sentence attaches “if the two missing services shrink toward 0” to the upper end of 605 minutes. Shrinking those services toward 0 approaches 535; 605 is the earliest-start maximum (service 35). The S2 analogue, floor 515 and earliest-start 570, is stated correctly.

Smaller slips. Central* mean elapsed S2 wait is 38.75, not 38.9. The ten-case S2 means are 29.5 and 39.5, printed as 30 and 40. “The gap equals the 360 censored case-minutes” equates a 1.0 WIP gap with 360 minutes; those minutes are unfinished cases’ in-window presence, not the 14:00 snapshot (6 cases). “Plausible” S1 service up to 35 sits above every fully observed S1 service (maximum 30). Adversarial rounding can move many durations by up to 5 minutes, a wider utilization envelope than the imputation band, and that envelope is never computed. The P12/P13 S1 waits (85 then 80) are not monotone.

Format, coverage of the mandatory cases, and the distinction between identified skeleton and imputed split are all met. The grade is held down by the false hourly figures, the contradictory outage prediction, the invalid Little’s comparison, and the reversed hard bound, not by the overall design of the analysis.
