7.2

The redesign is complete and mostly coherent, but it is not nearly flawless. Several evidence and causality problems are material under a strict reading.

The required structure is present: five scenarios cover mix shift, assay degradation, validator/specialist absence, courier compression, and a compound wave; each has a migration story; the package rejects unconstrained I1, I2, and I3; policies have on/off triggers; the table has the required columns; residuals include a redesign-caused release bunching effect; and the dashboard separates leading from lagging signals using the queue table. Downstream overload is discussed for the main recommendations, and the answer does not add capacity everywhere or chase average cycle time.

The process-mining use is weaker than the citation density suggests. Peak counts are compared raw to normal counts across unequal windows. Technical validation to hold is 610 versus 290, about 2.1 times in raw events, but about 5.6 times per week; direct-to-release completions per week are roughly flat while the medical path more than doubles, and the direct-release share falls from about 74% to about 50%. Those normalized shifts are the actual migration evidence. S1 also treats the confounded three-week peak snapshot as the signature of a mix-only scenario, so the same 93–101% utilizations and queues are reused as if they isolated mix. Specialist “5/36h” is the queue median, while the hold-to-medical edge is 18/48h; that distinction stays blurred into the dashboard. S2’s 15–25% capacity loss and 12–15% repeat share are asserted without a derivation. The peak log also fails to close (about 1,320 starts neither released nor cancelled), which the analysis never uses.

There is a clear flow-direction error: an evening medical shift does not move work to next-day technical validation. Technical validation is upstream. I5 is also over-constrained by the claim that it helps only if specialist hold is drained; in the peak log, direct technical-to-medical flow is 3,420 against 560 from hold. R4 is internally inconsistent: its own rule pulls technical validators to accession only when the validation queue is low, yet peak technical validation is already at 101% with a 16-hour queue, so R4 is inert in the S1 and S5 states where it is claimed to help. P-Compound turns on every M1 claim at once, while conflict control never decides whether urgent bypass or complex multi-assay blocks win the reserved machine. Early two-hour triage is recommended at a station whose peak median queue is 9.5 hours and utilization 96%, without treating the fast lane as preemption of the healthy-sample queue. The urgent benefit of triage also ignores that recollection still costs 42–60 hours and breaks the 18-hour obligation.

Smaller faults add up. S1’s “~22% complex” does not match the 20% peak mix. The closing claim that the package stays inside the candidate list is false once stop-the-line deferral is included. I9 does not apply to urgent cases, yet a policy says not to hold them under it. Policy thresholds and dashboard bands do not match (pre-analytical 6h versus 8h). An extra title and closing summary depart from the required section set.
