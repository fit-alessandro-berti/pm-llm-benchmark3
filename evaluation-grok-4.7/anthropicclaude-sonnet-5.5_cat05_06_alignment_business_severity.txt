7.4

The analysis is strong and mostly faithful to the alignments, but it is not nearly flawless: the required code does not run, and the severity framework contradicts several of its own scores.

The business reading is the best part. All twelve traces are projected correctly. P1, P2, and P3 are applied to the right cases: CL-609 violates P1 only, CL-610 violates P1 and P2, CL-604 is a real pay-then-approve failure on a >EUR 50,000 claim, and CL-602 is missing Fraud Screen evidence rather than proof that no screen occurred. Zero-cost policy failures are identified explicitly, silent `tau` in CL-605 is treated as fitting, and the six deviation types are separated rather than collapsed into raw cost. The CL-604 cost-2 alternative (insert Auto Approve, synchronize Pay Claim, log-move Manual Approve) is real and correctly kept subordinate to the supplied alignment. The CL-607/CL-612 point that the cheapest completion prefers Auto Approve because it is one model move, not two, is also right. Hypotheses, owners, and the remediation split (containment, payment gate, data-aware detection, model scope, correlation, label governance) match the evidence, including the month-level 74 / 9 / 4 counts, without inventing a denominator.

The severity scale is not internally usable. Severity 5 is defined as any payment without the required approval, which also covers CL-609, yet CL-609 is scored 4. Severity 3 is defined as unreliable proof on a high-value case, which is exactly CL-608 (EUR 250,000, mutable CRM link), yet CL-608 is scored 2. CL-607 and CL-612 are the same scope artifact with no payment; 607 is 2 and 612 is 1 even though 612 has the weaker notification evidence. Several rows mark P4 satisfied when the only global timing caveat is that the 24-hour test cannot be done. Section 6 overstates CL-607: a model move means the cheapest completion inserts Auto Approve, not that auto-approval happened. CL-610’s “P3” is a defensible stretch of “before the applicable approval,” but it is not the sequence breach P3 states and that CL-604 actually has. Confidence is flattened in the table (CL-602 and CL-604 are mixed in the prose and single-valued in the column).

The code block fails the executability requirement. `Series.map` of a function that returns a `Series` does not expand columns, so `computed_cost`, `log_moves`, and the later `assert` raise `KeyError` before any aggregation. Independently, PM4Py alignment steps are `((log_label, model_label), cost)`, but the unpacking treats the pair as the log label and the cost as the model label, so every move would be printed as synchronous and `visible_cost` would be 0. Trace reconstruction, the cost regex, and the separation of `technical_raw_cost` from `analyst_severity_1_5` are sound; the reproduction and aggregation path is not.
