7.8

The design is statistically serious and gets the central decision right: 90-day default is defined only after activation, rejected and withdrawn cases stay out of the negative class, and the 48,000-row accounting is complete and adds up. The censoring taxonomy, leakage table, assumption table, and deployment feedback list are concrete and mostly well matched to the data. The score is held down by a confirmatory-test design that is less honest than the rest of the write-up, an overstated weighting estimand, and several prose-versus-code contradictions.

The primary estimand, \(P(\text{default within 90 days of activation}\mid H(t_p), A=1, G=g)\), is the right primary target, and the multi-state alternative is correctly described as a different question. The maturity rule activation + 90 + 35 days is dated correctly (about 13 October 2025 at the 15 February 2026 cutoff; about 5 May 2026 for 31 December activations). Refusing to invent a split of the 3,600 pending activations is appropriate.

Section 7 then designates applications started 1 July–14 October 2025 as the post-policy test. Under the stated maturity rule, a case in that window is evaluable only if it activated by about 13 October 2025. July starts can linger for weeks and still enter; early-October starts almost cannot. The evaluable “test” is therefore a fast-activation subset of the new-policy book, concentrated in the first part of the window, which is exactly where a policy-shift claim needs to be clean. Reporting metrics by month would reveal the thinning, but the design still labels that slice as the confirmatory test. The post-migration shadow cohort is handled correctly; the pre-migration test is not given the same restraint.

The inverse-probability step has a similar slip. Weights fit among reporting-complete activations can reweight permanently missing feeds. They do not put still-immature, right-censored activations back into a binary 90-day estimand. The phrase “target all historically activated accounts” overclaims. Random K-fold cross-fitting of those weights would also ignore the temporal discipline used everywhere else. The early-positive rule is ambiguous: section 3 allows a default label before the negative class is mature, which inflates prevalence, while `mature_binary` in the code requires the full 125-day reporting deadline for both classes. Those are different estimators.

Policy timing is attached to the wrong clock. The split uses application start, and `policy_post` uses prediction time. Limits and acceptance are set at the later credit decision, so June 2025 starts can carry post-1 July outcomes. The text says to report boundary crossers separately and does not define the boundary on the decision that generated the outcome. In section 5, policy regime and migration regime are listed as attributes frozen at application start; both are prediction-time or decision-time facts.

At the chosen landmark the third canonical event is the first Credit Review, and document loops occur after that review. Document-request counts, review revisits, and review-to-document cycles are then structurally almost constant on the canonical path, so the rework block will not do the work section 5 implies unless a later \(K\) or an elapsed-time landmark is the one being scored. The code checks that event 3 is Credit Review and does not check that it is the first Credit Review.

The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points (`format_dataframe`, `get_prefixes_from_log`, `discover_dfg`), strips post-decision fields, splits on case start, and hides labels with `label_available_at` after the cutoff. It is about 210 lines against the requested 80–160. The leakage asserts are weak: prediction time is the max timestamp of the same already-truncated prefix, so the time assert cannot fail. `risk_score` is still aggregated from event rows even though section 6 says an unversioned score should be repaired or dropped. Same-timestamp order is stored as `_row_order` and then discarded by the PM4Py conversion. Linked journeys are required to share a split and are only mentioned in a comment. One global cutoff of 15 February 2026 is applied to every split, so the block does not implement the rolling as-of label rule section 7 describes. `default_censor_time` is an absolute timestamp and is set to the cutoff for non-activated cases, which is an easy footgun for a later survival model. The training-prefix DFG is a valid reference object only if it is read as a length-3 prefix graph ending at Credit Review.

Cohort construction, the leakage dispositions, and the decision-use boundary are strong enough that this would be a credible study protocol after a revision of the test cohort, the weighting estimand, and the landmark feature set. As written, those gaps sit on the claims the design most needs to keep honest.
