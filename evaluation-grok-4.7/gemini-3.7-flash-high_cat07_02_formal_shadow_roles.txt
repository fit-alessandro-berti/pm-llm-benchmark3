6.5

The role model is directionally sound and covers every required section, but it is not reliable as written. Several evidence figures are wrong, core and peripheral duties contradict each other, and a number of authority claims are stated as facts the log does not support. The code would not reproduce the structure argued in the prose.

The table assigns all 14 resources and correctly refuses to treat frequency twins as the same role: Nora versus front office, temps versus Amira and Ben, Chen versus Dina, Fatima versus Giorgio, and Hana versus Mateo. Complexity, certifications, shift, contract type, and the three substitution episodes are used, and shadow work is given both benefits and risks. Sections 4 and 6 are the strongest: Giorgio, Mateo, Chen/Dina, and the temps each get two real interpretations with deciding tests, and the two staffing cautions match the constraint that substitution and activity co-occurrence are not authority.

The evidence column does not hold up. Hana’s 138 approvals are 138/218 = 63%, not 55%. Erik’s 96 security reviews are 96/146 = 66%, not 68%, and his events sum to 170, not 167. Ivan and Julia are about 47–49% medium and 30–35% low, not “~60% medium, ~25% low.” The 0.93–0.98 cosine figures are Amira–Ben and Kai–Lina, not the temp-to-permanent comparison they are attached to. “Median” duration is rewritten as an average. Those are table-reading errors, not rounding.

Core versus peripheral is internally inconsistent. Section 1 makes A13 core for Hana and front-office work core for Giorgio. The section 2 diagram drops Hana’s A13 into the margin and lists Giorgio’s A1/A2/A4/A12 as peripheral, while the prose under that same diagram calls them core. Giorgio’s largest counts are front-office (A2 190, A4 150, A12 130 versus A7 72 and A8 88), so demoting them is also substantively wrong. Mateo’s core set likewise changes between the table (A9, A13, A2, A12) and the later list (adding A3, A4, A8).

Several inferences are asserted without a hedge. Erik is given “absolute security veto” and a “zero-compromise” posture even though Chen, Mateo, Dina, and Ivan also perform A6. Chen’s 24 security events are explained as late-shift coverage while Erik is off duty; the log only supports a late shift and a higher security-adjacent count. Fatima’s 16 approvals become “delegated commercial approvals.” Mateo’s filter is said to have “misclassified” two critical cases; the evidence is that two critical cases waited more than two days. Giorgio’s slower substitution window is turned into margin leakage, a missing audit trail, and, for Mateo, “compliance violations.” The scenario does not show those, and that last label treats shadow work as improper rather than risky.

The code names the requested PM4Py calls, separates a substitution slice, and warns against reading frequency as authority. It does not join `discover_organizational_roles` output to complexity or substitution; it builds a parallel heuristic instead. `role.resources` is not the PM4Py role attribute (`originators`), so the print loop would fail. `AgglomerativeClustering`, `cosine`, and `normalize` are unused. Complexity is taken from event rows, so cases with more events dominate the median. The thresholds miss the roles the essay depends on: Giorgio’s substitution share is about 49/820 ≈ 6%, below the 0.15 shadow cutoff, and Hana’s median 0.79 fails the 0.80 gatekeeper cutoff, so both fall through to “standard specialized.” The `except` branch then calls this unrun pipeline “validated.”

Operational advice is usable, but it inherits those overclaims, and “formalize Giorgio, then restrict Mateo” is more confident than the ambiguous-assignment section allows.
