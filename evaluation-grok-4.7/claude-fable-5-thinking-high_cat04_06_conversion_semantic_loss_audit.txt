The audit is unusually strong: the N-poset argument, the primary Petri-net pipeline, interpreted process-tree failure, tau-hiding with exact final markings, and the separation of diagram layout from behavior are all handled at a high level. The 24-trace bound is the right cardinality for complete visible traces of length ≤ 12 (2 no-LR one-pass, 7 with LR, 2 no-LR one-redo, 13 with LR). That does not make the write-up sound.

The validation boundary is misstated. `(12−7)//3` is one extra iteration (lengths 7/8/10/11). Three iterations start at length 13 and are outside the bound. The prose instead says “two redo iterations beyond the mandatory first” and “~3 loop iterations,” then places residual risk only at depth ≥ 4. The code comment and Sections 5 and 9 disagree about what was proved.

Reachability evidence is inflated. The code compares `len(rg.states)` only. The text calls the graphs equal and treats that as inductive proof for the infinite language. Equal state counts do not give isomorphism, bisimulation, or unbounded language equivalence. Section 9 both leans on that equality and denies it covers deep iterations.

The preservation table conflicts with itself. I2 is `approximated` because interleaving is not step-concurrency; the discussion says the same for I5, but the table marks I5 `validated preserved`. Co-enablement is also misdescribed: a marking where both transitions are enabled distinguishes an AND from an XOR of the two orders. Trace sets cannot show one-step simultaneity; marking tests can show concurrency. Residual uncertainty 1 denies that.

Smaller defects: the explorer never calls `pm4py.get_enabled_transitions` (it uses `ClassicSemantics` and an unused `deque` import); I7’s “CD then release” trace is not among the asserted negatives; “only an interleaving witness catches serialization” ignores the parallel footprint; playout can treat silent events as visible labels and the broad `except` would hide that. These are secondary to the overclaimed bound.
