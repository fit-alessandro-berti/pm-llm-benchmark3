5.5

The framework is serious and often well aimed: authority is separated by issue, GPIP-4 is not treated as automatically displaced by ECD-2 or the CFO email, A6 is not treated as curative, and scope, window, and actor limits are usually applied at commitment. The hierarchy, rule catalogue, and proposed clause are structured and mostly usable. That is not enough to carry the casework.

The final classifications rest on several false applications of the record. For P-501, the emergency form at 07-15 07:50 is about 23 hours after the 08:50 commitment, so B6 is met; the assessment still calls B6 violated, then offers an incoherent claim that the form was nearly a day late, and Section 6 later recasts the same point as a logging defect. For P-502, recording at 12:55 is roughly 2 hours 45 minutes after the 10:10 verbal authorization, inside C3’s four-hour allowance; the assessment says the recording exceeds four hours and treats that as a deviation. For P-506, Finance_Approve at 06:05 precedes Commit_to_Supplier at 06:20; the assessment says finance came after commitment and uses that reversed sequence as a controlling reason. Those are not close calls.

P-505 is also mishandled against the answer’s own evidence rule. The supplier call, 17:59:31 acknowledgment, and a fast ERP clock all point to a commitment before 18:00, but the primary result stays indeterminate mainly because of the ERP timestamp. The alternative that would flip the case then states that A2 is not triggered because EUR 88,000 is below EUR 100,000. A2 starts at EUR 50,000; A3 is the EUR 100,000 / new-supplier rule. That error makes the proposed flip legally unavailable.

The finance theory is internally inconsistent. Pseudo-logic says the CFO email is sufficient for finance matters up to EUR 150,000, while P-503, P-507, and the out-of-window branch of P-505 treat a missing separate finance event as an uncured A2 breach without applying that rule. P-507, at EUR 110,000, is the clearest casualty: timely compliance clearance, in-scope emergency, two Incident Command authorizers, and a signed PDF inside four hours are all present, yet it is still primary NONCOMPLIANT on a finance theory the catalogue does not consistently hold. Seven primary NONCOMPLIANT results and one indeterminate result therefore do not follow one stable interpretation.

Several outcomes would likely survive a corrected analysis, especially P-502 on pre-commitment A2/A3, P-503 on segregation, missing compliance clearance, and weak “critical” scope, P-504 as outside both the emergency definition and ECD-2, and P-508 because “as well” does not replace finance above EUR 150,000. The grade is held down because those durable conclusions sit beside arithmetic mistakes, a threshold mistake, and a contradiction between the stated rules and the case holdings.
