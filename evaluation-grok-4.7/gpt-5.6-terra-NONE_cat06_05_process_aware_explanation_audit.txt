8.2

The audit is strong and usable: it catches obvious post-t0 leakage, the subtler lineage bugs, and the difference between a normatively impossible order and a future-but-legal appeal reconsideration. It keeps congestion, prefix age, and registration-time complexity, and it separates prediction from cause, action, and fairness. It is not nearly flawless.

The required coverage is almost complete. All twelve global features and all five sequence patterns are classified with a remediation, and the local-only fields (`claimant_channel`, `final_decision`, `initial_reserve`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`) are handled. `queue_length_at_t0` is correctly treated as repairable rather than discarded. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is correctly kept as a legal retrospective reconsideration and still banned at `t0`. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is correctly tied to both future information and a missing sequence index. The SQL cutoff on `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, the 23:00 document snapshot, the closure-time handler field, the untimestamped risk score, and extraction-time employee experience are all identified. Sections 3 and 5 actually apply different standards instead of deleting every correlate. The case JSON matches the requested schema, refuses to retell leaked futures, and marks X and Y for recomputation and Z as not currently trustworthy.

These issues keep it well short of a top score.

The activity-status taxonomy the task asked for is blurred. At `t0`, damage assessment and a document request are enabled; settlement and appeal are future-possible but not enabled; an appeal already having occurred is prefix-inconsistent. The write-up folds the first two into “enabled or future-possible,” then calls appeal “impossible,” which overstates a conditionally reachable later activity. Section 2.C also treats `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as a hard conformance failure, while 2.D correctly says the tied timestamps make the trace indeterminate. “Likely nonconformant” is not supported.

Severity and admissibility are slightly loose. `initial_reserve` is given a nonstandard “low/medium” and then placed in Case Y’s credible drivers even though no as-of lineage is known; that is the same class of defect as `risk_score_current`, which was correctly withheld. Directions for queue, complexity, channel, reserve, and region are inherited from SHAP values of a model that still contains leaked features. Only elapsed time is explicitly flagged for re-estimation. Those signs are not yet identified.

A few process claims are softer than the normative model. Immediately after the first coverage check, a prefix-only document-request count is structurally zero unless the log is nonconformant, not “usually” zero, and a prefix-variant id is normatively almost constant, not merely low-variance. The repair note that turns `num_document_requests_total` into a count through `t0` can resurrect the rejected “early rework warning” story. Alignment and directly-follows limitations are thin: a frequent edge is correctly called non-normative, but interleaving from the allowed parallel fraud investigation is not addressed, nor is the risk that alignment “repairs” an illegal trace. The combined residual is audited for Z and removed in the JSON for X and Y, but X and Y never get their own table rows. `final_decision = Partial` is future outcome leakage; calling it target-adjacent blurs it with the 10-day breach label.

None of that reverses the main judgments. The score is 8.2 because the audit would survive a technical review and still needs tighter status categories, stricter separation of unproven lineage from credible drivers, and no dependence on contaminated explanation signs.
