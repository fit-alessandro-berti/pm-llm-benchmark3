7.6

The memo covers every required section and is substantively strong: use cases change the fitness/precision standard appropriately, all eight reported measures stay disaggregated, both submissions are challenged with concrete traces, the pathology ranking is decision-relevant, and the validation plan and dissent are specific enough to act on. The intended third design is the right one: both checks mandatory and unordered, Expedite after them, exactly one approval, correction as a redo with a subset recheck, and missing `source_type` or `affected_checks` yielding indeterminate rather than a silent pass.

The behavior matrix misreads Model C on B2. The only Auto Approval branch is `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`. B2 reverses the checks. Section 4 already says Auto Approval is unavailable on the alternate order, then Sections 5 and 8 mark Model C as accepting B2. That is an incorrect language-membership call on a required probe, repeated in the comparative audit.

The third specification is internally inconsistent. The displayed language places optional Supplier Review before the correction loop and optional Expedite Review after it, sequentially. The following prose and `model_third` put those two activities in parallel before the loop. Another sentence requires Expedite to precede correction; `assess_trace` only requires it to precede approval. The tree therefore excludes a late Expedite that still follows both checks and precedes approval, which P4 allows. The checker, meanwhile, accepts traces outside the tree, including an unknown activity, a first Technical Review that appears only inside the correction window, and a duplicated check inside one recheck segment, because it compares sets and never closes the alphabet. Section 7.1 calls that checker authoritative for P6–P7; Section 8 defines acceptance as the conjunction of tree and checker. Those procedures disagree.

Credit Check and Technical Review each occur three times in `model_third`. Duplicate labels are treated as a governance defect in Model C and are not inventoried for the proposed model. Item 7 of the Model S “admitted” list is a trace the same paragraph says is excluded. The validation skeleton is usable, but it does not define `model_s_string` or `model_c_string`.

A board using this memo would mis-score a valid alternate-order auto-approval and could implement either of two incompatible third languages. The review is well above a metric bake-off and short of a nearly flawless governance opinion.
