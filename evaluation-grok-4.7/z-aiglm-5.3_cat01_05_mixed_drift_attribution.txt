The final answer is complete, well structured, and strong on the criteria that matter: it separates March logging drift from later real drift, assigns different change points to different artifacts, ties rework to Supplier Z and queue growth to staffing, treats the July priority rule as redistribution rather than a capacity gain, and builds monitoring that can tell seasonal recurrence from new drift. The required tables, segments, monitors, decision rules, and PM4Py plan are all present and mostly accurate. Baseline deltas and the seven-edge L1 distances check out.

It is not nearly flawless. The opening claim that the raw–mapped fitness gap is 0.35–0.39 from March onward is wrong: the gap is about 0.32–0.40, peaks at 0.40 in April, and narrows later. The October senior-assessment jump is over-read as case mix. Complexity rose about 20 points while the senior edge rose about 18 per 100 cases, but senior assessments per high-complexity case rose from roughly 0.19 to 0.54, so propensity or recording changed; a constant label count does not rule that out. Calling October’s DFG distance “dominated” by that edge is also misleading, because the rework-loop pair contributes more of the L1 (28 versus 18). Section 2 and section 9 slightly exceed their word limits, and a few rounded figures (July p90/median 2.36 versus 2.35; some mapped-fitness deltas) do not match the stated rounding rule. Those are limited errors in an otherwise rigorous attribution.
