6.5

The diagnosis is strong and usually careful, but it is not nearly flawless: one headline wait statistic is arithmetically wrong, and the required analysis script fails before it can print the diagnostic the task demands.

The cluster rankings are the best part. G1 is correctly tied to the four-item fixture rather than to identical clocks alone. G2 separates a group-start stamp from serial service of about 8, 6, 7, and 6 minutes. G3 treats 11:30 as folder submission, with serial-implied service 8/7/7/6 against nominal 28/20/13/6. G4 and G8 are read as back-to-back single-server work, and the G8 counterfactual (0/5/10/16/20, mean 10.2 versus observed mean 48.2) is a real decomposition of downtime versus load. G6’s zero-duration mechanism is explained properly: `service = complete − start` collapses, and everything from arrival to 17:00 is dumped into wait (mean 724/7 = 103.4). Shared-account overlap in G5 is checked against the stated cap of three analysts and the four-way overlap from 13:12 to 13:20. Assumptions A1–A6 are labeled, and identical timestamps are not treated as proof of simultaneous physical work. The seven required sections, the diagnosis table, the signature matrix, and the redesign list are all present.

The clearest numerical error is G7 in the performance table. Stamped waits are +2, −1, −4, −7, and −11. The signed mean is −4.2; the mean of the absolute values is 5. The text prints “+2/1/4/7/11 (mean 4.2),” which matches neither definition. That is the cluster used to illustrate impossible negative waits, so the slip matters.

The single code block does not fulfill the execution requirement as written. `pm4py.discover_batches` takes a `parameters` dict; passing `merge_distance=` and `min_batch_size=` as keywords raises `TypeError` on the standard API, and the exception is swallowed, so both parameter sweeps never return categories. Even if that were fixed, adjacency does `(datetime differences).astype("timedelta64[s]").astype(float)`. NumPy refuses that cast to float, the line is outside `try`, and the crash happens on G1, before any cluster table is printed. Overlap is also distorted for the important edge case: zero-duration G6 rows are extended by one precision unit, so all seven ticket closures become a 7-wide overlap even though the labeler later calls them bulk entry. Batch parsing never walks dict-shaped events, so case lists come out empty whenever PM4Py returns its usual event objects. Interval censoring uses ±precision, a 30-minute window for a 15-minute grid; arrival and `recorded_at` caps repair some of that, but the prose “0 to ~18 min” still reflects the oversized interval.

Smaller consistency problems add up. The coarsening row says equality dissolves under a smaller tolerance; on these already-rounded stamps it does not. Finer source clocks would be new data, not a tighter tolerance. G4 is given confidence 70, then placed in “high confidence,” and also in “needs operational validation before any performance conclusion.” In the robustness section the 13:12–13:20 overlap is called a gap. G4’s five arrivals in four minutes are treated as a sustained 1/min rate against 0.25/min capacity.

The reasoning about batching versus logging is at a high level. The grade is held down because a required numeric claim is wrong and the required program does not run through to the comparison it promises.
