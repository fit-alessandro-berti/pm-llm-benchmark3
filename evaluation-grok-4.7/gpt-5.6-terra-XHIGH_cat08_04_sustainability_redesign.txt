The answer meets the required seven-section structure, stays inside the stated evidence, and handles service, quality, resilience, rework, and obsolescence as real constraints. The score is held down by decision-rule ambiguity, a few loose quantitative bindings, and incomplete use of the conformance and flow evidence.

The main design gap is in the route hierarchy. SP1 treats ground and consolidated truck as alternatives once on-time probability is above 85%. Section 6 defaults Standard-stock Service-contract orders to priority pick plus ground at that threshold, while Section 3.2 also assigns Service-contract orders with slack to twice-daily consolidation. When both modes clear 85%, the package does not say which mode wins, so the lower-emission feasible mode is not the rule.

The 24-hour air advantage is transport time, not cycle time. Stock-air median cycle time is 17h and stock-ground is 34h, on unmatched mixes. Stating that converted orders’ cycle time increases because air “saves about 24 hours” overstates the counterfactual, especially once priority picking is added. The partial-shipment statistics (7%, 79h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e) belong to the path-table variant, but they are attached in the same sentence to the backorder-wait edge, which the DFG shows as 220 cases against 1,010 partial shipments. That sizing is not secure.

Two evidence points are underused. SP2’s missing justification on 31% of dedicated-van cases never appears, even though van triage and the exception log are central. The answer also never deals with the DFG–path mismatch (about 620 dedicated-van shipments versus an 8% critical-van path; 11,730 terminal events versus 12,000 cases) despite the prompt’s low-precision warning. Section 1’s causal caution is applied to routes and not with the same force to CNC_B versus CNC_A, where part mix could explain part of the 18% versus 9% rework gap.

Section 2 labels several indicators as event-log measures when predicted on-time probability, original need date, part mass, return legs, and empty-van kilometres are not attributes in the log. Section 7 later asks for those fields, which makes Section 2 read as more immediately measurable than it is. In Section 4, reduced partial shipments are marked resilience-neutral, while the prose correctly says urgent lines must still be released. The 6% unshipped figure is sometimes repeated without the forecast-error condition that defines it.

The boundary, segment split, refusal to drop dedicated vans or critical air blindly, batch-wait caps, Q1 packaging trade-off, rebound set, and conformance/rollback plan are all sound and traceable to the paths given. The deductions are for coherence and measurement discipline, not for a wrong overall recommendation.
