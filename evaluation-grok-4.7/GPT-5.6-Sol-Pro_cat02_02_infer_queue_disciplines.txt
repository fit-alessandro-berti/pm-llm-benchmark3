The diagnosis is substantively strong: decision sets are reconstructed rather than inferred from start order, the four queues are not collapsed into one rule, and observational ties (A06, C05, D06 at 11:30, the nondiagnostic 08:58 and 11:21 starts) are usually recognized. Timestamp arithmetic, eligibility, and the requested comparisons check out, and the counterfactuals and data plan are concrete. It is not nearly flawless.

The clearest mistake is the claim that C02–C03–C04 support SPT “despite conflicting arrival order.” Their relative arrivals are 10:03, 10:05, 10:07, so that subsequence is collinear with FIFO. Only deferring C01 separates SPT from “set aside the oldest, then arrival order.” Priorities and deadlines do conflict; arrival order, as stated, does not. That sentence overstates how identified the SPT ranking is.

B02 is treated as direct preemptive-priority evidence without stating that shortest-remaining-time makes the same prediction (15 versus about 31 minutes left). The “urgent arrival” code is a partial discriminator, but the decision table’s non-preemptive SPT line hides the real tie. Later QB choices reject SRPT; that test should have been explicit. B05, also priority 1, is not compared with B02’s two-minute preemption lag.

For QD, D-X’s 11:20–11:30 idle is listed as contradicting the chosen diagnosis even though that diagnosis already includes appointment reservation. The idle supports the reservation component. The unexplained case is D-F staying idle while 12-minute D10 fits before 12:00.

Every recorded active duration equals `est_min`. That unused fact makes remaining-time arithmetic exact, validates the look-ahead fit claims, and lowers the plausibility of noisy or revised estimates, which are still ranked as an open alternative. QA’s confidence of 89 is also high for a composite whose appointment component is separated by only A03.
