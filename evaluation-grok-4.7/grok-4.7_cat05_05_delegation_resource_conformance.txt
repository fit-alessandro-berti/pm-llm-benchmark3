7.6

The case analysis is largely accurate and well controlled: aliases are normalized before segregation checks, `svc_qms` is kept non-human unless the session evidence is actually reliable, roles are read at the event time, and repeated Clara–Ben work is treated as a signal rather than collusion. CHG-501, 502, 503, 504, 507, 508, and 510 are classified on defensible grounds, including the correct point that a late QMS insert does not defeat the 9 August signed PDF and that D-IC-04 cannot cure Clara approving her own draft.

The RR4 clock is also operationally coherent. Tuesday 11 August plus two business days ends Thursday 13 August, so the Friday CHG-505 review is late on every ordinary reading, and the cooling-system review is outside D-QM-01 even if the date were ignored. That makes the numpy illustration an unforced error rather than the basis of the case result. `numpy.busday_count(date(2026,8,11), date(2026,8,14))` is 3, not 2. The start date is included; only the end date is excluded. It does not count “intervening” days with both endpoints dropped, so the claim that an exclusive-end count would make Friday look timely is wrong.

The checking code has a silent functional failure. `read_csv` leaves `true`/`false` as strings, `approvals["emergency"].eq(True)` therefore matches nothing, and the retrospective deadline, scope, and sick-leave checks never run. In `evaluate_approval`, `bool("false")` is also true, so the standing-IC branch is not actually gated on the emergency flag. D-IC-03 is coded as every `cooling_system` change, and both QM delegations are given `Retrospective_QM_Review` power that the instruments describe as QM approval. Those shortcuts are partly disclosed, but the empty emergency frame is not.

CHG-506’s segregation cell is logically wrong. RR2 is Diego versus Eva and does not depend on who used `u_bk`. RR1 fails only if that person was Clara. An unknown office user breaks RR3 and identity, which is the finding the rest of the row actually supports. The executive close then counts medium-confidence CHG-509, where Asha’s absence is unproved rather than disproved, among the established authority failures.
