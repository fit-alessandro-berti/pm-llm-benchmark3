5.5

The answer is complete and often sensible, but it is not internally reliable. The scoring rules, the eligibility logic, and the case table do not implement the same policy, and several stated constraints are missed or mislabeled.

The eligibility logic contradicts both the written constraints and the assignment table. For a high-complexity, high-handover case, a non-owner is rejected when the current owner’s skill is 0 or 1 and unsupervised. That forces continuity in the only situation where continuity is supposed to be dropped. On C001 this filter rejects R8_Hugo, yet the table assigns R8_Hugo. The same case also returns early for every Security skill-3 resource, before the batch cap and overtime checks run. `supervised` is never defined, and “no safer alternative” cannot be evaluated inside the per-resource loop. A Quality case flagged as a batch candidate makes every resource ineligible when batching is not allowed, instead of simply disabling batching.

The score does not do what section 2 claims. `handover_penalty` is used before it is assigned, is not clamped, and has no entry for handover cost `none` (C013). It is also subtracted for every candidate, including the current owner, so that term does not rank resources. The urgency multiplier depends only on the case, so it cannot change the within-case argmax; if a score is negative, a larger multiplier makes the urgent case more hostile to its better candidates only when signs differ across other transformations, and here it is simply inert for a positive multiplier. Training is added as 0.15 and then weighted by 0.10, a maximum of 0.015, which cannot move a decision against skill or workload. The comparison is also case-sensitive (`"diagnosis"` versus `"Diagnosis"`), so the bonus never fires on the given labels. Weights are not normalized, resilience uses undefined denominators, and the later sketch drops team concentration while the first formula reads `c["team"]`, a field the queue does not have.

Supervised pairs are not generated. The search loops over single resources, then says “if best is a supervised pair.” Exception relaxation is a separate prose list, not a procedure that builds a supervisor. Portfolio rules are also absent: there is no second pass for the 20% training quota, no batch-pairing step, and no update of hours or overtime as the batch fills. A static “low” overtime flag can still absorb four heavy cases.

The table breaks the training rule and misstates one training result. Nine cases are normal or low, so 20% means at least two assignments that match a documented training priority. Only C009 does: R1_Amal, diagnosis, skill 1. C010’s preparer is R10_Jonas, whose training priority is security, not approval. Nobody with training priority approval has approval skill above 0, so that case cannot satisfy the quota. The answer still marks it “Yes — approval preparation” and then says the hard constraints are respected. The 20% rule was demoted to soft preference S4, while the softer “should be preferred” continuity rule was promoted to hard constraint H8 and then broken. The feasible training set is almost empty: security and approval trainees are skill 0, negotiation cases are urgent or high, and the quality trainee is high overtime. That infeasibility is never stated. C015 correctly blocks R5_Eva and R10_Jonas because Security skill 0 cannot be supervised into eligibility; that finding is not carried through to the quota.

C001 is the sharpest unresolved trade-off. SLA remaining is 3 hours, an owner change on a high-complexity case is evidenced at about 2.7 hours of extra wait, and keeping a weak owner is evidenced at about 3.3 hours of extra service. Both options threaten the deadline. The table treats the handover as an SLA protection and does not apply H8. C013 gives a low-priority Eligibility case to R3_Celine, the scarce low-overtime Diagnosis and Rework specialist, already loaded with C003, C007, and C009. C014 goes to R10_Jonas at skill 2 while R8_Hugo has Rework skill 3, and it also breaks the Quality batch R10 was chosen to protect. R3 and R10 both sit on the cap of 4, with hours checked only as a soft term.

The comparison directions are broadly right and the “no single optimum” conclusion is sound, but first-available and specialist-only are not actually run. Resilience and skill development are overstated relative to one real training assignment and unchanged Security and Approval concentration. The worker text is clear and non-punitive, yet it says complex, already-handed-over work stays with the current owner, which is the opposite of the C001 decision.

The audit list covers the required indicators, but the thresholds (Gini 0.4, 5% SLA, 95% conformance, medium overtime under 20%) are not tied to the 1.8× rework, 1.4× appeal, 2.7-hour, or 3.3-hour evidence. Appeal is not in the log. A handover-of-work network is not a per-case handover count. The sketch names the right PM4Py areas, then calls them with non-existent arguments: `resource_key` on `format_dataframe`, `resource_key` on the SNA discovery wrappers, `how` and `positive` on `filter_event_attribute_values`, and `batch_type` on `discover_batches`. Actual assignee is estimated with `mode`, and the code never checks skill, training share, overtime, or SLA. The same broken score is pasted twice.
