7.0

The design correctly refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or open cases as non-defaults, separates administrative censoring from activation selection and permanently missing feeds, and keeps the estimand factual under the observed approval policy. The leakage taxonomy, as-of feature rules, and deployment feedbacks are strong. The score is held down by identification errors and a skeleton that would mis-build the very labels the design depends on.

The primary estimand is a 270-day composite (activation within 180 days and default within 90). Full maturity is correctly placed near 13 April 2025, so no post-1 July 2025 case has 270 days of follow-up by 15 February 2026. The headline test window, 1 July–31 December 2025, therefore has an empty risk set at the primary horizon: the earliest test landmark is about 226 days before extraction. IPCW AUC, Brier score, and Aalen–Johansen calibration at day 270 are undefined there, not merely noisier. The same gap undermines “fit pre- and post-policy models” and “include regime interactions”: the post-policy coefficient on the primary horizon is not identified in this extract. Censoring-aware training can use partial follow-up; it does not create a mature post-policy test. The 180-day activation window, which causes that non-identification, is never given a sensitivity analysis.

The cohort table assigns all 9,100 rejections to the competing-event estimand, while the at-risk rule drops every case that already ended before the 72-hour landmark. Those are different populations. The claim that conditional-on-activation risk “is” the ratio of the two cumulative incidences is only the share of resolved in-window activations, and only when both causes have finished by the horizon. It is not \(P(\text{default}_{90}\mid\text{activated})\) for eventual activation.

The skeleton does not implement the stated label rules. `default_90d` is never read; `Default Recorded` is then overwritten to censored whenever `label_available_at` is missing, so an in-log default is discarded. Competing events after day 180 stay censored at the extraction date, so those cases remain in the risk set and dilute the hazards. The primary \(C-35\) day label buffer is absent. `res_exp` is the previous event’s `cumcount`, not experience at the landmark, and `.last()` drops an earlier `risk_score` if the last prefix row is null. The future-activity assertion treats `Accepted` and `Contract Signed` as impossible before 72 hours, so a fast but still pre-activation case crashes the audit or is treated as leakage. `data_extract_time` is described as event-write time; in this schema it is the extract timestamp. The prose promises a left-truncation and maturity flow the code does not emit, and the pipeline is about 220 lines against the requested 80–160. The temporal split is by start time, but labels are still built at the final cutoff rather than re-censored at the training origin.

Section 8 is the right decision boundary: predictive association under the historical policy, not counterfactual default if rejected cases had been booked. That conclusion is more careful than the validation plan that precedes it.
