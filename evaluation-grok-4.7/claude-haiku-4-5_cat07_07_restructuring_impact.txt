The write-up covers every required heading and the right qualitative mechanisms (queue pooling, East versus North/South, tails, learning, accountability, a gated pilot). It is not reliable as a forecast or as a PM4Py procedure. Several load-bearing numbers contradict the given log summary, and the analysis sketch would not run or would measure the wrong thing.

The capacity model is off by about a factor of five. Volume is 820 + 760 + 520 = 2,100 cases per month, roughly 500 per week, not “100 cases/week.” “41 per 100” is a share, so technical-shared-service demand is on the order of 200 cases per week. The “3 specialists × 6h = 36 cases/week” and “ρ ≈ 1.14” figures are invented and then reused for the queueing cliff, the adverse “service rate ~30,” and the pilot sizing. Six hours per case is not in the data; the 3.2h figure is a median wait after handover. Extra load that staff flagged (legal/risk triage and difficult quality check) is named and then left out of that arithmetic. Legal and Risk are already at 91–93%, and the management table itself raises their volume (9 → 12 per 100) and wait (9.6h → 10.5h). A 15% utilization gain on that pair is infeasible. The optimistic scenario never faces that path.

Handover accounting is wrong in the same way. Management still expects about 36 returns per 100 from the technical pool (versus 31 today) and more approval and legal handovers. Summing the management rows gives roughly 343 handover occurrences per 100 cases now and about 359 after the change. The map’s “31 → ~15” reduction, and the scenario path 15 / 22 / 28, describe a different proposal. Nominal local-specialist edges disappear; total handover count does not fall.

Baselines are swapped or made up, then treated as measurements. 6.8d, 16.4d, and 4.3/5 are North’s results, not the organization’s. A volume-weighted cycle time is about 7.5d and satisfaction about 4.1/5. Risk 1’s “pre 90th = 12d” contradicts the published 16.4 / 18.2 / 22.8. “Cases over 20d ≈ 2%” cannot sit next to East’s 90th percentile of 22.8d. High-complexity “10d,” rework mix “60% vs 40%,” expertise-flag rates, and segment satisfaction scores have no source. East is given a gain in every scenario (+2.0d / +1.0d / +0.5d). That ignores demand share: East is about a quarter of volume, so a FIFO shared queue can make East wait behind North and South even while E_Tech’s personal overload disappears. The constraint was that East may differ, including by losing its only embedded specialist.

The fairness statistic does not measure fairness. A Gini on pooled case durations is mostly within-team spread (the 90th is already two to three times the median). Team or segment medians and tails are the comparison. `scipy.stats.gini` does not exist. M/M/1 is written as “W → W / (1 − ρ),” which is not the waiting-time formula. “Reality: N_Tech is compliance, S_Tech is validation, E_Tech is integration” is not in the case. “Headcount is unchanged” misses the removed supervisor.

PM4Py usage is systematically off. `get_service_time` is service time, usually seconds, not cycle time in days; sojourn/cycle time is first-to-last timestamp. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` returns a dict, not a frame with an `activity` column. `format_dataframe` does not accept an XES `EventLog`. The SLA flag calls `get_service_time` once per event, compares seconds with a made-up 15-day threshold, and the pilot snippet reads `service_time` before creating it. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is resource–resource similarity; the text treats it as activity–role entropy and then ignores the return value. Handover weights are raw counts with no per-100 normalization across unequal windows. `shift(-1)` is not time order. Expertise rates are event-weighted. The required sketch never calls `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network` (the A–B–A pattern that is this redesign), `discover_performance_dfg`, `discover_organizational_roles`, `filter_variants`, or `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. “Pending Specialist” / `TechSharedService_queue` are not log fields.

The pilot is narrower than the decision it is asked to support. It drops shared approval, Legal/Risk, and difficult quality check, adds a contractor, and takes a go/no-go at week 10. Those omitted pieces are the saturated resources and the role-clarity break. Learning loss is described as a month-3-to-6 effect and is not in the go criteria. In-flight cases have no rollback path. South-A’s “7.1d” baseline is not South’s 7.4d.

What holds the score up is coverage: three scenarios with the requested dimensions, leading versus lagging log indicators, seven average-blind risks, East called out, and a pilot-first recommendation that explicitly warns against reading early means, utilization, handover counts, or unsegmented satisfaction as success. The direction of the story is usable. The numbers and the code are not.
