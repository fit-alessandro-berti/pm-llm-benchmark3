4.8

The final write-up has the right eight-part structure and a usable qualitative story (weekday/weekend split, mix shift around 20 April, a later staffing change, and the outage not treated as healthy high throughput). Little’s Law is applied with explicit windows, both arrival and completion proxies, and several sensible “not defensible” calls. Pooled means for arrivals, completions, WIP, staff, and complex share are essentially right, and the static 6.0 FTE policy example is the right kind of pooled-versus-segmented warning.

Those strengths are undercut by unreliable evidence and broken reproducibility. Baseline and high-complex staff means are wrong (about 5.92 and 5.93, not 5.75 and 5.86). High-complex p90 is summarized as 82.6 with a range to 86, but 27 April is 88 and the mean is 83. Weekend service means are off (median about 32, not 30.9; p90 about 68, not 71). Post-intervention arrivals are given a top of 121, which is not in that window. Three days, including the real recovery days of 16–17 April, are left out of every regime while the outage row is still labeled “outage & recovery.” The 180-minute outage is 25% of the 12-hour day, not 15%. Implied \(L/\lambda\) time is called “active time,” which confuses sojourn with handling time. Completion Fano factors are wrong in a way that reverses the result: 21 April and 25 April completions are under-dispersed, not strongly over-dispersed. The across-day arrival CV is about 0.33, not 0.28. The 15–20% WIP reduction from peak smoothing is an unlabeled causal claim. The alternative staffing tiers contradict themselves (6.0 sits inside the stated “standard” band, but only non-6.0 days are counted; 16 April’s 7.0 FTE is ignored).

The required Python does not run: `outage_min` has 29 entries and places 180 minutes on 13 April, 14 April arrivals are 105 instead of 107, service percentiles are shifted from 27 April, and `groupby("regime")` uses a column that is never created. The printed change-point line is also hardcoded rather than produced by the CUSUM the text claims.
