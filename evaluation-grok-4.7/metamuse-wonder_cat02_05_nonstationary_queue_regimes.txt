7.2

The regime framework is strong, but several headline pooled figures are arithmetically wrong and then reused in the comparisons and the staffing recommendation. Those errors are large enough to keep this well below a near-flawless score.

The required eight-part structure is met. Weekday/weekend, outage, recovery, mix-shift, transition-day, and post-intervention boundaries are defensible. Outage recovery is correctly kept out of normal capacity. Assumptions A1–A3 are labeled. Median service is not substituted for mean flow time. The Little’s Law matrix uses the required verdicts and rejects trending, draining, and single-shock windows. Burstiness is properly separated from a fixed intraday profile, baseline weekday arrivals are correctly described as under-dispersed (variance/mean about 0.26), and the alternatives are genuinely different. Most regime-level counts check out: baseline weekdays 701 arrivals and 697 completions, mix-shift pre-intervention weekdays 798 and 704, recovery net +30, and load moving from about 0.59 to about 0.94.

The pooled totals do not. Over all 28 days, arrivals are 2519, or 90.0 per day, not 92.3; completions are 2409, or 86.0 per day, not 91.5; mean WIP is 836/28 = 29.9, not 28.7. Mean staff is 144/28 = 5.14 FTE, not about 5.3. The claimed balance of 91.5 versus 92.3 is false: completions lag arrivals by about 3.9 cases per day, or 110 cases in total. That false balance is repeated in §§4, 6, and 7, and it is the premise of the “do not cut staffing because the books balance” policy. The pooled mechanical flow time of about 3.7 open hours inherits the same bad inputs; the correct mechanical ratio is about 4.0, and it remains not defensible.

The FTE rate is internally impossible. Both baseline strata are given as about 16.7 arrivals per FTE-day, yet the pool is given as 15.4. A rate weighted by FTE cannot fall outside both components. 15.4 is essentially 92.3/6, i.e. weekday staffing used as if it were average FTE. Correct arrivals per FTE-day are about 17.5. The supplied code would have printed the true pooled means, so the prose contradicts the reproducibility section.

Smaller quantitative problems add up. Baseline weekday net flow is −4, not −3. “p90 roughly doubles” conflicts with the later, better “+75%” (about 47 to 83 minutes, 1.77×). “Understates post-shift load by roughly a third on the service component alone” is too large: pooled median service is already about 31 minutes versus about 35 post-shift, roughly a 13% gap. April 7 and April 21 arrival CVs are closer to 0.59 and 1.0 than to 0.65 and 1.1. Baseline weekday WIP 18, 20, 19, 21, 20, 21, 23 is called “no trend” despite a mild rise. “Apr 11–19 (4 d)” reads like a continuous window. Section 6 says the post-weekend flow time is shown in code; the code never computes it. The uncomputed claim that a pooled regression of completions on complex share “can be flat or positive” is only a sketch of a Simpson reversal.

Conceptually this is high-level queueing work with appropriate uncertainty. The grade is held down because the main pooled-versus-segmented numbers, the FTE comparison, and the policy hook that depends on them are not reliable.
