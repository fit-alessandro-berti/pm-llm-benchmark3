7.3

The final diagnosis is right on the main split: April is a schema and pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change without within-variant concept drift, August–September combine a real V3 performance shock with concept drift and then a Partner Z shift, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The six-way table, month narrative, staged train/recalibrate decision, alert suppression, and four failure modes all follow that evidence. That is the hard part of the task, and it is done with usable arithmetic (for example the August mix-weighted mature breach near 0.31 against a mean prediction of 0.19).

It is not nearly flawless. The prediction is fixed immediately after diagnosis, so realized `Order Part → Part Received` time is downstream of the prefix. Section 3.4 treats flat predictions as a failure to “translate longer delays into higher risk,” and section 4 then tells you to put part-delay duration into the V3 risk model. That confuses ex-post process evidence with features available at scoring time and would leak future path information unless the decision point is explicitly moved. The stable 3.8–4.6 h remote-fix edge, which is the clean negative control for V2, is barely used.

Several quantitative loose ends remain. Aggregate calibration flips sign from August (prediction 0.19 vs observed 0.28) to October (0.23 vs 0.16); that sign flip is exactly what positive-label censoring produces, and it is not called out. Variant-weighted mature rates (~0.31 in August, ~0.34 in September) are not reconciled with the lower cutoff rates. V3’s share jumps from 22% to 35% in August, before Partner Z, and supplier lateness explains duration, not routing share. June’s still-high AUROC is treated as proof of intact within-variant ranking, which aggregate AUROC cannot show. The containment override is pinned to July’s 0.46 V3 rate after August–September have already matured near 0.61–0.64. A pooled retrain on January–March plus August–September is recommended without a regime weight, so the new concept can be averaged away.

The code sketch has the right cohort idea (normalize the renamed diagnosis, exact-duplicate drop, case-start vs event-calendar, no invented drift detector), but it would not run. `pm4py.filter_log` is not a real entry point, `format_dataframe` output is mixed with EventLog trace handling, `log_converter` is imported and unused, performance-DFG results are tuples rather than dicts with a single duration, and `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` appears only in a comment. The duplicate count is global but labeled as if it were monthly.

Falsifiers are mostly implicit in the tests rather than stated per drift type. The process-drift row’s “contradicting” cell is vague. Those are secondary to the timestamp error and the non-executable monitor sketch.
