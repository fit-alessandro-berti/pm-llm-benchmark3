7.2

The review is structurally complete and often sharp: use cases are separated, metrics stay disaggregated, S/C probe judgments match standard process-tree semantics, and the governance split is earned. It is not nearly flawless. The proposed compliance artifact contradicts itself and false-accepts behavior the prose claims to forbid.

Model C is misdescribed. Branch 4 is Register, Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order, so alternate check order is not limited to branches lacking Supplier Review. That error undercuts the enumeration analysis. The “S accepts” list also ends with a trace S rejects.

The third model’s control-flow shape is mostly the right design, but the required code is not reliable. `OperatorPOWL(..., operands=)` does not match PM4Py’s `children` constructor, so `model_third` does not build as written. The checker, which Section 10 treats as the release oracle, never requires the first Credit Check and Technical Review to precede approval, never requires Correct Dossier or Supplier Review to precede approval, and treats the first sighting of a check as the initial check even when it follows Correct Dossier. Those are false accepts, not illustrative omissions. Model and checker also disagree on Expedite versus correction order, with no join rule.

Section 8 then contradicts the checker: missing `external` is defined as UNRESOLVED for every case, yet B1 and B2 are marked Accept, while an internal Supplier Review is marked Reject under a ban the code does not implement. B1/B2 without Supplier Review are also not unconditionally valid under P3; absence of Supplier Review is underdetermined if source is unknown. Token-based generalization is glossed as unseen-combination replay rather than the frequency penalty it actually is.

Sections 1–2, 5–6, and 9–11 are strong, and the dissent is a real counterargument within the length bound. The defects sit in the model audit and the executable alternative, which is the decision-relevant core.
