4.5

The write-up covers every required section and names the right ingredients (case types, calendars, non-stationary arrivals, cross-case coupling, D1–D10, stress runs, tails, fairness). Those ingredients are then specified in ways that contradict the supplied log, the process tree, and ordinary discrete-event practice, and the skeleton would not run or reproduce the stated baseline.

Process-mining grounding is the weakest part. Variant-group shares are treated as conditional probabilities: inspection-rework variants are 9% of cases, not a 20% rework chance, and “New residential with fire + public notice” at 18% of volume against a 28% case mix is not a 30% public-notice rate. Case mix is “validated” with variants, which are control-flow traces, not `case_type`. The estimation block calls APIs that are not PM4Py (`get_branching_factors`, `get_rework_frequency`, `add_service_and_waiting_times`, `get_arrival_rate` on the sojourn module, `discover_resource_profiles`). `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return dictionaries (and start/end maps), not edge objects with `.source`, `.count`, and `.performance`. `get_variants(...).most_common()` and `visualizer.view(tree)` are the wrong types. The tree is not parsed with `pm4py.parse_process_tree`; the hand-built fallback uses a nonexistent `ProcessTreeOperator` and makes public notice mandatory. With one timestamp per event and no lifecycle, service and waiting cannot be split by a sojourn call. The proposed rule that waiting time is an upper bound on service time reverses the data (planning is 3.5 h of service and 6 d of wait).

Assumptions fight the evidence. Planning, environmental, and legal p90 service times are 14 h, 18 h, and 16 h, so “service fits in one working day” is already false, and the engine still uses deterministic medians. D3/D5 longer travel is relabeled environmental sensitivity. “Duration > 14 d means an objection occurred” turns a missing event into an observation; the prompt forbids that. The code never sets `case.objections` anyway. Fire, environment, and public-notice flags are drawn nearly independently, so the 18% fire-plus-notice path is not reproduced. Application values use `lognormal(8, 0.5) * 1000`, whose median is about €3 million, while the comment and the excerpt say tens of thousands to about €480 thousand. Seasonal factors average about 1.35 and are not normalized to the 31,200-case base, so the baseline volume is already off. Architect “bursts” are independent draws, not clustered arrivals.

The simulation design does not implement the model it describes. The tree’s AND of planning, fire, and environment is a fixed sequence. Completeness never loops: after “Request missing documents,” `missing_docs` stays true and the same activity is chosen again. Baseline never opens a missing-document loop, while D1 turns that probability up. The single `portal` / `system` resource becomes busy, so a 14-day public notice blocks issuance. Environmental “40% reserved” is a fresh coin flip inside `is_available`. Priority uses `datetime.now()` rather than simulation time. Travel is a flat 30 minutes. Holidays, the Monday portal clerk, planner pools, and urgent legal work during council weeks are absent. D3–D9 and S1–S3 are comments or identical runs. Related-case links are stored and then unused, so the executable model is effectively independent cases.

Uncertainty treatment names replications, bootstrap percentiles, heavy tails, and sensitivity, then applies steady-state tools to a seasonal process. Welch’s method is misspelled and used to justify a fixed 6-month discard plus a check that queues “stabilize,” which a 1.7× seasonal arrival process should not do. Batch means assume stationary batches; ten batches across an 18-month seasonal run do not. Seeds are “per experiment,” so policies are not paired. One hundred replications are asserted to detect a 5% p90 shift with no variance or power argument, including for complex variance at 10% of volume. LHS plus Sobol on a noisy seasonal simulator is named without a replication scheme.

The experiment matrix has the right rows, but the trade-off cells lost their directions, and the effect sizes (60% fewer missing documents, 3-day batches, 50% of objections caught) are presented as if estimated. D2’s safety risk never becomes a measured outcome. Decision thresholds are not anchored to the log: an average activity queue under 10 cases is incompatible with ~57 arrivals per day and multi-day waits (planning alone is on the order of a few hundred cases). Absolute utilization and SLA caps are not compared with a baseline that may already miss them. Little’s law is both a validation check and silently violated by those backlog caps. Scoring weights read like a selection rule for a twin that the text never conditions on successful calibration.

The excerpt frame itself cannot be built: 13 P001 events are stored under 12 case-id copies, so the DataFrame lengths disagree. `format_dataframe` is called with the right keys, and the class split between log analysis and the engine matches the requested shape. That is not enough to offset an engine whose baseline path, capacity, calendars, dependence, and PM4Py measurements do not match the process it claims to compare.
