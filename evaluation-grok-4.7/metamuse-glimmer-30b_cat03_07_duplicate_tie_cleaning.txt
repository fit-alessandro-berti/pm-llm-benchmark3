**5.5**

The design gets the main merge verdicts right, but it misses required analysis, misstates several consequences, and the implementation would not carry out the pipeline it describes.

The duplicate policy is the strongest part. Exact duplicates are dropped; e03/e04 and e11/e16 are merged on the reconciliation and invoice evidence; e03/e05 and e13/e14 are kept as different objects; e06/e07, e08/e09, and the payment summaries are kept and relabeled; unresolved cases are quarantined. The conservative result of 15 events is right, ingestion time is not the business order, and original timestamps are not overwritten in the design. The explanation that `have_common_object=True` still merges e13 and e14, because they share O1 and V1 while M1 and M2 differ, is correct.

Those decisions are not carried through carefully enough.

The ordering section treats e08 before e09 as forced. The known facts only force handover after carrier acceptance. Release versus acceptance is a label collision, not a proven sequence. Invoice before authorization is likewise only a tie-break, but it is written into the admissible order without saying so. Putting e06 before e07 as “message causality” also creates a business directly-follows edge for what may be one transition at two abstraction levels.

The evidence-based scenario then merges e08 with e09 anyway. That contradicts the evidence that those activities differ. Four merges from 17 events leave 13, not about 12. The raw E2O count is 44, not about 41. Merging events that both touch Package reduces the Package event-type weight; the write-up says that weight increases.

Sensitivity stays at that level of sketch. OTG relations are never discussed. ET–OT, OC-DFG frequencies, lifecycle, and performance are not compared for each of the four logs. The six named edges are listed, not interpreted. The raw Item DFG also misses the false I1 pick rework caused by e03 and e04.

The failure-mode list contains the eight required cases, but the abstraction case is inverted: keeping two Pack events under one label creates the false rework; dropping the summary removes it. Safeguards are generic and not tied to those modes. Provenance fields are all named, yet reconstruction never says how merged relations or discarded attributes are rebuilt.

The Python block names every required PM4Py call and deep-copies `ocel_raw`, but it does not implement the pipeline. `original_activity` is set from a missing column rather than `activity`. `candidate_groups` is never called, and the evidence-based copy is unused. The verified-pair helper leaves the kept row in place, appends a second copy, and replaces relation handling with a comment, so the later integrity check fails. `order_key` is never created, yet sort and `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` depend on it. Global `ocel_drop_duplicates` on the conservative log is not justified and can remove the same-time events the prose promised to keep.
