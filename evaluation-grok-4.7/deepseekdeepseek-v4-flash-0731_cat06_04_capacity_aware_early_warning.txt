The answer covers the required structure and gets several hard calls right: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed for an unchanged repeat, I-07 is treated as new evidence but still low priority, one slot is reserved, and spillovers are allowed to make the adverse case negative. The portfolio does not follow the decision rule that is supposed to produce it. With the stated €2,400 spillover shadow cost, I-04 is the only clear Tier 1 case (about €6,550 net). I-09 nets about €1,150 and I-02 about €300, so both fall in Tier 3, yet both are selected and labeled Tier 2. I-08 nets about €2,150 and would outrank them, but it is rejected. Calibration adjustments are defined and then never applied to scores or used to cap absolute risk reduction. The later Non-Native safeguard (score on the lower effect bound) would make I-09 roughly break-even, contradicting its selection. Section 6 says to change the rule when the adverse net is negative, which the impact table already shows.

The impact table also leans on an untested €20,000 spillover value that can shrink or erase the base-case gain, omits the adverse 15–30 minutes of delay, and puts the full effect range inside the base row. False-alarm burden is deducted as a certainty, which distorts I-08. The reserve is treated as binding even though it was described as a preference, and non-monetary obligations do not affect the live allocation, including the urgent low-value SME case.
