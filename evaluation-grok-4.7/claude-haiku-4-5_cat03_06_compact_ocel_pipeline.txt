5.5

The pipeline is complete and the in-memory tables are mostly referentially intact, but the write-up contradicts its own tables on counts, timestamps, and which objects actually participate in which events. Those are core semantic failures, not polish issues.

The constructed log does include all 17 events, all 12 objects, timezone-aware timestamps, payment amounts on the event rows, the required O2O edges, and real uniqueness, orphan, consistency, and duplicate checks. Package, delivery, and invoice flattenings are largely consistent with those rows. The required discovery calls, result dictionaries, and commented visualization hooks are present.

The analysis does not match that model. Relations contain 43 E2O rows and 12 O2O rows; the audit reports 57 and 13. Order flattening is described as including E012 and E015 and as running Dispatch → Confirm → Issue Invoice → payments. In the code, payments touch only M1/M2 and V1, and Issue Invoice (11:00) precedes both Confirm Delivery events (13:00 and the next morning). O100’s real chain also includes E013 and E014, which the example identifier list drops. The same reversed order is repeated in the failure-mode traces.

Qualifier policy is unstable. Add Item and Reserve Stock are explicit order facts in the narrative, yet the order links are marked propagated. The stated rule that shared logistics events relate to items is not applied to dispatch or confirm. `source` is both a qualifier and a derivation flag, and `derivation` is never used by the analysis that is supposed to keep inferred links from being read as observations. Filtering to derivation = `source` would remove Add Item from orders entirely.

Object-centric expectations overclaim structure. The OTG is treated as the O2O table, then drawn as Order → Item → Package → Delivery and Order → Invoice → Payment, which those directed edges do not form; the same section also says Payment → Invoice. An ET–OT graph of these activity–type pairs has about 19 edges, not 40–50. The Petri-net sketch consumes the order token on Add Item and the invoice token on the first payment without reproducing them, places Issue Invoice after fulfillment despite the log, and invents settlement guards. That is one unsupported interpretation, presented as the expected net. M2 as `completes_settlement`, and 250 as “sufficient,” goes beyond two partial payments with no invoice total.

Failure modes and the six manual assertions cover the required topics, and several assertions do match the code. They do not catch the broken order view, and the payment-allocation and duplicated-event examples assume order traces the relations never create.
