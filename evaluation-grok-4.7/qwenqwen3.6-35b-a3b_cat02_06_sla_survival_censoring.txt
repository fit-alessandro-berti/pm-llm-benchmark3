5.5

The write-up covers every required section and correctly treats transfer, withdrawal, and rejection as something other than ordinary censoring, but the estimands, their worked examples, and the code do not agree with each other.

The 76-hour resolution is mishandled for the “no terminal disposition at 72 hours” estimand. That case has terminal time 76, so it still had no disposition at 72; the answer marks stagnation as no. The same definition-versus-application break appears in the estimator: cumulative incidence of resolution by 72, subtracted from 1, is the probability of not being resolved by 72, not the probability of resolution after 72. Late resolution is closer to the eventual resolution mass minus incidence at 72. Kaplan–Meier with transfers censored is also called appropriate for the first estimand, which is not the competing-risk quantity defined earlier. “Excludes withdrawals/rejections/transfers” further blurs whether those cases leave the denominator.

The code transcribes the 32 cases accurately, then does not implement that plan. Open cases younger than 72 hours are coded as non-breaches, so the printed “breach risk” is not a risk-set estimate. The transfer scenario never changes that flag, so the sensitivity comparison prints the same quantity twice, and the second estimand is never given its own encoding. Lifelines is imported and unused; no Kaplan–Meier or Aalen–Johansen input is actually built. Dummy event times 0, 1, 2, … are fabricated despite the instruction not to invent timestamps. `last_event_gap_h` is 0 on every terminal row and positive on every open row, so it is an openness indicator and a piece of age, not a usable baseline covariate; that is never noted.

Smaller but material slips: the immortal-time paragraph targets the hazard of escalation rather than the outcome hazard; the state diagram omits observed transitions (`Screen→Escalate`, `AutoCheck→Escalate`, `Rework→Resolve`); open cases below versus above 72 are not separated into censored versus already-breached; and the claim that cohort conclusions would reverse is not computed. Cohort confounding, leakage from post-start escalation, and the need for external transfer outcomes are discussed soundly, which keeps this from collapsing, but the core survival definitions are not reliable.
