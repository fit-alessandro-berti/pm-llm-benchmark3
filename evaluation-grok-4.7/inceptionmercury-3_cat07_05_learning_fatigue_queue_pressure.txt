7.5

The memo meets the required structure, respects every constraint, and gives a genuinely multi-mechanism account with accurate use of most tabulated figures. It is not nearly flawless. Several causal claims contradict the same tables, and parts of the proposed analysis are technically wrong or omit a required comparison.

The early-period story is the clearest logical error. Positions 1–5 have mean queue length 46, Early starts at 61, and Late starts at 70. Calling that “low queue pressure” is false on the memo’s own WIP bands. Night is also miscast as backlog carryover: it starts at queue 29 with a 56% high-complexity share, which the source describes as fewer events and more urgent exceptions. The claim that early errors stay moderate because complex work is “done more carefully” conflicts with the WIP table, where high complexity raises errors in every WIP band (2.1% to 4.2% even at low WIP). Senior selection is only a clue in the source, but the narrative treats it as the explanation of the aggregate 3.4% rate.

The learning row uses a confounded comparison as positive evidence: juniors’ 5.1% to 4.4% drop is exactly the contrast the notes attribute to early complexity. The circadian row says Early’s lower end-of-shift error shows that clock time matters, while that shift is also the one whose queue is falling. Section 2 then states as fact that workers skip checks and rely more on the bot. The source only supports more Bot_Risk corrections above queue 70 and deferred Quality Check.

Section 3 covers the requested designs, but resource-experience interactions are missing. Experience enters only as a main-effect covariate, despite the source pattern being experience group by WIP. The fitted model also drops clock hour after section 1 says it must be included. `filter_paths_performance` is the wrong function: it filters one pair of activities by a duration range, and the call supplies neither bounds nor a comparison of rework. The Bot_Risk merge keeps every Risk Classification, not `Bot_Risk`, and can many-to-many duplicate. The batch flag differences consecutive completion timestamps, so it proxies short service time rather than idle gap. Splitting events at WIP 70 and then building performance DFGs cuts cases apart and invents paths. The regression-discontinuity sketch creates a dummy and stops, without a running variable, outcome model, or acknowledgment that WIP is endogenous.

Intervention, ethics, and dashboard sections are complete and appropriately refuse raw-rate punishment and throughput-driven removal of quality checks. Remaining nits there are smaller: “rare events” overstates rates of 2–14% on thousands of events, and several monitoring ideas stay descriptive. Those do not offset the factual and methodological errors above.
