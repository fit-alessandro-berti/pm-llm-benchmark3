The diagnosis names the right contradiction and cites real artifacts (Auto→Fast approval 2550, complaint edges at mean 72 h / p90 150 h and 96 h / 180 h, 14.1% vs 3.3%, Premium/Standard high-value complaints 22% and 18%, the post-rework 9% cost gap, the 26% repeat-evidence rate, and the 140 h unclear-defect median). Those citations are outweighed by misreads. The 2550 figure is one directly-follows edge, not the case count of Intake→Auto→Fast approval→Dispatch (Fast approval→Dispatch is 2300). “390 repair paths” is only Customer approval→Repair device. Edge medians of 0.4 h and 1.8 h are presented as cycle time; segment fast-path medians are 8–28 h. Utilization “above 82%” is treated as observed, while 82% is only the stated queue-growth threshold. R1/R2 are mapped loosely onto the alignment list, and the third frequent deviation (reopen after close), R3, fitness 0.91, and 68% fitting traces are left out of the compliance problem.

Redesign A is a real, segment-specific design, but the mechanism overclaims. The 14.1% rate is a rework statistic, not a performance-DFG result, and it mixes low-risk fast paths (6–8% complaints) with high-value ones. “+6–18 h” counts only the gate and ignores the reviewed-path medians already in the data (96 h and 120 h versus 20 h and 28 h). Routing the 22% unclear-defect segment into manual/fraud does not match its failure mode (evidence loops and abandonment) and can make that segment worse. “Low-value devices” is not a defined segment, so mid-risk scope is unclear. The stated 15–35% manual inflow is a prompt range for rule changes; sending all high-value and unclear volume to two fraud specialists or seven reviewers is a larger capacity shock than that range describes.

Redesign B is the most defensible of the three, and the portal’s 30–45% repeat reduction and low-digital abandonment risk are used correctly. “22% + 26%” is not valid: 26% is conditional on entering evidence request, not an added population share. The table then replaces the prompt’s 30–45% with an unexplained −20–30% loop effect and an unsupported −40 to −70 h and −10–20% cost effect.

Redesign C contradicts the segment evidence it cites. Premium is 32% only if Premium high-value (14%) is included, and that segment’s fast path has 22% complaints versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path, plus high R1/R2 risk. The 20 h figure is the Premium high-value fast median, not Premium overall (low-risk fast median is 8 h). Forcing every Standard high-value claim onto repair treats a selected 3.3% repair-path complaint rate as transferable, ignores 12.5% high-value repair rework, quote waits caused by part-price confirmation, and Customer approval→abandoned (70). EUR 410 is the expected cost of a wrong replacement, not a saving on every diverted case. The design protects speed exactly where the data show speed destroys quality and compliance.

The comparison table invents effects the prompt does not support: +EUR 30–60, −4 to −7 pp, −10 pp overall, and the B ranges above. A’s cost column records added labor and omits avoided wrong-replacement and credit cost, so it conflicts with the diagnosis that after-rework savings shrink to 9%. C’s compliance cell records a gain for Standard high-value and omits higher Premium high-value violation risk. “Conditional” dominance is undefined. Several of these numbers also violate the instruction not to calculate exact effects the data cannot support.

Failure modes exist in form, but A’s migration “from fraud review to the gate” points the wrong way: A adds high-value and unclear volume to fraud/manual. The 10–15% misclassification rate and 18–30 h queue are invented. C misses its main failure: recreating the 22% Premium high-value complaint rate and R1/R2 breaches. The experiment pilots A only on Premium high-value, then justifies A as the highest-volume deviation; that segment is 14%. A six-week go/no-go cannot observe complaint tails whose p90 is 150–180 h, and the 48 h p90 go-line sits far above the Premium 24 h service-credit trigger, which is monitored but not gated. “Complaint rate 12%” has no absolute-versus-relative meaning. B is a single sentence, C is absent, and A and B use different resources, so the forced sequence is weakly motivated. The hold-out and 90-day total-cost check are the sound parts of the plan.
