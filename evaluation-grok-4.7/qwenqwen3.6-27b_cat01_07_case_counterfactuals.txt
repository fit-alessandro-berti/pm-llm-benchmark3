4.5

The write-up keeps the required sectioning, tables, labels, and the right qualitative conclusion that K-337 changes decision policy rather than merely deleting time. That is not enough to carry the counterfactuals.

Temporal consistency fails on the binding cases. K-337 runs from Mon 11:00 through a weekend to the following Wednesday (~214 h). The derivation treats the factual cycle as ~66 h, then contradicts itself (−36 to −48 h in the table, −24 to −30 h in best/base). K-104’s own timeline only moves the decision about an hour (Wed 18:00 to ~17:00–17:30) while the claimed gain is −4 to −8 h; D1 and the C1 backlog stay on the critical path, and a Mon 16:00 C1 start conflicts with the 18 h queue unless unstated preemption is assumed. K-219 starts D2 at Mon 18:30 against a 5 h queue at 16:00, then holds the decision at Wed 08:30 while still claiming cycle-time savings, so earlier readiness is not separated from earlier completion. K-411 starts D2 with I2 near Tue 10:00 despite D2’s 4 h queue.

The 100-case arithmetic is wrong: 100 × 15% × 16 h is 240 h, not 2400 h. Weekend effects are barely discussed, section 4 is short of 250 words, and section 7 is short of 220. The four-eyes constraint is missing its inequality, paths are not explicitly ordered, queue snapshots are used as point schedules, and the synthesis says K-411 adds no compliance risk after flagging false concurrency. Assumption 7’s “exactly 48 h” weekend does not match Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00, and assumption 8 is mislabeled.

Interference and the robustness ranking are directionally sound, but the durations do not support them.
