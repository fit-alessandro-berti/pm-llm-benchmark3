5.8

The write-up has the right skeleton: stage gate on I-03, different treatment of I-05 and I-07, spillover as a portfolio cost, segment calibration, label feedback, and a real monitoring plan. It is not a deployable policy, because the rule, the scores, and the chosen slots contradict each other.

The governing screen is \(RNB_{lo} = ARR_{lo}\times Value - 450 - \) false-alarm burden \(-\) a €1.5–2.5k spillover buffer, and eligibility requires that quantity to be non-negative. The reported “\(RNB_{lo}\)” figures omit the buffer. I-01’s +€0.9k is \(0.18\times9000-450-250\); I-02’s +€0.95k is \(0.08\times25000-450-600\); I-04’s +€3.55k and I-08’s −€0.45k match the same no-buffer arithmetic. With the buffer the rule actually states, I-01 and I-02 are clearly negative and fail the screen, yet both are selected. The I-01 rationale then claims a positive robust net “after costs and spillover buffer,” which those figures do not show. I-07 is worse: \(0.20\times5000-450-50-\) buffer is written as ≈ +€0.5k “after spillover.” That equality holds only if the buffer is about zero.

The same break appears in ranking. Using the printed score, \(RNB_{lo}\times\) urgency \(\times\) confidence, and the no-buffer nets the table actually uses, I-09 is about \(2350\times1.4\times0.55\approx1800\), above I-02 (\(\approx1130\)) and I-01 (\(\approx920\)). The portfolio still takes I-01 and I-02 and waits on I-09. \(p_{adj}\) is computed and then never enters the score; urgency is only time and stage. The SME confidence factor is unspecified. Filter 2 as an OR (“no prior swarm, or a new event”) admits I-05, which Sections 2 and 4 then suppress. An operator cannot execute this rule as written.

Section 3’s base gross benefit is auditable and correct: mid ARRs \(0.19, 0.15, 0.265\), \(0.19\times60\text{k}+0.15\times25\text{k}+0.265\times9\text{k}\approx€17.5\text{k}\), cost \(3\times450=€1350\), and \(3\times6\times0.02=0.36\) extra breaches. The net then drops the false-alarm burden the decision rule treats as a cost (€2,850 on these three cases). “Mildly negative” also softens the adverse band: €7–11k minus €1,350 minus €8–12k runs to about −€6k, and that band still assumes I-04 keeps its benefit. The case the model is most likely to have wrong is an over-predicted Strategic Enterprise case; if the null effect is I-04, gross benefit falls to about €3.6k and the adverse net is clearly negative. That case is not in the table.

I-08 is mishandled under the same criterion. At the midpoint, \(0.10\times100000-450-5000=+€4.5\text{k}\) before spillover, still positive after a ~€2k slot externality, with upside to \(0.15\times100\text{k}\). Hard-screening on \(ARR_{lo}\) alone discards that case while spending a slot on I-01, whose social net is roughly zero even at its midpoint once a full slot of spillover is charged. The reserve is likewise asserted (€5k override, otherwise hold) without an option value against the next case or against \(P(\) regulatory arrival by 15:00\()\).

Sections 4–6 are the strongest part. I-05 (refresh, \(\Delta p=+0.02\), no new event) versus I-07 (new log, re-enter, still lose on value and a 1.5 h window) is the right distinction. Fairness safeguards are concrete: a bounded segment floor, stratified rates, logged spillover, and a shadow set. The causal plan still ignores interference. Matching or difference-in-differences of intervened versus untreated diagnosis-stage cases attributes the controls’ spillover harm to the treatment effect and overstates ARR; congestion is a weak instrument when the policy itself creates the congestion. Weekly SME/SE offset updates also have no minimum sample size, which fights the problem’s small-sample warnings. Non-monetary duties are one closing sentence, not part of the rule.
