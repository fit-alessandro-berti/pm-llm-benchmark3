6.7

The advisory judgment is directionally sound: conditional go, digital claims first, no immediate document check where originals are statutory, and Site A’s −9 h median is treated as confounded. The write-up still fails several checks that the task makes central: DFG identification, quantitative fidelity, and internal consistency.

Process comparison is strong and covers the required features. Statutory gating, role silos, case locks, and timestamp back-entry are carried into the design. Weaker spots are causal wording for the small rework/appeal rise and an unsupported claim that Site A’s digital share rose above 90%.

The transport graph includes the required nodes and a real selection-node layer, and the prose correctly says a matching `+` control flow is not a transport license. Several arrows are mechanistically wrong: `case_complexity` and `API_latency` point into capacities, and `digital_documents → originals_required` reverses the legal dependence. The closing claim that the `S_*` nodes point into both `true_overlap` and the outcomes does not match the adjacency list, where they point only at covariates.

The evidence table correctly refuses to identify the −9 h as a treatment effect and separates FTE, API, mix, and huddles. It does not give rows to every major metric and co-intervention (volume, complexity share, appeals alone, absent negative control). The same digital synchronization saving is later stated as ~2–4 h, 4–6.5 h, and 4–6 h. Transport confidence 65 on a +0.6 pp rework change, under a simpler mix and no control, is too high.

The originals analysis is the largest error. Rules already allow `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` to start after registration, and section 1 says so. Section 5 then treats `Identity Check → Originals Receipt` (1,020) as staff waiting to start originals in 1,020/2,108 ≈ 48% of originals cases. The selected counts are not a partition: `Register` outgoing edges shown already sum to 3,910 > 3,400, and incoming edges to originals shown sum to at least 2,880 > 2,108. Those 1,020 edges are compatible with fast identity finishing before an exogenous mail arrival. Scenario 1 and the fleet −3.5 to −5.5 h recommendation depend on deleting that “delay.” Subgroup median shifts are also multiplied by mix shares and labeled fleet-median changes. Service-time versus FTE capacity is a real issue (3,400 × 3.4 h exceeds 4 identity FTEs), but the “~1–3 h hands-on” figure and the ~3.5 h identity queue split are invented. Scenario 3 does correctly show a better median with a worse tail.

The pilot has the right estimands, a logging-upgraded control, stop rules, and a three-step fidelity/mediator/bottleneck chain. It then contradicts itself: phase text uses “Stratum 1/2” for digital versus originals, while the strata row defines four cross-classes. Shares 22.8/15.2/37.2/24.8 assume independence of legal review and originals. Under the stated IQR ≈ 18 h, n = 250 per arm has roughly 50% power for a 3 h median difference, not >85%. A 3.5 document FTE split also puts a shared reviewer on both arms.

The candidate control flow is the right partial order: identity parallel to a document branch, originals before document check on that branch, assessment after both, supplement back to document check. The POWL loop still permits `Request Supplement` after the silent skip of legal review; the limitation is disclosed, but the dependency is not preserved. The Python structure matches that model and calls the required PM4Py entry points. `check_soundness` is both indexed with `[0]` and unpacked as a pair; the usual wrapper returns a bare Boolean, so the script raises `TypeError` before playout.

Assumption 6 hedges the originals-sequencing claim, but sections 5 and 9 still quote savings that require it. Eight assumptions, correct decision label, and a segmented rollout keep this above a weak answer. The quantitative and DFG over-identification keep it well below a near-flawless one.
