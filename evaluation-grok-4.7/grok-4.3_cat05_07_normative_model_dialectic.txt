The write-up keeps the required seven-part shape and reaches several defensible case calls, but it is not a strong conformance opinion. It misstates authority and model semantics, leaves both briefs short of the required concessions, and proposes a POWL whose language contradicts the pilot and emergency routes it claims to encode.

Stipulated facts are mostly accurate, including CS-9 precedence, the absent segregation rule, the PD-3 and FD-7 windows, the 312/289/47 figures, and CRM mirroring. They omit the nine Auto_Approve cases with missing eligibility attributes and the compliance observer’s later denial that she had approved weakening CS-9. Those are central to authority and to what remains unproved. “Mutable links” also overgeneralizes N4: mutability is described for older text links, while stable `notification_id`s exist. The burden rule is sensible as far as it goes, but it never separates M0 language-fitness from CS-9 compliance, so later conclusions slide between those standards.

The prosecution brief does not acknowledge genuine model or data defects, which the task required. It also asserts that SUP-703 breached an amount cap. PD-3 states no cap; FD-7’s EUR 20,000 limit was not in force on 2026-06-20. SUP-715 is grouped with SUP-712 as a CS-9 resource-rule breach. The different-human rule concerns requester and approver; SUP-715 is an undisclosed spouse/reporting-line conflict, and the materials say no rule expressly prohibits that relationship. Deviations hidden by M0, the observer’s authority disclaimer, missing master-data validation, retrospective label mapping, and the weak late CRM link are barely argued. The Request_More_Info sentence treats an extra activity as an “omission.”

The defense brief does not acknowledge genuine risk or the limit of informal practice. It places post-expiry SUP-714 with in-window pilot behavior and calls SUP-708 low-risk solely because no transaction occurred, without conceding that a preventive sanctions-before-activation control was still broken. “Concurrency assumptions in M0 are violated by legitimate overlays” is false. In M0, `checks` has no edge between sanctions and quality, so either order conforms. PD-3 and FD-7 break the precedence of the completed check block before activation, not concurrency inside that block.

The matrix covers every case and the right columns, but several cells are not opposing, process-aware readings. SUP-704’s issue is activation before quality and manual approval; sanctions-before-activation is what both CS-9 and FD-7 require, and the prosecution cell simply restates the defense. SUP-703’s defense is “None,” and “eligibility snapshot” would more naturally confirm the violation than reverse it. SUP-708 is rated low-risk in the matrix and highest severity in the judgment. A transaction before sanctions would aggravate that case; it would not reverse it. The stated facts already include no pre-clearance transactions, the same source transaction ID for SUP-711, and mapping approval one week after SUP-713. Those rows still list those facts as missing evidence, and SUP-713’s overlay is written as though the mapping had already been approved.

Several classifications are still the best available label: SUP-701 compliant; SUP-702 and SUP-704 justified while their windows and conditions held; SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, and SUP-714 noncompliant; SUP-706 indeterminate; SUP-707 and SUP-709 model or scope defects; SUP-712 noncompliant on segregation despite an M0-fitting trace. The judgment does not give the required dominant reason or a confidence for any case. “At least four” noncompliant cases undercounts the six classifications the answer itself just made. Calling M0 obsolete overshoots: it still describes the standard CS-9 path and is instead incomplete for overlays, loops, reactivations, and rejected leads. SUP-711 is more a demonstrated duplicate than an indeterminate compliance question. SUP-713’s content equivalence is evidenced, so indeterminacy is a weak label. SUP-715 can be a disclosure breach, but not on the ground given in the prosecution brief. SUP-710 is under-justified: CS-9’s silence is not yet an exclusion of reactivation from “activation.”

The architecture names the right layers and then stops. Withdrawals, required by the task, are absent. The DECLARE sketch says quality may follow activation only in emergencies, missing PD-3’s two-business-day allowance. There is no actual scope rule for rejected leads or reactivations, no expiry and non-retroactivity rule for overlays, and no alias-resolved segregation check beyond a slogan.

The code builds concurrent sanctions and quality checks, a manual-before-activate branch, a zero-or-more Request_More_Info loop, an XOR that includes rejection, and notification before close. That is where the semantics stop working. Quality is mandatory before every branch, so the model cannot accept the justified FD-7 order or a sanctions-only rejection. The loop sits only after both checks, so it cannot accept SUP-707, where both requests precede quality review. `Auto_Approve` and `Provisional_Activate` are terminal choices with no `Activate_Supplier` and, on the emergency path, no later quality review or manual approval. The resulting language both rejects the authorized pilot and emergency traces and accepts approval without activation. The task required that awkward POWL limits be stated and that the approximation be the most defensible one. Neither happens. The overlay “table” is only a column list, and the data proposal only echoes the required field names.

Taken together, the case sorting shows partial command of the facts, but the authority analysis, dialectical duties, matrix logic, and revised model are not reliable enough for a higher score.
