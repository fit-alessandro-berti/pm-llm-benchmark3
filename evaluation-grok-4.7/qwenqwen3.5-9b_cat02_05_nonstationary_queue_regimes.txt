4.2

The write-up has the required eight-part structure and a few sound qualitative judgments: the 15 April outage is separated from steady state, 16 April is treated as transient recovery rather than a high-throughput regime, median service time is not used as mean flow time, and Little’s Law is often marked not defensible. Those points do not carry the analysis. The quantitative core is frequently wrong, internally inconsistent, and in one place invented.

The primary segmentation is not a defensible partition. “Stable baseline” includes 25–28 April and is described as complex share 18–26%, median service 24–26 minutes, and WIP 12–23. Those four days are actually complex share 40–47%, medians 35–36, and WIP 20–50, including the series peaks of 49 and 50. 17–19 April are in no regime. The case-mix break is placed on 16 April, with an invented jump “from 25% to 33%.” The table moves from 25% on 15 April to 27% on 16 April and stays in the low-20s through 19 April; the step is 23% to 41% on 20 April, with p90 service moving into the high 70s–80s then. Confidence is marked high on the mislabeled regime.

Pooled “all 28 days” figures are not the pooled means. Arrivals average about 90, not 104; completions about 86, not 99; staff about 5.1 FTE, not 6.0; complex share about 33%, not 38%. WIP near 29 and a mean of medians near 31 are the only close headline numbers. The weekday mean of 108 and weekend mean near 45 are right, but the claim that the pooled arrival rate sits next to the weekday rate uses the wrong pooled value. Intervention “~115 arrivals and ~118 completions” averages in the 2–3 May weekend; over 29 April–3 May the means are about 89 and 92. The stated intervention WIP band 32–43 drops the weekend values 22 and 19.

Little’s Law arithmetic does not match the stated inputs. 29/104 is about 0.28 days, not 0.99 days (23.8 hours). 52/129 is about 0.40 days, not 0.86. Multiplying calendar days by 24 also ignores the 12-hour opening window defined in section 1, so open-hours WIP is converted as if the system ran overnight. Throughput proxies switch between arrivals and completions without a stable rule.

Hourly burstiness is mis-timed and mis-scaled. For 7 April, mean is 101/12, not 9, and variance is about 25, not 45, so the Fano factor is about 3, not 5. For 21 April it is about 9–10, not 18. With bins starting at 07:00, the 21 April spikes are 34 in 10:00–11:00 and 24 in 13:00–14:00. Section 7 calls an 08:00–09:00 count of 8 a massive spike; that hour has 3 arrivals, and 8 is not the spike. The daily `peak_08_10_pct` column also disagrees with these vectors (21 April’s 08:00–09:59 share is about 10%, not 47%), and that data conflict is never flagged. Coefficient of variation is not reported. No actual Simpson reversal is shown.

The Friday policy example says equal 6.0 FTE produced WIP 20, 35, and 43. Those Fridays are 5.5, 6.5, and 5.5 FTE. The 1 FTE = 1 case per hour assumption contradicts roughly 30-minute service times and is unused. The complex-share increase is treated as a real mix shift; the coding-change alternative is only a late data-collection aside. The earlier 7.0 and 6.5 FTE on 16–17 April is not separated from the logged 29 April intervention.

The Python does not reproduce the prose and does not run. `DataFrame.from_dict(..., columns=['arrivals_by_hour'])` does not match 12-hour rows, so execution stops before any print. Completions by hour are omitted. Regime dates are hardcoded, so there is no change-point heuristic. Two `complex_pct` cells are transcribed wrong (18 as 20, 41 as 42). A running script would print pooled means near 90 / 86 / 5.1 / 33%, contradicting section 4, and a flow time near 0.33 days, contradicting section 6.

Coverage of the requested topics is real. The numbers, regime membership, change point, burst clock times, and code are not reliable enough to use.
