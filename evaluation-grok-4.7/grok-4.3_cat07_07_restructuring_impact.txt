5.5

The write-up follows the required outline and reaches a conditional “pilot first” conclusion, but several causal claims contradict the supplied handover and utilization figures, the scenarios disagree with each other, and the PM4Py sketch omits required comparisons and would not measure the queues it claims to measure.

**Mechanism map.** Specialist utilization is attributed to the shared approval queue and the removal of a supervisor. Those changes affect approval waiting and ownership; the utilization effect comes from pooling N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech and from routing more work to them. High-complexity routing into Technical Shared Service is also fused with legal/risk/security routing, which is a different queue and, on management’s own table, a volume increase (9 to 12 per 100), not a handover reduction. The same table raises the technical and approval edges (36 to 41, 42 to 49) while cutting mainly cross-team and intake edges. “Nominal handover reduction” is therefore not reconciled with higher specialist and approval frequencies. Local-expertise loss is then observed as more cross-location handovers, which is the edge management expects to fall (6 to 3 per 100). Resilience is treated as uniformly worse, with no pooling benefit against individual absence and no separate central-queue failure mode. East’s different starting point (E_Tech at 82%, 8.6 d median, 22.8 d 90th percentile) is only a fairness aside.

**Scenarios.** The optimistic case puts median waits under 4 h. Management’s estimate is already 4.0 h after the technical handover and 10.5 h for legal/risk, and Legal/Risk are at 93% and 91% utilization before any extra volume. The central case’s “10–20% longer waits” is milder than management’s own shifts (about +25% on 3.2 h to 4.0 h, about +38% on 1.6 h to 2.2 h). The adverse case says fairness worsens for North/South, then uses SLA misses concentrated in East as the warning. Baseline cycle times, rework (8.2% / 9.0% / 11.5%), and SLA misses (6.5% / 7.2% / 9.8%) are never used as anchors. Learning and absence resilience are not forecast in any scenario.

**Indicators.** Waiting time is labeled with `pm4py.get_service_time`. Service time can stay flat while queue time explodes near the legal/risk utilization levels given here. Variant appearance and cross-team handover frequency are listed as lagging even though they are structural and visible within weeks. `local_expertise_flag` “usage variance” is not an operational measure. There is no indicator for nonlinear queue growth, absence recovery, or within-team learning.

**Hidden risks.** Tail delay, local knowledge, fairness, learning, accountability, and exception handling are all named, but tail latency and high-complexity cases are collapsed into one point. Accountability is equated with a higher `rework_flag`, which is a quality outcome, not ownership. The clearest average-hiding mechanism is missing: a 33% relative increase in legal/risk referrals onto resources already above 90% utilization, plus difficult quality-check and triage load moved onto the same shared pool.

**Pilot.** North and East are one treatment arm against South, despite the brief’s point that East is overloaded and North/South hold the local expertise. A 30% reroute does not say whether N_Tech and E_Tech move into the pool or stay embedded, so contamination is undefined. Legal/risk spillover onto South is uncontrolled. The utilization cap of 85% and the 6 h shared-queue wait cap are already breached by today’s legal/risk baseline (91–93%, 9.6 h) if those queues are in scope, and the pilot text never includes supervisor removal or the approval queue. “>15% rise in SLA misses” is not specified as relative or absolute, unlike the 2 percentage-point rework guardrail. Section 6 then uses an eight-week decision window against the twelve-week pilot.

**Recommendation.** Pilot-first, with an explicit warning not to read early mean cycle time as success, matches the decision the evidence supports. The go/no-go rule still inherits the pooled North/East design and does not require added legal/risk capacity or separate North versus East effects before scaling.

**PM4Py sketch.** `format_dataframe` is called with `resource_key`, which is not a parameter of that function. `discover_dfg` and `discover_performance_dfg` each return a graph plus start and end activities, so the single assignment does not yield a DFG. Pre-change networks are built on the full log; post-change networks are built only on North/East high-complexity cases, so edge weights are not comparable. `get_service_time` is called on “Technical Shared Service,” an organizational pool rather than an activity, and is used as if it were waiting time. The location loop discards its return value and searches for South inside a log already filtered to North and East. Working-together and subcontracting networks are absent. So are `required_specialty` and `customer_segment` filters, a post-period rework comparison, and any SLA-miss calculation.
