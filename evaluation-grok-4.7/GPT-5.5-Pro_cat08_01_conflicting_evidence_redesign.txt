The answer meets the required structure and is clearly above a generic Lean list. It keeps fast approval for low-risk accessories, treats high-value and missing-evidence work differently, states sacrifices, and sequences pilots so gains are checked against later complaints, abandonment, and queue shift. The score is held down by process-mining gaps and a few over-strong inferences.

Evidence use is concrete and mostly accurate: 3,600/2,550 volumes, the 0.8/1.0/3.0-hour fast edges, the 34-hour/p90-88 evidence return, fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, 14.1% versus 3.3% reopen, 31% versus 9% cost, segment rates, the 82% utilization cliff, EUR 95 versus EUR 410, the 15–35% inflow risk, the 30–45% portal range, and the 6–18-hour gate. The central contradiction is stated correctly: the frequent path stays somewhat cheaper after rework, so the problem is misuse, not the existence of fast approval.

The main reasoning shortfalls:

- The frequency graph is treated as a complete routing map. Listed manual-eligibility inflow is about 2,600 against about 1,400 listed outflow; fraud/risk, repair, dispatch, and complaint edges also fail to balance, and listed endings cover about 3,625 of 5,000 cases. That gap matters because Redesign A sends more work into manual review whose current exits are not fully observed. Edge counts are also not case counts once evidence repeats, rework, and reopens are admitted.
- Path outcomes are read too causally. The 18–22% versus 5–6% contrast, and the 14.1% versus 3.3% contrast, are selected paths. The stop/go target of “high single digits or low teens” treats the reviewed-path rate as the counterfactual for cases now on the fast path.
- Part-price confirmation is stretched. The resource note explains repair-quote delay. `Repair quote -> Customer approval` (mean 30 hours, p90 80) is then given the same cause, although that edge is consistent with customer decision time.
- Several high-signal artifacts are thin or absent. Low model precision, the complaint tails (fast-approval reopen mean 72 / p90 150 hours; dispatch reopen mean 96 / p90 180), and the visible R3 leak through escalation (120 cases to dispatch, repair, or denial without the manual-review edge) are not used in the diagnosis. “Risk review” in the alignment finding is bundled with R1’s broader “manual or fraud/risk” control, and “high-value” is bundled with “above EUR 400.”
- Redesign B reverses the stated normal order, which places evidence completion after eligibility. That can be a valid redesign, but it would change the normative net. The answer does not say how conformance would be re-baselined.
- Prompt wording is tightened from “could” / “may” to “expected” for the portal effect and the 15–35% inflow shift. Fitness 0.91 is called broadly acceptable while 32% of traces deviate on the exact control points at issue.
- The three redesigns are specific, segment-aware, and operationally different, but each is essentially one lever the prompt already names: rule update, evidence portal, pre-dispatch gate. Added pieces (hard workflow blocks, assisted intake, targeted gating, escalation reserved for exceptions) are sound. They are not a deeper redesign of capacity, remedy choice, or the premium high-value tension between a 20-hour fast path and a 96-hour reviewed path that is already flagged as too slow.
- The Pareto table correctly stays qualitative and conditional. “Strong expected reduction” is still firmer than the confounded evidence. The Redesign B abandonment threshold (“a small number of percentage points”) is too vague to act as a stop rule.
