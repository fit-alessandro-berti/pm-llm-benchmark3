4.5

The policy covers the required structure and makes three important qualitative calls: I-03 is ineligible once diagnosis is complete, I-05 is suppressed because nothing new occurred, and spillovers, segment bias, and label feedback are at least discussed. Those choices are undermined by an objective function that cannot be implemented as written and by an impact table whose quantities contradict the stated inputs.

The decision rule sets expected benefit equal to adjusted breach probability times value, minus €450, minus the false-alarm burden. That treats the swarm as if it removed the entire observational risk. The supplied causal quantity is the absolute reduction, which is much smaller and heterogeneous: about 0.15, 0.19, and 0.26 at the midpoints for I-02, I-04, and I-07. Calibration offsets therefore adjust the wrong object, and the stated spillover penalty is never calculated case by case. The Non-Native-Language “0.05 adjustment” has no direction and no basis in the stated bias ranges. A probability rise of 0.08 is enough for I-02 to be treated on a second alert, while the written rule requires a materially new event.

The portfolio then diverges from both formulas. Midpoint direct value is roughly €3,750 for I-02, €11,400 for I-04, and €1,300 for I-07 before costs. After €450, the false-alarm burden, and about €900–€2,700 of spillover per slot, I-07 is negative even at the low end of spillover, yet it receives a slot. I-01 and a confirmed I-06 clear that hurdle more plausibly. I-04 is the strongest euro case, but it is still in triage with 10 hours left, and the rule gives no waiting value, data-repair deadline, or marginal test of the third slot. Tiers do not determine the action: I-07 is intervened as Tier 2 while I-01 and I-06 wait in the same tier. I-08 would dominate the written probability-times-value score and is instead demoted for its small effect.

The base breach count of 0.6 matches the sum of the three midpoints. The euro figures do not. Case-level midpoint benefits sum to about €16.5k, not €35k–€45k; the upper ends sum to about €24k, not €60k–€80k. Optimistic “1.1 breaches” exceeds the sum of the three upper bounds, 0.82, so it lies outside the supplied effect ranges. Adverse “0.1” is likewise inconsistent with a €5k–€10k benefit on cases valued at €5k, €25k, and €60k. False-alarm burdens of €2,650 on the selected cases are omitted, the 15–30 minute adverse delay is never valued, and €15k is an unexplained spillover value. The footnote’s correct I-04 example, 0.19 × €60k ≈ €11.4k, cannot produce the portfolio total beside it.

I-05 versus I-07 is the right distinction, but a new log is treated as a reason to spend the slot rather than to recompute. I-07’s probability fell from 0.75 to 0.72, and a 30-minute cool-down equals the refresh cycle that manufactured I-05’s repeats, so it does not bind. The fairness examples say value weighting can starve I-07 while the portfolio selects I-07. Non-Native-Language cases are both automatically excluded and manually routed. A 20% quota and a rule forcing one SME intervention per hour are not reconciled with four daily slots or with negative-value interventions. Randomized holdout is the right causal instinct, but controls are contaminated by the same spillovers, and changing the live rule whenever the predefined adverse scenario loses would reject portfolios that remain positive in expectation.
