4.2

The write-up identifies the R5/R6 conflict, keeps a block-structured backbone, and correctly treats R12 as a warning. Those strengths do not survive execution. The DECLARE dictionary, the log skeleton, and the verdict matrix contradict one another, and several checks described in the mapping are never implemented.

DECLARE precedence is reversed and then not applied as written. In PM4Py, `precedence` on `(A, B)` means B may occur only after A. The dictionary encodes `(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` and `(Sanctions Escalation, Run Sanctions Screening)`. The second rule is violated by every T1–T8 trace, because screening always occurs and never after escalation. The semantic table states the opposite order. The matrix states a third semantics: T1 passes with no opinion, T6 fails, and T7 passes. Unconditional precedence also cannot express “only after escalation.” Even with the tuple repaired, T1, T2, T4, T5, and T8 would fail. R5 is conditional; the model never says so. `response` and `altresponse` are oriented correctly, and `noncoexistence` is the right template for R1 and R8, but that does not rescue the column.

The matrix does not follow the other artifacts either. The process tree contains neither clarification nor sanctions/legal activities, yet T2, T5, T6, and T7 are marked procedural passes. That is true only under an unstated projection. Standard alignment treats those events as log moves. `close`, `activate`, and `pay` are single objects reused in several branches, so the tree is not a tree and parent links are overwritten. `pt_operator.PARALLEL` is not a reliable current PM4Py constructor.

The log skeleton is likewise misused. `directly_follows` is an allow-list; listing only reject→close and payment→close rejects every normal succession. `always_before` of `(Receive Onboarding Request, Classify Risk)` does not mean receive is the start. Under PM4Py’s pair orientation it says classify precedes receive, which fails every test. `activ_freq` `{0,1}` is a sound reading of R10, and `never_together` matches R1, but the skeleton as written does not pass T1–T7.

R6 is not evaluated coherently. Resolution 1 is chosen, but the strict script would already fail T6 while the strict row marks the resource layer pass. The tolerant row marks DECLARE pass even though the only dictionary still contains the precedence rule. Ten hours is inside the 24-hour window, so calling T6 an SLA warning is wrong; the unverifiable fact is the missing joint authorization. The checker also assumes a reversed order proves an emergency.

Other encodings are missing or weaker than claimed. The promised R2 acceptance/rejection script does not exist. R3’s “before any final outcome” is deferred to a script that is not written; `altresponse` alone does not enforce it. R11 never sorts by timestamp, and it requires verification after suspension rather than merely before a later payment. Missing `org:resource` is specified as “not evaluated” but would raise `KeyError`. The mapping says authoritative layers are bold; none are.

T4’s four-eyes failure, T5’s missing reply, T7’s 34-hour opinion delay against `12h ± 2·6h`, T8’s double terminal outcome, and the seconds conversion are locally right. They are not enough, because a conformance result that does not follow from the published models is not a result.
