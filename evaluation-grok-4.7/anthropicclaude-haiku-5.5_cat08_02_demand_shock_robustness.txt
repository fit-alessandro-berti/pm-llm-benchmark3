The required structure is complete, and much of the frequency arithmetic checks out (weekly volumes, mix multiples, MV inflow ≈530 to ≈1,370/week, assay inflow ≈2,280 to ≈3,420/week, assay repeat branch 6.7% to 14.8%, hold share 1.8% to 7.5%, batch-wait share 4.4% to 12.7%, direct-release and queue figures). Conflict handling for raw I1, I2, I3, I4, I8, and I9 is directionally sound, hysteresis is specified, and the MV utilization-versus-throughput inconsistency is a real finding. The redesign is still materially wrong in places that change the recommendation.

The central MV claim is contradicted by the logs. In both periods, technical-validation → release is almost the same size as Routine volume (11,700 vs about 11,200 normal; 4,050 vs about 4,160 peak). MV inflow tracks Urgent and Complex, which the resource note says need a medical validator, and Complex is 3.1× more likely to need MV. “Much of that [peak 42% MV branch] is routine” is not supported. R4 therefore does not relieve the peak MV bottleneck if it only auto-releases clean Routine work, because that work already bypasses MV. Auto-releasing Urgent work conflicts with the stated requirement that medical validators are needed for Urgent cases. The governance caveat records the risk and still leaves R4 as a core robust lever, including a table cell that says Urgent release gets faster.

M1 is treated as the 98% pool. The 98% figure is for M1 and M2 together. Separate M1 slack is not in the data, and technical validation at 101% has less slack than the assay pool. “M1 has the least slack” is false, and it is repeated in the trade-off table. Under unmodified I3, Routine repeats are not shown to jump onto M1; that competition is introduced later as a policy, then described as if it were a consequence of I3 itself.

Repeat-after-failure orders and the Repeat-assay activity are merged. The order type moves only from 8% to 9% (about ×1.6 in weekly count). The activity branch moves from 6.7% to 14.8%. S2’s causal sentence assigns the activity surge to the order type.

Other concrete faults: “Batch wait → Assay doubles or worse” is not implied by changing batch size from 12 to 24. The 4-week cross-training note is attached to technical validators, then applied to preparation-bench staff. P1’s “weekly volume above 1.3× baseline for two consecutive days” is not an observable trigger. P1 also starts Complex preparation first in the morning while Urgent has the 18-hour penalty, without a starvation analysis. Peak median Urgent time is 17h, so “about half are near or over 18h” does not follow; that inference is reused as a dashboard baseline. I10’s 12h transport is called slower than waiting out a one-day specialist absence while the peak specialist queue is 36h and Hold → MV is 48h. “Recollection-loop cycle time” is the single edge Request recollection → Receive sample (42h/60h), not the full loop. R6’s “fewer defect-driven loops” conflates accession correction with recollection. Courier smoothing is marked neutral for Complex. The technical-validation band leaves 8–12h undefined. S6 says admission control is required, and no admission rule is specified. R1 never states the reserved share, while its assumption (“Urgent share below about 35%”) omits Complex, which is also inside the reserve and takes the combined peak share to about 49%.
