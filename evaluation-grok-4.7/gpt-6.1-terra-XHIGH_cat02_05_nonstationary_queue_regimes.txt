The calculations that can be checked from the table are accurate, the outage is not treated as capacity, and the main caveats are the right ones. The score is held down by an unlabeled data claim, a pooled Little’s Law diagnostic that conflicts with the essay’s own pooling thesis, and several places where a sharper comparison was available and not made.

The audit is strong. Totals 2,519 arrivals, 2,409 completions, and net +110 are correct, as are the pooled rates 7.50 and 7.17 per open hour and mean WIP 29.9. Quantiles are kept as ranges rather than averaged. The hourly vectors really do sum to the daily totals and really do contradict `peak_08_10_pct` (about 16.8, 18.1, 9.6, and 11.8 percent versus 31, 32, 47, and 22). Refusing to prefer either source is the correct evidence rule. Regime rates, nets, WIP, and FTE match the rows: early weekdays 8.35/8.30 with net +4 and WIP 20.3; outage 8.75/4.83 with net +47; recovery 8.79/10.04 with net −30; high-mix weekdays 9.50/8.38 with net +94; Apr 20–28 net +106; Apr 6–14 completions 776 over 108 hours, WIP 18.67, ratio 2.60. Sample Fano factors and CVs match, and the WIP autocorrelations are about 0.61 and 0.63. The change-point claim is consistent with a two-day standardized jump on those three features.

The serious evidence slip is in the audit and again in section 6. The prompt never says the 15-minute WIP snapshots are opening-hours only. The answer states that as fact, then uses it to put the Little’s Law ratio in “open hours.” Open-clock accounting is later called an assumption; the snapshot window itself is not. That unit is doing real work in the only reported flow time.

That flow time is also the weakest inference. Apr 6–14 is 7 weekdays and 2 weekend days, not a weekly cycle. On the same early definitions, weekday WIP and completion rate imply about 2.4 open hours, while the early weekend days imply about 4.0. The reported 2.60 is a composition blend, which is exactly the distortion sections 4 and 5 warn about. Calling the window non-homogeneous and the result “approximate only” limits the damage, but the stratified ratios were the required sensitivity check and they are absent. No row is wrongly marked `applicable`. Recovery and the outage are correctly `not defensible`.

The alternative segmentation is a real second story, but it stays schematic: no boundaries, no rates, no WIP. Lag-7 correlation nearly equal to lag-1 is reported and then folded into a list of causes. It is evidence against a short-memory carry-over AR, and that comparison is not drawn. Service quantiles worsen on Apr 15–17 while complex share is still about 25 percent, then ease after Apr 29 while complex share stays in the mid-40s. The table contains that separation of mix from other shocks; the text never states it. The Simpson discussion correctly refuses a fake reversal, yet it never shows a within-stratum contrast, so “Simpson-like” stays a composition slogan. `numpy` is imported and unused. None of these are arithmetic failures. Together they keep the piece short of nearly flawless.
