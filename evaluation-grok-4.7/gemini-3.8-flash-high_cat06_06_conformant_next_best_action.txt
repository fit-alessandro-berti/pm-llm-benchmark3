6.5

The required six-part structure is complete, the conformance labels match the permitted set, and the normative ordering is directionally right: A and B stay last, G stays disabled until both override conditions exist, and the preferred path is an independent second review with parallel screening and no immediate hit to P-901. The lexicographic point in section 4 is also right: a favorable expected-loss forecast cannot legalize a missing second review or a self-certified four-eyes check.

Those strengths do not make the analysis reliable. The rank-1 timeline is not entailed by the case. The only stated second-review duration is 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute. The memo instead uses an unexplained 15–25 minute “expedited” review, then treats S4 at 35 minutes as making both SLAs and S4 at 65 minutes as only a 0–15 minute miss. Under the stated durations, S4 at 65 minutes misses by a wide margin, and S4 at 35 minutes meets 90 minutes only on the fast side of review. That unsupported compression is what makes D look operationally cheap, so the €0–€35,000 harm cell and the “both meet their SLAs” claim in section 5 are overstated.

The trees add false precision. False-positive 0.60–0.70, true-positive 0.20–0.30, inconclusive 0.10, customer-response 0.65–0.80, and director-refusal 0.70–0.80 are not in the evidence. Neither are the 0.20, 0.45, and 0.80 cutoffs, the claim that alternate sanctions screening drops the risk score below 70, or a cryptographic-signature requirement for the director. Alternate screening can inform the unresolved 0.46 match; it is not shown to refresh the 12-minute-old score of 72, and a cleared name with a score still at or above 70 would still require second-officer review. Rule 3 would still bar auto-clear unless the score is below 40 and there is no alert. The memo never separates those branches.

Several comparisons are internally inconsistent or arithmetically loose. Section 2 sends a confirmed hit through S4 and then a possible SAR; section 6 reranks immediate rejection to first on an invented 0.80 score. The table calls E’s false-clear risk zero, then the E tree admits document-veracity uncertainty, and it narrows customer harm to €15,000–€50,000 against the given €15,000–€80,000. P-901’s €2,400–€9,800 spillover treats P-901’s loss as equal to P-884’s €20,000–€35,000, which is not given, and the table states that product as fact. Benefit and harm columns are mostly conditional scenario magnitudes labeled as expectations. “€0 regulatory risk,” “100% conformance,” and “completely eliminates” exposure overstate what a conformant review can guarantee. Action F is also mis-specified: demanding a confirmed SDN identity replaces the unresolved-match rationale, and Rule 6 requires the second officer before approval or execution, not clearly before rejection. A properly completed Rule 10 override is permitted; G2 still describes that completed path as an unlawful sanctions bypass.

The primary recommendation remains a plausible conformant plan, but the SLA arithmetic, invented branch probabilities, and threshold rules are material, and 0.85 confidence is too high for the stated uncertainty.
