5.5

The write-up covers all seven required sections and is right about the fitness collapse (0.96 → 0.71 → 0.82 → 0.90), the hospital fill-rate hold versus the pharmacy drop, and the need to retire skip-authorization and unscanned dispatch. Those strengths do not carry the judgment. Core classifications, several cited “facts,” and the recovery design are wrong or internally inconsistent on the exact points this case is meant to test.

The timeline merges T1 into T2 and dates both as Day 0 afternoon through Day 2, so the morning outage — the moment the inventory snapshot freezes — never appears as its own state. T2 and T3 cycle times and every p90 are omitted. Stabilization still has pharmacy p90 at 74h versus 49h and wholesale p90 at 120h versus 72h; median recovery for hospitals is treated as operational recovery. T3 is summarized as “metrics improved modestly,” with no figures, plus an unsupported claim that the audit queue began then. The audit activity appears in the stabilization directly-follows graph (2,300 cases in, 2,100 out). “Offset by critical-service protection” treats the hospital fill rate as compensation for the control break.

The table invents evidence and mislabels dispositions. Manual authorization is given a “lower error rate than the skip path” that the log does not show. “Prevented stock errors” and “reduced critical backlog” are stronger than the record: of 1,400 reconciliation cases, 300 were cancelled; of 1,500 backlog-prioritization cases, 450 were cancelled. The 7.8% cancellation figure is the phase-wide rate (about 430 of 5,600), not a pharmacy cancellation rate. “Dual-control missing only 2.9%” reads a 14× move from the 0.2% baseline as success, and that rate is of all cases, not of controlled-substance cases. Temporary release still feeds unscanned dispatch (1,450 of 1,650). Fast lane, deferral, and temporary release are marked Keep while their own rationales require new rules, sampling, or fairness constraints, which is Modify. Pattern E’s “throughput preserved” sits next to a pharmacy fill rate of 61% and wholesale median cycle time of 96h.

Section 3 then calls A, B, F, I, J, and K true resilience. A shipped before electronic authorization. F missed cold-chain documentation and did not stop unscanned handoff. K is after-the-fact repair, grouped in the source with the audit tail, yet L is the one called ambiguous. The closing sentence — hospital flow counts as resilience only when cold-chain and dual-control records survive — is sound and contradicts that list. C and G are the clean calls. Directly-follows volumes that would have made this precise are unused: 1,600 skip-authorization cases, 3,250 unscanned dispatches against 3,600 packs, 2,600 phone proofs, 2,200 spreadsheet invoice holds. Spreadsheet invoice hold, missing quality release, and the variable proof-of-delivery delay never enter the argument. Residual nonconformance at stabilization (76% fitting traces) is stated and not interpreted.

The recovery model has the required headings and correctly refuses to declare recovery while the audit queue is open. It also assigns a temperature log to controlled-substance and recall-sensitive orders, which is the cold-chain control, and it never states lot-level recall hold or contemporaneous dual control as separate gates. Lexical triage and a later weighted score conflict, and neither resolves a hospital ambient order against a pharmacy controlled-substance order. Evidence forms are scanned within four hours of restoration, which recreates multi-day reconstruction. The partial-recovery hazard — intake is back, inventory sync and scanning are not — has no gate. The BPMN line drops deferral, invoice hold, snapshot expiry, and the bad paths it claims to retire.

Indicators name backlog age, rework, traceability, dual control, cold-chain completeness, and a hospital-versus-pharmacy fill gap. They omit snapshot age, authorization-skip rate, dispatch-scan completeness, and throughput. The 98% tripwire is looser than baseline completeness (99.8% dual control, 99.6% temperature documentation) and is not tied to product-class denominators. Recommendations leave skip-authorization implicit, rate residual risk as low for rules that this incident already overrode, and do not say audit staffing is a backstop. The trade-off forbids skip-authorization and unscanned dispatch only for controlled and recall-sensitive products, so cold-chain remains outside the non-negotiable set, and “the recommendation changes” never says in which direction.
