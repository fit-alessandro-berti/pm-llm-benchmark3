7.5

The review is strong on structure, dialectical balance, authority, and case-level calls, but it is not nearly flawless. The required headings, both briefs, all 15 matrix rows, and the layered redesign are present and mostly well reasoned. The largest defect is in the required POWL: the repeatable `Request_More_Info` block is not a sound approximation.

PM4Py `Transition` equality and hashing are label-based, and `StrictPartialOrder` stores nodes in a graph/set. Two or more `Request_More_Info` transitions therefore collapse, and the added edges become self-loops. The unrolled “loop” reliably represents only zero or one request. SUP-707 has two, so the construct fails on the case that motivated it. `Operator.LOOP` (do/redo) would have been the defensible encoding. As written, `make_rmi_block(5)` can also make the model ill-formed at runtime. That is a material miss on a mandatory model feature, not a cosmetic nit.

Other concrete problems:

- SUP-702 is dated 2026-06-15, before the 2026-07-01 CRM cutover, but the matrix treats N4 as its notification overlay. The case does cite a stable CRM `notification_id`; the migration rule does not explain it.
- The defense was asked to address concurrency. It never really argues that M0 leaves sanctions and quality unordered, or that CS-9 requires both before activation rather than a fixed sequence.
- `Automated_Master_Data_Validation` is mandatory on the auto route, so the revised model would reject the very pilot trace classified as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That historical gap is not stated.
- SUP-706’s “Low” confidence is muddled: the facts are weak, but confidence that the case is indeterminate should be high.

The judgments that do work are crisp rather than split. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are correctly severe; SUP-705 and SUP-714 stay noncompliant after expiry; SUP-712 is separated from M0 silence and tied to CS-9; SUP-715 is correctly a disclosure failure, not a requester/approver breach; SUP-704 and SUP-709/710 are handled with the right authority and scope limits. The overlay table and event schema cover rule versions, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, emergency/pilot authority, and immutable timestamps. Those merits keep the answer well above a merely adequate review, but the broken RMI encoding and the traceability slips keep it out of the high range.
