The audit is substantively strong: it separates target leakage, complete-trace leakage, repairable as-of defects, process-impossible ordering, and legitimate t0 signals, and it does not treat Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded as the same kind of defect as Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed. Coverage of the global top 12, all five patterns, and the local-only fields is complete, and the four standards (predictive, causal, actionable, fairness) are actually distinguished. The score is held down by overclaims and a few internal contradictions.

The clearest error is the assertion that the ~35% of cases where `final_handler_team` differs from the t0 team “are disproportionately escalations of slow, complex, or fraud cases.” The evidence only says the closure field matches the t0 team about 65% of the time and has no availability timestamp. The escalation mechanism is an invented causal story, which is exactly what this audit is supposed to refuse.

Section 4 then contradicts Section 1. The answer correctly says Shapley values from the leaky model cannot be subtracted to recover a clean explanation, then still concludes that a prefix-only model will be “materially lower but still elevated” for Case X and “markedly lower” for Case Y “from the admissible contributions.” Once appeal, decision, and variant features are removed, correlated survivors such as complexity and reserve can absorb that mass. Sign and level after retraining are not identified.

`claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` are listed as credible drivers even though their t0 lineage is unverified. That violates the subsection’s own rule to use only information defensibly available at t0, and it is inconsistent with the stricter treatment of `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, which is excluded until the SQL is fixed. Both belong with the removed-or-repair list until an as-of value is shown.

Severity is misapplied against the answer’s own scale. Employee experience is an extraction-date join (unusable lineage, which the scale calls high) but is marked medium. Coverage Check → Fraud Investigation Opened encodes a post-t0 activity and should be critical beside the other future patterns, not high. `queue_length_at_t0` is called low (“routine checks”) while the write-up itself says the ±5-minute tolerance can admit post-t0 arrivals. “Almost certainly” bulk logging, and the later description of Claim Closed → Damage Assessment as a frequent directly-follows edge, are stronger than the evidence (one same-timestamp pair, a missing sequence index, and a high attribution).

The conformance suite over-encodes the normative model: a withdrawal decision is treated as possibly enabled immediately after coverage check, damage assessment is waived for withdrawal, and an appeal must be followed by a new decision or an explicit outcome. Those are plausible house rules, not rules given in the process. Smaller slips include calling duration > 240 hours a tautology of the label, referring to “reported accuracy” when no accuracy was reported, and the Case X narrative’s “will need at least one evidence cycle.”

What keeps this from falling further is the quality of the main distinctions: duration as target leakage, document counts and variant IDs as structurally empty at this prefix, the prior-claim predicate and the 23:00 snapshot as repairable lineage bugs, queue length and elapsed time retained, region and channel kept as proxies rather than deleted or declared causal, and a validation plan that actually specifies truncation tests, suffix perturbation, tie handling, and label censoring.
