The answer covers every required heading and constraint, treats East differently from North and South, and correctly refuses a full rollout. Most of the handover arithmetic checks out: 343 to 359 counted edges per 100 cases, exposure from about 588 to 722 hour-equivalents, about +1.3 hours per case, volume 2,100 with shares 39/36/25, tech pooling near 74% and a relative +15% near 85%, legal/risk referrals 189 to 252, and volume-weighted rework and SLA miss near 9.3% and 7.6%. The recommendation is conditional, and the risks section actually tracks tails, complexity, learning, fairness, accountability, and exceptions.

The approval-capacity claim is wrong. Three supervisors handle about 882 caseworker-to-supervisor handovers a month. Demand rises by 49/42 ≈ 17% while capacity falls to 2/3, so each remaining approver must deliver about (49/42)×(3/2) − 1 ≈ 75% more throughput to hold utilization steady, not “on the order of 40%.” That understatement sits in the mechanism map and is part of the case for keeping the supervisor cut out of the pilot. The direction of the warning is right; the magnitude is not.

Queueing language is loose. For a memoryless wait, upper quantiles scale with the median; what explodes near 85–90% utilization is the absolute level and the gap, not the quantile ratio. Section 1 also calls Technical Shared Service → caseworker a subcontracting loop, while the proposal lets any team pull advice and the central scenario later says the return is often not the same caseworker. PM4Py subcontracting requires a return to the same resource. The “roughly 60% never touch the new queue” composition story ignores shared approval at 49 per 100, so many supposedly untouched cases still change path. “Volume-weighted team-median” is an average of medians (about 7.46 days), not the pooled median.

The pilot is careful about phases, segments, and guardrails, but the difference-in-differences layout is weaker than claimed. Pooling N_Tech into the shared service contaminates North as a control, and North high-complexity is never assigned. Alternating weeks is a poor split next to a hashed case id.

The PM4Py sketch names the requested views, but several steps would mislead. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with `activity_key` and `resource_key`, which are not its parameters, and the inserted columns are never used. With only `time:timestamp`, service time collapses toward zero, so `get_service_time` cannot support the utilization claims. `wait_h` is the inter-event gap, not wait after handover. Edge “rework rate” is the case’s eventual `rework_flag` copied onto every edge, including rework that happened earlier. Filtering `team` at event level on a log that contains cross-team handovers drops events, shortens measured cycle time, and invents handovers. `DataFrame.assign(service_time=svc)` does not map a resource dict onto the index. `variant_shift` is unused, and sorting `get_variants` by `.get` is wrong when values are case lists rather than counts.

These are concrete numerical and methodological faults in an otherwise strong forecast, not polish. They keep the answer well short of nearly flawless.
