7.5

The write-up meets the required structure and is generally well calibrated: it separates the five network views, keeps several live hypotheses with support, limits, and predictions, treats centrality as descriptive, includes bots and vendors, and refuses to remove Mira without a replacement. The absence effect is correctly confined to exception cases, and the compliance discussion does not invent wrongdoing.

It is not nearly flawless on process-mining validity. `discover_subcontracting_network` is called with `n=2` and `n=3`; in PM4Py that argument is `beta` (an edge threshold), not a look-ahead window, so those calls would fail and do not implement the stated “within n later events” check. `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are proposed without the required start/complete pairing, despite `lifecycle:transition` being the column that makes service time identifiable. Activity-based similarity and role discovery are described as using activity order; those PM4Py routines use activity profiles, not sequence order. `discover_network_analysis` is treated as a centrality calculator; it returns edges.

A quantitative inconsistency is missed. Mira has 960 events, yet the listed outgoing handover frequencies already sum to 1640 (incoming listed edges also exceed 960). Direct succession cannot exceed event count, so those frequencies are not interpretable as simple handovers until lifecycle duplication or another counting rule is checked. Hypothesis 6 only gestures at this. The executive summary then settles on one switchboard explanation and states “limited control” as fact, which is stronger than the body’s own caveats.
