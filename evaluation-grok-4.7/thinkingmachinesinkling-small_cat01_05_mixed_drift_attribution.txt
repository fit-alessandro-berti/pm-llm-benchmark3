The final answer covers every required section, uses the specified table schemas, and gets the main mechanism split right: March is chiefly a label and duplicate-event break, while later rework, queue growth, priority redistribution, renewal mix, and the December intervention are separate and partly overlapping. Most baseline deltas, several Euclidean distances, and the refusal to treat post-May behavior as logging-only are sound.

It is not nearly flawless. The March fragment distance is \(\sqrt{3}\approx 1.73\), not 2.2, and the September distance is \(\sqrt{166}\approx 12.9\), not 14.0, so the quantitative section disagrees with what its own code would print. Section 2 is about 450 words and section 9 about 570, outside the required 180–280 and 250–400. Missing resources are incorrectly treated as corrupting alignment fitness; the mapped series only remaps eligibility and drops duplicate `Open Case`. The May cycle-time rise is underweighted when performance change points are assigned to June and October. The fast-variant drop is used as support for the July priority rule even though most of it occurs in May–June. Several timeline deltas are measured from months outside the named interval, and “exactly on 5 May” is stronger than monthly data allow.

The monitoring design is broad, but the mapped-fitness alert below 0.85 would miss the observed low of 0.86. Rule 3’s “p90 up more than 20% while median up less than 15%” does not occur in the staffing window, and rule 6’s post-recovery drop greater than 0.05 does not occur either. The code is split into two blocks; baseline changes are computed but not printed; the change-point helper is never applied; `format_dataframe` is called with `activity` and `timestamp` rather than `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`; and label comparison renames columns instead of replacing activity values.
