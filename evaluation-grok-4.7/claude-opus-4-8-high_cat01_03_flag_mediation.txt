7.3

The stage medians are arithmetically right, absent stages are kept as missing rather than zero, and the estimand section correctly prefers separable and interventional contrasts over natural direct/indirect effects because shared queues induce cross-world dependence. Occurrence and waiting are split (E3 vs E4), P2 is not treated as automatically best, and the pilot has a cluster assignment rule, a spillover guardrail, and quality co-outcomes. That is the core of a strong answer. It is not nearly flawless.

The total-effect row contradicts itself. E1’s contrast is P1, display suppression with the score and human information left intact, but the estimand is then described as the separable effect of the whole display-plus-routing bundle. Those are different interventions, and the prompt’s main caution is exactly that distinction. The same slip returns in the pathway table and the synthesis, where hiding the flag is treated as removing the priority lane and as what “genuinely” shortens triage. On these data the lane is not identified: flagged cases are exclusively T1/T2 and unflagged cases exclusively T3/T4, so priority, display, and resource are one bundle. The graph still draws `priority_lane → triage_service` as a structural arrow.

The risk-score supports are disjoint (flag 0 tops out at 0.61, flag 1 starts at 0.64). “Poor overlap” and “few unflagged near high risk” understate a hard positivity failure. A local-threshold design is the right future experiment, but this extract has no local sample, and complexity is already imbalanced across that gap (0.61 is High, 0.64 is Medium).

The unconditional cycle contrast of 987 vs 345 is mostly path mix. All 10 flagged cases are reviewed, versus 4 of 10 unflagged. Among reviewed cases the cycle gap shrinks to roughly 987 vs 810; among escalated cases, escalation waits are about 420 vs 390 (n=2). The distinctive descriptive facts are review incidence and manual-queue wait (405 vs 105), partly offset by triage wait (12 vs 150). Escalation service is the same 120-minute median. Section 3 and the synthesis still headline a threefold flag effect and attribute the tail to high arrival load, yet R10 is Normal load and the second-longest case (1512). Flagged high vs normal load is only 6/10 vs 5/10.

`arrival_load` is an at-arrival snapshot. For the index case it is pre-treatment, so it is not an exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounder. Post-treatment queue state and other cases’ routing are. The queue regression’s falsifier is also misstated: a flag coefficient that vanishes after conditioning on WIP is what you expect if the flag acts through congestion. That result does not show the delay is “not flag-caused.” The negative-control example, arrival→register, is not a pre-exposure interval in this log; in the code, that interval is the triage wait.

The P3 pilot is the right policy family, but eligibility at scores 0.55–0.85 drops the highest-score flagged cases that actually hold the long manual and escalation waits (0.86–0.93) and mixes an overlap design into a capacity design. Reversal items 1 and 3 mostly restate reasons to avoid P2; they do not overturn the stated P3 choice.

In the event log, Register runs from arrival to `triage_start`, so triage waiting is stored as Register service. End-to-end performance DFG edges then mix the next wait with the next service (Triage→Manual Review equals manual wait plus manual service). `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is called with a nonexistent `diff_start_end` argument, so that path only warns. Explicit stage arithmetic is correct and does not impute absent stages, which is why the numerical table stands, but the PM4Py objects the code builds do not measure the waits the prose discusses.
