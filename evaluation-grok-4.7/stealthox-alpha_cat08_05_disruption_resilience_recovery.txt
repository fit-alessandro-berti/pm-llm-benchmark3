The answer hits every required section, uses the DFGs and period metrics carefully in most places, and reaches the right core judgment: hospital protection and offline authorization can be resilience, skipped authorization and unscanned dispatch are concealed noncompliance, and a recovered cycle time with a lingering remediation queue is not restored compliance. The control-floor redesign, dispatch evidence gate, and “evidence existed at dispatch” indicator are concrete and well aimed. It is not nearly flawless.

The classification in section 3 contradicts both the table and the log. Deferral is called an explicit resilient policy there and an implicit rule in the table; backlog prioritization is treated as unambiguous resilience after the table itself flags informal, possibly unfair rules. The fast lane is listed as both true resilience and ambiguous even though its observed path bypasses the authorization activity. Temporary release is described as preserving control integrity, while controlled-substance dual-control gaps rose from 0.2% to 2.9%.

Several quantitative claims are tighter than the evidence. Summing reconcile, proof-of-delivery remediation, and audit-remediation arcs to “4,900 cases (~14%)” treats overlapping frequencies as unique cases, and that figure is then reused as a target. The fairness alarm (pharmacy ratio <0.85 while hospital >0.95) would not have flagged the stated T2 pattern of 61% and 91%. “Halving” and “3–7× baseline” in partial recovery are loose: traceability fell only from 9.5% to 5.4%, and those exception rates are about 6–7× baseline. Manual authorization is not shown to be part of the 44% fitting traces, and “correctly revalidated” is not what the stabilization arcs show.

Causal attribution is repeatedly overclaimed: period rework is assigned to pick lists, the reconciliation and cancel counts are called the bill for the stale snapshot, and the 2,300-case audit inflow is treated as the product of the workaround-phase throughput choice. Dispatch-without-scan is also labeled a trivial time saving without timing evidence.

Section 7 then undercuts the answer’s best point. After arguing that later reconstruction cannot prove a cold-chain or controlled-substance event, it says zero remediation findings would show the control floor was sufficient. That falsification rule does not follow.
