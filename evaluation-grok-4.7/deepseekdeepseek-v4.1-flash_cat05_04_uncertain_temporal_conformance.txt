**7.5**

The interval arithmetic, timezone conversions, SLA labels, and zeta labels are largely correct, including several razor-edge cases. The score is held down by a real bound-direction error, profile criteria mixed up with SLA limits, and a code block that does not finish the required comparison.

What holds up:
- Clock-offset direction is right: true time is recorded time minus an offset in `[a, b]`, after resolution.
- Asymmetric OPS-BER `[-90, +120]`, HUB `[R−1.5s, R+1.5s]`, SENSOR `±10s`, VENDOR-NY `[R−3min, R+4min)`, and the T-409 replacement bound `[-12, +12]` min are applied correctly and converted with the right offsets (CEST +2, EDT −4, BST +1).
- T-405 keeps both DST folds and then correctly leaves only Alert in CEST and Investigation/Containment in CET once ingestion order is enforced. Durations `[720, 1080]`, `[2040, 2400]`, and `[2940, 3300]` follow.
- Baseline classifications match those intervals: T-402 S1 and Alert→Investigation are certain violations; T-408 S2/S3 are certain violations; the 3-second and 1.5-second straddles (T-404 S2, T-408 Investigation→Containment at zeta 2, T-403 Alert→Investigation at zeta 3, T-402 Alert→Investigation at zeta 2) stay possible violations instead of being snapped to a pass or a violation.
- The tolerance and zeta matrices match those intervals, including T-402 S1 still possible at +5 minutes and T-408 still a certain violation at +5 minutes.
- T-404 and T-407 are inferred as intervals, not midpoints. The mobile entry delay is stated as an occurrence shift, not silently folded into the clock bound.

What costs the points:
- In §6, the T-407 “certain pass” condition is backwards. Max Alert→Investigation duration is `I_upper − A_lower`. Making that ≤ 900s requires a later earliest receipt (raise the lower bound to about 14:02), not a higher upper bound on receipt.
- The same paragraph groups T-401 S1 with the Alert→Investigation profile and tests both against 900s. At zeta 1 the profile band is `[420, 780]`, so that evidence target does not make the profile result certain.
- T-406 S1 and Alert→Investigation are listed with the entry-delay semantic fix, but dropping the occurrence shift only moves the lower end of Investigation_Start. The upper end stays 12:19:30Z, so those two results remain possible violations; only S2 and Investigation→Containment flip.
- §5 never actually compares midpoint diagnostics with the interval conclusions. It classifies only T-401, then prints PM4Py output. That misses the required comparison, and a midpoint log would hide exactly the straddles the interval analysis is built to catch.
- Weak T-409 evidence and absent Close events are never explicitly tested against the stated INDETERMINATE category, and §6 groups results instead of stating a minimum resolving fact for each possible violation.
