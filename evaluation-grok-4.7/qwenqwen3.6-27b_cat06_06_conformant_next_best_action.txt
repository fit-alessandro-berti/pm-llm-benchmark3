5.5

The six-section structure, conformance labels, and the decision to keep immediate execution and self-certification at the bottom are right. The ranking that is supposed to follow from those constraints is not.

The primary plan treats S4’s second review as a 5–15 minute record-and-execute step. In the case facts, 5–15 minutes is only the post-review recording window; the review itself is 35–75 minutes. Applied consistently, S4 available in 35–65 minutes implies roughly 75–155 minutes to a decision, so the 90-minute deadline is missed on most of that range. The table’s 40–70 minute continuation, the €20k–€35k benefit, the €0–€15k harm, “High” confidence, and the claim that this plan meets the deadline if S4 arrives within about 50 minutes all depend on that compressed clock. The parenthetical also adds screening, arrival, and review while the plan is described as parallel, and 15–30 + 35–65 + 5–15 does not equal 40–70.

That error collides with the rest of the answer. Section 3 says interrupting S2 minimizes P-884’s deadline exposure, while the table ranks the S4 composite as faster and more valuable. Section 5 shifts the ranking to S2 when S4 arrives at 65 minutes; section 6 switches at 50 minutes. The fallback is also backwards: a screening result that confirms a serious match is treated as a reason to interrupt P-901 and chase the deadline, rather than to reject, hold, or file a report.

Benefit figures are conditional upsides labeled as expectations. Approval probability 0.55–0.75 never enters the ranking, so on-time execution is valued as if approval and timeliness were assured. Harm bands are cut below the stated €15k–€80k customer range without a reason, and false-rejection loss is called financially recoverable without support.

Rule 6 is misread. It requires a second officer before approval or execution when risk is at least 70 or the fuzzy match is unresolved. If rescreening both clears the match and drops the score below 70, that requirement ends; the continuation says review may still be required. The same rule does not require a second officer before rejection, yet rejection is described as needing one. Alternate screening is mapped to the customer-evidence cycle, which it is not.

The stale 12-minute risk score is unused. Hold and suspicious-activity-report branches are named in the task and almost absent from the trees, including the constraint that a report does not authorize execution and must not be disclosed. Screening in parallel with an immediate S2 review is never compared, even though a 15–30 minute rescreen fits inside a 35–75 minute review and would avoid treating “wait for S4” as the only informed conformant plan.

The compliance hierarchy is sound. The operational ranking, timeline, and fallback are not reliable enough to score higher.
