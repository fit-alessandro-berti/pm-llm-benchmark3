The answer covers all seven required sections, stays on the real paths and segments, and refuses a blanket ban on air or dedicated vans. That structure is not enough to offset repeated misuse of the evidence and a large set of unsupported precise effects.

The service-safety case for shifting Standard-stock Service-contract air to ground is arithmetically impossible as written. Median cycle time for that segment is 18h end to end, and air only saves about 24h of transport versus ground. Treating 18h as warehouse dwell that can be cut to 4h, then claiming the 41h p90 “easily” absorbs ground, misreads both statistics. The same error reappears when the 62h p90 is treated as spare slack for consolidation.

Several other attributions do not match the log. CNC_A’s 1.3 kWh is energy per item at batch size 12–20, yet the indicator table uses ≤1.3 kWh as a stock-order benchmark while those orders already average 0.8–0.9 kWh. The CNC_B path’s 32 kWh and 1.8 kg are path totals, not the rework loop alone; the effects table then says energy falls 20–35% “by avoiding 32 kWh,” which cannot be both. The 31% SP2 finding is a missing justification event, not proof that those van trips were unnecessary, but the text treats them as trips to eliminate. Packaging rework of 620 is about 5.2% of all orders, not of packaged cases, and 2,040/11,800 uses a denominator the DFG does not support. Reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40%; the rebound section restates that as a 40% increase if packaging is lightened. Inspection is downstream of heat treatment on the 1,820-case heat path, so pacing the current inspection cell does not catch defects before heat treatment. Section 6 then assigns both the 470 production-rework loops and the 620 packaging-rework loops to inspection gates, after Section 3 correctly gave packaging rework to the compatibility check.

Dynamic batching is internally inconsistent: the narrative claims 1.4–1.7 kWh/item (about a 40–50% cut from 2.8), while the table claims −15% to −25%, and a 12h service-contract cap is unlikely to reach the batch sizes that produce 1.3 kWh. Many other figures are not in the evidence: late delivery to ≤7%, ≤8%, or ≤10%; critical transport to <18 kgCO2e; 100% on-time within 15h; “completely eliminates” 620 packaging loops; CMM checks every 15 cycles; an 80% efficiency capture; a 0.4 kgCO2e/kWh factor; and a 50x penalty. The prompt allowed qualitative directions or ranges grounded in the given evidence, not this precision. Promise-date negotiation can also make lateness fall simply by moving the promise, and the governance plan never monitors that gaming, obsolescence, CNC_A queue spillover, or false critical escalations—the rebounds the answer itself names.
