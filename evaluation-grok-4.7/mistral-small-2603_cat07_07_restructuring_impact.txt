4.8

The write-up covers every required section and reaches a conditional “pilot first” conclusion, but it is not a reliable forecast. Core quantities are reversed, several PM4Py calls would not perform the claimed comparison, and the pilot rules contradict the baseline.

The mechanism map treats frequency increases as reductions. Management’s own table moves local caseworker → technical specialist from 36 to 41 per 100 cases, the return handover from 31 to 36, and caseworker → approval from 42 to 49. The answer calls each of these a drop and files them under “nominal handover reduction.” Summing the listed handover types gives roughly 343 per 100 cases now and 359 after the change, so the headline claim is not supported by the table being interpreted. The same error reappears for supervisors: one post is removed while approval volume and wait both rise (2.4h to 3.0h), which is a capacity squeeze, not fewer handovers. Service time is used as if it were utilization, and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is treated as an idle-time measure. Those are different objects. Legal/Risk, already at 91–93% utilization, receive about one-third more volume for only a 0.9h wait increase in the management estimate. That wait is not credible under a convex queue, and the forecast never tests it. East is named, but the different starting points (E_Tech 82%, 8.6d median, 22.8d p90, 11.5% rework versus North/South) are not carried into the arithmetic.

Scenario bands do not match their own labels or the wait story. A volume-weighted baseline is about 7.5d median, 18.6d at the 90th percentile, 9.3% rework, and 7.6% SLA misses. The optimistic case then quotes a single ~5.5d median and ~14d tail, which is roughly a quarter off the pooled baseline and far more than 15–20%, while also assuming East lands on North’s improved numbers. Those gains are asserted while the main waits lengthen and specialist volume rises. There is no account of which loops would have to disappear to offset that. “Early warning signs: None” leaves the optimistic case without the monitoring the prompt requires. Cross-team handovers are rare (6 per 100, rework after that handover 18%), so cutting them cannot move aggregate rework the way the scenarios imply. Common-path rework dominates.

The indicator table repeats the same metrics in both columns and only changes the cadence. That does not separate leading signals (queue wait, WIP, reassignment, `handover_reason`, `local_expertise_flag`, central in-degree) from lagging outcomes (completed-case cycle time, SLA, satisfaction, skill loss). Several mappings are wrong: a performance DFG is not case cycle time, a DFG is not queue length, and role discovery is not entropy of handover reasons. Rework after a handover is misread as rework prevented (“prevents 6–9%”), and “20–30% longer waits” is stated as a fact without a model. Censoring of open cases, mix shift, and Simpson’s paradox—the main ways an early average improves while the operation worsens—are missing. Regional and language accommodation is barely addressed.

The pilot confounds the control. Pooling only North and East is not the proposed three-team shared service, and a global approval or legal queue contaminates South. Guardrails are anchored to East’s weak baseline, so North can slip from 6.8d toward 7.5d and still pass. The rollback rule “specialist utilization >90%” is already true for Legal (93%) and Risk (91%), so the pilot fails on current data. The 12-week success rule also disagrees with the 16-week window.

The sketch names the right analyses but does not compare them correctly. `filter_event_attribute_values` does not take a time range; `filter_time_range` (or timestamp indexing) does. `get_service_time(..., waiting=True)` is not a real parameter. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a graph plus start and end activities, not one viewable object. `view_handover_of_work_network` and `view_network` are not the SNA viewing API. The log is restricted to North and East before the pre/post split, `customer_segment` is never filtered, and `rework_flag`, `reassignment_count`, and `priority` are unused. Dates are hardcoded, and no numeric before/after difference is computed.

Headings are `## # 1. …` rather than the required top-level headings. The recommendation correctly refuses a blind rollout, but it still treats a 15% utilization increase as a benefit and does not say that early completed-case averages omit queued work, can be driven by easy cases, and can hide a North/South loss behind an East gain.
