6.8

The final model is a serious hybrid design: R5 and R6 are kept apart, strict legal-first is chosen and its operational risk is stated, R1–R12 are assigned to layers, and T1–T8 are mostly judged consistently with that choice. It is not nearly flawless.

The procedural artifact would not run in PM4Py. `ProcessTree` takes `label`, not `name`, and there is no `Operator.TAU`; a silent node is a leaf with no operator and no label. `tau()` therefore raises `AttributeError` before `procedural_model` exists. The loop comment also misstates that silent child: in PM4Py it is the redo, not the exit.

The tree is stricter than the policy it claims to implement. The only escalation branches put `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Compliance Approval`. R5 requires the opinion before `Activate Vendor`, so a trace with escalation, enhanced due diligence, compliance approval, then the opinion, then activation satisfies R5 and still fits no branch. That overconstraint is not stated. Replay of T6 therefore diverges at `Compliance Approval@12`, while the write-up locates the violation at `Activate Vendor@13`.

There is a direct internal contradiction on immediacy. Section 4 correctly refuses `chainresponse` because other events may occur between a clarification request and its response. The process tree nevertheless uses `Request Clarification · Receive Clarification`, and likewise chains `Reject Vendor · Close Onboarding` and `Release First Payment · Close Onboarding`. Section 5 then says legal opinion, clarification, or a bank change may sit between activation and payment. Under the chosen strict tree, legal opinion after activation is forbidden, and the external checker also rejects a clarification after activation.

The permissive T6 checker does not match the verdict table. When joint authorization is present and the opinion arrives within 24 hours, the code appends an R6 warning. The resolution table marks that resource/data cell `pass`.

Smaller semantic slips remain. Log-skeleton equivalence is described as co-occurrence; the actual constraint is equal occurrence counts. `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` also fails T8, but the explanation only cites non-coexistence. Those do not flip the main T1–T8 hard verdicts: T1–T3 pass, T4 fails on Cora’s four-eyes violation, T5 fails on the open clarification, T7 is only a 34-hour legal-opinion warning, and T8 fails when rejection follows activation. The temporal intervals and second conversions are right.

The layer split, the refusal to encode the emergency exception as a normal path, and the test matrix are strong. Invalid PM4Py construction plus the chain-versus-response and R5-scope contradictions keep it well below a near-flawless score.
