8.5

The case conclusions are correct and the event-time reading is disciplined, but the formalization and checker are not tight enough for a near-flawless score.

Classifications hold under the stated rules. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant; TX-302, TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are compliant; TX-305 and TX-310 are rightly indeterminate. Amount math is accurate, including 56.25%, about 4.55%, 24.21%, and 22.88%, and the 120,833.34 boundary. Release state is not taken from later settlement or from a same-timestamp update. EX-77 is accepted on the signature and outage record and correctly treated as unnecessary because the enhanced review already meets R1. EX-88 stays unresolved because the certificate postdates release and the only earlier-approval witness is interested. TX-311 correctly uses `event_index`, so the move to DE is after the review and R1 is off at release. Blanks are non-updates, the XZ list event is not treated as a case-attribute write, and DE plus the inclusive 100,000 threshold are applied correctly. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and logging gaps with case-specific content.

The checker disagrees with the prose. Section 2 says an unknown R1 trigger is resolved when a sufficient review or exemption exists. `result()` still returns `REVIEW_REQUIRED` when the trigger is unknown and fulfillment is true. That does not flip any supplied case, because TX-305’s review coverage is also unknown, but it is an internal logic error. R3 is not evaluated as a separate obligation: fulfillment is hardcoded `False`, and “trigger” already means an addition after the latest matching screen. The code also cannot record an exemption as affirmatively invalid; anything other than `VERIFIED` becomes unknown. Approval linkage depends on the literal note `standard review packet`, which happens not to decide TX-307 only because that case has no enhanced review. `raw_attrs` is unused. Building frames with `pd.DataFrame(list_of_iterrows_series, columns=...)` is fragile, and no output is shown. Item 8’s optional trace or DECLARE check is only described.

Two policy overlays are stronger than the text, though disclosed: any post-review change of jurisdiction, risk, or customer type is material, and a greater-than-20% spike permanently invalidates a review even if the amount later falls. No supplied case turns on the second choice. `fresh` is split across bullets rather than given as one predicate. Confidence is uniformly high where R1’s undefined “material data change” is doing real work, especially TX-306.
