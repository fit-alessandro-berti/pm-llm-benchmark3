6.5

The core language separation is right: Model A produces exactly one `Check Revision` per analysis attempt, Model B produces exactly one per case, and one-attempt traces still diverge because A places optional approval after both sides of the parallel block while B can approve before its case-level check. P2 is handled correctly, the valid witnesses (A2, A3, B1–B3) are real, `tau` is treated as a hidden approval skip, and the bounded search is conceptually sound. The write-up is not reliable enough for a higher score.

The counterexample table contradicts itself. A1 and B1 are the same trace, `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`, with opposite membership. A1 marks it A-only; B1 correctly marks it B-only. Leaving a known-false row in the required evidence table is a material defect, even with the later retraction.

The complexity figures are wrong and inconsistent with the counting code. Under ordinary n-ary process-tree structure the trees are A: 12 nodes, 5 operators, depth 4; B: 12 nodes, 5 operators, depth 5; simplified: 11 nodes, 4 operators, depth 4. The reported 13/6/5, 12/5/5, and 12/5/5 invent an operator for A and the simplification, then falsely say B is smaller than A and that the simplification merely ties B. The one-node advantage over A is only accidentally true.

The P1 notion is unstable. The written rule requires each analysis to have its own check before the next rework or close; it does not require the check to follow the analysis. Model A meets that wording, including check-before-analysis interleavings. The decisive reason nevertheless says A guarantees a check after every analysis, while the caveat says the opposite. The formal P1 equivalence only compares compliant observations, so A and the sequential simplification would come out equivalent, yet Sections 8 and 9 treat A’s extra interleavings as P1 failures. The sequential model is a defensible strengthening, but it is not “P1 as worded,” and it deliberately rejects traces literal P1 still accepts.

Smaller issues: the P3 caveat claims the unexecuted search establishes the bound; one listed positive regression trace is omitted from the assertion; EMD zero is first denied to fix support and then described as proving distributional equality.
