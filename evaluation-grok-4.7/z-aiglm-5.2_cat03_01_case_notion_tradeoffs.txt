5.5

The write-up has the required structure, a defensible lead-object choice (Order primary, invoice and delivery secondary), and several accurate local readings: payment singletons, item-trace truncation, P3/P4 co-packing, V2’s two-instalment lifecycle on the invoice object, and the converged event list. Those strengths do not offset repeated, checkable errors in the DFG claims the task asks for.

The order-log size is wrong: the converged events named in the matrix (e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27) produce 11 extra copies and 42 flattened events, not 10 and 41. That wrong total is reused as evidence in the lead-object argument. There are 8 activity types, not 9.

The central DFG claims do not match directly-follows relations. In every order trace, invoice and payment sit between dispatch and confirmation, and O1/O3 also have Dispatch→Dispatch. There is no order-log edge Dispatch→Confirm Delivery, so the “+3 frequency,” the “loss of 4,” and “3 deliveries, not 7” are false (dispatch and confirm copies are 6 each; the parenthetical itself only subtracts 3). Distortion 4 says O2’s order DFG is Issue Invoice→Record Payment→Record Payment. e24 falls between e22 and e25, so that edge does not exist. Section 7 then says this double-payment pattern is absent from the order DFG. Both cannot stand. The reconciliation paragraph says the invoice view has 3 Record Payment events while listing e21, e22, e25, and e26, which is 4; the gloss that e21 is doubled across O2 and O3 is also false.

“Divergence” is defined as one real entity being split across cases. That largely restates convergence and misses the actual within-case distortion: repeated Pack, Dispatch, and Confirm events for different packages and deliveries, which create false Pack→Pack and Dispatch→Dispatch edges. The sensitivity metric (total event-to-object rows divided by event count) treats ordinary multi-type events such as a single-order pack as convergence. The sketch also groups activity on the relations table, which has no activity column, and the propagated log is a no-op.

Correct object inventories and a coherent portfolio recommendation keep this above a failing analysis. The inaccurate directly-follows edges, contradictory payment counts, and off-by-one flattening arithmetic are material, not cosmetic.
