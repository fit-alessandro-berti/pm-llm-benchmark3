6.2

The design covers all nine required sections, refuses to code rejected or withdrawn cases as non-default, and correctly totals 48,000. It is not executable or internally consistent enough for a high score.

The primary estimand is conditional 90-day default given activation, with prediction fixed at the third event or 72 hours, before activation. That score is identified only on cases later activated. Section 8 then limits use to post-activation monitoring. Those are different decision problems: an early-prefix score applied only after funding needs an explicit store-and-apply rule, and a true post-activation monitor would use the whole pre-activation trace, not a three-event cutoff. Selection into activation after the 1 July 2025 policy change is named, but the primary analysis never models it.

The alternative multi-state target is mis-specified. An observed competing-outcome cumulative incidence (activated-default, activated non-default, rejected, withdrawn, transferred, censored) does not require a counterfactual for rejected applicants. That stronger assumption belongs only to a policy-intervention estimand. The answer merges the two, which the brief kept separate.

Censoring is classified correctly and then estimated incorrectly. Ordinary right censoring (horizon still open at 15 February 2026) is not the same object as a closed 90-day window whose feed is merely late. The sensitivity analysis censors those delayed labels at day 90, which treats unobserved but already-determined outcomes as event-free survival. Inverse-probability weights based on partner, region, and amount correct missingness only under missing-at-random; they do not identify the permanent, likely outcome-dependent partner-feed failures the brief flags. The cohort table also folds permanent missingness into the 3,600 “not yet mature” rows, and Section 2 puts those rows in training while Section 3 moves them to sensitivity only. The 12 October 2025 activation cutoff would shrink the labeled set, yet the flow table still treats all 21,600 warehouse rows as the evaluation cohort.

Validation has the right skeleton—a start-time split at the policy and migration dates, with a sound objection to random splits—but not a realistic as-of backtest. Training labels are those known by the February 2026 extract, including outcomes that would not have been available when the next period began. October–December starts cannot generally finish 90 days plus feed delay by 15 February 2026, so the mature test slice is a fast-activation subset. `post_policy_20250701` and `post_migration_20251015` do not vary inside training, so they cannot be estimated under this split. Prevalence is wrong: 2,700/21,600 is 12.5%; 2,700/18,900 is about 14.3%.

Feature rules conflict. Section 5 keeps the initial `risk_score`; Section 6 uses the last in-prefix value. Building the activity map from `decision_reason` can leak the decision. The noon rule for `documents_total` does not fix a nightly snapshot. Linked transfers that reappear under a new id are not checked for double counting. “Lower bound” performance under missing partner feeds is not justified.

The skeleton is about 250 lines, past the requested 80–160, and would not run. `default_90d` is referenced on `case_agg` but never aggregated. The feature merge expects `requested_amount`, region, channel, and `risk_score` on that table, which were not aggregated. Train/validation/test indexes are row numbers passed as case ids. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called and then ignored. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is only mentioned; prefixes are a hand-rolled three-event cut, with no elapsed-time cut and a broken activation-truncation slice. `discover_dfg` is unpacked into three return values. The leakage check misses `documents_total`, updated `risk_score`, and extraction-time resource experience.
