6.5

The final write-up covers every required section, keeps activity-sequence, typed-structural, and semantic partitions internally consistent, and treats X1/X2 renaming, X5 logistics sharing, X6 invoice sharing, and X7’s extra dispatch item correctly. It is not nearly flawless.

The preferred semantic partition is identical to the typed-structural partition on all eight executions, and that coincidence is never stated. Qualifiers therefore do no separating work on the given data, despite being sold as the reason for the third relation. X1 is also given a “settles-full” qualifier the log does not state, and the X7 invoice is said to reflect corrected contents without evidence.

The X3–X4 borderline is wrong on its own terms. A logistics-only view still separates one consolidated delivery from two deliveries; ignoring finance does not merge them. The X4–X8 “reduced structure” merge is also overstated: the first package in X8 holds both items, X4’s packages are already split, and X4 has two payments. Those differences survive deletion of Return/Inspect labels.

The canonical routine is not reliably reproducible. External IDs are renumbered on every occurrence rather than once per object, `lead_type` is unused, and `json.dumps` cannot serialize the tuples the routine builds. Section 6 asserts that `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` puts raw object identifiers in the key and that a small `max_objs` silently drops external objects until a shared execution looks like the baseline. Exact equivalence in that function is built from the ordered activity and typed co-occurrence neighborhood after appearance-based renaming; oversized neighborhoods are guarded or bucketed, not quietly rewritten into the isolated pattern. The outline also calls a nonexistent `ocel.get_execution`.
