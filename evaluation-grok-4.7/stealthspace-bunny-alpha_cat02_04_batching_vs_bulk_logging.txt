6.3

The cluster rankings are mostly defensible, and the separation of fixture batches, group launch, folder submission, serial handoffs, pooled identity, bulk-form timestamps, and coarse clocks is real process-mining work. The score is held down by repeated arithmetic errors, one inverted censoring window, a missing required bias, and a PM4Py block that does not implement the API it claims to call.

**Wrong quantities, some repeated.** G3’s logged intervals are [11:02, 11:30), [11:10, 11:30), [11:17, 11:30), and [11:24, 11:30). Under the answer’s own half-open convention, all four are active on [11:24, 11:30). Peak concurrency is 4, not the “at most three” stated in §§1, 2, and 5. G4’s five services are 4, 4, 4, 4, and 4 minutes (sum 20, mean 4.0, union 20). The claimed 4, 4, 5, 5, 4 pattern, mean 4.4, and 22-minute busy total are false, so the handover double-count warning has no basis in these rows. G7 naive waits are +2, −1, −4, −7, and −11 minutes (mean −4.2), with four negatives. Section 2’s “two negative” claim contradicts its own statement that four arrivals follow 14:00, and §3 reports those waits as positive 2, 1, 4, 7, 11 averaging +4.2. G1 recording lag is 2–3 seconds, not 2–5, and the gap from the last arrival (08:58) to 09:00 is 2 minutes, not 3. The G3 “distortion of 0, 16, 13, and 6 minutes” is not derivable from the table.

**Censoring direction.** “Down/up” is an undocumented rule, not an explicit floor-then-ceiling specification. If 14:15 were a ceiling on a 15-minute grid, the preimage would be (14:00, 14:15], not [14:15, 14:30). The claimed 0–30 minute service range depends on that swapped window.

**Section 4 does not do what it says.** `pm4py.format_dataframe` takes `activity_key`, `timestamp_key`, and `start_timestamp_key`, not `activity`, `timestamp`, and `start_timestamp`, and it has no `resource` argument that creates `org:resource`. `pm4py.write_log_interval` is not a PM4Py function. `getattr(..., "batch_cases")` does not read the dicts `discover_batches` returns. `whole_minute_start_complete_fields_n` requires both timestamps to fall on an exact hour (`minute == 0`), so it misses minute granularity. `min_batch_size=3` is a minimum: it does not drop 4-case batches, and G5 is a fourth 4-case cluster. The six settings would therefore not produce the comparison described. The code’s own overlap sweep would print G3 peak 4 and G7 mean wait −4.2, contradicting the prose.

**Coverage gaps.** Queue, service, utilization, throughput, and bottlenecks are discussed with mechanisms, including a clear account of why one bulk 17:00 stamp inflates wait and zeroes service. Conformance bias is never analyzed, though the task asks for it. The §5 “trusted IDs” cell says person-level utilization is unavailable for G4 and G8, which is the opposite of what trusting T-D and T-H implies. The signature matrix is cluster-by-observation rather than a hypothesis-by-signature matrix, although the columns requested are present.

**What holds up.** Assumptions A1–A4 are labeled. G1’s fixture reading stays provisional. G2’s common start is separated from unproven four-way manual work. G6 is correctly treated as lifecycle contamination, with 724/7 ≈ 103.43 minutes and the 420 cases/hour trap. G8’s safety-clock gap is not over-identified. The redesign covers execution versus recording time, clocks, badges, batch IDs, capacity, pause/resume, and precision metadata, and §7’s include / censor / validate split matches those diagnoses.
