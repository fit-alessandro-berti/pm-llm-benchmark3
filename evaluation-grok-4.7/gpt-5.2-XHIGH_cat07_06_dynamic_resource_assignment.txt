6.3

The write-up covers every required section and many individual assignments are defensible, but the scoring rule contradicts its own objective, two assignments break a constraint the answer itself labels as hard, and the training quota is met only by that breach. Those are design errors, not presentation nits.

The SLA term is inverted. `slack = remaining − (service + queue + handover)` and the code returns `-slack`, then picks the maximum. A slower or higher-handover resource produces a worse slack and therefore a higher score. With `w_sla = 5`, a few hours of extra service time outweigh the skill term (`w_skill = 3`). On an urgent case the published rule prefers the weak match. The queue table does the opposite (specialists on C001, C002, C011), so the assignments are not the output of the stated algorithm. `sort_by_priority_then_sla` is also undefined, because that score depends on the resource.

Overtime is declared a must-hold rule, then implemented only as a soft penalty of `0.8` inside `workload_fairness_score`, and then broken. C003 (normal, 18h, handover only medium, complexity 0.66) goes to R4_David, who already has 14 open cases, 3.0 hours left, and high overtime risk, even though R3_Celine has Diagnosis skill 3, low overtime risk, and 6.0 hours. Crossing a reassignment count of 2 is a real cost; it is not the exception the constraint allows (“urgent and no safer alternative”). C012 (low, 48h) goes to R2_Bruno (12 open, 4.0 hours, high overtime) for training. That case is the opposite of the allowed exception. The risk note admits the choice and does not repair it.

The 20% training figure is therefore unsafe. Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, and C015 (9). The only clean match is C009: R1_Amal, Diagnosis skill 1, training priority diagnosis, medium overtime, supervised by a skill-3 resource. Security trainees named in the scenario cannot be used: R5_Eva and R10_Jonas have Security skill 0, and skill 0 is forbidden even with R7/R8 supervision. Approval training priorities sit on R4 and R8, both Approval skill 0, so they cannot prepare or finalize C010. Negotiation has no normal/low case. Quality training exists only on high-overtime Bruno. A correct result is about 1/9, with an explicit shortfall, not 2/9 bought by C012. C015 and C010 never state that the scenario notes are infeasible under the skill rules.

Other policy defects: the resilience “bottleneck” term is identical for every candidate of a case, so it does not affect the choice. `quality_batch` is computed and never used. Continuity makes every non-owner ineligible when handover and complexity are both high and the owner’s skill is at least 2, with no hours or overtime escape. Random tie-breaks are not reproducible for audit. `normalize` is undefined. Reason strings in the algorithm (“top contributors”, “needs_supervision”) do not match the audit check for `SUPERVISED_BY=R7_Gita|R8_Hugo`.

Workload in the table is understated. Primary plus named supervision, using the answer’s own rule that supervision counts toward the cap of 4: R3_Celine is on C007, C003, C009, and C016 (at the cap); R1_Amal takes C004, C009, and C013 despite 9 open cases and 5.5 hours; R5_Eva takes C005, C010, and C016; R8_Hugo takes both C001 (urgent, complexity 0.88, 3h, high handover) and C015 even though C015’s handover cost is low and R7_Gita is a skill-3 alternative. C010’s rationale (“avoid overloading R6”) fights those loads: R6 has 5 open cases and 6.5 hours and is the only Approval skill 3.

The baseline section stays qualitative. Specialist-only concentration on R8 (Security) and R3 (Diagnosis/Rework) is right if ties break by open cases, but first-available is not actually run, so throughput, SLA, fairness, and handover claims are not evidenced on this queue. “Breaks continuity only when SLA, complexity, and compliance justify it” is also too narrow: C006, C010, C012, and C014 change owner for batching, overtime, or training.

The worker text and the indicator list are the strongest parts: hard versus soft constraints are separated, the tone is usable on a shop floor, and handover, reassignment, mismatch, workload, overtime, SLA, rework, training share, bottlenecks, and deviations are all named. The PM4Py sketch calls the organizational and performance functions it should, but `filter_case_performance(..., max_performance=7 days)` does not measure SLA misses, `get_service_time` is absent, and the log has no start timestamp, so `insert_case_service_waiting_time` cannot recover service time as written. The adherence check is described and not computed.
