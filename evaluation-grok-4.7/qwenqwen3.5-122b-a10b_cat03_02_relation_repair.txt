4.5

The write-up follows the required eight-part shape and correctly refuses a unique assignment of `ev11`, new objects or events, and a forced financial close. Those choices do not offset the factual and modeling errors that remain in the final repair.

The conservative bundle is not conservative. `ev08` relates only to `IT-B1`; `scanner_ref=PK8-2` is an attribute, not an observed package relation. Treating that as an accepted `PK-8 packs IT-B1` edge at 0.95 contradicts the scanner-outage rule and the answer’s own later warning that the scanner id may be generic. The same bundle adds `ev10`–`DEL-Y` but not `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, then states in the audit that `PK-8` is carried by `DEL-Y` “via” that event link. That is exactly the E2O/O2O circularity the audit was supposed to catch. `R07` cites `N-42*`, which belongs to `ev11`, not `ev10`. Both deliveries are `NORTH`, so route does not separate them. `ev09` already links `PK-7` and `DEL-X`; it is not an open assumption.

Rule strength is miscalibrated. “Normally one order” is not an OCEL 2.0 hard invariant. Amount equality is called both a strong default and a weak heuristic. The two bundles do not actually diverge on `ev08` or `ev10`, so they are not alternative lifecycles for two of the five required decision points. Bundle B’s “complete” `ORD-B` path is mostly O2O-only, while `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` change an object’s lifecycle only through E2O.

Downstream claims are largely wrong. One new delivery event does not create a `Dispatch Package → Delivery` OC-DFG edge; `Package`–`Delivery` already co-occur on `ev09`. `ORD-B` never reaches `Pack Item` or payment. `INV-44` is an object, not a DFG join point, and one-to-many invoice/payment links are mislabeled many-to-many. Equivalent-execution clustering ignores that a shared `PK-8` can connect the orders even without the invoice. The code sketch omits duplicate checks and O2O integrity, mixes column names, and calls a non-obvious `convert_to_ocel2` constructor.
