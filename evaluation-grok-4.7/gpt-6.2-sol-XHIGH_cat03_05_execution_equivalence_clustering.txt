7.5

The clustering is internally consistent and the hard distinctions are mostly right: X1≡X2 under renaming, X5 and X6 share X1’s activity sequence but not its boundary, X7 is not baseline once Dispatch’s item incidence and the removal/replacement qualifiers are kept, and X4 is split fulfillment plus staged settlement rather than X8’s return/repack. The three relations are explicit, RST is justified, ties are handled, and the robustness claims about these eight logs follow from the stated profile.

The preferred key does not mean what several cluster names assert. `(2,2,1,1,1)` counts packages and deliveries; it does not test that one delivery carries both packages. The same gap applies to “one delivery per package” in X4. `any(a != b for a in A for b in B)` is not set inequality: it is true whenever the union has two or more ids, including when the two package sets are equal. That bug is latent on this sample, but it sits in the canonical signature.

“Expanding explicit references to the baseline” conflicts with “omitted relations are unobserved,” so typed isomorphism is not fully reproducible from the notes. On this sample the semantic partition also coincides with the typed one, which the write-up never says, while still describing them as different decision procedures.

The PM4Py outline misreads the API. `cluster_equivalent_ocel` returns one merged OCEL per exact key, not a list of per-order sublogs. `for sublog in sublogs` does not walk executions, and the Create Order anchor check fails as soon as a class contains two leads. `semantic_key`, `products`, and `strict_chain` are never defined as runnable code, and “consecutive” precedence is ambiguous. The prose about renaming, raw ids, O2O being outside the exact key, and `max_objs` dropping large neighborhoods is sound; the code does not implement that account.
