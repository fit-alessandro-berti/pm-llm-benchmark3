The policy is decision-theoretically serious and mostly auditable. It refuses a probability-only rule, blocks I-03 on stage and effect, separates I-05’s unchanged refresh from I-07’s new log, prices false alarms and queue externalities inside the hurdle, and carries calibration, capacity, label contamination, and fairness into concrete controls. The scenario arithmetic checks: upper-half benefits, midpoint and ×0.85 Strategic shading, false-alarm loads (about €248 / €1,200 / €252), three-slot spillover counts (0.18 / 0.36 / 0.54–0.90), and the two-slot adverse band all reproduce.

The score is held down by judgment inconsistencies, not by missing sections. Non-Native-Language Support is the least validated cell, yet its supplied effect floor is treated as hard while Strategic effects are haircut; that is what makes I-09 Tier A and the first firm slot. On the paper’s own warning, that floor is not a floor, so the case is at best a gated Tier B. I-04 is “firm” and “robust” on an ARR* of about 0.07 versus a shaded floor of about 0.08, which normal congestion barely clears. “Tier A before Tier B” is then broken by running conditional I-02 at 12:45 ahead of I-04. “The best fourth candidate is worth roughly zero” is true of I-01 after λ, but not of I-08, whose own midpoint still leaves about €2k–€4k after spillover; the reserve argument therefore depends on an unstated queue-order convention. Eligibility is not a clean yes/no for I-05, I-06, and I-07. Congested Δp of 0.04–0.05 is an extrapolation past the stated 0.01–0.03 band. The proposed breach-count floor (midpoint effect ≥ about 0.12) is not applied to I-08 as a reserve claimant, and the segment slot floor sits on value-dependent tiers, so it cannot correct the SME exclusion it is meant to police. Non-monetary obligation is named for I-01 and then left out of the score.
