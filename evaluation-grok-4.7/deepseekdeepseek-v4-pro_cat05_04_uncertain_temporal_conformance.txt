6.5

The interval analysis is mostly coherent and, on the classifications that matter, largely right: clock-offset direction, CEST/EDT/BST conversion, the single surviving 2025-10-26 fold triple, inferred T-404/T-407 bounds, and the zeta and SLA-tolerance flips that were checked all hold. That is not enough for a high score.

The required code does not normalize anything. It pastes precomputed UTC bounds, never uses standard-library time zones, and does not represent the DST fold as alternative intervals. `conformance_temporal_profile` is then run against a profile discovered from the midpoint log, not the given `(mean, std)` pairs, so those diagnostics are not comparable to Sections 3–4. The “comparison” is only a generic disclaimer. T-406’s entry-delay choice is stated, but it is classification-changing and untreated: widening occurrence 5 minutes earlier makes S2 and the start-to-containment profile at zeta 1 possible violations; treating the delay as record latency only leaves both certain passes (`max` about 2611 s, under 2700 s and inside `[1500, 2700]`). T-408’s close duration is also wrong (`[138298.5, 138303]` instead of `[138297, 138303]`), and Section 6 groups findings instead of stating the minimum evidence for each indeterminate or possible-violation row.
