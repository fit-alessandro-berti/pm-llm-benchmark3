6.5

The diagnosis of the net is largely right: it is a workflow net and unsound, and the waiver join is the defect. The marking tables are accurate, R1 is the right minimum-cost policy-preserving repair, the repaired net code matches that edit, and soundness is correctly separated from business alignment. Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors.

The firing helper does not work. After several abandoned, directionally wrong token updates, it calls `PetriNetSemantics.fire(marking, target)`. In PM4Py that operation needs the net and has the form `fire(net, marking, transition)` (instance or class method). The required counterexample replay would raise before printing a marking. The helper also leaves a no-op `- 0` update and an unused “consumed” map that treats output places as inputs.

R3 is mischaracterized. Adding `p_analysis → t_waive_review` does not deadlock the net after `t_analyze`: `t_standard_review` remains enabled and the standard path can still reach `fm`. The real effect is a race that withdraws the waiver once analysis has fired. Calling that a deadlock, and pricing it as “1 or higher,” overstates both the failure and the edit cost. That one-arc change also does not make analysis cleanly optional on every waiver trace.

Section 7 says the two joins are enabled by disjoint place sets. They are not: both consume `p_analysis_done`. They are merely never enabled together, because `p_review_done` and `p_waived` are exclusive.

The section 3 summary says the leftover token in `p_analysis` cannot be consumed. The table itself shows `t_analyze` is still enabled; only after that firing does `p_analysis_done` become stuck. The final recommendation and the R1 code do not inherit these mistakes, but they are final claims, not corrected drafts.
