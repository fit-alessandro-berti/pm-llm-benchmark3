6.5

The policy is complete and directionally right on the main traps, but it is not internally consistent enough to score higher. The written rule, the portfolio, and the impact arithmetic do not agree with each other.

The decision rule is the right kind of rule: hard stop at Diagnosis Complete, observational `p` separated from treatment effect, segment calibration shifts in the correct directions, repetition distinguished from new evidence, a reserve, and an explicit spillover hurdle. The midpoint calibration shifts (+0.065 SME, −0.085 Strategic) match the stated bands. Sections 4–6 are concrete: I-05 versus I-07 is correctly differentiated, label contamination and untreated spillover are operationalized, and the monitoring triggers are usable.

The allocation does not follow that rule. Gate 5 and the robust score require lower-to-mid \(\Delta p \times V\) to clear €450, the false-alarm burden, and a per-slot spillover on six diagnosis-stage cases. On the answer’s own later spillover assumptions (about €1.3k–€2.7k per slot in the base case), midpoint direct net is roughly: I-04 strongly positive, I-09 and I-02 clearly positive, I-06 positive if the jump is real, I-01 marginal, I-07 negative (gross benefit only €1.0k–€1.6k against ~€500 of direct cost plus one slot’s externality). The selected set is I-01, I-04, and I-07. That keeps the best case, adds a borderline triage case with 7 hours left, and spends a scarce slot on the weakest euro case, while every Diagnosis Started case (I-02, I-06, I-09) waits. I-07 is also marked unconditional “Intervene” in the table, then “review, and deploy only if the log is actionable” in section 4. I-01’s rationale calls €1.6k–€3.2k gross “strong,” which is not true once spillover is included. The formula is never computed for any case, so the tiers are not auditable. I-05 is correctly denied extra priority, but “no prior swarm” is treated like a repeat-swarm ban; repetition without a new event should remove incremental evidence, not automatically erase a never-actioned case.

The base impact row contradicts itself. The component sketch (I-01 ~€2.3k, I-04 ~€7k–€9k, I-07 ~€1.0k–€1.3k) implies about 0.57–0.67 expected breaches avoided, not the stated 0.35–0.55. Endpoint gross benefits, €1,350 labor, €2,300 false-alarm burden, and the outer spillover product \(3 \times 6 \times (0.01\text{–}0.03) \times (€12\text{k}–€25\text{k})\) are arithmetically right, but “break-even to €6k” drops the adverse corner of those same base ranges, which is modestly negative. “0.200.52” and “clinical” urgency are small but sloppy.

I-03’s exclusion, the I-08 restraint, the I-06 imputation hold, the reserve, and the fairness/causal monitoring design are sound. As written, a duty manager could not reproduce the portfolio from the stated score, and one of the three deployments fails the answer’s own externality test.
