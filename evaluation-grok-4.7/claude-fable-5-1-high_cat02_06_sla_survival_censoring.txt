7.5

The document is a serious, estimand-first survival analysis: time origin, horizon, competing events, and right censoring are separated cleanly; open cases are kept in the risk sets; transfers are given three explicit encodings; immortal time and gap leakage are handled correctly; and the Aalen–Johansen figures that were checked (overall and by cohort, priority, landmark, and transfer scenario) are internally consistent and arithmetically right. The code path matches those figures and does not drop open cases.

That core is undermined by repeated factual and logical mistakes.

The competing-risk illustration is misaligned. Overall, \(1-\mathrm{KM}(72)=0.361\) versus \(F_{\mathrm{res}}(72)=0.304\) compares two resolution probabilities. For P1 the same notation is then set against E1: \(0.27\) versus \(0.75\). Those are not the same functional. On the P1 subset the net resolution probability is about \(0.27\) and the cumulative incidence of resolution is \(0.25\); the aligned non-resolution figures are about \(0.73\) versus \(0.75\). Censoring the three transfers does not create the “extreme” distortion claimed in Sections 3 and 4. The headline bias example is therefore wrong even though the overall comparison is right.

Compliance counts contradict each other. AutoCheck-first is 12 of 17 cohort-B cases, and all 5 non-compliant B cases are P3, including S17 (resolved at 32 h). Sections 3, 5, and 9 instead say 13/17 and list four non-compliant cases (S20, S23, S26, S30), omitting the timely resolution. Section 6 uses the correct 12 versus 5 split. The confounding conclusion can survive, but the case list used to support it does not.

Section 6’s claim that S02 is the only P3 resolution by 72 h is false: S17 is also P3 and resolves at 32 h. “The rest waited” is likewise false (S14 is rejected at 63 h, S30 withdraws at 70 h). The P3 incidence \(0.80\) itself is correct and, with no open P3 case younger than 72 h, is a plain proportion.

The restricted-mean range “47–52 h across strata” does not cover the priority strata. Overall RMST is about 50.4 h and the cohort values sit near 50–51 h, but P3 is about 45.7 h and P1 about 52.8 h.

Smaller problems: Section 3 calls the \([0.56, 0.72]\) interval assumption-free while it still conditions on transfers not counting as resolution (the transfer-as-success bound reaches 0.50); E3’s “0.36–0.44” band drops the optimistic bound of 0.28 already computed in the same section; independent censoring is glossed as “resolve like an average case”; and the lifelines branch never actually cross-checks the hand-rolled estimator.

The plan, state diagram, sensitivity reversals (especially P1 under transfer-as-success), and non-causal operational caveats are otherwise strong. The score is held down because the P1 bias claim and the compliance/P3 case lists are concrete errors in the results a reader would act on, not presentation nits.
