**5.0**

The write-up follows the required outline and gets the qualitative skeleton right: weekday versus weekend scale, a low-mix baseline, an outage that must not be pooled with steady operations, a recovery window whose high completions are backlog clearance, a step-up in complex share and morning concentration around 2026-04-20, and a later staffing increase whose throughput should not be read as a new stationary regime. It also refuses to treat median handling time as mean flow time, and the “cut staff because the pooled rate is about 8 per hour” policy is the right kind of Simpson warning. That is not enough to carry the grade. The quantities the task asks for are repeatedly wrong, mutually inconsistent, or defined in a way that cannot produce the figures reported.

Pooled intensity is not 8.0 arrivals and 7.9 completions per opening hour. Over 28 days the totals are 2519 arrivals and 2409 completions, so about 7.5 and 7.2 per hour. Weekday means are about 9.0 and 8.6 per hour, not 9.3 and 9.2; the “2.7× weekend” factor is inflated. Mean snapshot WIP is about 29.9, not 28.5; weekday and weekend WIP means are about 34.9 and 17.4, not 32.1 and 16.1. Baseline weekdays run near 7.7–8.9 arrivals per hour and about 8.3 completions per hour, so the stated 8.5–9.0 band for both is high and does not cover the days in the regime. The same pattern appears in recovery (Friday 2026-04-17 is 8.25 arrivals per hour, outside 9.0–9.3), in the high-mix weekdays (about 8.8–10.1 arrivals and 7.8–8.8 completions, not 9.0–9.9 and 8.3–8.8), and on weekends (later Saturdays exceed both stated bands). These are biased central estimates, not rounding.

Utilization is not a usable estimand here. With \(\mu\) defined as completion intensity, \(\lambda/(\mu \times \text{staff})\) is not occupancy. The code instead computes \((\text{arrivals}/\text{completions})\times\text{staff}\), which sits near the FTE count, while the prose reports \(\rho\) of 0.85–1.05. No service-rate derivation connects those values, and using the median in place of an unknown mean service time would understate load because the p90s show strong right skew. Staffing advice built on that \(\rho\), including “add 0.5 FTE to push \(\rho\) to 0.8” and “Fridays can fall to 5.0 FTE,” is not supported. Fridays in the high-mix window still arrive faster than they complete, with WIP in the 30s–40s.

The alternative segmentation does not implement its own rules. “Medium” runs through 2026-04-24 and therefore includes 23–24% days and the outage, while excluding the stated completion rate on the outage day (4.8 per hour) from the printed 8.3–10.8 band. “High” starts 2026-04-27, drops 2026-04-25 and 2026-04-26 entirely, and quotes 9.7–10.5 completions per hour even though that block contains a weekend and two weekdays near 8.8 per hour. Thresholds and membership disagree. The comparison of stories is directionally fair; the table is not evidence.

Little’s law is mishandled where the section is supposed to be careful. \(W=L/\lambda\) with \(L \approx 28.5\) and \(\lambda \approx 7.9\) per hour is about 3.6 hours, not 3.6 days. That cell fails \(L=\lambda W\) by roughly an order of magnitude (90 arrivals per day times 3.6 days is hundreds of cases, not a WIP near 30). The outage figure 5.3 hours matches arrivals (\(47/8.75\)), not completions (\(47/4.83 \approx 9.7\) hours), despite the note that completions are the proxy. High-mix weekdays are about 5.3 hours on mean WIP and completions, not 4.1. Pooled weekends are about 4.8 hours, not 4.2, and marking them `applicable` with stationarity “Yes” and carry-over “Low” contradicts the same answer’s observation that weekend WIP and service times rise after the mix shift. The code then multiplies an already hourly \(L/\lambda\) by 12, so a run would not reproduce Section 6. Boundary stability is graded “Good” where opening WIP is unobserved.

Dispersion figures are not computed from the vectors. For 2026-04-07, population variance of the hourly arrivals is about 24.9 on a mean of 8.42, so the Fano factor is about 3.0 and the coefficient of variation about 0.59, not 1.25 and 0.72. Those two reported numbers are not even the same distribution: \(CV^2 \times \text{mean}\) is not the stated Fano factor. The same break holds on the outage and burst days (2026-04-21’s arrival Fano factor is about 9, not 3.2). Peak labels are shifted: 34/115 is 29.6%, but that bin is 10:00–11:00, not 09:00–10:00. More important, every representative vector sums to the daily total and still disagrees with `peak_08_10_pct` (for 2026-04-21, about 10% in 08:00–09:59 versus a reported 47%). The audit never flags this, and Section 7 then recommends extra 08:00–10:00 staff from the daily peak field while citing the hourly spike as if it were the same phenomenon.

Flow conservation is ignored. From 2026-04-20 through 2026-04-28, arrivals exceed completions by about 106 cases, and over the full table the net is about +110. Mean WIP moves by far less. That does not by itself prove the extract is wrong — snapshot means are not opening and closing inventories — but a queueing argument that never checks \(\Delta\)inventory against \(\sum(a-c)\) cannot say how large the carry-over is. Recovery is also incomplete on that identity: the outage day adds about 47 cases, and 2026-04-16–17 remove only about 30. Lag-1 autocorrelation near 0.4 for arrivals and high persistence for WIP is plausible, but reading WIP autocorrelation as evidence of carry-over confuses backlog memory with a level shift. Assumption 1’s justification is false: 7.0 FTE already appears on 2026-04-16, so 2026-04-29 is not the first increased-staff day. Regime 4’s “rising complex share from 23% to 47%” describes a step (low 20s through 2026-04-19, then 39–47) and uses a Sunday value that is not in the weekday slice. The pre/post staffing table further re-pools that step, so the comparison offered as a Simpson diagnosis is itself mix-confounded. The clean contrast — similar complex share, higher FTE, completions moving from below arrivals to above them, WIP falling inside the three post days — is blurred by averages.

The Python transcribes the daily table correctly and would recompute dispersion more honestly than the prose, which is a reproducibility failure rather than a rescue. The CUSUM is an unstandardized one-sided path whose argmax is not a change-point estimator. The PM4Py note names two relevant helpers and correctly says these aggregates are not an event log, then spends the example on a pseudo-log that would not estimate arrival or sojourn structure.

Under a strict reading, format and regime recognition are real, but definitions, arithmetic, and uncertainty do not hold together. A 5.0 reflects a usable qualitative map whose reported rates, loads, flow times, and burst measures cannot be relied on.
