The answer meets every required section and is strong on partial-order transport, confounding, and a segmented pilot. It is not nearly flawless: several quantitative and model-fidelity errors are internal contradictions, not cosmetic wording.

The transport-confidence column is not a single scale. Outcome rows use low scores to mean “do not move this effect” (median cycle time at 15), while co-intervention rows use high scores for conclusions that the evidence does not transport (marketing mix at 95, no randomized control at 95, timestamp artifact at 95). Under the stated header, those high values read as the most transportable evidence. That inverts the column.

Scenario 1 calls Site A’s six-hour pre-assessment wait reduction a loose upper anchor, then sets the eligible median change at −6 to −10 hours. Whole-population median and p90 shifts are then treated as approximately eligible share times subgroup change. Quantiles do not aggregate that way, and p90 is especially mis-scaled. The 38% “non-originals” share is also equated with digitally complete claims; the problem only says originals are legally required for 62%.

The textual partial order has an unconditional `Register < Originals Receipt`, so a literal reading forces receipt on every case, including digital ones. The prose and the Python XOR do not. The supplement loop does not put approve, reject, and request-supplement as a choice at legal review: the redo is silent, and a later approve/reject XOR always follows a separate recheck activity. `cycle_time -> appeal` is an unsupported causal edge.

The pilot’s four-hour median difference and 200 cases per arm in every digital/originals × legal cell do not fit 12–16 weeks of Site B volume or the −1 to −3 hour whole-site effects in section 5. The smallest cells will miss that support unless enrollment is much longer than specified.

The recommendation, eligibility gate, wait-for-both assessment edge, co-intervention accounting, and mechanism-versus-fidelity pilot logic are otherwise sound, which is why this stays above a merely adequate score.
