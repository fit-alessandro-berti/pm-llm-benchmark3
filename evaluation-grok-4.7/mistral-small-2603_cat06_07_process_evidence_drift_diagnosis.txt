5.1

The headline timeline is the right shape—April as a pipeline break, June as a routing-mix change, August–September as overlapping supplier, variant, and Partner Z effects, October as label-immature—but the write-up is not reliable enough to act on. Several “contradicting evidence” cells are actually supporting evidence, covariate drift is assigned to the wrong months, and the remediation and tests contradict the diagnosis.

## 1. Drift diagnosis table
April as a data-pipeline failure is well supported (unknown-activity 17%, missing queue feature 14%, unmapped variants 18%, AUROC 0.67, ECE 0.110, breach rate still ~0.19). The contradicting cell does not contradict that diagnosis: 100% maturity and a stable breach rate are what you expect when scores break and labels do not.

Covariate drift is mislocated. The unrecomputed “events before diagnosis” cache and the post-20-April-only duplicate cleanup are residual pipeline defects, not a change in P(X). Real covariate shift is the June variant mix and the September arrival of Partner Z (rural distance, device age, V3 share), none of which are in that row.

Process drift is stretched across June–October. “AUROC and Brier recover after the May patch” is false for that window (August AUROC 0.68, Brier 0.197, ECE 0.120). June’s V2 jump is a mix change; July–October part-wait inflation is a performance change on the same edge and should not be pooled with June at high confidence.

Concept drift correctly uses the matured V3 breach jump (0.35 in June to 0.46 in July and 0.64 in August), but July is omitted, and Partner Z’s routing mix and missing covariates are population/omitted-variable evidence placed in the concept row. “AUROC remains >0.66” is false for September (0.66), and a Brier move from ~0.13 to 0.214 is large. ECE 0.12–0.14 supports miscalibration, not reassurance.

Label delay is right for October and too narrow. Maturity is already 93% / 82% in August / September, delay rises from 36 to 52 days, and the backlog hits long paths first. The sentence that the backlog disproportionately affects longer cases is supporting evidence for biased censoring, not contradicting evidence. October’s observed breach of 0.16 is downward-biased relative to mean predicted risk 0.23; calling the month simply “not a real performance drop” treats an unidentifiable rate as a finding that performance held.

Population shift is timed correctly (0% through August, 24% / 27%). “No evidence of unfairness once stratified” is not in the data; no stratified calibration is shown. Reweighting training back to 2026 Q1–Q2 removes Partner Z instead of representing it.

Falsifiers are not stated per explanation.

## 2. Timeline
January–March and April are sound. The April mechanism is incomplete: prefixes were built on the wrong event or not built, which is why mean predicted risk jumped to 0.28 while the breach rate stayed 0.19. May is only a partial repair (AUROC 0.78, ECE still 0.050, prediction 0.21 vs breach 0.18), and calling that covariate drift repeats the table error. Duplicate suppression limited to the new source after 20 April is absent.

June is the right qualitative call—the matured variant breach rates barely move, so the aggregate breach drop to 0.15 is mix—but “the model generalizes” ignores ECE rising to 0.060 and predictions staying near 0.20. July is not “no degradation”: part-wait goes 42 h to 65 h, matured V3 breach goes 0.35 to 0.46, and AUROC falls to 0.76, masked in the aggregate by the still-large V2 share.

August’s under-prediction (0.19 vs 0.28) and supplier timing are right. September notes immaturity, then still treats rural distance and device age as the reason the model is uncalibrated. Those features are a hypothesis. October correctly refuses to treat 0.16 as comparable, then overstates the conclusion as “no real performance drop” while the part edge is at its worst (155 h) and V3 is still ~41%.

## 3. Diagnostic tests
The required test names are present. Several expected results have the wrong sign or the wrong estimand.

Fixed-vintage backtesting is specified as “retrain on January–March and score later months,” which is not a maturity holdout. Under unrepaired pipeline failure, April AUROC should stay poor; the table expects ~0.81. Under concept drift it expects April AUROC ~0.67, which assigns April’s pipeline number to the wrong hypothesis. Under label delay it expects October AUROC ~0.64 “regardless of vintage,” which is the result that would fail the test: a maturity holdout is informative only if the metric moves once the window is fixed.

Label-maturity curves are clocked as “days since case closed,” not time-to-label or age since the prediction prefix. Pipeline failure does not predict a distinctive maturity curve; April–May are already 100% mature.

The conditional-outcome test regresses breach on the model score, variant, and month. That checks calibration of the score, not whether P(breach | same path) changed. October stratification says every variant looks equally bad; the backlog claim predicts a larger distortion on V3 than on V2.

Reweighting “training data” cannot move empirical breach rates by source. Partner Z did not exist in April, so a pipeline-failure column that predicts Partner Z’s rate falling to 0.27 is not a competing prediction. The missingness audit targets rural distance and device age, which were never in the pipeline; the real lineage targets are the activity map, the cached pre-diagnosis count, queue-feature nulls, and duplicate flags. Concept drift and label delay are given the same expected result, so the test does not separate them.

## 4. Recalibration versus retraining
Containment that rescoring April only after label normalization and deduping is right. Extending a freeze and V3 dispatch-threshold change to June is not: June is V2-heavy and fully mature.

Recalibrating on January–March reproduces the pre-supplier score-to-outcome map and leaves the August V3 under-prediction in place. A short-term calibrator has to be fit on maturity-filtered post-shift scores, by variant.

The training window contradicts itself. January–August is declared the retrain set while April–May are declared unsafe until reprocessed, and August is only 93% mature. Partner Z interactions are required in the same model that excludes September–October, the only months with Partner Z mass (share is 0% through August). Adding rural distance and device age and fitting them on January–March cannot identify Partner Z effects. A three-head ensemble is proposed before checking whether a part-delay feature plus variant recalibration closes the gap. September can be a mature-label validation slice; it cannot also be the omitted source of the Partner Z parameters.

Safe training mass, once features are recomputed, is January–March and June, with July usable and August only after a maturity filter. September is usable only on matured cases if the goal is to cover Partner Z. October is unsafe for fitting.

## 5. Monitoring and code
All eight monitor classes and a label-delay gate are present. The thresholds fight that gate: Partner Z breach >0.40 and aggregate AUROC/ECE alerts would fire on the immature October labels the dependency is meant to suppress. A remote-fix alert above 10 hours cannot fire (the edge stays 3.8–4.6 h). A part-wait alert above 100 hours misses July’s move to 65 hours. Suppressing all predictive alerts in an immature month also drops the mature-subset signal.

The sketch does not meet the API contract. It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.filter_time_range`, or `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. Imports such as `pm4py.algo.discovery.discover_dfg` and `pm4py.algo.filtering.filter_time_range` are not the top-level functions named in the task. `log_converter` is applied with `TO_DATA_FRAME` to a dataframe. `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return tuples, so `.get((src, tgt))` and `len(dfg)` do not mean “median edge time” and “edge count.” Case-id handling uses `case:concept:name` while activity/timestamp stay as plain columns, which is the standardization `format_dataframe` was supposed to do. Deduping happens before the duplicate-rate denominator is built, so the rate is not the pre-clean duplicate share. The part-wait metric is labeled in days while the evidence is in hours. Event-month and case-start cohorts are created and then not interpreted: the DFG is fit on a partial-trace event calendar, while `cases_started` uses an assumed `case_start_timestamp`. No drift detector is invented, and the function returns a summary rather than a model; those two constraints are met.

## 6. Risks
The four requested failure modes are the right ones. The October mitigation (do not fit immature labels) matches the risk. The April mitigation (repair before calibrating) matches the risk. The supplier mitigation is better here than in section 4 because it adds an explicit delay feature. The Partner Z mitigation still lists reweighting, which is ambiguous with the earlier reweight-to-Q1 action and can itself be the mistaken fairness intervention. The missing control is: no disparity claim until calibration is compared inside variant and inside rural/device-age strata on matured labels only.
