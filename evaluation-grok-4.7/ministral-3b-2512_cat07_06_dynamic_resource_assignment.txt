3.5

The write-up follows the seven required headings and names the right concerns, but the policy is not safe to apply: several assignments break hard rules, many stated reasons contradict the skill matrix and the queue, and the scoring rules cannot actually choose a resource.

**Objectives.** SLA, load, continuity, training, and resilience are all mentioned. The four-new-assignment cap is treated as a soft preference, while the conditional 20% training share is written as an unconditional hard quota. An open-case cutoff of 14 is introduced without basis. Quality-check batching (normal/low priority and more than 24h of SLA time) is omitted. The soft-constraint line that critical work should use specialist-only routing conflicts with the resilience objective in the same section.

**Scoring rules.** SLA urgency (`1 / hours remaining`), continuity (`1 / (reassignment_count + 1)`), and handover cost are case-level quantities. Applied equally to every candidate, they do not change the choice. The continuity term is described as a bonus for the current owner, but the formula never checks whether the candidate is that owner. The tie-break prefers the current owner when handover cost is low, which reverses the evidence: high handover cost and high complexity are exactly when continuity matters. Hours remaining never enter eligibility. There is no running tally toward the cap of four, no case order (urgent first), and no resilience or bottleneck term. The fallback “least loaded generalist with skill at least 1” can override the Security and Final Approval rules.

**Queue decisions.** These are the decisive failures.

- C010 goes to R4_David for “skill 3.” R4’s Approval skill is 0, so the assignment is forbidden. R4 is also high overtime, 14 open cases, and 3.0 hours left. The legal finalize set is skill 2–3 (R6 skill 3 and current owner, R5 and R2 skill 2). Skill 1 may prepare only.
- C005 (high priority) goes to R3, Negotiation skill 1, “supervised by R7.” R7’s Negotiation skill is 0, so this is not supervision. The owner, R5, has Negotiation skill 3 and a low handover cost. R3’s overtime risk is low, not medium.
- C002 (urgent, 2h, low handover) leaves owner R6, Approval skill 3, low overtime, 5 open cases, 6.5h, and goes to R2, skill 2, high overtime, 12 open cases. That violates both continuity and the high-overtime rule, because a safer specialist-owner exists.
- C001’s reason “no other option” is false. R8 is Security skill 3, lower load, more hours, and low overtime. R7 is a legal specialist and a weaker one on load.
- C003 calls R1 the current owner. The owner is R4. C011 calls R5 the current owner. The owner is R6, Negotiation skill 3, and the handover cost is low.
- C004 calls a normal-priority case low priority and calls skill 2 a trainee. Skill 1 is trainee; skill 2 is competent. Owner R1 already has Eligibility skill 3 and a low handover cost.
- C012 marks training for Quality. R10’s training priority is security, and R10’s Quality skill is 3, not 1–2. The documented Quality trainee path is R2.
- C015 assigns the work to R7 and says it is supervised by R7. A specialist performing the task is not a supervised trainee. The note’s Eva/Jonas path is illegal: both have Security skill 0, and supervision is allowed only at skill 1–2. Owner R8 (skill 3, low overtime) is left with no work, while R7 receives C001, C008, and C015 on 4.5 hours.
- C006 and C014 add non-urgent work to high-overtime resources (R9, R4) despite legal lower-risk alternatives. C006/C012 are never tested as a batch.
- C007 (keep R3, Rework skill 3, high handover, already three reassignments) and C016 (keep R5, Diagnosis skill 2, high priority, high handover) are sound. C008 (keep R7) is sound in isolation and becomes a bottleneck once C001 and C015 are added.

Valid training matches are essentially C003 and C009. C012 is labeled training incorrectly. High-overtime resources R2, R4, and R9 all receive work; R8 receives none.

**Baseline comparison.** Expected throughput is missing. First-available is credited with high SLA compliance, against the given evidence that weak-skill routing adds a median 3.3 hours of service time and more rework. The proposed column claims high continuity and high, quota-meeting development. The table itself breaks continuity on C002, C003, C004, C005, C009, C011, and C015, and it accepts high-priority skill mismatch as a trade-off. Neither baseline is actually run on C001–C016, so the comparison is unmeasured.

**Worker text and audit.** The worker note is a generic template with a `[Resource]` placeholder. It does not explain supervision, cross-team moves, batching, or how someone can contest a case, and it never uses `assignment_reason`. Several audit definitions are the wrong measurement: `required_skill` is a skill name, not a level, so “skill < required skill” is a type error; `sla_deadline` is a deadline, so adding it to `time:timestamp` does not detect a miss; overtime risk is a category, not “overtime hours remaining.” Appeal is not one of the seven activities. The deviation check covers only skill-1 high-priority work and the cap of four, so the skill-0 approval, invalid security supervision, and non-urgent high-overtime assignments would pass.

**PM4Py sketch.** The stack described is a dataframe with `case:concept:name` columns; the sketch opens an XES file and never calls `format_dataframe`. `filter_case_performance` is used as if it filtered `case_priority`; it filters duration. `filter_event_attribute_values` is called with keyword arguments and a value of 0 on `required_skill`, which does not detect mismatch. `resources`, `required_skill`, and `evaluate_score` are undefined, and a single `r.skill` cannot encode seven skills. Handover and similarity results are computed and then unused. `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_batches`, `get_service_time`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `get_variants`, and `view_sna` / `save_vis_sna` are absent from the runnable sketch. The code would not audit this policy.

Coverage of the outline keeps this above a minimal score. The assignment table and the decision rule are the deliverable, and those are unreliable on legality, ownership, skill level, overtime, and training.
