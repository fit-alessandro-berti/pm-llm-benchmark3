The case judgments are disciplined and mostly well supported. Same-user releases are not treated as automatic objective failures, and distinct-user releases are not treated as automatic passes. AP-203 and AP-207 are fairly `OBJECTIVE_MET` under ED-1. AP-201 is fairly only partial: convenience bypass, no token, thin review record. AP-202 and AP-208 are fairly `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`, including AP-208’s zero realized loss. AP-204 is fairly failed on a fabricated receipt and a common-mode pair despite a nominal four-eyes pass. AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-206 and AP-209 are fairly `INDETERMINATE`. The hybrid reading of a post-release, pre-settlement manager action is explicit and applied consistently. Sections 1, 2, 5, and 6 cover the required threat, equivalence, counterfactual, and monitoring points, and they keep alerts distinct from proof of misconduct. Timestamp arithmetic that was checked (Monday 4 May 2026, the 80-minute, 1-hour, 3-hour, and 4-hour gaps) is accurate under the answer’s own settlement-based recall clock.

The score is held down by concrete defects.

The ED-1 screen in section 7 does not implement the test it describes. `bank_change_7d` is a non-nullable boolean column, and `row["bank_change_7d"] is False` is false for both numpy `True_` and `False_`. The same identity test is used for other booleans. A case with no seven-day bank change therefore fails that check. AP-203 and AP-207, the two conforming emergencies, fall through to `screen_fail` whenever the three nullable fields are filled. Separately, any `pd.NA` among token, independence, or review substance returns `unknown_input` before hard failures are reported. AP-201 has no token, and AP-208 fails amount, bank change, AV-2 availability, manager timing, and audit timing, but both can be emitted as unknown. That output contradicts the prose verdicts. `vendor_days` of exactly 180 for AP-205 is invented at the threshold. The closing print says AP-205 appears only on the blocked-attempt list, but the flags frame also contains it. The PM4Py `positive=False` caveat and the pandas same-user cross-check are sound; the broken screen is the author’s own logic.

Condition 7 is misstated for AP-201. “Audit within one business day” is not the 24-hour recall test. On the answer’s own calendar, an audit timestamp at 09:00 on Tuesday meets a next-business-day close. Recall margin can be thin while condition 7 is still met. The missing token is enough to deny ED-1; calling condition 7 marginal mixes the two clocks. The one-business-day emergency standard is also stretched onto AP-204, which is not an emergency same-user payment.

AP-206’s prose says a logging delay would support `OBJECTIVE_MET`, while the facts do not name the documents m11 reviewed and the log shows `Manager_Approve` at 13:04 before `Automated_Validate` at 13:05. Rubber-stamp risk and an approval that could not have used the validation result are unresolved. The indeterminate classification can stand; the clean `OBJECTIVE_MET` counterfactual cannot. “Nominal four-eyes fails because the credential was borrowed” is the wrong mechanism for AP-202: prepare and release are the same person. The borrowed credential defeats the override and MG-4, which the rest of that row already says.
