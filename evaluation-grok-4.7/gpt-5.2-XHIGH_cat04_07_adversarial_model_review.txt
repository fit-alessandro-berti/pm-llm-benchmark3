7.3

The review of Models S and C is largely accurate: language-level verdicts on B1–B8 match the tree expressions, fitness is not confused with acceptance, precision-without-negatives and optionalized mandatory checks are treated as decision-critical, and no metric results are invented. The process-tree backbone is a real improvement on both submissions for P1, P2, P4, P5, and partial rechecks.

The recommended compliance artifact is not internally consistent. `assess_case` consumes the correction token on the first recheck, so a legitimate “repeat both checks” trace (`… Correct Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review …`) is marked suspicious, contradicting the stated inclusive-OR semantics. Missing `sourcing` falls through to `"compliant"`, contradicting §7.5’s own “uncertain / needs-review” rule and the instruction not to treat a conditional requirement as satisfied when the attribute is absent. Later P7 assignments overwrite an earlier `"noncompliant"`. The checker also accepts extra activities, supplier-after-approval, and correction-before-checks, while the tree rejects them, with no conjunction rule.

The tree itself over-constrains without disclosure: Supplier Review and Expedite Review are locked after the entire correction region, so `… Expedite Review, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval …` is rejected even though P4 only requires expedite after both checks and before approval. Duplicate labels for the two checks are reintroduced and barely discussed. `model_third_string = \"\"\"…\"\"\"` is not valid Python. The cost sweep scales log-move and model-move together, so it does not test alignment-cost sensitivity. Only one of the three new traces (A2) separates the third model from both inputs. The §11 rebuttal is serious but about 140 words, under the 150-word minimum.
