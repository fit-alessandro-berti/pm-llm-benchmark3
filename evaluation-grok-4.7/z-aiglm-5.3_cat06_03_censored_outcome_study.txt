7.2

The design is clearly on the strong side of the brief: it refuses to code rejected, withdrawn, open, or transferred cases as non-default, treats activation as selection rather than as a missing-label nuisance, keeps an as-of cutoff, and uses a case-start temporal split instead of a random split. The cohort arithmetic closes (48,000; 25,200 activated; 21,600 labeled; 3,600 unlabeled), the 12.5% base rate is right, and the maturity cutoff (activation on or before 2025-10-13 under a 90+35 day rule) is right. The leakage table, assumption testability split, and deployment feedback loops are substantive.

Those merits do not make the write-up nearly flawless.

The primary estimand is P(default | prefix, activated, label observed). Conditioning on label observation defines the target as the subpopulation whose feeds arrived. That is not the operational quantity, P(default within 90 days | prefix, activated). Partner feeds are called out later as MNAR, but the estimand itself defines that problem away, so the complete-case analysis is “unbiased” only for a data-availability target.

Section 3 then calls all 22,800 non-labeled, non-activated cases “never-activated.” The 5,100 still-open applications are right-censored and may still activate. The cohort table handles them correctly; the selection paragraph undoes that distinction and overstates how many outcomes are structurally undefined.

Alternative A puts Open in the outcome vector beside Rejected, Withdrawn, and Transferred. Open is censoring, not an absorbing competing risk. The later Aalen–Johansen hook is the right model, which makes the earlier multi-state target inconsistent.

The validation plan says post-policy calibration is the acceptance criterion, then says to fix calibration with Platt or isotonic regression fit only on April–June 2025. That window is entirely pre-policy. A calibrator fit there cannot repair a level shift caused by the 1 July 2025 policy change. Policy regime is also attached to case start, with no treatment of cases that start before the change and are reviewed after it.

The skeleton has a concrete failure on a required step. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is handled with `pref[-1] if isinstance(pref, list) else pref`. An `EventLog` is a `list`, and the classic helper returns a list of per-trace prefix lists, so `[-1]` is the last trace or the last case, not the length-k log. `convert_to_dataframe` on that object does not build fixed-length prefixes. `pref_df` is only printed, not used for features; features come from the landmark cut of `dfp`. Fixed-count prefixes are therefore neither correctly extracted nor protected as their own feature path. `get_variants` is discarded. `n_doc_requests` is optional, but `doc_loop` indexes it unconditionally and will throw if the activity name does not match the invented rename. The leakage assert only restates the filter that was just applied. Cohort reconciliation against 21,600 / 9,100 / 5,700 / 2,900 / 5,100 / 3,600 is a comment, not a check. Historical as-of repairs from section 5 (resource load, cumulative experience, trailing volume) are absent from the pipeline that claims to implement them.

Core statistical stance is sound and the nine sections are complete. The estimand slip, the open-case misclassification, the pre-policy calibrator, and a non-working prefix demonstration are enough to keep this well short of an excellent score.
