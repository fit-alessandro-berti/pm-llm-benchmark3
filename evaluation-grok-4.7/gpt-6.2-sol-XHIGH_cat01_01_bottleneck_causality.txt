The quantitative core is careful and the causal posture is mostly disciplined. The night/load hypothesis does not. Marginal night cycle time and wait are longer (21.74h vs 13.98h; waits about 12.76h vs 7.92h), and section 5 cites that gap as support for a shift/demand mechanism. Within complexity, night waits are shorter in Low, Medium, and High, because night work carries almost all high-complexity volume. Section 4 already says the marginal shift gap cannot be read as a shift effect; section 5 then uses it as supporting evidence. The Simpson structure applied to resource group is not applied to shift.

The same slip appears in other “supporting” cells. The compliance-trace imbalance (9/14 vs 4/16) is the complexity policy under the prompt’s assumption that a required check is performed; it does not show that Orion mediates delay through check handling. The crude Orion wait gap is listed as support for a genuine harmful effect after the rest of the answer shows that contrast is case-mix confounded.

The graph and the role table disagree on reviewer skill. The DAG has `resource_group → unobserved_reviewer_skill` and `shift → unobserved_reviewer_skill` only. Section 1 gives “not identifiable” with confidence 95, then calls the matched-cell pattern “a real Orion advantage.” Those cells are a descriptive direction in 1-versus-n samples. “Real” is a causal reading the verdict says the data cannot carry.
