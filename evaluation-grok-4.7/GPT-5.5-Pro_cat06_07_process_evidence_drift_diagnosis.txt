The diagnosis is substantively right: April is a schema and prefix-pipeline failure, June is a routing-mix change, August–September combine parts-performance deterioration with a changed outcome relationship, and October is not a comparable performance month. Falsifiers, stratified tests, training-month exclusions, and the label-maturity gate on retraining are all present and mostly well matched to the evidence.

The main logical soft spot is the June identification. Stable within-variant breach rates show that P(breach | eventual path) did not jump; they do not by themselves show that P(breach | features available immediately after diagnosis) was stable. The remote-routing rule sits after the prediction checkpoint, so the same diagnosis features can map to a lower breach rate. The write-up treats that as covariate or mix drift “not necessarily” concept drift, and the conditional-on-path test would eventually separate them, but the June conclusion is stated more cleanly than the checkpoint allows. Variant-specific calibration has the same gap: it is valid retrospectively, and at scoring time only if the route is already fixed.

Smaller evidence problems: October’s variant mix and the 155 h parts edge are observed, so process drift there is not “unresolved,” only its effect on breach labels is. August at 93% maturity is grouped with “slight” censoring even though the missing tail is exactly the long, breach-prone paths. May’s leftover AUROC and ECE gap is tied to the unrecomputed April cache, which the audit locates in April records, not in May cases after the 6 May patch. The slow Jan–Jun rise in part lead time (28 h to 42 h) with flat V3 breach rates is unused, and July is described as a general moderate worsening even though aggregate calibration stayed tight (0.18 vs 0.19, ECE 0.042) while AUROC fell.

The code meets the requested PM4Py checklist and correctly separates event-calendar from case-start cohorts, but the duplicate rule keeps `event_id` alone when that column exists, so re-ingested copies with new ids survive, and dedup runs before a time sort. Event-window filtering can invent directly-follows edges and still emits V1/V2/V3 shares on truncated traces. Temporal features are counted and then discarded, and the performance-DFG value is stored without units or aggregation.

None of this overturns the month-by-month account or the containment-before-retrain sequence. It is a strong audit with a few identification and implementation nicks, not a nearly flawless one.
