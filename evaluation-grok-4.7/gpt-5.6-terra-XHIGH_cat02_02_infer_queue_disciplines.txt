7.5

The write-up is a real decision-set analysis: assumptions are labeled, most eligible sets are right, observational ties are usually kept open, and the QA / QC deadline arithmetic is sound. It is not nearly flawless. The required prediction tables contain a repeated, checkable error, and that error sits on a comparison the diagnosis needs.

**Factual error (QB SPT).** At 10:14 and 10:28, B07 is already waiting with `est_min` 6. Pure SPT therefore selects B07, not B04 (`est_min` 8). Both rows list B07 in the eligible set and still predict B04. At 10:28 the appointment column treats B07 as a live alternative, so the SPT column is inconsistent with the same row. The mistake is material: the correct comparison separates FIFO (B04) from SPT (B07). As written, those two rules falsely agree, and the arrival-order evidence after B01 looks weaker than it is. The 10:36 row, where SPT is correctly B07, shows the 6-minute estimate was available.

**Strongest contradiction is mis-aimed.** The primary QB label is original-arrival FIFO with resume precedence, plus only “emergency” priority. Under that label, 09:48 can fit if the B01 stop is the recorded exogenous reassignment and the next choice is oldest-waiting (B03). The decision that actually breaks resume-FIFO is 10:00: B05 starts at a normal completion, ahead of paused B01 and older B04, with no urgent label. Section 3 mentions B05; the diagnosis table and the word “emergency” do not absorb it. “B03 receives a recorded dispatcher reassignment” also overreads the log: that reason is attached to B01’s second stop, not to a recorded choice of B03.

**Same packing test is applied unevenly.** For D-F, 30 minutes does not fit in 11:36–12:00 and D10’s 12 minutes does. That point is right. The analogous D-X fact is missing: from 11:20 to 11:30, D08’s 8-minute estimate finishes at 11:28, before D06’s 11:30 appointment, while D-X is idle. A hold that only protects the appointment would still run D08. Idleness is stronger than appointment protection, and it favors an unrecorded specialization or state. A second exact D10 window is also unstated: at 11:48, idle D-F still has exactly 12 minutes before 12:00.

**QC evidence is unbalanced.** The tables correctly show that at 11:34 C06 matches SPT and mismatches FIFO, priority, and earliest-deadline. Section 3’s support list never uses that episode, so the SPT component appears to rest only on the opening batch. C06 arrived during service and was chosen later; that cuts against “initial batch frozen at 10:10” and belongs with the 10:32 and 11:40 contradictions. Separately, once estimates are taken seriously, C05’s 10:50 deadline is already impossible at 10:18 (10:18+40=10:58). “Selected C03 despite the earliest deadline” overstates an earliest-deadline violation at that instant.

**Smaller slips.** A02’s service starts at 08:32, not 08:30; the two-minute gap is ignored. QA’s 86 treats a well-identified FIFO baseline and a weakly identified appointment rule as one confidence. QD’s 78 is high for a queue whose within-resource rule is explicitly unidentified.

What keeps the score here is the surrounding work. QA’s 08:20 versus 09:05 contrast is the right appointment test. QC’s 11:40 counterfactual (C07 would finish 11:58 and C01 would still finish by 12:34) is correct and decisive against a reliable deadline switch. Skill feasibility for D05 and D07 is cleanly separated from sequencing. The three discrimination tests and the collection plan are specific to these logs. The synthesis correctly ranks QA as most identifiable and refuses strict preemptive priority from B02 alone. The defects are local and quantitative, and they change the strength of two queue diagnoses rather than overturning the document.
