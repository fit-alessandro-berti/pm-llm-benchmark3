The answer’s causal judgment is largely right: availability is not use, rollout is endogenous, median and p90 must not be pooled, fraud-desk interference and co-interventions block a single “X%” claim, and an embedded Delta-then-Epsilon evaluation is a defensible policy. Estimands, threats, mechanisms, and uncertainty language are strong and mostly well matched to the data.

Quantitative discipline is not at the same level. Jan–Mar baselines mix the uniform March escalation jump (+2 pp in every branch) and the May complexity shift into “automation” contrasts, so escalation increases of about +2–3 pp are overstated; the adoption-timed bump is closer to +1 pp. The March-to-August 3–8 hour median “effect” uses favorable single-month endpoints, then section 5 drops minus signs (“8 h to +3 h,” “about 10 h to +15 h”) and switches sign convention within one paragraph, while section 7 and the claim ladder disagree with that wording. Pre-period `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is never treated as a measurement contradiction, and workload per FTE is not calculated.

The code meets the checklist on paper but does not implement the stated design: the event study is treated-only, has no calendar time or cohort split, and cluster-robust SEs with three clusters and about thirteen parameters are not a meaningful uncertainty estimate. Intensity results are never reported, only delegated to unrun output.
