6.4

The submission is a complete ten-section hybrid design with correct temporal arithmetic, a usable strict reading of most completed traces, and a real split between control flow, declarative rules, time, and resources. It is not coherent enough to treat as an executable policy. The chosen R5/R6 resolution, the artifacts, and the verdicts disagree with each other, and several encodings are stricter or weaker than the rules they claim to implement.

The policy section states two resolutions and names strict legal precedence as canonical, with the 24-hour emergency path deferred to an external audit. That choice is then undone. Section 1.3 says the DECLARE model also enforces the strict rule, but `declare_model` has no constraint that `Obtain Legal Opinion` precede `Activate Vendor`. T6 is correctly marked DECLARE-pass only because that rule is absent. Resolution 2 is described as a control-flow relaxation, as a data-layer warning, and as a procedural pass, including in the deployment quarantine path. Those cannot be the same implemented model. T6’s procedural cell therefore changes without a second tree.

The process tree does encode a strict order, but not R5. On the escalation branch the language is `Sanctions Escalation → Enhanced Due Diligence → Obtain Legal Opinion → Compliance Approval → Activate Vendor → Release First Payment`. R5 only requires the opinion before activation after escalation. The tree also forbids escalation outside that block, forbids an opinion after compliance and before activation, and forbids clarification interleaved with the three initial checks or after classification. Those approximations are not stated. Under this tree, T6 leaves the language at `Compliance Approval@12`, where `Obtain Legal Opinion` is the only enabled activity. The matrix’s “decisive violation at hour 13” is the later R5 story, not a replay of the model that was written. A Resolution 2 procedural pass is incompatible with that tree: removing only the opinion-before-activation edge still leaves compliance before the opinion, and activities absent from a relaxed tree would be extra events.

DECLARE is the strongest layer. `init`, `response`, `precedence`, `altresponse`, and `noncoexistence` are oriented correctly, and the distinctions among response, precedence, alternate response, chain response, and nonsuccession are right. The gaps are claim-versus-model. `altresponse` does not enforce “before any final outcome”: `Request Clarification`, `Activate Vendor`, `Receive Clarification` satisfies it and violates R3. R2’s “exactly one approval if accepted” is not in DECLARE; absence of both approvals is vacuously compliant. R11’s mapping cites an LTL/DECLARE encoding, yet neither `Change Bank Details` nor `Suspend Vendor` appears in `declare_model`.

The log skeleton has the same documentation failure. `always_after(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` is not present, while Section 9.2 says equivalence assumes one `Verify Bank Account` even though that pair was omitted and `activ_freq` allows `{1, 2}`. `directly_follows: set()` is not an abstention. In log-skeleton conformance that set is the whitelist of permitted direct successions, so every test trace with two or more events fails it; the pass cells are unsupported. `Request/Receive` frequency `{0, 1, 2}` invents a cap the scenario does not state. `always_after` on the clarification pair, checked from first indexes, accepts two requests and one response. T8’s skeleton failure is real, but the write-up omits the later failures of `equivalence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` and `always_after` on that pair; R10’s at-most-once payment versus R11’s later payment is never resolved.

The temporal profile is numerically right: seconds match the three SLAs, `zeta = 2.0` yields upper bounds 96h, 24h, and 144h, and the lower bounds sit at 0 because each mean is exactly `2σ`. T1, T2, T4, T6, and T7 are scored correctly, including T7’s z-score of `(34−12)/6 ≈ 3.67` and the non-violation of the 2-hour payment. Treating these as warnings matches R12.

The resource code covers both segregation rules, the missing emergency attribute, and the post-change suspend/re-verify pattern, and it fails closed when attributes are absent. The 24-hour branch is wrong for real timestamps: `total_seconds` is tested on the timestamp rather than the difference, so datetime inputs either take the wrong branch or compare a timedelta with `24.0`. The prose limits that failure to approval and verification tasks; the code aborts on any null resource. The mapping names `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`, which the code never calls. T4 itself is judged correctly.

Section 10 then runs the data layer, including an emergency audit queue, before a Resolution 1 Petri-net replay that rejects the same emergency trace, while the dashboard’s amber quarantine describes Resolution 2. A T6 case has no single user-facing verdict. The support/confidence warning in Section 9.6 is sound, but it cannot repair cross-layer drift already present in the artifacts.
