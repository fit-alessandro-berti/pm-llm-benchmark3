7.5

The memo is decision-grade and complete: E is chosen with explicit pause/scale/measure/threshold logic, A and B are rejected for the right reasons, and the evidence table keeps facts, interpretations, and assumptions apart. Metric conflicts, DFG/alignment/network evidence, and ranked value of information are all addressed rather than summarized.

It is not nearly flawless. The executive claim that coordinator ownership “is not the source of the harm,” and should therefore be scaled everywhere, is stronger than the evidence; 93% utilization, absence stalls, and longer failed-visit delays are left as mitigations rather than open causes. “47% of volume” understates harm: business and rural cases also worsen on tail, first-time-right, and complaints, so the damaged share is about 66%. “Improved on every dimension” for simple houses exceeds the segment metrics actually reported. Cheap-versus-expensive surveys and “compliance/legal” exposure are stated as facts; the log only shows utilization shifts and missing expected permit checks.

The fitness explanation misstates the mechanism: allowing unobserved combinations lowers precision; it does not cause fitness of 0.94. Treating a joint drop in precision and generalization as a clean signature of thin variants is technically loose, and the median gain is attributed too narrowly to simple houses when four of five segments improved at the median. The top-ranked measurement, M6, is justified with the wrong cases: skipped installs do not carry field-survey outcomes. Redeploying survey capacity freed by the pilot (78% to 52%) contradicts restoring surveys for most segments. Week-4 cost truncation is also over-read as proof that EUR 207 is a lower bound covering the densest complaint costs.
