7.2

The redesign is structurally complete and substantively strong: four mix-and-disruption scenarios, real bottleneck migration, conditional rather than blanket capacity, explicit non-simultaneous priority, a redesign-caused failure mode, and a leading-versus-lagging dashboard. It is not nearly flawless.

Factual slip in the migration analysis: peak recollection returns are 520 to receive and 370 to cancel, but the write-up anchors those edges at the normal-period counts 540 and 290 while extrapolating a worse-than-peak case. That mixes baselines in the same causal chain.

Policy logic is internally inconsistent. P-Surge says all trigger conditions must be met, then writes them with OR. I5 is armed one way in the robust package (queue >18 h or urgent/complex mix >35%, which is already true for the observed peak) and another way under P-Validation. P-Assay suspends the evening medical-validation shift “to preserve validators” during an assay-machine failure; those are different pools, so the switch does not protect assay or technical validation and can worsen an already long medical queue.

The guarded cross-training rule cannot fire in the observed peak, where accession wait is 14 h and technical validation is already 101% with a 16 h queue. That joint overload is exactly when correction help is needed, and the package never resolves the resulting hole. M1 is reserved for urgent, complex-panel, and repeat reruns without a priority rule when repeats and urgent demand coincide. The 24 h surge cap on routine batch/prep wait sits on top of a peak batch-to-assay median of 22 h, so the batching policy would largely defeat itself, and that interaction is under-examined.

Dashboard bands are also weakly calibrated: a recollection-plus-correction rate near 12% and a repeat rate near 15% are roughly the observed peak, yet those levels are treated as critical thresholds rather than the baseline the redesign must beat.
