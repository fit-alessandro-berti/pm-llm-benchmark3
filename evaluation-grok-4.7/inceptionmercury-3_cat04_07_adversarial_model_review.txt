7.5

The review is complete, structurally compliant, and mostly right about language membership for Models S and C. It still has several material errors a governance board could act on.

The probe matrix and the readings of S and C are the strongest part. S really does optionalize both mandatory checks inside `+(X(a, τ), …)`, so B3 and B4 are in its language, while `Correct Dossier` is not, so B6 and B8 are rejected only incidentally. C is exactly eight sequences: it accepts B1 and B7, rejects B2–B6 and B8, allows Auto Approval on only one path, and when both Supplier Review and Expedite Review appear, fixes Supplier before Expedite. The metric table correctly refuses a composite score and ties S’s fitness and token-based generalization to permissiveness, and C’s precision to a positive-only allowlist. P1–P7 and B1–B8 are covered, and A1 and A3 genuinely separate the third model from both inputs.

The third backbone is a real improvement: both initial checks are mandatory, approval is exactly one, the loop is `*(τ, redo)` so corrections are zero-or-more, and the redo is a three-way choice of Credit only, Technical only, or both. That is the right response to P2, P5, P6, and P7. Limitations about missing `external` / `affected` attributes and about corrections after Supplier or Expedite are stated.

Those merits do not make the artifact consistent. The policy function does not implement the semantics claimed for it. Missing `affected` hits `continue` and adds no problem; missing `external` likewise yields `[]`. The prose says those cases are “undetermined” and must not be treated as fully compliant. The only executable verdict treats them as clean. That is silent acceptance of missing data, and there is no composed accept / reject / conditional function, so alignment fitness on `model_third` never sees the policy layer. A2’s note that the filter “requires external/urgent attributes” is false: nothing checks urgency. Expedite is optional by P4, not predicate-gated; only Supplier is. The backbone also hard-rejects Supplier before or between the two checks, which the same review calls policy-ambiguous for S5, without listing that over-constraint.

Section 9 overclaims the code. `GroupShuffleSplit` is not stratified on correction, supplier, approval, or check order, and `case_id` is the wrong case key (`concept:name`). On current PM4Py, `read_xes` returns a dataframe, so `t.attributes` does not work. `move_on_log_cost` / `move_on_model_cost` are not alignment parameters; costs are trace/model/sync cost functions. Those skeletons would not run the sensitivity study they describe.

Section 11’s rebuttal is substantive and within length, then the falsifier is inverted and ungrammatical (“The the business owner explicitly approved the holdout combinations that Model C rejects, I would regard Model C’s behavior as correct”). If the owner approved the combinations C rejects, C is too strict, not thereby correct.
