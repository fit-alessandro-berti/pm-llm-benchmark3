6.8

The descriptive stage table is accurate, absent stages are correctly kept as missing, and the rejection of natural direct and indirect effects is well grounded in interference, exposure-induced queue confounding, and non-realizable cross-world nested counterfactuals. Occurrence mediation is separated from waiting-time mediation, and the required sections are all present.

Those strengths are undercut by concrete errors. P1 is defined as hiding the flag while leaving the score and other information unchanged, yet the policy table treats it as undoing priority routing, sends triage times to a pooled ~80 minutes, and says unflagged triage wait increases. Under pooling, unflagged waits should fall. The deterministic T1/T2 versus T3/T4 split also shows lane assignment is a routing rule, which the estimands themselves separate from display. In the pathway table, P2 is redefined as equalizing triage staff, contradicting both the prompt and Section 6.

P4’s claimed ~7-hour saving for medium-complexity cases is unsupported: only one of eight medium cases is escalated. “4 approved anyway” does not describe the seven escalated high-complexity cases (3 approved, 4 rejected). M2’s stated mean service of 180 minutes is about 189. The 0.63 threshold, 30–50% referral drop, 4–6 hour and 2–3 hour savings, and 40–50% backlog reduction are asserted without identification. Reject is also recoded as fraud detection.

E5 is the effect of escalation, not an effect mediated by escalation, and it is mislabeled a controlled direct effect. E1 is called well-defined for individual cases while the text says interference makes individual potential outcomes undefined. The pilot individually randomizes in one shared queue, despite the earlier SUTVA argument, uses an unsupportable 2-point stopping rule, and its congestion guardrail defaults toward direct closure. “Strong” evidence for P2 conflicts with the unidentified status in the synthesis.

The PM4Py code correctly avoids fabricating skipped stages and zero-filling them, but default performance-DFG values are means, so `stats['mean']` is likely to fail, and the small-sample warning is global rather than subgroup-specific.
