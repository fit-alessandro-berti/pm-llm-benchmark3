6.5

The case-level bounds and the full-window accounting are largely right, and the qualitative call (time-varying pressure, with S2 the structural constraint) is defensible. The write-up is not nearly flawless: several phase statistics are false, one causal claim about maintenance is mistimed, and the Little’s Law check does not survive a flow balance.

What holds up. The four missing-start windows are the correct resource-and-precedence intervals: P05 S2 in [09:45, 10:15], P07 S1 in [09:25, 10:00], P11 S2 in [12:00, 12:25], P15 S1 in [11:40, 12:15], with sojourn held fixed and only the wait/service split moved. Required cases are explicit. Elapsed service is separated from unobserved final service for P15, P17, and P21, and unstarted cases are kept as censored waits. Full-window arrival and completion counts (S1 22/20, S2 20/14), started-case wait means (S1 about 65.2–68.6, S2 about 36.6–40.0), completed-service means under the three imputations, mean WIP (about 5.79 and 3.72), peak WIP (8 and 7), peak system WIP 13, and busy-time bounds (S1 535–605 of 720; S2 515–570; net S2 over 705) all match the log. The invariance point is real: station sojourn and station WIP depend on arrivals and completions, not on the missing starts. Open busy time is included in utilization, so occupancy is not inferred from completions alone. The seven-heading structure and the three-scenario table meet the requested format.

What does not. Phase means are not the time averages of that same WIP definition. On a completion-before-arrival, half-open clock, S1 mean WIP is about 4.88, 7.58, and 4.92 in the three two-hour blocks, not 4.88, 6.92, and 4.54. S2 is about 1.92, 3.88, and 5.38, not 1.83, 3.58, and 5.77. S1’s WIP peak of 8 first appears at 10:10, when P15 arrives; at 09:25 the level is 6, and the 08:00–10:00 peak is 7. Section 5’s “peak of 8 at 09:25” is false, as is the Phase 1 peak of 8.

Phase utilization is internally inconsistent and too precise. Literal overlap on [10:00, 12:00) gives I2 about 105/120 = 87.5%, not 100%: the logged gaps 10:20–10:25, 10:55–11:00, and 11:30–11:35 are five minutes each. C2 on [12:00, 14:00) is about 105/120, not 100%, because 13:20–13:30 is idle while P15, P16, and P18 are already waiting. A recorded 10-minute gap still has a true gap of roughly 5–15 minutes after ±2.5 minute rounding, so it cannot be waved away as quantization. The 97.9% and 95.8% headlines are averages of those overstated component rates. Five-minute inspector gaps may be rounding or changeover; the table states 100.0% as a fact anyway.

The Phase 3 S2 service cell, mean 45 and range [45, 50], drops P10 (25 minutes, completed 12:10) and P11 (feasible service at most 25). The narrative then says Phase 3 calibration “averages 45.0 min” at a 95.8% ceiling. Both figures are load-bearing for the bottleneck story and both are wrong. The direction of the story still stands because end-of-window WIP sits at S2 (6 cases: P15–P20) while S1 is down to P21 and P22, and fully observed mean service is about 28 minutes at S1 versus 40 at S2. The magnitudes in that paragraph do not.

P08’s 55-minute S2 wait is misattributed. At 10:20, when P08 entered S2, C2 was still on P06 until 10:50; maintenance is 10:55–11:10. Only about 15 minutes of the wait is the outage. From 11:00, C1 is free of P07 and starts P09, who entered S2 at 10:35, ahead of P08. That overtake, plus P17 starting at 13:55 while P16 has waited since 12:35, contradicts A8’s nominal FIFO. A dedicated-server queue versus a single queue is an obvious alternative and is never compared, even though the prompt asks for competing interpretations. Maintenance removed 15 resource-minutes, about 0.4 of a case at a 40-minute mean, not a shock that by itself “locked S2 into a backlog it never recovered from.”

The 09:00–13:00 Little’s Law block has the right arrival and departure counts (15 and 14) and a plausible mean WIP near 6.96, but the balance is mishandled. Under the indicator used in Section 2, S1 WIP is 5 just after 09:00 and 5 at 13:00, not a rise from 5 to 6; the extra count is P08, already inside the 09:00 snapshot. The empirical sojourn 106.8 matches neither the 14 departures in the window (mean S1 sojourn about 96) nor the arrivals who also finish (about 110). The “4.1–11.5% agreement” is therefore not a result. Calling the interval quasi-stationary also ignores a swing up to WIP 8 and back.

Uncertainty is narrower than advertised, and also wider in a way that is ignored. Scenario means move little only because two imputations are diluted across 20 cases. The “central” midpoint is not central given the observed service distribution: feasible P05 service is at most 30 and feasible P11 service at most 25, while fully observed S2 times sit near 40, so the likelihood is piled at the long-service end of each window, not at 15 and 12.5. Zero-duration ALAP is a mathematical corner, not an operating scenario, and it is what produces the lower utilization bounds used to declare robustness. Conversely, ordinary rounded durations are published as exact integers after A2 derives a ±5 minute duration bound and a 2.04 minute standard error; that uncertainty never enters the case table or the three scenarios. The 1.28 case/hour capacity gap uses 28.1 and 40.0, which are fully observed means, and it changes once the imputed services are put back in. Marking it “100% invariant” overclaims Section 6.

The code reproduces the full-window sensitivity and does call `format_dataframe`, the two case-level inserts on P01–P14, and `convert_log_to_time_intervals`, with a fair warning that those inserts are trace-level. It does not compute the phase table or the Little’s Law numbers, so the false figures were never forced through the script. The closing comment says the pandas path models the C2 outage; the script never encodes 10:55–11:10 except as a hardcoded 705 in one denominator. Waiting cases are also shown with service “0 (Unobserved),” which reads as a measured zero.

Stable after those corrections: missing-start intervals, completed-case sojourns, full-window WIP and busy-time bounds, inclusion of censored work, and a time-varying bottleneck that ends at S2. Not stable: the phase WIP and utilization table, the 09:25 peak, the 45-minute Phase 3 service claim, the maintenance explanation for P08, FIFO, and the reported Little’s Law error.
