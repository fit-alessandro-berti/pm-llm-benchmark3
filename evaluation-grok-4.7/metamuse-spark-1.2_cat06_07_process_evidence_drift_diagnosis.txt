7.2

The month-level diagnosis is largely right and well separated: April is a schema and feature-pipeline failure, May is an incomplete backfill, June is a routing-mix change with stable within-variant breach rates, August–September combine a real parts-delay shift in \(P(y\mid V3)\) with population change, and October’s 0.16 breach rate is not comparable. The six required sections, falsifiers, training gates, and retraining risks are present and mostly proportionate.

The score is held down by concrete errors, not by structure.

The August control-flow shift is misexplained. `Order Part → Part Received` sojourn rising from 65 h to 130 h does not increase the share of traces that take V3. Partner Z is still 0% in August, so the 22% → 35% V3 jump is unexplained. Section 2 repeats the mistake (“more cases get stuck waiting”). That conflates performance drift with variant mix.

October covariates are handled inconsistently. Variant and partner shares are process and population facts; the outcome backlog censors \(y\), and the table still shows V3 at 41% with only 1% unmapped. The covariate row then calls those shares censored, while the population row uses October’s 27% Partner Z share.

The maturity test and monitor equate a 45-day lag with a mature cohort. Median label delay is already 33–52 days, so a 45-day snapshot is about a median lag and still drops the slow, breach-heavy tail. Fixed-lag alignment makes months comparable; it does not lift October’s observed 0.16 toward 0.30–0.35. Kaplan–Meier or inverse-probability weighting, or simply waiting, would. Calling the 45-day slice “mature” rebuilds the censoring the design is meant to block.

The reweighting check is numerically off. September matured rates on a 45/35/20 mix are about 0.25, not 0.20–0.22. Several other expected results are invented with false precision: unknown-activity self-loops, a V3 odds ratio of 3–4, and a stratum prediction of 0.3.

Variant-specific calibration is recommended without stating that V1/V2/V3 are realized after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`. Realized variant is valid for evaluation and illegal as a scoring feature at the prediction prefix. “Variant likelihood” appears only later, as a feature to build.

The required sketch would not run. `format_dataframe` renames `case_id`, `activity`, and `timestamp`, but later code still uses the old names. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a tuple of aggregated floats (mean by default, in seconds), not a dict with `"median"` in hours. Current `get_variants` maps variants to counts, so `len(v)` is wrong. The event-calendar cohort is built and then ignored. Normalization, deduplication, and the refusal to invent a drift detector are right; the API use is not.

Smaller defects: April’s V3 rate is compared with March’s 0.30 rather than the 0.30–0.32 baseline; the 18% unmapped slice can select the variant-specific rates; July’s aggregate ECE of 0.042 hides the V3 jump to 0.46; arrows are missing throughout the table, so many cells read as concatenated numbers.
