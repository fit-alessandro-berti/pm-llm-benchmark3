The analysis is unusually careful and almost fully checked against the aggregates: regime rates, nets, WIP and FTE averages, staff-minutes per arrival, the stock-flow lower bounds (45, 57, 59, 65), the peak-share contradictions, the Simpson reversal (7.19 to 7.08 unstratified, 8.75 at early day-type weights), Fano/CV figures, and the lag-7 arrival correlation all hold. Outage output is not treated as normal capacity, medians are not used as mean flow time, and Little’s Law is refused wherever balance, scope, or stationarity fails.

Deductions are for smaller but real defects. The code renames the alternative phases “partial drawdown” and “Net drawdown” even though April 15–19 is still +17 and the cumulative recorded balance stays largely uncleared; that contradicts the more accurate prose. Representative hourly “candidates” are then used as if they were audited clock times for those dates. `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` is never mentioned. Staff-minutes per arrival is defined as the capacity metric and then omitted for several regime rows. “33.8% pooled” is the 28-day mix, not the pool of the two weekday windows being compared (~33.5%). The 9-day Little’s Law window is not an integer number of weeks, so the repeating-calendar rationale fits it poorly even though the verdict is only “approximate.”
