6.2

The write-up meets the required seven-part structure, uses many of the right volumes and queue figures, and correctly treats mix, machine loss, validator absence, and courier bunching as different mechanisms. It also rejects blanket capacity adds and flags several real intervention conflicts. Those strengths do not offset several material reasoning errors.

The peak urgent batch-wait bypass is 58% after 74% in the normal period. Section 2 says that bypass “rises to 58%” and then builds S1 on increased fragmentation. The rate fell. Absolute urgent volume rose, and a larger share of urgent work was pulled into batch wait. That is the opposite mechanism, and it is visible in the variant note the answer cites.

The I5 row reverses the directly-follows graph. Hold-for-specialist branches from technical validation into medical validation. An evening medical-validation shift cannot push more cases into specialist review. The downstream exposure is release, or specialist review remaining upstream and untouched. The same table says the dynamic M1/M2 rule can reduce repeats by keeping routine work off M2. The stated default does the reverse: M1 favors urgent and complex-panel work, so routine volume concentrates on the higher-failure machine. That is the failure mode already given for I3.

R4 is defined as always-on in the robust package and as a policy that switches on only above a 40% urgent-plus-complex share. The large-batch branch is reachable only below a 20% urgent-plus-complex share, while the normal mix is already 31%, so the efficiency mode never occurs in the observed range. Halting urgent bypass during an assay outage is prescribed without accounting for batch-wait edges of 9h/22h against an 18h urgent penalty. Outsourcing is mainly triggered once the validation queue is already long, after which a further 12h transport cannot recover the clock; the intake diversion appears only in the compound row.

S3 claims the log shows overdue-result calls moving the bottleneck backward into registration. The log ties clerk load to recollection errors, not delay calls. S5 then treats technical validation, medical validation, and specialist review as the common terminus of every scenario. An assay outage remains bound at the failed machine until capacity returns, and courier compression binds first at intake, pre-analytical check, and the two-person correction pool.

Repeat-after-failure is the longest tail and double-consumes assay capacity, but the package never gives it a dispatch, quarantine, or machine rule. Several thresholds also mix unlike statistics: performance-edge medians are called queues, oldest-sample age is judged against a median-queue benchmark, and medical validation and specialist review are fused despite separate triggers. The missing arrow glyphs make the mix and utilization claims harder to read, but the logical inversions are the reason the score stays here.
