The answer is a strong process-level portfolio argument: all twelve candidates are ranked with the required columns, frequency is not the ranking key, the three portfolios stay inside the four-module cap, A11 is withheld, A9 is gated, medians and directly-follows counts are not added into a fake cycle-time ROI, and the A5–A6 pairing correctly reflects the analyst-overload warning. Those strengths do not make it nearly flawless.

The control gate in the rollout is inverted. Stopping a cohort for a “KYC-before-sign event” names the legally required order as the failure. The violation to stop is signing before recorded KYC clearance. Nearby sentences state the rule correctly, but the scale/stop criterion itself is the operational control, and it is wrong as written.

A4 is ranked first and given a very high process benefit because of the 31-hour wait and the 82% critical-path rate. Year one then implements it as a limited-coverage, read-only assistant with human sign-off and no core writeback, against a pool of three specialists concentrated on two seniors. That design can shorten the 55-minute service task. It does not, on the evidence given, clear a queue created by scarce specialist capacity. The impact rating and the deployment constraint are not reconciled. Direct benefit is also lifted from low–medium to medium without a reason.

A5 is released before A6. The resource network says cleaner inflow overloads the nine analysts unless normalization improves first. A later brake if analyst p90 rises is a recovery rule, not a prerequisite. The same final trade-off explains why registration should not go first, and why valuation and signing are left outside the portfolio, but it does not weigh the residual control-path risk of leaving classification manual: an 8% misroute can skip collateral or committee, and A9 cannot repair a decision path the case never entered. Four-eyes separation is also left to a non-module access control and is absent from the month 1–6 gates.

Smaller defects sit in the same direction. Interface “classification” is ambiguous next to the classification activity. The back-office sentence ties A1’s labor saving to the period when utilization is already under 65%. Concurrency of KYC and financial review is acknowledged, then barely applied to the recommended portfolio’s cycle-time claim. The 18% working-capital collateral rate and the KYC-to-credit arc (4,200) are unused. A4’s post-month-six writeback is promised and then not scheduled.

The recommendation is therefore well aimed and still short of a clean control and capacity argument.
