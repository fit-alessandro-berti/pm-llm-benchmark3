7.6

The analysis is strong on the core queueing judgments: outage versus recovery are separated, median service time is not treated as mean flow time, Little’s Law is conditioned on boundaries and throughput choice, and the weekday/weekend, mix-shift, and post-intervention reversals are real. Most checked totals, rates, Fano factors, and load proxies are accurate, and uncertainty is generally labeled well.

It is not nearly flawless. Early-weekend WIP is called a mean of 13.5; that is the median, and the mean is 13.75, which is then used as \(L\). The alternative “segmentation” is not a partition: April 15–17 sit inside the 14-day low-complex totals (net +22) and are also listed as an overlay, so most of that net is the outage window rather than a steady regime. The Python `np.select` drops those three days from `low_complex_calendar`, so the code’s alternative summary (11 days, about 79 arrivals/day) does not reproduce the prose (14 days, 84.6). The change-point score is dominated by ordinary Monday/Saturday staffing jumps and can miss April 29, yet that limitation is not diagnosed. One Little’s Law verdict also falls outside the required three-way label.
