The layered design is strong: R5/R6 is treated as a real conflict, the strict profile is the default, the emergency profile is separate, and the test matrix is largely right (T4 is resource-only, T5 fails the clarification closure, T6 splits by profile, T7 is a temporal warning, T8 is a terminal contradiction). The formalism split is also mostly well chosen. The score stays well below excellent because of concrete errors, not missing sections.

The temporal section contradicts itself. With `zeta = 2`, every band is exactly `μ ± 2σ` and the lower endpoint is 0, not a negative number: 0–96 h, 0–24 h, and 0–144 h. The table has those windows, then the prose says the lower bounds are negative and therefore unreachable. A zero delay is on the boundary and is not a deviation under `|Δ − μ| > ζσ`. The T1–T8 time verdicts happen to survive this mistake.

`check_R5_R6` is not equivalent to the strict DECLARE rule it claims to mirror. Under `STRICT`, any opinion timestamp earlier than activation returns pass, even if another opinion follows activation; `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` fails in that case. Under `EMERGENCY_EXCEPTION`, missing authorization returns `policy-dependent` before the 24-hour test, so an opinion that is already later than 24 hours, or a completed case with no opinion at all, is not reported as a definite fail.

Release after activation is mandatory in the tree and in the stated backbone, but optional in DECLARE and in the log skeleton (`precedence` / `always_before` only). The suspension rationale is not a rule in the scenario, and section 5 still says the skeleton restates the backbone order. The T6 write-up also blurs the policy violation at `Activate Vendor@13` with the nonsuccession witness at `Obtain Legal Opinion@23`.
