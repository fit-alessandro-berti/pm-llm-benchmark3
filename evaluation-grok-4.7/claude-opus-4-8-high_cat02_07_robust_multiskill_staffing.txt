6.2

The deliverable is a complete nine-section analysis with a real offered-load derivation, explicit objectives, three operational policies, and a substantial discrete-event scaffold. It is not reliable enough for the policy ranking it asserts. Several mechanisms used to justify the recommendation are mis-specified, unimplemented, or contradicted by the same answer’s table, and the headline gaps between pooling and hybrid are smaller than the sampling noise.

The capacity arithmetic in §2 is the strongest part. The mixture means (Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.8, Software 38.5, Validation 15.7) match uniform-within-band mixtures, and the block totals (444, 587, 455, 651, 391, 192 resource-minutes) and raw headcounts (480/600/600/600/240/120) reproduce correctly. The afternoon cliff is real: after 16:00 only Deepa and Emil remain, after 18:00 only Emil, and nobody on shift can do Mechanical. Multipliers are all at least 1, so those ρ figures are lower bounds on fully cross-trained load, and breaks (about 90 minutes inside 12:00–14:00 alone) make the “0.76 slack” look safer than it is. Skill-level load divided by skill-specific on-shift capacity is described qualitatively, not calculated. That omission matters: aggregate ρ treats Ada’s electrical minute and Deepa’s electrical minute as interchangeable.

The scenario layer does not implement the shocks the prose interprets.

- U5 unpacks an Intake outage at 09:30–10:00 and never reads it. §2 still describes a 30-minute Intake stoppage cascading into the morning backlog. The reported U5 column is U1 plus Chen’s absence only.
- U1/U5 add 8 points to the P1 weight and leave P2 and P3 in place, then normalize by the new sum. On the 08:00 block, 10/55/35 becomes 18/55/35, so the share rises by about 6.7 points, not 8. The same dilution hits 10:00–12:00.
- U4 doubles every activity’s tail probability, including Intake and Validation. The scenario doubles diagnostic tails only. The claimed “15–25%” mean increase is high: doubling the given tail probabilities raises Mechanical by about 13%, Electrical by about 13%, and Software by about 16%.
- U2 doubles the E and ES percentage weights and renormalizes at fixed `expected_arrivals`. That is a defensible reading of an ambiguous sentence, but it is not labeled, and it barely changes total work. On 14:00–16:00 the mix shift moves minutes from Mechanical to Electrical and leaves block load near 651. A volume reading (those case counts double, other types stay, proportions are recomputed) would push that block toward roughly 900 resource-minutes. The flat U2 completion result (about 0.51 versus a 0.50 base) is what a near-zero load change should produce, and it is presented as evidence that pooling absorbs an electrical incident.

Preemption in §1 and §3 does not match §8. The code restarts the whole activity and then adds 10% of the remaining time, tracks the once-only cap on the resource rather than the case, and leaves the original full interval in the event log. Cancelled work therefore remains visible to `get_service_time` and the handover network, and a later restart adds a second interval. Parallel branches share one `prev_res`, so the second branch can be charged a handover and a cross-team penalty against its sibling. The 60-minute rule is also degenerate and unremarked: ordinary-band means are 51.5 for ME and 61 for ES, so every ES case is parallel and only P1 ME cases are parallel. Serialized “parallel” branches still pay the 8-minute coordination delay.

P-Strict is an unnecessarily weak baseline. Deepa’s electrical multiplier is 1.00 and Ada’s mechanical multiplier is 1.00, yet the fixed queues are Electrical {Chen, Emil} and Mechanical {Ben, Deepa}. U3’s drop to a P1 SLA of 0.26 is partly this partition: Emil remains eligible, and Deepa, the fast electrical resource, is locked out. “No reroute” overstates the coverage that this particular strict policy destroyed.

Hybrid, as coded, is not the hybrid that §3 and §5 recommend. A reserved resource whose current best job is non-P1 is skipped entirely when any alternative is free, rather than released when no P1 is waiting, and it does not then take the next job. The §5 switches (grow R, release after 20 idle minutes, force parallelization, 15:00 overtime) are not in the sweep. Under U3 the reserved electrical/software resource is the absent person, so a hybrid-versus-pool P1 gap cannot be attributed to Chen’s reservation.

§4 and §5 then over-read noisy point estimates. A day has on the order of five or six P1 cases; ten seeds are about 50–60 trials. A SLA near 0.70 has a standard error around six points. Base 0.71 versus 0.69 is not an edge. U1’s 0.74 versus 0.64 is only marginal. The written table never shows the standard deviations the code computes. O1 asks for p95 and the table reports p90. The sentence that hybrid is top or joint-top in every scenario is false on this answer’s own grid: pooling has the higher P1 SLA in U4 (0.66 vs 0.63) and U5 (0.59 vs 0.57). O2, defined as worst-case P1 SLA with Jain at least 0.90 and max utilization at most 0.90, therefore ranks pooling slightly ahead. Calling that a tie and breaking it toward hybrid changes the stated objective. Realized utilization is also the wrong quantity for the 90% fragility rule: the denominator ignores breaks, handover, and setup, and unfinished or skill-infeasible work never occupies anyone. Max utilization near 0.78 can coexist with offered ρ of 1.6 precisely because the evening queue is stranded. That observation is in §2, then treated in §5 as if both flexible policies had passed a planned-load constraint.

The process-mining section names the right tools and correctly separates case-level service/waiting from `get_service_time`. The numerical reconciliation does not. Reported case sojourn is about 126 minutes, while conditional completion delay is about 154. Those clocks differ: pm4py sojourn runs from the first stamped start to the last completion, and arrival-to-intake wait is not an event. Handover, setup, and the 8-minute coordination gap sit inside waiting time. The claimed exact match between raw stage service and pm4py is expected when both read the same, preemption-contaminated log; it does not confirm the service model. §7 also lists queue entry, eligible set, pause/resume, policy version, shock flag, and SLA clock as monitoring attributes the log does not emit.

§6 and §9 are substantively good: P3 starvation, skill erosion, auditability, shadow replay, holding P1 out of randomization, and the warning that routing cannot invent evening capacity. “Zero patient risk” is the wrong domain. The surrounding chat narration, and the claim that the embedded script is exactly what produced the outage narrative, break the required single-document contract and the link between evidence and prose.

What survives is the structural result: specialization with no overflow fails when Chen is absent; flexible eligibility degrades less; conditional mean and p90 delay are survivorship-biased; and no tested rule meets a 90% P1 SLA inside 120 minutes because the roster ends. The finer claim that reserved hybrid dominates full pooling on a tail-and-fairness objective is not supported by the implementation or by the reported scenario grid.
