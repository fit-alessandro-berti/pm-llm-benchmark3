5.5

The write-up covers every required section and correctly refuses a blanket capacity add, but several decision-facing claims contradict the logs, the conflict hints, or the answer’s own policies.

**Process-mining evidence is mishandled.** Recollection counts of 760 (8 weeks) and 780 (3 weeks) are treated as stable; the weekly rate roughly triples, and the pre-analytical recollection rate rises from about 4% to about 8%. Cancels are compared the same way, and 540 peak cancels (all cancel paths, 3 weeks) are set against 280 normal register-only cancels. The 7h figure is the pre-analytical → request-recollection edge, not a recollection queue; the damaging edge is request-recollection → receive sample at 42h / 60h. Path shifts that dominate peak behavior are unused: direct technical release falls from roughly three-quarters of cases to about half, specialist holds rise from under 2% to roughly 8%, batch-wait share roughly triples, and assay → repeat rises from about 7% to about 15%. Invented loads (M1 at ~140%, medical validation at 150%+, intake at 130%+, repeats at 30%) are stated as analysis.

**Those inventions contradict the answer’s own scenario math.** A 50% assay-capacity loss at 98% utilization leaves about 196% load on the surviving machine, not 140%. A 60% cut in medical-validation capacity at 93% utilization is about 230% of remaining capacity before the heavier complex mix, not 150%. A total specialist absence does not settle at a 72h queue.

**Bottleneck migration is often the wrong mechanism.** Repeat assay returns to assay (or, rarely, medical validation); it does not add technical-validation work, and an M2 outage removes the higher-failure machine, so a jump to 30% repeats does not follow. Urgent-first dispatch holds work at preparation and assay; it does not, by itself, increase the technical-validation queue. I8 is labeled robust while the given hint is that faster registration exposes pre-analytical check and the assay machines, both already near saturation. I6 is assumed to reject 10–15% of samples even though observed recollection is below that, and the extra triage sits on the station already at 96% utilization and a 9.5h peak queue. I4 is always-on despite technical validation already at 101% and a 16h peak queue; “no net loss of technical-validation capacity” conflicts with pulling two validators out of that pool. Downstream of accession correction, preparation (89%, 11h) and assay (98%, 18h) are the stations that absorb the unlocked flow.

**The conditional policies are internally inconsistent.** I3 means “routine goes to M2,” but it is the response to an M2 outage, when that route does not exist. The same bundle is credited with fewer repeats, while M2 is the high-failure machine and section 6 later says I3 increases repeats. Assay utilization above 95% for 4 hours is already true across the observed peak (98%), so the rule would stay on for the whole peak and dump routine volume onto M2. I1 is listed as avoided and then reactivated; section 2 says it starves complex-panel cases, while the trade-off table marks complex-panel as neutral. I7 is both always active and scenario-triggered. Trigger logic disagrees across sections: urgent surge is AND in section 4 and OR in section 7; degradation thresholds appear as 20%, 15%, and 10%; specialist triggers appear as 30h, 36h, and 48h.

**The redesign misses the binding service constraint.** Urgent release past 18 hours is penalized. Peak urgent median is already 17h and p90 is 33h, and the technical-validation → medical-validation edge alone is 24h, with a 22h medical queue and a further 5h to release. The always-on package is scored neutral for urgent cases. Repeat-after-failure, which uses assay capacity twice and clusters after night shifts and M2, never gets a control. The core package therefore smooths intake while leaving the stations that actually blow the urgent and complex tails to poorly calibrated switches.

**What remains sound.** Scenario types match the brief and are not volume-only. Courier compression is traced into sample degradation, clerk time spent explaining recollections, and idle downstream benches. I5 and I10 are correctly kept conditional. I9 is correctly dropped. Section 6 does name redesign-caused failures (M2 repeat risk, the I4 raid on technical validation, outsource traceability). The repeat-rate bands (>10% / >15%) match the DFG; the p90 outcome targets sit on the contractual and routine limits. The trade-off table has the required columns, but several effect cells are blank or truncated, and the core rework claim of “10–15%” is undefined relative to the 7.5% and 11.8% baselines.
