The write-up follows the required structure and gets the main control-flow distinctions right: prerequisites, four-eyes, preliminary versus final compliance, readiness versus completion, and K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a pure time cut. The 100-case discussion and the ranking of K-337 last are substantively sound. It is not temporally reliable enough to score higher.

The case table’s ranges are internally inconsistent and only loosely tied to the snapshots. K-104’s factual duration of 58.5 hours is right, but “46–60 hours (0–12 hours faster)” is not: 60 hours is slower than the factual path. The further stretch to 60–70 hours “if it displaces an urgent case” assigns another case’s delay to this case. K-411 does the same. Its factual path is about 29.5 hours, yet the favorable band (24–30), the “near-zero” base, and the adverse band (29–36) overlap, and displacement is again treated as slowing the treated case under a single-case hold-other-behavior-fixed analysis.

K-337 contradicts itself. Assessment 2 ends Friday 10:30, and the derivation says the case is compliance-ready Friday. A short C1 wait and an M1 signature the same day, consistent with M1’s observed late-day decisions, would remove on the order of four to five days, not the stated best case of two to three days (24–72 hours). Best and base also overlap.

K-219 asserts that D2 can finish before D1 started or finished, and the path allows identity work in parallel with document work. Identity is already complete at 16:20, originals arrive at 17:30, and the Monday 16:00 snapshot gives D2 about five hours of queue. Without preemption or unstated evening capacity, D2 may not beat D1’s Tuesday 13:30 finish; the 36-hour best case is not shown to be feasible. End-to-end gain is also capped by the factual Wednesday-morning decision unless M1’s availability is established.

Section 3’s best cases (Tuesday-night C1, Tuesday-night notification) are more aggressive than the tables and ignore observed daytime patterns, while the assumption audit never states a working-hours assumption those best cases require. The K-219 pilot’s legibility band can screen out the handwriting cases that create the adverse tail. These are central feasibility and calibration failures, not formatting nits.
