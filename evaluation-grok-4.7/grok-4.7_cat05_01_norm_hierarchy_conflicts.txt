8.1

The analysis is unusually disciplined: issue-specific hierarchy, time-of-act testing, no automatic cure, new versus unapproved kept apart, ERP time subordinated to better evidence, and classifications that actually diverge. It is not nearly flawless.

The central soft spot is Rule R14. Calling joint Incident Command authorization “evidence” of a Finance Controller approval and of Plant_Approve is a substitution of actors. GPIP-4 A4/A7 reserve relaxation of those controls to a Chief Risk Officer writing, and the CFO’s stated authority is interpretation of finance controls. The email’s own closing sentence reserves essential integrity controls. Section 5’s global lever still lets the email deem the finance prong satisfied, so the stricter reading — the email cannot perform either A2 actor — is never fully pressure-tested. P-507 is the classification that depends on this move.

That case also breaks the answer’s own burden rule. Section 1 says an unproven exception plus a visible default breach is NONCOMPLIANT. P-507 never identifies a sterilization-line asset, and authorizer independence is silence, not proof. Eight-hour “critical replacement” seals are a reasonable inference, not established scope. Under the stated method this is INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, even at medium confidence, does not follow. P-503 and P-504 correctly refuse self-applied labels; P-507 is softer than that standard. P-505, by contrast, properly stays open because external times and a fast clock conflict with the ERP stamp.

P-501 is the other over-clean result. Severity None and COMPLIANT treat same-day Plant and Finance sign-offs as performing B2. B2 is silent on timing, but the controlling instruction is that post-hoc approval does not cure, and B5 keeps Finance_Approve in an emergency. That purposive reading is not in the P-501 alternative, which only debates who may place the call. Listing R1 as satisfied while approver names are missing is the same kind of overclaim.

Smaller defects: P-505’s 24-hour horizon is assumed rather than tested; post-window R6 is called unjustified although no PO is in the file; P-508 marks R12 satisfied with no recording time; the CFO email’s possible retroactive cover of 14 July is assumed away; “SHIFT-2 alone” is not tested against A1. The satisfied-rules column sometimes lists inapplicable rules or narrative. None of these flips a robust result. P-502, P-503, P-506, and P-508 are correctly noncompliant for the reasons given, and the remediation split among breach, logging delay, late proof of a timely act, and true inability to prove is precise.
