The write-up follows the required outline and understands the process: initial concurrency, split prerequisites, risk choice, optional supplier work, trial/approval overlap, repeat-until inspection, and release as a join. The dependency table and the process-tree shape are a coherent conservative under-approximation, and T5 is a real witness of that loss.

The formal models do not implement that story. Process-tree leaves are built with `Transition` imported from `pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj`; that class is not there, and a visible label `"tau"` is not a silent skip. The POWL is not executable and not the language claimed: `Operator.LOOP` is given three children, `from pm4py import Operator` is not a valid import, and XOR/loop children are also placed in the root `StrictPartialOrder` with edges from every alternative into `Release Material`. A strict partial order executes every node, so those edges do not mean “whichever branch ran.” They force supervisor review, both regulatory reviews, supplier consultation, and the skip together, and they deadlock the join. The claim that a node that does not occur stops constraining its successors is false for this formalism, so the POWL verdicts and the “exact” rows in the comparison table do not follow from the code.

T7’s reason is also wrong. `Inspect Trial` then `Adjust Parameters` is the legal redo prefix (it is a prefix of allowed T4). The first illegal event is `Release Material` after the redo without another `Execute Trial`–`Inspect Trial` cycle.
