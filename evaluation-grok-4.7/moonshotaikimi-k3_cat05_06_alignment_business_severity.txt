The business reading of all 12 cases is largely right: deviation types are separated cleanly, P1–P5 are applied correctly on the facts that matter, severity is not ranked by raw cost, and zero-cost policy failures (CL-609, CL-610) and the fitting tau move (CL-605) are handled explicitly. The required sections, columns, and PM4Py steps are all present, and the code keeps narrative severity separate from technical cost.

The alignment-semantics claims are not reliable. For CL-604 the text treats a “sync Pay, late-recorded Manual Approve” alignment as another optimum of cost 3. Under the stated uniform cost of 1, that reading is not what a standard alignment can express (order conflicts cannot sync both activities), and it is not optimal: the auto-approve branch fits in cost 2 (`M(Auto Approve)` + `L(Manual Approve)`), which is a cheaper and more misleading story the answer never identifies. The supplied cost-3 alignment is authoritative as a diagnostic, but it is not an optimum. CL-601 is also miscounted as “8 sync moves”; the trace has 6 synchronous moves and 3 log moves.

Smaller faults add up. “Five orders of magnitude” between CL-601 and CL-602 is wrong (exposure ratio is about 400×, cost ratio 3×). Level-4 wording (“without evidence substantive checks were skipped”) does not match CL-609, where the required manual approval was skipped. P3 is not applied to CL-610 even though the applicable approval never occurred before payment. Pattern G omits CL-604’s missing fraud screen. P6 is stretched to cover withdrawals and drops re-opened claims. Connector dedupe (severity 1) is prioritized above the €250k notification-evidence gap and the 9-case assessor-linkage gap. The reproduction calls alignments with PM4Py’s default costs, so it will not reproduce the supplied costs, and CL-607’s amount is invented as 0.
