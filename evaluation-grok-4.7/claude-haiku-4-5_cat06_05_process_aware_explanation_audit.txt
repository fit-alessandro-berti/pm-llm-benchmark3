6.5

The audit correctly catches the main future-trace leaks and keeps queue, prefix age, complexity, region, and calendar effects in play with different standards. The revised explanations and several process claims are still not reliable enough for a high score.

The required Section 1 table omits three global sequence patterns (`Documents Requested -> Documents Received`, `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`, and `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`). They appear only later, so the mandated inventory is incomplete. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is repeatedly called post-closure even though appeal and the second decision precede close; that pattern is future at `t0` but normatively legal, unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, which is an ordering impossibility or a same-timestamp artifact. Damage assessment is also mislabeled impossible at `t0`; on the fast track it is the next enabled activity, so it is future-possible, not forbidden. The appeal rule never states that appeal is enabled only after Partial or Deny.

`queue_length_at_t0` of 18 versus 4 producing opposite signs is treated as evidence against a real effect. Those signs are what a monotone congestion signal should do. `total_case_duration_hours` is not identified as near-label circularity with a 10-day settlement breach. The planted as-of-extraction join for employee experience is never mentioned. The prior-claim SQL bug is narrowed, without support, to “the same extraction batch.”

Section 3 titles the best prefix features as causal. Temporal availability is necessary for prediction and is not a causal identification result. The claims table then lists `initial_damage_complexity` under “actionable” while saying it is not actionable.

Section 4 is the weakest part. Both revised narratives keep the contaminated probabilities, 0.82 and 0.71, although appeal and the full-trace variant dominate those local attributions. Case Y invents an `initial_damage_complexity` driver that the local explanation does not contain, treats the known-bad prior-claim count as credible, and repeats the variant `REG-COV-DOC-DOC-DMG-DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE`, which restates leaked future activities. Case Z is called non-recomputable until event order is repaired, but both tied events are after `t0`; repairing their order cannot make that pattern a prefix feature. Week 52 remains a confounded but available signal and is not separated from the leaks.

The point-in-time join is reversed: `feature_valid_at_time >= t0` selects later versions. “Recalibration” is specified as AUC, F1, precision, and recall, not probability calibration after leakage removal. Forcing a semantic reorder of tied events can also hide genuine nonconformance. A frequent normal edge such as document request to receipt is never distinguished from a normative constraint.
