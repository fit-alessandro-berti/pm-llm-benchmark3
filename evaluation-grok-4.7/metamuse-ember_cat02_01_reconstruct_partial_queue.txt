6.5

The reconstruction is strong on definitions and on most case-level and integral calculations, but the required station-level picture contains concrete numerical errors, an inconsistent cohort rule, and one internal contradiction. Those are central deliverables, so the answer is not close to flawless.

Case-level work is largely right. Fully observed waits, services, and completed sojourns check out (S1 wait 1240/19 = 65.3, S1 service 505/18 = 28.1, S2 wait 510/14 = 36.4, S2 service 480/12 = 40, completed sojourn 2050/14 = 146.4). Missing-start bounds follow single-task precedence: P05 on C1 is 09:45–10:15, P07 on I1 is 09:25–10:00, P11 on C2 is 12:00–12:25, and P15 on I1 is 11:40–12:15. Open and blank cases are kept as elapsed lower bounds rather than dropped or treated as finished. Resource-level busy time is also right (I1 245–315, I2 290, C1 270–300, C2 245–270), as are available minutes (720 and 705), subperiod arrival and completion rates, and integrated mean WIP (S1 4.88 / 7.58 / 4.92 / 5.79; S2 1.92 / 3.88 / 5.38 / 3.72; totals 6.79 / 11.46 / 10.29 / 9.51). Subperiod busy minutes add to the full-window totals. Censoring bias, the C2 counterfactual (about 20 case-minutes on P08), and the stable-versus-unstable split are well argued. The S1 Little’s Law identity 20/360 × 92.25 = 5.125 versus integrated 5.79 is exact and correctly attributed to P21 and P22.

The queue and peak results do not match the reported clock. S2 station WIP reaches 7 from 13:45 to 13:50 (P14 and P15 in service; P16, P17, P18, P19, and P20 waiting), not a peak of 6. S2 queue length reaches 5 both at 13:25–13:30 and for about ten minutes at 13:45–13:55, not a peak of 4 at 14:00. The 14:00 snapshot (queue 4, service 2) is correct and was mistaken for the peak. S1 station WIP in 12:00–14:00 peaks at 8 only from 12:10 to 12:15, after P22 arrives, not at the interval start, where it is 7. Total WIP 13 is right, but it also holds from 11:50 to 12:00, not only 11:30–11:40. Rounding could shrink the five-minute WIP spike; that is a reason to report 6–7, not to state 6 with no mention of the overlap.

The S2 waiting summaries do not follow the stated “service-start interval” rule, which does fit S1. S2 in 08:00–10:00 (mean wait 12, n = 5, service mean 38) is the queue-entry cohort and includes P06, who starts at 10:10. S2 in 10:00–12:00 (37.5, n = 4) drops P06 and also drops P12 and P13, who entered in that interval. The five known S2 starts in 12:00–14:00 have waits 45, 50, 65, 75, and 65, whose mean is 60, not 56. The phrase “25 of 27” five-minute gaps cannot sit beside three named exceptions: 25 + 3 ≠ 27. The known adjacent gaps are 26 of 29, with the same three exceptions (C1 touch at 11:00, C2’s 25-minute outage gap, C2’s 10-minute gap before P15). “Reaching 95–100” attaches P16’s wait of 100 to the P09–P14 cohort, whose maximum is 95. “2.33/h × 146 min ≈ 5.7” is numerically the right conversion and dimensionally unstated.

The bottleneck direction itself holds: S1 binds on the morning surge, both stations are saturated in 10:00–12:00, and after arrivals collapse the backlog moves downstream. A peak S2 queue of 5 and peak S2 WIP of 7 would strengthen that late-shift claim rather than reverse it. Utilization bands, sojourns of the missing-start cases, and end-of-window composition (8 in system: 1 + 1 in S1, 4 + 2 in S2) are robust.

The Python distinguishes MISSING, OPEN, and blank, builds the right logical bounds, and gets the integral WIP and full-window utilization. It does not reproduce the station cohort table or the peaks. The 5-minute grid is computed and then printed only on the hour, which hides both the S2 queue of 5 and the S2 WIP of 7. The unused `q1` expression is dead and wrong. Interval rows are duplicated start and complete events under one activity name, and `format_dataframe` is not given a start-timestamp key, so the PM4Py case-level calls are only partially wired even though the complete-case restriction and the station-versus-case label are appropriate.

Score reflects correct bounds, integrals, utilization, and uncertainty handling, reduced for wrong peaks, a broken cohort definition, a bad cell mean, and a self-contradictory gap count in the main argument.
