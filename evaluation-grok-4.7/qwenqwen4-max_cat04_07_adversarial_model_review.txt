6.7

The S-versus-C review is strong and the required structure is complete, but the proposed model is semantically wrong under the answer’s own loop semantics, and that error falsifies the headline invariant and much of the behavior audit.

What holds up: Model S’s language (130 traces, 94 policy-violating, 36 correction-free and policy-shaped) and Model C’s (exactly 8 traces, 48 labeled leaves, 39 duplicate occurrences, 7/36 coverage) are computed correctly. The B1–B8 matrix for S and C matches the expressions, including C’s false reject of B2/B5/B6 and S’s acceptance of B3/B4. Metric interpretation stays disaggregated, treats the numbers as non-reproducible, and ranks the required pathologies with real decision impact. Use-case separation, three-valued P3/P6 handling, the negative-suite gate, and the dissent are serious rather than decorative.

The decisive flaw is the correction loop. Assumption A1 correctly states `*(body, redo) = body (redo body)*`, so the body runs at least once. The deployed tree is `*(->(Correct Dossier, recheck), tau)`, which is therefore `(Correct Dossier, recheck)+`. Every trace must contain a correction. Invariant I7 is false: there is no correction-free fragment, let alone one equal to the 36 valid traces. Section 8 is wrong wherever it says the third model accepts a no-correction trace: B1, B2, B5, B7, A5, and A9. The summary claim that the third model’s correction-free language is exactly the policy language does not follow from the formula. A4 is also misread: the net already rejects that trace for lack of `Correct Dossier`, so it does not show that only D5 catches a missing supplier review.

Related inconsistencies: D4 hard-fails supplier review on a non-external order while O2/D9 call the same case an unratified warning; the verdict lattice then collapses it to non-compliant. The E6 generator yields a trace that still contains `Correct Dossier` when `ncorr==0`, and `None` otherwise, so it cannot check I7. `in_language` double-calls alignment and branches on `tuple`, which is the wrong return type for a single-trace alignment dict.

The design intent (AND for the first checks, SR parallel to the check-then-expedite sequence, data rules only where attributes are missing) is the right architecture, and `X(tau, *(body, tau))` or `*(tau, body)` would have matched the prose. As written, the enforcement artifact fails the happy path while the audit says it passes. That is a core error, not a slip in an otherwise nearly flawless submission.
