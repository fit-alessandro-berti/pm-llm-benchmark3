The audit correctly catches the major leakage (closed-case duration, full-trace appeals/variants/counts, closure team, post-t0 snapshots, extraction-time SQL) and, importantly, separates the impossible same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern from a semantically valid but future `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` reconsideration. Queue length, prefix age, frozen complexity, and region-as-proxy are kept rather than deleted, and predictive, causal, and fairness limits are partly distinguished. That is the core of a strong answer.

It is not nearly flawless. “Lineage note 60” does not exist. Section 2 encodes appeal with Declare `Response` and “iff,” which would require an appeal after every Partial/Deny and a second decision after every appeal; the norm is only that an appeal may occur after Partial or Deny and can lead to another decision. “Damage Assessment Completed” is treated as enabled at t0, while that completion is still future, and parallel fraud enablement is missing from the state classification. `initial_reserve` is treated as a frozen pre-t0 fact without lineage. A constant `REG-COV` prefix is listed as a Case Y driver with `direction: "neutral"`, outside the required increase/decrease scheme, at a checkpoint where every prefix is the same. Case narratives still restate leaked appeal and document-cycle outcomes and replace them with unsupported mechanisms (broker latency, reserve-driven multi-tier approval, high complexity “precluding” fast track, week 52 as established holiday causation despite the policy-change confound). Case Z’s note omits that calendar week is available but confounded, and several negative full-trace patterns are condemned without reading what their signs were claiming.
