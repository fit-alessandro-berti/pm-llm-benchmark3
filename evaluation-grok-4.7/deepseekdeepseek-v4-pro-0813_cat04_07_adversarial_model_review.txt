7.5

The review is structurally complete and mostly right on the hard control-flow questions: Model S really does skip mandatory checks and reorder Expedite Review, Model C really does reject B2, B5, and B6, and the probe matrix matches the tree languages. The third model is a genuine improvement, the pathology ranking is usable, and the dissent is substantive rather than ceremonial.

It is not nearly flawless. The external checker that is supposed to enforce the suspicious-repeat rule flags ordinary valid traces. On B1 (`Credit Check` then `Technical Review`), any earlier mandatory event makes the next mandatory event look like a duplicate, so the walk stops before `Correct Dossier` and records a violation. The same false positive hits every both-checks recheck after one correction. That detector does not implement P7.

The validation rule then undermines the paper’s own distinction between language membership and alignment fitness: a fitness cutoff of 0.90 will not reliably catch a one-event reorder such as B4, which is exactly the metric pathology section 6 warns about. “Accepts if the affected check was X” in the audit table also overstates what the tree does; the tree accepts those traces unconditionally and cannot test the missing attribute. Residual duplicate labels in the third model, Supplier Review forced after both checks, and the unexplained 0.992 training fitness of a model with no `Correct Dossier` are left too soft. The rebuttal is real, but the 150-word dissent is only barely met once the “what would change my mind” sentences are included.
