The memo is organized, careful on alias-normalized segregation, service-account attribution, prohibited subdelegation, and the signed-PDF versus late migration record. Those strengths do not offset material errors on RR4, delegation scope, event-time identity, and the required code.

The business-day rule is self-contradictory. The memo defines day 1 and day 2 after Tuesday 2026-08-11 as 12 and 13 August, with the deadline at the end of day 2, then calls the Friday 14 August 16:00 review of CHG-505 timely. Under that definition the review falls on business day 3. The intended pandas deadline (implementation date plus two business days, end of 13 August) would mark the same review late. CHG-505 is therefore not shown to meet RR4’s timing condition, and the secondary path to VALID_SUBSTITUTION is overstated.

D-QM-01 is limited to production-equipment change controls. CHG-505 and CHG-510 are `cooling_system`. The case table still says Ben’s retrospective reviews are valid on scope. RR3’s validity conditions, including scope covering the change, are the right test for a “valid QM delegate” under RR4. A timely review also cannot be treated as established for CHG-505: `u_bk` acted at 16:00 on 14 August, inside Ben’s certified sick leave through 14 August 23:59, the same window in which he denies the CHG-506 approval. That review needed the same attribution hold as CHG-506. The memo applies the hold to only one of the two account uses.

Burden of proof is inconsistent. Missing CI-44 linkage makes CHG-505 INDETERMINATE, while unproved absence on 15 August makes CHG-509 UNAUTHORIZED at high confidence, even though the memo itself says absence is unproved and lists a valid absence as an alternative. CHG-504 is labeled AUTHORIZED while credential sharing is still open. Grace Li’s standing role is dated from 2026-01-01; the master says 2025-01-01.

The Python block does not run. The CSV column is `org:resource`, and the frame never creates `resource` before `log["resource"]`. `BDay(..., holidays=[], rollforward=True)` and `busday_count(..., roll=...)` are invalid for those APIs, so the RR4 section would fail even after the column fix. `incident_id` is unused; D-IC-03 is stored as scope `CI-44` and compared with `cooling_system`, so the incident link cannot be represented. The code also requires the implementer to hold the IC role, which RR4 does not require. Service-account handling and the refusal to auto-declare collusion are sound, but the script is not an executable check.

Sections 4 and 6 are the strongest: paired benign and adverse readings, discriminating evidence, and controls tied to migration timestamps, FIDO, rotation, and retention. The executive count of “clear” failures still inherits the CHG-505 and CHG-509 overconfidence and misses a possible failed retrospective cure. Highest-priority work remains the two emergency retrospectives, including scope, lateness, and the 14 August sick-leave session, plus CHG-503/CHG-506 attribution.
