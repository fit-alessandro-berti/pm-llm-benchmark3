6.5

The business reading is largely coherent and correctly refuses to treat raw alignment cost as risk, but it is not reliable enough on policy application, frequency evidence, or the required code.

The framework in §1 is sound: it separates severity from confidence and uses exposure, reversibility, timing, recurrence, evidence quality, and model scope. Most case narratives match the alignments. UI telemetry (CL-601), the retry duplicate (CL-603), the silent routing move (CL-605), the denial/withdrawal scope mismatches (CL-607, CL-612), the label synonym (CL-611), and the zero-cost P1 breaches (CL-609, CL-610) are classified in the right families. CL-602 and CL-604 are rightly treated as severe control failures despite low or moderate cost, and §4 does not treat a missing event as proof the activity never occurred. §6 actually uses the required mechanisms: uniform cost, scope, hidden moves, data conditions, telemetry, duplicates, external evidence, and irreversibility.

The policy and evidence errors are material. CL-610 is not only a P1 auto-approve breach. A bank change one hour earlier also triggers P2, so Fraud Screen and Manual Approve were required before payment, and P3 makes payment without that approval severe even though the alignment cost is 0. The case row cites only P1 and scores it 4, the same as the €18,000 medium-risk auto-approval, without that distinction. CL-608 is marked “P4 satisfied” even though P4 allows CRM or portal notice only if linked evidence is retained, and the link is a mutable text field. Population counts are inflated: the nine external-adjuster gaps and the four synchronous auto-approve violations already include the illustrated cases, but §3 reports CL-606 “plus nine” and CL-609/CL-610 “plus four.”

§5 covers model scope, label repair, data-aware gates, and control redesign, yet it has no explicit investigation item for the €480,000 missing fraud screen or the payment that cleared before approval. Priority 1 also claims “elimination” of the risk.

§7 misses the specified interfaces. It does not call `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` or a formatted pandas log, or `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments`. The alignment call uses constants that are not valid alignment parameters, so the block is not executable as written. The evidence table then labels CL-608 as an external-adjuster gap and aggregates only pattern frequency, total cost, and average severity, omitting amounts, risk levels, and policy-overlay flags.
