6.8

The design correctly refuses to treat non-activated cases as non-defaults and builds the cohort from all 48,000 applications. The flow table adds up, the leakage table drops the obvious post-decision fields, and the temporal split is aimed at the policy and migration breaks. Those are the right scientific commitments. The answer is still not close to clean, because the estimand, the censoring strategy, and the skeleton contradict each other in ways that would change the reported risk.

The primary target is not one estimand. Section 1 puts censoring, delayed labels, and permanent missingness inside \(Y\) as if they were process states. Section 3 then says to estimate transitions with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights and not to treat those mechanisms as outcomes. A multinomial probability for “open/censored” is mostly a function of how close the start date is to 15 February 2026. It is not \(P(\text{activated and default within 90 days}\mid\text{prefix})\) under the historical policy. The written key risk quantity is the right operational target, but it is not identified by the \(Y\) that was defined. The notation makes this worse: subscripts are broken (`default}*{90d}`, `X*{t_p}`), so the formal target is hard to read.

The censoring section names the right mechanisms, then blurs them again. Administrative immaturity after activation is essentially a deterministic function of activation time and the extraction date. Weighting it by a model that includes activation month will hit empty strata for late activations; positivity is never mentioned. Activation month is also listed with baseline covariates, even though open and rejected cases do not have it. Partner-feed missingness is described as likely MNAR, while the primary IPCW strategy only covers conditional censoring. The 1.5×/2× sensitivities are appropriate, but they are not part of the estimand that would actually be reported. Cause-specific hazards, cumulative incidence, and the clock (prediction time versus activation time) are never separated. The 90-day default clock starts at activation, so a single fixed-horizon model from the prefix is the wrong likelihood.

Cohort accounting in the prose is tight (18,900 + 2,700 + 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 + 3,600 = 48,000). The skeleton then undoes it. Event timestamps are cut at 31 December 2025 while process state is censored at 15 February 2026, so terminations and activations in that 46-day gap, if present, become false “open” cases. The prose says a missing `label_available_at` falls back to activation + 125 days. The code requires a non-null `label_available_at` before it will accept `default_90d`, and otherwise calls a mature activated case a missing feed. That can push the actual labeled accounts out of the labeled states. All 3,600 immature activated accounts stay in one “delayed/right-censored” row, with no place for permanently missing partner feeds.

Prefix rules in prose are mostly right: one prefix per case, short traces kept at lengths they actually reached, loops retained, normalization before cutting. The API claim is not. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` drops traces shorter than \(k\); it does not return them unchanged. The length check in code happens to be safe anyway. Elapsed-time prefixes are specified and then never built. “Accepted” is a real decision event in the stated process and is absent from both `TERMINAL_ACTS` and `POST_PREFIX_OUTCOME_ACTS`, so a control-flow feature can see acceptance, and contract signing, without failing the audit. The audit only marks that case “review.”

The leakage table itself is the strongest part: `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, same-day `documents_total`, as-of `risk_score`, extraction-time tenure, and label timing are handled with the right availability rules. The feature matrix then puts `split` and raw `prediction_time` into \(X\). Fold identity is direct leakage. Historical reference rates and resource workload are described carefully and not implemented, which is acceptable only because this is a skeleton.

Validation explains why a random case split crosses the July 2025 policy change and the October 2025 migration. The split points themselves do not match label maturity. Training on 2024 starts with labels matured by February 2026 uses outcomes that were unavailable at the train cutoff; the rolling-origin caveat is not what the code does. Test 1 is defined by start date through 14 October 2025, while a complete 90+35-day label requires activation by about 13 October 2025. Many of those “test” cases cannot have a default label yet. Metrics name IPCW Brier, calibration, and an activated-only AUROC, but not interval estimates, and not how censored cases enter the denominator of recall or lift. Slice-wise calibration on 2,700 defaults will be sparse.

The decision boundary is sound: association under the observed policy, not a counterfactual approval effect, with concrete feedback loops (stricter rejection, reviewer behavior, withdrawal, mix shift, partner routing, collections timing). That section is ahead of the estimator.

The skeleton is far outside the requested ~80–160 lines, and the extra length is where the contradictions sit. `format_dataframe`, a start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, train-only DFG/variants, and the refusal to call `insert_case_service_waiting_time` without a start timestamp are appropriate. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is computed on a resource-dropna extract and never used. Competing-risk and survival fitting is only a closing comment, after `y_state` has already stored censoring as a class label.
