8.5

The three candidates are genuine competing hypotheses, the visible languages are nested and correctly sized at 7, 14, and 18, and the audit, constraint violations, and D5 disjunction are internally consistent. That is not enough for a near-flawless score.

The balanced construction is right that one partial order cannot encode “C before S or E before S”: both edges would reject the observed `R C S E …` variant, and the XOR of the two gates is the appropriate union. The permutation oracle matches that union, including the seven balanced-only additions and the four S-first permissive additions. Conservative enumeration really is exactly V1–V7. Precedence of A before P, both checks before A and before X, and at most one S and one X are enforced as claimed. Bidirectional DFG edges are not treated as proof of concurrency. Missing supplier events are not quietly equated with true skips. Fitness is not used to pick a causal story. The validation search is length-bounded for a real reason: these nets are loop-free, with at most eight visible events, so the bound is exhaustive rather than a sample dressed up as inclusion.

The defects are smaller than that structure, but they are real.

“Interprets D5 literally” is false. D5 says confirmation may begin after either check has produced enough information and need not wait for both. It does not say that this information occurs before completion. The permissive tree is an extra projection that deletes an unlogged milestone, which the text later admits. Calling that reading literal overstates the rule’s support for S-first traces.

The fitness caveat is also wrong for the metrics being invoked. Default alignment cost of a silent model move is zero, so a language that accepts every observed case should score exact fitness, not fitness nudged down by silent-move costs. Token replay can miss 1 for other reasons; that is not what this sentence says.

`play_out(net, im, fm, noTraces=100, maxTraceLength=8)` is not the stable PM4Py calling convention; those controls belong in a parameters object. The handler catches only `AttributeError`, `TypeError`, `ImportError`, and `NotImplementedError`, so a `ValueError` or other failure still aborts the script. The prose then asserts a successful local run on PM4Py 2.7.23.8, including all four metric APIs, without showing a single returned value. The language and suite checks could pass while that run claim remains unevidenced.

Two softer gaps remain. “Before either check completes” is ambiguous between “before the first completion” and “before one of the two,” which is exactly the balanced/permissive distinction, though the later example resolves it. The 7/30 visible omissions versus a 5–10% loss rate is the sharpest available split between true skips and lost events, and it is only gestured at as “a few.”

The immediate-use choice of balanced, including unobserved engineering-enabled supplier completion, is a disclosed bias rather than a contradiction. Duplicate labels, non-identifiable alignments, and the fact that incomparable nodes do not prove overlap are handled correctly. The score is held down by the overstated D5 reading, the incorrect fitness mechanism, and the unverified execution claim, not by the model design.
