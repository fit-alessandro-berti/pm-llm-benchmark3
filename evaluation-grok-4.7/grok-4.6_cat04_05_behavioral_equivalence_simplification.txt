8.3

The verdicts are right and the strongest reasoning is formal: after hiding `tau`, \(L_v(A)\) pairs one check with every analysis and \(L_v(B)\) has exactly one check shuffled through the analyze/approve/rework sequence, so neither language contains the other. The one-attempt intersection is exactly `{OACK, OCAK, OACPK, OCAPK}`, with `OAPCK` only in B; for two or more analyses the check-count invariant separates them. T1–T5 match those semantics, optional approval is correctly treated as irrelevant to that split, and P3 disagreement is correctly taken as proof of unbounded inequivalence while agreement would not be. P2 is the right abstraction (analyze count, check collapsed to a Boolean, rework as a Boolean), and the two images really do coincide. The sequential simplification is honestly a strict subset of A, with valid regression and negative traces.

Points come off for precision, not for the main conclusion. The P1 bullets never forbid approve-before-check, yet OAPCK is later called correctly rejected by P1 and filed as a P1 witness; that confuses “approval placement is still observable” with a compliance ban. The footprint claim that Check is parallel with Rework in B and not in A is true causally, but it is not what `discover_footprints` reports: loop-carried both-directions directly-follows is typically stored as parallel in A as well. The BFS is the right exhaustive idea, but keying markings by `place.name` is not shown to be injective and can collide or raise `TypeError` when names are missing, so the exhaustiveness claim is stronger than the code. Petri-net simplicity is only scaffolded, not compared. Those are local holes in an otherwise careful argument.
