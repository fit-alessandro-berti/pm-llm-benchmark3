The write-up hits the required seven-section structure, stays conditional on a pilot, and correctly treats East differently from North and South while warning against median cycle time. Those are real strengths. The forecast itself is not reliable enough to score higher.

The central quantitative problem in the source data is missed. Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91% utilization, while the proposal raises their inflow from 9 to 12 cases per 100. That is about a one-third demand increase on resources that are already near saturation, before they also absorb triage and difficult quality checks. One local supervisor is removed while approval volume rises from 42 to 49 per 100. Neither capacity collision is calculated. Management’s small wait-time increases (9.6h to 10.5h; approval waits around 3.0h) are therefore left unchallenged, even though standard queueing makes those estimates implausible without added headcount. The scenario deltas—median cycle time −5% or +20%, 90th percentile +15%, rework falling to 7%—are asserted with no link to the day-scale cycle times versus hour-scale waits.

The mechanism map also accepts “nominal handover reduction” too easily. Summing the management table gives more listed handovers after the change (about 359 versus 343 per 100), with technical touches rising from 36+31 to 41+36. Role clarity is first treated as improved, then later as blurred. Resilience is reduced to “intake stays local,” which does not address specialist absence.

The pilot contradicts itself. Scope merges N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech, but the control says North stays fully local, which requires keeping N_Tech. The rationale says North is the team likely to be harmed, then places North in the untreated arm. A 3-month window also cannot observe the learning and fairness effects the indicator table itself calls quarterly.

Section 7 would not run against this log. `read_event_log` is used on a CSV, `format_dataframe` is applied in the wrong order, and filters depend on a `phase` column that is not in the schema. `get_service_time` is called with invented arguments even though the log has only `time:timestamp`, so service time cannot be separated from waiting time. `filter_event_attribute_values` is given a nonexistent `comparison` argument. Specialty and customer-segment filters, required by the prompt, are absent. Organizational-mining functions are also described as producing utilization, SLA misses, and skill decay, which they do not.

Exception handling is missing from the risk section. Coverage is broad, but the forecast, the experiment, and the code sketch each contain material logical or technical errors.
