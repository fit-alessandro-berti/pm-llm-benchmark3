The memo is decision-ready and constraint-compliant: it refuses national scale and a full rollback, keeps the simple-house gain, and treats tail time, rework, billing-before-activation, and permit gaps as binding. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are cleanly separated; the five metric conflicts are explained correctly; fitness versus precision is technically right; and A–E are judged with real conditions, not slogans.

Points come off for decision hygiene, not for the headline call. Survey skipping is suspended outright in the memo and then only for higher-risk or unclassified segments in the final actions, so it is unclear whether simple houses keep skipping tomorrow. Coordinator ownership is scaled now despite 93% utilization and absence stalls, while the absence test (M5) is ranked near the bottom. M7 is ranked first by mixing operational urgency with value of information, and M1 is ranked above M6 even though the memo says the skip trial waits on rule validation. “Deterioration is concentrated” in apartments and old buildings underplays adverse p90, first-time-right, and complaint moves for business and rural cases. The rollout gate says “agreed margins” without candidate numbers. The complaint-mix shift (waiting versus installed-but-not-working / billed-before-activation) is unused, and the memo is slightly more causal about the simple-house benefit than the incomplete pre-week-3 risk data allow.
