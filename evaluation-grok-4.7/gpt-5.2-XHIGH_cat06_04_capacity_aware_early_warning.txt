6.4

The memo clears the main traps and is internally auditable on most euro products, but it is not decision-theoretically coherent. Its own rule does not generate the portfolio it then recommends, and several uncertainty claims are stronger than the arithmetic.

The hard stage gate on I-03 is right, and I-05 is correctly treated as refresh spam while I-07 is correctly re-opened on the new log. Calibration directions match the brief (SME up, Strategic Enterprise down). Costs in §3 check: \(3\times450+800+600+2000=4750\). Midpoint benefits check: \(0.19\times60000=11400\), \(0.24\times20000=4800\), \(0.15\times25000=3750\). Upper-half points \(0.235/0.27/0.185\) and \(3\times6\times0.01=0.18\) also check. I-08’s lower bound is in fact net-negative before spillover (\(0.05\times100000-5450=-450\)), so holding it is justified.

The deployable score is never computed. \(S_i\) subtracts a spillover penalty, yet no \(\bar V_{\text{diag}}\), no \(\delta\), and no \(S_i\) appears for any case, so the Tier A/B/C labels are not reproducible. Using a constant per-slot penalty would not change rank anyway; it only changes how many slots clear a hurdle. That hurdle is then ignored. At a lower-bound effect, I-02 nets about \(0.08\times25000-1050=950\) before spillover. At \(6\times0.02\times€15\text{k}\approx1800\), \(S\) is negative. §2 still calls that conservative value “strong” and spends an immediate slot. I-05’s lower-bound direct net is about \(1200\), above I-02, before the SME shift to roughly \(0.61\)–\(0.66\); it is demoted to Tier C for alert repetition rather than scored. Repetition should suppress pages, not replace the objective.

The fairness rule contradicts the book. The stated rescue test, \(p_{\text{cal,mid}}>0.70\) and \(\Delta p_{\text{low}}>0.15\), is met by I-07 (\(0.72+0.065\), lower effect \(0.20\)). No such slot is reserved. The fourth slot is held for a possible regulatory arrival instead, and the conflict is not resolved. Non-monetary duty is essentially unaddressed beyond that reserve.

§3 does not carry §1’s robustification. Optimistic and base rows use raw supplied effects, including the upper half for two Strategic Enterprise cases the model overpredicts by \(0.05\)–\(0.12\), and the midpoint for sparse I-09 rather than the lower bound the gating rule requires. The adverse row sets \(\delta\in[0.03,0.05]\). The brief’s probability spillover stops at \(0.03\); \(15\)–\(30\) minutes of aggregate delay is not a stated \(0.05\) per case, and no mapping is shown. That unsupported tail produces the €22.5k harm figure. The “one case fails” stress also zeros I-02, the smallest ticket, leaving the €60k driver intact. Zeroing I-04 or I-09, the more plausible wrong-alert cases, would make the adverse net clearly worse. Base interpretation is slightly loose as well: worst base on their own figures is still about \(19950-4750-13500>0\), so “stay in the positive region” is not what that row shows.

Operational gaps remain. Two specialists deliver one swarm; I-09 (\(2.5\text{h}\)) and I-02 (\(3\text{h}\)) are both “now” with no serial order. Full false-alarm burdens are charged even in the optimistic row, where the alert is assumed right. The \(0.10\) re-entry bar is not reconciled with I-02 (\(+0.08\)) or I-09 (\(+0.07\)). §6 is the strongest part—segment calibration, logged policy decisions, doubly robust uplift, congestion caps, and explicit change triggers—but it cannot repair a same-day rule that does not bind the same-day choice.
