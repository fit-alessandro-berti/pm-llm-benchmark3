5.5

The write-up covers every required section and most queue decisions are defensible, but the recommended policy is not reliable. C010 is assigned to R8_Hugo for Final Approval on the claim that R8 has approval skill 3. R8’s approval skill is 0, so this violates the explicit rule that approval must be finalized at skill 2–3. The same error is used to claim a 2/9 (22%) training share. The only valid training match in the table is C009, about 11%, so the 20% constraint is missed and then reported as met.

That assignment also contradicts the stated algorithm: the approval filter rejects skill below 2, yet the table selects a skill-0 resource. Security supervision is required for every skill 1–2 review, but the pseudocode enforces it only for high-priority cases. Overtime handling in the code admits every high-priority case onto high-overtime resources instead of requiring that no safer alternative exist. Scoring functions, weights, and the fallback that “relaxes constraints” are undefined.

C001 keeps a skill-1 owner on an urgent enterprise security review with 3 hours remaining, despite the given evidence that weak-skill continuity adds about 3.3 hours of service time and the note that a specialist is needed. C003 is reassigned at an existing reassignment count of 2 without discussing the stated rework penalty. R3 is given four new cases, including that weak-skill urgent review, with 6 hours left and 6 cases already open; hours are never treated as real capacity.

The baseline comparison stays generic and does not compute first-available or specialist-only assignments on this queue, while still claiming the proposed policy assures skill thresholds. Several PM4Py calls are not valid: `get_service_time` and `discover_performance_dfg` are given a `variant_key`, `format_dataframe` is called incorrectly, `filter_case_performance` is used as an attribute filter, role output is passed to `save_vis_sna`, and working-together analysis is absent.
