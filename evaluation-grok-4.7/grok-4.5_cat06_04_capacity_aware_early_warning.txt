6.4

The policy is structurally complete and directionally right on the checks that matter most: I-03 is blocked by stage, I-05 is not treated like I-07, effects are taken from the ARR ranges rather than from `p_now`, one slot is held back, and calibration, spillovers, and label bias are operationalized. It is not coherent enough to score higher. The deployable rule and the portfolio contradict each other, and the base-case impact arithmetic does not match the midpoints the table itself cites.

The ranking rule is not applied. RENB uses the lower ARR, then multiplies by urgency and segment confidence. Under that rule, approximate pre-spillover scores are roughly I-04 €1,065, I-02 €710, I-09 about €650–€1,410 depending on whether the 0.6–0.7 effect haircut is stacked on the 0.6 segment weight, I-01 €370, and I-07 €340. I-07 does not clear a top-three cutoff, and it is near or below zero once any serious spillover charge is applied. The selected set is nevertheless I-02, I-04, and I-07, with I-09 only a reserve substitute. Tiers replace the scores the rule promised, so the allocation is not auditable from the decision rule. I-04 is also intervened on immediately despite a 10-hour triage clock whose own urgency weight was built to defer it.

Calibration is declared and then mostly unused. SME gets +0.06 and Strategic −0.08, but those adjusted probabilities never enter RENB. Segment confidence then discounts SME to 0.85, which pushes the opposite way from the stated under-prediction. NNLS is discounted twice, while the Public Sector ±0.03 band is never used. The urgency schedule is also incomplete: several stage and time combinations are undefined, and multiplying the whole net of swarm cost and false-alarm burden by urgency distorts the cost comparison.

The spillover term does not implement the stated mechanism. One slot’s expected harm is halved for an unexplained reason and then divided by the number selected, so total spillover is under-charged by about a factor of six. Because it is a common subtraction, it would not repair the ranking even if corrected.

Section 3 misstates its own base case. Midpoint ARRs 0.15, 0.19, and 0.26 sum to 0.60 breaches and about €16.5k of gross benefit, not 0.45–0.55 and €11.5k. Optimistic euros are likewise shifted down from the upper-half ARRs cited. Full false-alarm burden is deducted in every scenario, including the optimistic one. Adverse is described as near zero to negative, while the row’s own cost and spillover figures are plainly negative. Spillover money uses an ungrounded €8–15k exposure.

I-05 is mishandled. There has been no prior swarm, so the repeat-swarm ban does not apply. A stable 0.55 to 0.57 refresh is redundant information, not proof of a false alarm. Suppression should stop re-paging; it should not drop a case whose lower-bound net is about €1,200, above I-07, with SME risk still understated. The I-07 contrast is otherwise right: a new log plus a 1.5-hour clock is a real re-entry.

Two specialists deliver each swarm, so three selections are sequential, and I-07’s 1.5-hour deadline requires an explicit order. None is given. Sections 5 and 6 are the strongest part: concrete floors, an intervention feature, a block on unadjusted retraining, and numeric revision triggers. Even there, matched difference-in-differences is named the primary effect estimator under a deterministic RENB rule, which does not create overlap; the randomization and discontinuity ideas in section 5 are the designs that would actually identify effects.
