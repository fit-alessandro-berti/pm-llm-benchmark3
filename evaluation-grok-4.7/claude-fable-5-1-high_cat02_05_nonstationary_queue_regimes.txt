The answer is analytically strong and meets the required structure, but it is not nearly flawless: several numerical claims are wrong or internally inconsistent, and a few operational statements overreach the evidence.

What holds up. The regime design is the right one. Outage (15 Apr) and recovery (16–17 Apr) are kept out of any “normal high-throughput” baseline; the 29 Apr staffing change is treated as an untimestamped transition; demand, mix, capacity, and backlog are separated; and Little’s Law is marked applicable only for the baseline weekdays, with the population mismatch carried into every row. The flow-conservation paradox is real (net +110 versus `mean_wip` 18→19). The peak-column versus hourly-vector mismatch is real, including that no adjacent pair on 21 Apr sums to about 54. Most core figures check out: 2519 arrivals, 2409 completions, R1 701/697, R5 798/704 and L/λ brackets 5.28 h and 4.66 h, weekday/weekend mixture variance 902 and Fano about 10, R1 variance 26.5, and the 21 Apr shape χ² of 58.9. Assumptions A1–A4 are labeled, recovery is not used as a capacity norm, and the Python block covers the seven required computations, including an explicitly non-definitive change-point screen and PM4Py only as comments on a non-log.

What does not. These are material in a strict reading, not cosmetic.

- Alternative A says recovery and intervention completion rates “differ (120 vs ~116/day)”. R4 averages 120.5; R7 averages 121. The contrast used to separate those states is not in the data.
- Section 4’s pre-20 Apr epoch “21% / 25 min” disagrees with the table and with the same section’s later, better split (weekday median 25.5 vs weekend 29). Unweighted pre-20 Apr complex share is about 22%, and the median service across those 14 days is about 27, not 25.
- “After 29 Apr” completions/arrivals is given as 102%. From 29 Apr through 3 May it is 461/443 ≈ 104%; excluding 29 Apr it is still about 104%.
- The capacity definition “c × 720 − outage” is not the outage model actually used. ρ ≈ 1.2 on 15 Apr only follows if all 6 FTE are down for 180 minutes, i.e. c × (720 − 180). The written formula yields a different ρ.
- Section 7 says the outage backlog was “recovered in two days”. Section 2 correctly has only 30 of the +47 net cases cleared, with WIP still 35 on 17 Apr versus a baseline near 20.
- “Every” Friday→Saturday drop of 10–20 WIP cases fails for 10–11 Apr (20→14). Net inflow “within 20–28 Apr” is +106, not +100.
- The lag-1 sentence attributes ≈ −0.03 for net accumulation to regime-demeaned data. The code’s weekday net lag-1 is not demeaned; only arrivals are.
- The outage WIP “ramp from ~23 to ~70”, and R1 “WIP ≈ 20 at close ≈ 20 at open”, are unobserved and not labeled as assumptions. In the Little’s Law matrix the ramp is written as a boundary fact.
- L/λ_a as a lower bound on the arriving cohort’s eventual sojourn, while the queue is still growing, is stronger than the finite-window identity supports. The verdict “approximate only” is right; the bound claim is not.
- “13/h completion ceiling” is one complex-mix day’s maximum. The ordinary 7 Apr vector already reaches 15 completions in an hour. A 30-minute earlier open does not flatten a 10:00–11:00 spike. “4,600” handling minutes is 114 × 40 = 4,560. Recovery “18.4 per FTE-day” is 129/7 on 16 Apr alone, not both recovery days (about 17.9). Weekend ρ “0.75–0.97” drops the complex-mix weekend at about 1.0–1.15.

The lognormal mean is an acceptable labeled device, but ±15% is tight for a mix that may be bimodal rather than lognormal; that uncertainty feeds every ρ. The main segmentation, the refusal to pool the outage with ordinary capacity, and the Simpson staffing–WIP reversal still stand. The score is held down by the cluster of contradictory secondary numbers and the overstated recovery and staffing claims, not by the regime logic.
