The answer covers every required section, uses the norm IDs and counts, and reaches the right high-level calls: N3 and T6 are the real forbidden cases, N1 is an eventually-versus-directly-follows problem, E10 is primarily a suspected join error, and the unregulated edges are not treated as violations. Those strengths are undercut by definitional contradictions, a repeated cross-model error, and a code sketch that does not run.

The conformance definitions contradict themselves. The mandatory bullet first says a count gap is only potential noncompliance after scope, abstraction, and alternative evidence are checked, then says, “Missing mandatory proven violation until scope and abstraction are validated.” As written, that last sentence asserts the opposite rule. The same dropped-word pattern reappears in §4 as “Typical (desirable, absent violation),” which is not a usable classification. Unregulated behavior is also called “conforming.” Open-world silence supports no noncompliance judgment; it does not establish positive conformance.

The central cross-abstraction claim is wrong. Two `Dispatch Package` events lack a Delivery object and carry a carrier attribute. That can explain the ET–OT weight of 198, but it cannot explain the OTG weight of 200: an attribute is not an `object_interaction` with a Delivery, so those two events would not add to that edge. §2, §3, and §4 nevertheless treat T2 and E6 as one 198/200 gap and say the attribute is why OTG remains conforming. The same section misreads the N4 gap. ET–OT already shows Pack–Package 200/200 and Dispatch–Package 200/200, so the OC-DFG result of 198/200 is missing directly-follows precedence, not a missing object relation and not evidence that packing was skipped. §7 then recommends looking for off-system packs, contradicting §3.

E3 is fully met at 1450/1450, but §4 bundles it into the two-event Order-link gap. E10, which the snapshot itself marks as a likely reused-identifier join, is ranked above the scoped N4 gap on severity-if-true while its own confidence in a true violation is low. N3’s business rule is temporal (“after”), while the evidence is directly-follows; the answer treats 2 as the violation set and does not say that intervening events could hide further cases. “Within expected” and “statistical weight” are used even though the norm defines no tolerance bands. Ranks 11–15 also repeat the same benign edges.

The code calls the six named functions and keeps a separate semantic layer, but `classify_otg` is invoked without being defined, and the classifiers are empty `pass` stubs. The prose is also well above the 1,700–2,600-word target before counting code. The normative priorities are mostly sound; the definition of mandatory failure, the T2/E6 mechanism, and the N4 interpretation are not.
