The answer meets every required section, uses the supplied figures accurately, and respects the main constraints: raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination, case-mix differences are not treated as a full defense, risk scores are not treated as neutral, and support steps are kept while their delay is criticized. The deduction comes from real analytical and technical weaknesses.

The adjusted gaps are called “more concerning than the raw gaps.” They are more diagnostic; they are not larger or inherently more serious. Group A’s raw approval lead is 15, 9, and 20 points, against residual gaps of 5.5, 2.2, and 3.1.

The four-way framework merges discrimination with unfair burden. Under-resourced language and accommodation routing can be procedurally unfair without being discriminatory. That distinction is only recovered later.

The conformance plan misstates policy. Language support is required for minority-language requests, not for every non-majority `language_preference`. Only 57% of Group B needs language support, while the check would treat all non-majority preferences as violations. `bool(disability_accommodation_requested)` is also unsafe if that field is a non-empty string such as `"No"`.

Exact `filter_variants` matching is applied to a “path summary.” Those summaries omit Case Closure and likely other events, so the filter can miss the intended cases. Several PM4Py calls are unused or global only: service time is not compared by group, the regional performance DFG is overwritten, and the log skeleton and DECLARE model are not stratified. Rework, a headline disparity, is never operationalized from repeated activities.

Section 5’s “strong procedural fairness concern” is a step firmer than the identification limits and the final “indeterminate” conclusion. The substantive judgment about B and C is still well supported.
