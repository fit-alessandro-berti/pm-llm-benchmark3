The final answer meets the required structure, respects the constraints, and reaches a calibrated conclusion: residual disparities and support-step burden are real, while discrimination remains unproven. Figures that are stated match the scenario, and both legitimate routing and concerning mechanisms are covered.

It is not nearly flawless. The key adjusted approval results are written without direction (“Group B 5.5 percentage points approval”), so the main outcome finding is ambiguous and never clearly restated as lower approval. Raw-versus-adjusted attenuation is not calculated, even though that comparison is what separates case mix from residual disparity (for example, D’s approval gap shrinks far more than B’s; C’s cycle-time gap remains large). Several provided waits are unused, including Completeness Check to Request Missing Documents (1.6 vs 0.4 days), Supervisor Review to Decision Draft (1.9 vs 1.0), the dual role of accommodation specialists, and the two-region backlog. The adjustment strategy names the right tools but does not give decision rules for disparity versus legitimate routing versus discrimination versus uncertainty, and matching on language need and accommodation request would often lack overlap or absorb the support pathway under study. The PM4Py sketch calls the right families of techniques, but `resource_key` is not a valid `format_dataframe` argument, variant IDs such as “V1” are not filter keys, service-time insertion is not actually used in the wait calculation, and declare/log-skeleton output is not tied to specific fairness constraints.
