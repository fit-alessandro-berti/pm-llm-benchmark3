The answer is numerically careful, structurally complete, and strong on the central distinction between service protection and control failure. It still has enough local imprecision, classification drift, and unused conflicts in the evidence to fall short of a near-flawless analysis.

The timeline uses the right phase boundaries and the right summary figures. T1 is kept free of T2 metrics. The manual-phase split 2,500 / 1,600 / 1,000 sums to the 5,100 emergency-triage inflow, and 1,600 / 5,100 is accurately about 31%. Fitness, fitting-trace percentages, and the T4 branch counts (1,500, 1,400, 1,200, 2,300) match the logs. The window-boundary caveat, the refusal to treat 44% fitting traces as a 56% noncompliance rate, and the refusal to treat a missing quality node as proof that every dispatch skipped a check are all sound.

The T4 cycle-time sentence is not sound as written. Hospital median time is back to 8 hours and hospital p90 is 16 hours against a 15-hour baseline, while pharmacy and wholesale medians remain materially worse (28 versus 23, 58 versus 36). Calling that whole median/p90 set a degraded tail blurs a segment-specific recovery. The timeline also lists the T2 cycle times without using the asymmetry itself as evidence: hospital median rose only from 8 to 10 hours, whereas pharmacy and wholesale medians more than doubled. That asymmetry is the quantitative support for the fast lane and deferral.

Stabilization cancellations are reported as 3.0% versus 2.1% because that is the phase summary. The stabilization DFG does not support that comparison. Backlog cancellation (450) plus reconciliation cancellation (300) equals Cancel → END (750), and 750 / 35,000 is about 2.1%, the baseline rate. Baseline and T2 cancellation rates do match their Cancel → END counts. A grounded reading needed to surface that conflict instead of treating 3.0% as confirmed residual elevation.

The adaptation table is the best part of the answer. Intents, product exposure, and the keep/modify/retire rationale generally track the log. A few dispositions are unstable. B is marked keep while the rationale adds versioning, expiry, scope, and independent callback; those are modifications, and D, E, F, and H are classified as modify for the same kind of tightening. G is “retire as routine practice” while also preserving a bounded exception whose content never becomes concrete. I is described as 500 plus 580 deferrals “followed by backlog processing,” but only 960 of those deferrals enter the backlog.

Section 3 defines resilience and concealed noncompliance carefully, including the point that concealment here need not be intentional, and it correctly separates model fitness from compliance. The buckets then overlap without a net call. E is listed as demonstrated resilience and, separately, as a reconstruction shortcut. I is listed only as resilience even though the table’s own risk is opaque deferral, cancellations, and possible downstream clinical harm. J is both resilient and ambiguous. The reader can reconstruct the intended nuance, but this is the section that was supposed to make the distinction crisp.

The recovery model contains triggers, tiers, a product-risk control matrix, point-of-work evidence, backlog rules, and a real reconciliation exit. Two design statements are weaker than the rest. “High-risk exceptions daily” and “ordinary deferrals at least every 24 hours” set the same cadence. P1 puts controlled-substance security and cold-chain exposure on the same tier as urgent clinical need, which can reorder work against the clinical-priority rule stated later. FEFO is introduced without a definition.

The indicator set covers every requested dimension with leading and lagging measures, and the recommendations have effort, benefit, and residual risk in a sensible order. The trade-off paragraph states what may be accepted during disruption, what control floors remain, and what evidence would loosen the ambient-product rules. It leaves a small ambiguity about whether the still-elevated pharmacy and wholesale tails are an accepted steady state or only an incident cost.

Overall: accurate transcription, mature abductive caution, and a usable redesign, with a handful of real slips in temporal comparison, KPI-to-DFG reconciliation, and classification consistency.
