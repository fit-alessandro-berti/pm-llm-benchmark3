## What holds up

Sections 1–5 are the better half. M0 is read correctly: unordered sanctions and quality inside `checks`, both required before the XOR, manual approval strictly before activation on the approved branch, and notification before closure. CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 are kept distinct on source, form, and effective window. The burden split—prove scope, applicable duty, breach, and absence of a valid exception; put a heavier production burden on claimed exceptions; use indeterminacy for broken collection and scope defect for unresolved applicability—is coherent and then actually used.

The prosecution does not pretend M0 is adequate. It concedes the information loop, CRM loss, the duplicate register, label equivalence, and FD-7 for SUP-704, and still lands the real case: ineligible auto-approval, post-expiry continuation, activation before sanctions, and same-human approval hidden by a fitting trace. The defense, in turn, gives up SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 instead of laundering them as model noise. Rejecting “M0 fitness equals compliance” and “obsolescence explains the severe cases” is a real judgment, not a split.

Section 6 also puts the right rules in the right layers: route shape in POWL, cross-cutting ordering in declarative constraints, eligibility, amount, calendar, and snapshots in data checks, identity and conflicts in organizational controls, and pilots and emergencies as versioned overlays with hard expiry. Refusing to invent a spouse ban is correct.

## Material faults

**The revised language rejects the cases used to justify it.**

- SUP-707 is Register, sanctions, two `Request_More_Info`, then quality. `M1` forces the information loop to finish before the decision block, and the standard route starts with sanctions. That trace is not in the language. The limitation note admits requests can occur at several stages, but the implemented approximation still misses the one observed, desirable loop.
- SUP-709 is sanctions then reject. The reject child is only `Reject_Supplier`. Sanctions lives only inside the other three branches, each of which also requires later approval and activation activity. The comment that this branch “intentionally supports pre-screen rejection” is false on the code as written. A reviewed-then-rejected onboarding, which M0 allows, is also impossible.
- SUP-702’s CRM notice is at +2 hours and quality is the next business day, so notification precedes quality. The auto route puts quality inside the decision and notification only after that block. `M1` cannot accept the evidenced order. This is not disclosed.
- Historical auto traces contain no `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`, and SUP-704 contains neither `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` nor `Daily_Transaction_Monitoring`. Those activities are mandatory in the new routes. Future instrumentation can justify that, but then SUP-702 and SUP-704 are not fits without a mapping the code never states. The emergency sequence also forces one monitoring token before quality; FD-7 requires daily monitoring and a quality deadline, not that order.

**Shared transition objects.** `sanctions`, `quality`, `activate`, and `manual_approval` are single `Transition` instances placed in more than one XOR branch. POWL identity is object identity. One node in several parents is not n-ary choice among copies; conversion treats it as one shared activity and the XOR ceases to be an XOR. Distinct same-label transitions were required. The script can still print; it does not construct a valid POWL.

**Authority and classification are softer than the stated burden.**

- Section 1 leaves PD-3’s power to displace CS-9 disputed, and the observer’s denial is real. SUP-702 is nevertheless `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. Medium confidence records the doubt but does not resolve it. The system paragraph is more cautious than the case label.
- SUP-710 skips quality on an activation with no written reuse or reactivation authority. Under the answer’s own exception burden, that is an unproved exception. `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` treats silence as non-applicability. Those are different propositions; the judgment picks one without retiring the other.
- SUP-715’s missing disclosure is stipulated by the conflict policy (“requires,” and none was recorded). The relationship ban is unproved; the disclosure breach is not. `MIXED` is explained, but it is inconsistent with calling SUP-712 `NONCOMPLIANT` for a non-flow rule the log cannot see. The dominant proved breach is the missing disclosure.
- SUP-711 is described as strong evidence of one duplicated source transaction, then labeled indeterminate anyway. The label set is tight, but the reason and the label pull apart.
- Section 2 says “SUP-714 and six aggregate August cases.” The six post-pilot auto-approvals are the August population; SUP-714 is one of them, dated 2026-08-02. That sentence counts seven. Section 5 correctly says six.

Smaller overclaims: the SUP-705 defense cell says compensating controls “were met,” but daily monitoring and the two-business-day clock are explicit for SUP-704, not for SUP-705. SUP-713 is “medium” governance risk in the matrix and “Low” severity in the judgment. “Disclosure expectation” understates a policy that requires disclosure.

## Score

Dialectical fairness, traceability, and scope handling are at a high professional level. The required headings, both briefs, per-case classifications, overlay table, and collection proposal are all present and usually careful. The score is pulled down by the revised model: shared nodes, a reject path that cannot express sanctions-then-reject, an information loop that cannot express SUP-707, and a notification order that contradicts SUP-702, plus a few burden inconsistencies and one aggregate double-count. Those are central to model semantics and to the revision the task asked for, not cosmetic nits.
