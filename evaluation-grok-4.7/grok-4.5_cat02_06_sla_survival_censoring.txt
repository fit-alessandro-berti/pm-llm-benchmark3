6.5

The memo covers the right decision problem and is explicit about competing exits, open cases, leakage, and non-causal use of cohort B. Several definitions and counts are wrong, and the code does not implement the analysis it recommends.

The resolved-case count is wrong. The extract contains 13 resolves (S01, S02, S03, S04, S07, S13, S16–S20, S27, S28). Section 3 calls that set 14 cases and reports 4/14 ≈ 29% late. The late cases themselves (S03, S13, S20, S28) are correct, so the completed-case rate is 4/13 ≈ 31%. The direction of the bias argument still holds: under transfer-as-competing-failure, known non-resolution by 72h is 18/32, with 5 opens before 72h still unknown, so the population breach rate lies in [0.56, 0.72].

Estimand E2 is not cleanly defined. Putting `AdminCensor` in the cause set \(D\) treats administrative censoring as a cumulative-incidence cause. Censoring is a feature of the observation scheme; the probability of still being unresolved at 72h is the residual \(1-\sum F_r(72)\), not an event type. The “E2-tail” expression adds \(\Pr(T>72, D=\mathrm{Resolve})\) to \(\Pr(\text{still open at 72})\). The first term needs follow-up past the horizon, and the second already contains the people who may later resolve, so the sum is not an estimand. “Subdistribution mass among those who eventually resolve” is also the wrong object: a subdistribution is unconditional. E1, as coded later, is the same functional as \(1-F_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\) when competing exits count as never resolved; the text presents those as separate targets and then lets E1 change with the transfer scenario without rewriting the formula.

The archetype table is mostly right and then slips. An open case at 92h (S09) is a certain E1 breach because age already exceeds 72h; calling that contribution a “lower bound” confuses a case-level fact with a bound on a rate. The resolved-at-76h and transferred-at-68h rows reach the right E3 conclusions, but “2772” and “6872” are not readable time statements. E4’s cause-specific hazard is stated correctly.

Section 4’s method choice is otherwise sound: Aalen–Johansen under a competing-transfer coding fits these dispositions better than a resolve-only Kaplan–Meier, and \(n=32\) is a good reason to prefer stratified incidence over a multivariable Fine–Gray model. The RMST line is wrong. \(\int_0^{72} S(t)\,dt\) is restricted mean time until resolution (time still unresolved), not “expected hours resolved” or “successful resolution capacity.” Area under a cumulative incidence curve is not that integral either. The missing operator in “CS hazard ratios CIF ratios” leaves the main warning about cause-specific coefficients incomplete, though the surrounding text shows the intended point.

Section 5 handles immortal time well: escalation, rework, current stage, and full variants are correctly kept out of the baseline covariate set. The state diagram includes Review→Transfer, which never occurs; every transfer in the extract is Escalate→Transfer (S08, S21, S32). That edge is an unsupported process claim. H2–H5 are appropriately labeled as hypotheses. H4 is thin: only S13 among the three rework cases (S07 at 54h, S27 at 39h, S13 at 110h) misses the SLA.

Section 6’s reversal list is the strongest part. P1’s apparent SLA position can flip if S08, S21, and S32 are censored rather than treated as competing hand-offs, and a cohort-B advantage can shrink once late resolves and opens past 72h are kept. Those claims match the case list.

Section 7 is appropriately non-causal, but the workload examples are inaccurate. S25 is already open at 79h, so it is not “near 72h.” S10 is open at 47h. The open case actually closest to the horizon is S24 at 67h, and it is not escalated. S09, S11, S25, and S26 are the opens already past 72h.

The code keeps all 32 rows, builds proper E1 bounds for unknowns, and labels Kaplan–Meier with competing exits censored as optimistic relative to non-resolution. It does not, however, compute the recommended Aalen–Johansen estimator; `F_resolve_hat_crude` is the known timely count over 32. The horizon dictionary reports late resolves both inside `observed_still_nonterminal_at_72` and as their own field, so the printed pieces do not form a partition. TR-C writes the same cause codes, event indicators, and E1 outcomes as TR-A, so that sensitivity contrast is empty. `event_r_km` and `censor_for_km` are unused. The PM4Py branch fabricates timestamps (`2020-01-01` plus a one-second order offset) despite the requirement not to invent event times; the disclaimer does not remove the violation. Variant counts themselves do not need those clocks.

Section 9 asks for the right missing data: inter-event times, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff and submit dates, workload, assignment, and compliance.
