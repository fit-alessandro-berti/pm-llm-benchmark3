6.5

The write-up is organized, uses many of the right artifacts, and correctly refuses to lead with registration or to automate activation and signing before KYC is reliable. It is not nearly flawless. Several errors sit on the recommendation itself.

The clearest factual mistake is in the final trade-off: “already 72% digital” belongs to Receive application, not Register application. The matrix and later sections also treat KYC as a 3-FTE specialist queue. KYC is Compliance ops; the 3 specialists own entity-mismatch resolution. That mix-up makes A1 look as if it floods the specialist bottleneck, when only the mismatch variant (about 1,750 cases) does. Registration and classification also share the back-office pool, so automating registration frees the same people who classify; that offset is missed.

Process-level claims overreach. Entity mismatch is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it is present, not for the median application. Customer signing (44h, nearly all completions) and credit analysis (28h, ~8,900 cases) dominate typical duration more than a 14% variant. Portfolio B is still described as the largest median cycle-time cut, partly via the 58h external wait, while Section 2 says A7 cannot compress that wait and only improves reliability. Portfolio R then inherits B’s cycle-time story after dropping both A7 and A3.

Dependencies contradict the portfolio. Section 2 makes A6 depend on A5; the recommended set keeps A6, drops A5, and substitutes informal RM templates without dealing with RM resistance or customer-friction risk. Integration depth is inconsistent: A4 and A12 are light-touch in Sections 2 and 7, then become the two deep core-banking integrations in Phase 1. A9’s Phase 2 gate also assumes Phase 1 improves committee-decision data quality, which A4 and A12 do not produce.

Other gaps: recorded KYC/document concurrency is never used; Portfolio C is credited with cutting four-eyes and provisional-signing violations even though none of its modules enforces those gates; A12 is treated as almost pure upside despite new remediation demand; the “650510” DFG figure is unreadable; and Portfolio R never gets the required cycle-time, wait, rework, handover, resource, and control analysis. Phase 0 and Phase 1 both occupy month 1, and several gates stay at “an agreed margin.”

The four-module shape (A4, A6, A9, A12), the audit trail, false-clear limits, and the decision to hold A8, A10, and A11 are defensible. The reasoning used to get there is not tight enough for a higher score.
