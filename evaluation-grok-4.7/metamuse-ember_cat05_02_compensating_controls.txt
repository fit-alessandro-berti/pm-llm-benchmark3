7.6

The assessment is structured, case-differentiated, and mostly faithful to the control objectives, but it is not nearly flawless. Several timing, criteria, and code issues are material.

The strongest work is the non-mechanical case split. AP-202 and AP-208 are correctly failed despite later manager and audit activity. AP-205 is correctly treated as stopped before completion. AP-203 and AP-207 are reasonably treated as ED-1 packages because the token, amount, vendor tenure, bank-change, AV-2, pre-settlement manager, and same-cycle audit conditions line up. AP-204 is properly separated from formal four-eyes compliance because a fabricated receipt passed a distinct u12/u13 pair. The equivalence test, settlement-based hybrid semantics, counterfactuals, and alert-versus-proof monitoring rules are all substantive.

AP-201 is the clearest reasoning error. The log has no settlement event. The facts say the manager reviewed before settlement and that settlement was at 10:00; they do not say the 5 May 09:00 audit was before settlement. Every logged settlement in the set is same-day, roughly two to four hours after release, so 10:00 on 4 May is the better reading. Under that reading the audit is after settlement and about 25 hours after release, just outside a 24-hour recall measured from release. Section 3 nevertheless states that the audit was before settlement and that both manager and audit could still stop the payment. Section 4 then groups AP-201 with the timely audits. That overstates corrective capacity. The OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET label can still be defended from AV-2, the independent pre-settlement manager, low exposure, and the correct outcome, but the stated mechanism is not reliable. The code’s own `<= 24` test would also mark this audit late, so the narrative contradicts the code.

MG-4’s L3 requirement is acknowledged in the decomposition and then dropped. Independence is documented for m02, m04, m11, and m15; level is not. AP-203 and AP-207 are still graded OBJECTIVE_MET at high confidence without listing level as missing evidence. AP-206 has a similar gap: m11’s approval is treated as substantive, while the facts do not say the manager reviewed the invoice, bank evidence, and receipt. AP-204’s confidence is also muddled. The fabricated receipt makes prevention failure relatively clear; collusion remains unproven. Those are different confidence questions.

Smaller problems add up. “One business day” and “24 hours” are sometimes collapsed. AP-208’s manager action is exactly 24 hours after settlement, so “recall had expired” is too absolute. “Merely mitigating” is collapsed into OBJECTIVE_FAILED. AP-209’s payment-level residual risk is described as low while severity is Medium. The u12/u13 concentration of 37 is used narratively but not represented in the case-facts frame.

The Python meets the requested pipeline: embedded CSV, UTC parsing, `format_dataframe`, the specified four-eyes call, a separate blocked-attempt path, a facts join, the requested flags, and an explicit non-verdict label. Two implementation faults remain. `nominal_same_user_flag` ignores `Attempt_Release`, so AP-205 looks like a different-user case in the flag table. Any failure to recover case IDs from the PM4Py result becomes a silent empty violation list. The version caveat is necessary but does not cover that failure mode.
