6.5

The memo gets the decision right in outline: A and B stay disqualified, rescreening is not treated as a substitute for independent second-officer review, P-901 is protected, and the primary plan (parallel alternate screening, close the post-evidence analyst review, queue S4, gate any S2 interrupt) is a conformant composite with an explicit fallback. Structure, labels, rule mapping, and asymmetric-loss dominance are largely in place.

It is not nearly flawless. Branch probabilities are invented and then used as if estimated: a 0.46 unresolved fuzzy confidence does not support a 55–70% false-positive clearance, nor an 80–90% interventional approval rate, nor the C/G splits (50–70 / 20–35 / 10–20 and 75–90 / 10–25). Those figures drive the top continuation tree.

Timing is inconsistent and tilted toward the preferred plan. S4 review is variously 30–65 and 35–45 minutes, against Action C’s stated 35–75, and Rank 1’s “20–45%” chance of hitting the 90-minute deadline is not implied by those ranges. Section 5’s “strong chance” if S4 arrives at 35 minutes depends on that narrowed review time. “S440m” makes a key SLA condition unreadable, and a miss when S4 is later than 45 minutes is called minor even though the deadline loss is the full €20–35k.

Action C’s “~55%–80% conditional on approval” mixes the observational approval band with a conditional SLA probability. Rank 1 also adds €0–€15k delay friction on top of that deadline loss. The spillover cell says zero impact on P-901 while the same plan can still pull S2.

B is ranked above A with no justification, and the reasoning treats self-certification as the worse breach (false four-eyes record) while still placing it higher. Section 4’s missing comparison signs (“risk 72  70”, “S2 ( R17”) obscure the mandatory thresholds.

The recommendation would likely survive a cleaner uncertainty treatment, but the quantitative case for it does not.
