The answer covers every required section and is mostly well grounded in the timeline, DFGs, and conformance results. It correctly retires blind authorization skips and unscanned dispatch, refuses to treat post-close audit remediation as real-time control, and builds a tiered recovery model with triggers, evidence capture, backlog handling, and reconciliation. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

The resilience-versus-noncompliance split is the main failure. Pattern A is placed in “true resilience,” yet 920 of 1,000 fast-lane orders go straight to the inventory snapshot with no authorization activity. “Sometimes bypassed” repeats the prompt and understates a structural bypass. Pattern J is likewise treated as resilience despite a 450/1,500 (30%) cancellation branch and an explicit fairness ambiguity. Pattern I is marked Keep while the rationale, and recommendation 4, say it must be changed because pharmacy fill fell to 61%.

Several counts and attributions are wrong or over-precise. Skip-authorization reaches inventory in 1,500 cases, not 1,600. Blind dispatch is 1,800 + 1,450 = 3,250, but recommendation 1’s benefit cites only 1,800. Traceability rose 9.5/0.8 = 11.9×, not 12×. The table claims temporary release covered 100% of controlled-substance orders while the same phase shows 2.9% missing dual control, with no reconciliation of those facts. T4 rates of 2.2%, 0.9%, and 0.5% are contemporaneous period statistics, not residuals measured after the 2,300 audit cases, and the drop from T2 is largely a return to the normal path, not evidence that remediation worked. “Operational metrics look healthy” is incompatible with pharmacy p90 of 74h versus 49h, wholesale median of 58h versus 36h, and exception rates still more than double baseline. T2/T3 p90 tails are mostly omitted from the timeline.

The recovery design has internal contradictions. Conformance fitness below 0.85 is a retrospective mining result, not a real-time trigger, and it is then reused as a fallback-model target. The outer SEQ mixes a per-order flow with an incident-level sync phase. Criticality × age does not enforce the claimed 72-hour pharmacy cap. The closing gate sends any traceability gap, including ambient paperwork, to customer quarantine, which breaks the tiering. Recommendation 3’s “saving 2,500 manual compliance checks” misstates what those 2,500 cases already were. Section 7 still accepts spreadsheet invoice hold after the model rejects it as a compliance substitute.
