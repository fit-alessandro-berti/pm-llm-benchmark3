4.4

The final answer has the required seven-part shape and several defensible case calls, but it fails core model semantics, misstates case facts, and does not meet the dialectical or revision requirements at a level that can score higher.

The clearest classifications are SUP-701 as compliant, SUP-703 and SUP-708 as noncompliant, SUP-704 as a valid emergency exception, SUP-712 as a segregation-of-duties breach, and SUP-714 as post-expiry auto-approval. SUP-705 as noncompliant and SUP-706 as data-indeterminate are also reasonable. Those calls are not enough to carry the paper.

Model semantics are wrong on the central point. M0 leaves Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review unordered, so they are concurrent. The defense says M0 serializes them. The proposed code then adds `checks.order.add_edge(sanctions, quality)`, which removes the concurrency the revision was required to preserve. Deferred quality under PD-3 is also treated as if it were that same concurrency issue.

The Python block is not executable and does not implement the required model. `approved_branch` is used before it is assigned. `Auto_Approve`, `Request_More_Info`, and the emergency route are never placed in the POWL. There is no loop or silent-transition approximation, no choice among manual, auto, and provisional routes, and no integrated reactivation path. The result is essentially broken M0 plus unused names. POWL’s inability to enforce dates, amounts, and identity is correctly noted, then wrongly used to omit branches that POWL can represent.

Evidence handling contradicts the record. SUP-702’s eligibility fields are complete, yet the matrix says they are missing. SUP-704’s spend and monitoring are given, yet the matrix asks for spend-cap evidence. SUP-709 includes Register_Supplier; the defense says rejection occurred before onboarding, and the matrix gives the prosecution the “out of scope” position. SUP-710’s prior certification is given, not missing. The answer invents an “N10” source and a “CS-9_React” waiver, and it attributes the spouse disclosure rule to CS-9. Section 1 lists PD-3’s authority as disputed and then settles it as “Yes,” despite N2 never being incorporated into CS-9 and the observer’s later retraction.

Required fairness and architecture pieces are missing or thin. The prosecution brief does not concede genuine model and logging defects, and it barely uses the aggregates (312 auto-approvals, 14 ineligible, 9 unknown, 47 information requests, six post-pilot cases, audit focus on 703 and 708). The defense brief does not concede real risk from ineligible or expired auto-approval, sanctions-after-activation, or informal practice. The 70% CRM mirror rate is not reconciled with the rise in missing notifications to 29%. Withdrawals, DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints, and pandas-style data checks are omitted. Per-case severity and confidence are not stated. SUP-715 is treated as a high-severity prohibition rather than a disclosure failure where no rule forbids the relationship itself.
