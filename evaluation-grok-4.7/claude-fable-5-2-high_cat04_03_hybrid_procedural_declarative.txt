7.0

The hybrid design is strong on the policy conflict, DECLARE directionality, temporal arithmetic, and the eight-trace story, but it is not nearly flawless. One formalism is defined incorrectly, the execution claim does not hold up, and several encodings are stricter than the rules they cite.

**Log skeleton `directly_follows` is inverted.** In a log skeleton that set is an allow-list: every consecutive pair in the trace must belong to it. The answer defines it as a chain-response obligation (“every A is immediately followed by B”) and stores `set()`. Under the real semantics an empty allow-list rejects every trace longer than one event, so T1–T4, T6, and T7 fail the skeleton, and the section 8 aggregation then fails compliant cases such as T1. The orientation of `always_before`, `always_after`, `never_together`, and `equivalence` is coherent and matches the usual PM4Py reading; this relation does not.

**The reproducibility claim is not credible.** Section 8 says the matrix was produced by running sections 3–7 on PM4Py 2.7. No trace builder is shown, and a real log-skeleton check of the published dictionary would not yield those `pass` cells. The hour and z-score arithmetic itself is hand-checkable and correct.

**R11 is over-encoded and then described as the same rule.** The external monitor matches the text: a post-activation change must be followed eventually by `Suspend Vendor`, and a later `Release First Payment` needs a later `Verify Bank Account`. The POWL amend block is tighter: each change is bundled with both a suspension and a re-verification, and both must finish before payment, so `Activate → Change → Verify → Pay → Suspend` is rejected even though R11 allows it. `Suspend Vendor` also exists only inside those amend loops, so a suspension that is not a bank-detail amendment is a procedural deviation. Section 2 still says the backbone blocks “encode the same.” The unconditional DECLARE `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` and the skeleton `always_after` pair repeat that strengthening and will fail a pre-activation change that R11 does not regulate.

**The written policies disagree with each other.** Section 1 says an `R5_STRICT` finding is tagged with whether the 24-hour R6 clock was met. `check_legal_gate` returns only the activation-while-pending failure and does not compute that clock under the strict policy. Section 8 aggregates by “any hard layer fails.” Section 10 says a redundant-layer failure is a model deviation when the authoritative layer passes. Those rules give different overall verdicts as soon as the extra bank-change response fires. Assumption A10 says a tied timestamp makes the affected check `not evaluated`; only the legal gate actually refuses ties. R2 and R11 keep a stable sort and can decide from file order.

**Smaller faults.** The artificial cap `range(0, 6)` is described as non-normative, but `activ_freq` makes 6 a hard fail and the section 8 rule promotes that to an overall fail. The T5 alignment note fixes a single log move on the request and a point of no return at `Manager Approval`; an optimal alignment is also one missing `Receive Clarification`, cost 1. The T5 temporal cell is right, but the pair that is absent is only the clarification pair; activation-to-payment is present and inside 0–144 h (71 h). `keep_violations=` is not a dependable facade argument for `filter_four_eyes_principle`. Vacuous skeleton and DECLARE successes are reported as `pass`, which hides the fact, correctly noted later, that DECLARE accepts `<Receive Onboarding Request, Classify Risk, Close Onboarding>`.

**What holds.** Resolution A versus B versus the rejected timing-only merge is the right conflict analysis, and T6 is reported both ways. DECLARE keeps `response` and `precedence` in the proper directions, uses `altresponse` rather than `chainresponse` for R3, and uses `noncoexistence` rather than `nonsuccession` for R1, with T2 cited as the trace that `noncoexistence` would wreck. R5 is correctly left out of binary DECLARE: `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` kills cases with no escalation, and `nonsuccession(Sanctions Escalation, Activate Vendor)` kills T7. R10 is correctly kept out of `exactly_one`. The POWL partial order gives the three checks an AND-join into `Classify Risk`, an XOR of reject versus low-risk versus high-risk, explicit transitive edges, and projection of the two sanctions activities. Bands 0–96 h, 0–24 h, and 0–144 h and the T7 figures (34 h, z ≈ 3.67; 2 h, z ≈ −1.94) are right, and a fast payment inside the band is not turned into a violation. T1–T3 pass, T4 fails only on Cora, T5 fails on the open clarification, T7 is only a warning, and T8 fails on co-occurrence of the two terminals, with the missing payment identified as later. Those control-flow, resource, and time separations are the part of the matrix that is actually reliable.
