7.5

The assessment is unusually careful, hits every required heading, and respects the constraints: raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination, case-mix is not treated as a full excuse, `risk_score_band` is not treated as neutral, support steps are treated as possible burdens, and removing those steps is explicitly rejected. The dimension split, gross-versus-within-need estimands, common-support point on V4/V5, negative-control transition, and side-effect discussion are substantively strong. It is not nearly flawless.

The homemade standard errors are for unadjusted approval proportions, then applied to the adjusted residuals. That understates uncertainty. “C is not distinguishable from zero,” “D is borderline,” and the bottom line’s “only B is clearly outside sampling noise” are stronger than the reported model evidence. “Controls explain 63/76/85%” is only a rough residual subtraction, not a shown decomposition. Section 3.4 says D’s adjustment “runs entirely through” risk band and prior-case count; the other controlled imbalances are real, and that claim is not identified. Calling Group C something an outcome-parity audit would clear is wrong: 69% versus 78% approval, 10% versus 6% appeals, and 24% versus 11% rework already fail raw outcome parity.

The PM4Py plan names the right tools but would mis-measure the fairness question in several places. Edge shares are normalized to sum to 1, so the weighted sum is not cycle time; missing Group A durations are filled with 0, which dumps legitimate support-only edges into the “unequal service” term; medians are not additive. Handovers are coded as events−1 or distinct resources−1, which misses resource changes and the general→specialist→general ping-pong the scenario highlights. HHI shares need not sum to 1. Service/waiting insertion assumes a `start_timestamp` the log does not have, and the call’s parameter names do not match PM4Py. The performance DFG is commented as median while the call leaves the default aggregation. Logit coefficients are not the percentage-point estimands under discussion. A skeleton/DECLARE model learned only on Group A treats required language and accommodation paths as deviations. The Series merge as written does not align variants to cases.

A further available signal is left as a future check rather than examined: about 57% of B needs language support while V4 is only 24% of B, and about 62% of C requests accommodation while V5 is 31% of C. Path summaries may be abbreviated, but that discrepancy is exactly the under-provision question the plan is supposed to prioritize. The main procedural conclusion can still stand on the queues, staffing, and adjusted document-request and supervisor-review gaps; the significance claims and the code’s decomposition cannot.
