7.8

The design correctly refuses to treat rejection, withdrawal, transfer, or an open case as a non-default, and the primary estimand \(P(D\le 90\mid X_{\text{prefix}},A=1,\text{historical policy})\) is the right conditional quantity. The 48,000-row partition adds up, the leakage table makes the right keep/repair/drop calls, temporal validation is organized around the July 2025 policy change and the October 2025 migration, and the limitations section separates prediction, selection, and counterfactual approval. Those parts are strong. The score is held down by a biased label rule, several concrete pipeline errors, and a skeleton that misses its own specification.

The primary missing-label rule is internally inconsistent and can bias the estimand. Section 3 admits a positive as soon as it is observed, while a verified negative must wait out activation plus 90 days plus a 35-day feed lag. Any cutoff that falls inside that lag window therefore enriches the sample with early defaults. Section 2 is compatible with that asymmetry. The code then does the opposite and requires `activation + 125 days` for every label. The conservative code rule is the defensible one; the written primary strategy is not. The same section also calls an application that is still open “ordinary right censoring,” although under the chosen estimand that case has not entered the default clock at all.

Cohort accounting is softer than the narrative claims. Linkage is part of the stated unit of analysis, but the flow table leaves all 2,900 transfers in the warehouse partition and does not show how a 60% linkage rate would merge, drop, or double-count reappearing accounts. The code never resolves linkage and does not mark that omission.

Prefix logic slips in two places. Distinct prefix identifiers do not by themselves keep prefixes from the same application in one fold; the split key has to remain the original case. In the code, ties at the same timestamp are broken by activity name. That ordering is deterministic and substantively wrong: “Accepted” sorts before “Credit Review,” and “Account Activated” sorts before “Contract Signed.”

The skeleton is about 230 lines against the requested 80–160, and several of its checks do not test what they appear to test.

- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` already drops traces shorter than \(k\) and returns length-\(k\) traces, so `short_prefix_count` is structurally zero while the printout says short prefixes were reported.
- The timestamp assertion compares each prefix event with the maximum timestamp of that same prefix, which cannot fail.
- Duplicates are removed before feature construction, so `duplicate_indicators` is identically zero.
- `state()` labels every activated case with a non-null label as `activated_mature_labeled`, including defaults observed before day 125. Those cases are then excluded from `eligible`, so the printed cohort and the analysis set disagree.
- `documents_total` is absent from `FORBIDDEN_FEATURES`. `risk_score` is aggregated from prefix rows with no valid-from check and no comment that this assumes the value on each row is contemporaneous rather than a backfilled warehouse score.
- Terminal and post-activation activities are removed before `features` is built. That is appropriate for the early-warning model and inappropriate for the activation-propensity model the text says should be fit on prefix features: early rejects never enter that table.

The migration-era split is also ahead of the labels. With a 15 February 2026 extract and a 125-day maturity rule, applications started on or after 15 October 2025 have essentially no mature activated outcomes. The prose defers those cohorts to drift monitoring; the implementation still assigns them to `test_migration_era`. `policy_regime` cannot be learned from a training window that ends entirely before 1 July 2025.

Section 6 and Section 8 are the cleanest parts of the answer. The remaining design is usable only after the maturity gate is made symmetric in the written protocol, the cohort classifier is fixed, and the prefix audit is changed so that it can actually fail.
