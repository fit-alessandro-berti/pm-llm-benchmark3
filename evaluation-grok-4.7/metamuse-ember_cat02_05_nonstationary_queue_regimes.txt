7.7

The analysis is strong on regime logic, caveat handling, and format, but it is not nearly flawless: Section 5’s headline dispersion figures contradict both the data and the answer’s own code, and a few boundary labels and ratios are wrong.

The required eight-part structure is met. Definitions, units, and the opening-hours convention are clear. Assumptions A1–A4 are labeled. Outage and recovery are not treated as normal high-throughput states. Medians are not substituted for mean flow time. Little’s Law is restricted to opening-hour diagnostic ratios, with arrivals and completions kept separate when they diverge. The peak-field versus hourly-vector conflict is real (for example, 17/101 = 16.8% against a stated 31%), as are the 2,519 versus 2,409 mass balance and the 110-case closure problem. Primary regime means, ranges, WIP, staffing, and median-workload proxies check out, including baseline weekdays 701/7 and 697/7, complex pre-intervention weekdays 798/7 and 704/7, and proxies near 0.59, 0.94, and 0.76. The flux-state alternative is genuinely different, and its circularity and mix confounding are acknowledged. Pooled staffing, the bimodal complex share, and the staffing–WIP reversal are used appropriately. The Python scaffold matches the requested contents and would recompute the regime and Little’s Law figures correctly.

The dispersion table does not. With sample variance, arrival Fano/CV are about 3.23/0.62 on 2026-04-07 and 10.12/1.03 on 2026-04-21, not 3.91/0.68 and 12.36/1.14. Completion Fano on 2026-04-25 is about 0.78, not 0.82. Those are the quantities Section 5 claims to report, and the code’s `ddof=1` implementation would not print the prose values. Max/mean and top-two shares are right, and 2026-04-21 remains the burstiest day, so the qualitative conclusion survives, but the section is internally inconsistent.

Smaller faults: recovery completions/arrivals are 241/211 = 114.2%, not 114.4%; “$13.8/3.48=3.95$” does not follow from the rounded operands; the Section 6 label “Baseline weekdays, 2026-04-06–04-14” includes a weekend that the numbers correctly exclude; “WIP rising 37–50” is not monotone; and the weekday WIP span 20.3–44.3 omits the outage-day value 47. The rise in median and p90 on 2026-04-15–16 is also treated as service deterioration without enough weight on completion selection during an outage. Lag-1 dependence is discussed only as an expectation, despite being computed in code.
