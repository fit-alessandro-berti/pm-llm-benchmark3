3.7

The memo has the required six-part shape and catches the three headline traps in outline: I-03 is ineligible after Diagnosis Complete, I-05’s unchanged refresh is suppressed, and I-07’s new log is treated as new evidence. Those points are then undone by a non-executable scoring rule, a capacity plan that contradicts itself, and an impact table whose arithmetic does not support its conclusions.

## 1. Decision rule
The eligibility gate is the right kind of object: stage, first swarm versus repeat swarm, and an evidence change. It is not deployable as written. Comparison and arithmetic operators are missing in the priority formula, the tier cutoffs, and the “risk increase is 0.05” clause, so two operators could implement the same sentence.

Where the arithmetic can be checked, the score is the wrong objective. Several published scores equal unadjusted \(p_{\text{now}}\times\) breach value exactly (I-07 €3,600, I-08 €43,000, I-10 €5,060). That quantity is exposure under the observational prediction. The intervention’s value is absolute risk reduction times breach value, minus swarm cost and false-alarm burden. Effect ranges never enter the score, so I-08 (reduction 0.05–0.15, false-alarm burden €5,000) outranks cases with larger, more reliable effects. Swarm cost (€450) is absent. Remaining time is absent, so a 1.5 h deadline and a 12 h deadline are interchangeable. The spillover term is identical for every case, so it cannot change rank, and it subtracts a probability-like quantity from euros.

Calibration is collapsed to a midpoint shift of \(p\). SME +0.065 and Strategic Enterprise −0.085 are then not the inputs that generate the printed scores (I-02’s stated adjusted probability 0.575 implies about €14,375, against a printed €16,190; I-04’s 0.395 implies €23,700, against €36,720). Non-Native-Language Support is given an invented +0.10. The brief says that segment is small-sample and uncertain in both calibration and effect, with no stated direction of bias. A one-sided bump manufactures urgency. I-07 also fails the rule’s own repeated-alert test: \(p\) falls from 0.75 to 0.72, yet the case is marked eligible. Tier labels disagree with the stated €5,000 Tier 1 line: I-01 at €5,460 and I-10 at €5,060 are both called Tier 2.

## 2. Capacity allocation
The table covers I-01 through I-10 and correctly blocks I-03 and I-05. It then assigns **Intervene** to six cases (I-02, I-04, I-06, I-07, I-08, I-09). The portfolio sentence selects four (I-02, I-04, I-06, I-08) and also reserves one slot. Four slots exist, so four interventions plus a reserve is infeasible, and a reserve with four uses is an empty reserve. The rationale’s claim that I-07 and I-09 are deprioritized contradicts both the Intervene rows and Section 4’s later escalation of I-07 to Tier 1.

Timing and robustness are inverted. I-04 and I-08 are early Triage with 10 h and 8 h left; I-09 is already in Diagnosis Started with 2.5 h left and a higher stated effect band; I-07 has 1.5 h left. The scarce slots go to long-horizon, high-exposure Strategic cases, including I-08, whose lower-bound direct value is about \(0.05\times€100{,}000-€450-€5{,}000<0\). I-06’s jump is explicitly compatible with a failed queue field, and the rule has no quarantine for imputed drivers before a slot is spent.

## 3. Expected-impact table
On the stated portfolio, absolute risk reductions sum to about 0.95 (upper ends), 0.67 (midpoints), and 0.38 (lower ends). “3 / 2 / 1 breaches avoided” is not that sum. Gross midpoint benefit is about €28.5k and gross upper-bound benefit about €41.8k (I-02 €5.5k, I-04 €16.8k, I-06 €4.5k, I-08 €15.0k). The printed €60–100k / €40–66k / €20–33k bands are roughly double, and the only worked line does not reconcile “risk reduction × intervention effect” with \(0.28\times€60{,}000=€16.8k\).

False-alarm burden on these four cases is €7,900, larger than the €1,800 labor bill, and it is omitted. Spillover arithmetic does not match the parenthetical formula. \(4\times6\times0.01\times€100{,}000=€24{,}000\), \(4\times6\times0.02\times€100{,}000=€48{,}000\), and \(4\times6\times0.03\times€100{,}000=€72{,}000\), against printed ranges of €180–€420, €360–€840, and €720–€1,680. The €100k “average” is the maximum listed value, not a mean of the book (about €29k) and not a value for the untreated diagnosis queue. The adverse narrative says the net result is marginal or negative, while the adverse row as printed is still largely positive. “High” confidence on the optimistic scenario overstates a case the brief defines as uncertain. The adverse requirement that at least one selected case receives little or no benefit is not applied to any named case. Delay minutes (8–20, or 15–30 under congestion) are not monetized or distinguished from the probability spillover.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
I-05 versus I-07 is directionally right and then contradicted by the portfolio. The clocks do not fit the process. Deadlines here are 1.5–12 h; a 4 h cool-down, a 24 h suppression, and a 7-day re-entry ban all outlast the cases they govern. A 45-minute swarm on I-07 consumes a large share of a 1.5 h residual SLA, which the rule never balances. Re-entry after a swarm requires closure, or seven days with no new alerts. The operating constraint is the opposite: a materially new event is the condition that permits another swarm. “Escalate the third evidenced alert to Tier 1 regardless of score” also discards value, effect, and false-alarm cost after the score was introduced to carry them.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
All five requested risks are named, and the safeguards are specific enough to test. The low-value quota does not bind: the reserved slot for cases under €10k applies only when no higher-value case is eligible, which is when that slot would be free anyway. Value ranking plus Strategic overprediction and SME underprediction pulls capacity toward the segment whose scores are already too high; the memo never joins those two facts. The Non-Native rule “intervene only if risk reduction ≥ 0.25” conditions on an effect that is unknown at decision time, and it conflicts with the +0.10 probability bump that made I-09 Tier 1. Tracking treated cases for 30 days and calling for synthetic control misstates the label problem: breach labels arrive on the SLA clock, treatment changes those labels, and the untreated prediction needs a logged assignment plus a holdout or encouragement design. A €500 weekly spillover cap is below one ordinary missed breach, and it does not check which segment absorbs the extra 0.01–0.03.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
The five monitoring families are present, and there are numeric triggers. Identification is still thin for this design. Post-hoc synthetic control or difference-in-differences on a few daily swarms does not recover an effect when treatment is chosen from the same score that is being evaluated. A slot-level randomization among eligible cases, or a pre-registered threshold experiment, is the design that matches four slots a day. “False-alarm burden exceeds €2,000 per week” treats an input penalty as if it were an observed outcome, without a definition of a false swarm. The ≤20-minute delay target sits at the top of the already-expected 8–20 minute range. The 5% fairness disparity has no denominator. Imputed-feature alerts, specialist interruption, and congestion-dependent spillover have no control chart. The policy-drift trigger (causal effect below half the observational range for two months) is at least an explicit stop rule.

The memo recognizes stage limits, repeat-alert hygiene, segment miscalibration, externalities, and label feedback. The implemented policy ranks by exposure, spends an infeasible set of slots, and reports scenario results that cannot be rebuilt from the case table. That is below a usable operating rule.
