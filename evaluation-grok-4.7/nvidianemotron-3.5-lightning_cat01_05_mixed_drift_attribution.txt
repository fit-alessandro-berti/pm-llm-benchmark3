4.5

The write-up is complete and directionally right on the main story: March is mostly label and provenance drift, mapped fitness stays near 0.86–0.91, and later rework, queueing, priority, and seasonality are separate mechanisms. That qualitative frame is undermined by quantitative and process-mining errors large enough that the numbers cannot be used.

The month-to-month table mixes percent and percentage-point changes and is frequently wrong. Mapped fitness is shown falling 23.9% then 9.3% from February to April, when it moved from 0.93 to 0.90 to 0.91. Raw fitness is given a negative August–September change when it rose from 0.53 to 0.56. Duplicate-`Open Case` percent changes are mostly incorrect, including a fabricated +300% from a zero base. Several cycle-time and p90 cells are off, including May versus March rather than April.

The claimed Manhattan distances do not match the six normalized rates. March differs from January by about 2, not 18, and the write-up explains that 18 with raw label splits that normalization already removed. July, September, October, and December are also wrong. `Assess → Senior Assessment`, the edge that actually jumps to 22 in October, is omitted from the distance. The cycle-time split is inconsistent with itself: “1% complexity ≈ 0.8 h” would account for nearly the entire January–October median rise, yet case mix is assigned only 20–25%. Service is given 15–20% although assessor service rose only about 0.6 h against a roughly 16 h median increase. “Cycle times nearly tripling” is false; the median rose from 31.5 h to 49 h and the p90 from 69 h to 121 h.

Other factual misses repeat. October fast-variant share is 38% after 50% in September, not a rise from 35%. August is not a missing-resource spike. March is not a performance change point: median cycle time was flat. Senior assessment at 22 per 100 cases is not the dominant decision route. Section 2’s examples of detours, duplicate `Open Case`, and missing resources are the artifacts normalization removes, so they do not show that a mapped-conformant trace still differs. Section 9 runs well past the 250–400 word limit.

The Python does not implement the stated analysis. Candidate “change points” are level thresholds that flag most post-March months. `perf_cp.to_list()` crashes because `tolist()` already returned a list, and the January duplicate rate of 0 divides by zero. Part B invents PM4Py APIs and constants (`discover_dfg` with an alpha DFG variant, `discover_skeleton`, `pm4py.declare.learn`, and several parameter constants) and ignores the supplied time window in the monthly functions.

Monitoring is broad and correctly rejects one universal threshold, but Benjamini–Hochberg is invoked without p-values, May–August is treated as an ordinary off-peak season despite the supplier and staffing shocks, and renewal is started in September. Rule 5’s service filter of under 0.5 h would miss the stated 4.8 h to 5.4 h rise, and rule 4 conflates the senior-assessment edge with the decision edge.
