The primary estimand is the right one: 90-day default only after activation and feed maturity, with rejects, withdrawals, transfers, and open cases kept out of \(Y=0\). The 48,000-case flow adds up (25,200 activated = 21,600 matured + 3,600 immature), the leakage table is directionally correct, and temporal validation is rightly preferred to a random split. The answer is still not reliable enough to implement as written.

The survival sensitivity treats all 3,600 immature or delayed activated accounts as right-censored at \(\min(T_{\text{asof}}-t_{\text{act}}, 90\text{d})\). That is valid only when follow-up is still inside the horizon and a default would already have been seen. Many of those cases have finished 90 days and are waiting on a feed that arrives 7–35 days later, or on a partner feed that may never arrive. Censoring them as event-free understates default. The mechanism table makes the same blur by calling never-activated open applications “ordinary right censoring” and “eligible for survival analysis,” even though their default clock has not started. Sensitivity 2 then restricts survival to the 25,200 activated cases, so the two statements disagree.

Inverse-probability-of-activation weighting is offered as a robustness check on a target the rest of the answer says is undefined for non-activated cases. Reweighting activated outcomes to the applicant mix is an extrapolation under conditional exchangeability, which the answer elsewhere correctly marks as untestable. Fine–Gray is named without a time origin. Default and rejection are not competing events on one clock unless the outcome is an explicit multi-state absorbing state reached only through activation.

The regime flag is not identified in the primary split. Training stops on 2025-03-31, entirely before the 2025-07-01 policy change and the 2025-10-15 migration, so a pre/post feature is constant in fit and cannot absorb the shift it is meant to mark. The maturity rule inside each fold also selects fast activations: a March 2025 start that activates slowly is unlabeled at the 2025-08-15 train as-of. That length bias is never stated. Section 2 still says training and calibration “use the 21,600,” which reads as the full matured export rather than the maturity-safe temporal subset.

Prediction time is defined as strictly before Accept, but metrics are computed only on cases that later activate and mature. That is \(P(Y=1\mid x, \text{activated}, \text{mature})\), not the precision of an early-warning rank list over applications still open at \(t_{\text{pred}}\). Coverage and an activation model are mentioned; an end-to-end protocol on the scored prefix population is not. `documents_total` is “repaired from events” even though the extract describes a nightly snapshot, not a document-upload event stream. The transfer cell is garbled (“unlinked  non-default”) at the exact point where a false non-default label would re-enter.

The skeleton contradicts the design. `case_timeline` labels maturity once at 2026-02-15, and `temporal_case_split` only slices `t0`, so a training case can receive a label that was not knowable at the train as-of. Cutoffs use `<= "2025-03-31"`, which drops most of that day, and starts from 2025-10-01 through 2025-10-14 fall into no fold. `get_prefixes_from_log` is unused. The live prefix takes the first \(k\) events with no pre-decision gate, so Accept or Account Activated can sit inside the feature window while the audit only marks those activities REVIEW. The cohort report collapses rejects, withdrawals, and transfers, so it cannot reconcile to 9,100 / 5,700 / 2,900 / 5,100. Policy regime never enters \(X\). The block is about 250 lines against the requested 80–160.

What remains solid is the refusal to invent non-default labels, the as-of vocabulary, the leakage dispositions for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, extract-time experience, and `label_available_at`, and the explicit limit that this is association among booked, matured accounts. Those choices carry the design. The censoring slip, the non-identified shift feature, and a skeleton that leaks later labels across a supposedly temporal split are what keep it from being a sound protocol.
