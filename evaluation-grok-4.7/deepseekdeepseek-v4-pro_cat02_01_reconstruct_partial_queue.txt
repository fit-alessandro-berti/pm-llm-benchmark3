4.7

The final write-up follows the required section structure and gets much of the case-level arithmetic right, but it is not internally consistent, several station-level results are wrong, and the bottleneck story is stronger than the evidence.

What holds up is the fully observed case math and the feasible windows for the missing starts. S1 waits and services for completed, timestamped cases are correct (central means about 64 and 28.3 minutes over P01–P20). S2 completed-case central means (about 31.8 minutes wait and 38.2 minutes service) match the stated imputations. The resource-constrained windows are also basically right: P05 on C1 after 09:45 and before 10:15, P07 on I1 only after P05 ends at 09:25, P11 on C2 only after P08 ends at 12:00, and P15 on I1 only after 11:40. OPEN versus final service is distinguished, censored cases are kept in the case table, and the rough capacity figures (S1 about 4.2/h, S2 about 3.1/h, external arrivals about 3.7/h) are dimensionally sound. Busy-time bounds for the four resources are in the right neighborhood, and I2 at 290/360 minutes is correct.

The station-level and temporal sections do not survive that standard. The subperiod rate table is materially wrong. P21 arrives at 11:50, not after noon. S1 completions credited to 10:00–12:00 include P15 and P16, who finish at 12:15 and 12:35. S2 completions in 12:00–14:00 are not zero: P10, P11, P12, P13, and P14 all finish in that window (12:10, 12:25, 13:00, 13:20, 13:50). The claim that afternoon S2 throughput collapses is therefore false, and it is used to support the temporal bottleneck narrative.

WIP is also off. Total S1 WIP depends only on arrivals and S1 completions, not on the missing starts. It reaches 8 repeatedly from 10:10 onward, with the longest spell around 11:15–11:40, not as a peak localized to 12:10–12:20. Time-average S1 WIP over 08:00–14:00 is about 5.8, not 4.5. S2 WIP is likewise determined by S1 completion and S2 completion times; it hits 7 from 13:45 to 13:50 (P20 has entered and P14 has not left), so the reported peak of 6 is the end-state, not the peak. The reported S2 mean near 3.8 is the one aggregate that lands close.

The sensitivity section contradicts itself. “Central” S2 imputations are the earliest feasible starts (P05 service 30, P11 service 25), i.e. the optimistic corner, not a midpoint. The S1 scenario means 63.8 / 64.0 / 65.5 and service means 28.5 / 28.3 / 27.0 move only P07; P15’s [90, 120] wait and [5, 35] service are omitted. Pessimistic S2 mean wait 35.7 uses waits of 50 and 80, which imply zero service, while the pessimistic service mean 35.0 assumes 5-minute services (waits 45 and 75). Central busy times (I1 285, C1 290, C2 260) do not match those central services, which would put I1 near 305 minutes and push C1 and C2 to the tops of their reported ranges. The station table’s S1 wait band of 60–69 matches neither calculation.

The bottleneck conclusion is too clean. S2’s longer service and a completion rate slightly below S1 output make an afternoon S2 constraint defensible, and the end inventory (S2 still holding about six cases, S1 down to P21 in service and P22 waiting) supports a shift. It does not support “structurally S2 all morning,” and the mechanism is wrong: S1 completion releases the S1 resource into an S2 queue, so S2 congestion does not block S1. S1’s own load does. Two scheduling facts also falsify the work-conserving FCFS assumption and are never flagged: I2 is free from 13:45 to 14:00 while P22 waits, and P09 starts on C1 at 11:00 while P08, who reached S2 at 10:20, waits until 11:15 for C2.

Required checks are missing or only rhetorical. There is no numerical Little’s Law comparison on any interval. Case-level total sojourn for completed cases is defined and then never reported. Timestamp rounding is assumed at ±2.5 minutes and then not propagated, including through exact handoffs such as P07 ending and P09 starting at 11:00 on C1. Censoring is acknowledged, but the station wait means stay unadjusted even though several unfinished S2 waits already exceed the completed-case mean.

The Python does not repair this. It would raise `KeyError` on `s2_end_flag`, which is never created. The WIP updater is not case-identity-aware and ignores the stated end-then-arrival-then-start order. Uncertainty scenarios are hardcoded strings, not pandas calculations. The second PM4Py metric call overwrites the first. Open cases fall out of the printed case table. The closing claim that the script reproduces the analysis is not true.

Score reflects a real case-level reconstruction and usable missing-start bounds, offset by incorrect rates, inconsistent sensitivity, a WIP/bottleneck story that the event times do not support, omitted required checks, and non-runnable code.
