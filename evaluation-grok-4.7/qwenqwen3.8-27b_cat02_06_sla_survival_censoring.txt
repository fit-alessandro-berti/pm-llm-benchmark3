7.5

The answer is a strong, well-structured survival design: time origin and the 72-hour horizon are clear, the three estimands match different decisions, competing risks are not treated as ordinary censoring, open cases are retained, immortal time and `Escalate` leakage are handled correctly, and the case-level bias discussion uses real IDs and correct cohort counts.

It is not nearly flawless. The state diagram omits observed transitions (`AutoCheck→Resolve` in S01, `Screen→Resolve` in S16, `Rework→Resolve` in S27), so the multi-state model is incomplete. Section 6’s “about 0.40 to about 0.44” for no-terminal-by-72 does not match the answer’s own Kaplan–Meier encoding (about 0.38 under transfer-as-competing, about 0.44 under transfer-as-censoring). The opening time definition also overstates that every non-event by 72 hours is administratively censored with a known outcome; open cases younger than 72 hours are truly right-censored. The hybrid transfer rule is only weakly distinct for the at-72 estimands, and its justification mixes the SLA breach with late-resolution competing-risk coding. The code’s CIF/KM logic is coherent and keeps open cases, but the PM4Py path uses a non-working API (`pm4py.OOP`, flat `case_id`/`activity` events rather than traces and `concept:name`), so `get_variants` will fail and the required control-flow step falls back to manual counting. Primary estimates also lack interval estimates despite the small sample.
