**5.3**

The diagnosis has the right multi-mechanism outline and all ten required sections, but it is not reliable. Quantitative claims are repeatedly invented or arithmetically wrong, several process-mining interpretations are incorrect, and those errors change the attribution.

The usable core is the separation of a sudden March logging shift from later rework, queueing, mix, and seasonality, plus the conclusion that change points are mechanism-specific. Mapped fitness staying near 0.86–0.91 while raw fitness stays near 0.5 is read correctly as a persistent label/schema break. Supplier Z’s April-to-May share jump (18% to 43%) lining up with rework (13% to 24%) and the median (32.0h to 36.5h) is the strongest behavioral timing argument. Assess service time moving only from about 4.8h to 5.4h is correctly treated as too small to explain the cycle-time rise. December is correctly left confounded. Segmentation and monitoring ideas point at the right cuts: supplier, complexity, priority class, resources, and separate schema versus flow versus time monitors.

Those strengths are undercut by false precision.

The cycle-time decomposition is not identified from the data. Queue-before-Assess of ~4–5h rising to ~18–22h, 6h then 8h per rework loop, and a 16-assessor base are not in the evidence. The prose then says queueing is +15h and 60% of the +13.2h, while the table says +10.0h and 76%. The required case-mix term is absent. Adding `rework_variant_pct × assumed hours` into the median is the wrong shape: at a 12% rework share the median case is still a fast-path case. Fast-variant decline (about 60% to 39%) is the composition fact that should have been used.

The correlation “r ≈ 0.91” is wrong and is still called causal evidence. On the monthly series, supplier share versus rework is about 0.81; May–September is about 0.83; May–August, the actual spike window, is near zero because supplier share falls in August while median, p90, and rework stay bad or worsen. August (median 45.0h, p90 110h, supplier Z 38%) is skipped in the timeline, and November (49.0h, p90 121h, rework 29%, complexity 45%) is collapsed into an October–December “improvement.”

Hellinger distances do not match the stated formula. Normalized edge rates give roughly 0.009 in March (reported 0.018), 0.076 in May (reported 0.067), 0.104 in July (reported 0.089), and 0.123 in October (reported 0.074). October is the larger control-flow move, not a smaller one. Treating overlapping directly-follows rates as a probability distribution is unjustified, and calling the result a lower bound is unjustified. The control-flow alert at Hellinger > 0.15 sits above every month in this crisis, so that monitor would not have fired. Other month-to-month errors are concrete: June median change is +4.3h, not +4.8h; April mapped fitness moves by +0.01, not 0.02; March high-complexity change versus February is +2pp, not +1.5pp; Request Info rates are filled in for months with no DFG. Half-year volumes are wrong (January–June is 3,900, not 3,820; July–December is 4,330, not 3,630; January–November is about 7,540, not 3,615).

Process-mining claims fail in the same way. The ~0.35 raw-to-mapped recovery is attributed to removing duplicate `Open Case` events and to missing resources. March has a 4% duplicate rate and 26 `Open Case → Register` events; the fitness break is the label split, 500 `Eligibility Screen` versus 140 `Validate Eligibility`. Standard control-flow fitness does not use resources, and the mapped series only renames the label and drops duplicate opens. The decision column is edges per 100 cases, not an 83% approval rate; the 12% reject and 5% pending baselines are invented. January’s 75 `Assess → Request Info` edges are called April counts and a “tripling”; 163/75 is about 2.2×, and the per-case rate doubles from 12 to 24. Senior Assessment was historically recorded inconsistently, yet that alternative is barely used. The conditional senior-assessment intensity stays modest through July (about 9 per 100 cases at 28% high complexity) and jumps in October (22 per 100 at 41% high complexity). A high-confidence July behavioral change point for the priority rule is not supported; staffing is also pulled into May by an “anticipation” the timeline does not state.

The monitoring and code sections inherit these numbers. Bonferroni is applied to non-statistical thresholds. The primary month × supplier × complexity design produces cells the same section treats as too small. Part B is not one block, does not call `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.get_variants`, or `pm4py.discover_temporal_profile`, treats a temporal profile as a list of timedeltas rather than mean and standard deviation, and can lose column names after `format_dataframe`. Log skeletons and DECLARE models are absent. Sections 2 and 9 also overshoot the word caps, and section 9 restates the bad correlation, the 76% share, and the invented capacity loss at high confidence.

The account would be revisable into a strong diagnosis if the fabricated quantities were removed and only timing, bounded service-versus-cycle gaps, and explicitly assumed ranges remained. As written, a reader cannot tell which numbers were measured.
