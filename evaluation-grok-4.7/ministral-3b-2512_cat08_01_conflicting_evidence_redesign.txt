3.5

The required seven-section skeleton is present, and a few segment facts are used correctly (Premium high-value 22% vs 5% complaints and 20h vs 96h; unclear-defect median 140h; manual-review pressure above 82% utilization; portal effect cited as 30–45% in the diagnosis). That is not enough. The diagnosis and all three redesigns repeatedly misread the mining artifacts, invent operating volumes and euro amounts, and contradict themselves. An executive sponsor would be steered by false compliance rates, false capacity, and a reversed cost story.

Evidence errors that change the decision:
- Fast approval is described as causing “31% higher rework/cost.” The log says those cases are 31% cheaper before rework and still 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and credits. The advantage shrinks; it does not reverse.
- 9.2% is the complaint rate for all cases. Fast approval is 14.1%. 3.3% is only the repair-quote → approval → repair path, not “manual paths.”
- Dispatch → complaint is mean 96h / median 70h / p90 180h. The answer calls 96h the median and turns the separate 72h mean (fast approval → complaint) into “72% of complaints linked to dispatch errors.”
- Fitness 0.91 and 68% fitting traces are inverted into “68% of fitting traces violate R1,” and 2550/3600 auto→fast-approval transitions are falsely treated as claims above EUR 400. Aggregate DFG counts are also treated as weekly volumes even though no time window is given.
- The 24-hour service-credit threshold belongs to Premium low-risk accessories; the 72-hour tolerance with status visibility belongs to Standard low-risk accessories. Both are attached to the wrong segments. “Fast-path abandonment is 2× manual” and “30% re-requests” are not in the data (repeats are 26% of cases that enter evidence request).
- Manual intake is 1400/5000, not 14%. Fraud capacity is 2 × 36h, not 28h/week. Seven reviewers at EUR 34/h are not a EUR 1,000/week fixed cost.

Redesign logic does not survive those numbers. A, B, and C all keep the same EUR cutoffs, portal, and “dispatch only after evidence, except Premium low-risk” rule. A and B are parameter variants, not materially different designs. Invented gates at EUR 100 and EUR 200 are not policy. The portal’s 30–45% reduction in repeated requests is rewritten as a completion rate, then as a 60% reduction and a 45% failure rate. “6h + current 8h median = 8h median for 90% of Premium cases” is arithmetically impossible. Stated savings (EUR 1,200, 5,000, 7,200, 10,000, 10,920 per week) are unsupported and violate the instruction not to invent exact ROI.

Redesign C is internally invalid. It routes reopens to the escalation board instead of manual eligibility, then claims this enforces R3. R3 requires manual eligibility before close or denial. The board is one 45-minute daily slot and is already described as not scalable; sending the complaint population there is a capacity break, not a 30% load reduction or a 12h median. Quality check on repairs also misses the actual long tail: wrong replacement at dispatch. Inspectors are underused, and repair→quality check already dominates that path.

The comparison table has the requested columns, but the cells reuse the bad rates, split contradictory cost lines, and assert that A dominates B for high-value cases. Failure modes name real resources, yet several mechanisms are wrong (part-price delay blamed on risk review; repair-quote delay “displaced” by a portal that does not increase repair volume). The experiment plan has pilots, thresholds, and a control-group idea, but the thresholds (<5% violations, <10% Premium complaints, >20% improvement) have no baseline, complaint outcomes are long-tailed (p90 150–180h), and one-month pilots are not tied to that censoring. Sequencing treats three overlapping alternatives as a stack.

Net: format compliance with process-mining reasoning that is systematically unreliable.
