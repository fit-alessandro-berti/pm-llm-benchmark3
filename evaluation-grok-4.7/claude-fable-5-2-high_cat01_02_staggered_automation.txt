8.2

The analysis is unusually strong: staggered-adoption contrasts are arithmetically accurate, median and p90 are kept separate, bundled treatment and fraud-desk interference are taken seriously, and the rollout design actually repairs the identification failures. It is not nearly flawless.

The verified pre/post levels, DiD ranges, go-live steps, dose values, placebo leads, and permutation window all match the panel. The cohort-time contrasts, fast-path accounting identity, and refusal to convert a branch-median dose slope into hours per automated case are correct. Sections 1–10 and the code meet the required structure, and the script states assumptions as assumptions and warns about five clusters and aggregated quantiles.

Deductions:

- The confidence column is defined as the probability that the qualitative assessment is correct, then used as confidence in a benefit. “Not identified” at 30 for p90 and 25 for CSAT cannot mean both things. The p90 cell also mixes a certain raw increase of about 10% with an uncertain causal claim.
- “Nearly all of the median and p90 contrast comes from comparison branches getting slower” is true for Alpha and Beta and false for Gamma. On the May–June base, Gamma’s median fell 8.5 h while Delta and Epsilon also fell about 4.3 h.
- The opening line that every favourable number depends on shared-desk controls is too absolute. Gamma’s raw median drop and the go-live rework steps of −2.0, −2.4, and −2.0 are within-branch facts.
- The conditional claim reuses the −1 to −8 h interval after assuming away spillover and non-parallel trends, which are exactly what stretch the interval to −1 h and allow zero. Under those assumptions the reported control range is about −2.6 to −6.4 h. Calling p90 unidentified under the same assumptions also conflicts with the median logic: the p90 DiD is produced by the same contrast and is negative in every no-spillover specification shown (−4.8 to −9.4 h). Composition near the 10th percentile and staffing remain open, but that is a different claim from non-identification.
- Escalation is marked “Yes” identifiable while sharing the April cohort’s one-month post-fraud baseline and the same routing shock. Later text correctly lowers this to confidence 80; the estimand table overstates it.
- Gamma’s June FTE loss is listed as bias against a benefit. In the preferred May–June window that loss also worsens the immediate pre-period, which can inflate the apparent July drop.

These are local logic and wording failures in an otherwise disciplined argument, not arithmetic fabrication. They are enough to keep the answer out of the top band.
