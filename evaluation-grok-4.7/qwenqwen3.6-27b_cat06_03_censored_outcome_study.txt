5.5

The write-up correctly refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, transfers, or open cases as non-defaults, and it picks a conditional-on-activation 90-day default estimand with a competing-outcome alternative, a 48,000-row accounting that adds up, a leakage table covering the required fields, temporal rather than random splitting, and three real deployment feedback loops. Those choices match the decision the study has to make. The design then breaks the as-of and censoring rules that would make that estimand estimable, and the skeleton does not implement the pipeline it describes.

The estimand population is muddled. Section 1 scores every application that reaches the prefix, while the quantity is \(P(\text{default}_{90d}\mid\text{activated},\text{prefix})\). Training on activated prefixes and scoring everyone is a selection shift, and that transport problem is never stated. Prediction is “3 events or 5 days,” with no fence that keeps Accept, Contract Signed, or Account Activated out of fast prefixes, so the early-warning cutoff can contain the selection event itself. “Union of both constraints (whichever is earlier)” mixes two different prefix definitions.

Censoring is mis-specified. Delayed feeds are not survival censoring at day 90: censoring pending accounts at the horizon treats them as event-free and pulls default risk down. Immature follow-up should be censored at the last time non-default is known, which is before 90 days. IPAW “for selection” changes the estimand and still does not create outcomes for people who were never activated. Fine–Gray with activation as the entry event is the wrong competing-risk setup; the pre-activation exits have already occurred by then. Permanently missing partner feeds are called informative, then casually paired with multiple imputation.

Cohort and validation dates do not match the clock in the prompt. The 5,100 still-open cases are described as not having reached the prediction point; many will have long prefixes and simply no terminal state by 15 February 2026. Maturity is tied to application start. The 90-day clock starts at activation and the feed can lag 7–35 days, so a start on or before 1 November 2025 does not imply a mature label by the extract date. The test window is entirely after the 1 July 2025 policy change, so it does not span that change and cannot be stratified pre/post. Post-migration starts are only a short, least-mature slice and are pooled with the post-policy, pre-migration regime. The split also ignores label-availability lag: June starts are not mature in time to tune a model that is deployed in July.

The code is about 50 lines, short of the requested skeleton, and it does not build terminal state, censoring reason, or label maturity from `label_available_at`. `is_mature` is `start_time <= cutoff`. `has_default` maps a missing `default_90d` to false. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with `activity` and `timestamp` rather than `activity_key` and `timestamp_key`. Prefixes are computed and then unused. `discover_dfg`, `get_variants`, and temporal features are fit on full traces, including post-prefix and post-outcome events. The audit is `assert not df[leak_cols].notna().any()`, which both misunderstands leakage (those columns are populated later in the raw file) and is invalid Python because `not` is applied to a Series. The following print then declares the audit passed. The closing comment again censors immature activations at day 90.

Feature ideas and the leakage table are the strongest parts: drop `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, and future events; repair `risk_score` and resource experience; keep label time out of the model. The `documents_total` repair still assumes an event-level cumulative sum the schema does not provide, rather than the prior night’s snapshot. Taken together, the target stance is right and the execution of censoring, maturity, validation timing, and leakage control is not.
