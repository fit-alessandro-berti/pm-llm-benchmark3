The hybrid design is internally coherent, and the eight verdicts follow from the rules it actually states. The accepting net is replayable on the backbone: the three checks synchronize at classification, one `classified` token makes the routes exclusive, `before_outcome` keeps rejection and activation mutually exclusive, and payment and the two closures are at most once. Strict R5 stays out of binary DECLARE, so T1/T2 are not forced to carry a legal opinion, while T6 still fails the activation gate even though `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` holds. T4 fails only the resource rule (Cora verifies and approves). T5 is an unanswered request: the net permits it, alternate response and the skeleton’s equal-count/`always_after` rules do not. T7’s opinion is on time for the gate and late for the profile: `(34−12)/6 ≈ 3.67`, while the 2-hour payment gives `|2−72|/36 ≈ 1.94`, inside `zeta = 2`. T8 breaks the exclusive outcome at hour 10 and then closes without payment. R11’s dirty-bank versus eventual-suspension split matches the stated reading. Seconds, bounds (`>` at the boundary), and the two R6 clocks are calculated correctly.

The score drops because of executable and edge-case defects, not because the case outcomes are confused.

The empty `directly_follows` set does not mean “no adjacency rule” under `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`. That component is a whitelist of permitted consecutive pairs and is considered by default. An empty whitelist flags every consecutive pair, so T1–T8 do not receive the skeleton passes in the matrix when the dictionary is used as written. The write-up justifies omitting adjacency and warns about partial `activ_freq` and pair orientation; it never says to disable the directly-follows check or to treat the empty set as unused. Those skeleton cells are intent, not default-API results. The same gap does not affect the other five keys: unlisted frequencies are simply unchecked, and the stated anchor-first orientation (second component is the predecessor for `always_before`, the successor for `always_after`) is applied consistently, including later re-verification.

The R9 batch rule compares verification resources only after `Activate Vendor` has occurred. The same section’s online rule compares them when the approval occurs. A rejected or not-yet-activated case that still contains an approval is skipped by the batch procedure. T1–T8 are unchanged, and the narrowing is partly declared, but the two procedures disagree.

On the amended-policy row for T6, the policy is already fixed: the opinion is 10 hours after activation, and joint authorization is absent. The answer’s own aggregation rule then blocks certification, which is `not evaluated`. The overall cell is labeled `policy-dependent` anyway. The prose correctly adds that verified prior authorization would pass and that known invalid or late authorization would fail.

`Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` cannot occur before activation. R11 only adds obligations when a change is post-activation; the opening assumptions do not state this extra ban.

Layer ownership, the refusal to conjoin R5 with R6, vacuity, loop-broken frequency invariants, and the warning-only status of R12 are handled cleanly. The deductions are local specification and boundary mismatches, not a wrong policy or a wrong reading of T1–T8 under the rules as written.
