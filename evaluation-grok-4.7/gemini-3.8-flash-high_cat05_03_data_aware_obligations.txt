6.5

The case outcomes are mostly the right release-time readings, but the formalization, the checker, and parts of the control-flow critique do not meet the standard the narrative claims.

Temporal reconstruction is the strongest part. Blanks are treated as non-updates, state is carried forward only, settlement amount is not copied back onto TX-305, and event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are separated cleanly for EX-77 and EX-88. Release snapshots for all eleven cases match the log. The best classifications follow: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, and TX-309 are correctly decided, including the 56.25%, 4.55%, and 22.88% tests, the exact EUR 100,000 boundary, the non-invalidating decrease, and the rule that a standard-packet approval does not satisfy R2. TX-305 is correctly INDETERMINATE. TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT is a defensible application of R5: a post-release certificate plus the requester’s own oral claim does not establish timely approval. TX-311 is correctly COMPLIANT because release-time jurisdiction is DE.

Those results are undercut by inconsistent machinery.

The R3 formula is wrong. `∀ e_scr ≺ e_upd (act(e_scr) = Sanctions_Screen)` says every earlier event is a screen. Create_Transaction falsifies that, so the formula does not trigger on TX-304, contradicting the findings table. `e_upd` is also free in the obligation. The table’s “latest screen, then list update, then a new screen” reading is the actual rule; the pseudo-logic does not state it.

R2 never requires the Enhanced_Review to be the one that postdates the HIGH or PEP change, and it never encodes the standard-packet limit. R4’s formula only defines a >20% predicate. It does not state that a decrease preserves the review, that an increase from an unknown base is indeterminate, or that invalidation applies only to amount-dependent obligations. R6 marks R1 indeterminate whenever amount or jurisdiction is missing, even if the known conjunct already falsifies the trigger (known DE, or known amount under EUR 100,000). R1’s `IsMaterialChange` also treats creation, every jurisdiction edit, a move to HIGH, and a move to PEP as review-invalidating. The policy defines that effect for a >20% amount increase.

The findings table calls R4 “triggered” on TX-306 and TX-308, where the invalidator does not fire. TX-302 is labeled JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION even though the Enhanced_Review at 09:20, with no later material change, already discharges R1; the valid exemption is surplus. Section 4 admits COMPLIANT, but the primary row does not. TX-311’s decisive-evidence cell says the tie-break places the jurisdiction update before release. The 16:20 versus 16:30 timestamps do that. `event_index` only places Update_Jurisdiction after Enhanced_Review.

Section 4 covers the required cases, but the TX-305 reading in which a missing amount is treated as a small transfer and the case becomes COMPLIANT is the default R6 forbids.

The code recomputes the same labels on this fixture and correctly avoids back-filling TX-305. It still breaks the task constraints. R4 is a case-id lookup (`TX-301`/`TX-309` invalidated, `TX-308` decrease, `TX-306` small increase, everything else `NO_CHANGE`), so TX-311’s move from 90,000 to 115,000 is reported as `NO_CHANGE`. Ordering checks compare timestamps only, so equal timestamps never consult `event_index`. Sanctions detection special-cases `'XZ'`. `evidence_valid` is pre-labeled rather than derived. The DECLARE tail calls `pm4py.conformance_declare` and merges `case_id`, `no_dev_total`, and `dev_fitness`; that API and those columns are not a reliable executable contract, so the block can die after the diagnostics print. Tracked review and screen times never appear in the diagnostics frame.

Section 6’s TX-305, exemption, TX-309, missing-data, and tie-break modes are real. Two illustrations are not. A cut to a lower amount before release is already the release-time state, so final-state leakage does not “excuse” an earlier review. A static Enhanced_Review-before-release constraint does not false-alarm on TX-308, which has that review, and TX-304 is neither domestic nor a clean compliant case. Section 7 does hit immutable release payloads, the four timestamps, rule version, exemption scope, change provenance, and stable ordering.
