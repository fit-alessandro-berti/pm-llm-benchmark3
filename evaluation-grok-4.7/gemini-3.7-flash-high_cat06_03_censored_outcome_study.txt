6.2

The draft recognizes selective observation and refuses to code rejection as solvency, but it never locks one estimand, and the validation windows and label code contradict the design.

The primary target is several incompatible quantities at once. Section 1 mixes a multi-state cumulative incidence, a cause-specific hazard, and \(P(\text{default}, \text{activated}\mid X)\). The hazard has no defined time origin: rejection, withdrawal, and transfer occur on a pre-activation clock, while the 90-day default clock starts at Account Activated. “Activated–non-default” is survival past a fixed horizon, not a competing event. Prediction time is also two rules (“first Credit Review” or \(k=3\)). Section 3 then makes inverse-probability-of-activation weighting part of the primary analysis, which changes the estimand to a covariate-standardized or counterfactual risk and needs positivity and ignorability. Section 1 says positivity fails and unconfoundedness is untestable; Section 3 says weighting is valid “where positivity holds.” Section 8 retreats again and says the study only supports \(P(\text{default}\mid\text{activated}=1,X)\). Those are different decision questions. The factual joint risk is identifiable with censoring assumptions; \(E[Y(1)\mid X]\) is not. The write-up treats them as one analysis.

Cohort arithmetic does sum to 48,000, and the rejection/withdrawal/open distinctions are directionally right. The 3,600 immature activations are asserted to be exactly the post-2025-10-13 activations; the brief does not say that. Permanently missing partner feeds are drawn as an extra branch under 25,200 activations that are already fully allocated. “About 60%” linkage is turned into 1,740 cases with outcome follow-up. A linkage table is not a matured `default_90d` feed. The maturity rule also ignores per-case `label_available_at` and the 7–35 day lag variation.

The censoring taxonomy is the right checklist, but the primary estimator stacks Aalen–Johansen, cause-specific Cox, censoring IPCW, and selection IPW without saying which mechanism each weight addresses. Administrative end-of-study censoring is deterministic given activation time and the 15 Feb 2026 extract; it is not the same object as MNAR partner dropout. Open applications are lumped with immature activated accounts under “non-informative” censoring even though their default time has not started. Calling MAR of delayed feeds fully testable is wrong; comparing observed lags can only partially probe dependence on covariates. Manski bounds are aimed at a different, mostly counterfactual estimand, and they omit withdrawals, still-open cases, and immature activations. The bounds have no stated denominator.

Prefix rules correctly keep short traces, map renamed activities before cutting, and stop aggregations at the cut. They then put an absorbing terminal indicator on traces that already ended. A case rejected at event 2 has the competing outcome inside the feature window. Eligibility has to be a landmark risk set: still unterminated at \(t_{\text{pred}}\). Fixed-\(k\) and elapsed-time cuts are never tied back to one prediction time, so document-loop features are often post-decision relative to first Credit Review and pre-decision relative to \(k=5\).

The feature taxonomy covers control-flow, timing, queue, rework, and calendar regime, and it correctly separates static, prefix, and historical-reference fields. The resource default rate is only “resolved before \(t_{\text{pred}}\).” Resolution is not label availability. A default known in the retrospective extract but not by `label_available_at` \(\le t_{\text{pred}}\) is as-of leakage. Full-trace conformance applied to prefixes is also undefined without a prefix alignment and a model fit only on past training prefixes.

The leakage table is the most reliable section. Dropping `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, post-cut events, and the label fields, and repairing same-day `documents_total`, later `risk_score` updates, and extraction-time tenure, matches the risks in the export. `final_limit` is overstated as absent on rejects and as a perfect accept/reject separator; the brief only says it appears after the final credit decision. The code never builds the `documents_total` repair, and tenure repair depends on a hire date that is not in the schema.

The temporal-split rationale is right: a random case split crosses the 1 Jul 2025 policy change, the migration, and label maturity. The implemented windows do not do what they claim. Maturity depends on activation time plus 90 days plus feed lag, not case start. Cases started in the “fully matured” train and Jul–Sep validation sets can activate after 2025-10-13 and still be unlabeled on 2026-02-15. The supposed migration test is case starts from 1–13 Oct 2025, which ends before the 15 Oct 2025 migration. Activations on or after 15 Oct are not mature under a 125-day rule by 15 Feb 2026 (15 Oct + 125 days is 17 Feb). There is no fully labeled post-migration default test; prefix shift is all that can be checked. The IPCW Brier formula and Uno’s C-index are single-event survival metrics. They omit observed competing events instead of scoring a cause-specific cumulative incidence. That does not match the stated multi-state target.

Limitations correctly separate conditional association, selection, and counterfactual default, and the four deployment loops (auto-reject erasing labels, withdrawal of solvent applicants, reviewer behavioral drift, partner gaming) are concrete. “Statistically consistent” is stronger than the design can support under policy shift, MNAR feeds, and an unsettled estimand.

The skeleton does call `read_csv`, parse timestamps, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, and a guarded `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`, and it drops the worst columns before prefixing. It then undoes the paper’s main rule: inside the maturity window, a missing `default_90d` is filled with 0 and stored as `Activated_NonDefault`. `label_available_at` is parsed and never used. Rejected, withdrawn, and transferred cases are flagged `is_mature=True` although their activation outcome is undefined. No survival time, cause indicator, or censoring indicator is built, while the closing comment points at Cox, Fine–Gray, and IPCW as if those columns existed. Train, validation, and test ids are computed and never attached to the feature table. Datetime fields are compared with date strings. The `hasattr` fallback can silently replace the prefix API, and that path still keeps terminal events. At roughly 220 lines, with fabricated activity synonyms and no assumption comments on the imputation, the skeleton is not a reliable implementation of the study it describes.
