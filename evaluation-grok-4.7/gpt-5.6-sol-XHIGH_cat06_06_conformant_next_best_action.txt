The answer is a strong, usable ranking: unlawful shortcuts stay at the bottom, re-screening and self-certification are not treated as substitutes for independent second review, SAR filing is not treated as authorization, and the S2/S4, P-901, API, and override-timing tradeoffs are all confronted with ranges rather than a fake expected-utility number. It is not nearly flawless.

The main defect is in the action-forcing reranking rule. Section 4 correctly says a vendor clear cancels second review only if the fuzzy match is formally resolved and a fresh assessment shows the high-risk trigger is gone. Section 6 then tells the operator that a clear result “does not remove S2 while the risk score remains at least 70.” That drops the independent unresolved-match trigger. A refreshed score under 70 with the 0.46 match still open would still require a second officer. The checkpoint where someone would actually skip S2 is the place this is least precise.

The S4 analysis is arithmetically sound (35+35+5=75 through 125; 65+35+5=105) and rightly says a firm early S4 finish would outrank interrupting P-901. The primary plan still interrupts immediately, and the reranking trigger is described as the screen result “at the same decision checkpoint.” That leaves the rank-changing observation — a committed S4 start and finish inside the remaining 55 minutes — ambiguous as to whether it is checked before the P-901 interrupt. The 0.12–0.28 spillover is also applied as if it covered the whole 35–75 minute review, although the prompt ties it to a 45-minute interruption.

Smaller looseness: E’s benefit cell claims avoidance of the €100,000–€500,000 unlawful-execution exposure, which every non-executing conformant path shares; B is given that same execution-penalty range after the answer says B has no valid execution continuation; a valid override is credited with deadline value without a clear residual substantive sanctions risk. The “expected benefit” column is really conditional severity, though the answer later says so. None of these invert the conformance order.

A and B are correctly non-compensatory, F and G are gated on facts the case does not yet have, and the D-then-C fallback is explicit. Those are why the score stays in the low 8s rather than falling further.
