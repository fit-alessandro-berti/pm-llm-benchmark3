6.0

The regime design is the right operational story, and the Little’s Law verdicts are directionally sound. The write-up is not reliable enough to score higher: flow-conservation totals contradict each other and the table, several load-bearing rates are wrong, and draft uncertainty is left in the final text.

The primary segmentation is defensible. Separating weekends, the seven pre-outage weekdays, 2026-04-15, the 04-16/17 catch-up, the 04-20–04-28 mix/stress weekdays, and the 04-29 staffing change is the correct mechanism split. Treating recovery and the intervention as transients, and offering a pooled post-04-20 alternative plus a burstiness alternative, matches the task. Service time is correctly kept distinct from sojourn, opening hours are defined as 12 hours, and the matrix rightly marks the outage day, a single day, recovery, and the full 28-day pool as unfit for a steady-state law.

The arithmetic does not support those claims. R1 is 701 arrivals and 697 completions (net +4), not 720 and 707 (net +13). R4 daily nets are 14, 14, 16, 12, 10, 16, and 12, so +94, not +79; mean arrival rate is 114/day (9.50/h), not about 112 (9.3/h). R3 surplus is 17+13 = 30, not 44, so the outage deficit of 47 is not “nearly” cleared (about 17 remain before the weekends, which themselves net to about zero). R5 is −18, not −17. The same post-mix gap is given as +49, +62, and +79. All 28 days are 2519 arrivals and 2409 completions, net +110 (about +3.9/day), not 2676 − 2614 = +62 and not a near-balance of 95.6 versus 93.1. There are 20 weekdays, not 19; weekday mean WIP is about 34.9, not 36.2. Section 6 then doubts its own 12-hour convention (“24h?12h?”) and leaves the boundary check unfinished (“1.7× avg L??”).

Those errors change interpretation, not just rounding. The pooled system does not look balanced; it accumulated about 110 cases. Recovery throughput did not replace the outage deficit. R1 has essentially no observed surplus, so “~1 completion/h of spare R1 capacity” and “44 cases cleared in two days” are not in the data. Median-based load in §4 gives R4 utilization about 0.90 and “adequate” pooled staffing; the mean-service calculation in §7 gives required capacity above 6 FTE. Both cannot be the staffing conclusion. Slack is also misstated (the cited 21% does not follow from 6.26 and 4.65).

The hourly Fano and CV figures for the four vectors are largely correct, and completions being smoother than arrivals is a real point. The peak comparison is not. For 2026-04-21, 8+34 = 42 arrivals is 36.5% of 115, not 42% or 47%, and those bins are not 08:00–09:59. The same one-hour shift is used on the other vectors, and the text still says the vector shares match `peak_08_10_pct`. Marginal Fano is also over-read: a diurnal rate profile alone produces Fano well above 1, so R1 Fano ≈ 3 does not identify batch arrivals. The later claim that binning only underestimates burstiness misses that inflation. The “90% interval via IQR,” the uncomputed correlations (0.95 / 0.4 / 0.3), the “~15pp” SLA effect, and the uncomputed M/M/c “factor of 2” are stronger precision than the evidence.

The code would have caught several totals (`net_total` on the stated masks), but the prose was not brought into line with it. It also does not compute the KL divergence the narrative cites, hardcodes the change-point dates already chosen, and prints a fixed “145 vs 320 min” line instead of the values just calculated. Symbols are dropped in §1, and “7.8×?” remains in §4. Assumption A4 (mean/median 1.1–1.3) is applied as about 1.08 and 1.14.

Qualitatively, the outage is not treated as a high-productivity steady state, Simpson-style mix confounding is recognized, and the risky pooled policy is the right kind of recommendation. Under a strict standard, repeated breaks in the conservation identity, the false peak reconciliation, the unresolved utilization contradiction, and unfinished numerical reasoning keep this well below a near-flawless analysis.
