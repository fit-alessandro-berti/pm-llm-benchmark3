**5.5**

The memo has the right skeleton and a correct refusal of “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” but several decision-relevant numbers and signs are wrong, the required control contrast is misstated, and the code does not implement the design it advertises.

The pre/post means in §5 are arithmetically right (Alpha median 48.9→48.5, p90 98.5→108.1; Beta 45.9→47.8 and 92.9→102.7; Gamma 40.6→37.2). Almost everything built on a comparison with Delta and Epsilon is not. There are 40 branch-months, not 64, and that false count is repeated as the sample the event study “leverages.” Beta’s Jan–Mar medians drift slightly up (45.4, 45.9, 46.4), not “slightly improving.” Over the same windows the memo uses, Delta and Epsilon medians rise by about 4–6 hours and p90 by about 15–18 hours, more than Alpha (+9.6 h p90) or Beta (+9.8 h p90). Beta’s median rise of +1.9 h does not “exceed that counterfactual.” A crude difference-in-differences therefore points toward shorter medians and smaller tail increases among early adopters, with regression to the mean still able to explain the median gap. §7’s “p90 likely worsened,” range 0% to +12%, confidence 60, and the safe claim that tails “may lengthen” treat the raw adopter increase as the effect after the memo itself notes that controls rose.

Escalation is over-claimed in the other direction. Adopter pre/post increases of about +2 to +3 pp sit on top of a March fraud-desk jump already inside the pre window and on control increases of roughly +1.7 to +1.9 pp. The excess is on the order of 1 pp, not a conditionally causal +2–3 pp. Confidence 70 and the unhedged sentence “Automation increases specialist escalations” are not a safe leadership claim. The same averaging hides a real process pattern: rework falls in the first adoption month (Alpha 13.2→11.2, Beta 12.8→10.4, Gamma 14.6→12.6) and then jumps in May in treated and untreated branches together with complexity.

The intensity sentence is not an arithmetic fact. A within-adopter association is closer to about −1 hour of median per +10 pp fast-path share than to “0.6 h,” the sign is missing in §5, and the reported standard errors are not produced by any shown calculation. Fast-path share rises by roughly 40 pp while escalation rises only a few points, so the main substitution is manual fast path → automated fast path; that share arithmetic, which should discipline how large a cycle-time effect was even possible, is absent. Pre-period `automated_fast_path_pct` of exactly 4–6% with `automation=0` is never flagged. Alpha’s loss of two FTEs is folded into “one-FTE shocks.” The sensitivity range is written “4% to +4%,” which cannot contain 0, and no alternative window or adjusted contrast is shown.

Identification is more careful than the estimates. The estimand table separates availability from intensity, median from tail, and flags interference. The primary write-up still targets an ITT under parallel trends while the routing campaign is known to be assigned with treatment. “No concurrent shocks correlated with treatment” is already false. The justification paragraph is about 100 words, short of the required 150–250. §8 is also short of 250–400 and, after both Delta and Epsilon adopt, the scale-up rule calls for a difference-in-differences with no remaining untreated branch.

§4 and parts of §6 are full of stripped comparison signs, so several mechanism and bias-direction cells do not state a direction. The treatment-intensity threat row puts the bias direction in the test column. §10 labels as “Observed” a same-month intensity correlation that is not a raw fact; the escalation bullet repeats the 2–3 pp overclaim.

The Python block embeds the CSV and the printed pre/post medians would match §5, but it estimates static two-way fixed effects rather than the event study, never uses `rel_month` in a model, plots with `subplot` despite an explicit ban, reports only one outcome in the pre/post loop, labels the regression “ITT,” and clusters with G=5 (G=3 in the intensity model) while saturating month and branch dummies, so the cluster covariance is rank-deficient. Causal assumptions are not written as comments.

What keeps this above a failing memo is the pre/post table, the availability-versus-use distinction, a real threat list, low confidence on CSAT, and a rollout choice that at least staggers adoption and names queue, spillover, and stop rules. Those do not repair the control contrast, the tail and escalation conclusions, or the non-reproducible intensity results.
