6.3

The required headings, all 15 cases, a real two-sided argument, and a non-vacuous system judgment are present. Authority, expiry, and scope are mostly handled well: PD-3 versus CS-9, FD-7’s hard end, SUP-703 and SUP-708 as serious violations, SUP-705 and SUP-714 as unauthorized continuations, and SUP-709/SUP-710 as scope problems. The prosecution and defense each concede something real, and the judgment rejects specific claims on both sides rather than averaging them.

The model revision is not sound. The same `quality`, `activate`, and `manual` objects are reused across XOR branches, so this is not a valid POWL. `LOOP(Request_More_Info, silent)` makes the request mandatory on every path, including compliant SUP-701 and rejection; an optional repeatable request is `LOOP(silent, Request_More_Info)`. Sanctions and quality are serialized even though concurrency is required and was already expressible exactly as in M0. The code therefore rejects legal interleavings CS-9 permits. `silent=True` is also the wrong PM4Py constructor; `SilentTransition` was required in substance. The limitation note misstates that concurrency as something POWL cannot do.

Other analytical errors are material. “N11” is not a source. SUP-715 is grouped with CS-9’s different-human rule, although the actual issue is an unrecorded conflict disclosure between reviewer and approver. PD-3’s required master-data validation never appears. SUP-711 is determinate duplication, not an indeterminate conformance question. SUP-713’s mapping was approved after the case, so high-confidence COMPLIANT overstates the record. The architecture claims concurrent standard checks that section 7 then fails to build.
