5.6

The main verdicts are right: A and B are not visibly trace-equivalent, P1 and P3 fail, and P2 holds only after check counts and interleavings are collapsed. T1–T4 are real witnesses, loop scope and check cardinality are right, and the bounded-vs-proof warning is right. The write-up still misses the required standard by a wide margin because several precise claims are false, the required explorer cannot run, and numeric results are invented.

The P1 “equivalence” is not an equivalence. It says the compliance bullets hold for every trace, but it never says of which language, and it never defines the observation that two models must agree on. P1 also keeps approval count and placement visible; the definition drops that. The P2 map is closer, but encoding “starts with Open Case” and “ends with Close Case” as booleans records only success or failure of those tests, not the activities themselves. Duplicate labels are correctly treated as ordered occurrences.

Section 2 is largely accurate: A checks once per iteration, B checks once per case, approval is 0/1 per iteration, and Close Case waits for the preceding join. The placement row omits B’s important interleaving in which Approve Revision sits between Analyze Revision and Check Revision.

That omission becomes a false caveat in Section 3. Single-iteration languages are already different. `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` is in B and not in A, because A’s parallel must finish before the approval choice. Length 5 and one analysis, so it is inside the P3 bound. The claim that divergence starts only at two analyses, and that one-iteration traces are “identical in activity set,” is false for trace equivalence. For two or more analyses the languages are disjoint, since A has one check per analysis and B has exactly one check; that part is right. The P2 row should use the required verdict `equivalent only under stated abstraction`.

Section 5 does not implement Petri-net firing. `arc.source.multiplicity` is not a pm4py place or arc attribute; weights live on arcs, and the usual step is `semantics.execute` or `marking[place] ± arc.weight`. As written, the search raises `AttributeError` before producing a trace. Markings are also used raw inside a `set` key, complete traces are only sampled rather than printed, and the text then says this exhaustive search “successfully produces” T1 and T3. The exhaustive-versus-`play_out` distinction is the only solid part of the section.

Section 6’s general warning is fair: a footprint is pairwise, and a small EMD under an undeclared playout policy is not language equality. The concrete footprint claim is wrong. In A, the next iteration is `+(Analyze Revision, Check Revision)`, so Check Revision can directly follow Rework Case. A trace such as `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case` is in A. Footprints do differ, but for another reason: B allows Approve Revision directly before Check Revision, while A does not.

Section 7 reports exact figures that the supplied function does not produce. Hand expansion gives 12 nodes, 6 visible leaves, 1 silent leaf, and 0 duplicate labels for both trees; those four numbers are right. The same depth function returns 5 for A and 6 for B, while the table says 4 and 4. Edge depth is 4 for A and 5 for B, so the models are not equally deep under either reading. `0.682` and `0.714` are not derived. `simplicity_petri_net` was never run, and identical three-decimal scores for different nets are not credible. That violates the instruction to report exact values only when derived.

The simplified tree is a real, small reduction of A: drop the inner `+` and force Analyze Revision before Check Revision, 11 nodes instead of 12. Its three positive traces and two negative traces match that tree. Calling P1 preservation “exact” overstates it. P1 requires a check before the next rework or close; it still allows Check Revision before Analyze Revision inside the iteration, and A accepts those traces. The sequential model is a strict subset and a stronger policy than P1. P2’s dashboard image is preserved. The simplicity gain used to justify replacement is the same fabricated pair of scores. Node-count 11 versus 12 is the only demonstrated simplification, and B is not matched on depth.

Use A for operational compliance, either original for the dashboard abstraction, and neither language as a stand-in for the other in bounded testing. The sequential tree can replace A only if check-before-analyze must be forbidden. Bounded disagreement up to 12 events and 3 analyses is evidence of difference here only because the check-count argument already covers every longer trace; the script shown does not establish that evidence.
