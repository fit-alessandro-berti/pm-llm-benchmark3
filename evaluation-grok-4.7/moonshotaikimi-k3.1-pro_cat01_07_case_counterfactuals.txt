The answer meets the required structure and is strong on control-flow, uncertainty, and interference. It keeps one explicit path per case, separates timing, routing, and decision-policy changes, and correctly treats K-337 as a change in the allowed decision rather than a pure speedup. Four-eyes, originals-before-document-check, assessment after both checks, and Request More Info only from Assessment or Compliance are respected. Ranges are tied to queues, manager windows, and rework instead of a single invented duration. The ranking and pilots weigh quality, reversibility, and spillovers, not cycle time alone.

Two reasoning gaps keep it short of near-flawless. First, the K-411 base turns a 1-hour earlier assessment finish into a 2.5-hour earlier Notify by shrinking M1’s factual ~2-hour gap to ~30 minutes without a queue mechanism. Second, the 100-case claim that K-104 breaks feasibility because 100 final reviews are 300–600 hours conflates the baseline C1 load, which those high-risk cases already require, with the incremental cost of preliminary work and reservations. Smaller issues: the K-219 22:30 D2 start is loosely related to the 5-hour forecast, K-104’s 2.5-hour final review is at the aggressive edge of the stated savings band, and after-hours M1 availability is used optimistically in best cases.
