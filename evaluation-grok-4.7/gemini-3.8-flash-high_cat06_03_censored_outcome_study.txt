6.5

The write-up correctly refuses to code rejects, withdrawals, and open cases as non-defaults, reconciles all 48,000 applications, and separates several missingness mechanisms. It is not a coherent, identified study. The primary estimand, the censoring rule, the backtest, and the prefix pipeline contradict each other.

**Target.** One primary formulation was required. Section 1 instead stacks three different estimands: the factual incidence \(P(J=1\mid\mathcal{H}_k)\), the activation-conditional default probability, and, in Section 3, IPSW with weights \(1/\hat e(X_k)\). Those answer different questions. The product formula is an identity only for a fully observed latent default indicator. With immature accounts it is not an observed-data functional, and IPSW moves the conditional piece toward the counterfactual \(E[Y(1)\mid\mathcal{H}_k]\) that Section 1 says is untestable. Calling the conditional factor “whether, if activated, the applicant is toxic” treats conditioning on a future, selective activation as an intervention. The five listed states are not a partition: immature and still-open cases sit outside \(J\), so \(P(J=1\mid\mathcal{H}_k)\) is not a CIF. Default is on a post-activation clock; rejection, withdrawal, and transfer are on a pre-activation clock. A flat Fine–Gray (or a cause-specific Cox, which is not the same model) does not implement that nested multi-state process. Schoenfeld residuals do not validate subdistribution hazards.

**Cohort and censoring.** The flow counts add up, including the 60/40 transfer split, and the 13 October 2025 / 125-day date arithmetic is right. The statuses are not. All 3,600 immature accounts are treated as one administrative censoring pool, so permanent partner-feed failure is never separated from “not yet matured.” Linkage is treated as an observed partner outcome; a link is not a comparable `default_90d`. Transfers are described as never activated. The primary label rule requires activation + 125 days before any label is used. That is a blunt screen against late false negatives. It is the wrong rule for the stated survival/CIF analysis: a default already recorded before extraction is an observed event and must not be discarded. Independent administrative censoring is already handled by a properly defined risk set; IPCW is not automatically the primary likelihood fix, and stacking it with IPSW changes the estimand again. The Manski endpoints are bounds for a counterfactual portfolio rate, not for factual incidence, and with roughly half the book unlabeled they would be too wide to use. That is not stated.

**Prefixes and leakage.** Fixed-\(k\) and elapsed-time cuts are the right pair, and renaming plus same-day `documents_total` are recognized. \(k=3\) is still equated with first Credit Review. Padding short cases with `TERMINAL_REJECTED` / `TERMINAL_WITHDRAWN` puts the competing outcome into \(X\). A prefix that already contains Accept, Reject, Withdraw, or Transfer is not an early-warning state. Service versus waiting time is not identified from one timestamp per event and no lifecycle id. The code then violates the prose: `get_prefixes_from_log(..., length=3)` drops short cases, there is no canonicalization or dedup before that cut, `documents_total` and resource tenure never enter the audit, and activation time, terminal time, and censoring time are not stored. The lookahead assert compares each prefix event with the max timestamp of that same prefix, so it cannot fail. The printed “N=48,000” cohort is an inner join to length-3 prefixes.

**Validation and use.** A start-time split around 1 July 2025 and 15 October 2025 is the right skeleton, and a random split would leak policy, migration, and label delay. The maturity claim is still wrong. Maturity is determined by activation time plus horizon and feed lag, not by intake date. A 30 September 2025 start can activate after 13 October 2025 or still be open on 15 February 2026; the July–September block is not “fully matured.” There is no as-of backtest that freezes labels at what would have been known at each decision date. Train-only DFG discovery is stated and then unused. Tuning and calibration share one split. Section 8 is the clearest part: factual association, selection, and the three deployment feedback loops are real. It still interprets a high score as default risk given survival through underwriting, which is the conditional estimand, not \(P(\text{activate and default})\). Under the joint incidence target the riskiest applicants can score low because they are rarely activated.

The design sees the selection problem and then does not hold one estimand, one clock, one maturity rule, and one leakage firewall fixed through the skeleton.
