7.2

The release-time classifications for TX-301 through TX-311 match the rules. The temporal model is careful, and the control-flow critique is concrete. The score stays well below excellent because the required checker does not run, some of its rule flags contradict the formalization, and one counterfactual contradicts R4 and R6.

Temporal semantics are handled correctly. Blanks stay unchanged, state is a forward fold, `event_index` breaks timestamp ties, and event time, effective time, record time, and evidence time are kept apart. Final settlement and later inserts are kept out of the release snapshot. The TX-311 consequence is right: the review sees US / 115,000, and release sees DE / 115,000, so R1 is off at release. The >20% test is the right strict inequality (`new × 5 > old × 6`), decreases do not invalidate a review, and equality at 100,000 still triggers R1.

Primary case calls are sound. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are noncompliant. TX-302, TX-306, TX-308, and TX-311 are compliant. TX-305 is indeterminate because the release amount is unknown. TX-310 is a defensible noncompliant call: the signing certificate exists only at 10:30, after release, and the only oral witness is the requester. EX-77 is correctly treated as surplus once the enhanced review already satisfies R1. Ratios given for the amount paths are accurate.

The formalization then slips. `Invalidated(r, ρ)` is true only when `r` is the latest review of either kind, so it is the wrong predicate for an enhanced review that is not that latest review. The R4 table row states a freestanding duty to obtain a fresh review, while the prose correctly reduces R4 to a defeater of amount-dependent reviews. For a display-only exemption, the outcome rules list both INDETERMINATE and VIOLATION without a priority. Section 4 then says that a known release amount of at least 100,000, with the review amount still unknown, would make TX-305 noncompliant. R4 calls an increase from an unknown amount unclassifiable, and the pseudo-logic maps that to INDETERMINATE. The same paragraph says either the 95,000 or the 118,000 baseline invalidates the enhanced review that TX-309 relies on. The 95,000 figure is before that review; the review’s baseline is 118,000, and only 118,000 → 145,000 invalidates it. EX-77’s certificate-creation time is stated as 09:05. The evidence given is a qualified signature at 09:05, not a certificate-creation time.

The checker encodes the right idea for this log and then fails as an executable artifact. `itertuples` cannot expose `concept:name` or `time:timestamp` (colon names are renamed), so the first `getattr` raises. Even after that access is repaired, TX-306 still dies: the initial risk is HIGH and the old value is `pd.NA`, and `if ... and pd.NA` raises. `main` therefore never builds the diagnostics frame. Separately, R3 sets `NOT_TRIGGERED` whenever the latest screen is after the list update, which makes the later “screen before release” branch unreachable and would also clear a screen that occurs only after release. Inside a live R2 trigger, an unknown risk or customer type is labeled INDETERMINATE even when the known HIGH or PEP limb is already unmet; the written rule says a known HIGH or PEP limb is enough. `witness_is_requester` and `late_record_explained` are never read. The sample labels would be right only after those defects are removed. As written, the block does not produce them.
