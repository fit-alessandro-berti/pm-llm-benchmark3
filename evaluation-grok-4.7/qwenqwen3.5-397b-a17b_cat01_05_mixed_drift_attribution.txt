5.8

The required ten-part structure is present, and the main sequence is right: March is chiefly semantic, May rework tracks Supplier Z, later delay is mostly queueing, October is seasonal mix, and December is confounded. That is not enough to carry the answer. Quantitative execution and several explicit requirements fail.

The Manhattan distances contradict the stated measure. Absolute gaps versus January are 3, 40, and 64 for March, May, and October, not 2, 14, and 24. The code would compute 64 for October, so the prose and implementation disagree. October is mislabeled as the peak: November is worse on cycle time, complexity, rework, and volume, while raw-fitness, missing-resource, and rework lows or highs occur earlier. The cycle-time split is forced to 16.3 hours. A 1.5× complexity assumption does not produce about 4 hours, the 0.6-hour Assess service change is inflated to 1 hour of case cycle time, and queueing is too small given that cycle time rose by about 11 hours from May to October while rework stayed near 24–26%. Only one change point is given for logging, behavior, and performance, despite an explicit request for two of each. “Fitness recovered in March” is also too loose: mapped fitness only returns to 0.90 and then erodes.

The mechanism table has factual and logical errors. Stable service time supports staffing-driven queueing; it is listed as evidence against it. December cycle time did not fall “despite complexity,” because high-complexity share fell from 45% to 34%. September rework moved with Supplier Z, so “rework persisted after the supplier declined” is overstated. “Perfectly correlated” and “simultaneously” are too strong: rework peaks in July, supplier share in June, staffing changes on June 15, and the priority rule on July 1. The larger cycle step is June (+4.3 hours), not July.

Monitoring is broad but poorly specified. The primary population excludes Supplier Z and high-complexity cases, hiding the two largest operational drifts. Rolling three-month windows absorb level shifts; year-over-year or STL logic is not usable on this single year. “Baseline + 2” is undefined, no outcomes monitor is included, and seasonal versus mixed onset types are not explicitly separated. The PM4Py plan does not embed both required tables, does not validate them, uses invalid `format_dataframe` and `filter_time_range` arguments, and would not find `activity` after formatting.
