The answer is strong, case-specific, and unusually careful about completion-only logging, obligation triggers, and the difference between a lawful next action and the next feed-visible event. It still has enough local inaccuracies and overconfident orderings that it is not nearly flawless.

C6-A correctly keeps S3 as the visible state, treats high severity plus injury as requiring technical and regulatory work, and refuses to count one two-device complaint as two complaints. The 0.55–0.75 / 0.10–0.25 / 0.05–0.20 ranges are a coherent unbounded next-event mixture. The observation caveat is too strong: the stated 8–15% delay applies to cases created in the last 48 hours, while the migration flag marks extra exposure. “Normal” does not make that base rate inapplicable. “Strongly suspected” supplier analysis is also a little ahead of the evidence, although the later caveat repairs most of it.

C6-B properly separates a duplicate-like low-severity case from C6-A and does not copy triage-to-fast-close. Marking closure and a new request as not enabled until review completes is consistent. Two gaps remain. Requesting evidence instead of fast-closing at triage is itself evidence against an already-settled duplicate, but that signal is unused. A hidden review could also surface Disposition Recorded before Close — No Safety Issue; that competitor is absent.

C6-C is right to let the country rule override the more frequent technical-to-disposition edge, and right that different lots do not by themselves open supplier analysis. Calling another Customer Evidence Requested currently process-enabled is loose. The enabling rule attaches a new cycle to evidence review, and that review already completed into technical investigation. Unconfirmed root cause makes the loop possible, not presently authorized. The 0.60–0.80 interval also leaves little room for that fork.

C6-D is the best case. The worklist open at 12:40 is not treated as assessment completion, the attachment is not treated as a proven request/receipt/review cycle, and 8–15% is not misused as a posterior that regulatory work finished. The alternative state nevertheless bundles hidden regulatory completion with a separate missing evidence cycle. Rank 1 at 0.45–0.65 does not overlap the backfill interval even though service age is below the stated 2-hour floor and a source-system attachment is positive evidence of delayed events. That ordering is sharper than the evidence.

C6-E correctly makes supplier analysis mandatory for a six-complaint lot, blocks fast close, and discounts another evidence request at 40 minutes. Primary S3 plus an open supplier duty is a fair reading of a coarse abstraction. The material miss is reportability: a lot now linked to six complaints is a serious possible reportability signal, yet Technical Investigation Completed and Regulatory Assessment Completed never enter the top three, even at low probability. Review at 0.60–0.80 also does not overlap supplier completion, despite supplier work possibly having started after yesterday’s review and a 6–36 hour service range.

The cross-case discussion is the strongest part. It explains why identical prefixes do not justify identical forecasts, warns against normalizing an incomplete and confounded DFG, and separates batch timing from the 5.5-hour notification median. The claim that overlap expresses uncertain ordering is overstated: first- and second-ranked intervals do not overlap in any case. `process_enabled: false` is defined and applied consistently, including for backfill, but that extension is doing a lot of work that the boolean alone does not reveal.
