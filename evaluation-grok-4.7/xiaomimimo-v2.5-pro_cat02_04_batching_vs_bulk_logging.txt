The cluster rankings are substantively right: G1 simultaneous fixture batch, G2 logged common start, G3 folder-level completion, G4/G8 serial chaining, G5 shared-account overlap, G6 bulk entry, and G7 quarter-hour censoring. The signature matrix, G6 waiting-versus-service mechanism, ambiguity cases, redesign priorities, and the split between diagnostic confidence and KPI usability are strong process-mining work.

The score is held down by repeated quantitative errors and a non-reliable implementation. Queue-to-start for G3 is about 18–22 minutes, not 22–50; arrival-to-completion is about 25–50, not 38–50. G8 case waits are 38–58 minutes, not 30–38, and later waits mix safety downtime with serial queueing behind earlier repairs. G2’s “827 min” is not a duration in the data, and 4×27 overstates the summed apparent services (8+14+21+27=70). G1’s recording lag is a few seconds, not sub-second, and minute-formatted start/complete fields are treated as exact to the second. G7’s ±7.5-minute window is asserted despite an undocumented up/down rule, and the four-way G5 overlap versus a three-analyst pool is underweighted.

The Python block keeps suspect clusters and computes useful pandas diagnostics, but `format_dataframe` can reorder rows before `start_timestamp` is reattached, lifecycle rows are mixed with an interval log, zero-duration ties fail the overlap test, and `pm4py.discover_batches` is called with an unstable API and a comparison that only works if a `batch_type` column appears. The required parameter sweep is therefore not reliably executed.
