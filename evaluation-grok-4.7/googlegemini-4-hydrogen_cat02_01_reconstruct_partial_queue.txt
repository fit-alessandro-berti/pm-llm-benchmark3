The reconstruction is conceptually strong: station sojourn is kept separate from service, open and waiting cases stay in WIP and busy-time, missing starts are bounded from resource and queue constraints, and steady-state Little’s Law is correctly rejected over the full window in favor of the finite-horizon identity \(\bar L_{S1}=5.79\), \(\bar L_{S2}=3.72\). Case bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15, the completed-sojourn mean 146.4, peak WIP (8 and 7), and the early-S1 / later-S2 bottleneck story are defensible. The code distinguishes MISSING, OPEN, and blank, and the scenario utilities in §6 match the log.

It is not nearly flawless. §4’s S1 started-wait range endpoint 73.3 is wrong: the 21 observed-or-imputed waits give about 65.2 (earliest), 65.7 (5-minute central), 68.1 (5-minute late), and 68.6 (0-minute service). 73.3 is exactly that upper sum plus P21’s 100 minutes a second time, and it disagrees with the code’s own `mean_wait_started`. Subperiod mean WIP is also off: S1 is 4.875 in 08:00–10:00, not ≈4.6, and 4.92 in 12:00–14:00, not ≈5.2. The “C2 (2/2)” 5-minute-gap claim is false — P13→P15 is 10 minutes — and “C1 (5/6)” does not match the observed transitions, so the central-imputation justification is overstated. §4’s utilization floors (74.3%, 71.5%, and several subperiod floors) are the degenerate 0-minute scenario, not the operational 5-minute scenario, without saying so in the cells. The ±1–2 pp rounding claim is too tight for independent ±2.5-minute timestamps, and the quasi-stationary sojourn “mean ≈115–125” is a softer cohort than the departures in that window (closer to 105–112).
