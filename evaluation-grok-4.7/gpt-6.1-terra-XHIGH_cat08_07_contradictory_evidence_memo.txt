The memo is a strong, decision-ready synthesis: it selects hybrid option E, refuses both national scale-up and blanket rollback, and keeps facts, mechanisms, and open assumptions apart. Fitness versus precision, median versus tail, handovers versus resilience, visible versus downstream cost, and segment splits are all explained in the right causal direction. Process-mining evidence is used carefully, including move-on-log versus move-on-model, DFG shares, and the handover versus working-together networks. The A–E comparison and the M1–M8 ranking are actionable, and the rollout rule is explicit.

The score is held down by several concrete gaps. The 6-week intake window is never set against a 38.5-day p90 and a 62-day p95, so the central censoring and follow-up problem for the tail result stays generic. The pilot full-cost figure of €207 drops complaint handling after week 4, which likely biases that estimate downward; the memo calls it incomplete without stating that direction or tying it to the proposed 5% cost guardrail. Coordinator utilization of 93% in two regions is paired with the 92% second-visit-team figure as if both were the same regional statistic. Business and rural cases, together about 19% of the pilot, are folded into the survey and permit remedy even though their notes point to manual activation, billing disputes, and rescheduling. “Skip rule often wrong” is placed in the fact column although it is an analyst’s note. Post-billing activation was an intentional pilot rule, and the immediate reversal is only partly separated from that design choice.
