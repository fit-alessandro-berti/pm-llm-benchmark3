8.7

The design is rigorous, constraint-aware, and unusually strong on non-stationary demand, shared resources, latent objections, paired comparisons, censoring, and the refusal to treat an uncalibrated run as an optimum. The score is held down by a set of concrete inconsistencies and underspecifications.

The planner load screen is numerically right and correctly interpreted: about 400 cases/week times 5.5 median planner hours is about 2,200 hours, against 8×8×5 = 320 nominal hours, and multiplying medians is not a mean-load proof. The same screen is not carried through for inspectors, fire, or environment, where median effort times volume is similarly incompatible with the stated rosters (for example, four inspectors on Tuesday–Thursday field days). The text tells the reader to check every pool, then leaves the most decision-relevant implication—that several “service time” columns cannot be exclusive effort—hanging on one pool. Weekend timestamps are real (2026-01-03 and 2026-01-04 are Saturday and Sunday; 2026-01-17 is also Saturday), but section 2 calls them human completions while the skeleton correctly refuses to treat a point timestamp as a completion.

Europe/Berlin is stated as the municipality’s timezone. Euro amounts do not establish that zone. The 14-day notice clock, fire “FTE equivalent,” and scheduler headcount are properly marked provisional; the timezone is not.

`Routing.requirements` collapses latent truth and operational routing: missing sensitivity or area is replaced by a resolved boolean, while the narrative says missingness must not be treated as exemption. D8 later adds misclassification, so baseline routing and the observation model are not the same mechanism. String `"yes"` flags and the comment that marks are booleans also disagree. In-flight invalidation is described for joins and stale results, but cancellation, partial effort, and capacity release for work already under way are unspecified, which is enough to leak capacity or double-count reviews.

Section 4’s levels and section 8’s `Policy` object do not match. Uptake, missing-document reduction, abandonment, legal-effort reduction, triage error, objection-signal quality, and deferral limits have no fields. `audit_fraction` and `max_batch_hold_workdays` exist but the listed policies keep the defaults, so a literal implementation simulates full adoption, not the stated 50/80/100% design. D7’s “0 business days of deferral” cannot also spread a Monday burst across the week.

The decision rule mixes a Pareto screen, an aspirational 10%/15% improvement, a confidence interval that only has to exclude zero, and an 80% posterior probability of “net benefit” without a defined net-benefit function. Break-even costs are mentioned, so the probability statement is not yet a decision criterion. Calibration targets versus the 12/6 chronological split are not cleanly separated, so queue-fitting can still consume the holdout unless an extra rule is imposed.

PM4Py usage itself is sound: pandas ingestion, `format_dataframe` with the required keys, tree parsing, DFG and performance DFG, variant tuples, case-arrival average, and a real split between mining and a calendar-aware discrete-event engine. NaN service and wait columns on this excerpt are the correct reading. The arrival shock uses the lognormal mean correction, policy streams omit the policy name, and follow-up arrivals continue after the cohort window. Those parts are coherent. The skeleton is still not a faithful encoding of the experiment matrix it accompanies.

No major constraint is missed: cross-case dependence, calendars, objections as latent, wait versus effort, and non-claims of optimality are all handled seriously. The remaining gaps are local and fixable, but they are specific enough that the answer is not nearly flawless.
