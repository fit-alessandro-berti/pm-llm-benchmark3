The write-up follows the required section structure and reaches a few defensible classifications (T-401 S1 possible, T-402 S1 certain breach, T-408 S1 pass and S2 breach, S2 pass on several short containment gaps). Those hits sit on incorrect intervals. Multiple final classifications are reversed, and the required interval arithmetic is not actually implemented.

Clock composition is systematically incomplete. Resolution is supposed to be expanded first, then `true = recorded − offset`. HUB events keep only ±0.5 s and drop the [−1 s, +1 s] offset. OPS-BER floors are collapsed toward the displayed minute: 08:14:00 − 120 s is given as 08:13:00, and the upper end stops at 08:15:30 instead of about 08:16:30. SENSOR’s ±5 s rounding is labeled as if it already included the separate ±5 s offset. VENDOR-NY’s [−180 s, +180 s] never enters T-403, so Alert_Received stays inside 08:00–08:00:59.999 UTC. The feasible S1 gap is about 12–19 minutes, which is a possible 15-minute breach, not a certain one. The same truncation makes T-402 S2 look like a certain pass at [42 m, 43.5 m]; the endpoints already in that answer produce a maximum near 46 minutes.

T-405 is wrong at the timezone root. On 2025-10-26, Europe/Berlin repeats 02:00–02:59 as CEST (UTC+2 → about 00:xx UTC) and then CET (UTC+1 → about 01:xx UTC). The answer sends the later fold to 03:xx UTC and reports a 2 h 15 m–2 h 25 m start delay with certain S1 and profile breaches. Ingestion order leaves one feasible combination: alert on the early fold, investigation and containment on the late fold. S1 is then about 12–18 minutes (possible breach). Alert-to-containment is under an hour and inside the zeta=1 profile band (certain pass).

T-408 Close_Incident is 2026-06-03 06:30 against containment on 2026-06-01 16:05, about 38 h 25 m. That is a certain S3 breach of 36 h, and a certain profile breach at zeta 1 and 2. The answer reports 14 h 25 m and a certain pass, dropping one calendar day.

T-404 anchors “investigation duration” of 18–25 minutes on Containment_Complete and emits [10:30, 10:35], which is neither containment minus that duration nor Investigation_Complete (10:35) minus that duration. A close event at 2026-06-02 10:00 is introduced with no source row. T-407 treats delivery latency as receipt before send, giving received ∈ [13:40, 14:00] and a certain 17–37 minute S1 breach. Latency after Alert_Sent, cut by the optional receipt-before-start constraint, puts S1 in a range that still includes both sides of 15 minutes. S2 is about 38 minutes on two HUB stamps; the reported [38 m, 58 m] possible breach imports alert uncertainty into the wrong pair.

T-406 states occurrence = recorded − offset − entry delay with delays up to 300 s, then evaluates 300 + 300 s as six minutes and publishes [12:08, 12:17]. Nearest-minute rounding (±30 s) is omitted. Under the stated formula the occurrence window is wide enough that containment-minus-start can exceed 45 minutes, so the certain S2 pass does not follow.

Profile bands are mis-scaled in the rows that exist: alert→containment is treated as [45 m, 75 m] rather than 2700 ± 720 s, and investigation→containment as [35 m, 45 m] rather than 2100 ± 600 s. Zeta=3 is computed both as ±9 minutes and as ±5.4 minutes, and the wrong band is the one used to keep T-402/T-403 as certain profile violations. Most testable pairs have no row at all. The sensitivity matrix mixes SLA tolerance with zeta inside SLA-labeled rows and marks T-402 S1 a certain violation at +2 and +5 minutes; the corrected duration crosses both relaxed limits.

The Python block covers only a subset of events, does not convert zones or build offset intervals, hard-codes the wrong DST instants, never classifies durations, and calls `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` only at zeta 1 on a midpoint frame whose `format_dataframe` arguments do not match the library’s case/activity/timestamp fields. Section 6 misses several possible violations the answer itself named, gives T-407 a “17 m would pass” test that still fails a 15-minute SLA, and asks for new facts that would change history rather than evidence that would pin an existing interval. Governance names the right control areas, but it does not repair the timing conclusions above.
