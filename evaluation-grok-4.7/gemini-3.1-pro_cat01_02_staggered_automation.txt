5.8

The brief is complete and recognizes staggered adoption, endogenous targeting, intensity versus availability, fraud-desk interference, and small-cluster limits, but it is not quantitatively reliable and it overstates what is identified.

Several reported figures are wrong. Beta’s January–March median is 45.9 h, not 46.1 h. Gamma’s January–June escalation mean is 8.7%, not 8.2%. The July control drop is −3.1 h at Delta and −4.6 h at Epsilon, not a “3–4 h” drop. Alpha’s January–March medians (50.4, 46.9, 49.4) are not flat. No branch has a zero automated-fast-path dose; the floor is 4–6% in every untreated month, so the intensity design’s comparison group does not exist. “About 50% use” fits Beta in August and misses Gamma at 32%.

The April “DiD” is only a March–April 2×2. It lines up with Beta gaining an FTE and with the routing campaign, yet the write-up treats that co-timing as evidence for automation over regression to the mean. Those are the confounders, not a reason to prefer the treatment explanation. A full-window median contrast versus Delta and Epsilon is on the order of a 3–6 h relative decline, while the stated causal bound is −1.5 to −4 h and the claim ladder says 1–3 h. None of these intervals is derived from a shown adjustment. The “complexity-adjusted intensity analysis” is asserted without arithmetic.

The p90 conclusion has the wrong sign relative to the method used for the median. Over the same pre/post windows, control p90 rises by about 15–18 h and Alpha/Beta by about 10 h, so the crude relative contrast is a several-hour improvement. The assessment instead gives 0 to +5 h of harm, and the row’s own “supporting” evidence is that treated p90 “rises less.” Absolute post-period increases are the May shock plus later volume, not an isolated tail effect. Median and tail are not given the same comparison.

Identification is named more carefully than it is applied. Callaway–Sant’Anna is the right family to discuss, and the two-way fixed-effects warning is directionally fair, but covariate adjustment for workload and complexity is treated as fixing the design. April routing was assigned to the adopting branches in the adoption month, so never-treated branches do not net it out, and a linear workload term can be a bad control. The prose also assumes hidden benefits that raw changes “mask.” Estimands confuse non-identification with low power, bundle rework with escalation, and mark the tail “not identifiable” while Section 7 still publishes a signed range at confidence 60. Confidence 75 for a −1.5 to −4 h median effect, and 85 for escalation, is far too high for five clusters, a violated SUTVA, and a routing shock collinear with treatment. Section 1 also states causal claims (“driving up,” “triggered”) where description was required. Specialist review is repeatedly equated with the fraud desk.

The code does not implement the stated strategy. Relative time is built and then unused. Both regressions are pooled cross-sections with no branch or month effects, so the automation coefficient mainly compares chronically slow, treated branches with faster untreated ones. Clustering five groups and printing a warning does not repair that. Validation is a shape print. Identical repeated rework and escalation values, and the pre-availability fast-path floor, are never treated as measurement problems.

The rollout choice is the right class of decision, and a within-branch experiment is more informative than another targeted branch launch. The design then overclaims: a 50/50 case split inside one shared queue does not bypass SUTVA; it changes the interference. Delta is not a clean control if the fraud desk is congested. The scale-up rule treats a non-significant rework test as evidence of no harm and adds an arbitrary 5% median cut and p < 0.05 gate. The 120-hour guardrail sits above Epsilon’s observed p90, so it is not “approaching” that benchmark. “Eight-month baseline” does not supply the case-level pre-period the experiment needs.

The strongest justified statement in the material is descriptive and suggestive only: after April, Alpha and Beta’s medians worsened less than Delta and Epsilon’s during the complexity and volume surge, while fast-path use rose and escalation ticked up by about a point at adoption. A single percent cycle-time claim is not defensible, and this answer’s own conditionally causal hour range is not defensible either.
