The final answer is a strong, well-structured design: the decision tree, pair-level choices, event counts (17/15/14/10), and E2O counts (44/39/37/28) are consistent and conservative. e05 and e14 are kept, e03/e04 and e11/e16 are the only high-confidence merges, abstraction events stay separate, original timestamps are retained, and the `have_common_object` trap on M1/M2 is explained correctly. Provenance fields and the required failure modes are covered.

It is not nearly flawless. Order-flattened DFG frequencies are wrong: three Pick events after Validate produce one `Validate → Pick` directly-follows edge plus `Pick → Pick` self-loops, not a frequency of 3/2/1. Item-flattened DFG, OC-DFG, and ET–OT weights are missing or only implied. e08 before e09, and invoice before payment, are stated as required although they are inferences, not given facts. The PM4Py block uses the right function names and a sensible “refine labels before library drop” idea, but `duplicates_type`, `keep="first"`, and `ascending=True` are not a credible API, so the aggressive path would not run. `verified_pair_merge` also drops the non-canonical `payload_hash`, `source_seq`, and `ingested_at` even though the decision table says they are retained.
