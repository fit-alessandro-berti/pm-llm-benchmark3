7.6

The final JSON is case-specific, schema-complete, and process-aware. It correctly separates C6-A from C6-B, treats C6-C as an open regulatory obligation rather than a technical-to-disposition shortcut, uses the C6-D worklist and migration flag without treating missing events as proof, and makes supplier analysis mandatory for C6-E’s six-complaint lot. Queue times, the 16:00 batch, and completion-only logging are used in the right places. Intervals are broad rather than falsely precise, and `process_enabled: false` is applied correctly to C6-C’s authority notification and C6-D’s disposition.

The score is held down by several concrete judgment errors. Fast Close is marked process-enabled for C6-B after the case has already left S1 and completed an evidence cycle; the enabled terminal there is Close — No Safety Issue. C6-C ranks Authority Notification in the top three even though regulatory assessment and disposition are both still missing, the root cause is unconfirmed, and the 16:00 batch is only two hours away; that probability is too high for a non-enabled multi-step outcome. C6-E gives Supplier Analysis Completed about 0.08–0.25 as the next log event only 40 minutes after the second receipt, before the review that would open it and against a 6–36 h supplier cycle. Several other `process_enabled: true` flags rely on an unobserved predecessor (C6-A technical completion, C6-C disposition, C6-B closure). Midpoints for C6-B and C6-E also slightly exceed 1.
