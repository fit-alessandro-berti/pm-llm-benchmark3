The memo is structurally complete and causally careful: the crude Orion penalty reverses inside every complexity stratum, standardization is explicit, positivity failure is treated as a real identification barrier, queue time is separated from review service, completed-case selection is handled as conditioning, and the 50% reassignment is rejected in favor of measurement, a bounded routing pilot, and a capacity change. The Python diagnostics match the requested PM4Py design and the main cycle-time arithmetic.

The score is held down by repeated quantitative and graph errors. The claim that 82–96% of Orion’s within-stratum advantage sits in `wait_before_review_h` is wrong: the wait shares are about 75% (Low: 1.61/2.14), 85% (Medium: 1.88/2.21), and 96% (High: 3.28/3.43). That bound is used in the executive assessment, the decomposition, and hypothesis 1. Section 5 also says two of three Orion Medium compliance-not-required cases were rework; only C06 was (1/3). The other two waits are 9.5h and 11.1h, so the offered explanation of the 8.57h versus 9.93h contrast is false.

The DAG names certification as a supported `resource_group -> unobserved_reviewer_skill` arrow, but skill has no outgoing edge into service, rework, queue, or cycle time, so the mechanism is not actually in the graph. Skill could instead be a confounder of assignment. The executive also leads the backfire story with faster service and lower rework, which are the small components, after arguing that the stratified gap is mostly pre-review wait. Evidence against the “not identifiable” label and a credible success path for reassignment are thin, and the recommendation’s “unbiased” pilot claim ignores queue interference.
