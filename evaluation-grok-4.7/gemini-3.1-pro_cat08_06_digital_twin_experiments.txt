5.5

The memo covers every required section and is directionally competent (calendars, exogenous applicant delay, seasonal and Monday bursts, CRN, holdout split, no optimality claim). It is not a sound design: several methods contradict the process, the data gaps, or each other, and the skeleton would not implement the twin it describes.

Model scope misses the synchronization state that decision drafting needs after the parallel reviews, and it never models neighborhood objections propagating across related cases. Section 1 calls council-week stalls a cross-case dependency; section 3 then says inspection batching is the only interaction and that cases only couple through queues. Appeals, inspection no-shows, district travel, and the unknown council-week calendar are omitted. `has_objections` is treated as a case attribute even though those events were not recorded.

Parameter estimation uses DFG edge weights as routing probabilities. That is the wrong estimator inside the `+` block: interleavings are not XOR choices. Loop probabilities should come from per-case repeat counts or alignments, not from an assumed decaying backward edge. The simplified tree’s real limit is also missed: “Completeness rework” and “Inspection rework” are not the labels in the excerpt, which repeats the body activities. Two-percentile lognormal or gamma fits are stated without the inferred service/wait split’s bias. Prior violations, building area, and environmental sensitivity are not given an estimation treatment even though D2 and routing depend on them.

Assumptions include a direct constraint breach: public notice longer than 30 days is imputed as an observed objection. Duration is only a proxy. “Start timestamps can be robustly ignored” overclaims; the given service summaries are already partly inferred. Environmental capacity is left as either a uniform slowdown or blocked slots, which are different queueing models. Declaring dropped emergency cases negligible is unsupported. Assuming architect bursts stay structurally fixed also makes D7 unevaluable, because that intervention changes the arrival process.

The experiment table lists D1–D10 and three stresses, but levels are often a single invented number or “+X days,” and stresses are not crossed with policies. D5 drops Commercial cases. D8 turns triage into skipping review. D9 detects objections during planning, before the notice period that generates them. D2 drops the “no prior violations” condition because that field does not exist, without a proxy plan. “Drastically” reducing the inspector backlog is not supported by any value distribution.

Uncertainty treatment mixes incompatible ideas. Welch’s method looks for a steady-state mean; this demand process is seasonal and has no such mean, and discarding the first four months can delete a peak or a trough. Four months is also shorter than the stated 260-day tail, while the text simultaneously precommits to four months and defers the choice to Welch. One 18-month window per run yields a single peak season. “95% confidence intervals for medians and 90% credible intervals for p90 across 50 replications” confuses sampling distributions with posterior intervals; no prior or input-uncertainty bootstrap is defined. Pareto tails are endorsed without a shape restriction, so the mean cycle time may be infinite. CRN is only specified for arrivals and attributes, not for the service, rework, and objection streams that interventions change.

Validation misuses process-mining terms. Generating a synthetic log and comparing DFGs is not event-log replay; fitness and alignments of the real log on the tree are absent. A K-S test on 31,200 dependent, seasonal cycle times will reject trivial gaps and is not an i.i.d. test. Matching planner “high utilization” and environmental “~60%” to HR calendars validates a reservation, not observed busy time, which the missing start timestamps cannot show. The 12/6 split can hold out only one season.

Decision rules invent “statutory” 30- and 180-day SLAs, use a non-significant K-S test as proof of geographic fairness, and apply a flat 85% utilization cap without defining the clock (calendar versus Tue–Thu fire shifts). There is no rule for conflicts such as a better tail with a worse median or compliance risk. D2’s risk and D1’s applicant burden never become measured criteria.

The skeleton correctly avoids `pm4py.read_csv`, calls `format_dataframe` with the right keys, and uses plausible `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return shapes. It does not compute service or waiting columns, and the performance DFG collapses them with no caveat. The tree is not parsed; the excuse that PM4Py cannot handle `tau` and loops is wrong for this operator set. `FilterStore` is never seeded, so `get()` deadlocks, and `req.value` mixes Store and Resource APIs. Arrivals are stationary exponential while the comment claims seasonality; `avg_arrival_rate` is passed as an exponential mean. Completeness-check lognormal parameters do not match 25 minutes, `until=16000` is one replication with no warm-up discard, and estimated log statistics never enter the SimPy config.
