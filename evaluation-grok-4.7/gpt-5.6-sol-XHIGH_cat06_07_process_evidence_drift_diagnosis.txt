8.5

The diagnosis is substantively right and unusually careful: April is treated as a schema and feature-pipeline failure on mature labels, June as a routing-mix change with intact within-variant risk, August–September as overlapping parts-performance, within-V3 conditional risk, and (from September) population change, and October as operationally suspicious but not a valid supervised cohort. The arithmetic that matters checks out, opposite calibration errors are not collapsed into one recalibrator, eventual variant is kept out of score-time features, and label-delay alerts are gated off retraining. That is the hard part of the task.

The drift taxonomy is still looser than the evidence. Variant shares and Partner Z’s rural distance and device age are used as covariate-drift evidence even though those attributes are either post-diagnosis path outcomes or explicitly absent from the original feature set. That double-counts process drift and omitted-variable shift as \(P(X)\). “High that distributions changed” is only clearly earned for variant and source mix, not for the model’s score-time covariates; no feature-level distance is available, and the answer says so in the same row. The August–September “old-regime” reweight is directionally right and shows mix alone cannot produce the V3 jump, but setting it next to mean predicted risk overstates what that match shows: the mix-only counterfactual stays near the historical base rate, and June already showed the model did not follow a mix change (0.20 predicted versus 0.15 observed).

Smaller problems keep it short of nearly flawless. The censoring-only expectation for event-calendar versus case-start edges never states a real contrast (“change markedly” versus “less stable”). July “partial exposure” is stated as fact from a 10 July start date with no within-month split. May’s residual overprediction (0.21 versus 0.18, ECE 0.050), in the same direction as April, is noted only qualitatively. In the sketch, `drop_duplicates(..., keep="last")` runs before a time sort and can keep `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` when aliases share an event id; `len(case_ids)` assumes `pm4py.get_variants` returns case lists rather than counts; and the temporal-feature median does not fix or document the weekly grain of `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. None of those break the audit’s conclusions.
