The models are semantically faithful, internally consistent, and unusually precise about approximation. The POWL partial order matches the intended cross-branch dependencies, the process tree is a real duplicate-free under-approximation rather than a disguised exact tree, and the eight witnesses correctly separate low risk, high risk, supplier consultation, a two-iteration trial, the tree barrier, and a genuine cross-branch violation.

The language claim is also right. Under the stated completion-order abstraction, the tree is exactly the intended language cut by the early barrier (both containment and analysis before planning, design, the selected reviews, and a selected consultation), while the POWL language is the intended one. The reported bounded counts, 20,952 intended traces, 12,552 tree traces, and 8,400 excluded traces for one to three iterations and all four route/supplier choices, match an independent enumeration of that claim. T3 and T5 are the right strictness witnesses, and F3 correctly treats `J` as a valid failed-iteration continuation and locates the first violation at release.

Deductions are for residual, not structural, defects. The boxed equality is asserted more strongly than the prose proves: shuffle equivalence of the nested tree and POWL is argued, not formally established. `validate_partial_orders`, `.children`, and the soundness return-type handling were not executed, so the otherwise careful construction still has an untested API tail. “Existential outcome abstraction” is less clear than the projection rule that follows it. The early barrier is disclosed as non-optimal, but it is not compared with other safe duplicate-free cuts that trade a different concurrency. The headline enumeration is reproducible in result, yet the checker itself is only cited through inaccessible sandbox paths.
