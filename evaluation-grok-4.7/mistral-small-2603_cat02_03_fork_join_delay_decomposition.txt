The write-up follows the required section order and correctly names the fork-join pieces, but the definitions, case table, aggregates, counterfactuals, and code disagree with each other and with the log. Those are core failures, not presentation nits.

**Definitions.** Service, upstream, `join_ready = max(ends)`, downstream `final_start − join_ready`, final service, and total flow are stated correctly. Branch queue is reversed: `max(0, fork_release − start)` is 0 on every row, because every branch starts at or after `fork_release`. The operative quantity is `start − fork_release`. The double-count claim is also wrong. `sync_A + sync_B + sync_C = 3·join_ready − (end_A + end_B + end_C)`, so the gap versus `fork_join` is `2·join_ready − Σ ends + fork_release`, not `2·join_ready`. Summing sync waits alone is a sum of slacks; for F01 that sum is 27 minutes against a 50-minute fork-join stage. Triple counting appears only if each branch’s `queue + service + sync` is added, since each of those paths equals the same fork-join interval.

**Case table versus the log.** Upstream, all six queue/service columns, and final service match the timestamps. `join_ready`, `downstream_queue`, `critical_branch`, and several flow times do not. Correct fork-join durations are 50, 60, 65, 57, 60, 65, 75, 55, 65, 55, 50, 75. Correct downstream waits are 8, 15, 15, 18, 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 20, 20, 10 (only F01 matches the table). Flow time is wrong for F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, F11, and F12; the correct series sums to 1383 minutes, mean 115.25. Critical branches are A on F01 and F11, C on F04 and F08, and B on the other eight. The worked F04 line computes `max(09:45, 09:35, 09:52) = 09:52` and then labels that maximum A. Worked downstream waits for F02, F04, F07, and F11 are 15, 18, 15, and 20, while the table shows 65, 45, 95, and 82. F07 and F11 flow times in the worked lines are 20 and 40 minutes high.

**Aggregates.** B service is 37.5, not 36.3; C service is 20.17, not 19.8; B queue is 19.17, not 19.6; C queue is 10.42, not 11.3. Mean sync under the section 1 definition is about 11.0 (A), 4.3 (B), and 30.4 (C). Reporting C’s mean sync as 0 inverts the definition: the early branch waits, and the last branch does not. “C is never last” is false. A and C are tied at 2/12 critical cases; that tie is not reported. No tail contribution to `join_ready` is given. B still ranks first on critical frequency (8/12) and on queue plus service (~56.7 versus A’s 50), so the influence order can survive, but the cited statistics do not support it.

**Counterfactuals.** A 20% A-service cut is 10 minutes, yet slack caps it: F01 moves only until B at 08:55 (5 minutes), F11 moves the full 10 until a tie with B, and F04/F08 stay on C, so join-level savings are 15 minutes, not 40. Booking a uniform 9.8 minutes on eight B-critical cases ignores per-case queues and second-place caps; case-level join savings of a true 50% B-queue cut sum to 92.5 minutes, with F05 and F10 truncated. Downstream masking is visible in the F1/F2 chains (F05, F08, F09, and F11 start exactly when the same resource frees) and is not measured. CF3’s 47.5 minutes saved on every case (570 total) is impossible: all downstream delay in the log totals 176 minutes, and 570 also fails to equal half of the table’s own downstream column. “Secondary effects: none” for the A cut drops the slack shift onto B and any final-station absorption.

**Redesigns.** F1 and F2 already exist and each does 120 minutes of work, roughly two-thirds busy over its observed span, not ~90% falling to ~45%. Adding “a second” final resource misreads `final_resource`; a third server would add about half, not double, capacity. The 11% cycle-time cut, the B sojourn percentile path “75 to 65,” and the flow-time percentile “155 to 120” are not implied by the corrected distributions (flow times run from 93 to 130). Pooling B does change the stress-test resource pool.

**Code.** The script encodes the reversed queue, so every queue prints as 0. CF1 never moves `A_end`. CF2 sets `B_start = fork − queue`, which places the start before release. All three savings lines subtract the original `final_end − arrival` from itself, so every saving is 0. `format_dataframe(..., timestamp_key='start_timestamp')` treats the start as the event time and does not preserve an interval completion. The aggregate-only print skips the required per-case savings. The fork-join comment correctly says directly-follows performance does not recover synchronization wait.

**Limits.** Causality, unknown branch capacity, preemption, release policy versus queue, and the small sample are the right caveats. The F1/F2 roster already separates some waits that match the previous same-resource completion from waits that occur while that resource is idle; those are not equally unidentified.
