6.8

The analysis correctly separates the March conformance break from later operational drift, rejects a single global change point, and keeps supplier rework, staffing queueing, priority redistribution, and renewal season as interacting mechanisms rather than one cause. Sections 4–7 and 9–10 are the strongest part: discrimination artifacts, conditioning bias, censoring, non-universal thresholds, and the seven decision rules are usable. That does not offset the quantitative and reproducibility failures below.

The normalized distance sums and mean absolute gaps are right (March 3 and 0.43, May 40 and 5.71, July 59 and 8.43, September 28 and 4.00, October 64 and 9.14, December 36 and 5.14). The companion “total variation” figures are not. With January’s rate sum equal to 403, \(\sum|r_m-r_{Jan}|/(2\cdot403)\) is 0.0037, 0.0496, 0.0732, 0.0347, 0.0794, and 0.0447. The reported 0.0480, 0.0699, 0.0339, 0.0742, and 0.0428 do not match that formula. Calling the statistic total variation is also inaccurate: the seven rates are not normalized into distributions, and the two months do not share a common sum.

The “largest departure from January, over all twelve months” column is wrong twice. Supplier Z peaks in June at 46% (+32 pp, about +229%), not July at 44% (+30 pp, +214%). Raw fitness is lowest in June at 0.48 (0.44 below 0.92, about 48%), not March at 0.55 (0.37, 40%). Fast-variant and fitness departures are also unsigned while increases are signed, so the same column mixes magnitude with direction.

The May 5–June 14 row treats full-month June results (rework 27%, median 40.8 h, p90 95 h) as pre-staffing evidence. June 15–30 is inside those aggregates. It also states that normalized request-info edges rose from 24 to 27 per 100 cases. No June fragment is supplied; 27 is June’s rework-variant share. May’s edge rate is 24 and July’s is 29.

August median cycle time is 45.0 − 31.5 = 13.5 hours, which is the stated 42.9%. The decomposition instead uses 13.7 hours and a 13.1-hour non-service residual (13.7 − 0.6). The 0.6-hour and 12.5% service figures themselves match 5.4 versus 4.8. The additive split is appropriately caveated, but the residual is built on the wrong total.

Part A is not executable. `validate_aggregate_data` indexes `rates["RELATIONS_PLACEHOLDER"]` and raises `KeyError` on the path that is actually called. For columns already scaled 0–100, `_delta_pp = 100 * delta` turns a 24-point complexity change into 2400. Cases are excluded from the relative-change loop even though the prose table reports them. The code’s distance function would print the corrected half-L1 values, so the narrative table and the program disagree.

Part B does not match the PM4Py manual. `format_dataframe` takes `activity_key` and `timestamp_key` and returns a dataframe, not an `EventLog`; the call uses `activity`, `timestamp`, and `concept`. `filter_time_range` takes `dt1` and `dt2`, defaults to event mode, and is closed on both ends; the call uses `start`/`end` and documents a half-open interval. `discover_dfg` returns `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`, so `dfg, _ = ...` fails. `discover_temporal_profile` has no `variant` or `format="str"` argument. `get_variants` returns a map of variant to count, while `Counter(tuple(v) for v in variants)` counts each key once. Monthly event filtering also splits cases at month boundaries before DFG and variant discovery.

Two monitors would stay on after the known migration. Duplicate `Open Case` is baselined at the January–February rate of zero with a 0.5 pp band, and the conformance rule alerts when the raw-to-mapped gap exceeds 5 points, against a post-March gap that stays about 32–40 points. Those rules do not separate an established provenance regime from new drift. The decision-edge decline (83 to 72 per 100 cases by July) is folded into control-flow distance without treating right-censored, in-progress work as a mechanical explanation.

Fitness recovery (32–40 points, mean 35.4), the March versus post-May contrast, \(D_{avg}\) ordering, and the service-versus-wait direction are sound. The grade is held down by incorrect extremal months, a fabricated June edge rate, a contaminated pre-staffing window, inconsistent cycle-time arithmetic, a non-running Part A, and PM4Py calls that fail the stated manual.
