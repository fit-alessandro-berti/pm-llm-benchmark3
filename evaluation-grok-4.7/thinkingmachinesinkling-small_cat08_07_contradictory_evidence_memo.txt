The recommendation is the right one: do not scale Redesign R nationally, do not roll it back wholesale, and adopt a segment-conditional hybrid that keeps coordinator ownership and merged scheduling for simple low-risk houses, suspends survey-skipping and post-billing activation, and gates any wider rollout on further measurement. All six required sections are present. Alternatives A–E are compared with advantages, risks, and conditions. The five metric conflicts are addressed. M1–M8 are ranked by how they would change the decision, and the process-mining artifacts (fitness, precision, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal profile, and the handover versus working-together networks) are actually used.

Those strengths are undercut by material accuracy and compliance problems. The executive memo runs past the 700-word limit, roughly 780 words, because it restates the full analysis instead of deciding. In both the memo and the evidence table, the negative signs on the decisive segment deltas are missing, so stated “facts” such as median 6.0 days, complaints 1pp, and first-time-right 8pp / 11pp have no direction even though the prose calls one segment a beneficiary and the others failures. The rollout thresholds are similarly broken: “median 10 days,” “first-time-right 83%,” “cost EUR 180,” and “p90 ( baseline)” do not reliably encode the inequalities the decision depends on.

The fitness/precision explanation contradicts itself. Billing start before activation is correctly described as a frequent observed move-on-log, then later listed among “unobserved” combinations that supposedly cause low precision. Low precision means the model allows extra behavior, not that those observed deviations never occurred. The memo also states as fact that duration is measured from completion timestamps only; sparse activity start times do not establish that. Several evidence rows blend interpretation with assumption, and the recommendation leans hard on segment splits while treating attributes that are missing before week 3 as solid facts. Finally, “freed” survey capacity at 52% utilization does not survive the same recommendation’s restoration of surveys for medium- and high-risk addresses.

The decision is defensible. The quantitative record and the process-mining distinction the task specifically requires are not clean enough for a high score.
