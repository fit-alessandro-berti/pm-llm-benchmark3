5.5

The design covers every required section and makes several sound conservative calls: keep e05 and e14, treat e03/e04 and e11/e16 as supported same-event merges, keep summary events, split the harmonized dispatch collision, put acceptance before handover, and put authorization before capture. The duplicate classes, provenance fields, and eight failure modes are all present, and the warning about synthetic microseconds is right.

Those strengths do not survive the ordering, counting, and implementation claims. Concurrent picks are written as e03 before e05, and concurrent captures as e13 before e14 “by payment ID,” without admissible alternatives. Neither rule is semantic: `source_seq` is not comparable across systems, and payment identity is not time. Invoice-before-authorization is likewise asserted without a known constraint. That is the false total order the design itself warns against.

The conservative count of 15 is arithmetically right, but the evidence-based count of 13 is not. Dropping e07, e15, and e09 from 17 leaves 14; also merging e04 and e16 leaves 12. Aggressive damage is internally inconsistent: a key that includes the full object set would not collapse e03/e05 or e13/e14, while e10 cannot join e08/e09 because its activity differs. The shared-object explanation for M1 versus M2 is correct, then contradicted by that collapse story.

The code does not implement the stated policy. String-concatenated `source_seq` sorts across sources and can place the ERP pack summary ahead of the second pick. The verified-pair helper appends secondary relations and then deletes them. `original_timestamp` and `source_event_key` are not preserved on every event before the index timedelta. Candidate groups are computed and unused. `ocel_drop_duplicates` is applied only on the aggressive copy, where it is a no-op because no exact technical duplicate exists. Section 2 refines summary labels; section 4 keeps those same events unchanged.

Sensitivity figures are not earned. Raw event-to-object relations total 44, and a true union merge yields 39, not about 43. A single order-flattened trace cannot contain parallel branches; e15 has no payment object, so it cannot appear in a payment-flattened graph; item parallelism is object-centric, not an item-DFG edge. One case also cannot produce “high” directly-follows frequency.

The audit schema and safeguards are usable, but they describe a more careful pipeline than the one the order and code would actually build.
