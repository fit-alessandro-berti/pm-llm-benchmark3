5.5

The central descriptive finding is right: Orion’s crude cycle-time disadvantage reverses inside every complexity stratum, so the proposed 50% reassignment is not justified by the aggregate means. The required sections, hypothesis types, pilot-plus-capacity plan, and residual-uncertainty list are present. The answer is still substantially unreliable.

Section 1 is under the 180-word minimum and overclaims. Night shift and compliance do not inflate review service; within complexity, service times are nearly identical across groups, while compliance rates are also balanced. “Lower compliance throughput” is not in the evidence. Reassignment is treated as moving night work onto Nova’s day capacity, which the proposal does not say.

The DAG does not make `case_completed` a collider, yet the variable table calls it one. `complexity` is pre-routing, so the post-treatment collider warning is process-inconsistent. `shift -> arrival_load` is an unsupported direction. Supported and assumed arrows are not actually separated, and the prose cites a risk-to-completion arrow the graph does not contain.

`arrival_load` is measured on every case; the table says it is not. Day cases are not “~8–10 h,” and night cases are not “~15–34 h”; both ranges omit large parts of the sample. Wait is roughly 43–66% of cycle time, not 60–75%, and `wait + service` is systematically below `cycle_time`. Those comparisons are not auditable. Calling “shift drives queueing” and “group does not drive service” allowed causal readings treats unbalanced stratification as identification. Crude rework is used to support a harmful Orion effect even though within-stratum rework is not higher for Orion.

The shift/load test’s predictions do not discriminate: a group effect would also change Nova’s night waits. The compliance “front-door” test is misnamed and misspecified, because compliance is not a mediator of group. Matching on complexity, shift, load, and compliance ignores near-empty overlap. The day-shift, medium-only pilot cannot identify the high-complexity night reassignment being considered, and the closing recommendation is under 120 words while calling compliance the true bottleneck.

The Python is not reproducible: `pd.StringIO` is invalid, `discover_dfg` does not return a sortable frequency table, timestamps are avoided but the chosen API is still wrong, and trace variants are never compared. Standardization and n<3 overlap warnings are the only solid pieces. The five uncertainties are relevant, but several name multiple fields rather than one.
