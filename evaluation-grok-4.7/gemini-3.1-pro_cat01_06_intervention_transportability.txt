5.8

The recommendation is the right one — conditional go, limited to cases that do not legally need originals, with Site A’s before/after change treated as confounded — and the required sections, columns, selection nodes, and assumption format are present. The score is held down by structural mistakes, unused quantities, an internally inconsistent pilot, and a Site B model that does not implement the process it recommends.

Process structure is the largest analytical miss. Site B already allows `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` to start after `Register`; directly-follows counts 2,050 and 1,860 sum above 3,400, which is evidence of that concurrency. The comparison table marks Site B concurrency as absent and Site A prerequisites as absent. Site A before was a total order, so identity was a prerequisite of document checking. The intervention that can transfer is narrower than “turn concurrency on”: for the 62% originals cases the binding path is already originals delay (median 18 h) plus document service (8.1 h), while identity service is only 3.4 h and can run during the wait. Creating document tasks at registration for those cases adds WIP without shortening the critical path. The 38% figure is also treated as if it were Site A’s “digital at registration” channel. The evidence only says originals are not legally required.

Causal reading of Site A overweights the API upgrade. Identity service fell 0.4 h and document service 0.7 h, while wait before assessment fell 6 h and median cycle time 9 h. Overlap moved from 8% to 71%. The API change cannot account for most of the wait reduction; the serious confounders are +2 verification FTE, the mix shift from 48% to 40% high-complexity, huddles, and possible timestamp cleanup. The evidence table then contradicts itself: completed-case growth is attributed to FTE, mix, huddles, and API, while the transferable component is “throughput gains strictly from concurrency.” Throughput rose about 8% against an 11% FTE increase, so concurrency is not identified as a throughput effect. Several required signals are missing as rows: p90, document service time, appeal rate, high-complexity share, FTE, huddles, the absent negative control, and the timestamp artifact. Rework transport confidence of 70 is too high for a confounded +0.6 point change on a different loop structure.

The transport graph names the required nodes, but capacities and role specialization do not affect `true_overlap`, measured overlap is not separated from true overlap, and the shared-mechanism paragraph never states the actual common mechanism: when both checks are feasible and staffed, verification completion moves from a sum toward a maximum. Scenario ranges are not derived from that identity. A −3 to −5 h overall median from treating 38% of cases ignores the mixture problem: if the slower originals majority contains the overall median, the digital-only effect can be real inside the stratum and still leave the headline median nearly unchanged. The legal-congestion row does supply the required pattern of a better median and a worse tail, but +15 to +30 h at p90, and confidence 85 on the statutory-violation range, are unanchored. The DFG branch rates (legal review about 1,360/3,400, supplement about 360 of those) are never used.

The pilot’s intent is sound and its sample-size specification is not. About 3,400 cases in six months and a 38% stratum produce roughly 200 digital cases in four weeks, about 100 per arm, against a stated 400 per arm. Case-level randomization also does not make downstream capacity affect both arms equally when they share assessors and lawyers; the write-up asserts that while the interference row only partially counters it. Secondary outcomes are not specified, and “compare to Site A’s digital subset” refers to an effect Site A did not report. Four weeks plus a 110 h tail also leaves censoring unaddressed.

The code builds the two Site A trees, converts them, puts a partial order in front of assessment, and calls the requested checks. The Site B net is not the section 6 policy. It starts identity and document together for every case, omits `Originals Receipt`, and implements the supplement loop as Legal Review redone after Request Supplement, without repeating document check and assessment. That repeat is encodable as a loop body; it is not something POWL is unable to express. Alternative variants are likewise encodable as an XOR of two partial orders, so the comment’s claim that originals cannot be represented is too broad. `Operator.SEQUENCE` is not a POWL operator in the standard object model (sequence belongs on the partial order), and the same approve/reject XOR instance is reused under two parents, which breaks the tree shape converters expect.

The assumption list is usable, especially on digital classification, the case lock, and timestamps. Assumption 5 is already answerable from the service-time versus wait-time gap and should have been resolved in the effect decomposition rather than left open.
