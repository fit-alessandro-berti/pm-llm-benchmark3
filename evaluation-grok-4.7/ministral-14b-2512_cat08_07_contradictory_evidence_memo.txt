5.8

The memo has the right overall posture—do not scale nationally, do not fully roll back, keep the simple-house gains, and buy information—but it is not reliable enough for an executive decision. Several quantitative and logical errors change what a reader would believe.

The executive memo swaps the labels that the later sections use. Selective coordinator ownership with survey-skipping and post-billing activation stopped is called Alternative D, and the controlled experiment is called Alternative E. In the prompt, D is the experiment and E is the hybrid. Section 4 then uses the prompt’s labels and recommends “D + E,” so the document contradicts itself on the decision being made.

The median-versus-tail explanation is factually wrong. Simple, low-risk houses are 34% of the pilot, not 60%, and the other four segments are 66%, not 40%. Apartments, business, and rural cases also improved at the median while their tails worsened, so the split is not “a simple majority pulls the median and a complex minority pulls the tail.” That error is used as the mechanism for the central conflict.

Process-mining concepts are mixed together. High fitness with precision 0.51 means the discovered model replays the log but allows too much unobserved behavior; lower generalization (0.70 to 0.56) is never mentioned. Billing before activation and repeated visits are move-on-log conformance findings, which are behavior in the log, not examples of low precision. The complaint edge is also miscounted: 3% to 8% is about 2.7×, or +5 percentage points, not 5× and not “+3%.”

The rollout gates are not decision-ready. Both section 1 and section 6 say to scale if the experiment “shows” a 5% or 3% increase in p90, which is a worsening, and those two ceilings disagree. “Complaints + revisits do not exceed EUR 200” treats the fully loaded cost estimate (EUR 198 vs EUR 207) as if it were the downstream component alone. The “~60%” rule accuracy and the 85% bar are invented. Utilization is capped at 80% in one place and 85% in another.

The evidence table is the strongest part and does separate categories, but one “assumption” is the claim the data reject: that EUR 207 fully captures complaint cost, despite exclusions after week 4. Alternative C is misread as missing activation compliance even though C suspends post-billing activation. M1 is described as a test of rule accuracy when it is a randomized test of the skip policy; M5, which tests the ownership model the memo wants to keep, is ranked near the bottom.

The largest analytical omission is comparability. Pilot regions are more apartment- and old-building-heavy, segment and risk attributes are missing before week 3, the window is shorter, and start timestamps are sparse. Aggregate and even segment deltas are treated as redesign effects anyway. The recommendation direction survives those gaps; the stated causes, magnitudes, and scale-up thresholds do not.
