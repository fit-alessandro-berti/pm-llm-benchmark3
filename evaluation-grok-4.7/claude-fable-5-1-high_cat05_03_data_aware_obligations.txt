The answer is strong, but it is not nearly flawless: a same-timestamp ordering bug, a TX-311 counterfactual contradiction, and several formalization gaps keep it short of the top of the scale. **8.2**

The case verdicts themselves are largely right. Event-time state is reconstructed forward, blanks are not treated as nulls, settlement amount is not copied back onto TX-305, and TX-311’s `(timestamp, event_index)` order is applied correctly. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, and TX-309 are classified and justified well, including the inclusive EUR 100,000 boundary, the ≤20% increase, the standard-packet approval, and review-baseline rather than step-to-step amount tests. EX-77 versus EX-88 is handled with the right clocks (event, effective, record, evidence). The code computes verdicts instead of looking them up, and labeling TX-310 `REVIEW_REQUIRED` while arguing `NONCOMPLIANT` in prose is allowed.

The main defects:

- Section 4 contradicts itself on TX-311. It first says that discarding the DE update leaves US / 115,000 with an Enhanced Review after the amount change, hence still compliant, then says that a fraudulent or post-release DE update would flip the case to noncompliant.
- `ca_after_material` compares raw timestamps with `>`, so a compliance approval and a material change at the same time are not ordered by `event_index`, despite R7.
- The R1/R2 formalization does not match the table: `lastMaterialChange` is undefined when absent, jurisdiction changes are folded into an R2 test they do not belong to, and a standard-packet approval is only a comment, not a condition.
- Unknown jurisdiction is treated as an unknown R1 trigger even when amount is already below EUR 100,000 (`UNK ∧ FALSE` should be false). R3’s “no prior screen” case is described one way in the table and implemented another way.
- The diagnostics frame never records the latest `Sanctions_List_Update`, and TX-305’s “leaning compliant” confidence fights the missing-amount reasoning.
