The answer correctly spots the crude-versus-complexity reversal (Orion 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h overall; Orion shorter in every complexity band) and rightly warns against a blind 50% shift to Nova. That is the main insight. It does not carry the rest of the required causal argument, and several concrete claims are wrong.

The verdict is over-identified. “Mainly confounded,” confidence 70, is stated as if complexity stratification identifies the Orion effect. It does not. Shift is almost collinear with group (13/14 Orion cases are night; Orion-day is one low-complexity case), yet Section 1 says the reversal comes from “controlling for complexity and shift.” Shift was never controlled. The same paragraph says Orion handles night-shift cases faster than Nova. Crude night cycle time goes the other way (about 23h for Orion vs about 17.5h for Nova) because Orion’s nights are much more complex. Certified-reviewer capacity, named in the brief, is never used. Total, direct, and mediated effects are not distinguished.

Section 4 is not auditable and contains swapped figures. “Night wait (19.5h) > Day wait (5.1h)” matches high-complexity Orion wait and low-complexity Nova wait, not night versus day (night wait is about 13h, day about 8h). Orion “wait ~16h vs service ~6h” is also off (wait about 12.8h, service about 7.0h). Allowed interpretations treat mix differences as identified causes: compliance “adds ~10h,” queueing is the driver, quality is “unaffected” (two appeals, one in each group). No arithmetic is shown. Cycle time is not wait plus service; the residual is ignored. `review_service_h` already includes repeated review, so rework is not a separate additive path.

The graph is not a DAG: `shift` and `resource_group` point both ways. `case_completed → quality_outcome` contradicts the later collider claim, and nothing points into `case_completed`. `arrival_load`, `compliance_performed`, reviewer skill, and case risk are omitted, as is the obvious `complexity → review_service` link. The role table’s “estimand” column does not name estimands.

Section 5 misses a real beneficial Orion effect masked by case mix (hypothesis 1 predicts a null, not a remaining Orion advantage). The night test is reversed: if night queueing lengthens cases, night share above 60% should raise cycle time, not lower it. “Negative control” is misused for a compliance subgroup. Arrival load never becomes a hypothesis. Contradicting-evidence cells are often “none” or “no data.”

The intervention shape is the right kind (small routing pilot plus a capacity change that does not relabel the group), but the pilot randomizes high-complexity cases across groups without a shift or positivity safeguard, so it can mix a group contrast with night coverage. The closing recommendation is about 112 words, short of the required 120–200, and still treats queueing as an established majority cause.

The Python reproduces crude and complexity-specific means, a pooled-complexity standardization, and three-way counts. It does not compare variants or directly-follows graphs between groups. `parse_event_log_string` is applied to the first five raw traces, so the DFG block does not implement the requested group-level diagnostic, and warnings never attach to the three-way cells or the standardized contrast that those cells dominate.

Section 8 is the cleanest part: five real gaps and one data or design fix each. It cannot offset the false shift adjustment, the mislabeled durations, the cyclic graph, and the missing estimands.
