The policy split and the temporal layer are the only parts that hold together. Sections 1–2, 6, and the four-eyes idea in §7 show a real grasp of the problem: R5 and R6 are kept apart, Resolution B is chosen with an explicit compliance risk, R1–R12 are mapped with an authoritative layer, and the three temporal bands are in seconds with `zeta = 2.0`. T4 failing only on resources, and T7 as a temporal warning, are the right *kinds* of outcomes. The formal artifacts and the verdicts do not implement that design.

The procedural model is not a usable PM4Py net. The “authoritative” listing draws place-to-place arcs, never connects the three checks to their transitions, lets `Classify Risk` consume a single token after a three-way join, gives `Receive Onboarding Request` no outputs, and leaves escalation, legal opinion, activation, bank change, suspend, and payment with no arcs. `im`/`fm` are not part of `procedural_model`. The second bank check is labeled `Verify Again`, which is not an allowed activity name. §3.1’s semaphore and pre-classification clarification loop are not what the code does (clarification sits on `decision` after classify). §3.3 then abandons the explanation for unrelated filler, so concurrency, choices, and terminals are not actually specified.

The DECLARE dictionary contradicts both the requirements and §8. `precedence` is defined, correctly, as “target only if source already occurred,” and then both `(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` are encoded that way. Every accepted case misses one of those predecessors, so T1, T2, T4, T6, and T7 fail DECLARE. `nonsuccession (Sanctions Escalation, Compliance Approval)` forbids the legal high-risk path used by T6 and T7. The matrix still marks those DECLARE cells `pass`.

The log skeleton repeats that error. `always_before` pairs such as `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`, `(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)`, and `(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` are not vacuous when one activity is absent under PM4Py’s reading (the later activity requires the earlier one). The write-up treats them as order-only-if-both-occur, then marks T1 and T2 `pass`. `directly_follows` is populated as four mandatory adjacencies and explained as a sparse positive constraint; in `conformance_log_skeleton` that set is the allowed direct-succession relation, so every real trace deviates. `equivalence` on clarification and the `never_together` bans are fine; they do not rescue the model.

§7’s emergency gate has dead code: `opl is None` is already excluded by the `breach` condition, so a missing opinion is reported as `PASS` here. The T6 “warning” cell assumes `emergency_authorized == True` even though the scenario states that attribute is not in the log; that hypothetical should have been labeled as such in the verdict, not baked into the resource column.

§8 therefore does not evaluate the models that were written. Later violations are mostly omitted (T8 also has no `Release First Payment` and an approval on a rejected case; T5 is not only an open clarification). §9 names redundancy, vacuity, and support/confidence, but not these self-contradictions. §10 does not give a check order or a rule for conflicting findings; the deployment text is not about this process.

Taken as the final artifact, the answer detects the conflict and assigns some rules to the right family of formalism, then ships models and a matrix that disagree with each other and with T1–T8.
