7.0

The answer is a serious redesign, not a generic capacity plea. It normalizes the unequal windows, separates mix from volume, rejects the harmful standing rules, and traces how relief at one station loads the next. Those are the right moves. It is still not nearly flawless: several load-bearing numbers contradict the same logs, and those errors drive a policy and a dashboard that would misfire.

**What holds up.** Weekly rates are right: 2,300 vs 3,300 cases, Routine flat at ~1,400/week, Urgent ~2.3×, Complex ~2.2×. Branch rates check out (pre-analytical defects 7.0% → 12.3%, batch-wait 4.4% → 12.7%, repeat 6.7% → 14.8%, direct medical-validation share 24.5% → 42.3%, holds 1.8% → 7.5%, completion 89.3% vs 86.7%). The four required disruption types are present, plus a quality shock and a redesign-induced outage. I1, I2, I3, I4, and I9 are rejected for the reasons the logs support: absolute priority grows WIP, larger batches chase Routine volume that is not growing, dedicating M2 concentrates repeats, and lending technical validators raids the only pool already at 101%. Coupling early triage with registration, gating cross-training on a four-week lead time, metering post-outage release, and refusing to prioritize Urgent and Complex at once are sound. The trade-off table has every required column. At least one self-inflicted vulnerability is identified.

**Flow accounting is wrong, and it is used as a premise.** Unfinished orders over a finite window are an end stock (right-censoring plus anything still open), not “440 cases per week accumulating.” The normal period, which the brief calls stable, has almost the same completion gap (89.3% vs 86.7%). Dividing that stock by weeks invents a flow. The assay gap (about 584 visits/week at peak, and about 154/week even in the normal log) cannot be a case stock either: normal technical validation balances exactly (15,890 in and out), while a median assay queue of 4h at ~14 cases/hour is on the order of a few dozen tubes, not the 1,230-visit normal gap. Peak technical-validation exits (8,080) exceed entries (7,240) inside a cohort whose every case starts at Register during the window, so “draining a pre-existing backlog” is not available as an explanation. That should have been flagged as a conformance break. It is instead treated as proof the published medians are optimistic and is wired into P1 and L15.

**The medical-validation story is the wrong mechanism.** Direct medical-validation counts stay a stable ~68–70% of Urgent+Complex volume (3,900/5,704 normal, 3,420/4,851 peak). The jump from 24.5% to 42.3% of technical-validation output is the mix shift the brief already states, not a surge in discretionary referral. Holds are the rate that mix does not explain. R5 aims autoverification at the 24.5→42.3 gap anyway. Closing that gap means stopping medical review of the Urgent and Complex cases that create it. The evening-shift synchronization point is valid; the demand-reduction arithmetic is not. The hold argument later in the same section is the one the data support, and it is blurred into the other.

**Further quantitative and design errors.**
- R6 adds the 36h specialist queue to the 48h Hold→Medical validation edge. That edge is the path time; the queue sits inside it. The comparison that “favours” outsourcing is inflated by about a day and a half, and diversion would also hit Complex cases that never enter Hold.
- S5’s “~600 extra” assay runs at a 22% repeat rate is about three times the increment (peak repeats are 420/week; 22% versus 14.8% is on the order of 200 more, not 600).
- Accession correction does not “have no ability to absorb a doubling.” Work roughly doubles and utilization goes from 62% to 91%, not past 100%. The 3h→14h queue is a two-server variability cliff, which is a different claim.
- Summing median edges to “30h before preparation” is not a path time. The directional point (Urgent’s 17h median cannot be experiencing the pooled front end) is fair; stating 30h and “44% of the budget” as facts is not.
- “Roughly half” of peak Urgent cases miss 18h because the median is 17h, and penalty exposure “grows about 12×,” stacks that shortcut on a “roughly 10%” normal breach read off a p90 of 19h. P(>18) is unidentified from median and p90 alone.
- P6 “raises the internal Urgent target” during a quality shock so fewer cases count as protected. The penalty clock stays 18h. Loosening the internal target increases contractual breaches.
- M/M/1 ρ/(1−ρ) is applied to a two-machine pool to get a “4×” wait cut. The direction (high ρ is convex) is right; the factor is presented as an operational result.

**The dashboard would alarm in the normal regime.** L15’s green band is above 0.97, but the answer’s own normal completion ratio is 0.89, so P1’s “< 0.92” trigger is on in ordinary weeks. L7 sets first-pass yield green at ≥97% while the normal repeat rate is already 6.7% (yield ~93%) and the peak rate is 14.8%. L4 and L10 set oldest-item limits near median queue times; the oldest case is a maximum and will sit past those limits whenever the median is that large. L5’s green band (<24h) is far inside the normal 42h recollection edge. Sixteen “leading” indicators also miss the request for a few. Several of the underlying signals (mix, defect rate, M2 yield, cancellation-after-recollection, in-flight WIP) are the right ones and are simply banded against the wrong baselines.

The redesign would still be a better operating policy than I1–I3 or I9. The grade is held down because the steady-state claim, the medical-validation mechanism, the outsourcing arithmetic, and the control limits are not small wording issues; they are incorrect uses of the process-mining evidence the task exists to test.
