The final answer covers all seven required sections and gets the core distinction right: hospital service protection is not resilience when authorization, scan, temperature, and dual-control evidence disappear, and post-close audit remediation is not real-time compliance. Several claims are still factually or logically wrong, and the analysis largely restates the prompt instead of reading the event data.

The timeline misses the main temporal pattern. Hospital cycle time barely moved (median 8h to 10h to 8h) while pharmacy and wholesale medians and p90s stayed badly degraded through stabilization (wholesale median 58h versus 36h; p90 120h versus 72h). Cold-chain missing documentation (0.4% to 4.8% to 2.3% to 0.9%), cancellation rates, case volumes, fitting-trace percentages (91%, 44%, 59%, 76%), and the T2 deviations (spreadsheet invoice hold, missing quality release, variable proof-of-delivery delay, backlog age past baseline p95) are mostly unused. “Reconcile manual reservation” is treated as a partial-recovery variant, but that activity appears only in the stabilization graph.

The table’s keep/modify/retire calls are mostly defensible, but causal claims are loose. Baseline “backorder wait: 900” is compared with a higher T2 backorder rate that the workaround graph does not show. The 7.8% cancellation rate is overall, not pharmacy-specific. The hospital median slipping from 8h to 10h is attributed to manual authorization alone. The fast lane is described as sometimes skipping authorization, while the graph sends it straight to the snapshot check.

The recovery model contains every requested block, then contradicts itself: the trigger is availability under 15 minutes, and the constraint says not to enter before 30 minutes. “System wins” inventory conflicts can discard the physical record. Courier signature is treated as proof of delivery. Ambient priority flow has no explicit quality-release gate. Recall-sensitive product risk never appears. Partial recovery, when intake was back but sync and scanning were unreliable, is not a designed state. The 5% audit cap and 4-hour snapshot life are unexplained.

Indicators omit backlog age and a real throughput measure, both required, and do not track skip-authorization, dispatch-without-scan, snapshot age, cold-chain documentation, or dual control. The fairness ratio ignores the observed 91% versus 61% fill split. Recommending thermal printers for missing temperature records mismatches the failure. Controlled-substance dual control, still 0.5% versus a 0.2% baseline after stabilization, has no specific action. The trade-off paragraph correctly rejects skipped controls and a permanent audit tail, then invents blockchain as a decision-changing condition and misstates the actual trade the data show: hospital speed was protected while pharmacy service and controls absorbed the loss.
