7.2

The diagnosis is substantively strong: March is correctly treated as a label, duplicate-event, and resource-provenance break; later rework, queue growth, priority redistribution, seasonality, and their interactions are separated; and the monitoring, segmentation, and decision rules are usable. It is not nearly flawless. The quantitative core and the PM4Py plan contain concrete errors.

The baseline-relative table is accurate, including the Jan–Feb baselines and the reported deltas. The normalized DFG distances are not. Against January, L1 is 28 in September and 36 in December, not 27 and 35. Consecutive fragment steps are 31, 36, and 28 for September, October, and December, not 32, 37, and 29. Euclidean distances are wrong wherever the vector actually moves: May is \(\sqrt{366}=19.13\), not 18.87; July \(\sqrt{769}=27.73\), not 26.85; September \(\sqrt{166}=12.88\), not 12.69; October \(\sqrt{850}=29.15\), not 27.64; December \(\sqrt{292}=17.09\), not 16.40. Part A would print the correct values, so the write-up contradicts its own script. Those “step” distances also jump missing DFG months, so the May and September figures are two-month gaps described as monthly onsets.

The cycle-time split is directionally sensible and rightly keeps Assess service near +0.6 h, but the arithmetic does not close. A May sensitivity of 0.43 h per rework point times the July–August +16.5 point rework gap is about 7.1 h, not the stated ~5 h, and that May coefficient is already mixed with volume, complexity, and Supplier Z. The component ranges can sum above 100%. “Well under 1 hour” for all service time goes beyond the single Assess observation.

Other factual slips: Hypothesis 8 pairs the July–August service-time change with complexity rising to 45%, which is the October–November level, not the July–August level of 28–29%. The December alternative that the 5 December interventions were too late to move a monthly median is implausible; they cover most of the month. “Reverses” and “coincides exactly” are stronger than monthly aggregates support. Rank is undefined, while the best-identified logging break is ranked below noisier mechanisms. The Supplier Z × high-complexity cell uses 25% complexity rather than the observed ~20.5% base rate. Section 9 is about 430 words, above the 400-word cap. January’s p90/median ratio is 69/31.5 = 2.19, not 2.18.

Part B names the required PM4Py entry points correctly and preserves a copy under label mapping. `compute_monthly_process_artifacts` still defaults to `discover_log_skeleton(..., timestamp_key=...)`, which the log-skeleton signature does not accept, so the default path raises before returning the DFG, variants, and temporal profile. `fitness_alignments` does not return `log_fitness`; that key belongs to token-based replay, so the recovery metric becomes NaN. The unconditional `import pm4py` also blocks Part A when PM4Py is absent. The duplicate rule drops every later `Open Case` in a case, not only the technical duplicate.

The attribution and monitors remain the right operational reading: separate change points, mapped fitness as the conformance series, supplier-stratified rework, queue versus service, and a standard-case canary for priority starvation. The grade is held down by incorrect reported distances, an inconsistent decomposition, over-precise mechanism language, and code that does not run as specified.
