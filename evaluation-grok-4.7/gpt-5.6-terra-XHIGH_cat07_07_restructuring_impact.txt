7.8

The memo is complete, numerically careful on the listed handover totals, and rightly refuses a full rollout. It still has several analytical and technical problems that keep it short of a near-flawless forecast.

The 343 versus 359 handovers per 100 cases, and the 588 versus 722 hour exposure sums, are arithmetically right and correctly caveated as a proxy. The recommendation then treats “about +23% waiting exposure” as a reason not to proceed unchanged. That percentage is about +1.3 hours per case. Baseline median cycle time is 6.8–8.6 days. The memo never puts those units side by side, so a small absolute shift is allowed to sound like a first-order cycle-time result. Management’s own waits move by well under an hour on most paths.

The utilization stress test has the same looseness. “15% higher” is applied to E_Tech alone (82% to about 94%) and to Legal/Risk (already 93% and 91%, so past 100%). Pooling is the proposal: North and South are at 71% and 68%, and the 15% claim is not clearly aimed at Legal/Risk. The better capacity point is the one underused in the central scenario: Legal/Risk volume rises from 9 to 12 per 100 cases while those resources are already near saturation, yet that scenario still adopts management’s 9.6h to 10.5h wait. That wait increase is hard to reconcile with nonlinear queueing and is not flagged inside the central case.

One mechanism sentence conflates categories the tables keep apart. Embedded local technical handovers are logged and are forecast to rise (36 to 41, and 31 to 36). What falls is cross-team cover (6 to 3) and, possibly, unlogged informal advice. Calling the change “fewer informal or ad hoc local/cross-team handovers” blurs that.

The pilot, indicator split, East-versus-North/South effects, right-censoring warning, and conditional recommendation are strong. “Case-mix adjustment” is mostly stratification. Guardrails stay qualitative despite given baselines for rework and SLA misses. Rework is present but thinner in the mechanism map than queueing and utilization.

The PM4Py sketch covers the requested views and correctly says one timestamp cannot separate service from waiting. It also has real sketch bugs: `business_hours` is not a `discover_performance_dfg` argument; temporal-feature parameters are likely `*_column`, not `*_key`; the first 20 variant keys are not frequency-sorted; and home team taken from the first `Intake` event can capture `Bot_Intake` rather than the local team. Clipping negative inter-event times at zero will bias the handover-wait proxy.
