6.5

The write-up is complete, well structured, and directionally right: it refuses a full rollout, separates East from North/South, treats utilization above the mid-80s as delay rather than a free win, and judges the change on tails, segments, rework mix, and backlog rather than mean cycle time. Under a strict reading it is still analytically unreliable in several places that matter.

The mechanism map conflates queues the proposal and the management table keep apart. Technical Shared Service (41 per 100, 4.0h vs 3.2h) is not the Legal/Risk queue (12 per 100, 10.5h vs 9.6h) and not the shared approval queue (49 per 100). Section B puts technical work, legal/risk, and difficult quality check in one pool, then uses the technical 4.0h figure in the same argument as Legal/Risk loading. That mixes two different mechanisms. The saturation point itself is understated: at 91–93% utilization, 12/9 times the arrival rate implies a load above 1, so the queue is unstable, not merely past an “85% knee.”

The handover-network prediction is likely backwards. PM4Py’s handover graph is resource-level. Today each team’s caseworkers hand mainly to their own tech; after pooling, many caseworkers can hand to many techs, so distinct resource pairs tend to rise. “Fewer edges, higher weights” is the org-unit picture, not the default social network, and it would be a bad success test. The informal-advice claim is also not identifiable: advice that prevents a referral never creates a co-working event, so its loss cannot show up as disappearing ties in `discover_working_together_network`. Referral drift can proxy it; the working-together network cannot.

The 18% cross-team rework rate is treated as the “correct analogue” for context-distant complex work. Those six cases per 100 are absence-cover selections, so difficulty, disruption, and distance are confounded. Today’s 8–9% local specialist loop is the closer tech-review baseline. The “~55–60% stay local and fast” dilution story ignores the shared approval path at 49 per 100. Subjective scenario probabilities (about 25/50/25) add false precision.

The pilot has an operational contradiction and an identification gap. Legal utilization is already 93%, so a guardrail of “Legal/Risk stays <92%” is failed before the pilot starts. Keeping North as a concurrent control is only valid if N_Tech is not pulled into the pool and North cannot spill into it; otherwise the control is contaminated. The sketch never states that insulation, nor that a two-team pool does not have the capacity of the proposed three-team pool. `sla_days=10` cannot reproduce North’s 6.5% miss rate when the median is 6.8d and the 90th percentile is 16.4d. With only `time:timestamp`, service time is not separable from waiting time, so the waiting/service ratio is not identified. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` does not yield enqueue/dequeue counts or backlog slope.

The recommendation — pilot a narrowed technical pool, do not add Legal/Risk volume or remove a supervisor in the same step, and do not read early averages as success — still follows from the evidence. The measurement plan and the first mechanism section do not meet the same standard.
