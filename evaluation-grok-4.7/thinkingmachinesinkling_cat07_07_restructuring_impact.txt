6.5

The report hits every required heading and constraint: mechanisms, three scenarios, leading versus lagging measures, hidden risks, a conditional pilot-first recommendation, and a PM4Py-style sketch. East is treated differently from North and South, and average cycle time is not used as the success metric. It is not nearly flawless.

The largest analytical miss is in the given handover tables. Summing frequency per 100 cases gives about 343 handovers now and about 359 under management’s own “after” estimate. The local specialist round trip rises from 67 to 77, and the approval chain from 81 to 92. The stated benefit of fewer handover loops is contradicted by the estimate itself. The mechanism map only says local edge counts “may” fall.

Those same tables are hour-scale (roughly 5.9 handover-wait hours per case now versus about 7.2 under the management estimate, on cycle times of roughly 7–9 days). The central scenario then moves 90th-percentile cycle time by 1–3 days while stating a shared-queue standard deviation of about 3 hours. That spread cannot justify day-scale tail shifts, and the optimistic day-scale gains are not tied back to the wait data or to the small rework-rate changes.

The pilot does not test the proposal. Merging `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, and `E_Tech` is the intervention, but North stays fully local and South keeps `S_Tech`, so no shared technical pool is formed. East-versus-North also confounds treatment with an already slower, overloaded team. Legal/Risk is already shared at 91–93% utilization, yet the South arm treats a shared legal/risk queue as a new change. The main risks the report itself names—North/South expertise loss, cross-team queue fairness, and pooled-capacity failure—are left untested until after an expansion decision based mainly on East.

Smaller defects add up. Optimistic rework is described as a 1–2 point drop, but East is taken from 11.5% to about 9%. The “+20%” East tail guardrail (28 days versus 22.8) is about +23%. The sketch is explicitly pseudocode, but several contracts are wrong or misleading: `format_dataframe` has no `resource_key`; `filter_event_attribute_values` takes `values`, not `attribute_values`; `get_rework_cases_per_activity` does not take `rework_flag_key` (it detects repeated activities); and `filter_case_attribute_values` has no `filter_type='gte'`. `forecast_comparison` then analyzes only the after log. SLA logic depends on invented `outcome` labels rather than a cycle-time rule.
