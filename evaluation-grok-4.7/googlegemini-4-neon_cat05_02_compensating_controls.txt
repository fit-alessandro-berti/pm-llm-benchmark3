7.5

The analysis is strong on control objectives, case discrimination, and timing, but it is not nearly flawless. Several factual, logical, and data errors are material under a strict reading.

The best work is the refusal to equate a logged activity or a user-ID split with an effective control. AP-203 is rightly treated as meeting the objective under ED-1 because an independent manager substantively reviewed the invoice, bank account, and service report before release, AV-2 passed cleanly, and same-day audit stayed inside the recall window. AP-204 is correctly not scored as a pass merely because `u12` and `u13` differ: a fabricated receipt, a shared workstation, and a 37-payment pairing defeat the independence objective. AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-208 is correctly `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` despite a legitimate payment, and vendor tenure of about eight months is correctly not treated as an ED-1 failure. AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE`, rather than a mechanical pass, is the right contrast with AP-204. Section 4’s hybrid reading of post-release, pre-settlement ratification is coherent, and section 6 properly separates alerts from proof of misconduct.

The main logical break is internal. Section 2 says full equivalence for a post-release manager action requires a verified pre-settlement hold, and section 4 says that action earns preventive credit only if cancellation is actually enforceable. AP-207 is still classified `OBJECTIVE_MET` while the ability to stop the payment between 14:40 and 16:00 is listed as missing. On the answer’s own test, that is at most `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`. AP-206 is also closed too firmly: the facts leave the 13:02 token as either logging delay or a failed ED-1 precondition, but the chosen label resolves that fork. L3 status is a stated MG-4 requirement and is unconfirmed for every manager, yet AP-203 is still high-confidence `OBJECTIVE_MET`.

The PM4Py note is factually reversed. With the default positive filter, `filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps cases in which the two activities have different resources; it does not, in current 2.7, return violations. The runtime set comparison would often repair that mistake, but the required version explanation states the wrong polarity as fact. The case-facts frame also invents precision the evidence does not have: vendor age 1 day for a “new” vendor, 365 days for an “established” vendor of unknown age, and `pair_30d_count = 1` where repetition is simply unknown. Broken wording (“amounts EUR 100k”; “nominal four-eyes control-objective fulfillment”) leaves two threshold statements unreadable.
