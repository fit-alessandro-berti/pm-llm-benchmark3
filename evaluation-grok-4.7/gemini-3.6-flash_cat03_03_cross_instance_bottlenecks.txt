6.2

The write-up covers every required section and often uses the right object identifiers, but several core numerical and causal claims are wrong, internally contradictory, or invented. That keeps it well short of a high score.

The delay arithmetic is not reliable. J6’s wait is decomposed as 30 + 45 + 5 = 80 minutes, then summarized as a reduction “from 80 min to 30 min (saving 45 min).” 80 − 30 is 50, so the headline saving does not match the counterfactual start at 16:35. The FIX-A counterfactual is also too large: saving 100 minutes by starting J4 at 10:05 assumes CNC-2 stays free until 11:45, while the longest recorded CNC-2 gap is 48 minutes and the only documented idle interval is 10:05–10:47. The defensible tool-and-machine overlap is about 42 minutes, plus an unknown interval through 11:40, not a 100- or 115-minute machine-idle wait. The S1 counterfactual misreads the other shipments. S2 and S3 dispatch 25 and 35 minutes after their last packed job, not 30 minutes after the first; using that analogy to send J1 out at 14:00 is a different policy, not a like-for-like comparison. The 5 h 40 min from 13:30 to J4’s 19:10 pack time is the cleaner consolidation figure.

Section 2’s best point is then abandoned. Under a two-job B2 rule, RACK-7 cleaning until 14:35 is not binding because J5 is ready only at 14:40, so a second rack saves about zero. Rank 1 nevertheless treats “J4 waits 85 min for RACK-7” as support for the top bottleneck. The machine-capacity row puts 44–58% utilization in “supporting evidence,” even though those figures cut against a capacity bottleneck. Rank 1 also says the job flattening shows long pre-process gaps, which conflicts with the supplied job DFG (short medians, no job more than 1.8 SD above the mean).

The graph and narratives over-assert structure. J3 is drawn and listed as running on CNC-2, but the evidence only ties J4’s `Machine Process` to CNC-2 and FIX-A; the motif itself says J3 holds FIX-A “elsewhere.” Batch membership is renamed `contains`, blurring it with lot containment. The L7 diagram hangs J1’s 08:10–12:05 wait off the lot quarantine, yet J1’s component is C1 in L8, not L7. Narrative 3 invents an S3 dispatch at 18:25. Minimum batch size and a mandatory every-N-tests CAL-3 policy are stated as policy constraints, not as speculation. Narrative 2 then says the FIX-A delay “directly” moved J4’s pack to 19:10 and therefore held J1. J4 was batch-ready at 13:10 and B2 could not start until J5 at 14:40, so an earlier CNC start does not automatically move the pack or the shipment. That is temporal succession treated as a critical-path cause.

Flattening coverage names all six viewpoints and the low-utilization paradox is real, but the duplication effect of flattening is never identified, and the tool viewpoint is described as showing no tool problem. A tool log would show FIX-A occupancy, CAL-3 recalibration, and RACK-7 cleaning; what it would miss is the jobs that never logged a wait.

The PM4Py block names the requested functions, yet it does not assume an `ocel` variable, reloads a file, and ends on an unbound `print(ocel)`. The promised frequency comparison is only two flattened performance-DFG edge counts. Comments claim an event-frequency versus interaction-frequency comparison that the code never computes.

Falsifiers are six in number but not six tests of the top explanation. A fixed 3.5-hour outgassing hold also fits the data poorly: J1 and J2 wait unequal times and start together at 12:05, which is what synchronization predicts and a common per-job timer does not. A mandatory 100-minute clean on RACK-7 does not show that a second rack would be useless.
