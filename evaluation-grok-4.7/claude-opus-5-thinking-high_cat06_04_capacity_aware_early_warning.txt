The policy is complete and directionally strong: I-03 is refused on mechanism rather than rank, I-05 and I-07 are separated correctly, calibration is adjusted before scoring, and spillovers, label feedback, and segment bias are treated as design constraints. It is not reliable as a deployable rule, because the portfolio does not follow the rule that was just stated and several audited figures do not follow the arithmetic shown.

The authorisation rule requires `Net > 0` at the unfavourable end of both the calibration and effect bands, and an effect gate of shrunk midpoint ≥ 0.10. I-08’s shrunk midpoint is 0.10 × 0.8 × 0.85 = 0.068, so the effect gate fails, yet it is marked eligible. I-02 at the lower effect bound is about 0.064 × €25,000 − €450 − ~€276 − €1,800 ≈ −€900, and the answer’s own runway haircut already flips its working net to about −€250; it is still given slot 2. I-06 at its lower bound is about 0.15 × €15,000 − €450 − €192 − €1,800 ≈ −€190, yet it is conditionally given slot 3. The adverse-guardrail row then drops exactly those two cases. Section 3 therefore recommends a different portfolio from Section 2, while Section 1 forbids the Section 2 choices.

The evidence gate is also not applied. A repeat alert needs a material change, including `Δp_adj ≥ +0.10`. I-02 moves only +0.08 and I-09 only +0.07, with no documented new event, but both are treated as cleanly eligible. I-05 is held to that standard; the two repeat cases that actually receive slots are not.

The Section 2 working table is mostly reproducible. The claims around it and the Section 3 ranges are not. I-04 is “still +€2.5k at the lower bound,” but the shrunk lower bound after the congestion charge is about 0.068 × €60,000 − €450 − €1,210 − €1,800 ≈ +€0.6k; +€2.5k appears only if the shrinkage factors are dropped. I-01 is called break-even before the congestion charge when that pre-charge net is about +€1,477. Optimistic central benefit is €13.9k against €2.05k cost and €2.7k spillover, which is roughly 7× cost and 5× spillover, not “5× cost and 3× queue harm.” The optimistic net interval’s low end, €7.4k, is central benefit minus high spillover, while €12.0k is top benefit minus low spillover; the joint low corner is about €11.9k − €2.05k − €4.5k ≈ €5.4k. In the guardrail row, midpoint benefit €7.5k − €2.36k − €5.4k ≈ −€0.3k, and the best corner is about +€2.5k, not the reported −€1.2k and +€0.5k. “Most likely exactly one” breach avoided in the optimistic case is also wrong under the stated effects: P(none) ≈ 0.44 and P(exactly one) ≈ 0.42.

Smaller decision-theoretic slips accumulate. Log-spaced tiers are supposed to replace raw euros in the ranking, but rank is raw `effect × value`, and the tier labels are not even ordered by that score (I-02 is “Tier A-marginal” at about +€0.5k; I-08 is “Tier B” at about +€1.3k). The €1,800 congestion charge is constant, so it changes the cutoff and does not change order. The 30% low-value quota is not costed and would force negative-EV swarms whose spillover lands on the same lower-value queue the quota is meant to protect. The adverse cost note treats a smaller treatment effect as a higher false-alarm probability; a false alarm depends on baseline risk, not on effect size. The proposed instrument — a regulatory arrival or a missing specialist — also moves breach risk through congestion, so the exclusion restriction fails. The NNLS pooled mean is stated as ≈0.17; the case midpoints average about 0.20.

The qualitative safeguards in Sections 4–6 are concrete and usable, and the serial-specialist constraint, reserve release, and I-03 refusal are handled well. The grade is held down because a capacity policy whose own gates, repeat-alert rule, and impact table disagree is not yet an auditable decision rule.
