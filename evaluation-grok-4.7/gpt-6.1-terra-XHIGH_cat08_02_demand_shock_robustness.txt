The answer is a strong, usable redesign: all seven required sections are present, the four mandatory disruption types are covered, case types are not treated as interchangeable, and every standing intervention names a downstream station it can overload. The cited volumes, mixes, utilizations, queues, sojourn times, bypass rates, and DFG counts match the source. Right-censoring, loop double-counting, and the ban on adding edge times to queue medians are handled correctly, and I1, I3, and I9 are rejected for reasons the logs support.

The score stays well below excellent because several process-mining comparisons are still confounded. Hold, repeat, and recollection counts are set side by side across an 8-week and a 3-week window. “Longer normal period” signals the problem, but it does not replace a per-week or per-arrival rate. The recollection line, 780 versus 760, reads almost flat even though the weekly rate is roughly triple; that undercuts the quality-shock scenario the same paragraph is trying to justify. Batch-wait frequency (760 versus 1,180), rework (7.5% to 11.8%), and the share of cases with a queue over 12 hours (22% to 54%) are left unused.

A few mechanisms are loose. Early rejection is said to overload accession correction, although the log sends rejected samples to recollection and only some of those through correction; the clerk, courier, and intake load is the path the data actually support. An evening medical shift is said to reduce staffing available to Routine work, but most Routine orders release from technical validation, and medical validators are specified for Urgent and Complex-panel cases. Specialist review is first grouped with the 96–101% stations despite 87% utilization and the longest queue, 36 hours, which is a single-server and absence problem; the later absence paragraph repairs this only in part. Repeat-after-failure, which consumes a second assay and clusters after night shifts and M2, never receives its own control beyond “investigate.” Overlapping scenario rules are resolved only by “use the more restrictive,” with no precedence when urgent reservation, a validator floor, and a machine cap conflict. Switch-off still depends on an undefined local operating band.

What keeps the score from falling further is that the operational core is coherent: deadline slack plus protected non-urgent share, pull caps, conditional batching, a technical-validation floor before cross-training, partner diversion only when the full path including 12-hour transport is faster, and explicit redesign-caused risks (hidden upstream age, schedule fragmentation, custody breaks, false rejection).
