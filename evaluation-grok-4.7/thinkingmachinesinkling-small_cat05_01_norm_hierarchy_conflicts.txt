6.2

The assessment is structurally complete and often sharp, but it is not reliable enough for a high score. Several required judgments contradict each other or the stated facts, and one required table answers its own columns backwards.

The strongest work is the refusal to let ECD-2, the CFO email, or later approval silently erase GPIP-4. P-502 is rightly noncompliant because new-supplier compliance clearance came after commitment. P-504 is rightly outside both the sterilization scope and the local emergency definition. P-506 correctly rejects an unwritten overnight extension. P-508 correctly treats Legal clearance above EUR 150,000 as additive and still requires the missing budget and finance approvals. P-505 is properly separated from a clean compliance finding, and the signed-PDF analysis for P-507 respects the four-hour recording rule. The closing clause covers authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, records, expiry, and normalization.

Those results do not repair the following defects.

The hierarchy table reverses “what it can override” and “what can override it” for GPIP-4 and the SOP. A1–A3 are described as overriding nothing while the cells list the instruments that fail to override them. The table therefore does not state a usable hierarchy.

Segregation is formalized incorrectly. A1 prohibits the requester or the committer from being the final approver. The pseudo-logic fires only when requester, committer, and approver are all the same person. Section 1 also says Incident Command authorization is not final business approval, but P-503 treats that authorization as an A1 breach. P-508 then analyzes the same issue differently.

P-503’s controlling interpretation calls the supplier unapproved. The case states an approved supplier, and the same row later says the supplier is approved. Amount still triggers compliance clearance, so the classification can survive, but the stated rationale is factually wrong.

The section 1 assumption that every unapproved supplier is a “new supplier” would make P-504 an A3 breach at any amount. The P-504 row never applies that assumption. The independent budget-owner half of A2 is required in the catalogue and in P-507 and P-508, then dropped when P-506 is called satisfied on finance timing alone.

P-501 misreads the local emergency clause. Permission to commit before the PO is separate from the requirement that an absent plant manager be replaced only by an actively delegated shift lead. The row also lists missing Incident Command authorization as a deviation even though the directive was not yet effective. It then classifies the case noncompliant while admitting that the SOP may not require those approvals before commitment. That unresolved premise supports indeterminate, not a medium-high-confidence breach.

ON-44 is used for P-505 on 18 July, although the outage was on 14 July and describes delayed entry rather than a fast clock. The case’s separate “nine minutes fast” statement is a different fact. Section 6 also places the P-501 emergency form at 07:15; the record is 15 July at 07:50. Section 1 never states a later-in-time rule, despite that being a required part of the method.
