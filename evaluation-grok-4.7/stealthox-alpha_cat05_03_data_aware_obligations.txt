7.1

The case table is the strongest part of the answer: all eleven release-time reconstructions and the chosen labels are defensible. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, and TX-309 are correctly decided, including the 20% re-baseline at TX-309 (145/118), the sub-20% increase at TX-306, the exact 100,000 threshold and decrease rule at TX-308, and the refusal to let a standard-packet approval cure a missing enhanced review at TX-307. EX-77 versus EX-88 is handled with the right time notions, and TX-302 stays compliant because the enhanced review already discharges R1. TX-305 as indeterminate and TX-310 as noncompliant, with the checker deliberately stopping at human review, are both reasoned applications of R5 and R6. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and logging fixes in concrete, case-linked terms.

The same-timestamp analysis is wrong in the final write-up. At TX-311 the release is at 16:30, after both 16:20 events, so the release jurisdiction is DE whichever way those two events are ordered. `event_index` changes the state at the review, and whether the jurisdiction change counts as a later material change; it does not leave the release state able to be US. Section 4’s “had the jurisdiction stayed US” counterfactual and section 6’s claim that the release state is undefined without `event_index` both repeat that mistake.

The required checker does not run. `slu_t` is referenced in the diagnostics row and never assigned, so execution dies with `NameError` before any case label is produced. The surrounding logic is largely aligned with the narrative, but “executable” is not met. The diagnostics also export only an amount-unknown flag.

The formalization is looser than the prose. `at_or_before` is used and never defined. The R3 violation clause and the R5/R6 rules leave `u`, `c`, or `q` unbound. R6 requires a rule to be both triggered and missing its decisive attribute, which contradicts the R1 trigger that already demands known amount and jurisdiction. Materiality is stepwise against the immediate prior amount and treats every jurisdiction or customer-type event as material; that is broader than an increase of more than 20% measured from the amount in force at the latest review. No logged case changes label under that gap, but the rules as written do not say what was formalized.

Section 4 closes TX-305 too tightly. An enhanced review whose own document contains no amount can still fail an amount-dependent R1 obligation once a release amount at or above 100,000 is proved. That reading remains open, so “no reading produces a violation” is too strong. The normalized rule table, as presented, also drops several comparison operators, which makes the ordering column hard to apply without the later pseudo-logic.
