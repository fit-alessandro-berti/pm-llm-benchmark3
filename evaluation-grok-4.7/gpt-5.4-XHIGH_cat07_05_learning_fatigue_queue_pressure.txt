7.6

The narrative is strong, complete, and constraint-compliant: all six sections and all required mechanisms are present, table numbers are read correctly, early complexity is separated from warm-up, late speed is tied to queue pressure rather than learning, and punishment plus quality-check removal are rejected. The integrated account correctly treats the speed-quality split as conditional on WIP, experience, and batching.

The methods section is not reliable enough to match that reasoning. `filter_event_attribute_values` is used at the default case level, so the Day-shift and high-WIP slices retain other events from the same cases and would contaminate the performance DFGs and batch checks. `bot_assisted` is coded on `Bot_Risk` events, while the claimed errors are human corrections on later events. `log(service_time)` is unspecified for instant bot durations. Resource fixed effects and `resource_experience_months` are entered together without noting that a static experience main effect is absorbed. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` is activity repetition, not the 7-day `rework_flag`. `view_dotted_chart` is called with parameters that function does not take, and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` use non-matching argument names. Performance filters are given minute-like cutoffs where PM4Py expects seconds. `pd.cut` places WIP 30 in “low,” against the stated `<30` band.

Two reasoning slips remain. Backlog complexity in the Early first hour is listed as support for circadian effects, then correctly described as carryover. Senior stability under high WIP is treated as awkward for a fatigue account; experience-dependent fatigue predicts that pattern. WIP and queue, and sequence position and minutes since shift start, are both entered as if separately identified, despite the endogeneity caveat made earlier.
