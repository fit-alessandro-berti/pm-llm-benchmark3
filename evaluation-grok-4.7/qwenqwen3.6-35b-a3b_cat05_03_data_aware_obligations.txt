5.5

The write-up covers the required sections and reaches a defensible result on most cases, but it is not reliable as a formalization or as checking code. Several decisive errors remain in the final conclusions.

The clearest case error is TX-302. At release the state is GB, corporate, EUR 180,000, with an Enhanced_Review at 09:20 and no later attribute change. R1 is therefore met by the review itself. EX-77 is valid backup evidence, not the basis of compliance. Labeling the case JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, with “R1 waived” and “Deviations: None,” contradicts that timeline. Section 4 then analyzes the wrong hinge (the outage and database insert). TX-305 is correctly INDETERMINATE, but the amount-less review document is never used, and R4 is listed as if an unknown-increase event existed. TX-310 as NONCOMPLIANT is a sound reading of the post-release certificate and interested witness. TX-311 as COMPLIANT is right because release-time jurisdiction is DE, but “waived by DE” misstates a condition that simply does not fire, and the same-timestamp counterfactual does not explain why the classification is stable.

TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, and TX-309 are correctly noncompliant. TX-306 and TX-308 as compliant are the better reading of R4, yet Section 1 defines every attribute update as material while R1 demands a review after the latest material change. That conflict is acknowledged only for TX-306, then assigned high confidence anyway.

The R2 pseudo-logic is wrong: `not (Enhanced_Review or Compliance_Approve)` does not require both events, so it misses TX-303 and TX-307. R4 is not tied to the latest review or to “after that review.” The R2 table also invents an ordering constraint the rule does not state.

The code meets the CSV, `format_dataframe`, sort, and forward-fill setup, then fails the evaluation the narrative depends on. R2 is inverted (`not trigger and ...`), so triggered cases are always false and non-triggered cases are false unless both events exist. R3 is absent, the exemption register is never joined, R4 compares the latest amount update with the create amount, unknown amount becomes “not triggered,” and the diagnostics lack evidence timestamps and unknown-value flags. Unused alignment imports are likely to stop execution. Every case is then stamped `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, so the frame does not reproduce the case conclusions.

Sections 6 and 7 do cover the requested failure modes and logging improvements. Those strengths do not offset the misclassified required case, the unsound R2 formalization, and checker code that contradicts the written findings.
