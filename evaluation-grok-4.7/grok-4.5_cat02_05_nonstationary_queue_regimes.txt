6.4

The write-up is structurally complete and often conceptually careful, but it is not numerically reliable. Repeated arithmetic mistakes, mixed samples inside one regime row, and prose that the supplied code would not reproduce are enough to keep it well below a near-flawless score.

What holds up is the operating story. Baseline weekdays, the 15 April outage, the 16–17 April burn-down, the post–20 April mix/peak shift, the late staffing step, and weekends are a defensible primary cut. Recovery is correctly refused as a high-productivity steady state. Median service is not treated as flow time. Little’s Law is restricted, with opening-hour units, carry-over, and throughput-proxy choices stated. The backlog alternative is genuinely different, and the pooled “about 0.96 and flat output per FTE” policy is a real Simpson-style trap. Assumptions and the mix-versus-coding ambiguity are labeled.

The calculations do not meet that standard.

- Pooled means are wrong: arrivals are 2519/28 = 90.0, not 91.3; completions 2409/28 = 86.0, not 88.0; mean WIP 836/28 = 29.9, not 30.4; mean FTE 144/28 = 5.14, not 5.3. The pooled rate ≈ 7.6 is the downstream effect of the bad arrival mean (true value ≈ 7.50).
- R1 is 7 weekdays, not 8. WIP median 20.5 and complex median 21.5 match the 7 baseline days **plus the outage day**; arrival median 8.4 matches the 7 days **without** it. One regime row mixes two samples. Completion median is 100/12 = 8.33, not 8.4, and WIP median is 20, not 20.5.
- R4 totals are not 649/733. They are 704 completions and 798 arrivals (ratio still ≈ 0.88, so the direction survives, the counts do not). Complex median is 45, not 44. The top arrival rate is 121/12 = 10.1, not 9.9. R3’s median arrival rate is ≈ 8.8, not 9.0.
- R6 medians are off: arrival rate ≈ 3.71, not 3.6; p90 median (58+78)/2 = 68, not 69.5; complex median (24+39)/2 = 31.5, not 32.5.
- Hourly dispersion is misstated. For 21 April arrivals, sample variance is ≈ 97, not 94.4. Completions on that day have Fano ≈ 0.98, not “~1.5–2”. Weekend arrivals on 25 April have Fano ≈ 1.3, not 1.5–2. The smoothness conclusion is right; the index is not.
- Early-weekend sojourn ≈ 13/3.4 ≈ 3.8 h uses a slightly high throughput. Equal-weighted open hours give about 3.9 h.

Coherence problems sit on top of those errors. Section 6 applies Little’s Law to median WIP, while the code uses mean WIP. The narrative calls 29 April–1 May backlog drains, but the code’s `delta >= 10` and `mean_wip >= 35` rule classifies 29–30 April as residual high WIP and 1 May as stable. Section 4 says weekdays must be designed for ~9–10/h and also that the weekday range starts near 8.5; the observed weekday span is about 7.7–10.1, and the R1 level is about 8.4. Calling R4’s L/μ a “lower bound” is not justified.

Clarity is also damaged where relation symbols collapsed: “52→35”, “43→36→32”, “Δ”, and “λ” become strings such as “5235”, “433632”, and bare gaps. Several required table cells are not readable as published.

The Python block does cover both tables, regime summaries, Fano and CV, lag-1 correlation, a non-definitive two-mean scan, chosen Little’s Law windows, and the requested PM4Py comments with an explicit non-log warning. That satisfies the checklist, but it does not audit the narrative numbers. A reproducible appendix that contradicts the headline table is not yet a coherent calculation.
