The answer is structurally complete and conceptually strong: prerequisites, four-eyes separation, and final compliance after the latest assessment are preserved; K-337 is correctly treated as a decision-policy change rather than a pure time cut; K-411 correctly separates earlier verification readiness from earlier finish because assessment is the bottleneck; and the ranking is not driven by cycle time alone. Those are the hard points, and they are mostly handled well.

The score is held down by temporal and calibration problems. The K-104 path puts C1 on a preliminary review at Tue 08:00 despite a 16-hour compliance queue at that snapshot, then has M1 decide at 22:45. Observed manager sign-offs after evening completions wait until the next morning, so Tuesday-night notify is not supported. That makes the table’s 12–24 hour saving band inconsistent with the derivation’s own base of about 7–10 hours and its best case of about 19 hours; 24 hours is not earned by the path given. K-219’s no-rework floor of 10–20 hours likewise omits a slow-but-accurate D2 outcome in which the assessment queue and manager timing absorb almost all of the earlier document finish.

The 100-case discussion names the right mechanisms (C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, avoided versus added rework, assessment congestion, weekends, false concurrency) but is only about 180 words against a required 250–400, and it never checks weekly capacity. One hundred similar cases cannot be absorbed by a single compliance reviewer or by D2; “bottleneck” understates infeasibility. The pilots are concrete but have no comparison arm, and every intervention is labeled high information value, so that column does not discriminate.
