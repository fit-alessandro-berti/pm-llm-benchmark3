5.5

The write-up keeps the required structure, refuses a single “reduces cycle time by X%” claim, and correctly treats rollout as endogenous, availability as distinct from use, and median and p90 as different outcomes. The embedded-evaluation recommendation is directionally sensible. Those strengths do not offset material errors in the quantities and identification logic.

The adopter contrasts use a Jan–Mar window for Gamma, so the May–June rise and the July drop (median about 45.7 to 37.2 hours; rework 14.6 to 12.6) disappear and Gamma looks flat. Alpha/Beta pre/post averages are arithmetically fine, but the “~18%” figure subtracts percent changes from different baselines, then reappears as “−10%” and “10–15%” with no reconciliation. Load is never computed: Alpha arrivals per FTE rise by roughly 30% while median hours stay near 49, which is the main descriptive fact. July–August improvement also occurs in Delta and Epsilon, so Alpha’s late decline is not separated from a common rebound.

Rework is misstated. Alpha’s post mean is only slightly above 13.2, and it falls in the adoption month (13.2 to 11.2); Beta’s post mean falls (12.8 to about 12.2). Escalation’s large step is in March, before adoption, in every branch; calling the post-period pattern harmful at confidence 60 treats the fraud-desk shock as the treatment effect. p90 is judged harmful at confidence 70 from treated pre/post increases, while the same never-treated comparison used for the median shows control tails worsening more (Delta p90 about +24%). That switches estimands mid-argument.

Estimands mark availability, intensity, tails, rework, and dynamics as identifiable. Interference through the shared fraud desk and treatment versions (availability, fast-path share, learning, local practice) are never specified, despite being required. The preferred design is an event study, but covariates include post-rollout arrivals and FTE without a bad-control discussion, and the code instead fits branch FE with no month FE and no relative-time terms. The spillover row gives an effect direction, not a bias direction: a shared desk that also slows controls would push difference-in-differences toward a benefit.

The code embeds the panel and clusters by branch, but it does not validate the panel, plot or print all five outcomes, or report pre/post contrasts for Gamma or for non-cycle outcomes. Relative time is built and unused. The final “conditionally causal” 10–15% claim is the wrong sensitivity: the May complexity jump is a large part of the control deterioration, so conditioning on case mix would be expected to shrink, not underwrite, that relative gap. The within-Delta “step-wedge cluster” design is actually case-level randomization on a shared queue, and the p90 guardrail of 120 hours is far above Delta’s own baseline.
