7.4

The design is strong on structure, DES discipline, and experiment framing, but several quantitative and control-flow errors keep it well short of nearly flawless.

The core simulation stance is right: waiting is emergent, touch time is separate, calendars are first-class, objections stay latent, and pre-portal delay is counted inside D1/D7 lead time. PM4Py is used in a real way (`format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`, variants, DFG, performance DFG, case-arrival average) and is separated from the twin. CRN, nested input uncertainty, quantile metrics, and the D10 interaction note (D2 thins batches and can make D4 redundant) are credible.

The serious problems are these.

The control flow is internally inconsistent. Section 1 correctly parallelizes only planning, fire, and environment. Section 3 then puts public notice and site inspection into the same AND-join. The supplied tree runs notice after that parallel block and inspection after notice. Treating those as concurrent branches changes the critical path and the effects of D2, D3, D4, and D9. The code only encodes a completion set, so an implementer can start inspection before review and notice.

The run length does not support the tail metrics. Complex p90 is 260 days. Arrivals from months 1–12 with follow-up only to month 18 leave late complex and appeal cases censored, so the p90/p95 used for decisions stay biased. “6+ months to avoid right-censoring” is not achieved. The July WIP snapshot is also not tied to end-of-June seasonality, and “months 13–18 contain the spring surge” is true only if the log starts in January.

Stub logic contradicts the text and the log summary. Top-20 architects are 31% of volume, but `p=0.31` plus mean batch size 2 produces about 47% of cases. Case mix is drawn independently of architect after the text says it is conditional. D7 is specified as a top-20 Monday cap, but the multiplier flattens every weekday arrival. Environmental “40% non-permit” is implemented as Thursday–Friday afternoons, which is 10/40 hours, or 25%. Complex cases never get fire review. New-residential fire and notice draws do not reproduce the 18% variant share. Six CRN streams are described and two are spawned. Council weeks affect legal availability only; public notice is a lognormal draw, despite council calendar dependence being a stated driver of the complex tail.

Decision rules overclaim and can veto the policies under test. The 21/60/90/180-day cutoffs are labeled statutory although they are not in the evidence. The “no case type’s p90 may worsen by more than 5%” rule fights D3 and D6, which are explicitly allowed to slow some cases. Sensitivity covers travel savings and environmental availability, but not prior-violation prevalence, which dominates D2. Scheduler capacity is named as a queue and never staffed. A 0.5-day wait before unlimited system issuance would not emerge and is not explained. Service-time reconstruction from completion gaps ignores multitasking. Weekend timestamps in the excerpt are not reconciled with Monday–Friday calendars.

Skeleton API choices are mostly conventional, and placeholders were allowed. The deductions above are for incoherence with the design and the process tree, not for missing a full event loop.
