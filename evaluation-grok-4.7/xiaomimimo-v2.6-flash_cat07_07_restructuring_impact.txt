6.5

The required seven sections are all present and substantively filled: mechanisms, three scenarios, leading versus lagging indicators, tail and fairness risks, a staged pilot, a conditional modify-then-pilot recommendation, and a PM4Py sketch covering the requested views. East is treated differently from North and South, and the answer correctly refuses a full rollout and a cycle-time-only reading. The handover-count arithmetic (343 versus 359, +4.7%) is right and is a useful check on the “fewer handovers” claim.

The headline waiting-time figure is wrong. Frequency times median wait for the proposed routing is 722.2 hours per 100 cases (7.22 h/case, about +23% versus 588.3), not 622.2 hours (6.22 h/case, +5.8%). That error anchors the central-scenario band of 6.1–6.5 h/case. The rework index is also mislabeled: the summed 21.0 is per 100 cases under an additive handover model, not “21.0 per 100 handovers,” and stacking handover-level rework rates double-counts cases.

The claim that +15% utilization and a 3.2 h to 4.0 h wait “must” be inconsistent “under any standard queueing model” treats the merged technical pool as M/M/1. Three embedded specialists become a multi-server queue; pooling can raise utilization while holding or even cutting wait, so management’s two figures can be jointly plausible. That false inconsistency is then used as a main reason to modify the design. The 7.46 d and 18.6 d “portfolio” figures are volume-weighted averages of team medians and p90s, not portfolio quantiles.

The sketch has concrete defects: the ramp filter is invalid Python and drops four days rather than two weeks; the “fairness spread” is the within-team range of cell p90s, not the between-team gap; a 7-day SLA cutoff cannot reproduce a ~7% miss rate when medians are already ~7–9 days and p90s are 16–23 days; and the Herfindahl diagonal subtraction does not align when the matrix index is resource names.
