BENCHMARK CONTEXT
This is an open-ended process-mining benchmark task. There is deliberately no hidden reference answer and no single required numerical result. Your response will be evaluated by an LLM-as-a-judge for analytical depth, internal consistency, use of the supplied evidence, treatment of uncertainty, process-mining competence, and adherence to the requested output format.

General rules:
- Use only the information in this prompt. Do not invent observations that are not supplied.
- You may introduce assumptions, but label each one and explain why it is needed.
- Distinguish observations, estimates, bounds, hypotheses, and recommendations.
- Where several interpretations are defensible, compare them rather than forcing false certainty.
- Exact numerical agreement is less important than correct definitions, coherent calculations, and defensible uncertainty handling.
- Any Python must be self-contained: embed or parse the data shown in this prompt, use pandas for tabular work, and use PM4Py only where its semantics fit the task.


SCENARIO
A repair center exposes four logical queues. Management claims that each queue follows a documented rule, but the documentation is unavailable. Candidate mechanisms are:

- FIFO;
- non-preemptive priority;
- preemptive priority;
- shortest expected processing time (SPT/SEPT);
- appointment scheduling;
- skill-based routing;
- an undocumented hybrid of two or more mechanisms.

Priority 1 is highest, priority 3 is lowest. `est_min` is the estimate visible to dispatch at arrival time, not the realized duration. A semicolon in `service_segments` means that work stopped and later resumed. Timestamps are accurate to one minute, but arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes. Absence of an interruption reason does not prove that no operational reason existed.

ANALYTICAL TASK
Infer the most plausible dispatching discipline for each queue. Do not merely label the queues. Reconstruct the decision opportunities: at each service start or preemption, identify which jobs were plausibly waiting and eligible, then compare the observed choice with predictions from competing disciplines.

You must account for:
- appointment windows versus exact appointment times;
- resource availability and skill eligibility;
- preemption and resumption costs;
- delayed arrival scans;
- estimates that may be noisy or strategically inaccurate;
- deadlines and priorities that can produce observationally equivalent choices;
- the possibility that a queue changes policy when an SLA becomes endangered.

REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT
Return one Markdown document with exactly these top-level headings:

# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis
Provide a table with rows QA, QB, QC, and QD and columns:
`primary_discipline`, `secondary_or_hybrid_component`, `confidence_0_to_100`, `strongest_supporting_episode`, `strongest_contradicting_episode`, `main_identifiability_problem`.
Confidence must reflect evidential ambiguity, not rhetorical certainty.

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions
For each queue, analyze at least three concrete decision times. At each time list the plausibly eligible waiting jobs, the observed selection, and what FIFO, priority, SPT, appointment, and skill-based rules would have predicted. A compact table per queue is preferred.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases
For every queue, cite job IDs that support and challenge your primary diagnosis. Explain whether each apparent violation can be explained by known constraints or requires an undocumented exception.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests
Choose at least two ambiguous episodes from different queues. For each, compare the next-job prediction under the two strongest competing explanations and identify one additional observable that would discriminate between them.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations
Discuss at least four plausible exceptions, including data-quality explanations. Rank them by plausibility and state how each would alter the diagnosis.

# 6. Additional event data needed
Provide a prioritized collection plan. Include fields such as queue-entry time, dispatch-decision time, eligibility set, resource state, appointment tolerance, current estimated remaining time, preemption reason, and policy version, but tailor the list to the actual ambiguities in these data.

# 7. Final synthesis
Give a concise cross-queue conclusion: which queue is most identifiable, which is least identifiable, and which claimed policy would be most dangerous to infer from these observations alone.

QUALITY BAR
A strong answer will reason from decision sets rather than from start order alone, will not interpret every FIFO violation as priority scheduling, and will recognize observational equivalence between skill constraints, appointments, urgency, and shortest-job rules.
