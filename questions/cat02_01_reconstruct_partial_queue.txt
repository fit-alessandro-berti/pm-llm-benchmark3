BENCHMARK CONTEXT
This is an open-ended process-mining benchmark task. There is deliberately no hidden reference answer and no single required numerical result. Your response will be evaluated by an LLM-as-a-judge for analytical depth, internal consistency, use of the supplied evidence, treatment of uncertainty, process-mining competence, and adherence to the requested output format.

General rules:
- Use only the information in this prompt. Do not invent observations that are not supplied.
- You may introduce assumptions, but label each one and explain why it is needed.
- Distinguish observations, estimates, bounds, hypotheses, and recommendations.
- Where several interpretations are defensible, compare them rather than forcing false certainty.
- Exact numerical agreement is less important than correct definitions, coherent calculations, and defensible uncertainty handling.
- Any Python must be self-contained: embed or parse the data shown in this prompt, use pandas for tabular work, and use PM4Py only where its semantics fit the task.


SCENARIO
A precision-component repair process has two mandatory consecutive stations:

1. S1 = Incoming Inspection
2. S2 = Calibration

A case enters the S2 queue when its S1 activity completes. The observation window is 2026-05-12 08:00 through 14:00. All operational timestamps were rounded to the nearest five minutes before extraction, so a reported time t may represent an actual time in approximately [t-2.5 min, t+2.5 min]. The external-arrival timestamp was produced by a different system but was rounded in the same way.

Known capacity and availability:
- S1 normally has two single-task resources: I1 and I2.
- S2 normally has two single-task resources: C1 and C2.
- C2 was unavailable for maintenance from 10:55 through 11:10.
- A resource should not physically execute two cases at once, but rounding, missing timestamps, delayed logging, or undocumented assistance may create apparent conflicts.
- No case is intentionally allowed to bypass either station.

Timestamp/status semantics:
- MISSING means the activity occurred and its resource and completion are known, but its start timestamp was not recorded.
- OPEN means the activity had started but had not completed by 14:00.
- A blank S2 start/end for a case with completed S1 means the case was waiting for S2 at 14:00.
- A blank S1 start/end means the case was waiting for S1 at 14:00.

ANALYTICAL TASK
Reconstruct a defensible picture of both queues over the observation window. Estimate or bound, as appropriate:
- queue waiting time, active service time, and station sojourn time;
- case-level total sojourn time for completed cases;
- arrival and departure rates by meaningful subperiod;
- queue length and total WIP over time, including a clearly stated convention for simultaneous rounded events;
- resource busy-time and plausible utilization ranges;
- whether the evidence is more consistent with S1, S2, or a time-varying combination being the bottleneck;
- the effect of missing starts, right censoring, the C2 outage, and timestamp rounding;
- a Little's-Law consistency check only in intervals where you believe its assumptions are sufficiently defensible.

Do not silently replace missing starts with a single value. Give interval bounds, multiple-imputation logic, or an explicitly justified midpoint estimate. For each MISSING start, use constraints such as resource availability, prior/next work on the same resource, queue entry, completion, and nonnegative service time. For OPEN activities, distinguish elapsed service from final service time.
