ROLE
You are a senior process-mining and causal-inference analyst advising an insurance operations committee.

OBJECTIVE
Determine whether assignment to resource group Orion plausibly causes longer case completion times, is merely associated with longer times because of case mix and operating conditions, or influences performance through identifiable process mechanisms.

This is intentionally an underdetermined causal task. There is no single expected verdict. Do not invent missing facts, do not equate association with causation, and do not hide uncertainty behind generic caveats. Your response will be evaluated by another LLM for causal discipline, process-mining competence, use of the supplied evidence, and actionability.

PROCESS CONTEXT
All cases follow variants of this claims-review process:

Register -> Classify -> [optional Compliance Check] -> Review
       -> [optional Request Info -> Review] -> Close

Operational facts:
- A routing engine assigns a resource group after `Classify`.
- Orion has more certified reviewers and is usually selected for difficult cases.
- Orion covers most night work. Nova covers mostly day work.
- A compliance check is required by policy for some cases, primarily high-complexity cases, but the data records only whether it was required; assume it was performed whenever required.
- `wait_before_review_h` includes queueing after classification and, where applicable, waiting around the compliance step. It is not pure service time.
- `review_service_h` is the total observed service time spent in review activities, including a repeated review after `Request Info`.
- Only completed cases are present. There is no information about abandoned or still-open cases.
- The 30-day outcome is a coarse quality indicator and may be affected by post-process factors.

DECISION UNDER CONSIDERATION
Management proposes reassigning 50% of future Orion cases to Nova, expecting cycle-time improvement because Orion's overall mean is higher.

YOUR TASK
Produce a causal and process-aware assessment of that proposal. At minimum, you must:
- distinguish total, direct, and mediated interpretations of a possible Orion effect;
- identify the aggregate-versus-stratified pattern and explain why it matters without treating stratification as automatic causal identification;
- reason about complexity, shift, arrival load, compliance, rework, service time, queueing, quality, and selection into the completed-case sample;
- consider whether resource group is a treatment, a proxy for routing policy, a bundle of operational mechanisms, or some combination;
- propose analyses that could discriminate among plausible causal explanations;
- recommend an intervention that is safer and more informative than blindly moving half of Orion's work.

REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT
Use exactly the following top-level headings.

## 1. Executive causal assessment
Write 180-300 words. State:
- your provisional verdict using one of `likely causal`, `partly causal through mechanisms`, `mainly confounded`, or `not identifiable from the supplied evidence`;
- a confidence level from 0 to 100;
- the strongest evidence for and against that verdict;
- why the proposed 50% reassignment may succeed, fail, or backfire.

## 2. Causal graph
Provide a directed adjacency list in a fenced text block. Use variable names from this set where relevant:
`complexity`, `routing_rule`, `resource_group`, `shift`, `arrival_load`, `compliance_required`, `compliance_performed`, `queue_wait`, `review_service`, `rework`, `cycle_time`, `quality_outcome`, `case_completed`, `unobserved_reviewer_skill`, `unobserved_case_risk`.

After the adjacency list, add no more than 120 words explaining:
- which arrows are supported by supplied facts;
- which arrows are assumptions;
- at least one plausible feedback or time-varying relation that a static DAG suppresses.

## 3. Variable-role table
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| variable | possible causal role(s) | role relative to which estimand | evidence in prompt | ambiguity or conditioning risk |

A variable may have more than one role. Explicitly discuss at least one possible confounder, mediator, collider or selection variable, and proxy.

## 4. Descriptive decomposition
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| comparison | crude pattern | adjusted or stratified pattern | causal interpretation allowed | causal interpretation not allowed |

Include:
- overall Orion versus Nova;
- each complexity stratum;
- day versus night;
- compliance-required versus not required;
- queue wait versus review service;
- rework and 30-day quality.

Approximate calculations are acceptable, but show enough arithmetic or logic that the comparison is auditable.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |

Include at least five hypotheses. At least one must assign a genuine harmful effect to Orion, one must assign a beneficial effect masked by case mix, one must center on shift/load, one must center on compliance or rework mediation, and one must center on data selection or measurement.

Your proposed tests may include, but are not limited to:
- exact or propensity-score matching;
- overlap and positivity diagnostics;
- within-route or within-reviewer comparisons;
- front-door-like mechanism analysis;
- negative controls;
- interrupted routing experiments;
- queue-state reconstruction;
- conformance or variant comparison;
- sensitivity analysis for unobserved case risk.

Do not list a method without explaining what result would favor or weaken the corresponding hypothesis.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |

Include at least three phases. One phase must be a small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot, and one must target queueing or compliance capacity rather than changing the resource-group label itself.

Then provide a final recommendation of 120-200 words.

## 7. Reproducible Python diagnostics
Provide one self-contained Python code block that embeds the case-level CSV as a string and:
1. loads it with Pandas;
2. reproduces crude and complexity-stratified cycle-time comparisons;
3. computes a complexity-standardized Orion-versus-Nova contrast using a clearly stated common weighting distribution;
4. reports overlap counts for every observed combination of `complexity`, `shift`, and `resource_group`;
5. compares trace variants and directly-follows relations between groups;
6. prints warnings when a comparison has poor support or fewer than three cases in a cell.

For process-mining operations, use PM4Py consistently with the supplied manual. A valid approach is to parse the `trace` strings with `pm4py.parse_event_log_string(...)` and discover group-specific DFGs with `pm4py.discover_dfg(...)`. Do not fabricate event timestamps for performance analysis. The code should be diagnostic, not presented as proof of causality.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
List exactly five numbered uncertainties. For each, state the single most valuable additional data field or design change that would reduce it.

EVALUATION EMPHASIS
A strong answer does not need to choose a particular causal verdict. It should detect the reversal between crude and within-complexity comparisons, avoid claiming that this alone resolves confounding, preserve process semantics, distinguish queueing from service, propose tests with predicted outcomes, and recommend an intervention that learns while limiting operational risk.
