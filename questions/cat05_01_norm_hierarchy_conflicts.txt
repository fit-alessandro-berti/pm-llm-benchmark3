You are an independent process-compliance analyst. Construct the strongest defensible assessment from the materials below. There is deliberately no single canonical norm hierarchy or case classification. Your answer will be evaluated by an LLM judge for consistency, process awareness, treatment of scope and time, evidential discipline, and quality of argumentation.

Do not ask questions. Do not assume that the globally issued document, the newest document, or the document with the strongest wording automatically defeats every other source. Distinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations.

SCENARIO

Northbridge Sterile Systems operates an Aachen manufacturing site. During a contamination incident in July 2026, procurement personnel bought replacement components under several partially conflicting normative sources.

Relevant activities are:
- Request_Purchase
- Emergency_Authorize
- Compliance_Clearance
- Legal_Clearance
- Commit_to_Supplier
- Create_PO
- Plant_Approve
- Finance_Approve
- Record_Emergency_Form
- Receive_Goods

NORMATIVE SOURCE A — GLOBAL PROCUREMENT AND INTEGRITY POLICY GPIP-4
Status: board-approved policy.
Effective: 2026-01-01 onward.
Scope: all Northbridge entities and purchases.

A1. A requester or person who commits the company to a supplier may not be the final business approver of the same purchase.
A2. A purchase of at least EUR 50,000 requires approval by both an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller before the supplier commitment.
A3. A purchase above EUR 100,000, or any purchase from a new supplier, requires Compliance_Clearance before supplier commitment.
A4. Emergency status waives competitive tendering only. Other controls remain unless a written exception is issued by the Chief Risk Officer under clause A7.
A5. Local procedures may impose stricter controls and may define operational evidence, provided they do not silently weaken GPIP-4.
A6. A later approval can mitigate consequences but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
A7. The Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped, time-limited exception. GPIP-4 does not state whether an incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander counts as an A7 exception.

NORMATIVE SOURCE B — AACHEN SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17, REVISION 6
Status: controlled local operating procedure.
Effective: 2025-03-01 onward.
Scope: maintenance and production-support purchases at the Aachen site.

B1. Create_PO must occur before Commit_to_Supplier.
B2. Purchases of at least EUR 25,000 require Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve.
B3. Only suppliers in the approved-supplier register may be used.
B4. An emergency is an imminent safety harm or a production stoppage expected within six hours. In an emergency, commitment may precede the PO and an absent Plant Manager may be substituted by an actively delegated Shift Lead.
B5. The emergency procedure does not expressly waive Finance_Approve or the approved-supplier rule.
B6. Record_Emergency_Form must occur within 24 hours of the commitment.
B7. The SOP says that a corporate incident directive may supersede “ordinary local workflow steps,” but it does not define which steps are ordinary.

NORMATIVE SOURCE C — EMERGENCY CONTINUITY DIRECTIVE ECD-2
Status: incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander.
Effective window: 2026-07-14 09:00 CEST through 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST.
Stated scope: “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.”

C1. A supplier commitment may precede Create_PO.
C2. A supplier outside the approved-supplier register may be used.
C3. Before commitment, two distinct members of the Incident Command group must jointly authorize the purchase. The authorization may initially be verbal but must be recorded within four hours.
C4. ECD-2 “takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements.”
C5. ECD-2 does not expressly mention GPIP-4 segregation of duties, Finance approval, Compliance clearance, the EUR 100,000 threshold, or the Chief Risk Officer.
C6. “Critical sterilization-line lots” is not defined. Operations treats backup-line components as critical; Internal Audit argues that only parts needed within the next 24 hours are covered.

NORMATIVE SOURCE D — CFO EMAIL, “NO DELAYS DURING ECD-2”
Sent: 2026-07-15 07:20 CEST.
Recipients: Aachen Procurement, Finance, Incident Command.
Status: ordinary email, not entered into the controlled policy repository.

D1. “During the ECD-2 window, joint Incident Command authorization is sufficient for all operational approvals up to EUR 150,000.”
D2. “For amounts above EUR 150,000, obtain Legal clearance as well.”
D3. The CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls, but no document states that the CFO may waive Compliance controls or GPIP-4 segregation of duties.
D4. A follow-up sentence says, “Use judgment; this is not meant to remove essential integrity controls.”

EVIDENCE NOTE — SYSTEM OUTAGE ON-44
The procurement ERP was unavailable from 2026-07-14 08:30 to 13:00 CEST. Events entered during recovery may have an ERP timestamp up to 12 minutes later than the real action. Telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, and supplier acknowledgments may therefore be better evidence for some times. The outage notice changes evidential weight; it is not itself a normative exception.

AVAILABLE BUT INCOMPLETE ORGANIZATIONAL FACTS
- Incident Command members may make operational decisions, but their charter does not explicitly call them budget owners.
- The CFO owns the Finance control framework and is a member of the executive committee.
- The COO can issue operational directives but GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer.
- The Chief Risk Officer was unavailable during the incident and did not later ratify ECD-2.
- The organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays, but not in a case involving new-supplier Compliance clearance.

Construct a defensible norm hierarchy and assess every case. Do not hide ambiguity behind a single numerical score. A classification must follow from an explicit interpretation of authority, scope, timing, process purpose, and evidence.

## 4. Case Assessments

Allowed primary classifications are: COMPLIANT, JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, NONCOMPLIANT, or INDETERMINATE. You may add a secondary classification, but you must choose one primary classification under your stated interpretation.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations
For at least four cases, state the strongest reasonable interpretation that would change the primary classification. Explain exactly which legal/process premise must change and why the alternative is credible or weak.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

QUALITY CONSTRAINTS
- Apply rules at the time of the relevant act, not only at case completion.
- Do not treat a late database timestamp as conclusive when stronger contemporaneous evidence exists.
- Do not treat post-hoc approval as automatically curative.
- Do not call all emergency purchases compliant or all deviations noncompliant.
- Make uncertainty visible and identify evidence that would reverse a conclusion.
