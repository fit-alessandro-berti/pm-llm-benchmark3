ROLE
You are the lead process-mining scientist for a multi-branch service organization evaluating an automated triage system.

OBJECTIVE
Assess what can reasonably be concluded about the effect of automation on process performance and customer outcomes when adoption was staggered, targeted to historically weak branches, and accompanied by demand, staffing, routing, and case-mix changes.

This is intentionally an open causal-policy task. There is no hidden requirement to produce a particular effect estimate or rollout decision. Your answer will be judged by another LLM for the quality of the estimands, identification logic, process interpretation, quantitative discipline, and treatment of uncertainty.

PROCESS AND MEASURES
A case normally follows one of these broad variants:
- manual fast path: `Register -> Manual Triage -> Resolve -> Close`
- manual extended path: `Register -> Manual Triage -> Specialist Review -> [optional Rework] -> Resolve -> Close`
- automated fast path: `Register -> Automated Triage -> Resolve -> Close`
- automated escalated path: `Register -> Automated Triage -> Specialist Review -> [optional Rework] -> Resolve -> Close`

Definitions:
- `automation=1` means the branch had the system available in that month; it does not mean every case was automated.
- `automated_fast_path_pct` is the percentage of all cases that actually completed through the automated fast path.
- `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` are branch-month cycle-time summaries.
- `rework_pct` is the percentage of cases containing a repeated review or information-request loop.
- `escalation_pct` is the percentage sent to specialist review.
- `csat_1to5` is a monthly survey mean. Response rates varied between roughly 20% and 45%, but branch-month response counts are unavailable.
- `high_complexity_pct` is based on the risk class recorded at registration.
- FTE counts are average productive full-time equivalents, not headcount.

ROLLOUT AND CO-OCCURRING EVENTS
- Alpha and Beta received automation on 2026-04-01 because they had high volumes and poor historical cycle times.
- Gamma received automation on 2026-07-01 after a short readiness pilot.
- Delta and Epsilon had not adopted by the end of August.
- In March, a centralized fraud desk began serving all branches. Automated triage sent a somewhat larger fraction of borderline cases to that desk, but branch-level fraud-desk waiting times were not recorded.
- From April onward, a routing campaign sent about 10% more incoming cases to Alpha and Beta. Gamma received about 5% more after July.
- Alpha lost two productive FTEs in May. Beta gained one in April. Gamma lost one in June. Delta gained one in June.
- A product change in May increased the proportion of high-complexity cases in several branches.
- Teams learned the automation gradually; system availability was high, but actual use is better represented by `automated_fast_path_pct`.
- No branch was randomly selected. Managers could also change local work practices during rollout.

POLICY QUESTION
Leadership is considering immediate expansion to Delta and Epsilon and wants a single statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%.” Determine whether such a statement is defensible, what narrower claims might be defensible, and what rollout policy would maximize both operational benefit and causal learning.

REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT
Use exactly the following top-level headings.

## 1. What is observed
Write 150-250 words that distinguish:
- availability of automation;
- actual use or treatment intensity;
- branch selection into rollout;
- contemporaneous operational changes;
- outcome measurement limitations.

Do not make a causal claim in this section.

## 2. Causal estimands
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | population | time horizon | outcome | unit of analysis | interpretation | identifiable from supplied data? |

Define at least six estimands, including:
- intention-to-treat-like effect of branch availability;
- effect of actual automated-fast-path use;
- effect on median cycle time;
- effect on tail performance;
- effect on rework or escalation;
- effect on customer satisfaction;
- at least one heterogeneous or dynamic effect.

Clarify interference and treatment-version issues where relevant.

## 3. Identification strategy
Propose one primary strategy and at least two credible alternatives. For each strategy, use a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |

Your primary strategy may use a modern staggered-adoption/event-study design, matched controls, synthetic control, interrupted time series, or another defensible design. Do not use a naïve two-way fixed-effects regression without discussing heterogeneous treatment effects and timing.

After the table, provide 150-250 words explaining why your primary strategy is preferable for these data.

## 4. Process-aware causal mechanisms
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| mechanism | process artifact or metric affected | expected signature if beneficial | expected signature if harmful | evidence in supplied data | unresolved ambiguity |

Cover at least:
- fast-path substitution;
- specialist-queue congestion;
- learning and implementation fidelity;
- case-mix or routing changes;
- staffing pressure;
- rework displacement;
- tail amplification;
- survey selection.

## 5. Quantitative evidence
Provide all of the following:
1. A compact table of branch-specific pre/post changes for each adopter, using transparent windows that you define.
2. At least one comparison against not-yet-treated or never-treated branches.
3. At least one analysis using treatment intensity rather than the binary adoption flag.
4. A discussion of pre-trends and whether any apparent effect could be regression to the mean.
5. A sensitivity analysis or bounded/ranged conclusion for at least two outcomes.

Approximate calculations are acceptable. Clearly separate arithmetic facts from causal interpretation. Do not collapse median and p90 behavior into a single “cycle-time effect.”

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |

Include at least ten threats. At minimum discuss:
selection into adoption, nonparallel trends, time-varying confounding, treatment intensity, spillovers through the fraud desk, staffing, case mix, anticipation, survey nonresponse, measurement aggregation, small number of branches, and dynamic treatment effects.

## 7. Effect assessment
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| outcome | qualitative effect assessment | plausible range or scenario interval | confidence 0-100 | strongest supporting evidence | strongest counterevidence | claim leadership may safely make |

The table must include `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`, `rework_pct`, `escalation_pct`, and `csat_1to5`.

Ranges may be qualitative or numeric, but must be justified. It is acceptable to conclude that an effect is not identified.

## 8. Rollout recommendation
Choose exactly one of:
- `expand immediately`;
- `expand with an embedded evaluation`;
- `pause and redesign`;
- `do not expand`.

Then write 250-400 words specifying:
- which branch or branch subset should be next;
- treatment assignment or phase-in mechanism;
- minimum pre-period and post-period measurements;
- process-mining outcomes and queue-state data to collect;
- spillover monitoring;
- guardrails for customer harm;
- a decision rule for scale-up.

## 9. Reproducible Python analysis
Provide one self-contained Python code block that embeds the branch-month CSV as a string and:
1. loads and validates the panel;
2. visualizes or prints branch trends for the five outcomes without using subplots;
3. computes transparent adopter-specific pre/post contrasts;
4. constructs relative-time indicators for staggered adoption;
5. estimates at least one clearly labeled descriptive panel model and one treatment-intensity model;
6. uses branch-clustered uncertainty where the chosen library permits it;
7. prints warnings about the very small number of clusters and about interpreting aggregated medians;
8. leaves all causal assumptions as explicit comments rather than claiming the regression proves causality.

Use Pandas and, if needed, Statsmodels or another common Python package. Do not fabricate raw event records and do not apply PM4Py discovery algorithms to branch-month aggregates as though they were event logs.

## 10. Final claim ladder
Provide exactly four bullet points, ordered from the strongest claim you believe is supported to the weakest or least defensible claim. Each bullet must begin with one of:
`Observed:`, `Suggestive:`, `Conditionally causal:`, or `Not supported:`.

EVALUATION EMPHASIS
A strong answer recognizes endogenous rollout, staggered timing, changing treatment intensity, small-cluster uncertainty, mixed median-versus-tail behavior, queue spillovers, and the difference between process substitution and genuine capacity creation. It should offer a decision that improves both operations and identifiability, not merely name a causal estimator.
