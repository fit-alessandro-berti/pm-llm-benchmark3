ROLE
You are designing an uncertainty-aware predictive process monitoring study. Your task is not to maximize a benchmark score through a convenient but biased cohort. Your answer will be judged for statistical design, process-aware cohort construction, leakage prevention, honest target semantics, and the quality of a PM4Py-oriented implementation skeleton. Several valid designs are possible when their assumptions and limitations are explicit.

BUSINESS QUESTION
A lender asks for an early warning model “predicting 90-day default from the first few events of every credit application.” The available historical training export, however, contains outcome labels only for activated accounts whose 90-day outcome has matured. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open applications were omitted from that export.

PROCESS
A simplified process is:
Application Started -> Identity/KYC Check -> Credit Review
From Credit Review, a case may:
- request documents and return to Credit Review one or more times;
- be Accepted, followed by Contract Signed and Account Activated;
- be Rejected and closed;
- be Withdrawn by the applicant;
- be Transferred to a partner or specialist platform;
- remain open at the data-extraction date.

The target currently stored in the warehouse is `default_90d`, defined only for activated accounts as whether a default occurred within 90 days after Account Activated. The external outcome feed commonly arrives another 7–35 days after the 90-day horizon.

DATA SNAPSHOT
- Event observation window: 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2025.
- Data extraction date: 15 February 2026.
- Applications started: 48,000.
- Cases in the current labeled ML export: 21,600, consisting of 18,900 non-default and 2,700 default accounts.
- Omitted from the labeled export:
  - 9,100 rejected applications;
  - 5,700 withdrawn applications;
  - 2,900 transferred applications;
  - 5,100 still-open applications;
  - 3,600 activated accounts whose 90-day outcome or delayed feed has not yet matured.
- Transferred cases can reappear under a new identifier on another platform. A linkage table exists for only about 60% of transfers.
- Partner-originated activated accounts have an elevated probability of a permanently missing outcome feed.

EVENT DATA SCHEMA
Assume a local CSV file named `applications_events.csv` with one row per event and these columns:
- `case:concept:name`
- `concept:name`
- `time:timestamp`
- `org:resource`
- `case:channel`
- `case:region`
- `case:requested_amount`
- `risk_score` — may be updated at several events
- `final_limit` — populated only after the final credit decision
- `decision_reason` — populated at or after Accept/Reject
- `collection_status` — updated after activation and may directly reveal default
- `default_90d` — repeated on events when known, otherwise missing
- `label_available_at`
- `source_system`
- `data_extract_time`

KNOWN CHANGES AND DATA RISKS
1. On 1 July 2025, credit-policy thresholds changed. The same observed prefix can therefore have a different selection probability and different outcome relationship before and after that date.
2. On 15 October 2025, a system migration renamed several activities and created occasional duplicate events.
3. The event file includes events after the prediction point, including Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, and Default Recorded.
4. A nightly snapshot feature called `documents_total` can include documents uploaded later on the same calendar day than the nominal prediction time.
5. Resource experience is stored as the employee’s experience at extraction time, not necessarily at the event time.
6. The current business request informally treats rejected applications as “non-default,” although they were never exposed to credit and therefore have no observed default outcome under activation.

TASK
Design a study that avoids misleading outcome estimates. You must confront the fact that “default for every application” is not directly observed and may not be well-defined without an activation policy or counterfactual assumptions.

Your design should consider at least two possible target formulations, such as:
- default among accounts that are eventually activated;
- a multi-state or competing-outcome target for all applications;
- time to default after activation with right censoring;
- a policy-specific counterfactual target, clearly labeled as requiring stronger assumptions.

Select one primary formulation and explain why it is operationally and statistically defensible. Do not label rejected, withdrawn, or open cases as non-default merely for convenience.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
Use exactly the following nine sections.

## 1. Target definition
Define the primary prediction target, prediction time, horizon, unit of analysis, population, and estimand. Then describe at least one credible alternative target and why it answers a different question.

## 2. Cohort construction
Give explicit inclusion, exclusion, and as-of rules. Include a cohort-flow table that accounts for all 48,000 applications and indicates which cases are labeled, censored, competing outcomes, selectively unobserved, or not yet mature.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy
Distinguish at least:
- ordinary right censoring;
- delayed labels;
- selection through activation;
- transfer as a competing event or missing follow-up mechanism;
- permanently missing outcome feeds.

Propose one primary analysis strategy and at least one sensitivity analysis. Explain which assumptions are testable, partially testable, or fundamentally untestable from these data.

## 4. Prefix extraction
Specify how prefixes will be generated without looking into the future. Address both fixed event-count prefixes and elapsed-time prefixes. Explain how short cases, repeated document loops, and activity renaming will be handled.

## 5. Process-aware features
Propose process-aware features available at prediction time, including control-flow, timing, resource/queue, rework, and cohort/calendar features. Distinguish static case attributes, prefix-derived features, and features that require historical reference data.

## 6. Leakage audit
Provide a table with columns:
`Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale`

The table must discuss at least `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, updated `risk_score`, resource experience, and label availability.

## 7. Validation design
Design a realistic temporal validation and backtesting scheme. Address policy change, system migration, label maturity, calibration, subgroup performance, and distribution shift. Explain why a random case split can be misleading here. Include evaluation metrics appropriate to the chosen target and censoring strategy.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary
State what the study can and cannot claim. Distinguish predictive association, selection effects, and counterfactual default risk. Include at least three ways deployment could change the data-generating process.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton
Provide one coherent Python code block, approximately 80–160 lines, that demonstrates the data-engineering and audit pipeline. It does not need to fit a production prediction model, but it must be concrete enough to implement. Use only APIs that are appropriate to the described data.

The code should, where useful:
- read the CSV with `pandas.read_csv` and parse timestamps;
- call `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the supplied case, activity, and timestamp columns;
- build case-level start, activation, terminal, censoring, and label-maturity information as of a configurable cutoff;
- create a time-based train/validation/test split by case start time rather than blindly relying on a random split;
- demonstrate `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` for fixed-length prefixes;
- show how to prevent post-prefix columns and events from entering the feature table;
- use selected functions such as `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.get_variants`, `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, or `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` only when their interpretation is justified;
- produce cohort counts and a leakage-audit check;
- include comments marking assumptions and places where survival/competing-risk modeling would be connected later.

You may mention `pm4py.split_train_test` as a convenience baseline, but the primary validation implementation must remain temporal. Do not fabricate a PM4Py function for survival modeling.

QUALITY CRITERIA
Strong answers recognize that activation creates selective outcome observation, preserve an as-of perspective, and separate ordinary censoring from outcomes that are undefined under the observed policy. Weak answers train only on the 21,600 labeled rows without qualification, call every omitted case a non-default, use final or post-outcome fields, or apply a random split across policy and migration periods.
