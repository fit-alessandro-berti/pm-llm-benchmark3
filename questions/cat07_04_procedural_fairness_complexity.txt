Resource assignment:
- Group A cases are spread across 52 caseworkers.
- Group B cases are disproportionately assigned to 7 bilingual caseworkers and 3 overloaded regional officers.
- Group C cases are disproportionately assigned to 5 accommodation specialists, two of whom also do supervisor review.
- Group D cases are assigned to the fraud-review queue more often, consistent with higher measured risk_score_band.
- In two regions, Group B applicants have more handovers between general caseworker → bilingual caseworker → general caseworker, while Group C applicants have more handovers between general caseworker → accommodation specialist → supervisor.
- The policy states that language support and accommodation review are rights-preserving steps, but service-level targets do not include separate timeliness thresholds for those steps.

Qualitative policy context:
- Legitimate routing: higher risk_score_band requires Fraud Review; disability accommodation requests require Accommodation Review; minority-language requests require Language Support Contact.
- Potential fairness concern: rights-preserving support steps may create extra waiting time and extra handovers. Applicants using those steps may receive slower or more burdensome procedures even after controlling for documented complexity.
- Data limitations: risk_score_band may itself encode historical disparities; documentation_completeness may be affected by language barriers; case_outcome does not capture quality of explanation or dignity of treatment; resource workload is observed only at daily granularity.

QUESTION

Assess procedural fairness after controlling for process complexity. Do not focus only on equal approval rates. Explain what can and cannot be inferred from the evidence.
