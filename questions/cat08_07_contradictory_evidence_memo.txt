You are preparing an executive decision memo for a telecom provider's fiber-installation process. Process-mining evidence points in conflicting directions. A recent redesign reduced median duration and handovers, but tail duration, rework, and model precision worsened. Management must decide whether to scale the redesign, roll it back, run targeted experiments, or invest in more measurement.

There is no single hidden ground-truth answer. Your answer will be judged by an LLM-as-a-judge for strategic synthesis, clear separation of facts and interpretations, explanation of metric conflicts, value-of-information reasoning, and ability to make a defensible decision despite incomplete evidence.

Question:
Prepare a decision memo recommending what management should do next.

Process context:
Cases are customer fiber-installation orders. The core process is:
Order received -> Address validation -> Appointment scheduling -> Field survey if needed -> Installation visit -> Activation test -> Billing start.
Exceptions include permit check, customer reschedule, failed visit, second visit, manual activation, and complaint escalation.

Management recently piloted Redesign R:
- A single "installation coordinator" owns each case after address validation.
- Address validation and appointment scheduling were merged for simple cases.
- Field survey was skipped for some medium-risk addresses using a predictive rule.
- A smaller number of handovers was expected to improve accountability.
- Some activation checks were moved after billing start if the network signal looked acceptable.

Data coverage:
- Baseline period: 10 weeks, 24,000 cases.
- Pilot period: 6 weeks, 9,200 cases across four regions.
- Pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings than the baseline average.
- Customer-segment and building-risk attributes are incomplete before week 3 of the pilot.
- Cost data excludes downstream complaint-handling after week 4.
- The event log records completion timestamps; start timestamps are sparse.
- Process model discovered from pilot has high fitness but low precision.

Process-discovery and conformance findings:
- Baseline discovered model: fitness 0.87, precision 0.79, generalization 0.70.
- Pilot discovered model: fitness 0.94, precision 0.51, generalization 0.56.
- Interpretation offered by the process-mining team: Pilot R fits the observed pilot cases better because many cases follow the simplified coordinator path, but the model allows too many combinations of skipped survey, manual activation, second visit, and billing start.
- Alignment diagnostics in pilot:
  - frequent move-on-log: Billing start before successful Activation test
  - frequent move-on-log: Installation visit repeated without preceding Field survey
  - frequent move-on-model: expected Permit check not observed for older apartment buildings
  - frequent move-on-log: Complaint escalation after Billing start
- Temporal profile deviations:
  - Address validation -> Appointment scheduling improved for simple houses.
  - Installation visit -> Activation test worsened for apartments.
  - Billing start -> Complaint escalation became a new long-tail relation.
  - Failed visit -> Second visit delay increased in regions with high coordinator utilization.

Resource and organizational findings:
- Coordinator ownership reduced handovers, but coordinator utilization reached 93% in two pilot regions.
- Field-survey team utilization fell from 78% to 52%, but second-visit team utilization rose from 66% to 92%.
- Activation specialists report more manual exceptions and unclear ownership after billing start.
- Customer service complaints shifted from "waiting for appointment" to "installed but not working" and "billing before activation".
- The handover-of-work network became simpler, but the working-together network became more centralized around coordinators and activation specialists.
- Reduced handovers may have reduced resilience: when a coordinator was absent, cases stalled because context was not shared.

Decision alternatives:
A. Scale Redesign R nationally.
B. Roll back to the baseline process.
C. Scale only the coordinator-ownership part, but suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation.
D. Run a controlled regional experiment with better segment/risk measurement.
E. Hybrid: deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, and run measurement before wider scale.

Potential next measurements or experiments:
M1. Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum.
M2. Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation.
M3. Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation, and Second visit.
M4. Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start.
M5. Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence.
M6. Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes.
M7. Run conformance monitoring for Billing start before Activation test and Permit check absence.
M8. Collect customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" and "working service after installation".

Required output format:
Use Markdown. Produce exactly the following top-level sections.

1. Executive memo
Write an executive memo of at most 700 words. It must recommend what management should do next and explain the trade-off. The memo should be decision-oriented, not a generic summary.

2. Evidence table

- assumptions that remain uncertain

3. Explanation of metric conflicts

- high fitness but low precision
- improved median but worse tail duration
- fewer handovers but lower resilience
- lower visible cost but higher rework or downstream cost
- improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings

4. Decision alternatives
Evaluate alternatives A-E. Include advantages, risks, and conditions under which each would be reasonable.

5. Value-of-information analysis
Rank the potential next measurements or experiments M1-M8 by decision value. Explain which uncertainty each resolves and how it could change the decision.

6. Final recommendation
State the recommended decision clearly. Include:
- immediate action
- what to pause
- what to scale
- what to measure next
- what threshold or evidence would justify broader rollout

Constraints:
- Do not pretend one intervention dominates all metrics.
- Do not recommend national scaling without addressing the worsened tail, rework, and compliance-like activation issues.
- Do not roll back solely because some metrics worsened; segment-specific benefits matter.
- Do not merge facts, interpretations, and assumptions.
- Do not ignore the process-mining artifacts: fitness, precision, alignments, temporal profile, DFG changes, social/resource networks, and segment outcomes.
