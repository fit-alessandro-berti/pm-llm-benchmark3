You are advising a bank that wants to automate parts of its small-business loan-origination process. Senior management initially wants to automate the highest-frequency activity, but process-mining evidence suggests that frequency alone is misleading. Some lower-frequency activities create synchronization delays, rework, or risk exposure that dominate end-to-end performance.

There is no single hidden ground-truth answer. Your answer will be judged by an LLM-as-a-judge for portfolio reasoning, dependency analysis, process-level thinking, and ability to account for exceptions, controls, and shifted resource demand.

Question:
Prioritize an automation portfolio while accounting for process dependencies, exception handling, control risks, and changed resource demand.

Process context:
Cases are loan applications. The process normally includes:
Receive application -> Register application -> KYC check -> Financial document review -> Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation if required -> Committee decision -> Contract preparation -> Customer signing -> Loan activation.
Applicants are Micro, SME, and High-exposure SME. Products are Working-capital, Equipment finance, or Credit-line renewal. Some activities are partly automated but still require human exception handling.

Control findings:
- Four-eyes principle: Credit analysis and Committee packaging should not be completed by the same person in High-exposure SME cases. Violations occur in 5.8% of High-exposure cases during month-end rush.
- KYC clearance must precede Customer signing. Provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases; 1.1% required remediation.
- Contract terms must match Committee decision. Contract-remediation loops are 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal.
- Automation must preserve an auditable trail of model suggestion, human override, and final decision.

Budget and rollout constraints:
- Budget supports at most four automation modules in the first year.
- Compliance insists that at least one module in the first wave strengthen controls, not only reduce labor.
- IT can integrate only two modules deeply with core banking in the first six months.
- Relationship managers will resist changes that increase customer friction.
- A simple high-frequency automation win is politically attractive, but management has asked for a process-level recommendation.
