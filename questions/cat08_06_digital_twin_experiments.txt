You are advising a municipality that wants to build a simulation-based digital twin for its building-permit process. The goal is not merely to replay historical cases but to compare interventions under non-stationary demand, resource calendars, rework, dependencies between cases, and policy changes. The process-mining team has provided an event-log summary, a process model, empirical timing summaries, resource calendars, routing rules, and known data gaps.

There is no single hidden ground-truth answer. Your answer will be judged by an LLM-as-a-judge for simulation-design reasoning, process-mining grounding, experiment planning, uncertainty treatment, and the realism of assumptions. The judge will not run your code for exact correctness, but code should be plausible and consistent with PM4Py conventions.

Question:
Design a simulation-based digital twin for comparing process interventions.

Process context:
Cases are building-permit applications. Case types are Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, and Complex variance. Applicants submit documents through a portal. Some cases interact because the same architect may submit multiple applications, neighborhood objections can affect several related cases, and inspector site visits can be grouped geographically.

The nominal process:
Submit application -> Completeness check -> Request missing documents if needed -> Planning review -> Fire-safety review if required -> Environmental review if required -> Public-notice period if required -> Inspection scheduling -> Site inspection -> Decision drafting -> Legal check -> Issue permit or reject.
Rework can occur after Planning review, Inspection, Legal check, or applicant appeal.

Simplified process tree string:
->(
  'Submit application',
  *(
    ->(
      'Completeness check',
      X(
        tau,
        ->('Request missing documents','Receive missing documents')
      )
    ),
    'Completeness rework'
  ),
  +(
    'Planning review',
    X(tau,'Fire-safety review'),
    X(tau,'Environmental review')
  ),
  X(tau,'Public-notice period'),
  *(
    ->('Inspection scheduling','Site inspection'),
    'Inspection rework'
  ),
  'Decision drafting',
  X(tau,'Legal check'),
  X('Issue permit','Reject application')
)

Event-log summary, 18 months:
- Cases: 31,200
- Case mix: Simple renovation 45%, New residential 28%, Commercial 17%, Complex variance 10%
- Demand is non-stationary: March-May and September-November have 1.7x the arrival rate of January-February.
- Monday portal submissions are 2.2x Wednesday submissions.
- Large architects create bursts: top 20 architect_id values submit 31% of cases and often submit multiple related applications in the same week.
- Districts D3 and D5 have longer inspection travel times.
- Neighborhood objection events are not fully captured, but public-notice duration is much longer when objections occur.
- Some resources record only completion timestamps, not start timestamps.
- Calendar interruptions such as public holidays and council meetings affect Legal check and Public-notice period.

Resource calendars:
- Clerks: 5 FTE, Monday-Friday 08:00-16:00, one clerk dedicated to portal support on Mondays.
- Planners: 8 FTE, Monday-Friday 09:00-17:00; two planners only handle Simple renovation.
- Fire reviewers: 2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday-Thursday only.
- Environmental reviewers: 3 FTE, Monday-Friday, but 40% of time is reserved for non-permit duties.
- Inspectors: 4 FTE, field visits Tuesday-Friday; travel time depends on district; district batching is possible.
- Legal officers: 2 FTE, no work during council meeting weeks except urgent cases.
- System tasks: effectively unlimited capacity.
- Applicant and third-party response times must be modeled separately from municipal service times.

Known routing and dependency rules:
- Fire-safety review required when requires_fire=yes or building area exceeds a threshold not fully available in the log.
- Environmental review required when requires_environment=yes or district is environmentally sensitive; sensitivity data is incomplete.
- Public-notice period required for Complex variance and some New residential cases; objections can extend it.
- Decision drafting should wait for Planning, Fire, Environmental, Public-notice, and Inspection results that apply to the case.
- Related cases from the same architect and district can be bundled for inspection scheduling, reducing travel time but increasing waiting for the first case in the bundle.
- A policy change under consideration would allow Simple renovation permits to skip Site inspection if application_value < EUR 50,000 and no prior violations exist.
- Another policy would create a pre-submission document checker, reducing missing-doc loops but increasing applicant effort before submission.
- Another intervention would add one temporary inspector in peak months only.

Known data gaps and biases:
- Start timestamps are missing for many human activities; waiting/service split is partly inferred.
- Objection events are missing; Public-notice duration is used as a proxy.
- Rework reasons are unstructured text and not reliably coded.
- Some rare emergency decisions are removed from the log.
- Resource calendars were reconstructed from HR data, not event logs.
- The log is flattened by case and may underrepresent dependencies between related applications.

Candidate interventions to compare:
D1. Pre-submission document checker.
D2. Simple-renovation no-inspection rule for low-risk applications.
D3. District-batched inspection scheduling.
D4. Temporary peak-month inspector.
D5. Parallelize legal pre-check for Commercial and Complex variance cases.
D6. Reserve one planner each day for aging cases above a threshold.
D7. Architect-facing appointment slots to smooth Monday submission bursts.
D8. Environmental-review triage using district and project attributes.
D9. Public-notice objection early-warning process.
D10. Combined policy: D1 + D2 + D3 + D4.
