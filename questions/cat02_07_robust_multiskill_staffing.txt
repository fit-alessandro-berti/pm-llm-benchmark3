BENCHMARK CONTEXT
This is an open-ended process-mining benchmark task. There is deliberately no hidden reference answer and no single required numerical result. Your response will be evaluated by an LLM-as-a-judge for analytical depth, internal consistency, use of the supplied evidence, treatment of uncertainty, process-mining competence, and adherence to the requested output format.

General rules:
- Use only the information in this prompt. Do not invent observations that are not supplied.
- You may introduce assumptions, but label each one and explain why it is needed.
- Distinguish observations, estimates, bounds, hypotheses, and recommendations.
- Where several interpretations are defensible, compare them rather than forcing false certainty.
- Exact numerical agreement is less important than correct definitions, coherent calculations, and defensible uncertainty handling.
- Any Python must be self-contained: embed or parse the data shown in this prompt, use pandas for tabular work, and use PM4Py only where its semantics fit the task.


SCENARIO
A technical incident process has this control flow:

`Intake -> diagnostic work -> Validation`

Diagnostic work depends on case type:
- M: Mechanical only
- E: Electrical only
- S: Software only
- ME: Mechanical and Electrical branches, which may run in parallel on different resources or sequentially on one cross-skilled resource
- ES: Electrical and Software branches, with the same parallel/sequential choice

All cases require Intake and Validation. Priority P1 cases may preempt a lower-priority diagnostic activity, but an interrupted activity incurs a restart penalty equal to 10% of its remaining active service time. A case may be preempted at most once. Priority targets are:
- P1: at least 90% complete within 120 elapsed minutes;
- P2: at least 90% complete within 8 elapsed hours;
- P3: at least 90% complete within 24 elapsed hours.

COORDINATION AND HANDOVER RULES
- If Intake, a diagnostic activity, or Validation changes resource, add 7 minutes of handover delay.
- If a case moves between the mechanical team and the electrical/software team, add a further 5 minutes unless the same cross-skilled resource retains the case.
- If one resource switches diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases, add a 5-minute setup time.
- Parallel ME or ES branches add 8 minutes of coordination before Validation; sequential execution avoids that coordination delay but can lengthen the critical path.
- Overtime is limited to 30 minutes per resource.
- In the base scenario, planned utilization above 90% for any individual resource is considered unfair and operationally fragile.

| U3 Absence | Chen is absent for the full day |
| U4 Long-tail day | complex-tail probabilities for all diagnostic activities double |

POLICY SPACE
At minimum compare:
1. strict specialization with fixed queues;
2. full pooling to any qualified resource using a transparent dispatch rule;
3. a hybrid policy with reserved P1 capacity and controlled cross-skill overflow.
You may add policies such as shortest expected remaining processing time, threshold-based parallelization, dynamic skill reservation, or workload-balancing with fairness penalties.

ANALYTICAL TASK
Compare at least three concrete staffing/routing policies and recommend a robust policy. “Robust” must be defined across the supplied uncertainty scenarios, not only by base-case mean delay. Multiple recommendations can be defensible if their objective functions differ.

Your reasoning should integrate:
- approximate offered load by skill and time block;
- resource-specific speed and availability;
- parallel versus sequential branch trade-offs;
- handover/setup/preemption penalties;
- mean and tail delay, SLA attainment, utilization, fairness, and resilience;
- bottleneck migration under shocks;
- practical dispatch rules that operators can understand and audit.
