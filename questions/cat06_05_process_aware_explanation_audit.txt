ROLE
You are auditing explanations from a predictive process monitoring model. The model predicts, immediately after `Coverage Check Completed`, whether an insurance claim will breach a 10-day settlement target. You must distinguish temporal leakage, process impossibility, mutable feature lineage, correlated proxies, legitimate predictive signals, and unsupported causal stories. There is no single exact set of accepted features; your answer will be judged for the quality of the audit and the credibility of the revised explanations.

NORMATIVE PROCESS
A simplified claim process is:
1. Claim Registered.
2. Coverage Check Completed.
3. Either:
   - low-complexity fast track: Damage Assessment Completed; or
   - one or more evidence cycles: Documents Requested -> Documents Received -> Documents Validated, with another Documents Requested event possible.
4. Damage Assessment Completed.
5. A Fraud Investigation may be opened after a trigger and may run in parallel with evidence collection or damage assessment.
6. Settlement Decision Recorded: Approve, Partial, Deny, or Withdrawn.
7. An Appeal may occur only after Partial or Deny. An appeal can lead to another Settlement Decision Recorded event.
8. Claim Closed follows a final decision or withdrawal.

Important semantic qualifications
- A coarse activity label `Settlement Decision Recorded` can occur twice when an appeal causes reconsideration.
- Events with identical timestamps may be ordered incorrectly by the source system unless a reliable sequence index is used.
- The prediction time `t0` is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed` event. Only information available at or before `t0` is admissible.
- Predictive importance does not establish a causal effect or an operationally actionable reason.

FEATURE LINEAGE NOTES
- `total_case_duration_hours` is calculated after the case closes.
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, and `full_trace_variant_id` are calculated from the complete trace.
- `final_handler_team` is a warehouse field populated at closure. In about 65% of claims it happens to equal the team assigned at `t0`, but the warehouse does not retain a historical availability timestamp.
- `queue_length_at_t0` is joined from a timestamped queue snapshot and is normally valid within a ±5 minute tolerance.
- `initial_damage_complexity` is entered during registration and frozen before `t0`.
- `calendar_week` is known at `t0`, but an operating-policy change and a holiday period are strongly aligned with certain weeks.
- `claimant_region` is known at registration. Region is correlated with repair-network coverage, language, channel, and socioeconomic characteristics.
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is derived only from past timestamps.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is intended to use only earlier claims, but the current SQL uses `claim_created_at <= extraction_date` rather than `claim_created_at < t0`.
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot` is refreshed nightly at 23:00 and can include documents uploaded after `t0` on the same day.
- Employee experience is joined as of extraction date, not event date, although it is not shown in the top-12 global ranking.

TASK
Audit the explanations and construct more credible process-aware explanations. You must distinguish:
- information that is unavailable at prediction time;
- information that is temporally available but process-inconsistent;
- information that is predictive but should not be presented causally;
- features that may be valid after lineage repair;
- legitimate prefix-derived process signals.

Do not simply delete every correlated or sensitive feature. Explain the different standards for predictive validity, causal interpretation, operational actionability, and fairness review.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
Use exactly the following five sections.

## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings
Provide a table with columns:
`Item | Finding type | Severity (critical/high/medium/low) | Why | Required remediation`

Cover every global top-12 feature, every sequence pattern, and every local-only feature shown for X, Y, and Z.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks
Describe a concrete validation suite that checks:
- prefix-time availability;
- activity-order feasibility;
- same-timestamp ambiguity;
- whether a feature uses complete-trace information;
- whether an explanation refers to an activity that is enabled, impossible, or merely future-possible at `t0`;
- whether repeated Settlement Decision events are semantically legitimate after appeal.

You may refer to alignment-, footprint-, prefix-, DFG-, or declarative checks, but explain their role and limitations. A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule.

QUALITY CRITERIA
Strong answers notice both obvious leakage and subtler lineage defects, preserve legitimate congestion and prefix-age signals, treat `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` differently from the impossible-looking same-timestamp pattern, and separate “predictive” from “causal” or “actionable.” Weak answers accept full-trace SHAP values as early explanations or declare every correlated feature causal.
