ROLE
You are advising two organizations that execute nominally similar claims processes. Site A reports a large improvement after allowing identity and document verification to proceed in parallel. Site B is considering copying the intervention.

OBJECTIVE
Assess whether the observed effect at Site A is transportable to Site B, which differs in case mix, legislation, resource specialization, document channels, queueing, and IT constraints.

The evidence does not identify a unique transport effect. Your answer will be evaluated by another LLM for analogical reasoning grounded in process structure, causal transportability, recognition of co-interventions, quantitative scenario analysis, model fidelity, and pilot design.

SITE A PROCESS
Before the intervention, Site A used this process tree:

```text
-> (
    'Register',
    'Identity Check',
    'Document Check',
    'Assessment',
    X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ),
    'Notify'
)
```

After the intervention, the intended model became:

```text
-> (
    'Register',
    + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ),
    'Assessment',
    X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ),
    'Notify'
)
```

Site A context:
- About 90% of documents are digital at registration.
- Identity and document checks use mostly independent external data.
- Reviewers are cross-trained and may perform both checks.
- The case-management system supports simultaneous task ownership and event timestamps.
- A local rule limits each reviewer to six open verification tasks.
- High-value cases do not require a separate legal review.
- The intervention was introduced to all teams within two weeks; there was no randomized control.

- Managers also introduced daily queue huddles.
- No convincing negative-control process was tracked.
- The observed overlap measure may partly reflect improved timestamp recording.

SITE B CURRENT PROCESS AND CONSTRAINTS
Site B follows these rules:
1. `Register` occurs first.
2. `Identity Check` may begin after registration.
3. `Originals Receipt` may also begin after registration.
4. `Document Check` cannot begin until original documents are received when originals are legally required.
5. `Assessment` requires both identity and document checks to be complete.
6. Public-sector or high-value cases require `Legal Review`.
7. Legal review may issue `Request Supplement`, after which document checking and assessment may need to be repeated.
8. A final `Approve` or `Reject` decision is followed by `Notify`.

Site B context:
- Original documents are legally required for 62% of cases.
- Public-sector or high-value cases are 40% of volume.
- The legal requirement is stricter than at Site A.
- Identity, document, assessment, and legal work are separate roles.
- Productive pools are: 4 identity reviewers, 7 document reviewers, 3 assessors, and 2 legal reviewers.
- The legacy case system normally locks a case to one active task owner, although teams sometimes work around the lock and enter events later.
- External originals arrive with a highly variable delay.
- Site B has more complex cases and a heavier tail.
- Management can pilot changes but cannot remove statutory ordering constraints.
