ROLE
You are an expert in predictive process monitoring, conformance checking, operational decision support, and compliance-aware counterfactual reasoning. Rank actions for a live international-payment case. An action that looks operationally beneficial may be unacceptable because it violates the process or a mandatory control. There is no single exact utility function; your answer will be judged for integrating prediction, conformance, resource spillovers, and uncertainty.

NORMATIVE PAYMENT PROCESS
1. Payment Registered.
2. Sanctions Screening Completed.
3. If there is no alert and the risk score is below 40, Auto Clear may lead directly to Payment Executed.
4. Otherwise, Analyst Review is mandatory.
5. The analyst may request evidence. The cycle is Evidence Requested -> Evidence Received -> Evidence Validated -> Analyst Review, and it may repeat.
6. A risk score of at least 70, or any unresolved fuzzy sanctions match, requires Second Officer Review before Approve or Payment Executed.
7. The analyst performing the first review and the second officer must be different people (four-eyes principle).
8. After the required review(s), the case may be Approved, Rejected, or held while a Suspicious Activity Report is filed. Filing a report does not itself authorize payment execution.
9. Payment Executed is conformant only after a valid approval path.
10. An emergency manual override is permitted only when a Compliance Director authorizes it and a valid incident identifier is recorded. Neither condition is currently satisfied.
11. A Suspicious Activity Report must not be disclosed to the customer.

LIVE CASE P-884
Observed prefix:
Payment Registered -> Sanctions Screening Completed -> Analyst Review Completed -> Evidence Requested -> Evidence Received -> Evidence Validated

Current context
- Amount: €480,000
- Destination: established supplier in a sanctioned-border region
- Initial risk score: 78
- Current risk score after evidence validation: 72, but the score timestamp is 12 minutes old
- Fuzzy sanctions-name match confidence: 0.46; unresolved
- First analyst: R17
- Customer deadline: 90 minutes from now
- Estimated value of executing within the deadline: €20,000–€35,000 in avoided operational loss
- Estimated customer harm from a false rejection or prolonged hold: €15,000–€80,000
- Potential regulatory/compliance harm from an unlawful execution: highly asymmetric, plausibly €100,000–€500,000 plus non-monetary consequences
- Prediction under “continue normal processing”: SLA breach probability 0.48–0.64
- Prediction of ultimate approval after a conformant review: 0.55–0.75
- Prediction estimates are observational and may not transport unchanged under intervention.

RESOURCE STATE
- One eligible second officer, S2, is immediately available only by interrupting case P-901.
- P-901 is another high-value payment with a 70-minute deadline. Interrupting it for 45 minutes is estimated to increase P-901’s breach probability by 0.12–0.28.
- Another second officer, S4, is expected to become available in 35–65 minutes.
- The alternate screening vendor has three expedited API calls left today.
- Compliance Director response time is uncertain: 20–60 minutes if reachable. A genuine incident identifier would still need to be created and justified.

AVAILABLE ACTIONS

Action A — Execute payment immediately
- Time: 10–15 minutes
- Direct effect: sharply reduces P-884’s deadline-breach risk
- Constraint issue: no Second Officer Review despite risk >=70 and unresolved fuzzy match

Action B — Let R17 self-certify the second review
- Time: 5–10 minutes
- Direct effect: similar speed advantage
- Constraint issue: violates the four-eyes principle

Action C — Interrupt P-901 and request expedited review by S2
- Time for review: 35–75 minutes, then 5–15 minutes to record approval/rejection and, if approved, execute
- Constraint status: conformant if S2 independently reviews the evidence
- Spillover: increases risk for P-901

Action D — Run alternate sanctions screening, then obtain a second-officer review
- Alternate screening: 15–30 minutes
- It may reduce uncertainty or reveal a false match, but it does not substitute for the mandatory second review while the current conditions remain
- Second review can be by S2 or S4
- Uses one scarce API call

Action E — Request additional customer evidence
- Time: 6–24 hours
- Constraint status: conformant
- Benefit: may resolve the fuzzy match and reduce false-clear risk
- Harm: almost guarantees the current deadline will be missed

Action F — Reject now with documented unresolved-match rationale
- Time: 10–20 minutes
- Constraint status: potentially conformant if the documented evidence threshold for rejection is satisfied; the prompt does not establish this with certainty
- Benefit: controls unlawful-execution risk
- Harm: potentially large false-rejection cost

Action G — Seek an emergency override
- Time to obtain the required director authorization and incident identifier: 20–60 minutes or longer
- Constraint status: not currently enabled; it becomes a justified exception only if both requirements are actually satisfied
- Operational risk: may consume the remaining time without producing a valid override

TASK
Rank the next-best actions or action plans. You may rank a composite plan such as “alternate screening in parallel with waiting for S4, then second review,” but make the sequence, concurrency, and fallback explicit. Each recommendation must be either process-conformant or clearly identified as a currently unavailable exception path. Do not present a nonconformant shortcut as acceptable merely because its expected operational loss is lower.

For counterfactual reasoning, distinguish:
- what the process would permit;
- the likely continuation after the action;
- direct benefit to P-884;
- harm to P-884;
- spillover to P-901 or other cases;
- compliance and non-monetary risk;
- uncertainty about intervention effects.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
Use exactly the following six sections.

## 1. Ranked action table
Rank all Actions A through G. Use columns:
`Rank | Action or composite plan | Conformance status | Earliest plausible continuation | Expected benefit range | Expected harm range | Resource spillover | Confidence`

Use one of these conformance labels exactly:
- `CONFORMANT NOW`
- `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`
- `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT`
- `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`

## 2. Counterfactual continuations
For at least the top five ranked entries, provide a compact continuation trace or branching tree. Identify the likely approval, rejection, hold, report, or execution outcomes and the main uncertainty at each branch.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning
Explain how you compare deadline loss, false rejection/hold, regulatory exposure, and asymmetric non-monetary harm. Use ranges and dominance arguments where exact monetization is inappropriate.

## 4. Conformance constraints
Map every action to the relevant mandatory rules. Explicitly explain why Actions A and B cannot become recommended solely through a favorable numerical forecast. Explain what evidence would be required for Action F or G to become valid.

## 5. Resource spillovers
Analyze the S2-versus-S4 choice, the effect on P-901, the alternate-screening API quota, and the risk of consuming time while waiting for an exception. State how the ranking would change if S4 becomes available in 35 minutes versus 65 minutes.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence
Give one primary recommendation, one fallback, a confidence level, and the next event or observation that should trigger immediate reranking.

QUALITY CRITERIA
Strong answers usually favor a conformant second-review strategy, but they need not choose the same operational implementation. They explicitly rank fast but unlawful shortcuts low, consider parallel information gathering, and account for P-901. Weak answers optimize only P-884’s predicted deadline loss or treat a report, re-screen, or self-certification as a substitute for mandatory independent review.
