Historical handover-cost evidence:
- Cases with >2 reassignments have 1.8x higher rework rate and 1.4x higher appeal rate.
- Changing owner on high-complexity cases adds a median 2.7 hours of waiting.
- Keeping the current owner despite weak skill adds a median 3.3 hours of service time and increases rework.
- Specialist-only routing reduces immediate errors but creates queues in Security and Approval.
- First-available routing reduces average waiting time but increases reassignments and skill mismatch.

Fairness and resilience constraints:
1. No resource should receive more than 4 new assignments in the current decision batch.
2. No high-priority case should be assigned to a skill level below 2 unless supervised.
3. At least 20% of normal/low-priority assignments should support documented training priorities, if SLA risk remains acceptable.
4. Avoid assigning more work to resources with overtime risk="high" unless SLA risk is urgent and no safer alternative exists.
5. For high-complexity cases with high handover cost, continuity should be preferred unless the current owner has skill 0 or 1 and no supervision is feasible.
6. Security Review must be performed by skill level 3, or by skill level 1–2 only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.
7. Final Approval must be performed by skill level 2–3. Skill level 1 may prepare but not finalize.
8. Quality Check may be batched only when priority is normal/low and SLA time remaining >24h.

Baseline policies to compare:
- First-available routing: assign each case to the available resource with the lowest current open cases and enough hours remaining, ignoring specialization except skill 0.
- Specialist-only routing: assign each case to the highest skill resource for that required skill, breaking ties by current open cases.
- Proposed explainable dynamic policy: you design it.

QUESTION

Design an explainable resource-assignment policy and compare it with first-available and specialist-only routing. The answer should not claim a single obvious optimum; it should reason about trade-offs.

REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT

Use Markdown. Your answer must contain exactly these top-level headings:

# 1. Objectives and constraints
Define the optimization objectives and hard/soft constraints. Separate safety/compliance constraints from preference trade-offs.

# 2. Assignment policy
Give an algorithm or pseudocode. It must include:
- eligibility filtering by skill and rule constraints
- SLA urgency scoring
- skill-match scoring
- continuity/handover-cost scoring
- workload and overtime fairness scoring
- training/development scoring
- resilience or bottleneck avoidance
- tie-breaking and exception handling

# 3. Recommended assignment for the current queue
Provide a table assigning each case C001–C016 to a resource or supervised pair. Include:
- assignment
- main reason
- trade-off accepted
- whether it supports training
- risk to monitor

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies
Compare your policy with first-available and specialist-only routing on:
- expected throughput
- SLA risk
- error/rework risk
- fairness of workload
- skill development
- continuity of case ownership
- resilience to absence or bottlenecks

# 5. Explanation to workers
Write a transparent explanation that could be shown to workers. It should explain why assignments are made without sounding punitive or overly algorithmic.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators
Define indicators for auditing the deployed policy using event logs. Include at least:
- handover frequency
- reassignment count
- skill mismatch
- workload distribution
- overtime risk exposure
- SLA misses
- rework/appeal after assignment
- training assignment share
- specialist bottleneck indicators
- deviations from assignment policy

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch
Provide Python code or pseudocode showing how event-log data could be used to learn or audit the policy. Mention PM4Py functions for resource networks, role discovery/similarity, service/waiting times, performance DFGs, and filtering by case attributes.

CONSTRAINTS

- Do not optimize only for throughput.
- Do not assign high-priority work to trainees without supervision.
- Do not overload high-overtime-risk resources unless justified.
- Do not ignore continuity and handover costs.
- Do not treat the policy as purely mathematical; include socio-technical and explainability considerations.
