You are asked to forecast the process-mining impact of a proposed organizational restructuring. The current organization has local teams with embedded specialists. Management proposes a shared-service model that should reduce nominal handovers, increase specialist utilization, and standardize decisions. However, it may create central bottlenecks, weaken local expertise, reduce role clarity, and produce fairness issues.

The event log has columns:
- case:concept:name
- concept:name
- time:timestamp
- org:resource
- team
- location
- required_specialty
- priority
- case_complexity
- customer_segment
- handover_reason
- reassignment_count
- local_expertise_flag
- outcome
- rework_flag

Relevant PM4Py functions and concepts:
- pm4py.format_dataframe
- pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network
- pm4py.discover_working_together_network
- pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network
- pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity
- pm4py.discover_organizational_roles
- pm4py.discover_dfg and pm4py.discover_performance_dfg
- pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time
- pm4py.get_service_time
- pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values

SCENARIO

Current process:
1. Intake
2. Local Eligibility Review
3. Local Technical Assessment
4. Local Customer Contact
5. Specialist Review
6. Supervisor Approval
7. Implementation
8. Quality Check
9. Rework
10. Close

Current organization:
- Team North: N_Intake1, N_Intake2, N_Case1, N_Case2, N_Tech, N_Supervisor
- Team South: S_Intake1, S_Intake2, S_Case1, S_Case2, S_Tech, S_Supervisor
- Team East: E_Intake1, E_Case1, E_Case2, E_Tech, E_Supervisor
- Embedded specialists: N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech, plus part-time Specialist_Legal and Specialist_Risk
- Shared automation: Bot_Intake, Bot_Eligibility

Current handover network summary:

| Handover type | Frequency per 100 cases | Median wait after handover | Rework rate after handover | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bot_Intake → local intake | 92 | 0.3h | 3% | standard |
| local intake → local caseworker | 88 | 1.1h | 6% | expected |
| local caseworker → embedded tech specialist | 36 | 3.2h | 9% | local knowledge retained |
| embedded tech specialist → same local caseworker | 31 | 1.6h | 8% | local loop |
| local caseworker → local supervisor | 42 | 2.4h | 7% | approval loop |
| local supervisor → implementation | 39 | 1.5h | 5% | standard |
| local caseworker → legal/risk specialist | 9 | 9.6h | 14% | scarce specialists |
| cross-team handover | 6 | 8.9h | 18% | mostly absence cover |

Current team-level performance:

| Team | Cases/month | Median cycle time | 90th percentile cycle time | Rework rate | SLA miss rate | Local customer satisfaction | Specialist utilization |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North | 820 | 6.8d | 16.4d | 8.2% | 6.5% | 4.3/5 | N_Tech 71% |
| South | 760 | 7.4d | 18.2d | 9.0% | 7.2% | 4.1/5 | S_Tech 68% |
| East | 520 | 8.6d | 22.8d | 11.5% | 9.8% | 3.8/5 | E_Tech 82% |
| Legal/Risk shared | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | Legal 93%, Risk 91% |

Current role observations:
- North and South have strong local expertise and stable handover loops.
- East has high E_Tech utilization and slower cycle times.
- Legal/Risk shared specialists are already near capacity.
- Local teams know regional customer conventions; this reduces rework in complex cases.
- Cross-team cover works poorly because case notes are inconsistent across locations.

Proposed restructuring:
1. Merge local technical specialists N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech into a centralized Technical Shared Service.
2. Centralize Specialist Review, Legal Review, Risk Review, and difficult Quality Check in the same shared-service queue.
3. Keep Intake and simple Eligibility Review local.
4. Introduce shared-service routing rules:
   - all high-complexity cases go to Technical Shared Service
   - all cases with required_specialty in {legal, risk, security} go to Specialist Review shared queue
   - all teams can pull completed specialist advice from a shared queue
5. Remove one local supervisor position and create a shared approval queue for medium/high-value cases.
6. Expected management benefit: fewer local-specialist handover loops and 15% higher specialist utilization.

Expected post-change routing model, management estimate:

| Handover type | Expected frequency per 100 cases | Expected median wait after handover | Management rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bot_Intake → local intake | 92 | 0.3h | unchanged |
| local intake → local caseworker | 83 | 1.0h | slightly more automation |
| local caseworker → Technical Shared Service | 41 | 4.0h | more cases correctly routed to specialists |
| Technical Shared Service → local caseworker | 36 | 2.2h | advice returned through queue |
| local caseworker → shared approval queue | 49 | 3.0h | standardized approvals |
| shared approval queue → implementation | 43 | 1.8h | standardized approval output |
| local caseworker → Legal/Risk shared queue | 12 | 10.5h | broader detection of risk/legal cases |
| cross-team handover | 3 | 6.0h | fewer ad hoc transfers |

Countervailing risks raised by staff:
- Central Technical Shared Service may become a hidden bottleneck because local specialists also absorb legal/risk triage and difficult Quality Check.
- Reduced local tech availability may degrade early informal advice that currently prevents unnecessary specialist referrals.
- Shared approval may standardize decisions but reduce accountability and make ownership unclear.
- East may benefit because E_Tech was already overloaded; North/South may lose local expertise.
- High-complexity cases may experience more queue time even if average cycle time improves.
- Local staff may stop developing technical skill if all hard cases are routed away.
- Customers needing language or regional accommodation may face more handovers.
- Measuring only average cycle time may miss tail delays, fairness effects, and loss of resilience.

QUESTION

Forecast how the restructuring may affect handovers, queueing, role clarity, resilience, learning, and fairness. Connect organizational structure to observable process-mining measures.

CONSTRAINTS

- Do not assume the restructuring is either good or bad by default.
- Do not focus only on average cycle time.
- Do not ignore East as a team that may benefit differently from North/South.
- Do not ignore fairness and learning effects.
- Do not recommend full rollout without a monitoring/pilot logic.
