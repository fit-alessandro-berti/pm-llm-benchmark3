An OCEL has been assembled from ERP, WMS, handheld-scanner, carrier, billing, and payment-service systems. The integration harmonized several source activities into common labels. The result contains exact duplicates, likely cross-system duplicates, same-time but distinct events, and timestamp ties with partial ordering information.

Design a cleaning and ordering pipeline that minimizes semantic damage.

There is intentionally no hidden correct list of duplicates. Your answer will be evaluated on conservative reasoning, provenance preservation, deterministic ordering, PM4Py-aware implementation, and sensitivity analysis.

KNOWN SOURCE FACTS

1. e03 and e04 describe the same physical scan according to a later reconciliation report, but their payloads differ because WMS enriched the event with a location.
2. e05 is a distinct pick of I2, despite sharing activity and timestamp with e03/e04.
3. e06 is a detailed WMS packing transaction. e07 is an ERP status update generated from a message sent by WMS. They may be two observations of one business transition or two different abstraction-level events.
4. e08 means “released from warehouse.” e09 originally meant “accepted by carrier,” but activity harmonization mapped it to `Dispatch Package`. They share all currently modeled objects.
5. e10 is a separate carrier handover record and is known to occur after carrier acceptance, although all three logistics events have second-level timestamp 10:15:00.
6. e11 and e16 refer to the same invoice issuance according to invoice number V1, but each system is authoritative for different attributes.
7. e12 must precede e13 for M1. e14 concerns a distinct partial payment M2.
8. e15 is an ERP summary generated after payment messages. It has no Payment object because the ERP stored only the invoice-level balance update.
9. Ingestion order is not guaranteed to match business order.
10. The original timestamp must be preserved even if a deterministic tie-breaking timestamp is introduced.

YOUR TASK

Design a cleaning and ordering pipeline and compare conservative and aggressive alternatives. Do not simply apply every available duplicate-removal method in sequence.

REQUIRED OUTPUT

Use exactly the following top-level sections.

## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree
Provide a decision tree that distinguishes:
- exact technical duplicate;
- same physical event observed by multiple systems;
- summary event derived from a detailed event;
- harmonization collision between semantically distinct source activities;
- concurrent same-label events involving different objects;
- unresolved candidate.

For every terminal decision, state whether to:
- drop;
- merge attributes and relations;
- retain both with refined labels or qualifiers;
- retain both unchanged;
- quarantine for review.

## 2. Event-by-event decisions
Provide one table with these columns:

Event(s) | Candidate relationship | Decision | Confidence | Preserved canonical eid | Attribute merge policy | Relation merge policy | Provenance retained | Main risk

Cover at least:
- e03/e04;
- e03/e05;
- e06/e07;
- e08/e09;
- e09/e10;
- e11/e16;
- e13/e14;
- e13/e15;

It is acceptable to define more than one scenario for ambiguous pairs.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules
Define a deterministic partial-order-to-total-order policy. It must distinguish:
- domain constraints;
- within-source sequence;
- lifecycle constraints on shared objects;
- source-specific semantics;
- ingestion time;
- stable fallback order.

State which rules are semantic and which are merely deterministic. Explain why adding microseconds before deciding the semantic order can encode a false story.

Provide the intended order, or alternative admissible orders, for:
- e03–e07 at 10:00;
- e08–e10 at 10:15;
- e11–e16 at 12:00 after your duplicate decisions.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline
Describe a minimum-change scenario that:
- removes only strongly supported duplicates;
- preserves abstraction-level events;
- refines harmonized labels where justified;
- stores original identifiers, labels, timestamps, and sources;
- introduces deterministic ordering without deleting legitimate concurrency.

Explain the expected retained event count.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives
Define at least two more scenarios:
- evidence-based semantic merging;
- mechanically aggressive PM4Py merging.

For each, state the expected retained event count and the most likely semantic damage. Discuss why `have_common_object=True` is not sufficient to protect e13 and e14, because they share O1 and V1 while representing different Payment objects.

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation
Provide one executable-looking Python block that assumes `ocel_raw` exists and that its `events` table contains the metadata columns shown above.

The block must:
- use deep copies for independent scenarios;
- preserve `original_timestamp`, `original_activity`, `source`, and `source_event_key`;
- demonstrate a pre-check for candidate duplicate groups;
- use `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` in a justified scenario;
- use `pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates` in a separate aggressive scenario, with an explicit warning;
- use `pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column`;

- include a custom verified-pair merge or deduplication step for cases such as e03/e04 and e11/e16;
- validate referential integrity after changes;
- avoid modifying `ocel_raw` in place.

Any helper used for verified-pair merging must be defined or specified clearly enough to implement.

## 7. Sensitivity analysis
Compare at least four datasets:

For each, discuss expected changes in:

Name at least six concrete edges or patterns whose interpretation changes.

## 8. Audit trail and reversibility
Specify a provenance schema that records:
- canonical event ID;
- source event IDs;
- merge rule;
- reviewer or rule version;
- original and refined activity;
- original and adjusted timestamps;
- attributes retained from each source;

Explain how an analyst can reconstruct the raw view.

## 9. Failure modes and safeguards
Give at least eight failure modes. Include:
- collapsing distinct partial payments;
- converting abstraction-level updates into false rework;
- ordering by ingestion time;
- treating shared objects as proof of duplication;
- unioning incompatible E2O relations;
- changing durations through synthetic microseconds;
- losing source-authoritative attributes;
- applying duplicate functions to the whole OCEL without inspecting candidate groups.

CONSTRAINTS

- Do not merge all events with equal activity and timestamp.
- Do not drop e05 or e14.
- Do not use ingestion time as the primary business order.
- Do not erase the original timestamp.
- Do not claim that a deterministic total order proves the true physical order.
- Multiple pipelines are acceptable if trade-offs are explicit.
- Aim for 1,900–2,900 words, excluding code.
