ROLE
You are auditing a predictive process monitoring system over time. The model predicts, immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, whether a field-service repair case will breach its promised resolution date. Several forms of deterioration overlap. No single diagnosis is expected to explain all months. Your answer will be judged for triangulating predictive metrics with process evidence, separating label maturity from real performance, and proposing proportionate remediation.

BASELINE PROCESS VARIANTS
- V1 Standard visit: Case Created -> Triage -> Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed -> Case Closed
- V2 Remote resolution: Case Created -> Triage -> Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix -> Case Closed
- V3 Parts-dependent: Case Created -> Triage -> Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Order Part -> Part Received -> Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed -> Case Closed

SYSTEM AND BUSINESS TIMELINE
- 1 April 2026: workflow-system migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` in the new source. The feature pipeline initially recognized only the old label. Some events were also duplicated or assigned a generic `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` label.
- 6 May 2026: a mapping patch was deployed and most April records were backfilled, but not all derived features were recomputed.
- 1 June 2026: a new remote-routing policy increased V2 usage.
- 10 July 2026: a parts supplier began missing delivery targets; the issue became severe in August.
- 1 September 2026: Partner Z was onboarded. Partner Z serves more rural customers and routes a larger share of cases through V3.
- October 2026: the outcome API developed a backlog. Many recent breach labels had not matured by the reporting cutoff.

POPULATION EVIDENCE
- Partner Z share: 0% through August, 24% in September, and 27% in October.
- In September, 58% of Partner Z cases followed V3, versus 36% for non-Partner-Z cases.
- September’s currently observed breach rate is 0.45 for Partner Z and 0.27 for other sources, but Partner Z labels mature more slowly.
- Rural travel distance and device age are higher for Partner Z. These features were not present in the original model.

PIPELINE AUDIT FINDINGS
- April’s renamed diagnosis activity caused many prediction prefixes to be built at the wrong event or not built at all.
- The May patch remapped the activity label, but a cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature was not fully backfilled for April.
- Duplicate-event removal was applied only to the new source after 20 April.
- No major outcome-definition change was documented.
- October’s outcome API backlog disproportionately affects cases with longer resolution paths.

TASK
Determine which parts of the deterioration are best explained by:
- covariate drift;
- concept drift;
- process drift;
- label delay or censoring;
- population shift;
- data-pipeline failure.

Several diagnoses may be simultaneously true, and their timing may differ. Distinguish observed evidence from hypotheses and state what would falsify each explanation.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
Use exactly the following six sections.

## 1. Drift diagnosis table
Provide one row for each drift type listed above. Use columns:
`Drift type | Months implicated | Supporting evidence | Contradicting or insufficient evidence | Confidence | Immediate action`

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline
Construct a month-by-month narrative from January through October. Explain why April, June, August–September, and October should not be treated as one homogeneous failure.

## 3. Diagnostic tests
Propose concrete tests to separate the explanations. Include at least:
- label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting;
- recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning;
- calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month;
- comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges;
- tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed;
- population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z;
- feature missingness and lineage audits.

For each test, state the expected result under at least two competing hypotheses.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision
Give a staged decision for immediate containment, short-term recalibration, feature repair, and full retraining. State which months are safe or unsafe for training until labels and pipeline issues are resolved. Discuss whether a segment-specific or variant-specific model is justified.

## 5. Monitoring architecture
Design a monitoring architecture with predictive, calibration, label-maturity, data-quality, process-variant, performance-DFG, population, and intervention/deployment monitors. Include alert dependencies so that a label-delay alarm does not automatically trigger unnecessary retraining.

Include one concise Python code block that sketches monthly process-evidence extraction from a local `field_service_events.csv`. The code should:
- read with pandas and format with `pm4py.format_dataframe`;
- normalize the renamed diagnosis labels before feature extraction;
- show a duplicate-handling strategy;
- create both event-calendar and case-start cohorts, noting their different interpretations;
- use appropriate functions such as `pm4py.filter_time_range`, `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`, `pm4py.get_variants`, and `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`;
- return auditable monthly summaries rather than fitting a model;
- avoid inventing a PM4Py drift detector that does not exist.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis
Analyze at least four failure modes, such as retraining on immature labels, calibrating around a pipeline bug, ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift, or attributing Partner Z’s population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence. For each, give a mitigation.

QUALITY CRITERIA
Strong answers identify April primarily as a schema/pipeline incident, June as a real routing/process mix change that need not imply concept drift, August–September as overlapping performance/process, concept, and population shifts, and October as heavily confounded by label maturity. Alternative conclusions are acceptable when justified. Weak answers choose one drift label for the entire period or treat October’s observed breach rate as fully comparable to mature months.
