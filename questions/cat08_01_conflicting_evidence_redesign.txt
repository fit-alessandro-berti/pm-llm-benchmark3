You are advising the executive sponsor of a consumer-electronics warranty process. The organization wants to reduce cycle time and cost, but recent process-mining evidence is contradictory: the most frequent path is fast and cheap but produces many downstream complaints and compliance exceptions, while slower paths appear more expensive but have better quality and customer outcomes.

There is no single hidden ground-truth answer. Your answer will be judged by an LLM-as-a-judge for evidence use, process-mining reasoning, explicit trade-off handling, non-generic redesign logic, and the ability to synthesize conflicting artifacts into defensible redesigns.

Question:
Develop three materially different process redesigns that balance speed, cost, quality, compliance, and customer experience.

- Normative rule R3: Any reopened claim must pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial.
- Token-based replay summary against the normative Petri net: log fitness 0.91, percentage of fitting traces 68%.
- Alignment diagnostics: frequent move-on-log deviations are Fast approval before Evidence completeness, Customer complaint/reopen after close, and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices.

- Temporal profile deviations: Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote is often too slow for Premium cases; Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen is a long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection.
