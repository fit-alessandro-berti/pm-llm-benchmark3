TWO PROCESS TREES

Model A — the check is inside the revision loop:

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

Model B — one check runs concurrently with the entire revision loop:

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

Use the standard binary-loop semantics: execute the first loop child once; either exit, or execute the second child and then the first child again; repeat as desired.

BUSINESS PURPOSES AND ABSTRACTIONS

P1 — operational compliance view:
- every analysis revision must have its own Check Revision before the next Rework Case or before Close Case;
- Rework Case separates two analysis attempts;
- Approve Revision is optional because some revisions are auto-approved;
- the number and placement of approvals remains visible and potentially relevant.

P2 — executive dashboard view:
- Open Case must be first and Close Case last;
- at least one Analyze Revision and at least one Check Revision must occur;
- the dashboard collapses repeated occurrences of Check Revision to the Boolean fact “a check occurred”;
- it hides Approve Revision and all silent behavior;
- it records the number of Analyze Revision occurrences and whether any Rework Case occurred, but not the exact interleaving of Analyze Revision and Check Revision.

P3 — bounded testing view:
- hide silent transitions but retain all visible labels;
- compare only complete traces with at most 12 visible events and at most three Analyze Revision occurrences;
- bounded equivalence is evidence, not a proof of unbounded equivalence.
