ROLE
You are responsible for drift diagnosis in a document-eligibility process. Different stakeholders point to a system migration, a supplier disruption, staffing cuts, and seasonal case mix.

OBJECTIVE
Characterize the observed drift and determine which mechanisms most plausibly generated each part of it. Distinguish:
- control-flow or behavioral drift;
- performance drift;
- population/case-mix drift;
- logging or semantic drift;
- resource/organizational drift;
- recurring or seasonal drift.

The evidence is deliberately non-unique and partly aggregated. There is no hidden correct decomposition. Your answer will be judged by another LLM for temporal synthesis, process-mining rigor, causal restraint, quantitative reasoning, and the usefulness of the monitoring design.

BASELINE PROCESS
The intended process is:

Register
-> Validate Eligibility
-> Collect Documents
-> Assess
-> [Approve or Reject]
-> Close

`Request Info` may occur after `Assess`, followed by another `Collect Documents` and `Assess`. High-complexity cases may receive a `Senior Assessment`, but this distinction was historically recorded inconsistently.

ADDITIONAL OBSERVATIONS
- Median service time recorded for `Assess` changed only modestly from about 4.8 hours in January-February to about 5.4 hours in July-August; reconstructed queue waiting before `Assess` increased much more.
- Supplier Z cases were already somewhat more complex before May.
- The priority rule may reduce waiting for high-complexity cases while increasing it for standard cases; the monthly aggregate does not reveal this.
- Resource missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and is not obviously random by month or team.
- No formal change-point method has yet been applied.
- Variant labels were normalized only for the supplied mapped-fitness series; other monthly metrics were calculated from operational tables.
- December improvements coincide with both lower seasonal load and active interventions.

REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT
Use exactly the following top-level headings.

## 1. Drift timeline
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| interval | observed change | drift dimension | onset type | candidate mechanism | evidence strength 0-100 | alternative explanation |

Cover at least six intervals and explicitly distinguish sudden, gradual, recurring, seasonal, and mixed behavior where appropriate.

## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
Write 180-280 words explaining:
- what the raw/mapped fitness gap implies and does not imply;
- why a label mapping can recover conformance without proving the business process was unchanged;
- how duplicated technical events and missing resources affect different analyses;
- why the post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone.

## 3. Quantitative drift decomposition
Provide all of the following:
1. A table of month-to-month or baseline-relative changes for at least six metrics.
2. A normalized DFG-distance analysis using the supplied relation rates. State your distance measure and assumptions about omitted edges.
3. A decomposition of cycle-time deterioration into at least `case mix`, `control-flow/rework`, `service`, and `queueing` components. Ranges or qualitative shares are acceptable if clearly justified.
4. At least two candidate change points for each of logging drift, behavioral drift, and performance drift.
5. An explanation of whether the data support one global change point or multiple mechanism-specific change points.

Do not manufacture precision that the aggregates cannot support.

## 4. Competing mechanism hypotheses
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| rank | mechanism hypothesis | predicted process signature | evidence supporting | evidence against | confounding or mediation issue | discriminating analysis |

Include at least eight hypotheses. At minimum cover:
- pure logging-semantic migration;
- supplier-driven rework;
- staffing-driven queueing;
- priority-induced redistribution of delay;
- seasonal population drift;
- persistent behavioral adaptation;
- measurement missingness;
- interaction among two or more mechanisms.

## 5. Behavioral, population, and logging discrimination
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| comparison or segmentation | artifact to compute | pattern indicating behavioral drift | pattern indicating population drift | pattern indicating logging drift | limitation |

Include supplier, complexity, priority class, resource/team, pre/post migration, and pre/post staffing segments. Use process-mining artifacts such as DFGs, variants, alignments, temporal profiles, log skeletons, or DECLARE constraints where appropriate.

## 6. Segmentation plan
Propose a segmentation of the full event log that avoids both overaggregation and tiny cells. Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |

Then state which segment should be treated as the primary monitoring population and why.

## 7. Monitoring architecture
Return a Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |

Include at least ten monitors spanning:
- labels and schema;
- event ordering;
- DFG or variant distribution;
- rework;
- temporal profile;
- queue waiting;
- service time;
- case mix;
- resource completeness;

Do not use one universal threshold. Explain how you would handle seasonality and multiple testing.

## 8. Reproducible Python and PM4Py plan
