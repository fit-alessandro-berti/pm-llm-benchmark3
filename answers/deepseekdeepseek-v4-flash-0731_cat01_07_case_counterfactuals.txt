## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism:
Sanctions ambiguity was noticed at triage but no action was taken. Only after Assessment did S1 provide escalation advice, and C1 had to read the full compliance evidence from scratch on Wednesday. The case therefore lost the chance to use queue waiting time for compliance preparation.

Minimal intervention:
At triage, send a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and reserve a C1 slot. This does not change the mandatory sequence: ID and DOC must still complete before Assessment, and Assessment must still complete before final Compliance Review. It adds an early S1 screen and an early C1 pre-read.

Temporal propagation:
S1 can begin Monday morning while ID and DOC wait in their queues. Once Document Check completes early Tuesday morning, C1 can pre-read the verified documents before Assessment. By the time A1 completes Assessment, C1 has already seen the file, so the final Compliance Review is shorter. The reserved slot also gives C1 an earlier start, though only by displacing another case.

Process-feasibility check:
A1 performs Assessment, C1 performs final Compliance Review, satisfying four-eyes. Preliminary Compliance work is explicitly allowed and does not replace the final review. S1 advisory work may overlap with other checks. There is no originals requirement, so ID and DOC could have been concurrent, but they were not in the factual path; no rule is broken.

Best/base/worst case:
Best: C1 pre-read is fully usable, final review takes 2 h, decision ends Wednesday mid-afternoon; cycle saving about 6 h. Base: final review takes 3 h and the reserved slot is close to the factual start; saving about 2.5–3.5 h. Worst: the preliminary packet is incomplete, C1 must re-read everything, and the reservation delays an urgent non-target case; system-level outcome may be negative even if the case itself ends around the factual time.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If the Assessment changes the sanctions interpretation, the C1 pre-read is wasted. If C1 has no real reservable capacity, the reservation is fictional. If final review duration is not reduced because the pre-read is too shallow, the saving disappears.

### K-411

Factual mechanism:
Triage created only the Identity task, and a case lock prevented Document task ownership until Identity completed. This forced sequential verification: I2 ran 11:30–13:20, then D2 ran 15:00–19:30. The Assessment queue then pushed the case to the next morning.

Minimal intervention:
At Triage, create both Identity and Document tasks and grant a controlled workaround so D2 can claim the Document task immediately. Require real-time start and completion timestamps rather than later batch entry. This restores the concurrency already allowed by rule 3.

Temporal propagation:
The Identity Check should still start at roughly the same time, because its queue is unchanged. The Document Check becomes visible to D2 at 09:35; after D2’s existing backlog clears around noon, D2 can start at about 12:00 and finish around 17:00. Thus both checks are complete by late Tuesday afternoon, instead of 19:30. If A2 has capacity, Assessment could start Tuesday evening; otherwise it waits until Wednesday morning, and the final cycle-time gain is small.

Process-feasibility check:
Rule 3 explicitly permits Identity and Document Check concurrency when no original document is required. Rule 2 still holds: both complete before Assessment. I2 and D2 are separate resources, so no resource overlap occurs. The workaround removes a system lock, not a normative control. Timestamp validation is essential to prevent false concurrency.

Best/base/worst case:
Best: D2 completes 16:30, Assessment Tuesday evening, Decision Wednesday 08:30; cycle about 24 h, saving 5.5 h. Base: D2 completes 17:30, Assessment Wednesday 09:30–12:00, Decision Wednesday 14:00; cycle unchanged at 29.5 h. Worst: the workaround corrupts the case lock or timestamps are batch-entered; the apparent concurrency is false and must be re-audited, adding 1–2 days.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If Assessment is the binding bottleneck, upstream parallelization will not reduce final cycle time. If D2’s queue is already long at mid-morning, the document check cannot start earlier. If real-time timestamp infrastructure is not available, the intervention is not feasible because it creates an audit and process-mining artifact.

Applying these interventions to 100 similar cases in one week reveals interference that the single-case counterfactuals hide.

For K-104, reserving C1 capacity for 100 cases consumes early C1 time before those files are complete. C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer, so this is not free capacity; it is a reallocation of scarce work. If each preliminary review takes 1–3 hours, 100 cases consume 100–300 hours of C1 time. Final reviews may shrink, but total C1 demand may not fall. The Monday 16:00 urgent compliance cases are obvious losers, and priority inversions can create more delay than the target case saves.

For K-219, moving 100 handwritten checks from D1 to D2 shifts roughly 400 hours of work off D1 but adds 500–800 hours to D2. If even 20–30% require clarification, each adds 8–36 hours of external wait plus another Document Check and Assessment. The D2 queue can grow nonlinearly while D1 becomes underused. The correct pilot statistic is the D2 clarification probability, not the average service time.

For K-337, suppressing the second RMI removes about 350 hours of D1 work and 250 hours of A1 work across 100 cases, plus many weekend waits. But those cases still go to Compliance earlier. If C1 cannot absorb the Friday surge, the saved weekend becomes C1 queue time instead. Meanwhile, riskier approval-with-conditions or rejection decisions may generate appeals, loss events, or supervisory interventions that are not visible in current queue snapshots.

For K-411, parallelizing verification across 100 cases makes document and identity checks complete earlier, but it does not increase A1/A2 assessment capacity. The Assessment queue receives a compressed burst of ready cases. If Assessment is saturated, cycle-time gains are near zero. A poorly logged workaround is especially dangerous because batch-entered overlapping timestamps can create false concurrency; process mining cannot then distinguish real parallel work from data-entry artifacts. Real-time timestamp logging must be validated before scaling.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411. It does not change decision policy, it simply restores a concurrency that the process rules already allow. Its benefit is modest and may disappear if the Assessment queue is the binding constraint, but the downside is small when real-time timestamps are validated.

The most dangerous intervention is K-337 despite its large potential speed gain. Removing the second Request More Info changes the case from “seek more evidence” to “decide with residual uncertainty.” If the unresolved discrepancy is material, the resulting approval-with-conditions or rejection can create appeal risk, loss exposure, or a compliance override that dwarfs the saved days. It is only safe if a clear materiality threshold exists and conditions are enforceable.

The local counterfactual least transportable to a system-wide policy is K-104. One case reserving a C1 slot looks harmless, but 100 cases reserving the sole compliance reviewer’s capacity simply redistribute delay. Early C1 work is not free capacity; it is stolen capacity from other compliance cases. The system-wide effect can be negative even when each target case appears faster.

The most valuable additional data are: true per-case timestamps and resource-level queue interarrival times for C1 and A2; conditional D2 duration and clarification probabilities on handwritten documents; M1 sign-off windows; external-response delay distributions; and follow-up outcomes for decisions made without complete evidence, including appeal rates, conditions breaches, and losses. Without those data, counterfactual paths are plausible scenarios, not dependable predictions.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Rule 7 is structural: after a formal Request More Info, a new Document Check and new Assessment must occur before Decision; K-337 is feasible only because Assessment 2 follows Document Check 2 after RMI1.
2. Queue estimates are temporal: snapshot queues are forecasts, not exact FIFO guarantees, so all counterfactual durations are ranges rather than point estimates.
3. C1 is a resource constraint: C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer, so any preliminary compliance work or reservation consumes capacity that would otherwise serve other cases.
4. D2’s clarification tendency is behavioral: the probability that D2 asks for clarification on difficult handwriting is uncertain and can turn K-219 from a time saver into a rework generator.
5. Quality assumption: approval-with-conditions is a valid terminal decision only if conditions are explicit, monitorable, and proportionate to the unresolved risk.
6. Temporal/resource assumption: M1 sign-off timing is not captured by the snapshots; whether M1 signs at the end of the day or the next morning changes realized cycle time for K-219 and K-337.
7. Structural assumption: when no original document is required, Identity Check and Document Check are allowed to be concurrent; K-411’s workaround removes a legacy system lock, not a normative constraint.
8. Interference assumption: in the 100-case rollout, arrivals and the mix of non-target cases remain roughly unchanged, so queue growth for non-target cases is a real spillover, not simply a modeling artifact.
