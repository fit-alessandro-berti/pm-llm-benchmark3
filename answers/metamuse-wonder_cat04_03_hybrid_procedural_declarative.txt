# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

**Material assumptions**

- Activity labels are complete and case-sensitive; timestamps are event completion times, in timestamp order, with one event per activity occurrence except where repetition is explicitly allowed.
- The procedural backbone is normative for accepted/rejected routing, but cross-cutting activities — clarification, sanctions escalation, legal opinion, post-activation bank changes — may be interleaved where the backbone is silent. Where the procedural model restricts their placement for block-structured reasons, that restriction is disclosed as an approximation in Section 3.
- "Accepted case" in R2 means a case reaching `Activate Vendor`; "rejected case" means reaching `Reject Vendor`. R1 makes these mutually exclusive, so the distinction is well-defined once R1 holds.
- R2 "exactly one approval in an accepted case" is read together with backbone step 4: low-risk accepted  `Manager Approval` only; high-risk accepted  `Compliance Approval` only (preceded by `Enhanced Due Diligence`). Neither approval occurs before `Classify Risk` decides the route.
- R3's "before any final outcome" means before `Reject Vendor`, `Activate Vendor`, and `Close Onboarding`. A `Receive Clarification` with no preceding request is also anomalous, though R3 does not state it explicitly.
- R11's "after activation" and "later `Release First Payment`" are data/time-order conditions on occurrences, not merely activity presence; if `Change Bank Details` occurs before activation or no later payment exists, the suspension/re-verification obligation is read as triggered for suspension but the re-verification clause is vacuous for payment.
- R12 values are population statistics supplied by Compliance, not hard deadlines. No hard deadline exists in R1–R11 except R6's conditional 24-hour rule.
- Resources are identified by the `resource` attribute (`org:resource` in XES terms); identical strings denote the same person. No role hierarchy is available, so segregation of duties is checked on identity only.
- The event log contains no case attributes for risk class, emergency status, or joint authorization unless the deployment adds them (Section 7).

**The R5/R6 conflict**

R5 (Legal) states `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` *under all circumstances* once `Sanctions Escalation` has occurred. R6 (Operations) permits activation before the opinion in emergencies, conditional on (a) joint authorization held outside the log and (b) the opinion arriving within 24 hours of activation (read here as within 24 hours of the escalation/activation episode; the test data are consistent with measuring from escalation). These are directly incompatible as activity-only rules: the trace `… Sanctions Escalation … Activate Vendor … Obtain Legal Opinion …` satisfies R6's temporal shape but violates R5's precedence. No activity-only formalism can distinguish a legitimate emergency activation from an unauthorized one, because the discriminating evidence (emergency flag, joint authorization) is not in the log. Silently encoding either rule alone hides this.

**Resolution A — Strict legal precedence (activity-only, conservative).** Encode `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` in DECLARE and the corresponding `always_before` pair in the Log Skeleton. Every post-activation opinion is a conformance failure. *Advantage:* simple, auditable, never legitimizes an unauthorized activation. *Risk:* false positives on genuine emergencies; operational friction may push staff to delay recording escalation, degrading log quality.

**Resolution B — Conditional exception with external evidence (hybrid).** Keep R5 as the default DECLARE rule but route violations to the resource/data layer: a violation is *excused* only if case attributes `emergency_activation = true` and `joint_authorization_ref` (non-empty, referencing the out-of-log authorization) are present, and `Obtain Legal Opinion` occurs within 24 hours after `Activate Vendor`. Missing attributes  the case stays a failure (or is quarantined as `policy-dependent`), never auto-passed. *Advantage:* faithful to both stakeholders; creates an audit trail forcing emergencies to be declared. *Risk:* depends on attribute capture discipline; a free-text or self-declared flag can be gamed, so the authorization reference must be validated against the external system of record.

**Resolution C — Precedence as warning only.** Demote R5 to a temporal/alerting rule: post-activation opinions raise a warning, checked against the 24-hour window in R12/R6. *Advantage:* no false rejections. *Risk:* weakest compliance posture; a normative Legal requirement becomes advisory, and repeated warnings tend to be ignored (alert fatigue). Not recommended as the sole treatment.

**Chosen resolution for the implemented model: Resolution A in the activity-only layers, with Resolution B as the designed escape hatch in Section 7.** Concretely: DECLARE and the Log Skeleton encode strict precedence, so T6 *fails* those layers; the resource/data layer may reclassify that failure to excused only with external evidence. Because the supplied log has no emergency attribute, T6's overall verdict under the implemented model is `policy-dependent` leaning to `fail` (fail-closed). Compliance risk accepted: genuine emergencies will be flagged until the attribute capture in Resolution B is deployed; this is preferable to Resolution C's risk of silently normalizing unauthorized activations, and the flag is visibly labeled as a policy conflict rather than a plain process error, which mitigates the "cry wolf" effect.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

**Compact formal description.** Using `` sequence, `` parallel, `×` exclusive choice, `` silent, and `(do, redo)` loop:

```
[ Receive Onboarding Request,
   ( Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account ),
   ( , ( Request Clarification, Receive Clarification ) ),
   Classify Risk,
   ×( ( Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding ),
      ( Manager Approval, Activate Vendor, Release First Payment, Close Onboarding ),
      ( Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor, Release First Payment, Close Onboarding ) ) ]
```

**PM4Py code**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def tau():
    return ProcessTree()  # silent activity

clarification_loop = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        tau(),
        ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
            leaf("Request Clarification"),
            leaf("Receive Clarification"),
        ]),
    ],
)

low_risk = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    leaf("Manager Approval"),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
])

high_risk = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
    leaf("Compliance Approval"),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
])

reject_branch = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    leaf("Reject Vendor"),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
])

procedural_model = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    leaf("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[
        leaf("Capture Master Data"),
        leaf("Run Sanctions Screening"),
        leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
    ]),
    clarification_loop,
    leaf("Classify Risk"),
    ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[reject_branch, low_risk, high_risk]),
])
```

**Explanation.** Concurrency: the three checks sit under `PARALLEL`, so all six interleavings conform — required, since T1 and T2 order them differently. Choice: the post-classification `XOR` gives exactly one primary outcome, enforcing backbone step 4, R1, and the approval-routing half of R2 structurally. Clarification loop: `LOOP(, RequestReceive)` permits zero or more request/response cycles *before* `Classify Risk`; the loop's do-part being `` means the cycle is optional, and the redo-part forces each request to be followed by its response before another cycle can start, mirroring R3. Terminal outcomes: both accepted branches end `… Release First Payment  Close Onboarding`; the reject branch ends `Reject Vendor  Close Onboarding`, matching backbone step 5.

**Disclosed approximations.** (i) Clarification in reality may plausibly be requested at other points before a final outcome (e.g., during due diligence); a block-structured tree cannot express "a matched request/response pair may be inserted anywhere before the outcome" without exploding the model, so placement is restricted to the pre-classification window where the test cases exercise it. Traces clarifying later will fail the tree but can still pass DECLARE — this disagreement is intentional and surfaced, not hidden. (ii) `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, and `Suspend Vendor` are deliberately excluded: escalation/legal opinion are cross-cutting obligations governed by DECLARE R4/R5/R7, and post-activation change/suspend/re-verify (R11) is a non-block-structured, occurrence-indexed pattern governed by DECLARE approximations plus the external check. Including them as optional tree steps would either over-constrain interleavings (e.g., forcing legal opinion into one fixed slot, prejudging R5/R6) or add nothing. The cost: the tree alone does not enforce R7 or order escalation relative to classification.

**Rule-by-rule semantics**

| Rule | Template/direction | Semantics | Why this template |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init: Receive Onboarding Request` | unary | First event of every case is the request | Backbone item 1; cheap and exact |
| `(Reject, Activate)` | noncoexistence | The two never appear in the same trace, in either order | R1 is order-independent; `nonsuccession` would only ban one direction and miss T8's activate-then-reject |
| `(EDD, Manager)`, `(Compliance, Manager)` | noncoexistence | Route approvals/diligence never mix | R8; again order-independent, so noncoexistence, not nonsuccession |
| `(Reject, Manager)`, `(Reject, Compliance)` | noncoexistence | No approval in a rejected case | Second half of R2; complements tree routing |
| `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | response | Every request is *eventually* followed by a response | Necessary but insufficient alone: `Req, Req, Resp` satisfies response |
| same pair | altresponse | Every request is followed by a response before any *next* request | This is the clause that actually encodes R3's "before the next clarification request"; strictly stronger than response, hence both are listed (altresponse implies response — redundancy noted) |
| `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | altprecedence | Every response is preceded by a request with no intervening response | Catches orphan responses, which response/altresponse cannot see; confidence 0.9 because R3 doesn't state it explicitly |
| `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | response | Escalation is eventually followed by an opinion | R4; deliberately *not* chainresponse — other work (EDD, approvals) legitimately intervenes |
| `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | precedence: source = screening, target = escalation | Escalation may occur only if screening occurred earlier | R7; precedence, not response — screening does not oblige escalation |
| `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | precedence: source = opinion, target = activation | Activation only after an opinion has occurred (when one is required) | R5 strict (Resolution A). Distinct from response: response on (Escalation, Opinion) says the opinion *eventually comes*; precedence says activation *waits for it*. T6 satisfies the former, violates the latter |
| `(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` | response | A bank change is eventually followed by suspension | First half of R11 (approximation: not restricted to post-activation changes) |
| `(Verify Bank Account, Release First Payment)` | precedence | No payment before some bank verification | Weak global shadow of R11's re-verification; confidence 0.8 flagging it as approximate — the authoritative occurrence-indexed check is in Section 7 |
| `(EDD, Compliance Approval)` | chainresponse | EDD is *immediately* followed by Compliance Approval | Backbone step 4 says "followed by"; confidence 0.7 because clarifications/legal opinion may legitimately intervene in richer logs — flagged as the most dispensable rule; if stakeholders confirm interventions are legal, demote to `response` |

Not encoded in DECLARE: R9, R6, R10 counts, R12 timing (wrong formalisms), and "exactly one approval conditional on acceptance" (inexpressible; tree + noncoexistence jointly cover it).

**Orientation and defense.** In `always_before`, the pair `(A, B)` means: whenever `B` occurs, `A` has occurred earlier in the trace; `always_after (B, A)` is its exact mirror, and both are populated so the dictionary is internally consistent. Thus `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` in `always_before` encodes R7, and `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` encodes strict R5 — note this mirrors DECLARE `precedence` with source/target order preserved as (required predecessor, constrained activity). `equivalence (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` asserts the two always co-occur (equal presence, in the skeleton's occurrence sense), defending R3's pairing from the observed-behavior side. `activ_freq` is the authoritative home of R10; `Verify Bank Account {1,2}` accommodates one R11 re-verification while still bounding repeats (a third verification would flag for review — a deliberate, disclosed tightening). Activities with legitimately unbounded counts (`Request/Receive Clarification`, `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, `Close Onboarding`) are omitted from `activ_freq` rather than assigned invented bounds; `Close Onboarding` is omitted because post-activation suspension cycles could plausibly reopen handling.

**Overlap and disagreement.** `never_together` deliberately duplicates DECLARE `noncoexistence`, and `always_before` duplicates DECLARE `precedence` — same violations will fire twice and must be deduplicated in reporting (Section 10). `directly_follows` is intentionally empty: the parallel checks make early directly-follows pairs non-deterministic, approvals may be separated from activation by legal opinion, and payment may be separated from closure by bank-change handling; populating it from the eight test cases would overfit. Expected disagreement: the skeleton is descriptive in origin — if mined from real logs containing T6-like emergencies, a *mined* skeleton would drop the `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` pair; here it is prescribed normatively under Resolution A, which is why the skeleton must be versioned alongside the policy decision, not re-mined silently.

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800.0, 86400.0),    # 48h ± 24h

# Evaluate with: pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)
```

Hours are converted to seconds (48h = 172,800s, etc.). With `zeta = 2.0`, an observed elapsed time is flagged when it lies more than two standard deviations from the mean: clarification replies outside [0h, 96h], escalation-to-opinion outside [0h, 24h], activation-to-payment outside [0h, 144h] (lower bounds truncate at zero since all means equal exactly 2 here).

**Why warnings, not violations.** R12 is explicitly introduced as expectations Compliance wants to "flag but not automatically reject." The numbers are distributional (mean/standard deviation of presumably healthy behavior), so by construction roughly 5% of *normal* cases fall outside a 2 band; treating that as control-flow nonconformance would manufacture failures from statistical noise. Timing also says nothing about authorization or correctness — a fast legal opinion can still be missing (R4 catches absence; the profile only measures present pairs) and a slow one may reflect vendor responsiveness outside the bank's control. Hence this layer emits `warning`, feeding case review queues and process-improvement statistics, while only the resource/data layer's hard 24-hour emergency deadline (R6, a genuine normative bound) can contribute to a `fail`.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required attributes: event attributes `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`; case attributes (for the emergency check) `emergency_activation` (bool) and `joint_authorization_ref` (string). **Missing-attribute policy:** a missing `org:resource` makes the affected segregation check `not evaluated` for that case — never a silent pass — and raises a data-quality flag; missing emergency attributes under a claimed emergency means the exception is *not granted* (fail-closed, consistent with Resolution A/B).

```python
import pm4py

def check_four_eyes(log, act_a, act_b):
    """Cases where the SAME resource performs both act_a and act_b.
    Returns {case_id: (resource, act_a, act_b)} violations.
    pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a, b) keeps cases in which
    one resource performs both activities, i.e. exactly the violators;
    the manual scan below is authoritative and also reports WHO."""
    violations = {}
    for trace in log:
        res_a = {e["org:resource"] for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == act_a and "org:resource" in e}
        res_b = {e["org:resource"] for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == act_b and "org:resource" in e}
        if not res_a or not res_b:
            violations[trace.attributes["concept:name"]] = ("not evaluated", act_a, act_b)
            continue
        both = res_a & res_b
        if both:
            violations[trace.attributes["concept:name"]] = (sorted(both), act_a, act_b)
    return violations

# R9a: Verify Bank Account  vs  applicable approval
v1 = check_four_eyes(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")
v2 = check_four_eyes(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")
# R9b: Capture Master Data  vs  Activate Vendor
v3 = check_four_eyes(log, "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor")
# Cross-check with the library (violating sub-logs should match v1/v2 keys):
suspects_mgr = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")
suspects_cmp = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")
```

Note on R9a: "the applicable approval" is route-dependent, so both approval pairings are checked; in a conforming trace only one approval exists, making the other check vacuous. The check is symmetric in resources — it does not matter which event came first.

```python
def check_emergency_exception(trace):
    """Resolution B escape hatch for an R5 precedence violation.
    Returns 'excused', 'fail', or 'policy-dependent'."""
    names = [e["concept:name"] for e in trace]
    if "Sanctions Escalation" not in names:
        return "fail"  # not an escalation case; handled by other rules
    act_idx = names.index("Activate Vendor") if "Activate Vendor" in names else None
    op_idx  = names.index("Obtain Legal Opinion") if "Obtain Legal Opinion" in names else None
    if act_idx is None or op_idx is None or op_idx < act_idx:
        return "fail"  # no post-activation opinion to excuse (or R4 handles absence)
    # Opinion arrived after activation: excusable ONLY with external evidence.
    emergency = trace.attributes.get("emergency_activation")
    auth_ref  = trace.attributes.get("joint_authorization_ref")
    if emergency is None or auth_ref is None:
        return "policy-dependent"  # discriminators absent from log: cannot excuse
    if emergency is not True or not str(auth_ref).strip():
        return "fail"
    delta_h = (trace[op_idx]["time:timestamp"] - trace[act_idx]["time:timestamp"]).total_seconds() / 3600
    return "excused" if delta_h <= 24 else "fail"
```

Also in this layer (occurrence-indexed R11, authoritative over the DECLARE approximation): scan events in order; if `Change Bank Details` occurs after `Activate Vendor`, require a later `Suspend Vendor`, and require a `Verify Bank Account` strictly after the change and strictly before any subsequent `Release First Payment`. And the data-completeness precondition for R2: if a case attribute `risk_class` exists, verify route consistency (low-risk  Manager route; high-risk  EDD + Compliance route); if `risk_class` is missing, route correctness is only structurally checked by the tree, and risk misclassification is a stated blind spot.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement.** R1, R5, R7, R8 are each enforced in two or three layers (tree XOR, DECLARE, skeleton). This is deliberate defense-in-depth — the layers have different failure modes (tree: block structure; DECLARE: vacuity; skeleton: descriptive origin) — but naive reporting would triple-count one root cause, as T8 shows. Verdicts must be deduplicated by (requirement, case), with layers listed as corroborating evidence.

**Inconsistency under loops and post-activation behavior.** The tree's clarification loop and R3 interact subtly: altresponse permits a new request only after a response, and the loop enforces the same, but if clarification were allowed *after* classification (plausible in reality), tree and DECLARE would disagree structurally. R11 creates a deeper tension: after `Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor  Verify Bank Account`, a second `Release First Payment` would violate `activ_freq {0,1}` for payment and `Verify Bank Account {1,2}` bounds if repeated — i.e., the skeleton as populated *forbids* a fully R11-compliant second payment cycle in some readings. The resolution adopted: R11's re-verification-before-later-payment implies later payments are contemplated, so `activ_freq` for `Release First Payment` should be widened to `{0,1,2}` if the business confirms repeat payments post-suspension; as delivered, a case exercising R11 fully would draw a skeleton flag — disclosed here rather than silently "fixed," because which reading is correct is a policy question.

**Vacuity.** DECLARE response/precedence/noncoexistence rules pass trivially when their trigger never occurs: T3 passes all approval and escalation rules by doing nothing. Vacuous satisfaction is logically correct but operationally dangerous if dashboards report "100% DECLARE conformance" over mostly trigger-free traces. Reporting must separate *activated* rules (trigger occurred) from vacuously passed ones. Symmetrically, `precedence (Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is satisfied by traces with activation but no escalation at all — correct, because R5's obligation is conditional on escalation; the conditionality lives in the conjunction with R4/R7, not in the single rule.

**Missing case attributes.** The log has no `risk_class`, no `emergency` flag, no authorization reference, and no vendor/bank identifiers. Consequences: route *correctness* (was this vendor really low-risk?) is uncheckable — only route *shape* is; R6 is undecidable from the log (hence `policy-dependent`); R11 cannot distinguish a bank change for the same account from a new account. Any deployment claiming full conformance from activity data alone is overstating.

**Timestamp-only limitations.** All timing checks trust recorded timestamps: backdated legal opinions, clock skew across systems, and events logged at batch-entry time rather than occurrence time are invisible. The temporal profile measures *recorded* elapsed time; T6's 10h activation-to-opinion gap is only as reliable as the timestamps. Same-timestamp events also make precedence checks order-sensitive to log serialization rather than real causality.

**Support/confidence as normative truth.** In mined DECLARE models, support/confidence are frequencies in a historical log; here they are set to 1.0 (or analyst weights 0.7–0.9) by fiat. Treating `confidence: 1.0` as evidence that a rule *should* hold confuses description with prescription: a mined model would "learn" from emergency cases that opinion-before-activation has confidence < 1 and might drop R5 entirely. These metadata fields record *our enforcement decision*, nothing more; the normative source is the stakeholder text and the Section 1 resolution, and the dictionaries must be change-controlled accordingly. The same warning applies to the Log Skeleton if ever re-mined: mining would silently repeal Resolution A.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation.** The Section 3 ProcessTree is the canonical backbone: it is block-structured, directly executable/visualizable in PM4Py, and its disclosed approximation (clarification placement) is the only place where it claims more than the stakeholders stated. It should be the artifact shown to Operations for routing, simulation, and what-if analysis. The POWL/Petri-net alternative is unnecessary here since no non-block-structured *routing* (as opposed to cross-cutting obligations) is required.

**Authoritative policy layer.** DECLARE is the authoritative layer for cross-cutting activity obligations (R1, R3–R5, R7, R8), because those rules are conditional, negative, or interleaving-tolerant in ways the tree cannot state. The Log Skeleton is authoritative *only* for occurrence counts (R10) and serves elsewhere as corroboration. The temporal profile is authoritative for timing *warnings* and for nothing else. The resource/data layer is authoritative — indeed sole — for R9, R6 exceptions, and full R11. In case of conflict between layers, precedence of authority follows the requirement's row in Section 2, not a global layer ranking; the one standing global rule is that the strict R5 encoding prevails over any activity-only relaxation until Resolution B's attributes exist and are validated.

**Order of conformance checks.** Run cheapest and most decisive first, short-circuiting per requirement (not per case): (1) data-completeness gate — verify required attributes exist; cases failing this are `not evaluated` downstream, never auto-passed; (2) procedural conformance (tree alignments/token replay) for routing; (3) DECLARE conformance for cross-cutting obligations; (4) Log Skeleton, deduplicated against (2)–(3) by requirement; (5) temporal profile, emitting warnings attached to the case; (6) resource/data checks including four-eyes and the emergency-exception adjudicator, which runs *last* because it can reclassify an R5 failure from step (3) to excused — and only with validated external evidence.

**Surfacing contradictory findings.** Never collapse layers into one unexplained score. Each case report should show the per-layer verdict matrix (as in Section 8), the earliest decisive violation with its requirement ID, and explicit conflict badges: T6-type cases display "POLICY CONFLICT R5/R6 — legal precedence violated; emergency exception unevidenced" rather than a bare red cross, and are routed to Compliance with the evidence request attached. Duplicated hits (T8) are grouped under one root cause with corroborating layers listed. Warnings (T7) go to a separate review queue with aging, so they neither block cases nor vanish. Vacuously passed rules are reported as "not triggered," keeping activation rates visible so that a quiet log is not mistaken for a compliant one. Finally, all dictionaries and the tree are versioned together with the Section 1 policy decision; any re-mining of skeleton or DECLARE parameters from production logs requires Compliance sign-off, precisely because descriptive statistics must never silently amend normative policy.
