# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership
**From:** Process Excellence Team
**Subject:** Decision on Fiber Installation Redesign R
**Date:** October 26, 2023

**Recommendation:** Adopt **Option E (Hybrid Deployment)** immediately. Scale coordinator ownership for low-risk segments but suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation for apartments and old buildings. Initiate targeted measurements (M1, M4, M7) before considering wider rollout.

**Rationale:**
Redesign R delivers significant efficiency gains for simple cases (median cycle time -24%, visible cost -12%) but creates severe risks for complex segments (p90 cycle time +24%, complaints +60%). The process mining data reveals a fundamental segmentation issue: the predictive rules skipping field surveys are inaccurate for older buildings, leading to rework loops that inflate the tail duration and downstream costs.

Scaling nationally (Option A) is inadvisable due to compliance risks (billing before activation) and potential reputation damage from increased complaints. Rolling back entirely (Option B) forfeits proven gains in low-risk segments. A hybrid approach captures value where the redesign works while containing risk where it fails.

**Key Trade-offs:**
We are trading maximum velocity for stability. By restricting the aggressive "skip survey" rule to low-risk houses, we accept slightly higher average costs in those segments to prevent the extreme delays seen in apartments. We prioritize process precision over raw fitness to ensure compliance and customer satisfaction.

**Resource Implications:**
Coordinator utilization is currently 93%, indicating a bottleneck. The hybrid model reduces load by returning complex cases to the field-survey team (utilization currently dropped to 52%). This rebalances resource usage without requiring immediate headcount increases.

**Next Steps:**
1.  **Deploy:** Coordinator ownership nationally, but restrict survey skipping to "Simple House/Low Risk" segments.
2.  **Pause:** Post-billing activation and survey skipping for apartments/old buildings.
3.  **Measure:** Implement full downstream cost tracking (M1, M4, M7) to validate true profitability.
4.  **Review:** Re-evaluate scaling in 8 weeks based on p90 cycle time and complaint rates.

This decision balances immediate efficiency wins with long-term operational resilience.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

*   **High Fitness vs. Low Precision:** The model has high fitness (0.94) because most cases follow the new "coordinator" path. However, precision is low (0.51) because the redesign allows many variations (skipped surveys, manual activations) that create unique, unstructured exception paths not seen in the baseline.
*   **Improved Median vs. Worse Tail:** The median improved because simple cases (34% of pilot) flowed faster without surveys. The tail worsened because complex cases (apartments/old buildings) got stuck in rework loops (failed visits, permit checks) that were previously caught by mandatory surveys.
*   **Fewer Handovers vs. Lower Resilience:** Consolidating ownership reduced handover counts (34% -> 12%) but created single points of failure. When a coordinator was absent, cases stalled because context was not shared across a team.
*   **Lower Visible Cost vs. Higher Rework:** Visible costs dropped because fewer surveys were paid for. However, rework costs (second visits, complaint handling) increased, which are partially excluded from the current cost data view.
*   **Simple House Improvement vs. Apartment Deterioration:** The predictive rule for skipping surveys works well for low-risk structures but lacks accuracy for older buildings requiring permits, causing delays specifically in that segment.

# 4. Decision alternatives

*   **A. Scale Redesign R nationally:**
    *   *Advantage:* Maximizes median speed and visible cost savings.
    *   *Risk:* High risk of regulatory breach (billing before activation) and customer churn due to tail delays.
    *   *Condition:* Only reasonable if downstream costs are proven negligible (unlikely).
*   **B. Roll back to baseline:**
    *   *Advantage:* Restores stability and compliance.
    *   *Risk:* Loses efficiency gains for simple cases; ignores resource rebalancing opportunities.
    *   *Condition:* Only if complaint costs exceed all efficiency gains.
*   **C. Scale coordinator ownership only:**
    *   *Advantage:* Captures accountability benefits without survey risks.
    *   *Risk:* Leaves potential speed gains for simple cases on the table.
    *   *Condition:* Reasonable if survey skip logic cannot be fixed quickly.
*   **D. Run controlled regional experiment:**
    *   *Advantage:* Generates high-quality data on risk stratification.
    *   *Risk:* Delays value capture; pilot fatigue.
    *   *Condition:* Best if leadership is risk-averse regarding compliance.
*   **E. Hybrid (Deploy R for simple, controls for complex):**
    *   *Advantage:* Captures low-hanging fruit while containing high-risk variance.
    *   *Risk:* Increased process complexity (two tracks).
    *   *Condition:* **Recommended.** Balances value and risk given current evidence.

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

1.  **M4 (Measure full downstream cost):** Highest value. Resolves uncertainty on whether efficiency gains are erased by complaint handling. Directly impacts ROI calculation.
2.  **M1 (Randomized pilot of survey skipping):** High value. Validates if the predictive rule can be tuned to prevent apartment delays without sacrificing simple case speed.
3.  **M7 (Conformance monitoring Billing/Activation):** High value. Critical for compliance risk management. Determines if regulatory fines outweigh process benefits.
4.  **M3 (Structured reason codes):** Medium value. Helps diagnose *why* visits fail, supporting M1.
5.  **M6 (Validate risk rule):** Medium value. Similar to M1 but less rigorous; good for quick check.
6.  **M2 (Instrument start timestamps):** Low/Medium value. Improves cycle time accuracy but doesn't change the decision logic on risk.
7.  **M5 (Coordinator vs. Shared ownership):** Low value. Organizational design tweak; secondary to process flow risks.
8.  **M8 (Customer experience split):** Low value. NPS is already declining; knowing the specific driver doesn't change the need to fix the service failure.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Decision:** Proceed with **Option E (Hybrid Deployment)**.

*   **Immediate Action:** Configure process engine to route "Apartment" and "Old Building" cases to mandatory Field Survey and pre-activation Billing. Route "Simple House" cases through Redesign R.
*   **Pause:** Suspend the predictive survey-skip rule for any building risk score above "Low". Suspend post-billing activation until M7 confirms compliance safety.
*   **Scale:** Roll out the "Installation Coordinator" role nationally to reduce handovers, but implement a backup protocol for coordinator absence to restore resilience.
*   **Measure Next:** Prioritize **M4** (Full downstream cost), **M1** (Risk stratification experiment), and **M7** (Compliance monitoring).
*   **Threshold for Broader Rollout:** Re-evaluate in 8 weeks. Broader scaling of survey skipping is justified only if:
    1.  p90 cycle time remains below 35 days.
    2.  Complaint rate within 30 days drops below 7%.
    3.  Full downstream cost (including rework) is confirmed lower than baseline.
