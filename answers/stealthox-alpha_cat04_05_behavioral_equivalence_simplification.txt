# 1. Equivalence Notions

Let  = {`Open Case`, `Analyze Revision`, `Check Revision`, `Approve Revision`, `Rework Case`, `Close Case`}. For a model M with accepting Petri net (N, im, fm), a **complete trace** is a firing sequence im * fm; its **visible projection** deletes all  (label-`None`) firings. `L(M)` denotes the set of visible projections of complete traces.

**Duplicate-label convention (applies to all four notions).** A trace is a word over . Two occurrences of the same label are distinguished **only by their position in the word**; there is no occurrence identity carried across models, and no hidden ID per event. "The same check" in Model A and Model B can only be matched by label + position. P2 additionally forgets `Check Revision` positions entirely (Boolean) while still counting `Analyze Revision` positions.

**(a) Visible trace equivalence after hiding  (language equivalence).**
M _L M  L(M) = L(M). This is the strictest notion; it sees multiplicity, order, and approval placement.

**(b) Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3.**
L(M) = { t  L(M) : |t|  12 and |t|`Analyze Revision`  3 } ( already hidden; complete traces only).
M _P3 M  L(M) = L(M). Decidable here by exhaustive BFS over markings. A bounded *refutation* is a genuine refutation of _L (a bounded counterexample is a full trace); a bounded *match* is evidence only — it says nothing about behavior with > 12 events or > 3 analyses.

**(c) Business-relevant equivalence under P1.**
Observation boundary: the **full visible trace** (P1 keeps approval count/placement and check placement visible;  carries no business meaning). Business abstraction: the compliance predicate

> 1(t)  (i) t starts with `Open Case` and ends with `Close Case`;
> (ii) split t at separators (`Rework Case`, final `Close Case`); every segment contains **exactly one** `Analyze Revision` and **exactly one** `Check Revision` (order inside the segment free) — i.e., every analysis has its *own* check before the next Rework or before Close;
> (iii) consequently #Rework = #Analyze  1;
> (iv) `Approve Revision` is unconstrained in count ( 0) but its positions are observed.

M _P1 M  L(M) = L(M) **and** every trace of both models satisfies 1. (Since P1 observes everything, P1-equivalence collapses to trace equivalence plus universal compliance; a model that emits any non-compliant trace is disqualified outright.)

**(d) Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2.**
Abstraction , applied after deleting `Approve Revision` and , forgetting interleaving:

> (t) = (open-first/close-last valid?, n = #`Analyze Revision`, check-occurred  {0,1}, rework-occurred  {0,1})

M _P2 M  (L(M)) = (L(M)) as a set of dashboard records (set vs. multiset semantics coincide here, since every record is realizable for every n  1 in all models).

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope of `Check Revision` | Inside the loop body, parallel with `Analyze Revision` **each iteration** | Outside the loop: one single leaf concurrent with the **entire** loop | A: #Check = #iterations (k  1, unbounded); B: #Check = **exactly 1** always |
| Min / max `Check Revision` per case | 1 / unbounded | 1 / 1 | Every A-trace with  2 iterations is outside L(B); no B-trace can ever show a second check |
| Possible placement of `Check Revision` | Within its own iteration: before or after that iteration's `Analyze Revision`; always before that iteration's optional approval and before the next `Rework Case` / `Close Case` | Anywhere interleaved with loop events: before the first analysis, between analyses/reworks, after the final approval, immediately before `Close Case` | B admits orderings A forbids (e.g., Approve  Check) and lacks A's per-iteration checks; A's check always "closes" its iteration |
| Optional approvals (`X('Approve Revision', tau)`) |  1 per iteration; strictly **after both** `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` of that iteration (choice follows the parallel block) |  1 per iteration; strictly after that iteration's `Analyze Revision`; order w.r.t. `Check Revision` free | Ordering divergence exists already with one iteration (witness T3) |
| Concurrency | Only `Analyze Revision`  `Check Revision` inside an iteration | `Check Revision`  (whole loop: analyses, approvals, reworks) | B's interleaving space per iteration is larger, but its total check count is frozen at 1 |
| `Rework Case` separation | Always between iterations, strictly after the preceding iteration's check | Between iterations; a still-pending `Check Revision` may fire after a `Rework Case` | In A every attempt is closed by its own check before rework; in B not |
| Join before `Close Case` | Loop exit after a completed iteration (check included, approval optionally taken) | True synchronization of two parallel branches: single check **AND** exited loop | B's final join can defer the only check to the very end of the case |
| Min / max `Analyze Revision` | 1 / unbounded | 1 / unbounded | No divergence arises from analysis count alone |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

Abbreviations: O=`Open Case`, A=`Analyze Revision`, C=`Check Revision`, P=`Approve Revision`, R=`Rework Case`, X=`Close Case`.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | O  A  C  R  A  C  X |  admits (loop k=2,  chosen twice) |  rejects (only one C possible) | Loop scope of `Check Revision`: per-iteration in A, singleton in B  B cannot emit a second check. **Contains two analysis attempts.** | P1, P3 |
| T2 | O  C  A  P  R  A  C  P  X |  admits (iter 1: CA then P; iter 2: AC then P) |  rejects (two checks) | Same check-multiplicity cause; also shows C-before-A interleaving is legal in A. **Two analysis attempts.** | P1, P3 |
| T3 | O  A  P  C  X |  rejects (in A the choice X(P,) follows completion of +(A,C), so P must come after C) |  admits (check branch scheduled last) | Approval placement vs. the parallel block: A's approval can never precede its iteration's check; B's check is concurrent with the whole loop. | P1, P3 |
| T4 | O  A  R  A  P  C  X |  rejects (needs one C per iteration, and C before P) |  admits (single check deferred past the second approval) | Combination: check multiplicity + approval placement. **Two analysis attempts.** | P1, P3 |
| T5 | O  C  A  R  A  X |  rejects (second iteration has no check) |  admits (check fired up front, loop then runs twice) | Check multiplicity: second analysis runs with no check before Close. **Two analysis attempts.** | P1, P3 |

**Strongest structural statement (inclusion analysis).** Neither language contains the other:

- L(A)  L(B): for every k  2, w_k = O, (A, C, R)×(k1), A, C, X  L(A) \ L(B) (B emits exactly one C; A emits k).
- L(B)  L(A): T3  L(B) \ L(A) (A's approval strictly follows its iteration's check).

Moreover the intersection is exactly **A's one-iteration sublanguage**:
L(A)  L(B) = { O  (A,C | C,A)  [P]  X } — 4 traces.
*Proof.* () Each such trace is one A-iteration (parallel order + optional approval), and B realizes it by scheduling its single C at the same position (which precedes P whenever P occurs). () If t  L(A)  L(B), then t has exactly one C (B), so A used k = 1 iterations; A's one-iteration traces have C before P, which is precisely the B-traces of the displayed form; B-traces with C after P (T3) or with  2 iterations fail A's per-iteration check. 

**Effect of optional `Approve Revision` and hidden .** Hiding  turns `X('Approve Revision', tau)` into "approval optional" in *both* models;  adds no visible behavior and cannot mask the divergence. The root cause is **loop scope of the check**, not silence: even in the -only sublanguage (approvals never taken), T1 vs. T4 still separate the models. The approval optionality contributes a *second*, independent divergence (T3): in A the optional approval can only sit at iteration end (after the check), in B the still-pending check may follow it. Making approval mandatory would keep T3; making approval impossible (replacing the choice by ) would remove T3 but leave T1/T4/T5. Under every variant, the models remain non-equivalent.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

```python
# =====================================================================
# P3 bounded language comparison: Model A vs Model B
# Primary method : EXHAUSTIVE breadth-first exploration of markings
#                  (state = (canonical marking, visible trace)).
# Secondary      : random playout, clearly labelled NON-exhaustive.
# =====================================================================
from collections import deque
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

TREE_A = ("->( 'Open Case', "
          "*( ->( +( 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision' ), "
          "X( 'Approve Revision', tau ) ), 'Rework Case' ), "
          "'Close Case' )")

TREE_B = ("->( 'Open Case', "
          "+( 'Check Revision', "
          "*( ->( 'Analyze Revision', X( 'Approve Revision', tau ) ), "
          "'Rework Case' ) ), "
          "'Close Case' )")

AR_LABEL = 'Analyze Revision'
MAX_VISIBLE, MAX_ANALYZE = 12, 3                    # P3 bounds

# ---------- 1) parse trees, 2) convert to accepting Petri nets ----------
tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_A)
tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_B)
net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

# ---------- 3) soundness ----------
def is_sound(net, im, fm):
    res = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    return res[0] if isinstance(res, (tuple, list)) else res

print("sound(A) =", is_sound(net_a, im_a, fm_a),
      "| sound(B) =", is_sound(net_b, im_b, fm_b))   # both True (block-structured)

# ---------- helpers ----------
def mkey(marking):
    # canonical, hashable marking representation -> explicit marking equality
    return tuple(sorted((p.name, c) for p, c in marking.items()))

def fire(marking, trans):
    m = Marking()
    for p, c in marking.items():
        m[p] = c
    for a in trans.in_arcs:
        m[a.source] = m.get(a.source, 0) - a.weight
        if m[a.source] <= 0:
            del m[a.source]
    for a in trans.out_arcs:
        m[a.target] = m.get(a.target, 0) + a.weight
    return m

# ---------- 4)+5) EXHAUSTIVE bounded language via BFS over markings ----------
def bounded_language(net, im, fm, max_visible=MAX_VISIBLE, max_analyze=MAX_ANALYZE):
    """
    Exhaustive enumeration of all COMPLETE traces (im ->* fm) with
    <= max_visible visible events and <= max_analyze 'Analyze Revision'.
    - silent transitions are fired but never appended (tau hiding);
    - visible-length and Analyze-count bounds are enforced before firing;
    - silent moves remain explorable at the visible bound (bound counts
      visible events only);
    - visited-set on (canonical marking, trace) handles repeated markings
      and guarantees termination AND completeness for the stated bound
      (the nets are 1-safe, so the state space is finite).
    """
    fm_key = mkey(fm)
    seen = {(mkey(im), ())}
    queue = deque([(im, ())])
    language = set()
    while queue:
        marking, trace = queue.popleft()
        if mkey(marking) == fm_key:                  # explicit final-marking equality
            language.add(trace)
        for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            if t.label is None:                      # silent: fire, do not record
                new_trace = trace
            else:
                if len(trace) >= max_visible:        # visible-length bound
                    continue
                if t.label == AR_LABEL and trace.count(AR_LABEL) >= max_analyze:
                    continue                         # Analyze-occurrence bound
                new_trace = trace + (t.label,)
            nxt = fire(marking, t)
            state = (mkey(nxt), new_trace)           # repeated-marking handling
            if state not in seen:
                seen.add(state)
                queue.append((nxt, new_trace))
    return language

lang_a = bounded_language(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
lang_b = bounded_language(net_b, im_b, fm_b)

only_a = sorted(lang_a - lang_b)
only_b = sorted(lang_b - lang_a)
common = sorted(lang_a & lang_b)

print(f"|L_A^P3|={len(lang_a)}  |L_B^P3|={len(lang_b)}  "
      f"|common|={len(common)}  |A-only|={len(only_a)}  |B-only|={len(only_b)}")
# Hand-derived expectation (from tree semantics, independent of the net
# translation): 83, 109, 4, 79, 105.  The BFS must reproduce these numbers.

print("\nA-only traces (first 8):")
for t in only_a[:8]:
    print("  ", " -> ".join(t))
print("B-only traces (first 8):")
for t in only_b[:8]:
    print("  ", " -> ".join(t))

# ---------- witnesses from Section 4 ----------
WITNESSES = {
 "T1 (A-only, 2 analyses)":  ("Open Case","Analyze Revision","Check Revision",
                              "Rework Case","Analyze Revision","Check Revision","Close Case"),
 "T2 (A-only, approvals)":   ("Open Case","Check Revision","Analyze Revision","Approve Revision",
                              "Rework Case","Analyze Revision","Check Revision",
                              "Approve Revision","Close Case"),
 "T3 (B-only)":              ("Open Case","Analyze Revision","Approve Revision",
                              "Check Revision","Close Case"),
 "T4 (B-only, 2 analyses)":  ("Open Case","Analyze Revision","Rework Case","Analyze Revision",
                              "Approve Revision","Check Revision","Close Case"),
}
print("\nWitness membership:")
for name, tr in WITNESSES.items():
    print(f"  {name}: in A = {tr in lang_a}, in B = {tr in lang_b}")

# ---------- 7) random playout: HEURISTIC, NOT exhaustive ----------
print("\n--- random playout cross-check (NOT exhaustive) ---")
try:
    try:
        log = pm4py.play_out(tree_a, num_traces=300)
    except Exception:
        log = pm4py.play_out(net_a, im_a, fm_a, num_traces=300)
    sampled = {tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in tr) for tr in log}
    inside = sum(1 for t in sampled if t in lang_a)
    print(f"playout(Model A): {len(sampled)} distinct sampled traces, "
          f"{inside} inside the P3 window, {len(sampled)-inside} outside "
          f"(longer than 12 events or >3 analyses).")
except Exception as ex:
    print("playout skipped:", ex)
print("NOTE: playout is Monte-Carlo simulation. It can miss rare behavior and "
      "can NEVER prove absence of a trace. Only the BFS above is exhaustive "
      "for the stated bound; using play_out alone would justify no equivalence "
      "claim whatsoever.")
```

**Why the BFS is exhaustive and playout is not.** The BFS enumerates every path through the (finite, 1-safe) marking graph whose visible projection stays inside the bound; the visited set on `(canonical marking, trace)` removes only *duplicate* explorations, never behaviors. `play_out` samples paths with some probability law: rare traces may never be drawn, and absence in the sample proves nothing.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

```python
# ---------- footprints (order abstraction) ----------
fp_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
df_a = {tuple(p) for p in fp_a["directly_follows"]}
df_b = {tuple(p) for p in fp_b["directly_follows"]}
print("DF pairs only in A:", sorted(df_a - df_b))   # expected: []
print("DF pairs only in B:", sorted(df_b - df_a))
# expected: [('Approve Revision', 'Check Revision')]
print("start activities equal:",
      set(fp_a["start_activities"]) == set(fp_b["start_activities"]))  # True ({Open Case})
print("end activities equal:",
      set(fp_a["end_activities"]) == set(fp_b["end_activities"]))      # True ({Close Case})

# ---------- bounded stochastic languages (DECLARED weights) ----------
def bounded_stochastic_language(net, im, fm,
                                max_visible=MAX_VISIBLE, max_analyze=MAX_ANALYZE):
    """
    DECLARED policy: at every marking, each enabled transition (tau included)
    is chosen with equal probability. Trace probability = sum over all firing
    paths realizing the trace inside the bound. The result is a TRUNCATED
    distribution (mass of bound-exceeding continuations is dropped).
    No empirical or business probabilities are used anywhere.
    """
    fm_key = mkey(fm)
    layer = {(mkey(im), ()): (im, (), 1.0)}
    language = {}
    while layer:
        nxt = {}
        for (mk, trace), (marking, _t, prob) in layer.items():
            if mk == fm_key:
                language[trace] = language.get(trace, 0.0) + prob
            enabled = list(pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking))
            if not enabled:
                continue
            p_each = prob / len(enabled)             # declared uniform choice
            for t in enabled:
                if t.label is None:
                    new_trace = trace
                else:
                    if len(trace) >= max_visible:
                        continue
                    if t.label == AR_LABEL and trace.count(AR_LABEL) >= max_analyze:
                        continue
                    new_trace = trace + (t.label,)
                nmark = fire(marking, t)
                key = (mkey(nmark), new_trace)
                if key in nxt:
                    m2, _tr2, p2 = nxt[key]
                    nxt[key] = (m2, new_trace, p2 + p_each)
                else:
                    nxt[key] = (nmark, new_trace, p_each)
        layer = nxt
    return language

prob_a = bounded_stochastic_language(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
prob_b = bounded_stochastic_language(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
print("truncated mass A:", round(sum(prob_a.values()), 6),
      "| B:", round(sum(prob_b.values()), 6))        # < 1.0: bound truncation

# ---------- Earth Mover's Distance on the bounded languages ----------
def levenshtein(x, y):
    prev = list(range(len(y) + 1))
    for i, xi in enumerate(x, 1):
        cur = [i]
        for j, yj in enumerate(y, 1):
            cur.append(min(prev[j] + 1, cur[-1] + 1, prev[j - 1] + (xi != yj)))
        prev = cur
    return prev[-1]

lang1 = {list(k): v for k, v in prob_a.items()}
lang2 = {list(k): v for k, v in prob_b.items()}
try:
    emd = pm4py.compute_emd(lang1, lang2, distance_function=levenshtein)
except Exception:
    emd = pm4py.compute_emd(lang1, lang2)
print("EMD (P3 window, declared uniform policy):", emd)

# Exact stochastic language via pm4py would need a stochastic net, e.g.:
#   spn, sim, sfm = pm4py.convert_to_stochastic_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
#   lang = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(spn, sim, sfm)
# Caveats: (i) default weights are an implementation choice, not business
# data; (ii) both models are cyclic, so the exact stochastic language has
# infinite support and full enumeration may not terminate; (iii) hence the
# bounded, explicitly declared version above is used instead.
```

**Interpretation.**

- **Footprints.** Start/end activities coincide; the directly-follows sets differ in **exactly one pair**: (`Approve Revision`  `Check Revision`) exists only in B — precisely witness T3's ordering. This is genuine evidence of non-equivalence for every view that sees order (P1, P3), and invisible to P2 (which forgets order). **But equal footprints would not prove trace equivalence**: footprints are a two-point abstraction (start/end/adjacency) that loses multiplicity and all long-range dependencies. Concrete counterexample: the trees `*( 'a', 'b' )` and `X( 'a', ->( 'a', 'b', 'a' ) )` have *identical* footprints (start {a}, end {a}, DF {ab, ba}) yet languages {a, aba, ababa, …} vs. {a, aba}.
- **Stochastic language / EMD.** Both are quantitative aggregates over **declared** weights (here: uniform over enabled transitions,  included — an assumption, not data; any other declared policy changes the numbers). A low non-zero EMD does not identify *which* traces differ (here: T1–T5), is sensitive to the truncation at the P3 bound (masses < 1), and depends on the ground distance. Even EMD = 0 on the bounded window would only certify equality of the truncated distributions under the declared policy — it would still say nothing about behavior beyond 12 events or 3 analyses. Neither tool can establish trace equivalence.

from collections import Counter

def tree_metrics(tree, name):
    m = {"model": name, "nodes": 0, "operators": 0, "visible_leaves": 0,
         "silent_leaves": 0, "depth": 0, "labels": []}
    def rec(n, d):
        m["nodes"] += 1
        m["depth"] = max(m["depth"], d)
        if n.children:
            m["operators"] += 1
            for c in n.children:
                rec(c, d + 1)
        else:
            if n.label is None:
                m["silent_leaves"] += 1
            else:
                m["visible_leaves"] += 1
                m["labels"].append(n.label)
    rec(tree, 1)
    m["duplicate_labels"] = {l: c for l, c in Counter(m["labels"]).items() if c > 1}
    return m

# 8. Simplified Model

**Target semantics: P2 (executive dashboard).** Justification: P1 is already minimally realized by Model A — any structural simplification either breaks the per-iteration check or hides approvals, both of which P1 observes; P3 forbids abstraction by definition (it needs every visible label). P2, by contrast, is *designed* to be lossy, so a minimal generator of the same dashboard records is the right shared artifact.

**Expression (process tree; POWL sketch: `StrictSequence(Open, Loop(Analyze, Rework), Check, Close)`):**

```text
->( 'Open Case', *( 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case' ), 'Check Revision', 'Close Case' )
```

NEGATIVE = [
    ('Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case'),                    # no analysis
    ('Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case'),                  # no check
    ('Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision',
     'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case'),                               # missing Rework between attempts
]
for t in REGRESSION:
    assert t in lang_s, f"regression trace rejected: {t}"
for t in NEGATIVE:
    assert t not in lang_s, f"negative trace accepted: {t}"
print("all 3 regression traces accepted; all 3 negative traces rejected.")

# ---- structural comparison against BOTH originals ----
print(tree_metrics(tree_a, "A"), tree_metrics(tree_b, "B"),
      tree_metrics(simplified_model, "S"))

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **P1 (operational compliance):** use **Model A**; it is the only model whose every trace satisfies the compliance predicate (own check per analysis, check before rework/close, approvals visible at iteration end). **Model B is unsuitable**: it admits cases where the second (and later) analyses run without any check, and cases where the check follows the approval. Do **not** replace A with the simplified model — S loses per-iteration checks and approvals entirely.
- **P2 (executive dashboard):** A and B are dashboard-equivalent, and the **simplified model S is dashboard-equivalent to both** while being strictly smaller on every structural count (7 nodes, depth 3, no silent leaves, no concurrency). Recommend S as the canonical dashboard model, replacing either original *for this view only*.
- **P3 (bounded testing):** keep **both** A and B as test oracles. The bounded battery must include the A-only witnesses (T1, T2) and the B-only witnesses (T3, T4, T5); S is not a valid P3 oracle because its full visible language matches neither original.

**Warning on the limits of bounded comparison.** The P3 comparison is exhaustive only inside its window ( 12 visible events,  3 analyses). It can *refute* equivalence conclusively — and here it does, with witnesses as short as 5 and 7 events, corroborating the unbounded proof via check multiplicity (w_k  L(A)\L(B) for every k  2, T3  L(B)\L(A)). But no bounded run, playout sample, footprint match, or low EMD can ever *prove* unbounded equivalence: divergences may first appear at the 4th iteration, in longer approval/check interleavings, or in regions the window never reaches. Any claim of full equivalence requires a formal argument over the unrestricted languages — which is exactly what the structural loop-scope analysis in §2 and §4 provides here, with the negative answer.
