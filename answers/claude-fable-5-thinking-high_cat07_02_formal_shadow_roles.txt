# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automated Intake Gateway (R14_Nora).** Core: A1 and A2 prechecks at massive volume. Peripheral: nothing. The key distinction is not frequency but discretion: Nora's A2 events are prechecks (median complexity 0.11, zero high/critical), while human A2 events on the same activity label carry entitlement judgment. Any analysis that treats "A2 Validate" as one homogeneous activity will overstate resource similarity across the automation boundary.

**Front-Office Generalists (R01, R02).** Core: A2 (390/415), A4 (310/335), A12 (260/280), A1. Peripheral: A3 diagnosis (75/60), negotiation, Amira's 18 pricing events, and reopen rework. Frequency and complexity diverge here: Amira's peripheral work skews to medium/high cases (58 high, 9 critical), suggesting she is the informal senior of the pair. The substitution evidence (fragment 6) shows their peripheral rework absorbs temp-agent quality failures — an invisible but real duty. Neither holds approval authority.

**Temporary Surge Intake (R11, R12).** Core: the same four intake activities, but restricted to low-complexity cases (0 critical, 2–4 high). Peripheral: essentially none — and that is the point. The 17 reopened cases traced to their incomplete A2 notes show that identical activity labels do not imply identical activity quality. Their core is "surge absorption under supervision," not full front-office work.

**Technical Resolution Specialists (R03, R04).** Core: A3–A5–A10 pipeline (each 215–260 events). Peripheral: Chen's A6-adjacent security work (24 events), negotiation touches, quality/KB contributions. Complexity splits the pair: Chen's median 0.71 and 42 critical cases versus Dina's 0.48 and 9 critical indicate a case-routing policy (hard cases to Chen) that frequency alone cannot see. Chen's peripheral security work makes him the natural backup awareness point for Erik, though he holds no security certification.

**Security Gatekeeper (R05_Erik).** Core: A6 Security Review — 96 events, effectively a monopoly. Peripheral: technical support activities (A3/A5/A10) and KB updates that keep him embedded in the technical flow. Authority dimension: A6 is a control gate on the riskiest cases (median 0.86); its low frequency is a property of the risk distribution, not of the role's importance. The weeks 7–8 episode shows both the benefit (fewer false escalations when Mateo prefiltered) and the risk (two critical cases waited >2 days) of touching this gate.

**Commercial Exceptions Owner (R06_Fatima).** Core: A7 (122) and A8 (90) with pricing certification. Peripheral: entitlement validation, 16 preliminary approvals, closes. The substitution episode is decisive for the core/peripheral boundary: when she was absent, throughput continued via Giorgio but median exception duration doubled — so A7 is core to her specifically, not just to her team.

**Senior Front-Office / Commercial Shadow (R07_Giorgio).** Core: front-office intake work (A1/A2/A4/A12 at roughly half Amira's volume). Peripheral-but-strategic: A7 (72), A8 (88), A9 (20). His pricing work is peripheral by formal title yet substantial by volume and validated by dual certification. The constraint cuts both ways: his substitution performance (durations doubled) shows competence but not equivalence.

**Approval Authority (R08_Hana).** Core: A9 (138) — low frequency, maximal authority, very high complexity (0.79). Peripheral: negotiation support, reopen adjudication, quality spot checks. The week-5 recheck of 13/21 of Mateo's approvals demonstrates that authority is core here and cannot be read off the activity matrix: Mateo executed the same activity label without carrying the same decision right.

**Cross-Functional Escalation Broker (R13_Mateo).** Core: reopen coordination (A13: 42), escalation routing, preliminary approvals (A9: 44). Peripheral: small counts in every other activity — which is core-defining rather than noise, because breadth is the job. He handles the most critical cases (76) of any resource. His A6 filtering in weeks 7–8 is peripheral work with core-level risk consequences.

**Quality & Knowledge Gate (R09, R10).** Core: A11 (310/298) and A14 (96/104). Peripheral: implementation and configuration support that gives them technical context for their checks. Their KB updates are the process's organizational memory; frequency-wise A14 looks minor, but it is the only systematic knowledge-capture activity in the log.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow.** Formally a Senior Service Agent, he ran 72 A7 and 88 A8 events in ten weeks and fully covered Fatima's six-day absence (31 A7, 18 A8). Benefits: resilience (the pricing function did not stop), cross-training, and a natural escalation valve for pricing questions arising inside front-office cases. Risks: the doubled median duration (38→74 min) shows a real competence gap under load; his 20 A9 events hint at authority creep; and if capacity planning counts him as front office while he silently absorbs commercial work, both teams are mis-sized. He should be recognized as a designated deputy — with the explicit caveat that substitution episodes prove availability, not permanent equivalence.

**R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker.** He appears in all 14 activities, holds the highest critical-case count (76), and sits at the center of fragments 3 and 5. He bridges technical, commercial, security, and management clusters; in a handover-of-work network he would show the highest betweenness. Benefits: reopened and escalated cases get an owner; false security escalations dropped when he prefiltered Erik's queue. Risks: he is a hidden single point of failure that no formal org chart captures; his preliminary approvals were rechecked 13/21 times, meaning part of his work is duplicated effort masking an authority gap; and his security triage — done without security certification — contributed to two critical cases waiting >2 days. The brokerage is valuable and should be formalized with explicit triage criteria, not eliminated.

**R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist.** 96 A6 events is a small number that gates the highest-complexity cases in the log (median 0.86, 55 critical). By pure frequency he is a minor resource; by risk exposure he may be the most important individual in the process. Benefits of the current arrangement: deep specialization and a clean control gate. Risks: bus-factor of one (his 6.8y tenure means deep tacit knowledge would leave with him), queue sensitivity (the weeks 7–8 delays), and invisibility in frequency-driven dashboards, which makes him an easy target for misguided "efficiency" cuts.

**R14_Nora — automation boundary role.** The bot defines where machine work ends and human judgment begins: she registers 760 requests and prechecks 610 validations, then hands over. Risk: because humans repeat A2 after her precheck, activity-level statistics double-count validation and blur the human/bot boundary; role mining should either separate her into a system lane or relabel her events (e.g., "A2-precheck").

**R11/R12 — temporary-workforce shadow of the front office.** They mimic the generalist activity profile at 0.98 mutual similarity but with a quality shadow: 17 reopens from incomplete entitlement notes. They are a distinct role ("supervised surge intake") whose output requires a downstream rework loop staffed by R01/R02/R13 — a hidden cost that clustering by activity alone never surfaces.

**R09/R10 — quality gatekeeping and knowledge stewardship.** Their A11 gate bridges technical execution and closure, and their A14 work quietly maintains the knowledge base that standard responses depend on. This is a bridging role between "doing" and "institutional learning."

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- 1. Load and format ---------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_events.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
# Keep org:resource plus the contextual attributes
# (case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
#  temporary_substitution, escalation_level) as extra columns.

# Separate the automation boundary BEFORE role discovery, so the bot's
# huge A1/A2 volume does not distort human role clusters.
df_human = df[df["org:resource"] != "R14_Nora"].copy()
df_bot   = df[df["org:resource"] == "R14_Nora"].copy()

# --- 2. Organizational role discovery -------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    df_human,
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
# Each discovered role groups activities with the resources that execute
# them plus per-resource frequencies. IMPORTANT: these are execution
# clusters, NOT authority assignments.
for role in roles:
    print(role.activities, role.originator_importance)

# --- 3. Activity-based resource similarity ---------------------------------
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    df_human,
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity_key="concept:name",
)
# sim is a dict {(res_a, res_b): cosine_similarity}. Inspect high-similarity
# pairs, but treat them only as hypotheses (e.g. Chen–Dina 0.94 hides a
# seniority split; Kai–Lina 0.98 hides a quality gap).
suspects = {p: s for p, s in sim.items() if s > 0.85}

# --- 4. Handover-of-work / working-together / subcontracting ---------------
how = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    df_human, resource_key="org:resource",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
wt = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    df_human, resource_key="org:resource",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
sub = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    df_human, resource_key="org:resource",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
# Broker detection: compute betweenness on the handover network.
import networkx as nx
G = nx.DiGraph()
for (a, b), w in how.items():
    G.add_edge(a, b, weight=w)
betweenness = nx.betweenness_centrality(G, weight="weight")
# Expect R13_Mateo to rank first: broad, bridging position rather than
# membership in any single activity cluster.

# --- 5. Join roles with complexity and substitution context ----------------
res_profile = (
    df_human
    .groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        n_events=("concept:name", "size"),
        median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        p90_complexity=("case_complexity", lambda s: s.quantile(0.9)),
        share_substitution=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
        max_escalation=("escalation_level", "max"),
    )
    .reset_index()
)

role_rows = []
for i, role in enumerate(roles):
    for res in role.originator_importance:  # resource -> importance weight
        role_rows.append({"role_id": i,
                          "activities": tuple(role.activities),
                          "org:resource": res})
role_df = pd.DataFrame(role_rows).merge(res_profile, on="org:resource")

# Flag candidate SHADOW roles: work done predominantly during
# substitution windows should not be read as permanent competence.
shadow_flags = (
    df_human[df_human["temporary_substitution"] == True]
    .groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"]).size()
    .rename("events_during_substitution").reset_index()
)
normal_counts = (
    df_human[df_human["temporary_substitution"] != True]
    .groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"]).size()
    .rename("events_normal").reset_index()
)
shadow = shadow_flags.merge(normal_counts, how="left",
                            on=["org:resource", "concept:name"]).fillna(0)
shadow["substitution_ratio"] = (
    shadow["events_during_substitution"]
    / (shadow["events_during_substitution"] + shadow["events_normal"])
)
# e.g. Giorgio's A7 events: high substitution_ratio in weeks 3-4 ->
# deputy behavior, not a permanent second commercial specialist.

# Duration check for substitute performance (do not equate coverage
# with competence): compare service times per performer per activity.
pricing = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df_human, "concept:name", ["A7 Pricing Exception Review"],
    level="event", retain=True,
)
svc = pm4py.get_service_time(
    df_human, aggregation_measure="median",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
# Or insert case-level service/waiting times and slice by performer:
df_timed = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df_human, case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# --- 6. Guardrails against over-interpretation ------------------------------
# (a) Never map "same activity label" to "same authority": check
#     downstream corrections (e.g. approvals later re-executed by R08).
reapproved = pm4py.get_variants(df_human)
# Inspect variants containing 'A9 ... A9' patterns by different resources:
double_a9 = {v: c for v, c in reapproved.items()
             if list(v).count("A9 Supervisor Approval") > 1}
# (b) Stratify similarity by complexity band before concluding two
#     resources share a role:
for band, chunk in df_human.groupby(
        pd.cut(df_human["case_complexity"], [0, .33, .66, 1.0])):
    sim_band = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        chunk, resource_key="org:resource", activity_key="concept:name")

# (c) Report roles to management as "execution clusters + evidence",
#     never as an org chart.
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.** The mined structure shows real capacity is not what the org chart says: the commercial function is effectively 1.5 people (Fatima plus Giorgio's shadow share), security review is a single person, and front-office throughput depends on a bot plus a rework loop that absorbs temp-agent defects. Staffing plans should size the pricing desk for the demand Giorgio silently absorbs, budget explicit rework/supervision capacity whenever temps are onboarded, and treat Erik's queue as a constrained critical resource with monitored waiting times (the weeks 7–8 delays are the warning signal).

**Training.** Convert accidental shadows into deliberate deputies: give Giorgio formal pricing-exception training so substitute durations converge toward Fatima's 38-minute baseline; cross-train Chen (or Mateo, with certification) on defined slices of security review to reduce the Erik bus-factor; close the temps' entitlement-validation gap with a checklist and targeted coaching, since 17 reopens trace to one documentable defect. Use Dina's complexity trend to manage her progression toward Chen's profile.

**Risk control.** Formalize authority boundaries that the log shows being crossed informally: define exactly which preliminary approvals Mateo may finalize (13 of 21 rechecked is expensive duplication and an audit exposure), write explicit triage criteria for anyone filtering the security queue, and set an SLA alarm on critical cases waiting for A6. Keep the human re-validation after Nora's precheck as a deliberate control, or consciously remove it — but do not let the boundary stay implicit.

**Resilience.** The substitution episodes are free stress tests. Institutionalize them: named deputies with documented playbooks for pricing (Giorgio), approvals (a properly delegated scheme instead of ad-hoc rechecks), and security (a trained backup). Mateo's brokerage is the process's main resilience mechanism today; document his routing knowledge in the KB (leveraging Ivan/Julia's A14 stewardship) so it survives his absence.

**Cautions before changing staffing on mined roles alone:**
1. *Execution frequency is not competence or authority.* Giorgio covered pricing but at double the duration, and Mateo's approvals were largely rechecked. Ten weeks of event counts — partly distorted by absences, seasonal temps, and an incident surge — cannot establish that a substitute can permanently own a function, nor that a low-volume resource like Erik is dispensable. Validate any reorganization with interviews, certification records, and outcome quality data (rework, reopens, durations), not activity counts.
2. *Mined shadow roles may be load-bearing controls or may be control weaknesses — the log alone cannot tell you which.* Removing the "redundant" human A2 after Nora's precheck, disbanding Mateo's informal triage, or splitting Giorgio's hybrid position could silently delete error-catching and coordination capacity (see the temp-agent reopen loop). Conversely, formalizing every observed behavior could entrench authority creep. Each shadow practice needs a deliberate decision — sanction it with training and mandate, or redesign it — accompanied by monitoring (reopen rates, approval overrides, security waiting times) after the change.
