# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention.** The service center operates 07:00–19:00 (exactly 12 hours). No service occurs while closed; cases that remain incomplete at 19:00 carry over to the next open day. All daily counts (`arrivals`, `completed`) and the supplied service-time quantiles refer to activity inside the open window. `mean_wip` is the mean number of cases present in 15-minute snapshots; we treat it as an open-hours average (Assumption A1: snapshots are taken only while open; if they include closed periods the reported L is biased downward).

**Core queueing quantities and units**
- Arrival intensity \(\lambda\): daily arrivals / 12 (cases per open hour).
- Completion (throughput) intensity \(X\): daily completions / 12.
- Active service time: median and p90 of handling time excluding waiting (minutes). Mean service time is unavailable.
- System content \(L\): `mean_wip` (cases).
- Capacity: `staff_fte` (mean concurrent full-time-equivalent servers).
- Case-mix indicator: `complex_pct` (% of arrivals on a multi-step variant).
- Peak concentration: `peak_08_10_pct` (% of daily arrivals in the two-hour bin 08:00–09:59).
- Outage duration: `outage_min` (minutes of capacity loss).

**Limitations of the aggregates.** No case-level event log is supplied, so individual arrival/departure timestamps, exact sojourn times, queue-wait versus service split, opening backlog, and hour-by-hour staffing schedules are unobserved. Completions on a given day need not be the same-day arrivals. Service statistics are quantiles of active handling only; they cannot be substituted for mean flow time \(W\). Daily rows are serially dependent.

**Threats to naive inference (at least five)**
1. Non-stationarity from multiple simultaneous drivers (case-mix shift, outage, staffing intervention, backlog state) makes any single pooled \(\lambda,\mu,c\) model unrepresentative.
2. Overnight carry-over and unknown start-of-day backlog violate closed-system boundary conditions required by elementary Little’s-Law applications.
3. Service medians/p90s exclude waiting; they are not flow times and cannot be inserted into \(L=\lambda W\) or utilization formulas.
4. Intraday overdispersion and burstiness (hourly Fano factors 3–9 on representative days) imply that daily means understate peak load.
5. Strong serial dependence (lag-1 autocorrelation of `complex_pct` \(\approx 0.93\), of `mean_wip` \(\approx 0.61\)) and a short four-week window render independence and asymptotic queueing approximations unreliable; change-point dating is imprecise (staffing “began during 2026-04-29”).
6. Recovery days after the outage exhibit high completions that clear backlog rather than reflect sustainable capacity; treating them as ordinary high-throughput observations reverses the operational story.
7. `complex_pct` may partly reflect a coding change rather than a true mix shift (Assumption A2: we treat the observed jump as primarily a real mix change, but note the alternative).

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

Regimes are defined by the joint occurrence of weekday/weekend, discrete outage, recovery dynamics, abrupt complex-mix jump, and the staffing intervention. Weekends are stratified because volume, staffing and peak concentration differ sharply from weekdays.

| Regime | Date boundaries | Evidence | Arrival estimate (per day / per h) | Completion estimate | Service behavior (med / p90) | WIP / capacity interpretation | Confidence |
|--------|-----------------|----------|------------------------------------|---------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|------------|
| Baseline weekday | 2026-04-06–14 (excl. weekends) | Stable complex \(\approx 21\%\), net flow \(\approx 0\), WIP \(\approx 20\) | 100 / 8.3 | 100 / 8.3 | 25 / 47 min | Balanced; \(L/\)FTE \(\approx 3.4\) | High |
| Outage | 2026-04-15 | 180 min outage, completions 58, WIP jumps to 47 | 105 / 8.8 | 58 / 4.8 | 31 / 68 min | Severe capacity loss; backlog spike | High |
| Recovery | 2026-04-16–17 | Net +15/day, WIP 52 \(\to\) 35, elevated staff | 106 / 8.8 | 121 / 10.0 | 31 / 66 min | Backlog clearance under temporary capacity | Medium-High |
| High-mix pre-intervention weekday | 2026-04-20–28 | Complex \(\approx 45\%\), peak 08-10 \(\approx 46\%\), net –13/day, WIP rising 37\(\to\)50 | 114 / 9.5 | 101 / 8.4 | 35 / 83 min | Overloaded; \(L/\)FTE \(\approx 7.5\); backlog accumulation | High |
| High-mix post-intervention weekday | 2026-04-29–05-01 | Staff 6.5–7.0, net +6/day, WIP declining 43\(\to\)32 | 115 / 9.6 | 121 / 10.1 | 33 / 74 min | Recovering under higher capacity; residual elevation | High |

Outage is isolated as a discrete shock. Recovery is kept separate from both baseline and high-mix periods because the elevated completions are backlog-driven, not steady-state capacity. The complex-mix jump on 20 April is treated as a structural break (Assumption A2). The staffing intervention is dated from the first elevated `staff_fte` day (29 April) onward.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different segmentation collapses the data into three broad temporal eras:

- **Era A – Stable low-mix** (06–14 April + associated weekends): everything before the outage.
- **Era B – Disruption & incomplete recovery** (15–19 April): outage plus the two recovery weekdays and the following weekend; residual elevated WIP is allowed to persist.
- **Era C – Elevated-mix era** (20 April–03 May): the entire high-complex period, with a soft sub-division only at the staffing change.

**Operational story.** Era C is interpreted as a single non-stationary regime driven primarily by the case-mix shift; the staffing increase is treated as a reactive within-era adjustment rather than a regime boundary. This view emphasizes the temporal continuity of the mix change and the fact that recovery from the outage was incomplete before the mix shift arrived.

**Comparison with primary.**  
Strengths of the alternative: fewer small-\(n\) cells, simpler change-point narrative, and less risk of over-fitting labels to the intervention date.  
Weaknesses: it obscures the clear operational contrast between backlog-building (pre-intervention) and backlog-clearing (post-intervention) phases inside the high-mix period; it also merges the distinct recovery dynamics with residual outage effects. The primary segmentation is preferred for staffing and recovery policy design; the alternative is useful for purely statistical change-point detection.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Pooled summaries (all 28 days or all weekdays) systematically mask heterogeneity.

1. **Weekday/weekend composition.** Pooled mean arrivals \(\approx 90\), mean WIP \(\approx 30\), mean staff \(\approx 5.1\). Segmented: weekdays \(\approx 108\) arrivals / WIP 35 / staff 6.2; weekends \(\approx 45\) / 17 / 2.6. Capacity-pressure proxy \(L/\)FTE is 3.4 in baseline weekdays versus 7.5 in high-mix pre-intervention; the pooled figure \(\approx 5.8\) looks moderate.

2. **Complex-case mix.** Pooled complex \(\approx 33\%\), median service \(\approx 31\) min, p90 \(\approx 67\) min, peak 08-10 \(\approx 32\%\). Segmented: low-mix (complex \(\approx 21\%\)) median 25 / p90 47 / peak 30%; high-mix (complex \(\approx 45\%\)) median 35 / p90 83 / peak 46%. Pooling averages two distinct service distributions and understates both the heavier right tail and the sharper morning peak that accompany the complex variant.

3. **Outage, recovery and staffing intervention.** Overall net flow \(\approx -4\) cases/day and completion ratio \(\approx 0.96\). Segmented nets: outage –47, recovery +15, high-mix pre –13, high-mix post +6. The high-completion recovery days and the post-intervention surplus are averaged with the deficit days, producing an apparently near-balanced system. The staffing intervention’s effect (net turning positive, WIP declining) is diluted to invisibility.

These are classic Simpson-type reversals: a policy that appears adequate under the grand mean (e.g., “6 FTE handles mean demand with acceptable WIP”) fails inside the high-mix pre-intervention regime where backlog accumulates at 13 cases per day.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Daily sequence.** Weekday arrivals are mildly under-dispersed relative to Poisson (Fano factor \(\approx 0.66\)). Strong positive serial dependence appears in case mix (lag-1 autocorrelation of `complex_pct` \(\approx 0.93\)) and in WIP (\(\approx 0.61\)); arrivals themselves show moderate lag-1 autocorrelation (\(\approx 0.41\)). The cumulative net-flow path declines steadily through the high-mix pre-intervention window (reaching –128 by 28 April) and only partially recovers thereafter, confirming non-stationarity.

**Representative hourly vectors.**  
- Ordinary weekday (04-07): Fano \(\approx 2.96\), CV \(\approx 0.59\), max-hour fraction \(\approx 0.20\).  
- Outage day (04-15): Fano \(\approx 3.8\), completions drop to zero for three consecutive hours.  
- Bursty high-mix weekday (04-21): Fano \(\approx 9.3\), CV \(\approx 0.98\), max-hour fraction \(\approx 0.30\), with non-adjacent peaks (34 then 24).  
- Weekend (04-25): Fano \(\approx 1.2\), closer to Poisson.  

**Change-point evidence.** A clear jump in `complex_pct` (+18 points) occurs between 19 and 20 April; the outage is an instantaneous capacity shock; staff_fte steps upward on 29 April and net flow changes sign. A simple heuristic (absolute day-to-day change in complex >10 or outage_min >0 or staff change >0.5) recovers these three dates.

**Limits of the four hourly vectors.** They illustrate possible intraday patterns but cannot identify inter-arrival distributions, multi-day stochastic structure, or variance of the arrival process across days inside a regime. Stationarity fails across regimes; even inside the baseline window the sample is only seven weekdays.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law \(L=\lambda W\) requires long-run averages under stable boundaries and a well-defined throughput. Median service time is never used as \(W\). Implied flow times are reported only for windows judged at least approximately stationary; units are hours (or minutes) of open time.

| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow-time (if defensible) | Verdict |
|-----------------|--------------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------|---------|
| Baseline WD (06–14 Apr) | High (net \(\approx 0\), WIP flat) | Arrivals \(\approx\) completions | Open-hour snapshots comparable | Low (cum-net near 0) | Acceptable within short window | \(L\approx 20.3\), \(\lambda\approx 8.3\)/h \(\Rightarrow W\approx 2.43\) h (146 min) | approximate only |
| Outage (15 Apr) | Low (net –47) | Completions (disrupted) | Spike | High | None | — | not defensible |
| Recovery (16–17 Apr) | Low (WIP falling) | Completions (backlog clear) | Transient | High residual | Transient | — | not defensible |
| High-mix pre WD (20–28 Apr) | Low (net –13/day, WIP rising) | Neither (arr > comp) | Rising trend | High | Non-stationary | Formal numbers 4.7–5.3 h are invalid | not defensible |
| High-mix post WD (29 Apr–01 May) | Medium (net +6, WIP declining) | Completions preferred | Declining but elevated | Medium-High | Improving but short | \(\approx 3.7\)–3.9 h if forced | approximate only |
| Weekend low-mix | Medium-High | Arrivals \(\approx\) completions | Stable low | Low | Acceptable | \(\approx 4\) h | approximate only |
| Pooled all days | Low | Misleading average | Heterogeneous | High | None | Meaningless | not defensible |

Even the baseline figure is only approximate: the window is short, opening backlog is unobserved, and the implied sojourn (\(\approx 146\) min) greatly exceeds median active service (25 min), indicating substantial queue wait.

# 7. Operational implications

- **Staffing.** Baseline weekdays are sustainable near 6 FTE. High-mix weekdays require the elevated capacity (6.5–7 FTE) that began on 29 April; without it backlog accumulates at \(\approx 13\) cases/day. Staff to the high-mix peak intensity and to the observed morning concentration, not to the pooled average of \(\approx 5.1\)–6 FTE.

- **Backlog recovery.** After the outage, residual WIP remained elevated when the complex-mix shift arrived, compounding the problem. Explicit recovery protocols (temporary capacity boost until WIP falls below \(\approx 25\) and daily net is near zero) should be triggered by any large negative net-flow day.

- **Peak smoothing.** High-mix days concentrate 45–50 % of arrivals into 08:00–09:59 and exhibit extreme hourly bursts. Appointment systems, staggered opening, or overflow routing would reduce the effective peak load that daily averages conceal.

- **Data-collection recommendations.** Record daily opening backlog, case-level timestamps (arrival, start-of-handling, completion), variant label, and hour-by-hour productive staffing. Only then can true mean flow times, utilization, and precise Little’s-Law checks be obtained. Current aggregates are insufficient for strong queueing-law claims outside the most stable windows.

**Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under segmentation.**  
“Average daily staffing of approximately 6 FTE is adequate because overall completion ratio is 0.96 and mean WIP is only 30.”  
Under the segmented view this policy leaves the high-mix pre-intervention regime chronically under-staffed, allowing backlog to grow by more than 90 cases in nine weekdays and driving implied sojourns well above four hours. The post-intervention improvement demonstrates that the extra FTE was necessary; a pooled average would have recommended against it.

"""
Self-contained analysis of service-center daily aggregates.
Constructs tables, regime summaries, dispersion/serial indicators,
a transparent change-point heuristic, and Little's-Law calculations
only for explicitly chosen windows.  Comments indicate how a true
event log could be summarised with PM4Py; the supplied data are NOT
an event log.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from io import StringIO

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Construct daily table in memory
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
daily_csv = """date,day,arrivals,completed,median_service_min,p90_service_min,mean_wip,staff_fte,complex_pct,outage_min,peak_08_10_pct

2026-04-24,Fri,106,96,35,83,43,5.5,45,0,40
2026-04-25,Sat,51,43,36,85,23,2.5,42,0,22

2026-04-30,Thu,120,126,32,72,36,7.0,45,0,43
2026-05-01,Fri,109,116,33,74,32,6.5,43,0,39

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(daily_csv), parse_dates=["date"])
df["net"] = df["completed"] - df["arrivals"]
df["is_wd"] = ~df["day"].isin(["Sat", "Sun"])
df["wip_per_fte"] = df["mean_wip"] / df["staff_fte"]
df["cum_net"] = df["net"].cumsum()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Representative hourly vectors (bins 07-08 ... 18-19)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
hourly = {
    "2026-04-07": {
        "arr": np.array([3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4]),
        "comp": np.array([4,7,10,14,15,11,9,8,7,6,5,3]),
        "note": "ordinary weekday",
    },
    "2026-04-15": {
        "arr": np.array([4,6,13,22,17,11,9,7,6,4,4,2]),
        "comp": np.array([5,7,10,8,0,0,0,4,6,7,6,5]),
        "note": "outage ~11-14h",
    },
    "2026-04-21": {
        "arr": np.array([2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4]),
        "comp": np.array([3,4,7,13,10,8,12,10,8,9,9,8]),
        "note": "bursty high-mix",
    },
    "2026-04-25": {
        "arr": np.array([1,2,4,7,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,2]),
        "comp": np.array([1,2,3,5,6,6,5,4,4,3,2,2]),
        "note": "weekend",
    },
}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Regime labels (primary segmentation)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def regime(row):
    d = row["date"]
    if d == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15"):
        return "Outage"
    if d in (pd.Timestamp("2026-04-16"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-17")):
        return "Recovery"
    if d < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15") and row["is_wd"]:
        return "Baseline_WD"
    if d < pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20") and not row["is_wd"]:
        return "Baseline_WE"
    if pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20") <= d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-28") and row["is_wd"]:
        return "HighMix_Pre_WD"
    if pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20") <= d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-28") and not row["is_wd"]:
        return "HighMix_WE"
    if d >= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29") and row["is_wd"]:
        return "HighMix_Post_WD"
    if d >= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29") and not row["is_wd"]:
        return "HighMix_Post_WE"
    return "Other"

df["regime"] = df.apply(regime, axis=1)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Pooled and regime summaries
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
summary_cols = ["arrivals", "completed", "median_service_min", "p90_service_min",
                "mean_wip", "staff_fte", "complex_pct", "peak_08_10_pct", "net", "wip_per_fte"]

print("=== POOLED (all days) ===")
print(df[summary_cols].mean().round(2).to_string())
print("\n=== POOLED (weekdays only) ===")
print(df.loc[df["is_wd"], summary_cols].mean().round(2).to_string())

print("\n=== REGIME MEANS (primary segmentation) ===")
reg_summary = df.groupby("regime")[summary_cols].agg(["mean", "count"]).round(2)
print(reg_summary.to_string())

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Burstiness / dispersion indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def fano(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    return np.var(x) / np.mean(x) if np.mean(x) > 0 else np.nan

def cv(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    return np.std(x) / np.mean(x) if np.mean(x) > 0 else np.nan

print("\n=== DISPERSION (daily arrivals) ===")
print(f"Weekday Fano: {fano(df.loc[df['is_wd'], 'arrivals']):.3f}")
print(f"Weekend Fano: {fano(df.loc[~df['is_wd'], 'arrivals']):.3f}")

print("\n=== HOURLY FANO / CV ===")
for day, vec in hourly.items():
    print(f"{day} ({vec['note']}): Fano={fano(vec['arr']):.2f}, CV={cv(vec['arr']):.2f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Simple serial-dependence indicator (lag-1 autocorrelation)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== LAG-1 AUTOCORRELATION ===")
for col in ["arrivals", "mean_wip", "complex_pct", "net"]:
    print(f"{col}: {df[col].autocorr(lag=1):.3f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Transparent change-point / segmentation heuristic
#    (NOT presented as definitive ground truth)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
df["d_complex"] = df["complex_pct"].diff()
df["d_staff"] = df["staff_fte"].diff()
heuristic_flags = (
    (df["outage_min"] > 0)
    | (df["d_complex"].abs() > 10)
    | (df["d_staff"].abs() > 0.5)
)
print("\n=== HEURISTIC CHANGE-POINT FLAGS (outage | |Δcomplex|>10 | |Δstaff|>0.5) ===")
print(df.loc[heuristic_flags, ["date", "day", "complex_pct", "staff_fte", "outage_min", "net"]].to_string())

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Little's-Law implied flow times ONLY for chosen windows
#    Opening hours = 12 h.  Never substitute median service for W.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def littles(window_df, label):
    L = window_df["mean_wip"].mean()
    lam_arr = window_df["arrivals"].mean() / 12.0
    lam_comp = window_df["completed"].mean() / 12.0
    W_arr_h = L / lam_arr
    W_comp_h = L / lam_comp
    print(f"\n{label}:")
    print(f"  L={L:.1f}, λ_arr={lam_arr:.2f}/h, λ_comp={lam_comp:.2f}/h")
    print(f"  Implied W (from arrivals)  = {W_arr_h:.2f} h ({W_arr_h*60:.0f} min)")
    print(f"  Implied W (from completions)= {W_comp_h:.2f} h ({W_comp_h*60:.0f} min)")
    print(f"  Median active service       = {window_df['median_service_min'].mean():.0f} min")
    print(f"  Net flow (mean)             = {window_df['net'].mean():.1f}")

print("\n=== LITTLE'S LAW (only for windows judged approximately usable) ===")
base = df[(df["date"] < "2026-04-15") & df["is_wd"]]
littles(base, "Baseline WD (approximate only)")

post = df[(df["date"] >= "2026-04-29") & df["is_wd"]]
littles(post, "High-mix post-intervention WD (approximate only)")

# High-mix pre is deliberately omitted: net strongly negative → non-stationary
print("\nHigh-mix pre-intervention WD: NOT computed (net ≈ -13/day, WIP rising → not stationary)")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Compact comparison tables for reporting
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== COMPACT REGIME COMPARISON (weekday means) ===")
cmp = pd.DataFrame({
    "Baseline_WD": base[summary_cols].mean(),
    "HighMix_Pre_WD": df[(df["date"] >= "2026-04-20") & (df["date"] <= "2026-04-28") & df["is_wd"]][summary_cols].mean(),
    "HighMix_Post_WD": post[summary_cols].mean(),
}).round(2)
print(cmp.to_string())
