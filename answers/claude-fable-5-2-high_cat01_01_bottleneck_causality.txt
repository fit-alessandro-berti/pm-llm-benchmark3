## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded`.** **Confidence: 70/100** that the crude Orion penalty is a routing and case-mix artefact, not an effect of Orion assignment. Confidence that Orion is actually *faster* is only about 40/100, and the proposed policy's effect is not identified by 30 completed cases.

**Strongest evidence for.** Orion's crude mean is 21.96 h against Nova's 15.24 h (+6.7 h), but Orion carries 43% High-complexity cases against 12.5%, as the stated routing rule predicts. Within every complexity stratum the sign reverses (Low −2.1 h, Medium −2.2 h, High −3.4 h). Standardised to the pooled case mix the contrast is −2.5 h: +9.4 h of the crude gap is mix and −2.7 h is within-complexity. Read as a total effect there is no sign of harm. About 85% of the within-stratum gap sits in pre-review waiting (a mediated, capacity-type pathway), while the direct review-service difference is about −0.3 h.

**Strongest evidence against.** The reversal rests on two Orion Low cases and two Nova High cases. Thirteen of fourteen Orion cases are Night/High-load, so group, shift and load are nearly aliased; matching on them shrinks the gap to between −1.0 and −2.4 h in single-case cells. Complexity is a coarse label, routing may select on unobserved risk, and unfinished cases are invisible, so a harmful Orion effect in some segment cannot be excluded.

**The 50% reassignment.** It may succeed if Orion is congested while Nova has idle certified capacity, because the gap is a queueing gap. It will probably fail on its own logic: at Nova's observed stratum means, Orion's mix would average 24.7 h, not 15.2 h. It may backfire through Nova queue saturation, thin certification for High/compliance cases, night arrivals waiting for day staff, and more rework or appeals.

## 2. Causal graph

```text
unobserved_case_risk      -> complexity, routing_rule, compliance_required, review_service, rework, quality_outcome, case_completed
complexity                -> routing_rule, compliance_required, queue_wait, review_service, rework, quality_outcome
shift                     -> routing_rule, arrival_load, queue_wait
arrival_load              -> routing_rule, queue_wait, review_service
compliance_required       -> routing_rule, compliance_performed
routing_rule              -> resource_group
resource_group            -> unobserved_reviewer_skill, queue_wait, review_service, case_completed
unobserved_reviewer_skill -> review_service, rework, quality_outcome
compliance_performed      -> queue_wait, rework, cycle_time
queue_wait                -> cycle_time, review_service, quality_outcome
review_service            -> cycle_time, quality_outcome
rework                    -> review_service, cycle_time, quality_outcome
cycle_time                -> case_completed, quality_outcome
```

**Supported by supplied facts:** `complexity -> routing_rule -> resource_group` (difficult cases usually go to Orion); `shift -> routing_rule` (Orion covers most nights); `complexity -> compliance_required -> compliance_performed` (policy; performance assumed); the definitional arrows into `cycle_time`; `rework -> review_service`; `compliance_performed -> queue_wait` (the wait field includes compliance waiting).

**Assumptions:** every arrow leaving `resource_group`, both unobserved nodes, the `arrival_load` and `queue_wait` effects, and all arrows into `quality_outcome` and `case_completed`.

**Suppressed dynamics:** each routing decision changes a group's backlog, which changes later cases' `queue_wait` and possibly later routing (`queue_wait[t] -> routing_rule[t+1]`). Cases therefore interfere, and reviewers may work differently as backlog grows.

## 3. Variable-role table

Four estimands are used below and in the later sections.

- **E-total:** the effect on cycle time of assigning a given case to Orion rather than Nova, with all other routing unchanged. Only pre-assignment variables may be adjusted for.
- **E-direct:** the part of E-total not transmitted through queue wait, compliance waiting or rework, which is what the reviewer pool does to review service at a fixed queue state.
- **E-mediated:** the part transmitted through `queue_wait`, compliance handling and `rework`.
- **E-policy:** the change in system-wide cycle time and quality caused by a routing rule such as the 50% transfer. Because cases share queues, it is not the sum of case-level E-total values, and it is the estimand management actually needs.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Baseline and instrumentation (2 to 4 weeks) | No routing change. Suspend the 50% transfer. Extract an event-level log with start and complete timestamps, reviewer IDs, routing reason, and all open and abandoned cases. | Measurement and selection (H5); baseline for queue state | None (observational) | No operational risk; data-quality checks on timestamps and case reconciliation | Performance DFG by segment; queue length and staffed reviewers at each queue entry; working-calendar waits; censoring-aware time to close; variant and conformance check that compliance was performed when required | Tests H5 directly, shows whether Orion or Nova is the congested queue, and gives variances for sizing the pilot | Do not proceed to phase 1 until open cases reconcile with the log and waits can be split into queueing and compliance. Escalate if aged open cases concentrate in one group. |
| 1. Randomised routing pilot (4 to 8 weeks) | Among cases the engine would send to Orion, divert a random 10 to 15% to Nova, starting with Low and Medium. Include High only where Nova has certified reviewers, at a lower rate. Keep the engine's original recommendation on record. | E-total by stratum; reviewer-pool versus queue pathways (H2, H3, H4, H7) | Individual case, randomised after `Classify`, blocked by complexity, shift and compliance requirement | Certification check before diversion; cap on Nova work in progress; any diverted case waiting beyond the baseline 90th percentile returns to default routing; sampled quality audit of diverted cases | Segment waits, service, remainder, rework, Request Info loops, hand-overs, queue state at entry, appeals; intention-to-treat and as-treated views | First unconfounded estimate of what happens to an Orion-type case at Nova. With within-cell standard deviations of 1.0 to 1.5 h (from very small cells), about 10 cases per arm per stratum detect a 2 h difference and about 36 detect 1 h | Stop a stratum if diverted cases are slower by more than 2 h or 15% (whichever is larger) over two consecutive weeks, if Nova's 90th-percentile wait rises more than 20% over baseline, if rework rises by more than 10 points, or on any compliance breach. |
| 2. Queue and compliance capacity test (parallel, 4 to 6 weeks) | Leave group labels and routing unchanged. Add capacity where phase 0 shows the queue peaks, for example one more eligible reviewer or compliance slot in the pre-review queue for High cases, or starting the compliance check at queue entry instead of at reviewer pick-up. | `queue_wait` and compliance waiting, which hold 58% of cycle time | Time blocks (week or shift), alternated on and off in a switchback design | No reduction of review depth; overtime limits; compliance sign-off unchanged | Queue length over time, wait split into queueing and compliance, utilisation, service time (to detect hurrying), rework and appeals | Shows whether waiting responds to capacity regardless of group label (supports H2/H3 over H7) and gives the wait-per-capacity relationship needed to price alternatives to rerouting | Roll back if pre-review wait for targeted cases has not fallen by at least 1 h after four on-blocks, or if rework or audit findings worsen. |
| 3. Conditional scale-up | Replace the fixed 50% with a state-dependent rule: divert only the case types shown non-inferior in phase 1, and only when Orion's queue exceeds a threshold while Nova's is below one. Increase in steps of about 10 points and keep a permanent 5 to 10% randomised holdout. | E-policy, including congestion spill-over between groups | Case-level rule with randomised holdout; step size by period | Step up only after two stable periods; report complexity-standardised cycle time rather than group means | Standardised cycle time and tail percentiles for all cases, not just moved ones; both queues' length; rework and appeals with adequate follow-up | Measures the system-wide effect and its dependence on diversion intensity, which case-level estimates cannot give | Step back one level if system-wide standardised cycle time or either group's 90th-percentile wait worsens for two periods, or if a quality guardrail is breached. |

**Final recommendation.** Do not implement the 50% transfer. The evidence behind it is a case-mix artefact: once complexity is held fixed, Orion's cases finish 2 to 3 h sooner, and the difference lies in waiting rather than reviewing. That within-complexity pattern is not causal proof either, since it rests on cells of one or two cases, on a group that is almost synonymous with night work, and on completed cases only. The committee should (1) freeze the reassignment and spend two to four weeks building an event-level log with open cases, routing reasons and queue states; (2) run a blocked, randomised diversion of roughly 10 to 15% of Orion-bound Low and Medium cases, with certification, backlog and waiting-time guardrails; (3) in parallel, test a capacity change on the pre-review queue and compliance step, where 58% of cycle time is spent; and (4) scale only what the pilot supports, as a state-dependent rule with a permanent randomised holdout. Performance should be reported complexity-standardised, with rework and appeal guardrails, so that composition shifts are not mistaken for improvement.

MIN_CELL = 3
GROUPS = ["Nova", "Orion"]
LEVELS = ["Low", "Medium", "High"]
pd.set_option("display.width", 200)
pd.set_option("display.max_columns", 40)


WARNINGS = []


def warn(msg):
    """Print a support/identification warning and keep it for the closing summary."""
    WARNINGS.append(msg)
    print(f"  [WARNING] {msg}")


def section(title):
    print("\n" + "=" * 100 + f"\n{title}\n" + "=" * 100)


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- 1. load
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV)).rename(columns={"group": "resource_group"})
df["complexity"] = pd.Categorical(df["complexity"], categories=LEVELS, ordered=True)
# Remainder = cycle time not labelled as pre-review wait or review service
# (intake, compliance execution, Request-Info waiting, closing ...). It is derived, not measured.
df["other_h"] = (df["cycle_time_h"] - df["wait_before_review_h"] - df["review_service_h"]).round(2)
df["rework"] = (df["rework_count"] > 0).astype(int)
df["appeal"] = (df["outcome_30d"] == "Appeal").astype(int)
COMPONENTS = ["cycle_time_h", "wait_before_review_h", "review_service_h", "other_h"]
assert len(df) == 30 and df["case_id"].is_unique

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- 2. crude + stratified
section("2a. CRUDE comparison (association only: routing sends difficult cases to Orion)")
crude = df.groupby("resource_group").agg(
    cases=("case_id", "size"), mean_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "mean"), median_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
    mean_wait=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"), mean_service=("review_service_h", "mean"),
    mean_other=("other_h", "mean"), high_pct=("complexity", lambda s: 100 * (s == "High").mean()),
    night_pct=("shift", lambda s: 100 * (s == "Night").mean()),
    compliance_pct=("compliance_required", lambda s: 100 * (s == "Yes").mean()),
    rework_pct=("rework", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()), appeals=("appeal", "sum"))

    cases=("case_id", "size"), mean_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "mean"), median_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
    min_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "min"), max_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "max"),
    mean_wait=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"), mean_service=("review_service_h", "mean"),
    mean_other=("other_h", "mean"), rework_pct=("rework", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()))
print(strat.round(2))

    "Orion's own mix (what Orion's cases would average)": df.loc[df.resource_group == "Orion", "complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),

        print(diff.round(2).to_string())
        for name, w in weights.items():

            print(f"\n  weights = {name}: " + ", ".join(f"{k}={v:.3f}" for k, v in w.items()))

            print(f"    standardized Orion mean: {(cell['Orion'] * w).sum():6.2f} h")
            print(f"    standardized Orion - Nova: {(diff * w).sum():+.2f} h   (crude was {crude_diff:+.2f} h)")

print("\nDirectly-follows frequencies (absolute and per case):")

vs = df.groupby(["complexity", "resource_group", "trace"], observed=True).size().rename("cases").reset_index()

                   .transform("sum")).round(1)

section("6. Fragility of the within-complexity contrast")
print("Leave-one-case-out range of the pooled-weight standardized Orion - Nova difference:")
w = df["complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True).reindex(LEVELS)  # weights held fixed
loo = []
for cid in df["case_id"]:
    cell = df[df.case_id != cid].groupby(["complexity", "resource_group"], observed=True)["cycle_time_h"].mean().unstack()
    loo.append(((cell["Orion"] - cell["Nova"]) * w).sum())
print(f"  min {min(loo):+.2f} h, max {max(loo):+.2f} h (sampling fragility only; says nothing about confounding)")

print("\nBias needed from an unobserved, cycle-lengthening case-risk factor to erase the standardized gap:")
gap = abs(std_diff_pooled)
print("  bias ~= (prevalence among Nova cases - prevalence among Orion cases) x (hours added by the factor)")
for effect_h in [2, 4, 6, 10]:
    print(f"  factor adds {effect_h:>2} h -> needs a within-stratum prevalence imbalance of {100 * gap / effect_h:5.1f} "

print("  Reference: the Low -> Medium step in mean cycle time is about "
      f"{df[df.complexity == 'Medium'].cycle_time_h.mean() - df[df.complexity == 'Low'].cycle_time_h.mean():.1f} h, "
      "so coarse complexity labels leave room for such a factor.")

print("DIAGNOSTIC ONLY: none of the above identifies a causal effect of resource group; "

When run, the script reports a crude difference of +6.71 h, stratum differences of −2.14, −2.21 and −3.43 h, a pooled-mix standardised difference of −2.51 h, identical edge sets in the two group DFGs (they differ only in frequencies that mirror compliance and rework mix), and 40 support warnings. ## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Selection on unobserved case risk within complexity levels.** It is unknown why two High cases went to Nova and two Low cases to Orion, and whether the router sees difficulty that the three-level label hides. Most valuable addition: the routing decision log, with the rule or reason code fired and the inputs used for each case.
2. **Group, shift and load cannot be separated.** Thirteen of fourteen Orion cases are Night/High-load and every Night case is High load, and the wait field mixes queueing with compliance waiting. Most valuable addition: start and complete timestamps for every activity with the resource ID, which allow queue length and staffing to be reconstructed at each queue entry.
3. **Completed-case selection.** Slow, open or abandoned cases may be missing unevenly between groups. Most valuable addition: the inventory of open and abandoned cases with registration time and current state, so that time to close can be analysed with censoring.
4. **How each queue responds to changed volume.** Waiting is a system state, so the effect of moving work depends on utilisation and on certification at Nova, neither of which is observed. Most valuable design change: randomised diversion at two or more intensities in alternating time blocks, which measures the spill-over onto Nova's existing cases.
5. **Quality and rework consequences.** Two appeals and seven rework cases cannot show whether speed would be bought with worse decisions. Most valuable addition: a structured quality-audit score recorded at closure for a random sample of cases in each group, which is not dependent on post-process factors.
