# Robust Redesign of the Diagnostic-Lab Sample Process

The core problem is not that the average is bad; the median Routine (38h) and Urgent (11h) numbers hide a process whose bottleneck **migrates** depending on which case type dominates and which resource degrades. Peak data already shows this: as the mix shifts toward Urgent (18%->29%) and Complex-panel (13%->20%), the binding constraint jumps from Pre-analytical check to Assay machines to Technical/Medical validation, and Complex-panel p90 explodes from 118h to 210h. Any redesign optimized for the historical average will fail the moment the mix or a resource shifts. The design below is therefore built around **priority segregation, load-adaptive triggers, and monitoring the leading edge of migration** rather than blanket capacity.

---

## 1. Stress scenarios

Each scenario fixes a case-mix and disruption combination so the bottleneck-migration logic can be traced against the DFG, queue metrics, and performance edges.

**S1 — Urgent + Complex surge (mix shock, no equipment failure).**
Mix moves further than the observed peak: Urgent 35%, Complex-panel 22%, Routine 35%, Repeat 8%; total volume only +15% over normal. This isolates *mix* from *volume*. Urgent bypass of Batch wait (74%->58% and falling further under load) fragments assay schedules; Complex-panel drives 3.1x Medical validation and 2.4x Hold-for-specialist demand.

**S2 — Assay-machine degradation (M2 partial outage).**
M2 (higher failure rate, faster runtime) drops to ~50% availability for one week during ordinary peak mix. All Routine that I3 would route to M2 crowds onto M1, and Repeat-after-failure cases (which consume assay capacity twice and cluster after M2 use) multiply. Assay utilization is already 98% at peak, so there is no slack.

**S3 — Medical validator / specialist-review absence.**
Two of the pool of medical validators are out (illness) and it is the "second Friday" when one specialist is unavailable. Medical-validation queue is already 22h and specialist-review 36h at peak. This scenario hits Complex-panel and Urgent hardest because both *require* medical validation, and Urgent has an 18h contractual ceiling.

**S4 — Courier compression / delay (front-door shock).**
Winter road disruption compresses both the 10:00 and 17:00 route spikes into a single late-afternoon flood and delays ~30% of intake by 3–6h. Courier intake queue (7.5h peak) balloons; arrivals hit Pre-analytical check (already 96% util, 9.5h queue) as a wall rather than a stream.

**S5 (combination stressor for robustness testing) — Winter wave: S1 mix + S2 M2 degradation + intermittent S3 absence.**
Used only to test whether the redesign degrades gracefully rather than collapsing; it is the realistic "bad week."

## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Baseline chain of stations: Courier intake -> Registration -> Pre-analytical check -> (Accession correction loop) -> Preparation (Batch wait) -> Assay (Repeat loop) -> Technical validation -> Medical validation -> (Specialist hold) -> Release.

**S1 — Urgent + Complex surge.**
1. First pressure lands at **Preparation + Assay**: more Urgent means more Batch-wait bypass, so preparation loses batching efficiency and assay setups fragment (M1/M2 at 98% has no room to absorb setup churn). `Test preparation -> Batch wait` and `Batch wait -> Assay run` performance edges (6/14 and 9/22h) worsen for the *Routine* residue that still batches.
2. Relieving assay throughput (e.g. Urgent-first dispatch, I1) immediately **migrates the bottleneck downstream to validation**: more Complex-panel means the `Technical validation -> Medical validation` edge (8->24h) and Medical-validation queue (2->22h) dominate. Technical validators are already at 101% peak util.
3. If you then add medical-validation capacity, the bottleneck migrates again to **Specialist review** (Complex-panel is 2.4x more likely to enter Hold; `Hold -> Medical validation` edge is 18->48h). So S1 is a three-hop migration: Assay -> Medical validation -> Specialist review, with Routine and Complex-panel starving if I1 is left unguarded.

**S2 — M2 degradation.**
1. Bottleneck is immediately **Assay (M1)**. With M2 down, either Routine floods M1 (if I3 is active) or everything competes on M1. Utilization pins at 100%+, `Assay run -> Technical validation` (5->15h) stretches for all types.
2. **Repeat loop amplifies**: Repeat-after-failure already consumes assay capacity twice and clusters after M2 usage; forcing volume onto the machine mix raises `Assay run -> Repeat assay` and `Repeat assay -> Assay run` (10->26h), a positive-feedback congestion loop.
3. Relieving assay by pushing Routine to a partner or holding it migrates pressure to **Technical validation** once the backlog clears in bursts (bursty release = bursty validation demand). So S2: Assay -> Repeat-loop self-amplification -> bursty Technical validation.

**S3 — Medical validator / specialist absence.**
1. Upstream stations look *healthy* (this is the trap): assay and technical validation keep producing, so **work piles at Medical validation** (queue 2->22h even normally under load) and **Specialist review** (5->36h).
2. Because Urgent and Complex-panel *both* require medical validation, Urgent breaches its 18h penalty ceiling even though every earlier step met target — the failure is invisible until Release.
3. Any upstream acceleration (I8 automation, I1 dispatch) **makes S3 worse** by delivering more cases to a closed downstream gate. This is the clearest case where relieving one station overloads the next.

**S4 — Courier compression/delay.**
1. Bottleneck starts at **Courier intake + Pre-analytical check**. A compressed arrival wall against a 96%-utilized pre-analytical step spikes the `Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check` (2->9h) and `Pre-analytical check -> Test preparation` (4->13h) edges.
2. Pre-analytical failures create **recollection loops** (`Request recollection -> Receive sample` 42->60h) that *reduce intake capacity further* because clerks explain errors to physicians — a second feedback loop that shrinks the front door exactly when it is flooded.
3. Once the wave clears intake, it propagates as a **bolus** to Assay and then validation, recreating S1/S2 downstream migration with a time lag. So S4 front-loads a bottleneck that then travels the whole line.

## 3. Robust redesign package

These are chosen to hold up across S1–S4, not to optimize the average. Each names the station it may overload.

**R1 — Two-lane segregation with a capped Urgent lane (refined I1 + I3).**
Run a dedicated Urgent/Complex assay lane on **M1** and a Routine/batched lane on **M2**, but cap the Urgent lane at a WIP ceiling so it cannot fully starve Routine/Complex (addresses the I1 conflict hint).
- *Why robust:* protects the 18h Urgent penalty in S1 and S3-adjacent surges, and keeps Routine batching efficient.
- *Assumption:* M2 is available. **Fails in S2** (M2 down) — see scenario policy P2. Also risks overloading **Technical validation** because faster assay throughput arrives sooner; monitor validator queue.

**R2 — Priority-aware batching, not bigger batches (bounded I2).**
Keep Routine batching but **bound batch size dynamically** (12 default, up to 24 only when Urgent share < a threshold) and allow controlled Urgent exceptions rather than full bypass.
- *Why robust:* preserves preparation setup efficiency without the schedule fragmentation that pure Urgent bypass causes.
- *Fails where:* if Urgent share is very high (S1 extreme), batches shrink toward 12 and preparation-bench efficiency drops; it then leans on R1's lane separation. Downstream risk: smaller batches push more discrete jobs to **Assay**.

**R3 — Early pre-analytical defect triage (I6) + partial automated registration (guarded I8).**
Reject/flag poor samples within 2h and auto-register only orders with complete digital metadata **but route auto-registered orders through the same pre-analytical check** (do not let I8 bypass it).
- *Why robust:* attacks the root of recollection loops that shrink intake in S4 and inflate rework everywhere (rework 7.5%->11.8% at peak). Cutting recollection frees clerk time (the "explaining errors to physicians" drain).
- *Assumption/failure:* I8 alone would "expose pre-analytical check and assay bottlenecks" (hint) — so R3 deliberately couples automation *with* triage. Overload risk: triage concentrates load on **Accession correction specialists** (only 2, 91% peak util, 14h queue), so R3 depends on R4.

**R4 — Elastic accession-correction capacity (guarded I4).**
Cross-train technical validators for accession correction **but activate the second role only when accession-correction queue crosses a trigger**, and never pull more than one validator at a time.
- *Why robust:* corrections "unblock many cases"; relieving the 2-specialist chokepoint helps S3 and S4.
- *Named overload:* the hint warns I4 "may create a shortage in technical validation" — hence the trigger + one-at-a-time cap. Monitor technical-validation queue when this activates.

**R5 — Courier slot smoothing (I7).**
Shift pickup slots to flatten the 10:00/17:00 spikes.
- *Why robust:* purely front-door, low downstream risk, directly mitigates S4 and reduces the arrival-wall effect that seeds all downstream migration.
- *Fails where:* cannot help if the disruption is external road delay (S4b); then it must combine with triage (R3) to avoid recollection amplification. Slight risk of shifting the arrival peak onto Registration if slots are mis-tuned.

Together R1–R5 give: protected Urgent path, controlled batching, lower rework at the source, an elastic correction valve, and a smoother front door — robust because each addresses a *different* migration hop and none assumes blanket capacity.

## 4. Scenario-specific policies (with triggers)

**P1 — Urgent-strict + Complex-protect dispatch (activate in S1).**
- *On when:* rolling 6h Urgent share > 30% **and** Complex-panel WIP rising.
- *Action:* enforce R1 caps tightly; add I5 evening medical-validation shift; pre-reserve a medical-validator slot per hour for Complex-panel so it is not starved by Urgent.
- *Off when:* Urgent share < 25% for 6h.

**P2 — Assay-degradation reroute (activate in S2).**
- *On when:* M2 availability < 70% **or** assay-repeat rate > baseline +50%.
- *Action:* suspend "Routine->M2" split; run Routine in *large* batches on M1 during low-Urgent windows; activate I10 partner outsourcing for Complex-panel overflow to protect M1 for Urgent (accept 12h transport delay because Complex target is 64–118h, not 18h).
- *Off when:* M2 restored and repeat rate normalized for 12h.

**P3 — Downstream-gate protection (activate in S3).**
- *On when:* medical-validation queue > 12h **or** specialist-review queue > 24h **or** a known specialist-absent Friday.
- *Action:* (a) **throttle upstream acceleration** — do not run I8 full-speed into a closed gate; (b) add I5 evening medical shift; (c) trigger I10 for Complex-panel to bypass the local specialist-review queue; (d) reserve remaining medical-validation capacity for Urgent first to protect the 18h penalty.
- *Off when:* medical queue < 6h.

**P4 — Front-door smoothing / recollection clamp (activate in S4).**
- *On when:* courier-intake queue > 4h **or** pre-analytical queue > 6h.
- *Action:* enforce R5 slot smoothing; escalate R3 triage aggressiveness; temporarily add a registration clerk from overtime **only up to 9h shifts** (error rate rises after 9h — do not exceed).
- *Off when:* pre-analytical queue < 3h.

Policies are mutually composable; in S5 (winter wave) P1+P2+P3 co-activate, and the WIP caps in R1 prevent Urgent from consuming the whole line while I10 offloads Complex-panel.

| **R4** Elastic accession correction (I4, triggered) | S3, S4 | + | + | + | − (fewer stalls) | Technical validation (if over-pulled) | Low–Med (4wk cross-train) | Accession queue; tech-val queue |

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Specialist review remains the deepest single point of failure.** Even with I5 and I10, specialist review (43%->87% util, 5->36h, one absent every second Friday) has almost no true elasticity; a two-specialist-absent week under S3 will still breach Complex-panel and possibly Urgent.
- **Repeat-loop feedback under S2 is only mitigated, not solved.** If M1 also degrades while M2 is down, the twice-consumed assay capacity of Repeat-after-failure cases has no escape valve short of full outsourcing.
- **Vulnerability introduced by the redesign itself:** the **WIP-capped Urgent lane (R1) can strand Routine**. When the cap forces Routine to wait behind Urgent during a long S1 surge, Routine p90 (already 120h at peak) can drift past the 72h target for a subset of cases — we traded average performance for penalty protection, exactly the intended bias, but it *is* a self-inflicted Routine tail risk that must be watched.
- **Second self-inflicted risk:** R4 cross-training means the *same people* can be pulled two ways; a poorly-tuned trigger can oscillate staff between accession correction and technical validation, degrading both (bottleneck ping-pong).
- **I10 traceability debt:** outsourcing adds chain-of-custody complexity; a mislabel at the partner reappears as rework with a 12h+ round-trip.

## 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Monitor daily during peaks; separate **leading** (predict migration, act now) from **lagging** (confirm outcome, too late to fix today).

**Leading indicators (act on these):**
- **Rolling 6h Urgent + Complex share.** Warning band: >30% amber, >35% red -> arm P1.
- **Assay queue and M2 availability / repeat rate.** Amber: assay queue >8h or repeat rate >baseline+30%; red: M2 <70% avail -> arm P2. (Assay is the most volatile hop: 4h->18h.)
- **Medical-validation queue and specialist-review queue.** Amber: medical >12h / specialist >24h; red: medical >18h -> arm P3 and throttle upstream. This is the invisible-failure guard.
- **Accession-correction queue.** Amber >6h, red >10h -> arm R4 (watch tech-val queue simultaneously).
- **Courier-intake queue + arrival spikiness + recollection rate.** Amber: intake >4h or recollection rate rising; red: pre-analytical queue >6h -> arm P4/R5.
- **Pre-analytical failure %.** Leading indicator of the whole rework cascade; band: >8% amber, >11% red.

**Lagging outcome metrics (weekly / confirmatory, do NOT wait for these to act):**
- Urgent p90 vs 18h and count of penalty breaches.
- Routine / Complex-panel p90 vs 72h / target.
- Overall rework % (7.5% normal, 11.8% peak) and repeat-assay count.
- % of cases with any queue >12h (22% normal, 54% peak).

The design principle for the dashboard: because the bottleneck **migrates**, no single utilization number is sufficient — we watch the *queue at the next-in-line station* and the *mix* that predicts where pressure will land, so policies switch on before the outcome metrics degrade.
