## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January.** Establish the mature baseline. AUROC is 0.81, ECE is 0.025, the breach rate is 18%, and V3 accounts for 20% of starts. The median parts wait is 28 h.
- **February.** Discrimination and calibration remain close to January. The 31 h parts wait and near-identical variant mix provide a reference for ordinary variation over this short baseline.
- **March.** Use the last pre-migration month as an additional reference. AUROC is 0.81, ECE is 0.024, and the parts wait is 35 h. The three baseline months provide limited evidence about seasonality.
- **April.** The migration is the primary incident. Labels are fully mature and the parts median is 34 h, while AUROC drops to 0.67, Brier rises to 0.184, and mean predicted risk is 28% against a reported 19% breach rate. Wrong or absent prefixes, duplicates, and 18% unmapped traces undermine both scores and variant assignments. Missing predictions can also change which cases enter the metrics. Confirm the size of the performance impact with a paired replay and a separate scoring-coverage analysis.
- **May.** Following the 6 May patch, unmapped traces return to 1%, unknown events fall to 2%, and AUROC recovers to 0.78. ECE is still 0.050 versus 0.022–0.025 at baseline. Check pre- and post-patch serving records and source-specific deduplication. Historical April feature rows still require explicit recomputation.
- **June.** The new policy shifts work toward the faster, lower-breach V2. Its share increases by 18 percentage points from May, and the mature overall breach rate falls to 15%. AUROC of 0.79 is near baseline, but the mean prediction is 20% and ECE is 0.060. A changed path mix can move aggregate risk even if path-specific risks are stable. The policy can also change risk for otherwise similar prefixes by changing where they are routed. The summary establishes the routing shift and calibration gap, while conditional tests are needed to distinguish these mechanisms.
- **July.** The supplier misses targets from 10 July. The parts edge reaches 65 h and matured V3 breaches reach 46%, up from 35% in June. V2 remains close to June on share and breach rate. At 98% maturity the monthly result is much less exposed to ascertainment bias than autumn results, although pre- and post-10 July orders and comparable suppliers offer sharper contrasts.
- **August.** The parts wait doubles to 130 h, V3 rises from 22% to 35% of starts, and its matured breach rate rises from 46% to 64%. Standardizing with July’s variant rates and August’s mix yields 24.4%, versus 19.9% at July’s mix. Applying August’s variant rates at the same August mix yields 31.3%. This illustrates both a mix and a within-variant component. The shares cover starts and the rates cover matured outcomes, so reconciling these figures to the dashboard’s 28% requires common denominators. Mean risk is 19%, AUROC is 0.68, and maturity is 93%. The supplier evidence supports genuine deterioration, with uncertain magnitude on the full cohort.
- **September.** The parts wait reaches 148 h, V3 rises to 42% of starts, and V3’s matured breach rate stays high at 61%. This rate is slightly lower than August’s even as the reported overall rate rises to 31%. Relative to an all-non-Z mix at September’s non-Z routing rate, adding Partner Z raises the expected V3 share by roughly 5 percentage points (0.24 × (0.58 − 0.36)). The supplier slowdown precedes onboarding, so both the existing operational problem and the new population matter. At 82% overall maturity and slower Partner Z labeling, the observed source risk gap and AUROC of 0.66 are preliminary.
- **October.** The recorded parts median is 155 h and the mix remains parts-heavy. Only 55% of labels are available, with long paths disproportionately missing. The reported breach rate falls to 16% while mean risk is 23% and AUROC is 0.64. The apparent reversal from underprediction to overprediction can arise from selective availability. The cohort’s eventual breach rate, discrimination, and calibration remain unresolved, and the performance DFG should also be checked for open-order censoring.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Measure maturity and replay fixed vintages.** Freeze the case-start cohort, prediction eligibility, original scores, and model version. Record case age, diagnosis time, promise deadline, outcome occurrence, and first label availability separately. Plot fractions labeled by age for each month, source, and available route or duration group. Compare metrics at common follow-up ages and again on the same cohorts once labels settle. Use retrospective complete routes for audit only, and show coverage for paths still open. Under API delay, the October curve shifts right, especially for longer paths, and the metrics move as those same cases acquire labels. Under genuine model deterioration, adverse differences persist at comparable high maturity and within matched segments. Leave unresolved labels pending. If early estimates are needed, report bounds or sensitivity analyses. Use inverse-probability-of-observation weighting only with credible conditional ascertainment and adequate coverage.
2. **Replay the activity and duplicate repairs.** Build an immutable replay from raw events with a versioned old/new diagnosis alias, consistent idempotent deduplication, and deterministic event ordering. Recompute prefixes and cached counts with the frozen model and only events available at the intended scoring instant. Compare original and replay scoring coverage, actual versus intended anchors, feature values, scores, and metrics on paired cases. Report newly recovered cases separately. Under a pipeline failure, April’s unknown or duplicate artifacts and missing prefixes shrink, and April scores or metrics materially recover on a clean replay. Under a real risk change, correct anchors and features can recover while paired predictive deterioration persists. Compare old and new sources and periods around 20 April and 6 May.
3. **Stratify discrimination and calibration.** For month × source × variant, report eligible, scored, and labeled counts, observed and expected breaches on the same cases, reliability curves, calibration intercept and slope, Brier, ECE with consistent bins, and AUROC with uncertainty. Suppress unstable cells or pool them transparently. Also track all-eligible score distributions. Standardize performance through common stratum weights on case-level data to preserve comparisons across strata for AUROC. Under composition change, much of the aggregate difference shrinks under common weights and performance within comparable strata is broadly stable. Under within-stratum risk or ranking deterioration, calibration or AUROC changes remain in the affected strata. Compare fixed-vintage results to see whether incomplete labels explain apparent subgroup gaps.
4. **Compare routes and performance DFGs on clean events.** Compare exact trace and route shares, edge counts and case support, new or disappearing edges, and elapsed-time quantiles on matched event-calendar and case-start views. Use cleaned logs with comparable follow-up. For parts, also cohort by order date, count pending and canceled orders, and compare time to receipt with pending orders right-censored and cancellations accounted for separately. Check the remote edge and, where available, unaffected suppliers or locations as references. Under the routing-policy explanation, V2 transitions gain share in June while time from diagnosis to Remote Fix stays stable. Under the supplier explanation, waits lengthen within the parts path after 10 July, even after controlling for route share. Under the migration-artifact explanation, much of April’s graph change disappears with canonical activity names and deduplication.
5. **Test risk at the same prefix and on the same path.** On clean, mature cohorts, match or standardize to common diagnosis-time X, source, and promise slack where available, and compare breach risk before and after July. Test time and time-by-path effects with uncertainty, especially for V3 and the same supplier or service area. First estimate changes conditional on the deployed X. Then use realized path for a retrospective within-path comparison, recognizing that it is downstream of diagnosis and its membership can change. Under pure route or population composition, standardized risks within comparable paths stay broadly stable. Under supplier-related conditional drift, excess V3 risk persists at comparable prefixes and is concentrated in exposed orders or periods. Check whether adding lead-time information actually available at diagnosis accounts for the residual. Keep realized route and receipt time for retrospective analysis.
6. **Transport the Partner Z comparison.** Compare September non-Z cases against earlier non-Z cases under comparable supplier exposure and follow-up. At a common vintage, reweight source groups over overlapping region, diagnosis-time route eligibility, severity, promise slack, and available case attributes. As a retrospective decomposition, also standardize realized variant mix. Report overlap, effective sample sizes, and how much of Z falls outside support. Under population composition, the source gap shrinks under common weights, and weighting to the September mix predicts much of the aggregate movement. Under a residual within-source or operational change, conditional gaps persist or non-Z deteriorates relative to its own history. Estimate how slower Z labels affect both comparisons before attributing a residual to model behavior.
7. **Audit missingness and feature lineage.** By day, source, and schema version, reconcile raw records, canonical diagnosis counts, duplicate IDs, unknown activities, prediction coverage, join failures, queue missingness, cached feature timestamps, and online/offline parity on sampled cases. Monitor cleaned marginal diagnosis-time feature distributions within stable sources and on the pooled population, with reference ranges for ordinary variation. Check the lineage of the promise date and outcome API against the agreed target definition. Under pipeline failure, breaks align with schema, deduplication, or cache boundaries and disappear after correction. Under genuine covariate change, features remain reproducible from upstream events and distribution differences persist on intact records. Under a label-only incident, feature and scoring audits remain healthy while event-to-label availability changes.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Contain active failures.** Route cases with an invalid diagnosis anchor or unreliable required features to an established manual triage path. Audit who received a score and what actions it triggered. Mark September and October outcome metrics as preliminary, reconcile the outcome backlog, and use direct parts-delay evidence for supplier escalation and case review. Preserve the original scores and data vintages for audit.
2. **Repair features before fitting anything to affected periods.** Apply a versioned alias map and verified duplicate rules to both sources and historical partitions, rebuild prefixes and all dependent features, invalidate stale caches, and verify online/offline parity at the diagnosis cutoff. Run the repaired pipeline alongside serving and compare coverage, anchors, and risk on the same cases before switching over.
3. **Recalibrate only on comparable, mature post-change data with adequate ranking.** June is a candidate for a routing-regime calibration check once feature health and stable within-segment discrimination are verified. Estimate intercept-only or intercept-and-slope recalibration on an appropriate mature window and verify on a later holdout in the same intended regime. June’s calibrator has limited relevance to a supplier-stressed deployment. A monotone recalibrator can improve probabilities when ranking holds and cannot repair the within-segment ranking losses suggested for August–September. Validate thresholds against decision costs and service capacity on the same mature target population.
4. **Select training windows by feature validity, label completeness, and deployment relevance.** January–March are clean, mature baseline data. April is unsafe as logged and becomes eligible only after replay and verification of each included case’s prefix and features. May is eligible by source and period after validation of repair coverage and feature lineage. June is label-safe and useful for the new routing regime after ordinary feature checks. July is transitional, so preserve the 10 July breakpoint and check the remaining labels for selection before using it. Defer routine training on August and September cutoff extracts, particularly Partner Z, until label completeness or a defensible ascertainment analysis supports their use. October’s cutoff extract is unsafe for outcome training. Preserve genuinely later cohorts for time-ordered evaluation, and weight historical regimes according to which operating conditions the deployed model will face.
5. **Retrain if mature evidence shows persistent conditional error or poor ranking.** Compare the repaired model, a recalibrated version, and a retrained candidate on forward holdouts covering the relevant supplier regime and sources. Consider promise slack, supplier backlog or expected lead time, rural travel, and device age where measured consistently and available at diagnosis. Audit whether the selected features and model still work when supplier conditions change again. A shared model with interactions or partial pooling is a reasonable first candidate if mature data confirm heterogeneity. A separate Partner Z or route model needs enough labeled positives and negatives, stable performance gains on later data, and a segment or route signal known at scoring. The final V3 trace can define an audit slice. Use diagnosis-time eligibility or routing information available at that instant for serving-time segmentation.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Use an immutable prediction ledger linked to versioned event and outcome records by case ID. Store the intended and actual diagnosis anchor, scoring timestamp, feature and schema versions, score, threshold, and deployment version. Attach first outcome availability and later label corrections as separate timestamps. Maintain case-start cohorts for outcome evaluation and event-calendar views for immediate operational monitoring. Show the as-of timestamp, denominators, and coverage in both.

| Monitor | Signals and alert dependencies |
|---|---|
| Data quality | Monitor anchor coverage and uniqueness, scoring coverage, unknown activities, duplicate and order anomalies, queue missingness, freshness, and feature parity by source and version. A landmark or parity failure alerts the pipeline owner and can gate affected scores and feature-based drift conclusions. |
| Label maturity | Monitor label fractions at fixed ages, ingest backlog, age and path distributions of unlabeled cases, partner-specific delays, and corrections. A maturity alert marks affected evaluation slices preliminary and opens a feed reconciliation incident. It gates label-dependent retraining decisions. |
| Predictive performance | Track mature AUROC, precision and recall at operating thresholds, Brier, and threshold utility with case counts and uncertainty. Show same-vintage comparisons for early warning. Escalate persistent changes on sufficiently mature, pipeline-valid cohorts and use affected strata to choose the next diagnostic. |
| Calibration | Track mean observed and expected risk on the same cohort, reliability curves, calibration intercept and slope, and ECE with fixed bins. Assess calibration by variant, source, and month. Open a recalibration review when a gap persists with adequate maturity and ranking. |
| Process variants | Track variant frequencies, unmapped and incomplete shares, canonical DFG edges, and new routes in event-calendar and case-start cohorts. Check source and schema alarms before assigning a structural interpretation. |
| Performance DFG | Monitor activity-pair counts and elapsed-time quantiles, including both the parts and remote edges. Use consistent time units and follow-up, and track open-order ages and cancellations alongside completed-edge times. Route credible supplier wait alerts to operations even while labels are backlogged. |
| Population and features | Track source and partner shares, rural and device profiles where captured, as-of feature and score distributions, missingness, and overlap by segment. Use clean input data and adequate case support. Review mix-standardized and within-segment performance once labels permit. |
| Intervention and deployment | Record routing policies, partner launches, mapping and cache changes, supplier incidents, model and threshold deployments, and decisions triggered by scores. Track review, overrides, and expedites by segment. Use timestamps and exposed versus comparable cohorts to assess how these changes affect outcomes and metrics. |

Require sufficient support and persistence for performance alarms, with reference ranges derived from comparable clean cohorts. A label-delay alarm leaves feed recovery and label-independent operational monitoring active. After feature integrity and maturity checks pass, a calibration gap with stable ranking opens a recalibration review. Sustained conditional or ranking deterioration opens a retraining review and forward validation.

The sketch assumes a frozen UTC event export with globally unique `case_id`, `activity`, and `timestamp`, ordered by source sequence for timestamp ties. It uses optional `event_id` and `source` when available. Stable IDs support dropping repeated copies of the same canonical record. Conflicting payloads and rows without verified IDs remain available for investigation. Event-calendar traces describe activity occurring inside the month and can be partial at either boundary. Case-start traces follow cases from their recorded creation through the export cutoff. Compare full-path shares at comparable follow-up and report the number of incomplete traces.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACTIVITY, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"


def monthly_process_evidence(as_of, path="field_service_events.csv"):
    cutoff = pd.to_datetime(as_of, utc=True)  # Exclusive export cutoff
    raw = pd.read_csv(path, dtype={"case_id": "string", "event_id": "string"})
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    invalid = raw[["case_id", "timestamp"]].isna().any(axis=1)
    events = raw.loc[~invalid & raw["timestamp"].lt(cutoff)].copy()
    events["activity"] = events["activity"].replace(
        {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
    ).fillna("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")

    duplicates = pd.Series(False, index=events.index)
    if "event_id" in events:
        keys = ["event_id", "case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
        if "source" in events:
            keys.append("source")
        duplicates = events["event_id"].notna() & events.duplicated(keys)
    dropped = events.loc[duplicates, "timestamp"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").value_counts()
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        events.loc[~duplicates].copy(),
        case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )
    starts = log.loc[log[ACTIVITY].eq("Case Created")].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()

    def summarize(frame):
        summary = {
            "cases": frame[CASE].nunique(),
            "events": len(frame),
            "unknown_events": int(frame[ACTIVITY].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").sum()),
        }
        if not frame.empty:
            summary["dfg"], _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(frame)
            summary["performance_dfg_seconds"], _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(frame)
            summary["observed_trace_variants"] = pm4py.get_variants(frame)
            summary["temporal_features"] = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
                frame, grouper_freq="MS"
            )
        return summary

    boundaries = pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-11-01", freq="MS", tz="UTC")
    months = {}
    for left, right in zip(boundaries[:-1], boundaries[1:]):
        month = left.strftime("%Y-%m")
        window = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, left.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"),
            right.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), mode="events",
        )
        window = window.loc[window[TS].ge(left) & window[TS].lt(right)]
        start_ids = starts.index[starts.ge(left) & starts.lt(right)]
        cohort = log.loc[log[CASE].isin(start_ids)].copy()
        closed_ids = cohort.loc[cohort[ACTIVITY].eq("Case Closed"), CASE]
        closed = cohort.loc[cohort[CASE].isin(closed_ids)]
        started = summarize(cohort)
        started["cases_with_close"] = closed_ids.nunique()
        started["closed_trace_variants"] = (
            pm4py.get_variants(closed) if not closed.empty else {}
        )
        months[month] = {
            "event_calendar": summarize(window),
            "case_start": started,
            "duplicate_rows_removed_by_event_month": int(dropped.get(month, 0)),
        }
    return {
        "as_of_exclusive_utc": cutoff,
        "invalid_case_or_timestamp_rows": int(invalid.sum()),
        "cases_without_creation_event": log[CASE].nunique() - len(starts),
        "months": months,
    }
```

Historical prediction replay and label-maturity analysis also require availability timestamps or preserved as-of snapshots, since an event’s business timestamp can precede its arrival.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Wrong action | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retrain on the currently labeled October cases or code outstanding outcomes as nonbreaches. | Training favors short paths and can suppress warnings on long parts cases. Offline gains may disappear after the backlog clears. | Freeze scores, reconcile the feed, and validate on fixed vintages and mature cohorts with coverage by path and source. |
| Calibrate around April’s broken prefixes. | The probability adjustment absorbs a temporary engineering defect and still leaves some eligible cases unscored or incorrectly scored. It can miscalibrate repaired traffic. | Repair and replay first, verify coverage and feature parity, then assess calibration on valid scores. |
| Explain August solely by a higher V3 share or by delayed labels. | The rise in V3 risk and parts waits goes unaddressed, leaving high-risk jobs underprioritized. | Monitor pending orders and within-V3 outcomes, escalate the supplier bottleneck, and test whether mature conditional drift warrants new features or retraining. |
| Infer Partner Z unfairness from the unadjusted September breach gap or fit a separate model immediately. | Unequal label maturity, route selection, and rural case mix can create misleading comparisons. A small standalone sample can produce unstable decisions. | Audit calibration, errors, and allocation of service interventions with mature labels and overlap-aware comparisons. Require forward evidence before adopting a separate model. |
| Treat June’s aggregate calibration gap as proof that ranking collapsed. | Full retraining could add variance and lose useful ranking while the main change is who takes the remote route. | Check stable-stratum discrimination and conditional calibration, standardize the mix, and validate a small calibration update if the evidence supports it. |
| Use final V3 status or actual part-receipt time to improve historical scores. | Look-ahead leakage creates performance gains that cannot be reproduced after diagnosis. | Enforce as-of joins and feature lineage. Restrict complete path and elapsed-time evidence to audits and use only eligible diagnosis-time signals in serving. |
