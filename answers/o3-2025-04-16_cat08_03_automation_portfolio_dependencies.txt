## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map  

• Up-stream data quality  
– A1  A2  A3/A5: dirty or duplicate applications degrade classification and KYC pre-screen accuracy.  
– A5  A6: missing-doc portal must provide parsable statements for extraction.  

• Legal / compliance controls  
– A3 must log every score and human override for audit; output feeds A4, A9, A10.  
– A9 consumes Committee decision, KYC status, collateral values; wrong sequence violates four-eyes and legal-term match rules.  
– A12 provides a safety net for any control violations generated by A3, A9, or provisional signing.  

• Human escalation capacity  
– A3 false-positive queue must stay within 3 FTE specialist bandwidth; A4 likewise needs their expertise.  
– A6 errors escalate to analysts; if extraction accuracy < 85 % it can swamp 9 FTE pool.  

• External valuation dependency  
– A7 can only triage; final SLA remains outside control. Parallel requests or preferred-valuer routing reduce the 58 h wait but do not remove it.  

• Core-banking integration constraints (max two deep links in first six months)  
– Deep integration candidates: A1 (intake API), A9 (contract templates & booking), A3 (KYC engine), A11 (activation posting).  
– Limiting rule: pick only two of those for phase-1; the rest must use file/interface stubs initially.  

## 3. Portfolio alternatives  

1. Quick-win portfolio  
Modules: A1, A5, A10, A9  
Optimises: visible FTE savings and customer-facing speed.  
Sacrifices: KYC control strengthening; still exposed to entity-mismatch and collateral waits; risk of analyst overload if cleaner inflow spikes.  

2. Bottleneck / synchronisation portfolio  
Modules: A4, A5, A6, A7  
Optimises: removes three longest median waits (entity mismatch 31 h, missing docs 8 h loop, financial normalisation 20 h, collateral 58 h variance).  
Sacrifices: fewer political “volume” wins; needs analyst error-handling; only moderate direct FTE savings.  

3. Compliance-first portfolio  
Modules: A3, A4, A9, A12  
Optimises: KYC accuracy, entity resolution, contract/decision alignment, audit trail.  
Sacrifices: still leaves document and collateral waits; minimal customer-visible speedups in first months.  

## 4. Expected process-level effects  

1. Quick-win  
Cycle time: 10-15 % (front and tail).  
Waiting time: 44 h signature wait down to ~12 h where e-sign adopted; 22 h contract wait cut by A9.  
Rework: contract remediation loops drop from 8.5 % to <3 %.  
Handovers: back-office load down; Legal ops peaks smoothed.  
Resource demand: Analysts may see +11-13 % inflow speed; risk of queue formation.  
Control violations: Slight improvement on contract/decision mismatch; no change to four-eyes or provisional signing.  

2. Bottleneck / synchronisation  
Cycle time: 20-28 % on cases hitting entity mismatch, missing docs, or collateral; 12 % overall.  
Waiting time: 31 h (entity)  6 h; 8 h doc loop  2 h median; collateral variance 15 h spread.  
Rework: Missing-doc repeated loop down from 52 % to ~20 %.  
Handovers: Fewer ping-pong loops between RM  back office  customer.  
Resource demand: Analyst touch time down 35%; queue remains balanced.  
Control violations: Little direct effect, but fewer provisional signings because A4 clears KYC earlier.  

3. Compliance-first  
Cycle time: 8-12 % overall; larger on KYC-heavy cases.  
Waiting time: KYC queue reduced by 40 %; contract wait stable (A9 helps).  
Rework: Contract remediation down; KYC false-hit rework down.  
Handovers: Fewer escalations to two senior specialists.  
Resource demand: Specialists freed 25 %, but back-office and analyst load almost unchanged.  
Control violations: Four-eyes breach monitor embedded; provisional signing prevented (system hard stop).  

## 5. Risks and compensating controls  

1. Auditability risk (A3, A2, A6)  
Control: store model version, input snapshot, confidence score, human override flag. Quarterly model-drift review.  

2. False negative KYC hits (A3)  
Control: 100 % human review of scores below threshold for first 3 months; dual approval on overrides.  

3. Mis-extracted financials (A6)  
Control: sampling QA by analysts; auto-highlight low-confidence fields. Escalation queue capped at 20/day.  

4. Contract rule library drift (A9)  
Control: weekly diff check against legal master; forced regression test before deployment.  

5. Four-eyes principle violation after automation speed-up  
Control: enforcement rule in workflow engine; automatic block if same user ID appears in Credit-analysis and Committee-packaging for same case.  

6. Overload of analysts or specialists due to cleaner upstream flow  
Control: WIP cap and real-time dashboard; temporary bottleneck diverts back to manual pool.  

## 6. Recommended phased rollout  

Phase 0 – foundation (Month 0-1)  
• Stand-up process KPIs from mining model: case cycle time, KYC queue, document-loop count, specialist utilisation.  
Gate: baseline metrics locked.  

Phase 1 – first deep integrations (Month 2-6)   limited to two  
• Deep link 1: A4 Entity-mismatch assistant (needs customer & core party tables).  
• Deep link 2: A5 Missing-doc portal & nudging (integrated to customer-facing portal).  
Parallel light integrations: A6 pilot on 15 % of statements (file drop), A7 triage using email routing.  
Metrics: Reduction of entity-mismatch median wait 70 %; doc-loop rate 35 %.  

Phase 2 – scale and compliance hardening (Month 7-12)  
• Promote A6 to 80 % coverage; integrate into credit model.  
• Formalise A7 with preferred valuer API.  
• Activate four-eyes enforcement rule and provisional-signing hard stop (configuration, not counted as a new module  meets compliance requirement).  
Gate: Analyst queue time not > 20 h median; collateral SLA variance ±20 %.  

Phase 3 – prepare next-year wave  
• Evaluate adding A3 or A9 based on residual pain points and freed IT bandwidth.  

## 7. Final recommendation  

Adopt the Bottleneck / Synchronisation portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A7) in the order shown above.

Rationale:  
• Process-mining shows that low-frequency but high-wait activities (entity mismatch, missing documents, external valuation) dominate end-to-end performance. Addressing them yields a larger cycle-time reduction ( 25 %) than automating the highest-frequency 8-minute registration step (would save only  1 % of total lead time).  

• Sequencing starts with A4 and A5 because they remove critical-path blockers without creating uncontrolled inflow. Only after missing-doc quality improves is it safe to speed up analyst work with A6. A7 then mitigates the longest external wait, smoothing downstream utilisation so Legal ops and Operations are not subjected to month-end spikes.  

• This set still honours compliance: A4 ensures KYC clearance earlier, indirectly preventing provisional signing breaches; four-eyes enforcement can be configured in the workflow without consuming a module slot.  

• It respects the two deep-integration limit (A4 & A5 in phase-1) and keeps Relationship-manager friction low by giving them clearer instructions rather than new forms.  

Therefore, although automating the highest-frequency “Receive/Register” activity is politically tempting, the evidence shows that attacking the hidden, synchronisation-heavy waits delivers materially better business outcomes with acceptable risk and within budget.
