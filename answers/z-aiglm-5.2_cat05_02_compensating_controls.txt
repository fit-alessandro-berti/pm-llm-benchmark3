## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition
FEP-1 aims to prevent a single individual from both initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. This can be decomposed into the following threat scenarios and objectives:

1. **Accidental Payment to Wrong Vendor/Account:** An operator selects the wrong vendor master record or types an incorrect account number.
   - *AV-2:* Can prevent (checks bank-account ownership, vendor history).
   - *MG-4:* Can prevent if the manager manually reconciles invoice to bank details.
   - *RA-7:* Can detect; can correct if within the 24-hour recall window.
   - *ED-1:* Fails to address unless AV-2 and MG-4 are fully operational.

2. **Duplicate Invoice Payment:** The same invoice is paid twice.
   - *AV-2:* Prevents (explicit duplicate check).
   - *MG-4:* Can prevent if the manager checks payment history.
   - *RA-7:* Detects; corrective if funds can be recalled.
   - *ED-1:* Fails to address unless AV-2 is operational.

3. **Fraudulent Transfer to Employee-Controlled Account:** The preparer changes bank details to their own account and releases the payment.
   - *AV-2:* Can prevent/detect (bank change within 7 days triggers a warning).
   - *MG-4:* Can prevent if the manager independently verifies bank ownership; fails if the manager is colluding or rubber-stamps.
   - *RA-7:* Detects; fails to correct if past the recall window.
   - *ED-1:* Partially mitigates by requiring no bank changes in the last 7 days, but fails if the change occurred earlier or if collusion exists.

4. **Payment for Goods/Services Not Received:** A phantom invoice is processed.
   - *AV-2:* Fails (does not establish goods receipt).
   - *MG-4:* Can prevent if the manager reviews supporting receipts and validates commercial reasonableness.
   - *RA-7:* Can detect; corrective if past settlement but within recall window.
   - *ED-1:* Fails to address directly.

5. **Limit Bypass / Policy Violation:** An operator processes a payment above their authorized limit.
   - *AV-2:* Prevents (checks amount against operator limit).
   - *MG-4:* Can prevent if the manager checks the limit.
   - *RA-7:* Detects.
   - *ED-1:* Prevents by enforcing the EUR 100,000 limit.

6. **Collusion Between Preparer and Releaser:** Two individuals work together to bypass controls, defeating nominal four-eyes.
   - *AV-2:* Fails (assumes independent inputs; cannot detect behavioral collusion).
   - *MG-4:* Fails if the manager is part of the collusion or has an unresolved conflict of interest.
   - *RA-7:* Detects anomalies, but may fail to correct if loss is realized.
   - *ED-1:* Fails (same as above).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test
A compensating-control package must be evaluated against the control objective, not merely the occurrence of activities. The structured test uses the following dimensions:

- **Timing:** Preventive controls act before the loss event (release/settlement). Detective controls act after. A control acting before settlement is hybrid (detective of release, preventive of irrevocable loss).
- **Independence:** The compensating actor (e.g., MG-4 manager) must not have a conflict of interest with the preparer/releaser. Shared credentials or direct reporting lines nullify independence.
- **Coverage:** The package must cover the specific risk vectors present (e.g., bank changes, vendor age).
- **Overrideability:** If the preparer can unilaterally override the control (e.g., AV-2 warning), the control is merely advisory and does not break the causal chain of fraud.
- **Recoverability:** If the loss is caught after irrevocable settlement, the control is purely detective and fails the corrective objective.
- **Evidence Quality:** A "clean pass" or manager approval is weak if it is a rubber stamp without substantive review.
- **Collusion Resistance:** The package must assume that nominally distinct actors may act as one if they share workstations or report to each other.

**Standards of Equivalence:**
- **Fully Equivalent:** Meets all dimensions; breaks the causal chain before settlement with independent, collusion-resistant review.
- **Partially Equivalent:** Breaks the causal chain before settlement but has minor independence, coverage, or timing weaknesses.
- **Merely Mitigating:** Reduces expected loss but fails to act before settlement, or relies on potentially compromised actors.
- **Ineffective:** Overrides are accessible by the preparer, or controls act after irrevocable settlement.

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard:** Expected loss is a backward-looking financial metric. Control design also values *deterrence* and *systemic integrity*. A control package that relies on a colluding supervisor might have the same expected loss as a random audit, but it fails to deter internal fraud and creates systemic vulnerability if the collusion is systematic.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

To monitor control effectiveness without equating alerts with proof of misconduct, implement the following rules:

- **Same-User Rate (FEP-1 Violation):** Monitor the percentage of completed payments where the preparer equals the releaser. *Alert threshold:* > 5% without active ED-1 tokens. *Purpose:* Detects systemic policy drift.
- **Override Rate:** Monitor the ratio of AV-2 warnings overridden by the preparer/releaser. *Alert threshold:* Any override by the preparer themselves. *Purpose:* Identifies breakdown of automated preventive controls.
- **Late Manager Approval:** Monitor cases where MG-4 occurs after Bank_Settlement. *Alert threshold:* Any occurrence. *Purpose:* Identifies loss of corrective capacity.
- **Audit-Before-Recall-Window:** Monitor cases where RA-7 completes > 24 hours after release. *Alert threshold:* > 1 day. *Purpose:* Ensures corrective capacity remains viable.
- **Repeated Resource Pairs:** Track the frequency of specific preparer-releaser combinations. *Alert threshold:* Same pair handling > 20% of payments in a 30-day window. *Purpose:* Detects potential two-person collusion cells.
- **Shared Workstations:** Correlate preparer/releaser machine IDs. *Alert threshold:* Same machine ID for both activities in a case. *Purpose:* Behavioral indicator of collusion.
- **Bank-Change Proximity:** Monitor payments to vendors with bank details changed in the last 7 days. *Alert threshold:* High-value payments to recently changed accounts. *Purpose:* Targets the highest-impact fraud vector.
- **Control Outages:** Track payments released while AV-2 is unavailable. *Alert threshold:* Any high-value release during outage. *Purpose:* Detects exploitation of system weaknesses.
