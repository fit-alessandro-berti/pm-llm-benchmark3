### 1. Executive memo

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Executive Management

**SUBJECT:** Fiber-Installation Process Redesign (Pilot R) Evaluation & Strategic Decision

#### Bottom-Line Recommendation
We recommend **Alternative E (Hybrid Deployment with Guardrails)**: Immediately halt the national scaling of Redesign R, but do not roll it back entirely. Restrict Pilot R’s simplified routing and survey-skip rules exclusively to low-risk, simple residential houses (where median duration dropped by 6 days and first-time-right improved by 3pp). Concurrently, reinstate mandatory field surveys and permit checks for apartments, old buildings, and business segments. Pair this selective retention with targeted measurement and structural guardrails before considering any broader rollout.

#### The Strategic Dilemma & Trade-Offs
Pilot R presents a classic operational paradox: it successfully streamlined the happy path while breaking the exception path. 

By merging task ownership under a single "installation coordinator" and eliminating early bottlenecks, we accelerated simple orders. Median cycle time dropped from 14.2 to 10.8 days, visible direct costs fell from EUR 184 to EUR 161, and 3+ handovers plunged from 34% to 12%. 

However, these gains were bought at the expense of our operational tail, compliance, and service quality. Moving activation checks *after* billing start and applying predictive survey-skipping to high-risk buildings backfired severely. P95 cycle time blew out from 45 to 62 days, 30-day complaints jumped from 6.1% to 9.8%, second visits surged from 11% to 18%, and estimated costs including rework rose to EUR 207. 

Process-mining evidence proves that Pilot R’s improved fitness (0.94) was achieved by sacrificing precision (0.51). The process model became dangerously permissive, allowing unvalidated billing starts, skipped permits, and cascading repeat visits. Furthermore, coordinator utilization hit 93%, creating a fragile single-point-of-failure network where staff absences caused cases to stall indefinitely.

#### Immediate Action Plan
1. **Scope Down:** Confine Redesign R's coordinator ownership and survey-skipping rules strictly to the "Simple house, low building risk" segment (34% of volume).
2. **Reinstate Guardrails:** Revert apartments, old buildings, and business customers to the baseline workflow, restoring mandatory field surveys, permit checks, and pre-billing activation validation.
3. **De-bottleneck Resources:** Reallocate field survey staff (whose utilization dropped to 52%) back to complex risk segments to relieve the overloaded second-visit teams (92% utilization).
4. **Deploy Immediate Measurement (M2, M3, M4):** Instrument missing start timestamps, enforce structured reason codes for rework, and track 60-day post-billing costs.

Scaling Redesign R blindly nationwide (Alternative A) would institutionalize high churn and compliance failures. Full rollback (Alternative B) would discard massive efficiency gains for simple customers. Alternative E captures the best of both worlds while insulating the business from structural tail risks.

### 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

* **High fitness vs. low precision:** The process-mining algorithm finds high fitness (0.94) because the dominant volume of cases follows the simplified coordinator path, which matches the model's new expected happy path. However, precision plummeted (0.51) because the pilot introduced too many unconstrained behavioral variations (skipped surveys, out-of-order billing, manual activations, second visits). The model allows virtually any path, capturing noise rather than disciplined process structure.
* **Improved median vs. worse tail duration:** The median reflects simple, low-risk cases (34% of volume) that benefited directly from merged address/schedule tasks and single-coordinator ownership. Conversely, the tail (p90/p95) bloomed because complex orders (apartments, old buildings) skipped necessary field surveys and permits, resulting in catastrophic failures, failed installation visits, and prolonged rework loops.
* **Fewer handovers vs. lower resilience:** The coordinator model successfully eliminated handovers along the happy path (34% down to 12%). However, because one coordinator owned the entire case without distributed documentation or shared context, they became a single point of failure. When a coordinator was absent, cases completely stalled because context was siloed in their individual workflow, destroying organizational resilience.
* **Lower visible cost vs. higher rework/downstream cost:** Visible direct labor costs dropped (EUR 184 to 161) because tasks were truncated, surveys skipped, and initial handovers removed. Yet, total cost rose (EUR 198 to 207) because premature billing and skipped surveys triggered a surge in second visits (+7pp), manual activations (+8pp), and complaint escalations (+3.7pp), which consume expensive specialist labor.
* **Improvement for simple houses vs. deterioration for apartments/old buildings:** Simple houses have uniform physical infrastructure where address validation and scheduling can safely merge without physical site checks. Apartments and old buildings possess hidden infrastructural complexities (risers, missing permits, narrow conduits). Applying universal "simplification" rules to non-uniform assets guaranteed physical mismatch, failed first visits, and customer friction.

### 4. Decision alternatives

* **A. Scale Redesign R nationally**
  * *Advantages:* Maximizes upfront labor efficiency; capitalizes on fast median cycle times for simple orders; minimizes initial direct operational costs.
  * *Risks:* Institutionalizes massive tail blowouts, billing disputes, and regulatory compliance failures in urban/old building markets; drives up churn and downstream rework costs.
  * *Conditions for reasonableness:* Only viable if all national markets consist exclusively of standardized, low-risk single-family homes with zero permit requirements.
* **B. Roll back to the baseline process**
  * *Advantages:* Restores compliance, predictability, and baseline customer satisfaction; eliminates abnormal tail durations and permit risks.
  * *Risks:* Destroys the proven efficiency gains achieved for simple houses; reinstitutes unnecessary handovers and higher direct labor costs where simplification worked.
  * *Conditions for reasonableness:* Reasonable only if change management failure is systemic and the organization lacks the analytical capability to govern a segmented process.
* **C. Scale coordinator ownership, suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation**
  * *Advantages:* Preserves the benefit of single accountability and low handovers while eliminating the primary drivers of rework and compliance breaches.
  * *Risks:* Leaves field survey teams underutilized (52%) while overloading coordinators with administrative tasks that could be automated; treats all buildings identically.
  * *Conditions for reasonableness:* A safe interim operational fallback if segment-routing rules cannot be reliably automated in IT systems.
* **D. Run a controlled regional experiment with better segment/risk measurement**
  * *Advantages:* Generates clean, scientifically sound data on true segment profitability and risk profiles before committing capital.
  * *Risks:* Delays necessary corrective action on failing apartment/old-building segments; incurs ongoing customer dissatisfaction and financial bleed in pilot regions during the test window.
  * *Conditions for reasonableness:* Reasonable if executive leadership refuses to act without academic-grade causal proof and can tolerate 6 additional months of high churn.
* **E. Hybrid: Deploy R for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, run measurement**
  * *Advantages:* Aligns process design with infrastructural reality; captures fast cycle times and cost savings where safe; protects the tail, compliance, and customer experience where risky.
  * *Risks:* Increases operational complexity by maintaining a segmented/conditional workflow rather than a universal standard process.
  * *Conditions for reasonableness:* The optimal strategic posture given current conflicting evidence; balances velocity with operational integrity.

### 6. Final recommendation

* **Recommended Decision:** **Alternative E (Hybrid Deployment with Guardrails)**, backed by targeted instrumentation and structural controls.
* **Immediate Action:** 
  * Restrict Redesign R (single coordinator, merged tasks, survey skipping) exclusively to simple, low-risk residential houses.
  * Revert all apartments, old buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance orders to the baseline workflow (mandatory field surveys, permit checks, pre-billing activation validation).
  * Rebalance staffing: shift 15% of underutilized field survey capacity (currently at 52% utilization) to support overloaded second-visit teams (92% utilization).
* **What to Pause:**
  * Pause all predictive survey-skipping rules in multi-unit and permit-sensitive buildings.
  * Pause the practice of moving activation checks after billing start; enforce technical activation confirmation prior to generating any customer invoices.
* **What to Scale:**
  * Scale coordinator ownership *only* within the simple, low-risk residential segment where it has proven highly effective (-6 day median duration, +3pp first-time-right).
  * Scale the automated merging of address validation and appointment scheduling for simple addresses.
* **What to Measure Next:**
  * Immediately implement **M3** (structured reason codes for rework/complaints), **M4** (60-day downstream cost tracking), and **M6** (building-risk predictive rule validation).
* **What Threshold or Evidence Would Justify Broader Rollout:**
  * Broader rollout of survey-skipping or simplified routing to apartments/complex buildings will only be justified if:
    1. Validation data (**M6**) proves the predictive rule achieves a false-negative rate below 2%.
    2. Total cost including downstream rework (**M4**) demonstrates a net financial savings of at least 10% compared to baseline.
    3. P90 cycle times for complex segments drop below baseline levels without exceeding a 3% 30-day complaint rate.
