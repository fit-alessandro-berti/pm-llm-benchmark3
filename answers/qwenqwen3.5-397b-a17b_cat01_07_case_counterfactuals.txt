| K-411 | Assessment arrival earlier | Document (Wait) | Assessment | Low-risk cases in Assess queue | Neutral (Visibility) | Queue Saturation Point |

Scaling these interventions to 100 cases weekly reveals significant interference patterns and bottleneck migrations. For K-104, reserving C1 slots for high-risk cases would consume approximately 200-300 hours of C1's weekly capacity (assuming 2h prelim per case). Given C1 is a single resource with existing backlogs exceeding 20 hours, this policy would displace non-target compliance cases, potentially violating SLAs for urgent matters not flagged at triage. The bottleneck migrates from Assessment to Compliance, creating a systemic blockage that outweighs individual case savings.

For K-219, shifting standard handwritten documents to D2 relieves D1 but increases total processing time per case by 2-4 hours on average due to D2's slower standard rate. If D2 encounters higher clarification rates due to handwriting expertise gaps, rework loops could increase Document queue volume by 15%. This substitutes a queue delay for a processing delay, which may not reduce overall cycle time if Assessment queues remain congested. Furthermore, D1 becomes underutilized while D2 becomes a new bottleneck.

K-337's policy change eliminates external dependency delays but concentrates risk. Across 100 cases, suppressing rework loops saves approximately 300-500 hours of wait time but increases decision error probability. If error rates exceed 5%, appeal volumes could overwhelm Escalation (S1), creating a secondary bottleneck downstream of Decision. This shifts load from external suppliers to internal escalation resources.

K-411's parallelization pushes cases into the Assessment queue earlier. With A1/A2 capacity fixed, advancing 100 cases by 4 hours each simply fills the Assessment queue sooner, potentially causing earlier daily peaks without reducing total throughput. However, real-time timestamping improves visibility, allowing dynamic load balancing that could mitigate this congestion if management acts on the data. Without active management, this intervention merely shifts idle time from the Document queue to the Assessment queue.

The key nonlinearity lies in queue saturation. Local improvements vanish when downstream queues are saturated; thus, K-411 and K-104 require downstream capacity expansion to realize system-wide gains. Weekend effects remain dominant for K-337, as external suppliers do not accelerate responses due to internal policy changes. Timestamp behavior versus true concurrency is critical; if the workaround logs false start times, performance metrics will degrade, masking the actual resource contention.

## 7. Final synthesis

The K-411 intervention (parallel verification) appears most robust because it addresses a technical constraint without altering risk policy or consuming scarce specialist capacity. It improves visibility and reduces idle time, provided the Assessment queue is managed. This technical fix offers immediate cycle time relief without shifting burden to other human resources. Conversely, the K-104 intervention (compliance reservation) is most dangerous despite potential speed gains. Reserving the single Compliance resource (C1) for 100 cases weekly would likely collapse the compliance function for non-target cases, creating severe systemic inequity and SLA breaches elsewhere. The dependency on a single point of failure makes this policy fragile under load.

The K-337 counterfactual (suppressing rework) is least transportable to system-wide policy. While effective for individual cases with slow suppliers, applying this broadly increases organizational risk exposure. A local decision to accept uncertainty does not scale when aggregate loss exposure exceeds risk appetite. The decision policy change requires executive approval, not just operational tweaking, because it fundamentally alters the control environment.

To validate these findings, additional event data regarding queue entry and exit timestamps are most valuable. Currently, snapshots provide aggregate queue estimates, but individual case flow data is needed to detect starvation. Specifically, tracking the exact moment tasks become "ready" versus when they "start" would reveal hidden waiting times caused by the case lock in K-411. Furthermore, capturing the reason codes for `Request More Info` would allow better prediction of K-337 rework probabilities. Without granular event logs, counterfactual reasoning relies on aggregate averages that mask variance-driven bottlenecks. Finally, tracking D2 error rates on handwritten documents is essential for K-219 to ensure quality does not degrade as volume shifts.
