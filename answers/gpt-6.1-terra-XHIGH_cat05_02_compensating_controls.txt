## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 separates preparation and release to reduce the chance that one person can cause an erroneous or fraudulent transfer without an independent challenge. Its formal actor test is not itself proof that the payment is valid or that two people acted independently. ED-1 permits a narrowly defined exception to that actor test; it does not eliminate the objective. The following are distinct threats, and a control's effect depends on its actual operation and evidence.

| Objective or threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent a duplicate payment or an amount outside an operator's limit | Pre-release check can block or warn; an override can defeat it. | An independent substantive review can challenge the invoice and amount before commitment. | Can detect a duplicate or improper amount later; recall or recovery may correct it if still feasible. | Requires a clean, operational AV-2 pass, independent manager approval before settlement, and timely audit; it is a conditional permission, not a separate technical check. |
| Prevent diversion through a changed or unauthorized bank account | Checks ownership and recent changes before release, subject to the quality of its data and overrides. | Independent review of bank details can challenge diversion; before irrevocable settlement it may prevent loss if the payment can still be stopped. | Can detect and seek recall or recovery, with declining effectiveness after the 24-hour recall period. | Excludes changes in the previous seven days and requires the other listed safeguards, but does not guarantee that ownership evidence is correct. |
| Prevent payment for goods or services not received, or for fabricated evidence | Basic PO matching does not establish receipt or detect a fabricated receipt. | Substantive independent review of invoice and supporting receipt can challenge the payment, but may be deceived by convincing fabrication. | Can investigate underlying delivery or service evidence and detect a defect; corrective value depends on recoverability. | Requires MG-4 and RA-7 but does not independently establish receipt. |
| Prevent one operator initiating and authorizing their own unauthorized payment | System checks reduce specified errors but do not supply an independent human authorization or identify who controlled a compromised account. | A qualified, conflict-free manager can provide an independent challenge, strongest before release or settlement. A rubber stamp or compromised credential is weak. | Independent retrospective examination can detect anomalies but cannot guarantee prevention or recovery. | Allows a single operator only if every precondition and subsequent safeguard is met; a manager must still be independent. |
| Resist collusion or compromised identities and credentials | Does not detect collusion or prove who controlled an account. | Can mitigate a lone-operator threat if genuinely independent; collusion, conflicts, or credential sharing can defeat it. | Independent audit can investigate patterns and identity evidence; it may detect collusion but does not assure detection or recovery. | Does not itself establish actual independence or prevent collusion; the safeguards' independence and evidence matter. |
| Prevent sanctions or other prohibited payments | Checks sanctions-list status before release, subject to data quality and override. | May challenge supporting facts, but is not necessarily a substitute for the sanctions check. | Can identify a missed check after the fact; recall or recovery may be uncertain and cannot erase all consequences. | Requires the AV-2 pass; permission does not waive other legal or policy obligations. |
| Prevent commercially unreasonable or wrongly directed payments despite technically correct data | Does not assess commercial reasonableness and covers only its specified checks. | A meaningful, qualified review can challenge documents and purpose if the reviewer has suitable information and authority. | May detect problems through deeper investigation, with limited corrective ability after settlement. | Conditions mitigate selected risks but do not prove commercial reasonableness or overall objective equivalence. |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Apply the test to the actual payment and threat, not just the number of recorded activities:

1. Establish the decision point and formal result: identify the human preparer and releaser, release, bank settlement, and any stop or recall opportunity. A shared or compromised account does not establish two independent humans. Check every ED-1 condition individually, including the token's activation before preparation, strict amount below EUR 100,000, vendor age, no recent bank change, a full unoverridden AV-2 pass, independent qualified approval before settlement, and completed audit within one business day. Missing evidence is not a pass; a known failed precondition cannot be cured by later approval.
2. Examine independence and authority: confirm the manager is at least L3, is neither operator, has no unresolved conflict, actually controlled their credentials, and substantively reviewed the invoice, bank details and supporting receipt. For two-user cases, check whether people and their work were genuinely independent, including potential collusion and common workstations. A direct reporting relationship is a conflict risk to assess, not automatically a disqualification on the stated facts.
3. Assess coverage and reliability: map each credible loss mechanism to the checks actually performed, including receipt, bank ownership, duplicate, sanctions and amount. Verify data quality, service availability, warnings, overrides, the authority to override and whether the control could actually block or stop the payment. AV-2's clean pass does not establish receipt or commercial reasonableness.
4. Assess timing and recoverability: a check before release can prevent release. A manager intervention after release but before irrevocable settlement can prevent settlement only if an effective hold or stop remained available; otherwise it is detective. Audit is detective, and only partly corrective if the transfer can still be recalled or recovered. Compare actual completion times with the one-business-day audit requirement and the bank's normally available 24-hour recall window; neither is interchangeable with the other.
5. Weigh corroboration and residual exposure: review actual documents, reviewer actions, system and bank records, anomalies, and the plausibility and severity of loss, including repeatable or correlated failures. Assess coverage for collusion and threats outside automated checks. Classify uncertainty explicitly and reassess when evidence arrives.

A **fully equivalent** package provides a timely, effective independent challenge and sufficiently covers the material case-specific threats, with effective stop authority and reliable evidence, so that the remaining risk is comparable to a properly operating four-eyes process. ED-1 compliance is a relevant policy permission, but does not alone prove equivalence for every threat. A **partially equivalent** package supplies a meaningful independent challenge or other strong protection but has a material gap in timing, coverage, independence, or evidence. A **merely mitigating** package reduces some risk without replacing the core independent authorization, for example automated checks followed by late audit. An **ineffective** package fails to interrupt the plausible loss path, for example an overridden warning plus late, non-independent review and an expired recall opportunity. Actual absence of loss does not prove a package was sufficient; a stopped payment can be effective even though there is no completed payment.

Equal expected loss is not the only standard: the control objective may require separation or independent authorization as a process safeguard, and risk acceptance can depend on tail losses, distribution and concentration of loss, fraud deterrence, regulatory or policy requirements, recovery uncertainty, and confidence in the evidence. An equivalent average calculated from uncertain assumptions can conceal very different catastrophic or systematic risks.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive | Detective | Corrective | Limits and timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FEP-1 | Independent second human can challenge or refuse before release. | May expose suspicious preparation during review. | Usually none by itself. | Formal distinct IDs do not prove independent judgment, receipt or absence of collusion. |
| AV-2 | Blocks specified defects before release when operational and not overridden. | Flags duplicates, sanctions, bank ownership/change, limit and PO mismatches. | A blocked transfer avoids the need for recovery; the check does not recover released funds. | Warnings may be overridden; coverage excludes receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion and account control. |
| MG-4 | Independent, substantive approval before release can prevent release; before settlement it can prevent settlement if the payment remains stoppable. | Can identify defects during review, including after release. | May initiate a stop or recall when authorized and feasible. | A qualified independent L3+ manager, review of supporting evidence and effective intervention matter. Rubber-stamping or a conflict weakens it. |
| RA-7 | Does not prevent the original release; credible oversight may deter future abuse. | Retrospective audit can identify defects or patterns. | Recall or recovery can correct or reduce loss if possible and acted on in time. | Same-user emergency cases require 100% completed audit within one business day; the usual recall window is 24 hours, and post-window recovery is uncertain. |
| ED-1 | Conditional permission requires preventive safeguards including AV-2 and independent pre-settlement approval. | Requires retrospective audit. | Depends on the actual stop, recall and recovery mechanisms. | It is a bundle of conditions, not an independent control or a blanket waiver; every condition must hold. |

A manager's review after release but before settlement is **hybrid** relative to the whole payment: detective of the already released instruction and preventive of irrevocable settlement only if a real hold, cancellation or stop remains available and is used when needed. If settlement cannot be stopped, call it detective even when its timestamp precedes settlement. ED-1 specifies approval before settlement, but that timing alone does not prove practical control equivalence.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Preserve the original event log, timestamps, timezone and clock synchronization; reconcile releases to bank acceptance, irrevocable settlement, holds, cancellations, recall requests and recovery outcomes. For each case retain the invoice, PO, independent receipt or service evidence, vendor master and bank-ownership verification as of release, validation results and configuration, warnings and overrides, and full audit findings and completion time. Establish the business calendar and precise one-business-day policy, as well as the bank's actual recall cutoff and any stop rights. Verify the emergency token's activation time separately from its log creation time, and retain the incident justification.

Verify manager grade, conflict disclosures, review scope, documented decision, stop authority and actual identity; investigate credential assignment, access and possible sharing. For AP-204 and AP-209, obtain payment-cell design and assignment records, workstation and authentication logs, source documents, and independent confirmation of services. Confirm whether the fabricated receipt affected payment entitlement and whether related-party links represent an actual conflict or misconduct. A shared workstation or repeated pairing is an alert for investigation, not proof of collusion, and a clean transaction outcome is not proof of adequate control.

Monitor with documented denominators, time windows and thresholds appropriate to payment volume and risk:

- **Same-user rate:** completed payments with the same verified human preparer and releaser divided by all completed payments; separately count attempted blocked releases and unknown/shared identities. Track ED-1 condition failures and missing evidence.
- **Override rate:** warnings overridden divided by warnings, with breakdowns by operator, credential owner, reason, warning type, vendor and amount; alert on use of another person's credential and repeated overrides.
- **Late manager approval:** count approvals after release, after settlement, and missing or unqualified approvals; distinguish post-release reviews with a documented effective stop from those without one. Track ED-1 pre-settlement approval specifically.
- **Audit before recall window:** track completion and any action before the actual bank cutoff, as well as completion within one business day; alert on outstanding reviews early enough to act, and separately report late or unverified completion.
- **Repeated resource pairs and shared workstations:** compare pair frequency and concentration with documented assignment patterns; investigate identity, actual independence and conflicts. Control for dedicated cells and work schedules; alerting alone is not proof.
- **Bank-change proximity:** flag changed accounts in the seven days before release, especially very recent changes, new vendors, missing ownership evidence or overrides. Investigate the change and independent verification.
- **Control outages:** record service availability, duration, affected payments, attempted releases, fallback evidence and restoration; alert on release during outage or unsigned fallback and escalate exposure until reviewed.
