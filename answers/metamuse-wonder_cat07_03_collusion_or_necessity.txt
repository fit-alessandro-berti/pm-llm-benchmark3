# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Pattern A: Buyer_CarlaApprover_Gita fast emergency approvals and PO four-eyes exceptions**
- Benign 1: Genuine emergency medical channel — Carla specializes in emergency medical supplies and Gita is delegated for urgent cases; pre-approved frameworks, phone pre-review, or standing delegation may occur before logged PO creation. Support: valid emergency criteria, clinical/operational urgency records, framework contracts, pre-review emails/messages, delivery need dates. Refute: emergencies declared retrospectively without need evidence, repeated non-urgent goods, split orders, or approvals before request documentation existed.
- Benign 2: Shared-account / attribution artifact — 23 approvals used shared "Approver_Gita" while assistants appear in `entered_by`. Support: access logs, workstation/session records, delegation schedules showing different actual approvers. Refute: confirmed single-user sessions, same credentials/device, or approval authority used outside delegation.

**Pattern B: Buyer_DanManager_Hugo weekend facilities repairs**
- Benign 1: Weekend repair escalation — Hugo handles exceptions and Dan specializes in facilities repairs; fast override may reflect safety, downtime, or contractor call-out. Support: work orders, incident tickets, weekend rosters, site attendance, completion certificates. Refute: no incident, routine planned work repeatedly labelled emergency, or repeat vendors without competition where competition was feasible.
- Benign 2: Small on-call pool causing apparent concentration — few staff cover weekends, so repeated pairs are expected. Support: HR rosters and on-call payments showing limited coverage. Refute: qualified approvers were available but bypassed, especially for high-value or repeated suppliers.

**Pattern C: VendorMaster_LenaAP_Kim, V-77x, fast bank change to payment**
- Benign 1: Legitimate mass maintenance after V-77x banking-platform change — many bank changes could be vendor-initiated and batched, then payments released on normal due dates shortly after. Support: vendor notifications, mass-maintenance ticket, bank letters, callback records to independently sourced numbers, payment due dates predating the change. Refute: missing tickets, changes shortly before unscheduled payments, amounts altered, new accounts not matching vendor legal entity, or confirmations sent only to contacts supplied in the change request.
- Benign 2: Portfolio specialization / higher-risk supplier assignment — Kim handles higher-risk suppliers and Lena handles bank changes, so co-occurrence may be designed. The 19-minute median could reflect scheduled payment runs after cleared maintenance. Support: assignment rules, risk-tiering, payment-run calendars, independent verification step performed by a third person not captured as an event. Refute: no assignment rule, no third-party check, manual uploads bypassing workflow, or payments concentrated just below approval thresholds.
- Benign 3: Timing artifact from manual upload — events may be uploaded in sequence after offline verification, compressing logged intervals. Support: original signed forms, ticket timestamps, bank validation logs preceding upload. Refute: system audit trails showing actual entry and approval minutes apart by the same session.

**Pattern D: Same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification**
- Benign 1: Batch migration after quarter close — 12/17 have identical minute timestamps from `quarter_close_upload`; justification may have been reviewed by another person offline. Support: spreadsheet provenance, original justification documents, reviewer sign-off, migration mapping. Refute: no underlying documents, justification authored and approved by same user in source system, or justifications created only after audit inquiry.
- Benign 2: Emergency exception misunderstood or poorly configured — staff may believe same-day override permits same-person closure. Support: training, SOP ambiguity, workflow configuration allowing closure. This explains control failure without intent. Refute: repeated warnings, training records showing clear rule, or concealment.

**Pattern E: Subcontracting loops and high co-working**
- Benign 1: Clarification/correction loops — Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix loops (33 and 30) resemble routine approval comments and correction; Gita/Carla and Hugo/Dan may be emergency clarification. Support: comment fields, changed fields between iterations, error codes. Refute: loops with no substantive change, approval after rejection without new evidence.
- Benign 2: Automated exception handling — Bot_MatchAP_KimBot_Match (52) and Bot_MatchAP_Kim within 6 min likely reflect exception queues. Support: match exception codes, tolerance rules. Refute: manual override of match results without reason codes, mainly relevant if amounts or payees changed.

**Pattern F: Temp_NinaBuyer_Carla in 4 minutes, 65 cases**
- Benign 1: End-of-quarter triage/data entry — Nina entered backlog and routed to the emergency specialist. Support: backlog tickets, entry timestamps vs source documents, assignment rules. Refute: Nina created and steered cases outside her role to benefit particular vendors or bypass buyers; currently no such evidence is given.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would increase concern**
- Confirmed identity evidence (SSO/session/device/access logs) showing the same human truly performed bank-detail change and payment approval, after excluding shared accounts and mapping errors.
- Beneficiary account not owned by the contracted vendor legal entity; bank change shortly before an unscheduled, accelerated, split, or unusually large payment; payment recalled, reissued, or diverted.
- Missing or backdated vendor callback using contact details supplied only in the change request; absent maintenance ticket for V-77x despite the mass-change explanation; vendor denies requesting the change.
- Repeated threshold avoidance: split POs/payments just below approval limits, round amounts, duplicate invoices, or goods receipt/invoice mismatch waived by the same pair.
- Emergency overrides where need evidence, incident tickets, or delivery records do not exist, are created after approval, or recur for plannable purchases; retrospective justifications authored after audit selection.
- Communications or workflow comments showing pre-arrangement to bypass controls, deletion/alteration of audit fields, or use of manual upload specifically to avoid workflow checks.
- Permissions data showing Lena, Kim, Carla, Dan, Gita, or Hugo held incompatible roles beyond the cases logged, especially vendor creation + bank change + payment release.

**Evidence that would reduce concern**
- V-77x banking-platform migration pack: vendor notices, bank letters, mass-maintenance ticket covering the cases, independent callback logs to pre-existing contacts, and account-ownership validation predating payment.
- Payment due-date evidence showing payments were already due on normal runs and not expedited after bank changes; no change to beneficiary between approval and release.
- Emergency substantiation: clinical urgency, facilities incident/work orders, on-call rosters, framework/delegation authority, goods/service receipt, and timely independent retrospective review in source documents.
- Access logs proving different actual users behind shared "Approver_Gita", corrected `legacy_erp` mapping showing Bot_PO cases are not human four-eyes violations, and spreadsheet provenance with independent reviewer sign-off for the 12 batch justifications.
- Role-design documentation assigning Carla/Gita, Dan/Hugo, and Kim to defined specialist/risk portfolios, with compensating controls (third-person verification) evidenced outside the event log.
- Field-level version history showing subcontracting loops involved substantive corrections, price/quantity checks, or rejection-and-resubmission rather than unchanged approvals.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified control exceptions and concentrated working patterns that warrant targeted follow-up, but the log alone does not establish fraud, collusion, or intent. Most high-frequency pairings are consistent with routine team structures and informal specialization. The emergency pathways involving Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan–Manager_Hugo show fast approvals and some four-eyes exceptions, largely alongside emergency flags, specialist categories, and weekend or urgent demand; these require confirmation that emergency criteria, retrospective justifications, and delegated authority were documented.

The subgroup requiring priority review is the VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim pattern for vendor family V-77x, where bank-detail changes were followed relatively quickly by payment approvals, with higher amount share, concentrated vendor exposure, predominantly manual source-system records, and a small number of cases in which bank-detail change and payment approval were recorded under the same resource. This combination departs more clearly from routine comparators, although legitimate mass bank-detail maintenance following a banking-platform change remains a plausible explanation pending ticket and vendor-confirmation evidence.

Other findings have material data-quality limitations, including shared-account use, batch-migrated justification timestamps, and a legacy resource-mapping error affecting automated purchase-order events. These issues may create or obscure apparent violations and should be resolved before conclusions are drawn about individual conduct. We recommend risk-based case sampling, independent verification of bank changes, review of access and audit-trail fields, and remediation of segregation-of-duties and account-mapping controls, rather than a general misconduct finding at this stage.
