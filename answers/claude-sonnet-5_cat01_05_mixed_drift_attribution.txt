## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap (0.55 vs 0.90 in March) shows that most of the *apparent* conformance collapse is attributable to label and event-duplication artifacts introduced by the migration, not to a changed business process — mapping `Eligibility Screen` back to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicate `Open Case` events recovers fitness close to baseline. This implies the underlying control-flow skeleton (RegisterValidateCollectAssessDecision) was largely preserved through the migration. It does **not** imply nothing changed operationally: the mapped fitness still drifts gradually downward from 0.91 in April to 0.86–0.87 by July, which is a real, independent signal that must be explained by mechanisms other than labeling.

A label mapping can recover fitness syntactically while masking real shifts in timing, resource assignment, or event completeness, because conformance checking on activity labels alone is blind to duration, resource, and ordering-frequency changes that don't violate the control-flow model. Duplicated technical events inflate apparent rework/repetition counts and bias throughput-time reconstruction; missing resources bias any resource-based analysis (workload, handover, staffing attribution) without necessarily affecting control-flow fitness — these are different failure modes needing different corrections.

The post-May DFG changes (rising Request-Info loops, shifting Senior Assessment routing) and performance changes (cycle time, p90) cannot be dismissed as logging artifacts because they appear in the *mapped* series, after correcting known migration effects, and correlate temporally with independently documented external events (supplier disruption, staffing loss, priority rule) rather than with any further migration or logging event.

### 3.4 Candidate change points by mechanism (at least two each)

- **Logging drift:** (1) 2026-03-01 migration (label rename, duplicate Open Case, resource loss) — strongest; (2) 2026-10 (jump in recorded Senior Assessment routing, possibly partly a recording-completeness change rather than pure behavior).
- **Behavioral drift:** (1) 2026-05 (rework loop increase tied to Supplier Z); (2) 2026-07-01 (formal priority rule altering queue order/control-flow for high-complexity cases).
- **Performance drift:** (1) 2026-06-15 (staffing cut visible in queue-time growth from June onward); (2) 2026-07/08 (p90 divergence peak, compounding staffing+supplier+priority effects).

### 3.5 One global change point or multiple?

The data support **multiple, mechanism-specific change points**, not a single global one. March is essentially a provenance/logging discontinuity with negligible mapped-fitness or DFG-distance impact. May, June/July, and October each introduce distinct, only partially overlapping signatures (rework vs. queueing vs. case-mix/routing) with different onset shapes (sudden external shock, step organizational change, gradual seasonal ramp). Treating this as one global change point would conflate artifact-correction with real drift and would obscure the seasonal/recurring nature of the October–November shift.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global monthly | month only | Baseline drift screening, coarse trend detection | n/a (already monthly aggregate) | Overall trend, seasonal cycle | Masks all mechanism-specific effects; over-aggregation |
| Migration era | pre-2026-03 vs. post-2026-03, label-mapped | Isolate logging/semantic effect | 300 cases per side | Raw vs. mapped fitness, resource completeness | Bundles migration with any concurrent real change |
| Supplier × complexity | supplier_z flag × high/std complexity × month | Separate supplier effect from pre-existing complexity confound | 30 cases per cell per month, else pool bi-monthly | Rework rate attribution to supplier vs. mix | Small cells for supplier-Z + high-complexity in low-volume months |
| Priority class × pre/post 07-01 | priority flag × month | Detect redistribution of delay | 50 cases per class per month | Standard vs. expedited queue-time trajectories | Priority class may be reconstructed retroactively, introducing classification noise |
| Resource/team × pre/post 06-15 | team id × month, restricted to non-missing resource cases | Attribute queueing to organizational capacity | 20 cases per team per month | Team-level workload and queue-time shift | Missing-resource cases systematically excluded, biasing team comparison toward better-instrumented teams |
| Seasonal cohort | calendar month × complexity share × year (if multi-year data exists) | Confirm recurrence vs. novel drift | 1 full prior year for comparison | Year-over-year seasonal pattern validation | Only one year of data supplied here; comparison currently unavailable, flagged as a limitation |
| Case-level composite (primary monitoring population) | month × supplier_z × complexity × priority class, mapped labels only | Ongoing operational monitoring at manageable granularity | 25 cases per cell; merge adjacent months if below threshold | Cross-cutting view of all major hypothesized mechanisms simultaneously | Four-way stratification risks sparse cells in low-volume months (e.g., Jan/Feb, Sep) |

**Primary monitoring population:** the *case-level composite segment* (month × supplier_z × complexity × priority class, on mapped labels) should be the primary monitoring population, because it is the finest grain that still meets minimum-support thresholds in most months and simultaneously supports discrimination among the supplier, complexity/seasonal, and priority-rule hypotheses — the three mechanisms with the strongest documented, ongoing evidence. Team/resource segmentation should run as a secondary, resource-completeness-gated view given its data-quality limitations.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label/schema monitor | Count of distinct activity labels, new label appearance rate | Rolling 3-month window, re-baselined after known migrations | Alert if new label(s) appear affecting >1% of cases | Check against change log for scheduled system changes; attempt auto-mapping | Legitimate new activity types (e.g., new decision path) misread as drift | Data/process engineering |
| Event-ordering monitor | Directly-follows violation rate vs. reference DFG (log skeleton "always-before" checks) | Prior stable quarter | Alert if violation rate rises >2x reference | Inspect specific violating traces for technical duplication (e.g., Open Case) | Migration transiently breaks ordering without real behavior change | Process mining team |
| DFG/variant distribution monitor | Normalized relation-rate vector, L1/Jaccard distance to reference | Trailing 3-month median (seasonally adjusted per §below) | Alert if distance exceeds a month-specific threshold (e.g., 1.5x seasonal IQR) | Decompose which edges drive the distance; segment by supplier/complexity | Small-sample variant noise in low-volume months | Process mining team |
| Rework monitor | AssessRequestInfo loop rate, stratified by supplier_z and complexity | Pre-disruption baseline (Jan-Apr) | Alert if supplier-Z-specific rework rate exceeds non-supplier rate by >1.5x for 2 consecutive months | Contact supplier ops; check document completeness metadata | General complexity increase misattributed to supplier | Operations liaison |
| Temporal profile monitor | Discovered temporal profile (min/max/typical inter-activity durations) per activity pair | Rolling 6-month, activity-pair specific | Alert if observed duration falls outside typical profile bounds for >10% of cases in a month | Separate service-time vs. queue-time component of the deviation | Aggregation across resource pools hides local effects | Performance analytics |
| Queue-waiting monitor | Reconstructed pre-Assess waiting time distribution, stratified by priority class | Pre-priority-rule baseline (before 07-01) per class | Alert if standard-class p90 waiting rises while high-complexity waiting falls (divergence pattern) | Confirm against current staffing levels and rule configuration | Aggregate median masking divergent within-class trends if not stratified | Capacity planning |
| Service-time monitor | Assess activity duration, median and p90, resource-normalized | Trailing 6-month | Alert only on service-time (not queue) shift >15% median-to-median | Distinguish real skill/complexity effect from measurement noise | Missing-resource cases bias resource-normalized estimates | Assessment team lead |
| Case-mix monitor | high_complexity_pct, volume, supplier_z_pct trend | Same calendar month prior year(s) where available; else trailing 12-month seasonal decomposition | Alert if deviation from seasonal-adjusted expectation exceeds ±10 pts | Check renewal calendar, supplier contract status | Genuine one-off demand shock misread as new baseline | Demand planning |
| Resource completeness monitor | missing_resource_pct by activity and team | Pre-migration baseline (Jan-Feb) | Alert if missingness >2x baseline for 2+ consecutive months, or highly uneven by team | Flag for IT/data-capture remediation; exclude from resource-based KPIs until resolved | Legitimate resource-anonymization policy change misread as data loss | Data governance |
| Conformance monitor | mapped_model_fitness (not raw) plus alignment-based deviation types | Pre-migration baseline (mapped) | Alert if mapped fitness drops >0.03 from trailing reference for 2 consecutive months | Distinguish frequency-based (variant share) vs. severe (skipped mandatory activity) deviations | Model mis-specification (e.g., undocumented Senior Assessment branch) mistaken for behavioral drift | Process mining team |
| Outcome monitor | Approve/Reject/Withdraw rate, decision lag, stratified by complexity | Trailing 12-month, complexity-adjusted | Alert if rejection rate or decision lag shifts >10% independent of mix adjustment | Cross-check with document-quality campaign and rework rate | Seasonal complexity swings misread as a policy/outcome change | Quality assurance |

**Seasonality and multiple testing:** every threshold above is defined relative to a *mechanism-appropriate reference window* (pre-migration baseline, pre-disruption baseline, or same-calendar-month prior year) rather than one universal cutoff, because January–February, the supplier/staffing window, and the October–November renewal season have structurally different expected variability. To control multiple testing across ~10 concurrent monitors, apply a false-discovery-rate correction (e.g., Benjamini-Hochberg) across the monitor set per month before escalating, and require two consecutive months of exceedance (except for sudden-onset monitors like labels/schema and event-ordering, where a single-month sudden spike is itself diagnostic) before treating an alert as a structural change rather than noise.

    if row["p90_cycle_h"] > 1.5 * baseline_mean["p90_cycle_h"]:
        flags.append("queueing/performance flag")
    if row["high_complexity_pct"] > baseline_mean["high_complexity_pct"] + 10:
        flags.append("case-mix/seasonal flag")
    return ", ".join(flags) if flags else "no flag"

df["change_point_flags"] = df.apply(flag_changes, axis=1)

import pm4py

def load_and_format(event_df, case_id_col="case_id", activity_col="activity",
                     timestamp_col="timestamp", resource_col="resource"):
    """
    Format a raw event dataframe into a pm4py-compatible event log object.
    Does not mutate the original dataframe.
    """
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        event_df.copy(),
        case_id=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )
    return formatted


def slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_str, end_str):
    """
    Extract a time-bounded slice of the log, e.g. one calendar month,
    for monthly monitoring comparisons.
    """
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_log, start_str, end_str, mode="events")


def monthly_dfg(formatted_log, start_str, end_str):
    """
    Discover the (frequency-annotated) DFG for a given time window.
    """
    window_log = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_str, end_str)
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(window_log)
    return dfg, start_activities, end_activities


def monthly_variants(formatted_log, start_str, end_str):
    """
    Compute variant frequencies for a time window, to compare
    fast/rework/other variant shares against the monthly summary table.
    """
    window_log = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_str, end_str)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(window_log)
    return variants


def monthly_temporal_profile(formatted_log, start_str, end_str):
    """
    Discover a temporal profile (typical min/max inter-activity durations)
    for a reference window, to compare against later windows for drift.
    """
    window_log = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_str, end_str)
    temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window_log)
    return temporal_profile


def discover_behavioral_constraints(formatted_log, start_str, end_str, method="log_skeleton"):
    """
    Optionally discover a log skeleton or DECLARE model for a window,
    to check for new/violated 'always-before', 'response', or
    'existence' constraints (e.g., Assess => SeniorAssessment).
    method: 'log_skeleton' or 'declare'
    """
    window_log = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_str, end_str)
    if method == "log_skeleton":
        from pm4py.algo.discovery.log_skeleton import algorithm as lsk_discovery
        model = lsk_discovery.apply(window_log)
    elif method == "declare":
        # Placeholder for pm4py's declare discovery, name consistent with
        # the manual's declare module; exact call may evolve with pm4py version.
        model = pm4py.discover_declare(window_log)
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unknown method")
    return model


def compare_raw_vs_mapped(event_df, mapping: dict, case_id_col="case_id",
                           activity_col="activity", timestamp_col="timestamp"):
    """
    Build two parallel formatted logs -- raw and label-mapped -- WITHOUT
    overwriting the original event dataframe, then return both DFGs
    and fitness-relevant statistics for side-by-side conformance checking.
    mapping: dict e.g. {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    """
    raw_df = event_df.copy()
    mapped_df = event_df.copy()
    mapped_df[activity_col] = mapped_df[activity_col].replace(mapping)

    raw_log = load_and_format(raw_df, case_id_col, activity_col, timestamp_col)
    mapped_log = load_and_format(mapped_df, case_id_col, activity_col, timestamp_col)

    raw_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(raw_log)
    mapped_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(mapped_log)

    return {
        "raw_log": raw_log,
        "mapped_log": mapped_log,
        "raw_dfg": raw_dfg,
        "mapped_dfg": mapped_dfg,
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The March fitness collapse is attributed with high confidence (85–90%) to logging/semantic drift from the system migration: raw fitness fell from 0.93 to 0.55 while mapped fitness stayed at 0.90, distinct labels rose only via the known renamed activity, and missing-resource/duplicate-event rates jumped in exactly the migrated activities. This is the strongest, least ambiguous conclusion in the dataset.

The May–August cycle-time and rework increase is attributed to a combination of the Supplier Z disruption (moderate-high confidence, 65–75%, given the tight temporal alignment of supplier_z_pct and rework_variant_pct) and the June staffing cuts (moderate confidence, 60%, supported by the growing gap between flat service time and rising queue time). These two mechanisms are mediated by each other — reduced capacity likely amplified the visible effect of supplier-driven rework, and cannot be cleanly separated using monthly aggregates alone; this is only plausible, not firmly established, without case-level stratification.

The July priority rule's redistribution effect is plausible but weakly evidenced (50–55%) at the aggregate level: the disproportionate p90-vs-median divergence is consistent with the documented rule, but the same pattern could arise from unrelated tail-heavy queueing under staffing pressure. This requires class-stratified data to confirm and should not be treated as established.

The October–November case-mix and cycle-time surge is attributed with moderate-high confidence (70%) to seasonal renewal, given the historical pattern and simultaneous complexity/volume rise, but the sharp jump in recorded Senior Assessment routing is only plausibly a real behavioral increase — it may partly reflect improved recording completeness at higher volume, an alternative not resolvable from aggregates.

December's improvement is explicitly under-attributable: it coincides with both the overtime/document-quality intervention and the natural seasonal decline, and the current data cannot separate their contributions.

I would revise this account if case-level, complexity-stratified queue-time data showed no divergence between standard and high-complexity classes after July (undermining the priority-rule hypothesis), or if year-over-year data showed October–November complexity spikes were *not* historically typical (undermining the seasonal hypothesis in favor of a structural population shift).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw-model fitness drops sharply while mapped-model fitness (using current label mappings) stays within 0.03 of its rolling baseline, THEN treat the event as a logging/schema issue and update the mapping dictionary rather than opening a behavioral investigation, BECAUSE the discrepancy is diagnostic of label/technical-event artifacts, not process change.

2. IF mapped-model fitness itself drops more than 0.03 from its rolling baseline for two consecutive months, THEN open a control-flow/behavioral investigation using alignments to identify which deviation types (skip, insert, reorder) dominate, BECAUSE this indicates a real conformance change independent of known logging artifacts.

3. IF the AssessRequestInfo/rework rate rises disproportionately in Supplier-Z-flagged cases relative to matched-complexity non-supplier cases, THEN escalate to supplier quality management and monitor document-completeness metadata, BECAUSE this isolates a supplier-specific rework mechanism from general complexity effects.

4. IF service time stays flat while p90 cycle time diverges sharply from the median, THEN prioritize queue-waiting reconstruction segmented by priority class and team, BECAUSE this pattern is diagnostic of queueing/organizational drift (staffing or priority redistribution) rather than a genuine processing-time change.

5. IF standard-priority queue time rises while high-complexity queue time falls or stays flat after a known priority-rule change, THEN confirm the rule is producing redistribution rather than a global slowdown, and report both class-level trends (not just the aggregate), BECAUSE aggregating across classes can hide a redistribution effect entirely.

6. IF high-complexity case share and volume rise together in a pattern matching the same calendar months in prior years, THEN classify the change as recurring/seasonal and reset the monitoring reference window to the seasonal cohort rather than the annual baseline, BECAUSE conflating seasonal recurrence with novel drift will generate persistent false alarms every renewal cycle.

7. IF missing-resource percentage remains elevated for multiple months without a corresponding change in mapped fitness or cycle time, THEN flag it as a standalone data-quality issue for remediation and exclude resource-dependent KPIs from drift conclusions until resolved, BECAUSE persistent missingness independent of performance signals indicates an instrumentation gap rather than an operational drift.
