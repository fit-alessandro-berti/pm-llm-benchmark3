# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

**Handover-of-work network**  
This directed network measures sequential work transfers between resources. Mira_Coordinator shows extreme centrality (out-degree 0.82, in-degree 0.51, betweenness 0.77). The strongest incoming edges are from Bot_Rules (920), specialists returning results, and temps; outgoing edges go to Omar_Medical, Priya_Legal, the four adjusters, and later to payment release paths. This implies she is a frequent routing and assignment hub, especially for exception traffic. It does **not** imply sustained collaborative ownership of cases, deep case-work performance, or that she is a bottleneck on every path—many of her events are short (median 4.2 min) assignment or clarification steps. Automated resources (Bot_Ingest → Bot_Rules) and vendors remain high-volume independent paths that largely bypass her.

**Working-together network**  
This undirected network records co-occurrence of resources on the same cases. Mira’s weighted degree is low (0.16) and pairwise rates with adjusters and team leads sit at only 4–8 %. This implies she rarely shares the bulk of a case’s lifecycle with others. It does **not** imply isolation, low importance, or that her interventions are irrelevant; brief routing/assignment events produce high handover counts while leaving the case free to proceed without her ongoing presence. The low co-occurrence is therefore expected for a pure broker and reconciles the apparent contradiction with the handover and subcontracting networks.

**Subcontracting network**  
With n=2 this network captures A–B–A patterns. Mira appears in the highest-frequency loops (Mira → Omar → Mira 126 times; Mira → Priya → Mira 98 times; also loops with adjusters and Bot_Rules). High subcontracting betweenness (0.69) implies she initiates specialist or rework work and receives the result back, functioning as an orchestrator of exception loops. It does **not** imply she performs the specialist work herself or that every case is subcontracted through her—ordinary claims largely avoid these loops.

**Performance / time statistics**  
Median service time of 4.2 min is consistent with short routing events. High cycle-time contribution variance and the 48 % exception-flag share, together with the 6-day absence effect (90th-percentile cycle time for exception cases rising from 8.4 to 13.9 days while ordinary claims were unaffected), imply selective criticality for the exception path. Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h) dominate waiting-time components independently. These statistics do **not** show that Mira slows every case or that she is a processing bottleneck rather than a routing one.

**Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity**  
Her dominant activities (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) form a profile distinct from Team Leads (Triage / Approve), Adjusters (Assess / Negotiate), specialists, bots, or vendors. Organizational-role discovery would therefore place her in a singleton or narrow exception-handler cluster with low similarity scores to the regional pairs (Alice–Carla, Bruno–Diego). This does **not** prove formal hierarchy, only that her observed activity signature is unique.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1: Formal coordinator / exception desk**  
- **Mechanism**: Bot_Rules (and occasionally temps) route exception-flagged or incomplete cases to her; she performs short assignment, reopen or escalate actions and clears the queue.  
- **Supporting evidence**: 920 handovers from Bot_Rules, activity set matches, 48 % exception share, absence produces exception backlog only, process-owner description as “queue cleaner,” short median service time.  
- **Contradicting / limiting evidence**: Adjusters describe knowledge-based routing beyond pure queue-cleaning; high subcontracting loops indicate oversight rather than one-way hand-off; surprisingly low working-together with the two Team Leads.  
- **Observable predictions**: Her event volume will track exception rates; formal SOPs would reduce routing-time variance; a trained backup would preserve cycle-time metrics for exception cases.

**Hypothesis 2: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency**  
- **Mechanism**: Tacit knowledge and structural position create a single funnel for complex paths; overload or absence delays everything downstream of the exception branch.  
- **Supporting evidence**: Absence effect on the 90th percentile, 64 % of reopened claims and 71 % of complaints, high betweenness in both handover and subcontracting networks, high cycle-time variance.  
- **Contradicting / limiting evidence**: Ordinary claims unaffected; service times short; bulk volume handled by bots and Team Leads; vendor waiting times independent.  
- **Observable predictions**: Exception cycle-time tails will spike whenever she is unavailable; capacity expansion or knowledge capture will disproportionately compress those tails.

**Hypothesis 3: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker**  
- **Mechanism**: She bridges automation, the two regional teams, medical/legal specialists, junior adjusters, temps and vendors by matching case attributes to expertise.  
- **Supporting evidence**: Diverse outgoing handovers, adjusters’ comment that she “knows where hard cases should go,” high betweenness, low working-together consistent with short spanning interventions, specialist return edges.  
- **Contradicting / limiting evidence**: Process owner downplays the role; peripheral position relative to Team Leads in the working-together network; compliance concern about uncontrolled discretion.  
- **Observable predictions**: Assignment patterns will correlate with amount_band, region, priority or case_type; specialist utilization will be more balanced while she is present; departure will raise formal escalations and mis-routes.

**Hypothesis 4: Quality / compliance gatekeeper with discretion**  
- **Mechanism**: She both detects problems (missing information, reopen, escalate) and selects the next specialist, exercising judgment that doubles as an informal quality gate.  
- **Supporting evidence**: Compliance reviewer’s explicit worry, high touch rates on reopens and complaints, Mira–specialist–Mira subcontracting loops.  
- **Contradicting / limiting evidence**: No direct outcome-bias or policy-breach evidence in the log; short service times limit depth of review; routing could still be rule-driven under the surface.  
- **Observable predictions**: Settlement or reopen outcomes will vary systematically by her assignee choices; formalizing selection criteria will reduce measured discretion variance.

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive; the data are consistent with a hybrid (exception desk that also functions as boundary spanner and tacit gatekeeper).

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk** – Selective and moderate-to-high for the exception path only. The absence window supplies direct evidence that the 90th-percentile cycle time of exception_flag=True cases rose sharply while ordinary claims were unaffected. High betweenness amplifies the effect, yet the bulk automated and regional-team paths remain resilient.

**Key-person risk** – Elevated. She concentrates 64 % of reopens and 71 % of complaints and appears to hold tacit routing knowledge. No parallel resource currently performs the same activity mix at comparable volume, creating single-point exposure.

**Coordination risk** – Moderate. Her broker function absorbs exceptions that would otherwise generate formal escalations or overload specialists (operations-manager view). Loss without a replacement mechanism would increase those secondary loads.

**Compliance / control risk** – Legitimate structural concern, not proven misconduct. The dual role of problem detection plus specialist selection creates opportunity for uncontrolled discretion or inconsistency. No log evidence of bias, favoritism or policy breach exists; the risk is therefore one of governance design rather than demonstrated wrongdoing.

**Workload fairness risk** – Possible but under-evidenced. Her exception share (0.48) is markedly higher than that of juniors or temps. Preferential routing of hard cases to senior adjusters could create uneven burden, yet the data currently show only volume differences, not outcome or quality differentials.

**Data-quality risk** – Low-to-moderate. Short routing events and “Assign Specialist” transitions may be logged more granularly than other resources’ work, inflating handover centrality while the working-together network under-states impact. Vendor and bot edges appear clean; her interactions are the more ambiguous segment.

- **Subcontracting**: Extract full A–B–A sequences with `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` (vary n); focus on Mira–specialist–Mira–adjuster loops and correlate frequency with subsequent reopen rates.  
- **Activity-based resource similarity / organizational roles**: Apply `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` and `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`; expect Mira to form a singleton or tight exception cluster distinct from the Alice–Carla and Bruno–Diego pairs. Visualize with `pm4py.view_sna` or `save_vis_sna`.  
- **Filtering**: Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on exception_flag, activity names (Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint), or variants; also filter the six-day absence window for before/after comparison of SNA metrics and cycle times.  
- **Service / waiting-time**: Run `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`; compare waiting times immediately after Mira events versus after Team-Lead or Bot_Rules events, especially for Vendor_Verify and specialist hand-overs. Sliding-window SNA metrics can further detect load spikes.

# 5. Recommendation

**Immediate**  
Document Mira’s current routing criteria and decision heuristics (even if informal) so that tacit knowledge is captured. Establish backup coverage for the exception desk—train at least one secondary coordinator or senior adjuster to absorb short absences. Introduce simple queue-length monitoring for exception_flag=True cases.

**Monitor**  
Exception-case cycle-time percentiles, specialist and adjuster workload balance, reopen and complaint rates by assignee, and Mira’s own utilization versus capacity. Periodic compliance sampling of her assignment decisions for consistency with policy.

**Do not change yet**  
Do not remove or fully automate the role until the captured rules have been tested (shadow mode or controlled A/B). The coordination and boundary-spanning functions she currently supplies should be explicitly replaced before any reduction; bots, vendors and regional Team-Lead pairs can remain unchanged. Any redesign of specialist selection must preserve an exception-path capacity equivalent to the present one.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator occupies a structurally central yet operationally selective position in the claims process. She is highly central in handover and subcontracting networks because exception cases funnel through her for short routing, assignment and reopen actions; she is peripheral in the working-together network because those interventions are brief and do not keep her on the case. Evidence supports several overlapping interpretations—formal exception desk, key-person dependency for complex paths, and organizational boundary spanner—without a single ground-truth label.  

Her absence demonstrably lengthens only exception cases, creating bottleneck and key-person exposure, while the dual detection-and-assignment role raises a legitimate (but unproven) compliance-discretion concern. Ordinary claims, bots and vendors remain largely unaffected.  

Management should immediately document her routing knowledge and arrange backup coverage, monitor exception-cycle tails and assignment consistency, and refrain from removing or fully automating the function until an equivalent coordination capacity is in place and tested.
