# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Conventions used throughout: a *decision opportunity* is every service start, every preemption, and every moment a resource became free but stayed idle. A job is *eligible* at time t if its arrival scan is ≤ t (jobs scanned ≤ t+2 min are flagged as "possibly present" because scans can only be late, never early), it is not finished, not in service, and the deciding resource holds all of its required skills. `remaining` = `est_min` minus minutes already served. Rule predictions use arrival time as tie-breaker. "Observation" means it is in the tables; "estimate/hypothesis" is labelled as such.

| queue | primary_discipline | secondary_or_hybrid_component | confidence_0_to_100 | strongest_supporting_episode | strongest_contradicting_episode | main_identifiability_problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QA | FIFO (by arrival) | Appointment override at the exact appointment time (not before); priority field ignored | 80 | 09:05: A05 (p3, arrived 08:21) chosen while A08 (p1), A07 (shorter, 9 min) and A06 (appointment in 10 min) were all waiting; only FIFO predicts A05 | 08:20: A03 chosen over the FIFO head A02 and over p1/SPT choice A04; explained only by the 08:20 appointment | Whether the two p1 jobs (A04, A08) were really dispatch-ready when scanned; if they were not, several "FIFO wins" collapse into ties. Appointment tolerance (early window) is not observable because no appointment was ever started early |
| QB | Preemptive priority, but only for priority 1 (two-class "urgent vs. rest") | FIFO/resume-first among non-urgent work; appointments not honoured; one undocumented dispatcher intervention (09:48) | 60 | 09:16: B01 stopped two minutes after B02 (p1) arrived, reason "urgent arrival"; 10:00: B05 (p1) chosen over the half-finished B01 and the shorter B04 | 09:48: B01 stopped again ("dispatcher reassignment"), yet B03 (p2) was started while B05 (p1, arrived 09:46) waited until 10:00 | Cannot tell whether p2 outranks p3 (the one clean test, 10:28, says it does not), nor whether preemption is rule-driven or dispatcher discretion; SRPT also predicts the first preemption |

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

"–" means the rule has no applicable candidate. `*` marks a job whose scan is up to 2 min after t and may therefore actually have been present.

## Reproducible reconstruction (self-contained pandas)

PM4Py is not used: dispatching-rule inference is a resource/queue-perspective question on decision sets, not a control-flow discovery problem, and PM4Py's discovery and conformance semantics do not fit it. Plain pandas suffices.

```python

def m(t): h, mi = t.split(":"); return int(h)*60+int(mi)
def hm(x): return f"{x//60:02d}:{x%60:02d}"

                         for _, r in e.sort_values("arr_m").iterrows())
        print(f"{hm(t)} {res:4s} chosen={chosen} | {elig} | FIFO={fifo} PRIO={prio} SPT={spt} "
              f"APPT={ap.job.iloc[0] if len(ap) else '-'} EDD={ed.job.iloc[0] if len(ed) else '-'}")
```

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA — FIFO with exact-time appointment override

Supporting: A02 (08:32), A05 (09:05), A07 (09:32) are each the oldest waiting job and were chosen although a p1 job (A04, later A08) and a shorter job were available; A06 (09:20) is both FIFO head and overdue appointment; A03 (08:20) is chosen exactly at its appointment time against every other rule.

Challenging:
- A03 at 08:20 violates FIFO. Explained by the documented appointment (the note "appointment arrived early" confirms A03 was physically present since 08:08 and was still held until 08:20), so this is a *known* constraint, not an exception, and it establishes that appointments outrank both FIFO and priority in QA.
- A04 (p1) waited 45 min and A08 (p1) 61 min. Under the FIFO diagnosis this is expected; under the claimed priority documentation it is inexplicable. The only rescue for priority is an undocumented eligibility hold on both p1 jobs, which is implausible for two independent jobs.
- The 2-min idle gaps (08:00–08:02, 08:30–08:32) and the fact that A02 ended exactly at the calibration start are consistent with dispatch latency; a "fit the longest job that ends before calibration" reading is possible but is observationally identical to FIFO here.
- A06 was not started at 09:05 although its appointment was 10 min away and A1 was free; and A07 (9 min) was not slotted in front of it. So there is no early window ≥10 min and no fit-before-appointment planning; the late tolerance is at least 5 min.

## QB — p1-preemptive over a FIFO base

Supporting: B02 (09:16) triggers a preemption with a recorded urgent-arrival reason; B05 (10:00) is taken ahead of the resumable B01 and the shorter B04; B01 is resumed at 09:31 and 10:14 ahead of waiting p2 jobs (B03, B06), and B04 (10:28) is taken ahead of B06 (p2), which together show that only p1 carries weight.

Challenging:
- 09:48: B01 is stopped without an urgent-arrival reason and B03 (p2) is started while B05 (p1) waits 12 more minutes. Known constraints do not explain it; it requires an undocumented exception. Candidate explanations, in order: (i) an SLA/aging escalation for B03 (28 min waited), (ii) B05 not yet dispatch-ready despite its scan, (iii) manual dispatcher discretion (the label literally says "dispatcher reassignment"), (iv) B01 itself blocked (parts/test bench) and the stop misrecorded as a reassignment.
- The first preemption is equally predicted by SRPT (15 < 31 remaining), so preemptive priority is not uniquely identified by 09:16; it is the 10:00 choice (B05 over shorter B04) and the 09:31/10:14 resumptions (against SPT) that rule SRPT out.
- B07's appointment (10:30) is not honoured at 10:28 or 10:36 (26 min late, recorded as "not honored on time"); either QB does not implement appointment logic or the appointment referred to a different desk/resource.

## QC — SPT tendency with overrides

Supporting: C02 (10:10) and C03 (10:18) are the two shortest jobs and were chosen over the older C01 and over the higher-priority C03/C05; C06 (6 min) at 11:34 is chosen over the older C01 and the higher-priority, earlier-deadline C07. The note that C01, the oldest and longest job, ran last among the initial backlog is exactly what SPT produces.

Challenging:
- C04 over C06 at 10:32 (22 vs 6 min). Not explained by any rule with the recorded arrivals. Explained if (a) C06 was not dispatch-ready at 10:32 (7 min after scan), or (b) the dispatcher sequenced the 10:10 backlog once by SPT and did not re-plan — but (b) is contradicted by C06 jumping ahead of C01 at 11:34.
- C05 at 10:54 after a 43-min wait as p1 with a 10:50 deadline. Priority and strict EDD predict C05 at 10:18 and 10:32 as well; its selection only after the deadline had passed (the note "already at risk" understates this: 10:54 > 10:50, and the deadline was infeasible from arrival, 10:11 + 40 = 10:51) fits a *reactive* take-over once a deadline is breached, not proactive deadline management.
- C01 over C07 at 11:40. Choosing C07 (18 min) would have met both deadlines; choosing C01 missed C07's by 34 min. This is unexplained by SPT, priority, EDD, slack or any cost-aware rule. It requires either an aging/FIFO override (C01 had waited 100 min, C06 was chosen at 94 min, so any such threshold lies in the 95–100 min band and is fitted, not observed), unrecorded ineligibility of C07 until 12:16, or deadlines simply not being visible to dispatch.
- The C07 case shows that whatever QC does, it is not a deadline-protecting policy: two of two endangered-but-feasible deadlines that required a non-SPT choice were lost.

## QD — skill filter, FIFO inside, appointment hold

Supporting: every assignment respects skills (D02, D05 only on D-F; D07 only on D-X). D04 (11:25) and D07 (11:48) are the oldest eligible jobs and were chosen over shorter p1 jobs. D06 starts exactly at its 11:30 appointment, on the resource that had been kept free.

Challenging:
- D-X idle 11:20–11:30 while D08 (p1, 8 min) or D04 (10 min) would have fitted exactly. Explained if the appointment implies a resource hold with a safety margin, if D06 was pre-assigned to D-X at booking, or if D-X's true availability began later than the nominal 11:20 — all three are undocumented.
- D-F idle 11:36–12:00 while D10 (p1, electrical, 12 min) was eligible and fitted. Not starting D05 (30 min) is a defensible non-preemptive fit-before-unavailability decision; ignoring D10 is not explainable by any listed rule and requires an exception (D10 not ready, D-F left early, unlogged work, or D10 pre-routed to D-X).
- D05 (p2, only D-F qualified) waited 73 min. A skill-aware router would reserve the scarce resource; QD's skill logic is a pure eligibility filter, not an optimising one.
- D10's note ("D-F still unavailable until 12:20") is the one place where the dispatcher visibly reasons about resource state; it confirms availability-awareness at 12:16 but makes the 11:36 idleness harder to excuse.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Test 1 — QB at 09:48: rule-driven priority with an eligibility gap vs. SLA/aging escalation

- Explanation E1 (two-class preemptive priority, B05 not dispatch-ready until ~10:00): the stop at 09:48 should not have occurred at all; if it occurred for an external reason, the next job should be B01 resumed or B03 by FIFO. Prediction for the actual next job: B03 (matches), but E1 must also predict B05 starting the moment it became ready — which would be 10:00 only if readiness happened to coincide with B03's completion (a coincidence E1 cannot explain).
- Explanation E2 (SLA slack escalation: a p2 job approaching a ~30-min wait limit pre-empts the running p3, and p1 has a ~15-min limit): predicts the stop at 09:48, B03 first (slack 2 min), then B05 (slack 1 min at 10:00), then B01 (still within its p3 limit) — matching all three observations. Its weakness is that the limits are fitted to the data.
- Discriminating observable: the *SLA target table per priority class* (or, failing that, the readiness timestamp of B05 and the target job named in the "dispatcher reassignment" event). If B05 was ready at 09:46 and no SLA table exists, E2 collapses into "discretion"; if a p2 target of about 30 min exists, E2 is confirmed and QB is an SLA-slack hybrid rather than a priority queue.

## Test 2 — QC at 11:40: SPT with an age override vs. C07 not dispatch-ready

- Explanation F1 (SPT plus age cap ≈ 95–100 min): predicts C01 at 11:40 and predicts that at 11:34 (C01 at 94 min) C06 is still chosen — matching. It also predicts that, had C07 arrived 10 min earlier, it would still have lost to C01 at 11:40.
- Explanation F2 (pure SPT; C07 not dispatchable until ~12:16, e.g. waiting for a part or a customer signature): predicts C07 at 11:40 if it was ready; the observed C01 is then a data artefact, not a policy.
- Discriminating observable: a *ready-for-dispatch* timestamp or a parts/customer-status change event for C07 between 10:40 and 12:16. A second discriminator is any future decision in which a job older than ~95 min competes with a much shorter new job: F1 predicts the old job, F2 the short one.

## Test 3 — QD at 11:20–11:30: appointment hold vs. late actual availability of D-X

- Explanation G1 (D-X reserved for the 11:30 appointment, possibly pre-assigned at booking): D-X starts nothing that could overrun 11:30; because D08 (8 min) would have fitted with 2 min to spare, G1 implies a safety margin > 2 min or a strict "appointment-holder does nothing before the appointment" rule.
- Explanation G2 (D-X actually became available at ~11:30; the 11:20 window is nominal): identical predicted observation (first job D06 at 11:30), so the two are observationally equivalent on this log.
- Discriminating observable: D-X's login/clock-in or "available" state event, or the booking record showing whether D06 was booked against D-X specifically. A behavioural discriminator: a day on which D-X is free 15+ minutes before an appointment — G1 predicts idleness or only very short jobs, G2 predicts normal FIFO behaviour.

## Test 4 — QA at 08:20: appointment override vs. A04 not ready

- Under "appointment override" A03 is chosen at 08:20 regardless of A04's state; under "A04 not ready" plus pure FIFO the choice should have been A02, not A03, so this pair is already separated by the data — the appointment explanation wins. The remaining ambiguity is the *early* tolerance: A03 was present from 08:08 but A1 was busy until 08:20, so nothing is learned about whether it would have been started early. Discriminating observable: an appointment that falls due while its resource is idle.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from most to least plausible; each entry states how it would alter the diagnosis.

1. Arrival scan is not dispatch-readiness (data-quality). Jobs may need triage, parts, or a present customer before they enter the dispatchable set; the log records only the scan. Affected decisions: A04/A08 in QA, B05 at 09:48, C06 at 10:32, C07 at 11:40, D08 at 11:20/11:25, D10 at 11:36/11:48. If true for the p1 jobs, the "priority ignored" finding in QA and QD would weaken to "untested"; if true for C06 and C07, QC becomes a clean SPT queue with breached-deadline take-over (6/6). It is ranked first because it is the single explanation that touches every anomaly, but its extreme form (every bypassed p1 job was unready, seven times across three queues) is not credible.

2. Resource availability differs from the nominal table (data-quality / operational). D-X may have started at 11:30 rather than 11:20; D-F may have left for the 12:00 meeting early or done unlogged work 11:36–12:00; A1's 2-min gaps are dispatch latency. Would remove both QD idle anomalies without any policy change and raise QD confidence to roughly 80. Ranked second because the D-X window is precisely the kind of "planned" figure that differs from reality, whereas D-F's early absence is speculative.

3. Appointments are "not-before" constraints with resource pre-assignment (semantics). Explains exact-time starts (A03, D06), the absence of any early start, the D-X hold, and the late start of A06 (resource busy). Under this reading, QB's failure to honour B07 means the appointment belonged to another channel or that B1 never receives appointment information; the QB diagnosis would be unchanged, and the QA/QD diagnoses would become "FIFO/skill with a hard appointment constraint" rather than a hybrid discipline.

4. Realized durations equal `est_min` for all 32 jobs (data-quality). Zero estimation error is implausible in a repair shop; more likely completion times were derived from the estimates when the log was compiled, technicians work to the estimate, or estimates were entered after the fact. Consequences: SPT and SEPT are indistinguishable; strategic estimate inflation (e.g. C01 = 36 to justify deferring it) cannot be tested; preemption/resumption overhead in QB (B01 = exactly 45 min) is either zero or invisible; and any conclusion about "fit before break/appointment" precision is unreliable. This does not change the labels but caps confidence in QC and in all SPT claims.

5. Undocumented SLA/aging escalation (policy). B03 at 09:48 (28 min waited), C05 at 10:54 (just after its deadline passed) and C01 at 11:40 (100 min waited) each look like a job being forced once a time threshold is crossed. If confirmed, QB becomes an SLA-slack hybrid, QC becomes "SPT with age and breach overrides", and priorities are re-interpreted as SLA classes rather than dispatch ranks. This is also the mechanism the task flags ("policy changes when an SLA is endangered"); note it is *reactive* in QC (C07's feasible deadline was not protected).

6. Manual dispatcher discretion (operational). The "dispatcher reassignment" label at 09:48 and the D10 note indicate a human in the loop. Discretion cannot be falsified with these data; the more decisions attributed to it, the less any queue can be said to have a discipline. It should be treated as the residual once 1–5 are excluded.

7. Priority is a customer/billing label not consumed by dispatch (semantics). Consistent with 14 bypasses of p1 jobs and with p2 never outranking p3. If true, "management's documented rule" for QA, QC and QD is simply not implemented, and only QB's urgent-arrival preemption uses the field. This is less an exception than the leading interpretation of the priority evidence.

8. Delayed arrival scans (data-quality, explicitly stated). Because scans are only late, the true eligible set at any time is a superset of the one reconstructed; this can only make more jobs eligible (C05 at 10:10, A02 at 08:02, D03 at 11:01, D09 at 11:20) and therefore strengthens, never weakens, the "higher-priority/shorter job was bypassed" findings. It cannot rescue any priority claim.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritised by how many open ambiguities each item resolves.

1. Dispatch-ready (queue-entry) timestamp, distinct from arrival scan, plus state-change events (triage complete, parts available, customer present). Resolves exceptions 1 and 8; decisive for A04/A08, B05, C06/C07, D08/D10.
2. Dispatch-decision event: timestamp, decision-maker (rule engine vs. dispatcher), and the *candidate/eligibility set as displayed* at that moment, including each candidate's priority, estimate, appointment and deadline as shown. Turns every "reconstructed" decision set into an observed one and exposes discretion directly (QB 09:48, QC 11:40).
3. Resource state log: login/logout, actual break/meeting/calibration start and end, unavailability reasons. Needed for D-X 11:20–11:30, D-F 11:36–12:00, A1 08:30–08:32, C1's "prior job ends 10:10".
4. Policy configuration and version per queue with effective timestamps: which fields the rule engine consumes (priority? deadline? appointment?), tie-break rules, preemption thresholds (minimum remaining time), aging/SLA escalation parameters and SLA targets per priority class. Directly tests exceptions 5 and 7 and Test 1.
5. Appointment metadata: booking time, early/late tolerance window, whether a specific resource was pre-assigned, and no-show/late rules. Discriminates G1 from G2 and explains QB's disregard of B07.
6. Preemption events with structured reason codes and the triggering job ID, plus resumption setup time (or a separate "resume" activity). Needed to separate rule-driven from discretionary preemption and to measure the real resumption cost hidden by B01's exact 45 min.
7. Independent completion timestamps (device or system-generated) and the estimate author/time of entry, together with periodic "estimated remaining time" updates during service. Needed to test SPT vs. SEPT, strategic estimates, and the suspicious zero estimation error.
8. Deadline visibility flag for QC: whether the deadline column is shown to dispatch or is only a customer promise. Decides whether EDD/slack explanations are even admissible.
9. Skill/certification matrix with validity dates and tool/bench constraints for QD, to check whether D10 could really have been served by D-F at 11:36.
10. Longer horizon: several days per queue. Most disciplines here rest on 5–7 discriminating decisions; a few hundred would allow rule-fit statistics instead of case-by-case argument.

# 7. Final synthesis

Most identifiable: QA. Six discriminating decisions, five of which are predicted only by FIFO and the sixth only by the documented appointment; two p1 jobs were bypassed five times in total, so the priority claim can be rejected rather than merely doubted. The residual uncertainty is about appointment tolerance and dispatch-readiness, not about the base rule.

QD is second: skill eligibility is respected in every assignment (necessarily observable), the two clean within-set choices are FIFO, and appointments act as a hard not-before constraint. Its two idle episodes cannot be attributed to policy versus availability with these data, and the D10/D-F episode is the one QD observation no rule explains.

QB is identifiable as a two-class system (priority 1 preempts, everything else FIFO with resumption first, appointments ignored) but not as the "preemptive priority" queue management presumably documents: p2 never beats p3, and the 09:48 stop-and-reassign episode is either an SLA escalation, an eligibility artefact, or discretion.

Least identifiable: QC. Priorities and deadlines are perfectly collinear, the only p1 job had an infeasible deadline from the moment it arrived, realized durations equal estimates, and every single-rule explanation fails at least three of six decisions. The most defensible summary is "SPT-like, with a job taken over only after its deadline has already been breached, and at least one late choice (C01 over C07) that a deadline- or priority-aware dispatcher would not make."

The claimed policy most dangerous to infer from these observations alone is priority scheduling. Priority-1 jobs were passed over at 14 decision opportunities across QA, QC and QD and were never preferred where the choice was not also explainable by another rule, except in QB. Labelling any of QA, QC or QD as "priority" would be contradicted by the majority of their own decisions. A close second is inferring a deadline-driven (EDD/slack) policy for QC from the C05 note: the one deadline-consistent choice occurred after the deadline was already lost, while the one protectable deadline (C07) was sacrificed. Conversely, the safest cross-queue statement is that arrival order (FIFO) is the default in three of the four queues, modulated by hard constraints — appointments, skills, resource availability — and, in QB only, by urgent preemption.
