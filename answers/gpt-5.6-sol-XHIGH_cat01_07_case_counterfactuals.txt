## 1. Feasibility model

```text
For each case c:

Register_c  Triage_c  every later activity.

If originals are not required:
    Triage_c  Identity_c
    Triage_c  Document_c
    Identity_c  Document_c
    join(Identity_c, Document_c)  Assessment_0,c

If originals are required:
    Triage_c  Identity_c
    Triage_c  OriginalsReceipt_c  Document_0,c
    Identity_c  (OriginalsReceipt_c  Document_0,c)
    join(Identity_c, Document_0,c)  Assessment_0,c

For any formal rework j:
    (Assessment_j,c or Compliance_j,c)  RequestMoreInfo_j,c
    RequestMoreInfo_j,c(response complete)  Document_(j+1),c
    Document_(j+1),c  Assessment_(j+1),c

For high-risk or high-value cases:
    Assessment_final,c  Compliance_final,c  Decision_c  Notify_c

For other cases:
    Assessment_final,c  Decision_c  Notify_c

Permitted preliminary branch:
    Triage_c  EscalationAdvice_pre,c  ComplianceScreen_pre,c
    ComplianceScreen_pre,c may overlap verification
    join(Assessment_final,c, ComplianceScreen_pre,c)  Compliance_final,c
```

```text
FIRST: Register is the first event and Triage is the next process activity.
ORIGINALS: If originals are required, no Document Check may start before receipt.
RMI AUTHORITY: Only Assessment or Compliance may issue a formal Request More Info.
RMI REWORK: Every formal Request More Info requires another Document Check and Assessment.
HIGH RISK/VALUE: A new or explicitly reaffirmed final Compliance Review is required after rework.
PRELIMINARY REVIEW: Early compliance work never substitutes for post-assessment final review.
FOUR EYES: resource(Assessment_final) != resource(Compliance_final).
CAPACITY: No resource has overlapping service intervals; queuing is permitted.
QUEUES: Snapshot estimates are uncertain forecasts and do not confer priority.
K-104: A provisional C1 reservation cannot authorize final review before Assessment.
K-219: Assignment to D2 at originals receipt means immediate queue entry, not guaranteed immediate service.
K-337: After Assessment 2, no further RMI is allowed; the discrepancy must be recorded and resolved by rejection or lawful conditions.
K-411: Concurrency is valid only when server or activity logs show actual overlapping work, not merely identical batch timestamps.
```

K-411 changes task release and timing only; its mandatory activity set and decision policy remain unchanged. K-104 augments preparation and reserves capacity, but does not remove any mandatory review. K-219 changes routing from D1 to D2. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by removing a rework branch and forcing a decision on incomplete evidence.

The case-level traces are treated as separate scenarios. If interpreted as one joint schedule, the supplied K-219 and K-337 D1 intervals overlap and therefore cannot both satisfy the stated resource-capacity rule.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Identity and documents were completed in their factual order, but neither caused the late delay directly. Assessment ended Tuesday at 19:10, after which the same sanctions issue generated two hours of S1 advice. C1 then waited until Wednesday 13:10 and needed 4.5 hours because little reusable analysis existed.

Minimal intervention: At Monday 08:50, create an S1 preliminary-advice task, send the available sanctions indicators, and reserve a tentative C1 continuation slot after the forecast Assessment. Keep I1, D1, A1, the final C1 review, and the decision authority unchanged. The reservation is a capacity hold, not permission to conduct final review early.

Temporal propagation: S1 can analyze the ambiguity while identity and complex-document work continue. C1 may prepare search results, issue lists, and evidence references in a nonoverlapping preliminary interval. Identity, D1 completion, and A1’s Tuesday 15:10–19:10 Assessment need not move. The gain arises after Assessment: late S1 work is avoided, reusable notes reduce final C1 service, and the provisional slot can replace much of the factual overnight queue. Notification moves only if M1 has an earlier signing window.

Process-feasibility check: Identity and Document Check still precede Assessment. Final C1 review begins only after A1 completes the final Assessment, and A1 and C1 satisfy four eyes. The preliminary screen is explicitly non-final. Separate C1 preliminary and final intervals must not overlap any C1 work already committed to other cases; any priority effect is displacement, not added capacity.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, the held slot starts just after Tuesday 19:10, final review takes roughly 2–3 hours, and notification occurs Tuesday late or Wednesday morning, saving about 15–20 hours. Base case, C1 starts Wednesday morning and notification is 6–14 hours earlier. Worst case, later evidence contradicts the packet or the forecast slot is missed, causing a full reread and no gain or a small delay.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Low reuse rates for preliminary work, high reservation no-show rates, evidence that M1 cannot sign before Wednesday evening, or an increase in urgent-case C1 waiting larger than K-104’s gain would make the intervention unattractive.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Originals arrived Monday at 17:30, but the case waited until Tuesday 09:30 for D1. D1’s four-hour accurate service avoided rework. Thus the intervention trades a long wait for a potentially slower and less reliable document reviewer.

Minimal intervention: Preserve Identity Check and originals receipt, but place the document task in D2’s queue at 17:30. Do not claim that D2 starts immediately. If D2 flags illegible evidence, A2 conducts the Assessment and is the actor that issues any formal Request More Info.

Temporal propagation: The Monday 16:00 snapshot forecasts about five hours at D2. A plausible no-rework service interval therefore begins Monday evening and finishes approximately Tuesday 02:30–09:00, versus D1’s factual 13:30 completion. This moves the join of identity and documents earlier. The case then enters A2’s congested queue earlier, but the Tuesday training condition and M1’s signing calendar can absorb the entire readiness gain.

Process-feasibility check: Originals precede every D2 Document Check, and completed identity plus documents precede Assessment. No compliance review is needed. In the adverse branch, D2 records the handwriting problem, A2 formally requests information, and the response is followed by a new D2 check and A2 Assessment before decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, D2 clears the handwriting and the case catches earlier A2 and M1 windows, saving 10–18 hours. Base case, it becomes assessment-ready several hours earlier but still waits for Wednesday’s M1 window, saving 0–8 hours. Worst case, clarification adds 8–36 external hours plus D2 and A2 queues and service, making the case roughly 18–60 hours slower.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 handwriting first-pass yield, actual queue-entry and start logs, clarification-response distributions, assessor cutoff rules, and M1 availability are decisive. A low clarification rate and routinely usable Tuesday signoff would favor D2; otherwise D1 may dominate.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The second request began Friday at 10:30, and the supplier did not respond until Monday. The case then incurred D1 queue and service, an overnight wait for A1, another assessment, C1 waiting and service, and an overnight wait for M1. Almost five days elapsed after Assessment 2.

Minimal intervention: Designate Assessment 2 as the final Assessment, carry its unresolved inconsistency explicitly into C1’s final review, and forbid C1 from opening another information loop. C1 must recommend either rejection or a legally supportable conditioned approval; it cannot mark the evidence complete without qualification.

Temporal propagation: At Friday 10:30 the case moves directly to C1 rather than to the supplier. The Thursday snapshot suggests a five-hour C1 queue, although Friday priority arrivals could change it. A Friday review could finish that evening; absent a Friday M1 window, the decision may wait until Monday. The intervention removes the weekend response, Monday D1 work, Tuesday A1 work, and their associated queues, but it pulls C1 demand forward.

Process-feasibility check: Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 remain valid prerequisites. Because the case is high risk and high value, C1 still performs a post-Assessment final review, distinct from A1. Decision follows C1 and notification follows Decision. The unresolved discrepancy is stated in the recommendation and decision rationale, satisfying the prohibition on silently ignoring it.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, C1 and M1 act Friday and notification is approximately 105–115 hours earlier. Base case, C1 reviews Friday or Monday and M1 signs Monday, saving about 48 hours. Worst case, C1 remains queued until Tuesday, producing little cycle gain while still forcing a rejection or constrained decision; a later appeal can make total resolution slower than factual.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Proof that the discrepancy is immaterial and conditions are effective would improve the policy’s quality case. Conversely, a legal requirement for the missing confirmation, high appeal rates, or large losses from decisions on incomplete schedules would outweigh the initial-cycle saving.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Triage created only the identity task. The document task did not exist until I2 completed at 13:20, after which D2 started at 15:00 and completed at 19:30. The late join time placed K-411 into an assessment queue that was worsening during Tuesday.

Minimal intervention: At Tuesday 09:35, create both verification tasks against one controlled case version, permit I2 and D2 to own their respective tasks simultaneously, and capture server-side start and completion times. Keep I2, D2, A2, and M1 unchanged.

Temporal propagation: With the Tuesday 08:00 D2 forecast of four hours, the early document task might start around midday and overlap part of I2’s 11:30–13:20 interval. A plausible D2 completion around 16:00–19:00 advances joint readiness by zero to several hours. Earlier queue entry can produce a larger downstream gain if it catches a late-Tuesday or early-Wednesday A2 slot, but it can also merely change queue position and displace another case.

Process-feasibility check: Originals are not required, so identity and documents may overlap. Both still complete before A2 starts; Decision remains after Assessment and Notify after Decision. I2 and D2 are different resources. Valid concurrency requires actual service intervals and authoritative-version logs, not tasks backdated to their creation time.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, D2 starts early, the case catches a Tuesday assessment window and notification is 10–15 hours earlier. Base case, the case advances within the Wednesday assessment queue and saves 2–8 hours. Worst case, Assessment remains the binding constraint and there is no cycle benefit; a version-integrity hold could add several hours.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If early D2 creation rarely changes its actual start, A2 has no usable earlier capacity, or workaround cases have materially higher version conflicts, the intervention becomes a logging improvement rather than a cycle-time improvement.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early preparation and reservation | Adds S1 work and early C1 work; final C1 service may shorten, but total C1 effort may rise through duplicate reading or unused holds. | Target’s post-assessment compliance wait; possibly final-review workload if preparation is reusable. | C1 waiting for urgent, nonreserved cases. | Urgent high-risk cases and cases whose Assessment finishes without a reservation. | Ambiguous; positive only with high reuse and disciplined slot release. | One C1 means reservations, no-shows, and priority arrivals have disproportionate effects. |
| K-219 D2 routing | Removes one first-pass job from D1 and adds it to D2; clarification adds external, D2, and A2 demand. | D1 queue. | D2 and possibly Assessment queues. | Standard D2 cases; complex cases that need D1 after D2 becomes congested. | Ambiguous and sensitive to clarification probability. | A small increase in rework can erase all waiting-time savings. |
| K-337 suppress second loop | Avoids one external response, one D1 check, and one A1 Assessment; moves C1 and Decision demand earlier; may create appeals. | D1 and A1 rework queues. | Near-term C1 queue and later appeal or reconsideration queues. | Cases competing for Friday/Monday C1 and M1 capacity. | Operationally positive for initial cycle time, but welfare and compliance effects may be negative. | Weekend avoidance creates a large apparent gain, while rare adverse decisions can dominate expected loss. |
| K-411 concurrent verification | Verification volume is unchanged but D2 work and assessment arrivals occur earlier and more synchronously. | Case-lock delay and sequential verification waiting. | Assessment queue; transient D2 queue at release peaks. | Cases already waiting for A2 and cases displaced by earlier D2 queue entry. | Neutral to positive if Assessment has spare capacity; near zero if it is binding. | Earlier release changes queue order and burstiness without increasing throughput. |

At 100 cases per intervention, the ceteris-paribus assumption fails. For K-104, one C1 cannot turn reservations into capacity. One hundred high-risk cases already imply roughly 300–600 hours of final review before preliminary work. Provisional holds, late Assessments, and no-shows would fragment the calendar and delay urgent non-target cases unless holds have release deadlines and priority override rules. Preliminary reuse could reduce final service, but duplicate reading could make the compliance bottleneck worse.

K-219 substitutes D2 demand for D1 demand. Initially this relieves the specialist and may exploit unused D2 time. At scale, D2’s queue grows, its early-start advantage disappears, and difficult handwriting generates extra document and assessment work. Once the clarification probability crosses a modest threshold, rework amplifies both queues and can send cases back across weekends.

K-337 removes D1 and A1 rework from 100 cases, but it advances 100 incomplete-evidence cases to C1 and M1. Its single-case gain is unusually dependent on suppressing a Friday-to-Monday supplier delay. Requests made earlier in the week show less benefit, while appeals, reconsiderations, or loss investigations can create a delayed workload not visible in initial-cycle metrics.

K-411 leaves verification service volume nearly unchanged but synchronizes releases into Assessment. If A2 is already constrained by training or arrivals, verification queues shrink while Assessment becomes the bottleneck; target cases may gain queue position only by delaying non-target cases. Real-time timestamps improve measurement and expose this displacement, but timestamps do not create true concurrency or capacity. Batch-entered starts could falsely suggest overlap, so server, ownership, and case-version logs must be reconciled before attributing any throughput gain.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | K-411: low-risk, low-value, standard digital cases with no originals and one authoritative electronic file. | K-104: high-risk or high-value, no-originals cases with a triage-detectable ambiguity and a minimum usable preliminary packet; exclude cases already requiring immediate escalation. |
| assignment rule | Randomize at Triage within day, D2 queue band, and assessor-availability band to legacy sequential release or controlled dual-task release. | Randomize within ambiguity type and forecast Assessment-completion band to usual late escalation or early S1 packet plus provisional C1 reservation. |
| sample/support logic | Begin with a monitored technical run, then target roughly 50–75 cases per arm; extend until the interval for median readiness improvement excludes a trivial effect and integrity events remain rare. | Use a constrained-capacity pilot of roughly 25–40 cases per arm across several weeks; reserve only a pre-agreed fraction of C1 time and analyze by treatment assignment. |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify time, reported with quantiles and time-to-event analysis rather than only a mean. | Register-to-Notify time plus Assessment-completion-to-final-Compliance-completion time. |
| process-path outcome | True overlap duration, time to both-check readiness, and both-check readiness-to-Assessment start. | Preliminary-work reuse rate, final C1 service time, reservation utilization, missed-slot rate, and late S1 work avoided. |
| quality outcome | Duplicate ownership, wrong-version access, verification discrepancies, reopenings, and decision reversals. | Compliance corrections, omitted evidence, decision changes, conditions, reopenings, and four-eyes exceptions. |
| queue-spillover outcome | D2 and Assessment median and 90th-percentile waits for non-target cases, by hour and day. | Urgent and nonreserved C1 waits, slot fragmentation, overtime, and priority overrides. |
| logging validation | Reconcile task creation, ownership, server access, field-edit, start, and completion logs; reject batch-entered overlap as evidence. | Link reservation creation, release, C1 access, preliminary artifacts, final review references, and actual service intervals. |
| stopping rule | Stop for any material wrong-version event, repeated false concurrency, or sustained non-target Assessment-wait deterioration beyond a prespecified control limit. | Stop for any final review before Assessment, four-eyes breach, material quality event, or sustained urgent-case C1 delay beyond a prespecified control limit. |

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust. It restores concurrency already permitted by the normative process, preserves all decision and quality gates, uses separate resources, and is readily reversible. Its benefit should nevertheless be stated as earlier verification readiness rather than guaranteed earlier notification. When Assessment is binding, the intervention mainly moves the case forward in that queue and may displace another case.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potentially large speed gain. The factual second loop crossed a weekend and generated substantial D1, A1, and C1 delay, so deleting it produces an unusually attractive initial-cycle counterfactual. But the intervention is not merely faster execution: it forces a high-risk, high-value decision on acknowledged incomplete evidence. Rejection, conditioned approval, appeal, loss exposure, and compliance defensibility can dominate the saved hours. A shadow-decision study is safer than a live pilot.

K-104’s reserved-slot benefit is least transportable from one case to a system-wide policy. A single target can gain by receiving early C1 attention, but 100 reservations cannot expand a one-person compliance pool. Slot fragmentation, inaccurate Assessment forecasts, duplicate reading, and urgent-case displacement can reverse the system-wide effect. K-219 similarly loses its advantage if D2 congestion or clarification rework rises, while K-411 can migrate congestion into Assessment.

The most valuable additional data are event-level queue entry, assignment, actual start, interruption, completion, and release timestamps; resource calendars and training periods; reservation creation, release, no-show, and override records; C1 preliminary artifacts and their reuse in final review; external request-send and response timestamps; M1 signing windows; authoritative-version access logs; and decision reasons, conditions, appeals, reopenings, and realized losses. Non-target queue distributions are essential because target cycle time alone cannot identify displacement.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The four traces are separate scenario realizations rather than one joint schedule; otherwise the supplied K-219 and K-337 D1 intervals violate the no-overlap rule.
2. `temporal` — Event timestamps represent actual elapsed time, but unreported shift calendars and weekend availability remain uncertain.
3. `resource` — Snapshot queue values approximate waiting workload at arrival and do not guarantee service order or start time.
4. `behavioral` — D2 flags handwriting problems to A2, which issues any formal Request More Info in accordance with the stated authority rule.
5. `quality` — K-337’s remaining discrepancy is potentially material and must appear explicitly in C1’s recommendation and the decision rationale.
6. `interference` — Single-case derivations hold other behavior approximately fixed, whereas the 100-case rollout allows endogenous queue, priority, and staffing responses.
7. `resource` — K-104 preliminary and final C1 intervals do not overlap, and preliminary work saves time only when its evidence references remain reusable.
8. `temporal` — Earlier downstream readiness produces earlier notification only when A2 or M1 has a usable earlier slot; readiness alone is not treated as completion.
