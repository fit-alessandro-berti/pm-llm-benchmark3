## 1. Conformance semantics

A deviation is classified by comparing the observation against the **status** of the normative relation, not by checking whether a relation exists in the graph.

- **Forbidden behavior** (closed-world): Explicitly disallowed (N3, T6, E10). Any observed instance within the stated scope is a potential violation until disproved by evidence (e.g., event ordering error). The burden is on the observation.
- **Missing mandatory behavior** (closed-world within scope): Mandatory relations (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8) must hold when their scope applies. A “gap” is not automatic noncompliance if (a) the scope is uncertain (e.g., N4 applies only to “packages intended for external shipment”), (b) the abstraction is too strict (N1 uses a directly-follows approximation for an “eventually” rule), or (c) the event is present but unextracted. I distinguish *scope exclusion* from *process failure*.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: Relations absent from the norm and not marked forbidden (e.g., Validate Order  Issue Invoice, Create Order  Manual Compliance Review). The norm is open-world here; presence is allowed but not required.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: Typical relations (N2, N5, T3) can legitimately lack instances (credit terms, open invoices, intermediate review events). Absence is not a deviation.
- **Likely data-quality error**: Suspicious join artifacts (Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice via reused numeric key), attribute-to-object omissions (carrier reference stored but no Delivery object), or count mismatches (Add Item: 1448 Order vs 1450 Item; Dispatch Package: 200 Package/ET-OT vs 198 Delivery). These are log defects, not business-process violations.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: The OC-DFG summary approximates “eventually expected” rules (N1) with direct follow. A missing direct edge can mask an intermediate event (e.g., hidden compliance review). This is a modeling gap, not necessarily a process gap.
- **Insufficient evidence**: When a count is very low (2 orders, 1 enrichment, 3 compliance reviews) or attribution is ambiguous, I retain uncertainty rather than escalate severity.

**Open-world vs. closed-world impact**: Only forbidden relations and the mandatory columns within an explicitly stated scope are closed-world. Everything else—intermediate events, unspecified object types (T4, T5, N6), unmodeled relations (ValidateIssue Invoice), and codeath—remains open. Thus, “not in the norm” is not “against the norm” unless the relation is forbidden or mandatory and within scope.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**What it establishes:** Order-level process flow with object-lifecycle transitions (Create, Validate, Issue Invoice, Close, Cancel, Reopen, Pack, Dispatch). It uses counts to approximate compliance with normative relations.

**What it cannot establish:** It cannot verify “eventually” properties without intermediate-event coverage. It also cannot distinguish a missing order event from an unrecorded one, and it lacks object-level interaction detail (that requires OTG).

**Interpretation of counts and scope:**
- **N1 (mandatory)**: 98/100 suggests high compliance, but the summary explicitly warns that the two missing orders may have an unrecorded review event. Because N1 is approximated by direct follow, absence in this abstraction is *not* conclusive evidence of non-validation. The open-world assumption applies: intermediate events are permitted unless forbidden.
- **N2 (typical)**: 83 Direct ValidateClose means 17 orders have intermediates (Issue Invoice, review, fulfilment). This is expected and desirable per the norm.
- **N3 (forbidden)**: 2 instances of CancelDispatch are unambiguous violations of an explicit disallowance, assuming event ordering is correct.
- **N4 (mandatory)**: 198/200 externally shipped packages have the direct edge. For the 2 missing, scope is decisive: if they were not intended for external shipment, they are not violations. The presence of a carrier reference in an event attribute suggests extraction failure rather than process failure.
- **N5 (typical)**: 92/110 with 14 open and 4 write-off is a healthy ratio; absence of the direct edge is legitimate.
- **N6 (unspecified)**: The 1 ReopenClose is permitted because reopening is outside the norm’s governance.

**Danger of “eventually expected” as direct-follow:** If N1 is interpreted as requiring *immediate* validation, the 2 missing edges become false positive violations. The correct reasoning is: (a) check for hidden events between Create and Validate; (b) if none exist and the order is still unvalidated, it is a missing mandatory behavior; (c) if hidden events exist, it is an abstraction mismatch.

### OTG

**What it establishes:** Object-to-object interaction (Order-Item, Package-Delivery, Invoice-Payment) and lifecycle co-occurrence (cobirth, codeath).

**What it cannot establish:** Interaction weights do not prove business correctness of each link; they only show frequency. Cobirth/codeath absence/presence is not governed for most pairs.

**Interpretation:**
- **T1 (mandatory)**: 1450 Order-Item edges align with 1450 Add Item–Item events (E3) and 1448 Add Item–Order (E2). The 2-event discrepancy suggests weak extraction, not a process violation.
- **T2 (mandatory)**: 200 Package-Delivery edges correspond to 200 Pack/Dispatch events; however, ET-OT shows 198 Delivery objects, meaning 2 interactions may be missing objects, not missing business actions.
- **T3 (typical)**: 92 Invoice-Payment links match 92 Record Payment–Invoice edges. The 1 extra Payment (93 total) is consistent with unapplied cash, permitted by the norm’s open-world exception.
- **T4/T5 (unspecified)**: 100 Customer and 3 RiskCase interactions are fully legitimate because the norm explicitly excludes them from coverage.
- **T6 (forbidden)**: The 1 Order-Payment cobirth is a direct violation of an explicit system constraint.
- **Codeath (unregulated)**: 7 Package-Delivery co-death edges arise from shared confirmation events; the norm says nothing, so they are benign.

**Cross-abstraction consistency:** A relation can conform at ET-OT (Dispatch Package event exists) but be suspicious at OTG (no Delivery object extracted) or at OC-DFG (no direct PackDispatch because object missing). Thus, conformance at one layer can mask data-quality gaps at another.

### ET-OT

**What it establishes:** Mandatory or permitted event-type-to-object-type mappings for each event instance.

**What it cannot establish:** It does not enforce ordering, frequency, or interaction semantics; those belong to OC-DFG and OTG.

**Interpretation:**
- **E1–E3**: Near-perfect coverage; the 2-event gap in Add ItemOrder is likely extraction error.
- **E4–E6**: 200/200 for Package; 198/200 for Delivery confirms object-link gap, not event absence.
- **E7–E8**: 93 Payment vs 92 Invoice records suggests the 1 unapplied-cash case. Because the event is not labeled “unapplied,” it is ambiguous, but the norm permits exceptions.
- **E9**: 3 Manual Compliance ReviewRiskCase is expected given T5/E9 unspecified status.
- **E10**: 1 Sensor HeartbeatInvoice is forbidden, but the context (reused numeric ID) strongly suggests a data-quality error rather than a true system violation.

**Cross-layer reasoning:** The 2 missing PackageDispatch in OC-DFG can be explained by missing Delivery objects (ET-OT E6 gap) or missing external-shipment scope, not necessarily by missing business action.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 lacks abstraction clarification** (direct-follow vs. eventually).
   - *Risk*: False positive violations for orders with valid hidden reviews.
   - *Clarification*: State that direct-follow is an approximation; require intermediate-event verification before violation.
   - *Status*: Should remain mandatory but with explicit abstraction note.

2. **N4 has no “external shipment” criteria**.
   - *Risk*: Internal packages incorrectly flagged; or external packages incorrectly exempted.
   - *Clarification*: Define external-shipment flag (e.g., delivery address type, carrier contract).
   - *Status*: Mandatory, with clarified scope.

3. **E8 lacks “unapplied cash” labeling rules**.
   - *Risk*: 1 ambiguous event; potential false mandatory failure.
   - *Clarification*: Require explicit event subtype or attribute tag for unapplied cash.
   - *Status*: Mandatory with explicit exception labeling.

4. **No intermediate-event specification** (ValidateIssue Invoice, Manual Compliance Review).
   - *Risk*: Unmodeled relations are treated as suspect by analysts.
   - *Clarification*: Add permitted-intermediate list or note that intermediate events do not violate mandatory direct-follow approximations.
   - *Status*: Unregulated / permitted; add to norm appendix.

5. **No codeath / co-termination policy** (Package-Delivery).
   - *Risk*: 7 instances interpreted as errors or as violations depending on analyst bias.
   - *Clarification*: Explicitly state that concurrent lifecycle termination is permitted.
   - *Status*: Explicitly unspecified / permitted.

6. **No frequency / statistical tolerance**.
   - *Risk*: 2/100 or 1/200 cannot be objectively distinguished from process drift or noise.
   - *Clarification*: Add statistical control band (e.g., 95% confidence interval) or require investigation only if deviation exceeds threshold.
   - *Status*: Should be unspecified (norm is prescriptive, not statistical).

7. **No data-quality rules for object extraction / reused IDs**.
   - *Risk*: Sensor Heartbeat and missing Delivery objects are conflated with process violations.
   - *Clarification*: Define join-key uniqueness and attribute-to-object promotion rules.
   - *Status*: Should be unspecified for process norm, but required as data-quality policy.

8. **T4 and T5 (Customer, RiskCase) have no linkage rules** despite active use.
   - *Risk*: Future conflicts if another policy mandates/forbids these links.
   - *Clarification*: Confirm they are maintained by external policies and not required/forbidden here.
   - *Status*: Explicitly unspecified.
