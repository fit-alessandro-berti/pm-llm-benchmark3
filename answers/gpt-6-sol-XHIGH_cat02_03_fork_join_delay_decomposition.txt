# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

For case \(c\) and branch \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\), let \(a_c\) be arrival, \(r_c\) fork release, \(s_{ci}\) branch start, \(e_{ci}\) branch completion, \(f_c^s\) final start, and \(f_c^e\) final completion. All durations below are elapsed minutes.

- Upstream elapsed time: \(U_c=r_c-a_c\).
- Branch queue and active service: \(Q_{ci}=s_{ci}-r_c\), \(P_{ci}=e_{ci}-s_{ci}\). Thus branch release-to-completion time is \(X_{ci}=Q_{ci}+P_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c\).
- Join readiness and fork–join stage: \(J_c=\max_i e_{ci}\) and \(H_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i X_{ci}\). A branch is critical when its completion equals \(J_c\); ties count for every tied branch.
- Branch synchronization wait: \(W_{ci}=J_c-e_{ci}=H_c-X_{ci}\). It is zero on a critical branch.
- Downstream wait and final service: \(D_c=f_c^s-J_c\), \(V_c=f_c^e-f_c^s\).
- Total flow time: \(T_c=f_c^e-a_c=U_c+H_c+D_c+V_c\). Equivalently, for **any one** branch \(i\), \(T_c=U_c+Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci}+D_c+V_c\).

The three branch paths run concurrently. In particular, \(Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci}=H_c\) for *each* branch, so adding these three paths would count the fork–join stage three times. Summing synchronization waits as an additional serial stage is also invalid: those waits overlap each other and the unfinished branch work. For F02, the waits are 10, 0, and 35 minutes, yet the entire fork–join stage is 60 minutes. Under the stated join rule, \(D_c\) is the observed gap after readiness; calling it a capacity queue requires further evidence.

**F02:** From the 08:25 fork, A takes \(0+50=50\) minutes and ends 09:15; B takes \(20+40=60\) and ends 09:25; C takes \(5+20=25\) and ends 08:50. Hence \(J=09{:}25\), B is critical, and A/B/C synchronization waits are 10/0/35. The decomposition is \(20+60+15+20=115\) minutes.

**F04:** From 08:55, A takes 50 minutes, B \(10+30=40\), and C \(35+22=57\). C ends last at 09:52; the synchronization waits are 7/17/0. Thus \(20+57+18+20=115\) minutes. C's queue, not its relatively short service, determines this join.

**F07:** From 09:40, A takes 50 minutes, B \(30+45=75\), and C \(5+18=23\). B ends at 10:55; synchronization waits are 25/0/52. Total flow is \(20+75+15+20=130\) minutes, the sample maximum.

**F11:** From 10:40, A takes 50 minutes, B \(10+30=40\), and C \(5+18=23\). A ends at 11:30; synchronization waits are 0/10/27. Total flow is \(20+50+20+20=110\) minutes.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

| Branch | Service mean (range) | Queue mean (range) | Queue + service mean (maximum) | Last-completing cases | Mean synchronization wait | Influence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 50.00 (50–50) | 0.00 (0–0) | 50.00 (50) | 2/12 | 11.00 | Second, narrowly by equal-time sensitivity |
| B | 37.50 (30–45) | 19.17 (5–30) | 56.67 (75) | 8/12 | 4.33 | Strongest |
| C | 20.17 (18–30) | 10.42 (5–35) | 30.58 (57) | 2/12 | 30.42 | Close to A; important on queue outliers |

B controls the join in eight cases despite having less mean service than A. Its queue-plus-service reaches 75 minutes in F07 and F12, versus A's fixed 50 and C's maximum 57. Both 75-minute B paths occur among the longest flow times (130 and 125 minutes). Mean fork–join time is 61 minutes; mean total flow is 115.25 minutes, comprising 19.58 upstream, 61.00 fork–join, 14.67 downstream, and 20.00 final service.

A and C tie in critical frequency. As a sensitivity measure, the most that accelerating **only** one branch can reduce each join is its lead over the second-latest branch. Summed across these cases, those leads are B 120 minutes, A 15, and C 12. That supports B first and only a narrow A-over-C ranking. C's administrative queue spikes make it consequential for F04 and F08; the ordering of A and C could change under a different intervention or case mix. These are within-sample path sensitivities, not causal capacity estimates.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumptions for the calculations:** Each intervention changes only its named duration; all other branch starts, durations, and fork releases remain as observed. For the A and B scenarios, join savings become flow-time savings only if final starts move earlier with the join while the observed downstream wait duration stays fixed. If final starts stay on their observed schedule, their immediate **flow-time** savings are zero, even though join readiness improves. Half-minute results are permitted in these hypothetical interventions despite minute-resolution observations.

| case | A service −20%: join saving | B queue −50%: join saving | Three F resources with immediate dispatch: flow saving |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| F01 | 5 | 0 | 8 |
| F02 | 0 | 10 | 15 |
| F03 | 0 | 10 | 15 |
| F04 | 0 | 0 | 18 |
| F05 | 0 | 10 | 10 |
| F06 | 0 | 15 | 15 |
| F07 | 0 | 15 | 15 |
| F08 | 0 | 0 | 15 |
| F09 | 0 | 12.5 | 15 |
| F10 | 0 | 5 | 20 |
| F11 | 10 | 0 | 20 |
| F12 | 0 | 15 | 10 |
| **Total / mean** | **15 / 1.25** | **92.5 / 7.71** | **176 / 14.67** |

1. **A service −20%** means 10 minutes less A work per case. Only F01 and F11 advance the join: by 5 and 10 minutes. F01 becomes B-critical; F11 becomes an A/B tie. The other ten cases gain zero because B or C remains last. If F responds immediately, mean flow becomes 114 minutes, while the 130-minute maximum remains. Secondary effects could include earlier A resource release and more synchronization wait on A, neither identifiable from the case timestamps alone.
2. **B queue −50%** advances the join in the eight B-critical cases, by 5–15 minutes each; F01, F04, F08, and F11 gain zero because another branch controls readiness. The saving is capped when A catches B, as in F05 and F10. With an equally responsive F stage, mean flow becomes about 107.54 minutes and the sample maximum falls from 130 to 115. Earlier B completions can move the bottleneck to A, C, or F; queue reduction across many cases may also change branch workloads and scheduling.
3. **Final-stage dispatch/capacity:** As an illustrative upper bound, assume three interchangeable, continuously available F resources, first-come-first-served dispatch at `join_ready`, and unchanged 20-minute final service. These twelve releases can then all start F immediately. All twelve cases benefit because every observed downstream gap is positive; mean flow becomes about 100.58 minutes and the maximum 115. A two-resource, work-conserving dispatch simulation with the same assumptions saves 171 rather than 176 total minutes: only F11 waits, for five minutes. Therefore the data do not establish that a third resource is necessary. For example, F03 is join-ready at 09:45 while F1 appears idle from 09:28 to 10:00. Eligibility rules, a release schedule, setup, or unlogged duties may explain the gap. Adding capacity without changing such rules may save nothing.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Pilot B queue reduction.** Record when B becomes eligible, its queue entry, resource assignment, actual availability, and any priority or batching rule. Then test earlier B dispatch or targeted flexible capacity during its long waits. B is the strongest observed control on join time, so this has the clearest potential to reduce the flow-time tail, particularly F07 and F12. Throughput could rise if B capacity actually constrains completions; twelve cases do not establish a throughput rate or utilization. More B capacity might lower its resource utilization, whereas better scheduling might use existing capacity more effectively. Earlier B completions will increase B synchronization wait when A or C becomes critical and could shift the bottleneck to F. Measure join times, tail flow time, B utilization, and the new critical-branch mix in the pilot.

**Audit and pilot immediate F dispatch across eligible resources.** Log the moment F becomes authorized, F1/F2 availability, routing eligibility, setup, and reasons for delaying starts. Test a work-conserving assignment rule before committing to another F resource; add peak cover only if measured simultaneous demand warrants it. Shorter downstream gaps could reduce the tail and make branch improvements visible in completed-case flow time. Throughput improves only if the final stage is truly constraining output. A third resource may have low utilization, while more prompt F starts may move the constraint back to B. This redesign does not change branch synchronization waits directly; it removes a later wait that can otherwise absorb earlier join readiness. Pilot against the observed 8–20-minute downstream gaps and monitor F resource utilization, final-start latency, and any new queue at the next constraint.

# 6. Reproducible Python

The code needs pandas and PM4Py. A/B savings printed below are join savings and conditional flow savings under a responsive F stage; the F3 savings use the explicit three-resource schedule.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = '''case,arrival,fork_release,A_start,A_end,B_start,B_end,C_start,C_end,final_start,final_end,final_resource
F01,07:55,08:10,08:10,09:00,08:15,08:55,08:20,08:38,09:08,09:28,F1
F02,08:05,08:25,08:25,09:15,08:45,09:25,08:30,08:50,09:40,10:00,F2
F03,08:20,08:40,08:40,09:30,09:00,09:45,08:45,09:03,10:00,10:20,F1
F04,08:35,08:55,08:55,09:45,09:05,09:35,09:30,09:52,10:10,10:30,F2
F05,08:50,09:10,09:10,10:00,09:35,10:10,09:15,09:35,10:20,10:40,F1
F06,09:05,09:25,09:25,10:15,09:55,10:30,09:40,10:00,10:45,11:05,F2
F07,09:20,09:40,09:40,10:30,10:10,10:55,09:45,10:03,11:10,11:30,F1
F08,09:35,09:55,09:55,10:45,10:00,10:30,10:20,10:50,11:05,11:25,F2
F09,09:50,10:10,10:10,11:00,10:35,11:15,10:15,10:35,11:30,11:50,F1
F10,10:05,10:25,10:25,11:15,10:45,11:20,10:30,10:50,11:40,12:00,F2
F11,10:20,10:40,10:40,11:30,10:50,11:20,10:45,11:03,11:50,12:10,F1
F12,10:35,10:55,10:55,11:45,11:25,12:10,11:00,11:18,12:20,12:40,F2'''
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw))
clock_cols = ['arrival', 'fork_release', 'final_start', 'final_end'] + [
    f'{i}_{x}' for i in 'ABC' for x in ('start', 'end')]
for col in clock_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime('2026-05-19 ' + df[col])
minutes = lambda td: td.dt.total_seconds() / 60
for i in 'ABC':
    df[f'{i}_queue'] = minutes(df[f'{i}_start'] - df.fork_release)
    df[f'{i}_service'] = minutes(df[f'{i}_end'] - df[f'{i}_start'])
df['join_ready'] = df[[f'{i}_end' for i in 'ABC']].max(axis=1)
# Deterministic ties: report every tied branch in A/B/C order.
df['critical_branch'] = df.apply(
    lambda r: '/'.join(i for i in 'ABC' if r[f'{i}_end'] == r.join_ready), axis=1)
for i in 'ABC':
    df[f'{i}_sync'] = minutes(df.join_ready - df[f'{i}_end'])
df['upstream_elapsed'] = minutes(df.fork_release - df.arrival)
df['fork_join_elapsed'] = minutes(df.join_ready - df.fork_release)
df['downstream_queue'] = minutes(df.final_start - df.join_ready)
df['final_service'] = minutes(df.final_end - df.final_start)
df['total_flow_time'] = minutes(df.final_end - df.arrival)
assert (df[['upstream_elapsed', 'fork_join_elapsed', 'downstream_queue',
            'final_service']].sum(axis=1) == df.total_flow_time).all()

# Fork/join maxima and synchronization waits are custom pandas calculations:
# standard directly-follows performance does not recover synchronization wait.
end_A20 = df.A_end - pd.to_timedelta(.20 * df.A_service, unit='m')
end_B50 = df.B_end - pd.to_timedelta(.50 * df.B_queue, unit='m')
join_A20 = pd.concat([end_A20, df.B_end, df.C_end], axis=1).max(axis=1)
join_B50 = pd.concat([df.A_end, end_B50, df.C_end], axis=1).max(axis=1)
df['A20_join_save'] = minutes(df.join_ready - join_A20)
df['B50_join_save'] = minutes(df.join_ready - join_B50)
# With fixed observed final starts, A20/B50 save zero completed-case flow time.
# With final starts advancing and the same downstream wait duration, flow
# savings equal the corresponding join savings.

# F3: three interchangeable, continuously available final resources, FCFS
# at join readiness, with observed final-service durations unchanged.
available = [pd.Timestamp('2026-05-19')] * 3
new_starts = {}
for row in df.sort_values(['join_ready', 'case']).itertuples():
    resource = min(range(3), key=lambda k: (available[k], k))
    start = max(row.join_ready, available[resource])
    new_starts[row.case] = start
    available[resource] = start + pd.Timedelta(minutes=row.final_service)
df['F3_start'] = df.case.map(new_starts)
df['F3_flow_save'] = minutes(df.final_start - df.F3_start)
assert (df.F3_start == df.join_ready).all()

# PM4Py interval events represent active branch and final work, not waits.
rows = []
for r in df.itertuples():
    for i in 'ABC':
        rows.append({'case:concept:name': r.case, 'concept:name': i,
                     'start_timestamp': getattr(r, f'{i}_start'),
                     'time:timestamp': getattr(r, f'{i}_end')})
    rows.append({'case:concept:name': r.case, 'concept:name': 'F',
                 'start_timestamp': r.final_start,
                 'time:timestamp': r.final_end})
events = pd.DataFrame(rows)
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name',
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp', start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')
service_seconds = pm4py.get_service_time(
    events, start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')

case_cols = ['case', 'upstream_elapsed', 'A_queue', 'A_service',
             'B_queue', 'B_service', 'C_queue', 'C_service', 'join_ready',
             'critical_branch', 'fork_join_elapsed', 'downstream_queue',
             'final_service', 'total_flow_time']
shown = df[case_cols].copy()
shown['join_ready'] = shown.join_ready.dt.strftime('%H:%M')
print('CASE DECOMPOSITION (minutes except clock time and labels)')
print(shown.to_string(index=False))
summary = pd.DataFrame([{
    'branch': i,
    'service_mean': df[f'{i}_service'].mean(),
    'service_max': df[f'{i}_service'].max(),
    'queue_mean': df[f'{i}_queue'].mean(),
    'queue_max': df[f'{i}_queue'].max(),
    'release_to_end_mean': (df[f'{i}_queue'] + df[f'{i}_service']).mean(),
    'release_to_end_max': (df[f'{i}_queue'] + df[f'{i}_service']).max(),
    'critical_count': df.critical_branch.str.split('/').map(
        lambda labels: i in labels).sum(),
    'sync_mean': df[f'{i}_sync'].mean()}
    for i in 'ABC'])
print('\nBRANCH SUMMARY (minutes)')
print(summary.round(2).to_string(index=False))
print('\nCOUNTERFACTUAL SAVINGS (minutes; A/B flow gains require F to move)')
print(df[['case', 'A20_join_save', 'B50_join_save',
          'F3_flow_save']].to_string(index=False))
print('\nSAVINGS SUMMARY (minutes)')
print(df[['A20_join_save', 'B50_join_save', 'F3_flow_save']]
      .agg(['sum', 'mean', 'max']).round(2).to_string())
print('\nPM4Py mean service minutes:',
      {k: round(v / 60, 2) for k, v in service_seconds.items()})
```

# 7. Limits of inference

These twelve one-day traces identify elapsed intervals and the last recorded branch under the stated all-branches-required rule. They do not show why a branch waited, whether staff or machines were available, branch resource capacity or utilization, competing cases outside the sample, or whether work was preempted during a recorded start-to-end interval. “Service” is therefore an observed active interval, not proven uninterrupted effort. One-minute timestamp resolution also limits close-order and fractional counterfactual claims.

The timestamps do not prove that B queue reduction, faster A work, or an added F resource would causally produce the calculated savings. Shared-resource effects, changed priorities, feedback, and a new critical branch can alter outcomes. In particular, `final_start − join_ready` could reflect scheduling, authorization, assignment restrictions, or unlogged work rather than a physical F queue; apparent F idle time makes a capacity-only explanation doubtful but does not identify the actual policy. Upstream elapsed time has no finer decomposition in these data. PM4Py interval service summaries are useful, whereas ordinary directly-follows performance alone cannot attribute parallel synchronization waits. Throughput and long-run tail effects require more cases and resource-state observations.
