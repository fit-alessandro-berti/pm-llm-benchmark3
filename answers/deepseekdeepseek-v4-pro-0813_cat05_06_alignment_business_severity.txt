## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is assessed on a 1–5 scale combining business control impact, not raw alignment cost.

**Severity 1 — No business-control issue.** Only technical noise, duplicate logging, harmless model artifacts, label synonyms, or out-of-scope cases with no customer/financial impact.  
**Severity 2 — Minor model/evidence or scope issue.** Monitoring distortion or data-quality weakness, but no substantive policy breach.  
**Severity 3 — Material evidence gap or control-evidence weakness.** The activity may have occurred, but evidence is missing, external, non-immutable, or poorly linked; audit/regulatory exposure is elevated.  
**Severity 4 — Clear policy/control violation with meaningful exposure.** Unauthorized auto-approval, missing required approval path, or high-risk evidence failure; may require corrective action and review.  
**Severity 5 — Severe control failure.** Irreversible payment before approval, high-value/high-risk claim with missing fraud control, or strong fraud indicator such as a recent beneficiary-bank change.

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Confidence reflects how certain we are in the interpretation of the evidence and alignment. A case can be high severity but medium confidence if the evidence gap is real but the underlying activity may still have occurred. Conversely, a low-severity case can have high confidence because the evidence clearly shows only technical noise.

The framework considers:

- **Control objective:** approval before payment, fraud screen, notification, assessment.
- **Amount/exposure:** proximity to EUR 5,000, EUR 50,000, or high-value thresholds.
- **Reversibility:** whether payment was sent, accepted, or recallable.
- **Timing:** whether approval, assessment, or notification occurred before/after required points.
- **Customer/regulatory harm:** potential fraud, regulatory breach, claimant harm.
- **Recurrence:** one-off vs repeated pattern.
- **Evidence reliability:** core logs vs mutable links, external documents, retry patterns.
- **Model scope:** whether the case is truly in the payable-claim model scope.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

A missing event is **not** proof that an activity did not occur. Evidence must discriminate between missing execution, missing logging, external execution, clock/timestamp artifacts, and label/scope mismatches.

**1. CL-602 — Missing Fraud Screen on high-risk/high-value claim**  
- H1: Fraud Screen was not performed.  
- H2: Fraud Screen was performed verbally or outside the core system and not recorded.  
- H3: Fraud Screen was performed in a fraud tool but failed to write to the core log.  
- Discriminating evidence: fraud tool access logs, interviewer notes, call recordings, emails, system integration error logs, user attestations, and timestamps. If no independent evidence exists, the control remains unsupported regardless of H2/H3.

**2. CL-604 — Payment before approval**  
- H1: Payment was genuinely released before Manual Approve.  
- H2: Timestamps are misleading due to clock skew or timezone difference.  
- H3: Approval occurred earlier in another system but was recorded later in the core log.  
- Discriminating evidence: bank confirmation timestamp, approval system timestamp, NTP/clock-sync records, payment batch logs, user session logs, and payment gateway audit trails.

**3. CL-606 — Missing Assess Loss with external adjuster report**  
- H1: Assess Loss occurred externally and the core log was not created.  
- H2: Assess Loss was performed in the core system but lost due to integration failure.  
- H3: Assess Loss did not occur as a distinct activity; the report was backfilled or misdated.  
- Discriminating evidence: stable document-to-case ID, report metadata, adjuster system logs, email timestamps, core interface error logs, and sign-off behavior.

**4. CL-608 — Missing Notify Claimant with CRM portal message**  
- H1: Notification was sent via secure portal and the CRM link is accurate.  
- H2: Notification was not actually sent; the CRM link is erroneous or unreliable.  
- H3: Notification occurred but outside the required timeframe or to the wrong contact.  
- Discriminating evidence: portal delivery/read logs, message ID, claimant login/IP, immutable audit fields, CRM link creation timestamp, and outbound gateway records.

**5. CL-609/CL-610 — Data-aware auto-approval failures**  
- H1: The decision service did not enforce P1/P2 amount/risk/bank-change rules.  
- H2: The required data was unavailable, stale, or updated after the approval decision.  
- H3: A manual action or misconfigured service forced the Auto Approve branch.  
- Discriminating evidence: decision-service input snapshots, risk and bank-change timestamps vs approval timestamp, API request/response logs, configuration history, and user access logs.

**6. CL-603 — Duplicate notification retry**  
- H1: One business notification was sent; the connector retry created a duplicate event.  
- H2: Two notifications were actually sent to the claimant.  
- Discriminating evidence: same message ID, transport retry logs, read receipts, claimant confirmation, outbound gateway logs, and CRM delivery history.

**7. CL-611 — Label mismatch between `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss`**  
- H1: The two labels refer to the same substantive activity and only terminology changed.  
- H2: The activity scope changed materially, so the process model should be updated.  
- Discriminating evidence: signed procedure, UI screens, data mapping version history, training material, and control objective.

**8. CL-607/CL-612 — Denied/withdrawn out-of-scope cases**  
- H1: Denied/withdrawn claims are outside the payable model scope and should be filtered.  
- H2: The model should cover the full lifecycle including denial and withdrawal.  
- Discriminating evidence: process owner scope decision, model owner scope statement, P6, case outcome frequencies, and audit requirements.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is an edit-distance measure under a chosen cost function. It is not a business-risk score.

**1. Uniform cost function ignores differential impact.**  
CL-602 has raw cost 1 for missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change. CL-603 also has raw cost 1 for a duplicate retry notification. These are not equivalent risks.

**2. Zero-cost alignment can hide policy violations.**  
CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous with raw cost 0, but both violate P1/P2 because Auto Approve was used despite amount/risk/bank-change conditions. The process tree does not encode data-aware rules, so conformance and policy compliance can diverge.

**3. Out-of-scope cases inflate raw cost without risk.**  
CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4 because the payable model expects activities that should not occur after denial or withdrawal. These are process-model scope mismatches, not severe operational failures.

**4. UI telemetry and retries create misleading costs.**  
CL-601 has raw cost 3 from `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, and `Close Workbench`, all harmless UI telemetry. CL-603 has raw cost 1 from a duplicate notification event with the same message ID. The cost function counts events, not business meaning.

**5. Hidden/silent moves are fitting and can produce zero cost.**  
CL-605 includes `T(tau,c=0)`, a silent model transition from Petri-net conversion. Raw cost 0 is technically correct and harmless here, but zero cost cannot be interpreted as “zero risk” because zero-cost policy failures exist.

**6. Missing core evidence is not proof of missing activity.**  
CL-606 has raw cost 1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, but an external adjuster report shows assessment occurred. CL-608 has raw cost 1 for `M(Notify Claimant)`, but CRM evidence shows a portal message at +3 hours. The raw cost captures the core-log gap, not the true operational state.

**7. Activity granularity and label mismatch create artificial deviation.**  
CL-611 has raw cost 2 because the log says `Assess Damage` while the model expects `Assess Loss`. This is an event-label mismatch, not an operational failure.

**8. Irreversibility and sequence are not encoded in cost.**  
CL-604 has raw cost 3, but payment was accepted before manual approval. The cost assigns one for `L(Pay Claim)`, one for `M(Fraud Screen)`, and one for `M(Pay Claim)`. It does not penalize the temporal ordering, bank acceptance, or irreversibility. A raw cost of 3 understates the severity.

Therefore, raw alignment cost is useful as a diagnostic signal of where the log and model differ, but it must be interpreted with business context, policy overlays, event evidence, data conditions, timing, amount, reversibility, and recurrence.
