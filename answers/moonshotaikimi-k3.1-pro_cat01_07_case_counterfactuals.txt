Temporal propagation: Assessment-readiness is governed by the later verification completion: max(13:20, 16:15), not Identity Check’s 13:20 completion. Readiness therefore improves by 3 hours 15 minutes over factual 19:30. Earlier A2 queue entry may produce only a modest benefit; in the base path A2 runs Wednesday 08:30–11:00 and Notify ends at 12:00, 2.5 hours early. If A2 retains its factual start, the saving is zero. D2’s earlier start can simultaneously delay another D2 case and move waiting into A2’s queue.

Process-feasibility check: There are no originals, the documents are digital, and I2 and D2 are different resources, so genuine concurrent service is permitted. Assessment still waits for both checks. No Compliance Review is required, and M1 Decision precedes Notify. Real-time records must demonstrate actual work and ownership; retrospectively entered overlapping timestamps are not sufficient. Invalid document work may need to be repeated before a valid Assessment can rely on it.

Best/base/worst case: Best: A2 can assess Tuesday 16:30–19:00 and M1 decides that evening, making Notify about 19 hours early; if M1 instead waits until Wednesday morning, the saving is only about 5.5 hours. Base: the saving is 2.5 hours. Worst: A2 congestion absorbs the entire readiness gain, or a full-length D2 check starts too late to help. An invalid override or logging audit can add repeat work and make the case later, although an otherwise valid clean path should not change the approval.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Actual D2 availability, A2 arrival-and-service logs, and non-target waiting times would show whether earlier readiness changes A2 starts. If A2 starts remain unchanged and D2/A2 spillovers grow, the system benefit disappears. Lock-audit records showing backfilled ownership or service events would invalidate the claimed concurrency and require the apparent timing benefit to be discarded.

For any one policy, a 100-case weekly rollout makes queue positions endogenous. K-104 is the clearest feasibility break: using the stated 3–6-hour final-review range as a scale guide, 100 reviews represent roughly 300–600 service hours before preliminary work. Preparation savings may reduce that total, but reservations still cannot be treated as guarantees for one C1. They should be readiness-triggered and automatically released. Otherwise late documents or Assessments create idle protected capacity while urgent cases wait; incomplete packets can also add effort without reducing final effort. S1 can overlap checks, but is not unlimited.

K-219 initially resembles load balancing: moving standard work off D1 can free the specialist for complex corporate files. Fixed substitution, however, pushes longer standard service and handwriting clarifications onto D2. Once D2 saturates, waiting grows nonlinearly, and clarification loops add external waits plus new D2 and A2 work. K-337 moves in the opposite direction, avoiding third checks and Assessments and freeing D1/A1 capacity. Its net benefit must nevertheless include appeals, post-decision remediation, losses, and potentially harder C1 reviews.

Earlier readiness is not equivalent to earlier completion. K-219 and K-411 inject cases into A2 sooner; if A2 is saturated, they mostly reorder who waits and can delay non-target Assessments. K-104 shifts pressure toward C1, while K-337 can shift the bottleneck from D1/A1 and external response to C1/M1. Avoiding a Friday response that would otherwise return Monday makes K-337’s weekend effect especially valuable; internal staffing cannot compress an external wait when the request remains.

Finally, K-411 reduces elapsed serialization, not total I2/D2 service. At scale, simultaneous starts can create peak-load and downstream bursts. Real-time ownership, lock-override, start, and completion events are needed to demonstrate true concurrency. Batch-created overlapping timestamps merely change the apparent log and can conceal invalid work or overstate capacity benefits.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411’s controlled parallel verification. It uses concurrency already permitted for digital cases without originals, preserves the assessment gate and decision policy, and is readily reversible. Its expected benefit is modest, however: earlier verification readiness is valuable only if A2 and M1 can act earlier. The pilot should therefore be judged on completed cases and non-target queues, not attractive overlapping timelines.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite offering the largest potential acceleration. Deleting the second information request removes a weekend-dominated wait, but also changes the evidence supporting a high-risk, high-value decision. A faster conditional approval can create loss or compliance exposure; a faster rejection can create appeals and inappropriate customer harm. It is unsuitable wherever confirmation is mandatory.

K-104 is the least transportable from one case to a system-wide policy. A single provisional slot might be carved out of C1’s calendar, but many reservations cannot coexist with urgent work and variable document/assessment readiness. Idle reservations, preemption, and duplicated preliminary reading can overwhelm final-review savings. K-219’s fixed D2 routing is similarly less transportable than a load- and legibility-sensitive rule.

The highest-value additional data are resource-level task-created, ready, queue-entry, ownership, start, and completion events; originals-receipt and external-response timestamps; and reasons for every rework loop. For K-104, C1 reservation calendars, preemptions, preliminary/final effort, and urgent-case delays are essential. For K-219, legibility-stratified D1/D2 durations and clarification outcomes matter. For K-411, lock-override audit records must be reconciled with real-time service events. Finally, A2 queue composition, M1 decision windows, downstream appeals, losses, and condition performance are needed to distinguish genuine improvement from displaced waiting or deferred risk.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** Every counterfactual preserves Register/Triage, verification prerequisites, required rework, final compliance and four-eyes rules, and Decision-before-Notify. Only K-337 intentionally changes the decision information set and policy.

2. **temporal:** Illustrative timestamps are scenarios, not forecasts of a unique duration. Queue snapshots are uncertain, and evening, overnight, or weekend C1/A2/M1 availability is used only where explicitly stated.

3. **resource:** In the single-case analysis, other arrivals and resource schedules are approximately fixed. Earlier insertion or reservation displaces queued work rather than creating staff hours, and no same-resource service intervals overlap.

4. **temporal:** K-104’s roughly two-hour final-review saving and K-219’s seven-hour clean D2 check are plausible values within the guidance, not estimated means. Other service durations remain factual unless a downside scenario explicitly changes them.

5. **behavioral:** S1/C1 can sometimes derive reusable preparation from an incomplete packet, while M1 uses an earlier decision window only in the stated scenario. External responders do not accelerate merely because internal routing changes.

6. **quality:** On clean K-219 and K-411 paths, the earlier checks are as accurate and complete as the factual checks; on K-104, early preparation does not necessarily introduce anchoring. The stated downside paths relax these assumptions.

7. **interference:** Single-case estimates include only first-order queue displacement and do not assume that background queues instantly adapt. The 100-case analysis relaxes this assumption and rejects linear scaling of local benefits.

8. **quality:** For K-337, C1 and M1 are permitted to make an enforceable conditional approval or rejection while expressly documenting the discrepancy. If confirmation is legally mandatory, the path is infeasible; appeal, loss, and compliance outcomes remain uncertain.
