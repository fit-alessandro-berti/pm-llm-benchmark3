**1. Disruption timeline**

T0 (Day -30 to -1): Baseline state with high conformance (fitness 0.96, 91% fitting traces). Median cycle times were Hospital-critical 8h, Pharmacy-standard 23h, Wholesale-bulk 36h; traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain missing 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control missing 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellations 2.1%. Dominant flow followed the normative receivevalidatereservepickpackqualitydispatchproofinvoice path.

T1–T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2): System unavailable. Dominant variants shifted to emergency triage, manual authorization/inventory checks, skip-system paths, manual pick lists, dispatch without scan, and phone proof of delivery. Fitness fell to 0.71 (44% fitting traces). Hospital-critical fill rate held at 91% while Pharmacy-standard dropped to 61%; traceability exceptions rose to 9.5%, cold-chain missing 4.8%, dual-control missing 2.9%, rework 12.5%, cancellations 7.8%. Risk posture: high hidden compliance gaps offset by critical-service protection.

T3 (Day 3–6): Order intake restored but synchronization unreliable. Fitness 0.82 (59% fitting). Metrics improved modestly but remained degraded; audit-remediation queue began forming.

T4 (Day 7–21): Systems largely restored. Fitness recovered to 0.90 (76% fitting). Cycle times approached baseline for Hospital-critical but lagged for others; traceability exceptions 2.2%, rework 5.8%. Hidden workload persisted in audit remediation (2,300 cases) after operational metrics appeared recovered.

**3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance**

True resilience preserved mission-critical service while keeping minimum controls or explicitly trading lower-risk segments: A, B, F, I, J, and K. Risky shortcuts removed required controls without compensating measures: C and G created authorization and traceability gaps that violate regulatory requirements for controlled substances and recall-sensitive items. Ambiguous cases (D, E, H, L) are resilient only when time-boxed, immediately logged, or paired with later remediation; otherwise they conceal noncompliance by shifting work and risk into an invisible post-recovery tail. The case shows resilience when throughput for hospitals is protected without erasing cold-chain or dual-control records; it shows concealed noncompliance when dispatch-without-scan or skipped authorization paths are used for controlled products and later “fixed” only in audit remediation.

**4. Recovery process model**

Trigger: WMS unavailable >2 h or >5% of critical orders at risk.  
Triage: Hospital-critical and cold-chain/controlled-substance orders first; Pharmacy-standard next; Wholesale-bulk last.  
Minimum controls by risk: Controlled-substance and recall-sensitive require dual-staff sign-off and temperature log before dispatch; ambient non-critical may use manual snapshot for 24 h only.  
Manual evidence capture: Every pick, pack, and dispatch logged on pre-printed forms with timestamp, staff ID, and batch/lot; forms scanned within 4 h of system restoration.  
Backlog prioritization: Explicit weighted score (customer criticality × product risk × age).  
Reconciliation and audit flow: Within 48 h of system return run automated matching of manual logs to system records; flag exceptions for 100% review of controlled/cold-chain items; close audit queue before declaring operational recovery complete.  
Notation (BPMN-style): Start  Risk Triage Gateway (Hospital-critical/Cold/Controlled vs. others)  Parallel: (Manual Auth Check + Manual Inventory Check)  Minimum Control Gate (dual sign-off for high-risk)  Manual Pick/Pack with Evidence Form  Dispatch with Photo/POD  System Reconciliation Sub-process  Audit Remediation End.

**5. Resilience indicators**

Leading: % of orders triaged by explicit risk rules within 1 h; % of high-risk orders with real-time dual-control or temperature log captured; backlog age distribution by customer segment.  
Lagging: Traceability exception rate by product type; controlled-substance dual-control completion rate; cold-chain documentation completeness; rework/correction rate; audit-remediation queue size and age; Pharmacy-standard vs. Hospital-critical fill-rate delta; cancellation rate by customer group.

**6. Recommendations for future disruptions**

1. Pre-define and codify triage and minimum-control rules (low effort, high benefit, low residual risk).  
2. Maintain printable daily inventory snapshots and standardized manual evidence forms (low effort, high benefit, low residual risk).  
3. Require real-time logging of manual events for controlled and cold-chain items; retire dispatch-without-scan (medium effort, high benefit, medium residual risk).  
4. Resource a dedicated audit-remediation team activated at T2 start (medium effort, high benefit, low residual risk).  
5. Instrument leading indicators in a dashboard visible during incidents (high effort, high benefit, low residual risk).

**7. Management trade-off statement**

Management should accept explicit, time-limited manual processes for Hospital-critical ambient orders when supported by immediate evidence capture and rapid reconciliation; it should not accept skip-authorization or dispatch-without-scan paths for controlled-substance or recall-sensitive products. The recommendation changes if leading indicators show dual-control or temperature documentation falling below 98% for high-risk items or if the audit-remediation queue exceeds 5% of weekly volume after 72 h.
