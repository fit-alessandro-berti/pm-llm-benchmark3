## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The 0.38-0.42 drop in raw model fitness in March 2026—accompanied by mapped fitness remaining high at 0.90-0.91—implies a logging/semantic drift rather than an execution breakdown. The migration renamed `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen` and introduced duplicate `Open Case` events, causing the normative model to treat valid traces as non-conformant. Mapped fitness confirms syntactic recoverability under the baseline schema.

However, recovering conformance through label harmonization does not prove the underlying business process was unaffected. A 1:1 label mapping assumes semantic equivalence but can mask subtle behavioral shifts, such as altered screening depth or bypassed validation rules. Furthermore, technical artifacts distort downstream analyses: duplicate `Open Case` events corrupt directly-follows relations, cycle times, and loop-counting algorithms, while non-random resource missingness (jumping from 1.0% to ~5-6% on migrated steps) invalidates social network analysis, handover-of-work metrics, and assessor-level productivity tracking.

Crucially, the post-May operational deterioration cannot be dismissed as logging drift. While logging anomalies remained stable from March onward (missing resources at 5-6%, duplicate events at 4-6%), May witnessed a doubling of rework variants (13% to 24%), a doubling of `Assess -> Request Info` transitions (12 to 24 per 100 cases), and severe cycle-time inflation (median 31.2h in March to 45.0h in August). These shifts mirror external shocks—Supplier Z document failures, assessor attrition, and queue congestion—which are genuine behavioral, population, and performance drifts captured in mapped DFGs, completely distinct from the March schema mutation.

### 3. Cycle-Time Deterioration Decomposition (Peak Deterioration: November 2026)
Total median cycle time inflation relative to baseline is $\Delta \text{Median} = +17.85\text{ hours}$ (from 31.15h to 49.00h, +57.3%), while P90 increased by $\Delta \text{P90} = +53.0\text{ hours}$ (from 68.0h to 121.0h, +77.9%).

The decomposition across the four core structural drivers is:
1. **Service Time Component ($\approx 0.6 - 1.0\text{ h}$, $3\% - 6\%$ of $\Delta\text{Median}$):**
   Recorded median service duration for `Assess` increased modestly from 4.8h to 5.4h (+0.6h direct execution increase). Upstream screening and document collection service durations remained automated or stable.
2. **Control-Flow / Rework Component ($\approx 2.5 - 4.0\text{ h}$, $14\% - 22\%$ of $\Delta\text{Median}$, up to $28\%$ of $\Delta\text{P90}$):**
   Rework variant rate rose from 11.5% to 29.0% (+17.5% absolute). Each rework cycle injects an extra pass of `Request Info`, customer document turnaround, and secondary `Assess`. At nominal service and transmission times, this adds 8–12 hours of processing per reworked case, translating to an average population-level burden of ~3.0 hours.
3. **Case Mix / Population Component ($\approx 2.0 - 3.5\text{ h}$, $11\% - 20\%$ of $\Delta\text{Median}$):**
   High-complexity cases expanded from 20.5% to 45.0% (+24.5% absolute), driving `Assess -> Senior Assessment` transitions from 4% to 22–26%. Senior assessments require secondary reviews, specialized documentation, and longer handling, contributing ~2.8 hours of net delay across the population.
4. **Queueing / Waiting Time Component ($\approx 10.0 - 12.5\text{ h}$, $56\% - 68\%$ of $\Delta\text{Median}$, over $65\%$ of $\Delta\text{P90}$):**
   The primary driver of delay is queue congestion before `Assess` and `Senior Assessment`. The loss of 4 assessors in June combined with +29% volume growth (790 cases) created an acute capacity deficit. In accordance with Kingman's queueing approximation, high resource utilization inflated queue waiting non-linearly, accounting for the dominant share of median delay and the +53h tail blowout.

### 4. Candidate Change Points by Dimension
- **Logging Drift:**
  1. *2026-03 (System Migration):* Instantaneous drop in raw model fitness from 0.93 to 0.55 ($\Delta = -0.38$), duplicate `Open Case` rising from 0% to 4%, missing resource IDs rising from 1.0% to 4.8%.
  2. *2026-10 (Senior Assessment Schema/Recording Shift):* Escalation logging rate jumps from 4–9% to 22% in the DFG, reflecting both real intake and formalization of senior review event logging.
- **Behavioral / Control-Flow Drift:**
  1. *2026-05 (Supplier Z Bundle Disruption):* Rework variant share jumps from 13% to 24% (+11.0% MoM); `Assess -> Request Info` jumps from 12 to 24 per 100 cases.
  2. *2026-07 (Priority Dispatch Implementation):* Introduction of non-FIFO queueing policy prioritizing complex cases.
  3. *2026-09 (Supplier Z Quality Recovery):* Rework variant drops from 27% to 20% (-7.0% MoM).
- **Performance Drift:**
  1. *2026-06 (Assessor Attrition / Capacity Deficit):* Median cycle time jumps from 36.5h to 40.8h (+4.3h MoM; P90 jumps to 95h) as unreplaced staff loss drives queue congestion.
  2. *2026-10 (Seasonal Volume Surge):* Median cycle time jumps from 41.5h to 47.8h (+6.3h MoM; P90 jumps to 116h) under seasonal volume overload.
  3. *2026-12 (Intervention Recovery):* Median cycle time drops from 49.0h to 42.3h (-6.7h MoM; P90 drops to 99h) following overtime and quality initiatives.

### 5. Global vs. Mechanism-Specific Change Points
The data emphatically refute a single global change point. A single-change-point model would either flag March (attributing genuine operational failures to a harmless logging change) or October (missing months of supplier failures and staffing bottlenecks). The process exhibits **multiple asynchronous, mechanism-specific change points**:
- *2026-03:* Pure logging/semantic change point (zero operational performance impact).
- *2026-05:* Behavioral & supplier population change point (rework surge).
- *2026-06:* Organizational & performance change point (queueing escalation).
- *2026-07:* Control-flow dispatching change point (priority queueing).
- *2026-10:* Seasonal population & volume change point (renewal surge).

### Primary Monitoring Population
**Primary Subgroup: Standard-Complexity Cases from Non-Disrupted Suppliers (`Supplier != Supplier_Z` $\land$ `Complexity == Standard`).**

*Justification:* This subgroup represents the unperturbed "control group" of the document-eligibility process. Because it excludes Supplier Z (avoiding external document quality defects) and high-complexity cases (avoiding senior escalation and priority dispatch distortion), its control-flow should remain strictly linear (`Register -> Validate/Screen -> Collect Docs -> Assess -> Decision`). Any observed drift in this population's conformance, rework rate, or service duration directly isolates pure internal behavioral drift or IT logging alterations. Furthermore, increases in its idle waiting time before `Assess` provide an uncontaminated measure of organizational queue congestion caused by staffing deficits.

def format_event_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id_col: str = 'case_id',
    activity_col: str = 'activity',
    timestamp_col: str = 'timestamp',
    resource_col: str = 'resource'
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Formats a raw event dataframe into a PM4Py-compliant event log structure.
    """
    df_formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col
    )
    if resource_col in df.columns:
        df_formatted['org:resource'] = df[resource_col]
    return df_formatted


def slice_event_log_window(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    start_time: str,
    end_time: str,
    mode: str = 'events'
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Slices an event log dataframe to a specific temporal window using PM4Py filtering.
    """
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start_time, end_time, mode=mode)


def compute_dfg_and_matrices(df: pd.DataFrame):

    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)

    """
    Discovers log skeleton constraints (always-after, always-before, equivalence).
    """

def compare_raw_vs_normalized_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: dict = None,
    duplicate_activity: str = 'Open Case'
) -> dict:
    """
    Compares raw and normalized event logs without mutating the original dataframe.
    Harmonizes activity labels and cleans technical duplicate events.
    """
    if label_mapping is None:
        label_mapping = {'Eligibility Screen': 'Validate Eligibility'}

    # Work strictly on copies to prevent overwriting original log
    df_raw = df.copy()
    df_norm = df.copy()

    # Apply semantic label mapping
    df_norm['concept:name'] = df_norm['concept:name'].replace(label_mapping)

    # Remove immediate consecutive duplicate technical events per case
    df_norm['prev_activity'] = df_norm.groupby('case:concept:name')['concept:name'].shift(1)
    is_duplicate = (df_norm['concept:name'] == duplicate_activity) & (df_norm['prev_activity'] == duplicate_activity)
    df_norm = df_norm[~is_duplicate].drop(columns=['prev_activity'])

    # Discover DFGs and Variants for both representations
    raw_dfg, raw_sa, raw_ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_raw)
    norm_dfg, norm_sa, norm_ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_norm)

    raw_variants = pm4py.get_variants(df_raw)
    norm_variants = pm4py.get_variants(df_norm)

    return {
        'df_raw': df_raw,
        'df_norm': df_norm,
        'raw_dfg': raw_dfg,
        'norm_dfg': norm_dfg,
        'raw_variant_count': len(raw_variants),
        'norm_variant_count': len(norm_variants)
    }
