# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

Abbreviations used throughout (the Python code uses the full labels):

RD = Register Deviation, CM = Contain Material, AR = Analyze Root Cause, PV = Plan Verification, DC = Design Corrective Action, SR = Supervisor Review, QR = Quality Review, RR = Regulatory Review, CS = Consult Supplier, ET = Execute Trial, IT = Inspect Trial, AP = Adjust Parameters, RL = Release Material, AC = Archive Case.
Submodels: APPR = approval route, SUPP = optional supplier consultation, TRIAL = repeat-until trial loop.

1. **Event granularity.** Each activity instance produces exactly one event, recorded at completion. A rule such as "Y may start only after X is complete" is therefore observable only as "the completion of Y comes after the completion of X". Start-time constraints are not directly observable, and start and complete lifecycle events are out of scope.
2. **"May overlap" / "proceed independently".** This means *absence of an ordering constraint*: both completion orders must be accepted. With completion-only events, true concurrency (overlapping durations) cannot be observed. So the requirement at trace level is interleaving freedom. I still note where a representation would lose true concurrency under a later start/complete refinement: the process tree does, the POWL model does not.
3. **Risk route.** The route is an exclusive choice `X(SR, +(QR, RR))`, placed after AR. Low risk means exactly one SR. High risk means exactly one QR and exactly one RR, in either order and overlapping. The model contains no risk attribute; the XOR branch *is* the risk abstraction. Mixed routes (for example SR plus QR) and mid-case escalation from low to high risk are excluded.
4. **Optional supplier consultation.** This is `X(CS, tau)`: either one CS event or a silent skip. The skip leaves no trace. The node is ordered after AR and before RL and is unordered with everything else. The data condition "supplier involved" is not modeled; it is abstracted into a non-deterministic choice.
5. **Passing inspection.** Inspection outcomes are not labels. The loop `*( ->(ET, IT), AP )` encodes the outcome structurally:
   - an IT followed by AP is a failed inspection;
   - the IT after which the loop exits (silently) is the passing inspection.

   So "passing IT" means "the last IT of the case". This makes "AP never after the passing inspection" hold by construction. The consequence is that a log cannot distinguish "passed and then someone adjusted anyway" from "failed and released prematurely". Both are rejected (see witness F3). Every case is assumed to reach a passing inspection eventually; there is no scrap or abandon path.
6. **Multiplicities.** RD, CM, AR, PV, DC, RL and AC occur exactly once. SR, QR and RR occur at most once, according to the route. CS occurs at most once. ET and IT occur k ≥ 1 times, and AP occurs k − 1 times. A failed trial reworks parameters only; design and planning are not repeated.
7. **Approval independence.** Approvals depend only on AR, not on CM, PV, DC or the trial. They must be complete before RL.
8. **Duplicate visible labels.** They are *forbidden* in the POWL model, which is the canonical model. They are *permitted, deliberately and documented,* in the process tree, because that is the approximation policy chosen for it (point 9).
9. **Process-tree approximation policy: duplication-based, trace-exact.** A process tree with unique labels cannot express the intended order (sketch of the argument in Section 3.4). I duplicate the post-analysis subtree three times, once for each possible position of AR's completion relative to the chain CM → PV:
   - AR completes before CM;
   - AR completes between CM and PV;
   - AR completes after PV.

   Within each case the remaining order is series-parallel. The union is *trace-language-equal* to the intended behavior at completion-event granularity. It is *not* equal structurally (labels are duplicated), causally (the tree states false sequential dependencies), or under lifecycle refinement (point 2).

   For tools that require unique labels, I also document a label-unique conservative under-approximation `T_U` (Section 3.4). It is used only as a comparison point in the witnesses.
10. **Scope of the models.** RL follows all work streams, AC directly follows RL, and nothing else occurs between them.

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

## 3.1 Compact expression

```text
T = ->( RD,
        X( ->( AR, POST[ +( ->(CM, PV), DC ) ] ),         # copy 1: AR completes before CM
           ->( CM,
               X( ->( AR, POST[ +(PV, DC) ] ),            # copy 2: CM < AR < PV
                  ->( PV, AR, POST[ DC ] ) ) ) ),         # copy 3: CM < PV < AR
        RL,
        AC )

POST[prep] = +( ->( prep, TRIAL ), APPR, SUPP )           # everything that depends on AR
TRIAL      = *( ->(ET, IT), AP )                          # do = one trial cycle, redo = AP
APPR       = X( SR, +(QR, RR) )                           # low risk | high risk
SUPP       = X( CS, tau )                                 # optional, at most once
```

After macro expansion, AR, PV, DC, SR, QR, RR, CS, ET, IT and AP appear three times each, and CM appears twice. RD, RL and AC are unique.

**Mapping to the narrative.**
- Rule 1 is the outer sequence RD … RL, AC.
- Rule 2 (CM ∥ AR) is realized by the two nested XORs. Their three branches enumerate the only three possible completion positions of AR relative to the chain CM → PV. The branch is determined by the next observed event:
  - AR right after RD selects copy 1;
  - CM then AR selects copy 2;
  - CM then PV selects copy 3.
- Rules 3, 4 and 7 are the `prep` part followed by TRIAL inside each copy.
- Rules 5 and 6 are APPR and SUPP. They sit in parallel with `->(prep, TRIAL)`, so they overlap design, planning and the trial. They are still after AR, because each copy starts after AR has been emitted.
- Rule 8 is the loop.
- Rule 9 is the end of the parallel block, which joins TRIAL, APPR and SUPP before RL.

## 3.2 Python code (explicit constructors, explicit parent/child handling)

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

RD = "Register Deviation"
CM = "Contain Material"
AR = "Analyze Root Cause"
PV = "Plan Verification"
DC = "Design Corrective Action"
SR = "Supervisor Review"
QR = "Quality Review"
RR = "Regulatory Review"
CS = "Consult Supplier"
ET = "Execute Trial"
IT = "Inspect Trial"
AP = "Adjust Parameters"
RL = "Release Material"
AC = "Archive Case"


def op(operator, parent):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, parent=parent)
    if parent is not None:
        parent.children.append(node)
    return node


def leaf(label, parent):
    # label=None creates a silent (tau) leaf
    node = ProcessTree(label=label, parent=parent)
    parent.children.append(node)
    return node


def add_trial(parent):
    loop = op(Operator.LOOP, parent)          # *( do, redo )
    cycle = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, loop)       # do   = ->(ET, IT)
    leaf(ET, cycle)
    leaf(IT, cycle)
    leaf(AP, loop)                            # redo = AP
    return loop


def add_approval(parent):
    route = op(Operator.XOR, parent)
    leaf(SR, route)                           # low risk
    high = op(Operator.PARALLEL, route)       # high risk
    leaf(QR, high)
    leaf(RR, high)
    return route


def add_supplier(parent):
    opt = op(Operator.XOR, parent)
    leaf(CS, opt)
    leaf(None, opt)                           # supplier not involved
    return opt


def add_after_analysis(parent, pending):
    # POST[prep] = +( ->(prep, TRIAL), APPR, SUPP ); pending = work not yet done when AR completes
    block = op(Operator.PARALLEL, parent)
    work = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, block)
    if pending == "CM,PV":
        prep = op(Operator.PARALLEL, work)
        chain = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, prep)
        leaf(CM, chain)
        leaf(PV, chain)
        leaf(DC, prep)
    elif pending == "PV":
        prep = op(Operator.PARALLEL, work)
        leaf(PV, prep)
        leaf(DC, prep)
    else:
        leaf(DC, work)
    add_trial(work)
    add_approval(block)
    add_supplier(block)
    return block


tree = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, None)
leaf(RD, tree)

race = op(Operator.XOR, tree)

copy1 = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, race)               # AR before CM
leaf(AR, copy1)
add_after_analysis(copy1, "CM,PV")

contain_first = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, race)       # CM before AR
leaf(CM, contain_first)
after_contain = op(Operator.XOR, contain_first)

copy2 = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, after_contain)      # CM < AR < PV
leaf(AR, copy2)
add_after_analysis(copy2, "PV")

copy3 = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, after_contain)      # CM < PV < AR
leaf(PV, copy3)
leaf(AR, copy3)
add_after_analysis(copy3, "")

leaf(RL, tree)
leaf(AC, tree)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(tree)
```

Every node is created with its `parent` set and is appended to `parent.children` in the intended child order. Each call to a helper creates fresh node objects, so no `ProcessTree` object is shared between copies. Sharing objects would silently corrupt the parent pointers.

## 3.3 Is the tree exact?

**Why the case split is exact at trace level.** The only non-series-parallel ingredient is that AR is a predecessor of DC, APPR and SUPP, while CM and PV (which also feed the trial) are not. In any intended trace, AR completes at exactly one of three positions relative to the chain CM < PV: before CM, between CM and PV, or after PV. Once that position is fixed, the remaining constraints form a series-parallel order: `+(->(prep, TRIAL), APPR, SUPP)`. The three cases are disjoint and cover all interleavings, so their union is the intended language.

I checked this mechanically; see Section 7. The visible languages of the tree net, the POWL net, and an independent enumeration of all linearizations of the intended partial order were identical: 7,964 traces up to length 16, which includes up to two trial iterations.

## 3.4 What the tree cannot represent exactly, or represents only through approximation

**Why a label-unique tree cannot work (sketch).** In a label-unique tree:
- The lowest common ancestor (LCA) of PV and SR must be a parallel operator, because both orders are intended. It cannot be a choice or a loop, since both occur exactly once in every low-risk case.
- If the first ET lies above that parallel node, SR is forced before ET, which forbids approval–trial overlap. So ET lies in PV's branch.
- AR must precede SR and ET. If AR lies in SR's branch, it is parallel to ET, so the tree admits ET before AR. If AR lies outside the parallel node, then the LCA of AR and PV is a sequence that forces AR before PV. That forbids `RD CM PV AR …`.

Hence exactness requires duplication or loses behavior.

**Requirements affected in the delivered tree.**

- **Two initial work streams (rule 2).** Their freedom is not represented as concurrency. It is represented as a three-way choice about which stream "wins the race". The tree therefore asserts sequential structure that is not causal: copy 3 reads `->(PV, AR, …)`, as if analysis depended on verification planning. Under a start/complete refinement, the model would forbid genuinely overlapping executions of CM and AR, and of PV and AR.
- **Split dependencies into PV and DC (rules 3, 4, 7).** These are exact within each copy, but PV, DC, AR and CM exist in two or three copies.
- **Approvals overlapping planning, design and trial (rules 5, 7), and supplier overlap (rule 6).** These are exact at trace level, but APPR, SUPP and TRIAL are each triplicated. Frequencies, durations and conformance diagnostics for SR, QR, RR, CS, ET, IT and AP are spread across three structurally different nodes. Any per-node annotation (performance, stochastic branch weights, simulation parameters) is fragmented.
- **Branch frequencies are timing artifacts.** The frequencies of the race XORs do not reflect any business decision.
- **Risk route, optional supplier and repeat-until loop.** These are exact. They are abstracted from data exactly as stated in Section 1, and they are identical in every copy.
- **Model size.** The converted net has 49 transitions, 14 of them silent, and 50 places. The POWL net has 18 transitions, 4 of them silent, and 20 places.

**Label-unique conservative fallback `T_U`** (not the delivered `tree`; documented for comparison):

```text
T_U = ->( RD, +( ->(CM, PV), ->(AR, DC) ), +( TRIAL, APPR, SUPP ), RL, AC )
```

`T_U` admits only intended traces, so it is sound as an under-approximation. It keeps approval–trial overlap, which rule 7 states explicitly. However, it rejects every trace in which SR, QR, RR or CS completes before both PV and DC; see witnesses W2, W3 and W4. Its permissive counterpart would move APPR and SUPP out of AR's scope, for example `->(RD, +( ->(+(->(CM,PV), ->(AR,DC)), TRIAL), APPR, SUPP), RL, AC)`. That admits approval before root-cause analysis, which is unsafe, so I reject it.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

## 4.1 Compact POWL description

```text
TRIAL = *( PO(nodes={ET, IT}, order={ET-->IT}), AP )
APPR  = X( SR, PO(nodes={QR, RR}, order={}) )
SUPP  = X( CS, tau )

ROOT  = PO( nodes = {RD, CM, AR, PV, DC, APPR, SUPP, TRIAL, RL, AC},
            order (covering edges; the code adds their transitive closure) =
              RD-->CM, RD-->AR,
              CM-->PV,
              AR-->DC, AR-->APPR, AR-->SUPP,
              PV-->TRIAL, DC-->TRIAL,
              TRIAL-->RL, APPR-->RL, SUPP-->RL,
              RL-->AC )
```

This model has no duplicate labels. It is exact at completion-event trace level, and it states the cross-branch dependencies directly. AR → APPR and PV → TRIAL coexist without PV → APPR, which is exactly the "N" that the tree cannot express.

## 4.2 Python code

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

register = Transition(label="Register Deviation")
contain = Transition(label="Contain Material")
analyze = Transition(label="Analyze Root Cause")
plan = Transition(label="Plan Verification")
design = Transition(label="Design Corrective Action")

supervisor = Transition(label="Supervisor Review")
quality = Transition(label="Quality Review")
regulatory = Transition(label="Regulatory Review")
high_risk = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality, regulatory])   # no edges: concurrent
approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supervisor, high_risk])

consult = Transition(label="Consult Supplier")
supplier = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[consult, SilentTransition()])

execute = Transition(label="Execute Trial")
inspect_trial = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
trial_cycle = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[execute, inspect_trial])
trial_cycle.order.add_edge(execute, inspect_trial)
adjust = Transition(label="Adjust Parameters")
trial = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[trial_cycle, adjust])

release = Transition(label="Release Material")
archive = Transition(label="Archive Case")

powl = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[
    register, contain, analyze, plan, design,
    approval, supplier, trial, release, archive,
])

powl.order.add_edge(register, contain)
powl.order.add_edge(register, analyze)
powl.order.add_edge(contain, plan)
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, design)
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, approval)
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, supplier)
powl.order.add_edge(plan, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(design, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(trial, release)
powl.order.add_edge(approval, release)
powl.order.add_edge(supplier, release)
powl.order.add_edge(release, archive)

# StrictPartialOrder must be transitive; add the implied edges explicitly.
edges = {(s, t) for s in powl.order.nodes for t in powl.order.nodes if powl.order.is_edge(s, t)}
grew = True
while grew:
    grew = False
    for (a, b) in list(edges):
        for (c, d) in list(edges):
            if b is c and (a, d) not in edges:
                edges.add((a, d))
                powl.order.add_edge(a, d)
                grew = True

powl.validate_partial_orders()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(powl)
```

The model uses `StrictPartialOrder` for the root, the high-risk pair and the trial cycle. It uses `OperatorPOWL` for the risk XOR, the supplier XOR and the loop, `Transition` for all 14 activities, and `SilentTransition` for the supplier skip. The loop exit is silent by construction. Every dependency from Section 2 is added with `.order.add_edge`. The closure step only adds edges implied by the listed ones; for example, RD → PV follows from RD → CM → PV.

## 4.3 Why the intentionally unconnected pairs may remain concurrent

After closure, these are exactly the pairs of root nodes with no edge in either direction.

| Unconnected pair | Why no edge is correct |
|---|---|
| CM – AR | Rule 2: independent work streams that may overlap. |
| CM – DC | DC depends only on AR (rule 4); containment may still be running. |
| CM – APPR, CM – SUPP | Approvals and supplier consultation depend only on AR (rules 5, 6). |
| AR – PV | PV depends only on CM (rule 3). |
| PV – DC | Two independent work products; the trial joins them (rule 7). |
| PV – APPR, DC – APPR | The route needs only AR; rule 6 explicitly lets approvals overlap design and planning. |
| PV – SUPP, DC – SUPP | Rule 6: CS may overlap design and verification planning. |
| APPR – SUPP | Rule 6: CS may overlap approvals. |
| APPR – TRIAL | Rule 7: the route need not finish before the trial and may overlap the whole cycle, including AP. |
| SUPP – TRIAL | Rule 6: CS may overlap trial work. |
| QR – RR (inside APPR) | Rule 5: concurrent, either completion order. |

None of these pairs has a narrative constraint. Adding an edge would over-constrain the model; the missing edges into RL make sure nothing escapes the release synchronization.

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

**Canonical representation: the POWL model.**
- It is exact at the declared granularity.
- It is label-unique and states every narrative dependency as one edge, so each rule is traceable to an edge or an operator.
- It keeps concurrency as concurrency, so it survives a later refinement to start/complete lifecycles.
- It converts to a small sound workflow net: 18 transitions, 4 silent.

The duplicated tree is kept only as a derived artifact for tools that require process trees. It is trace-equivalent but structurally misleading. `T_U` is a fallback when both trees and unique labels are mandatory, and it must be reported as an under-approximation.

**Validation plan.**

1. **Conversion to accepting Petri nets.** Run `net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)` for `powl`, `tree` and `T_U`. Inspect the transition counts and the label multiplicities, rather than treating a successful conversion as proof.
2. **Soundness.** Run `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)` on each net. All three nets are sound in pm4py 2.7.23. The function is deprecated there, and WOFLAN warns about scipy stability without PuLP. Soundness is expected by construction for trees and POWL, so it is necessary but weak evidence: an over-permissive model is still sound.
3. **Bounded exhaustive playout and language comparison.** Enumerate all visible traces up to length L by DFS over the reachability graph with silent moves. Separately, generate all linearizations of the intended order as the oracle, for each combination of risk route, supplier yes/no and 1–3 trial iterations. Assert that the three sets are equal. Result for L = 16: 7,964 traces each, identical. L = 16 covers two trial iterations, the high route and the supplier.
4. **Sampled playout.** Run `pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)`, which gives 1,000 random traces by default and includes long loop runs (traces of length up to about 40 were observed). Check every sampled trace against an independent rule-based oracle: multiplicities, D3–D17, and the regular pattern of the ET/IT/AP projection. All samples conformed for both models.
5. **Explicit positive and negative trace tests.** Build an event log from W1–W5 and F1–F3 and run `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)`. Assert fitness = 1 for W1–W5 and fitness < 1 for F1–F3. For the negatives, also assert that the first misaligned move is the one named in Section 5. Map results by case ID: pm4py returns alignments in the log's case order, not in insertion order.
6. **Silent-behavior and duplicate-label checks.**
   - (a) Label-determinism of the duplicated tree net: explore every reachable marking, take its closure under silent moves, and assert that no label has two distinct enabled transitions. Result: 247 markings, 0 ambiguities. So every trace selects a unique copy, and the silent XOR/parallel routing never commits prematurely.
   - (b) Assert the expected label multiplicities: AR, PV, DC, SR, QR, RR, CS, ET, IT and AP 3× each; CM 2×; RD, RL and AC 1×.
   - (c) For the POWL net, check that no cycle of silent transitions exists. Also check that the skip of `X(CS, tau)` cannot fire after CS: CS occurs at most once in every enumerated trace.
   - (d) Check that AP is never the last ET/IT/AP event before RL.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

def visible_language(net, im, fm, max_len):
    result, seen, stack = set(), set(), [(im, ())]
    while stack:
        marking, trace = stack.pop()
        key = (frozenset(marking.items()), trace)
        if key in seen:
            continue
        seen.add(key)
        if marking == fm:
            result.add(trace)
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            nxt = trace + ((t.label,) if t.label is not None else ())
            if len(nxt) <= max_len:
                stack.append((semantics.execute(t, net, marking), nxt))
    return result

nets = {name: pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(m) for name, m in [("tree", tree), ("powl", powl)]}
for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    print(name, pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)[0])
lang = {name: visible_language(*nets[name], 16) for name in nets}
assert lang["tree"] == lang["powl"]          # and both == the oracle's linearizations
```

**Limitations.**
- The exhaustive comparison is bounded: two trial iterations for full enumeration, with longer runs only sampled. Equality for more loop iterations is argued, since every iteration re-enters the same loop, but not enumerated.
- The oracle, the witnesses and the models all come from *my* reading of the narrative. Validation shows internal consistency, not correctness of the interpretation. Examples are mixed routes, escalation, or rework of design after a failed trial.
- Data conditions (risk level, supplier need, inspection result) are untested by construction. A trace can fit while the business decision was wrong.
- Completion-only traces cannot detect violations of "may start only after" when an activity started early but completed late. Lifecycle-level validation would need start events, and the tree would then fail (Section 6).
- WOFLAN-based soundness checking depends on numerical solvers and a deprecated API.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **Semantic: exactness is claimed only at completion-event granularity.** The narrative says "may *start* only after". My models constrain completion order, so a PV that started before CM completed but finished after it is accepted. The same holds for early-started trials or releases. With start events, the POWL model would need lifecycle refinement, and the duplicated tree would no longer be exact, because it forbids genuine overlap of CM and AR. "Trace-exact" is therefore a statement about an abstraction I chose, not about the process.
2. **Semantic: the data abstraction is lossy in a way conformance cannot detect.**
   - Risk level, supplier need and inspection outcome became non-deterministic choices, so a high-risk case approved only by a supervisor fits perfectly.
   - "Passing inspection = last IT" makes rule 8 true by definition rather than checking it.
   - I also assumed away escalation, mixed routes, abandonment and design rework after failed trials. The narrative does not strictly exclude these; a stakeholder might expect them.
3. **Representational and practical: the delivered process tree is exact but hard to maintain and to read.** It triplicates the whole post-analysis block, so any narrative change must be made consistently in three copies. Tree-based metrics and annotations become distorted. The impossibility argument for label-unique trees is only a sketch, not a full proof over all operator combinations, including loops with silent parts. The code also relies on pm4py 2.7 internals (`BinaryRelation.is_edge`, `validate_partial_orders`) that could change between versions.
