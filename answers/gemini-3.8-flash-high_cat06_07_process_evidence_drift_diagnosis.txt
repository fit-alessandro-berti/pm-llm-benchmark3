## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

### January – March: Stable Baseline Equilibrium
- **System state**: The system operated in a stable baseline. Monthly volume ranged from 3,200 to 3,410 cases. Label maturity at reporting cutoff was 100%, with median label delay stable at 32–34 days.
- **Process & predictive alignment**: Process variants were steady (V1: 44–45%, V2: 35–36%, V3: 20%). The parts delivery edge `Order Part -> Part Received` averaged 28–35 h. Within-variant breach rates were stationary: V1 at 0.15–0.16, V2 at 0.10–0.11, and V3 at 0.30–0.32.
- **Model performance**: The model exhibited strong discrimination (AUROC 0.81–0.82) and near-perfect calibration (observed breach rate 0.18–0.19 vs mean predicted risk 0.18–0.19; Brier score 0.130–0.132; ECE 0.022–0.025). Data quality was clean (unknown activities 0.2–0.3%, missing queue features 1%).

### April: The Schema Migration and Pipeline Failure (Synthetic Deterioration)
- **Observations**: Mean predicted risk spiked from 0.18 to 0.28, AUROC collapsed from 0.81 to 0.67, Brier deteriorated to 0.184, and ECE surged to 0.110. Unknown activity share jumped to 17.0%, missing queue features rose to 14%, and unmapped variants reached 18%.
- **Deconstruction**: A naive audit would classify this as sudden, severe model drift. However, physical process metrics confirm that operational reality was unchanged: observed breach rate was 0.19 (identical to baseline), parts transit time was 34 h (normal), and within-variant breach rates were stable (V1: 0.18, V2: 0.13, V3: 0.34).
- **Root cause**: The April 1 workflow migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. The feature extraction pipeline did not recognize the new string, causing prediction prefixes to trigger at incorrect events or fail entirely. When prefixes failed or evaluated at partial traces, missing value imputations artificially inflated predicted risk to 0.28. Duplicate event logging further corrupted trace structures.
- **Verdict**: April was exclusively a data-engineering pipeline incident, not operational or concept drift.

### May: Patch Deployment and Lingering Technical Debt
- **Observations**: AUROC recovered substantially to 0.78, predicted risk dropped to 0.21, observed breach rate was 0.18, unknown activities fell to 2.0%, and missing queue features fell to 3.0%.
- **Deconstruction**: The May 6 mapping patch re-established the alias between the new and old activity names and cleaned post-April 20 duplicates. However, AUROC did not fully return to baseline (0.78 vs 0.81–0.82) and ECE remained elevated (0.050).
- **Root cause**: Derived historical features—specifically the cached "number of events before diagnosis"—were not recomputed during the backfill. This created lingering feature distortion in cases initiated in April and early May.
- **Verdict**: Confirmation of April's pipeline diagnosis, with residual data debt.

### June: Deliberate Policy Shift and Covariate Drift
- **Observations**: Observed breach rate dropped significantly from 0.18 to 0.15. Mean predicted risk remained at 0.20. AUROC was solid at 0.79, while ECE was 0.060.
- **Deconstruction**: On June 1, management implemented a new remote-routing policy. V2 (Remote Resolution) share surged to 52% (from 34% in May), while V1 fell to 30% (from 43%). Crucially, within-variant breach rates were completely unchanged: V1 was 0.16, V2 was 0.09, and V3 was 0.35. The macro breach rate dropped purely due to compositional math:
  $$P(Y=1) = \sum P(Y=1 \mid V_i) P(V_i) = (0.30 \times 0.16) + (0.52 \times 0.09) + (0.18 \times 0.35) = 0.048 + 0.0468 + 0.063 = 0.1578 \approx 0.15$$
- **Root cause**: The model overpredicted risk (0.20 vs 0.15) because it operated on historical base-rate priors (which assumed ~35% V2, ~45% V1). The conditional outcome distributions $P(Y \mid X, \text{variant})$ were invariant.
- **Verdict**: Pure covariate / process-routing drift induced by business policy, with zero concept drift.

### July: Incipient Supplier Bottleneck
- **Observations**: DFG edge `Order Part -> Part Received` rose from 42 h to 65 h. V3 breach rate jumped from 0.35 to 0.46. Total observed breach rate rose to 0.19, while mean predicted risk was 0.18. AUROC ticked down to 0.76. Label maturity was 98%.
- **Deconstruction**: On July 10, a key parts supplier began missing delivery SLAs. The SLA buffer absorbed some initial delays, but V3 cases began to fail.
- **Verdict**: Earliest emergence of external performance-driven concept drift.

### August: Catastrophic Supplier Failure and Unobserved Concept Drift
- **Observations**: Observed breach rate jumped to 0.28. Mean predicted risk was stuck at 0.19. AUROC plunged to 0.68, Brier score degraded to 0.197, and ECE spiked to 0.120.
- **Deconstruction**: Data pipeline metrics were clean (unknown activities 0.7%, missing queue 2%), ruling out a data bug. The physical process evidence reveals the crisis: median elapsed time on `Order Part -> Part Received` exploded to 130 h (more than 4x baseline). V3 breach rate skyrocketed to 0.64 (from 0.31 baseline). V3 variant share expanded to 35% as delayed cases accumulated parts dependencies.
- **Root cause**: The model predicts immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`. At that instant, parts orders have not yet occurred, and the feature vector contains no supplier lead-time or inventory metrics. The mapping $P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})$ fundamentally shifted due to an unmodeled downstream latent shock.
- **Verdict**: Severe concept drift driven by external process performance degradation.

### September: Compound Crisis (Population Shift + Supplier Crisis + Emerging Label Delay)
- **Observations**: Volume surged to 5,100 cases. Observed breach rate reached 0.31, while mean predicted risk was 0.21. AUROC was 0.66, Brier score 0.214, and ECE 0.140. Label maturity fell to 82%, and median label delay lengthened to 42 days.
- **Deconstruction**: Three distinct phenomena coincided:
  1. *Population shift*: Partner Z was onboarded on September 1 (24% of total volume), bringing rural customers, longer travel distances, and older equipment—covariates absent from the model.
  2. *Process routing shift*: 58% of Partner Z cases followed V3 (vs 36% for legacy partners), driving overall V3 share to 42%.
  3. *Ongoing supplier crisis*: Parts transit delay worsened to 148 h, keeping V3 breach rate at 0.61. Partner Z experienced an observed breach rate of 0.45 (vs 0.27 for legacy partners).
  4. *Emerging censoring*: Slower case resolutions lengthened median label delay to 42 days, leaving 18% of cases unobserved at cutoff.
- **Verdict**: Multi-factor failure combining demographic population shift, process routing drift, persistent concept drift, and emerging label delay.

### October: Severe Censoring and Observability Breakdown (Deceptive Recovery)
- **Observations**: Volume was 5,250 cases. Observed breach rate appeared to plunge to 0.16. Mean predicted risk was 0.23. Label maturity collapsed to 55%, and median label delay spiked to 52 days.
- **Deconstruction**: A naive reading would suggest that breach rates resolved back to 0.16 and the model was now overpredicting risk. This is an illusion caused by severe right-censoring and survivorship bias.
- **Root cause**: The outcome API developed an acute backlog in October, disproportionately affecting longer-duration cases (V3 parts cases and rural visits). Fast-resolving cases—specifically V2 remote fixes that resolve in ~4 hours—completed and logged their non-breach outcomes immediately. Complex, delayed cases (parts transit at 155 h, rural travel) remained open or stuck in the API queue. Evaluating the model on the available 55% slice is biased toward non-breaches.
- **Verdict**: Severe label delay / right-censoring. True underlying breach rate is likely $\ge 0.32$.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### Test 1: Label-Maturity Curves and Fixed-Vintage Backtesting
- **Methodology**: Plot cumulative label resolution as a function of days elapsed since case creation ($t+15, t+30, t+45, t+60, t+90$) for each monthly cohort. Rather than evaluating performance at a variable calendar cutoff, evaluate metrics on fixed vintages (e.g., all cases evaluated exactly at day 60 post-creation). Apply Kaplan-Meier survival curves to estimate breach probability under right-censoring.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (October breach rate genuinely improved to 0.16)*: The vintage curve at day 60 will remain flat at ~0.16; the hazard rate of breach after day 30 will be negligible.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (October is truncated by API backlog and duration censoring)*: As backlogged labels mature between day 45 and day 90, the cohort breach rate will rise from 0.16 to >0.32, with the newly mature cases consisting overwhelmingly of V3 and Partner Z rural traces.

### Test 2: Recomputation after Activity-Label Normalization and Duplicate Cleaning
- **Methodology**: Re-ingest raw April event logs. Map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\to$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, deduplicate identical consecutive activity timestamps, re-extract prefix states at the true diagnosis timestamp, and recalculate derived features ("events before diagnosis", queue depth). Re-score April cases using the frozen pre-April model.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (April deterioration was physical process/concept drift)*: Model AUROC remains depressed (~0.67) and mean predicted risk remains elevated (~0.28) even on clean features.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (April deterioration was entirely a data-pipeline failure)*: Re-extracted data yields an AUROC recovery to ~0.80, mean predicted risk realigns with the observed 0.19 rate, and unmapped variants drop from 18% to <1%.

### Test 3: Calibration and Discrimination Stratified by Variant, Source, and Month
- **Methodology**: Compute AUROC, ECE, Brier score, and calibration curves separately across subsets partitioned by variant (V1, V2, V3), source (Partner Z vs Legacy), and month (June vs Baseline).
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (June drop in breach rate reflected within-process concept drift)*: Within-variant calibration curves will show significant drift, and within-variant AUROC will degrade.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (June was pure covariate/routing drift)*: Within V1, V2, and V3 strata, AUROC (V1 ~0.81, V2 ~0.82, V3 ~0.79) and ECE (<0.03) in June are identical to baseline; only the aggregate population-weighted average shifted.

### Test 4: Comparison of Process Variants and DFG / Performance-DFG Edges
- **Methodology**: Discover control-flow DFGs and performance DFGs (median transition times) across monthly sub-logs using PM4Py. Measure bottleneck edges: `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Diagnosis -> Dispatch Technician`, and `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (August failure was internal technician dispatch failure)*: Median duration on `Diagnosis -> Dispatch Technician` increases significantly, while supplier edges remain normal.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (August failure was external supplier bottleneck)*: The edge `Order Part -> Part Received` exhibits an acute spike from 35 h to 130+ h, while internal technician and remote fix edges remain stable within baseline tolerances (3.8–4.6 h).

### Test 5: Outcome Relationship Conditional on the Same Execution Path ($P(Y \mid \text{path})$ Stability)
- **Methodology**: Perform a logistic regression predicting breach outcome given execution path, controlling for the actual elapsed duration of `Order Part -> Part Received`, comparing Jan–Mar against Jul–Aug.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (Parts delay fully mediates the increased breach risk)*: Conditional on parts delay duration, the month indicator coefficient (Aug vs Baseline) is statistically insignificant ($p > 0.05$, odds ratio $\approx 1.0$), proving that conditional process physics did not change outside of the supplier delay.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (Unmeasured internal execution breakdown occurred)*: The month coefficient remains positive and significant ($p < 0.01$), indicating that August cases failed even when parts arrived promptly.

### Test 6: Population Reweighting and Transportability Checks for Partner Z
- **Methodology**: Apply Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) or covariate matching on rural travel distance, device age, and variant propensity to reweight Partner Z cases to match the legacy customer demographic. Test whether the breach rate differential (0.45 vs 0.27) persists.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (Partner Z has intrinsic operational execution deficiencies)*: Reweighted Partner Z cases still show a statistically significant breach premium over legacy cases ($>0.08$ gap).
  - *Under Hypothesis B (Partner Z differences are driven by demographics and V3 routing share)*: The breach gap between reweighted Partner Z cases and legacy cases vanishes (gap $<0.02$, $p > 0.10$).

### Test 7: Feature Missingness and Lineage Audits
- **Methodology**: Profile raw event ingestion tables through each stage of feature generation. Track schema conformity, null rates of `queue_feature`, unknown activity proportions, and prefix sequence length distributions per batch from March through October.
- **Expected Results**:
  - *Under Hypothesis A (October degradation was caused by upstream feature pipeline corruption)*: Upstream feature tables will show spikes in missing features, unknown tokens, or altered prefix lengths similar to April.
  - *Under Hypothesis B (October is an API downstream ingestion backlog)*: Upstream feature extraction is clean (unknown activities <1%, queue missingness 3%), but the join to outcome labels reveals a massive right-skewed lag in label timestamp availability.

### Architectural Recommendation: Segment-Specific vs. Variant-Specific Model
A monolithic single-stage model evaluated at diagnosis is structurally inadequate because the primary drivers of breach (remote resolution feasibility, supplier delivery speed, rural travel distance) diverge immediately after diagnosis. 

A **Two-Stage Hierarchical Architecture** is strongly justified:
1. **Stage 1 (Routing Classifier at Diagnosis)**:
   - Predicts the operational routing path: $P(V_1), P(V_2), P(V_3) = f(X_{\text{triage}}, X_{\text{device\_age}}, X_{\text{symptoms}}, X_{\text{partner}})$.
   - Predicts whether a case can be solved remotely ($V_2$) or requires parts ($V_3$).
2. **Stage 2 (Conditional SLA Breach Models)**:
   - *Remote Branch ($V_2$)*: Deterministic low-risk model ($P(\text{Breach}) \approx 0.10$). Driven purely by internal queue depth.
   - *Standard Visit Branch ($V_1$)*: Driven by technician availability, customer scheduling, and rural travel distance.
   - *Parts-Dependent Branch ($V_3$)*: Integrates live supply-chain context: $P(\text{Breach} \mid V_3) = g(X_{\text{device}}, X_{\text{rural\_distance}}, \text{Supplier\_Backlog\_Index}, \text{Current\_Median\_Lead\_Time})$.
   - Final risk is the expectation: $\sum_{k \in \{1,2,3\}} P(V_k) \cdot P(\text{Breach} \mid V_k)$.

### Alert Dependency Rules
1. **Rule 1 (Data-Quality Dominance)**: If Tier 0 alerts on `Unknown Activity Share > 1%` or `Schema Validation Failure`, all Tier 3 performance degradation alerts (AUROC drop, ECE spike) are suppressed. The incident is classified as a *Data Engineering Defect*.
2. **Rule 2 (Label-Maturity Gating)**: If Tier 1 alerts on `Cohort Maturity < 90%` or `Outcome API Backlog > 100 cases`, automated model retraining triggers are hard-blocked. Predictive metrics are watermarked as *Unreliable / Right-Censored*.
3. **Rule 3 (Supply-Chain Root-Cause Attribution)**: If Tier 2 detects that median duration on `Order Part -> Part Received` exceeds baseline $+ 3\sigma$ (>50 h), an automated *Supply Chain Exception* is raised. Rather than retraining the baseline model on shifted data, the system flags cases as operational supply bottlenecks.

### Process Evidence Extraction Code

```python
"""
audit_process_evidence.py
Extracts monthly process-mining metrics, variant distributions, bottleneck edge durations,
and temporal features from field service event logs using PM4Py and pandas.
"""

import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from datetime import datetime
from typing import Dict, Any


def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    # 1. Read raw event log
    df_raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

    # 2. Activity label normalization (repairing April schema migration)
    activity_normalization_map = {
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    }
    df_raw["activity"] = df_raw["activity"].replace(activity_normalization_map)

    # 3. Duplicate handling strategy:
    # Sort chronologically within case and deduplicate exact consecutive duplicate events
    df_raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df_raw["timestamp"])
    df_raw = df_raw.sort_values(by=["case_id", "timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)
    consecutive_dup_mask = (
        (df_raw["case_id"] == df_raw["case_id"].shift(1)) &
        (df_raw["activity"] == df_raw["activity"].shift(1)) &
        (df_raw["timestamp"] == df_raw["timestamp"].shift(1))
    )
    df_clean = df_raw[~consecutive_dup_mask].copy()

    # Filter out unmapped generic unknown activities for control-flow mining, but track count
    unknown_count = (df_clean["activity"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").sum()
    df_clean = df_clean[df_clean["activity"] != "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY"].copy()

    # 4. Format dataframe for PM4Py compliance
    df_pm = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_clean,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )

    # 5. Cohort Definition and Analysis:
    # A. Case-start cohort: Traces grouped by timestamp of 'Case Created'.
    #    Interpretation: Evaluates full lifecycle, final SLA breach rate, and eventual maturity
    #    of cases originating in that calendar month. Essential for retrospective audit.
    # B. Event-calendar cohort: Events filtered by calendar execution window.
    #    Interpretation: Evaluates active system load, concurrent operational bottlenecks,
    #    and instantaneous throughput times across active cases, regardless of start date.

    case_starts = df_pm.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().reset_index()
    case_starts.rename(columns={"timestamp": "case_start_time"}, inplace=True)
    df_pm = df_pm.merge(case_starts, on="case_id", how="left")
    df_pm["case_start_month"] = df_pm["case_start_time"].dt.to_period("M")

    # Extract temporal features across traces
    temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df_pm,
        case_id_key="case_id",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )

    monthly_audit_summaries = {}
    available_months = df_pm["case_start_month"].dropna().unique()

    for month_period in sorted(available_months):
        month_str = str(month_period)
        
        # Filter Case-Start Cohort
        cohort_df = df_pm[df_pm["case_start_month"] == month_period].copy()
        
        # Filter Event-Calendar Cohort (active window)
        start_dt = pd.to_datetime(f"{month_str}-01 00:00:00")
        end_dt = (start_dt + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(1)) + pd.Timedelta(hours=23, minutes=59, seconds=59)
        calendar_df = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df_pm,
            start_dt,
            end_dt,
            mode="events"
        )

        # Discover Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) on case-start cohort
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort_df)
        
        # Discover Performance DFG on active calendar window to capture instantaneous bottlenecks
        perf_dfg, p_start, p_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(calendar_df)

        # Extract variant distribution
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort_df)
        total_cases = len(cohort_df["case_id"].unique())
        variant_distribution = {str(k): len(v) / total_cases for k, v in variants.items()}

        # Extract median duration for critical supply-chain edge: Order Part -> Part Received
        parts_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        median_parts_transit_hours = None
        if parts_edge in perf_dfg:
            edge_stats = perf_dfg[parts_edge]
            # PM4Py performance DFG stores duration in seconds (median or mean depending on aggregation)
            raw_seconds = edge_stats.get("median", edge_stats.get("mean", 0.0))
            median_parts_transit_hours = round(raw_seconds / 3600.0, 2)

        # Extract median duration for remote fix edge: Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix
        remote_edge = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
        median_remote_hours = None
        if remote_edge in perf_dfg:
            r_stats = perf_dfg[remote_edge]
            raw_r_sec = r_stats.get("median", r_stats.get("mean", 0.0))
            median_remote_hours = round(raw_r_sec / 3600.0, 2)

        monthly_audit_summaries[month_str] = {
            "case_start_cohort_size": total_cases,
            "active_calendar_events": len(calendar_df),
            "top_variants_share": sorted(variant_distribution.values(), reverse=True)[:3],
            "parts_transit_median_hours": median_parts_transit_hours,
            "remote_fix_median_hours": median_remote_hours,
            "temporal_feature_columns": list(temporal_features.columns)[:5]
        }

    return {
        "monthly_summaries": monthly_audit_summaries,
        "total_cleaned_events": len(df_pm),
        "filtered_unknown_activities": int(unknown_count)
    }
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### Failure Mode 1: Retraining on Immature Labels (The October Censoring Fallacy)
- **Mechanism**: The observed breach rate in October dropped to 0.16 because only 55% of cases had matured by the reporting cutoff, confounded by an outcome API backlog. If an ML engineer treats this 0.16 as genuine performance and retrains the model on available October labels, unobserved open/backlogged cases will be implicitly or explicitly labeled as non-breaches.
- **Consequence**: The model learns that high-complexity, parts-dependent, and rural cases (which take longer to resolve) do not breach SLAs. In production, it severely underestimates risk for complex repairs. Technicians are not dispatched with urgency, parts are not expedited, and actual real-world SLA breaches skyrocket, causing catastrophic customer SLA penalties.
- **Mitigation**: Enforce a strict cohort maturity gate ($\ge 95\%$) before any cohort can enter the training or evaluation set. For operational tracking of incomplete cohorts, utilize survival-analysis estimators (e.g., Kaplan-Meier or right-censored Cox models) that explicitly model the censoring horizon rather than assuming unobserved labels are negative.

### Failure Mode 2: Calibrating Around a Pipeline Defect (The April Schema Trap)
- **Mechanism**: In April, predicted risk spiked to 0.28 against an observed breach rate of 0.19, driving ECE to 0.110. An engineer misdiagnosing this as "model overconfidence" would deploy a post-hoc recalibration layer (e.g., Platt scaling or temperature scaling) to depress predicted probabilities by ~30%.
- **Consequence**: The overprediction was entirely an artifact of feature corruption (renamed activity and missing queue features). When the May 6 patch repaired activity naming, the raw model outputs naturally settled back to baseline risk. If the April recalibration dampening factor remains in production, the model will systematically *underpredict* risk on clean data in subsequent months, blinding operations to genuine breach risks.
- **Mitigation**: Establish a mandatory policy: *Never mathematically recalibrate a model on data containing unresolved pipeline defects*. Implement data-quality circuit breakers (unknown activity rate $>1\%$, feature missingness $>3\%$) that trigger data engineering remediation rather than ML parameter adjustments.

### Failure Mode 3: Treating External Supply-Chain Shocks as Random Model Noise (Ignoring August Concept Drift)
- **Mechanism**: When breach rates surged to 0.28 in August (and 0.64 in V3) due to parts delivery delays expanding to 130 h, an auditor might dismiss the drop in AUROC (0.68) as transient noise or attempt standard retraining on raw diagnosis features without investigating process mining evidence.
- **Consequence**: Retraining the model on August data without adding supplier lead-time features forces the model to fit high breach probabilities onto static diagnosis symptoms (e.g., device age or initial error codes). The model overfits to spurious correlations while remaining blind to the true physical cause (parts transit time). When supplier performance eventually recovers, the model will continue falsely predicting breaches for benign repairs.
- **Mitigation**: Combine predictive process monitoring with performance-DFG bottleneck tracking. When an external process transition time crosses $+3\sigma$ (e.g., parts transit $>50$ h), trigger an operational supply-chain alert and ingest dynamic external features (supplier backlog index, live parts delivery ETA) directly into the feature store. Decouple predictions by re-scoring cases dynamically when a part order event is recorded.

### Failure Mode 4: Attributing Partner Z’s Elevated Breaches to Model Bias (Misdiagnosing Population Shift)
- **Mechanism**: In September, Partner Z cases exhibited a 0.45 breach rate vs 0.27 for legacy partners. Without stratified process evidence, leadership might accuse Partner Z of poor operational execution or accuse the model of algorithmic bias against rural customers, prompting ad-hoc downward risk adjustments for Partner Z cases.
- **Consequence**: The elevated breach rate is physically driven: 58% of Partner Z cases require parts (vs 36% legacy) during a national supplier breakdown, compounded by longer rural travel distances. Artificially discounting predicted risk for Partner Z cases will lead to under-resourcing, delayed dispatches, and guaranteed service failures in rural territories.
- **Mitigation**: Conduct formal covariate transportability and stratified performance audits. Decompose Partner Z's performance into constituent structural factors: route distribution (58% V3), supplier delay exposure, and travel distance. Ingest physical geographic and equipment covariates into the model so risk predictions transparently and accurately reflect physical logistical realities.
