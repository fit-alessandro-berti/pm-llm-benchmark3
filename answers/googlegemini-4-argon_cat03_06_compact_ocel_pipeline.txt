## 1. Modeling assumptions

- **Object types and capitalization**: We define six exact object types using singular Title Case: `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice`, plus `Payment`.
- **E2O qualifier vocabulary**:
  - `created_order`, `closed_order`, `context_order`, `shared_order`, `billed_order` (for `Order` involvements)
  - `added_item`, `reserved_item`, `packed_item` (for `Item` involvements)
  - `created_package`, `dispatched_package` (for `Package` involvements)
  - `created_delivery`, `confirmed_delivery` (for `Delivery` involvements)
  - `issued_invoice`, `settled_invoice` (for `Invoice` involvements)
  - `recorded_payment` (for `Payment` involvements)
- **O2O direction conventions**: All O2O relations follow a strict top-down or chronological dependency convention (`ocel:oid` $\rightarrow$ `ocel:oid_2`):
  - `Order` $\xrightarrow{\text{contains}}$ `Item`
  - `Package` $\xrightarrow{\text{packs}}$ `Item`
  - `Delivery` $\xrightarrow{\text{carries}}$ `Package`
  - `Invoice` $\xrightarrow{\text{bills}}$ `Order`
  - `Payment` $\xrightarrow{\text{partially\_settles / completes\_settlement}}$ `Invoice`
- **Downstream E2O scoping (Direct vs. Propagated)**: We adopt a **strictly direct source-relation policy** supplemented only by explicitly stated operational sharing:
  - Events E6 (`Reserve Stock`) and E7 (`Pack Package` for P1) are explicitly stated in the prompt as operationally shared by or relating to `O100` and `O200`. Likewise, E8 (`Pack Package` for P2) occurs in the warehouse fulfillment context of `O100`. Thus, `Order` is linked to `Pack Package` and `Reserve Stock`.
  - Downstream logistics (`Dispatch Package`, `Confirm Delivery`) and settlement events (`Record Payment`) do **not** link directly to `Order` or `Item`. In a real ERP/WMS architecture, a carrier webhook confirming Delivery `D1` only knows about `D1` (or at most `P1`), not the originating sales orders or individual line items inside the box. Similarly, a bank feed recording `M1` against `V1` only references the invoice and payment ID, not the underlying orders.
- **Preventing analytical relation propagation from corrupting source facts**: If an analyst wants `Order` traces to include `Confirm Delivery` or `Record Payment`, that enrichment should happen via a post-hoc graph traversal over `o2o` (creating a derived view or synthetic qualifier like `propagated_order`), keeping the base `relations` table faithful to actual system foreign keys recorded at event execution time.
- **Version-dependent PM4Py assumptions**: In `pm4py >= 2.7.x`, `OCEL` is imported from `pm4py.objects.ocel.obj`. The constructor accepts `(events=..., objects=..., relations=..., o2o=...)`. Some versions of PM4Py only expose `pm4py.discover_ocdfg` and `pm4py.discover_oc_petri_net` directly at the top level while requiring `o2o` or graph helpers for `discover_otg` / `discover_etot` depending on minor releases. In our code, we isolate the `OCEL(...)` instantiation and provide safe fallbacks for top-level discovery calls so the script remains resilient across minor 2.x releases.

### Consequential events for convergence and divergence
- **Convergence (Synchronization / Merging)**:
  - **`E6` (`Reserve Stock`)**: Jointly reserves `I1` (from `O100`) and `I3` (from `O200`), synchronizing two previously independent order lifecycles.
  - **`E7` (`Pack Package` for `P1`)**: Consolidates items `I1` and `I3` across two separate orders into a single physical handling unit `P1`.
  - **`E11` (`Issue Invoice` for `V1`)**: Merges the financial billing of `O100` and `O200` into a single consolidated commercial document `V1`.
- **Divergence (Splitting / Fan-out)**:
  - **`O100` across `E2`/`E3` and `E7`/`E8`**: `O100` spawns two items (`I1`, `I2`) that diverge into two separate packages (`P1` at `E7` and `P2` at `E8`) and subsequently travel on two distinct deliveries (`D1` and `D2`).
  - **`V1` across `E12` and `E15`**: A single invoice `V1` diverges into multiple sequential settlement events (`M1` and `M2`).

| **`Delivery`** | **`D1`**: `E9 -> E13`<br>**`D2`**: `E10 -> E14` | None. | Everything upstream of dispatch (`Pack Package`, `Order` context) and all financial flows. | `(Dispatch Package, Confirm Delivery): 2` | Clean two-step carrier lifecycle, but masks the fact that `D1` carries consolidated goods for two different orders while `D2` carries a split shipment for `O100`. |
| **`Invoice`** | **`V1`**: `E11 -> E12 -> E15` | None (only one `Invoice` exists). | All order capture, fulfillment, shipping, and order closure milestones. | `(Issue Invoice, Record Payment): 1`<br>`(Record Payment, Record Payment): 1` | Shows a self-loop on `Record Payment`, which correctly reflects partial payments on `V1`, but completely hides that `V1` is financing two distinct orders (`O100`, `O200`) with asynchronous delivery schedules (`E13` happens between `E12` and `E15`, whereas `E14` happens right before `E15`). |

### Why flattening on `Payment` is particularly weak here
Each `Payment` object (`M1`, `M2`) participates in **exactly one event**: `E12` (`Record Payment` for `M1`) and `E15` (`Record Payment` for `M2`). Flattening on `Payment` produces two degenerate single-event traces: `M1: [Record Payment]` and `M2: [Record Payment]`. The resulting DFG has **zero edges** (empty dictionary `{}`), where `Record Payment` is simultaneously the sole start and end activity. It completely erases the temporal ordering between `M1` and `M2`, the link to `V1`, and the preceding `Issue Invoice` event.

## 5. Expected object-centric structures

- **OC-DFG (`pm4py.discover_ocdfg`)**:
  - Preserves exact event cardinalities (`Issue Invoice` = 1, `Reserve Stock` = 1, `Pack Package` = 2) while overlaying typed directed arcs for each object type.
  - Shows `Order` arcs flowing from `Create Order` $\rightarrow$ `Add Item` $\rightarrow$ `Reserve Stock` $\rightarrow$ `Pack Package` $\rightarrow$ `Issue Invoice` $\rightarrow$ `Close Order`.
  - At `Pack Package`, `Item` arcs terminate (`I1`, `I2`, `I3` have no further E2O events), while `Package` arcs originate (`P1`, `P2`) and flow to `Dispatch Package`, where `Delivery` arcs (`D1`, `D2`) take over and flow to `Confirm Delivery`.
  - At `Issue Invoice`, `Order` arcs (`O100`, `O200`) synchronize with the birth of `Invoice` `V1`. From `Issue Invoice`, the `Invoice` arc flows to `Record Payment` (with a self-loop for `V1` across `E12` and `E15`), where `Payment` objects (`M1`, `M2`) enter and immediately terminate.
- **OTG (`discover_otg`)**:
  - Reveals the structural backbone of the enterprise schema: `Order` $\rightarrow$ `Item` (`contains`), `Package` $\rightarrow$ `Item` (`packs`), `Delivery` $\rightarrow$ `Package` (`carries`), `Invoice` $\rightarrow$ `Order` (`bills`), and `Payment` $\rightarrow$ `Invoice` (`partially_settles` / `completes_settlement`), alongside E2O co-occurrence bridges (e.g., `Package` and `Delivery` meeting at `Dispatch Package`).
- **ET–OT Graph (`discover_etot`)**:
  - Maps the bipartite participation matrix between the 9 activity types and 6 object types. It highlights **synchronization hubs**—specifically `Pack Package` (degree 3: touches `Order`, `Item`, `Package`), `Issue Invoice` (degree 2: `Invoice`, `Order`), `Dispatch Package` (degree 2: `Package`, `Delivery`), and `Record Payment` (degree 2: `Invoice`, `Payment`).
- **Object-Centric Petri Net (`pm4py.discover_oc_petri_net`)**:
  - Models typed places for each object type and transitions for activities, distinguishing **variable arcs** (where multiple objects of a type are consumed/produced in a single firing) from **normal arcs** (1-to-1 token flow).
  - Transition `Issue Invoice` consumes a variable number of `Order` tokens (`O100` and `O200` simultaneously) and produces one `Invoice` token (`V1`).
  - Transition `Pack Package` consumes multiple `Item` tokens (`I1`, `I3` for `P1`) and `Order` tokens while producing a single `Package` token (`P1`).
  - *Note*: The exact place/arc topology of an OCPN is not uniquely determined; it depends on the underlying discovery thresholds, how silent transitions ($\tau$) handle optional loops (like multiple `Add Item` or `Record Payment` executions), and whether variable-arc inference marks `Order` at `Pack Package` as variable (due to `E7` touching two orders) versus singular (at `E8`).
- **Object-Interactions Summary (`pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary`)**:
  - Produces a tabular breakdown of every co-occurring object pair per event. It explicitly surfaces that:
    - `O100` and `O200` interact directly at `E6`, `E7`, and `E11`.
    - `P1` bridges `I1` (from `O100`) and `I3` (from `O200`) at `E7`.
    - `V1` binds `O100` and `O200` at `E11`, and sequentially interacts with `M1` at `E12` and `M2` at `E15`.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

### Policy 1: Strict Source-Minimal Relations (Pure Local Foreign Keys)
- **Rule**: An event only links to the immediate foreign keys stored on the transactional record that generated the event.
  - `E6` (`Reserve Stock`) links only to `I1` and `I3` (no `Order` links).
  - `E7` and `E8` (`Pack Package`) link only to `P1`/`P2` and the packed `Item`s (`I1`, `I3`, `I2`), dropping `O100` and `O200`.
- **Impact on Discovery**:
  - Flattening on `Order` yields much shorter traces: `Create Order -> Add Item -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order`. Warehouse operations (`Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package`) disappear completely from the `Order` DFG.
  - In the OC-DFG and OCPN, the handover from `Order` to `Package` must be mediated entirely through `Item` tokens (`Order` creates `Item` at `Add Item` $\rightarrow$ `Item` goes to `Reserve Stock` $\rightarrow$ `Item` meets `Package` at `Pack Package`).

### Policy 2: Full Operational Involvement (Current Baseline Implementation)
- **Rule**: Events link to their immediate transactional objects plus explicitly stated operational context objects (`O100` and `O200` on shared warehouse events `E6`, `E7`, and `E8`), while keeping external carrier/bank events (`Dispatch Package`, `Confirm Delivery`, `Record Payment`) scoped strictly to their respective logistics (`Package`, `Delivery`) and financial (`Invoice`, `Payment`) objects.
- **Impact on Discovery**:
  - Balances cross-order visibility in warehouse fulfillment (`E6`, `E7`) with clean modular separation when packages leave the warehouse (`D1`, `D2`) and when finance collects cash against the consolidated invoice (`V1`, `M1`, `M2`).

### Policy 3: O2O-Propagated Analytical Relations (Transitive Closure Enrichment)
- **Rule**: Propagate E2O links upstream across all `o2o` paths so that `Order` and `Item` are linked to every downstream event affecting their packages, deliveries, invoices, and payments:
  - `E9` (`Dispatch P1 in D1`) and `E13` (`Confirm D1`) inherit `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200`.
  - `E10` (`Dispatch P2 in D2`) and `E14` (`Confirm D2`) inherit `I2` and `O100`.
  - `E12` (`Record Payment M1`) and `E15` (`Record Payment M2`) inherit `O100` and `O200` via `V1`.
- **Impact on Discovery**:
  - Flattening on `Order` now contains all 17 events across the two traces (`O100` gets 14 events; `O200` gets 11 events).
  - Reveals the critical chronological interleaving at the order level—namely that `O200`'s sole delivery (`D1` at `E13`) is confirmed on April 6, whereas `O100`'s second delivery (`D2` at `E14`) is only confirmed on April 7, right before final payment `M2` (`E15`) and order closures (`E16`, `E17`).
  - **Downside**: Massive **convergence distortion** in flattened DFGs (`Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`, `Dispatch D1`, and `Confirm D1` are all double-counted in the `Order` DFG) and dense spaghetti arcs in the OC-DFG (`Order` arcs connect to every single activity in the process).

## 7. Failure modes

1. **Relation inflation (Spaghetti OC-DFGs)**: Linking every upstream object (`Order`, `Item`) to every downstream event (`Dispatch`, `Confirm Delivery`, `Record Payment`) turns every activity into a multi-object synchronization hub, burying true handovers (like `Item` $\rightarrow$ `Package` $\rightarrow$ `Delivery`) under redundant `Order` arcs.
2. **Missing direct E2O links (Disconnected lifecycles)**: If `Add Item` only links to `Item` (omitting `Order`), or `Dispatch Package` only links to `Delivery` (omitting `Package`), the OC-DFG splits into disconnected islands with no handover transitions between object types.
3. **Treating O2O propagation as source observation**: Storing transitively inferred links in `relations` with the same qualifiers as native ERP links makes it impossible to tell whether the carrier's `Confirm Delivery` message actually referenced `O100` or if an analyst joined it via `D1 -> P1 -> I1 -> O100`. Furthermore, if an item was repacked or reassigned later, static O2O propagation retroactively corrupts historical event context.
4. **Duplicate shared events after flattening (Convergence bias)**: Flattening on `Order` duplicates `E6`, `E7`, and `E11` into both `O100` and `O200`. Any KPI or frequency counter on the flattened log will falsely report 2 invoices issued and 2 stock reservations instead of 1.
5. **Ambiguous payment allocation**: `M1` (100 units) and `M2` (150 units) settle consolidated invoice `V1` (total 250 units), which bills both `O100` and `O200`. Neither the events nor the O2O model specify how much of `M1` pays for `O100` vs. `O200`. Propagating `M1` to both orders frequently leads analysts to sum `payment_amount` per order and falsely conclude that $100 + 150 = 250$ was paid *per order* (500 total).
6. **Overly short `Payment` traces**: Because `M1` and `M2` only exist at the moment `Record Payment` executes, flattening on `Payment` yields single-event traces with an empty DFG (`{}`), providing zero behavioral insight unless combined with `Invoice` in an object-centric view.
7. **Constructor / API version mismatch**: Across PM4Py 2.x sub-versions, `OCEL` attribute names and discovery functions have evolved (e.g., whether `o2o` is passed in `OCEL.__init__` and whether `discover_otg` / `discover_etot` are top-level functions). Hard-coding unguarded calls without isolating the constructor causes runtime `TypeError` or `AttributeError` failures.
8. **False total order for same-time events (Tie-breaking illusions)**: Although our 17 events have strictly distinct timestamps, real logs often batch events at the same second (e.g., `E2` and `E3` adding items at `08:05:00`, or `E16` and `E17` closing orders in a batch job). Standard DFG discovery sorts tied timestamps deterministically by row order or event ID, inventing sequential edges like `(Close Order -> Close Order)` when the events were actually concurrent.
