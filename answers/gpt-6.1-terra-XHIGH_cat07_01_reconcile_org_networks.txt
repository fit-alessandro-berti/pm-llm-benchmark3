# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

The networks describe different relationships, so their scores need not agree. Mira appears on a minority of all claims, disproportionately on reopened claims and complaints, and may connect otherwise separate parts of the process without belonging to a stable team pair. The figures support an exception-coordination interpretation, but not a definitive job title or a causal attribution of delays.

- **Handover-of-work network.** A directed edge summarizes observed succession from one resource's event to another's under the chosen event-ordering and handover rules. Mira's high out-degree and betweenness, together with Bot_Rules → Mira and Mira → specialists and adjusters, are consistent with routing work between automation, exception handling, and operational teams. Her in-degree and specialist return edges suggest information or work also returns to her. The counts are transitions, potentially including repeated transitions in a single case; they do not prove that she assigned work, that the recipient accepted it, or that a direct interpersonal transfer occurred. Betweenness depends on graph construction, weights, direction, and the treatment of bots and vendors; it is neither proof of authority nor proof of a bottleneck.
- **Working-together network.** An undirected edge here represents case co-occurrence, not necessarily simultaneous work or collaboration. Strong Alice–Carla and Bruno–Diego ties are consistent with recurring team patterns; Mira's weaker individual ties are consistent with a coordinator whose cases are spread across many recipients and exception pathways. She could also be active on a different subset of cases from the team leads. Short routing events explain why a case appearance may represent a brief intervention, but duration alone cannot lower binary case co-occurrence: one short event still counts. Her disappearance for several events likewise does not erase co-occurrence on the same case. Check the denominator, case population, deduplication, and whether reported strengths are percentages of all cases or a different normalization before explaining the numerical gap. A frequent handover can recur within cases, while a co-case measure counts a pair at most once per case; if edges are deduplicated, 350 distinct Mira–Carla handover cases out of roughly 4,100 cases would already imply about 8.5% co-occurrence, broadly compatible with the rounded 8%. Low pairwise scores also do not imply that Mira has few partners in total.
- **Subcontracting network.** An A–B–A sequence within the specified window is consistent with A involving B and later resuming work. Mira–Omar–Mira and Mira–Priya–Mira fit specialist consultation and follow-up; Temp_1–Vendor_Verify–Temp_1 shows a similar pattern across an external boundary, while Bot_Rules–Mira–Bot_Rules may reflect an automated/manual loop. The pattern alone does not establish delegation, reporting lines, a contract, causation, or that the intermediate activity was requested by A. Its frequency depends on window definition, repeated events, lifecycle records and ordering; normalized betweenness depends on graph construction. Confirm exactly how n=2 is implemented before interpreting the patterns.
- **Performance/time statistics.** Mira's short median event service time and high-variance cycle-time contribution are consistent with brief interventions in heterogeneous, sometimes difficult cases. Her absence coincided with exception queues and an increase in exception-case p90 cycle time from 8.4 to 13.9 days, while the ordinary low-value median was unchanged. This is a useful warning signal, not a controlled causal estimate: arrivals, case mix, staffing, backlog, holidays and censoring may also have changed. Specialist and vendor durations may reflect work, external turnaround, waiting or logging conventions. Elapsed time between two events is not automatically the sender's service time, a queue wait, or delay caused by either resource. A cycle-time contribution needs a reproducible attribution rule; activity times can overlap and should not simply be summed as causal contributions.
- **Role discovery or activity-profile similarity.** Similarity and discovered roles group resources by their observed activity profiles and algorithm settings. Mira's missing-information, reopen, complaint and specialist-assignment mix may distinguish her from adjusters and leads, or place her with other exception handlers if such resources exist. Sharing activities does not prove interchangeable skills, permissions, authority or a formal role; different activity labels can also hide similar work. The scenario's activity list omits “Assign Specialist” even though it appears in Mira's profile and fragments, so its presence and meaning must be checked before drawing conclusions.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses can coexist. The proposed predictions are tests, not conclusions already established by the tables.

**Formal coordinator / exception desk**

- **Mechanism:** An explicitly assigned desk receives exceptional or incomplete claims, requests information and routes them to appropriate specialists or adjusters.
- **Support:** Bot_Rules → Mira is frequent; her activity mix and 48% exception-case share, plus involvement in 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints, fit such a desk. Specialist return patterns fit follow-up.
- **Limits:** We do not have a mandate, assignment records, decision rules or staff roster; the process owner's “queue cleaner” description could also fit a narrower administrative function. Many claims and exceptions may follow other routes.
- **Predictions:** Exception-filtered logs should show reproducible routing or clarification activities before several recipient types, with documentation of the assignment and its reason; other authorized desk staff may show similar patterns when covering the function.

**Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency**

- **Mechanism:** Many difficult cases depend on a scarce routing decision or accumulated knowledge concentrated in Mira; queues grow when she is unavailable or capacity is exceeded.
- **Support:** High handover and subcontracting betweenness, high-variance case-time contribution, and exception queues during her six-day absence are consistent with dependency.
- **Limits:** Centrality is not utilization, queue length or causal delay. The absence is one uncontrolled episode, and specialists, external vendors, changing arrivals or unobserved staffing may drive the tail. Ordinary claims did not show a median change.
- **Predictions:** Decision-stage queue age and backlog should rise when her available capacity falls, after adjustment for arrivals and complexity; an adequately trained and authorized substitute should mitigate the rise. Repeated absences or coverage changes would provide stronger evidence than one episode.

**Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker**

- **Mechanism:** Mira translates an automated exception into a suitable specialist or team pathway and follows up across boundaries, without necessarily managing the people involved.
- **Support:** Bot_Rules → Mira, diverse outgoing adjuster and specialist edges, specialist return loops, and adjuster descriptions all fit brokerage. Low pairwise co-case strength can coexist with a broad recipient portfolio.
- **Limits:** Event adjacency does not demonstrate meaningful translation or decision-making; Bot_Rules may determine the destination, or assignments could be administrative. Network centrality alone cannot distinguish these explanations from the exception-desk hypothesis.
- **Predictions:** Routing destinations and return paths should vary systematically with exception type and required expertise; decision notes or assignment records should show whether Mira changes or confirms the automated recommendation. Cross-boundary paths should persist when bots and vendors are explicitly modeled, though their centralities may change.

**Logging or process-design artifact**

- **Mechanism:** Generic assignment events, a shared account, timestamp ties, lifecycle duplication, or a system that records all exception routing under Mira's identifier inflate her apparent brokerage or make co-case measures incomparable.
- **Support:** Her events are short and include routing labels; “Assign Specialist” is absent from the supplied activity list. Automated resources also create high-volume transitions. No raw event definitions or network settings are provided.
- **Limits:** The observed absence-period queue build-up and staff descriptions are independent clues consistent with a real function, although neither rules out a measurement artifact.
- **Predictions:** After auditing identifiers, activity mappings, timestamps and lifecycle transitions, centrality or pairwise rates may change materially; shared-account use or generated assignment events would be visible in system audit records. If the pattern remains under reasonable alternate constructions, the artifact-only explanation weakens.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck:** Exception queues and the p90 increase during the absence indicate a plausible capacity risk for complex work. Neither the figures nor a high centrality score establish that Mira causes the delays or that she is fully utilized. Specialist and vendor turnaround may constrain the same cases.
- **Coordination:** If routing knowledge is dispersed informally or held by one person, cases may be misdirected, duplicated, or delayed when she is unavailable. Conversely, her intervention may prevent unnecessary escalations or specialist overload. Automated decisions, external verification and payment handoffs also require clear ownership.
- **Key person:** The absence evidence warrants testing continuity coverage, especially for complaints and reopened claims. It does not show that all exception handling stops without Mira or quantify the benefit of a replacement.
- **Compliance and control:** Detecting an issue and selecting a specialist can concentrate discretion. This is a control-design question, not evidence of wrongdoing. Check delegated authority, objective routing criteria, reason codes, audit trails, overrides, conflicts of interest, and independent review for decisions where policy or regulation requires it. Avoid inserting blanket extra approval steps without testing their effect.
- **Workload fairness:** Counts and median service times do not measure total effort or difficulty. Mira may carry disproportionate interruptions and emotional or coordination work; specialists may carry difficult reviews; temps, adjusters and vendors have different mixes and service arrangements. Compare workload after accounting for complexity, availability, hours, rework and off-log work, without treating raw volumes as a league table.
- **Data quality:** Shared or missing resource identifiers, bot-generated events, inconsistent case IDs, ties and time zones, duplicated lifecycle transitions, incomplete vendor timestamps, changed activity names, and the unexplained “Assign Specialist” label can distort all networks and time measures. Verify definitions and denominator consistency, including how exception_flag is populated and whether it changes during a case.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

1. **Validate and prepare the log.** Confirm the 12-week observation window, case inclusion, unique case IDs, resource identities and bot/vendor account conventions. Inspect activity labels, including “Assign Specialist,” timestamp precision, missing values, and the meaning and availability of start and complete transitions. Define a stable ordering for tied timestamps and test sensitivity to plausible alternative orderings. Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with explicit case, activity and timestamp columns, retaining resource, lifecycle and case attributes. Document the PM4Py version and the parameters and actual definitions of each metric; do not assume the displayed centralities can be reproduced by defaults.
2. **Handover.** Use `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on a consistently defined event stream. Inspect edge counts and distinct cases per edge, incoming/outgoing totals and paths through Mira, alongside transition activity pairs. Compare the full graph with human-only and human-plus-vendor views, and with automated steps retained but separately marked. These are alternative views, not evidence that bots or vendors are unimportant. Recompute centrality with documented direction, normalization, edge thresholds and distance interpretation; stronger frequency should not automatically be treated as longer path distance. Compare results across choices and use `pm4py.view_sna` or `pm4py.save_vis_sna` for labeled, readable views.
3. **Working together.** Use `pm4py.discover_working_together_network`, checking its actual normalization against the scenario's co-case percentages. Independently calculate distinct shared cases for each pair divided by the stated eligible-case denominator, plus overlap conditional on Mira's cases and on each partner's cases. Examine unique partners, partner concentration, team communities and co-occurrence within exception cases. Separate event counts from case counts and test whether apparent weak ties reflect the denominator or different case populations. Co-occurrence remains an association, not evidence of direct collaboration.
4. **Subcontracting.** Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` or a transparent sequence query implementing the stated n=2 rule, after verifying the installed version's semantics. Count both occurrences and distinct cases for Mira–specialist–Mira, lead–adjuster–lead, temp–vendor–temp and bot–Mira–bot patterns. Inspect intervening activities, time gaps and ordering, and compare alternative window sizes. Validate sampled cases against assignment and audit records before calling a sequence delegation. Inspect whether activities such as “Approve?” are actual labels or merely descriptions.
5. **Activity profiles and roles.** Apply `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` or `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`; inspect the underlying activity counts and case-normalized profiles, including rare exception activities. Compare Mira with leads, adjusters, temps, bots and vendors while keeping their different operating contexts visible. Test sensitivity to thresholds and time periods, then validate candidate backup skills and permissions with process owners and staff. Similarity is a starting point for training, not authorization to substitute.
6. **Stratify and compare.** Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` with care about event versus case semantics, or equivalent case-level filtering, and `pm4py.filter_variants` to examine exception, complaint, reopen and common routing variants. Compare like-for-like case cohorts by case_type, priority, amount_band, region and channel, checking small samples and whether exception status was known before routing. Restrict to activities or time ranges only with clear awareness that removing events can create artificial adjacent handovers or destroy loops; preferably select whole cases, compute networks on their complete traces, and separately inspect activity-specific sequences. Compare the absence with comparable pre- and post-periods and, if feasible, ordinary claims as a comparison group, adjusting for arrivals, case mix, staff coverage and backlog. Account for right-censored ongoing cases and report cohort sizes, uncertainty and sensitivity; the comparison remains observational.
7. **Service, waiting and queues.** Use `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` only after checking their installed-version definitions, required lifecycle/timestamp inputs and aggregation assumptions; supplement them with explicit timestamp calculations where necessary. With reliable start and complete events, estimate active durations and time from a defined assignment or queue-entry event to work start. Otherwise report inter-event elapsed time or vendor turnaround as such, without labeling it active service or queue wait. Examine distributions, p90, queue age, arrivals, completion rates and backlog by exception category and resource availability; account for overlapping work and incomplete cases. Distinguish internal waiting from Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay turnaround, and assess whether payment and verification logging captures external start and completion consistently.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately:** Preserve the exception-routing function and create documented, authorized coverage for Mira's absence. Identify and train at least one backup, with an escalation path for cases beyond the backup's authority. Record routing reason, destination, time, overrides and decision owner in the existing workflow where feasible. Review access and decision controls with compliance, and audit the “Assign Specialist” label, resource identities and time definitions. Start with a limited coverage exercise and collect queue and outcome measures; maintain existing specialist capacity and vendor interfaces while the exercise is assessed.

**Monitor:** Track exception arrivals, backlog and age, routing and specialist turnaround distributions, p90 cycle time, rework, complaints, overrides, specialist workload and coverage periods, segmented by case mix. Include automated routing and vendor stages. Review both timeliness and decision quality, along with staff workload and false or unnecessary escalations; check for unintended consequences of added controls. Compare trends with suitable baselines rather than judging individuals by network scores.

**Do not change yet:** Do not remove Mira's position or redistribute all her work on the basis of these networks or a single absence period. If a future redesign replaces her role, explicitly provide trained and authorized triage, assignment, follow-up, coverage and audit ownership, and evaluate the effect on specialists and escalations. Do not equate the process owner's or adjusters' descriptions with a verified formal title, or impose a universal extra approval on every case before assessing control requirements and delay. Revisit structural changes after validating the data, observing coverage, and comparing outcomes across similar cases.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to perform a valuable exception-routing function, although the log does not establish her formal authority or prove why difficult cases take longer. She handles a minority of claims but appears disproportionately in reopenings and complaints. Her frequent connections to specialists and adjusters can coexist with low overlap with any single colleague: she may direct different cases to different people, and the measures count different things. Short events alone do not explain low case overlap.

During her absence, exception queues grew and the slowest exception cases took longer, while ordinary low-value claims were stable. This raises a continuity concern, but one absence cannot prove the cause. Provide a trained, authorized backup now and document routing decisions and overrides. Monitor exception queues, case mix, specialist capacity, and vendor turnaround; review controls without assuming misconduct. Validate the event data and test coverage before changing the organizational structure or removing the coordination function.
