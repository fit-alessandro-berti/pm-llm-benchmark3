# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material Assumptions
- The event log contains only activity labels, resource identifiers, and timestamps relative to case start. No case-level attributes (e.g., "risk level", "emergency flag") are assumed present unless explicitly modeled.
- All cases follow the visible activity labels exactly as given; no hidden activities exist.
- The procedural backbone defines the control-flow structure; cross-cutting requirements (R1–R12) are partitioned across formalisms as designed.
- `zeta = 2.0` is applied for temporal-profile deviations, meaning a deviation is flagged if the elapsed time falls outside `mean ± 2 * standard_deviation`.

## R5/R6 Conflict: Two Resolutions
- **R5 (Legal department):** `Sanctions Escalation`  `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances.
- **R6 (Operations department):** Emergency activation may occur before `Obtain Legal Opinion` if jointly authorized outside the event log; the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours. The event log contains no "emergency" attribute or joint-authorization field.

**Resolution A (Strict R5):** Any `Activate Vendor` occurring before `Obtain Legal Opinion` is an automatic violation of R5, irrespective of timing or R6's emergency clause. Since the log lacks the required authorization attribute, such traces are treated as `policy-dependent` or `fail` requiring manual review. This resolution prioritizes the legal constraint but risks blocking legitimate emergency activations that cannot be verified from the log alone.

**Resolution B (24hour temporal guard):** `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` is permitted *iff* `Obtain Legal Opinion` follows within 24 hours (86,400 seconds) of `Activate Vendor`. The timestamps in the log allow automated checking of this window. If the opinion arrives after 24 hours, it is a violation. If it arrives within 24 hours, the trace passes, but the invisible joint authorization is assumed absent, introducing a compliance risk: nonemergency activations could exploit the 24hour window, or genuine emergencies with delayed opinions ( >24h) would be missed.

## Chosen Resolution for Implemented Model
**Resolution B (24hour temporal guard)** is selected for the hybrid model. This choice leverages the available timestamp data to provide an automated check rather than silently defaulting to a hard fail that cannot be nuanced by the log's granularity.

**Compliance risk of the choice:** By allowing activations where the legal opinion arrives within 24 hours, the model may pass cases that are not genuinely emergencyauthorized (since the attribute is missing). Conversely, if a real emergency occurs and the opinion takes >24 hours, the model would flag a violation under Resolution B, whereas Resolution A would have also flagged it. The primary risk is the potential relaxation of the "under all circumstances" intent of R5, requiring users to manually verify the absence of emergency authorization when a trace passes under this resolution.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 1. Compact Formal Description
The procedural model is a **ProcessTree** that captures the controlflow backbone:
- **Start:** `Receive Onboarding Request` triggers every case.
- **Concurrent checks:** After receipt, `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute in parallel (ANDsplit).
- **Synchronization:** `Classify Risk` fires only after all three concurrent checks complete.
- **XOR outcome split** after classification:
  - **Reject path:** `Reject Vendor`  `Close Onboarding`.
  - **Lowrisk path:** `Manager Approval`  `Activate Vendor`  `Release First Payment`  `Close Onboarding`.
  - **Highrisk path:** `Enhanced Due Diligence`  `Compliance Approval`  `Activate Vendor`  `Release First Payment`  `Close Onboarding`.
- **Clarification loops:** `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` cycles may occur before `Classify Risk` but must resolve before any terminal outcome (activation/rejection/closure). The tree models this as a loop that can repeat but must exit before the XOR outcomes.
- **Postactivation behavior:** After activation, `Release First Payment` always follows; `Close Onboarding` follows either activation (first payment) or `Reject Vendor`. `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and subsequent `Verify Bank Account` are modeled as crosscutting additions that do not alter the primary XOR split but are checked via the resource/data layer.

**Approximation disclosure:** The ProcessTree does not hardwire crosscutting constraints (R4–R6, R8–R12) into the XOR branches; these are enforced by the separate formalisms (DECLARE, temporal profile, resource/data layer). The tree models the "happy path" control flow, and policy violations are detected by the layered checks.

## 2. PM4Py Python Code
```python
# Section 3: Procedural Backbone - PM4Py Code
from pm4py.objects.process_tree import ProcessTreeNode, Operators

# --- Create activity nodes ---
receive = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Receive Onboarding Request")
capture = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Capture Master Data")
screen  = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Run Sanctions Screening")
verify  = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Verify Bank Account")
classify= ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Classify Risk")
reject  = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Reject Vendor")
activate= ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Activate Vendor")
pay     = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Release First Payment")
close   = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Close Onboarding")
manager = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Manager Approval")
compliance = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Compliance Approval")
enhanced= ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Enhanced Due Diligence")
req_clar= ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Request Clarification")
recv_clar= ProcessTreeNode(Operators.ACTIVITY, "Receive Clarification")

# --- Concurrency after receipt: Capture, Screen, Verify in parallel ---
and_checks = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [capture, screen, verify])

# --- Postclassification branches ---
# Lowrisk: Manager Approval -> Activate Vendor
low_branch_and = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [manager, activate])

# Highrisk: Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval -> Activate Vendor
high_and1 = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [enhanced, compliance])
high_branch_and = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [high_and1, activate])

# --- Postactivation: Release First Payment -> Close Onboarding ---
# Reject branch: Reject Vendor -> Close Onboarding
reject_and = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [reject, close])

# Low branch with payment and close
low_full = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [low_branch_and, pay, close])

# High branch with payment and close
high_full = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.AND, [high_branch_and, pay, close])

# --- XOR outcomes: Reject OR Lowrisk OR Highrisk ---
xor_outcomes = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.XOR, [reject_and, low_full, high_full])

# --- Full process tree: Sequential flow ---
# Receive Onboarding Request -> Concurrent checks -> Classify Risk -> XOR outcomes
sequential_model = ProcessTreeNode(Operators.SEQUENCE, 
                                    [receive, and_checks, classify, xor_outcomes])

# Assign to procedural_model as required
procedural_model = sequential_model
```

## 3. Explanation of Model Behaviors
- **Concurrency:** The ANDsplit (`and_checks`) immediately after `Receive Onboarding Request` models the backbone rule that the three initial checks may proceed concurrently. In a Petri net or POWL this would be parallel places/edges; in the ProcessTree, the AND operator forces all three to be enabled simultaneously after the root.
- **Choices:** The XOR (`xor_outcomes`) after `Classify Risk` models the primary outcome split (reject / lowrisk / highrisk). This is a deterministic choice based on risk classification; the tree does not model probabilistic branches.
- **Clarification loops:** The ProcessTree as written does not embed the clarification loop directly into the main spine; instead, the loop is handled by the DECLARE `response` template (R3) and the resource/data layer. The procedural model assumes that `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` can occur before `Classify Risk` but must be resolved before the XOR outcomes are taken. If a trace contains `Request Clarification` without a preceding `Receive Clarification` before the outcome, it is flagged by the procedural/DECLARE layers (see Test Case T5).
- **Terminal outcomes:** The three XOR branches terminate in `Close Onboarding`. The "Reject" branch ends immediately with `Reject Vendor`  `Close Onboarding`. The accepted branches include `Release First Payment` before `Close Onboarding`, reflecting the backbone rule that release follows activation.
- **Postactivation behavior:** The model includes `Release First Payment` and `Close Onboarding` within each accepted branch. Crosscutting postactivation rules (R11: `Change Bank Details`  `Suspend Vendor`  later `Verify Bank Account`) are not wired into the tree structure; they are enforced by the separate resource/dataaware validation layer (Section 7). The tree deliberately excludes them from the controlflow backbone to avoid overconstraining the procedural model, acknowledging that such behavior depends on data states not captured in activity sequences alone.

```python
# Section 4: DECLARE Model
declare_model = {
```

### Table: Direction and Semantics of Every Encoded Rule

| Rule (Key) | Template | Direction/Semantics | Related Requirements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")` | `noncoexistence` | **Noncoexistence**: These two activities must never appear in the same trace. If one occurs, the other cannot. | R1 |
| `("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")` | `noncoexistence` | **Noncoexistence**: Manager Approval and Compliance Approval cannot occur together in a single case. | R2 (part), R8 (part) |
| `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | `response` | **Response**: Whenever `Request Clarification` occurs in a trace, `Receive Clarification` must occur at some later point in the same trace. If `Request Clarification` never occurs, the rule is vacuously satisfied. | R3 |
| `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` | `precedence` | **Precedence**: `Sanctions Escalation` must eventually be followed by `Obtain Legal Opinion` somewhere later in the trace. If `Sanctions Escalation` never occurs, the rule is vacuously satisfied. | R4 |
| `("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` | `precedence` | **Precedence**: `Obtain Legal Opinion` must eventually precede `Activate Vendor`. This is the formal expression of R5; its enforcement is mediated by the R5/R6 conflict resolution (Resolution B in this model). | R5 |
| `("Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` | `precedence` | **Precedence**: `Sanctions Escalation` can only occur after `Run Sanctions Screening`. If `Sanctions Screening` never occurs, `Sanctions Escalation` may or may not occur (the rule is vacuously satisfied when Escalation is absent). | R7 |

**Distinctions made:**
- **Response vs. Precedence:** `response` (R3) ensures a paired activity (`Receive Clarification`) follows a specific trigger (`Request Clarification`) later in the trace; it is a paired obligation. `precedence` (R4, R5, R7) ensures one activity eventually occurs before another, but does not require a onetoone pair—it is a general order constraint.
- **Noncoexistence vs. Nonsuccession:** `noncoexistence` (R1, R2/R8) means the two activities never appear in the same trace, regardless of order. `nonsuccession` (not explicitly encoded here but distinguished in the template list) would mean activity A cannot be immediately followed by activity B; the chosen rules use noncoexistence because the stakeholder constraints are about mutual exclusion in a case, not immediate succession.
- **Alternate response / chain response:** Not used in this encoding; the `response` template suffices for the singlepair request/response cycle (R3). If multiple clarification cycles were needed, `altresponse` or `chainresponse` could be considered, but the stakeholder statement "Several request/response cycles are possible" is adequately captured by the procedural/DECLARE combination with the note that each cycle must resolve before the outcome.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
# Section 5: Log-Skeleton Model
log_skeleton = {
    # Equivalence: no pairs defended as "always appearing together"
    "equivalence": set(),
    # Alwaysbefore: structurally forced forward order based on backbone
    "always_before": {
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Verify Bank Account"),
        ("Capture Master Data", "Classify Risk"),   # implied after concurrency completes
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk"),
    },
    # Alwaysafter: dual of alwaysbefore (left empty; orientation covered by always_before)
    "always_after": set(),
    # Nevertogether: mutual exclusions defended across layers
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"),
    },
    # Activ_freq: occurrencecount constraints (R10: at most once)
    "activ_freq": {
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},   # at most once (R10)
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},     # at most once (R10)
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1}, # at most once (R10)
    },
    # Directly_follows: no specific immediatelyfollow pairs defended as mandatory
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

### Explanation of Overlap and Disagreement with Other Layers
- **`equivalence`**: Left empty set. No activities are defended as "always appearing together" in every case (e.g., `Receive Onboarding Request` always starts, but `Classify Risk` does not always follow if a case is rejected early—though the backbone says the three checks complete first; however, equivalence in log skeleton typically pairs two activities that cooccur in every trace, which is not the case here).
- **`always_before`**: Contains defensible pairs where the backbone guarantees temporal order. For instance, `Receive Onboarding Request` must precede the three concurrent checks; the checks must complete before `Classify Risk`. These are "always before" in the sense that in any valid trace conforming to the procedural backbone, the timestamp of the first activity will be less than the second. Note that concurrency means `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` can overlap, so `always_before` between them and `Classify Risk` reflects that *all* must finish before classification starts, not that each individually starts after receipt (they start after receipt, but classification waits for all).
- **`never_together`**: Mirrors DECLARE `noncoexistence` rules (R1, R2/R8). The log skeleton enforces these at the tracelevel during conformance checking; DECLARE does so via rule mining/templates. Agreement is intentional—both layers reinforce the same prohibitions.
- **`activ_freq`**: Maps to R10 ("at most once"). The set `{0, 1}` exactly captures "at most once" (the activity may occur zero or one time). If set to `{1}`, it would enforce "exactly once", which would reject rejected cases where `Activate Vendor` does not occur—a mismatch with R10's phrasing, hence `{0, 1}` is chosen for the log skeleton. DECLARE's `activ_freq` in Section 2 mapped R10 to this requirement but noted the exactonce vs. atmostonce nuance; the log skeleton here provides the precise "at most once" guard.
- **Disagreement/Redundancy**: The `always_before` set introduces constraints (e.g., `Capture Master Data`  `Classify Risk`) that are also implicitly covered by the ProcessTree's sequential flow. The log skeleton makes these explicit for tracelevel checking, while the procedural model encodes them structurally. This is not a conflict but a layered redundancy that strengthens conformance detection.

```python
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),  # 48 hours mean, 24 hours SD
    # R12: Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion (normative, flag but do not reject)
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),    # 12 hours mean, 6 hours SD

}
```

### Why Temporal Deviations Are Warnings, Not Automatic ControlFlow Violations
The R12 table explicitly states that the temporal expectations are "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject a case." In the design, the temporal profile operates as a **soft conformance check**:
- A deviation (elapsed time outside `mean ± zeta * SD`, where `zeta = 2.0`) triggers a `warning` verdict in the temporal layer.
- The controlflow layers (procedural, DECLARE, Log Skeleton) remain the primary gates for pass/fail. A trace can have perfect controlflow compliance but still receive a temporal warning if, for example, `Sanctions Escalation` to `Obtain Legal Opinion` takes 20 hours (outside the 12h ± 12h zeta=2 window of [0, 24h]).
- This design respects the stakeholder intent: these are "normative" expectations guiding monitoring and process improvement, not hard legal or structural prohibitions. If a case violates a temporal window but follows all controlflow and resource constraints, it is surfaced as a warning for process owners to review, rather than being blocked automatically.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

```python
# Section 7: Resource- and Data-Aware Checks
# Provided as executablestyle Python/pseudocode using PM4Py patterns.
# Required event attributes: 
#   - case: concept:name (or case identifier)
#   - activity: string matching the visible labels
#   - resource: string identifier of the resource performing the activity
#   - time: timestamp (datetime or numeric hour) of the event
# 
# What to do when an attribute is missing:
#   - If 'resource' is missing: the check is conservative; assume potential violation and flag for manual review, or skip the check with a "data missing" note.
#   - If 'time' is missing: the temporal profile check cannot run; the trace is marked 'not evaluated' for the temporal layer.
#   - If 'case' identifier is missing: the check cannot be scoped per case; treat the entire log as a single case or flag as invalid.

# --- Check R9: Resource segregation ---
def check_R9(log):
    """
    R9: 
    - The resource performing Verify Bank Account must differ from the resource performing the applicable approval.
    - The resource performing Capture Master Data must differ from the resource performing Activate Vendor.
    Applicable approval = Manager Approval (lowrisk) or Compliance Approval (highrisk), determined by the path taken.
    """
    violations = []
    # Iterate per case
    for case_id in pm4py.get_case_ids(log):
        case_log = pm4py.filter_case_id(log, case_id)
        # Get resources for Verify Bank Account
        verify_events = pm4py.filter_activity(case_log, "Verify Bank Account")
        if not verify_events:
            continue  # No verification in this case; R9 check for this part is vacuous
        res_verify = verify_events[0]["resource"]  # Assuming single event; adapt for multiple
        
        # Determine applicable approval based on risk path
        # Simplified: check if Manager Approval or Compliance Approval occurred
        manager_events = pm4py.filter_activity(case_log, "Manager Approval")
        compliance_events = pm4py.filter_activity(case_log, "Compliance Approval")
        
        applicable_res = None
        if manager_events:
            applicable_res = manager_events[0]["resource"]
        elif compliance_events:
            applicable_res = compliance_events[0]["resource"]
        
        # If an applicable approval exists, check resource difference
        if applicable_res is not None:
            if res_verify == applicable_res:
                violations.append(
                    f"R9 violation in case {case_id}: Verify Bank Account resource ({res_verify}) "
                    f"same as applicable approval resource ({applicable_res})"
                )
        
        # Second part: Capture Master Data resource vs. Activate Vendor resource
        capture_events = pm4py.filter_activity(case_log, "Capture Master Data")
        activate_events = pm4py.filter_activity(case_log, "Activate Vendor")
        
        if capture_events and activate_events:
            res_capture = capture_events[0]["resource"]
            res_activate = activate_events[0]["resource"]
            if res_capture == res_activate:
                violations.append(
                    f"R9 violation in case {case_id}: Capture Master Data resource ({res_capture}) "
                    f"same as Activate Vendor resource ({res_activate})"
                )
    
    return violations

# --- Check R6: Emergency-policy condition ---
def check_R6(log, resolution="B"):
    """
    R6: 
    - Emergency activation may occur before Obtain Legal Opinion, but only when jointly authorized outside the event log.
    - The event log does not contain an "emergency" attribute or the joint authorization.
    - Under resolution "B" (chosen model): if Activate Vendor precedes Obtain Legal Opinion, 
      Obtain Legal Opinion must arrive within 24 hours (86400 seconds) of Activate Vendor.
    - Under resolution "A" (strict): any Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion is a violation.
    """
    violations = []
    for case_id in pm4py.get_case_ids(log):
        case_log = pm4py.filter_case_id(log, case_id)
        events = pm4py.get_event_attributes(case_log, ["activity", "time", "resource"])
        
        # Extract positions/timestamps for relevant activities
        act_activate = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Activate Vendor"]
        act_opinion  = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"]
        act_screen   = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Run Sanctions Screening"]
        
        if not act_activate or not act_opinion:
            continue  # If either activity missing, no R6 violation possible for this pair
        
        activate_idx = act_activate[0]  # Assuming first occurrence; adapt for multiple
        opinion_idx  = act_opinion[0]
        
        activate_time = activate_idx["time"]
        opinion_time  = act_opinion_idx["time"]
        
        if activate_time < opinion_time:
            # Activate Vendor occurs before Obtain Legal Opinion
            if resolution == "A":
                violations.append(
                    f"R6 strict violation in case {case_id}: Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion "
                    f"(R5 hard constraint breached, no emergency attribute to invoke R6 exception)"
                )
            else:  # resolution == "B" (chosen)
                diff_seconds = (opinion_time - activate_time).total_seconds()
                if diff_seconds > 86400:  # 24 hours
                    violations.append(
                        f"R6 lenient warning in case {case_id}: Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion, "
                        f"but opinion takes {diff_seconds/3600:.1f}h > 24h window"
                    )
                # else: within 24h, passes per Resolution B; note that joint authorization is unverifiable from log
    return violations

# --- Check R11: Postactivation Change Bank Details ---
def check_R11(log):
    """
    R11: 
    - If Change Bank Details occurs after activation, Suspend Vendor must eventually follow it.
    - Another Verify Bank Account must occur before any later Release First Payment.
    """
    violations = []
    for case_id in pm4py.get_case_ids(log):
        case_log = pm4py.filter_case_id(log, case_id)
        events = pm4py.get_event_attributes(case_log, ["activity", "time", "resource"])
        
        # Find activation position
        act_activate = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Activate Vendor"]
        if not act_activate:
            continue
        act_activate_time = act_activate[0]["time"]
        
        # Find Change Bank Details after activation
        change_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Change Bank Details" and e["time"] > act_activate_time]
        if not change_events:
            continue  # No change details after activation; R11 not triggered
        
        # For each Change Bank Details event, check subsequent Suspend Vendor
        for ce in change_events:
            # Look for Suspend Vendor after this Change Bank Details
            suspend_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Suspend Vendor" and e["time"] > ce["time"]]
            if not suspend_events:
                violations.append(
                    f"R11 violation in case {case_id}: Change Bank Details at {ce['time']} occurred, "
                    f"but Suspend Vendor never followed"
                )
                # No need to check further for this case if one violation found; break to next case
                break
            
            # Check that a later Verify Bank Account occurs before any subsequent Release First Payment
            # Get subsequent Release First Payment events after the Suspend Vendor
            suspend_time = suspend_events[0]["time"]
            # Find Release First Payment after suspend
            later_payments = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Release First Payment" and e["time"] > suspend_time]
            if later_payments:
                # There must be a Verify Bank Account between suspend and this later payment
                # Simplified: check if any Verify Bank Account exists between suspend_time and the first later payment
                vba_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Verify Bank Account" 
                              and e["time"] > suspend_time and e["time"] < later_payments[0]["time"]]
                if not vba_events:
                    violations.append(
                        f"R11 violation in case {case_id}: After Suspend Vendor at {suspend_time}, "
                        f"Release First Payment at {later_payments[0]['time']} occurs without a preceding Verify Bank Account"
                    )
    return violations
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

## Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers
- **R1 (Mutual exclusion of Reject/Activate)** is enforced in three layers: DECLARE (`noncoexistence`), Log Skeleton (`never_together`), and Procedural (XOR outcome split). This triple enforcement is intentional for robustness: the procedural model prevents the controlflow from even reaching both activities in the same branch; DECLARE and Log Skeleton provide tracelevel checks that catch violations if the procedural model is approximated or if a trace slips through (e.g., due to data corruption). The redundancy is acceptable because each layer uses different formalism primitives, reducing the chance that a single bug in one formalism goes undetected.
- **R8 (Manager/Compliance noncoexistence)** similarly appears in DECLARE (`noncoexistence`) and procedural resource checks, though the procedural layer’s resource checks are delegated to the separate resource/data layer (Section 7). The DECLARE rule remains as an activityonly guard.

## Constraints Inconsistent Under Loops or PostActivation Changes
- **R3 (Clarification loops)** interacts with the procedural XOR outcomes. If a trace has multiple `Request Clarification`/`Receive Clarification` cycles (as allowed by R3), the procedural model must ensure the loop resolves before the XOR is taken. In T2, one cycle is completed; in T5, a cycle is incomplete. The DECLARE `response` template assumes a single pair; multiple cycles can make the rule vacuously true or cause ambiguity if not all `Request Clarification` events have corresponding `Receive Clarification` events. The model handles this by requiring the procedural layer to track the *last* unresolved request before the outcome, a nuance not fully captured by a single `response` template.
- **R11 (Change Bank Details  Suspend  Verify)** introduces post-activation state changes. The procedural backbone includes `Release First Payment` and `Close Onboarding` as terminal, but R11 allows `Change Bank Details` after activation, followed by `Suspend Vendor`, then a later `Verify Bank Account` and `Release First Payment`. This creates a "secondary" controlflow path that the main ProcessTree does not model. The log skeleton’s `activ_freq` and `never_together` do not capture this sequence, leading to a blind spot where a trace could pass all primary layers but violate R11—a gap filled only by the resource/dataaware check in Section 7.

## Vacuity in Declarative Rules
- **R2 "exactly one in accepted / neither in rejected"** mapped to DECLARE `noncoexistence(Manager, Compliance)`. The `noncoexistence` template only prohibits cooccurrence; it does not enforce that exactly one occurs in accepted cases (they could both be absent, which would violate the stakeholder intent but pass DECLARE). The procedural XOR split compensates by ensuring one branch is taken, but if a trace ends in acceptance without either approval (e.g., abandoned before classification), DECLARE would pass while the stakeholder requirement would fail. This vacuity is acknowledged in the mapping table (Section 2) and resolved by the procedural layer’s branch structure.
- **R9 resource checks** are vacuous when the relevant activities do not occur (e.g., T3 rejected case has no approval). The resource/data check explicitly skips if the applicable approval is absent, which is a necessary approximation since DECLARE/Petri nets cannot express "ifthen" resource conditions without case attributes.

## Missing Case Attributes
- The **R5/R6 conflict** itself hinges on the missing "emergency" attribute. The chosen Resolution B (24h window) works because the log has timestamps, but the joint authorization is forever invisible in the event log. Any model relying on DECLARE or Petri nets alone cannot distinguish emergency from nonemergency activations; the conflict must be resolved externally (as done here).
- **R12 temporal expectations** require mean and standard deviation, which are normative metadata not derivable from activity sequences alone. The temporal profile dictionary in Section 6 is external input; the conformance check (`pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile`) applies it, but if the metadata is missing or incorrect, the check cannot run (cell value `not evaluated` in the matrix).
- **R6’s "jointly authorized outside the event log"** has no representation in any of the four primary formalisms (process tree, DECLARE, log skeleton, temporal profile). This is deliberately placed in the separate resource/dataaware validation layer (Section 7), with the design note that if the attribute is missing, a conservative approximation (e.g., flag as warning or policydependent) is used.

## TimestampOnly Limitations
- All formalisms here operate on **timestamps and activity labels**. Datalevel constraints (e.g., "the resource performing Verify Bank Account must differ from the resource performing approval") require the `resource` attribute. If the log omits resources, the resource/data layer (Section 7) must either skip the check or approximate it (e.g., assume all events are performed by the same role, which would flag many false violations). The quality criteria explicitly acknowledge this: "resource and data conditions cannot be recovered from activity sequences alone."
- **Temporal deviations** (R12) use mean and standard deviation in seconds. If events lack precise timestamps (e.g., only daily granularity), the temporal profile check would fail or be meaningless, again defaulting to `not evaluated`.

## Risk of Treating Support/Confidence Metadata as Normative Truth
- The DECLARE model (Section 4) assigns `support = 1.0` and `confidence = 1.0` to every rule. In a mined DECLARE model, these statistics reflect how often the rule holds in the log. Here, because we are *designing* the model *a priori* based on stakeholder requirements, setting support/confidence to 1.0 asserts that the rule is **normatively true for all cases in this design**—it is a design choice, not a mined observation.
- **Risk**: If a trace violates a rule with `support = 1.0`, the conformance check (`pm4py.conformance_declare`) will flag it as a violation. However, because the support is set to 1.0 based on the modeler's assumptions (e.g., "R1 always holds"), a real-world log might contain cases where R1 is intentionally violated (e.g., a fallback reject path). Treating the metadata as truth would then incorrectly mark those cases as failures. The quality criteria warn against this: support/confidence metadata should not be mistaken for absolute normative truth without explicit justification. In this task, the 1.0 values are defended by the explicit policy interpretations in Section 1 and the layered redundancy discussion, but in a deployment scenario, they would need to be calibrated against actual log data.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

## Canonical Procedural Representation
The **ProcessTree** (as constructed in Section 3) is the canonical representation for the controlflow backbone. It compactly captures the concurrency at start, the XOR riskclassification split, and the terminal outcomes (rejection/activation with payment/closure). The tree is "block-structured" enough to be expressed in PM4Py’s native API, with the explicit approximation noted: crosscutting policies (R4–R12) are not hardwired into the tree but are enforced by the layered formalisms. If a POWL representation were preferred, every precedence edge would need to be added explicitly (e.g., `Receive Onboarding Request`  `Capture Master Data`, `Capture Master Data`  `Run Sanctions Screening`, etc., plus all XOR choice edges), which would be verbose and less readable than the ProcessTree’s hierarchical XOR/AND structure.

## Authoritative Policy Layer
The **resource/dataaware validation layer** (Section 7) is the authoritative layer for R6 (emergency policy), R9 (segregation of duties), and R11 (postactivation changes). For requirements that *can* be expressed in activityonly formalisms, the **DECLARE model** (Section 4) is authoritative, with the Procedural backbone (Section 3) serving as the controlflow guard. The **temporal profile** (Section 6) is authoritative for R12 normative expectations but yields only warnings, not controlflow failures. The **Log Skeleton** (Section 5) provides tracelevel sanity checks (e.g., nevertogether pairs) but is secondary to DECLARE and procedural checks.

## Order of Conformance Checks
Conformance should be checked in the following order, from most structural to most granular:
1. **Procedural layer** (`pm4py.conformance_processtree` or equivalent): Checks if the trace follows the ProcessTree backbone (concurrency, XOR choices, terminal outcomes). If a trace fails here, deeper checks are moot because the controlflow is fundamentally broken.
2. **DECLARE layer** (`pm4py.conformance_declare`): Checks the encoded rules (R1–R5, R7, R8). This catches mutual exclusions, precedence, and response obligations that the procedural tree might approximate or miss due to its abstraction level.
3. **Log Skeleton layer** (`pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`): Checks the `never_together`, `activ_freq`, and `always_before` sets. This is a quick tracelevel filter; violations here often overlap with DECLARE but provide an independent perspective.
4. **Temporal profile layer** (`pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` with `zeta=2.0`): Checks the three elapsedtime expectations. This runs last because it is "normative flagging" rather than pass/fail; a trace that passes 1–3 may still receive temporal warnings, which are logged for processimprovement but do not block the conformance verdict.
5. **Resource/dataaware validation layer** (custom Python/pseudocode from Section 7): Performs the R6, R9, R11 checks. This is the final arbiter for constraints requiring resource identities or external attributes. It may override earlier layers (e.g., a trace passing procedural/DECLARE but failing R9 due to resource conflict).

## Surfacing Contradictory Findings
When a trace yields contradictory findings across layers (e.g., passes procedural but fails DECLARE, or passes all activityonly layers but fails the resource/data layer):
- **Prioritize controlflow integrity**: If the procedural layer fails, the case is fundamentally noncompliant regardless of other layers’ results. Report the procedural violation as the primary finding.
- **If procedural passes but DECLARE fails**: Evaluate the nature of the DECLARE violation. If it is a mutual exclusion (R1, R8) or a strict precedence (R4, R7), flag it as a **hard policy violation** and surface it alongside any procedural notes. If it is a vacuity or a rule dependent on the R5/R6 conflict resolution (as in T6), mark the finding as `policy-dependent` and display both resolution outcomes (Resolution A: fail; Resolution B: pass/warning) as recommended in the T6 verdict explanation.
- **If all activity layers pass but the resource/data layer fails**: This indicates a **dataquality or segregationofduties issue** that the controlflow model cannot see. Surface the specific resource conflict (e.g., "Cora performed both Verify Bank Account and Manager Approval") and recommend a dataaudit or processredesign to separate duties. The conformance verdict should be `fail` with the explanation citing the resource/data layer as the decisive violation.
- **Temporal warnings** should always be appended to the verdict explanation, never as the sole reason for failure, per the R12 design. If a trace passes 1–5 but has temporal warnings, the overall verdict is `warning` (as in T7), with the explanation detailing which time windows were exceeded and recommending processtiming reviews.

This layered, ordered approach ensures that the most critical controlflow and policy constraints are checked first, that resource/data conditions are not hidden by activityonly formalisms, and that users receive clear, actionable feedback on where and why a case deviates from the normative onboarding process.
