## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is **intentionally incomplete**. Classification therefore depends on explicit normative status, not on presence/absence in a normative graph.

- **Forbidden behavior:** A relation explicitly marked `forbidden` (closed-world). Any single observation is a violation if scope applies and data quality is confirmed. Frequency does not create tolerance. N3, T6, E10. Severity is driven by business risk, not count. Requires containment even at n=1.

- **Missing mandatory behavior:** A relation marked `mandatory` expected whenever its **stated scope** applies. A gap between expected and observed counts indicates potential noncompliance, but only after checking (a) scope definition (b) abstraction level (c) alternative evidence. N1, N4, T1, T2, E1-E8. Closed-world only for the mandatory columns *within scope*. Missing mandatory  proven violation until scope and abstraction are validated.

- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** Observed relation not listed in norm where status is `unspecified` or open-world. By design it is **conforming** unless another forbidden/mandatory rule is triggered. Not a violation due to rarity alone. E.g., N6, T4, T5, E9, codeath, Validate->Issue Invoice. Requires no remediation unless it masks a mandatory gap.

- **Typical-pattern absence:** Observed absence of a `typical` pattern. Desirable and common, but norm explicitly allows legitimate exceptions. Gap in count (N2, N5/T3) is an *expected variance* that only becomes suspicious with unexplained magnitude, bias, or downstream impact. Not a violation without case-level evidence.

- **Likely data-quality error:** Pattern more plausibly explained by extraction, join, identifier reuse, or logging error than by business execution. Indicators: reused numeric IDs, attribute-level alternative (carrier reference), inconsistency between related counts (Add Item Order vs Item). Requires data repair before business judgment. E.g., E10 Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice explicitly flagged as suspected join error.

- **Abstraction mismatch:** Diagnostic artifact from how a summary is built. The critical case is treating an **eventually-follows** business rule as a **directly-follows** edge. The OC-DFG summary collapses intermediate events, timing, and parallelism into adjacent pairs. Absence in directly-follows does not prove absence eventually. Applies especially to N1 where the norm states *eventually* but the summary approximates with directly-follows, and to intermediate events between Validate->Close.

- **Insufficient evidence:** Graph summaries (counts/weights) alone cannot decide. Needed: object-level traces, event attributes, scope flags (e.g., which Packages are externally shipped, which Orders are standard), labeling of tolerances, or process context. Without it we can only rank and propose verification. The norm explicitly has no minimum frequencies or statistical tolerance bands.

**Open-world vs closed-world effect:** The norm is closed-world **only** for (i) relations explicitly `forbidden` and (ii) the mandatory columns *within their stated scope*. For these, absence of permission = prohibition, and absence of expected behavior = gap. For everything else the norm is open-world: absence from the normative edge set implies **no judgment**. Rarity, novelty, or incompleteness of model cannot be read as violation. The three model families inherit this: an OC-DFG edge absent from norm, an OTG edge with unspecified status, or an ET-OT link with unspecified status is benign by default. PM4Py diagnostics that flag “unexpected edge” must be filtered through this outside-graph metadata.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**What it can establish:** Object-type-scoped control-flow precedence as directly-follows counts. Can detect forbidden sequencing (N3 Cancel->Dispatch), mandatory sequencing gaps within scope (N1, N4), and frequency of typical paths (N2, N5). Counts are per object type, not global, so denominators matter: 98/100 vs 198/200 have different statistical weight and scope conditions.

**What it cannot establish:** It cannot prove **eventually-follows** from directly-follows alone, cannot distinguish missing event vs alternative path vs logging delay, and cannot validate scope flags (standard Order vs externally shipped Package). It also cannot capture multiplexing where one Order interleaves with multiple object lifecycles. 110 Invoices with mixed successors (92 paid, 4 written off, 14 open) need time windows; OC-DFG snapshot without cutoff cannot judge typical absence.

**Counts and scopes:** N1 is mandatory but scoped to *standard* Orders and defined as eventually. Observing 98/100 directly-follows leaves 2 that may still be compliant via intermediate review. Without knowing which Orders are standard and whether Validate occurs later, the gap is insufficient evidence. N4 is mandatory but scoped to *externally shipped* Packages; the observation helpfully states denominator is exactly 200 externally shipped, making the 2 missing more concerning than a random 1% gap, but still needs package-intent flag verification. N3 is forbidden with no scope qualifier: 2 observations = 2 violations regardless of denominator if linkage is correct. N2 and N5 are typical: Validate->Close 83 and Issue->Record 92/110 show typical behavior is present; absence does not imply breach.

**Abstraction danger:** The norm says *every newly created standard Order should eventually enter validation* but *the summary approximates this with a directly-follows expectation*. This is the textbook eventually-as-directly trap. A review, manual compliance check, or data completion event between Create and Validate breaks the direct edge while preserving compliance. Additional context explicitly warns the 2 missing Creates may have an unrecorded review between them. Converse also risky: a direct edge could be spurious if multiple Orders share events. Trace-level **eventually** query (e.g., `Create Order` precedes `Validate Order` for same Order object) is required; OC-DFG counts alone cannot convict or acquit N1.

### OTG

**What it can establish:** Structural coexistence of object types via `object_interaction` and lifecycle coupling via `object_cobirth/_codeath`. Can validate mandatory interactions for business events (T1 Add Item needs Order-Item, T2 Dispatch needs Package-Delivery) and forbidden co-creation (T6 Order-Payment). Weights show interaction strength: 1450 Order-Item interactions align with 1450 Add Item Item links; 200 Package-Delivery interactions vs 7 codeaths.

**What it cannot establish:** OTG is graph-level co-occurrence, not event-level validation. A weight of 200 for Package-Delivery interaction does not guarantee every Dispatch event had a Delivery; that requires ET-OT. Conversely OTG can be conforming while ET-OT is not (2 Dispatch events lack Delivery at event level but OTG still shows 200 interactions from other events). OTG also cannot judge typicality magnitude: T3 Invoice-Payment 92 is consistent with 92 Record Payments linked to Invoices, but the 1 unapplied payment's absence from OTG is expected for a typical relation. Silence on codeath (7 Package-Delivery codeaths from shared confirmation) is open-world: norm says nothing, therefore benign. Unspecified edges (T4 Customer, T5 RiskCase) are explicitly allowed; their weights (100, 3) inform business context but not conformance.

**Cross-abstraction tension:** OTG T2 and ET-OT E6 both concern Dispatch-Delivery but at different abstractions. OTG can be 200/200 conforming while ET-OT shows 2 gaps because two Dispatches use a carrier attribute instead of a Delivery object. This is a representation mismatch, not necessarily a business violation. Similarly OTG T1 is mandatory but validated event-wise via ET-OT: 2 Add Item events missing Order link will also depress the *event-level* fulfillment of T1 despite high OTG weight.

### ET-OT

**What it can establish:** Per-event-type object linkage completeness – the finest-grained check for mandatory participation. E1 100/100 Create Order-Order, E3 1450/1450 Add Item-Item, E4/E5 200/200 Pack/Dispatch-Package are fully compliant. Gaps at E2 1448/1450, E6 198/200, E8 92/93 pinpoint which events are incomplete.

**What it cannot establish:** Without attribute semantics, it cannot decide if an alternative attribute satisfies the requirement (carrier reference vs Delivery object) or if missing link is labeled exception (unapplied cash). It also cannot alone judge forbidden: Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice 1 (E10) is forbidden, but ET-OT discovery can create false edges via bad joins. Need lineage. Finally, counts without instance filters can mislead: E8 is mandatory *except* events explicitly typed as unapplied cash; since none are so typed, the 1 gap appears as violation, but business reality per T3 and finance input is that unapplied cash is possible – indicating a **labeling** gap, not a cash handling breach.

**Counts/scopes and cross-model reading:** ET-OT must be read with OTG and OC-DFG. Package Pack->Dispatch missing 2 in OC-DFG plus ET-OT Pack/Package 200/200 and Dispatch/Package 200/200 suggests the control-flow gap is not a missing event but a missing *relation* between two existing events. Add Item Item 1450 vs Order 1448 shows 2 items created without order context – diverges from OTG weight that would hide the gap. Record Payment Invoice 92 vs Payment 93 shows exactly 1 unapplied case; coupling with OC-DFG Invoice no successor 14 and Write Off 4 distinguishes typical openness (14 pending) from forbidden co-birth risk.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory (expected whenever scope applies; gap = needs verification, not auto-violation):**
- N1 Create->Validate (98/100 directly, but *eventually* semantics) – gap may be abstraction mismatch; verify traces and `standard` flag. Condition: becomes violation only if same Order object never validates and is standard.
- N4 Pack->Dispatch for externally shipped Packages (198/200) – 2 missing are gaps if scope truly external. Condition: no violation if 2 are not externally shipped or Pack logged under alias.
- T1 (Order-Item interaction) / E2+E3 Add Item-Order/Item – 2 Adds lacking Order are gaps. Condition: violation unless event mistyped.
- T2 (Package-Delivery interaction) / E6 Dispatch-Delivery (198/200) – gap, but condition: satisfied if carrier attribute is accepted substitute per clarified norm.
- E1 Create Order-Order 100/100 – **met**.
- E4/E5 Pack/Dispatch-Package 200/200 – **met**.
- E7 Record Payment-Payment 93/93 – **met**.
- E8 Record Payment-Invoice – 92/93 *would be* missing mandatory under strict reading (no labeled exceptions), but condition: reclassifies to legitimate typical exception if event labeled unapplied cash.

**Forbidden (any confirmed observation = violation):**
- N3 Cancel Order->Dispatch Package (2) – forbidden, no scope exception.
- T6 Object cobirth Order-Payment (1) – forbidden.
- E10 Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice (1) – forbidden **if** relation is real; suspected data-quality error suspends business interpretation until join is validated. Condition: forbidden violation if lineage confirms real sensor-to-ledger link; data-quality error otherwise.

**Typical (desirable, absent  violation):**
- N2 Validate->Close (83) – typical, intermediate events allowed, remainder not violations.
- N5 Issue->Record Payment (92/110) together with T3 Invoice-Payment interaction (92) – typical; 18 not paid (4 write-offs legitimate, 14 pending) are expected variance. Condition: becomes investigable only if 14 are aged overdue or show bias.
- E8/T3 interplay for 1 unapplied payment – under typical reading, 1 missing Invoice link is legitimate variance; under mandatory reading it is gap until labeling fixed.

**Unregulated / Explicitly Unspecified (open-world, benign by design regardless of frequency):**
- N6 Reopen->Close (1) – unspecified.
- T4 Order-Customer interaction (100) – unspecified, managed elsewhere.
- T5 Order-RiskCase interaction (3) – unspecified.
- E9 Manual Compliance Review-RiskCase (3) – unspecified.
- Package object_codeath Delivery (7) – norm silent, therefore unspecified.
- Validate->Issue Invoice (72), Manual Compliance Review->Customer (3) – absent from normative edge set, **must not be classified as violations** per constraint; unspecified.
- All other observed relations not covering a mandatory/forbidden column remain unregulated.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions:** Two Orders with Cancel->Dispatch (N3) are forbidden violations if object linkage is correct; Pack->Dispatch for externally shipped Packages is mandatory (N4) and therefore 198/200 indicates a gap pending scope check; forbidden cobirth Order-Payment (T6) and forbidden Sensor-Invoice linkage (E10) are high-risk patterns (E10 highly likely a data error). **Depend on norm incompleteness:** Whether the 2 missing Create->Validate (N1) are violations depends on undefined `standard` scope and on interpreting eventually vs directly-follows; whether 2 missing Dispatch-Delivery (E6/T2) are violations depends on whether carrier attribute satisfies the norm; whether 1 missing Record Payment-Invoice (E8) is violation depends on undisclosed labeling rule for unapplied cash and the T3 typical allowance.

**Depend on suspected data-quality issues:** Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice (1, reused ID) and potentially the 2 Add Item-Order gaps and the 1 cobirth depend on join/extraction correctness; confirming lineage may reclassify forbidden/mandatory findings to data repair.

**Cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** Individual case compliance for mandatory eventually rules, scope membership (standard vs non-standard, externally shipped), legitimacy of typical exceptions (aging of 14 open invoices, reason for 4 write-offs), business justification for 3 compliance reviews, and true severity/priority all require trace-level sequences, object attributes, timestamps, and domain labels. Counts without denominators, time windows, and scope flags are insufficient for final legal or control judgment.
