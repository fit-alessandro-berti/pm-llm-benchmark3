### Alias Normalization Methodology
In multi-system enterprise environments, individuals frequently accrue divergent user identifiers (e.g., legacy LDAP username `b.koch` and enterprise ERP/QMS identifier `u_bk`). Prior to evaluating any segregation-of-duties (SoD) invariant or role permission, an automated pre-processing step resolves all identifiers against a canonical Employee Identity Table:
$$\text{norm}(r): \mathcal{R}_{\text{raw}} \to \mathcal{H}$$
where each resource string maps deterministically to a unique human person $\mathcal{H}$ (e.g., $\text{norm}(\text{'b.koch'}) = \text{norm}(\text{'u_bk'}) = \text{Ben Koch}$). Applying pairwise SoD checks without alias normalization yields false negatives, as demonstrated in CHG-508 where a textual check of `'b.koch' != 'u_bk'` masks self-approval.

### Service-Account Attribution Methodology
Under 21 CFR Part 11 and EU GMP Annex 11, electronic signatures and GxP verifications must be attributable to a unique natural person. Service accounts (e.g., `svc_qms`) possess no legal personhood. A two-tier forensic gate is applied:
1. **Tier 1 — Conclusive Attribution**: Requires concurrent physical access telemetry (badge swipe into terminal suite) plus multi-factor hardware cryptographic binding (e.g., FIDO2 token challenge or biometric re-authentication at the terminal). When satisfied (as in CHG-509: WS-IV-02 badge swipe and FIDO token identifying Eva Novak at 11:58 UTC), the event is provisionally attributed to that individual for SoD and competence verification.
2. **Tier 2 — Non-conclusive / Unattributed**: When telemetry indicates a shared terminal, absent CCTV, and no individual cryptographic re-authentication (as in CHG-503: WS-DM-04 in Diego's office where Diego badged into the room but the terminal was shared), attribution fails. The event is classified as `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` / `UNAUTHORIZED`, and worst-case SoD conflict (self-verification by implementer Diego) is formally flagged.

## 2. Rule Interpretation

### Formalization of Resource Rules (RR1–RR6)
- **RR1 — Drafter/Approver Separation (Four-Eyes Principle)**:
  $$\forall c \in \text{Cases}: \text{norm}(\text{Actor}(\text{Draft\_Change\_Request}, c)) \neq \text{norm}(\text{Actor}(\text{Approve\_Change}, c))$$
  Applies universally across all risk tiers (LOW and HIGH) and scopes. Prevents self-authorization and ensures independent quality/technical review.
- **RR2 — Implementer/Verifier Separation**:
  $$\forall c \in \text{Cases}: \text{norm}(\text{Actor}(\text{Implement\_Change}, c)) \neq \text{norm}(\text{Actor}(\text{Verify\_Implementation}, c))$$
  Applies universally across all risk tiers and scopes. Prevents self-verification and confirmation bias in engineering execution.
- **RR3 — High-Risk Approval Authority**:
  $$\forall c \in \text{Cases} \text{ where } Risk(c) = \text{HIGH} \land Emergency(c) = \text{false}: \text{Actor}(\text{Approve\_Change}, c) \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{QM}}(t_{\text{approve}}, Scope(c))$$
  An actor $u$ belongs to the authorized QM set $\mathcal{A}_{\text{QM}}(t, s)$ if and only if:
  1. $u$ holds the active standing role of Quality Manager at timestamp $t$, OR
  2. $u$ is the designated delegate under a valid instrument $D = (P, u, [t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}], S_{\text{del}}, \text{cond})$ satisfying:
     - Principal $P$ possessed QM authority at delegation creation;
     - Event timestamp $t \in [t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$;
     - Change scope $s \subseteq S_{\text{del}}$;
     - Principal $P$ is absent/formally unavailable as required by delegation conditions;
     - Delegate $u$ is active, qualified, and available (not on certified sick leave or medical incapacitation);
     - $D$ is directly issued by $P$ without violating subdelegation prohibitions.
- **RR4 — Emergency Substitution**:
  $$\forall c \in \text{Cases} \text{ where } Risk(c) = \text{HIGH} \land Emergency(c) = \text{true}:$$
  1. *Immediate Approval Authority*: $\text{Actor}(\text{Approve\_Change}, c) \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{IC}}(t_{\text{approve}}, Scope(c))$, where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{IC}}$ comprises the standing Incident Commander (Diego) or an authorized delegate holding an active delegation covering the incident (e.g., Farid under D-IC-03).
  2. *Mandatory Retrospective Review*: The case must include `Retrospective_QM_Review` executed by an actor $v \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{QM}}(t_{\text{review}}, Scope(c))$.
  3. *Review Timing Constraint*: $t_{\text{review}} \le t_{\text{implement}} + 2 \text{ business days}$.
  4. *Substantive Justification*: Emergency flag and documented safety rationale must be verified as credible.
- **RR5 — Identity Evidence**:
  Non-human service accounts (`svc_qms`) cannot satisfy human GxP responsibilities. Transactions executed under service accounts represent non-conformances unless mapped via immutable, contemporaneous session evidence (hardware MFA, dedicated IP, physical badge correlation) to an authorized natural person. All aliases belonging to the same human must be normalized before evaluating SoD rules.
- **RR6 — Suspicion versus Proof**:
  Statistical and organizational metrics (repeated handovers, reciprocal pairs, shared workstations) are audit indicators (red flags) that warrant forensic scrutiny. They do not constitute legal or regulatory proof of willful collusion or misconduct without corroborating non-process evidence (intent, covert communications, kickbacks, or deliberate circumvention).

### Specific Interpretive Questions

#### 1. Counting Business Days for RR4
In pharmaceutical quality systems (EU GMP Annex 11 / 21 CFR Part 211), "business days" are defined as standard working weekdays (Monday through Friday), excluding official plant calendar holidays. Weekend days (Saturday and Sunday, such as 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16) are excluded.
- **Counting Convention**: Day 0 is the date of implementation. Business Day 1 is the next working day; Business Day 2 ends at the close of the second business day (23:59:59 UTC, or 48 elapsed business hours from implementation).
- **Application to Case CHG-505**: Implementation occurred on Tuesday 2026-08-11 at 13:10 UTC.
  - Business Day 1: Wednesday 2026-08-12.
  - Business Day 2: Thursday 2026-08-13 (deadline expired 2026-08-13 23:59:59 UTC, or 13:10 UTC under a 48-business-hour metric).
  - Business Day 3: Friday 2026-08-14.
  The retrospective review executed on Friday 2026-08-14 at 16:00 UTC occurred on the *third* business day (74.8 elapsed hours), exceeding the mandatory two-business-day limit under both end-of-day and elapsed-hour interpretations.

#### 2. Treatment of Principal Action While on Leave
From a regulatory and administrative law perspective, being on annual leave is an employment status, not a legal revocation or disqualification of standing professional authority. When Asha Rao logged into the corporate VPN and approved CHG-504, she possessed full standing regulatory authority as Quality Manager.
However, exercising authority while on recorded leave creates two distinct compliance issues:
1. *Identity Integrity*: Remote access during leave warrants heightened scrutiny to rule out credential sharing or account hijacking. If corroborated by telemetry (VPN endpoint matching Asha's device and personal MFA challenge), the action is substantively `AUTHORIZED`.
2. *Impact on Active Delegation*: D-QM-01 is conditioned on Asha being "absent". When Asha actively accesses the system and assumes jurisdiction over a case, the absence condition is temporarily interrupted for that case. Her standing authority supersedes the delegation for that specific transaction.

#### 3. Evidentiary Weight of Later Database Entries
Under ALCOA+ principles (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate), electronic records must be contemporaneous. A transactional database record entered post-hoc (e.g., D-QM-01 entered into the QMS database on 2026-08-12 at 09:15 UTC during migration) *cannot by itself* retroactively establish authority for actions taken on 2026-08-11.
However, if an external, tamper-evident, cryptographically timestamped primary instrument exists (specifically, the digitally signed PDF dated 2026-08-09 18:00 UTC), the delegation was procedurally and legally established prior to the event. The later database entry represents a *data capture/migration latency defect* rather than a substantive delegation failure, provided the underlying signed PDF and its cryptographic hash are verified.

## 6. Control Improvements

To prevent recurrent authority breaches, identity ambiguity, and unmonitored organizational risks, the pharmaceutical plant should implement the following engineering and administrative controls:

1. **Delegation Creation & Pre-Registration Workflow**:
   - Eliminate paper-based or informal email delegations. All delegations must be created through a structured QMS workflow requiring dual cryptographic digital signatures (principal and delegate).
   - The QMS must enforce a mandatory pre-registration gate: a delegation cannot take effect until it is approved and indexed in the production database.
   - Granular scope constraints must be codified in database schemas (e.g., separating `production_equipment` from `facility_cooling`), preventing unauthorized scope expansion. Subdelegation must be programmatically disabled by default.
2. **Effective-Time Recording & Migration Synchrony**:
   - Delegation records must feature immutable, UTC-synchronized timestamps (using RFC 3161 trusted timestamp authorities).
   - IT migration procedures must mandate that active authorization tables are fully migrated and validated before operational transactions resume, eliminating evidentiary discrepancies between paper PDFs and transactional database tables.
3. **Automated Principal & Delegate Availability Enforcement**:
   - Integrate the QMS with the enterprise HR Information System (HRIS) and leave management portal via real-time webhooks.
   - When an employee enters certified sick leave or annual leave, the system must automatically:
     - Suspend the employee’s active delegation rights (preventing sick employees from acting as delegates);
     - Activate designated backup delegates without manual intervention;
     - Flag or prompt for step-up multi-factor authentication (MFA) if a principal on leave attempts an emergency sign-off, routing an automated notification to Quality Assurance.
4. **Identity & Alias Management (Single Source of Truth)**:
   - Establish a centralized Identity and Access Management (IAM) directory (e.g., Active Directory / SCIM) linked to a single employee ID number.
   - Decommission legacy local usernames (`b.koch`). All applications must resolve authorization against the canonical employee ID rather than disparate user account strings, rendering cross-account self-approval technically impossible.
5. **Individual Authentication & Shared-Account Deprecation**:
   - Formally deprecate generic shared accounts (`svc_qms`) for all interactive, human-facing GxP workflow steps.
   - Implement FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware tokens or biometric authentication for electronic signatures (compliant with 21 CFR 11.200).
   - Configure shared shop-floor terminals (e.g., WS-DM-04) with strict 2-minute inactivity screen locks and individual smart-card tap-to-authenticate readers.
6. **Automated Emergency Review (RR4) Hard Timers**:
   - Configure the QMS workflow engine to calculate the 2-business-day retrospective review deadline upon completion of `Implement_Change` on emergency cases.
   - Implement automated countdown timers, dispatching high-priority escalation alerts to the QM at 24 and 40 business hours.
   - If a retrospective review exceeds 48 business hours, the system must automatically apply an electronic quality hold on the affected manufacturing asset or batch, preventing product release until QM review occurs.
7. **Algorithmic Pair Rotation**:
   - Implement automated workflow dispatching that prevents prolonged, concentrated drafter-approver pairing.
   - Enforce an algorithmic constraint capping consecutive approvals (e.g., no single delegate may approve more than 3 consecutive high-risk changes drafted by the same engineer, or no dyad may exceed 50% of monthly cell approvals).
8. **Continuous Process Mining & Network Monitoring**:
   - Deploy real-time process mining monitors using PM4Py algorithms to analyze transactional change-control logs continuously.
   - Configure automated audit dashboards to track handover-of-work centrality, reciprocal approval matrices, and atypical weekend or after-hours sign-offs. Statistical anomalies must trigger internal quality surveillance audits before regulatory inspections.
9. **Evidential Retention & Forensic Audit Logging**:
   - Mandate long-term retention of physical security telemetry (turnstile badge logs, room access logs, CCTV archives, and VPN connection logs) synchronized with QMS change-control records.
   - Establish an integrated audit-trail repository where physical access, network authentication, and transactional GxP timestamps can be correlated instantaneously during investigations.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

### Systemic Judgment
An exhaustive evaluation of the ten change-control cases reveals that apparent resource non-conformances stem from three distinct systemic vulnerabilities:
1. **Genuine Authority & Governance Failures (~40%)**: Substantive regulatory violations occurred due to inadequate software-enforced gating and unauthorized informal delegation. Clara Stein self-approved changes in both routine (CHG-502) and emergency (CHG-510) contexts; CHG-510 relied on a legally void email subdelegation; Eva Novak approved a high-risk change (CHG-507) on a Sunday with zero delegated QM authority; and CHG-505 breached post-emergency quality review timelines and scope constraints.
2. **Identity Architecture & Data Quality Defects (~30%)**: The plant’s IT environment suffers from fragmented identity management. Un-normalized username aliases permitted disguised self-approval (CHG-508), delayed database migration created artificial evidentiary gaps (CHG-501), and reliance on shared service accounts (`svc_qms` in CHG-503 and CHG-509) obscured individual accountability, creating critical segregation-of-duties risks.
3. **Organizational Pattern & Security Vulnerabilities (~30%)**: Critical vulnerabilities exist at the intersection of physical security, credential integrity, and workplace culture. CHG-506 represents an alarming instance of either credential compromise or deliberate record repudiation; Asha’s remote vacation approval (CHG-504) exposes the plant to credential-sharing risks; and the overwhelming concentration of drafter-approver handoffs between Clara Stein and Ben Koch (18 of 23 high-risk changes) reflects an unchecked operational silo vulnerable to rubber-stamping.

### Uncertainty Boundaries
Log data and electronic records alone cannot establish physical identity or subjective intent for two critical events:

- In **CHG-506**, whether Ben Koch worked while on sick leave and disclaimed it, or whether an unauthorized colleague used his office terminal, remains unresolved pending physical security correlation.
- In the **Clara–Ben Collaboration**, process mining confirms concentrated pairing, but whether this reflects lean engineering efficiency or illicit collusion cannot be decided without reviewing engineering query depth and deviation histories.
