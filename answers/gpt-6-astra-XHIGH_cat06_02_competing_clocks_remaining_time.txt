## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Competing clocks.** Let C and M be the absolute times at which chemistry and microbiology first have usable results, including required rerun or confirmation. For a batch continuing toward release,

`join time J = max(C, M)`.

QA waiting begins at J and depends on the queue then. Initial review, any subsequent loops, and final disposition follow that join. Each human activity advances on its own working calendar. Thus a release path is evaluated as a calendar-aware sequence, not as `mean chemistry + mean microbiology + mean QA`.

**Chemistry waiting versus service.** There are two active samples plus four waiting ahead, not merely four total jobs. The mean of triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) is about 2.83 working hours. Taking active residuals of approximately 0.65 and 1.6 h:

- Under risk-priority, the first free analyst takes the urgent sample and the next can take B-417. Its representative service start is Friday about 17:06. Approximately 0.9 h of its 2.83 h service fits on Friday; the remaining 1.9 h finishes Monday about 09:55. A useful ordinary range is Monday 09:00–11:00.
- Under FIFO, both analysts process two waiting samples before a slot reaches B-417. Using mean predecessor service, the first available slot is about `min(0.65 + 2 × 2.83, 1.6 + 2 × 2.83) = 6.3` laboratory working hours after now: Monday about 11:50. Its own service then finishes around Monday 14:40. An ordinary range is roughly Monday 12:30–17:00.
- Friday has only 2.5 working hours left. The Friday 18:00–Monday 08:00 closure adds 62 elapsed hours whenever work crosses it. Under the assumed urgent-first ordering and predecessor-service proxy, even an unusually early B-417 start at Friday 16:42 plus minimum 1.5 h service misses closing. Its fastest chemistry completion is approximately Monday 08:12.
- Slow predecessor services and a long B-417 service can push FIFO chemistry into Tuesday morning. A priority rerun adds 1–3 working hours, mean about 1.93 h. It commonly moves priority-path completion to Monday 11:00–14:00, or FIFO completion to Monday late afternoon/Tuesday morning. A late Monday rerun incurs another 14-hour overnight closure.

This calculation uses the uncertain dispatch policy to construct alternatives; it does not average queue discipline into a fictitious intermediate start time.

**Microbiology incubation versus staffing.** Friday 14:00 plus 46–54 h is **Sunday 20 September, 12:00–20:00**, or 44.5–52.5 h after the forecast origin. Already elapsed incubation is not added again.

- With on-call authorization, primary readout is approximately Sunday 13:00–23:00. QA is closed Sunday and chemistry still ordinarily needs Monday, so this authorization does not usually advance smooth-path release by a full day.
- Without authorization, primary readout is approximately Monday 08:30–09:30, or 65–66 remaining hours. This is a staffing delay following technical readiness, not extra incubation.
- If a contamination indication is read on Sunday at 13:00–23:00, another 20–30 h makes confirmation technically ready between Monday 09:00 and Tuesday 05:00. Applying staffed readout gives usable confirmation roughly Monday 09:30–Tuesday 09:30.
- If the initial indication is first read Monday at 08:30–09:30, confirmation becomes technically ready Tuesday 04:30–15:30 and is ordinarily read Tuesday about 08:30–17:00. This is where weekend authorization can save substantial elapsed time.

A confirmation must resolve the indication into a usable result or a disposition. An unresolved indication is not treated as a normal result that permits ordinary release.

**QA queue at the future join.** Carrying today's five-batch queue unchanged into Monday would be misleading. From now until Monday opening, QA has 4.5 reviewer-hours on Friday and 4 on Saturday: 8.5 in total. Expected arrivals during those opening hours are `0.35 × 8.5 ≈ 3` batches. With the explicit 2.5 h service-mean proxy, approximately 3–4 batch-equivalents can be processed. A rough Monday-opening backlog is therefore `5 + 3 − 3 to 4`, or about 4–5 batches, with substantial dispersion and possible residual work.

From Monday, two reviewers provide approximately 0.8 completions/hour against 0.35 arrivals/hour. The expected backlog can decline, but a burst of arrivals or a few long reviews can prevent that. I use approximately 2–6 QA working hours of initial waiting for common Monday joins, sometimes 0–3 h at later joins after the backlog drains, and 6–12 h or more in congestion scenarios. Waiting is followed by review service: median 1.5 h, P80 about 3.5 h, and a longer unbounded tail in the proxy.

Under FIFO, arrivals after B-417 joins normally go behind it; they should not all be added to its own wait. Also, later chemistry completion may coincide with a smaller QA backlog. Chemistry delay and QA delay are therefore not automatically additive at their worst values. Conversely, uncertain priorities or overdispersed arrivals can overturn that benefit. QA closes Monday–Friday at 20:00, works Saturday only 09:00–13:00, and has no Sunday service.

**Scenario assembly.** The following decompositions use those branch and queue calculations.

1. **Smooth path.** Chemistry finishes Monday morning under priority or Monday afternoon under FIFO. Microbiology is usable Sunday evening or Monday 08:30–09:30. The join is the later result: commonly Monday 09:00–11:00 under priority and Monday 12:30–17:00 under FIFO. Add the queue at that time, initial QA service, and 0.25–0.75 h Electronic Release. A representative priority case is chemistry Monday 10:00, microbiology Monday 09:00, join 10:00, QA waiting about 4 h, review 1.5 h, and release about 16:00: **roughly 72.5 h remaining**. A FIFO case with chemistry at 14:40 and a drained QA queue can still release Monday evening. Reviews reaching closing finish Tuesday. There is no rework on this scenario.

2. **Chemistry rerun.** Use the same initial chemistry waiting and service, then insert 1–3 additional laboratory working hours before C. For an uncomplicated microbiology branch, M remains Sunday evening or Monday morning; `max(C, M)` usually selects rerun chemistry. If chemistry finishes its initial test Monday 16:30 and needs a 2 h rerun, 1.5 h fits before 18:00 and 0.5 h resumes Tuesday, making C approximately Tuesday 08:30. QA then waits and reviews on Tuesday, followed by electronic release. An early Monday rerun can instead be absorbed partly by the time QA would have spent clearing its backlog. A rerun also increases investigation risk, so its effect is not limited to the approximately two extra laboratory hours.

3. **Delayed routine microbiology readout.** Chemistry waiting/service are unchanged. Incubation ends Sunday, then the readout clock waits until Monday staffing. With fast priority chemistry at Monday 08:15 and microbiology at 09:15, the join is 09:15. With FIFO chemistry at 14:40, the same Monday microbiology result does not move the join at all. Add the actual future QA wait and review, then release. There is no extra incubation or investigation merely because readout waited overnight.

4. **Contamination confirmation.** Chemistry follows its normal or rerun path, while M includes the staffed primary readout, 20–30 continuous hours, and a second staffed readout. Without weekend authorization, M commonly falls Tuesday 08:30–17:00 and dominates Monday chemistry. With early Sunday authorization, confirmation may be usable on Monday and compete closely with chemistry. Following a noncritical usable confirmation, join with chemistry, apply the future QA queue and review, and assess the higher investigation probability before release. If confirmation establishes a critical defect and rejection is authorized, terminate with 0.5–1.0 h recording instead; do not additionally impose successful-release QA service.

5. **Documentation Query.** First reach the parallel join and complete enough initial QA review to raise the query. Then insert 4–12 working hours, followed by any resumed queue wait, follow-up review, and release. For example, a query raised Monday 17:00 requiring 8 working hours uses 3 h Monday and 5 h Tuesday, so information becomes available Tuesday 13:00. With 0–3 h waiting, 0.5–1.5 h follow-up review, and release, completion is approximately Tuesday 14:00–18:30. Chemistry and microbiology are not restarted just because documentation is missing.

6. **Documentation-only investigation.** Reach the branch join and initial QA assessment, then add 8–20 working hours and resumed QA. An investigation opened Monday 17:00 with a 14 h information delay uses 3 h Monday and 11 h Tuesday, finishing Tuesday 19:00. A short immediate follow-up might release Tuesday evening; a longer review or resumed wait moves completion to Wednesday. A 20 h delay from the same start finishes Wednesday 13:00 before follow-up review. A query may also occur: add only sequential work, using overlap where both information-gathering tasks run together. A confirmed critical defect instead leads to rejection recording once disposition is authorized.

7. **Laboratory investigation.** Reach initial QA through the same maximum of branch clocks and future queue. An investigation opened Monday 17:00 adds 3–7 calendar days, reaching Thursday 24 September 17:00 through Monday 28 September 17:00, before follow-up QA and final recording. An investigation ending Saturday after 13:00 or on Sunday must await Monday QA; for example, Saturday 15:00 to Monday 08:00 is a further 41-hour calendar wait. Queries, a late initial join, and follow-up congestion explain Tuesday 29 September or later outcomes. If a critical defect is confirmed during the investigation, rejection can end the process before the entire release-oriented interval is exhausted.

**Dependent paths and illustrative weights.** Let E denote a confirmed excursion. One transparent calibration is:

- `P(E) = 0.30`;
- rerun probability 0.10 without E and 0.27 with E, giving marginal probability about 0.15;
- contamination-indication probability 0.05 without E and 0.15 with E, giving marginal probability about 0.08.

These conditional numbers are assumptions demonstrating a coherent mixture, not measured uplift estimates. Even if rerun and contamination are conditionally independent given E for this calculation, they are positively dependent overall: their joint probability is about 1.6%, versus about 1.2% from multiplying the marginals. Residual conditional dependence remains possible.

This calibration makes `E or rerun or contamination indication` occur on about 40% of paths. Applying investigation probabilities of approximately 0.425 on these paths and 0.065 otherwise gives about 21% investigations. Taking 70% documentation-only gives approximately 15% documentation investigations and 6% laboratory investigations. Using a 0.22 query probability as an independent illustration gives approximately 52% with neither laboratory anomaly nor QA loop. Query independence is only a weight-calculation shortcut; it is not needed to assume independence of the laboratory anomalies.

The elevated investigation rule applies when E is confirmed even if both tests are normal. These weights explain why the median remains near Monday/Tuesday while a small laboratory-investigation component extends well into the following week. They are not a fitted probability model, and the supplied ranges allow materially different weights.

## 4. Forecast summary

These are approximate quantile brackets across reasonable queue and path assumptions, rather than statistical confidence intervals or outputs of a calibrated simulation. Hours are elapsed from Friday 18 September at 15:30; dates and hours are rounded consistently.

| Forecast measure | Remaining calendar hours | Approximate terminal date/time |
|---|---|---|
| Plausible earliest completion | About 66–68 h | Monday 21 September, roughly 09:00–11:30 |
| Central / P50-like range | About 72–94 h | Monday 21 September 15:30–Tuesday 22 September 13:30 |
| P80-like range | About 96–124 h | Tuesday 22 September 15:30–Wednesday 23 September 19:30 |
| P90-like range under the illustrative central path mixture | About 120–168 h | Wednesday 23 September 15:30–Friday 25 September 15:30 |
| Severe-tail planning range, also relevant to P90 if laboratory-investigation incidence is near or above 10% | About 168–312 h or more | Friday 25 September afternoon–Thursday 1 October afternoon, potentially later |

The earliest range requires fast priority chemistry, a promptly usable microbiology result, little QA waiting, and a short review. It is not a likely promise. On-call microbiology alone does not make Sunday release feasible under the stated routine calendars.

The P50-like range reflects that roughly half the illustrative paths avoid a query, investigation, and laboratory anomaly, while another portion has small delays absorbed within Monday. The P80-like range includes many query and documentation-investigation paths. Around P90, the uncertain laboratory-investigation incidence becomes decisive: at roughly 6%, P90 can still sit in the documentation/congestion tail; above roughly 10%, it can move into the several-day laboratory path, potentially to Monday 28 or Tuesday 29 September. Overlap between the reported quantile brackets reflects parameter uncertainty; quantiles remain ordered within any one fixed model.

**Electronic Release is the most plausible terminal event**, particularly on the smooth and documentation-only paths. **Batch Rejected** is a material alternative after confirmation of a critical defect. The available information does not support a numerical rejection probability. Rejection may shorten an anomalous path compared with completing every investigation needed to support release. No finite worst-case bound is justified because repeat-loop counts and the far tail of queue/service delays are unspecified.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Whether QA requires additional laboratory work — mainly path uncertainty.** The 3–7 calendar-day addition is much larger than ordinary service variation. Its uncertain incidence determines whether it mainly affects the severe tail or enters P90 itself.
2. **Resolution of the temperature-excursion flag — observation and dependence uncertainty.** It simultaneously changes both laboratory anomaly risks and the investigation probability, including when test results are normal. Separate independent rerun and contamination models would miss this shared cause and misrepresent combinations of delays.
3. **QA workload and dispatch at the actual join — queue uncertainty.** Today's five batches are only an initial condition. Arrivals, long reviews, Friday/Saturday processing, and Monday's second reviewer determine whether B-417 waits minutes, several hours, or crosses closing time.
4. **Microbiology indication and staffed confirmation timing — path and calendar uncertainty.** Routine readiness falls on Sunday. Weekend authorization has modest smooth-path value but can start confirmation almost a day sooner; a second readout outside staffed hours creates another wait.
5. **Chemistry dispatch, predecessor workload, and rerun timing — queue, service-time, and calendar uncertainty.** Priority versus FIFO can move ordinary completion from Monday morning to afternoon. Long work ahead or a late rerun crosses a laboratory closure, making chemistry the slower branch and increasing investigation risk.

Documentation queries also materially widen the Tuesday–Thursday range; their smaller delay is included in the QA rework scenarios rather than displacing the five drivers above.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**The single observation with the largest conditional effect is QA's investigation-scope decision: additional laboratory work required versus no additional laboratory work.** A laboratory-work decision shifts the remaining forecast by roughly 3–7 calendar days, then requires final QA availability. A documentation-only decision instead shifts it by approximately 8–20 working hours plus resumed review. A decision that no investigation is needed removes most of the multi-day tail, leaving the current queue, any documentation query, and final disposition. This is the largest forecast change, although the decision is likely to arrive later than the next test milestone.

One secondary update trigger is **Engineering resolving the temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation raises the joint risk of rerun, contamination, and investigation and shifts probability toward longer paths; clearance reduces that shared source of tail risk while leaving baseline anomaly probabilities intact. This earlier observation would improve the forecast before QA can classify the investigation scope.
