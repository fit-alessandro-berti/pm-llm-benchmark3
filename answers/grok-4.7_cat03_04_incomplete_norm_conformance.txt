## 1. Conformance semantics

A finding is **forbidden behavior** only when a relation the norm marks forbidden is present inside its stated scope. N3, T6, and E10 are closed-world: one qualifying occurrence is enough. A graph hit still needs a check that the edge is the business relation described, because a bad join or a shared multi-object event can imitate it.

A finding is **missing mandatory behavior** when a mandatory relation fails inside an applicable scope. Mandatory coverage is closed-world for that scope and open outside it. N1 applies to newly created standard orders; N4 applies to packages intended for external shipment; E1–E8 apply to every event of the named type, with E8’s single stated exception. Scope has to be shown, not assumed from a nearby aggregate.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is an observed relation that this norm marks unspecified, or that it does not mention. Presence is compatible with the norm. Rarity does not create a prohibition. **Typical-pattern absence** means a desirable path did not occur. N2, N5, and T3 explicitly allow exceptions, so the absence is an operational residue, not a breach.

A **likely data-quality error** is an edge whose generating mechanism is already in doubt, especially where an identifier collision can manufacture a forbidden link. A **abstraction mismatch** arises when the business rule is about eventual participation or lifecycle order while the summary counts direct succession. **Insufficient evidence** covers missing scope flags, missing object identities, possible right-censoring, and weights that can be produced by different events than the rule talks about.

Open-world interpretation applies everywhere else. Unlisted edges, unspecified edges, and skipped typical edges stay outside the violation set. Closed-world interpretation is limited to forbidden relations and to mandatory requirements whose scope predicate is true. A bare diff against a normative edge set cannot apply that distinction: extra log edges and missing model edges are diagnostics until the status metadata is consulted.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An object-centric directly-follows graph can show, for one object type, which activity labels sit next to each other and how often. It can support N3 strongly: on 2 orders, Cancel Order is the immediate predecessor of Dispatch Package, so dispatch is after cancel on that order object. It can show that N5’s payment path covers 92 of 110 invoices, that 4 invoices move to Write Off, and that 14 stop. It can show a single Reopen Order → Close Order path.

It cannot show whether an activity occurs later with something in between. It cannot show that a dispatch package is the package created for that order if several objects share the event. It cannot compute a rate when the denominator is unknown: Validate Order → Close Order is 83, and the number of validated orders is not given. It cannot apply “standard”, “external”, or “open credit term”.

N1 is the clearest illustration of treating an eventual rule as a directly-follows rule. The norm says every newly created standard order should eventually enter validation, and it says the summary only approximates that with a direct edge. Two missing direct edges are therefore a failed approximation. They become missing mandatory behavior only if those order objects have no later Validate Order. The three Create Order → Manual Compliance Review successions are a concrete way a direct validation edge disappears while validation can still follow. Three and two are different counts, so the review path does not identify the two gaps by arithmetic.

The same danger runs in the other direction for N3. Direct succession is sufficient for those two orders. Any Cancel Order, then other activities, then Dispatch Package would also be forbidden and would be invisible to a pure directly-follows check. The count of 2 is a lower bound on the forbidden pattern if the business rule is “anytime after”.

N4 is worded as a mandatory Pack Package → Dispatch Package relation for externally shipped packages, without N1’s “eventually” caveat. The 198 of 200 figure is a literal mandatory-DF gap inside a scope the summary already calls external. Because Pack Package–Package and Dispatch Package–Package are both 200, the two gaps may be intermediate events rather than unpacked shipments. That alternative is an abstraction question to be settled on traces. It is not a reason to drop the gap.

Validate Order → Issue Invoice (72) and Reopen Order → Close Order (1) sit outside any forbidden or mandatory DF rule. The invoice hop is the kind of intermediate N2 already contemplates.

### OTG

An object-type graph can show that a relationship type between two object types occurs, and it can show a weight. Presence of (Order, object_interaction, Item) at 1450 supports T1 at graph level. Presence of (Package, object_interaction, Delivery) at 200 would look like T2 is fully met. Presence of (Invoice, object_interaction, Payment) at 92 is consistent with T3. Customer (100) and RiskCase (3) are unspecified. One (Order, object_cobirth, Payment) is enough for T6. Seven (Package, object_codeath, Delivery) edges have no normative status.

The OTG cannot show which event produced an edge, whether every event of a type carries the relation, or whether two objects born “together” were born by one business event or by a loader. Weight is not a conformance result. T2’s weight of 200 coexists with only 198 Dispatch Package–Delivery qualifications. T1’s weight of 1450 coexists with only 1448 Add Item–Order qualifications. A mandatory interaction edge can be present, and even heavy, while the mandatory event-object qualification fails. T3 and E8 classify one payment without an invoice differently: the OTG reading is an allowed missing interaction; the event reading is a mandatory invoice link whose exception was not typed.

Codeath is conforming with an open world. Joint confirmation is a plausible operational reading of the 7 edges and is not a violation.

### ET–OT

Event-type to object-type counts can test per-event mandatory qualification better than either other family. E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 have weights that line up with the available denominators (100, 1450, 200, 200, 93) and show no gap in this summary. E2’s 1448 against E3’s 1450 is the Add Item gap. E6’s 198 against E5’s 200 is the Delivery gap. E8’s 92 against E7’s 93 is the unlabeled payment. E10’s single Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is the forbidden pattern with a suspected bad join. E9’s three RiskCase links, and the three unlisted Customer links, match the three review events and stay unregulated.

ET–OT still cannot order activities, so it cannot see N3 or N1. It cannot see cobirth or codeath. A full event count that equals the object weight does not prove each event carries exactly the expected objects if some events carry several and others carry none; the Add Item and Dispatch comparisons are informative because the paired object columns disagree inside the same event type.

One relation can be conforming in one family and suspicious in another. Dispatch versus Delivery is the sharp case: the OTG edge’s weight of 200 is compatible with “the relationship exists and is common”, while ET–OT shows two dispatch events outside E6. Add Item versus Order is the same pattern against T1 and E2. Issue Invoice → Record Payment at 92 of 110 is a typical-pattern gap under N5; the single Record Payment with no invoice is a separate E8 question about one event, not about those 18 invoices.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**

- E6/T2: two Dispatch Package events lack a Delivery object. Scope applies to every such event. The carrier attribute keeps them traceable and does not meet the object requirement as written. Reclassify only if the norm is changed so that this attribute satisfies the rule.
- E2: two Add Item events lack Order, against a rule with no exception. Reclassify if those events are mistyped and are outside Add Item.
- N4: two of 200 externally shipped packages lack Pack → Dispatch as a direct edge. Reclassify as abstraction mismatch if the same package has an earlier Pack, or as out of scope if the package is not an external shipment.
- E8: one Record Payment has a Payment and no Invoice, and no event in the snapshot carries the unapplied-cash type. This is a mandatory miss under E8. It leaves this bucket if the event is truly unapplied and the type is corrected; T3 then covers it as a typical exception.
- N1: the two orders missing Create → Validate as a direct edge enter this bucket only when the object trace shows no later validation and the order is standard. Until then they stay in abstraction mismatch / insufficient evidence.

**Forbidden**

- N3: two orders with Cancel Order directly followed by Dispatch Package. Reclassify only if the order is not actually a participant, the clock is wrong, or a reopen restored the order before dispatch.
- T6: one Order–Payment cobirth. This stays forbidden behavior unless the shared birth is shown to be an extraction artifact.
- E10: the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is forbidden if the object identity is real. The suspected reused identifier moves the working classification to likely data-quality error until the join is reproduced or refuted.

**Typical**

- N5: 92 of 110 invoices are paid in the observed succession; 4 written off are the exception N5 names; 14 with no successor are typical-pattern absence, including possible open terms or right-censoring. They become a mandatory follow-up only if a future term rule says the term has expired.
- N2: 83 direct closes after validation, with 72 validations going to Issue Invoice. Missing direct closes are the incompleteness N2 allows.
- T3: the invoice–payment interaction weight of 92, and unapplied cash in principle. The unlabeled payment is a typical T3 case only together with the E8 label; without the label, E8 governs.

**Unregulated**

- Validate Order → Issue Invoice on 72 orders.
- Create Order → Manual Compliance Review on 3 orders.
- Reopen Order → Close Order on 1 order (N6).
- Order–Customer interaction at 100 (T4) and Order–RiskCase interaction at 3 (T5).
- Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase at 3 (E9) and Manual Compliance Review–Customer at 3.
- Package–Delivery codeath at 7.
- These move into another bucket only if a revised norm assigns them a mandatory, forbidden, or typical status. Their absence from the current edge set does not move them.

    if meta is None or meta["status"] == "unspecified":
        # Open world: unlisted and unspecified relations are unregulated.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1’s eventual rule is published as a directly-follows count.** False breaches will be raised whenever a review sits between create and validate, and true skips that happen later will be missed. Clarify that validation is mandatory as eventually-follows for standard orders, and that the direct edge is unspecified as a conformance rule.

2. **“Standard” order is undefined.** The two orders without a direct validation edge may be outside N1. Clarify the type or flag that marks a standard order. Keep validation mandatory for that type, and leave other order types explicitly unspecified until a rule exists.

3. **N4 does not say whether anything may occur between pack and dispatch, nor how “external” is recorded.** Strict adjacency will flag ordinary warehouse steps; a loose reading will ignore a real unpacked shipment. Clarify a mandatory eventual Pack before Dispatch for packages flagged external, and leave unnamed intermediate activities explicitly unspecified.

4. **E8 and T3 disagree about unapplied cash, and the label’s field name is undefined.** The same payment is a mandatory miss and a typical exception depending on the family. Clarify one attribute that marks unapplied cash. With that mark, a missing invoice is typical. Without the mark, the invoice link stays mandatory.

5. **E6/T2 require a Delivery object while operations already store a carrier attribute.** Two real shipments will keep failing, or staff will fabricate Delivery objects to clear the diagnostic. State that the carrier attribute does not satisfy the rule. Keep the Delivery object mandatory.

6. **Codeath is uncovered, and cobirth is forbidden for only one pair.** The seven package–delivery end links cannot be monitored consistently, and a similar birth link for other types has no status. Leave package–delivery codeath explicitly unspecified. Keep Order–Payment cobirth forbidden. Leave other birth and death pairs explicitly unspecified until named.

7. **N3 does not define cancelled status across reopen, and it does not define dispatch when the order object is omitted from the event.** A later reopen can make a true dispatch look forbidden, and a dispatch that drops the order object can hide a real one. Keep dispatch forbidden while the order is cancelled. Make dispatch after a completed reopen explicitly unspecified until fulfilment policy covers it. Define the forbidden pattern on the package that belongs to the order, not only on the order’s direct edge.

8. **No time window is given for the 14 silent invoices, and no tolerance policy is stated.** Open credit will be chased as breach, or a growing backlog will be ignored. Silence inside an agreed term should be typical. Escalation after that term should be a separate mandatory rule once the business names the term. Statistical bands should stay explicitly unspecified and should not waive forbidden or mandatory rules.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** Start with the two N3 orders: identify the packages on those dispatch events, stop further movement, and arrange recall if the goods have left. Next, isolate the single T6 order–payment pair from automated posting until the birth event is inspected. In parallel, drop the suspected Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link from financial extracts so E10 cannot pollute invoices while the join is checked. Do not freeze the three compliance reviews, the one reopen-and-close, the seven codeaths, or the customer and risk-case links.

**Data repair.** Reproduce the sensor join and split the reused numeric domain if it collides. For the two dispatches, attach an existing Delivery object if one can be found from the carrier reference; otherwise leave the gap visible. For the unlabeled payment, restore an invoice key only if the source system has one; if finance confirms unapplied cash, add the type the exception requires. Repair order identifiers on the two Add Item events only where the source has them.

**Process investigation.** Reconstruct full object traces for the two cancelled orders, including any reopen and the package identity. Reconstruct the two externally shipped packages that lack a direct pack-to-dispatch edge, and the two orders that lack a direct create-to-validate edge, specifically looking for a later Validate Order. Read the cobirth event in the source application. These traces decide abstraction versus breach. The 14 silent invoices are an aging review against terms, not a compliance case.

**Norm revision.** Resolve the eight gaps above before the next conformance cycle, in this order: N1 eventual versus direct succession, the E8 label versus T3, the Delivery object versus the carrier attribute, the N3 definition across reopen and object attachment, then N4 intermediates, codeath, the standard-order flag, and invoice terms. Leave rarity alone. One reopen, three risk reviews, and seven joint confirmations do not justify new prohibitions.

**Monitoring.** Alert at count ≥ 1 for N3, T6, and a confirmed E10. Alert on E2, E6, and N4 as mandatory exception queues with object identifiers, not as rates. Track N5’s unpaid invoices as an aging report. Suppress alerts for unlisted and unspecified relations, including codeath, reopen-then-close, customer links, and Validate Order → Issue Invoice. Keep the raw PM4Py graph diagnostics, and publish the semantic class beside them so a log move on an unregulated edge cannot be read as a breach.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The robust conclusions are that the two N3 direct successions are forbidden if the object trace confirms participation and order, that T6’s single cobirth is forbidden unless it is an extraction artifact, that E10 is a suspected identifier collision rather than a demonstrated operational act, and that the invoice hop, the customer and risk links, the three reviews, the one reopen-and-close, and the seven codeaths are unregulated here. The 4 write-offs and the 14 silent invoices sit under N5’s typical allowance. Conclusions that depend on incompleteness are the N1 gaps, the N4 gaps, the conflict between E8 and T3, and any claim about orders that are not known to be standard. Conclusions that depend on data quality are the sensor–invoice link, the possible dropped order keys on two Add Item events, the two missing Delivery objects that still have a carrier attribute, and the unlabeled payment. Graph summaries cannot decide eventual validation, whether the two unpacked-looking shipments were packed earlier, whether any non-direct cancel-then-dispatch path exists, whether the 14 invoices are simply unfinished, or which physical objects sit behind the aggregate weights.
