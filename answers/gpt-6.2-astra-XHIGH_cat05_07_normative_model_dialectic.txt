## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

M0 admits four complete visible traces. After one Register_Supplier, both checks must occur, in either order. The case then takes exactly one branch, either Manual_Procurement_Approval followed by Activate_Supplier, or Reject_Supplier. Both branches end with Notify_Requester followed by Close_Case. Concurrency permits either recorded order of the checks. It does not permit activation between them. M0 requires both checks even for rejection. CS-9 (N1) expressly requires them before activation and allows abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads.

The supplied traces establish what the onboarding log records. The case evidence also establishes facts outside those traces, including the common human identity in SUP-712, the duplicate source transaction in SUP-711 and the equivalent review content in SUP-713. An exact trace match proves control-flow fit only. Conversely, an unmatched label or a missing row calls for reconciliation before a finding against a person or operation.

CS-9 is the controlled baseline. PD-3 (N2) establishes that the steering committee authorized running a bounded pilot. Its competence to waive particular CS-9 obligations is disputed. The observer's contemporaneous assent and later qualification both matter. FD-7 (N3) is presented as an operative directive with a defined population, interval and compensating controls. Continuing flood conditions establish need after expiry. An extension requires separate authority. N4 describes an observation mechanism. N5 evidences a practice and support for changing the model. Neither supplies a waiver. Lead admission, the treatment of reactivations, the completeness of the disclosure record, and some case-to-message links remain unresolved.

**Decision standard.** For a material noncompliance finding, the reviewer bears the burden of identifying an applicable obligation, bringing the case within its scope at the relevant time, and showing a breach on the balance of reliable evidence. Identity, source coverage, event time and the applicable exception must be examined where they affect the result. Severity determines investigative priority. The evidence of occurrence must stand on its own. The party relying on an exception must substantiate the issuing authority, clauses displaced, eligibility at activation, effective window and compensating controls. A proven baseline departure with a plausible but unverified waiver stays open as such. Missing evidence alone establishes failure to perform a control only if the source was sufficiently complete, or establishes a documentation breach if recording was itself required. A scope dispute should be decided against the actual policy boundary before it is counted in a compliance rate.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 protects the decision to enable a supplier. A review or signature after that decision cannot supply the protection required at the point of activation. SUP-708 opened a high-risk supplier 40 minutes before sanctions clearance and also obtained manual approval late. The absence of purchases reduces realized exposure. SUP-703 applied the pilot to a new high-risk foreign supplier, substituted auto-approval, and deferred quality review. Even the committee's own terms exclude this case. The EUR 180,000 first purchase makes the delegation failure consequential. It does not establish a loss or a breach of FD-7's unrelated spend cap.

The most favorable reading of PD-3 still leaves 14 of 312 June–July auto cases clearly outside its eligibility rules and nine without enough attributes to decide. The other 289 *appear* eligible. That count establishes neither authority to suspend CS-9 nor performance of master-data validation in each case. An operator-selected “pilot” flag cannot settle either issue. For SUP-702, the committee minutes are real evidence of authorization to operate, but no delegation resolving the conflict with CS-9 or result of automated validation is supplied. SUP-714 ran the route on August 2, after its sunset. Six auto cases in the first week of August corroborate a failure to retire the practice. A pending permanent amendment gave no authority on those dates.

FD-7 supplies a stronger basis for the temporary ordering in SUP-704. Its very specificity matters in SUP-705. Ongoing recovery, EUR 12,000 initial spend and eventual sign-off explain the July 13 decision but cannot extend a July 12 sunset. Even within the window, initial spend below the cap cannot prove cumulative spend through approval. Beyond ordering, SUP-712 defeated the explicit two-human rule while fitting M0 perfectly. SUP-715 poses a distinct disclosure question. A relationship cannot be converted into an unwritten disqualification, but the absence of a disclosure record warrants an accounting.

Enforcing CS-9 also requires discounting bad allegations. There is positive evidence of a technical duplicate in SUP-711, equivalent work in SUP-713, and a notification outside the onboarding trace in SUP-702. M0 cannot represent the evidence-gathering in SUP-707. These cases give no basis for disciplining operators for the corresponding trace discrepancies. Obtaining certificates, completing a late review, or observing no loss can improve the outcome without retroactively authorizing a different decision rule.

## 3. Defense Brief

The exact-match population conflates control execution, process boundaries and instrumentation. Even a baseline replay that serializes sanctions before quality invents a precedence absent from M0. In SUP-707 the two requests occurred between otherwise valid checks and produced the missing certificates before approval. Across 47 cases with requests, none activated before the requested evidence arrived. Compliance itself favors information gathering. The extra events expose a missing loop and an opportunity to model evidence receipt. They give little support to an allegation of premature approval.

A committee decision, follow-up email, monitored-exception language and a bounded eligible cohort make reliance on PD-3 substantially more defensible for SUP-702 than for SUP-703. SUP-702 met the stated supplier criteria and completed quality review the next business day. The strongest defense would establish the committee's delegated power and retrieve the validation result. The facts currently establish neither. FD-7 expressly permitted provisional ordering for the critical pump supplier in SUP-704, with monitoring, limited initial spending and next-business-day review and approval. M0 has no language for such a conditional authorization. The same visible sequence in SUP-705 has a different authority status two days later.

Missing notifications rose from 2% to 29% after the July 1 CRM move, consistent with about 70% mirror coverage. An onboarding-only replay will overstate omissions. The stable ID in SUP-702 supports occurrence independently, although that June case predates the migration. In SUP-706 a real message with a later editable link is weaker evidence of *which case* was notified. The aggregate rate can explain a class of missing rows but cannot authenticate an individual link. Collapsing the two register rows by source transaction in SUP-711 and applying the documented semantic equivalence in SUP-713 each resolves a different technical discrepancy.

SUP-709 turns on whether a lead ever entered full onboarding. CS-9 expressly contemplates an abbreviated exit. SUP-710 turns on a lifecycle boundary and possible reuse of an earlier, still-valid certification. Requiring a fictitious second registration or inventing a current-case review would corrupt both records. Conversely, the defense cannot use uncertainty about these boundaries to exempt an ordinary high-risk onboarding, make one person count as two, or perpetuate a pilot. These examples establish specific errors in M0-based measurement. The share of all deviations attributable to those errors is unknown.

Review the 14 ineligible and nine incomplete auto cases individually, verify validation and authority for the 289 apparently eligible, and reconcile all six August activations. Establish the exposure through clearance or retrospective approval using actual transactions.

M0 is a valid description of the ordinary full-onboarding control flow and an inadequate standalone compliance test. I reject the prosecution's inference from raw replay error to policy breach where provenance, equivalent work, an operative directive or an undefined case boundary explains the mismatch. I reject the defense's claim that mismeasurement explains away the proven failures or authorizes permanent automation. There is no total number of in-scope onboardings, joint distribution of discrepancies, or fully adjudicated exception population here. The 15 cases illustrate mechanisms and cannot supply a prevalence estimate. Report raw trace fit, reconciled fit, substantiated policy breaches, justified exceptions and unresolved cases separately, with versioned scope denominators and overlap identified.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

1. **Define the case before replay.** Introduce a lead ID and a recorded admission decision linked to the onboarding case ID. A failed pre-screen can end with a reasoned lead rejection; notification and closure are recorded in the intake lifecycle. Admission starts full onboarding, whose rejection branch retains both checks. Record unresolved historical boundaries explicitly. Give reactivation its own case and link to the prior supplier and case. Require current sanctions clearance and independent manual approval before activation. Credit a prior quality review only under an approved reuse rule covering supplier identity, certification scope, expiry, revocation and material changes. Pending such a rule, require fresh review for prospective reactivation. For withdrawal, record the authorized initiator, reason, time and lifecycle state. A verified withdrawal before disposition can terminate without completing activation gates, with notice and closure. After activation use a separately governed suspension or offboarding process. Preserve completed checks and distinguish withdrawal from rejection and abandoned work. These are prospective scope decisions requiring owner approval.
2. **Represent visible ordering in POWL.** Use a standard route with unordered mandatory checks, bounded-by-decision information requests, and a manual-approve/activate or reject choice. Model pilot and emergency provisional activations as separate alternatives. Require a notification after disposition and before closure on every completed route. Allow notification after provisional activation while follow-up is still pending. Make completion of outstanding required work a condition of closure. Replay the original M0 as issued and the revised design as separately versioned views.
3. **Use declarative constraints for obligations across events.** Examples include activation preceded by a successful, in-scope sanctions check, mutually exclusive activation and rejection in an onboarding episode, and closure preceded by notification for that disposition. Correlate each information request to a response or evidence receipt before the decision that depends on it. Use a response obligation for outstanding quality review after provisional activation and for outstanding manual approval on the emergency route. Track open, overdue and fulfilled obligations as of the audit cut. Plain DECLARE or a log skeleton can test presence, exclusivity and precedence. Matching IDs, result values and business-day deadlines require data-aware evaluation.
4. **Evaluate factual predicates in a data checker.** Use as-of joins in pandas or equivalent queries for risk, supplier country, approved-group membership, route, policy version and authority at activation. Compare source occurrence times and results for sanctions, master-data validation and approval. Use the applicable local timezone and holiday calendar for the two-business-day limit. Aggregate the covered spend through retrospective approval and reconcile daily monitoring against expected days. Record pass, fail or unknown for each predicate. Enforce resolvable gates in the transactional system before enabling the supplier. Reserve unknown historical facts for adjudication.
5. **Apply identity and conflict rules independently.** Resolve actor aliases at the event time to human or service principals. For every required manual approval, compare the requester and actual approving human and verify the latter's authority, including any delegation in force. Record both the actual actor and the delegator. The same person using a delegated login still fails separation. Record the automation principal and grant on an authorized auto decision. Test disclosure obligations against effective relationships and timely disclosures in the designated channels. Any new recusal or disqualification rule needs its own prospective authority.
6. **Govern overlays as narrow grants.** Name the issuing body, delegation chain, displaced clauses, residual obligations, scope, effective instants and sunset. Compliance and the competent policy owner must resolve the historical status of PD-3 from contemporaneous instruments. For future changes require a controlled decision, signed version, implementation owner and enforced expiry. Bind one applicable exception grant to the activation, with a reason. In overlapping July windows, evaluate the chosen grant in full and retain the protections it leaves in force. Combining a pilot substitution with emergency timing would need express authority. Expiry prevents new uses while monitoring and follow-up continue for qualifying activations already made. N5 supports proposing the request loop. Permanent auto-approval requires a new effective policy decision.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

This executable model covers completed, admitted onboarding cases. The XOR selects a structural route. In enforcement or adjudication, bind the route to the applicable grant *before* accepting a replay. An unconstrained alignment could otherwise accept SUP-705 by selecting the emergency branch. The late sanctions check in SUP-708 fails every route. The intake, interrupted-withdrawal and reactivation lifecycles in section 6 have separate entry and termination rules.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def event(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def sequence(*steps):
    order = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(steps))
    # Store the transitive relation, including every earlier/later pair.
    for index, earlier in enumerate(steps):
        for later in steps[index + 1:]:
            order.order.add_edge(earlier, later)
    return order


def parallel(*steps):
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(steps))


def choice(*branches):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(branches))


def information_requests():
    # Binary POWL loop: do the silent body, then optionally redo and repeat.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), event("Request_More_Info")],
    )


def checks_and_standard_decision():
    before_decision = parallel(
        event("Sanctions_Check"), event("Quality_Review"), information_requests(),
    )
    approve = sequence(
        event("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), event("Activate_Supplier"),
    )
    return sequence(
        before_decision, choice(approve, event("Reject_Supplier")),
        event("Notify_Requester"),
    )


def pilot_route():
    before_activation = parallel(
        event("Sanctions_Check"),
        event("Automated_Master_Data_Validation"),
        sequence(information_requests(), event("Auto_Approve")),
    )
    activate_and_notify = sequence(
        before_activation, event("Activate_Supplier"), event("Notify_Requester"),
    )
    return parallel(activate_and_notify, event("Quality_Review"))


def emergency_route():
    before_activation = parallel(event("Sanctions_Check"), information_requests())
    activate_and_notify = sequence(
        before_activation, event("Activate_Supplier"), event("Notify_Requester"),
    )
    return parallel(
        activate_and_notify,
        event("Quality_Review"),
        event("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    )


M1 = sequence(
    event("Register_Supplier"),
    choice(checks_and_standard_decision(), pilot_route(), emergency_route()),
    event("Close_Case"),
)
M1.validate_partial_orders()
```

The loop has visible language zero or more Request_More_Info events. On the standard route it can interleave requests with either check and must finish before the decision. The loop does not count outstanding documents; evidence IDs carry that obligation. On provisional routes the independent quality node permits both early and deferred review, including notice before deferred review. Emergency manual approval can likewise occur on either side of activation. The join before Close_Case is a proposed closure safeguard. Use the activation-state attribute and bound grant to distinguish a provisional Activate_Supplier from a standard activation with the same label. Open provisional cases need deadline checks even if no complete trace is yet available.

**Event and data collection.**

- Give each event an immutable ID, case/episode ID, supplier ID, source system, source transaction and event-within-transaction key, raw label, lifecycle transition, decision/result, effective occurrence timestamp with timezone, and first-recorded/ingested timestamp. Capture actual activation and release of provisional restrictions as state changes. Represent corrections by append-only superseding records. Preserve raw rows and the reconciliation used for replay. Deduplicate SUP-711 using the source event identity and migration provenance; legitimate repeated registrations in different episodes must survive.
- Maintain versioned activity mappings with source procedure/UI version, equivalence evidence, effective interval, approval and recorded-at times. This supports both a replay using the dictionary available on the case date and an evidence-corrected replay for SUP-713. Require attested reason and provenance for a mapping applied to older events.
- Bind risk, supplier country, legal entity, group membership and the group's approved status to an immutable eligibility snapshot, with source values, validity intervals and record times. Link the sanctions outcome, quality work product, automated validation run/result, request/response/evidence IDs and timestamps to the actual decision. Record admission, pre-screen rejection, withdrawal and reactivation reasons and prior-case/certificate references. Missing eligibility or validation yields an unknown check in historical analysis and prevents prospective automated release.
- Record the activation route, provisional status, applicable policy and rule/version IDs, emergency incident or pilot enrollment, issuer, decisions and delegation evidence. Link transaction-level spend to activation and approval using timestamps and currency amounts. Define the covered ledger and, where needed, the governed EUR conversion basis. Check the cumulative balance at each spend, including a threshold crossing before a later correction. Store daily monitoring attestations, calendar/timezone version, due times, completion times and retrospective approval. Treat a late review as late even if it is eventually recorded as complete.
- Persist source actor aliases, identity-provider IDs, versioned alias-to-person links, service principal, requester, actual approver, delegator, authority scope and validity. Link restricted-access conflict declarations, relevant relationship/reporting-line dates, disclosure time and any disposition to the case. A missing link or an unresolved alias should trigger investigation.
- Generate a stable notification_id at dispatch and persist it in both systems with immutable case and disposition IDs, intended recipient, send status, source send time, receipt where applicable and record time. Compare the verified send time with closure. Keep mirrored duplicates identifiable and retain link-edit history. Reconcile CRM sends to expected notices by source, date and coverage period. Report success, complete-source omission, and unresolved linkage distinctly. The post-July coverage estimate cannot repair an individual missing link.

The audit output should let a reviewer move from each case finding to the raw events, applicable grant, rule evaluation and evidence available at the time. That permits enforcement of the high-risk failures while making corrections to the model or log visible and reproducible.
