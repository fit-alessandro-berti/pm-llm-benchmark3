## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is a rule that assigns each event to zero or more case identifiers. Flattening on type T makes every T-object a case. An event appears in the trace of each T-object that is directly in its E2O set, and events with no T-object are dropped. It is a lens chosen for a question, not a property of the data.

**Convergence.** One event related to k > 1 objects of the chosen type is replicated k times. For example, e16 appears in O1, O2 and O3 under Order flattening. This inflates activity and edge frequencies and gives shared durations k-fold weight.

**Divergence.** One case contains several instances of the same activity that belong to different objects of another type. Flattening orders them only by timestamp, which produces self-loops and false pairings. Examples in O1 are e11/e12 (for P1/P2) and e15/e16 (for D1/D2).

**Copied shared events are both evidence and duplication.** They are evidence because e16 really did dispatch goods of O1, O2 and O3, so for per-object questions ("when did O3's goods leave?") the copy is legitimate. They are duplication because for workload or occurrence questions ("how many dispatches happened?") one event is one unit. My operating rule is that every metric must declare its unit:
- event-based (deduplicated on event id);
- object-based (copies counted);
- weighted (each copy weighted 1/k).

**Permitted transformations:**
- Deduplication on event id and 1/k copy weighting.
- Relabelling activities with existing O2O qualifiers: e22 becomes "Record Payment [partially_settles]" (via M2) and e25 "[completes_settlement]" (via M3). No events are created.
- O2O propagation, but only as a named variant that is kept separate from the direct-E2O baseline. It must be scoped: push Order-level events to Items through `contains` only for events that have no direct Item relation.
- No inferred events and no timestamp repair. The tie between e29 and e30 (both 11:05) is harmless inside per-order traces but matters for any global ordering.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

The traps are numbered T1–T10 so they are not confused with delivery identifiers.

**T1 – Cross-order package P3 (Order flattening).** e13 is copied into O2 and O3, so "Pack Package" has 6 rows for 4 events. In O3, e13 (10:12) followed by e14 (10:20) creates a Pack→Pack self-loop that reads like repacking. In reality O3 is split across P3 and P4, and each of those packages is shared with a different order. *Context-dependent:* the loop correctly shows that O3 was split, but it is harmful as a rework signal and as a frequency.

**T2 – Cross-order package P4 couples O3's and O4's performance (Order flattening).** e14, e17 and e27 are shared by O3 and O4, so both inherit D3's overnight transit of 22h45m. O3's own item I31 was confirmed at 16:00 on Feb 3 (e24), but O3's delivery phase only ends at e27 (Feb 4, 10:00). Order-level "time to delivery" therefore blames O3 for a delay caused by consolidating I32 with O4's I41. *Informative* about real coupling, but *harmful* if read as O3's own performance.

**T3 – Cross-order packages become invisible (Package flattening).** P3 and P4 look like P1 (Pack→Dispatch→Confirm), and only e16/e24 are duplicated. The trap is false cleanliness: the log cannot show that 2 of 4 packages mix orders. *Harmful* for consolidation questions, *informative* for pack-to-dispatch durations.

**T4 – Mispaired durations from divergence (Order flattening).** In O1, the Pack→Dispatch edge pairs e12 (P2 packed at 10:10) with e15 (D1 dispatches P1 at 11:00), giving 50 min. In O3, it pairs e14 (P4, 10:20) with e16 (D2, which carries P3 and not P4), giving 45 min. Neither pair shares a package. The true package-level times are P1 60, P2 55, P3 53 and P4 55 min. *Harmful.*

**T5 – Consolidated delivery D2 (Order and Delivery flattening).** e16 and e24 are each tripled (O1, O2, O3). As a result, Dispatch has 6 rows for 3 events and Confirm Delivery has 6 for 3. O1 shows Dispatch→Dispatch after 5 min (e15→e16) and Confirm→Confirm after 2h (e23→e24), which look like re-dispatch or redelivery. D2's 4h55m transit is weighted three times in order-level averages. Under Delivery flattening, D2 is one of three identical two-event cases with no sign that it served three orders. *Context-dependent:* triple weighting is right for "customer orders affected" but wrong for "carrier performance".

**T6 – Consolidated invoicing V2 (Order and Invoice flattening).** e19 is copied into O2 and O3, so there are 4 "Issue Invoice" rows for 3 invoices. Summing invoice amounts per order would double-count V2. Under Invoice flattening, V2 looks like a normal invoice, and the fact that it bills two orders is lost. *Harmful* for counts and money. *Informative* in one respect: O2's and O3's closures both depend on V2's settlement (e25), which is a real shared dependency.

**T7 – Partial payments M2/M3 (Order and Invoice flattening).** O2 and O3 each show Record Payment (e22) → Confirm Delivery (e24) → Record Payment (e25). That reads as each order being paid twice: 4 payment rows for what is really two instalments of one invoice (6 Record Payment rows from 4 events overall). In the Invoice log, V2 gets a Record Payment→Record Payment self-loop lasting 20h20m. The Issue→Record Payment edge also averages V2's partial payment (35 min) with V3's full payment (21h20m), which understates V2's real time to settlement (20h55m). The activity label does not carry the `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement` qualifier. *Harmful* unless relabelled; *informative* once it is.

**T8 – Near-singleton lifecycles (Payment and Delivery).** M1–M4 are four one-event traces: one variant ("Record Payment"), no DFG edges, zero case duration, and a trivial log skeleton. Delivery traces have only two events, one variant and three cases, so no conformance question can be asked. Delivery durations are clean (3h, 4h55m, 22h45m), but D3's figure spans a night, which biases averages unless business hours are applied. *Payment is harmful* as a case notion. *Delivery is useful* as a KPI table and useless for discovery.

**T9 – Cross-lifecycle interleaving (Order flattening).** Record Payment→Confirm Delivery occurs 5 times: O1 e21→e23, O2 e22→e24, O3 e22→e24 and e25→e27, and O4 e26→e27. Confirm→Record Payment occurs twice (O2, O3). These are timestamp interleavings of two independent branches (invoice and delivery), not causal links. Because Dispatch is always followed by an invoicing event, **Dispatch→Confirm Delivery never appears as a direct edge**, so transit time cannot be read from the Order DFG at all. *Harmful:* the DFG suggests that payment triggers delivery confirmation.

**T10 – Close Order batching (Order flattening).** All Close Order events fall between 11:00 and 11:10 on Feb 4, so order cycle times look uniform (26h00–26h04). Yet O1 was paid by 12:30 (e21) and fully delivered by 16:00 (e24) on Feb 3, leaving 19h idle before e28, while O3 and O4 close about 1h after e27. Order cycle time therefore measures closing policy, not fulfilment. *Context-dependent.*

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs worth creating:**

1. **Order log, copy-aware.** Keep the event id and add columns for `copy_weight = 1/k` and `is_shared`. Safe questions:
   - per-order milestones (first pack, last Confirm Delivery, final payment, Close);
   - order-level compliance, e.g. "Close Order after the last Confirm Delivery and after full settlement", which holds for O1–O4;
   - start and end activities.

   Unsafe: raw activity and edge frequencies, and any duration between Pack, Dispatch, Confirm and Record Payment (T4, T9).
2. **Package log.** Use it for pack→dispatch→confirm durations (only +20% duplication, no divergence).
3. **Item log.** Use it only for per-item completeness: each of I11–I41 is packed, dispatched and confirmed exactly once.
4. **Invoice log with Record Payment relabelled by qualifier.** Use it for invoice-to-first-payment vs invoice-to-final-settlement, and for instalment prevalence (1 of 3 invoices).

**Not created as logs:** Delivery (report the three transit times as a table) and Payment (treat it as an attribute of the invoice lifecycle).

**Questions that need object-centric artefacts:**
- **OC-DFG:** activity counts by events, unique objects and total objects, and edges separated by object type. Record Payment→Confirm Delivery appears only on Order arcs and never on Invoice or Delivery arcs, which shows it is interleaving (T9).
- **ET–OT graph:** shows two branches joined only by Order. Pack, Dispatch and Confirm relate to Item, Package and (for the latter two) Delivery. Issue Invoice and Record Payment relate to Invoice and (for payments) Payment. Create and Close relate to Order only.
- **OTG:** type-level cardinalities. Order–Package is many-to-many (O1→P1, P2; P3→O2, O3), Order–Invoice is many-to-one (V2), and Invoice–Payment is one-to-many (V2→M2, M3).
- **Object interaction summary:** answers "whose performance is entangled?":
  - O2–O3 share e13, e16, e19, e22, e24, e25;
  - O3–O4 share e14, e17, e27;
  - O1 shares e16 and e24 with both O2 and O3;
  - O1–O4 share nothing.

  All four orders therefore form one connected interaction component.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering on Order:** tests whether the four Order variants reflect four structural patterns or mostly interleaving noise.

**Reconciling contradictions:**
1. Make every metric state its unit, and recompute contested counts at event level from the OC-DFG. For example, Dispatch has 6 order-rows but 3 events.
2. Accept an ordering claim only if some object type that is related to both activities supports it as an OC-DFG arc. Otherwise label it interleaving.
3. For performance, report event-weighted and object-weighted values side by side. "D3 delayed two orders" and "D3 is one of three deliveries" are both true, for different stakeholders.
4. Trace every contested finding back to identifiers.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: Order as primary lead object, with Package/Item (fulfilment) and Invoice (finance) as explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria:**
- **Event coverage.** Order covers 31/31 events, Item 16, Package 10, Invoice 7, Delivery 6 and Payment 4.
- **Bridging role in the ET–OT graph.** Only Order links the logistics and finance branches.
- **Business meaning of "end-to-end".** The customer order is created (e01–e04) and closed (e28–e31) only at order level.
- **Correctable distortion.** Duplication is measurable (11 copies) and can be neutralised with weighting.
- **Separability.** Each secondary question has a lower-distortion log of its own.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** No other type sees both "Pack Package" and "Issue Invoice". Choosing any other lead would drop between 15 (Item) and 27 (Payment) of the 31 events, and would drop Create Order and Close Order entirely. So "end-to-end" is only definable through Order.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order is exactly where both many-to-many consolidations bite:
- 6 of the 10 logistics events are multi-order (e13, e14, e16, e17, e24, e27);
- 3 of the 7 finance events are multi-order (e19, e22, e25);
- the 4 orders produce 4 distinct variants;
- O3's 13-event trace is mostly inherited from objects it shares (P3, P4, D2, D3, V2).

On this reading, Order is a hub rather than a case, which argues for C. B answers this by limiting Order to milestones and compliance, moving performance questions to Package and relabelled Invoice, and making the OC-DFG the arbiter of frequency and ordering claims.

**Residual risk:**
- Stakeholders may still read the Order DFG, with its false Payment→Confirm edges, as "the process".
- Order cycle time stays dominated by batch closing (T10), and O3's delivery KPI by O4's consolidation (T2).
- With four orders, every order-level statistic is anecdotal.

**Conditions under which I would change my decision:**
- **Switch to C** if payments settled several invoices (many-to-many on the finance side as well), or if Order E2O links on logistics events were missing and only recoverable by propagation.
- **Switch to A with Package** if the scope narrows to warehouse and carrier performance.
- **Switch to A with Order** only if cross-order packing and invoicing become rare (for example, fewer than about 10% of events being multi-order).
- **Revisit** if equivalence clustering shows the order variants collapsing into a single class, which would mean the variants are interleaving noise.

```python
import copy
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------- 0. Inventory ----------------
print(ocel)

events_df = ocel.events
objects_df = ocel.objects
rel_df = ocel.relations          # ocel:eid, ocel:activity, ocel:timestamp, ocel:oid, ocel:type, ocel:qualifier
o2o_df = ocel.o2o                # ocel:oid, ocel:oid_2, ocel:qualifier
n_events = events_df["ocel:eid"].nunique()

activity_freq_events = events_df["ocel:activity"].value_counts()
object_type_freq = objects_df["ocel:type"].value_counts()
object_types_per_activity = pm4py.ocel_object_type_activities(ocel)
activity_x_type = rel_df.groupby(["ocel:activity", "ocel:type"]).agg(
    events=("ocel:eid", "nunique"),
    unique_objects=("ocel:oid", "nunique"),
    total_objects=("ocel:oid", "size"),
)
print(activity_freq_events, object_type_freq, object_types_per_activity, activity_x_type, sep="\n\n")

objs_per_event_type = rel_df.groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:eid"])["ocel:oid"].nunique()
shared_events = objs_per_event_type[objs_per_event_type > 1].reset_index(name="n_objects")
print(shared_events)

# ---------------- 1. Baseline flattenings (direct E2O only) ----------------
OBJECT_TYPES = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
flat_logs, flat_dfg, flat_perf_dfg, flat_skeleton, flat_rows = {}, {}, {}, {}, []

for ot in OBJECT_TYPES:
    log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flat_logs[ot] = log
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    flat_dfg[ot] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_acts, "end": end_acts}
    flat_perf_dfg[ot] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)[0]
    flat_skeleton[ot] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log, noise_threshold=0.0)
    covered = log["ocel:eid"].nunique()
    flat_rows.append({
        "object_type": ot,
        "cases": log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "rows": len(log),
        "events_covered": covered,
        "coverage": covered / n_events,
        "duplication_factor": len(log) / covered,
        "variants": len(pm4py.get_variants(log)),
        "mean_trace_length": log.groupby("case:concept:name").size().mean(),
    })

flat_summary_df = pd.DataFrame(flat_rows).set_index("object_type")
print(flat_summary_df)

# ---------------- 2. Copy-aware Order log ----------------
order_log = flat_logs["Order"].copy()
copies = order_log.groupby("ocel:eid")["case:concept:name"].transform("nunique")
order_log["copy_weight"] = 1.0 / copies
order_log["is_shared_event"] = copies > 1

order_activity_freq = pd.DataFrame({
    "rows": order_log["concept:name"].value_counts(),
    "weighted": order_log.groupby("concept:name")["copy_weight"].sum(),
    "events": order_log.groupby("concept:name")["ocel:eid"].nunique(),
})
order_log_unshared = order_log[~order_log["is_shared_event"]]
order_dfg_unshared = pm4py.discover_dfg(order_log_unshared)
order_trace_len_unshared = order_log_unshared.groupby("case:concept:name").size()
print(order_activity_freq, order_trace_len_unshared, sep="\n\n")

order_milestones = order_log.pivot_table(
    index="case:concept:name", columns="concept:name", values="time:timestamp", aggfunc=["min", "max"]
)
closed_after_last_delivery = (
    order_milestones[("max", "Close Order")] > order_milestones[("max", "Confirm Delivery")]
)

# ---------------- 3. Payment relabelling from O2O qualifiers ----------------
payment_ids = set(objects_df.loc[objects_df["ocel:type"] == "Payment", "ocel:oid"])
payment_qualifier = o2o_df[o2o_df["ocel:oid"].isin(payment_ids)].set_index("ocel:oid")["ocel:qualifier"]
eid_to_payment = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Payment"].set_index("ocel:eid")["ocel:oid"]
eid_to_payment_label = eid_to_payment.map(payment_qualifier).map(lambda q: f"Record Payment [{q}]")

invoice_log_relabelled = flat_logs["Invoice"].copy()
is_payment = invoice_log_relabelled["ocel:eid"].isin(eid_to_payment_label.index)
invoice_log_relabelled.loc[is_payment, "concept:name"] = (
    invoice_log_relabelled.loc[is_payment, "ocel:eid"].map(eid_to_payment_label)
)
invoice_dfg_relabelled = pm4py.discover_dfg(invoice_log_relabelled)
invoice_perf_relabelled = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(invoice_log_relabelled)[0]

# ---------------- 4. Explicit propagation variant (Order -> Item via 'contains') ----------------
contains = o2o_df[o2o_df["ocel:qualifier"] == "contains"][["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]]
contains.columns = ["order_oid", "item_oid"]
item_linked_eids = set(rel_df.loc[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Item", "ocel:eid"])

order_rel = rel_df[rel_df["ocel:type"] == "Order"]
order_level_rel = order_rel[~order_rel["ocel:eid"].isin(item_linked_eids)]

def propagate_to_items(order_relations):
    return (order_relations[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:oid"]]
            .merge(contains, left_on="ocel:oid", right_on="order_oid")
            .drop(columns=["ocel:oid", "order_oid"])
            .rename(columns={"item_oid": "ocel:oid"})
            .assign(**{"ocel:type": "Item", "ocel:qualifier": "propagated_via_contains"}))

def with_relations(base_ocel, extra_rel):
    new_ocel = copy.deepcopy(base_ocel)
    new_ocel.relations = (pd.concat([base_ocel.relations, extra_rel], ignore_index=True)
                          .drop_duplicates(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]))
    return new_ocel

ocel_prop_scoped = with_relations(ocel, propagate_to_items(order_level_rel))
ocel_prop_naive = with_relations(ocel, propagate_to_items(order_rel))

item_log_scoped = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_prop_scoped, "Item")
item_log_naive = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_prop_naive, "Item")
propagation_comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "direct": [len(flat_logs["Item"]), len(pm4py.get_variants(flat_logs["Item"]))],
    "scoped_propagation": [len(item_log_scoped), len(pm4py.get_variants(item_log_scoped))],
    "naive_propagation": [len(item_log_naive), len(pm4py.get_variants(item_log_naive))],
}, index=["rows", "variants"])
false_item_links = item_log_naive[
    (item_log_naive["case:concept:name"] == "I11") & (item_log_naive["ocel:eid"].isin(["e16", "e24"]))
]
print(propagation_comparison, false_item_links, sep="\n\n")

# ---------------- 5. Object-centric artefacts ----------------
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

def ocdfg_activity_metrics(ocdfg_dict, activity):
    act_ot = ocdfg_dict["activities_ot"]
    return pd.DataFrame({
        ot: {
            "events": len(act_ot["events"][ot][activity]),
            "unique_objects": len(act_ot["unique_objects"][ot][activity]),
            "total_objects": len(act_ot["total_objects"][ot][activity]),
        }
        for ot in act_ot["events"] if activity in act_ot["events"][ot]
    }).T

def edge_support_by_type(ocdfg_dict, edge):
    return {ot: len(couples[edge])
            for ot, couples in ocdfg_dict["edges"]["event_couples"].items() if edge in couples}

frequency_metrics = {a: ocdfg_activity_metrics(ocdfg, a)
                     for a in ["Pack Package", "Dispatch", "Confirm Delivery", "Record Payment", "Issue Invoice"]}
suspect_edges = [("Record Payment", "Confirm Delivery"), ("Confirm Delivery", "Record Payment"),
                 ("Pack Package", "Pack Package"), ("Dispatch", "Dispatch"), ("Pack Package", "Dispatch")]
edge_support = {edge: edge_support_by_type(ocdfg, edge) for edge in suspect_edges}
interleaving_edges = [e for e, support in edge_support.items() if set(support) == {"Order"}]

order_rel_pairs = order_rel[["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]]
order_coupling = (order_rel_pairs.merge(order_rel_pairs, on="ocel:eid", suffixes=("_a", "_b"))
                  .query("`ocel:oid_a` < `ocel:oid_b`")
                  .groupby(["ocel:oid_a", "ocel:oid_b"])["ocel:eid"].apply(sorted))
print(frequency_metrics, edge_support, interleaving_edges, order_coupling, sep="\n\n")

# ---------------- 6. Equivalent-execution clustering on the primary lead ----------------
order_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")
order_cluster_sizes = {key: len(members) for key, members in order_clusters.items()}
print(order_cluster_sizes)

# ---------------- 7. Same KPI under different leads ----------------
def mean_hours(perf_dfg, edge):
    stats = perf_dfg.get(edge)
    return None if stats is None else stats["mean"] / 3600

transit_by_lead = {ot: mean_hours(flat_perf_dfg[ot], ("Dispatch", "Confirm Delivery"))
                   for ot in ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery"]}
pack_to_dispatch_by_lead = {ot: mean_hours(flat_perf_dfg[ot], ("Pack Package", "Dispatch"))
                            for ot in ["Order", "Item", "Package"]}

semantic_review = {
    "flattening_summary": flat_summary_df,
    "order_rows_vs_events": (len(order_log), n_events),
    "shared_order_events": sorted(shared_events.loc[shared_events["ocel:type"] == "Order", "ocel:eid"]),
    "edges_supported_only_by_order": interleaving_edges,
    "transit_hours_by_lead": transit_by_lead,
    "pack_to_dispatch_hours_by_lead": pack_to_dispatch_by_lead,
    "close_after_last_delivery": closed_after_last_delivery.to_dict(),
    "propagation_comparison": propagation_comparison,
}
for key, value in semantic_review.items():
    print(f"\n=== {key} ===\n{value}")
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**S1 – Removing or down-weighting copied shared events.** Compare raw, 1/k-weighted and "unshared-only" Order logs.
- Expected: Dispatch drops from 6 rows to 3 weighted units, and Record Payment from 6 to 4.
- Removing shared events entirely cuts O2 to e02, e07, e29 and O4 to e04, e10, e20, e26, e31. That collapse is the finding: most of each lifecycle is shared.
- Decision rule: a frequency or bottleneck finding is kept only if its ranking survives weighting.

**S2 – Direct versus explicitly propagated relations.** Scoped propagation (Order-level events pushed to items only where no Item link exists) grows the Item log from 24 to 51 rows (+65% over 31 events). V2's events e19, e22 and e25 each land in three item traces (I21, I31, I32).
- I32 thus inherits V2's instalments even though it travels on D3 with O4's I41.
- Naive propagation yields 67 rows and false links, such as I11 receiving e16/e24 (I11 went on D1) and I32 receiving e13 (I32 was packed in P4).
