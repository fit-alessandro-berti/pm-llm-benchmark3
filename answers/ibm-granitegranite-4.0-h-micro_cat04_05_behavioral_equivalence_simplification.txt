# 1. Equivalence Notions

### Visible Trace Equivalence after Hiding 

Two process trees are considered to have equivalent visible traces after hiding  if they result in identical sequences of observable events when the  delays are abstracted away. This means that only the sequence of process steps, including approvals, revisions, and case closures, matter, not the time delays between steps.

### Bounded Visible-Language Equivalence under P3

For bounded testing (P3), equivalence is determined by comparing two process trees that have been simplified to contain at most 12 visible events and at most three occurrences of the Analyze Revision activity. The trees must produce identical sequences of visible actions within this constraint. The order of identical events is significant, as is the presence or absence of approval actions (Approve Revision) and the exact interleaving of Analyze Revision and Check Revision activities.

### Business-Relevant Equivalence under P1

Under operational compliance (P1), equivalence is defined by ensuring that every analysis revision must be followed by a Check Revision before a Rework Case or a Close Case. This means that the sequence of events, including the mandatory placement of Check Revision after each Analyze Revision, must be identical between Models A and B. The presence or absence of the optional Approve Revision action does not affect equivalence, as it is considered silent behavior.

### Abstracted Dashboard Equivalence under P2

For executive dashboard purposes (P2), equivalence requires that the visible sequence of events must be the same when only the key actions are considered: Open Case, at least one Analyze Revision, at least one Check Revision, and Close Case. The exact interleaving of these actions is irrelevant, as well as the exact placement of approval actions, which are collapsed into a Boolean fact indicating whether any approval occurred.

**Note:** Duplicate visible labels are distinguished by their occurrence position under P2 and P3, and by identity under P1, meaning that the sequence and placement matter for P1 and P3, while only identity matters for P2.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

Stochastic analysis using `pm4py.discover_footprints` and `pm4py.get_stochastic_language` could provide insights into the probability distribution of observable events in both models. However, these analyses do not establish trace equivalence. Equal footprints or a low Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) between languages would indicate similarity in the distribution of observed behaviors but would not prove that every possible behavior in one model can be replicated by the other. Trace equivalence requires a one-to-one correspondence of every possible sequence of events, which stochastic and footprint analyses alone cannot guarantee.

# 9. Final Recommendation

Based on the analysis, Model A is more appropriate for operational compliance (P1) due to its detailed representation of approval processes and concurrency. Model B is more suitable for executive dashboard (P2) scenarios where the focus is on the high-level sequence of events without the complexity of approvals and concurrency. The simplified model derived from P1 captures the essential operational compliance aspects and is recommended as the primary model for operational use. It should replace Model B for P1 and P3 views due to its more precise representation of the required behaviors. The simplification does not compromise the core operational compliance requirements, making it a robust choice for these business views. However, caution is advised when transitioning from bounded testing to real-world scenarios, as the simplification abstracts certain details that may be critical in specific contexts.
