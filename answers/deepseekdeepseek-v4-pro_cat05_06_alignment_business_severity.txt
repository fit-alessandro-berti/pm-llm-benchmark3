## 1. Materiality Framework

A severity framework must go beyond raw alignment cost. It evaluates business harm and control impact. The following dimensions are weighed, but no single dimension dominates; human judgment assesses overall severity on a 1–5 scale, where 1 = negligible, 2 = low, 3 = moderate, 4 = high, 5 = critical.

**Control objective**: How badly is a mandatory control violated? A missing fraud screen before payment to a highrisk beneficiary is far more damaging than a duplicate notification retry.

**Amount / exposure**: Higher amounts (e.g., >€50000) elevate severity, but not mechanically; a €4500 autoapproval with a bank change is higher risk than a €18000 clean autoapproval without other red flags.

**Reversibility**: An irreversible payment (money already left the bank) is typically worse than a missing event that can be retrodocumented. Payment before approval (CL604) is severe because it cannot be easily unwound.

**Timing**: Regulatory deadlines (notification within 24h) or sequence violations (“approve after pay”) affect severity. Delayed notification may be minor if it still meets the contractual limit; payment before approval is a temporal control failure.

**Customer / regulatory harm**: Financial loss to claimant, breach of dataprivacy (not in scope here), or breach of regulatory rules (e.g., no approval for large claims) drive severity upwards.

**Recurrence**: A single incident with a plausible oneoff cause is less alarming than a repeated pattern indicating systemic weakness. For example, four dataaware autoapproval failures across a month signal tooling or training gaps.

**Evidence reliability**: Strength of proof that the deviation actually occurred. A missing `Fraud Screen` event with only a verbal claim has lower confidence than a bankconfirmed payment timestamp before approval. Confidence is assessed separately: High (multiple reliable sources), Medium (plausible but not fully corroborated), Low (untrusted or missing evidence).

**Model scope**: Cases outside the model’s intended domain (denied claims, withdrawals) generate high raw cost but almost zero business risk, because process owners never intended the model to cover them. Scope disputes must be resolved before alignment cost can be interpreted as nonconformance.

Confidence differs from severity: a highseverity finding may have low confidence (e.g., suspicion of fraud without proof), while a lowseverity artefact may be confirmed with high confidence. Remediation decisions must rely on severity, but investigative steps depend on confidence gaps.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost, calculated as the sum of move penalties under a uniform cost function (every visible log/model move costs 1), cannot substitute for businessrisk assessment. The following reasons, illustrated with supplied cases, show the gap:

**1. Costfunction design ignores control severity.**  
Case CL604 (cost=3) registers a payment before approval – a critical, potentially irreversible control failure. Case CL601 (cost=3) contains three harmless UItelemetry moves. Both receive the same raw cost, yet their business risks are worlds apart. The cost function has no weight for control criticality or reversibility.

**2. Zerocost can conceal policy violations.**  
CL609 and CL610 have raw_cost=0 because the process tree permits the Auto Approve branch; the alignment is perfectly fitting. However, both violate business policy P1 (amount >€5000, bank change). If a manager relied on cost alone, they would miss highrisk noncompliance.

**3. Scope mismatches inflate cost without business risk.**  
CL607 (denial) and CL612 (withdrawal) have costs of 4 because the model requires activities that were never planned. P6 underscores that the model scope is disputed. Treating these costs as operational deviations would absorb investigative effort on legitimate outcomes.

**4. Hidden moves (tau) are fitted with zero cost but may hide model overengineering.**  
CL605 shows a silent routing transition (cost=0) that is an artefact of Petrinet conversion. While harmless, it illustrates that zero cost does not mean a simplified or “good” process; it merely fits the model.

**5. Activity granularity and telemetry capture nonbusiness events.**  
CL601 demonstrates that `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench` are UI telemetry excluded by P5. The cost function penalises them equally with missing fraud checks. Without business context, an alarm would be raised for pure system noise.

**6. Duplicate events produce low cost but mask system health issues.**  
CL603 (cost=1) duplicates a notification because of a retry connector. The business risk is negligible, but the root cause (lack of idempotency) could affect future critical messages. Cost alone cannot flag the need for connector remediation.

**7. Missing events with external evidence (low cost, audit risk).**  
CL606 (cost=1) lacks an Assess Loss event, but an external adjuster report proves the activity occurred. The low cost misrepresents the genuine audittrail weakness, which could allow future undetected omissions. Confidence in cost is not confidence in business completeness.

**8. Irreversibility is not captured.**  
A payment before approval (CL604) has the same uniform move cost as a duplicate notification (CL603) if the number of displaced moves is similar. But the former has irreversible financial and regulatory consequences; the uniform cost function is blind to this.

In summary, raw alignment cost is a syntactic distance under a given model and a uniform cost function. Business risk requires semantic interpretation of which moves occurred, under what data conditions, and with what control impact. The two metrics must be kept strictly separated: cost informs potential anomalies; business context converts them into risks.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The code below:
- Parses the normative process tree.
- Converts it to an accepting Petri net.
- Constructs a minimal event log from the known log traces (only the events actually logged per the supplied alignments).
- Runs alignment diagnostics and notes that PM4Py may produce alternative optimal alignments or formatting differences.
- Builds a pandas DataFrame of the supplied move summaries, case attributes (amount, risk, policyoverlay flags), and aggregates patterns.
- Produces a riskprioritisation evidence table that separates technical alignment cost from business severity, without replacing human judgement with a formula.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree
from pm4py.objects.conversion.process_tree import converter as pt_converter
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as alignments
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.util import constants
import pandas as pd
import datetime

# 1. Parse process tree
tree_string = "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)

# 2. Convert to Petri net with visible transitions, hiding tau if any (the tree may introduce silent transitions)
net, im, fm = pt_converter.apply(tree, variant=pt_converter.Variants.TO_PETRI_NET)

# 3. Build event log from the logside events of the supplied cases (S and L moves only, no M moves)
log_data = []  # list of (case_id, trace_list, amount, risk, policy_ok_flag)

    "CL-607": (["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim", "Notify Claimant"], 0, "N/A", True),  # denied, no amount
    "CL-608": (["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim"], 250000, "HIGH", True),  # missing Notify
    "CL-609": (["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"], 18000, "MEDIUM", False),  # policy violation amount
    "CL-610": (["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"], 4500, "LOW", False),  # policy violation bank change
    "CL-611": (["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Damage", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"], 62000, "HIGH", True),  # label mismatch

# Note: PM4Py output format may differ from the supplied authoritative notation.

print("PM4Py alignment results (first 3 traces):")
for i, ali in enumerate(alignment_results[:3]):
    print(f"Case {log[i].attributes['concept:name']}: cost {ali['cost']}")
    # alignment steps are a list of tuples, could be printed but will differ in representation

# 5. Construct dataframe of supplied move summaries and case attributes
move_summaries = [
    ("CL-601", "S(Reg),S(Val),L(OpenWB),S(Assess),L(Autosave),S(AutoApp),S(Pay),S(Notify),L(CloseWB)", 3, 1200, "LOW", True, 1),
```
