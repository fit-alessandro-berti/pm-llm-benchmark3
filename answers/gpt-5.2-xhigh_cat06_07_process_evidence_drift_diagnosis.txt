## 2. Competing explanations and timeline
**January–March (stable baseline):** Clean data quality (UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY ~0.2–0.3%, missing queue feature 1%), full label maturity, and consistent breach (~0.18–0.19). Discrimination and calibration are strong (AUROC ~0.81–0.82; ECE ~0.022–0.025). This anchors “normal” model behavior.

**April (pipeline/schema incident; not a real behavioral shift):** The workflow migration renames `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`, but the feature pipeline initially only recognizes the old label and also emits duplicated/UNKNOWN activities. April shows a classic signature of **feature corruption** rather than true risk inflation: observed breach stays ~0.19, but mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28 and calibration collapses (ECE 0.110) with AUROC 0.67. Process evidence aligns: 17% UNKNOWN activity share, 14% missing queue feature, and 18% unmapped/other variants.

**May (partial recovery; residual feature-lineage debt):** A mapping patch (6 May) and backfill reduce UNKNOWN activity to 2% and missing queue feature to 3%, and AUROC recovers to 0.78. However, calibration remains worse than baseline (ECE 0.050) and mean risk remains elevated (0.21 vs breach 0.18), consistent with the audit note that some derived features (e.g., cached “events before diagnosis”) were not fully recomputed for April/backfilled data.

**June (real routing/process mix change; not necessarily concept drift):** The remote-routing policy increases V2 share to 52% and reduces overall breach to 0.15, while diagnosis→Remote Fix time remains stable (3.8–4.6h). Discrimination remains reasonable (AUROC 0.79), suggesting the model can still rank cases, but the mean score (0.20) is high relative to the new base rate—consistent with **process mix/intervention** shifting outcomes without the model being recalibrated for the new post-diagnosis regime.

**July (early supplier-driven performance drift; localized concept/process shift):** Supplier issues begin 10 July; the parts edge `Order Part -> Part Received` median increases to 65h. The V3 breach rate rises sharply to 0.46, while V1/V2 remain near prior levels. Overall AUROC drops to 0.76, indicating the model is starting to lose ranking power in the affected segment (parts-dependent cases) even though global mean prediction and breach are still close (0.18 vs 0.19).

**August (severe supplier impact + rising V3 share; genuine risk shift):** Parts lead time balloons to 130h; V3 share rises to 35% and V3 breach to 0.64. Observed breach jumps to 0.28 while mean predicted risk stays 0.19, flipping the April pattern (April overprediction from pipeline bug; August underprediction from real operational change). AUROC and calibration deteriorate substantially (AUROC 0.68; ECE 0.120). This is best explained by **process-performance drift** (longer parts waits) inducing **concept drift** (higher breach probability conditional on pre-diagnosis features).

**September (overlapping supplier drift + population shift from Partner Z; partial label maturity):** Partner Z arrives (24% of starts) with more rural customers and older devices (unmodeled), and routes more to V3 (58% vs 36%). V3 share rises to 42%; parts lead time further worsens (148h). Observed breach remains high (0.31) with mean risk 0.21 and AUROC 0.66. Multiple effects overlap: ongoing supplier-driven concept/process drift, **population shift** (new partner), and **label-delay bias** (only 82% mature; Partner Z matures more slowly), so September cannot be reduced to a single drift label.

**October (evaluation heavily confounded by label maturity/backlog):** Label maturity collapses to 55% with median label delay 52d; the outcome API backlog disproportionately affects longer, complex paths (which are also higher risk). The observed breach rate (0.16) is therefore not comparable to prior mature months; the apparent overprediction (mean risk 0.23) and degraded AUROC (0.64) likely reflect censored labels and selective missingness. October is primarily a **label-maturity/instrumentation** month, not a clean signal of model deterioration.

Why these are not one homogeneous failure:
- **April**: corrupted prefixes/features (overprediction + data-quality spikes).
- **June**: genuine routing intervention changing base rate and variant mix (needs recalibration/causal awareness, not “broken model”).
- **August–September**: real operational degradation in the parts pathway (performance-DFG evidence) plus increased V3 share and new partner population (underprediction + within-segment drift).
- **October**: metrics dominated by delayed/missing outcomes (do not compare directly).

## 3. Diagnostic tests
Below, each test includes expected outcomes under competing hypotheses.

1) **Label-maturity curves + fixed-vintage backtesting**
- **Method:** For each month’s start cohort, compute breach rate and model metrics as a function of case age (or time since promised resolution). Recompute AUROC/Brier/ECE on a fixed vintage (e.g., all cases started in month, evaluated only once they are ≥90 days old or once labels are ≥95% mature).
- **Expected if label delay/censoring dominates (especially Oct):** Observed breach rises as labels mature; mean-predicted vs observed gap shrinks; AUROC volatility reduces and may improve when evaluated on fully mature vintages. “At-cutoff” October metrics are systematically biased.
- **Expected if true model drift dominates:** Even on fully mature vintages, AUROC/Brier/ECE remain degraded vs Jan–Mar (and vs post-fix May–Jun), and score-vs-outcome curves stay shifted.

2) **End-to-end recomputation after diagnosis-label normalization + duplicate cleaning**
- **Method:** Rebuild April–May event logs with (i) `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` → canonical diagnosis label, (ii) consistent duplicate-event removal across sources for all of April, (iii) full recomputation of derived features (including cached “events before diagnosis” and queue features), then rescore and reevaluate.
- **Expected if data-pipeline failure explains April:** April mean predicted risk falls toward the ~0.18–0.19 range; AUROC and ECE rebound near Jan–Mar; unmapped/UNKNOWN shares drop; performance metrics become comparable to adjacent months.
- **Expected if genuine behavioral drift occurred in April:** Even with corrected logs, AUROC/ECE remain poor and score distribution stays shifted.

3) **Stratified calibration and discrimination by month × (eventual) variant × source**
- **Method:** Using mature labels only, compute AUROC, Brier, ECE and reliability curves within slices: eventual V1/V2/V3; Partner Z vs non-Z; and their intersections where sample sizes allow. Compare mean predicted risk to observed breach within each slice.
- **Expected under process-mix change only (June):** Within each eventual variant, calibration/discrimination remain stable; only the overall mixture changes (overall breach drops as V2 rises). Any miscalibration is mostly an intercept/base-rate shift.
- **Expected under supplier-driven concept drift (Jul–Aug):** Within eventual V3, observed breach at a given score increases (upward shift in calibration curve) and AUROC may drop; V1/V2 slices remain closer to baseline.
- **Expected under population shift (Partner Z):** Degradation concentrates in Partner Z (and covariate regions unique to Z); non-Z resembles August baseline when conditioned on variant.

4) **Process-variant and DFG / performance-DFG comparisons**
- **Method:** Monthly variant distribution (including “unmapped”), DFG structure drift (new/unknown activities), and performance-DFG edge times—especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`.
- **Expected under pipeline failure (April):** Structural artifacts: high UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY, elevated unmapped variants, and inconsistencies in prefixes around diagnosis; after normalization, structure stabilizes.
- **Expected under supplier/process-performance drift (Jul–Sep):** DFG structure may remain similar, but performance-DFG shows large increases on the parts edge; diagnosis→Remote Fix remains stable, isolating the drift to parts-dependent flow.

5) **Outcome relationship conditional on the same path**
- **Method:** For eventual V3 cases, compare breach rate by score decile across time windows (Jan–Jun vs Jul–Sep). Alternatively fit simple per-month logistic calibration within V3: \(\text{logit}(P(\text{breach}))=\alpha_m+\beta_m \cdot \text{score}\).
- **Expected under concept drift:** \(\alpha_m\) increases (and/or \(\beta_m\) changes) from July onward for V3, indicating the same score implies higher breach probability.
- **Expected under pure covariate drift:** Calibration parameters within V3 are stable; only the distribution of scores/covariates changes across months.

6) **Population reweighting / transportability checks for Partner Z**
- **Method:** Estimate diagnosis-time covariate density ratios and reweight non-Z cases to match Partner Z (or train a domain classifier to derive weights). Evaluate whether Z performance is explained by covariate shift. Also test “support” (how much of Z lies outside non-Z feature ranges).
- **Expected if population shift dominates:** Reweighting reduces the calibration/discrimination gap; remaining error is concentrated in out-of-support regions and improves after adding missing covariates (distance/device age).
- **Expected if global concept drift dominates:** Both Z and non-Z degrade similarly once conditioned on variant; reweighting yields limited improvement.

7) **Feature missingness + lineage audit (pipeline-level)**
- **Method:** Track per-month rates: missing queue feature, fraction of cases with recognized diagnosis event, events-before-diagnosis distribution, duplicate-rate, UNKNOWN activity share, and schema version; join to model-score logs to see which features are defaulted.
- **Expected under pipeline failure:** April spikes in missingness/UNKNOWN/duplication coincide with score shift; after recomputation, these metrics normalize and the score shift disappears.
- **Expected under real drift:** Pipeline metrics remain stable while performance metrics degrade.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision
**Immediate containment (days):**
- Treat **April as a data incident**: remove it from performance trendlines and any automated triggers; recompute April logs and derived features end-to-end after activity normalization and consistent deduplication.
- Add **label-maturity gating**: compute AUROC/Brier/ECE and breach rates only on cohorts with high maturity (e.g., ≥95%) or on fixed vintages; flag October metrics as provisional.
- Freeze automated retraining and any “self-healing” loops until pipeline + label maturity are green.

**Short-term recalibration (1–3 weeks, once pipeline is clean):**
- Perform a **global recalibration** (e.g., Platt/temperature/isotonic) using **mature, pipeline-clean months**. Practically: Jan–Mar and May–Jul are candidates, but only include May after confirming the cached-feature backfill issue is resolved.
- Because June’s routing policy changes the base rate, prefer **time-aware or regime-aware calibration** (e.g., separate intercept pre/post June) rather than refitting the ranking model.
- Do not use October for calibration; use September only with fixed-vintage maturity, and consider a separate calibrator for Partner Z if a stable gap persists after maturity adjustment.

**Feature repair (weeks to 1–2 months):**
- Add diagnosis-time features needed for the new regimes: **partner/source ID**, **geography/rural distance proxies**, **device age**, and parts-risk proxies available at diagnosis (device family/part family, historical parts lead time by region/supplier).
- Introduce a **routing/variant-likelihood component** at diagnosis (predict probability of ending in parts-dependent flow) and use a **mixture-of-experts** style risk estimate:
  - \(P(\text{breach}) = \sum_{v \in \{V1,V2,V3\}} P(v \mid x_{\leq \text{diagnosis}})\, P(\text{breach} \mid x_{\leq \text{diagnosis}}, v)\).
  - This adapts to June’s policy shift and August’s V3 inflation without leaking post-diagnosis events.

**Full retraining (after labels mature and outcome backlog is resolved):**
- **Unsafe for training now:** April (pipeline failure), October (55% maturity + biased missingness), and September until fixed-vintage maturity (Partner Z matures more slowly).
- **Potentially safe:** Jan–Mar (clean), May–Jun (post-fix + post-routing change), and July once confirmed mature and pipeline-consistent.
- **Conditional:** August (93% mature) can be included only after verifying the remaining 7% missing labels are not systematically the longest/highest-risk cases.
- Retrain with regime coverage (post-June routing, post-July supplier changes, Partner Z) once those months have mature labels.

**Segment-/variant-specific model?**
- Justified if diagnostics show persistent, stable residual structure by **partner** or by **predicted parts-likelihood**, not merely by month.
- Prefer a **shared backbone + segment calibrators** (or hierarchical effects) before splitting into separate models to avoid sample-size brittleness and to reduce operational complexity.

## 5. Monitoring architecture
**A. Data-quality & lineage monitors (ingestion/pipeline first)**
- Schema/version monitor (pre/post migration).
- Activity mapping coverage for the diagnosis event; fraction of cases where the prediction prefix is built at the correct event.
- UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY share; unmapped/other variant share.
- Duplicate-event rate (by source and by time window); parity of deduplication rules across sources.
- Feature completeness monitors: missing queue feature rate; “events-before-diagnosis” distribution; default-value rates in the scoring logs.
- Alert routing: these alarms page the data/pipeline owner and **suppress** model-performance alarms for affected vintages.

**B. Label-maturity / outcome ingestion monitors (evaluation validity)**
- Label maturity at cutoff by case-start month and by partner; median/percentile label delay.
- Backlog monitor: unserved outcomes by case age; missingness correlated with path length.
- Alert dependency: if maturity < threshold or backlog correlates with long paths, mark model metrics “provisional” and block retraining triggers.

**C. Predictive performance & calibration monitors (on mature cohorts)**
- Discrimination: AUROC/AUPRC by month and slice.
- Calibration: Brier, ECE, reliability curves; mean predicted risk vs matured breach rate.
- Slice monitors: by eventual variant (post-hoc), by partner/source, by geography buckets, and by predicted parts-likelihood.

**D. Process-variant and process-evidence monitors**
- Variant mix: V1/V2/V3/unmapped share by case-start month (captures June policy and Partner Z mix).
- DFG structure drift: new/rare activities, sudden increases in “other” variants (captures April-style mapping failures).
- Performance-DFG edges: medians/quantiles for `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`, and any dispatch/repair edges; alert on sustained shifts.

**E. Population & support monitors**
- Partner Z share; covariate drift at diagnosis time (distance/device age proxies, rurality, device family).
- Out-of-support detection: fraction of Partner Z cases outside training feature ranges; triggers feature expansion or conservative prediction policies.

**F. Intervention/deployment monitors**
- Routing-policy changes; supplier SLA changes; onboarding events; pipeline patch deployments.
- Explicitly annotate dashboards so regime shifts (June policy, July supplier) are not misread as unexplained model failure.

**Alert dependencies (example gating logic)**
- If **label-maturity alarm** fires → freeze retraining and suppress performance regressions for the affected months; page outcome-ingestion owner.
- Else if **data-quality alarm** fires → invalidate metrics for impacted vintages and page pipeline owner.
- Only if label maturity is high AND data quality is green AND mature-cohort performance degrades for ≥N weeks → open a retraining ticket.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pandas.tseries.offsets import MonthEnd

CANON_DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
DIAG_ALIASES = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": CANON_DIAG}

def monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str = "field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path, parse_dates=["timestamp"])

    # Normalize renamed diagnosis label before any feature/process extraction
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(DIAG_ALIASES)

    # Duplicate handling: drop exact repeats and repeated scans at same timestamp
    df = (df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
            .sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]))

    # Standardize columns for PM4Py
    df = df.rename(columns={
        "case_id": "case:concept:name",
        "activity": "concept:name",
        "timestamp": "time:timestamp",
    })
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )

    # Two cohorting views:
    # - event-calendar months: what events arrived in that month (good for pipeline/schema drift)
    # - case-start months: which cases started in that month (good for "cases started" performance evidence)
    case_start = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()

    months = pd.period_range(df["time:timestamp"].min().to_period("M"),
                             df["time:timestamp"].max().to_period("M"),
                             freq="M")
    rows = []
    for m in months:
        start = m.to_timestamp()
        end = start + MonthEnd(1)

        df_evt = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
        unknown_share = float(df_evt["concept:name"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()) if len(df_evt) else 0.0

        case_ids = case_start[(case_start >= start) & (case_start <= end)].index
        df_cases = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(case_ids)]

        # Structure + performance evidence
        _dfg, _sa, _ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_cases)
        perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_cases, aggregation_measure="median")
        edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        part_edge_median_h = (perf_dfg[edge] / 3600.0) if edge in perf_dfg else None

        variants = pm4py.get_variants(df_cases)
        top_variants = sorted(((v, len(c)) for v, c in variants.items()),
                              key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:3]

        _temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df_cases)  # auditable features, no modeling

        rows.append({
            "month": str(m),
            "events_in_month": int(len(df_evt)),
            "unknown_activity_share": unknown_share,
            "cases_started": int(len(case_ids)),
            "top_variants": top_variants,
            "orderpart_to_partreceived_median_h": part_edge_median_h,
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis
1) **Retraining on immature / censored labels (especially October)**
- **Failure mode:** The model learns an artificially low breach rate (because long, high-risk cases are missing labels) and becomes systematically underpredictive once labels catch up.
- **Mitigation:** Fixed-vintage training windows; maturity gating (≥95%); censoring-aware objectives or delayed-label correction; exclude months with outcome backlog until resolved.

2) **Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April)**
- **Failure mode:** Recalibration “fits” the corrupted score distribution caused by misrecognized diagnosis events and missing features, locking in wrong mappings and masking the need for pipeline repair.
- **Mitigation:** Treat pipeline anomalies as a separate incident class; block calibration updates when unknown-activity/missing-feature monitors breach; recompute features end-to-end before any calibration.

3) **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept/process drift and only “fixing metrics”**
- **Failure mode:** The system continues to underpredict V3 breaches in Aug–Sep regimes; operations rely on falsely reassuring scores and miss proactive escalation/contingency actions.
- **Mitigation:** Couple model monitoring with performance-DFG edge monitors (parts lead time) and operational playbooks; add parts-risk features; retrain with post-drift data once mature.

4) **Attributing Partner Z population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence**
- **Failure mode:** Overcorrecting globally (e.g., blanket threshold shifts) harms non-Z performance and may still not fix Z underperformance, while mislabeling a representativeness/feature-support problem as “bias.”
- **Mitigation:** Mature, stratified evaluation by partner and covariates; support checks; transport/reweighting; add missing covariates; apply partner-specific calibration only if gap persists after maturity adjustment.

5) **Overreacting to June’s routing intervention as “model decay”**
- **Failure mode:** Retraining on a transient policy regime encodes policy effects and can degrade robustness when routing changes again; may conflate intervention impact with predictive signal.
- **Mitigation:** Treat routing changes as interventions; prefer recalibration/regime-aware intercept shifts; monitor variant mix explicitly; use causal/regime annotations in evaluation.

6) **Chasing month-to-month noise instead of regime shifts**
- **Failure mode:** Frequent ad hoc updates increase operational risk and obscure root causes (pipeline vs label maturity vs real process degradation).
- **Mitigation:** Define retraining triggers that require (i) mature labels, (ii) clean data-quality lineage, and (iii) sustained degradation on mature cohorts with corroborating process evidence (e.g., performance-DFG shifts).
