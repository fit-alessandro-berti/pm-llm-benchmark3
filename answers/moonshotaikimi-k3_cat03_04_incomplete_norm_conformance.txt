## 1. Conformance semantics

- **Forbidden behavior:** An observed relation explicitly marked **forbidden** and falling within the stated scope. Frequency is irrelevant: one valid instance is a violation. If the edge may be an extraction artifact, the finding remains a containment issue until the artifact is disproved.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** An in-scope object or event lacks a relation marked **mandatory**. This conclusion requires confirmation that the scope applies, the objects/events are correctly identified, and the abstraction can actually observe the required relation.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** An observed relation is neither mandatory, typical, nor forbidden. Under the open-world assumption, it is not a violation merely because it is absent from the normative graph. It may still motivate a separate business, security, or data-quality review.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** A relation marked **typical** is desirable but not required. Its absence is compatible with the stated exceptions and cannot be treated as nonconformance without an additional frequency or threshold rule.
- **Likely data-quality error:** The reported edge or missing edge is probably caused by identifier reuse, faulty joins, incomplete extraction, timestamp problems, or truncated history. This concerns the reliability of the observation; it does not by itself prove that the underlying business behavior was compliant.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** The business rule and the observed abstraction answer different questions. In particular, an “eventually reaches” rule cannot be faithfully evaluated by a directly-follows edge when intermediate events are allowed.
- **Insufficient evidence:** The summary cannot establish scope, identity, timing, event semantics, or lifecycle completeness well enough to classify the observation definitively.

The norm is closed-world for explicitly forbidden relations and for mandatory relations inside their stated scope. It is open-world otherwise. Consequently, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, risk-review relations, customer relations, and `object_codeath` edges are not violations solely because no normative edge represents them. Conversely, a mandatory gap cannot be dismissed merely by calling the model open-world: scope and abstraction must first be examined.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can establish observed directly-follows behavior within an object lifecycle and is strong evidence for an explicitly forbidden sequence such as N3. It cannot, without further querying, establish causality, eventual reachability, missing events, concurrency, or lifecycle completeness.

Counts and denominators matter. N4 concerns all 200 externally shipped Packages, so the two missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` relations are in scope and cannot be ignored. N5 concerns only a typical pattern: 92 payments, 4 write-offs, and 14 open or unfinished Invoices are compatible with the stated exceptions. The counts for `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` and `Validate Order -> Close Order` need not be exclusive and therefore cannot be used to infer missing behavior.

The most important danger is N1. The norm requires every newly created standard Order to **eventually** enter validation, while the summary uses a **directly-follows** approximation. If an unrecorded review sits between creation and validation, the eventual rule may be satisfied even though the direct edge is absent. Conversely, observing the direct edge does not prove that validation was timely or correctly performed. Eventual-path queries are therefore required before calling the two N1 gaps violations.

### OTG

The OTG establishes object-to-object relationships such as interaction, cobirth, and codeath. Edge weights are not necessarily unique object counts and do not, by themselves, prove that a relationship occurred at a particular event.

Thus, 200 Package–Delivery interaction edges do not cure the two Dispatch Package events lacking Delivery under E6: those OTG edges may arise from later confirmation events. Likewise, the single Order–Payment cobirth edge is forbidden only if “cobirth” means true creation in the same business event, not merely identical import timestamps. The seven Package–Delivery `object_codeath` edges are unregulated because the norm is silent about co-death.

### ET–OT

ET–OT evidence is event-specific and is therefore the appropriate abstraction for E2, E6, and E8. It can show that two `Add Item` events lack an Order, two `Dispatch Package` events lack a Delivery, and one `Record Payment` event lacks an Invoice. It cannot establish process ordering or causality.

The same fact can be acceptable at one abstraction and suspicious at another. Invoice–Payment interaction is only typical under T3, so its general absence is permissible; however, E8 currently makes an Invoice mandatory for every `Record Payment` event unless explicitly typed as unapplied cash. Because no such event is typed, the one missing Invoice is formally a mandatory gap even though the business explanation may ultimately be legitimate. Similarly, a Package and Delivery may interact somewhere in the OTG while the required Dispatch-event relation remains absent in the ET–OT view.

```python
        elif observation.get("suspected_data_quality_error"):
            deviation_class = "missing_mandatory_or_data_quality_error"
        else:
            deviation_class = "missing_mandatory_behavior"

    elif status == "typical":
        deviation_class = "typical_pattern; absence_is_not_a_violation"

    elif status == "unspecified":
        deviation_class = "unexpected_but_unregulated"

    else:
        deviation_class = "insufficient_evidence"

    return {
        "norm_id": observation["norm_id"],
        "norm_status": status,
        "semantic_class": deviation_class,
        "metadata": metadata,
        "observation": observation,
        # Refer to, but do not replace, the raw PM4Py diagnostic family.
        "raw_pm4py_family": metadata["family"],
    }

semantic_findings = [semantic_classify(o) for o in observation_register]

combined_results = {
    # Preserve PM4Py output exactly as returned.
    "pm4py_raw_diagnostics": copy.deepcopy(raw_diagnostics),

    # Add business interpretation separately.
    "semantic_findings": semantic_findings,

    # Keep status and scope metadata outside the graph objects.
    "norm_metadata": NORM_METADATA,
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

| Gap or ambiguity | Operational risk | Proposed clarification | Recommended status |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 mixes eventual validation with a directly-follows diagnostic | Valid Orders may be falsely flagged; genuinely unvalidated Orders may be missed | Require eventual validation and define permitted intermediate events and maximum delay | Eventual validation: mandatory; timing rule: mandatory if required |
| N2 does not define closure prerequisites | Orders might close without required fulfillment, billing, or review | State which conditions must precede `Close Order`, or explicitly leave closure elsewhere | Mandatory for true prerequisites; otherwise explicitly unspecified |
| N4’s external-shipment scope lacks exceptions | Prepacked, digital, pickup, or internal movements may be misclassified | Define external shipment and any excluded package categories | Mandatory for in-scope external shipment; exclusions explicitly unspecified |
| T2/E6 do not say whether a carrier attribute can replace a Delivery object | Teams may treat an attribute as satisfying an object relationship | Require a Delivery object at dispatch; treat carrier reference as supporting data | Mandatory |
| E8’s unapplied-cash exception depends on absent labeling | Legitimate cash may appear noncompliant, or broken reconciliation may be excused | Define allowed type values and require controlled labeling before invoking the exception | Label: mandatory; Invoice–Payment relation: typical |
| N3 lacks effective-time and simultaneous-event rules | Timestamp order, time zones, or in-flight shipments may create disputes | Define cancellation effective time and state that dispatch at or after that time is prohibited | Forbidden |
| T6 does not distinguish true cobirth from shared import timestamps | Batch loading can create false violations | Define business-event identity and object-birth attribution | True cobirth: forbidden; shared load timestamp: explicitly unspecified/DQ |

| No tolerance or minimum-frequency semantics are stated | Typical patterns may be turned into arbitrary statistical violations | State that every forbidden instance matters and typical patterns have no threshold unless separately defined | Mandatory interpretation rule |

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**

1. **Cancel-to-dispatch cases:** Verify the two N3 instances, hold or recall shipments where possible, preserve Order/Package/event evidence, and assess customer and inventory impact.
2. **Sensor–Invoice edge:** Quarantine the edge and the responsible join or synchronization rule before it propagates into financial reporting. Preserve the raw records before repair.
3. Inspect the single Order–Payment cobirth case and prevent posting if the objects were genuinely created in one prohibited transaction.

**Data repair**

- Introduce namespace-aware identifiers to prevent reused numeric keys from joining sensor and Invoice objects.
- Reconstruct the two missing Dispatch–Delivery links from carrier and delivery source records; do not treat a carrier attribute alone as norm satisfaction.
- Verify and repair the two Add Item–Order links.
- Confirm whether missing Pack events were unrecorded and backfill only with authoritative evidence.
- Label the one payment as unapplied cash only after finance approval; otherwise link it to the correct Invoice.

**Process investigation**

- Determine why cancellation did not block dispatch and whether warehouse or carrier integration ignored the cancellation state.
- Determine whether the two N1 Orders eventually reached validation.
- Investigate packing exceptions and the one unlinked payment.
- Review open Invoices operationally, while recognizing that N5 does not make them conformance violations.

**Norm revision**

Resolve the ambiguities in Section 6, especially eventual-versus-direct validation, Delivery-object requirements, unapplied-cash labeling, cancellation effective time, and cobirth semantics.

**Monitoring**

- Alert on every N3, T6, and E10 candidate, with provenance attached.
- Monitor mandatory completeness by exact scope and event, not only aggregate OTG weights.
- Track typical patterns as trends without automatic violation escalation.
- Do not escalate risk reviews, reopening, customer links, or `object_codeath` merely because they are rare.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The robust conclusions are the reported counts, the in-scope mandatory gaps for N4, T1/E2, T2/E6, and E8, and the fact that N3, T6, and E10 describe forbidden relations if the observed identities and semantics are accurate. Conclusions about extra relations depend on the norm’s deliberate incompleteness: `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, risk-review links, customer links, reopening, and `object_codeath` are not violations under this norm. The Sensor–Invoice edge, missing Delivery links, and cobirth case depend materially on suspected data-quality or attribution problems. Graph summaries alone cannot establish eventual validation, causality, effective cancellation timing, true object birth, complete lifecycle history, whether the same two Packages account for multiple gaps, or the business legitimacy of the unapplied payment.
