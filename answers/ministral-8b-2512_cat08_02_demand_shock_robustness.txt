Here is the structured redesign proposal for the diagnostic-lab sample process, adhering to the constraints and requirements:

---

### **1. Stress Scenarios**
Four plausible scenarios based on case-mix shifts, disruptions, and their compounding effects:

| Scenario | Case-Mix Shift | Disruption | Key Stressors | Likely Bottlenecks |
|----------|----------------|------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Winter Spike** | Urgent +20%, Complex-panel +15% | Courier delays (10h+ arrival spikes) | Pre-analytical check, Assay M1/M2, Medical validation | Recollection loops, batch waits, specialist review |
| **Machine Outage** | Routine +10%, Urgent +5% | Assay M2 fails (50% capacity loss) | Assay run, Batch wait | Routine cases starved; Urgent cases rerouted to M1 |
| **Specialist Absence** | Complex-panel +25% | Medical validator unavailable (3 days) | Hold for specialist review, Medical validation | Backlog in Complex-panel cases; Urgent cases delayed |
| **Courier Collapse** | Repeat-after-failure +12% | Night-shift courier strikes (no sample intake after 16:00) | Receive sample, Pre-analytical check | Recollection loops; Assay machines idle due to lack of input |

---

### **2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis**
#### **Winter Spike**
- **Initial bottleneck**: Pre-analytical check (9.5h median queue) due to increased Complex-panel cases (higher error rates).
- **Secondary bottleneck**: Assay M1/M2 (18h queue) as Urgent cases bypass batching, fragmenting machine schedules.
- **Tertiary bottleneck**: Medical validation (22h queue) as Complex-panel cases and Urgent cases compete for validators.
- **Migration risk**: Relieving pre-analytical check (e.g., I6) may push more cases into Assay M1/M2, exacerbating fragmentation.

#### **Machine Outage (M2 Failure)**
- **Initial bottleneck**: Assay M1 (100% utilization) as Routine cases are rerouted, increasing batch wait times.
- **Secondary bottleneck**: Batch wait (14h queue) as Routine cases accumulate, delaying Urgent cases.
- **Tertiary bottleneck**: Technical validation (16h queue) as Assay M1 output spikes.
- **Migration risk**: Urgent cases bypassing batching (I1) may starve Routine cases, increasing rework.

#### **Specialist Absence**
- **Initial bottleneck**: Hold for specialist review (36h queue) as Complex-panel cases pile up.
- **Secondary bottleneck**: Medical validation (24h queue) as cases wait for review.
- **Tertiary bottleneck**: Assay M1/M2 (18h queue) as Complex-panel cases are delayed, increasing repeats.
- **Migration risk**: Outsourcing Complex-panel (I10) may relieve review but add transport delay, worsening traceability.

#### **Courier Collapse**
- **Initial bottleneck**: Receive sample (7.5h queue) as night-shift samples are delayed.
- **Secondary bottleneck**: Pre-analytical check (9.5h queue) as recollection loops form.
- **Tertiary bottleneck**: Assay M1/M2 (18h queue) as input is sporadic.
- **Migration risk**: Automating registration (I8) may expose assay bottlenecks if samples arrive in uneven bursts.

---

### **3. Robust Redesign Package**
**Core Interventions (Activated Always)**
| Intervention | Robustness Rationale | Assumptions | Failure Mode |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------|--------------|
| **I6: Pre-analytical defect triage** | Reduces recollection loops (760  300 cases) by rejecting poor samples early, freeing pre-analytical check capacity. | Requires 24/7 triage staff; must not delay Urgent cases. | If triage is slow, queues shift to Receive sample. |
| **I4: Cross-train 2 technical validators** | Relieves accession correction (62%  40% utilization) without starving technical validation. | Validators must be trained in 4 weeks; must not reduce validation quality. | If validation errors rise, rework increases. |
| **I7: Smooth courier pickup slots** | Reduces arrival spikes (10:00/17:00) by staggering routes, lowering Receive sample queues. | Requires courier coordination; may not work for urgent samples. | If Urgent cases still arrive in spikes, Assay M1/M2 may fragment. |
| **I2: Increase batch sizes (12  24)** | Improves preparation efficiency for Routine cases (reduces batch wait from 6h  3h). | Must pair with strict priority rules for Urgent cases. | If Urgent cases are delayed, rework may rise. |

**Why Robust?**
- **I6 + I4** address the most volatile steps (pre-analytical and validation) without creating new bottlenecks.
- **I7** smooths input variability, preventing cascading delays.
- **I2** optimizes Routine case throughput without starving Urgent cases if paired with I1 (discussed below).

### **6. Residual Vulnerabilities**
1. **Fragmentation Risk from I1 (Urgent-first dispatch)**:
   - If Urgent cases bypass batching, Routine cases may cluster in Assay M1/M2, increasing repeats.
   - *Mitigation*: Monitor batch wait time; if >12h, pause I1 temporarily.

2. **Outsourcing Dependency (I10)**:
   - Partner lab transport delay (12h) may violate Urgent (18h) SLA if cases are rerouted late.
   - *Mitigation*: Only activate I10 if Hold for specialist review >36h.

3. **Assay M2 Reliability**:
   - M2’s higher failure rate (seen in peak periods) could overwhelm M1 if M2 fails.
   - *Mitigation*: Preemptively reroute Routine cases to M1 during M2 degradation.

4. **Redesign-Induced Starvation**:
   - If I3 (dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex-panel) is active, Routine cases may be delayed even if M2 is available.
   - *Mitigation*: Use dynamic routing rules based on M2 utilization.

**Qualitative Warning Bands**:
- **Fragmentation**: >30% of Assay M1/M2 runs are single-Urgent cases.
- **Starvation**: Routine cases wait >24h in Assay M1/M2 while M2 is idle.
- **Triage Failure**: >10% of rejected samples are incorrectly accepted.

---
This redesign avoids optimizing for historical averages, accounts for bottleneck migration, and uses process-mining evidence to guide interventions. The core package (I6, I4, I7) is robust, while scenario-specific policies adapt to disruptions without overloading downstream steps.
