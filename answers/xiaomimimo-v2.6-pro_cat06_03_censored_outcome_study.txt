### 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

Can claim:
- Within the observed policy _obs and the matured activated cohort, an as-of-prefix predictor of 90-day default risk, with quantified calibration and discrimination on temporally forward data.
- Descriptive selection analysis: how activated cases differ from rejected/withdrawn/transferred/open at t0.
- Competing-outcome route probabilities (T2) for all applications.

Cannot claim:
- Default risk for rejected/withdrawn applicants (undefined under _obs; requires counterfactual assumptions + positivity which hard policy rules violate).
- Causal effect of acceptance/limit on default (final_limit is a mediator; no randomized variation).
- Validity for partner-originated accounts with missing feeds without extra assumptions.
- Validity after2025-10-15 migration (label-maturity prevents evaluation at this extract).
- Calibration in sub-populations with poor overlap.

Distinguish: predictive association (P(Y|X)) vs selection effects (conditioning on A=1, plus label availability R=1) vs counterfactual risk (E[Y(1)|X]).

At least three ways deployment changes the DGP:
1. **Policy feedback / performative prediction**: using the score to accept/reject changes the composition of activated accounts  the conditional estimand's population changes; base rate shifts; the model's calibration breaks (self-confirming bias) and rejected-by-model applicants' outcomes are never observed (no feedback  cannot detect errors).
2. **Threshold & limit effects**: if the score sets `final_limit` or pricing, it changes exposure  changes default severity/probability (treatment-confounder feedback). Intervening on the mediator.
3. **Collection/allocation effects**: if the score routes high-risk cases to enhanced verification or collections, it changes both the observed label (more `Collections Contact`, earlier `Default Recorded`  detection/ascertainment bias in `default_90d`) and the true outcome (mitigation).
4. **Operational/queue effects**: model-driven prioritization changes resource queues and cycle times  shifts the prefix timing features the model uses (feedback into features).
5. **Channel/partner substitution**: stricter underwriting pushes volume to partners/transfers  increases the unobserved-outcome fraction (selectively missing feeds)  the monitored population drifts away from the training population.
6. **Migration/version drift**: system renames and snapshot semantics change again.

Good — that's 5-6.

Also decision-use boundary: the score may be used as (i) post-activation early warning/limit review/collections triage (defensible, target defined); (ii) pre-decision as a *conditional* score "risk if activated", usable for ranking among applications the policy would accept (defensible with overlap diagnostics), but not as a binary default label for the whole funnel; (iii) it must NOT be used as the accept/reject rule without a T3-style analysis + prospective monitoring + a champion/challenger with a randomized holdout to restore identifiability (recommend: retain a small randomized "accept-override" holdout (e.g., 1–2% of marginal cases) to generate labels on currently-unobserved strata — that's a nice recommendation to mention).
