The shared causal mechanism between sites is task concurrency: when independent verification activities execute simultaneously, duration compresses from serial addition (\(T_{\text{id}} + T_{\text{doc}}\)) toward the supremum (\(\max(T_{\text{id}}, T_{\text{doc}})\)), directly reducing `waiting_before_assessment` and `cycle_time`, accompanied by a shared quality risk where decoupled checks slightly elevate `rework`.

Site-specific mechanisms dominate Site B's environment. First, physical document gating (`originals_required`) introduces an 18-hour postal arrival delay for 62% of cases, preventing immediate document verification. Second, downstream statutory constraints (`legal_capacity` and legal review loops) route 40% of claims through a 2-FTE bottleneck with rework loops (`Request Supplement`), absorbing upstream time gains. Third, legacy software locking (`IT_case_lock`) mechanically prevents simultaneous multi-user case editing.

The critical effect modifiers are `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, and `case_complexity`. These variables govern the fraction of cases capable of true concurrency and determine whether parallelization alleviates or exacerbates queue congestion.

A similar control-flow diagram (AND-split/join) does not guarantee transportability because graphical workflow equivalence only defines logical reachability. It omits operational physics: physical prerequisite delays, mutex locking, queueing capacities across specialized resource pools, and downstream rework loops. When background contextual variables diverge (represented by \(S\)-nodes), identical structural interventions yield radically divergent empirical outcomes.

### Quantitative Mapping and Scenario Reasoning
1. **Digital Overlap Calculations:** For digital claims (38%), sequential service time is \(3.4\text{h} + 8.1\text{h} = 11.5\text{h}\). Under parallel execution, duration is \(\max(3.4, 8.1) = 8.1\text{h}\), yielding an immediate 3.4h direct service overlap. Eliminating intermediate queue handover saves an additional 2.5h to 3.5h, producing a 6h to 9h reduction for the digital cohort. Weighted across the full population (\(0.38 \times 7.0\text{h}\)), this contributes approximately -2.7h to the overall median.
2. **Physical Claims Delay Barrier:** For physical claims (62%), the 18h median delay for Originals Receipt acts as an uncompressible lower bound for document checking. Running Identity Check (3.4h) during this 18h postal window absorbs the identity service duration completely, saving ~3.4h of post-arrival processing only if the case lock is relaxed.
3. **Bottleneck Migration Dynamics:** Accelerating front-end verification increases arrival rates at Assessment (\(\lambda_{\text{assess}}\)). With only 3 Assessors handling ~130 cases/week and 2 Legal Reviewers handling ~52 cases/week, queue utilization (\(\rho\)) increases from ~0.82 toward ~0.95. According to Kingman's approximation, waiting time explodes non-linearly in the tail, expanding p90 by +8h to +18h even while the median shows a net drop of -1h to -3h.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

### Process Specification
1. **Eligibility Rules & Segmentation:**
   - *Stratum A (Digital Fast-Track, ~23% of volume):* Digital documents present at registration, non-legal. Eligible for immediate simultaneous creation of `Identity Check` and `Document Check`.
   - *Stratum B (Digital Legal, ~15% of volume):* Digital documents present, public-sector/high-value. Parallel verification initiated immediately; early pre-notification dispatched to Legal Review.
   - *Stratum C (Physical Claims, ~62% of volume):* Physical originals required. Upon `Register`, system spawns `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` in parallel. `Document Check` task creation is strictly gated and triggered only upon logging the `Originals Receipt` event.
2. **Statutory Prerequisites:**
   - `Document Check` cannot start for Stratum C until `Originals Receipt` is completed.
   - `Assessment` strictly requires completion of both `Identity Check` and `Document Check`.
   - `Legal Review` remains mandatory for all public-sector and high-value claims prior to final approval/rejection.
3. **Queue & WIP Controls:**
   - Enforce a hard WIP limit of maximum 6 open active tasks per reviewer across all roles.
   - Daily 15-minute cross-functional queue synchronization huddles between ID, Doc, Assess, and Legal pool leads.
4. **Resource Responsibilities & IT Locking:**
   - Refactor case-management software to implement task-level token locks, enabling concurrent edit access to independent verification forms while preventing conflicting field edits.
   - Introduce a structured "Verification Handoff Summary" completed at the end of each check to ensure shared context before Assessment.
5. **Handling of Legal-Review and Supplement Loops:**
   - Cases exiting `Legal Review` with `Request Supplement` are tagged as "Priority Rework" and bypass standard intake queues, entering a dedicated re-verification lane to prevent compound tail delays.
6. **Logging Requirements:**
   - Automated event capture recording micro-lifecycle states: `task_offered`, `task_started`, `task_completed`, and `lock_contention_wait`.

The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by deploying orthogonal process-mining telemetry alongside strict fidelity gates.

Implementation failure occurs when the designed protocol is not executed as intended in practice. This is diagnosed by monitoring: (1) IT lock collision rates and offline bypasses, (2) adherence to the reviewer WIP cap of six open cases, (3) true concurrent execution overlap (target >60% in the digital stratum), and (4) compliance with the structured verification cross-check protocol. If the treatment group shows low concurrency, high lock contention, or delayed batch timestamp entry, the trial reflects implementation breakdown rather than a flawed concept.

Mechanism failure occurs when the intervention is executed with high fidelity (verified via automated start/complete event logs and zero lock violations), yet produces adverse or negligible operational effects. For example, if digital claims achieve >65% true overlap but pre-assessment waiting times fail to decrease due to queue starvation in specialized resource pools (4 ID vs 7 Doc FTE), or if total cycle time balloons due to downstream accumulation at Assessment (3 FTE) and Legal Review (2 FTE), the failure is fundamentally structural and causal.

By conditioning outcome evaluations on process-conformance metrics, management cleanly isolates execution barriers from structural unviability.

## 9. Recommendation

**Recommendation: `conditional go`**

Site B should proceed with a strictly gated, segmented implementation rather than an unconstrained copy of Site A's blanket parallelization.

**What should be transferred:**
Site B should adopt the concurrent execution logic exclusively for cases where inputs are digitally complete at intake, alongside Site A's disciplined queue management practices: enforcing a strict WIP limit of six open verification tasks per reviewer and instituting daily cross-functional queue synchronization huddles.

**What should not be copied:**
Site B must not replicate Site A's blanket task generation immediately after registration for all claims. Creating un-gated document verification tasks for the 62% physical originals cohort is futile and counterproductive, as work cannot legally begin before postal arrival (median 18-hour delay). Furthermore, Site B must not attempt parallel assignment without first modifying the legacy IT single-owner case lock.

**Which subgroup should enter first:**
The initial rollout must be restricted to Stratum A: non-legal digital claims (representing approximately 23% of total volume: 38% digital × 60% non-legal). This cohort has zero external physical dependencies, bypasses the downstream legal bottleneck, and possesses the lowest baseline rework risk.

**Expected benefits and risks:**
For the digital entry cohort, median cycle time is projected to decrease by 6 to 9 hours, driven by genuine verification overlap and eliminated inter-task queueing. However, operational risks include lock collisions in legacy IT, queue imbalances between the 4 identity and 7 document reviewers, and potential discrepancy-driven rework feeding into Assessment and Legal Review.

**Trigger evidence for progression:**
- *Expansion to Stratum B (Digital Legal) and Stratum C (Physical with Originals):* Triggered if the pilot achieves >60% true verification overlap, maintains IT lock collisions under 2%, reduces stratum median cycle time by ≥4 hours, and keeps rework below 18%.
- *Modification (Adjusting WIP/Ratios):* Triggered if pre-assessment queues grow by >2 hours or reviewer idle time exceeds 15%, prompting adjustments to WIP caps or dynamic cross-skilling.
- *Abandonment (Full Rollback):* Triggered if rework exceeds 21%, if p90 cycle time exceeds 130 hours, or if IT lock contention causes pervasive off-system workarounds.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Task independence assumption** — why needed: Legitimates executing identity and document verification in parallel without requiring intermediate findings exchange during active checking — how to test: Audit historical discrepancy logs and cross-check defect rates between sequential and concurrent pilot cohorts — consequence if false: Parallel reviewers miss cross-field fraud or mismatch patterns, elevating rework and 90-day appeal rates beyond baseline 17%.
2. **IT lock modularization assumption** — why needed: Enables multiple reviewers to work on separate verification sub-tasks simultaneously without encountering system record locks — how to test: Execute automated multi-user concurrency tests on identical case IDs in a pre-production staging environment — consequence if false: Reviewers face system lockouts, forcing offline workarounds, batch timestamp back-entry, and distorted process logs.
3. **Digital intake classification accuracy assumption** — why needed: Required to correctly route incoming claims into immediate parallel dispatch versus the postal transit gating track — how to test: Calculate the classification confusion matrix comparing intake digital tags against downstream physical `Originals Receipt` events — consequence if false: Physical claims are erroneously dispatched to document reviewers prematurely, generating idle queue clutter and false task starts.
4. **Specialized capacity balance assumption** — why needed: Assumes the ratio of 4 Identity Reviewers (3.4h service) to 7 Document Reviewers (8.1h service) can process parallel arrivals without severe asynchronous queue starvation — how to test: Measure inter-task queue arrival-to-start delay ratios and inbox accumulation rates during pilot operations — consequence if false: Identity checks complete rapidly and sit idle waiting for document checks, eroding anticipated cycle-time gains.
5. **Downstream assessment absorption assumption** — why needed: Ensures the 3 Assessors and 2 Legal Reviewers possess adequate capacity to absorb faster front-end verification completions without shifting the bottleneck downstream — how to test: Track queue residence time and utilization metrics at the pre-assessment and pre-legal stages during the pilot — consequence if false: Bottleneck shifts downstream, leaving total cycle time unchanged while blowing out p90 tail latency.
6. **Stationary case mix assumption** — why needed: Guarantees that observed cycle-time reductions during the pilot are causally driven by the intervention rather than exogenous shifts in claim complexity (as occurred at Site A) — how to test: Perform two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov and chi-squared tests on claim complexity and legal review shares between baseline and pilot periods — consequence if false: Treatment effect estimates become confounded by volume or complexity fluctuations, yielding misleading transportability conclusions.
7. **Logging fidelity and timestamp granularity assumption** — why needed: Necessary to accurately compute true verification overlap, service durations, and queue wait times in process-mining event logs — how to test: Analyze event log timestamp distributions for artificial clustering, zero-duration tasks, or off-hours bulk entries — consequence if false: Process mining metrics reflect data-entry habits rather than operational reality, compromising pilot evaluation validity.
8. **Non-interference (SUTVA) assumption** — why needed: Ensures that processing treatment cases in parallel does not alter service times or queue delays for control cases sharing the same resource pools — how to test: Compare control arm service durations and waiting times during the pilot against historical pre-pilot baseline distributions across identical strata — consequence if false: Spillover effects and queue competition bias causal treatment effect estimates.
