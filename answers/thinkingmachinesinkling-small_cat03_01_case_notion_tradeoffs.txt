## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** In a flattened OCEL, the “case” is a single object instance of the chosen object type; its directly related events, ordered by timestamp, form the trace. I do not propagate relations through the object–object (O2O) graph unless explicitly stated as an analytical transformation. Thus, if an event `e18` relates to `O1` and `V1` but not to `I11`, then an `Item` flattening on `I11` does **not** automatically inherit `e18`.

**Convergence vs. divergence.** I treat *convergence* as the phenomenon where one event is related to multiple objects of the same case type, so the event is copied into multiple cases (e.g., `e13` relates to both `O2` and `O3`, so it converges into both order-cases). I treat *divergence* as the phenomenon where one object participates in events that involve different partner objects, so its trace branches across contexts (e.g., `O1` participates in `e11`/`P1` and `e12`/`P2`, diverging into two package branches).

**Copied shared events as evidence, duplication, or both.** They are both. They are *evidence* that logistics, invoicing, and payment are genuinely shared (e.g., `P3` physically contains `I21` and `I31`). They are *duplication* if the analyst treats each case as independent, because counting `e13` once per order double-counts the packaging operation.

**Permitted transformations.** I will explicitly propose one transformed variant: propagating relations along O2O links (e.g., `P1`  `I11`  `O1`, or `V2`  `O2`/`O3`) to test sensitivity, but I will not silently embed it in the base comparison.

If measuring “package-to-delivery” duration, `P3` appears slightly faster (1h48m from pack to dispatch; ~5h48m to confirm) than `P2` (55m to dispatch; ~5h50m to confirm), yet they are physically on the same truck. The DFG edge `Pack Package`  `Confirm Delivery` is shared.
- **Effect:** Harmful if delivery attribution is needed; informative for consolidation analysis.

### 3.5 Invoice flattening: consolidated invoicing (`V2` via `e19`)
- **Affected:** Invoice.
- **Cited:** `e19` (12:05, `Issue Invoice`) relates to `O2`, `O3`, `V2`; `e22` (12:40) and `e25` (09:00 next day) relate to `V2`, `O2`, `O3`, `M2`/`M3`.
- **Distortion:** `V2` consolidates `O2` and `O3`. The invoice case includes three events (`e19`  `e22`  `e25`), which is correct at invoice level. However, the split settlement (`M2` partial, `M3` completes) means the DFG for `Invoice` shows `Issue Invoice`  `Record Payment`  `Record Payment`. If the analyst uses an activity-only classifier, this looks like a loop (same activity twice) rather than a two-stage settlement. Moreover, because `O2` and `O3` close separately (`e29`, `e30`), the invoice case lacks natural “close” endpoints, truncating the financial arc.
- **Effect:** Context-dependent; informative for billing, harmful if used to infer order-close timing.

### 3.6 Payment flattening: partial payments (`M2`/`M3` for `V2`)
- **Affected:** Payment.
- **Cited:** `e22` (`Record Payment`, 12:40) with `M2` (`partially_settles`); `e25` (`Record Payment`, 09:00+1) with `M3` (`completes_settlement`).
- **Distortion:** `M2` and `M3` are separate objects, each with exactly one event. The flattened `Payment` log contains two singleton cases of the same activity. No `DFG` edge exists because there is no intra-case transition. The critical business fact—partial then complete—is only recoverable by comparing `M2` and `M3` via `V2` using O2O relations. Performance (settlement duration from invoice to full payment) is undefined within either singleton.
- **Effect:** Harmful for process-flow discovery; informative only when cross-referenced to `V2`.

### 3.7 Delivery flattening: near-singleton lifecycle (`D1`, `D2`, `D3`)
- **Affected:** Delivery.
- **Cited:** `D1` only in `e15`, `e23`; `D2` only in `e16`, `e24`; `D3` only in `e17`, `e27`.
- **Distortion:** Each delivery case has length 2 (`Dispatch` then `Confirm Delivery`). No `Create`, `Pack`, `Invoice`, `Payment`, or `Close` events relate directly to delivery objects. Thus the DFG is a single edge. Lifecycle duration is accurate (1h, 4h55m, 22h45m), but the “process” is just a micro-cycle embedded in a larger flow. Analysts risk over-interpreting this as a complete process if presented alone.
- **Effect:** Harmful for end-to-end analysis; informative as a delivery-cycle module.

### 3.8 Order flattening: artificial branching for split order (`O1`)
- **Affected:** Order.
- **Cited:** `O1` participates in `P1` (`e11`) and `P2` (`e12`); `D1` (`e15`,`e23`) and `D2` (`e16`,`e24`).
- **Distortion:** The `O1` trace is `e01`  `e05`  `e06`  `e11`  `e12`  `e15`  `e16`  `e18`  `e21`  `e23`  `e24`  `e28`. This contains two “packages” and two “deliveries” for one order. The DFG will show `Pack Package`  `Pack Package` (same activity repeated) and `Dispatch`  `Confirm`  `Dispatch`  `Confirm`. Variant discovery will likely split `O1` into a unique “split-order” variant, but frequency counts of activities will be inflated relative to `O4` (single package, single delivery).
- **Effect:** Harmful if analysts assume each order follows one linear path; informative because it reveals the split-order design explicitly.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs to create (with stated limits):**
- **Order (`O1`–`O4`):** Primary for order-level KPIs (time to close, fill rate, settlement time). Must be filtered by event-type to create “pure order” vs. “shared logistics” sub-views. Safe questions: How long from `Create Order` to `Close Order`? What fraction of orders have split deliveries?
- **Package (`P1`–`P4`):** Secondary for logistics consolidation. Safe questions: How frequently do packages contain items from multiple orders (`P3`, `P4`)? What is the average time from pack to dispatch per package?
- **Invoice (`V1`–`V3`):** Secondary for billing. Safe questions: Average invoice-to-full-payment time? Frequency of partial settlement (`V2`)?
- **Item / Delivery / Payment:** Create only if the specific sub-question demands them; never use them as standalone end-to-end logs.

**Questions requiring object-centric views (not solvable in any single flattened log):**
- How does consolidation affect delivery time?  **OC-DFG** (to see `Package`  `Delivery` frequency and time) and **object-interaction summary** (to quantify `P2`–`P3` co-occurrence in `D2`).
- Does `O2` pay slower because of `O3` consolidation, or because of its own items?  **ET–OT graph** (event-type to object-type) to compare `O2` vs. `O3` direct relations.
- What is the true number of physical packaging acts, independent of order duplication?  **OTG** (object-type graph) and direct counting of unique `(activity, package)` pairs.
- Are orders with split deliveries (`O1`) structurally different?  **Equivalent-execution clustering** (`cluster_equivalent_ocel`) using multi-dimensional feature vectors, not just flattened traces.

**Reconciliation between flattened and object-centric views:**
- If `Order` flat reports average `Pack Package` frequency = 1.75 per order (because `O1` has 2, `O3` has 2 via shared events), but the object-centric view shows 4 unique package events (`e11`, `e12`, `e13`, `e14`), reconcile by reporting “physical packaging acts: 4; order-copies: 6 (due to `e13`/`e14`/`e16` duplication).”
- If `Delivery` flat says `Confirm Delivery` takes 22h for `D3`, but `Package` flat says `P4` was packed at 10:20 and confirmed at 10:00 next day (~23h40m), reconcile by aligning event times rather than inferring from case durations.
- Always cross-check `DFG` edge frequencies with `discover_ocdfg` event frequencies; if an edge count in `Order` flat is double the `OC-DFG` count, duplication is the cause.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position: C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

**Decision criteria:**
1. **Full activity coverage:** Only `Order` directly relates to all 7 activities (`Create Order`, `Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`, `Confirm Delivery`, `Close Order`). `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment` all suffer lethal truncation under direct E2O.
2. **Distortion under duplication:** `Order` has very high convergence (`e13` 2×, `e16`/`e24` 3×). `Delivery` and `Payment` have near-singleton lifecycles (2 events, 1 event respectively). No type achieves both completeness and low distortion.
3. **Structural multi-dimensionality:** The process is defined by cross-object relations: orders split into packages (`O1`  `P1`,`P2`), packages consolidate into deliveries (`P2`,`P3`  `D2`), orders consolidate into invoices (`O2`,`O3`  `V2`), and payments split (`V2`  `M2`,`M3`). A single case notion cannot represent all four dimensions simultaneously without copying or truncating.

**Strongest supporting evidence:**
- `Payment` (`M2`, `M3`) produces singleton cases (`e22`, `e25`) with empty `DFG`; without `V2` and `O2`/`O3`, settlement dynamics vanish.
- `Order`’s `O1` trace is not a simple sequence but a “split diamond.” If used alone, analysts must invent rules to handle two `Dispatch`/`Confirm` pairs, two `Pack` events, and constant cross-referencing to `D2`.
- The business facts explicitly state: `O1` split across `P1`/`P2`; `P3` contains items from `O2` and `O3`; `V2` consolidates `O2` and `O3`; `V2` is paid in two instalments. These are multi-object patterns, not single-case patterns.

**Strongest counterargument:**
- `Order` is the only object that participates in `Create` through `Close`. In many process-mining contexts, analysts accept duplication and filter later. If the goal is “track an order to billing and close,” `Order` is the least-bad flat log.

**Residual risk:**
- Analysts will default to `Order` because of intuitive “order-to-cash” framing, ignoring the duplication artifacts. This can lead to inflated cycle-time estimates (blending `D1` and `D2`) and false conclusions about split-order frequency.

**Conditions under which I would change to B (primary + secondaries):**
- If the OCEL were modified so that `P3` and `P4` each mapped to a single order (no cross-order packages) and `D2` carried only one package, then `Order` duplication would drop sharply, and `Order` flat would become defensible with only `Invoice`/`Payment` secondaries.
- If the tool environment required a single lead object for a dashboard, I would select `Order` with explicit “shared event” flags, not pure flattening.

import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------

print("OCEL object types:", ocel.object_types)

# Note: Visual inspection of event counts does NOT resolve whether e16 is one

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Frequency inspection (direct E2O only per task rules)
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Activity frequency
act_freq = pm4py.get_event_attribute_values(ocel, "activity")  # conceptual; adjust to PM4Py OCEL API
# For true frequency: count unique events per activity
activity_counts = {}
for e in ocel.events:
    activity_counts[e["activity"]] = activity_counts.get(e["activity"], 0) + 1
print("Direct event counts by activity:", activity_counts)

# Object-type frequency (unique objects per type)
ot_counts = {}
for ot in ocel.object_types:
    ot_counts[ot] = len(ocel.get_objects(ot))  # adjusted to PM4Py OCEL object access
print("Object counts:", ot_counts)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Flattening on all six types (preserves direct E2O)
# ------------------------------------------------------------
object_types = ["order", "item", "package", "delivery", "invoice", "payment"]
flattened = {}
for ot in object_types:
    # Assumes pm4py.ocel_flattening accepts case_object_type
    log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flattened[ot] = log
    print(f"Flattened '{ot}': cases={len(log)}, events={len(log)}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. DFG discovery per flattened log (distortion will be visible)
# ------------------------------------------------------------
dfgs = {}
for ot, log in flattened.items():
    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    dfgs[ot] = (dfg, start_act, end_act)
    # The DFG for Delivery will show 1 edge; for Payment likely 0 edges.
    print(f"DFG '{ot}': nodes={list(dfg[0])}, edges count={len(dfg[1])}")

log_order = flattened["order"]

# Object-Type graph
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**Check 1: Down-weight / remove copied shared events.**
Construct a filtered `OCEL` or log where events with `|Related objects of case type| > 1` are excluded or weighted 0.5 (e.g., exclude `e13`, `e14`, `e16`, `e19`, `e22`, `e24`, `e25`). Recompute `Order` flat `DFG`. Expected change: `Pack Package` count drops from 6 to 4; `Dispatch`/`Confirm Delivery` drop from 9/6 to 3/3; `O1`’s artificial double-dispatch disappears; average fulfillment time shifts upward because fast `D1` (1h) is no longer averaged with slow `D2` (4h55m).

**Check 2: Explicit propagation versus direct E2O.**
Build a transformed relation set: propagate `O2O` links to events (e.g., add `I11` to `e18`; add `P1` to `e21`; add `V2` to `e13`). Re-run flattening on `Item`, `Package`, and `Payment`. Compare `DFG` edges: `Item` flat will suddenly gain `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment`; `Payment` flat will gain upstream links; `Package` flat will gain invoice links. If conclusions change radically, direct E2O was hiding consolidation.

**Check 3: Changing the lead object.**
Compare metrics `avg_trace_length`, `variant_count`, and `DFG_edge_density` across `Order`, `Package`, `Invoice`, and `Delivery`. Expect: `Order` longest, `Payment` shortest; `Package` medium but lower duplication than `Order`; `Delivery` single-edge. If analysts choose `Order` solely because it is longest, this check exposes that `Payment` is shortest because of truncation, not because it is “unimportant.”

**Check 4: Frequency metrics comparison in OC-DFG.**
Compare three counts per event/activity: (a) event frequency (unique event IDs); (b) unique-object frequency (distinct objects of any type related); (c) total-object-occurrence frequency (sum of object relations). For `e16`, (a)=1, (b)=6 objects (`O1`,`O2`,`O3`,`I12`,`I21`,`I31`,`P2`,`P3`,`D2` = 9 distinct objects), (c)=9. If flattened `Order` reports `Dispatch` count = 3 but `OC-DFG` reports 1, duplication is quantified. If `OC-DFG` total-occurrence is used to weight performance, `D2`’s 5h delivery time should not be triple-counted just because three orders share it.

**Validation rule:** No conclusion about “correct” process flow will be drawn from a single flattened `DFG`. Every finding will be tagged with the case-object type used and cross-checked against `OTG`, `ETOT`, and interaction summary.
