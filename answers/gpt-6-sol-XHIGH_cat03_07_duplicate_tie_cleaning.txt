## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree

First preserve an immutable copy of every source row and E2O relation. Compare candidates using source keys, source semantics, payloads, object identities, and known causal links; a common harmonized label and timestamp only nominate candidates. Apply the following branches in order, recording the evidence and rule version.

1. **Exact technical duplicate:** Same source and source event key, timestamp, original activity, substantive payload, and qualified E2O set, with no conflicting attributes? Keep one canonical row and **drop** the repeated ingestion row from the analytical view. Record both raw IDs. A matching payload hash alone is insufficient.
2. **Same physical event observed by multiple systems:** Independent reconciliation or a shared business transaction identifier establishes identity? **Merge attributes and relations** under one stable canonical ID, retaining each source record and its own attributes in provenance. Resolve conflicts by attribute authority, not arrival order.
3. **Summary derived from detail:** A message lineage establishes that the summary is merely another representation *and* the analysis uses one transition per business action? **Merge attributes and relations** only if the summary's meaning and object scope can be represented faithfully. Otherwise **retain both with refined labels or qualifiers** (for example, packing transaction versus ERP status update). In the absence of that evidence, use the latter decision.
4. **Harmonization collision:** Original source activities describe different milestones? **Retain both with refined labels or qualifiers**. Equal modeled objects do not override different source meanings.
5. **Concurrent same-label events on different objects:** **Retain both unchanged**, including their distinct object links. This applies even when they share a parent order, invoice, or package.
6. **Unresolved candidate:** **Quarantine for review** from any automatic merge rule, while keeping both visible, flagged, in the reversible working view. Quarantine is not deletion.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules

Build a directed acyclic graph for each timestamp, then take a deterministic topological sort. Add edges in this priority: (1) explicit domain facts, such as e09 before e10 and e12 before e13; (2) source sequence *within the same source only*, provided the source documents that it reflects event order; (3) lifecycle prerequisites on a shared object, such as picking an item before packing it; (4) source-specific meanings, such as warehouse release before carrier acceptance and a derived ERP status after its WMS message. These are semantic constraints, with differing strengths; log each edge's justification. Do not infer a global sequence by comparing WMS 100 with PSP 400 or ERP 30.

For remaining incomparable nodes, choose a fixed priority, such as a documented source priority followed by original source key and stable eid. Ingestion time may break a remaining tie for reproducibility, but is *merely deterministic* and must never displace a semantic edge. A lexical eid fallback makes the result independent of row-loading order. Detect cycles and investigate instead of silently choosing a winner. Keep the partial-order graph and an “incomparable” flag alongside the chosen total order.

At 10:00, after e03/e04 merge, e03 and e05 are distinct picks. Scanner 551 before 552 supports e03/e04 before e05 as a source-record order if scanner sequence has that guarantee; otherwise they remain physically unordered. Both item picks precede e06 under the packing lifecycle, and e06 precedes e07 as the source of its status message. One admissible display order is **e03, e05, e06, e07**. Under the optional packing merge it ends at e06. At 10:15 use **e08, e09, e10**: release, acceptance, then the explicitly later handover. At 12:00, after invoice merging, use **e11, e12, e13, e14, e15** for display. The firm PSP chain is e12 before e13 before e14 by PSP sequence, with authorization before M1 capture; e15 follows the payment messages. ERP sequence also places e11 before e15. The relative position of invoice issuance and PSP authorization/captures is not proved solely by their common timestamp, so topological orders respecting those constraints are admissible; do not report an invoice-to-authorization latency from this tie.

Only after this graph is settled may an analysis copy receive index-based microseconds. Doing that first turns arbitrary row order into apparently precise business times, spurious DFG edges, and nonzero durations.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline

Normalize timestamps to UTC and source activities to a documented mapping, but retain original activity and timestamp columns. Snapshot raw events, objects, E2O, source identifiers, payload hashes, and source-specific attributes. Check candidate groups by activity/time and by source or business keys; inspect their E2O sets and original source meanings. Apply exact technical deduplication only to exact signatures; there are none in this fixture. Merge e04 into e03 from the reconciliation report, and e16 into e11 from the V1 invoice identity, with source-aware conflict resolution. Keep e05, e06, e07, e08, e09, e10, e12–e15 independently. Refine e07, e08, e09, and e15 as described above, preserving their original harmonized labels.

Compute the semantic partial order, then a reproducible total order and a separate adjusted-timestamp view. Preserve original eids and source keys in the sidecar even where one canonical eid represents two source rows. Recheck object references and qualified E2O relations after every rewrite. This leaves **15 events and 39 E2O relations**, assuming exact duplicate E2O rows are coalesced and no unshown qualifiers differ. It preserves legitimate concurrent picks and partial captures.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives

**Evidence-based semantic merge:** In addition to the two certain merges, treat e07 as another observation of e06 *only after* a WMS-to-ERP message identifier and the intended one-transition abstraction are confirmed. Keep its ERP status and narrower O1/P1 scope in provenance; keep e15 separate because no individual capture can stand for the invoice-wide update. The projected view has **14 events and 37 E2O relations**. The chief risk is removing a meaningful ERP status milestone and its WMS-to-ERP delay. Without message-level proof, use the conservative view.

**Mechanically aggressive library merge:** On a separate copy, merge equal activity/time candidates sharing any object, for example with `pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(..., have_common_object=True)`. If it merges the five implicated groups—e03/e04/e05, e06/e07, e08/e09, e11/e16, and e13/e14/e15—the projected result is **10 events and 28 distinct E2O relations**. Treat actual count as version/data dependent and inspect its output. This view destroys I1/I2 pick multiplicity, release versus acceptance, and the distinction between M1 and M2 captures. `have_common_object=True` is insufficient: e13 and e14 share O1 and V1, yet differ precisely in their Payment objects. An E2O union produces one fabricated capture linked to both M1 and M2. The same shared-invoice shortcut also invites e15 into that group despite its different abstraction level.

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation

The example assumes standard PM4Py `ocel:*` columns plus the supplied metadata, and retains a raw sidecar outside PM4Py. Its exact-duplicate call is protected by a synthetic signature: only candidates proven equal can acquire the same temporary activity. The aggressive call is intentionally isolated.

```python
from copy import deepcopy
from hashlib import sha256
import json
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

E, A, T = "ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"
O, Q = "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"
raw = deepcopy(ocel_raw)
raw.events[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw.events[T], utc=True)
raw.events["original_timestamp"] = raw.events[T].copy()
raw.events["original_activity"] = raw.events[A].copy()
assert {"source", "source_event_key", "source_seq", "payload_hash"} <= set(raw.events)
assert raw.events[E].is_unique

# Immutable source evidence, including full rows and qualified relations.
audit = {"raw_events": deepcopy(raw.events),
         "raw_relations": deepcopy(raw.relations), "decisions": []}

def check(x):
    assert x.events[E].is_unique
    assert set(x.relations[E]) <= set(x.events[E])
    assert set(x.relations[O]) <= set(x.objects[O])
    # A relation must describe its current event, including after timestamp adjustment.
    attrs = x.events.set_index(E)[[A, T]]
    for _, r in x.relations.iterrows():
        assert r[A] == attrs.loc[r[E], A]
        assert pd.Timestamp(r[T]) == pd.Timestamp(attrs.loc[r[E], T])

def sync_relations(x):
    by_id = x.events.set_index(E)
    x.relations[A] = x.relations[E].map(by_id[A])
    x.relations[T] = x.relations[E].map(by_id[T])
    return x

def relation_signature(x, eid):
    r = x.relations[x.relations[E] == eid]
    cols = [c for c in (O, "ocel:type", Q) if c in r]
    return sorted(tuple(str(v) for v in z) for z in r[cols].itertuples(index=False, name=None))

def exact_signature(x, row):
    # Exclude only technical ingestion identity/time and derived display columns.
    excluded = {E, "ingested_at", "original_timestamp", "original_activity",
                "tie_rank", "adjusted_timestamp"}
    fields = [(c, str(row[c])) for c in sorted(row.index) if c not in excluded]
    blob = json.dumps([fields, relation_signature(x, row[E])], sort_keys=True)
    return sha256(blob.encode()).hexdigest()

base = deepcopy(raw)
signatures = {r[E]: exact_signature(base, r) for _, r in base.events.iterrows()}
# Pre-check every proposed group; an activity/time match is only a candidate.
candidates = base.events.groupby([A, T], dropna=False)[E].apply(list)
for ids in candidates:
    if len(ids) > 1:
        audit["decisions"].append(("candidate", tuple(ids),
                                   tuple(signatures[i] for i in ids)))

protected = deepcopy(base)
protected.events[A] = protected.events[E].map(lambda i: "exact:" + signatures[i])
sync_relations(protected)
exact_clean = pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(protected)
removed = set(base.events[E]) - set(exact_clean.events[E])
assert all(any(signatures[i] == signatures[j] for j in exact_clean.events[E])
           for i in removed)
# For this fixture, there are no exact technical duplicates.
assert not removed
original_labels = base.events.set_index(E)[A]
exact_clean.events[A] = exact_clean.events[E].map(original_labels)
sync_relations(exact_clean)
check(exact_clean)

def missing(v):
    return v is None or (isinstance(v, float) and pd.isna(v))

def merge_verified(x, keep, remove, rule):
    x = deepcopy(x)
    a = x.events.set_index(E).loc[keep].copy()
    b = x.events.set_index(E).loc[remove].copy()
    conflicts = {}
    reserved = {A, T, "original_activity", "original_timestamp", "source",
                "source_event_key", "source_seq", "payload_hash", "ingested_at"}
    for col in x.events.columns:
        if col in reserved or col == E:
            continue
        if missing(a[col]) and not missing(b[col]):
            x.events.loc[x.events[E] == keep, col] = b[col]
        elif not missing(a[col]) and not missing(b[col]) and str(a[col]) != str(b[col]):
            conflicts[col] = (a[col], b[col])
            # Source authority can override the displayed value; both remain below.
            if rule == "same_invoice" and col.startswith(("tax_", "billing_")):
                x.events.loc[x.events[E] == keep, col] = b[col]
    audit["decisions"].append({"rule": rule, "canonical": keep,
        "sources": [keep, remove], "attributes": [a.to_dict(), b.to_dict()],
        "conflicts": conflicts, "relations": [relation_signature(x, keep),
                                            relation_signature(x, remove)]})
    x.events = x.events[x.events[E] != remove].copy()
    x.relations.loc[x.relations[E] == remove, E] = keep
    keys = [c for c in (E, O, "ocel:type", Q) if c in x.relations]
    x.relations = x.relations.drop_duplicates(subset=keys).copy()
    sync_relations(x)
    check(x)
    return x

conservative = merge_verified(exact_clean, "e03", "e04", "reconciled_scan")
conservative = merge_verified(conservative, "e11", "e16", "same_invoice")
for eid, refined in {"e07": "ERP Packing Status Update",
                     "e08": "Release from Warehouse",
                     "e09": "Accept by Carrier",
                     "e15": "ERP Payment Balance Update"}.items():
    conservative.events.loc[conservative.events[E] == eid, A] = refined
sync_relations(conservative)
check(conservative)

evidence = merge_verified(conservative, "e06", "e07", "confirmed_pack_message")
# WARNING: use only if the WMS-to-ERP message identity is verified in a real log.

# WARNING: this library scenario can merge distinct items, payments, and milestones.
aggressive = pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(deepcopy(raw), have_common_object=True)
check(aggressive)

rank = {"e03": 0, "e05": 1, "e06": 2, "e07": 3,
        "e08": 0, "e09": 1, "e10": 2,
        "e11": 0, "e16": 1, "e12": 2, "e13": 3, "e14": 4, "e15": 5}
def ordered_views(x):
    x = deepcopy(x)
    stable = {eid: i for i, eid in enumerate(sorted(map(str, x.events[E])))}
    x.events["tie_rank"] = x.events[E].map(
        lambda eid: rank.get(eid, 100 + stable[str(eid)]))
    ordered = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(x, "tie_rank")
    sync_relations(ordered)
    check(ordered)
    adjusted = pm4py.ocel_add_index_based_timedelta(deepcopy(ordered))
    adjusted.events["adjusted_timestamp"] = adjusted.events[T].copy()
    sync_relations(adjusted)
    check(adjusted)
    return ordered, adjusted

raw_ordered, raw_adjusted = ordered_views(raw)
cons_ordered, cons_adjusted = ordered_views(conservative)
evid_ordered, evid_adjusted = ordered_views(evidence)
aggr_ordered, aggr_adjusted = ordered_views(aggressive)
# Report counts from the unadjusted views; inspect aggr_ordered before using it.
for name, x in [("raw", raw_ordered), ("conservative", cons_ordered),
                ("evidence", evid_ordered), ("aggressive", aggr_ordered)]:
    print(name, len(x.events), len(x.relations))
```

The code's simple authority rule is illustrative. A production attribute registry should define the authoritative source per field and send every disagreement to the conflict ledger. If PM4Py creates new IDs or drops metadata in a particular version, map its groups back to the raw sidecar and reject the view until provenance and referential checks pass.

## 7. Sensitivity analysis

The counts below are projections from the displayed records and qualified E2O sets; library behavior and unshown qualifiers can change the aggressive count. Count both distinct events and E2O incidences, since merging can remove an event while preserving or fabricating object links.

| View | Events / E2O | Expected process-mining effect |
|---|---:|---|
| Raw | 17 / 44 | Duplicate scan and invoice observations inflate frequencies; harmonized labels hide different milestones. |
| Conservative | 15 / 39 | Reconciled redundancies disappear; item and payment concurrency remain; source-specific milestones become visible. |
| Evidence-based pack merge | 14 / 37 | One fewer packing/status event and no separate ERP packing-status edge; all distinct payment events remain. |
| Aggressive library merge | about 10 / 28 | Item and payment objects become co-participants in fabricated common events; logistics stages disappear. |

For the **Order-flattened DFG**, raw tie sorting can show `Pick Item → Pick Item` twice around e03/e04/e05, `Pack Package → Pack Package` for e06/e07, `Dispatch Package → Dispatch Package` for e08/e09, `Issue Invoice → Issue Invoice` for e11/e16, and `Capture Payment → Capture Payment` for e13/e14/e15. These do not all mean rework. Conservative cleaning removes the reconciled pick and invoice repetitions, exposes `Pick Item → ERP Packing Status Update`, `Release from Warehouse → Accept by Carrier → Handover to Carrier`, and `Capture Payment → ERP Payment Balance Update`. Evidence merging removes the separate packing-status edge. Aggressive merging erases the second distinct item pick, release/acceptance distinction, and both partial-capture separation and ERP summary. DFG transitions across otherwise incomparable invoice/payment events depend on the display order and must be marked as tie-derived.

For **Item-flattened DFGs**, I1's raw `Pick Item → Pick Item` (e03/e04) disappears conservatively, while I2 retains its own pick e05 before packing e06. Aggressive merging makes one pick event touch I1 and I2, falsely suggesting a joint item action; its item-level counts may look superficially similar after flattening. For **Payment-flattened DFGs**, M1 still has `Authorize Payment → Capture Payment` and M2 has its own capture in the conservative and evidence views. An aggressive union turns e13/e14/e15 into one capture linked to both M1 and M2; the M1 DFG may still show the authorization edge, but the joint event and loss of two capture occurrences corrupt frequency and amount interpretations. e15 never had a Payment relation and should not be assigned to M1 or M2.

**OC-DFG** edge and frequency metrics change by both event collapse and E2O union: the I1 pick repetition disappears correctly in conservative cleaning, while the aggressive view suppresses real I2 pick and carrier-stage frequencies. An OC-DFG conditioned on O1, P1, D1, I1/I2, or M1/M2 can acquire or lose apparent cross-object adjacency. **ET–OT** weights for Pick Item–Item and Capture Payment–Payment must distinguish event frequency from E2O incidence: aggressive union can keep two Payment incidences while reducing capture events to one. Refined activity labels shift weights from generic Dispatch Package to warehouse release and carrier acceptance, and from generic Capture Payment to the ERP balance-update type. A co-occurrence-derived **OTG** gains a false M1–M2 edge under aggressive union; the merged pick also adds I1–I2 co-occurrence at the pick stage, even though the pair already co-occurs at packing. A native, separately sourced O2O table should remain unchanged; only a recomputed event-derived OTG changes.

Apparent loops or rework fall when genuine duplicate observations are removed, but can also fall falsely when distinct partial captures or warehouse/carrier milestones are collapsed. Lifecycle measurements should use original timestamps and partial-order uncertainty: same-second ties imply zero measured gap or an interval below timestamp resolution, not a known microsecond duration. E03/e04 or e11/e16 merges reduce duplicate starts; e06/e07 merging removes measurable status latency; e08/e09 merging destroys release-to-acceptance time; e13/e14 merging understates capture multiplicity. Report sensitivity of DFG edges, frequencies, and durations across all four views, and flag edges supported only by deterministic tie-breaking.

## 8. Audit trail and reversibility

Use an append-only decision record keyed by `canonical_eid`, containing `source_eids`, source systems and event keys, source payload hashes, merge rule and version, reviewer or automated rule version, confidence, reason and evidence reference. Store each source's original activity and timestamp, the refined activity, the adjusted timestamp and tie rank, all raw attributes by source, selected canonical attributes with their authoritative source, every discarded display-value conflict, and the complete original and resulting qualified E2O sets. Record ordering edges separately with their semantic or deterministic class. Keep raw event/object/relation tables and the decision ledger under versioned snapshots; never make the adjusted timestamp the sole timestamp.

To reconstruct the raw view, select the immutable snapshot or expand each canonical event through its `source_eids`, restore each source row's original activity/time/attributes, and restore its original E2O rows. To reproduce a cleaned view, replay decision versions and ordering rules. A reversal must restore e04, e16, or e07 as independent events and undo only their associated relation rewrites; it must not guess their attributes from the canonical row.

## 9. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Collapse M1 and M2 captures:** require Payment identity or verified transaction identity, never just shared O1/V1; assert both payment-specific captures survive conservative cleaning.
2. **Make abstraction updates look like rework:** retain or refine e07/e15 labels and document derivation; do not interpret same-label DFG loops as repeated work without source review.
3. **Order by ingestion time:** classify `ingested_at` as technical; let it break only otherwise unresolved ties, and mark that choice.
4. **Use shared objects as proof of duplication:** compare original activity semantics and object scope; e08/e09 and e13/e14 are counterexamples.
5. **Union incompatible E2O relations:** verify role, qualifier, and object type before union; never infer M1/M2 links for e15.
6. **Change durations with synthetic microseconds:** retain UTC original time, calculate business durations from it, and label adjusted times as display aids.
7. **Lose source-authoritative attributes:** use a field-authority registry and conflict ledger, especially for ERP versus BILLING invoice fields and WMS location.
8. **Run duplicate functions over the whole OCEL blindly:** inspect candidate groups, test signatures and semantics, use isolated copies, and assert which IDs changed.
9. **Compare source sequences across systems:** enforce sequence edges only inside a source; reject cross-source numeric comparisons.
10. **Treat a deterministic total order as historical fact:** persist incomparable pairs and edge reasons; make tie-derived DFG transitions distinguishable from causal ones.
11. **Break references after a merge:** validate unique canonical eids, existing object IDs, event/relation timestamp and activity agreement, and qualified relation counts.
12. **Hide a cyclic or inconsistent order:** detect cycles in the semantic graph, quarantine disputed edges for review, and keep the raw view usable.
