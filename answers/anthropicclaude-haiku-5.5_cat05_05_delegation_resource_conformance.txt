## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 (drafter/approver separation).** Violation if the normalized human who drafted equals the normalized human who approved, for any risk level. Service-account events are never the same human as a person.

**RR2 (implementer/verifier separation).** Violation if the normalized implementer equals the normalized verifier. If the verifier is a service account or an unresolved identity, the verification cannot be credited as conformant, but the case is not automatically a violation until the identity is determined.

**RR3 (HIGH-risk approval).** Approval is evaluated at the event timestamp using four statuses:
- **VALID:** all conditions are evidenced.
- **CONDITIONAL:** the delegation is valid on its face, but a key instrument is unverified, such as a registry record created after the event.
- **UNVERIFIED:** a required condition, such as principal absence, has no evidence.
- **INVALID:** the interval, scope, principal-absence, delegate-availability, or subdelegation condition fails.

The conditions are:
- (a) the effective interval covers the event;
- (b) the scope covers the change;
- (c) the principal is absent or formally unavailable on record, where the delegation requires it;
- (d) no prohibited subdelegation occurred;
- (e) the delegate is personally available and actually performed the act (an interpretation added here).

**RR4 (emergency).** An emergency HIGH change may be approved by a valid IC or IC delegate. The emergency flag must be credible, meaning the case is linked to an incident (CI-44 here) and a safety rationale is recorded. A QM or valid QM delegate must then perform Retrospective_QM_Review within two business days.
- Business-day counting: Monday to Friday, UTC dates, no site holidays in the period. The deadline is the approval event date plus two business days, ending at 23:59:59 UTC. If a holiday existed it would be added to the busday calendar.
- Here, 2026-08-11 is a Tuesday, so the deadline is 2026-08-13 23:59:59 UTC.

**Acting while on leave.**
- A principal who acts while on recorded leave is not automatically unauthorized. Leave does not create or remove authority.
- The act conflicts with the delegation's premise (that the principal is absent) and must be explained.
- The act needs identity corroboration (MFA, device binding, and session evidence), because a session can be used by someone other than the account holder.
- A delegate on certified sick leave cannot be treated as an available delegate without explanation.

**Whether a later database entry can prove an earlier delegation.** A later database entry is evidence of registration and the time of creation, not of effective authority. A signed instrument that predates the effective interval, with a validated signature and audit trail, can establish issuance. A registry record created after the event cannot by itself prove the delegation existed then. It may be a backfill or a migration artifact, and its audit trail must be checked.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

No indicator here is proof of collusion (RR6). Each indicator is listed with benign and adverse explanations and the evidence that would discriminate between them.

**1. Clara–Ben repeated pair (18 of 23 HIGH production changes in 60 days).**
- Benign: both are in the same production cell. The Quality Engineer is the natural reviewer. Ben held a QM delegation during 08-10 to 08-14, which explains only a few of these approvals.
- Adverse: Ben approved most HIGH changes without any QM authority, and the pattern may reflect habitual reliance on an unauthorized approver.
- Discriminating evidence: the timestamp of each of the 18 approvals against all delegation intervals; any pre-08-10 delegations in the registry; whether other Quality Engineers or QMs were available; whether the approvals were reviewed by someone else.
- Note: the pack provides no delegation evidence before 08-10. If none exists, the 18 approvals are a population-level RR3 exposure larger than any single case.

**2. Reciprocal pattern (Ben drafted four LOW training changes that Clara approved).**
- Benign: low-stakes training documents, with both users as trainers. Reciprocal review can be efficient.
- Adverse: mutual approval avoids independent review.
- Discriminating evidence: the four approvals' log records (the log in this pack does not show Clara approving any Ben draft; CHG-508 shows Ben approving his own draft). This discrepancy must be resolved. Whether the changes were verified independently, and whether the content was reviewed by someone outside the pair.

**3. Shared workstation and service account (WS-DM-04 and svc_qms in CHG-503).**
- Benign: Diego was at his usual terminal to implement. Another verifier used the same station, or an automated check ran under svc_qms.
- Adverse: Diego self-verified by operating the service-account session.
- Discriminating evidence: application-level session logs; actions performed in the session after 12:00; the svc_qms action history; whether Diego was logged in; other users present; verification artifacts.

**4. Absence conflicts (Asha VPN and approval on 08-13; Ben approval and office IP on 08-13; Ben review on 08-14).**
- Asha. Benign: she acted on an urgent case despite leave and used a legitimate session. Adverse: someone else used her credentials, or her account was used during an unrecorded absence.
- Ben. Benign: his sick-leave record is inaccurate, or he attended the office. Adverse: someone used his session while he was off duty.
- Discriminating evidence: MFA and device-binding logs; source IP and physical location; badge logs; leave and sick-leave records; testimony.

**5. Credential compromise.** The pattern across 08-13 and 08-14 (Asha's VPN session, Ben's sick-leave approval, Ben's review during sick leave) could indicate shared or compromised credentials. The benign explanation is simply inaccurate records, but the presence of two unexplained absence conflicts on one day raises the priority of an account-integrity check.

**6. Clara as a common node.** Clara appears in CHG-502 (self-approval), CHG-506 (her draft was approved by Ben under contested authority), CHG-508 (implementation after Ben's approval), and CHG-510 (self-approval relying on an invalid subdelegation). Repeated presence in irregular events is an indicator. Benign: she is the frequent drafter and therefore has more exposure. Adverse: she may be pressing for approvals or relying on informal authority chains. Discriminating evidence: who initiated each approval request; Clara's location and session at 13:00 on 08-13 (to test whether she was at the same office workstation). This is a test to run, not an inference. Her location must not be assumed.

**7. Other concentrations.**
- Eva approved two HIGH changes outside her authority (CHG-507, CHG-509). Benign: she was treated as a reviewer. Adverse: she assumed an approval role.
- Diego is IC and implementer across most changes. Benign: small team. Adverse: concentration weakens independent checks.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation.** Require a signed instrument, a registry entry, and an effective-time record created before the effective date. Reject delegations with no registry entry at the time of use. Make scope a closed list (production_equipment, cooling_system, training_document) rather than free text.
- **Effective-time recording.** Record the effective interval in the QMS at creation, and retain the creation audit trail through any migration. Migration jobs must preserve original timestamps and must not create new records that look like originals.
- **Principal availability.** Record leave, sick leave, and VPN or remote access status in a system the QMS can query at approval time. Block a delegate's approval if the delegate is on certified sick leave, and block a principal's approval while a delegation is active unless the delegation is suspended and the suspension is recorded.
- **Alias management.** Maintain a governed alias table with an owner and periodic review. Require the IdP to map every login to one person ID. Prevent drafting and approval under different aliases of the same person (CHG-508 pattern).
- **Individual authentication.** Require individual re-authentication for every approval and verification action, including those performed via shared stations. Eliminate or restrict the `svc_qms` account for approval and verification, or require a per-person signature on each service action.
- **Emergency review.** Assign a backup QM for absence periods. Automate the two-business-day clock and escalate missed deadlines. Require the emergency flag to link to an incident ID and a safety rationale. Prohibit onward subdelegation in the system, not only in the instrument.
- **Pair rotation.** Introduce pair-rotation or second-reviewer requirements for HIGH changes. Add a monitor for drafter-approver concentration, with thresholds that trigger review rather than sanctions.
- **Network monitoring.** Run the handover and working-together networks monthly. Alert on reciprocal approvals, self-approvals, and approvals during absence records. Review alerts manually.
- **Evidence retention.** Retain session logs, badge data, and VPN records for the investigation window. Retain CCTV where it exists. Retain delegation signing metadata and registry audit trails.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Of the ten cases, nine carry an apparent nonconformance. Only CHG-501 is substantially conformant, and only conditionally on the delegation instrument.

**Genuine authority or SoD failures (about half):** CHG-502 (self-approval), CHG-507 (approval by a non-QM with no delegation), CHG-508 (self-approval after alias normalization), CHG-510 (self-approval relying on a prohibited subdelegation), and CHG-505's late and sick-leave retrospective review. These are control failures that would occur regardless of who the individuals are.

**Identity or data problems (about a quarter):** CHG-503 (unresolved verifier on a service account at a shared terminal), CHG-506 (approval during a delegate's sick leave from an office IP), and CHG-504 (principal's approval during leave on a VPN session). These may reflect credential sharing, stale availability records, or migration artifacts. They cannot be resolved from this pack.

**Indeterminate (the remainder):** CHG-509 (weekend approval whose authority depends on an unverified absence condition) and CHG-504 (depends on identity).

**Organizational-pattern risk.** The sample understates the exposure. The 60-day record shows Ben approving 18 of 23 HIGH production changes, while the only delegation in evidence starts on 08-10. Unless earlier delegations exist, most of those approvals lack RR3 authority. This is a systemic risk, not a set of individual lapses. Collusion is not established. The Clara–Ben pair, the reciprocal training approvals, and Clara's presence in several irregular events are indicators requiring discriminating evidence, not conclusions.
