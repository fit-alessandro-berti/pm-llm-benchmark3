

## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is defined by operational impact, not technical fit. We assess deviations against five dimensions: (1) Control Criticality (direct financial risk vs. procedural variance), (2) Exposure (amount involved and reversibility), (3) Regulatory Exposure (fraud or customer harm), (4) Evidence Reliability (traceability and immutability), and (5) Recurrence (systemic vs. isolated).

Confidence reflects the certainty of evidence. A missing event may have high severity if the control is critical, but low confidence if external corroborating documents exist. Conversely, high alignment cost (e.g., logging errors) carries low severity. Severity measures harm potential; confidence measures our ability to prove it.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**Pattern 1: Silent Policy Violations (CL-609, CL-610)**
*   **Frequency:** 2 cases explicitly cited; pattern evidence notes 4 total Auto approvals violating P1/P2.
*   **Business Risk:** High financial exposure to fraud or ineligible beneficiaries.
*   **Cause:** Auto-approve logic ignores data conditions (bank changes, amounts) not checked by workflow engine.
*   **Owner:** Product Owner / Policy Engine.
*   **Recommendation:** Enforce data-aware gates before allowing Auto-approve transition.

**Pattern 2: Pre-Payment Execution (CL-604)**
*   **Frequency:** 1 explicit case; payment timestamp precedes approval.
*   **Business Risk:** Irreversible financial loss; severe control failure.
*   **Cause:** Asynchronous payment gateway integration allows release without blocking call to approval service.
*   **Owner:** Payments Engineering.
*   **Recommendation:** Implement blocking callback to core system before releasing funds.

**Pattern 3: External Evidence Gaps (CL-602, CL-606, CL-608)**
*   **Frequency:** 9 high-value cases show missing Assess Loss; external adjuster documents exist.
*   **Business Risk:** Incomplete audit trail; regulatory fines for missing evidence.
*   **Cause:** Third-party systems (external adjusters) not integrated into core audit log.
*   **Owner:** IT Integration / Compliance.
*   **Recommendation:** Mandate API handshake for all external assessments before approval.

**Pattern 4: Scope Mismatch (CL-607, CL-612)**
*   **Frequency:** Denied/withdrawn cases account for highest raw costs.
*   **Business Risk:** Noise in compliance reporting.
*   **Cause:** Process tree covers payable claims; denied flows diverge.
*   **Owner:** Process Owner.
*   **Recommendation:** Extend model with denial/withdrawal branches or exclude from conformance checks.

**Pattern 5: Notification Telemetry (CL-601, CL-603)**
*   **Frequency:** 74 cases show retry/telemetry duplicates.
*   **Business Risk:** Low; administrative noise.
*   **Cause:** Retry logic on connectors without idempotency checks in core log.
*   **Owner:** Infrastructure.
*   **Recommendation:** Aggregate retries at notification service layer before logging.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

**Case CL-604 (Payment Before Approval)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** User manually triggered payment before approval step.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Batch job released payments automatically based on prior approval not linked to this log.
*   **Test:** Check batch job logs at 10:03. If no batch entry, Hypothesis A.

**Case CL-610 (Auto Approval with Bank Change)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** Auto-approve logic ignores bank change status field.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Bank change was recorded in legacy system; new policy field not populated.
*   **Test:** Audit DB for `beneficiary_change_flag` value at time of approval.

**Case CL-602 (Missing Fraud Screen)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** Fraud screen executed but not logged due to system error.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Approver verbally skipped fraud screen.
*   **Test:** Check audit logs of fraud screening module for matching case ID timestamps.

**Pattern: Missing Assess Loss (CL-606, 9 high-value cases)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** External adjuster report filed directly to CRM; core system not notified.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Adjuster assessment occurred, but core UI label mapped incorrectly.
*   **Test:** Match adjuster contract start date to approval decision time window in CRM.

**Pattern: Scope Mismatch (CL-607, CL-612)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** Model designed solely for payable claims; denied claims treated as exceptions.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Model should cover full intake lifecycle.
*   **Test:** Review process definition document to validate P6 scope claim.

**Pattern: Notification Evidence (CL-608)**
*   **Hypothesis A:** Notification sent externally; link in CRM is unreliable artifact.
*   **Hypothesis B:** Notification was delayed beyond 24 hours.
*   **Test:** Verify secure portal message timestamp against payment time.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost sums technical edit distance, ignoring business consequences.

1.  **CL-609 (Cost 0):** Auto approval used on 18k claim (Policy P1: <= 5k). Model matches log perfectly (synchronous), but business logic failed. Risk: High.
2.  **CL-610 (Cost 0):** Auto approval used despite bank change (Policy P2). Perfect fit, but fraud control bypassed. Risk: High.
3.  **CL-604 (Cost 3):** Payment logged before approval. Alignment repair is cheap, but payment is irreversible. Risk: Critical.
4.  **CL-602 (Cost 1):** Missing fraud screen on 480k high-risk claim. Cost is low, but financial exposure is massive. Risk: Critical.
5.  **CL-601 (Cost 3):** UI events (Open/Close) added cost. These are irrelevant to financial control. Risk: Low.

Cost function weights events equally. Policy violations and irreversibility are unweighted. Silent moves (CL-605) fit perfectly but hide no risk; synchronous moves (CL-609) fit perfectly but hide policy breaches. Alignment measures traceability; risk measures consequence.
