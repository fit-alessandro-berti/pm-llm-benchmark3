## 1. Feasibility model

```text
PARTIAL-ORDER RELATION SET (A < B denotes A must complete before B starts; A || B denotes permitted concurrency)

Core spine (all cases):
  Register < Triage
  Triage < Identity Check
  Triage < Document Check
  Identity Check < Assessment_1
  Document Check_1 < Assessment_1
  Assessment_final < Decision
  Decision < Notify

Verification concurrency / originals gating:
  IF originals_required = no:
    Identity Check || Document Check_1
  IF originals_required = yes:
    Originals Receipt < Document Check_1
    Identity Check || Originals Receipt   (Identity Check may start after Triage without waiting for Originals Receipt)

Optional advisory and preliminary compliance overlap:
  Triage < Escalation Advice (S1)
  Escalation Advice || {Identity Check, Document Check_k, Assessment_k}
  Triage < Preliminary Compliance Screen (C1)
  Preliminary Compliance Screen || {Identity Check, Document Check_k, Assessment_k}

Mandatory compliance gate (high-risk OR high-value):
  Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final
  Compliance Review_final < Decision

Rework loop k -> k+1 (if Request More Info_k is issued by Assessment_k or Compliance Review_k):
  Request More Info_k < Document Check_{k+1}
  Document Check_{k+1} < Assessment_{k+1}
  IF high_risk OR high_value:
    Assessment_{k+1} < Compliance Review_{k+1} (new or explicitly reaffirmed) < Decision
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS

1. Control-flow entry/exit:
   - First(case) = Register; ImmediateSuccessor(Register) = Triage; Last(case) = Notify.
2. Originals prerequisite:
   - originals_required(case) => complete(Originals Receipt) <= start(Document Check_1).
3. High-risk / high-value compliance mandate:
   - (high_risk(case) OR high_value(case)) => exists Compliance Review_final with complete(Assessment_final) <= start(Compliance Review_final) AND complete(Compliance Review_final) <= start(Decision).
4. Preliminary screen non-substitution:
   - Preliminary Compliance Screen NEVER satisfies Constraint 3; final Compliance Review remains mandatory after the latest Assessment.
5. Rework authority and mandatory re-verification:
   - Only Assessment or Compliance Review may issue Request More Info.
   - Each Request More Info_k mandates a subsequent Document Check_{k+1} and Assessment_{k+1} before Decision (plus new/reaffirmed Compliance Review if Constraint 3 holds).
6. Four-eyes separation:
   - resource(Assessment_final) != resource(Compliance Review_final).
7. Single-server resource exclusivity:
   - For every resource r in {T1, T2, I1, I2, D1, D2, A1, A2, C1, S1, M1} and any two distinct service intervals e1, e2 assigned to r: complete(e1) <= start(e2) OR complete(e2) <= start(e1).
8. Queue-forecast uncertainty and logging validity:
   - Queue snapshots are non-binding forecasts subject to arrival bursts, shifts, and priority preemption.
   - Logged start/complete intervals must reflect actual work execution (real-time timestamps), not post-hoc batch entry.
```

**Classification of the four interventions:**
- **Timing / concurrency only (K-411):** Removing the legacy case lock unlocks normative parallelism (`Identity Check || Document Check`) without changing resource pools, mandatory gates, or decision standards.
- **Timing plus routing / scheduling (K-104 and K-219):** K-104 shifts advisory escalation earlier, adds an optional preliminary compliance screen concurrently with verification, and reserves C1 capacity while preserving the mandatory post-Assessment final compliance gate. K-219 alters resource routing (`D1 -> D2`), which changes service-time distributions and indirectly raises downstream rework probability without altering normative rules.
- **Decision-policy alteration (K-337):** Prohibiting `Request More Info 2` does not merely delete waiting time; it changes the admissible evidentiary threshold and outcome space (forcing conditional approval or rejection on incomplete evidence while still requiring C1 compliance review).

Downstream `Assessment` (A2) waited 6.5 h (Tue 20:00–22:30), missing M1's daytime window and waiting overnight until Wed 08:30–09:00 (48.0 h total).

Temporal propagation: At Mon 16:00, D2's queue forecast was 5 h (versus 11 h for D1), so entering at Mon 17:30 implies starting around Mon 21:00–22:30 (~3.5–5 h wait). Because D2 takes 4–8 h on standard documents and handwriting slows generalist verification, D2 likely requires 5–8 h, completing around Tue 02:30–06:30—roughly 7–11 h earlier than factual D1 completion (Tue 13:30). Both verification prerequisites are now met by early Tuesday morning. Crucially, finishing A2 on Tuesday afternoon instead of Tue 22:30 allows M1 to sign Tuesday afternoon (~Tue 13:00–18:00), eliminating the 10 h overnight M1 wait and producing a 15–20 h cycle-time reduction *if no rework occurs*. However, if D2 cannot verify the handwriting and flags ambiguity to A2, A2 must issue `Request More Info` (Rule 6), introducing an 8–36 h external response delay plus mandatory `Document Check 2` (4–8 h, likely escalated to D1) and `Assessment 2` (2–4 h), pushing completion to Wed evening–Fri afternoon (+10 to +52 h slower than factual).

Process-feasibility check: Satisfies Rules 1, 2, 4 (`Originals Receipt` Mon 17:30 precedes D2 start), 6, 7, and 8. Because K-219 is neither high-risk nor high-value, `Compliance Review` and four-eyes separation are not triggered. Importantly, under Rule 6, D2 cannot unilaterally issue `Request More Info`; any handwriting clarification must flow through `Assessment` (A2), and under Rule 7 any `Request More Info` requires both a fresh `Document Check` and fresh `Assessment` before `Decision`.

Best/base/worst case: **Best case:** D2 starts Mon 21:00, finishes cleanly in 5 h (Tue 02:00), A2 finishes Tue 11:30, and M1 notifies Tue 13:00 (~20 h saved, total 28 h). **Base case (bimodal):** On a clean read (plausible ~60–75% of handwritten standard cases), finishes Tue 14:00–18:00 (~15–19 h saved); on a clarification trigger (~25–40%), finishes Thu morning–Thu evening (+24 to +36 h slower). **Worst case:** D2 takes 8 h, flags handwriting ambiguity, external clarification takes 30–36 h, and rework queues behind D1 and A2, completing Fri afternoon (+50+ h slower) or suffering a silent misread that passes incorrect data to M1.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Historical D2 rework rates on handwritten documents exceeding ~25–30% (which would make expected cycle time worse than waiting for D1), evidence of silent error/defect rates when D2 handles handwriting, or evidence that D2's queue grew abruptly Monday evening above 9–10 h.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: On a high-risk, high-value case with an inconsistent supplier, `Assessment 1` (Wed 11:30) issued `Request More Info 1` (20.5 h external wait). After `Document Check 2` (Thu 12:00–17:00) found one remaining inconsistency, `Assessment 2` (Fri 08:00–10:30) issued `Request More Info 2`, which hit a 70.5 h weekend delay (Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00), followed by `Document Check 3` (Mon 13:00–16:30), `Assessment 3` (Tue 08:00–10:30), `Compliance Review` (Tue 15:30–19:30), and M1 approval Wed W2 09:10 (214.2 h total).

Minimal intervention: Leave execution identical through `Assessment 2` (Fri 10:30), then prohibit `Request More Info 2` and route the case directly to mandatory `Compliance Review` and `Decision` on the existing record, requiring either conditional approval (explicitly addressing the discrepancy) or rejection.

Temporal propagation: Suppressing `Request More Info 2`, `Document Check 3`, and `Assessment 3` removes ~96 h of serial external wait and rework processing between Fri 10:30 and Tue 10:30. The case enters C1's queue at Fri 10:30 instead of Tue 10:30. By Thursday 08:00, C1's queue had normalized to 5 h; assuming a 4–8 h Friday load, C1 starts around Fri 14:30–18:30. However, C1's 3–6 h service range applies *when evidence is complete*. Because one supplier discrepancy remains unresolved, C1 must evaluate compliance defensibility, possibly consult S1 concurrently, and draft enforceable conditions or rejection grounds, extending C1 service time to ~4.5–7 h. If C1 finishes Friday evening (Fri 19:00–23:00), M1 most plausibly issues `Decision` and `Notify` Monday morning (Mon 08:30–09:10)—saving exactly 48.0 h relative to Wed W2 09:10 (and up to ~109 h only if M1 executes an expedited Friday evening sign-off). If Friday backlog or S1 coordination pushes C1 completion to Monday morning/afternoon, M1 decides Mon afternoon or Tue morning (~24–40 h saved).

Process-feasibility check: Satisfies Rules 1–5, 8, and 9: `Compliance Review` (C1) still follows the latest `Assessment` (`Assessment 2` by A1) and precedes `Decision` (M1), maintaining four-eyes separation (`A1 != C1`). However, this intervention **modifies the decision policy** and restricts Rule 6 by barring both A1 and C1 from issuing a second `Request More Info`. Because silent omission is prohibited, the decision must legally and operationally internalize the unresolved discrepancy via binding conditions or rejection.

Best/base/worst case: **Best case (speed):** C1 finishes Fri 19:00 and M1 approves with enforceable conditions Fri 19:30 (~109.7 h saved), with conditions fully protecting against the supplier discrepancy. **Base case:** C1 finishes Fri night or Mon morning, M1 decides Mon 08:30–17:00 (~40–48 h saved), shifting the factual clean `approve` to `approve with conditions` or `reject`. **Worst case:** Unresolved ambiguity triggers prolonged C1/S1 escalation pushing decision to Tue morning (~24 h saved), AND either (a) a rejection provokes customer appeal/re-application exceeding factual time, or (b) conditional approval fails an audit or results in financial/compliance loss.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Audit or legal review confirming that conditional approval without reconciling supplier totals violates regulatory standards (making rejection mandatory), evidence that >50% of second-rework inconsistencies hide material fraud/sanctions breaches, or evidence that rejected high-value clients routinely re-submit and consume more total staff hours than a single weekend wait.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Although K-411 was a digital, standard, low-risk case requiring no originals, a legacy system case lock prevented `Triage` (Tue 09:35) from spawning `Document Check` until `Identity Check` (I2) completed at Tue 13:20. D2 then waited 1.7 h and worked Tue 15:00–19:30 (4.5 h). Reaching A2 at Tue 19:30—after Tuesday's assessment backlog grew with an assessor in training—caused a 14 h wait until Wed 09:30, with M1 finishing Wed 14:30 (29.5 h total).

Minimal intervention: At `Triage` (Tue 09:35), create both `Identity Check` (I2) and `Document Check` (D2) tasks simultaneously using a controlled workaround that allows concurrent ownership with mandatory real-time start/complete timestamps, leaving all resource assignments and downstream steps unchanged.

Temporal propagation: `Identity Check` remains unchanged (I2: Tue 11:30–13:20). `Document Check` enters D2's queue at Tue 09:35 instead of Tue 13:20 (3.75 h earlier). Using the Tue 08:00 snapshot (D2 queue: 4 h), wait time from Tue 09:35 is plausibly 2.5–4.0 h, putting D2 execution at Tue 12:00–13:35 start and Tue 16:30–18:05 completion (4.5 h service time, overlapping I2). Thus, **readiness for Assessment** advances by ~1.5–3.0 h (up to ~3.5 h if D2's queue drained faster). However, earlier readiness does **not** translate automatically into equal final time savings because A2 was the bottleneck (Tue 08:00 queue: 6 h; one assessor in training; factual wait: 14 h). If K-411 arrives at Tue 16:30–18:00, it jumps ahead of late-Tuesday arrivals but still faces heavy Tuesday evening A2 load. Most plausibly, A2 starts earlier on Wednesday morning (~Wed 07:00–08:30, finishing Wed 09:30–11:00) and M1 finishes Wed 11:30–13:00 (saving 1.5–3.0 h). Only if entering by Tue ~16:00 lets K-411 capture a Tuesday late-evening A2 slot (as observed on K-219 ending Tue 22:30) does it unlock M1's Wed 08:30 window, amplifying savings to ~5.5 h.

Process-feasibility check: Fully compliant with Rules 1, 2, 3 (`originals_required = no` permits `Identity Check || Document Check`), and 8. No compliance or four-eyes gates apply. Single-server exclusivity (Rule 11) holds because I2 and D2 are distinct resources. Feasibility hinges operationally on preventing concurrent record-write corruption and enforcing real-time timestamps so event logs reflect true parallelism rather than backfilled completion times.

Best/base/worst case: **Best case:** D2 starts Tue 11:45, ends Tue 16:15, K-411 secures a Tue 20:00–22:30 A2 slot, and M1 decides Wed 08:30–09:00 (~5.5 h saved, total 24.0 h). **Base case:** D2 ends Tue 16:30–18:00, A2 backlog absorbs part of the lead but starts Wed 07:30–08:30, and M1 finishes Wed 11:30–13:00 (~1.5–3.0 h saved). **Worst case:** Lock-workaround friction delays D2 or A2's overnight gap plus M1 afternoon batching (14:00) absorbs the entire 1.5–3.0 h lead, yielding -0.5 to +0.5 h net change while risking timestamp corruption.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: System logs showing that A2 operates in strict daily morning batches (meaning any Tue 16:00–22:00 arrival starts at the same Wed 09:30 slot), high incidence of concurrent-edit data loss under the manual workaround, or evidence that D2 actually requires identity-verification outputs before starting standard digital checks.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104: Earlier S1 escalation + prelim C1 screen + C1 slot reservation | +S1 early advisory load (shifts earlier); C1 prelim adds 1–2 h upfront to save 1–3 h later (roughly neutral to slightly negative if packet incomplete) | Target cases' post-Assessment C1 wait | Non-target C1 queue (and S1 early-week queue) | Unreserved urgent high-risk/high-value compliance cases (e.g., Monday urgent arrivals) | Mixed to **negative** at scale (positive only if tightly targeted without hard reservations) | Reservation-induced starvation: upstream D1/A1 variance leaves single-server C1 idle during reserved slots while unreserved queues explode exponentially near saturation. |
| K-219: Route handwritten standard docs to D2 instead of D1 | Net Document work rises (+25–50% longer D2 service); if rework triggers, adds extra D1/D2 + A2 work | D1 queue (complex corporate cases benefit slightly) | D2 queue, and A2/D1 queues if rework triggers | Standard digital/paper cases relying on fast D2 turnaround; A2 queue cases | **Ambiguous to negative** at scale (depends sharply on D2 rework threshold) | Rework amplification feedback: once D2 utilization nears 100% and rework rate exceeds ~20–25%, re-entering cases cause queues in both D2 and A2 to blow up superlinearly. |

Scaling any of these four interventions to 100 similar cases in a single week invalidates the single-case *ceteris paribus* assumption because shared queues couple cases nonlinearly. The sharpest failure occurs with reserving scarce compliance capacity (K-104). Because C1 is a single fully certified server already carrying a 16–20 hour backlog early in the week, reserving 100 provisional slots creates severe queue displacement and artificial starvation: C1 sits idle waiting for delayed upstream assessments or works on incomplete preliminary packets, while unreserved urgent compliance cases experience exploding waits. Even if preliminary work saves 1–3 hours at final review, spending 1–2 hours upfront yields little net capacity gain unless S1 absorbs substantive screening.

Substituting D2 for D1 on 100 handwritten cases (K-219) relieves D1 congestion but rapidly saturates D2, whose 2–5 hour queue is much thinner than D1's and whose service time is 25–50% slower. Worse, if D2 triggers additional rework on even 20–30% of cases, those cases re-enter `Document Check` and `Assessment`, creating a feedback loop that raises total demand across both pools and shifts congestion onto A1 and A2. Conversely, prohibiting second rework loops (K-337) avoids substantial Document, Assessment, and external-wait load—especially eliminating 70-hour weekend and external-response stalls—but migrates complexity to C1 and M1, who must craft defensible conditional approvals or handle appeals and re-applications that re-enter the system later.

Finally, parallelizing verification across 100 digital cases (K-411) does not increase total verification work, but it compresses arrival bursts into the assessment queue. With one assessor in training and A2 already carrying a 4–7 hour queue, earlier verification readiness primarily migrates waiting time downstream rather than cutting end-to-end cycle time proportionally. Moreover, at scale, manual workarounds invite batch timestamp entry, producing illusory concurrency in event logs while true execution remains staggered or stalled by lock contention. Across all four policies, local cycle-time improvements systematically overestimate system-wide gains whenever interventions shift load toward single-server bottlenecks (C1, M1) or unstable rework loops.

## 7. Final synthesis

Parallelizing verification on digital, no-originals cases (K-411) appears most robust across plausible assumptions. It restores a behavior already permitted by normative rules, requires no policy concessions on risk or four-eyes oversight, adds virtually no service workload, and is readily reversible. Even when assessment congestion absorbs much of its readiness gain, K-411 rarely creates catastrophic downside as long as timestamp discipline prevents logging corruption.

By contrast, suppressing the second rework loop (K-337) is the most dangerous intervention despite delivering the largest nominal speed gain (often 40–109 hours by avoiding weekend supplier stalls). Deleting a rework loop alters the allowed decision policy rather than merely accelerating flow: on high-risk, high-value cases, forcing a decision with unresolved discrepancies shifts risk into regulatory non-defensibility, credit or sanctions loss, and customer appeals that create hidden downstream rework.

Local counterfactuals are least transportable to system-wide policies in K-104 (reserving C1 slots) and K-219 (rerouting handwritten work to D2). Locally, reserving C1 looks like pure wait-time elimination, but across 100 cases it becomes zero-sum or negative-sum displacement on a single-server compliance bottleneck. Similarly, D2's short local queue collapses rapidly at scale, and handwriting-induced rework multiplies demand across Document and Assessment pools.

The most valuable additional data to sharpen these counterfactuals are: (1) task-ready (queue-entry), start, suspend, resume, and completion timestamps to separate true concurrency from waiting and batch entry; (2) explicit resource shift calendars and M1 decision-window schedules; (3) granular rework reason codes linking D2 handwriting flags to Assessment info requests; and (4) post-decision audit, appeal, and loss outcomes to measure K-337's true policy cost.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **(`structural`)** Normative precedence and compliance gates—specifically `Originals Receipt < Document Check`, post-Assessment final `Compliance Review`, four-eyes separation (`A1/A2 != C1`), and Assessment/Compliance monopoly over issuing `Request More Info`—remain strictly enforced under all interventions except K-337's explicit ban on a second rework loop.
2. **(`temporal`)** Operational verification, assessment, and compliance pools (`I1/I2`, `D1/D2`, `A1/A2`, `C1`) operate extended or shift-based schedules (consistent with observed overnight D1 and late-evening A2 work), whereas manager sign-offs (`M1`) and external supplier responses face daytime/weekday availability windows that can quantize or amplify cycle-time changes.
3. **(`resource`)** Queue-snapshot estimates represent approximate remaining work at snapshot times rather than deterministic waiting times; actual single-case waits vary with unobserved arrivals, priority rules, and Tuesday's assessor-in-training capacity dip.
4. **(`resource`)** `C1` is an unsubstitutable single-server bottleneck throughout the operating window, meaning neither `S1` advisory input nor preliminary screening can legally expand final certified compliance capacity except to the extent preliminary work genuinely reduces net `C1` reading time.
5. **(`quality`)** Routing handwritten standard documents to generalist `D2` (K-219) raises the probability of unresolved ambiguity that prompts `A2` to issue `Request More Info`, creating a bimodal cycle-time distribution rather than a smooth average shift.
6. **(`quality`)** Prohibiting `Request More Info 2` on K-337 does not eliminate the underlying supplier discrepancy; it converts temporal rework delay into elevated compliance/credit risk (under conditional approval) or commercial/appeal loss (under rejection).
7. **(`behavioral`)** Under K-411's manual workaround, staff adhere to real-time logging only when supported by technical controls or audits; without enforcement at scale, workers may revert to batch timestamp entry, producing spurious concurrency in process logs.
8. **(`interference`)** Single-case counterfactuals treat background arrivals and queue lengths as approximately fixed, whereas a 100-case weekly rollout induces strong inter-case coupling through C1 slot displacement, D2 saturation, rework feedback loops, and A2 arrival-burst compression.
