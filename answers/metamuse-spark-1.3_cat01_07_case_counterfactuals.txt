## 1. Feasibility model
```
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Originals Receipt [if originals_required]
Triage < Document Check [if NOT originals_required]
Originals Receipt < Document Check [if originals_required]
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < { Request More Info | Compliance Review | Decision }
Request More Info < Document Check_next
Document Check_next < Assessment_next
Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final [if high-risk OR high-value]
Assessment_final < Decision [if NOT high-risk AND NOT high-value]
Compliance Review_final < Decision [if high-risk OR high-value]
Decision < Notify
Triage < Escalation Advice < Compliance Review_final  // advisory, may overlap
Triage < Compliance-prelim < Compliance Review_final // preliminary, may overlap
Document Check_i || Identity Check [if NOT originals_required, or Identity may precede Originals Receipt]
Escalation Advice || {Identity Check, Document Check}  // S1 advisory may overlap
Compliance-prelim || {Identity Check, Document Check, Assessment}
```

```
C1: IF high-risk OR high-value THEN Compliance Review_final AFTER latest Assessment AND BEFORE Decision.
C2: IF Request More Info THEN next Document Check AND next Assessment REQUIRED BEFORE Decision; IF high-risk/high-value THEN new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review REQUIRED.
C3: Four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
C4: Preliminary compliance screen != final Compliance Review.
C5: Originals Receipt MUST precede Document Check WHEN originals_required = true; Identity Check MAY precede Originals Receipt.
C6: No resource performs two service intervals overlapping in time; EXCEPTION: S1 advisory may overlap with other checks.
C7: Queues are forecasts, not guarantees; start = max(ready_time, resource_available).
C8: Decision policy: Assessment/Compliance may approve / approve-with-conditions / reject; cannot silently ignore open material discrepancy.
```

Specified interventions by type:

- **K-104 earlier escalation / compliance preparation:** timing and preparation only. Adds a parallel advisory branch `Triage -> Escalation Advice` + `Compliance-prelim` concurrent with verification. Does not delete or reorder mandatory prerequisites; final `Assessment < Compliance Review_final < Decision` and four-eyes remain. Alters *when* evidence is read and whether C1 capacity is reserved, not what is required.
- **K-411 parallelize despite lock:** timing / concurrency restoration only. Removes an artificial `Identity Check < Document Check` precedence imposed by IT lock. True normative model already allows `Identity || Document` when no originals required. No routing change of resources in principle, no decision-policy change.
- **K-219 different document resource:** routing change with temporal consequences. Reassigns `Document Check: D1 -> D2` and therefore changes ready-time, service-time distribution, and rework probability. Does not waive prerequisites but may *create* an additional `Request More Info -> Document Check -> Assessment` loop.
- **K-337 suppress second rework:** decision-policy change, not merely faster timing. Prohibits `Assessment_2 -> Request More Info_2` and forces `Assessment_2 -> Compliance Review -> Decision` under unresolved uncertainty. Cycle-time reduction is inseparable from change in approval basis, loss exposure, appeal and compliance defensibility.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations
### K-104
Factual mechanism: Sanctions ambiguity noted at Triage Mon 08:50 but not acted on. Identity Mon 10:40-12:50 and Document Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40 (8h service, long D1 queue) proceeded without context. Assessment Tue 15:10-19:10 rediscovered ambiguity, triggering Escalation Tue 19:20-21:20 and cold-start Compliance Wed 13:10-17:40 (4.5h, C1 queue 8h Wed morning). Total ~58.5h dominated by D1 and C1 waits plus late learning.

Minimal intervention: At Triage 08:50 send incomplete packet to S1 and ask C1 for provisional hold. S1 advises Tue-equivalent guidance on Mon, C1 pre-reads corporate structure/sanctions sources in parallel with ID/Doc. Final Assessment still by A1 and final Compliance by C1 after Assessment, preserving four-eyes (A1 != C1).

Temporal propagation: Early start does not change D1 start (D1 queue 11-13h binds) or A1 start (A1 queue binds Tue afternoon), so readiness for Compliance remains Tue ~21:20 after Assessment+Escalation. Gain comes only at C1 stage: pre-read saves 1-3h service (4.5h -> 2.5-3.5h) and reservation moves C1 start earlier from Wed 13:10 toward Wed 08:00-11:00 if hold honored, or Tue evening if C1 overtime/cancellation. Hence Notify moves from Wed 18:40 to Wed morning/early afternoon. Earlier readiness != earlier completion unless C1 queue actually yields.

Process-feasibility check: Satisfies Register<Triage<Identity,Document<Assessment<Compliance<Decision<Notify, concurrency allowed for prelim, four-eyes preserved (A1 final assessor, C1 final reviewer), originals not required so Identity||Document allowed. Reservation is administrative, not a waived check. Preliminary screen does not replace final review.

Best/base/worst case: Best -11h (Tue evening C1 start, 2.5h review, Notify Wed 08:00); Base -6h (Wed 10:00 start, 3.5h review, Notify ~13:30); Worst -1 to +2h net single-case (packet too incomplete, prelim discarded, reservation unused or causes re-scheduling friction) with spillover harm to others.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Log showing C1 fully occupied Tue-Wed with non-preemptible urgent cases despite reservation; or S1 prelim note stating packet unusable without Assessment output, implying zero saved reading time; or time-stamps showing A1 Assessment would itself shift later due to displaced preparation effort.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: Triage routed handwritten standard documents to D1 for accuracy. Identity Mon 14:20-16:20, Originals 17:30, then wait for D1 until Tue 09:30-13:30 (4h accurate), Assessment Tue 20:00-22:30, Decision Wed 08:30. Waits reflect D1 queue (11h Mon 16:00, 8h Tue 08:00) traded for low rework risk.

Minimal intervention: Keep everything identical except Document resource D1->D2, started as soon as originals arrive (eligible Mon 17:30 since Originals Receipt < Document Check satisfied). No other prerequisite waived.

Temporal propagation: D2 queue Mon 16:00 5h, Tue 08:00 4h, so D2 start ~Mon 22:00-Tue 01:00 vs D1 Tue 09:30: ~9-11h earlier start. D2 service slower: 4-8h standard, upper end for handwriting (factual D1 4h -> D2 likely 6-10h), completing Tue ~04:00-09:00 vs Tue 13:30: net readiness gain ~5-9h. Assessment A2 queue 6-7h means earlier-ready case can claim earlier A2 slot Tue afternoon/evening instead of 20:00, pulling Decision to Tue evening. If D2 triggers clarification, propagation reverses: external collection 8-36h + Document redo 4-8h + Assessment redo 2-5h dominates, pushing Notify to Wed evening-Thu.

Process-feasibility check: Satisfies Triage<Identity, Originals< Document(D2)<Assessment<Decision<Notify, low-risk so no compliance needed, four-eyes N/A. D2 is qualified pool member; routing to D2 is allowed. Any rework path still requires Document->Assessment before Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best -16h, clean D2 5h, early A2, Notify Tue 17:00; Base -12h no rework but average D2 speed, Notify Tue ~21:00; Worst +30h with RMI loop (external 24h + redo), Notify Thu; expected value near -4h once 30% rework risk weighted.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Sample of D2 handwritten cases showing >50% clarification rate or >10h service; or A2 log showing no earlier slot Tue (assessor in training fully booked), so earlier document readiness yields zero final gain; or D2 concurrent workload proving D2 start no earlier than D1.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: Inconsistent supplier totals caused Doc1->Assess1->RMI1 (Wed 11:30-Thu 08:00 ~20.5h) ->Doc2->Assess2 (Fri 08:00-10:30) which found one residual inconsistency, triggering RMI2 over weekend Fri 10:30-Mon 09:00 (~70.5h) ->Doc3->Assess3->Compliance->Decision Wed. External waits + weekend explain ~90h of ~8-day cycle; service times secondary.

Minimal intervention: Prohibit RMI2 after Assessment2 Fri 10:30. Require Assessment2 evidence to go directly to Compliance Review and Decision with explicit treatment: conditional approve (limits, reserves, covenants) or reject/defer. No silent ignore. This is a policy truncation, not a resource swap.

Temporal propagation: Deleting RMI2 deletes the dominant external+weekend wait and Doc3 (3.5h)+Assess3 (2.5h). Compliance that factually occurred Tue 15:30 can occur Fri 11:00-17:00 (C1 queue Friday unknown but assumed available), Decision Fri afternoon or Mon morning depending on M1. Saving is calendar-driven (~70h weekend + 20h work), not service acceleration. Downstream times change because ready-time for Compliance moves from Tue to Fri, not because C1 works faster.

Process-feasibility check: Still satisfies Identity, Originals<Doc, Doc<Assess, Assess2<Compliance<Decision<Notify and four-eyes (A1 != C1). Compliant only if Decision explicitly addresses discrepancy with conditions/reject and documented rationale; clean unconditional approve would violate defensibility rule C8.

Best/base/worst case: Best -90h (Fri afternoon conditional approve, Notify Fri 17:00); Base -80h (Fri compliance, Mon morning decision); Worst -65h (C1/M1 unavailable Fri, decision Mon) but with adverse quality tail: higher loss/appeal probability that dwarfs time value.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that residual inconsistency was material fraud indicator requiring rejection as only defensible option, making conditional approve indefensible and implying large expected loss; or C1 log refusing to review incomplete evidence Fri, forcing wait anyway; or policy proof that conditional-approve authority does not exist, so truncation only yields reject with high appeal cost.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: Legacy system created only identity task at Triage. Identity I2 Tue 11:30-13:20 completed, then 1.7h gap for task creation, Document D2 Tue 15:00-19:30 (4.5h), Assessment Wed 09:30-12:00 after overnight assessment queue growth, Decision Wed 14:00. Artificial sequentiality added creation gap and prevented overlap.

Minimal intervention: At Triage create both tasks and authorize controlled workaround allowing I2 and D2 to claim concurrently, with real-time start/complete stamps (no batch entry). No change to required checks or decision rule.

Temporal propagation: Identity service ~1.8h, Document ~4.5h. Parallel start Tue ~11:30 would complete Identity ~13:20 and Document ~16:00-17:30 vs factual 19:30, gaining 2-3h readiness. However Assessment queue (A2 6h Tue, 5h Wed) means next Assessment start is Wed 09:30 regardless of Tue afternoon vs evening readiness; case waits overnight. Final Decision therefore moves at most 0-2h unless Assessment slot happens to free earlier. Earlier readiness does not equal earlier completion when downstream bottleneck binds.

Process-feasibility check: Satisfies Register<Triage<{Identity,Document}<Assessment<Decision<Notify with true concurrency allowed for digital standard docs without originals. Requires distinct resources I2 and D2 with no overlapping service by same person (satisfied), and accurate logging to prove no false concurrency. Workaround must be audited.

Best/base/worst case: Best -4h (Document starts 11:30, finishes 15:30, catches Tue evening Assessment gap, Notify Wed 10:30); Base -1.5h (readiness gain absorbed, Notify Wed ~13:00); Worst 0h + logging burden (no final gain, extra coordination).

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Assessment queue logs showing Tue evening A2 idle slot that a 16:00-ready case could have taken (would upgrade saving to ~15h overnight); or resource logs showing I2=D2 person overlap or batch-entered timestamps, proving claimed concurrency false; or evidence that workaround delayed task creation further.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis
| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 reserve + prelim (S1/C1) | +0.5-2h S1 prelim, +1-2h C1 prelim, but -1-3h final C1; net C1 ~neutral, S1 up | Compliance final service queue per treated case | Compliance reservation queue / urgent non-target wait; S1 queue | 2-3 urgent high-risk cases awaiting C1; standard cases needing S1 advice | Mixed: treated faster, others slower; net ~zero-sum unless prelim truly saves reading | Threshold: beyond C1 utilization ~85%, reservations cause disproportionate priority inversion |
| K-219 D1->D2 | -4h D1, +6-10h D2, +expected rework (0.3* (D+A+external)) | D1 queue (frees senior for complex) | D2 queue + Assessment rework queue + external waiting | Standard cases queued for D2; complex corporate cases needing D1 benefit | Ambiguous: positive if D2 slack, negative if D2 overload triggers rework cascade | Rework tipping: small rise in clarification rate overwhelms D2 capacity and creates retry loop |
| K-337 suppress RMI2 | -1 Doc + -1 Assess + -1 external wait; +higher monitoring/appeal work later | Document, Assessment, external-response inventory (WIP falls) | Post-decision remediation, appeal, compliance-defense queue | Future cases (precedent); portfolio exposed to same supplier risk | Short-run positive cycle-time, long-run negative risk-adjusted value | Weekend/calendar cliff: deleting a weekend-spanning wait yields lumpy large saving once, but loss tail is fat |
| K-411 concurrent verification | Same total service, but simultaneous I+D demand Tue; +logging overhead | Identity-document idle gap; document waiting-for-identity | Assessment queue (earlier arrivals bunch) | Standard cases awaiting A2 Wed morning | Near-zero or slightly negative: gain evaporates, bottleneck migrates to Assessment | Bottleneck migration: upstream parallelism without downstream capacity merely moves queue |

Single-case analysis holds other arrivals fixed, so earlier readiness translates to earlier completion. At 100 cases per week that ceteris-paribus breaks. Reserving scarce compliance capacity for 100 K-104-like cases would exhaust C1, the only certified reviewer with 16-20h queues Mon-Tue. Provisional holds become priority inversion: two urgent compliance cases noted Mon 16:00 would wait 3-8h longer each, and prelim reading on incomplete packets wastes 100-200h of C1/S1 time if packets lack Assessment context. Benefit per case shrinks as reservation contention rises, with threshold effects once utilization exceeds ~85%. Substitution between D1 and D2 shows similar fragility. Moving 100 standard handwritten cases from D1 to D2 frees D1 for complex work (D1 queue 13h lower) but floods D2 (2-5h 15h+). D2's slower handwritten service (8-14h complex, 4-8h standard) plus higher clarification propensity creates additional rework: 25-40 extra RMI loops, each consuming 8-36h external response plus Document and Assessment redo, more than erasing the initial 10h gain and spilling into Assessment queues. Avoided rework in K-337 looks opposite: deleting 100 RMI2 loops removes weekend external waits and Doc/Assess load, sharply lowering WIP, but concentrates risk in decisions and downstream remediation, appeal and monitoring queues that are not measured in cycle time. Assessment-queue bottlenecks bind all speedups: A1 9-12h and A2 6-7h queues Mon-Tue mean earlier document readiness merely advances arrival into an already congested A-queue, with FIFO or priority reordering harming non-target cases. Weekend and external-response effects make savings lumpy and non-transportable: a Fri-Mon RMI spans 70h largely idle, so suppression saves calendar time once but cannot be replicated mid-week. Finally, timestamp behavior versus true concurrency matters: at scale, batch-entered completions for K-411-style workarounds can simulate parallelism while I2/D2 actually worked sequentially, overstating capacity and hiding the Assessment migration. Real-time logging validation is therefore essential before interpreting faster readiness as system improvement.

| quality outcome | Document/Assessment rework rate, approval correctness, audit logging errors | Adequacy of conditions, compliance defensibility score, reversal/appeal within 90 days |

## 7. Final synthesis
The most robust intervention is K-411 concurrent verification. It restores intended parallelism without waiving checks, changing resources' skill match, or altering decision policy, and its downside is bounded to logging overhead and minor Assessment bunching. Benefit is modest — often zero final gain when the Assessment queue binds — but it is safe, fully reversible, and highly informative about true versus recorded concurrency. The most dangerous despite potential speed is K-337 suppression of the second rework loop. Saving 65-90h by skipping a weekend-spanning external wait is seductive, yet it conflates faster completion with equivalent decisions: the case must then be conditionally approved or rejected on known-inconsistent evidence, raising expected loss, appeal, and defensibility costs that can dwarf calendar savings and cannot be reversed after disbursement. Local counterfactuals are least transportable where scarce or downstream capacity binds. K-104 early C1 reservation looks 6h beneficial single-case with others fixed, but with 100 cases C1 — the sole certified reviewer — becomes a zero-sum priority system where treated cases improve only by delaying equally urgent others. Similarly K-219 D1-to-D2 gains evaporate once D2 floods and rework multiplies, and K-411 readiness gains vanish into the Assessment queue. Most valuable additional data are resource-level event logs with real-time start/complete stamps and queue snapshots, C1/S1 rework-reuse traces linking prelim to final hours, D2 accuracy and clarification rates by handwriting complexity, external supplier response distributions including weekends, and spillover waits for non-target cases around each intervention.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. Preliminary S1/C1 work can save 1-3h of final compliance reading without anchoring bias. [behavioral]
2. Queue forecasts at snapshots approximate resource availability for single-case counterfactuals with ±50% uncertainty. [temporal]
3. C1 is the sole fully certified reviewer, so reservations directly displace other high-risk cases at high utilization. [resource]
4. D2 has higher clarification risk on handwritten documents than D1, with rework requiring external 8-36h plus redo. [quality]
5. Assessment queues (A1/A2) remain binding Wed morning, so earlier upstream readiness does not guarantee earlier Assessment start. [interference]
6. Suppressing RMI2 forces a conditional-approve or reject decision; silent unconditional approve is non-compliant. [structural]
7. Workaround concurrency in K-411 reflects true simultaneous work only if timestamps are real-time, not batch-entered. [structural]
8. External supplier and weekend delays are calendar-driven and independent of internal resource effort. [temporal]
