"""
Staggered-adoption diagnostics for the automated-triage rollout (Jan-Aug 2026).
Self-contained: the branch-month panel is embedded below. Requires pandas;
statsmodels and matplotlib are used if installed.

CAUSALITY NOTE (applies to every number below):
- Adoption was NOT random. Alpha/Beta were targeted for high volume and poor

  adopted. Rollout months coincide with a routing campaign (+10%/+5% volume),

- Identification would require: conditional parallel trends of never-treated
  controls, no anticipation, and no interference. The fraud desk plausibly
  violates no-interference, and the routing campaign bundles a second
  intervention with availability. None of these assumptions is testable here.
- 5 branches = 5 clusters. Cluster-robust SEs are asymptotically unjustified;
  treat them as rough flags. Prefer the transparent window contrasts printed
  first.
- median_cycle_h / p90_cycle_h are branch-month AGGREGATES. They can move

"""

Alpha,2026-01,0,1023,34,28.0,4,50.4,101.3,13.2,7.8,3.83
... all 40 rows ...

NEVER = ["Delta", "Epsilon"]

def wmean(branch, months, col):

    return s[col].mean()

def pct(a, b):
    return 100.0 * (b - a) / a

WINDOWS = {
    "Alpha": {"pre": ["2026-01", "2026-02", "2026-03"],
              "early": ["2026-04", "2026-05", "2026-06"],

## 1. What is observed

Three things that are often conflated must be kept apart. First, **availability**: the automation flag is 1 from April for Alpha and Beta and from July for Gamma, marking only that the system existed in that branch-month. Second, **use**: `automated_fast_path_pct` — the realized treatment intensity — sits at 4–6% even before any availability (itself a definitional puzzle) and ramps to roughly 50% at Alpha and Beta and 32% at Gamma by August, so dose grows for months after "adoption" and never approaches 100%. Third, **selection**: no branch was randomized. Alpha and Beta went first precisely because they had high volumes and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot; Delta and Epsilon had not adopted by August, so treated and untreated branches differ systematically in scale and baseline performance. Contemporaneous operational changes include the March centralization of the fraud desk (escalation_pct steps up exactly 2.0pp in every branch that month), an April routing campaign adding roughly 10% arrivals at Alpha and Beta (about 5% at Gamma from July), FTE changes in April–June (Alpha 2, Beta +1, Gamma 1, Delta +1), a May product change lifting high-complexity share by 3–8pp everywhere, and a July–August demand dip at all branches. Finally, **measurement limits**: all outcomes are branch-month aggregates; CSAT is a monthly mean with 20–45% response rates and no response counts; no case-level timestamps, queue states, or fraud-desk waiting times exist.

| Alternative B: comparative interrupted time series (CITS) per adopter | Level + slope break at adoption month; controls' break used to de-trend | Controls' change over identical calendar windows | Complexity, volume; control-derived trend | E1, E7 | Deterministic pre-trends; common seasonal shape | Pre-fit quality; placebo breakpoints (March, May) | Only two uncontaminated pre-months for Alpha/Beta (March contains the fraud-desk onset); Gamma's post-period coincides with the common Jul–Aug dip |
| Alternative C: within-adopter treatment-intensity (dose) model, optionally instrumented by availability-tenure ramp | Continuous `automated_fast_path_pct` with branch and month FE | Within-branch over time; cross-branch within month | Complexity, workload/FTE, month FE | E6 | Use exogenous conditional on FE (implausible); IV variant requires the availability-driven ramp to be excludable (dubious: the ramp coincides with learning) | First-stage strength; over-identification across cohorts; dose-vs-calendar-time separation | Endogenous selection into use; ramp collinear with tenure and calendar shocks |

**Why the primary strategy.** The primary design is preferable for four reasons. First, Delta and Epsilon remain untreated through August, so both cohorts have contemporaneous never-treated comparisons; we never have to extrapolate a pre-period trend across the intervention, which is the weakest link of interrupted time series here — Alpha and Beta have only two uncontaminated pre-months, because March already contains the fraud-desk onset. Second, staggering gives two cohorts with different adoption dates and different selection histories (Alpha/Beta targeted on poor performance, Gamma pilot-gated). Agreement between cohort-specific estimates — both suggest a single-digit to low-teens relative median improvement and a roughly +1pp escalation step — is more informative than any single contrast, and disagreement (Beta's complexity-adjusted median gain near zero) is itself evidence about heterogeneity. Third, the design forces honesty about the routing campaign: post-April volume is part of the as-implemented package, so the estimand is explicitly a package effect, with a separately stated, more assumption-laden "system-alone" calculation. Fourth, with five clusters, formal inference is not credible; the design's value lies in transparent window contrasts, leave-one-control-out checks, and a placebo pseudo-adoption that reveals a ±4–5pp noise floor — better discipline than regression coefficients with unjustified standard errors. Synthetic control (two or three donors with very different levels) and dose models (endogenous use) serve as sensitivity tools, not primary evidence.

### 5.3 Treatment intensity rather than the binary flag

Within-adopter post-period correlations (arithmetic): AFP% vs median  **0.39 (Alpha), 0.34 (Beta)**; AFP% vs p90  **+0.20 (Alpha), +0.31 (Beta)**. Months with use 41% (Alpha Jul–Aug, Beta Jun–Aug) average median 46.7h vs 48.5h in months at 25–34% use, while average p90 is essentially flat across those splits (104.5h vs 103.0h). So intensity co-moves with *median* improvement but not with *tail* improvement. However, dose is confounded with tenure and calendar (the Jul–Aug dip), and Gamma improved at only 25–32% use — so no defensible "threshold" claim; the code below also runs a dose regression, whose coefficient must be read as descriptive because teams choose their level of use.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Pre-period slopes are small but noisy (Alpha 50.446.949.4h). Critically, the **March fraud-desk onset hit branches unevenly before any treatment** (median changes: Gamma +8.5%, Delta +10.4%, Alpha +5.3%, Beta +1.1%, Epsilon +2.3%) — direct evidence that common shocks have branch-varying effects, which weakens parallel-trends confidence. A **placebo** (pseudo-adopting Delta in May, Epsilon as control) yields DiDs of **+3.9pp (median) and +4.6pp (p90)** with no treatment at all — a noise/confound floor of roughly ±4–5pp. Regression to the mean is a genuine concern because Alpha/Beta were selected on poor cycle times, and the observed pattern (treated flat/better, controls worse) is qualitatively what RTM produces; however, controls' early deterioration is largely explained by observables (complexity-adjusted residuals only +1.4 to +1.8h), and Alpha/Beta did *not* decline immediately as pure RTM would predict. RTM cannot be excluded; I widen the median bound by ±3–4pp accordingly.

### 5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions

- **Median:** unadjusted DiD 8 to 17pp; complexity-adjusted 1 to 17pp; placebo floor ±4pp; RTM allowance ±3–4pp; spillover inflation unquantified (could remove a further third). Defensible **package-effect range: 3% to 15% (point  8%)**. A "system-alone" reading — applying the untreated volume elasticity (~1.0, from Delta/Epsilon JanJun) to the ~11pp volume differential created by the routing campaign — would roughly double the effect (10% to 25%), but rests on additional assumptions.
- **p90:** unadjusted DiD 7 to 12pp; complexity-adjusted 1 to 11pp; but if even part of Delta's early complexity-adjusted p90 residual (+8.6h) is fraud-desk congestion caused by adopters, the adjusted DiD shrinks toward zero. Absolute p90 remained **+7–11% above pre-rollout** at Alpha/Beta through the ramp. Defensible **relative range: 8% to +2%; absolute range +2% to +12%** — sign not established. Median and tail behavior must not be merged into one "cycle-time effect."
- **CSAT:** observed DiD +0.07 to +0.13, but if post-rollout response composition shifted (rates moved between 20% and 45%), plausible shifts of ±0.15 in the mean are possible  **range 0.10 to +0.25**, effectively unidentified.
