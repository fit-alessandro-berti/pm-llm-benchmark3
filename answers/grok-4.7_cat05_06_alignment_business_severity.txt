## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity scores the business consequence of the interpretation supported by the case brief and the supplied alignment. It is independent of edit distance. Confidence scores how firmly that interpretation is supported. A severe case can rest on weak evidence and still need investigation; a certain case can be immaterial and should stay out of the control queue.

The rubric uses eight considerations together, with no numeric weight:

- **Control objective.** Pre-payment anti-fraud and approval gates outrank logging hygiene. Paying without the approval path required for that claim is the top failure. A missing core event for work that a signed external record already shows is an evidence gap.
- **Amount and exposure.** Euros at risk sit on a failed gate, not on alignment cost. Exposure is potential loss under a failed control, not proven fraud.
- **Reversibility.** A payment a bank has already accepted outranks a decision that can still be stopped. Post-hoc approval does not unwind settlement.
- **Timing.** Approval or fraud screening after payment release fails the gate. A portal message at three hours can still meet a 24-hour notice rule if the timestamp is trustworthy.
- **Customer and regulatory harm.** Wrong-account payment, an unnotified claimant, and a denial or withdrawal with no acknowledgement are different harms. UI noise has none.
- **Recurrence.** A silent failure mode seen on four cases in the month outranks a one-off label defect of the same case-level severity, because the next case will also look perfectly fit.
- **Evidence reliability.** An immutable message id, a bank timestamp, and a signed report with a stable case key support a conclusion. A mutable CRM text field, a verbal assurance, and a document with no stable case key do not.
- **Model scope.** The tree describes payable claims. Denied and withdrawn cases can generate large edit distance while the operational outcome is the one the business wanted. Scope is disputed, so those alignments are weak compliance evidence until the owners decide the population.

**Severity scale**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fitting behavior, silent routing, or technical noise. No payment-control or customer-harm consequence. |
| 2 | Evidence, label, or scope gap where the payment controls either held or no payment was due. Limited customer-treatment residue only. |
| 3 | Real control or evidence weakness whose harm is conditional, with substantial exposure but the anti-fraud payment path evidenced. |
| 4 | Policy-prohibited approval path actually used and payment executed. Exposure bounded, or the fraud indicators incomplete, and the monitoring design is blind. |
| 5 | Pre-payment control failed on a high-value or already-settled payment: missing required fraud control at very large exposure, or payment accepted by the bank before approval. |

**Confidence is not severity.** High confidence means the facts decide the narrative (timestamps, amounts, risk, bank-change flag, and which activities truly synced). Low confidence means a missing core event is compatible with the activity having happened elsewhere, or the only corroboration can be edited. Competing hypotheses in section 4 are unresolved tests, not extra deviations. A model move means the mined alphabet has no matching event. It does not, by itself, prove the activity never occurred.

**Portfolio priority can diverge from both raw cost and severity.** A EUR 4,500 bank-change auto-approval ranks above larger but better-controlled claims because the gate failed completely, recall may still be open, and conformance cost is zero so the next case will not surface. Severity stays case-level. Priority adds silence, recurrence, and whether money can still be stopped.

Two zero-cost results are different objects. `T(tau,c=0)` is a fitting hidden route into a branch the net allows. A fully synchronous `Auto Approve` on a claim that violates the amount, risk, or bank-change rules is a data-aware policy failure with the same raw cost.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**Retry duplicates of claimant notification.** CL-603, plus 74 cases in the month with `L(Notify Claimant)` immediately after a synchronous notice and the same message id, all on one retrying connector. Business risk is dashboard contamination and, only if the channel actually delivered twice, a minor customer nuisance. Likely cause is connector logging, not two business decisions. Confidence is high. Owner: integration engineering for that connector. Claims communications owns the separate double-delivery count.

**External-adjuster assessment gap.** CL-606, inside a month pattern of nine high-value cases with `M(Assess Loss)` and an external-adjuster document. Two of the nine lack a stable document-to-case identifier. Business risk is an incomplete audit trail on large claims, and a real unsigned or mis-linked assessment on the weak pair. The seven with a stable key are logging gaps if the signature predates fraud screening. Likely cause is an unintegrated adjuster channel. Confidence is medium at pattern level and should be split: higher where the key is immutable, low for the two weak links. Owner: claims operations jointly with the document-integration owner.

**Label mapping.** CL-611. `Assess Damage` in the core log versus `Assess Loss` in the procedure and UI. The map was updated after the case, so historical alignments still double-count. No month frequency was supplied; the population is at least this case and any peer events produced before the map change. Business risk is false nonconformance on a HIGH-risk EUR 62,000 path that otherwise shows fraud screening and manual approval, plus the chance the labels are truly different steps. Likely cause is an emitter name drifting from the procedure. Confidence in the synonym reading is medium-high, pending the procedure version. Owner: process architect and master-data owner. Do not collapse the labels until they confirm.

**Out-of-scope denial and withdrawal.** CL-607 and CL-612. Denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with raw cost at least 4. In this sample they are the only two at cost 4. Business risk on the cases themselves is low: no improper payment. The operational risk is indirect: they flood the conformance queue and can teach analysts to ignore high cost. A second risk appears only if denials are excluded with no separate model and a real denial-control failure is then invisible. Likely cause is a payable-claim tree (P6, disputed for denials; withdrawals are the same structural gap) plus unit cost preferring the short auto-approve repair. Confidence is high on the mechanism and medium on the governance choice, because the owners disagree. Owner: process owner and model owner together. Neither should “fix” this by lowering model-move costs on the payable path.

**Data-aware auto-approval failures.** CL-609 and CL-610, inside four fully synchronous auto-approve cases in the month that violate P1 or P2. Business risk is straight-through payment outside the written gates, including beneficiary-diversion (bank change one hour before payment) and over-threshold MEDIUM-risk payments. Conformance cost is zero, so recurrence is silent. Likely cause is a control-flow tree and a payment rule with no amount, risk, or bank-change guard. Confidence is high that these traces are policy failures on the stated attributes, and medium on the exact rule defect until decision snapshots are read. Owner: payments control owner and claims-rules engineering. Process mining is the detective net, not the preventive gate.

**Payment released before the required approval, with no fraud-screen event.** CL-604 is the evidenced instance. No month frequency was given, so this is not claimed as a rate. Business risk is irreversible disbursement: bank acceptance at 10:03, approval at 10:20, amount EUR 90,000, fraud screen absent from the alignment. Likely cause is a payment file that can leave before the approval record exists. Confidence is high on timing. Owner: payments operations and the control designer for the release gate.

**Manual approval without a retained fraud screen on a P2 claim.** CL-602. Business risk is EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, bank changed the day before, paid after an approval whose only stated basis is verbal. Likely cause is an approval screen that does not require a fraud-screen record id. Confidence is high that the retained gate failed. Owner: financial-crime operations, with payments operations on the release hold.

**Multi-channel notice with a weak link.** CL-608, distinct from the retry pattern. Business risk is an unnotified claimant on a EUR 250,000 payment, or a false breach if the portal message is real. Likely cause is a CRM text field used as the case link. Confidence is low either way until the portal store is read. Owner: customer communications and compliance evidence.

**UI telemetry in the business log.** CL-601. No month count was supplied. Business risk is inflated conformance cost (here, 3) and analyst time. Likely cause is an unfiltered clickstream. Confidence is high on the names given (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`). Owner: the process-mining pipeline. P5’s exception still applies: if an autosave payload contains a control-relevant mutation, that payload stays in scope.

**Fitting silent routing.** CL-605. Frequency is structural: every XOR conversion can emit tau. Business risk of the move itself is none. The analytical risk is treating every cost-0 trace as compliant. Likely cause is Petri-net translation, not operations. Confidence is high. Owner: process-mining analyst, as documentation only.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

A missing log event is an alphabet gap. It becomes an operational conclusion only after the places the activity could have been recorded are checked.

**CL-602, missing fraud screen on a paid HIGH-risk bank-change claim.** Hypothesis A: no fraud screen was performed; the approver substituted a conversation. Hypothesis B: a screen was performed in a fraud platform or mailbox and never integrated. Hypothesis C: it was logged under another label. Discriminator: a persisted artifact carrying this case id, an analyst, an outcome, and a timestamp at or before manual approval. Finding that artifact supports B or C and downgrades the case to an evidence-integration defect, while the verbal-only story stays a control failure if the artifact is an unstructured chat. Finding no artifact in the fraud platform, case file, and mail archive supports A. The approver interview alone does not discriminate.

**CL-604, payment at 10:03 and approval at 10:20.** Hypothesis A: the outbound payment was released before approval, and 10:20 is retrospective. Hypothesis B: approval happened earlier outside the core system and the core write lagged. Hypothesis C: 10:03 is bank settlement of a payment that had been properly released after approval, and clocks disagree. Discriminator: the payment-file creation and send timestamps, the approval-record created time (not a later status time), and clock-sync evidence. A send timestamp before any approval token supports A and leaves the P3 breach standing even if a fraud-screen document is later found. An approval token that predates the outbound file supports B. A fraud-screen search is a separate test and does not choose among A, B, and C.

**CL-610, auto-approval one hour after a bank change.** Hypothesis A: the rule engine does not read the bank-change flag. Hypothesis B: the flag was stale at decision time even though master data now shows a change one hour earlier. Hypothesis C: an override forced auto-approval. Discriminator: the immutable decision snapshot (amount, risk, bank-change flag, rule version, override code) at the auto-approve timestamp. Flag true and rule still auto supports A. Flag false while master data was already changed supports B. An override code supports C. The synchronous alignment does not help; it is the same shape as a clean auto-approval.

**CL-609, auto-approval at EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk.** Hypothesis A: the engine has no P1 thresholds. Hypothesis B: at decision time the snapshot was at or under EUR 5,000 and LOW, and amount or risk was revised after release. Hypothesis C: an override. Discriminator: the same style of decision snapshot. A snapshot that already shows 18,000 and MEDIUM supports A or C and confirms a prohibited straight-through payment. A snapshot inside P1 supports B and moves the defect to “no re-route after reassessment,” which is a different control. Do not rewrite history from the current case attributes alone.

**CL-606 and the nine-case adjuster pattern.** Hypothesis A: the licensed adjuster completed the assessment and the core event was never integrated. Hypothesis B: the PDF belongs to another case or is unsigned draft, especially on the two cases with no stable id. Hypothesis C: the report was attached after the fact to fill a skipped assessment. Discriminator: signature time versus fraud-screen time, document hash, and a case id inside immutable document metadata rather than a mutable link. For CL-606, a 10:05 signature, metadata for this case, and fraud screening after that time support A. Absence of a stable id leaves B open: the model move is not proof the work was skipped, and the PDF is not proof it was done. Keep the two weak cases out of any automatic “pass” used for the other seven.

**CL-608, core notice missing, CRM text at +3 hours.** Hypothesis A: the claimant was notified in the portal at +3 hours and only the core event is missing. Hypothesis B: the CRM text was added or edited later and no reliable notice exists inside 24 hours. Hypothesis C: a message exists but went to the wrong recipient. Discriminator: the portal message store, not the CRM text field — immutable id, recipient, timestamp, case foreign key, delivery state. A matching record supports A, satisfies P4, and leaves an integration fix. Only the mutable field supports neither A nor a failure; confidence stays low and the claimant should be contacted. Recipient mismatch supports C.

**CL-611, `Assess Damage` versus `Assess Loss`.** Hypothesis A: one activity, two names, map fixed after the case. Hypothesis B: `Assess Damage` is a different step and `Assess Loss` was skipped on a EUR 62,000 HIGH-risk claim. Discriminator: the procedure version effective on the case date, the UI screen the user had open, and whether the mapping change record describes a rename or a merge of two procedures. UI labeled Assess Loss with an API emitting Assess Damage supports A. Two procedures in the signed standard support B, and the downstream fraud screen does not fill the gap.

**CL-603 and the 74-case retry cluster.** Hypothesis A: one successful delivery logged twice by an at-least-once connector. Hypothesis B: the claimant received two messages. Hypothesis C: two different notices incorrectly share a message id. Discriminator: connector retry counter, downstream accept count per message id, and the claimant-visible inbox. Concentration on one connector strongly favors A as the systemic cause. A downstream accept count of two separates B. Two different templates under one id would support C and would stop the dedupe rule from hiding a real second notice.

**CL-607, denied after coverage pre-screen.** Hypothesis A: correct early denial; the payable suffix is a model artifact. Hypothesis B: coverage failure was later overridden and a payable path was still required. Hypothesis C: `Deny Claim` is a screen label rather than a decision. Discriminator: the coverage-rule result code, the denial communication content, and any reserve or payment instruction. A result code that matches the pre-screen failure, a denial notice, and no payment instruction support A. This test decides the case. It does not decide P6 for the whole population; that remains an owner decision.

**CL-612, withdrawal after assessment.** Hypothesis A: genuine claimant withdrawal; the model lacks an end-state. Hypothesis B: withdrawal is real and the claimant never received an acknowledgement. Hypothesis C: withdrawal was used to avoid review. Discriminator: who initiated the withdrawal, on what channel, and whether an acknowledgement exists anywhere. For a single EUR 2,000 LOW-risk claim with no fraud indicator, C is speculative. A pattern of withdrawals immediately after HIGH risk or a bank change would reopen C; this case does not show that pattern.

**CL-601, telemetry.** Hypothesis A: clicks and autosave with no business effect. Hypothesis B: an autosave payload changed amount, beneficiary, or approval state and would then be a control event under P5’s exception. Discriminator: payload inspection for those fields. Names alone support A. A payload that mutates the beneficiary would move the event out of the noise class.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied costs use a uniform price of 1 for every visible log move and every visible model move, and 0 for a sync move or a silent move. That function measures how many alphabet edits make the trace fit the tree. It has no term for euros, fraud risk, bank-change recency, settlement finality, evidence quality, duplicate delivery, or whether the tree was meant to cover the case. Several mechanisms follow directly.

**The cost function prices a click and a missing anti-fraud gate the same.** CL-602 costs 1 because one model move inserts `Fraud Screen` on a paid EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim whose beneficiary bank changed the day before. CL-603 costs 1 because a connector retried one message id. CL-606 and CL-608 also cost 1. Those four business situations are an unrecorded fraud gate, a transport duplicate, an external assessment, and a weakly linked notice. Equal cost is what uniform weights are defined to produce.

**Scope manufactures the largest costs.** CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4, the maximum in the sample, because a payable tree inserts assessment, an approval branch, and payment after a denial or a withdrawal. The month’s alignments at raw cost at least 4 are mostly this class. The operational outcome on both sample cases is that no payment was made. Unit cost also inserts `Auto Approve` rather than fraud screen plus manual approval, because one model move is cheaper than two. On CL-607 that inserted branch is an accounting artifact. High cost here is evidence the population and the model disagree, which P6 already says is disputed.

**Hidden moves and zero cost cut in opposite directions.** CL-605 costs 0 with `T(tau,c=0)` before a permitted auto-approval of EUR 800. That tau is fitness: the XOR became a silent route in the Petri net. CL-609 and CL-610 also cost 0, with fully synchronous moves and no tau required to understand them. Their auto-approvals violate P1, and CL-610 also violates P2 because the bank changed one hour before payment. The tree can express “auto-approve or the manual branch.” It cannot express “auto-approve only if amount, risk, and bank-change allow it.” Perfect fitness is the expected output when the violated rules live entirely in data. Four such synchronous cases occurred in the month. A conformance program that ranks by raw cost will investigate CL-601 (cost 3, telemetry) and close CL-610 (cost 0, new beneficiary) as clean.

**Activity granularity double-counts one deed.** CL-611 costs 2 because `Assess Damage` fails string equality with `Assess Loss`, producing a log move and a model move. The procedure and the UI use one name; the map was fixed after the case. The fraud screen and manual approval on this HIGH-risk EUR 62,000 claim sync. The edit distance is responding to a label, not to a second missing assessment. Until replay, the historical cost stays in any extract that uses the old dictionary.

**Duplicated telemetry and transport events buy cost without a control event.** CL-601’s three UI events cost 3, enough to tie CL-604. CL-603’s extra `Notify Claimant` costs 1, and 74 cases share that retry shape. P5 says these are not business activities unless the payload shows a control issue. The cost function has no such filter, so noise accumulates linearly with event count.

**External evidence is invisible, so a model move is not absence.** CL-606 costs 1 for `M(Assess Loss)` while a licensed adjuster’s signed report exists at 10:05 and the fraud and approval events follow it. CL-608 costs 1 for `M(Notify Claimant)` while a portal message at +3 hours may satisfy P4, if and only if the link is durable. The aligner sees the core alphabet. It does not see PDF metadata or a CRM field, and it cannot tell an immutable key from a mutable one. Treating either model move as proof the activity did not happen overstates the log. Treating the external object as proof without a stable key overstates the file. Confidence has to carry that distinction; cost does not.

**Irreversibility and alignment choice are outside the number.** On CL-604 the bank accepted EUR 90,000 at 10:03 and manual approval was recorded at 10:20. The supplied alignment costs 3 and, as a side effect, inserts `M(Pay Claim)` for a payment that already happened. Under the same unit costs, a cheaper alignment can sync that early payment against an inserted `Auto Approve` and discard `Manual Approve` as a log move. That cheaper explanation would erase the manual path and the pay-before-approval fact. The supplied alignment is the right diagnostic reading, and it is not uniquely optimal. Neither alignment encodes that settlement is hard to reverse, which is why P3 separates this failure from its edit distance.

Taken together: raw cost is informative when the question is control-flow fitness against this tree, as on CL-605, and it is a poor risk metric everywhere the business defect is data, time, evidence channel, label, scope, or money. The severity column in section 2 and the narrative priority in section 5 are therefore kept separate from `raw_cost`.

def build_event_dataframe() -> pd.DataFrame:

        seen = {}

            seen[activity] = seen.get(activity, 0) + 1
            stated = STATED_CLOCK.get((case_id, activity))
            if stated and seen[activity] == 1:
                when = pd.Timestamp(stated)

            else:

                cursor = cursor + pd.Timedelta(minutes=5)

                    "time:timestamp": when,

        event_log, net, initial_marking, final_marking

                "pm4py_cost": diag.get("cost"),
                "pm4py_fitness": diag.get("fitness"),
                "pm4py_alignment": diag.get("alignment"),

        )

def explode_moves(supplied: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for record in supplied.itertuples(index=False):
        for step in record.alignment.split("|"):
            kind = step[0]  # S, L, M, or T
            label = step[step.find("(") + 1 : step.rfind(")")]
            rows.append(
                {
                    "case": record.case,
                    "move_kind": kind,
                    "activity": label,
                    "amount_eur": record.amount_eur,
                    "risk": record.risk,
                    "raw_cost": record.raw_cost,
                    "severity": record.severity,
                    "zero_cost_policy_failure": record.zero_cost_policy_failure,
                }
            )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def policy_flags(supplied: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Attribute checks the process tree cannot see. Not a severity formula."""
    out = supplied.copy()
    out["p1_auto_allowed"] = (
        (out["amount_eur"] <= 5000)
        & (out["risk"] == "LOW")
        & (~out["bank_change_7d"])
    )
    out["p2_manual_path_required"] = (
        (out["amount_eur"] > 50000)
        | (out["risk"] == "HIGH")
        | (out["bank_change_7d"])
    )
    out["used_auto_approve"] = out["alignment"].str.contains("S(Auto Approve)", regex=False)
    out["synced_fraud_screen"] = out["alignment"].str.contains("S(Fraud Screen)", regex=False)
    out["synced_manual_approve"] = out["alignment"].str.contains("S(Manual Approve)", regex=False)
    out["overlay_auto_forbidden"] = out["used_auto_approve"] & ~out["p1_auto_allowed"]
    out["overlay_p2_missing_synced_controls"] = out["p2_manual_path_required"] & ~(
        out["synced_fraud_screen"] & out["synced_manual_approve"]
    )
    return out


def risk_evidence_table(supplied: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Evidence laid beside the judgment. narrative_priority is an input.

    Sorting by raw_cost produces a different order. No column here replaces
    the written severity assessment with a score.
    """
    table = supplied[
        [
            "narrative_priority",
            "case",
            "amount_eur",
            "risk",
            "bank_change_7d",
            "raw_cost",
            "severity",
            "confidence",
            "technical_deviation_type",
            "policy_overlay",
            "zero_cost_policy_failure",
            "overlay_auto_forbidden",
            "overlay_p2_missing_synced_controls",
        ]
    ].sort_values(["narrative_priority", "amount_eur"], ascending=[True, False])
    table["decision_basis"] = "narrative judgment; raw_cost and flags are evidence only"
    return table.reset_index(drop=True)

    moves = explode_moves(SUPPLIED)
    enriched = policy_flags(SUPPLIED)

    comparison = enriched.merge(reproduced, on="case", how="left")
    comparison["cost_agrees_with_supplied"] = comparison["pm4py_cost"] == comparison["raw_cost"]
    # Disagreement is expected and does not override the supplied alignment.
    print("PM4Py cost versus supplied authoritative cost")
    print(
        comparison[
            ["case", "raw_cost", "pm4py_cost", "cost_agrees_with_supplied", "severity"]
        ].to_string(index=False)
    )
    print(
        "\nNote: another optimal alignment, or another PM4Py move rendering "
        "(tau names, skip symbols), may differ from the supplied notation. "
        "CL-604 is the clearest example: unit cost can prefer an auto-approve "
        "repair over the supplied manual-path alignment."
    )

    move_pattern = (
        moves.groupby(["move_kind", "activity"], as_index=False)
        .agg(
            occurrences=("case", "size"),
            cases=("case", lambda s: ",".join(sorted(set(s)))),
            sum_case_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
            max_severity=("severity", "max"),
        )
        .sort_values(["move_kind", "occurrences"], ascending=[True, False])
    )
    print("\nSupplied move patterns")
    print(move_pattern.to_string(index=False))

    by_type = (
        enriched.groupby("technical_deviation_type", as_index=False)
        .agg(
            cases=("case", "count"),
            sum_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
            sum_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
            max_severity=("severity", "max"),
            zero_cost_policy_failures=("zero_cost_policy_failure", "sum"),
        )
        .sort_values("max_severity", ascending=False)
    )
    print("\nAggregation by deviation type")
    print(by_type.to_string(index=False))

    evidence = risk_evidence_table(enriched)
    print("\nRisk-prioritization evidence table (narrative order)")
    print(evidence.to_string(index=False))

    by_raw_cost = enriched.sort_values(
        ["raw_cost", "amount_eur"], ascending=[False, False]
    )["case"].tolist()
    by_narrative = evidence["case"].tolist()
    print("\nOrder by raw_cost:", by_raw_cost)
    print("Order by narrative priority:", by_narrative)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
