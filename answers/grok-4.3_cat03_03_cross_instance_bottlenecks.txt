## 4. What flattening misses

Job flattening shows only direct job events, hiding batch synchronization waits, lot quarantine intervals, and tool contention; it duplicates shipment dispatch edges while omitting 5 h 55 min J1 post-pack delay, yielding the false conclusion that no job exceeds 1.8 .  

Machine flattening reports utilization and idle gaps but cannot attribute 42 min CNC-2 idle to FIX-A hold or 50 min TEST-1 idle to CAL-3 recalibration.  

Batch flattening reveals synchronization but hides that RACK-7 quarantine after B1 blocks B2 start.  

Tool flattening (absent in supplied tables) would expose acquire/release but is invisible in job/machine views.  

ComponentLot flattening shows quarantine/release yet is inconsistently linked to jobs, so J2 override and J5 wait remain hidden.  

Shipment flattening measures only Create–Load–Dispatch (0.3 h median) and conceals early-job waiting inside consolidations.  

Low machine utilization coexists with long waits because contention occurs on non-machine objects (tools, lots, shipments) whose constraints never appear in machine-centric event logs.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

Narrative 1 (J1–B1–OVEN-1–RACK-7–S1): J1 ready 08:10; B1 batches J1/J2/J3 at 12:05 on OVEN-1/RACK-7; J1 packed 13:30; S1 waits for J4 until 19:25. Temporal precedence holds; resource feasibility requires rack availability; policy constraint is batch-size rule; causal speculation is synchronization barrier.  

Narrative 2 (J3–FIX-A–CNC-2–J4–B2): J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; J4 starts 11:45; J4 enters B2 at 14:50. Precedence and tool exclusivity are observed; feasibility limited by single fixture; policy is implicit FIFO tool allocation.  

Narrative 3 (J2/J5–L7–Quarantine–B1/B2): L7 quarantined 10:20–13:50; J2 already in B1 via override; J5 waits until after release. Precedence clear; resource feasibility tied to lot integrity; policy allows override for some jobs only.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. Reduce batch size to 2 (target: synchronization). Benefit: cuts J1/J2 wait ~3 h. Helped: J1–J3. Harmed: throughput on OVEN-1. New risk: more setups. Metric: batch utilization (perverse: smaller batches). Evaluation: compare OCEL before/after on B1-type objects.  

2. Duplicate FIX-A and CAL-3 (target: tool contention). Benefit: removes 42 min/50 min idles. Helped: J3–J6. Harmed: capital cost. Risk: calibration drift. Metric: tool utilization (perverse: over-purchase). Evaluation: track acquire/release intervals per tool.  

3. Decouple shipments (target: consolidation). Benefit: J1 dispatch at 13:30. Helped: early jobs in S1. Harmed: S1 logistics cost. Risk: partial loads. Metric: shipment fill rate (perverse: delay early jobs). Evaluation: measure post-pack wait per job–shipment pair.  

4. Enforce lot-release logging to all related jobs (target: data artifact). Benefit: consistent visibility of L7 events. Helped: J2/J5. Harmed: none. Risk: logging overhead. Metric: event coverage (perverse: over-logging). Evaluation: recompute job DFG with/without lot links.  

5. Pre-stage RACK-7 cleaning (target: batch–tool interaction). Benefit: removes 14:35–14:50 gap for B2. Helped: J4/J5. Harmed: cleaning labor. Risk: premature cleaning. Metric: rack idle time (perverse: schedule cleaning during true idle). Evaluation: object-interaction summary on Batch–Tool edges.
