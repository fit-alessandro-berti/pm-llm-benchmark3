# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A naive analysis would filter the data to only `resolved` cases (S01, S02, S03, S04, S07, S13, S16, S17, S18, S19, S20, S27, S28) and calculate the mean duration or distribution. This introduces **Survivor Bias**:
*   **Selection:** Fast cases are over-represented. Slow cases (S03, S13, S20, S28) are included, but cases that never resolve (Withdraw/Reject) or are transferred (S08, S21, S32) are excluded.
*   **Bias Example:** If we only look at resolved cases, we might calculate a mean duration of ~45 hours. However, the presence of S09 (Open 92h, Prefix `Submit>Screen>Review`) indicates that cases can stall indefinitely. Excluding S09 underestimates the tail risk.
*   **Cohort Imbalance:** Cohort A (S01-S15) has 3 transfers (0%), Cohort B (S16-S32) has 3 transfers (10%). Cohort A has 4 reworks, Cohort B has 1. A naive comparison of means ignores the structural difference in process paths (e.g., `AutoCheck` in Cohort B).
*   **Open Cases:** S09 is open at 92h. If we treat this as "incomplete" and drop it, we ignore evidence that the process is failing to close.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Methods Comparison:**
1.  **Kaplan-Meier (KM):** Estimates $S(t) = P(T > t)$. Treats all non-resolved outcomes (Withdraw, Reject, Transfer, Open) as censored.
    *   *Pros:* Simple, handles right censoring.
    *   *Cons:* Treats competing risks (Withdraw/Reject) as censored, which biases the estimate of resolution probability upwards.
2.  **Aalen-Johansen / Cumulative Incidence:** Estimates probability of specific terminal events in the presence of competing risks.
    *   *Pros:* Correctly accounts for Withdraw/Reject/Transfer as competing risks.
    *   *Cons:* More complex; requires defining all terminal states.
3.  **Fine-Gray Subdistribution Hazards:** Models the cumulative incidence directly.
    *   *Pros:* Allows for covariate analysis of competing risks.
    *   *Cons:* Requires larger sample size for stability (n=32 is small).
4.  **Restricted Mean Time (RMT):** Average time to event up to a horizon (72h).
    *   *Pros:* Robust to censoring.
    *   *Cons:* Less intuitive for "breach probability".

**Recommendation:**
*   **Primary Approach:** **Aalen-Johansen** (or KM with competing risk labeling) to estimate the probability of `Resolve` by 72h while accounting for `Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer` as competing risks. This matches the operational goal of "successful resolution".
*   **Secondary Approach:** **Kaplan-Meier** restricted to `open` vs `resolved` to estimate the risk of "stalling" (Estimand A), but explicitly noting that `withdrawn`/`rejected` cases are also terminal.

**Justification:**
With n=32, complex multi-state models may overfit. However, distinguishing `Transfer` as a competing risk is crucial because it represents a structural exit from the internal SLA. KM will overestimate the probability of resolution (by censoring transfers). Fine-Gray is preferred if sample size allows, but Aalen-Johansen provides the full state transition probabilities.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Missing Data for Stronger Inference:**
1.  **Exact Event Timestamps:** `duration_or_age_h` is the total duration. We do not know the intermediate event times (e.g., when exactly `Review` happened). This prevents calculating time-varying covariates like "Time since last event" accurately.
2.  **External Outcomes:** For `Transfer` cases, we do not know the outcome in the external system. This makes `Transfer` a true competing risk only if we assume they never resolve internally, which is an assumption.
3.  **Cutoff Dates:** We do not know the absolute observation date. This prevents calculating calendar effects (e.g., holiday slowdowns).
4.  **Workload:** We do not know the volume of cases per hour. A high duration might be due to low workload rather than inefficiency.
5.  **Assignment Mechanism:** Cohort B started after a policy change, but assignment was not randomized. There is potential selection bias (e.g., more complex cases might have been sent to Cohort A).
6.  **Policy Compliance:** Rollout compliance was incomplete. Some Cohort B cases might have followed old paths.

**Recommendation:**
To improve inference, collect raw event logs with timestamps. Implement a randomized control trial for the screening policy if possible. Track external resolution times for transferred cases to validate the "Transfer" outcome assumption.
