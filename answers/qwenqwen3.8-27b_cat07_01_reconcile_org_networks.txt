# 5. Recommendation

**What should be changed immediately.**  
Management should make Mira_Coordinator’s coordination function less dependent on one person without removing her role. Immediate actions should include:

- Appoint a trained backup coordinator for the exception desk.
- Document the routing rules currently used for exception cases, including when a case goes to medical, legal, senior adjuster, junior adjuster, temporary staff, or vendor verification.
- Add audit fields to the log or case system: exception reason, routing rationale, case complexity indicator, SLA target, and selected specialist or adjuster.
- Create a daily exception-queue dashboard tracking Bot_Rules  Mira_Coordinator waiting time, exception P90 cycle time, specialist load, rework loops, and reopen/complaint volume.
- Keep ordinary low-value claims on the automated routine path. Do not add Mira_Coordinator to cases that do not require exception handling.
- Pilot limited delegation for low-risk exception types, such as simple missing-information reassignments, by region or channel, while Mira_Coordinator remains the escalation point.

**What should be monitored.**  
Management should monitor both performance and control indicators:

- Exception_flag=True cycle time, especially P90 and P95.
- Waiting time before and after Mira_Coordinator handovers.
- Handover and subcontracting centrality over time.
- Frequency of Mira  specialist  Mira and Mira  adjuster  Mira loops.
- Specialist and adjuster workload, especially exception share and service time.
- Reopen and complaint rates by resource, team, region, and channel.
- Variant frequency for exception paths.
- Data-quality metrics: resource attribution stability, exception_flag consistency, and event-label completeness.
- Vendor waiting time for Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay, to avoid attributing vendor latency to human coordination.

**What should not be changed until more evidence is available.**  
Management should not remove Mira_Coordinator or transfer all exception authority to team leads without a tested replacement. The absence evidence suggests that removing her would likely increase exception queue buildup, formal escalations, and specialist overload. It is also not advisable to assume compliance wrongdoing or intentional misrouting without direct evidence. The compliance concern should be addressed through audit fields, sampling, and routing rules, not by immediately changing accountability.

Management should not over-automate specialist selection until the routing logic is validated. If the current routing depends on tacit judgment, an automated rule may simply encode inconsistency at scale. It is also not advisable to change vendor payment or verification paths based on this evidence alone, because Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay are external dependencies with their own waiting-time risks.

If management later considers reducing Mira_Coordinator’s scope, it should be done through a shadowing or pilot: a backup routes cases in parallel, outcomes are compared, and exception cycle time, rework, complaint recurrence, and specialist load are measured before any permanent change.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator appears to be a short-touch exception broker rather than a routine processor. The data show she is central where work changes hands after automated triage: she receives exception cases from Bot_Rules, routes missing-information, reopen, and complaint work to adjusters and specialists, and often receives the work back. This explains high handover and subcontracting centrality. Her low working-together score is not contradictory: she rarely stays on a case for long collaborative work, so she does not form stable team pairs. The absence test is important: ordinary low-value claims were stable, but exception cases accumulated and their 90th-percentile cycle time rose sharply. This suggests a real key-person dependency in the exception path, not a universal bottleneck. The main risks are queue buildup, inconsistent routing, and limited auditability when one person selects specialists. Management should create a documented backup, add routing reason codes, and monitor exception queues and specialist load. Removal is not advisable unless a tested replacement preserves the coordination function.
