## 1. Interaction-graph description

The manufacturing system operates not as six independent job flows, but as a coupled hypergraph of typed objects. In a typed interaction graph $G = (V, E)$, the node set $V$ comprises seven object types: **Job** (`J1`–`J6`), **Component** (`C1`–`C6`), **ComponentLot** (`L7`, `L8`), **Machine** (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, `TEST-1`), **Tool** (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`), **Batch** (`B1`, `B2`), and **Shipment** (`S1`–`S3`).

Edges $E$ fall into two epistemological categories: **direct observations** (explicitly recorded in Object-to-Object [O2O] tables or joint Event-to-Object [E2O] relations) and **inferred edges** (reconstructed via transitive closure over O2O tables, overlap of acquire/release intervals, or external override logs).

*   **Direct O2O Structural Qualifiers:** `requires` (Job $\rightarrow$ Component), `contains` (ComponentLot $\rightarrow$ Component), `batches` (Batch $\rightarrow$ Job), and `consolidates` (Shipment $\rightarrow$ Job).
*   **Direct E2O Co-participation Qualifiers:** `co_cured_with` (linking Batch, Machine, Tool, Jobs, and Components at `Batch Cure`), `co_processed_with` (Job, Component, Machine, Tool at `Machine Process`), and `co_tested_with` (Job, Machine, Tool at `Final Test`).
*   **Inferred Coupling Edges:** 
    *   `lot_gates` (ComponentLot $\rightarrow$ Job): Inferred via transitive composition `L7 --contains--> C5` and `J5 --requires--> C5`, or dependent on ETL pipeline rules for `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot`.
    *   `blocks_via_tool` (Job/Batch $\rightarrow$ Job/Batch): Inferred when one object's `Acquire` to `Release` (or cleaning/recalibration) interval overlaps another object's readiness window on an idle machine (e.g., `J3` holding `FIX-A` while `J4` waits at idle `CNC-2`).
    *   `delays_dispatch_of` (Job $\rightarrow$ Job): Inferred when early-packed jobs wait for co-consolidated jobs to pack before `Dispatch`.

### Higher-order motifs (>2 object types)

1.  **The Multi-Resource Synchronization Hyperedge (5 types: Job, Component, Batch, Machine, Tool):** At 12:05, `Batch Cure` binds `B1`, `OVEN-1`, `RACK-7`, `{J1, J2, J3}`, and `{C1, C2, C3}`. A delay in any upstream prerequisite (e.g., `J3` finishing late) stalls all co-batched jobs, while the batch itself locks both `OVEN-1` and `RACK-7`.
2.  **The Auxiliary-Resource Starve Motif (3 types: Job, Machine, Tool):** A primary resource (`CNC-2` or `TEST-1`) sits idle while a queued `Job` (`J4` or `J6`) cannot execute because a secondary `Tool` (`FIX-A` or `CAL-3`) is held by another `Job` (`J3`) or undergoing state maintenance (recalibration after `J5`, or `RACK-7` cleaning delaying `B2` on idle `OVEN-1`).
3.  **The Bow-Tie Fork-Join Cascade (5 types: Tool, Machine, Job, Batch, Shipment):** Upstream contention on a `Tool` (`FIX-A` held by `J3`, delaying `J4` on `CNC-2`) propagates through a `Batch` (`J4` enters `B2` late due to `RACK-7` turnaround and `J5` lot-release wait), culminating in a downstream `Shipment` join (`S1`) where `J4` delays the dispatch of `J1`—even though `J1` was processed in a completely different batch (`B1`).

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All calculations below decompose observed elapsed intervals rather than proving strict causality; removing one constraint could expose a hidden secondary constraint (e.g., labor availability).

1.  **Synchronization-induced waiting for `J1` and `J2` in `B1`:**
    *   *Calculation:* `B1` starts `Batch Cure` at 12:05. `J1` is ready at 08:10 ($12\text{:05} - 08\text{:10} = \mathbf{3\text{ h } 55\text{ min}}$). `J2` is ready at 08:25 ($12\text{:05} - 08\text{:25} = \mathbf{3\text{ h } 40\text{ min}}$). By contrast, `J3` is ready at 11:55 ($12\text{:05} - 11\text{:55} = \mathbf{10\text{ min}}$). Assuming the 10-minute loading overhead after `J3` arrives is standard operational handling, the avoidable synchronization wait is $3\text{ h } 45\text{ min}$ for `J1` and $3\text{ h } 30\text{ min}$ for `J2` (totaling **7 h 35 min** of raw wait, or **7 h 15 min** net of loading).
    *   *Counterfactual assumption:* Assumes `OVEN-1` and `RACK-7` were free and operable at 08:10/08:25, batching policy permitted running `J1` and `J2` without `J3`, and recorded readiness timestamps accurately reflect physical staging at `OVEN-1` (ignoring `L7` quarantine on `J2` at 10:20).

2.  **Delay plausibly associated with `FIX-A` for `J4` at `CNC-2`:**
    *   *Calculation:* `J4` reaches `CNC-2` at 09:50, and `Machine Process` starts at 11:45 (**1 h 55 min** total wait). `CNC-2` is busy from 09:50 to 10:05 (15 min) and again from 10:47 to 11:40/11:45 (presumably processing another job not needing `FIX-A`), while `J3` holds `FIX-A` from 09:00 to 11:40. If `J4` had an independent fixture, its earliest feasible start on `CNC-2` was 10:05 when `CNC-2` went idle. Thus, $11\text{:45} - 10\text{:05} = \mathbf{1\text{ h } 40\text{ min}}$ is the total downstream delay plausibly associated with `FIX-A` unavailability (comprising **42 min** of pure `CNC-2` starvation from 10:05–10:47, plus **53 min** of secondary displacement from 10:47–11:40 if another job jumped the queue, plus **5 min** setup/handover from 11:40–11:45).
    *   *Counterfactual assumption:* Assumes `J4`'s processing duration on `CNC-2` would fit cleanly or preempt whatever used `CNC-2` at 10:47, and that `C4`, CNC programs, and an operator were ready at 10:05.

3.  **Delay imposed on `J1` by `S1` shipment consolidation:**
    *   *Calculation:* `J1` is packed at 13:30; `J4` is packed at 19:10; `S1` dispatches at 19:25. Total post-pack wait for `J1` is $19\text{:25} - 13\text{:30} = \mathbf{5\text{ h } 55\text{ min}}$. Comparing against post-last-pack dispatch times (`S1`: 15 min; `S2`: 25 min; `S3`: 35 min; median = 25 min), `J1` waited $19\text{:10} - 13\text{:30} = \mathbf{5\text{ h } 40\text{ min}}$ purely for `J4` to be packed (or **5 h 30 min** against the 25-min median dispatch overhead).
    *   *Counterfactual assumption:* Assumes a carrier truck/dock slot would have been available around 13:45–13:55 if `J1` were shipped solo, and that customer receiving rules allow split deliveries.

4.  **Delay plausibly associated with `CAL-3` for `J6` at `TEST-1`:**
    *   *Calculation:* `J6` is ready at 16:05 and starts `Final Test` at 17:25 (**1 h 20 min** total wait). From 16:10 to 16:35, `J5` occupies both `TEST-1` and `CAL-3` (ordinary queue wait of **30 min** from 16:05 to 16:35). From 16:35 to 17:25 (**50 min**), `TEST-1` sits completely idle while `CAL-3` recalibrates from 16:35 to 17:20 (**45 min**), followed by 5 min of setup (17:20–17:25). Thus, **45 to 50 min** of `J6`'s delay is directly associated with `CAL-3` recalibration blocking an otherwise idle `TEST-1`.
    *   *Counterfactual assumption:* Assumes a second pre-calibrated `CAL-3` unit (or longer calibration interval) could have immediately served `J6` on `TEST-1` at 16:35.

5.  **Decomposed `B2` start delay (`J4`, `RACK-7` quarantine, and `L7`/`J5` lot release):**
    *   *Calculation:* `OVEN-1` becomes idle at 12:55, and `J4` is ready for batching at 13:10, yet `B2` starts at 14:50 (**1 h 40 min** wait for `J4`, **1 h 55 min** idle window on `OVEN-1`). Two overlapping non-machine barriers explain this: (a) `RACK-7` is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, creating a hard tool floor of $14\text{:35} - 13\text{:10} = \mathbf{1\text{ h } 25\text{ min}}$ for `J4`; and (b) `J5` is held by `L7` quarantine (10:20–13:50), only becoming batch-ready at 14:40 (**50 min** post-release processing), adding an incremental $14\text{:40} - 14\text{:35} = \mathbf{5\text{ min}}$ synchronization wait beyond `RACK-7` availability, plus **10 min** batch loading (14:40–14:50).
    *   *Counterfactual assumption:* Assumes `B2` requires both `RACK-7` and both jobs (`J4`, `J5`); note that eliminating *only* `RACK-7` cleaning (ready at 13:10) saves 0 minutes for `B2` unless `J5` is also expedited or split from `B2`, demonstrating over-determined blocking!

## 4. What flattening misses

Flattening projects a multi-dimensional hypergraph onto a single object type, causing three well-known process mining pathologies: **deficiency** (dropping events not directly linked to the chosen object type), **convergence** (duplicating shared batch/shipment events across multiple case instances), and **divergence** (interleaving independent sub-object lifecycles within one case).

*   **Job Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* End-to-end individual job lead times, rework loops (`Inspect -> Rework` = 2), and median stage-to-stage durations.
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* **Duplicates (convergence)** `Batch Cure` (`B1` counted 3 times, `B2` twice) and `Dispatch` (`S1`–`S3` counted twice each), distorting resource frequency counts. **Hides (deficiency)** `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot` on `L7` due to strict direct-link ETL filtering, as well as `RACK-7` cleaning and `CAL-3` recalibration.
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* Median `Pack -> Dispatch` (1.1 h) and `Stage Component -> Machine Process` (1.1 h), alongside all jobs falling within $1.8\sigma$ of the mean, suggest a stable, decoupled process—completely masking `J1`'s 5.9 h dispatch wait and `J4`'s 1.9 h fixture wait because heavy delays hit different stages in different jobs, smoothing out total job variance!
*   **Machine Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* True duty cycles (44%–58% utilization), canonical machine state cycles (`Setup -> Process -> Release`), and maximum inter-event idle gaps (39–73 min).
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* Hides *job readiness* and *tool blocking*. Note also an implicit nuance: `OVEN-1` is idle for 115 min (12:55–14:50), yet its longest recorded inter-event gap is 73 min—implying `Load Batch` occurred mid-window (e.g., loading `J4` or staging around 14:08) or shift boundaries split the gap, further disguising true starvation!
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* "Machines have 42%–56% spare capacity and max gaps under 73 min, so jobs cannot be suffering multi-hour resource starvation."
*   **Batch Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* Oven cycle times and turnaround between `B1` and `B2`.
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* Hides pre-batch queuing of early jobs (`J1` waiting 3 h 55 m before `B1` starts) and **diverges** if individual job inspections/packs are projected onto the batch. Also omits `J6` if `J6` bypassed batch curing or ran unbatched.
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* "`B1` took only 10 minutes from last-job readiness (11:55) to cure start (12:05), proving batching adds negligible wait."
*   **Tool Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* Sequential lock durations (`FIX-A` 09:00–11:40), cleaning (`RACK-7` until 14:35), and recalibration (`CAL-3` 16:35–17:20).
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* Hides *unfulfilled demand* (cannot see `J4` waiting at `CNC-2` at 09:50 or `J6` waiting at `TEST-1` at 16:05 because there is no `Request Tool` / `Wait for Tool` event) and hides downstream consolidation ripple effects (`S1`).
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* "`CAL-3` recalibration took 45 min when no job was actively attached to it, representing harmless offline maintenance."
*   **ComponentLot Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* Quality hold duration on `L7` (10:20–13:50 = 3.5 h).
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* **Diverges** across downstream jobs (`C2` in `J2` vs. `C5` in `J5`), misses jobs outside `L7`/`L8` (`J4`, `J6`), and hides the out-of-band supervisor override that let `J2` enter `B1` at 12:05 while `L7` was still quarantined!
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* "Quarantining `L7` halted both `C2` and `C5` equally for 3.5 hours."
*   **Shipment Viewpoint:**
    *   *Makes visible:* Warehouse dock execution time from `Create Shipment -> Load -> Dispatch` (0.8 h + 0.3 h = 1.1 h).
    *   *Hides or duplicates:* Hides `Pack` timestamps of constituent jobs if `Create Shipment` is only triggered once *all* consolidated jobs (`J1` and `J4`) are packed.
    *   *Misleading conclusion:* "Outbound logistics adds only 1.1 hours to order fulfillment" (missing `J1`'s 5 h 40 m pre-shipment wait).

### Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting
In classical single-resource queueing ($M/M/1$), long waiting requires high utilization ($\rho \rightarrow 1$). In an object-centric manufacturing network, transitions are **conjunctive Petrinets (AND-joins)**: firing an activity requires simultaneous availability of a `Job`, a healthy `ComponentLot`, a `Machine`, a `Tool` in a valid state, and—for batching/shipping—all partner `Jobs`. When `FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, or `CAL-3` are unavailable, or when `B1`/`B2` await stragglers, **mutual blocking (starvation)** occurs: jobs wait in queues *while* primary machines (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, `TEST-1`) sit idle awaiting the missing conjunctive token. In fact, tighter synchronization barriers and longer tool turnarounds *simultaneously increase job waiting and depress machine utilization*.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative A: The `J3` $\rightarrow$ `FIX-A` $\rightarrow$ `J4` $\rightarrow$ `B2` $\rightarrow$ `S1` (`J1`) Cross-Lineage Cascade
*Spanning 6 object types: Tool (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`), Machine (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`), Job (`J3`, `J4`, `J1`), Component (`C4`), Batch (`B1`, `B2`), Shipment (`S1`).*
*   **Temporal precedence:** `J1` ready for batch (08:10) $\prec$ `J3` acquires `FIX-A` (09:00) $\prec$ `J4` reaches `CNC-2` (09:50) $\prec$ `CNC-2` idle (10:05–10:47) $\prec$ `J3` releases `FIX-A` (11:40) $\prec$ `J4` starts `CNC-2` with `FIX-A` & `C4` (11:45) $\prec$ `J3` ready for `B1` (11:55) $\prec$ `B1` cures `J1,J2,J3` on `OVEN-1`/`RACK-7` (12:05–12:55) $\prec$ `J4` batch-ready (13:10) $\prec$ `J1` packed (13:30) $\prec$ `RACK-7` cleaned (14:35) $\prec$ `B2` cures `J4,J5` (14:50) $\prec$ `J4` packed (19:10) $\prec$ `S1` dispatches `J1,J4` (19:25).
*   **Resource feasibility:** `J4` physically required both `CNC-2` and `FIX-A` (`Machine Process` E2O at 11:45); `B1` and `B2` both required `OVEN-1` and `RACK-7`. `J4` could not start on `CNC-2` at 10:05 while `J3` held `FIX-A`, nor could `B2` use `RACK-7` before 14:35.
*   **Policy constraints:** `B1` held `J1` until `J3` arrived (batch size policy); `S1` held packed `J1` from 13:30 to 19:25 until `J4` was packed (consolidation policy).
*   **Causal speculation:** *Did `J3` holding `FIX-A` delay `S1` by 1h40m?* **Not proven!** Even if `J4` had started `CNC-2` at 10:05 and become batch-ready at ~11:30 instead of 13:10, `J4` was assigned to `B2`, which was independently gated by `RACK-7` cleaning until 14:35 and `J5` lot release until 14:40! Unless an earlier `J4` arrival at 11:30 would have caused planners to dynamically swap `J4` into `B1` (ahead of `J3`), `B2` absorbed `J4`'s `FIX-A` delay as slack! Furthermore, the 4h20m gap between `B2` starting (14:50) and `J4` packing (19:10) likely includes `J4` hitting one of the two `Inspect -> Rework` loops or queuing behind `J6` at `TEST-1` after 17:50.

### Narrative B: The `L7` Bifurcation—Compliance Override (`J2`) vs. Starvation (`J5`)
*Spanning 5 object types: ComponentLot (`L7`), Component (`C2`, `C5`), Job (`J2`, `J5`), Batch (`B1`, `B2`), Tool (`RACK-7`).*
*   **Temporal precedence:** `J2` batch-ready (08:25) $\prec$ `L7` (`C2`, `C5`) quarantined (10:20) $\prec$ `B1` starts with `J2` & `C2` (12:05) $\prec$ `L7` released (13:50) $\prec$ `RACK-7` cleaned (14:35) $\prec$ `J5` batch-ready (14:40) $\prec$ `B2` starts with `J5` & `C5` (14:50).
*   **Resource feasibility:** `C2` and `C5` were physically present on the floor, as proven by `C2` entering `OVEN-1` at 12:05.
*   **Policy constraints:** QA quarantine rules forbade advancing `L7` components between 10:20 and 13:50. `J5` obeyed this policy constraint; `J2` bypassed it via a supervisor override logged in an external system—likely to avoid holding up `B1` (`J1` and `J3`) at 12:05!
*   **Causal speculation:** We speculate that `J2` (which used quarantined `C2` under override) may have subsequently failed `Inspect` and triggered one of the 2 recorded `Rework` events once QA findings materialized, whereas `J5`'s compliance delayed its readiness to 14:40, barely nudging `B2` past `RACK-7`'s 14:35 cleaning finish by 5–15 minutes.

### Narrative C: Sequential Cascade into Auxiliary Testing Bottleneck (`L7` $\rightarrow$ `B2` $\rightarrow$ `J5` $\rightarrow$ `CAL-3` $\rightarrow$ `J6` $\rightarrow$ `S3`)
*Spanning 5 object types: ComponentLot (`L7`), Batch (`B2`), Job (`J5`, `J6`), Machine (`TEST-1`), Tool (`CAL-3`), Shipment (`S3`).*
*   **Temporal precedence:** `L7` released (13:50) $\prec$ `B2` cures `J5` (14:50) $\prec$ `J5` test-ready (16:00) $\prec$ `J6` test-ready (16:05) $\prec$ `J5` tests on `TEST-1`+`CAL-3` (16:10–16:35) $\prec$ `CAL-3` recalibrates while `TEST-1` idles (16:35–17:20) $\prec$ `J6` tests (17:25–17:50) $\prec$ `S3` dispatches `J5,J6` (35 min after last pack).
*   **Resource feasibility:** `TEST-1` requires `CAL-3` for every `Final Test`. Physical/metrological drift or usage counters forced `CAL-3` offline for 45 min after `J5`.
*   **Policy constraints:** FIFO dispatching at `TEST-1` put `J5` (16:00) ahead of `J6` (16:05); mandatory recalibration prevented `J6` from testing at 16:35; `S3` consolidated `J5` and `J6`.
*   **Causal speculation:** Because `L7` quarantine and `B2` pushed `J5` to arrive at `TEST-1` at 16:00 (just 5 minutes before `J6`), `J5` consumed the last valid cycle on `CAL-3` before recalibration, pushing `J6` out by 75 minutes (30m queue + 45m recalibration) and delaying `S3` dispatch for *both* `J5` and `J6`! Had `J5` arrived 10 minutes later (16:10), `J6` would have tested first (16:05–16:30), recalibration would have hit `J5` instead, and `S3` completion time might have been nearly identical due to `S3`'s symmetric consolidation join.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Six observations that would weaken our top-ranked explanation (Tool Contention + Synchronization Cascades)

1.  **Operator single-tasking scarcity:** Shop-floor staffing logs reveal only one cross-trained operator runs `CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, and `TEST-1`, and that operator was taking mandatory breaks or unloading another line during 10:05–10:47, 12:55–14:50, and 16:35–17:20—making labor, not `FIX-A`/`RACK-7`/`CAL-3`, the true binding constraint.
2.  **Unconstrained spare fixtures on the shelf:** Tool-crib inventory records show two identical, idle `FIX-A` clones (`FIX-A2`, `FIX-A3`) were available at 09:50, proving `J4` waited at `CNC-2` for missing NC code or raw material `C4` rather than fixture contention with `J3`.
3.  **Virtual vs. physical readiness discrepancy in `B1`:** Warehouse scan logs show `C1` and `C2` were not physically delivered to staging until 11:45, meaning `J1` (08:10) and `J2` (08:25) "ready for batch" timestamps were merely ERP paper releases, shrinking `B1`'s 3h55m synchronization wait to <20 minutes.
4.  **Fixed carrier departure cutoffs:** Carrier contracts show the `S1` freight truck only docks once daily at 19:30 regardless of readiness; had `J4` been packed at 14:00, `S1` (`J1` and `J4`) would still have dispatched at 19:25, falsifying `J4`'s upstream delays as the cause of `J1`'s 19:25 dispatch.
5.  **Thermal pre-heating on `OVEN-1`:** Telemetry shows `OVEN-1` was ramping temperature between 12:55 and 14:45 (unlogged as an event) to switch cure profiles from `B1` to `B2`, meaning `RACK-7` cleaning (ending 14:35) was completely inside `OVEN-1`'s thermal shadow.
6.  **`J4`'s afternoon delay occurred in Rework, not upstream:** Detailed timestamps between 14:50 and 19:10 show `B2` unloaded at 15:40 (`J4` and `J5` together), `J5` passed inspection, while `J4` failed `Inspect` twice due to a `C4` casting defect—meaning upstream `FIX-A`/`RACK-7` delays contributed poorly or non-linearly to `S1`'s late pack.

### Six additional fields or event types to discriminate among hypotheses

1.  **Explicit `Resource Request` / `Enter Queue` events with `blocking_reason` qualifiers:** Logging `Request_Start(J4, CNC-2, FIX-A)` at 09:50 directly separates machine-busy queueing, tool starvation, and material staging without relying on interval subtraction.
2.  **`Operator_ID` as a first-class OCEL Object Type:** Linking technicians to `Setup`, `Machine Process`, `Clean`, `Calibrate`, and `Inspect` tests whether human labor concurrency masks itself as tool or machine idleness.
3.  **Full Tool & Machine State-Transition Events (`Maintenance_Start/End`, `Thermal_Ramp`, `Setup_Changeover`):** Capturing start/end timestamps for `RACK-7` cleaning, `CAL-3` recalibration, and `OVEN-1` thermal transitions clarifies whether maintenance is usage-triggered (cycle count) or time-triggered.
4.  **Explicit `Policy_Override` events & `Quarantine_Scope` attributes in the primary OCEL:** Recording the supervisor override on `J2`/`C2`/`L7` with timestamp, reason code, and link to `Inspect`/`Rework` outcomes tests whether compliance overrides cause downstream rework loops.
5.  **Physical Material Location / Staging Events (`Component_Arrived_At_Workstation`):** Separates logical ERP release readiness from physical floor readiness for `J1`–`J6` and `C1`–`C6`.
6.  **Order/Shipment Policy Metadata (`earliest_carrier_cutoff`, `allow_split_shipment`, `batch_min_size`, `due_date`):** Distinguishes hard physical constraints (e.g., `S1` waiting for a scheduled 19:30 truck, or minimum thermal mass in `OVEN-1`) from modifiable scheduling heuristics.
