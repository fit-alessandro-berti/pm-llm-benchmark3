# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

The criterion used here is **not** "highest count wins". An activity is *core* when it satisfies **three** of four conditions: (i)  60 % of the resource's own events in the role's activity set; (ii) the resource holds a substantial share ( 25 %) of the **global** volume of that activity; (iii) the work is done at the resource's *natural* complexity level (not on handover/rework cases); (iv) the resource holds the **authority** (certification, signoff right, delegation) for it. Peripheral activities fail on at least one of these — typically because they are *surge, substitution, rework or review* work.

**R1 Automated Intake & PreCheck (R14_Nora).**
*Core:* A1 (46 % of all registrations) and A2 (27 % of all validations) — 100 % of its events. *Peripheral:* none; the bot has **no** A3–A14 activity, so it cannot escalate, negotiate, implement or approve.
*Frequency:* 13 % of all events from one "resource" would make it the dominant player in any frequency chart. *Complexity:* 0.11 median, 0 critical — the bot's "work" is definitionally the low end; the 881 human A2 events are the exception stream it cannot handle. *Substitution:* none; it absorbs the *precheck* part of the surge in weeks 8–10 but generated no additional judgement. *Authority:* zero — its A1/A2 output is a *statement of fact*, not a validated entitlement decision, which is exactly why fragment 6 reopens cluster at "incomplete Validate Entitlement notes".

**R2 FrontOffice Resolution Agents (R01_Amira, R02_Ben).**
*Core:* A2 / A4 / A12 / A1 Register — 86 % (Amira) and 91 % (Ben) of their events and 20–25 % of the global volume of each. They own routine endtoend closure (fragment 1). *Peripheral:* A3, A5, A10 (technical spillover), A7/A8 (commercial spillover), A11 (occasional selfreview), A13/A14 (small rework and knowledge contributions).
*Frequency:* the core is genuinely routine/highvolume — this is a legitimate volume role. *Complexity:* the peripheral activities are where the two diverge: Amira takes 58 high + 9 critical cases vs Ben's 31 + 3 and runs a 4–5× larger technical/commercial tail at median complexity 0.34 vs 0.29. That is a **routing/tenure** difference (4.2 y experienced generalist vs 3.7 y routine specialist), not evidence that Ben "cannot" do more. *Substitution:* they are the *recipients* of substitutiondriven rework — fragment 6 shows R01/R02/R13 redoing work after temp closures. *Authority:* no approval (0 A9), no security (0 A6). Their peripheral A7/A8 events are *customer contact* on routine matters, not pricingexception decisions.

**R3 Contingent FrontOffice Support (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).**
*Core:* the same four labels as R2 (A1/A2/A4/A12) — 98–99 % of their events and 27 % of all A4 / 30 % of all A12. *Peripheral:* 8–12 A3 and 3–4 A8 (statistical noise at 0.3–0.4 % of their events).
*Frequency:* high volume (871 / 821 events) makes them look like fullyfledged agents. *Complexity:* 0.18/0.19, **zero critical cases** and only 2–4 highcomplexity cases each — the sharpest complexity ceiling in the log. *Substitution:* they *are* the substitution mechanism for the Front Office, but they also *generate* substitution for others: 17 reopens needed R01/R02/R13 rework. *Authority:* none (no certifications, temporary contract, 0.3–0.4 y tenure). Conclusion: core/peripheral is identical to R2, so **activity analysis alone cannot separate R2 and R3** — only complexity ceiling, contract and rework signature can.

**R4 Technical Diagnosis & Configuration (R03_Chen, R04_Dina).**
*Core:* A3 + A5 + A10 = 81 % (Chen) / 84 % (Dina) of their events, and 63–78 % of the global volume of those three labels. *Peripheral:* A6 Security Review (Chen 24, Dina 6), A13 Reopen, A14 KB, A11 QC, A8 negotiation on technical aspects.
*Frequency:* a clean specialization — the clearest routine specialization in the process. *Complexity:* the pair splits: Chen at 0.71 with 42 critical cases and 24 securityadjacent reviews on the **late** shift; Dina at 0.48 with 9 critical. Peripheral A6 is therefore *coreadjacent* for Chen (a secondline security capability) and merely occasional for Dina. *Substitution:* they are the buffer behind L1 overflow (Amira/Ben's A3 tails hand over to them in fragment 2) and the technical endpoint in the reopen path (fragment 5). *Authority:* no approval right (0 A9) and no independent security signoff — their A6 events are reviews within their technical remit, not Riskfunction authorisations.

**R5 Security Assurance (R05_Erik).**
*Core:* A6 alone — 56 % of his own events and **66 %** of the global A6 volume. *Peripheral:* A10 Implement (18), A3 (16), A5 (10), A14 (9), A11 (8), A13 (6), A8 (4) — i.e. the *followthrough* of his own reviews (implementing and documenting what he reviewed).
*Frequency:* 170 events in 10 weeks is the secondsmallest profile in the log and would be called "low activity". *Complexity:* 0.86 median and 33 % critical cases — the most concentrated risk exposure per event in the process. *Substitution:* weeks 7–8 are the warning: when Mateo filtered referrals, Erik's A6 fell below expectation, falseescalation complaints *dropped*, but **two critical cases waited > 2 days**. Substituting around him works until it does not. *Authority:* he is the only resource whose *presence* is a control (fragment 3: `…  R05_Erik:Security Review  R13_Mateo:Coordinate  …`), so his peripheral activities are all downstream of an authorisation he owns.

**R6 Commercial Exception & Negotiation (R06_Fatima).**
*Core:* A7 (52 % of global) + A8 (27 % of global) = 73 % of her events. *Peripheral:* A2 (22), A12 (18), A9 (16), A3 (8), A10 (6), A4 (5), A13 (4).
*Frequency:* only 291 events, so she looks "light" — but 52 % concentration of the pricing gate is a monopoly, not a low load. *Complexity:* 0.67, with 105 high + 25 critical — commercial decisions cluster on the hard cases by construction. *Substitution:* her 6day absence is the cleanest natural experiment in the data: same activity mix performed by Giorgio, cycle time 38  74 min. Similarity of *activity profile* (0.71) coexisted with a 95 % performance gap — proof that profile similarity  role equivalence. *Authority:* the 16 peripheral A9 events need interpretation (see §4): they may be thresholdlevel delegated approvals or mere logging of Hana's decision; only 7 % of global A9 sits with her and she does not own the approval activity.

**R7 Supervision & Approval (R08_Hana).**
*Core:* A9 (63 % of global) plus the reopen triage that gates rework (A13, 17 % of global). *Peripheral:* A8 (22), A2/A11 (12 each), A3 (10), A7/A12 (8), A10 (6), A4 (5) — i.e. the supervisor "touches" the work to understand it before deciding (sample checks, escalations calls).
*Frequency:* 245 events, spread over 10 labels — frequency analysis reads this as a thin generalist. *Complexity:* 0.79 with 62 critical cases (23 %) — the decision load is concentrated exactly where the risk is. *Substitution:* week 5 is decisive. Mateo's 21 preliminary A9 events are 15 % of a quarter's approvals, and **13 were rechecked (62 %)**. Delegation therefore transfers *workload* but not *authority*, and the cost is a second pass. *Authority:* the only unambiguous decision right in the process; her peripheral A13 (24) shows she also arbitrates rework ownership.

**R8 Quality Gatekeeping & Knowledge Stewardship (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).**
*Core:* A11 (45 %/44 % of global) + A14 (37 %/40 % of global) = 75 %/77 % of their events. *Peripheral:* A10 (38/35), A12 (32/30), A5 (26/22), A3 (20/16), A13 (12/8), A6 (4/0), A4/A1.
*Frequency:* their A10 Implement counts (73 combined = 12.6 % of global A10) tempt a frequency model to call them implementation staff. *Complexity:* 0.52/0.48 — they review *after* the hard work is done, so their own touch is midcomplexity even though the cases they check can be critical. *Substitution:* none observed; instead they are the **verification layer** that would catch substitution failures (and did not catch the 17 temp reopens until reopening occurred). *Authority:* they hold a *veto* (quality gate) and a *memory* function (KB) — the 200 combined A14 events are the process's institutional memory, invisible to any authoritybyvolume reading. Different shifts (late vs day) mean their nearidentical profiles are **coverage, not redundancy**.

**R9 Escalation Coordination (R13_Mateo).**
*Core:* coordination itself — an activity not in the alphabet. Its measurable shadow is the **reopen/rework intake** (A13: 42 = 30 % of global, the largest single share) and the hub position between technical, commercial, security and supervision. *Peripheral:* literally all 14 labels, each at 10–52 events (max label = 12.5 % of his own events).
*Frequency:* the flattest profile in the log  "generalist". *Complexity:* 0.77 median with **76 critical cases** — he touches 429 cases and the hardest ones; he is not a junior float. *Substitution:* he is the universal substitute (A9 for Hana, A6 filtering for Erik, rework for the temps) — but every substitution he performs is *constrained* (62 % recheck rate on approvals; a filtering decision in weeks 7–8 that saved noise but delayed 2 critical cases). *Authority:* explicitly **preliminary/interim** — delegated and revocable. This is the case where "core activity" and "formal authority" come apart most sharply.

**R10 Hybrid / Commercial Shadow (R07_Giorgio).**
*Core (dual):* L1 core A2/A4/A12/A1 (68 % of events) **plus** A7 (31 % of global) + A8 (26 % of global) = 20 % of his events; 88 % of his work sits in these two blocks. *Peripheral:* A9 (20, 2.4 %), A3 (40), A5/A10 (~22), A13 (10), A11/A14 (8).
*Frequency:* he is the 6th largest producer in the log with 820 events, which makes his hybrid profile highimpact, not marginal. *Complexity:* the flattest distribution of any resource (36 %/38 %/21 %/6 %) — the signature of a resource assigned *by availability across bands* rather than by specialization. *Substitution:* his weeks 3–4 cover for Fatima is real, sanctioned and **slower**; permanent equivalence is therefore not established. *Authority:* certifications include pricing, but the measured substitution data and his 20 A9 events leave the *threshold* of his decision right undefined (§3, §4).

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow roles are **not** improper. In this process they are loadbearing: they supply surge capacity, redundancy, and rare coverage that the formal structure does not staff. The problem is not their existence but their **invisibility and unstated limits**, which create unbounded risk when the person is absent or when the informal rule is mistaken for a formal one.

### 3.1 R07_Giorgio — Commercial shadow reviewer / hybrid bridge
**What he is:** a permanent Senior Service Agent whose formal home is Front Office but who performs 72 A7 + 88 A8 commercial events ( 31 % / 26 % of global volume) and 20 A9 events, including a sanctioned 6day cover for Fatima.
**Benefits:** eliminates a single point of failure in the pricing gate; keeps enterprise/complex frontoffice cases moving without a handover (fewer queue hops than fragment 4's full chain); his 0.84 similarity to Amira means he can also backstop L1. His existence is why the weeks 3–4 absence produced *delay*, not *stoppage*.
**Risks:** (a) **Authority ambiguity** — is his pricing review an authorised exception review or an informal precheck? If the latter, a commercial control is being executed by an uncertifiedequivalent reviewer. (b) **Quality/performance gap** — 38  74 min median, i.e. 49 % throughput on the pricing gate while "covered". (c) **Accountability blur** — his 20 A9 events could be read as approvals; if they are only recorded/preliminary, a control has been logged as satisfied. (d) **Load distortion** — 26 % of negotiation volume being produced from the front office makes "commercial demand" look smaller than it is, so the Commercial team is chronically underresourced on paper.
**Proper control:** codify him as a **named, thresholded delegate** (e.g. "pricing exceptions  X on frontofficeowned cases may be reviewed by R07; above X, R06 or delegated signoff required"), log delegation explicitly, and monitor his exception duration as a leading indicator.

### 3.2 R13_Mateo — Crossfunctional escalation broker (bridging)
**What he is:** the routing/handover layer between L1, technical, commercial, security and supervision: he owns 30 % of reopen intake, appears as the pivot in 5 of 6 sequence fragments, is the only resource touching all 14 activity types, and holds 76 critical cases at median complexity 0.77.
**Benefits:** he is the organisation's *transfer function*. He prevents pingpong handovers, keeps reopen handling in one place, and — crucially — performs triage at the boundary (weeks 7–8 he filtered lowrisk security referrals). That filter **reduced false escalation complaints** and protected Erik's scarce capacity.
**Risks:** (a) **Undocumented discretion** — triage rules live in one person's head; the same decision that saved noise delayed 2 critical cases > 2 days. (b) **Delegated authority drift** — 21 preliminary approvals, 13 rechecked (62 %): the delegation saved Hana 21 units of work but cost 13 units of doublehandling and creates a period in which a nonapprover's decision is in the record. (c) **Single point of failure for routing** — if he is absent, the *edges* of the network degrade, not just one node's throughput. (d) **Clustering harm** — any mined structure labels him a generalist and would justify "merging coordination into the supervisors", destroying the bridge.
**Proper control:** externalise his triage rules as explicit routing criteria (risk tier, product line, complexity threshold), keep approvals *preliminaryflagged* in the log with a mandatory recheck lane, and treat his absence as a resilience event.

### 3.3 R05_Erik — Rarebutcritical specialist (shadow *exposure*, not shadow authority)
**What he is:** 66 % of the security gate with only 1.6 % of the events and the highest perevent risk exposure in the log (0.86 median complexity, 33 % critical cases). He is "shadow" in a specific sense: **mining makes him look marginal** while the control depends on him.
**Benefits:** a genuine compliance gate on the critical path (fragment 3); rare, highvalue judgement that justifies not staffing it broadly; he also implements and documents his own findings (A10/A14 peripheral work), which keeps security knowledge in the case record.
**Risks:** (a) **Capacity/risk mismatch** — 96 A6 in 10 weeks is ~10 security reviews/week; the "Enterprise Security" surge in weeks 7–8 exceeded that, and the organisation compensated by filtering referrals *around the control*. (b) **Keyperson risk** — Chen (24 A6) and Dina (6 A6) are the only adjacent capabilities and neither holds the Risk remit. (c) **Falsenegative asymmetry** — false escalations cost noise; missed criticals cost days (observed) and possibly incidents (unobserved). (d) **Expendability in a frequencydriven cut** — a "utilisation" table ranks him last but one.
**Proper control:** cap referrals by *rules* (documented risk tier) rather than by a person's judgement of load; certify a second security reviewer (Chen is the evidenced candidate — the technical senior already doing securityadjacent reviews at 0.71 complexity); alert when A6 demand exceeds a weekly threshold instead of absorbing it silently.

### 3.4 R14_Nora — Automation boundary role
**Benefits:** 1 370 events of 24/7 intake and precheck removed from human queues; standardisation of registration; frees R01/R02/temps for exceptions and closure.
**Risks:** the boundary is where the 17 reopens happened — incomplete Validate Entitlement notes produced by the automated precheck and accepted by temps. A bot is not a "role" with competence, escalation sense or continuity, but in a mined org chart it is the largest single "worker" in A1/A2. **Control:** model it as a *channel* with a handover rule ("if precheck completeness flag = false, route to R01/R02/R07") and monitor note completeness as a dataquality KPI.

### 3.5 R11_Kai / R12_Lina — Contingent boundary (shadow *capacity*)
**Benefits:** 1 692 events of surge absorption in weeks 8–10; 30 % of all A12 closures; the only reason the seasonal peak did not push L1 waiting time up.
**Risks:** contingent capacity is cheap to add and expensive to audit — 17 reopens traced to their validation notes = rework redistributed to R01/R02/R13. Because their activity profiles are 0.98similar to permanent agents', the mined structure will call them equivalent and the organisation will treat temporary substitution as permanent competence. **Control:** restrict their activity *set* (no A3/A8), require a completeness check before Close, and measure their contribution net of rework cost.

### 3.6 R09_Ivan / R10_Julia — Quality gatekeeping as a shadow veto
**Benefits:** 608 A11 checks and 200 A14 KB updates — the veto that makes "quality" enforceable and the memory that makes the process learn (fragments 2 and 3 terminate in their checks).
**Risks:** a gate with no countersignature can become a de facto second approval authority (their peripheral A10/A12 work means they can also *finish* work they reviewed); and, as the 17 temp reopens show, gatekeeping that is samplebased does not catch systematic note defects. Their shift split also means a single quality standard is applied by two people with no observed reconciliation mechanism. **Control:** rotate calibration sessions between the day and late QA pair, and require the gate to trigger *systemic* KB updates (their A14 work) whenever a defect cluster appears.

"""
Organizational mining for an order-to-resolution log.
Goal: derive a ROLE structure that distinguishes
  - routine specialization (stable activity sets)
  - cross-functional / bridging work (handover hubs)
  - temporary substitution (episode-bounded competence)
  - rare-but-critical interventions (low volume, high risk)
and explicitly AVOIDS turning activity-frequency clusters into claims about
formal authority or job titles.
"""

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 0. setup
import pandas as pd, numpy as np, networkx as nx
from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import linkage, fcluster
from scipy.spatial.distance import squareform
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = ("case:concept:name", "concept:name",
                      "time:timestamp", "org:resource")
CPLX, CSUB, EESC = "case_complexity", "temporary_substitution", "escalation_level"

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. load + format
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution.csv", parse_dates=[TS])   # or read_parquet
# the log is already PM4Py-shaped; format_dataframe makes it an EventLog and
# normalises the four key columns.  NOTE: the parameter that controls timestamp
# parsing is (historically) called `timest_format`; passing None means
# "do not re-parse, they are already datetimes".
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    case_id_key=CASE,
    timestamp_key=TS,
    resource_key=RES,
    timest_format=None,
    keep_original_columns=True,          # keep case_complexity, temporary_substitution, ...
)
log["week"] = log[TS].dt.isocalendar().week.astype(int)
log["hour"] = log[TS].dt.hour

# service / waiting times
