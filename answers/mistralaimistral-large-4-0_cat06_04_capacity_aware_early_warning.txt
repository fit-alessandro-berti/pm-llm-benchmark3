## 1. Decision rule

I do **not** use a single breach-probability threshold. The rule has four gates and a score.

**Eligibility gates**
- **Stage gate:** only cases before `Diagnosis Complete` (Triage or Diagnosis Started). I-03 fails this gate.
- **Time gate:** at least 1.5 hours to SLA remain, because the swarm itself takes 45 minutes and downstream action must be possible.
- **Data gate:** if a critical feature is missing or imputed (I-06), the case is not auto-intervened; it is held for manual validation.
- **Alert-history gate:** no repeated swarm on the same case without a material new event. A material new event is a new customer log, new error signature, severity escalation, SLA clock change, or a `p_adj` change of at least 0.08. A model refresh alone is not material.

**Calibration adjustment**
- SME: `p_adj = min(1, p_now + 0.065)` [range +0.04 to +0.09].
- Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = max(0, p_now  0.085)` [range 0.05 to 0.12].
- Public Sector: `p_adj = p_now`.
- Non-Native-Language Support: `p_adj = p_now`, but flagged as high uncertainty; require human review before a swarm.

`p_now` is observational; it is not automatically the probability under intervention. The score therefore uses the supplied effect range for expected benefit, and `p_adj` only for urgency, alert persistence, and tie-breaking.

**Prioritization score**
- Expected direct net benefit = midpoint effect × value  €450 swarm cost  false-alarm burden (charged in full as a conservative penalty).
- Spillover cost per slot = 6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases × €20,000 average value × p (0.01–0.03) = €1,200–€3,600; use €2,400 midpoint for scoring.
- Priority score = expected direct net benefit  €2,400.
- Robustness check: lower-bound net = low effect × value  €450  false-alarm burden. Cases with negative lower-bound net are deprioritized or require confirmation.
- Tie-breakers: positive lower-bound net, shorter time to SLA, higher `p_adj`, segment calibration confidence.

**Capacity rule**
- Allocate slots to the highest priority score until slots are filled. Keep one slot in reserve if management preference and a possible regulatory incident before 15:00; release the reserve only to the first alternate if the regulatory incident does not materialize.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down**
- After a swarm on a case, no second swarm on that case for at least 4 hours or until a material new event occurs, whichever is later. If the SLA deadline is within 2 hours, do not repeat the swarm; escalate to a human duty manager.

**Suppression**
- A case with repeated alerts is suppressed if there is no material new event and `p_adj` has changed by less than 0.08 since the last alert. Suppression lasts 60 minutes or until a material new event. A model refresh alone is not a material new event.

**Material new event**
- New customer log, new error signature, severity escalation, new stakeholder, SLA clock change, or `p_adj` change  0.08.

**Escalation**
- If a case remains eligible and `p_adj`  0.75 with time to SLA < 2h, escalate to human review even if it does not get a swarm slot.
- If a case has been suppressed twice and still has `p_adj`  0.70, escalate to a human triage lead.

**Re-entry**
- A suppressed case re-enters the scoring queue when a material new event occurs or `p_adj` increases by  0.08. Re-entry does not guarantee a slot; the case is re-scored.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases**
- Risk: I-08 (€100k) and I-04 (€60k) get slots; I-07 (€5k) and I-05 (€7k) may breach. This can harm SMEs and public-sector customers and create regulatory exposure.
- Safeguards: apply a non-monetary value uplift (e.g., 1.25× for Public Sector and SME) in scoring; reserve one slot per day for cases with `p_adj`  0.70 and value < €10k if they have a positive lower-bound net benefit; monitor breach rates by value band and adjust if low-value cases breach at >1.5× the rate of high-value cases.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support**
- Risk: I-09 has sparse calibration and effect estimates; it may be over- or under-prioritized.
- Safeguards: treat Non-Native-Language Support as high-uncertainty; require human review before a swarm; cap slots to this segment until more data; oversample this segment in evaluation.

**Differential calibration across segments**
- Risk: SME underprediction (+0.04–0.09) means SME cases are under-alerted; Strategic Enterprise overprediction (0.05–0.12) means they may be over-alerted. This can create systematic bias in who gets slots.
- Safeguards: apply segment-specific `p_adj` adjustments as in Section 1; monitor calibration weekly by segment; if expected calibration error > 0.05, adjust `p_adj` or suspend automated alerts for that segment.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels**
- Risk: if we intervene on high-risk cases, they may avoid breach, so the model learns that similar cases breach less often (label bias). Untreated cases may breach more due to spillover, further biasing labels.
- Safeguards: use randomized slot allocation where possible; otherwise use propensity-score weighting or uplift modeling; include treatment status as a feature; hold out a random subset of eligible cases from intervention to maintain unbiased labels; monitor label drift.

**Spillover harms systematically fall on untreated groups**
- Risk: each slot delays other diagnosis-stage cases; if untreated cases are disproportionately low-value, SME, or non-native-language, the policy may exacerbate inequities.
- Safeguards: track spillover harm by segment and value band; require that a slot’s expected direct benefit exceeds a multiple of expected spillover harm (e.g., 1.5×); if spillover harm is concentrated in a group, reduce slots or add capacity; publish a fairness dashboard.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
- Daily dashboard: slots used/reserved/released, swarm duration, time-to-intervention, delay to other cases (minutes), SLA breaches, false alarms, customer complaints.
- Track queue congestion: number of diagnosis-stage cases, average time to SLA, specialist utilization.

**Predictive monitoring**
- Weekly calibration by segment: reliability curves, Brier score, expected calibration error (ECE). Compare `p_adj` vs observed breach rate.
- Alert persistence: number of repeated alerts per case, suppression rate, re-entry rate, `p_adj` changes.
- Drift detection: monitor feature distributions (e.g., queue length, time to SLA) and outcome drift.

**Causal-effect monitoring**
- Estimate intervention effect using randomized slot allocation where possible: when slots are scarce, randomly assign a small fraction of eligible cases to swarm vs control (if ethically/operationally acceptable). Otherwise use quasi-experimental methods: propensity-score matching, difference-in-differences, or slot availability as an instrument.
- Track effect ranges by segment, stage, and case characteristics. Update effect priors monthly.
- Monitor spillover: measure breach probability changes in untreated diagnosis-stage cases when slots are used; estimate congestion elasticity.

**Capacity monitoring**
- Track slot demand (eligible cases with positive spillover-adjusted net) vs supply. If demand exceeds capacity for >2 consecutive days, consider adding capacity or raising the priority threshold.
- Track reserve slot usage; if the reserve is used frequently, review the reserve policy.

**Fairness monitoring**
- Track breach rates, intervention rates, and spillover harm by segment (SME, Strategic, Public, Non-Native-Language), value band, language, region.
- Monitor calibration error by group.
- If any group has systematically higher breach rate or lower intervention rate, investigate and adjust.

**Recalibration plan**
- Monthly: update segment calibration adjustments using recent data.
- Quarterly: re-estimate intervention effect ranges using causal estimates; update priority score weights.
- Trigger-based: if calibration error > 0.05 for any segment, or if spillover harm exceeds direct benefit for two consecutive weeks, or if slot demand exceeds capacity by >20%, then review and adjust threshold/allocation rule.
- Change control: any change to threshold or allocation rule must be approved by a human review board and tested in shadow mode for two weeks before deployment.
