## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Established facts (not assumed):** GPIP-4 is board-approved, effective 2026-01-01, global scope. SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev.6 is controlled, effective 2025-03-01, Aachen maintenance/production-support scope. ECD-2 is jointly signed COO + Incident Commander, effective 09:00 07-14 to 18:00 07-18 CEST, stated scope “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.” CFO email 07:55 07-15 is ordinary email, not in policy repository. CRO was unavailable and did not ratify ECD-2. ERP was unavailable 08:30-13:00 07-14; timestamps during recovery can be up to 12 min late; clock may be fast. Incident Command charter does not explicitly designate members as budget owners; CFO owns Finance control framework; COO can issue operational directives but GPIP-4 reserves formal exception to CRO.

**Separating interpretation from fact:** Where text is silent or undefined (A7 whether COO+IC = CRO exception; B7 what is “ordinary”; C4 scope of “ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding”; C6 definition of “critical”; D1 whether CFO can waive GPIP duties) we treat as interpretation requiring explicit premise, not as fact.

**Decision method:**

1. **Authority does not equal auto-preemption.** Board policy (GPIP-4) is presumptively highest for integrity controls (segregation of duties, independent financial approval, compliance clearance). A later, more specific, or more local source does not automatically defeat it. A5 explicitly forbids silent weakening of GPIP-4 by local procedures; same logic extends to incident directives.

2. **Specificity controls narrow issues.** Within its stated window and scope, a specific emergency rule can override a general workflow rule for that issue only. ECD-2 is strongest for *timing (PO before commitment)* and *supplier register* when it speaks expressly (C1, C2, C4). It is weakest where it is silent (A1, A2 budget-owner, A3 compliance, CRO authority).

3. **Later-in-time is defeasible.** ECD-2 (July 14) is later than SOP (March 2025) and GPIP (Jan 2026) but lateness alone does not create power to repeal higher-level controls. Validity depends on an enabling clause (A7) and scope/time limits.

4. **Emergency necessity is narrowly construed.** B4 defines emergency as imminent safety harm or stoppage within 6h. C6 “critical” is contested; we apply both narrow (needed within 24h for affected line) and broad (Operations “critical contingency” backup line) tests and identify which is outcome-determinative. Necessity waives only what the rule says it waives (A4: competitive tendering only, unless A7 written exception).

5. **Retrospective evidence:** A late database timestamp is not conclusive if stronger contemporaneous evidence exists (telephone recording, signed PDF creation time, supplier acknowledgment email, access log). A late *record of a timely act* (C3 recording within 4h, B6 form within 24h) can prove compliance. A late *approval after commitment* does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval per A6.

6. **Burden of proof:** The party asserting an exception, expanded scope, delegated authority, or clock error must prove it by contemporaneous evidence. Absence of written extension, joint authorization, or delegation = failure to meet burden. Indeterminate is not used to avoid a hard call; it is reserved where decisive evidence is missing even after exhausting available proof sources.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-502 - Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (instead of NONCOMPLIANT)**
*Premise change:* Interpret ECD C4 “supplier-onboarding requirements” to implicitly include Compliance clearance for new suppliers, and treat ECD as de facto A7 exception because COO (executive committee) co-signed.
*Why credible:* Prior organizational practice treated signed incident directives as temporary overlays; Operations views new-supplier vetting as onboarding; single-source filter emergency arguably warrants relief.
*Why weak under strongest defensible view:* GPIP-4 A5/A7 explicitly require CRO for GPIP weakening; CFO email D3 denies waiver of Compliance; history explicitly notes “but not in a case involving new-supplier Compliance clearance” - negative precedent; integrity purpose of supplier vetting is distinct from administrative onboarding.

**P-505 - Alternative: NONCOMPLIANT (instead of JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**
*Premise change:* (a) Treat ERP commit timestamp 18:06 as conclusive act time  commitment after ECD expiry 18:00, so no ECD relief. (b) Reject CFO D1 as valid waiver of GPIP A2 budget-owner limb because A2 requires two approvals not one and IC members are not budget owners per charter.
*Why credible:* Strict textualist adherence to system record; CFO lacks authority to waive board-level dual control; D4 caveat warns against removing essential controls; budget-owner independence is core to anti-fraud.
*Why our primary is defensible:* ON-44 instructs contemporaneous evidence outweighs ERP lag; supplier ack 17:59:31 is stronger; commitment *started* 17:57 before expiry - rule applies at act time; even if after, amount 88k with pre-Compliance and dual IC suggests low residual risk; we disclose budget-owner ambiguity and keep confidence medium.

**P-506 - Alternative: INDETERMINATE (instead of NONCOMPLIANT)**
*Premise change:* Accept buyer verbal claim that Incident Command verbally extended ECD overnight as creating ambiguity about expiry, and argue cleaning chemicals are still “critical sterilization-line lots” during recovery phase.
*Why credible:* Contamination recovery is continuous; Operations may have oral instruction; lack of written record could be logging defect not policy breach post-outage.
*Why weak:* Burden of proof requires written, scoped, time-limited extension per A7 framework and C3 recording within 4h; no evidence of joint authorization or document; verbal extension after formal expiry is prohibited emergency normalization; even if emergency, B4 6h test not met with stock chemicals.

**P-507 - Alternative: NONCOMPLIANT (instead of JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**
*Premise change:* Reject CFO interpretive authority to defer GPIP A2 Finance pre-approval; require literal Finance Controller timestamp before 09:00 commitment irrespective of ECD or amount cap.
*Why credible:* GPIP-4 text is absolute; CFO interpretive authority is not waive authority; A6 bars later cure; amount 110k is large; IC  Finance Controller.
*Why our primary is justifiable but uncertain:* D3 explicitly grants CFO authority over Finance controls; amount under 150k with Compliance done and 8h critical need fits purpose of ECD; recording within 4h via PDF shows good-faith compliance; we flag missing budget-owner designation as residual gap needing closure.

**P-508 - Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (instead of NONCOMPLIANT)**
*Premise change:* Treat sole-source pump “only available … capable of restoring line” as meeting necessity for implied CRO exception and view Legal clearance + dual IC auth + Compliance as functional equivalent of Finance for amounts just over cap; de minimis 5k overrun.
*Why credible:* Proportionality and operational necessity; Legal at 16:00 shows senior review; 155k vs 150k cap is formalistic.
*Why weak:* Thresholds are essential controls precisely to prevent normalization creep; No document lifts cap; Finance control is not substitutable by Legal; GPIP reserves exceptions to CRO, who was unavailable and did not ratify; accepting would effectively amend GPIP by email.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinctions applied:**
- **Policy breach:** Act violated substantive control at time of act (e.g., missing pre-Compliance for new supplier, SOD self-approval) - cannot be fixed by late paperwork alone.
- **Logging defect:** Timely act occurred but system shows late (e.g., P-507 PDF 12:40 vs ERP 14:30; P-505 ERP 18:06 vs ack 17:59) - cured by producing contemporaneous artifact.
- **Late evidence of timely act:** Authorization recorded within 4h via PDF/voice proves R-C3 satisfied even if ERP lagging.
- **Inability to prove compliance:** No contemporaneous proof of required pre-approval, delegation, scope, or extension  treated as noncompliant due to burden of proof, even if actor claims oral approval.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Draft Replacement Clause for GPIP-4 Section 7 & ECD Framework:**

> **7. Hierarchy, Emergency Modifications and Evidence**
>
> **7.1 Authority Ranking (issue-specific).** For any conflict, the following presumptive precedence applies to that issue: (a) *Integrity controls:* Board-approved GPIP-4 (segregation of duties, dual financial approval 50k including one independent budget owner + one Finance Controller, and Compliance clearance >100k or any new/unapproved supplier *before* commitment) prevails over all local SOPs, incident directives, or email guidance. (b) *Operational workflow:* A controlled Aachen SOP may impose stricter thresholds (e.g., 25k Plant+Finance) but may not weaken (a). (c) *Emergency operational relief:* A signed incident directive may, *only within its written scope and time window*, waive timing (PO before commitment) and approved-supplier register use, and define substitute actors for Plant approval when the Plant Manager is demonstrably absent and delegation is actively recorded. It *may not* waive (a) unless it satisfies 7.2. (d) Informal guidance (e.g., email) may interpret (b) and defer timing of Finance approval up to EUR 150,000 when coupled with dual Incident Command authorization, but cannot waive (a), increase thresholds, or extend scope/time.
>
> **7.2 Formal Exception.** Any waiver of (a) requires a *written, scoped, time-limited exception* issued by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) before commitment, stating issue, amount, suppliers, and expiry. Joint signature by COO + Incident Commander is *operative* for (c) only; it is not a CRO exception for (a). If CRO is unreachable, a COO+CRO-deputy verbal authorization may be relied upon for max 12h but must be ratified in writing by CRO within 24h or all actions become retroactively noncompliant.
>
> **7.3 Scope and Time.** “Critical” must be defined in each directive (e.g., “affected sterilization line lots where stoppage is expected within 24h and no approved stock exists”). Backup/contingency stock beyond 24h is *not* critical unless explicitly listed. Every directive auto-expires at stated time; verbal extension is invalid. Thresholds (50k, 100k) are absolute at commitment; no rounding or netting.
>
> **7.4 Retrospective Records vs Retrospective Approvals.** A contemporaneous voice recording, supplier acknowledgment, or signed PDF timestamp proving a timely authorization before commitment, recorded in ERP within 4h (or within 24h for emergency form), *proves* compliance despite ERP lag during outage. A pre-commitment approval obtained *after* commitment is not proof and does not cure the breach per 7.1(a), unless 7.2 applied at act time.
>
> **7.5 Safeguard Against Normalization.** No more than two consecutive ECD extensions without Board Risk Committee notice. Post-incident, Internal Audit must review within 10 days all (i) new-supplier, (ii) >100k, and (iii) self-approved transactions during the window. Repeated reliance on emergency relief for the same component class triggers mandatory sourcing remediation.
