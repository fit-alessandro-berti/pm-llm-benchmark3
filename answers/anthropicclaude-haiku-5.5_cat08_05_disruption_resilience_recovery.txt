**Key temporal reading:**
- Hospital cycle times returned to baseline by T4. Pharmacy and wholesale did not. Aggregate "recovery" hides segment-level lag.
- Compliance exceptions recovered more slowly than throughput. In T4 they were still roughly 2 to 2.5× baseline.
- Case counts in T3 and T4 are inflated by backlog drain, so volume-based recovery claims overstate normal capacity.
- Roughly 3,500 of the T4 cases (about 10%) touched a remediation path (some overlap is possible). That workload sits after operational close and is absent from the baseline.

**Data limitations:** Several DFG nodes do not balance. For example, Cancel order exits (430) exceed its recorded entries (320), and several manual-phase nodes have unlogged outflows. The DFGs are therefore indicative, not complete. Causal attribution of outcomes to specific adaptations is inferential.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

### The distinguishing test

An adaptation is **resilience** when all of the following hold:
1. **Declared:** the deviation is visible to management and tagged in the record (for example, a workaround code).
2. **Compensated:** a control of equivalent assurance replaces the bypassed one (for example, pharmacist review replaces system dual control).
3. **Risk-proportionate:** the scope matches the product and customer risk.
4. **Bounded:** it has an expiry, owner, and exit criterion.
5. **Reconcilable:** the evidence can be completed contemporaneously or with a short, auditable backfill.

Concealed noncompliance fails one or more of these tests, especially where a control is removed and the substitute is undocumented or not equivalent.

A useful conformance signal: **move-on-log deviations** (steps that appear in the log but are not in the model, such as Skip system authorization check or Dispatch without scan) are at least visible and therefore detectable. **Move-on-model deviations** (required steps that never appear, such as missing Quality/release check, missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature record) are where concealed noncompliance hides, because nothing in the record shows the step was skipped.

### True resilience (preserve the adaptation, with guardrails)
- **F. Temporary release control.** A real compensating control. Its gap (temperature documentation) is a scope issue, not a design flaw.
- **B. Manual authorization check.** Preserved authorization integrity at a capacity cost.
- **K. Reconcile manual reservation.** Restoring record integrity is itself resilience, provided it is time-bounded.
- **I. Defer noncritical order.** Explicit prioritization that protected life-critical supply, albeit at a fairness cost that must be managed.

### Risky shortcuts (concealed noncompliance or near-concealed)
- **C. Skip system authorization check.** Assumed standing authorization with no evidence. Removes a control without a compensating one.
- **G. Dispatch without scan.** Bypassed scan and, in most cases, formal release. The largest traceability gap in the event.
- **Move-on-model gaps.** Roughly 650 dispatches without observed POD, and 1,800 packed orders with no visible quality/release check, are invisible unless reconciled. They are the most dangerous part of the record because they look like completed flows.
- **L as a closure mechanism.** Using remediation to declare compliance restored overstates the position.

### Ambiguous (depends on risk appetite)
- **A. Hospital fast lane.** Life-critical priority is defensible. Authorization bypass is not, unless pre-verified and retrospectively checked. Acceptability depends on whether leadership accepts a short window of unverified hospital shipments.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot.** Early use is reasonable; later use becomes risky as staleness grows and recalls can be missed. The threshold for "too stale" is a risk-appetite decision.
- **H. Phone POD.** Acceptable for ambient with backfill; not acceptable for CS or cold-chain without stronger evidence.
- **J. Backlog prioritization.** Resilient if rules were explicit and communicated; unfair if informal. The log alone cannot distinguish these. This requires governance evidence.
- **E. Manual pick list.** Physical flow is protected, but traceability cost is high. Acceptable with contemporaneous lot capture.

## 4. Recovery process model

### Notation
This model uses BPMN-style structured text. `` indicates sequence; `[X]` indicates a gate that must pass before continuing; `XOR` indicates a mutually exclusive branch; `AND` indicates parallel activities; `{WA-xx}` is a workaround code that must be recorded on every manual event.

### 4.1 Trigger conditions (explicit, declared, time-bound)

Declare a **disruption tier** using measurable triggers, not judgment alone. Each declaration has an incident commander, a named authority to waive specific controls, and an automatic expiry (for example, 24h, renewable only by a documented decision).

| Tier | Trigger (any one) | Permitted adaptations | Expiry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0: Normal** | All systems operating; scan success 98%; snapshot age not applicable | Standard process | n/a |
| **Tier 1: Degraded** | Scan success <98% for >1h; inventory sync lag >2h; intake partially available | Live inventory with manual scan fallback; sample-based release for ambient only | 24h, renewable |
| **Tier 2: Manual** | WMS unavailable >4h; authorization service unavailable; inventory integrity unknown | Offline authorization list; inventory snapshot 12h old; manual pick with WA codes; triage rules apply | 24h, renewable with review |
| **Tier 3: Severe** | WMS unavailable >24h; any recall posted during disruption; suspected data integrity compromise | Hospital and CS-only prioritized dispatch; deferral of wholesale and pharmacy with customer notice | Reviewed every 12h |

**Automatic escalations:** any recall alert, any CS discrepancy, or any cold-chain excursion moves the affected lot or order to the strictest applicable control set regardless of tier.

### 4.2 Triage rules

```
Receive order
 [Classify product risk]
   XOR
    Recall-sensitive or CS  Strict lane (no fast lane, live or lot-verified inventory)
    Cold-chain  Cold lane (temperature capture mandatory)
    Ambient  Standard lane
 [Classify customer criticality]
   XOR
    Hospital-critical  Priority within the product lane
    Pharmacy-standard  Standard within lane; max wait declared
    Wholesale-bulk  Deferrable with notice and revised ETA
 [Authorization verification]
   XOR
    Pre-verified standing list (verified 24h)  Proceed
    First-time customer or any CS item  Manual authorization check (B), mandatory
    Unverifiable  Hold; no skip path
 Allocate to lane queue with WA code and timestamp
```

**Hard rules:** no skip of authorization; no fast-lane entry for CS or recall-sensitive items without verification; deferrals require customer notice.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product and customer risk

| Product \ Customer | Hospital-critical | Pharmacy-standard | Wholesale-bulk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient** | Pre-verified authorization (24h); inventory snapshot 12h; dual-ID pick; dispatch with paper manifest plus lot photo; POD electronic within 24h (phone POD fallback with backfill) | Offline authorization list (24h); snapshot 12h; paper pick with lot capture; manifest plus lot photo; POD backfill | May be deferred; if released, same controls as pharmacy; full scan reconstruction after recovery |
| **Cold-chain** | As ambient, plus temperature record at pack and dispatch; QA release (not sample); scan or dual-signed manifest; temperature at receipt | As hospital ambient, plus QA release and temperature record | Deferrable; if released, full cold-chain controls apply |
| **Controlled-substance** | **Verified licensure and authorization; no fast lane skip; 100% pharmacist dual review; dual-control at pick and pack; signed receipt (no phone-only POD)** | Same as hospital, with full dual control | Same as pharmacy; no exceptions |
| **Recall-sensitive** | **Live or lot-verified inventory only; no snapshot; hard stop on any recall-flagged lot; QA release; scan or lot-verified manifest** | Same as hospital | Same as pharmacy; no exceptions |

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture

- **Pre-printed, numbered forms and labels** issued at Tier 2 declaration, with unique case IDs and sequence numbers, produced before the outage where possible.
- **Contemporaneous capture:** each event recorded at the time it occurs, with time, person, and WA code. Late entries are flagged as late and never backdated.
- **Dual-witness rule** for CS and recall lots: two identities on pick, pack, and release.
- **Photo capture** of lot labels at pick and of loaded vehicles at dispatch.
- **Signed manifest** for every dispatch without scan, with courier signature and handoff time.
- **Sealed, versioned offline lists** for authorization and inventory snapshots, each with its generation timestamp.

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization

Score each backlog case on: clinical urgency (hospital > pharmacy > wholesale), product risk (recall and CS > cold > ambient), and age (SLA clock). Rules:
- Publish the scoring rules before recovery begins.
- **Age caps by segment:** no pharmacy or wholesale case waits beyond its declared maximum without a customer contact and revised ETA.
- **Fairness check:** daily review of fill rate and cancellation rate by segment. Pharmacy-to-hospital fill ratio should stay at or above an agreed floor (suggested 0.90 of baseline ratio).
- Cancellations require customer notification before the order closes.

### 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

```
Tier declared end
 AND
    Reconcile inventory and reservations (SLA 48h)  Close K items
    Reconcile workaround events (SLA 72h)  Classify each case:
         CLEAN: evidence complete, contemporaneous
         REMEDIATED: evidence recovered with late-entry flag, root cause recorded
         OPEN DEBT: evidence absent  risk assessment (recall check,
              regulatory notification assessment, customer contact)
    Backfill electronic POD and temperature records (SLA 24h from delivery)
 [Compliance debt gate]
   Operational recovery is declared only when reconciliation is complete.
   OPEN DEBT is reported in a separate register with age and owner.
   Debt closure requires compliance or QA sign-off, not operational sign-off.
 Post-incident review: tag every WA code outcome; update standing lists.
```

**Principle:** operational recovery and compliance closure are separate milestones with separate owners and separate metrics. Remediation after invoice close is recorded as debt, not as restored compliance.

## 5. Resilience indicators

### Leading indicators (detect degradation early)

| Dimension | Indicator | Suggested threshold / use |
|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Share of cases on Tier 2+ lanes; queue time at manual authorization check | Alert if manual-check queue exceeds 2× normal intake SLA |
| Service protection | Hospital fill rate; hospital-critical share of capacity reserved | Hospital fill  95% of baseline during any tier |
| Traceability | Share of events tagged with a WA code; scan success rate | Scan success <98% triggers Tier 1 |
| Control completeness | Share of CS and cold-chain orders with full dual control and temperature record at release | Target  99.5% at release (before recovery) |
| Control substitution | Rate of dispatch without scan by product type; authorization skip count | Target zero skips for CS, cold-chain, recall-sensitive; alert on any non-zero |
| Inventory integrity | Snapshot age at pick; recall-flag coverage | Snapshot >12h triggers mandatory live check |
| Backlog age | Median and p90 age of backlog by segment; share exceeding segment age cap | Any pharmacy or wholesale case past age cap triggers customer contact |
| Fairness | Pharmacy-to-hospital fill ratio; cancellation rate by segment | Ratio 0.90 of baseline ratio; flag if pharmacy cancellation exceeds 2× baseline |

### Lagging indicators (confirm outcomes)

| Dimension | Indicator | Baseline reference | Disruption reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Median and p90 cycle time by segment, measured after backlog is removed | Hospital 8/15h; pharmacy 23/49h; wholesale 36/72h | T4 pharmacy 28/74h; wholesale 58/120h |
| Traceability | Traceability exception rate | 0.8% | 9.5% (T2); 2.2% (T4) |
| Control completeness | Cold-chain documentation missing; CS dual-control missing | 0.4%; 0.2% | 4.8%/2.9% (T2); 0.9%/0.5% (T4) |
| Rework | Rework and correction rate | 3.5% | 12.5% (T2); 5.8% (T4) |
| Cancellation | Cancellation rate by segment | 2.1% | 7.8% (T2); 3.0% (T4) |
| Compliance debt | Open debt count, age, and closure time; share of cases remediated post-close | 0 | ~2,300 remediated (6.6%); ~200 open at end |
| Conformance | Fitness and percentage of fitting traces; move-on-model rate | 0.96; 91% | 0.71/44% (T2); 0.90/76% (T4) |
| Fairness | Segment-level fill, cycle time, and cancellation ratios at incident close | Ratio  1.0 | Pharmacy 61% fill vs. 91% hospital in T2 |

**Interpretation rule:** the incident is not declared recovered when cycle time returns. It is declared recovered when cycle time, control completeness, traceability, fairness, and compliance debt all return to threshold.

## 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Implementation effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Retire the skip-authorization path (C).** Replace with a versioned, pre-verified standing-authorization list for hospital and pharmacy accounts, refreshed weekly and held offline. | Low to medium: list build and governance | Closes the clearest concealed gap; restores assurance on ~29% of intake that bypassed authorization | Stale lists if refresh fails; mitigated by expiry |
| **2** | **Gate dispatch for CS, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products.** No dispatch without scan or dual-signed manifest. Retire G as a default. | Low: procedure and manifest templates | Eliminates the largest traceability gap for the highest-risk products | Some throughput loss during outage; acceptable for risk class |
| **3** | **Adopt tiered triggers with declared authority and automatic expiry.** Define Tier 0 to 3 with measurable thresholds and named incident commander. | Low: governance and documentation | Makes adaptations deliberate and time-bound; prevents silent normalization of workarounds | Triggers may be mis-set; review after each event |
| **4** | **Create an offline "break-glass" pack.** Pre-printed numbered forms and labels, sealed inventory snapshot refreshed every 12h, standing authorization list, and manifest templates. | Medium: process and periodic refresh | Restores contemporaneous evidence; reduces reconstruction (E, H, K) | Snapshot still lags live stock; mitigated by delta log |
| **5** | **Separate compliance debt from operational recovery.** Register, age, and assign owners; close reconciliation within 48 to 72h; require QA or compliance sign-off for closure. | Medium: register and workflow | Prevents hidden post-close workload from being mistaken for recovery (L, K) | Debt may still persist for complex cases; reported transparently |
| **6** | **Formalize backlog prioritization with age caps and fairness monitoring.** Publish criteria; notify customers; daily segment review. | Medium | Converts J from potentially unfair to explicitly resilient; mitigates pharmacy starvation (I) | Trade-offs remain; hospitals are protected by design, so pharmacy will still wait |
| **7** | **Tighten proof-of-delivery fallback.** Signed receipt for CS; temperature-at-receipt for cold-chain; electronic backfill within 24h. | Medium: courier and customer process | Reduces weak POD (H) and the variable delay observed | Customer compliance with signing may vary |
| **8** | **Deploy offline temperature data loggers for cold-chain.** Battery-based loggers with offline read-out at receipt. | High: capex and training | Closes cold-chain documentation gap (4.8% in T2) | Equipment failure and read-out lag; requires maintenance |
| **9** | **Build instrumentation for workaround codes.** Require WA codes in the WMS-independent record so manual events are visible in logs. | Medium: tooling | Reduces move-on-model blind spots; enables leading indicators | Adoption risk under pressure; requires drills |
| **10** | **Stage compliance capacity.** Cross-train pharmacists and compliance staff; maintain a reserve roster for disruptions. | High: staffing and training | Relieves the authorization and release bottleneck (B) | Cost of standby capacity |
| **11** | **Run disruption drills twice yearly** using the DFG-based scenarios, including recall during outage and a stale-snapshot scenario. | Low to medium | Validates triggers, forms, and fairness rules before the next event | Drill fatigue; mitigated by scenario variety |

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** a short, declared window in which hospital-critical orders take priority, using pre-verified standing authorization and offline lists with retrospective checks within a fixed period. It should also accept deferral of wholesale-bulk orders, a longer pharmacy-standard cycle within published limits, and paper-based picking for ambient products, as long as each adaptation is tagged, time-limited, and reported.

Management should **not** accept: skipping authorization for any customer without a verified record; dispatching controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive products without a scan or signed manifest; using a stale snapshot for recall-sensitive stock; or treating audit remediation as restored compliance. Remediation after invoice close must be reported as compliance debt, and operational recovery should not be declared until that debt is aged, owned, and either closed or explicitly risk-accepted.

Evidence that would change this recommendation includes: offline authorization list accuracy testing showing error rates above about 1% (which would make list-based dispatch unacceptable even for hospitals); hospital outcome data showing that scan or authorization gating caused measurable clinical delay (which would justify a narrower gate for hospital ambient items); compliance debt that remains open beyond 30 days; a pharmacy-to-hospital fill ratio that stays below 0.90 of baseline after backlog is cleared (which would require a revised deferral policy); or regulatory guidance that changes the minimum controls for emergency dispensing or distribution. Absent such evidence, the recommended posture is to keep the resilient adaptations (F, B, K, and a formalized I and J), modify A, D, E, H, and L, and retire C and G as defaults.
