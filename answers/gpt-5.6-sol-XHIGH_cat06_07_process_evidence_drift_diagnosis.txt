## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January:** Stable reference month. Labels are fully mature, observed breach rate is 0.18 versus mean prediction 0.19, AUROC is 0.81, and ECE is 0.025. Variant mix and part-delivery performance are consistent with the baseline process.

- **February:** Still stable. Small changes are within the normal January–March range: AUROC 0.82, ECE 0.022, and part delivery 31 h.

- **March:** Stable end of the clean baseline period. The 35 h part-delivery median and variant-specific breach rates remain close to January–February. January–March are the best reference cohorts for detecting later changes.

- **April:** Primarily a **schema and feature-pipeline incident**, not evidence of a sudden business concept change. The renamed diagnosis activity caused predictions to be anchored at the wrong event or not constructed. The 17% unknown-activity share, 14% queue-feature missingness, and 18% unmapped variants are mutually consistent. Duplicate treatment also changed mid-month. Labels are 100% mature, so censoring does not explain the 0.67 AUROC, 0.184 Brier score, or severe overprediction of 0.28 versus a 0.19 breach rate. Part delivery remained 34 h, and no major outcome-definition or operational change is documented. April must be replayed before deciding whether any residual real drift occurred.

- **May:** Partial recovery after the mapping patch. Unknown activities fall to 2%, queue-feature missingness to 3%, AUROC recovers to 0.78, and ECE to 0.050. This pattern strongly supports the April pipeline diagnosis. May is not necessarily perfect: the patch arrived on 6 May, and lineage and duplicate handling for early-May cases still require verification. The unrecomputed cached feature primarily corrupts historical April records.

- **June:** A genuine **routing/process-mix change**, but not strong evidence of concept drift. V2 rises from 34% to 52%, while V1 and V3 decline. The remote-fix edge remains stable and V2 breach rate falls to 0.09. The approximate variant-weighted breach rate is  
  \(0.30(0.16)+0.52(0.09)+0.18(0.35)\approx0.158\), close to the observed 0.15. Thus the lower base rate is largely explained by routing more cases to a lower-risk path. AUROC remains reasonably strong at 0.79. Mean prediction remains 0.20, suggesting prior/calibration lag under the new mixture rather than a broken ranking relationship.

- **July:** The supplier issue begins on 10 July, creating a transition month. The part-delivery edge rises from 42 to 65 h, and V3 breach rate rises to 0.46. AUROC declines to 0.76. With 98% maturity, label delay is a relatively small but nonzero concern. This is the first month with credible evidence that the V3 outcome relationship is changing, although only part of the cohort was exposed to the new supplier regime.

- **August:** The supplier issue becomes severe. The part-delivery edge doubles from July to 130 h; V3 share rises to 35%; and the reported V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. V1 and V2 remain around 0.18 and 0.11. Using old-regime variant rates with the August mix would imply a breach rate near 0.19, close to the model’s mean prediction of 0.19. Using August’s variant rates gives roughly 0.31. This isolates an important within-V3 deterioration beyond simple variant-mix drift. The observed overall rate is only 0.28 partly because labels are 93% mature and the component tables may use different selected subsets. The low AUROC and high ECE therefore reflect overlapping performance-process drift and likely V3 concept drift, with some censoring.

- **September:** Three effects overlap:
  1. the supplier slowdown continues, with the part edge at 148 h and V3 breach rate around 0.61;
  2. V3 grows to 42%;
  3. Partner Z contributes 24% of cases and has a substantially different population and routing pattern.

  Applying old-regime variant rates to September’s mix gives approximately 0.20, close to the mean prediction of 0.21; applying September’s reported variant rates gives about 0.34. This again suggests that mix alone is insufficient and that the V3 relationship changed. However, only 82% of labels are mature, and Partner Z matures more slowly. The currently observed 0.45 versus 0.27 source gap is therefore evidence requiring stratified analysis, not a final causal or fairness conclusion.

- **October:** The operational parts problem appears to persist: the observed completed `Order Part -> Part Received` edges have a 155 h median, V3 remains at 41%, and Partner Z reaches 27%. But predictive evaluation is heavily confounded by the outcome backlog: only 55% of labels are mature, with a 52-day median delay, and long paths are disproportionately missing. The apparent reversal to a 0.16 observed breach rate versus 0.23 predicted risk must not be interpreted as recovery or simple overprediction. October AUROC, Brier, and ECE are provisional selected-label metrics. Even the 155 h edge can understate the eventual duration because the longest uncompleted edges have not yet entered the performance DFG.

Thus April, June, August–September, and October are different incidents: April is primarily feature construction failure; June is a real but mostly benign routing-mix change; August–September combine supplier-driven process and conditional-outcome deterioration with a September population shift; October remains operationally concerning but is not yet a valid supervised-performance cohort.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment**

1. Quarantine April’s reported predictive metrics and any April training rows until the feature replay is complete. The correct response is feature repair, not calibration or retraining.
2. Keep October performance dashboards explicitly provisional. Do not disable or retrain the model solely because of October’s current AUROC or observed breach rate.
3. Independently escalate the V3 supplier problem. A temporary conservative V3 risk adjustment or operational review queue may be appropriate if route/part dependency is known at score time and capacity impact is assessed.
4. Preserve immutable score-time feature snapshots, raw events, outcome vintages, and model/pipeline versions so replay does not overwrite the original evidence.

**Short-term recalibration**

- June is a reasonable recalibration candidate because labels are mature and the main change is the V2 routing mixture. A post-policy intercept or route-aware calibration layer may correct the 0.20 predicted versus 0.15 observed base rate.
- Do not fit one global calibrator across June–September. June is an overprediction/mix regime, whereas August–September exhibit substantial V3 underprediction and reduced discrimination.
- A temporary V3-specific calibration layer could be used after August–September labels mature, but it will not restore lost ranking performance. It should have an expiry condition tied to supplier recovery.

**Feature and pipeline repair**

- Establish a canonical activity dictionary with schema-contract tests for renamed events.
- Recompute all April prefixes and derived features, including cached event counts and queue values.
- Apply one duplicate policy across both sources and run sensitivity checks to avoid deleting legitimate repeated work.
- Add features available immediately after diagnosis that represent planned part dependency, supplier/part category, current part backlog, queue pressure, rural travel burden, and device age. Do not use realized `Part Received` duration or eventual process variant as training features unless they are genuinely known at score time.

**Training eligibility at the current cutoff**

| Cohort | Training status |
|---|---|
| January–March | Safe for data quality and label maturity; represents the old operational regime only. |
| April | Unsafe as-is. Potentially usable only after full replay and validation; otherwise exclude. |
| May | Conditionally safe after verifying early-May mapping, duplicates, and feature lineage. Labels are mature. |
| June | Safe after lineage validation and important for representing the remote-routing policy. |
| July | Nearly mature but is a supplier-transition month. Use only after resolving the remaining labels or through an explicitly fixed-vintage/censor-aware method. |
| August | Not safe for ordinary naive training yet at 93% maturity. Eventually essential for learning the severe supplier regime. |
| September | Unsafe now because only 82% are mature and Partner Z has differential delay. Eventually essential for population and post-supplier coverage. |
| October | Unsafe for supervised training at 55% maturity. |

“Safe” here means fit for data use, not necessarily sufficient to represent the future regime.

**Full retraining**

Full retraining is justified only after:

- April is repaired or excluded;
- August–September labels are sufficiently mature by variant and source;
- relevant supplier and population features are available at score time;
- temporal validation explicitly tests pre-policy, post-routing, supplier-disruption, and Partner Z periods.

A variant-aware or mixture-of-experts model is plausible because V3 has a distinct failure mechanism and substantial volume. It is justified only if the route or planned part dependency is known at prediction time. Otherwise, using the eventual variant would leak future process information. Separate V1/V2/V3 calibration layers are a lower-risk first step.

A Partner Z-specific model is premature: there is only one partially mature observed month, and much of the difference may be explained by route, rurality, device age, or supplier exposure. Prefer a pooled model with validated covariates and source/route interactions, followed by source-stratified calibration and fairness evaluation.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The monitoring system should retain two clocks: **case-start month**, for predictive cohorts, and **event-calendar month**, for current operational activity.

1. **Predictive-output monitors**
   - Score volume, mean and quantiles of predicted risk, threshold crossing rates, and score distribution by variant/route decision, source, geography, and model version.
   - Compare feature and score distributions with both a fixed baseline and the immediately preceding stable window.

2. **Calibration and supervised-performance monitors**
   - AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, ECE, and reliability diagrams.
   - Report aggregate and variant/source strata, with confidence intervals and minimum sample-size gates.
   - Publish provisional and mature versions rather than silently revising one dashboard.

3. **Label-maturity monitors**
   - Fraction known by days since diagnosis and promised date, median and tail label delay, revision rate, and unresolved counts.
   - Stratify by variant, source, predicted-risk band, and actual resolution-path length.
   - Alert on differential maturity because overall 90% maturity can still conceal severe V3 censoring.

4. **Data-quality and lineage monitors**
   - Unknown-activity share, diagnosis-prefix coverage, duplicate-event rate, event-order violations, queue-feature missingness, and source-schema changes.
   - Track raw-source, mapping, feature-pipeline, and model versions for every prediction.
   - Add contract tests requiring either diagnosis alias to map to the canonical event.

5. **Process-variant monitors**
   - Variant shares, new/unmapped variants, directly-follows edge counts, loop/rework rates, and route-policy adherence.
   - Compare event-calendar cohorts, which describe work executed during the month, with case-start cohorts, which describe the eventual paths of demand entering during the month.

6. **Performance-DFG monitors**
   - Median, upper quantiles, and censored counts for operationally important edges such as `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis-to-remote-fix.
   - These can generate an operational supplier alert before labels mature.

7. **Population monitors**
   - Source mix, geography/rurality, travel distance, device age, part type, promised duration, and workload.
   - Measure overlap and standardized distribution changes, not only a single global PSI.
   - Maintain Partner Z/non-Z transport and fairness views.

8. **Intervention and deployment monitors**
   - Overlay workflow migrations, mapping patches, routing-policy changes, supplier incidents, partner onboarding, model deployments, calibrator changes, and decision-threshold changes.
   - Monitor whether model-triggered interventions alter routing or outcomes, since this can create feedback-induced drift.

**Alert dependencies**

- A feature-quality or schema alarm should first trigger replay and quarantine; it should not automatically trigger retraining.
- A label-maturity alarm should mark supervised metrics provisional and suppress retraining recommendations. Process, population, and data-quality monitors should continue operating.
- A process or population alarm with stable mature within-stratum performance suggests operational action, reweighting, or recalibration—not necessarily retraining.
- A concept-drift investigation should open only when mature, pipeline-valid, within-stratum performance deteriorates.
- Retraining should require all three gates: valid feature lineage, adequate and non-differential label maturity, and evidence that repair or recalibration is insufficient.

The following sketch extracts auditable process evidence without fitting a predictive model:

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
    if not required.issubset(raw.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"Missing columns: {required - set(raw.columns)}")

    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)
    renamed = raw["activity"].eq("Diagnostic Assessment Completed").sum()

    # Normalize before deduplication and feature extraction, so old/new aliases match.
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    })

    # Prefer immutable source event IDs. The semantic fallback removes only exact
    # duplicates and should be sensitivity-tested against legitimate repeated work.
    removed_by_id = 0
    if "event_id" in raw.columns:
        id_keys = (["source_system"] if "source_system" in raw.columns else ["case_id"])
        id_keys += ["event_id"]
        before = len(raw)
        raw = raw.drop_duplicates(id_keys, keep="last")
        removed_by_id = before - len(raw)

    semantic_keys = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
    semantic_keys += [c for c in ["lifecycle", "resource"] if c in raw.columns]
    before = len(raw)
    raw = raw.drop_duplicates(semantic_keys, keep="last")
    removed_semantic = before - len(raw)

    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    cid, act, ts = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
    log[ts] = pd.to_datetime(log[ts], utc=True)
    log = log.sort_values([cid, ts])

    summaries, edges, variants_out, temporal_out = [], [], [], []
    case_starts = log.groupby(cid)[ts].min()
    periods = pd.period_range(
        log[ts].min().tz_localize(None).to_period("M"),
        log[ts].max().tz_localize(None).to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    def summarize(piece, month, basis):
        if piece.empty:
            return

        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(piece)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(piece)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(piece)

        ordered = piece.sort_values([cid, ts]).copy()
        ordered["_next_activity"] = ordered.groupby(cid)[act].shift(-1)
        ordered["_next_time"] = ordered.groupby(cid)[ts].shift(-1)
        ordered["_hours"] = (
            ordered["_next_time"] - ordered[ts]
        ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
        pairs = ordered.dropna(subset=["_next_activity", "_hours"])
        pairs = pairs[pairs["_hours"] >= 0]
        stats = pairs.groupby([act, "_next_activity"])["_hours"].agg(
            edge_n="size", median_hours="median"
        )
        stats = stats.to_dict("index")

        n_cases = piece[cid].nunique()
        summaries.append({
            "month": month, "cohort_basis": basis,
            "cases": n_cases, "events": len(piece),
            "unknown_share": piece[act].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
            "diagnosis_case_coverage": (
                piece.loc[piece[act].eq("Initial Diagnosis Completed"), cid].nunique()
                / n_cases
            ),
            "variant_count": len(variants),
        })

        for edge, count in dfg.items():
            s = stats.get(edge, {})
            edges.append({
                "month": month, "cohort_basis": basis,
                "from": edge[0], "to": edge[1], "dfg_count": count,
                "median_hours": s.get("median_hours"),
                "edge_n": s.get("edge_n", 0),
                # PM4Py's configured/default performance-DFG aggregate:
                "pm4py_performance_value": perf_dfg.get(edge),
            })

        for variant, case_ids in variants.items():
            label = " > ".join(map(str, variant)) if isinstance(variant, tuple) else str(variant)
            variants_out.append({
                "month": month, "cohort_basis": basis,
                "variant": label, "cases": len(case_ids),
            })

        tf = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(piece)
        for feature, value in tf.select_dtypes(include="number").median().items():
            temporal_out.append({
                "month": month, "cohort_basis": basis,
                "feature": feature, "median": value,
            })

    for period in periods:
        start = period.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        end = (period + 1).start_time.tz_localize("UTC")

        # Work executed in the calendar month; traces/edges may be boundary-truncated.
        event_calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, start.isoformat(),
            (end - pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1)).isoformat(),
            mode="events",
        )

        # Cases entering in the month, retaining their full trace available in the file;
        # recent cohorts are still right-truncated at the file cutoff.
        ids = case_starts[(case_starts >= start) & (case_starts < end)].index
        case_start_cohort = log[log[cid].isin(ids)].copy()

        summarize(event_calendar, str(period), "event_calendar")
        summarize(case_start_cohort, str(period), "case_start")

    return {
        "cleaning_audit": pd.DataFrame([{
            "input_rows": len(pd.read_csv(path)),
            "renamed_diagnosis_events": renamed,
            "removed_by_event_id": removed_by_id,
            "removed_exact_semantic_duplicates": removed_semantic,
            "output_rows": len(log),
        }]),
        "monthly_summary": pd.DataFrame(summaries),
        "edge_summary": pd.DataFrame(edges),
        "variant_summary": pd.DataFrame(variants_out),
        "temporal_feature_summary": pd.DataFrame(temporal_out),
    }
```

| Calibrating around April’s pipeline bug | A calibrator would learn corrections for malformed prefixes, missing queue values, and duplicate artifacts. Once the pipeline is repaired, it could create systematic underprediction. | Replay and repair April first. Recalibrate only on feature-lineage-valid cohorts using the frozen production feature definition. |
| Treating supplier deterioration as only a base-rate calibration issue | Calibration may correct average V3 risk but cannot restore the fall in AUROC or identify which parts-dependent cases are most exposed. Operational causes would continue unaddressed. | Add supplier/part-backlog predictors, test conditional V3 drift, retrain when mature, and pursue supplier and inventory remediation in parallel. |
| Retraining unnecessarily on June’s routing change | A stable model could be destabilized even though within-variant outcomes and remote-fix performance remained stable. The new model might sacrifice ranking quality to chase a changed aggregate prior. | First standardize by variant and try post-policy or route-specific recalibration.
