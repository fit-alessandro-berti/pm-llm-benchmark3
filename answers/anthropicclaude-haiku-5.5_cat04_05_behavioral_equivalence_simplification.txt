# 1. Equivalence Notions

**Notation.** Let $\Sigma_V$ be the visible labels: Open, Analyze (AR), Check (CR), Approve (AP), Rework (RW), Close. Let $\tau$ be the silent label. For a model $M$, $L(M)$ is the set of *complete* accepting runs projected to visible labels (initial marking to final marking, with $\tau$ erased). Let $\#x(w)$ be the number of occurrences of label $x$ in $w$.

**Observation rule for duplicate labels.** Trace equivalence distinguishes occurrences by *position in the sequence* but not by *identity* of the underlying transition or leaf. Two CR events in $\langle$Open, AR, CR, RW, AR, CR, Close$\rangle$ are indistinguishable by label, and the two CR events are not tracked by transition identity. Under P1, however, occurrence position is relevant because each CR must be tied to a specific revision segment. Under P1 I therefore define segments by position, not by transition identity.

**1. Visible trace equivalence (reference, unbounded).**
$M \equiv_V N \iff L(M) = L(N)$, where $L$ is the set of visible label sequences of complete runs. Prefixes are not compared and each run is compared as a whole.

**2. Bounded visible-language equivalence (P3).**
Let $\mathcal{B} = \{w : |w| \le 12,\ \#AR(w) \le 3\}$. Then $M \equiv_{P3} N \iff L(M) \cap \mathcal{B} = L(N) \cap \mathcal{B}$.
A difference inside $\mathcal{B}$ refutes equivalence. Agreement inside $\mathcal{B}$ is only evidence.

**3. Business-relevant equivalence (P1).**
Let $C_1$ be the P1 compliance predicate on a complete visible trace $w = \text{Open}\cdot S_1\cdot RW\cdot S_2\cdots RW\cdot S_k\cdot\text{Close}$ (the $S_i$ are the segments between Open/Rework/Close boundaries). Under the **strict reading** I adopt:

- $C_1(w)$ holds iff for every segment $S_i$: $S_i$ contains exactly one AR and exactly one CR (any order); $S_i$ contains at most one AP; and if AP occurs, it is the last event of $S_i$.

The **literal reading** drops the "AP last" condition. I note where the verdict depends on this choice.

Business equivalence under P1 is then: $M \equiv_{P1} N \iff L(M)\cap C_1 = L(N)\cap C_1$, and additionally a model is *P1-compliant* iff $L(M) \subseteq C_1$. The verdict is reported on both.

**4. Abstracted dashboard equivalence (P2).**
Define the abstraction $\alpha_2$ on complete traces. Let $\text{Open}$ be first and $\text{Close}$ last. Erase AP and $\tau$. Let $n = \#AR(w)$, let $c = [\#CR(w) \ge 1]$, and let $r = [\#RW(w) \ge 1]$. Then $\alpha_2(w) = (n, c, r)$ if $n\ge1$, $c = 1$, and the Open/Close constraints hold; otherwise $\alpha_2(w)=\bot$.
$M \equiv_{P2} N \iff \alpha_2(L(M)) = \alpha_2(L(N))$.
Since $r = [n\ge2]$ is determined by $n$ in both models, the observation reduces to $(n,c)$. The interleaving of AR and CR is not observed.

# 2. Structural Comparison

**Derived language characterisations.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the interleaving of two events, and write $R=$ RW.

- **Lemma A.** $L(A) = \{\text{Open}\cdot S_1 \cdot R \cdot S_2 \cdots R\cdot S_k\cdot \text{Close} : k\ge1,\ S_i \in \{AR\,CR,\ CR\,AR\}\cdot(\varepsilon\mid AP)\}$.
  Proof: $P = {\to}(+(AR,CR),\ X(AP,\tau))$ emits one AR and one CR in either order, then either AP or nothing. The binary loop $*(P,R)$ executes $P(RP)^*$.
- **Lemma B.** $L(B) = \{\text{Open}\cdot u\cdot \text{Close}: u\in \text{shuffle}(\{CR\}, W)\}$, where $W \in L\big(AR(\varepsilon\mid AP)(R\,AR(\varepsilon\mid AP))^*\big)$.
  Proof: the top-level $+$ runs the single CR concurrently with the loop $*(Q,R)$, $Q = {\to}(AR, X(AP,\tau))$. The join is always reached because the loop terminates in finitely many steps, so Close occurs after both branches complete.

**Invariants (used in the proofs below).**
- (I-A1) In every complete run of A: $\#CR = \#AR = k \ge 1$.
- (I-A2) In A, every AP is preceded by a CR in the same segment.
- (I-B1) In every complete run of B: $\#CR = 1$ exactly.
- (I-B2) In B, AP is preceded only by its own AR, and CR may occur anywhere between Open and Close.

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | The loop body is $P$ = (AR  CR) then optional AP. CR is inside the loop, once per iteration. | The loop body is $Q$ = AR then optional AP. CR is outside the loop, in a parallel branch. | In A, check multiplicity is tied to revision count. In B, it is decoupled from the loop. |
| Min/max CR occurrences | min 1, max unbounded; always $=\#AR$ (I-A1). | exactly 1 (I-B1). | Any trace with $\#CR \ne \#AR$ separates A from B. Traces with $\#CR\ge2$ are not in B. |
| Min/max AR occurrences | min 1, max unbounded. | min 1, max unbounded. | Both models allow arbitrary revision counts. This is the same under P2 and P3. |
| Possible CR placement | Inside each segment, before or after the segment's AR, and before the segment's AP or exit. | Anywhere between Open and the join, including after Rework and after Close's predecessors. | Strict P1 compliance is violated by B whenever a revision's segment lacks a check. |
| Optional AP occurrences | 0 or 1 per segment (X with ); AP always after both AR and CR of that segment. | 0 or 1 per AR; AP directly after its AR and independent of CR. | AP-before-CR traces exist only in B. The auto-approve branch is silent () in both models. |
| Concurrency | Local `+` inside each segment, closed before AP or RW. | Global `+` between CR and the whole loop. | In A, concurrency cannot cross a RW boundary. In B, it can. |
| Join before Close | Sequential: all segment CRs precede Close by construction. | Parallel join: Close waits for CR and for loop termination. | In B, the join enforces "CR happened before Close" only, not "CR happened per revision". |
| Rework placement | Rework only follows a completed segment (after its CR). | Rework can precede the CR of the same revision (CR is free). | Traces like Open·AR·RW·AR·CR·Close are B-only. |

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unbounded visible trace equivalence (reference) | not equivalent | high (proof by witness) | $T_1 \in L(A)\setminus L(B)$ by invariant I-B1; $T_3\in L(B)\setminus L(A)$ by I-A1. | None. |
| P1 (strict reading, "same compliant behaviour") | not equivalent | high | $L(A)\cap C_1 = L(A) \supsetneq L(B)\cap C_1$, since $T_1\in C_1$ is not in B. | Under the literal reading the sets still differ (see the T4 caveat). |
| P1 (A is compliant) | A is P1-compliant ($L(A)\subseteq C_1$); B is not ($T_3\notin C_1$). | high | Lemma A and I-A1 give A's compliance. | Depends on the strict AP-last rule for T4 only. |
| P2 (dashboard) | equivalent only under stated abstraction | high | $\alpha_2(L(A)) = \alpha_2(L(B)) = \{(n,1,[n\ge2]) : n\ge1\}$. | This is not trace equivalence. Check multiplicity, CR position and AP are all hidden by the abstraction. |
| P3 (bounded, $|w|\le12$, $\#AR\le3$) | not equivalent | high | $T_1$ (length 7, 2 ARs) and $T_3$ (length 6, 2 ARs) are in the bound and separate the sets. | Refutation is a finite proof. Equality could only be bounded evidence. The bound is not proof. |

**Overall answer.** A and B are *not* equivalent under P1 or P3. They are equivalent only under P2, and only under the dashboard abstraction.

# 4. Counterexample Traces

Each row has been checked against Lemmas A and B and the invariants. The code in Section 5 (`admits`) confirms the same memberships.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Open, AR, CR, RW, AR, CR, Close | yes (two segments, each with own CR,  branch) | no | B has exactly one CR (I-B1); this trace has two. | P1 (per-revision check), P3 |
| T2 | Open, AR, CR, AP, RW, AR, CR, Close | yes (AP after both events of segment 1) | no | Same CR-count violation as T1. Also shows AP is legal in A after a complete segment. | P1 (approval after check), P3 |
| T3 | Open, CR, AR, RW, AR, Close | no (segment 2 has AR but no CR; I-A1 gives #CR=1#AR=2) | yes (CR runs in parallel before the loop) | A requires a CR in every segment. | P1 (violated), P2 (admitted: n=2, c=1), P3 |
| T4 | Open, AR, AP, CR, Close | no (AP before CR violates I-A2) | yes (AP follows its AR; CR runs later in parallel) | Approval-before-check ordering, possible only in B. | P1 strict (violated), P1 literal (admitted), P2 (admitted), P3 |
| T5 | Open, AR, RW, AR, CR, Close | no (segment 1 has no CR) | yes (loop runs first, CR joins later) | Rework before the CR of the first revision. | P1 (violated), P2 (admitted: n=2, c=1), P3 |

**Two analysis attempts.** T1, T2, T3 and T5 each contain two AR occurrences, satisfying the requirement.

**Invariant-based proofs of separation.**
- T1  L(A)L(B): T1 has #CR = 2  1, contradicting I-B1. T1  L(A) by Lemma A with $S_1=S_2=$ AR CR.
- T3  L(B)L(A): T3 has #AR = 2  #CR = 1, contradicting I-A1. T3  L(B) by Lemma B with CR in the parallel branch and $W$ = AR RW AR.

**Effect of optional Approve.** AP is optional, and the auto-approve path is silent. A's optional AP is placed after both events of its segment, so the only difference visible through AP is ordering. B's AP is only tied to its AR, which is why T4 separates the models. Under P2, AP is hidden, so T4's projection (Open, AR, CR, Close) is a trace of both models and the separation disappears. Under P3, AP is visible, so the approval-ordering difference is retained and T4 is a witness.

**Effect of hidden .** The  branch of $X(AP,\tau)$ does not change the visible language; it only means "no AP". It does not create any separation by itself. A's segment still requires a CR even when AP is skipped, so the  path cannot be used to avoid CR. Had the  branch been placed to skip the whole segment, the verdict would change. It does not do so here.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following program is exhaustive within the P3 bound. It runs a BFS over (marking, visible prefix) pairs. It prunes prefixes that exceed 12 visible events or 3 AR occurrences, which is sound because both counts are monotone along a run. Silent transitions do not advance the prefix. Repeated (marking, prefix) pairs are skipped. The program also checks soundness and verifies the counterexample traces with a separate word-membership search. It reports only exhaustive sets. A separate random playout is labelled as heuristic.

```python
"""
Bounded comparison of Model A and Model B under P3.
- Exhaustive part: BFS over (marking, visible prefix) with pruning.
  Complete = visible word reached with final marking. Silent steps are free.
- Heuristic part: random playout. Not exhaustive; used only as a cross-check.
"""
import random
from collections import deque

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

MODEL_A = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

MODEL_B = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYZE = 3
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"


def load_accepting_net(tree_str, name):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    res = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    sound = bool(res[0]) if isinstance(res, tuple) else bool(res)
    if not sound:
        raise RuntimeError(f"Model {name} is not a sound workflow net")
    return net, im, fm


def to_dict(m):
    return {p: c for p, c in m.items() if c > 0}


def mkey(m):
    # Place names are unique after conversion; key is hashable and order-free.
    return frozenset((p.name, c) for p, c in to_dict(m).items())


def enabled(net, m):
    # Uses pm4py's enabling semantics.
    return pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, Marking(to_dict(m)))


def fire(m, t):
    new = dict(to_dict(m))
    for a in t.in_arcs:
        new[a.source] -= 1
        if new[a.source] == 0:
            del new[a.source]
    for a in t.out_arcs:
        new[a.target] = new.get(a.target, 0) + 1
    return new


def bounded_visible_language(net, im, fm):
    """Exhaustive BFS. Returns (set of complete visible traces, #states expanded)."""
    init = (to_dict(im), ())
    seen = {(mkey(im), ())}
    queue = deque([init])
    complete, expanded = set(), 0
    while queue:
        m, trace = queue.popleft()
        expanded += 1
        if mkey(m) == mkey(fm):
            complete.add(trace)
        for t in enabled(net, m):
            if t.label is None:                       # silent: prefix unchanged
                nt = trace
            else:
                nt = trace + (t.label,)
                if len(nt) > MAX_VISIBLE or nt.count(ANALYZE) > MAX_ANALYZE:
                    continue                          # monotone prune
            nm = fire(m, t)
            key = (mkey(nm), nt)
            if key not in seen:
                seen.add(key)
                queue.append((nm, nt))
    return complete, expanded


def admits(net, im, fm, word):
    """Exact membership: is `word` a complete visible trace of the net?"""
    stack = [(to_dict(im), 0)]
    seen = {(mkey(im), 0)}
    while stack:
        m, i = stack.pop()
        if i == len(word) and mkey(m) == mkey(fm):
            return True
        for t in enabled(net, m):
            if t.label is None:
                ni = i
            elif i < len(word) and t.label == word[i]:
                ni = i + 1
            else:
                continue
            key = (mkey(fire(m, t)), ni)
            if key not in seen:
                seen.add(key)
                stack.append((to_dict(fire(m, t)), ni))
    return False


def random_playout(net, im, fm, n, seed=0, max_steps=200):
    """HEURISTIC, NOT EXHAUSTIVE: uniform random choice among enabled transitions.
    It may miss traces with low probability and does not prove anything about the
    bounded language. Used only as a cross-check that sampled traces lie in the
    exhaustive set."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    out = set()
    for _ in range(n):
        m, trace = to_dict(im), []
        for _ in range(max_steps):
            if mkey(m) == mkey(fm):
                out.add(tuple(trace))
                break
            ts = list(enabled(net, m))
            if not ts:
                break
            t = rng.choice(ts)
            if t.label is not None:
                trace.append(t.label)
            m = fire(m, t)
    return out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    netA, imA, fmA = load_accepting_net(MODEL_A, "A")
    netB, imB, fmB = load_accepting_net(MODEL_B, "B")

    LA, nA = bounded_visible_language(netA, imA, fmA)
    LB, nB = bounded_visible_language(netB, imB, fmB)
    print(f"Exhaustive bounded exploration (<=12 visible, <=3 AR): "
          f"A: {len(LA)} traces ({nA} states), B: {len(LB)} traces ({nB} states)")

    print("\nTraces unique to A:")
    for w in sorted(LA - LB, key=lambda w: (len(w), w)):
        print("  A-only:", " -> ".join(w))
    print("\nTraces unique to B:")
    for w in sorted(LB - LA, key=lambda w: (len(w), w)):
        print("  B-only:", " -> ".join(w))

    # Verify the Section 4 witnesses by exact membership.
    T = {
        "T1": ("Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Rework Case",
               "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Close Case"),
        "T3": ("Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision", "Rework Case",
               "Analyze Revision", "Close Case"),
        "T4": ("Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Approve Revision", "Check Revision",
               "Close Case"),
    }
    for k, w in T.items():
        print(k, "A:", admits(netA, imA, fmA, w), "B:", admits(netB, imB, fmB, w))

    # Heuristic cross-check (not exhaustive): sampled in-bound traces must lie in LA|LB.
    sample = random_playout(netA, imA, fmA, 2000, seed=1)
    sample = {w for w in sample if len(w) <= MAX_VISIBLE and w.count(ANALYZE) <= MAX_ANALYZE}
    print("\nHeuristic sample of A not in exhaustive A-set:", len(sample - LA))
```

**Exhaustiveness.** The BFS terminates because the net is safe (process-tree nets are bounded). The visible-length and AR-count prunes are finite, and the visited set blocks silent or repeated cycles. Complete traces are recorded whenever the final marking is reached, including after trailing silent steps. Because pruning is monotone, every complete trace within the bound is found.

**Sampling caveat.** `random_playout` is not exhaustive because it samples a particular resolution of choices. It cannot establish absence of a trace. `pm4py.play_out` has the same limitation and is not used for the verdict.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

**Footprints.** `pm4py.discover_footprints` gives direct-succession (`sequence`), `parallel`, start and end activities, from the model's behaviour. It can add evidence that the models disagree on local orderings, but it is an abstraction. Expected differences, derivable from Lemmas A and B:
- In A, every RW is preceded by a CR in the same segment, so CR  RW holds.
- In B, RW  CR (T5 is a run with RW before CR; T3 has CR before RW).
- In A, AP is after CR. In B, CR  AP (T4).

Thus the footprints should differ. If they were equal, that would still not establish trace equivalence: footprints ignore counts (T1 vs T3) and any dependency across three or more events.

```python
import pm4py

def fp_rel(fp, a, b):
    """Return the relation between a and b. Key names follow pm4py's footprint dict;
    verify them against your pm4py version."""
    if (a, b) in fp["sequence"]:
        return "->"
    if (b, a) in fp["sequence"]:
        return "<-"
    if (a, b) in fp["parallel"] or (b, a) in fp["parallel"]:
        return "||"
    return "#"

tree_A = pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_A)   # MODEL_A/B from Section 5
tree_B = pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_B)
fpA = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_A)
fpB = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_B)
for a, b in [("Check Revision", "Rework Case"), ("Check Revision", "Analyze Revision"),
              ("Approve Revision", "Check Revision")]:
    print(a, b, "A:", fp_rel(fpA, a, b), "B:", fp_rel(fpB, a, b))
```

**Stochastic language and EMD.** `pm4py.get_stochastic_language` returns a distribution over traces that depends on probabilities attached to the model. The process trees here have no probabilities, so using `get_stochastic_language` directly would invent them. The following is a *declared* assumption: an empirical distribution from random playout with uniform choice among enabled transitions. This is not a model of real frequencies.

```python
def empirical(samples):
    """Declared assumption: distribution induced by uniform choice among enabled
    transitions (fixed seed). Not a real-world frequency distribution."""
    d = {}
    for s in samples:
        k = ",".join(s)
        d[k] = d.get(k, 0) + 1
    n = sum(d.values())
    return {k: v / n for k, v in d.items()}

sa = random_playout(netA, imA, fmA, 5000, seed=7)  # random_playout from Section 5
sb = random_playout(netB, imB, fmB, 5000, seed=7)
emd = pm4py.compute_emd(empirical(sa), empirical(sb))
print("EMD (declared uniform-resolution distributions):", emd)
```

**Why these do not establish equivalence.**
- Equal footprints are a local relational abstraction. They cannot express counts (T1 vs T3) or cross-segment dependencies.
- A low EMD over a declared distribution measures similarity of sampled mass, not inclusion. The two languages could differ on traces that carry negligible probability under the declared distribution, and the distance would still be small. The EMD could even be zero if the sampled traces happen to coincide.
- Both measures depend on the chosen distribution. Only a set-level comparison (Section 5) gives a refutation.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Structural counts are computed by traversing the process tree. Node count is all nodes, leaves are those with no children. Depth is the maximum number of edges from the root to any node, with the root at depth 0. Duplicate-label count is $\sum_x \max(0, \#\text{leaves labelled } x - 1)$.

```python
import pm4py

def tree_stats(tree):
    nodes, maxd, vis, sil, labels = 0, 0, 0, 0, []
    stack = [(tree, 0)]
    while stack:
        n, d = stack.pop()
        nodes += 1
        maxd = max(maxd, d)
        if not n.children:
            if n.label is None:
                sil += 1
            else:
                vis += 1
                labels.append(n.label)
        for c in n.children:
            stack.append((c, d + 1))
    dup = len(labels) - len(set(labels))
    return dict(nodes=nodes, depth=maxd, visible_leaves=vis,
                silent_leaves=sil, duplicate_label_leaves=dup)

def simplicity(tree_str):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    return pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)

print("A:", tree_stats(pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_A)), simplicity(MODEL_A))
print("B:", tree_stats(pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_B)), simplicity(MODEL_B))
```

**Derived values (process-tree counts, derived by hand).**

| Measure | Model A | Model B |
|---|---|---|
| Operator nodes | 5 (`->`, `*`, `->`, `+`, `X`) | 5 (`->`, `+`, `*`, `->`, `X`) |
| Leaves | 7 | 7 |
| Total nodes | 12 | 12 |
| Visible leaves | 6 (Open, AR, CR, AP, RW, Close) | 6 (Open, CR, AR, AP, RW, Close) |
| Silent leaves | 1 () | 1 () |
| Maximum depth | 4 (root`*``->``X`AP) | 5 (root`+``*``->``X`AP) |
| Duplicate-label leaves | 0 | 0 |

The depth difference is the one structural difference derived from the tree itself. The sizes are equal.

**Petri-net simplicity.** `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net` is not computed here. Its value comes from the converted net, which I have not run. The printed values from the code above are the authoritative numbers for this comparison.

**Interpretation.** Process-tree size does not distinguish the models. The difference lies in where the CR leaf sits relative to the loop, which is a semantic property, not a size property.

# 8. Simplified Model

**Target semantics: P2 (dashboard).** I choose P2 as the purpose for simplification. The reason: P2 is the only view that deliberately abstracts, and the abstraction is precisely defined. P1 is the compliance view and must be served by the detailed model A. Simplifying A for P1 would not produce a meaningfully smaller tree, because A is already close to minimal in the process-tree grammar. The grammar has no optional operator other than `X(·, )`.

**Simplified expression.**

$$S_2 = {\to}\big(\text{Open},\ +(\text{CR},\ *(\text{AR}, \text{RW})),\ \text{Close}\big)$$

Visible language: $\text{Open}\cdot\text{shuffle}(\text{CR},\ \text{AR}(\text{RW}\,\text{AR})^*)\cdot\text{Close}$. This is B with AP and  erased. Under $\alpha_2$ it is equivalent to A and to B.

**Structural comparison.**

| Measure | A | B | $S_2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total nodes | 12 | 12 | 8 |
| Operators | 5 | 5 | 3 |
| Leaves | 7 (6 visible, 1 silent) | 7 (6 visible, 1 silent) | 5 (all visible) |
| Depth | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Duplicate labels | 0 | 0 | 0 |

$S_2$ is smaller than both originals in nodes, operators, leaves and depth. The removed elements are AP, , the `X` operator, and the extra `->` level. Those are exactly the parts that P2 hides.

**Code.**

```python
import random
from collections import deque
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

SIMPLIFIED_STR = ("->('Open Case',+('Check Revision',*('Analyze Revision','Rework Case')),"
                  "'Close Case')")

ORIGINAL_A = """->('Open Case',*(->(+('Analyze Revision','Check Revision'),
X('Approve Revision',tau)),'Rework Case'),'Close Case')"""

MAX_VISIBLE, MAX_ANALYZE = 12, 3
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SIMPLIFIED_STR)
_net, _im, _fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_model)
_res = pm4py.check_soundness(_net, _im, _fm, print_diagnostics=False)
assert (bool(_res[0]) if isinstance(_res, tuple) else bool(_res)), "S2 not sound"

def _to_dict(m): return {p: c for p, c in m.items() if c > 0}
def _key(m): return frozenset((p.name, c) for p, c in _to_dict(m).items())
def _en(net, m): return pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, Marking(_to_dict(m)))
def _fire(m, t):
    new = dict(_to_dict(m))
    for a in t.in_arcs:
        new[a.source] -= 1
        if new[a.source] == 0: del new[a.source]
    for a in t.out_arcs:
        new[a.target] = new.get(a.target, 0) + 1
    return new

def _bounded(net, im, fm):
    seen = {(_key(im), ())}; q = deque([(_to_dict(im), ())]); out = set()
    while q:
        m, w = q.popleft()
        if _key(m) == _key(fm): out.add(w)
        for t in _en(net, m):
            nw = w if t.label is None else w + (t.label,)
            if t.label is not None and (len(nw) > MAX_VISIBLE or nw.count(ANALYZE) > MAX_ANALYZE):
                continue
            nm = _fire(m, t)
            if (_key(nm), nw) not in seen:
                seen.add((_key(nm), nw)); q.append((nm, nw))
    return out

def _admits(net, im, fm, word):
    stack = [(_to_dict(im), 0)]; seen = {(_key(im), 0)}
    while stack:
        m, i = stack.pop()
        if i == len(word) and _key(m) == _key(fm): return True
        for t in _en(net, m):
            if t.label is None: ni = i
            elif i < len(word) and t.label == word[i]: ni = i + 1
            else: continue
            nm = _fire(m, t)
            if (_key(nm), ni) not in seen:
                seen.add((_key(nm), ni)); stack.append((nm, ni))
    return False

def alpha2(w):
    """P2 abstraction. Returns None if the Open/Close constraints fail."""
    vis = [x for x in w if x != "Approve Revision"]   # AP hidden in dashboard
    if not vis or vis[0] != "Open Case" or vis[-1] != "Close Case": return None
    n = vis.count(ANALYZE); c = vis.count("Check Revision") > 0
    r = vis.count("Rework Case") > 0
    if n < 1 or not c: return None
    return (n, c, r)

netA, imA, fmA = (lambda t: (pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(t)))(pm4py.parse_process_tree(ORIGINAL_A))
```

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **P1 (operational compliance):** Use **Model A** as the system of record. It is P1-compliant by Lemma A and invariant I-A1. Do **not** use Model B. It admits non-compliant runs, including T3 (a revision analysed without its own check), T4 (approval before check), and T5 (rework before the first check). Model B also cannot produce compliant runs with two revisions (T1 and T2 are impossible in B).

- **P2 (dashboard):** Either model suffices, because they agree under $\alpha_2$. The simplified **$S_2$** is appropriate for a dashboard implementation, since it is the smallest model that preserves $\alpha_2$. Do not use $S_2$ for compliance checks, because it loses per-revision checks and approvals.

- **P3 (bounded testing):** Use **Model A** as the reference, because A is the compliant model. Use Model B's traces (T3, T4, T5) as negative tests if B is ever implemented. The bounded comparison refutes equivalence, and the witnesses are finite proofs of non-equivalence. Agreement within the bound would be evidence only.

**Should the simplified model replace either original?** $S_2$ should replace neither. It is not P1-equivalent to A and it is not P3-equivalent to either. It should be used only as the derived dashboard model. Model B should be retired, since its only P2 advantage is that it matches A's dashboard abstraction, which $S_2$ already gives more compactly.

**Limits of bounded comparison.**
1. The bound (12 visible events, 3 AR) is finite. Behaviour with four or more revisions, or with longer prefixes, is untested. This matters most for B, where the single CR could interact with long loops in ways not visible at small bounds.
2. Bounded agreement is evidence, not proof. Here the bounded comparison is sufficient for a refutation only, because the witnesses T1, T3 and T4 are finite and checked exactly.
3. The P1 verdict depends on the strict reading of approval placement (AP last in its segment). The literal reading still separates the models (T1 is compliant and in A only), but the status of T4 changes.
4. Random playout and footprints are not proofs of anything and are included only as cross-checks.
