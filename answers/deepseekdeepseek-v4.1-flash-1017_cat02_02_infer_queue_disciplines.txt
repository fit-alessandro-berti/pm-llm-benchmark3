# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from most to least plausible as explanations for apparent rule violations:

1. **Resource/eligibility holds and availability constraints** — high plausibility. B01’s dispatcher reassignment, A1 calibration, C1’s prior job ending at 10:10, D-F’s meeting, and D-M/D-X availability windows can remove jobs from the eligible set. 2. **Appointment windows/reservations rather than exact start times** — high plausibility. A03 at 08:20 and D06 at 11:30 support reserved-capacity or appointment-window behavior; B07’s late service suggests appointments are not universally honored. If appointment tolerances and buffers are known, QA’s appointment component strengthens and QD’s pre-appointment idling becomes intentional reservation rather than slack.
3. **Arrival-scan delay and minute rounding** — medium-high. The up-to-2-minute delay can make a job invisible at an exact decision time, especially B05 at 09:48. It cannot by itself explain QC’s C04-over-C06 gap or C01-over-C07 unless additional scan/hold delays occurred, so it primarily lowers confidence in QB and exact decision-set reconstruction.
4. **Noisy or strategically inaccurate `est_min`, setup, and hidden remaining work** — medium-high. QC’s SPT pattern is strong early but contradicted by C04-over-C06 and C01-over-C07. If visible estimates differ from actual remaining work or include setup/changeover, the SPT diagnosis weakens. Likewise, B07’s e6 not being used before B06 could be explained if its true remaining work or readiness differed.
5. **Batch/frozen worklist or periodic dispatch** — medium. QC’s early C02C03C04C01 order looks like an initial SPT snapshot; C06 arriving later may not be inserted immediately. If dispatch is periodic or worklists are frozen, C04 before C06 and C01 before C07 could be batch effects rather than violations of SPT or FIFO. 6. **SLA/deadline escalation or aging policy switch** — medium. C05 is selected only when its deadline is already at risk, and C01 is selected before C07 despite C07’s earlier deadline. This could indicate an aging rule, a policy that changes when an SLA becomes endangered, or a non-deadline FIFO backlog rule. If confirmed, QC becomes a hybrid rather than pure SPT.
7. **Hidden skills, qualifications, or multiskill resource reservations** — medium. D-F idling before its meeting despite D10 being eligible, D07 being chosen over D10, and D05 waiting for D-F could reflect hidden routing reservations or qualification rules not shown. This would strengthen QD’s skill-based diagnosis and reduce the credibility of priority/SPT inferences there.
8. **Data omissions or errors** — low-medium but always possible. Absence of an interruption reason does not prove no interruption existed; timestamps are accurate only to one minute.
