```text
GLOBAL (all cases)
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check

                                                      when originals are required, Identity may run
                                                      before or alongside Originals Receipt]
Identity Check < Assessment ; Document Check < Assessment      (latest instances)
Assessment < Decision < Notify                                 [low risk and low value]
Assessment(latest) < Compliance Review(final) < Decision < Notify   [high risk or high value]
Assessment | Compliance Review -> Request More Info -> Document Check' < Assessment' (< Compliance Review')

   but Preliminary Screen < Final Compliance Review and never replaces it

K-219 (low, originals required, handwritten)

Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check(D2)
{Identity Check, Document Check} < Assessment(A2) < Decision < Notify

K-337 (HR+HV, originals required)

K-411 (low, digital)
Register < Triage < {Identity Check(I2) || Document Check(D2)} < Assessment(A2) < Decision < Notify
```

**2. Conditional and declarative constraints**

```text
C1  IF high_risk OR high_value THEN exists Compliance Review with start >= end(latest Assessment)
    AND end <= start(Decision).
C2  IF Request More Info is issued THEN a new Document Check and a new Assessment must complete
    before Decision. IF HR/HV, a new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review is also required.
C3  Request More Info may be issued only by Assessment or Compliance Review. A clarification flagged
    by Document Check must be passed to Assessment, which issues the formal request. No informal loop.
C4  Four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
    (K-104 A1 vs C1 and K-337 A1 vs C1 satisfy this.)
C5  Final Compliance Review: C1 only. Decision and Notify: M1. No resource has overlapping intervals.
C6  A preliminary screen or escalation advice never satisfies C1.
    The same person may do preliminary and final compliance work.
C7  Decision < Notify. Register is first, Triage second.
C8  K-104 reservation: a provisional C1 slot has an expiry. If the packet is incomplete or the slot
    lapses, the case rejoins the normal C1 queue.
C9  K-337 policy: after Assessment2, count(Request More Info) <= 1.
    Decision is in {approve with conditions, reject}. The unresolved discrepancy must be named in the
    Assessment2, Compliance Review and Decision records. Compliance Review may not issue a new RMI.
C10 K-411: both tasks are created at Triage completion. Overlap is valid only between different
    resources (I2, D2) with real-time timestamps. Assessment start >= max(end Identity, end Document).
C11 Queue figures and service times are distributions, not guarantees.
```

**3. Type of change**

- **K-104: timing plus light routing.** It adds a preliminary escalation packet and screen, and moves S1 advice before Assessment. It also reserves C1 capacity. The final control flow and decision policy are unchanged.
- **K-219: timing and resource assignment only.** The control flow is the same. It opens a stochastic routing risk, because rework becomes more likely and would add a Document Check and Assessment loop.
- **K-337: decision policy and routing.** It deletes the second rework loop (Request More Info 2, Document Check 3, Assessment 3). It also changes the allowed decision inputs, so the evidence standard and the outcome distribution change.
- **K-411: timing only in control-flow terms.** Concurrency was already allowed. It is also a task-creation and logging change in the system, with an integrity requirement on timestamps.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Identity was off the critical path. The critical path ran Triage to D1 (queue 13 h at Mon 08:00, done Tue 05:40), A1 (wait 9.5 h, done Tue 19:10), S1 advice (2 h, post-assessment), C1 queue (about 15.8 h wait) and a 4.5 h review because evidence was read from scratch. The ambiguity was known at Triage but was not acted on until Assessment rediscovered it.

**Minimal intervention:** Only the Triage output changes. T1 adds an escalation packet (+10–20 min), S1 advice moves to Monday, and C1 holds a provisional slot with a preliminary screen. Identity, Document Check and Assessment keep their factual resources and queue positions.

**Temporal propagation:** D1 start (Mon 21:40) is set by the D1 queue, not by the 15-minute packet delay, so Document Check ends about Tue 05:40. A1 still starts about Tue 15:10 because the A1 queue binds. Assessment may shorten to 3–4 h because the ambiguity is already framed. The preliminary screen, about 1.5 h, runs best on Tue morning after document findings exist. The final review cannot start before Assessment ends (about Tue 18:40). The reserved slot therefore starts in the C1 window from Tue evening to Wed morning, and base is Wed 08:30 with 2.5–3.5 h of work, because the preliminary screen saves 1–3 h. Decision and Notify follow within about 30–40 min, ending about Wed 12:00–14:00. Earlier *readiness* (Mon) does not become earlier *completion* until Assessment finishes, since A1 is the bottleneck that precedes C1.

**Process-feasibility check:** Final Compliance Review follows the final Assessment and precedes Decision, and the preliminary screen does not substitute for it. A1 and C1 differ, so four-eyes holds. The same person doing preliminary and final compliance work is allowed. C1 is not double-booked if the reservation is placed at a non-overlapping interval.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: final C1 Tue 19:00–21:45, M1 Wed 08:30, end about Wed 08:50 (10 h). Base: C1 Wed 08:30–11:30, end about Wed 12:30 (6 h). Worst: the packet is incomplete, S1 requests more material, the slot lapses to the two urgent cases, C1 starts Wed 13:10 (0 h) or Thu 08:30 (+18 h).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 utilization above about 90% in the week. Preliminary-to-final review time savings under 30 min. Slot expiry or no-show rates above about 30%. Packet-completeness data showing frequent S1 re-requests.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** The case waited 16 h for D1 after originals arrived at 17:30. D1 did accurate work in 4 h. A2 then added a 6.5 h queue wait, and M1 a further 10 h overnight wait. The case was not slow because of service times but because of queue position and shift boundaries.

**Minimal intervention:** Only the Document Check resource changes (D1 to D2), effective at originals arrival. Identity, Assessment resource (A2) and Decision resource (M1) are the same.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's queue (about 5 h at Mon 16:00) plus 5–9 h handwritten service replaces D1's 16 h wait plus 4 h. Whether the saving exists depends on whether D2 works evenings or overnight. If it does, the check completes about Tue 04:30 and the case joins the A2 queue early. A2 (queue about 6 h at Tue 08:00, one assessor in training) still starts about Tue 12:00–14:00, not at once. Assessment takes 2.5–3 h, so M1 can decide Tue afternoon or evening instead of Wed 08:30. If D2 cannot start before Tue 08:00, it finishes about Tue 15:00, later than D1's factual 13:30. A2 then starts about 20:00 as before, and the gain disappears. If D2 flags illegible handwriting, the formal request must come from Assessment (rule 6), and external response (8–36 h) plus a new DC and Assessment add 9–50 h.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt precedes Document Check. The rework loop returns to DC and Assessment as required. D2 is not double-booked if the start respects its queue.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 Mon 21:00–Tue 03:30, A2 about 12:00–15:00, M1 Tue 16:00, end Tue about 16:30 (16 h). Base: D2 completes early Tue, decision Tue evening (about 10 to 12 h). Worst: D2 flags handwriting, RMI, DC' and Assessment' push the end to Thu or Fri (+9 to +50 h). Break-even rework probability is roughly G/(G+L) with G of 10–16 h and L of 25–40 h, so about 0.2–0.35.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2 clarification rates on handwritten items above about 30%. No out-of-hours capacity at D2. Assessment queues that absorb the early arrival. D1 queues falling below about 4 h by Tue 08:00.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Three rounds of Document Check and Assessment, each followed by slow external responses. The first RMI took about 20 h (Wed 11:30 to Thu 08:00) and the second about 70 h (Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00, weekend). Assessment 3 accepted the evidence. Compliance and Decision followed in about 24 h.

**Minimal intervention:** Only the second Request More Info and everything after it in the loop is removed. After Assessment 2, the case proceeds to Compliance Review and Decision on the evidence available. This changes the decision policy because the evidence standard changes.

**Temporal propagation:** Without RMI 2, Document Check 3 (3.5 h) and Assessment 3 (2.5 h) disappear, together with the 70 h external wait. Assessment 2 ends about Fri 10:30–11:15 (possibly longer because it must write conditions). C1 is ready Fri PM (Thursday snapshot 5 h) and takes 4–6 h because the discrepancy must be documented. M1 then decides Fri evening or Mon 08:30. The weekend effect is the largest lever: a Friday completion avoids the 70 h wait, but an unavailable M1 on Friday evening reintroduces a Monday start. C1 cannot proceed in parallel with Assessment 2, because the final review follows the latest Assessment.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 7 is not triggered, because no new RMI is issued. Assessment 2 is the latest assessment, C1 follows, and A1 and C1 differ. The policy forbids silent ignorance, so the discrepancy is named in Assessment 2, Compliance Review and Decision. C1 may not issue its own RMI under this policy.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: C1 Fri 13:00–17:00, M1 Fri 19:00, end Fri 19:10 (110 h). Base: C1 Fri 13:30–18:30, M1 Mon 08:30, end Mon 09:10 (48 h). Worst: C1 slips to Mon PM or needs an S1 adviser, end Tue about 12:00 (20 h). Decision scenarios: approve with conditions (modal), reject (material), or approve with conditions followed by a failed condition. Because the factual confirmation was benign, the factual outcome cannot tell us the counterfactual outcome.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Historical cases where the second confirmation changed the decision. Supplier-specific failure rates. Appeal and loss data for conditional approvals. A C1 position that unresolved discrepancies cannot be conditioned.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The legacy system created only the Identity task. The case lock blocked document task ownership until Identity completed (13:20). The document task then waited 1.7 h and D2 worked 4.5 h. Assessment started next morning (queue grown), then Decision after a 2 h wait.

**Minimal intervention:** Both tasks are created at Triage completion (09:35) and the lock workaround lets I2 and D2 work concurrently with real-time timestamps. All other resources and queues are held fixed.

**Temporal propagation:** Identity is unchanged (11:30–13:20). D2's start moves from 15:00 to about 12:30 (queue about 2–4 h at 09:35) and finishes about 17:00 (2.5 h). This is earlier *readiness*, not necessarily earlier completion. A2's queue (5–6 h) and overnight gap bind. The case may gain FIFO position over cases arriving 17:00–19:30 and so start Wed 08:30–09:00 instead of 09:30. The decision wait (2 h) partly persists. Base end is about Wed 13:15.

**Process-feasibility check:** Identity and Document Check use different resources, so no service overlap violates rule 11. Assessment waits for both. The case is low risk, so no compliance review is needed. Real-time timestamps are required for the concurrency to be credible.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: A2 has a Tue 17:15 slot, Decision Wed 08:30, end about Wed 09:00 (6 h, with a tail to 18 h if M1 signs Tue evening). Base: end Wed 12:00–14:00 (1 to 3 h). Worst: D2 queue unchanged or workaround friction, end 0 to +2 h.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Assessment-queue wait after check completion consistently exceeding the earlier-readiness gain. Logs showing batched entry. Identity failures after document work has started. A2 utilization above about 90%.

**100-case weekly rollout**

Applied to 100 similar cases in a week, the single-case gains stop being additive, because C1, D1/D2 and the assessors are shared and heavily loaded early in the week (Monday: C1 20 h, D1 13 h, A1 10 h).

*Compliance reservation.* C1 is the only certified reviewer. If it has roughly 40–50 productive hours per week (assumption), that is about ten 4.5 h reviews, so a 100-case HR/HV class would already overload it. Reserving slots plus 1.5–3 h screens for every such case burns C1 time on incomplete packets and no-shows and displaces urgent cases. The bottleneck then migrates from A1 to C1. Reservations must be capped and expire.

*D1/D2 substitution.* Diverting handwritten cases saves D1 about 4 h but costs D2 about 7 h, so document hours rise about 3 h per diversion. It pays only while D1's forecast wait exceeds D2's by more than the extra service time. Unconditional diversion makes D2 the new constraint.

*Rework.* If D2 raises clarification probability by 10–15 points, the extra loops (each 8–36 h external, then new DC and Assessment) load D1 and A2 and erase the gain above p of about 0.2–0.35. Suppressing second loops frees D1 and A1 hours but shifts work to C1 and M1 and creates later appeal handling.

*Assessment bottleneck.* K-411, K-219 and K-337 all deliver cases to A1/A2 sooner. With queues of 4–12 h and training reductions, earlier arrival mostly becomes waiting, so savings appear only where assessors would otherwise idle.

*Weekend and external response.* Loop suppression removes the 70 h weekend response wait, the biggest saving, but a Friday-evening completion still waits for M1 until Monday.

*Timestamps.* Batch-logged workarounds create false concurrency: apparent gains without real capacity change, and invisible double-booking. Later mining would learn wrong patterns.

## 7. Final synthesis

**Most robust.** K-411 parallel verification. It changes timing only, cannot breach a compliance rule, and its downside is bounded near zero. Its benefit is modest and mostly limited by the assessment queue, but it teaches the system the most about logging integrity and queue absorption. K-104 is a close second, provided C1 reservations are capped.

**Most dangerous despite speed.** K-337 loop suppression. It offers the largest time saving, and the reason is that it deletes evidence-gathering rather than speeding it up. The decision policy, and with it the outcome distribution, changes. Decisions are irreversible in ways that show up in appeals and losses long after cycle time is measured. The factual approval does not show what would have happened if the confirmation had failed.

**Least transportable.** The K-104 and K-219 gains are the least transportable to a system-wide policy. A single-case counterfactual holds C1 and D2 queues fixed. C1 is a single server, so a policy that reserves capacity for everyone changes the queue it depends on. K-219 depends on D2 being able to start early and on clarification rates that change with volume. K-411 also transports imperfectly, because earlier readiness is absorbed by the assessment queue at scale.

**Most valuable additional data.**
- Per-resource shift calendars (especially overnight availability for D2, A2 and M1) and real-time queue snapshots at case arrival.
- Clarification and rework rates by document type and resource.
- C1 utilization, slot no-show rates and the distribution of preliminary-to-final review savings.
- Historical outcomes of second-loop confirmations for K-337-type cases (did they change the decision?), plus appeal and loss data.
- Timestamp quality: event time vs entry time and batch-entry rates.
- Assessment queue arrival patterns by hour.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** The factual partial order is as stated. In particular, only Assessment or Compliance Review may issue Request More Info, so a D2 clarification must pass through Assessment.
2. **temporal:** Queue snapshots are forecasts with error, and service times are drawn from the stated ranges. Base cases use midpoints and the ranges cover the uncertainty.
3. **resource:** D2 and other pools can work evenings or overnight, as the factual K-104 D1 interval suggests. If they cannot, the K-219 gain disappears.
4. **resource:** C1 has about 40–50 productive hours per week and can honor a reserved slot by Wed morning. M1 decides the same day when Assessment ends in daytime.
5. **behavioral:** The clarification probability for D2 on handwritten documents is 20–35% (judgment, not data). External supplier responses follow the stated 8–36 h range.
6. **quality:** Preliminary compliance work saves 1–3 h of final review only when packets are complete. Incomplete packets waste S1 and C1 time.
7. **quality:** In K-337, the decision outcomes (approve with conditions modal, reject material) are judgmental scenarios. The unobserved counterfactual confirmation cannot be inferred from the factual benign outcome.
8. **interference:** In the single-case analysis other cases' behavior is held approximately fixed. At 100 cases, queues, reservations and assessor capacity change, so those case-level ranges do not transfer directly.
