# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

The hybrid model separates **permitted activity sequences**, **cross-cutting obligations**, **occurrence-count checks**, **temporal warnings**, and **resource/data-dependent authorization**. Passing one layer does not compensate for failing an authoritative rule in another.

**Implemented policy: strict legal precedence.** An activation with an outstanding sanctions escalation fails R5, even when a legal opinion arrives shortly afterward. The emergency exception is an alternative policy version, not an implicitly available escape clause. The supplied requirements explicitly distinguish unconditional legal precedence from an externally authorized emergency exception. 

**Material assumptions**

| Assumption                   | Interpretation adopted                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Completed-case boundary      | T1–T8 are complete traces. `Close Onboarding` ends the case; eventual obligations must be discharged before closure. For live prefixes, an outstanding obligation is not automatically a violation.                                                                                                     |
| Event semantics              | Events represent completed activities. For these tests, verification and approval labels represent successful completion, resources identify distinct people, and the supplied event order is reliable. Production validation must check these assumptions.                                             |
| Normative strength           | R2 and R8 are treated as hard requirements despite using “should,” because their restrictions agree with the specified exclusive procedural routes. R12 remains warning-only.                                                                                                                           |
| Procedural scope             | Initial checks and classification occur once. Rejection is a primary branch selected after classification, not an alternative exit after approval. Additional pre-activation verification or reclassification is outside this implemented procedural scope.                                             |
| Clarification boundary       | For R3, the relevant final outcome is `Activate Vendor` or `Reject Vendor`. Neither may occur with an unanswered clarification. Classification and approval may occur while clarification is pending.                                                                                                   |
| Clarification matching       | Each response answers one outstanding request. Unsolicited responses and multiple responses to one request are excluded. This is an explicit strengthening of the request-oriented wording of R3.                                                                                                       |
| Sanctions scope              | New escalations occur before activation/rejection. A subsequent case-wide legal opinion discharges all currently outstanding escalations. Where opinions have narrower scope, production checks must use escalation/opinion identifiers instead. An opinion before an escalation does not discharge it. |
| Repeated verification and R9 | Every `Verify Bank Account` occurrence, including post-change verification, must be performed by someone other than the applicable approver. An initial-verification-only interpretation would be a defensible but less conservative alternative.                                                       |
| Bank-change semantics        | R11 applies only to changes after activation. A later suspension may discharge several outstanding changes. Verification after the latest change can satisfy the ordering obligation for an ensuing payment. No suspension-before-payment or second-activation requirement is invented.                 |
| Emergency timing             | Under the alternative emergency policy, the 24-hour period starts at activation, includes its endpoint, and requires authorization effective no later than activation. The authorized parties and scope must be defined in that policy.                                                                 |
| Missing risk data            | The activity-only model offers low- and high-risk branches but cannot verify that the chosen branch matches the actual classification result. A missing `risk_class` is reported as `not evaluated`, not inferred from the approval activity.                                                           |

**Two resolutions of R5/R6**

| Resolution                                   | Policy definition                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Consequence and compliance risk                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **S — strict legal precedence, implemented** | R5 is non-overridable; the R6 permission is not enabled. Every escalation relevant to activation must have a later legal opinion before activation.                                                                            | Conservative toward the legal requirement. It can classify operations-authorized emergency behavior as noncompliant with this selected policy and can delay activation.                                                 |
| **E — formally amended emergency exception** | Replace R5’s unconditional wording with a conditional rule: ordinary activation requires prior opinion; emergency activation with outstanding escalation requires verified joint authorization and an opinion within 24 hours. | Preserves the exception only after an authoritative policy amendment. Without that amendment, operations authorization alone does not resolve the contradiction. Missing authorization evidence prevents certification. |

These statements do **not** make every possible case unsatisfiable: ordinary cases can satisfy both. The conflict concerns a class of traces that R6 purports to permit and R5 forbids. Simply enforcing both as hard restrictions silently eliminates the exception.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

“Primary formalism” below identifies the authoritative implementation of the requirement. Other layers provide corroborating diagnostics, not additional votes. The mapping follows the supplied R1–R12 rather than learning normative rules from the eight examples. 

| Requirement | Primary formalism                            | Exact encoding                                                                                                                                                                                          | Why this formalism                                                                                     | Redundancy or limitation                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R1          | **DECLARE**                                  | `noncoexistence("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")`                                                                                                                                                    | Symmetric prohibition independent of order.                                                            | Also enforced by the procedural exclusive choice and skeleton `never_together`.                                                                                                 |
| R2          | **Procedural net**                           | Completed accepted branch: exactly one approval occurrence, either manager or compliance. Rejected branch: neither approval. High-risk approval follows due diligence.                                  | Combines conditional existence, exclusive choice, ordering, and termination.                           | DECLARE excludes both approvals together and either approval with rejection. Those exclusions alone do not require an approval. Actual risk-to-route matching requires data.    |
| R3          | **Procedural clarification-state component** | `idle → Request Clarification → pending → Receive Clarification → idle`; activation and rejection require `idle`.                                                                                       | Enforces both repeatable cycles and the outcome deadline.                                              | DECLARE `altresponse` enforces the next-request boundary; `altprecedence` implements the one-to-one assumption. Skeleton count equality is only a necessary check.              |
| R4          | **DECLARE**                                  | `response("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")`                                                                                                                                              | Requires a subsequent opinion for each escalation.                                                     | Case-wide matching is assumed. It does not enforce opinion-before-activation.                                                                                                   |
| R5          | **Separate policy monitor**                  | At activation under S: the set of outstanding escalations must be empty.                                                                                                                                | Implements conditional, history-sensitive precedence without requiring opinions in un-escalated cases. | Expressible in an enriched Petri net, but deliberately outside the procedural envelope to support policy versions. An unconditional binary precedence rule would be too strong. |
| R6          | **Resource/data-aware policy monitor**       | Under E, activation with outstanding escalation requires valid, timely joint authorization covering the case/activation/escalations; the opinion deadline is activation time + 24 hours.                | Authorization is absent from the activity sequence.                                                    | Disabled under S. Under E, missing evidence gives `not evaluated`, not an assumed exception.                                                                                    |
| R7          | **Procedural net**                           | Screening produces a persistent `screened` token; escalation requires it.                                                                                                                               | Directly represents enabling.                                                                          | DECLARE `precedence("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` provides a backstop.                                                                                    |
| R8          | **DECLARE**                                  | Noncoexistence of due diligence/manager approval and compliance/manager approval.                                                                                                                       | Expresses case-wide exclusions.                                                                        | Duplicated by branch choice and skeleton exclusions.                                                                                                                            |
| R9          | **Resource-aware checks**                    | Compare every bank verifier against the observed approval performer(s); compare every master-data capturer against the activator.                                                                       | Activity labels do not identify people.                                                                | Approval applicability and uniqueness remain R2 checks. Missing or unresolved identities prevent evaluation.                                                                    |
| R10         | **Log Skeleton**                             | Counts for activation, rejection, and first payment belong to `{0, 1}`.                                                                                                                                 | Explicit occurrence bounds, including permitted absence.                                               | Also enforced procedurally. `exactly_one` would incorrectly require optional outcomes.                                                                                          |
| R11         | **Stateful resource/data-aware monitor**     | For every post-activation change, require a later suspension; before any later payment require verification after the latest preceding change. Validate account/version correspondence where available. | Requires activation context, event-instance ordering, and potentially bank-version data.               | The net admits the activities but does not authorize payment after a change. A global change→verification response would overconstrain cases with no later payment.             |
| R12         | **Temporal profile**                         | For matched event instances, flag \(\lvert\Delta-\mu\rvert/\sigma>2\).                                                                                                                                  | Separates timing anomalies from forbidden control flow.                                                | Warning-only. Missing targets are obligation/coverage issues, not computable elapsed-time deviations.                                                                           |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

The core language is:

```text
sequence(
  'Receive Onboarding Request',
  parallel(
    'Capture Master Data',
    'Run Sanctions Screening',
    'Verify Bank Account'
  ),
  'Classify Risk',
  xor(
    'Reject Vendor',
    sequence(
      xor(
        'Manager Approval',
        sequence('Enhanced Due Diligence', 'Compliance Approval')
      ),
      'Activate Vendor',
      'Release First Payment'
    )
  ),
  'Close Onboarding'
)
```

This is the specified backbone, augmented below with interleaving clarification, sanctions, legal-opinion, and bank-maintenance behavior. The activity ordering of the initial checks is unrestricted; all three must finish before classification. 

**Canonical representation: an accepting Petri net.** The following self-contained model-construction code uses PM4Py’s `PetriNet`, `Marking`, and arc-construction helper. Self-loops implement read-like enabling conditions using ordinary Petri-net arcs. ([GitHub][1])

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils.petri_utils import add_arc_from_to


def build_procedural_model():
    net = PetriNet("Controlled vendor onboarding — procedural envelope")
    places = {}

    def add(name, label, inputs, outputs):
        """Create a transition; place names are space-separated strings."""
        input_names = inputs.split()
        output_names = outputs.split()

        for place_name in set(input_names + output_names):
            if place_name not in places:
                place = PetriNet.Place(place_name)
                net.places.add(place)
                places[place_name] = place

        transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
        net.transitions.add(transition)

        for place_name in input_names:
            add_arc_from_to(places[place_name], transition, net)
        for place_name in output_names:
            add_arc_from_to(transition, places[place_name], net)

    # Receipt starts the three independent checks and auxiliary components.
    add("receive", "Receive Onboarding Request",
        "start", "open pre idle c0 s0 b0")

    add("capture", "Capture Master Data", "c0", "c1")
    add("screen", "Run Sanctions Screening", "s0", "s1 screened")
    add("bank_initial", "Verify Bank Account", "b0", "b1")

    # AND-join: all initial checks must have completed.
    add("classify", "Classify Risk", "c1 s1 b1", "classified")

    # Exactly one primary branch.
    add("manager", "Manager Approval", "classified", "approved")
    add("edd", "Enhanced Due Diligence", "classified", "diligent")
    add("compliance", "Compliance Approval", "diligent", "approved")

    # Final outcomes require no outstanding clarification.
    add("reject", "Reject Vendor",
        "classified pre idle", "rejected")
    add("activate", "Activate Vendor",
        "approved pre idle", "unpaid post")

    add("pay", "Release First Payment", "unpaid", "paid")

    # Alternative transitions have the same exact visible closing label.
    add("close_reject", "Close Onboarding",
        "rejected open screened", "end")
    add("close_paid", "Close Onboarding",
        "paid post open screened", "end")

    # Repeatable, one-outstanding-request clarification component.
    add("clarify_request", "Request Clarification",
        "pre idle", "pre pending")
    add("clarify_receive", "Receive Clarification",
        "pre pending", "pre idle")

    # Escalation is enabled only after screening and before a final outcome.
    add("escalate", "Sanctions Escalation",
        "pre screened", "pre screened")

    # Legal obligations and policy-specific deadlines are checked separately.
    add("opinion", "Obtain Legal Opinion", "open", "open")

    # Changes are admitted while onboarding is open.
    # R11's conditional obligations are enforced by the external monitor.
    add("bank_change", "Change Bank Details", "open", "open")

    # 'post' means historically activated, not currently unsuspended.
    add("suspend", "Suspend Vendor", "post", "post")
    add("bank_again", "Verify Bank Account", "post", "post")

    initial_marking = Marking({places["start"]: 1})
    final_marking = Marking({places["end"]: 1})
    return net, initial_marking, final_marking


procedural_model = build_procedural_model()
```

**Behavior and scope.** Receipt creates three independent check tokens. Classification consumes their three completion tokens. The shared `classified` token makes rejection, manager approval, and due diligence mutually exclusive. Activation consumes the approved state; payment consumes the unpaid state, preventing repeated activation or first payment.

The clarification component permits other work between request and response, including classification and approval. It prevents a second request while one is pending and blocks activation/rejection until the response arrives.

Legal opinions can interleave with the main workflow, including after rejection but before closure. This permits R4 to be satisfied without inventing a legal-opinion-before-rejection rule. The net deliberately allows T6’s sequence: its acceptability belongs to the selected R5/R6 policy.

Post-activation suspension and verification are repeatable. They do not require another `Activate Vendor`, which would conflict with R10. `post` records activation history rather than the vendor’s operational suspension status. R11’s payment guard is external.

An independent finite-state replay of these arcs found 27 reachable markings, with completion reachable from every marking and all 20 transitions reachable. The procedural outcomes below were checked with that replay; execution of the PM4Py package itself is not claimed.

# 4. DECLARE Model

```python
def rules(keys):
    return {
        key: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}
        for key in keys
    }


declare_model = {
    "init": rules([
        "Receive Onboarding Request",
    ]),

    "exactly_one": rules([
        "Receive Onboarding Request",
        "Capture Master Data",
        "Run Sanctions Screening",
        "Classify Risk",
        "Close Onboarding",
    ]),

    "noncoexistence": rules([
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    ]),

    "altresponse": rules([
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
    ]),

    "altprecedence": rules([
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
    ]),

    "response": rules([
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),
    ]),

    "precedence": rules([
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    ]),
}
```

For a binary key `(A, B)`, **response** looks forward from A, whereas **precedence** looks backward from B. PM4Py’s alternate-response and alternate-precedence checks implement the intervening-occurrence boundaries described below. ([GitHub][2])

| Encoded rule or group                                         | Direction and semantics                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `init`                                                        | `Receive Onboarding Request` is the first activity.                                                                        |
| Five `exactly_one` entries                                    | Each listed activity occurs once in a completed trace. They do not constrain bank re-verification.                         |
| Five `noncoexistence` pairs                                   | Neither ordering of a listed pair is allowed; either activity may be absent.                                               |
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`   | Each request has a later response before another request. Other activities may intervene.                                  |
| `altprecedence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Each response has a prior request since the preceding response. Implements the no-orphan/no-duplicate-response assumption. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`        | Every escalation has a later opinion; one later case-wide opinion may discharge several escalations.                       |
| `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)`            | Every activation eventually reaches first payment within the completed case.                                               |
| Two responses ending in `Close Onboarding`                    | Every rejection and every first payment has a later closure.                                                               |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)`   | Every escalation has earlier screening. Screening does not require escalation.                                             |
| `precedence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval)`     | Every compliance approval has earlier due diligence.                                                                       |
| `precedence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)`          | Every first payment has earlier activation.                                                                                |

**Why no chain-response rule?** Chain response would require the target to be the immediately next visible activity. Clarification can coexist with other work, so adjacency would be an unjustified restriction.

**Why noncoexistence rather than a directional negative rule?** R1 prohibits both `Reject Vendor … Activate Vendor` and the reverse. A one-direction “A must not be followed by B” restriction is not a substitute.

**Two deliberate limitations matter.** First, the clarification DECLARE rules alone could accept the fragment:

```text
Request Clarification, Activate Vendor, Receive Clarification
```

The procedural pending-state gate supplies the missing outcome deadline. Second, unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` would incorrectly require a legal opinion in T1. The conditional R5 check therefore remains outside this dictionary.

The support/confidence values are representation metadata for manually specified rules, not empirical findings or exception probabilities. Changing confidence is not an implementation of emergency authorization.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

The skeleton contains necessary count, ordering, and exclusion checks. It is deliberately not a second complete process model.

```python
def symmetric(pairs):
    return set(pairs) | {(b, a) for a, b in pairs}


log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": symmetric({
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    }),

    "always_before": {
        ("Classify Risk", "Capture Master Data"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Verify Bank Account"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Activate Vendor"),
    },

    "always_after": {
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
    },

    "never_together": symmetric(
        set(declare_model["noncoexistence"])
    ),

    "activ_freq": {
        **{
            activity: {1}
            for activity in declare_model["exactly_one"]
        },
        **{
            activity: {0, 1}
            for activity in [
                "Enhanced Due Diligence",
                "Manager Approval",
                "Compliance Approval",
                "Reject Vendor",
                "Activate Vendor",
                "Release First Payment",
            ]
        },
    },

    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

**Pair orientation is not interchangeable across keys.** In PM4Py’s trace-skeleton representation, `always_before(A, B)` places B before A; `always_after(A, B)` places B after A. Equivalence concerns equal occurrence counts, not activity interchangeability. ([GitHub][3])

| Entry                                                        | Interpretation                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Clarification equivalence, both orientations                 | Request and response counts must match. This does not establish correct matching or order.                  |
| Activation/payment equivalence, both orientations            | Both are absent in rejected cases or both occur once in accepted completed cases.                           |
| Three `always_before` pairs starting with `Classify Risk`    | Each initial check must have an occurrence before classification. Later bank verification remains possible. |
| `always_before(Compliance Approval, Enhanced Due Diligence)` | Due diligence precedes compliance approval.                                                                 |
| `always_before(Release First Payment, Activate Vendor)`      | Activation precedes payment.                                                                                |
| `always_after(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)`       | Payment follows activation.                                                                                 |
| Two `always_after` pairs ending in `Close Onboarding`        | Closure follows rejection or payment.                                                                       |
| `never_together`                                             | Mirrors the five DECLARE exclusions; the reverse pairs make the symmetric intention explicit.               |
| Empty `directly_follows`                                     | No immediate adjacency is required across potentially interleaved auxiliary work.                           |

The constrained first activities in the ordering pairs have at most one permitted occurrence. This avoids relying on skeleton pair checks to enforce per-occurrence loop semantics.

**Important runtime detail: sparse frequency dictionaries.** The inspected PM4Py implementation reports an activity missing from `activ_freq` as a frequency deviation. Leaving repeatable activities out and calling the default checker directly would therefore create false violations. ([GitHub][4])

The following adapter supplies unrestricted counts **within the current finite evaluation input**, without inventing a normative repetition cap:

```python
from copy import deepcopy


def skeleton_for_log(log):
    """
    log: non-empty pandas DataFrame with standard PM4Py columns.
    Keep the sparse policy specification above unchanged between calls.
    """
    if log.empty:
        raise ValueError("Cannot evaluate an empty log.")

    model = deepcopy(log_skeleton)
    horizon = int(log.groupby("case:concept:name").size().max())

    known_activities = {
        transition.label
        for transition in procedural_model[0].transitions
        if transition.label is not None
    }

    # No activity can occur more often than its entire input trace length.
    # This is an evaluation bound, not a business-policy bound.
    unconstrained_counts = set(range(horizon + 1))
    for activity in known_activities:
        model["activ_freq"].setdefault(
            activity, unconstrained_counts.copy()
        )

    return model


# Use the expanded runtime model:
# skeleton_results = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(
#     log, skeleton_for_log(log)
# )
```

In particular, `Verify Bank Account` is not limited to one occurrence. Equating its count with `Capture Master Data` would also be inappropriate once R11 re-verification is allowed.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"):
        (48 * HOUR, 24 * HOUR),

    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"):
        (12 * HOUR, 6 * HOUR),

    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"):
        (72 * HOUR, 36 * HOUR),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

For a matched pair,

$$
z=\frac{|\Delta-\mu|}{\sigma},
\qquad
\text{warning}\iff z>2.
$$

The supplied parameters produce these inclusive non-warning intervals. They are profile tolerances, not minimum processing times or independently justified service-level deadlines. 

| Pair                             | Non-warning elapsed-time interval |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------: |
| Clarification request → response |                        0–96 hours |
| Escalation → legal opinion       |                        0–24 hours |
| Activation → first payment       |                       0–144 hours |

The upper and lower comparisons are strict outside the interval in the inspected PM4Py implementation. It also examines eventually-following event pairs, which requires care with repeated activities. ([GitHub][5])

**Match obligations before timing loops.** In production, pair each clarification request with its own response, and each escalation with the opinion discharging it. Do not compare an early request with responses to later requests. That can manufacture late-response warnings despite every individual cycle being timely.

The matching layer can construct a separate two-event case for each matched pair before calling:

```python
# For T1–T8, each relevant pair occurs at most once:
# temporal_results = pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(
#     log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta
# )
```

A missing response or payment is not an elapsed time of zero. It is an outstanding or violated obligation handled elsewhere. For an open case, an overdue unmatched obligation can trigger a separately identified ageing warning.

**R12 and R6 are different.** A 24-hour escalation-to-opinion profile limit is warning-only. Under amended policy E, the 24-hour activation-to-opinion emergency deadline is a hard condition of the exception. Identical numerical durations do not make the two rules interchangeable.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

**Required attributes and missing-data treatment**

| Check                                | Required information                                                                                                                                        | Missing-data treatment                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ordering and case completion         | Case identifier, activity, reliable sequence or timestamps, completeness/closure status                                                                     | `not evaluated` for affected checks; do not fabricate ordering for tied or unreliable events.                                      |
| R9 bank verification versus approval | Canonical person identity for every verification and approval event                                                                                         | Missing/unresolved identity gives `not evaluated`; it is neither proof of difference nor proof of sameness.                        |
| R9 master data versus activation     | Canonical person identities for capture and activation                                                                                                      | Same treatment.                                                                                                                    |
| R5 strict policy                     | Ordered escalation, opinion, and activation events; opinion scope under non-case-wide semantics                                                             | Evaluate activity ordering under the stated case-wide assumption; otherwise missing scope prevents certification of discharge.     |
| R6 amended emergency policy          | Emergency designation; authorization identifier; valid joint signers and required roles; authorization time; case/activation/escalation scope; opinion time | Missing authorization gives `not evaluated`. Verified invalid authorization or a missed deadline gives `fail`.                     |
| R11 ordering                         | Activation, bank changes, suspension, verification, and payment events in reliable order                                                                    | Unknown ordering gives `not evaluated`. A known payment without intervening verification gives `fail`.                             |
| R11 account correctness              | Bank-account/version identifiers and verification result                                                                                                    | Ordering can still be evaluated; correctness for the changed account/version remains `not evaluated` when identifiers are missing. |
| Risk-to-route consistency            | Immutable classification result at `Classify Risk`                                                                                                          | `not evaluated` when absent; never infer the classification from the selected approval route.                                      |

**Segregation of duties**

This executable-style function checks the two R9 obligations. It checks all recorded candidate approvals; R2 separately determines whether their presence and number are permissible.

```python
import pandas as pd


def sod_findings(case):
    """Return R9 findings for one case DataFrame."""
    activity_key = "concept:name"
    resource_key = "org:resource"

    activity_pairs = [
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"),
    ]

    def known_person(value):
        if value is None or pd.isna(value):
            return False
        return str(value).strip().upper() not in {
            "", "UNKNOWN", "UNKNOWN_RESOURCE"
        }

    findings = []

    for source, target in activity_pairs:
        left = case[case[activity_key].eq(source)]
        right = case[case[activity_key].eq(target)]

        # Absent optional targets do not instantiate this comparison.
        # Missing mandatory activities remain procedural/DECLARE findings.
        for _, first in left.iterrows():
            for _, second in right.iterrows():
                person_1 = first.get(resource_key)
                person_2 = second.get(resource_key)

                if not known_person(person_1) or not known_person(person_2):
                    findings.append({
                        "rule": "R9",
                        "status": "not evaluated",
                        "pair": (source, target),
                        "reason": "Missing or unresolved person identity",
                    })
                elif person_1 == person_2:
                    findings.append({
                        "rule": "R9",
                        "status": "fail",
                        "pair": (source, target),
                        "reason": f"Same person: {person_1}",
                    })

    return findings
```

An empty findings list means no issue was found among instantiated comparisons. It does not prove that all mandatory activities occurred.

**Policy and post-activation monitor**

The following precise pseudocode makes the selected policy and the unknown-evidence state explicit:

```text
POLICY_MODE := "strict"              # implemented version
pending_legal := empty set
emergency_deadlines := empty map
pending_suspension := empty set
activated := false
latest_post_activation_change := none
latest_matching_reverification := none

Process events in reliable occurrence order.

On Sanctions Escalation:
    Add this escalation to pending_legal.

On Obtain Legal Opinion:
    covered := pending escalations covered by this opinion.
               Under the test assumption, covered = all pending_legal.

    For each covered escalation with an emergency deadline:
        If opinion_time > deadline:
            emit fail(R6, deadline, opinion event)

    Remove covered escalations from pending_legal and deadline tracking.
    An opinion never clears escalations that occur later.

On Activate Vendor:
    If pending_legal is nonempty:
        If POLICY_MODE == "strict":
            emit fail(R5, activation event, outstanding escalations)

        Else if POLICY_MODE == "emergency_amended":
            authorization := verify external authorization for:
                this case and activation;
                the outstanding escalation scope;
                the required joint authorizers;
                authorization effective no later than activation.

            If authorization is verified invalid:
                emit fail(R6, activation event)
            Else if authorization cannot be established:
                emit not evaluated(R6, missing authorization evidence)

            For each pending escalation:
                emergency_deadlines[escalation] :=
                    activation_time + 24 elapsed hours

    activated := true

On Change Bank Details:
    If activated:
        Add this change to pending_suspension.
        latest_post_activation_change := this change.
        latest_matching_reverification := none.

On Verify Bank Account:
    If a post-activation change is outstanding for payment purposes:
        If this verification follows the latest change:
            Record the ordering witness.
            If account/version/result attributes prove a matching success:
                latest_matching_reverification := this event.
            Otherwise:
                retain "account/version correctness not evaluated".

On Suspend Vendor:
    Discharge earlier changes covered by this suspension.
    Under the case-wide assumption, clear pending_suspension.

On Release First Payment:
    If a post-activation change occurred earlier:
        If there is no verification strictly between the latest change
        and this payment:
            emit fail(R11, payment event)
        Else if account/version/result correctness is unknown:
            emit not evaluated(R11 account/version correctness)
        Else if verification concerns the wrong version/account:
            emit fail(R11 account/version correctness)

At an emergency deadline:
    If the relevant opinion is still outstanding after the deadline:
        emit fail(R6), even if no new event has arrived.

On Close Onboarding:
    If pending_legal is nonempty:
        emit fail(R4).
    If pending_suspension is nonempty:
        emit fail(R11).
```

A later opinion clears the outstanding obligation but does **not** erase an earlier R5 violation or missed R6 deadline.

For R11, a change after the only first payment still requires suspension, but does not create a requirement for verification before a nonexistent later payment. Also, the literal requirement permits `Change Bank Details → Verify Bank Account → Release First Payment → Suspend Vendor`; forbidding that sequence would require an additional suspension-before-payment rule.

**Risk guard**

```text
For an accepted case:
    Read the classification result as recorded at Classify Risk.
    If missing:
        report risk-to-route consistency = not evaluated.
    If low:
        require the manager-approval route.
    If high:
        require due diligence followed by compliance approval.
    If another classification value occurs:
        require an explicit policy mapping; do not invent one.
```

The matrix below assesses the observable R1–R12 checks. The absent classification attribute and account-version attributes are reported as coverage limitations, not silently reconstructed. Thus an overall `pass` means conformance within that stated observation boundary, not unrestricted certification of decision correctness.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement is useful only with shared rule identities.** R1 appears in the net, DECLARE, and skeleton. R3 appears as a procedural state gate, alternate constraints, and count equality. These are independent checks of overlapping obligations, not three independent incidents. T5 should produce one primary unanswered-clarification finding with supporting diagnostics.

**Several tempting encodings create contradictions or unjustified restrictions.** Setting both approval activities to `exactly_one` would force the co-occurrence R2 and R8 prohibit. Setting bank verification frequency to `{1}`, or equating its count with master-data capture, would reject legitimate R11 re-verification. Requiring clarification-response adjacency would reject interleaved work. Unconditional legal-opinion-before-activation would reject un-escalated accepted cases.

**Count equality does not establish correct loop behavior.** Two requests followed by two responses satisfy clarification count equality but violate the one-outstanding-request policy. Conversely, a live prefix with one pending request naturally has unequal counts; completed-trace skeleton semantics must not be applied as an immediate live-case failure.

**Declarative vacuity is not positive evidence.** Escalation→opinion response passes when no escalation occurs. Approval exclusions pass when both approvals are absent. Such outcomes do not prove that screening occurred or that an accepted case was approved; the backbone and existence/count constraints supply those obligations. A rejected case’s lack of temporal deviations similarly says nothing about response speed.

**The R5/R6 distinction survives favorable timing.** T6’s opinion is timely under both numerical windows but occurs after activation. Temporal regularity cannot establish strict precedence or external permission. Conversely, T7 is structurally legal under S despite its temporal warning.

**R11 leaves an operational-state gap.** The specification does not define when suspension is lifted or how a payment service recognizes restored eligibility. Adding another `Activate Vendor` would collide with R10. Deployment therefore needs an explicit operational-state contract, potentially outside onboarding, rather than an invented visible activity or an implicit reactivation.

**Missing attributes limit certification.** Resource account names may not identify individual people; classification results may be mutable; opinions may cover only selected escalations; a verification may concern an obsolete bank version. The tests do not supply evidence to resolve those possibilities. The declared assumptions make the example evaluation possible, but must remain visible in production.

**Timestamps do not prove concurrency or causality.** The model permits independent checks to overlap, but completion order alone does not show that they actually ran concurrently. Equal timestamps may also conceal ordering relevant to “before,” and a recording timestamp may differ from the business-effective authorization time.

**Support/confidence metadata is not normative authority.** The dictionary values reflect the selected policy representation. They are neither estimates learned from these eight cases nor proof of universally compliant behavior. A discovered pattern with high support could reproduce a systematic violation; a rare deviation could be an authorized exception.

**Test coverage is incomplete.** None of T1–T8 exercises a bank-change cycle, multiple clarification cycles, or multiple scoped escalations. The matrix therefore does not establish implementation coverage of those mechanisms.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Use the **accepting Petri net as the canonical procedural representation**, together with a **versioned policy registry** that records each rule’s authoritative checker, scope, severity, assumptions, and policy owner. The registry selects S or the formally amended E; analysts must not choose whichever version makes a trace pass.

Run checks in this order:

| Stage                | Action                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Evidence validation  | Validate labels, case boundaries, ordering, timestamp semantics, identity resolution, and required attributes. Preserve unknowns rather than fabricating values.                              |
| Policy selection     | Resolve the effective policy version and retrieve any applicable external authorization. Missing critical evidence must remain visible.                                                       |
| Hard-rule evaluation | Run procedural conformance and the stateful policy/resource monitors, then DECLARE and skeleton backstops. Continue collecting diagnostics after a failure rather than hiding later problems. |
| Timing evaluation    | Match event instances and calculate temporal-profile warnings separately from hard failures.                                                                                                  |
| Consolidation        | Deduplicate by requirement and triggering event; report the earliest decisive violation, subsequent findings, and evaluation coverage.                                                        |

Hard conformance is an **intersection of applicable obligations**, not an average fitness score. A known authoritative failure yields `fail`; unresolved critical evidence prevents a complete `pass`; temporal deviations alone yield `warning`.

For example, the user-facing T6 finding under S should state:

> **R5 failure at hour 13:** activation occurred while the escalation raised at hour 6 was unresolved. The opinion at hour 23 satisfies R4 but does not repair the earlier precedence violation. No emergency exception is enabled in policy S.

Under E, the same finding should instead distinguish verified timing from missing authorization:

> **R6 not evaluated:** the opinion arrived within 24 hours of activation, but the required joint authorization could not be established.

For live enforcement, gate activation on clarification completion, applicable approval, and the selected legal/emergency policy. Gate payment on the R11 verification condition and any separately approved operational suspension-state rule. Missing critical authorization can justify holding an action while remaining an **evidence gap**, not a claimed historical violation.

Before release, pin the PM4Py version and regression-test the dictionaries, pair orientation, repeated-label handling, and runtime frequency expansion. Add explicitly synthetic tests for interleaved clarification cycles, opinions predating later escalations, wrong-version bank verification, changes after first payment, and changes followed by payment before suspension. Those tests should distinguish what the present specification permits from additional controls that stakeholders may wish to adopt.
