## 2. Competing explanations and timeline
Jan–Mar show stable metrics, mature labels, and steady variant mix; no material drift. April is a schema/pipeline incident: the renamed diagnosis activity produced malformed prefixes, UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY labels, and missing derived features, directly explaining the AUROC/ECE collapse and elevated predicted risk. May is a partial recovery after the mapping patch; residual feature gaps remain but the core pipeline is restored. June reflects a genuine process-mix change (remote-routing policy) that increases V2 share without altering conditional breach rates within any variant; this is process drift, not concept drift. July–August introduce supplier-driven part delays that lengthen V3 paths and raise V3 breach rates while V1/V2 remain stable; this is overlapping process drift and concept drift. September adds a population shift (Partner Z) whose higher rural/device-age profile and V3 preference further elevate observed breach rates. October is dominated by label censoring: the outcome-API backlog leaves only 55 % maturity, disproportionately affecting longer V3 paths, so the low observed breach rate cannot be compared with prior months. The four highlighted periods therefore reflect distinct, non-homogeneous mechanisms.

## 3. Diagnostic tests
- Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting: plot cumulative label availability by case-start month; backtest on cases whose labels were mature at each historical cutoff. Expected under label delay: Oct observed breach rate rises toward 0.28–0.31 once fully mature; under concept drift: rate stays elevated after maturity correction.
- Recompute after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning: rebuild April prefixes with the May mapping and remove duplicates. Expected under data-pipeline failure: April AUROC recovers to ~0.80 and ECE falls below 0.05; under concept drift: metrics remain degraded.
- Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month: compute AUROC/ECE per V1/V2/V3 and per Partner Z vs others. Expected under concept drift: V3 ECE rises sharply Aug–Sep while V1/V2 stay stable; under population shift: Partner Z shows miscalibration only after Sep.
- Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges: extract monthly DFG and performance DFG on Order Part  Part Received. Expected under process drift: median elapsed time doubles Aug onward and correlates with V3 breach increase; under concept drift: elapsed-time change is small yet breach probability conditional on elapsed time rises.
- Test whether outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed: fit logistic regression of breach on path features within each variant-month. Expected under concept drift: coefficient on part-delay increases significantly Aug–Sep; under process drift: coefficient stays constant but path distribution shifts.
- Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z: reweight Sep–Oct cases to Jan–Aug covariate distribution excluding Partner Z. Expected under population shift: reweighted breach rate matches pre-Sep levels; under concept drift: gap persists.
- Feature missingness and lineage audits: trace “events before diagnosis” and queue features for April–May. Expected under data-pipeline failure: April missingness >10 % and backfill incomplete; under other drifts: missingness remains <3 %.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision
Immediate containment (next 2 weeks): freeze model, suppress alerts on April/Oct, and report only Jan–Mar, May–Jul metrics. Short-term recalibration (2–6 weeks): apply isotonic or Platt scaling on May–Jul data stratified by variant after confirming pipeline normalization; do not touch April or Oct. Feature repair (next release): add rural distance, device age, and supplier-delay features; recompute April with normalized labels. Full retraining only after (a) all labels through August reach 95 % maturity, (b) pipeline audit confirms zero missing derived features, and (c) stratified ECE <0.04 on Jul–Aug. Safe training months: Jan–Mar, May–Jul. Unsafe: April (pipeline), Aug–Oct (immature labels + population shift). A variant-specific model (or at least variant-stratified calibration) is justified because V3 breach dynamics have diverged while V1/V2 remain stable.

## 5. Monitoring architecture
Predictive monitor (AUROC/ECE by variant), calibration monitor (reliability diagrams monthly), label-maturity monitor (maturity % and median delay by start month), data-quality monitor (unknown-activity share, missing-feature %), process-variant monitor (V1/V2/V3 %), performance-DFG monitor (median edge durations), population monitor (Partner Z share, rural/device-age means), intervention/deployment monitor (policy or supplier changes). Alert dependencies: label-maturity alarm blocks predictive and calibration alerts; data-quality alarm blocks retraining; only performance-DFG or concept-drift alarms after maturity confirmation may trigger recalibration.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis
Retraining on immature October labels will embed the artificially low breach rate and produce over-optimistic future predictions; mitigation: enforce 95 % maturity gate before any training cohort is accepted. Calibrating around the April pipeline bug will bake in the inflated predicted risk and UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY noise; mitigation: require explicit pipeline-audit sign-off and normalized-label recomputation before any recalibration. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift in V3 will leave the model miscalibrated for parts-dependent cases; mitigation: run variant-stratified calibration drift tests monthly and trigger targeted recalibration only on V3. Attributing Partner Z’s higher breach rate to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence risks unnecessary fairness interventions or model rollback; mitigation: publish Partner Z vs non-Partner Z metrics and missing-feature impact before any fairness review.
