# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## 1.1 Material assumptions

| ID | Assumption | Consequence for the model |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | One case = one onboarding request. Full conformance verdicts are only issued for **completed** cases, meaning cases containing `Close Onboarding`. Open cases are checked only for safety rules (R1, R5, R7, R8, R9, R10). | Liveness rules such as response and `always_after` are not evaluated on open cases. |
| A2 | "Final outcome" means `Reject Vendor` or `Activate Vendor`. Approvals are decisions, but not final outcomes. An **accepted** case contains `Activate Vendor`. A **rejected** case contains `Reject Vendor`. | R3 is anchored on Reject/Activate. Under this reading, an approval issued while a clarification is still open is not by itself an R3 violation. |
| A3 | R2 and R8 use "should", but I treat them as hard constraints because the backbone's exclusive choice already entails them. R12 is explicitly soft. | R2 and R8 produce `fail`. R12 produces `warning`. |
| A4 | Policy allows clarification cycles anywhere between receipt and the final outcome. The procedural backbone deliberately places them only in the pre-classification phase, in parallel with the three checks. | The procedural model is stricter than R3 and is disclosed as such. DECLARE is authoritative for R3. |
| A5 | `Sanctions Escalation` belongs to the high-risk route. It happens after classification and in parallel with EDD, and an escalated vendor cannot use the low-risk route. This is a **procedural** modelling decision only. I do not encode it declaratively because the requirements do not state it. | T6 and T7 fit this assumption. An escalation on the low-risk route fails only the procedural layer. |
| A6 | `Obtain Legal Opinion` happens only in response to a `Sanctions Escalation`. | `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` |
| A7 | The "applicable approval" in R9 is whichever of Manager/Compliance Approval occurred. R9 is checked against **every** `Verify Bank Account` occurrence, including re-verifications. This is the conservative choice. | The segregation-of-duties (SoD) check compares the set of verifiers with the set of approvers. |
| A8 | Within an onboarding case, `Change Bank Details` only happens after activation, which is when the vendor becomes a live master record. A bank-detail correction before activation is handled by re-capture/re-verification, not by this activity. The onboarding case ends at `Close Onboarding`. Events after that belong to a vendor-lifecycle case. | `precedence(Activate Vendor, Change Bank Details)`. R11 is only relevant between activation and first payment. |
| A9 | The log has no reinstatement activity after `Suspend Vendor`, and `Activate Vendor` is capped at one occurrence (R10). | R11 can force "payment to a suspended vendor". This gap is surfaced, not hidden (section 9). |
| A10 | Timestamps are completion times at hour granularity. There are no start/complete lifecycle pairs. Equal timestamps give an ambiguous order. | Ties are flagged. Checks that depend on ordering fail closed on ties where sanctions are involved. |
| A11 | R6's 24-hour window is measured from `Activate Vendor`, the emergency act. The alternative reading measures it from `Sanctions Escalation`. The window is configurable. | In T6 both readings hold (10 h after activation, 17 h after escalation), so the verdict does not change. |
| A12 | Resource names are stable personal identities. `Vendor` and system/batch accounts are not personal identities and cannot satisfy or violate SoD. | If an SoD check involves them, it returns `not evaluated`. |
| A13 | The log records no risk class, no screening hit/no-hit result, no emergency flag, and no authorization identifier. | The route is inferred from activities alone. The emergency condition is unobservable in the log. |
| A14 | Occurrence bounds for repeatable activities are operational expectations, not stated norms: at most 5 clarification cycles, at most 1 bank change, at most 2 bank verifications. | If a Log Skeleton frequency violation concerns only these bounds, it is triaged as a warning. |

## 1.2 The R5/R6 conflict

R5 is unconditional: after an escalation, the legal opinion precedes activation "under all circumstances". R6 allows activation before the opinion when there is joint authorization, followed by an opinion within 24 hours. The two cannot both be implemented as stated, because a T6-shaped trace (escalation, then activation, then opinion) must fail under R5 and may pass under R6. The event log also cannot tell an authorized emergency apart from an ordinary violation, because the only discriminating facts (the emergency and the joint authorization) sit outside the log.

Possible resolutions:

1. **Option A: Legal prevails (strict precedence). This is the implemented option.** Any activation that follows an escalation without a prior legal opinion is a hard violation. Emergencies are handled by a separate *waiver workflow*: a reviewer may disposition the finding as "waived, emergency authorization #…". The conformance verdict still stays `fail`, and the waiver is recorded outside the model.
2. **Option B: Evidence-gated operations exception.** Activation before the opinion passes only if **all** of the following hold:
   - an external authorization register, joined by case ID, shows at least two distinct authorizers covering the required roles;
   - the authorization is timestamped no later than the activation;
   - the opinion arrives within 24 h;
   - no first payment is released before the opinion. This safeguard is my addition.

   If the register is unavailable, the verdict is `policy-dependent`. If it is available but has no record, the verdict is `fail`.
3. **Option C: Adjudication only.** Every activation-before-opinion is reported as `policy-dependent` and goes to a human, with no automatic verdict. This is honest, but it does not scale, and it creates no deterrent.
4. **Rejected option: time window only.** This would accept any opinion that arrives within 24 h of activation. It silently merges the two policies: it drops R6's joint-authorization precondition and turns an exception into the default. It also makes R5's "under all circumstances" meaningless. I list it only so that it is explicitly excluded.

**Choice: Option A** for the implemented model. The reasons are:

- R5 explicitly rules out exceptions.
- Sanctions exposure is a legal/regulatory norm and ranks above operational convenience in the norm hierarchy.
- R6's precondition is unverifiable from the log. Accepting R6 automatically would mean accepting a claim nobody can check.

**Compliance risk of the choice:**

- Option A produces **false positives** for emergency activations that were genuinely and legitimately authorized. That leads to alert fatigue, friction with Operations, and a model that contradicts documented operational practice.
- If waivers are granted loosely, the waiver workflow becomes a shadow exception process. So waivers must be counted, reported, and audited.
- The opposite risk would come from Option B: activating, for up to 24 h, a vendor who later turns out to be a sanctions match. B also depends on the integrity of an external register. Under Option A, this risk is kept structurally low.

Option B is kept as a **fully specified, switchable variant** (`policy="operations_exception"`) and is never mixed with A. Only three artefacts differ between the options:

| Artefact | Option A (implemented) | Option B (variant) |
|---|---|---|
| Procedural tree | The escalation branch runs in parallel with EDD→Compliance Approval, and **both** must finish before `Activate Vendor`. | The escalation branch runs in parallel with EDD→Compliance Approval→Activate Vendor, and must finish before `Release First Payment`. |
| DECLARE | `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | That rule is replaced by `nonsuccession(Release First Payment, Obtain Legal Opinion)`. |
| Resource/data layer | `check_opinion_before_activation` | `check_emergency_exception`, which uses the external register and the 24-hour window. |

The Log Skeleton and the temporal profile are identical under both options, because neither can express R5 or R6.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description (process tree, Option A)

Operators: `→` sequence, `×` exclusive choice, `∧` parallel, `↺(do, redo)` loop.

```
→( Receive Onboarding Request,
   ∧( Capture Master Data,
      Run Sanctions Screening,
      Verify Bank Account,
      ↺( τ, →(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) ) ),
   Classify Risk,
   ×( Reject Vendor,
      →( ×( Manager Approval,
            ∧( →(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval),
               ×( τ, →(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) ) ) ),
         Activate Vendor,
         Release First Payment ) ),
   Close Onboarding )
```

Option B variant, which is not implemented: the accepted branch becomes

```
→( ×( →(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor),
      ∧( →(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor),
         ×( τ, →(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) ) ) ),
   Release First Payment )
```

This variant duplicates the `Activate Vendor` label. PM4Py process trees allow duplicate labels.

## 3.2 PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def _leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)


def _tau():
    return ProcessTree(label=None)


def _op(operator, *children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
        node.children.append(child)
    return node


SEQ, XOR, AND, LOOP = Operator.SEQUENCE, Operator.XOR, Operator.PARALLEL, Operator.LOOP


def build_procedural_model(policy="legal_prevails"):
    clarification_loop = _op(
        LOOP,
        _tau(),
        _op(SEQ, _leaf("Request Clarification"), _leaf("Receive Clarification")),
    )
    checks = _op(
        AND,
        _leaf("Capture Master Data"),
        _leaf("Run Sanctions Screening"),
        _leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
        clarification_loop,
    )
    optional_escalation = _op(
        XOR,
        _tau(),
        _op(SEQ, _leaf("Sanctions Escalation"), _leaf("Obtain Legal Opinion")),
    )

    if policy == "legal_prevails":
        high_risk = _op(
            AND,
            _op(SEQ, _leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"), _leaf("Compliance Approval")),
            optional_escalation,
        )
        accepted = _op(
            SEQ,
            _op(XOR, _leaf("Manager Approval"), high_risk),
            _leaf("Activate Vendor"),
            _leaf("Release First Payment"),
        )
    elif policy == "operations_exception":
        low_risk = _op(SEQ, _leaf("Manager Approval"), _leaf("Activate Vendor"))
        high_risk = _op(
            AND,
            _op(SEQ, _leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"), _leaf("Compliance Approval"),
                _leaf("Activate Vendor")),
            optional_escalation,
        )
        accepted = _op(SEQ, _op(XOR, low_risk, high_risk), _leaf("Release First Payment"))
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"unknown policy: {policy}")

    outcome = _op(XOR, _leaf("Reject Vendor"), accepted)
    return _op(
        SEQ,
        _leaf("Receive Onboarding Request"),
        checks,
        _leaf("Classify Risk"),
        outcome,
        _leaf("Close Onboarding"),
    )


procedural_model = build_procedural_model("legal_prevails")

# Accepting Petri net for alignment-based checking:
# net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(procedural_model)
# alignments = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
```

## 3.3 Explanation

- **Concurrency.** The three checks form an AND-block after receipt, and `Classify Risk` synchronizes on all of them, which is the AND-join required by backbone step 3. Any interleaving fits: T2's order is Screening, Verify, Capture, and T3's is Capture, Verify, Screening.
- **Clarification loops.** The loop `↺(τ, →(RqC, RcC))` is a fourth parallel branch of the pre-classification block. In PM4Py, `↺(do, redo)` runs *do*, then zero or more times *(redo, do)*. With do = τ, this gives zero or more complete request/answer pairs, strictly alternating. That is why a request can never be followed by another request before its answer. The block must finish before classification.
  - This is **stricter than R3** (A4). A clarification requested during EDD would be procedurally non-fitting even though R3 allows it. I accept that disagreement because DECLARE is authoritative for R3.
  - In T5 the alignment's first deviation therefore appears at `Classify Risk@5`, even though R3's decisive violation happens later, at activation.
- **Choices.** An outer XOR chooses between rejection and acceptance. The accepted branch has an inner XOR between the low-risk route (`Manager Approval`) and the high-risk route (EDD → Compliance Approval). Exactly one approval is therefore produced on accepted paths and none on the reject path, which enforces R2 and R8 structurally.
- **Escalation (Option A).** The optional escalation→opinion sequence runs in parallel with EDD→Compliance Approval, and both must finish before `Activate Vendor`. This expresses R4, R5, and R7 exactly on the high-risk route and does not force the opinion before Compliance Approval: T7 interleaves Escalation@6, EDD@8, Opinion@40, CA@42.
  - The tree has no escalation on the low-risk or reject routes (A5). This is a conservative approximation. For example, "escalate, then reject" is non-fitting. The declarative layers do not impose this.
  - The Option B tree lets the opinion come after activation, but still before first payment. It **over-generalizes**, because it also allows an escalation after activation. The data layer guards against this: an escalation at or after activation gives `not evaluated` and a referral to sanctions case management.
- **Terminal outcomes.** Both branches end in `Close Onboarding`: after `Reject Vendor`, or after `Activate Vendor → Release First Payment`. Activation, rejection, and release are not inside any loop, so the tree allows each at most once (R10), and it cannot produce T8's activate-then-reject.
- **Post-activation behaviour deliberately excluded.** `Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` are not in the tree.
  1. They are not part of the stated stable backbone.
  2. With no reinstatement activity and `Activate Vendor` capped at one (R10), any positive procedural path Change → Suspend → Verify → Release would *model paying a suspended vendor as normal behaviour*. I refuse to bake that into the reference model.
  3. The trace-level obligations of R11 are handled declaratively and in the data layer.

  Consequence: a trace containing these activities is procedurally non-fitting. The aggregator reports this as "outside backbone scope" rather than as a policy violation. None of T1–T8 contains them.
- **Block-structure approximations.** The tree is sound by construction. The approximations are disclosed above: clarification placement, escalation only on the high-risk route, and, for Option B, escalation allowed after activation plus a duplicated `Activate Vendor` label.

## 4.1 Rule semantics and direction

In the table, "A" is the first activity of the pair and "B" is the second.

| Template | Rule (A, B) | Semantics (direction) | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| init | Receive Onboarding Request | The first event of every trace is this activity. | Backbone 1 |
| exactly_one | Receive, Capture, Screening, Classify, Close | Occurs exactly once. | Backbone 1–5 |
| existence | Verify Bank Account | Occurs at least once. It is not exactly_one, because R11 allows re-verification. | Backbone 2, R11 |
| precedence | (Capture/Screening/Verify, Classify Risk) | Every B is preceded by some A. This constrains the **past** of B. It does not require A to be followed by B. | Backbone 3 |
| precedence | (Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation) | An escalation needs an earlier screening. | R7 |
| precedence | (Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) | A legal opinion needs an earlier escalation. | A6 |
| precedence | (Classify Risk, Reject / MA / EDD / Activate) | Outcomes and routes need an earlier classification. | Backbone 4 |
| precedence | (Activate Vendor, Change Bank Details) | A bank change needs an earlier activation. | R11 scope (A8) |
| response | (Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) | Every A is **eventually followed** by B. This constrains the **future** of A. | R4 |
| response | (Manager Approval / Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor) | An approval must lead to activation. | R2, backbone 5 |
| response | (Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor) | Every bank change is eventually followed by a suspension. | R11(a) |
| succession | (EDD, Compliance Approval) | response + precedence. Each EDD is followed by CA, and each CA is preceded by EDD. | Backbone 4 (high-risk) |
| succession | (Activate Vendor, Release First Payment) | Each activation is followed by a payment, and each payment is preceded by an activation. | Backbone 5 |
| altresponse | (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | After each A, B occurs **before the next A**. It is not required *immediately*: other events may come in between. | R3 |
| altprecedence | (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | Each B is preceded by an A with **no other B in between**. This rules out unsolicited or duplicate answers. Together with altresponse it equals altsuccession. | R3 |
| chainresponse | (Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding) and (Release First Payment, Close Onboarding) | Each A is **immediately** followed by B. This is the difference from alternate response, which only forbids another A in between. | Backbone 5, A8 |
| noncoexistence | six pairs | A and B never occur in the same trace, **in either order**. | R1, R2, R8 |
| nonsuccession | (Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion) | No B occurs anywhere **after** an A. B before A is allowed. Combined with response(Esc, Opinion), this places the opinion before activation. | R5 (Option A) |
| nonsuccession | (Activate / Reject, Request / Receive Clarification) | No clarification event after a final outcome. Combined with altresponse, every request is answered before the outcome. | R3 |

**Key distinctions:**

- `response(Esc, Opinion)` is a future obligation on escalations. `precedence(Esc, Opinion)` is a past requirement on opinions. I need both, because they rule out different traces: an escalation without an opinion, and an opinion without an escalation.
- `altresponse` tolerates intervening events but not an intervening second request. `chainresponse` tolerates no intervening event at all. That is why chainresponse is used only where immediacy is part of the backbone (the closing step).
- `noncoexistence(Reject, Activate)` forbids both orders, which is what R1 requires. `nonsuccession(Activate, Reject)` would still allow "reject, then activate", so it is not used for R1. Conversely, `nonsuccession(Activate, Opinion)` is the correct choice for R5, because an opinion *before* activation is required, not forbidden.

## 4.2 Declarative limitations

- R10 cannot be stated: PM4Py has no at-most-one template.
- R2's disjunctive "at least one approval before activation" cannot be stated.
- R6 and R12 cannot be stated: there is no metric time.
- R9 cannot be stated: DECLARE has no resource dimension.
- R11(b), "Verify between Change and Release", cannot be stated.
- All of these are delegated to other layers (section 2).

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

Orientation convention, consistent with PM4Py:

- `always_before (A, B)`: if A occurs, then B occurred before A.
- `always_after (A, B)`: if A occurs, then B occurs after A.
- `equivalence (A, B)`: if A occurs, then B occurs the same number of times.
- `never_together (A, B)`: A and B never occur in the same case. The relation is symmetric, and one orientation is listed.
- `directly_follows (A, B)`: every occurrence of A is immediately followed by B.

## 5.1 Overlap and disagreement with the other layers

- **R10 is authoritative here.** `activ_freq` {0, 1} for Activate, Reject, and Release is the only place where "at most once" is stated directly.
- **R1, R2, and R8.** `never_together` duplicates DECLARE `noncoexistence` exactly. Orientation is irrelevant because both relations are symmetric.
- **R3 is weaker here.**
  - `equivalence` in both orientations gives equal counts of requests and answers.
  - `always_before(Receive, Request)` says an answer needs an earlier request.
  - `always_after(Request, Receive)` says a request needs a later answer.
  - None of these sees alternation: RqC RqC RcC RcC passes the Skeleton but fails DECLARE `altresponse`. The Skeleton also does not bind the answer to come before the final outcome. **DECLARE is authoritative.**
- **R4.** `always_after(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` together with the count equivalence is *stronger* than R4: two escalations would need two opinions, whereas DECLARE `response` accepts one opinion after both. This is a deliberate disagreement. I treat each escalation as needing its own opinion, and the Skeleton's stricter reading is the one I prefer.
- **R5 and R6 are not expressible here.** The Skeleton has no conditional ordering. `always_before(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` would require an opinion in *every* activated case, which is wrong for T1, T2, T4, and T5. The Skeleton is therefore identical under both policy options and **passes T6**. That is an expressiveness gap, not an exoneration.
- **R7.** `always_before(Sanctions Escalation, Run Sanctions Screening)` duplicates DECLARE `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)`. Note that the pair is written in the opposite order: the Skeleton lists (trigger, required-earlier), while DECLARE lists (earlier, later).
- **R11.** `always_after(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` duplicates DECLARE `response`. `always_before(Change Bank Details, Activate Vendor)` duplicates DECLARE `precedence(Activate Vendor, Change Bank Details)`. The re-verification part is not expressible: `always_after(Change, Verify)` would wrongly demand a verification even when no later payment exists. I omitted it, and the data layer is authoritative.
- **Deliberately omitted.** There is no Skeleton rule for "activation requires an approval", because that is a disjunction. `directly_follows` is limited to the two closing steps. For example, `(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` holds in all eight tests, but DECLARE allows a clarification between the two, so encoding it would create an unnecessary conflict.
- **Arbitrary bounds (A14).** Clarifications ≤ 5, Verify ≤ 2, Change ≤ 1. The procedural loop and DECLARE are unbounded, so a sixth clarification cycle fails only the Skeleton. The aggregator downgrades violations of these specific bounds to warnings, because no requirement limits the number of cycles.
- **Completeness.** `always_after` and `equivalence(Activate, Release)` presuppose completed cases (A1). On open cases they would produce false failures.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800.0, 86400.0),   # 48 h, 24 h
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200.0, 21600.0),      # 12 h, 6 h
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200.0, 129600.0),        # 72 h, 36 h
}
zeta = 2.0

# Usage: pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta)
```

The acceptance band is mean ± zeta·σ:

| Pair | Band (hours) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Request → Receive Clarification | [0, 96] | Only waits over 96 h are flagged. The lower bound is 0, so nothing can be "too fast". |
| Escalation → Legal Opinion | [0, 24] | T7 (34 h, z ≈ 3.67) is flagged. T6 (17 h, z ≈ 0.83) is not. |
| Activate → Release First Payment | [0, 144] | T7 (2 h, z ≈ −1.94) is **not** flagged, although it is only just inside the band. |

**Why temporal deviations are warnings, not control-flow violations:**

1. R12 itself says the expectations should be flagged but not trigger automatic rejection. They are descriptive statistics of normal practice, not obligations.
2. Under a ±2σ band, even a perfectly compliant, normally distributed population would be flagged about 5 % of the time. A hard failure would therefore punish compliant cases by construction.
3. Some of the durations are controlled by third parties. The clarification wait is driven by the vendor and the legal-opinion time by Legal. The durations also ignore calendars: there are no business hours, weekends, or holidays.
4. Because σ = mean/2 for all three pairs, the lower bound is 0. The profile cannot detect suspiciously *fast* behaviour, such as a payment 2 h after activation or a rubber-stamp opinion.
5. The 24 h upper band for escalation→opinion equals R6's 24 h deadline *numerically*, but the two are different norms:
   - R6 is a hard condition on emergency activations, measured from activation (A11), and it lives in the data layer under Option B.
   - R12 is a soft expectation on every escalation, measured from the escalation.

   Keeping them in separate layers stops a statistical warning from being mistaken for an authorization verdict, and the other way round.

The PM4Py profile evaluates **every** pair of an earlier A and a later B in the trace. With several clarification cycles, the first request is also paired with the second answer, which inflates the durations. For that pair, the authoritative duration is the **matched cycle** (i-th request ↔ its answer), computed in section 7. PM4Py findings for that pair that are not matched cycles are discarded.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

```python
import re
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
PASS, FAIL, WARN, NE, PD = "pass", "fail", "warning", "not evaluated", "policy-dependent"
GENERIC_ACCOUNTS = {"Vendor", "system", "batch"}

RECEIVE, CAPTURE, SCREEN, VERIFY = ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data",
                                    "Run Sanctions Screening", "Verify Bank Account")
CLASSIFY, REQ, REC = "Classify Risk", "Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"
ESCALATION, OPINION, EDD = "Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion", "Enhanced Due Diligence"
MANAGER, COMPLIANCE = "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"
REJECT, ACTIVATE, CHANGE = "Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor", "Change Bank Details"
SUSPEND, RELEASE, CLOSE = "Suspend Vendor", "Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"


def parse_trace(case_id, text, base=pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01")):
    rows = []
    for item in text.split(", "):
        activity, hour, resource = re.fullmatch(r"(.+)@(\d+)\[(.+)\]", item.strip()).groups()
        rows.append({CASE: case_id, ACT: activity,
                     TS: base + pd.Timedelta(hours=int(hour)), RES: resource})
    return rows


def _finding(rule, status, detail, vacuous=False):
    return {"rule": rule, "status": status, "detail": detail, "vacuous": vacuous}


def _missing(values):
    return values.isna() | values.astype(str).str.strip().eq("")


def _times(case_df, activity):
    return case_df.loc[case_df[ACT] == activity, TS].sort_values()


def check_data_quality(case_df):
    findings = []
    for col in (ACT, TS):
        if col not in case_df or _missing(case_df[col]).any():
            findings.append(_finding("DQ", FAIL, f"{col} missing: ordering and timing checks untrusted"))
    if TS in case_df and case_df[TS].duplicated().any():
        findings.append(_finding("DQ-ties", WARN, "equal timestamps: order inside ties taken from log position"))
    if CLOSE not in set(case_df[ACT]):
        findings.append(_finding("DQ-open", WARN, "case not closed: liveness rules not evaluated"))
    return findings


def check_segregation(case_df, first_acts, second_acts, rule):
    first = case_df[case_df[ACT].isin(first_acts)]
    second = case_df[case_df[ACT].isin(second_acts)]
    if first.empty or second.empty:
        return _finding(rule, PASS, "rule not activated", vacuous=True)
    if RES not in case_df or _missing(first[RES]).any() or _missing(second[RES]).any():
        return _finding(rule, NE, "resource missing: manual review; block payment release until resolved")
    involved = set(first[RES]) | set(second[RES])
    if involved & GENERIC_ACCOUNTS:
        return _finding(rule, NE, f"non-personal account(s) {sorted(involved & GENERIC_ACCOUNTS)}")
    shared = set(first[RES]) & set(second[RES])
    if shared:
        return _finding(rule, FAIL, f"same resource on both sides: {sorted(shared)}")
    return _finding(rule, PASS, "distinct resources")


def check_approval_cardinality(case_df):
    counts = case_df[ACT].value_counts()
    approvals = case_df[case_df[ACT].isin([MANAGER, COMPLIANCE])]
    accepted, rejected = counts.get(ACTIVATE, 0) > 0, counts.get(REJECT, 0) > 0
    if accepted and rejected:
        return _finding("R2", FAIL, "case is both accepted and rejected")
    if rejected:
        return _finding("R2", PASS if approvals.empty else FAIL,
                        "no approval in rejected case" if approvals.empty else "approval in rejected case")
    if accepted:
        if len(approvals) != 1:
            return _finding("R2", FAIL, f"{len(approvals)} approvals in accepted case")
        if approvals[TS].iloc[0] >= _times(case_df, ACTIVATE).iloc[0]:
            return _finding("R2", FAIL, "approval does not strictly precede activation")
        return _finding("R2", PASS, "exactly one approval before activation")
    return _finding("R2", NE, "no final outcome yet")


def check_opinion_before_activation(case_df):
    """Option A (implemented): R5 without exceptions."""
    esc, opn, act = (_times(case_df, a) for a in (ESCALATION, OPINION, ACTIVATE))
    if esc.empty:
        return _finding("R5", PASS, "no escalation", vacuous=True)
    if act.empty:
        return _finding("R5", PASS, "no activation; R4 covers the open obligation", vacuous=True)
    if pd.concat([esc, opn, act]).isna().any():
        return _finding("R5", NE, "timestamp missing: manual review; block payment release")
    t_act = act.iloc[0]
    if (esc >= t_act).any():
        return _finding("R5", NE, "escalation at/after activation: refer to sanctions case management")
    for t_esc in esc:
        if not ((opn > t_esc) & (opn < t_act)).any():
            return _finding("R5", FAIL, f"activation {t_act} without legal opinion after escalation {t_esc}")
    return _finding("R5", PASS, "legal opinion strictly between escalation and activation")


def check_emergency_exception(case_df, auth_register, window=pd.Timedelta(hours=24),
                              required_roles=frozenset({"Operations", "Legal"}),
                              clock_start="activation"):
    """Option B (variant): R6 exception gated by external evidence."""
    base = check_opinion_before_activation(case_df)
    if base["status"] != FAIL:
        return {**base, "rule": "R5/R6"}
    case_id = case_df[CASE].iloc[0]
    if auth_register is None:
        return _finding("R5/R6", PD, "activation before opinion; authorization register not available")
    auth = auth_register[auth_register[CASE] == case_id]
    if auth.empty:
        return _finding("R5/R6", FAIL, "activation before opinion without recorded joint authorization")
    t_act = _times(case_df, ACTIVATE).iloc[0]
    t_esc = _times(case_df, ESCALATION).iloc[0]
    prior = auth[auth["authorized_at"] <= t_act]
    if prior["authorizer"].nunique() < 2 or not required_roles <= set(prior["role"]):
        return _finding("R5/R6", FAIL, "authorization not joint, missing a role, or after activation")
    opn = _times(case_df, OPINION)
    opn = opn[opn > t_esc]
    if opn.empty:
        return _finding("R5/R6", FAIL, "emergency activation but no legal opinion")
    start = t_act if clock_start == "activation" else t_esc
    if opn.iloc[0] - start > window:
        return _finding("R5/R6", FAIL, f"opinion {opn.iloc[0] - start} after {clock_start} (> {window})")
    rel = _times(case_df, RELEASE)
    if not rel.empty and rel.iloc[0] < opn.iloc[0]:
        return _finding("R5/R6", FAIL, "first payment released before legal opinion")
    return _finding("R5/R6", PASS, "authorized emergency activation; opinion within window")


def check_bank_change(case_df):
    act = _times(case_df, ACTIVATE)
    changes = _times(case_df, CHANGE)
    if act.empty or changes[changes > act.iloc[0]].empty:
        return _finding("R11", PASS, "no post-activation bank change", vacuous=True)
    suspends, verifies, releases = (_times(case_df, a) for a in (SUSPEND, VERIFY, RELEASE))
    status, notes = PASS, []
    for t_chg in changes[changes > act.iloc[0]]:
        if not (suspends > t_chg).any():
            return _finding("R11", FAIL, f"bank change {t_chg} never followed by suspension")
        for t_rel in releases[releases > t_chg]:
            if not ((verifies > t_chg) & (verifies < t_rel)).any():
                return _finding("R11", FAIL, f"payment {t_rel} without re-verification after change {t_chg}")
            if ((suspends > t_chg) & (suspends < t_rel)).any():
                status = WARN
                notes.append(f"payment {t_rel} released after suspension; no reinstatement event exists")
    if "bank_account_id" not in case_df:
        notes.append("bank_account_id missing: cannot confirm the NEW account was verified")
    return _finding("R11", status, "; ".join(notes) or "suspension and re-verification present")


def check_route_matches_risk_class(case_df):
    cls = case_df[case_df[ACT] == CLASSIFY]
    if "risk_class" not in case_df or cls["risk_class"].isna().all():
        return _finding("ROUTE", NE, "risk_class not logged: route correctness inferred from activities only")
    risk = cls["risk_class"].dropna().iloc[-1]
    acts = set(case_df[ACT])
    if risk == "high" and MANAGER in acts:
        return _finding("ROUTE", FAIL, "high-risk case approved on the low-risk route")
    if risk == "high" and ACTIVATE in acts and not {EDD, COMPLIANCE} <= acts:
        return _finding("ROUTE", FAIL, "high-risk case activated without EDD and Compliance Approval")
    return _finding("ROUTE", PASS, f"route consistent with risk class {risk}")


def clarification_cycles(case_df, mean_h=48.0, std_h=24.0, zeta=2.0):
    """Matched request/answer durations for R12, replacing the all-pairs PM4Py pairing."""
    ordered = case_df[case_df[ACT].isin([REQ, REC])].sort_values(TS, kind="stable")
    open_req, cycles = None, []
    for _, ev in ordered.iterrows():
        if ev[ACT] == REQ:
            open_req = ev[TS]
        elif open_req is not None:
            hours = (ev[TS] - open_req) / pd.Timedelta(hours=1)
            z = (hours - mean_h) / std_h
            cycles.append({"hours": hours, "z": z, "status": WARN if abs(z) > zeta else PASS})
            open_req = None
    return cycles


def run_resource_data_layer(df, policy="legal_prevails", auth_register=None):
    if policy not in {"legal_prevails", "operations_exception"}:
        raise ValueError(policy)
    results = []
    for case_id, case_df in df.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        case_df = case_df.sort_values(TS, kind="stable")
        findings = check_data_quality(case_df)
        findings += [
            check_segregation(case_df, [VERIFY], [MANAGER, COMPLIANCE], "R9a"),
            check_segregation(case_df, [CAPTURE], [ACTIVATE], "R9b"),
            check_approval_cardinality(case_df),
            check_opinion_before_activation(case_df) if policy == "legal_prevails"
            else check_emergency_exception(case_df, auth_register),
            check_bank_change(case_df),
            check_route_matches_risk_class(case_df),
        ]
        results.extend({"case": case_id, "policy": policy, **f} for f in findings)
    return pd.DataFrame(results)


def four_eyes_cross_check(df):
    """Independent cross-check of R9 with PM4Py; the pandas check above stays authoritative
    because it reports missing resources explicitly instead of silently keeping the case."""
    violating = set()
    for first, second in [(VERIFY, MANAGER), (VERIFY, COMPLIANCE), (CAPTURE, ACTIVATE)]:
        flagged = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
            df, first, second,
            activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE, resource_key=RES,
            keep_violations=True,
        )
        violating |= set(flagged[CASE].unique())
    return violating
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated enforcement across layers.**
  - R1, R2-exclusion, and R8 are enforced by the procedural XOR, DECLARE `noncoexistence`, and the Skeleton `never_together`. R4 and R7 are enforced by all three as well.
  - T8 therefore fails about ten individual constraints that stem from **one** root cause. Findings must be de-duplicated by requirement ID. Otherwise one bad case outweighs several distinct ones in dashboards.
  - Redundancy is kept on purpose as a cross-check: if the DECLARE and Skeleton verdicts diverge on a shared requirement, the model artefacts have drifted apart.
  - Each requirement has exactly one authoritative layer (section 2), and only that layer determines the requirement's verdict.
- **Constraints that become inconsistent under loops.**
  - DECLARE `response(Esc, Opinion)` is satisfied by one opinion after two escalations, whereas the Skeleton equivalence requires two. This is a real semantic disagreement about whether escalations are individually answered. I chose the stricter reading.
  - Clarification loops break the PM4Py temporal profile, because it pairs across cycles. They also break the Skeleton's count-only view, which cannot see RqC RqC RcC RcC.
  - If a clarification required re-capturing master data, `exactly_one(Capture Master Data)` would fail, and R9b's "the resource performing Capture Master Data" would become ambiguous (the check covers every performer).
  - R9a is checked against every verification, so a legitimate re-verification after a bank change, done by someone who happened to approve earlier, is flagged. The intended control probably concerns only the verification that fed the approval, which cannot be identified without a linking attribute.
  - Frequency bounds for looping activities (A14) are arbitrary.
- **Constraints that become inconsistent under post-activation changes.**
  - R11 demands a suspension after a bank change. There is no reinstatement activity, and R10 forbids a second activation. A compliant Change → Suspend → Verify → Release sequence therefore **pays a suspended vendor**. The data layer raises a warning. The policy owner must either add a `Reactivate Vendor` activity or require the first payment before any change.
  - A post-activation `Sanctions Escalation` (for example from periodic re-screening) makes `response(Esc, Opinion)` and `nonsuccession(Activate, Opinion)` **jointly unsatisfiable**: every such trace fails, whatever happens next. R5 and R4 must be scoped to escalations before activation. The data layer does this and refers later escalations to sanctions case management.
  - `nonsuccession(Activate, Request/Receive Clarification)` forbids clarifications after activation, including one legitimately needed for a bank change.
- **Vacuity.**
  - T3 satisfies `response(Esc, Opinion)`, all nonsuccession rules, R9, and R11 without ever triggering them. T1–T5 satisfy R4, R5, and R7 vacuously.
  - A dashboard that counts vacuous satisfactions as "passed controls" inflates compliance. Results should report activations (how often a rule's trigger occurred) and treat "not activated" separately from pass.
  - Vacuity also hides design errors. For example, if the label "Sanctions Escalation" were misspelled in the log, every escalation rule would be vacuously satisfied.
- **Missing case attributes.** The log has:
  - no risk class, so a high-risk case approved by a manager looks like a valid low-risk case;
  - no screening result (hit/no hit), so a missing escalation after a positive hit cannot be detected;
  - no emergency flag or authorization ID, which is why R6 is unobservable;
  - no bank account identifier, so R11 cannot confirm the *new* account was verified;
  - no payment amount or vendor jurisdiction.

  Every route-level verdict is therefore an inference from activity labels. The fix belongs in logging requirements, not in cleverer models.
- **Timestamp-only limitations.**
  - With completion timestamps only, "concurrently" in the backbone cannot be distinguished from "sequentially in any order".
  - Waiting time cannot be separated from processing time.
  - Equal timestamps make ordering-based verdicts (R5, R3, R11) depend on log position. The design fails closed for sanctions-related ties and flags others.
  - Hour-granularity relative times hide timezones and business calendars. The 24 h window in R6 and the temporal bands are wall-clock durations, which may not be the intended reading.
  - A 2 h payment (T7) cannot be flagged because σ = mean/2 puts the lower band at 0.
- **Resource blind spots not covered by stated rules.**
  - In T6 and T7, the same person (Hana) performs Sanctions Escalation and Enhanced Due Diligence. In T3, Elena rejects and closes. Ana receives and captures.
  - None of these violates R9, and I do not invent violations. They are, however, candidate SoD rules for the policy owner.
- **Risk of treating support/confidence as normative truth.**
  - Every rule here carries `support = 1.0` and `confidence = 1.0` because the rules are prescribed, not discovered. In this design the metadata is **not** used as a severity or threshold signal. Severity comes from the requirement registry.
  - If the same dictionary were *discovered* from practice, the confidence would measure frequency, not legitimacy. For example, if emergency activations are common, a discovered `precedence`-type rule for R5 might show a confidence of 0.8, and a naive reader would conclude that R5 is "80 % true" or that R6 practice is acceptable.
  - Frequency must never settle the R5/R6 conflict. That is a governance decision, documented in section 1.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

- **Canonical procedural representation.**
  - The Option A process tree (`procedural_model`), converted to an accepting Petri net with `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, is versioned together with a policy identifier (`legal_prevails`, v1).
  - It is the **reference and diagnostic model**: it drives alignments, earliest-deviation localization, and communication with process owners. It is deliberately *not* the policy authority, because it over-constrains in documented ways (clarification placement, escalation only on the high-risk route, post-activation behaviour excluded).
  - The Option B tree and DECLARE variant are kept in the repository but are only deployed after a documented policy decision. They are never run in combination with Option A.
- **Authoritative policy layer.** A requirement registry maps each R-ID to exactly one authoritative check, and a requirement's verdict comes from that check alone:

  | Requirement(s) | Authoritative layer |
  |---|---|
  | R1, R3, R4, R7, R8, R11(a) | DECLARE |
  | R10 | Log Skeleton `activ_freq` |
  | R2, R5 (Option A) or R5/R6 (Option B), R9, R11(b) | Resource/data layer |
  | R12 | Temporal profile, advisory only |

- **Order of checks.** Run all layers on every completed case, without short-circuiting, so that every finding is visible. Then aggregate:
  1. The data-quality and completeness gate. It checks required attributes, ties, and closure, and routes open or defective cases to restricted evaluation.
  2. The Log Skeleton: a cheap screen for cardinality and co-occurrence, and authoritative for R10.
  3. DECLARE: the authoritative control-flow policy.
  4. The resource/data layer: SoD, approval cardinality, R5 (or R6), R11(b), and risk class once logged. The PM4Py four-eyes filter runs as a cross-check.
  5. The alignment against the procedural model, for the earliest-deviation diagnosis and a readable explanation.
  6. The temporal profile, with the clarification pair replaced by matched-cycle durations. It only produces warnings.
- **Surfacing contradictory findings.** Each case gets one card containing:
  - **the overall verdict**: the worst authoritative verdict, where `fail` > `policy-dependent` > `warning` > `pass`, and `not evaluated` is listed explicitly;
  - **the violated requirement IDs** with the earliest decisive event (e.g., "R9a at Manager Approval@8"). Duplicate detections are collapsed ("R1: confirmed by 3 layers");
  - **layer disagreements** labelled either as an *expressiveness gap* (e.g., the Log Skeleton passing T6 because it cannot express R5), which is informational, or as a *model-scope deviation* (e.g., a procedural-only failure for a clarification during EDD), which goes to model maintenance rather than to compliance;
  - **policy-dependent findings** shown with both resolutions side by side and the exact missing evidence, e.g., "joint authorization record for case T6 required". Under Option A they appear as `fail` with a waiver field;
  - **vacuous passes** shown as "not activated";
  - **warnings** in a separate queue with z-scores and no automatic consequence;
  - **data gaps** (missing resource, risk class, or authorization) reported as findings in their own right. Gaps that affect sanctions or payment controls block payment release until resolved.
