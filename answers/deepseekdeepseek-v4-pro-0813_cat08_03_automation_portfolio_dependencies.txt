## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data quality dependencies

- A2 classification recommender depends on clean registration, application, and exposure data from A1/portal. If registration fields are incomplete or duplicated, classification errors persist.
- A6 financial-statement extraction depends on document completeness. A5 should reduce missing-statement loops before A6 can achieve its full benefit. Low-quality PDFs require analyst fallback.
- A8 committee-pack assembly depends on normalized statements from A6, KYC status from A3/A4, collateral valuation from A7, and credit-analysis notes. Automating packaging without clean upstream artifacts creates garbage-in/garbage-out risk.
- A9 contract generation depends on clean KYC status and committee-decision data. If decision terms are unstructured or inconsistent, contract rules will produce false exceptions or disputes.

### Legal/compliance controls

- KYC clearance must legally precede Customer signing. A10 and A11 must enforce this gate. Provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases and required remediation in 1.1%.
- Contract terms must match Committee decision. Contract-remediation loops are 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal. A9 must compare against structured decision terms and block mismatches.
- Four-eyes principle: Credit analysis and Committee packaging should not be completed by the same person in High-exposure SME cases. Violations occur in 5.8% of High-exposure cases during month-end rush.
- Any automated suggestion must preserve an auditable trail of model suggestion, human override, and final decision.

### Human escalation capacity

- A3 KYC pre-classifier will still require compliance escalation. Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE, and the network shows high dependency from KYC operations to two senior specialists.
- A4 entity-mismatch assistant depends on the same scarce senior specialists. Low volume but high cognitive load and high risk.
- A6 exceptions, especially low-quality PDFs, will continue to require credit analysts. Analysts are central in the working-together network; automation that increases clean inflow may overload them unless normalization is improved.
- A5 requires relationship-manager behavior change. RMs often trigger missing-document loops through incomplete customer instructions. Automation cannot fully substitute for RM adoption.

### External valuation dependency

- A7 can triage and route external valuers, but it cannot directly control the 58h external valuation wait or the 19% late rate.
- If A7 successfully speeds valuation, Committee packaging and Committee decision may become the next constraint, especially for Equipment finance cases requiring collateral.

### Core-banking integration constraints

- Only two modules can be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months.
- The strongest candidates for deep core integration are A2 classification routing and A9 contract generation/control checks, because they need core workflow, party/product data, and system-of-record controls.
- A5/A10 can be integrated through CRM/portal layers; A6 through document-intelligence pipelines; A3/A4 through compliance case management. A11 activation bot is core-dependent but should not be deep-integrated until upstream controls are reliable.

### Process-mining concurrency note

KYC check and Financial document review are often concurrent in practice, though the system records them sequentially. Automation should preserve or enable parallel work where legally possible, rather than forcing sequential handoffs.

**Modules: A1, A5, A9, A10**

- Sacrifices upstream classification quality, entity-mismatch resolution, financial-statement normalization, and collateral wait.
- Risk: A1 may push more cases into classification, KYC, and document-review bottlenecks.

### 3.2 Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

**Modules: A4, A5, A6, A7**

- Optimizes end-to-end wait time and rework in the longest blocks: entity mismatch, missing-document loops, financial-statement normalization, and collateral valuation.
- Targets the 31h entity-mismatch wait, 8h missing-doc loop, 20h normalization wait, and 58h external valuation wait.

- Sacrifices rapid visible wins in registration, contract drafting, and signing.
- External valuation dependency remains; A7 can only triage and route, not control valuers.
- Less direct control improvement than a compliance-first portfolio.

### 3.3 Compliance-first portfolio

- Optimizes control-path integrity, auditability, and remediation.

- Sacrifices speed and direct labor savings.
- May increase compliance and legal oversight workload in the short term.

### 3.4 Recommended hybrid portfolio

**Modules: A2, A5, A6, A9**

- Optimizes foundational data quality, document-cycle rework, analyst capacity, and contract control.
- A2 fixes routing; A5 reduces missing-document loops; A6 reduces normalization effort; A9 enforces control-rule-based contract generation.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### 4.1 Quick-win portfolio: A1 + A5 + A9 + A10

- **Cycle time:** Improves early registration and late-stage contracting/signing. Cases with missing documents or signing delays improve; entity-mismatch and collateral-heavy cases improve less.
- **Waiting time:** Registration wait falls; missing-document request wait falls; customer signing wait falls. A1 may increase waiting at classification, KYC, and financial review because more cases enter downstream queues.
- **Rework:** Duplicate detection reduces duplicate applications; A5 reduces the 52% repeated missing-document loop; A9 reduces contract-remediation loops, especially for Credit-line renewal; A10 reduces the 21% signing reminder loop.
- **Handovers:** Fewer back-office, legal, and customer handovers. A9 rule exceptions may create new legal-ops exception handoffs.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office demand falls; legal ops shifts from drafting to exception handling; credit analysts may face more clean inflow.
- **Control violations:** A9 reduces decision-contract mismatch. A10 must enforce KYC clearance; otherwise provisional signing risk remains.

### 4.2 Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4 + A5 + A6 + A7

- **Cycle time:** Largest improvement for cases with entity mismatch, incomplete financials, or collateral requirements. Equipment finance cases with collateral benefit materially.
- **Waiting time:** Reduces entity-mismatch wait of 31h, missing-doc wait of 8h, normalization wait of 20h, and external valuation wait of 58h.
- **Rework:** Reduces 38% entity-mismatch rework, 52% repeated missing-doc requests, 16% normalization rework, and 19% late valuations.
- **Handovers:** Fewer repeated requests between financial review and back office; fewer KYC-to-entity-resolution loops; fewer analyst-to-valuer delays.
- **Resource demand:** Credit analysts free capacity from normalization; back office freed from missing-doc chasing; collateral team triage work increases; external valuer capacity remains external.
- **Control violations:** Limited direct effect on KYC/signing, contract-decision alignment, or four-eyes. Cleaner financials indirectly reduce misinformed credit decisions.

### 4.3 Compliance-first portfolio: A2 + A3 + A9 + A12

- **Cycle time:** Moderate improvement from fewer misroutes, faster KYC classification, and fewer contract remediation loops. High-exposure cases should see fewer compliance-driven delays.
- **Waiting time:** KYC wait of 4h improves; misrouted cases avoid wrong queues; contract preparation wait improves modestly.
- **Rework:** Reduces 8% classification errors, 11% KYC rework, 8.5% contract-remediation loops, and control-remediation loops.
- **Handovers:** More escalation and audit handovers initially, but fewer downstream rework handovers.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance and legal teams shift from firefighting to review and audit; model oversight workload increases.
- **Control violations:** Strongest reduction. KYC-before-signing, decision-contract match, and four-eyes audit trails are directly reinforced.

### 4.4 Recommended hybrid portfolio: A2 + A5 + A6 + A9

- **Cycle time:** Improves from classification through financial review, normalization, credit analysis, and contract preparation. This is likely the broadest end-to-end cycle-time impact among four-module portfolios.
- **Waiting time:** Reduces misrouting into KYC/financial review queues, missing-document wait, normalization wait, and contract-prep wait.
- **Rework:** Reduces classification misroutes, repeated missing-doc loops, normalization rework, and contract-remediation loops.
- **Handovers:** Fewer back-office and analyst loops; fewer legal remediation loops. Adds audit/exception handoffs for classification overrides and contract rule exceptions.
- **Resource demand:** Back office and credit analysts gain capacity, but analysts may still be a bottleneck for judgment-heavy High-exposure SME cases. Legal ops shifts to rule maintenance and exception handling.
- **Control violations:** A2 reduces wrong-control-path risk; A9 enforces decision-contract alignment. It does not directly enforce KYC-before-signing in the first wave unless A10 or A12 is added later.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

### Auditability risk

Automated decisions may become a black box, making it difficult to demonstrate who approved what.

**Compensating controls:**

- Log model suggestion, human override, and final decision for A2, A3, A6, and A9.
- Preserve evidence of control checks for KYC, committee decision, and contract generation.
- Conduct periodic sample audits of overrides and exceptions.

### False positives and false negatives

- KYC false negatives are severe because they could allow non-compliant customers through.
- Financial extraction false positives could misstate financial statements and distort credit analysis.

**Compensating controls:**

- Use conservative thresholds for KYC pre-classification; escalate ambiguous hits to compliance specialists.
- Use confidence scoring for financial extraction; route low-confidence PDFs to analysts.
- Monitor false-positive and false-negative rates continuously, especially for High-exposure SME cases.

### Model drift

Classification, KYC, and extraction models may degrade as applications, products, and customer behavior change.

**Compensating controls:**

- Version models and route future cases through shadow mode before full go-live.
- Monitor accuracy against sampled human decisions.
- Retrain on new product variants, especially Credit-line renewal and Equipment finance.

### Exception handling risk

Automation can create queues of exceptions that are harder to clear than the original manual work.

**Compensating controls:**

- Preserve human escalation paths for compliance specialists, credit analysts, and legal ops.
- Monitor specialist utilization and queue age.
- Do not auto-forward High-exposure SME cases without human review.

### Four-eyes compliance risk

Automation could make it easier to bypass four-eyes controls, especially under month-end pressure.

**Compensating controls:**

- Enforce distinct-user rules in workflow for Credit analysis and Committee packaging in High-exposure SME cases.
- Block same-person completion in the system.
- Produce an exception report for any attempted four-eyes violation.

### Legal and contract risk

Automated contract generation may use outdated rules, creating legal exposure.

**Compensating controls:**

- Require legal ops to own the contract rule library.
- Dry-run A9 against historical contracts and committee decisions.
- Quarterly rule review and sign-off.

### Customer friction risk

Missing-document nudging and e-sign adoption may feel rigid or confusing.

**Compensating controls:**

- Allow RM-assisted exceptions for non-digital customers.
- Use plain-language checklists.
- Monitor customer complaints and opt-outs.

### Downstream control risk

Automating A11 or A10 without upstream control completion could activate a loan or permit signing before KYC clearance.

**Compensating controls:**

- Do not deploy A11 activation bot until upstream control completion is proven.
- A10 must include a hard KYC-clearance gate before signing.
- Monitor provisional-signing rate and remediation loops.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Recommended first-year portfolio: A2 + A5 + A6 + A9

### Integration constraint

First six months allow only two deep core-banking integrations.

Use them for:

- **Deep integration #1: A2 Product/exposure classification recommender**
- **Deep integration #2: A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks**

A5 and A6 are integrated through CRM/portal/document-pipeline layers, not deep core-banking integration.

### Phase 1: Months 0–6

**Module order:**

1. Start A2 classification recommender in shadow mode, then deep-integrate with core workflow routing.
2. Deploy A5 missing-document checklist and portal nudging through CRM/portal.
3. Run A6 financial-statement extraction and normalization in shadow mode for standard financials.
4. Prepare A9 rule library and integrate contract generation with control rules by the end of month 6.

**Why this order:**

- A2 fixes routing before downstream automation.
- A5 reduces missing-document loops so A6 receives cleaner input.
- A6 stays in shadow until extraction quality is proven.
- A9 uses the second deep integration because it must enforce core control rules.

**Monitoring metrics:**

- A2: override rate, misroute rate by product/exposure, wrong-control-path incidents.
- A5: repeated missing-document-loop rate, customer response time, RM adoption, complaint volume.
- A6: extraction confidence, low-quality PDF fallback rate, analyst review time, normalization error rate.
- A9: decision-to-contract mismatch rate, contract-remediation loop volume, rule exception rate, legal queue age.

**Gate criteria:**

- A2 go-live: shadow accuracy exceeds baseline; override rate acceptable; misroutes reduced; no increase in wrong-control-path incidents.
- A5 continue: repeated missing-document loops fall materially from 52%; RM adoption meets threshold; customer complaints do not rise.
- A6 go-live: extraction confidence high/medium on covered financials; analyst review time reduced; low-quality fallback manageable.
- A9 go-live: dry-run matches committee decision in sampled cases; contract-remediation loops fall; legal sign-off obtained.

### Phase 2: Months 6–12

- Expand A6 to more financial-statement formats and document types.
- Broaden A9 rule library to more product variants, especially Credit-line renewal and Equipment finance.
- Scale A5 across relationship managers and customer segments.
- Monitor credit-analyst utilization and KYC/compliance queues.
- Begin discovery for A4 entity-mismatch resolution and A7 collateral triage if the data show persistent critical-path waits.

### Do not start in first year

- A11 Loan activation bot should wait until upstream control completion is proven.
- A8 Committee pack auto-assembly should wait until A6, A4/A3, and A7 data quality is reliable.
- A1 Auto-registration should be considered only after downstream bottlenecks are better managed.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend the hybrid portfolio: **A2 Product/exposure classification recommender, A5 Missing-document checklist and portal nudging, A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization, and A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks.**

This portfolio should not lead with the highest-frequency activity, A1 auto-registration. Although A1 is highly feasible and politically attractive, process-mining evidence shows that registration is not the dominant bottleneck: back-office utilization falls below 65% after 15:00, while downstream waiting, rework, and synchronization delays dominate end-to-end performance. Automating A1 first would push more cases into classification, KYC, financial-review, and credit queues, increasing inventory without addressing the real constraints.

The recommended portfolio is defensible because:

- A2 reduces misrouting that creates wrong control paths and downstream rework.
- A5 attacks the 52% repeated missing-document loop, one of the highest-leverage rework cycles.
- A6 reduces high analyst workload and improves credit-model and committee-pack inputs.
- A9 satisfies the compliance requirement by enforcing decision-to-contract alignment and control rules.

It sacrifices the visible quick win of registration automation and defers entity-mismatch and collateral automation. That trade-off is acceptable because the selected modules address the process bottlenecks and control risks most supported by the process-mining artifacts, resource network, and control findings.
