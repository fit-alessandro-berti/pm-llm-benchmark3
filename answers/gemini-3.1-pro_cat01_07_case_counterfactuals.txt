## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Compliance Review (if high_risk OR high_value)
Assessment < Decision (if NOT (high_risk OR high_value))
Compliance Review < Decision (if high_risk OR high_value)
Decision < Notify
```

```text
IF originals_required == True THEN Originals Receipt < Document Check
IF (high_risk == True OR high_value == True) THEN Assessment < Compliance Review < Decision
IF Request More Info is issued THEN (Request More Info < Document Check_new < Assessment_new)
IF Request More Info is issued AND (high_risk == True OR high_value == True) THEN Assessment_new < Compliance Review_new < Decision
Four-eyes rule: Resource(Assessment_final) != Resource(Compliance Review_final)
Resource constraint: No resource can perform two tasks concurrently.
```

Explanation:
- **Timing only:** K-411 (parallelizing verification checks alters when tasks are executed by removing an artificial lock, but does not change the routing path, resources, or decision criteria).
- **Routing:** K-104 (sending a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and C1 alters routing by adding parallel preliminary steps) and K-219 (assigning D2 instead of D1 alters resource routing).
- **Decision policy:** K-337 (suppressing the second rework loop forces a decision on incomplete/inconsistent evidence, altering the allowed decision policy and potentially the final outcome).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
Factual mechanism: The sanctions ambiguity was noticed at Triage but not escalated. The case waited sequentially through Identity, Document, and Assessment before A1 escalated it to S1, who advised C1. C1 had to read evidence from scratch on Wednesday.
Minimal intervention: At Triage, send a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot, allowing preliminary compliance work to run alongside Identity and Document checks.
Temporal propagation: S1 and C1 begin preliminary review on Monday. Because C1 processes the preliminary packet early, C1 saves 1-3 hours on the final review. Consequently, the final Compliance Review can start immediately after Assessment on Tuesday evening, rather than waiting until Wednesday afternoon, pulling the Decision forward.
Process-feasibility check: Final compliance still follows final Assessment, satisfying rule 5. Four-eyes rule is maintained.
Best/base/worst case: Best case: C1 finishes preliminary work on Monday, final review takes 2 hours on Tuesday evening, decision on Tuesday night (saves ~20 hours). Base case: Final review finishes Wednesday morning (saves ~8 hours). Worst case: Preliminary packet is incomplete, C1 wastes time, final review still takes full time on Wednesday (no savings, wastes C1 capacity).
Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If C1's queue is so congested (20h on Monday) that preliminary work cannot start until after Assessment anyway, the intervention provides no temporal benefit.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: Triage routed the case to D1 due to handwritten documents. The case waited in D1's long queue (11-13 hours) until Tuesday morning, even though originals arrived Monday 17:30.
Minimal intervention: Assign the Document Check to D2 as soon as originals arrive on Monday at 17:30.
Temporal propagation: D2's queue on Monday 16:00 is 5 hours, meaning D2 starts the check Monday evening instead of waiting for D1 to start Tuesday morning. However, D2's slower processing speed (8-14h) pushes the completion time to Tuesday mid-day. This shifts the Assessment start time earlier, but the gain is partially offset by D2's slower execution.
Process-feasibility check: D2 is a valid resource for Document Check. Originals Receipt precedes Document Check.
Best/base/worst case: Best case: D2 starts Monday 22:30, finishes early Tuesday morning, Assessment finishes Tuesday afternoon (saves ~12 hours). Base case: D2 is slower, finishes Tuesday mid-day, Assessment finishes Tuesday evening (saves ~2 hours). Worst case: D2 requests clarification, triggering rework, delaying Assessment to Wednesday or Thursday (adds 10-24 hours).
Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If D2 is strictly prohibited from handling specific handwritten documents by unstated policy, or if D2's rework rate on handwriting is 100%, the intervention would strictly degrade performance.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: A1 requested more info twice due to supplier inconsistencies, causing a weekend delay. The case took from Monday to the following Wednesday to complete.
Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 on Friday morning, prohibit the second `Request More Info` loop and force a decision with available evidence.
Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 finishes Friday 10:30. By prohibiting the external request, the case avoids the weekend delay entirely. It immediately moves to Compliance Review (C1) on Friday, finishing Friday afternoon, which pulls the final Decision forward by 5 full days.
Process-feasibility check: The process path is valid, but the decision policy is altered. Compliance Review follows Assessment, and Decision follows Compliance Review.
Best/base/worst case: Best case: Case is rejected safely on Friday, saving 5 days of cycle time. Base case: Case is approved with conditions on Friday, saving 5 days, but requiring post-approval monitoring. Worst case: Case is rejected on Friday, leading to a costly appeal, or approved and results in a severe compliance breach/loss.
Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If the unresolved discrepancy legally precludes any decision (e.g., missing mandatory regulatory form), forcing a decision is process-infeasible and would result in an automatic compliance failure.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: A system lock prevented Document Check from starting until Identity Check finished on Tuesday 13:20. Document Check finished Tuesday 19:30, pushing Assessment to Wednesday.
Minimal intervention: Implement a workaround at Triage to create both tasks concurrently, allowing I2 and D2 to work in parallel.
Temporal propagation: D2 starts Document Check earlier (Tuesday 11:30 alongside I2) rather than waiting for I2 to finish. D2 finishes around 16:00 instead of 19:30. This earlier completion pushes the case into the Assessment queue earlier on Tuesday, meaning it can be picked up by A2 sooner, provided the Assessment queue isn't completely gridlocked.
Process-feasibility check: Identity Check and Document Check can execute concurrently when no originals are required (Rule 3).
Best/base/worst case: Best case: Assessment starts Tuesday late afternoon and finishes Tuesday evening, saving ~18 hours. Base case: Assessment queue is long, so the case waits overnight anyway, finishing Wednesday morning (saves 2-4 hours). Worst case: The workaround corrupts system logs, causing a compliance audit failure, with no actual cycle time saved due to the Assessment bottleneck.
Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If I2 and D2 are actually the same person cross-trained, or if the Assessment queue is strictly ordered by Triage time rather than task completion time, parallelization yields zero end-to-end benefit.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 (Early Escalation) | Increases C1 and S1 demand (preliminary work). | Assessment (slightly, due to faster final C1). | Compliance (C1). | Other high-risk cases waiting for C1. | Negative (slows other cases). | C1 is a single point of failure; early reservations heavily penalize urgent cases. |
| K-219 (D2 Routing) | Shifts demand from D1 to D2. | Document (D1). | Document (D2), Assessment (rework). | Standard cases in D2's queue. | Mixed/Neutral. | D2's rework rate on handwriting creates a feedback loop into Assessment. |
| K-337 (Suppress Rework) | Decreases external wait and D1/A1 rework demand. | Document (D1), Assessment (A1). | None (absorbs capacity). | Other complex cases needing D1/A1. | Positive (frees capacity). | Eliminating a weekend delay drastically reduces mean cycle time, but risk scales non-linearly. |
| K-411 (Parallelize) | No net change in work hours, just shifted earlier. | Document (D2). | Assessment (A2). | Cases already in Assessment queue. | Neutral. | Shifting work earlier hits the Assessment bottleneck, yielding diminishing end-to-end returns. |

Applying these interventions to 100 similar cases in one week reveals significant system-wide interference and bottleneck migration.

For K-104, the reservation of scarce compliance capacity (C1) for preliminary reviews on 100 cases would be catastrophic. C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer available in this window. Front-loading C1's queue with provisional, incomplete packets will displace mature cases that are ready for final review. This queue displacement means that while a few target cases might see minor speedups, the overall cycle time for the vast majority of high-risk cases will degrade severely as the C1 bottleneck tightens.

For K-219, the substitution between D1 and D2 on 100 handwritten cases initially balances the document queues. However, D2's lower proficiency with complex handwriting will likely generate additional rework. This new rework loop will flood the downstream assessment-queue bottlenecks, as Assessors must repeatedly kick cases back to D2 for clarification. This creates a vicious cycle that could ultimately make D2's queue worse than D1's original backlog.

For K-337, suppressing the second rework loop on 100 cases avoids massive external-response effects and weekend effects. By forcing a decision, the system benefits from avoided rework, freeing up significant D1 and A1 capacity. This is the only intervention that injects net capacity into the system rather than just shifting it. However, it trades operational cycle time for severe compliance risk.

For K-411, parallelizing 100 cases through a workaround shifts the bottleneck downstream. While Identity and Document checks finish faster, these 100 cases will immediately pile into the assessment-queue bottlenecks. Because Assessment capacity remains fixed, the end-to-end cycle time will barely improve. Furthermore, if the workaround relies on manual logging, the timestamp behavior versus true concurrency becomes a major risk; staff might simply enter overlapping timestamps in batches, creating the illusion of parallel work without actual efficiency gains.

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the proposed interventions, the **K-411 (Parallelize)** workaround appears most robust. It aligns with the normative rules (Rule 3 explicitly allows concurrency when no originals are required) and structurally removes an artificial legacy constraint. While its end-to-end benefit may be dampened by downstream Assessment bottlenecks, it does not inherently increase risk or rework, making it a safe structural improvement.

Conversely, **K-337 (Suppress Rework)** is the most dangerous despite offering the greatest potential speed and capacity gains. By forcing a decision on unresolved, inconsistent supplier data for high-risk/high-value cases, it alters the fundamental decision policy. This trades operational cycle time for severe compliance, legal, and financial risk, which could be catastrophic in a regulated environment.

A local case counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy in **K-104 (Early Escalation)**. Locally, reserving C1 for a preliminary review seems to save a few hours for one urgent case. System-wide, however, C1 is a single point of failure (the only certified reviewer). Front-loading C1 with provisional packets for 100 cases will instantly gridlock the compliance queue, delaying final reviews and causing systemic failure.

To better evaluate these interventions, the most valuable additional data would be **Assessment queue processing logic and D2's historical rework probabilities**. Knowing exactly how often D2 triggers rework on handwriting, or whether the Assessment queue operates on a strict First-In-First-Out basis versus triage-time priority, would definitively prove whether shifting tasks earlier actually reduces end-to-end cycle time or merely moves cases to wait in a different queue.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: The legacy system lock in K-411 is purely a software limitation and not a hidden regulatory requirement for sequential processing.
2. `temporal`: Preliminary compliance work in K-104 strictly reduces final compliance review time rather than duplicating effort.
3. `resource`: D2 possesses the minimum necessary skills to process handwritten documents without a 100% failure rate (K-219).
4. `behavioral`: Assessors (A1/A2) will not artificially slow down their work when they see a growing Assessment queue (K-411).
5. `quality`: The unresolved discrepancy in K-337 is not a mandatory regulatory field that legally prohibits a decision.
6. `interference`: Reserving provisional slots for C1 in K-104 displaces mature cases that are ready for final review.
7. `temporal`: External supplier response times (weekends/nights) are exogenous and cannot be compressed by internal process changes (K-337).
8. `interference`: Shifting handwritten cases to D2 will proportionally increase wait times for standard digital cases already in D2's queue (K-219).
