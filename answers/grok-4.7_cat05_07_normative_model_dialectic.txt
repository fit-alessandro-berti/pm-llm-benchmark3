## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts

The record establishes the following.

**M0’s language, under ordinary POWL semantics.** Each node of a `StrictPartialOrder` executes once. An edge is a precedence constraint. Nodes with no path between them may occur in either order; they are not required to overlap in time. `checks` therefore makes `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` both mandatory and unordered. The XOR executes exactly one child: either `Manual_Procurement_Approval` then `Activate_Supplier`, or `Reject_Supplier`. `Register_Supplier` precedes both checks; both checks complete before the XOR; `Notify_Requester` precedes `Close_Case`. M0 has no loop, no silent skip, no second route, and no resource perspective. A second `Register_Supplier`, an unknown label, a missing mandatory label, or quality/approval after activation is outside that language.

**Standing rule CS-9, effective 2025-01-01, controlled and formally approved.** Sanctions and quality are required before activation. Manual procurement approval is required before `Activate_Supplier`. The requester and the manual approver must be different humans. That resource rule is absent from M0. Rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen. CS-9 does not say whether those leads are M0 cases.

**PD-3 is a steering-committee decision in minutes and a follow-up email.** It was not written into CS-9 or M0. Its window is 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It allows `Auto_Approve` instead of manual approval only for a LOW-risk DE supplier already in an approved corporate group. Sanctions and automated master-data validation must precede activation. Quality may follow activation by up to two business days. The compliance observer recorded that the pilot could proceed as a monitored exception, and later said she had not approved weakening CS-9.

**FD-7 is an emergency directive effective 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59.** For critical relief suppliers needed to prevent shutdown, provisional activation may precede quality review and manual approval. Sanctions must precede activation. Spend is capped at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval. Daily monitoring and quality within two business days are required. The text expires automatically.

**Collection and practice.** From 2026-07-01, notification sits in CRM. About 70% of notifications are mirrored; missingness rose from 2% to 29%. A stable `notification_id` can link CRM evidence. Older links are mutable text. Operations uses one or more `Request_More_Info` cycles. Forty-seven such cases did not activate before the requested evidence arrived. Compliance regards that behavior as desirable. No permanent auto-approval amendment exists.

**Aggregates.** Of 312 June–July `Auto_Approve` cases, 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack eligibility attributes. Six further auto-approvals occurred in the first week of August.

**Case-file facts used below as evidence, not as legal conclusions.** SUP-701 is a completed high-risk onboarding with two different humans. SUP-702 on 2026-06-15 is low-risk, DE, in an approved group, with complete pilot eligibility fields, quality on the next business day, and a CRM notification at +2 hours under a stable `notification_id`. SUP-703 on 2026-06-20 is a high-risk new foreign supplier; an operator selected “pilot” although the supplier was ineligible; the first purchase was EUR 180,000. SUP-704 was a critical pump supplier activated on 2026-07-11, spend EUR 18,000, with daily monitoring and next-business-day quality and approval. SUP-705 repeated that activation pattern on 2026-07-13; operations asserts that the flood still existed; no written extension exists; spend was EUR 12,000. SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours linked by mutable text entered two days later. SUP-707’s two information requests obtained certificates before approval. SUP-708 is high-risk, activated 40 minutes before sanctions, with no emergency, no pilot, and no transaction before clearance. SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen and has no quality review; the process owner and the model owner disagree about scope; the scope document is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation six months after closure, with a prior quality certification that remained valid, and with no register or new quality event. SUP-711’s two register rows share one source transaction id. SUP-712’s requester and manual approver resolve to one human through two aliases. SUP-713 records `QA_Clearance`; audit documents show content equivalent to quality review; the label mapping was approved one week later. SUP-714 on 2026-08-02 was low-risk and otherwise pilot-eligible; operations believed a permanent amendment was imminent. SUP-715 fits the standard activity order; the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line; no rule bans that relationship; the conflict-of-interest policy requires disclosure; none was recorded. Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

### Disputed propositions

These are not settled by the trace text alone.

- Whether a steering-committee monitored exception can excuse a time-bounded departure from CS-9.
- Whether the observer’s later statement erases PD-3 inside its written window.
- Whether FD-7 displaces CS-9 only while it is in force, which is how the text is written, or whether an ongoing flood silently extends it.
- Whether a rejected sanctions pre-screen is an M0 case that must contain `Quality_Review`.
- Whether reactivation is “onboarding” under CS-9.
- Whether SUP-706 was actually notified.
- Whether automated master-data validation happened inside pilot cases, given that the log has no such activity label.
- Whether the 9 auto-approvals with missing eligibility attributes were in fact eligible.
- Whether a post-hoc catalog mapping cures SUP-713. The equivalence evidence is strong; the governance timing is what is debatable.
- Whether M0 fitness is a compliance metric. It is not, because M0 is blind to aliases, disclosure, amount, risk, and expiry.

### Unproved

- Master-data validation timestamps and results on the auto-approval path.
- The CRM message in SUP-706 belonging to that case.
- Eligibility of the 9 incomplete pilot rows.
- That flood conditions continued after 2026-07-12. Even a proved continuation would not, on these materials, extend FD-7.
- Any text of the scope document beyond the stipulated ambiguity.
- Total pre-approval spend on SUP-704 and SUP-705 beyond the stated initial amounts.
- The full conflict-of-interest policy beyond the stipulated disclosure duty.
- That a permanent auto-approval amendment was actually imminent.
- Certificate identifiers and expiry dates for SUP-710, beyond the statement that the prior certification remained valid.
- Withdrawal behavior. No withdrawal case is in the log.

### Burden of proof

A **noncompliance** finding requires all four of the following: the case is inside the scope of an identified rule; that rule was in force at the activation or decision timestamp; reliable evidence shows the breach; and no written overlay in force at that timestamp authorized the departure and had its own conditions met. Ambiguous scope, or an unreliable identity or notification link, blocks a noncompliance finding on that point.

An **exception** is accepted only if all of the following are shown: written authority; an effective window that covers the activating event; an eligibility or emergency snapshot that meets every condition of that authority; compensatory controls actually performed within the stated time; and automatic expiry respected. Operator belief, an imminent amendment, a later personal clarification, and factual pressure after expiry do not carry this burden. Where eligibility attributes are missing, the exception is not proved. Those rows are unjustified. They are not automatically assigned the same severity as a proved ineligible high-risk activation.

A **model or scope defect** requires that the behavior is permitted by CS-9, or sits outside any procedure CS-9 actually states, or differs from M0 only by a logging or labeling artifact, and that an independent in-force prohibition is not proved.

**Desirability never legalizes a past departure.** It is relevant only to whether the prospective model should grow a construct M0 omitted, such as `Request_More_Info`.

**Identity burden.** A segregation or conflict finding is affirmative and is made where the file resolves identity. Silence about identity in the other case narratives is not treated as a proved breach and is not treated as a performed certification. Going forward, missing canonical identity fails closed.

**Authority characterization used for the judgments below.** CS-9 stays the standing standard and is not amended by pilots, emergencies, or practice. FD-7, as a directive with its own effective interval and automatic expiry, temporarily displaces the two CS-9 timing rules it names, and only inside its scope and window. PD-3 authorizes a monitored exception inside its window and eligibility rule. The observer’s two statements are read together: the pilot may proceed as an exception, and that exception does not weaken CS-9. If this organization’s constitution in fact says that nothing but a CS-9 revision can excuse any case, that constitution is not in the record, and SUP-702 would flip to noncompliant.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

M0 is a bad sole detector, and several apparent deviations are logging or scope artifacts. That concession does not decide the cases in which an in-force control was missed, an exception was misused, or an expired exception was continued.

CS-9 is the controlled norm. It requires sanctions and quality before activation, manual approval before activation, and two different humans for request and manual approval. M0 correctly encodes the standard activity order and incorrectly omits the human rule. A trace that replays M0 can still be a CS-9 breach. SUP-712 is the clear example: the activity order is perfect, and the requester and approver are the same human under two aliases. SUP-715 also replays M0 while leaving a required conflict disclosure unrecorded. A conformance program that publishes M0 fitness would certify both.

PD-3 never entered CS-9. It is minutes plus email. The observer’s phrase “monitored exception” is authority for a narrow, watched departure. Her later statement confirms that CS-9 was not relaxed as a standard. A monitored exception is violated by ineligible use. SUP-703 is that violation: high-risk, new, foreign, operator-selected “pilot,” quality only after activation, no manual approval, first purchase EUR 180,000. The date falls inside the calendar window and still falls outside every eligibility condition. Internal Audit was right to treat it as potentially severe. The same logic condemns the 14 clearly ineligible auto-approvals in the aggregate. The 9 rows with missing eligibility attributes do not get the exception; the burden was on the operation to snapshot eligibility.

Expiry is part of the authority, not a soft preference. FD-7 expired at 2026-07-12 23:59 by its own terms. SUP-705 activated on 2026-07-13. Operations’ statement that the flood still existed is a party assertion. There is no written extension. Sanctions-first sequencing, a EUR 12,000 spend, and next-day retrospective review show that staff could copy the emergency pattern. They do not re-enact a directive that had expired. PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. SUP-714 on 2026-08-02, and the six August auto-approvals in the first week, are the same defect: a temporary route had become the habit. Belief that a permanent amendment was imminent is evidence of normalization, not of legal force. Desired future policy does not rewrite completed cases.

SUP-708 is a direct CS-9 breach with no overlay available to argue about. A high-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance and before manual approval. Quality had already occurred, so this is not a generalized failure to review quality. It is a failure of the two gates that stop a supplier from becoming active. The absence of transactions reduces realized loss. It does not undo the breach. For those 40 minutes the supplier was active and uncleared. Luck is not a control.

M0 also hides misconduct in the other direction and over-states misconduct in ways a prosecutor should not rely on. It cannot see SUP-712. It flags SUP-707 only because a lawful information request has no symbol in the model. It would flag SUP-711 because a migration wrote the same source transaction twice. It would flag SUP-713 because the UI name changed, even though audit content is equivalent. It would treat CRM-era missing `Notify_Requester` rows as process failures even though missingness moved from 2% to 29% when the activity left the onboarding log. Those are real model and data defects. They are why raw deviation counts cannot be the prosecution’s headline number.

They are not a defense of the severe tail. Adding `Auto_Approve` and a post-activation quality edge to the model, without an eligibility and window guard, would make SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714 look conforming. That update would launder the misconduct. The operation’s claim that the high deviation rate “mostly” reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope explains the information-request cases, the in-window eligible pilot volume, the in-window emergency case, the rename, the duplicate, and much of the notification gap. It does not explain SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, the 14 ineligible auto-approvals, the 9 unproved eligibilities, SUP-705, or the August continuation. A monitored exception that cannot keep ineligible suppliers out of the route is not under control. SUP-703 shows the route was operator-asserted rather than system-enforced.

Desirable outcomes are irrelevant to retrospective legality. SUP-707’s certificates were a good idea and can be added to the model prospectively. They do not imply a general power to depart from CS-9 whenever the departure feels operationally sound. SUP-714’s low-risk profile similarly reduces harm. It does not create the amendment that had not been issued.

## 3. Defense Brief

SUP-703 and SUP-708 are real control failures. Alias self-approval in SUP-712 is a real segregation breach even though M0 fits. SUP-705 and SUP-714 continued routes whose written authority had ended. Informal practice cannot extend PD-3 or FD-7. Those concessions leave a separate, equally evidence-based claim: M0 is not a valid description of the norm, the scope, or the log, and a large share of its deviations are not business misconduct.

M0 is stricter than CS-9 on rejection and looser than CS-9 on resources. CS-9 requires quality before activation, not before every rejection, and it expressly allows an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads. SUP-709 registers, screens sanctions, rejects, notifies, and closes. There is no activation. Forcing `Quality_Review` before that reject is M0’s invention. The process owner’s view (the lead is an intake case) and the model owner’s view (M0 starts only after pre-screen acceptance) cannot both be enforced. The scope document does not decide them. Either reading makes a noncompliance verdict unavailable: the case is outside M0, or M0 contradicts the abbreviated path CS-9 permits.

M0 also has no `Request_More_Info` construct. SUP-707, and all 47 such cases, request information and only then move to approval and activation. That behavior satisfies sanctions, quality, manual approval, and activation order. The fitness hit is the closed alphabet. A partial order of the two checks is the right idea for the standard route, and M0 already gets that idea right: either order of sanctions and quality conforms. The defect is the missing loop, not the concurrency.

The pilot and the emergency are written overlays, not folk custom. SUP-702 meets the overlay that exists: 2026-06-15, low-risk, DE, approved group, eligibility fields complete, sanctions before activation, quality on the next business day, notification evidenced by a stable `notification_id` two hours after the relevant event. M0 fails the trace because `Auto_Approve` is absent from the alphabet, quality is after activation, and the onboarding log omits notification. Those are model and logging gaps against a route the steering committee had authorized as a monitored exception. The observer’s later refusal to “weaken CS-9” is consistent with an exception that leaves the standard in place. SUP-704 is the cleaner overlay. Activation on 2026-07-11 sits inside FD-7. Sanctions precede activation. Initial spend is EUR 18,000. Monitoring exists. Quality and retrospective approval occur on the next business day. Retrospective work can fall after 2026-07-12 and still satisfy “within two business days,” because the directive limits when provisional activation may happen, and it separately sets the deadline for the compensating acts. M0’s single order, “both checks, then manual approval, then activation,” cannot express that written temporary order.

The CRM cutover makes notification-fitness unusable as a misconduct metric after 2026-07-01, and it complicates SUP-702 even though that case is earlier. The reliable key, on the collection note’s own terms, is an immutable `notification_id`. SUP-702 has it. SUP-706 has the unreliable alternative: mutable text entered two days later. The defense does not claim SUP-706 is proved notified. It claims the opposite error is also unavailable: absence from the onboarding log is not absence of the act. A 2% to 29% movement at a known system cutover is a measurement break.

SUP-710 is a reactivation, which neither CS-9 nor M0 defines. The case repeated sanctions, obtained manual approval before activation, relied on a quality certification that remained valid, and closed six months after the prior case. Inventing a full new-onboarding duty, including a second `Register_Supplier` and a second quality review, is an application of a model outside its stated object. SUP-711 is one registration written twice under one source transaction id. SUP-713 is a rename. The procedure and UI called the same work `QA_Clearance`; audit content matches; the catalog mapping lagged by a week. Label inequality is not a second control failure.

Trace deviation and business misconduct are different predicates. M0-fitness collapses them. An extra lawful activity, a duplicate row, a synonym, a CRM-resident notification, an authorized provisional order, and an out-of-scope reactivation all reduce fitness. So do SUP-703 and SUP-708. Operations is therefore right that an unsegmented deviation rate mostly measures model obsolescence and collection change, once the severe and ultra vires minority has been pulled out and judged on its own evidence. The 289 apparently eligible in-window auto-approvals are exception cases to be governed, watched, and then ended at the written expiry. They are not 289 instances of the SUP-703 pattern.

The limit on this defense is sharp. After 2026-07-31 the auto route has no normative existence. After 2026-07-12 23:59 the emergency route has none either. A low-risk outcome, a small euro amount, a completed retrospective review, and a sincere belief do not replace the missing instrument. The permanent auto-approval branch operations wants is a prospective policy project. It is not a description of the law that governed SUP-714.

## 5. Final Judgment

**Rule applied.** CS-9 governs. A written overlay excuses only the clauses it suspends, only inside its window and scope, and only when its conditions are evidenced. M0 is evidence of the intended standard path. It is not the compliance oracle. Desirability does not cure. Data doubt blocks only the finding that depends on the doubtful fact. Expired text has no residual force.

**System judgment.** Operational compliance is unsatisfactory. Model validity of M0, as a complete norm or as a detector, fails. These fifteen cases are a curated edge set, so they are not a compliance rate. The population evidence is. Among 312 June–July auto-approvals, 289 appear eligible and in-window; those can stand as monitored exceptions only to the extent their own snapshots match SUP-702. Fourteen are clearly ineligible and are noncompliant. Nine have missing eligibility attributes, so the exception is not established; they are unjustified and, on severity, ungraded until the snapshot is recovered. Six auto-approvals in the first week of August are noncompliant continuations of the kind in SUP-714. Notification missingness moving from 2% to 29% at the CRM cutover is a broken metric, not a finding that 29% of cases failed to notify. The forty-seven information-request cases are consistent with a lawful loop M0 cannot say. Internal Audit’s concern about SUP-703 and SUP-708 is sustained, and those two are not rescued by the model critique.

M0 remains a correct skeleton of the standard happy path: register, unordered mandatory sanctions and quality, then manual approval before activation, exclusively against rejection, then notification, then closure. It is incomplete relative to CS-9 because the distinct-human rule is absent. It is over-constrained relative to CS-9 on abbreviated rejection. It has no information-request loop, no time-sliced pilot or emergency language, no reactivation scope, and no label catalog. Enforcing raw M0 would punish SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-711, and SUP-713 alongside the real breaches, and it would pass SUP-712 and SUP-715. Operations’ claim that the deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope is accepted as an explanation of fitness and rejected as an operational conclusion.

| SUP-706 | DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE | Unassessed | Medium | The only open duty is notification, and the mutable late link neither proves nor disproves it. |
| SUP-707 | MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT | None | High | Information requests before a complete standard approval are lawful; M0 has no loop. |
| SUP-708 | NONCOMPLIANT | Severe | High | High-risk activation before sanctions and before manual approval, with no overlay. No transaction mitigates harm only. |
| SUP-709 | MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT | Low | Medium | Abbreviated rejection matches CS-9’s pre-screen sentence and there was no activation; M0’s mandatory quality-before-reject, and the scope document, do not support a violation finding. |
| SUP-710 | MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT | Low | Medium | No written reactivation procedure. Fresh sanctions and approval-before-activation occurred; omitting a new quality review depends on a certification the file says remained valid. |
| SUP-711 | COMPLIANT | None | High | One business registration, duplicated under one source transaction id. Raw replay fails until idempotent collapse; the supplier decision conforms. |
| SUP-712 | NONCOMPLIANT | High | High | One human requested and manually approved, via aliases. M0’s acceptance of the trace is a detector failure. |
| SUP-713 | MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT | Low | High | `QA_Clearance` is an equivalent rename of `Quality_Review`; the catalog lagged an already changed procedure and UI. |
| SUP-714 | NONCOMPLIANT | Moderate | High | Pilot-eligible profile used two days after PD-3 expired. Belief in an imminent amendment has no force. |
| SUP-715 | MIXED | Moderate | High | Dominant reason: the activation path complies with M0 and with CS-9’s stated activity and distinct-requester rules, and the undisclosed spousal relationship still breaches the disclosure duty. The relationship is not itself prohibited. MIXED is used because those two findings are both proved and neither erases the other. |

### Where the prosecution case is rejected

- SUP-702 and SUP-704 are not misconduct. The written window, scope, and compensating conditions are evidenced. PD-3’s failure to amend CS-9 is acknowledged by classifying them as exceptions rather than as a new standard.
- SUP-707 is not noncompliance. CS-9 does not forbid obtaining missing certificates, and the record shows the certificates arrived before approval.
- SUP-709 is not a proved quality-control breach. The abbreviated-pre-screen sentence and the scope ambiguity carry enough weight to block the finding.
- SUP-711 is not two registrations.
- SUP-713 is not a substitute control. Equivalence is audited.
- SUP-706 is not a proved missed notification.
- The 289 apparently eligible in-window auto-approvals are not treated as SUP-703 clones.
- Mass failure of every pilot case for lack of a never-logged `Master_Data_Validation` activity is rejected. That control is unproved and must be snapshotted prospectively. It is not the same fact as the 9 rows whose eligibility attributes, which the pilot itself defined, are missing.
- Desirability of `Request_More_Info` is accepted as a reason to change the model going forward, and rejected as a source of authority.

### Where the defense case is rejected

- PD-3 and FD-7 do not amend CS-9, and practice does not extend them.
- SUP-703 is not a pilot case. Ineligibility and the EUR 180,000 exposure are proved.
- SUP-705 is not covered by a factual emergency. The directive expired.
- SUP-714 is not covered by imminent policy.
- SUP-708 is not harmless. The uncleared active interval is the breach; the empty transaction log is only the harm finding.
- SUP-712 is not merely a modeling issue. The model explains the detection failure. CS-9 explains the breach.
- SUP-715 is not fully compliant. Disclosure was required and was not recorded. The further claim that the spousal pairing is independently forbidden is also rejected, because no such rule is in the record.
- “The deviation rate is mostly obsolescence” is rejected as the operational verdict, and accepted only as the explanation of M0-fitness after the severe and expired-authority cases have been removed.
- An unguarded revision that adds auto-approval and post-activation quality to the standing language is rejected, because it would make SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714 look conforming.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The normative package is layered. A trace is accepted only when the control-flow layer, the data guards, the resource rules, and the overlay clock all accept it. Fitness to any one layer is not compliance.

### Control flow

Use a time-sliced POWL, not one standing language that always contains the risky routes.

- **Baseline language**, in force whenever no overlay is active: `Register_Supplier`, then an exclusive choice between a standard approval route and an abbreviated rejection route, then `Notify_Requester`, then `Close_Case`.
- **Standard route:** `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are both mandatory and unordered. Zero or more `Request_More_Info` activities may interleave with those checks. All of that completes before `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, which completes before `Activate_Supplier`.
- **Rejection route:** sanctions are mandatory; quality is optional; information requests may occur; `Reject_Supplier` follows; activation is absent. This closes the SUP-709 ambiguity prospectively. Historical SUP-709 stays a model/scope defect rather than being retroactively convicted.
- **Auto route,** present in the language only during a version whose clock is on: sanctions before `Auto_Approve` before `Activate_Supplier`; quality mandatory and unordered relative to activation, so provisional activation can precede it; information requests finish before auto-approval.
- **Emergency route,** present only during an emergency version: sanctions before `Activate_Supplier`; quality and manual approval both mandatory and unordered relative to activation, so retrospective completion fits and a missing retrospective completion does not.
- **Reactivation is a separate model**, `M_reactivation`, and is not an XOR child of onboarding. Putting a silent skip of `Register_Supplier` inside the onboarding model would also accept unregistered new suppliers. Reactivation requires fresh sanctions and manual approval before activation. Quality is a certificate precondition in data, not a silently optional activity in onboarding.
- **Withdrawals** stay outside the POWL until `Withdraw_Case` is a defined event. Until then, a withdrawn case is marked out of scope. It is not forced through `Reject_Supplier`.

`QA_Clearance` is not a second activity. A versioned label catalog maps it to `Quality_Review` before alignment. Duplicate rows that share a source transaction id are collapsed before alignment.

The loop is a real POWL loop of zero or more information requests, not a data-aware “until certificates are complete” construct. It is ordered to finish before the route’s decision activity. It does not authorize information requests after approval or after activation.

### Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time

These do not belong in POWL.

- Auto route: risk = LOW, country = DE, approved corporate group = true, decision time inside the overlay window, sanctions timestamp before activation, master-data validation result = pass and validation timestamp before activation, quality timestamp no later than two business days after activation and in any case before closure.
- Emergency route: authority id matches the directive, activation timestamp inside the directive window, critical-relief flag and business justification present, sanctions timestamp before activation, cumulative spend before retrospective approval ≤ EUR 20,000, a monitoring record for each day the provisional activation remains open, quality and manual approval within two business days.
- Standard route: both check timestamps before activation; manual-approval timestamp before activation.
- The route flag is computed by the engine from the snapshot. An operator “pilot” checkbox cannot select the route. That is the SUP-703 preventive control.
- Business-day deadlines are computed and stored by the control service as `deadline_at`. Conformance compares timestamps; it does not re-implement the calendar ad hoc in every notebook.

### Resource and conflict of interest

Also outside POWL. Resolve aliases to a canonical person id with validity intervals before testing.

- CS-9: requester canonical id ≠ manual approver canonical id. This catches SUP-712 even though every activity label conforms.
- Current conflict policy, as evidenced: specified relationships, including spouses and the same reporting line, require a disclosure record before approval. Absence fails the data check. The relationship is not independently prohibited, so the check looks for the disclosure, which is the SUP-715 rule that actually exists.
- Prospective recommendation, not current law and not a basis for regrading SUP-715: require quality reviewer ≠ manual approver, and block undisclosed close relationships in the workflow engine rather than detecting them afterward.
- Auto-approval has no manual approver. Accountability then sits on the rule version and the eligibility snapshot, not on a fictional human approver.
- Delegation, where used, needs its own evidence id and a principal who is still distinct from the requester under CS-9.

### Overlay governance

- Every overlay cites the baseline clauses it suspends, the clauses it leaves untouched, the effective timestamp, and the automatic expiry timestamp. PD-3 suspends only “manual approval before activation” and “quality before activation,” and only under its eligibility rule. It does not suspend sanctions-before-activation or the distinct-human rule. FD-7 suspends the same two timing rules inside its narrower scope and does not suspend sanctions-before-activation.
- A pilot or directive is not incorporated into CS-9 by usage. Incorporation is a formal revision.
- The conformance clock is bi-temporal: the rule version applicable at the event’s effective time, and the record time of the log row. Late narrative does not move the effective time.
- At expiry, the engine removes the route in the same minute. SUP-705 and SUP-714 are the design target of that job.
- Ineligible attempts against a live route are incidents, even if a human later stops activation.
- Personal concurrence, or a later personal clarification, does not open, widen, or silently close a written window.
- Historical analysis uses the sliced language for the period and still applies the guards. The slice says which family was even possible. The guard says whether this supplier was allowed onto it.

### Scope rules

- `M_onboarding` starts at `Register_Supplier` for a new supplier case.
- Rejected leads that fail pre-screen are inside the abbreviated rejection route. Quality is allowed and not required. They are not dropped from the population, and they are not scored against the standard approval route.
- Reactivation is a distinct case type. It may use `M_reactivation` only with a snapshotted, unexpired quality certificate. Otherwise it must enter full onboarding. SUP-710’s historical treatment remains a scope gap, not a retroactive conviction, on the certificate statement in the file.
- Withdrawal before activation is a scope code until the activity exists. It is not rejection and it is not an incomplete approval.

### Where each constraint lives

| Constraint | Layer |
|---|---|
| Route families, concurrency of the two standard checks, approval-before-activation on the standard route, sanctions-before-activation on every activating route, optional quality on rejection, zero-or-more information requests, notification before closure | POWL, time-sliced by enabled routes |
| Global safety net independent of branch choice: sanctions precede activation; notification precedes closure; reject and activate do not co-occur | DECLARE or log skeleton |
| Risk, country, group membership, euros, business-day deadlines, certificate expiry, immutable notification link, source-transaction dedup, alias resolution, disclosure record | pandas or equivalent data checks over a normalized event and snapshot table |
| Who may issue a directive, automatic route removal at expiry, prohibition on operator-selected pilot flags, label-catalog approval, preventive block on same-person approval | Organizational and application controls |

POWL cannot turn those data conditions into structure. Encoding auto and emergency orders in the standing unsliced model would be unsafe: alignment would then accept SUP-703, SUP-705, and SUP-714. The sliced constructor below is the control-flow proposal. The overlay table is what makes a slice legal.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Limitations encoded on purpose

- One POWL cannot enforce risk, amount, identity, disclosure, certificate validity, or effective dates. The constructor therefore builds a language for a declared set of enabled routes. The baseline export enables only `standard` and `reject`. Pilot and emergency languages are historical or currently authorized slices, and they remain insufficient without the guards.
- `Request_More_Info` is `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)` under PM4Py loop semantics: execute the silent child, then zero or more times execute the request and the silent child again. The loop is concurrent with the checks on that route and precedes that route’s decision activity. It does not say “repeat until the missing certificate arrives,” and it does not allow requests after the decision activity.
- Master-data validation and daily monitoring are not invented activities. No observed label exists for them. They are mandatory attributes and records in the overlay. Inventing transitions would make every current pilot and emergency trace look deviant for a naming reason.
- Reactivation is a separate object, `M_reactivation`. It must not be united with onboarding by a silent skip of registration.
- `QA_Clearance` is handled by `LABEL_CATALOG`, not by a second XOR branch.
- Notification remains mandatory before closure. A CRM repair may insert `Notify_Requester` only from an immutable `notification_id`. SUP-706’s mutable link does not qualify.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def rmi_loop():
    # PM4Py LOOP executes children[0], then zero or more (children[1], children[0]).
    # children[0] is silent, so the visible language is zero or more Request_More_Info.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(label="Request_More_Info")],
    )


def standard_route():
    sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    requests = rmi_loop()
    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, quality, requests, manual, activate]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(sanctions, manual)
    route.order.add_edge(quality, manual)
    route.order.add_edge(requests, manual)
    route.order.add_edge(manual, activate)
    return route


def auto_route():
    sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
    activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    requests = rmi_loop()
    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, auto, activate, quality, requests]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto)
    route.order.add_edge(requests, auto)
    route.order.add_edge(auto, activate)
    # Quality_Review is mandatory and unordered relative to activation.
    # "Within two business days" is enforced by the overlay, not by an edge.
    return route


def emergency_route():
    sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    requests = rmi_loop()
    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, activate, quality, manual, requests]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
    route.order.add_edge(requests, activate)
    # Quality and manual approval are mandatory and may be retrospective.
    # Spend cap and daily monitoring are overlay/organizational checks.
    return route


def reject_route():
    sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    optional_quality = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label="Quality_Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    requests = rmi_loop()
    route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, optional_quality, requests, reject]
    )
    route.order.add_edge(sanctions, reject)
    route.order.add_edge(optional_quality, reject)
    route.order.add_edge(requests, reject)
    return route


def build_onboarding_model(enabled_routes):
    """Build the onboarding language for one policy slice.

    enabled_routes is a subset of {"standard", "auto", "emergency", "reject"}.
    Baseline after expiry is {"standard", "reject"}. A slice that includes
    "auto" or "emergency" is not normative unless POLICY_OVERLAYS has a
    matching version whose window covers the activation timestamp and whose
    case-level guard passes.
    """
    builders = {
        "standard": standard_route,
        "auto": auto_route,
        "emergency": emergency_route,
        "reject": reject_route,
    }
    unknown = set(enabled_routes) - set(builders)
    if unknown or not enabled_routes:
        raise ValueError("enabled_routes must be a non-empty known subset")
    branches = [builders[name]() for name in enabled_routes]
    decision = (
        branches[0]
        if len(branches) == 1
        else OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=branches)
    )
    register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
    model.order.add_edge(register, decision)
    model.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
    model.order.add_edge(notify, close)
    return model


def reactivation_model():
    """Separate case type. Do not XOR this into onboarding.

    Omitting Quality_Review is safe only when a data guard has snapshotted an
    unexpired prior quality certificate. Otherwise the case belongs in
    build_onboarding_model(("standard", "reject")).
    """
    sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
    model = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanctions, manual, activate, notify, close]
    )
    model.order.add_edge(sanctions, manual)
    model.order.add_edge(manual, activate)
    model.order.add_edge(activate, notify)
    model.order.add_edge(notify, close)
    return model


M_baseline = build_onboarding_model(("standard", "reject"))
M_pilot_window = build_onboarding_model(("standard", "auto", "reject"))
M_pilot_and_emergency = build_onboarding_model(
    ("standard", "auto", "emergency", "reject")
)
M_reactivation = reactivation_model()

def overlay_in_force(overlay, effective_ts):
    start = overlay["effective_from"]
    end = overlay["effective_to"]
    if effective_ts < start:
        return False
    if end is not None and effective_ts > end:
        return False
    return True


def auto_guard(facts):
    """Fail closed. facts are an eligibility snapshot, not operator assertions."""
    reasons = []
    if facts.get("risk") != "LOW":
        reasons.append("risk is not LOW")
    if facts.get("country") != "DE":
        reasons.append("country is not DE")
    if facts.get("approved_corporate_group") is not True:
        reasons.append("approved corporate group not evidenced")
    if facts.get("sanctions_before_activation") is not True:
        reasons.append("sanctions did not precede activation")
    if facts.get("master_data_validation_passed_before_activation") is not True:
        reasons.append("master-data validation before activation not evidenced")
    latency = facts.get("quality_business_days_after_activation")
    if latency is None or latency > 2:
        reasons.append("quality review outside two business days or missing")
    return (not reasons), reasons


def emergency_guard(facts):
    reasons = []
    if facts.get("critical_relief_supplier") is not True:
        reasons.append("critical-relief scope not evidenced")
    if facts.get("sanctions_before_activation") is not True:
        reasons.append("sanctions did not precede activation")
    spend = facts.get("spend_eur_before_retrospective_approval")
    if spend is None or spend > 20000:
        reasons.append("spend cap not evidenced or exceeded")
    if facts.get("daily_monitoring_recorded") is not True:
        reasons.append("daily monitoring not evidenced")
    quality_latency = facts.get("quality_business_days_after_activation")
    approval_latency = facts.get("manual_approval_business_days_after_activation")
    if quality_latency is None or quality_latency > 2:
        reasons.append("retrospective quality outside two business days or missing")
    if approval_latency is None or approval_latency > 2:
        reasons.append("retrospective approval outside two business days or missing")
    return (not reasons), reasons


def distinct_human_guard(facts):
    requester = facts.get("requester_canonical_id")
    approver = facts.get("manual_approver_canonical_id")
    if requester is None or approver is None:
        return False, ["canonical identity missing; fail closed for a certification"]
    if requester == approver:
        return False, ["requester and manual approver are the same human"]
    return True, []
```

`distinct_human_guard` is the preventive certification check for new cases. It is fail-closed when identity is missing. The historical case grades in section 5 do not convert silence in the narrative into a breach, because identity was supplied precisely where the file makes it the issue. Those are different burdens: designing the next control, and judging a completed record.

### Event and data-collection proposal

One normalized event row, append-only:

- `case_id`, `event_id`, `activity` (canonical label after catalog mapping), `label_as_recorded`, `label_catalog_version`
- `source_transaction_id` for idempotent collapse, which is the SUP-711 rule
- `effective_ts` (business time) and `record_ts` (immutable write time)
- `actor_canonical_id`, `actor_alias_at_time`, `delegation_id`
- `rule_id`, `rule_version`, `route_selected` computed by the engine
- `eligibility_snapshot_id` pointing at an immutable document: risk, country, group id, group-approval reference, snapshot hash, snapshot time
- `activation_mode`: `standard`, `provisional_auto`, or `provisional_emergency`
- `master_data_validation_result` and `master_data_validation_ts` on the auto-approval event
- `amount_eur`, `currency`, `spend_eur_before_retrospective_approval`
- `quality_certificate_id`, `certificate_valid_to` for any reactivation that omits a new quality review
- `coi_disclosure_id` where a declared relationship exists
- `authority_id` of the pilot or directive, empty on the standard route
- `notification_id` assigned at send time, immutable, joinable from CRM to the case; `notification_link_type` is `immutable_id` or is rejected as evidence
- deadline fields already resolved by the control service: `quality_deadline_at`, `approval_deadline_at`

CRM rows that cannot be joined on `notification_id` stay in a quarantine table. They are not used to insert `Notify_Requester`. Alias maps are versioned with `valid_from` and `valid_to`, so two accounts can be proved to be one person as in SUP-712, and a later merge cannot rewrite the historical decision. The application blocks activation when the selected route’s guard fails, rather than logging a free-text override and discovering it at audit.
