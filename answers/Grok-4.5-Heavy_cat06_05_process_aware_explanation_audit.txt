## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite must be executed on every training case, every scored prefix and every generated explanation. It comprises six interlocking checks:

1. **Prefix-time availability**  
   Reconstruct the event prefix that ends exactly at the first Coverage Check Completed (t0). Recompute or retrieve every feature using only events, attributes and external joins whose effective timestamps are ≤ t0.  
   Implementation requires a feature store that persists (value, as_of_timestamp) pairs; any feature whose as_of > t0 or whose source query lacks a ≤ t0 filter is rejected. Registration-time and coverage-time attributes must be explicitly frozen.  
   Limitation: many warehouse “final_” or “current_” fields lack historical versions; absence of an as_of timestamp forces exclusion.

2. **Activity-order feasibility**  
   Maintain a normative process model expressed as declarative Declare constraints or a Petri-net / BPMN.  
   - Precedence / succession: Damage Assessment Completed ≺ Claim Closed; Settlement Decision (Partial or Deny) ≺ Appeal ≺ possible second Settlement Decision.  
   - Absence: Claim Closed cannot precede Damage Assessment.  
   Replay each full trace (alignment-based fitness) and each candidate pattern; flag any deviation that reduces fitness or violates a hard constraint.  
   Footprint / DFG comparison is used only diagnostically: compute the observed directly-follows relation and contrast it with the *allowed* DFG derived from the normative model. A frequent observed edge is never treated as normative; the edge Claim Closed → Damage Assessment, even if present because of timestamp errors, is forbidden.

3. **Same-timestamp ambiguity**  
   Detect every pair of events that share an identical timestamp. Require a reliable secondary sequence index (ingestion order, event_id or source-system sequence). When the index is missing (Case Z), the pair and any pattern derived from it are marked unreliable and excluded from feature construction and explanation. Optional repair: order the pair according to normative constraints when the ordering is unique; otherwise drop the pair.

4. **Complete-trace information detection**  
   Heuristics plus static analysis of feature definitions flag any aggregation that references case-end, max(event_time), “anywhere”, “total”, “final_”, “last_”, “variant_id of full trace” or post-t0 activity counts.  
   Empirical check: recompute the feature on the true prefix; a large value change indicates leakage. All such features are barred from early-prediction models.

5. **Enabled / impossible / future-possible activities at t0**  
   From the process state reached after Coverage Check Completed determine:  
   - Enabled or immediately possible: Documents Requested, Damage Assessment (fast-track), Fraud Investigation Opened (trigger-dependent, may run in parallel).  
   - Impossible: Claim Closed, Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded, any post-decision activity.  
   - Future-possible only after further progress: later evidence cycles, second Settlement after Appeal.  
   Any explanation that asserts an impossible activity has already occurred, or that treats a purely future-possible activity as observed, is invalidated. Token-based replay or a state machine implements the check.

6. **Repeated Settlement Decision legitimacy**  
   For any trace or pattern containing multiple Settlement Decision Recorded events, verify that each subsequent decision is preceded by an Appeal that itself follows a Partial or Deny decision. Unconditioned counts of Settlement Decision remain leakage if they include post-t0 events, but the semantic pattern “Appeal → Settlement” is process-legitimate once the appeal path is taken. The check therefore separates data-quality errors from genuine multi-decision traces.

**Suite governance**  
Critical violations (impossible order used as a feature, full-trace aggregates present, missing sequence index on ambiguous pairs) block model training and explanation emission. Medium violations (proxy features, calendar drift) are logged for fairness and robustness review.

**Limitations**  
The normative model itself may be incomplete (unmodelled exceptions, parallel Fraud). Alignment fitness quantifies conformance, not causality. Purely discovered DFG / footprint matrices overfit logging errors and cannot replace expert constraints. Same-timestamp resolution ultimately requires source-system improvements. The suite therefore distinguishes pure temporal leakage from process-semantic inconsistency and from merely future-possible activities.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Surviving or repairable signals are classified as follows. Predictive validity, causal interpretation, operational actionability and fairness review are deliberately separated standards.

**Defensible predictive signal**  
- `initial_damage_complexity` (High raises risk): known and frozen before t0. May claim a statistical association with longer processing (more evidence or specialist review). May **not** claim that complexity “causes” breach or is the sole actionable lever; complexity is largely exogenous to operational decisions.  
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`: legitimate prefix-derived age. Higher values leave less remaining slack to the fixed 10-day target and are therefore predictive of breach. The feature does not cause the process delays themselves.  
- (After SQL repair) `prior_claim_count_before_t0`: prior history may indicate claimant complexity or fraud propensity. Predictive validity is restored once the filter is strict `< t0`. Not automatically actionable at the individual-case level without policy.

**Process-state or congestion signal**  
- `queue_length_at_t0`: valid within the documented tolerance. Reflects system load at prediction time; higher values are associated with higher breach risk via delayed handling. Operationally relevant for capacity planning, yet the contribution to an individual case remains correlational. More actionable at aggregate (staffing) than at single-claim level.

**Proxy requiring fairness / robustness review**  
- `claimant_region`: available at registration. Correlated with repair-network density, language, channel mix and socioeconomics; predictive power may be real via those mediators. Must **not** be presented as “region causes delay.” Fairness review (disparate impact, equalised odds) is required because region may proxy protected attributes. Robustness to distribution shift must also be checked.  
- `claimant_channel` (Broker): similar proxy status; channel may associate with claim complexity, documentation quality or service-level agreements. Retain only after lineage confirmation and fairness review.  
- `initial_reserve` (if confirmed available at/before t0): high reserve may proxy severity or complexity. Predictive use is acceptable; causal stories are limited and the feature may itself influence prioritisation.

**Drift / calendar proxy**  
- `calendar_week`: known at t0. Strong historical alignment with policy changes and holidays means the feature captures temporary capacity or rule shifts. Predictive in the observed window, but fragile under concept drift. Do not claim an inherent “week X is worse”; prefer explicit holiday or policy indicators when available and monitor drift continuously.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**  
- `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team` (no historical timestamp), `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `prior_claim_count` (current SQL), `risk_score_current` (mutable, no timestamp), `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, and all full-trace sequence patterns: pure leakage or mutable. Remove from the model and from explanations until they are recomputed on true prefixes or with proper event-time joins and immutability guarantees.  
- After repair (e.g., `handler_team_at_t0`, `num_doc_requests_up_to_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`) the repaired features must be reclassified according to the categories above.

Standards distinction  
- Predictive validity requires only statistical association on admissible (prefix) features and proper calibration.  
- Causal interpretation requires intervention assumptions and counterfactuals; SHAP-style contributions never establish causality.  
- Operational actionability requires that the feature be controllable and that the effect under intervention be estimated; congestion is more actionable than region.  
- Fairness review is required for any proxy that may correlate with protected attributes, even when the feature is predictively useful.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Prioritized plan:

1. **Feature-store redesign (immediate)**  
   Persist every feature value together with an explicit as_of_timestamp. Enforce that training, scoring and explanation pipelines may read only rows whose as_of ≤ t0. Ban any “current_”, “final_” or “extraction-date” join that lacks historical versioning.

2. **Historical feature timestamps and lineage repair (weeks 1–4)**  
   - Correct the `prior_claim_count` SQL to `claim_created_at < t0`.  
   - Convert `same_day_document_count_snapshot` and `risk_score_current` to true point-in-time snapshots.  
   - Materialise `handler_team_at_t0`, `resource_role_at_t0` and `num_document_requests_up_to_t0`.  
   - Store employee-experience as-of the relevant event date.

3. **Prefix-only recomputation (weeks 2–5)**  
   Rebuild the entire feature matrix on prefixes truncated at the first Coverage Check Completed. Re-train and re-explain; quantify the drop in apparent importance of previously leaked features. Any feature whose importance collapses is confirmed leakage.

4. **Process-conformance test suite (weeks 3–6)**  
   Implement the six checks of Section 2 (prefix availability, declarative/alignment order, same-timestamp index, complete-trace detection, enabled-activity state, repeated-Settlement legitimacy). Gate both model training and explanation generation on critical-violation-free status. Treat frequent DFG edges as diagnostic only, never as normative rules.

5. **Calibration after feature removal (weeks 5–7)**  
   After stripping all critical and high-severity leakage features, re-calibrate probabilities (isotonic or Platt). Report both discrimination and calibration metrics on a pure prefix hold-out set. Expect a drop in apparent performance that reflects removal of leakage rather than true loss of signal.

6. **Explanation stability and local fidelity (ongoing)**  
   For every local explanation recompute SHAP/LIME-style values on the repaired prefix feature set. Flag any local explanation that still references a removed or repaired feature. Require that the sum of retained contributions plus base value approximately recovers the model output.

7. **Proxy and fairness analysis (parallel, weeks 4–8)**  
   For `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel` and any other demographic or geographic proxies run disparate-impact, equalised-odds and counterfactual-fairness checks. Document residual predictive value after controlling for the legitimate mediators (network coverage, language, complexity). Decide retention policy explicitly rather than by automatic deletion.

8. **Monitoring for schema and process drift (production)**  
   - Track calendar-week and policy-change indicators for concept drift.  
   - Monitor the frequency of same-timestamp pairs and missing sequence indices.  
   - Alert when new activity labels, new parallel paths (e.g., expanded Fraud triggers) or changes in the normative model appear.  
   - Periodically re-run the full conformance suite on recent prefixes and refresh explanations.

Execution of the above sequence converts the current leakage-dominated explanations into process-aware, temporally consistent and fairness-reviewed accounts that respect the information actually available at the Coverage Check Completed prediction point.
