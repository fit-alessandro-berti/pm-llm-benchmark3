## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 80/100** that case allocation is a major driver of the crude difference, not that Orion has zero causal effect.

Orion averages 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24 hours, yet Orion is faster within every recorded complexity category: by 2.14 hours for low, 2.21 for medium, and 3.43 for high complexity. This aggregate-versus-stratified reversal makes the overall mean an unsuitable justification for moving work. Orion also receives more difficult cases and disproportionately covers nights.  

The strongest evidence against treating this as a settled confounding explanation is weak joint comparability. Orion has only two low-complexity cases; Nova has only two high-complexity cases. There are no medium- or high-complexity daytime Orion cases, and every shared complexity–shift cell contains a group with fewer than three observations. Finer risk differences, selected completion, and genuine group effects can therefore coexist with the reversal. 

Process mechanisms remain plausible. Recorded waiting accounts for most of the complexity-standardized difference, while review-service differences are smaller. However, that waiting includes compliance-related delays; it is not a clean measure of reviewer productivity.  

Reassignment could succeed if Orion has remediable congestion and Nova has qualified spare capacity. It could fail because transferred cases retain their difficulty and mandatory checks. It could backfire by removing specialist support or congesting Nova’s existing workload. Neither spare capacity nor these counterfactual responses is measured. **Reject the blanket 50% transfer; test targeted capacity changes and limited randomized routing instead.**

This is a candidate DAG, not an identified model. Routing-to-group, difficulty-related routing, required-to-performed compliance, and repeated review contributing to service have supplied support. Other directional effects are hypotheses. Here shift/load precede assignment; skill means the selected reviewer’s skill, not skill created by a label. If shift is the actual work shift, it may instead follow assignment. The static DAG suppresses Review–Request Info–Review dynamics and cross-case feedback: today’s routing and rework change tomorrow’s backlog, waiting, and routing. 

## 3. Variable-role table

**Estimands must precede adjustment.** For an individual-assignment comparison, define a population of cases eligible for both groups, specify staffing and routing intensity, and compare assignment to Orion versus Nova. A prospective primary outcome should be assignment-to-close time. The supplied whole-case cycle time includes a pre-assignment prefix that reassignment cannot change, although that prefix could differ between the observed groups.

The **total effect** allows assignment to change the selected reviewer, waiting, compliance coordination, and rework. A **controlled direct effect** compares assignments while imposing the same *feasible joint regime* for specified mediators. It is direct only relative to those mediators—not an intrinsic effect of the group’s name. Simply conditioning on observed waiting and service does not identify it.

A **mediated effect** concerns pathways such as assignment → reviewer selection → rework → completion, or assignment → compliance coordination → waiting → completion. Numerical component differences are not automatically causal indirect effects.

The **50% reassignment policy effect** is different again: it changes the workload distribution and potentially other cases’ waiting. An individual comparison at today’s operating conditions cannot identify that system-wide effect.

**Auditable standardization.** The pooled completed-case complexity distribution is Low \(12/30\), Medium \(10/30\), High \(8/30\). Thus:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar Y_O^{std}
&=\frac{12}{30}(7.50)+\frac{10}{30}(17.1667)+\frac{8}{30}(31.5667)
=17.140,\\
\bar Y_N^{std}
&=\frac{12}{30}(9.64)+\frac{10}{30}(19.375)+\frac{8}{30}(35)
=19.6477.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are standardized **descriptions of this sample**, not estimates validated for future arrivals.

Using Orion’s own complexity distribution instead gives a reweighted Nova mean of **24.681 hours**, versus Orion’s observed **21.957 hours**. With Nova’s stratum means as the reference, the crude difference decomposes into a **+9.437-hour composition term** and a **−2.724-hour within-stratum term**, summing to **+6.713 hours**. This explains the reversal arithmetically; it does not establish causal attribution. 

**Time accounting is not mediation identification.** Define the residual \(R=Y-W-S\). Under the pooled weights:

$$
\Delta Y^{std}
=\Delta W^{std}+\Delta S^{std}+\Delta R^{std}
=-2.145-0.269-0.094=-2.508\text{ h}.
$$

Recorded waiting represents approximately **85.5% of this arithmetic difference**. The residual is not an identified process stage: it can contain other elapsed components and measurement discrepancies. A physical decomposition additionally requires compatible clocks and non-overlapping duration definitions. Repeated review service is already included in \(S\); adding a separate rework-service penalty would double-count it. 

**Overlap is the binding limitation.** There are no daytime Orion observations for medium or high complexity. Every complexity–shift cell containing both groups has at least one group with fewer than three cases. Adding load and compliance requirement leaves only four shared cells, covering 20 of the 30 cases; each still has a group with one or two observations. Those absences demonstrate missing empirical support, not necessarily operational ineligibility. 

**Process structure also differs mainly in frequencies.** The four variant counts, Orion/Nova, are: neither optional step **4/10**; compliance without rework **6/3**; information request without compliance **1/2**; both **3/1**. Both groups have the same seven observed directly-follows relations. Compliance-path frequency is \(9/14\) versus \(4/16\), and `Review → Request Info` frequency is \(4/14\) versus \(3/16\). Identical edge support does not establish equal timing, quality, or causal mechanisms. The recorded traces agree with the requirement flags, but that is not an independent audit of compliance execution.

**Analysis boundaries:** propensity scores cannot manufacture comparators for empty cells. Restrict observational estimates to stated support and report how the target population changes. A front-door-style identification claim is also unsupported: risk and operating conditions may confound mediator relationships, and complete mediation through the measured components has not been established.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

The following safeguards are **proposed governance choices**, not tolerances supplied by management. Immediately pause for a mandatory-check failure or an ineligible assignment. A greater than 15% deterioration in Nova’s p90 queue age at two prespecified reviews can serve as an operational pause trigger against matched control periods—not as a statistical significance test. Pre-register effectiveness, quality, and sequential-monitoring criteria before randomization.

**Final recommendation.** Do not approve the proposed 50% reassignment on the strength of overall means. Retain the present allocation as the operational default while correcting the information gaps; this is not an endorsement that current routing is optimal.

Start with a capacity intervention that preserves mandatory checks and reviewer qualifications. Run the routing experiment separately, or factorially only with enough support to distinguish the interventions. Its initial scope should be dual-eligible cases that both teams can handle in the same operating window, not a convenience sample of daytime low-risk work used to justify moving difficult night cases.

Make the decision on intention-to-treat resolution, aged open work, completed quality follow-up, and spillovers to nonpilot cases. A faster transferred-case average is insufficient if Nova’s original cases deteriorate. Conversely, improvement from extra compliance coverage with unchanged group labels would favor a capacity solution over organizational reassignment. Expand diversion only after separate evidence on increased routing intensity; a small pilot cannot validate a 50% system-wide change.

C28,Register>Classify>Review>Request Info>Review>Close,Medium,Nova,Day,High,No,12.0,6.2,1,20.5,No appeal
C29,Register>Classify>Compliance Check>Review>Close,High,Nova,Night,High,Yes,22.6,9.2,0,34.0,No appeal
C30,Register>Classify>Compliance Check>Review>Request Info>Review>Close,High,Nova,Day,Normal,Yes,22.9,9.4,1,36.0,No appeal"""

GROUPS = ["Orion", "Nova"]
LEVELS = ["Low", "Medium", "High"]


def warn_small(counts: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> None:
    """Report sparse cells and missing comparison groups, without imputing."""
    for cell, row in counts.iterrows():
        for group in GROUPS:
            n = int(row[group])
            if n < 3:
                print(f"WARNING [{label}] {cell!r}, {group}: n={n} < 3.")
        if row.eq(0).any():
            print(f"WARNING [{label}] {cell!r}: no empirical two-group overlap.")


def support(df: pd.DataFrame, keys: list[str], label: str,
            full_index=None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    counts = (df.groupby(keys + ["group"], dropna=False).size()
              .unstack("group", fill_value=0)
              .reindex(columns=GROUPS, fill_value=0))
    if full_index is not None:
        counts = counts.reindex(full_index, fill_value=0)
    print(f"\n{label} (case counts):\n{counts.to_string()}")
    warn_small(counts, label)
    return counts


def summary(df: pd.DataFrame, keys: list[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    return df.groupby(keys, dropna=False).agg(
        n=("case_id", "size"),
        mean_cycle_h=("cycle_time_h", "mean"),
        median_cycle_h=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
        mean_wait_h=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"),
        mean_service_h=("review_service_h", "mean"),
        rework_fraction=("has_rework", "mean"),
        appeal_fraction=("has_appeal", "mean"),
    )


def main() -> None:
    df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(CSV))
    if len(df) != 30 or not df["case_id"].is_unique or df.isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Expected 30 unique, nonmissing supplied case records.")
    if set(df["group"]) != set(GROUPS) or set(df["complexity"]) != set(LEVELS):
        raise ValueError("Unexpected group or complexity labels.")
    numeric = ["wait_before_review_h", "review_service_h", "rework_count", "cycle_time_h"]
    df[numeric] = df[numeric].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors="raise")
    if df[numeric].lt(0).any().any():
        raise ValueError("Negative duration or rework count.")
    sequences = df["trace"].str.split(">")
    if not sequences.map(lambda s: s[0] == "Register" and s[-1] == "Close").all():
        raise ValueError("Unexpected trace boundary.")
    recorded_check = sequences.map(lambda s: "Compliance Check" in s)
    if not recorded_check.eq(df["compliance_required"].eq("Yes")).all():
        raise ValueError("Compliance flag/trace disagreement.")
    if not sequences.map(lambda s: s.count("Request Info")).eq(df["rework_count"]).all():
        raise ValueError("Request Info counts disagree with supplied rework counts.")
    df["has_rework"] = df["rework_count"].gt(0)
    df["has_appeal"] = df["outcome_30d"].eq("Appeal")
    df["residual_h"] = (df["cycle_time_h"] - df["wait_before_review_h"]
                        - df["review_service_h"])
    print("Pandas version:", pd.__version__)
    print("All contrasts are Orion minus Nova; negative means shorter for Orion.")
    print("WARNING: completed cases only; no exchangeability or policy-effect claim.")
    print("WARNING: recorded waiting includes compliance-related waiting, not just queueing.")
    print("WARNING: total review service already includes repeated review service.")

    crude = summary(df, ["group"]).reindex(GROUPS)
    print("\nCrude comparisons:\n", crude.round(4).to_string())
    print("Crude mean contrast (h):",
          round(crude.loc["Orion", "mean_cycle_h"] - crude.loc["Nova", "mean_cycle_h"], 6))
    print("\nComplexity-stratified comparisons:\n",
          summary(df, ["complexity", "group"]).round(4).to_string())
    support(df, ["complexity"], "Complexity support",
            pd.Index(LEVELS, name="complexity"))
    for key in ["shift", "arrival_load", "compliance_required"]:
        print(f"\n{key} summaries:\n{summary(df, [key, 'group']).round(4).to_string()}")
        support(df, [key], f"{key} support")

    # Common target: the pooled empirical complexity distribution of these 30 COMPLETED cases.
    # Low=12/30, Medium=10/30, High=8/30. This is not a future-arrival distribution.
    weights = (df["complexity"].value_counts().reindex(LEVELS) / len(df))
    print("\nCommon complexity weights:\n", weights.to_string())
    metrics = ["cycle_time_h", "wait_before_review_h", "review_service_h", "residual_h"]
    standardized = []
    for metric in metrics:
        means = (df.groupby(["complexity", "group"])[metric].mean()
                 .unstack("group").reindex(index=LEVELS, columns=GROUPS))
        if means.isna().any().any():
            raise ValueError("Missing complexity/group mean; refusing to extrapolate.")
        values = means.mul(weights, axis=0).sum(axis=0)
        standardized.append({"measure": metric, "Orion": values["Orion"],
                             "Nova": values["Nova"],
                             "Orion_minus_Nova": values["Orion"] - values["Nova"]})
    result = pd.DataFrame(standardized).set_index("measure")
    print("\nComplexity-standardized descriptive comparisons:\n", result.round(6).to_string())
    print("WARNING: the component split is arithmetic, not causal mediation.")

    # Include zero cells, not merely cells observed for both groups.
    full_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
        [LEVELS, ["Day", "Night"]], names=["complexity", "shift"])
    support(df, ["complexity", "shift"], "Complexity x shift x group", full_index)
    joint = support(df, ["complexity", "shift", "arrival_load", "compliance_required"],
                    "Joint PRE-ASSIGNMENT support (timing assumption)")
    shared = joint.gt(0).all(axis=1)
    print("Cases in joint cells having both groups:", int(joint.loc[shared].to_numpy().sum()),
          "of", len(df))
    if shared.any() and (joint.loc[shared].min(axis=1) < 3).all():
        print("WARNING: every shared joint cell here has a group with fewer than three cases.")
    if df.loc[df["shift"].eq("Night"), "arrival_load"].eq("High").all():
        print("WARNING: all observed night cases have high load; no night/normal-load contrast.")
    print("WARNING: missing cells do not establish structural impossibility; eligibility is unknown.")

    # PM4Py is used for the process-mining operations. Missing dependency is explicit.
    try:
        import pm4py
        from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
    except ImportError as exc:
        print("WARNING: PM4Py diagnostics NOT executed. Install with: python -m pip install pm4py")
        print("Import error:", exc)
        return
    print("\nPM4Py version:", getattr(pm4py, "__version__", "unknown"))
    variants_by_group, dfg_by_group = {}, {}
    for group in GROUPS:
        sub = df.loc[df["group"].eq(group)]
        # A sequence-only EventLog avoids inventing clock times.
        # resource_group is a case assignment attribute, not an observed event-level reviewer.
        log = EventLog()
        for row in sub.itertuples(index=False):
            trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": row.case_id, "resource_group": group})
            for activity in row.trace.split(">"):
                trace.append(Event({"concept:name": activity}))
            log.append(trace)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples(log)
        variants_by_group[group] = {
            ">".join(variant): int(members) if isinstance(members, Integral) else len(members)
            for variant, members in variants.items()
        }
        dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)  # FREQUENCY only; no performance API.
        dfg_by_group[group] = {f"{a} -> {b}": int(n) for (a, b), n in dfg.items()}
        if sum(variants_by_group[group].values()) != len(sub):
            raise ValueError("Variant frequencies do not sum to case count.")
        print(f"{group}: start activities={starts}; end activities={ends}")

    variants = (pd.DataFrame(variants_by_group).fillna(0).astype(int)
                .reindex(columns=GROUPS).sort_index())
    sizes = df["group"].value_counts().reindex(GROUPS)
    print("\nVariant case counts:\n", variants.to_string())
    print("\nVariant percentages of group cases:\n",
          variants.div(sizes, axis=1).mul(100).round(2).to_string())
    warn_small(variants, "Variant support (post-assignment, NOT an adjustment set)")
    edges = (pd.DataFrame(dfg_by_group).fillna(0).astype(int)
             .reindex(columns=GROUPS).sort_index())
    print("\nDirectly-follows occurrence counts:\n", edges.to_string())
    print("\nDirectly-follows occurrences per 100 group cases:\n",
          edges.div(sizes, axis=1).mul(100).round(2).to_string())
    print("DFG counts are occurrences, not generally distinct-case counts or transition probabilities.")
    print("WARNING: shared DFG edges do not establish equal process quality or causal equivalence.")
    print("WARNING: no real event timestamps; performance DFG and queue reconstruction are unavailable.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case-risk confounding:** Cases with the same recorded complexity may still differ substantially in difficulty. **Most valuable additional field:** the immutable pre-assignment risk score actually used by the routing engine. It would improve baseline comparability without conditioning on downstream process behavior.

2. **Expertise versus organizational context:** Any genuine group effect may arise from reviewer selection rather than group-specific practices. **Most valuable additional field:** `reviewer_id` on review events. This enables carefully qualified within-reviewer comparisons where reviewers work across contexts, without pretending those comparisons identify the full expertise-selection effect.

3. **Where waiting actually occurs:** The current waiting field cannot separate review queueing, compliance readiness, calendar gaps, and other delays. **Most valuable design change:** lifecycle instrumentation of classification, compliance, and every review visit, recording readiness, start, and completion as distinct events.

4. **Completed-sample selection and incomplete quality ascertainment:** The supplied sample cannot establish performance for all arrivals or comparable follow-up after different completion patterns. **Most valuable design change:** a prospective inception cohort following every assignment through closure or abandonment and the specified post-closure quality window, retaining unresolved cases in the analysis.

5. **Transferability from a small pilot to a 50% policy:** Low-intensity reassignment may behave differently once Nova’s queues and Orion’s staffing utilization change. **Most valuable design change:** randomized diversion-intensity experiments at queue–time-block level, measuring outcomes for both transferred and nontransferred cases.
