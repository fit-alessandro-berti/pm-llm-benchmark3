## 1. Conformance semantics

Conformance should be assessed in two stages: first identify graph differences, then interpret each difference using status, scope, temporal semantics, and data lineage.

* **Forbidden behavior** exists when an observed relation is explicitly forbidden and its identity and scope are valid. Frequency is irrelevant: one valid occurrence is sufficient. A suspected extraction error can make the graph nonconforming without proving that the business process itself behaved improperly.
* **Missing mandatory behavior** is a mandatory relation not satisfied for an applicable event or object. It remains a candidate finding until the denominator, lifecycle completeness, and required abstraction—directly follows versus eventually follows—are verified.
* **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is observed behavior absent from the normative edge set but neither forbidden nor governed. Under the open-world assumption, it is not a violation.
* **Typical-pattern absence** means a common path was not observed. This may warrant operational analysis, but it is not noncompliance because legitimate exceptions are expressly allowed.
* **Likely data-quality error** is a relation better explained by extraction, joining, identifier, timestamp, or event–object-link corruption than by real business behavior.
* **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the graph relation does not represent the actual business rule. Most importantly, an eventually-follows obligation cannot safely be tested as a directly-follows obligation.
* **Insufficient evidence** applies when aggregate graph counts cannot establish event identity, per-event cardinality, temporal ordering, applicability, or lifecycle maturity.

The norm is **closed-world** only for explicitly forbidden relations and mandatory requirements within their proven scope. Thus, N3, T6, and E10 have zero-tolerance semantics, while N1, N4, and E1–E8 require coverage testing for applicable units. It is **open-world** for typical and unspecified relations: an additional edge is not a violation merely because it is absent from the normative graph.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can establish that two activities were observed as adjacent along objects of a given type, together with aggregate frequency information. It cannot establish causality, business legitimacy, complete eventual-follow behavior, or why an edge is absent.

The main danger is N1. Its business meaning is eventual entry into validation, but the summary approximates it using `Create Order -> Validate Order`. A trace such as:

`Create Order -> Manual Review -> Validate Order`

satisfies the eventual obligation while violating the direct-edge proxy. Therefore, the two missing direct edges are not two proven unvalidated Orders. They require an object-level eventual-follow query.

The reverse problem applies to N3. A direct `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` edge is strong evidence of an after-cancellation dispatch, but a direct-follows graph may **miss** other forbidden cases such as:

`Cancel Order -> Administrative Update -> Dispatch Package`.

N3 therefore needs a temporal “any later dispatch” check, not merely edge comparison.

N4 has a similar potential adjacency issue: the two missing Pack-to-Dispatch edges could represent absent packing, or valid intervening inspection and carrier-booking events. N2 is only typical, so its absence is not a compliance failure. Counts also need care: edge-level object sets can overlap when an Order has repeated validations or several lifecycle branches.

### OTG

The OTG establishes that object types participate in interaction, cobirth, codeath, or other object-level relationships. It cannot establish that every triggering event had all required object types.

Consequently, Order–Item weight 1450 does not prove E2 for every `Add Item` event, and Package–Delivery weight 200 does not clear the two dispatch events lacking Delivery links. Those object pairs may interact in another event. An OTG can therefore appear conforming at the type level while ET–OT evidence remains suspicious at the event level.

T6 is different because cobirth is explicitly forbidden. One occurrence is a candidate violation regardless of its rarity, although the lifecycle-construction logic must be verified. Package–Delivery codeath is neutral because it is unregulated. Likewise, Order–Customer and Order–RiskCase edges remain outside this norm.

No minimum frequency or tolerance is specified. A graph-comparison algorithm may flag a frequency difference, but the norm supplies no authority for translating that difference into a violation.

### ET–OT

ET–OT is the most relevant family for E1–E10 because it represents event-type-to-object-type attachment. Even here, aggregate weights do not automatically prove per-event cardinality. The 1450-versus-1448 Add Item difference strongly suggests missing Order links, but distinct event IDs are needed to exclude multi-object events.

For E6, the carrier-reference attribute is useful evidence that shipment information exists, but it is not a Delivery object under the current rule. The two events therefore remain mandatory-link candidates until repaired or the norm expressly accepts an attribute-only legacy representation.

The 93-versus-92 payment discrepancy is conforming under T3’s broad typical semantics if it is genuine unapplied cash, yet nonconforming under E8 because the event was not explicitly typed that way. Thus, the same observation can be acceptable at the OTG business-pattern abstraction and suspicious at the ET–OT typing abstraction.

The Sensor–Invoice edge violates E10 as represented, but the supplied lineage makes a data-quality explanation much more credible than real process behavior.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**

* Aggregate evidence supports E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7, although per-event coverage should still be checked.
* N1 has two candidate gaps only if the Orders are standard, lifecycle-complete, and never later validated. Otherwise the direct-edge difference is an abstraction mismatch.
* N4 has two candidate gaps only if no earlier Pack event exists for those externally shipped Packages.
* E2 has a two-link aggregate discrepancy; it becomes confirmed missing behavior when individual Add Item events without an Order are identified.
* E6 has two candidate missing Delivery attachments. Carrier attributes do not satisfy the current wording.
* E8 has one current mandatory gap unless the event is relabeled as explicitly unapplied cash.
* T1 and T2 are present in aggregate, but event-local compliance depends on E2/E3 and E5/E6.

**Forbidden**

* N3: two cancellation-to-dispatch observations are candidate business violations unless object identity or temporal data are wrong.
* T6: one Order–Payment cobirth is forbidden if both lifecycles truly begin in the same business event.
* E10: one Sensor–Invoice relation is graph-level nonconformance, most likely caused by the faulty identifier join.

**Typical**

* N2 is observed for 83 Orders. Its absence on other variants is not a violation because intermediate events are allowed.
* N5/T3 are represented by 92 payment relations; four write-offs are legitimate exceptions, and 14 successor-less Invoices require maturity analysis rather than automatic noncompliance.
* Genuine unapplied cash is compatible with T3, but must be explicitly typed to qualify for E8’s exception.

**Unregulated**

* `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order`.
* Order–Customer, Order–RiskCase, Manual Review–RiskCase, and Manual Review–Customer relations.
* Package–Delivery codeath.
* These become violations only if another applicable policy forbids them or imposes mandatory qualifiers; rarity alone does not change their status.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

PM4Py’s high-level functions return an OC-DFG dictionary, an OTG tuple, an ET–OT tuple, and separate diagnostic dictionaries from the corresponding conformance calls. The implementation below preserves those raw diagnostics and applies normative status metadata independently. 

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from copy import deepcopy
from typing import Any, Dict, Hashable, Mapping

import pm4py


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Discover the observed models.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
observed_models = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Preserve PM4Py's raw graph-comparison diagnostics unchanged.
#    These outputs are technical diagnostics, not the final business
#    or legal classification.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
raw_pm4py_diagnostics = {
    "ocdfg": deepcopy(
        pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(observed_models["ocdfg"], norm_ocdfg)
    ),
    "otg": deepcopy(
        pm4py.conformance_otg(observed_models["otg"], norm_otg)
    ),
    "etot": deepcopy(
        pm4py.conformance_etot(observed_models["etot"], norm_etot)
    ),
}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Policy metadata MUST be maintained outside the bare graph models.
#
# A bare graph cannot represent:
# - mandatory versus typical versus forbidden versus unspecified;
# - scope predicates;
# - eventually-follows versus directly-follows semantics;
# - permitted exceptions such as explicitly typed unapplied cash.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
POLICY: Dict[str, Dict[Hashable, Dict[str, Any]]] = {
    "ocdfg": {
        ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): {
            "id": "N1",
            "status": "mandatory",
            "scope": "new standard Order",
            "business_semantics": "eventually_follows",
            "graph_representation": "directly_follows_proxy",
        },
        ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"): {
            "id": "N2",
            "status": "typical",
            "scope": "validated Orders",
        },
        ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): {
            "id": "N3",
            "status": "forbidden",
            "scope": "same Order",
            "business_semantics": "no_later_occurrence",
            "graph_representation": "directly_follows_under-approximation",
        },
        ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): {
            "id": "N4",
            "status": "mandatory",
            "scope": "Package intended for external shipment",
        },
        ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): {
            "id": "N5",
            "status": "typical",
            "scope": "issued Invoice",
        },
        ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"): {
            "id": "N6",
            "status": "unspecified",
        },
    },
    "otg": {
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"):
            {"id": "T1", "status": "mandatory", "scope": "Add Item interaction"},
        ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"):
            {"id": "T2", "status": "mandatory", "scope": "Dispatch Package interaction"},
        ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"):
            {"id": "T3", "status": "typical"},
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer"):
            {"id": "T4", "status": "unspecified"},
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"):
            {"id": "T5", "status": "unspecified"},
        ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"):
            {"id": "T6", "status": "forbidden"},
    },
    "etot": {
        ("Create Order", "Order"): {"id": "E1", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Add Item", "Order"): {"id": "E2", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Add Item", "Item"): {"id": "E3", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Pack Package", "Package"): {"id": "E4", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Dispatch Package", "Package"): {"id": "E5", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): {"id": "E6", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Record Payment", "Payment"): {"id": "E7", "status": "mandatory"},
        ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): {
            "id": "E8",
            "status": "mandatory",
            "exception": "event explicitly typed as unapplied cash",
        },
        ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"):
            {"id": "E9", "status": "unspecified"},
        ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): {
            "id": "E10",
            "status": "forbidden",
            "likely_data_quality": True,
        },
    },
}


def extract_edge_counts(family: str, graph: Any) -> Dict[Hashable, int]:
    """Normalize the three discovered graph forms into edge -> weight."""
    if family == "ocdfg":
        result: Dict[Hashable, int] = {}
        by_object_type = (
            graph.get("edges", {})
                 .get("event_couples", {})
        )
        for object_type, flows in by_object_type.items():
            for (source, target), event_couples in flows.items():
                result[(object_type, source, target)] = len(event_couples)
        return result

    if family == "otg":
        # (set_of_object_types, edge_to_weight)
        return dict(graph[1])

    if family == "etot":
        # (activities, object_types, edge_set, edge_to_weight)
        return dict(graph[3])

    raise ValueError(f"Unsupported family: {family}")


# Results of separate instance-level validators belong here.
# None means that aggregate graphs cannot settle scope/coverage.
#
# Example shape:
# COVERAGE_FACTS[("etot", ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"))] = {
#     "applicable_units": 200,
#     "satisfied_units": 198,
#     "scope_confirmed": True,
# }
COVERAGE_FACTS: Dict[tuple[str, Hashable], Dict[str, Any]] = {}


def classify_edge(
    family: str,
    edge: Hashable,
    count: int,
    metadata: Mapping[str, Any],
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    status = metadata.get("status", "unspecified")
    present = count > 0
    coverage = COVERAGE_FACTS.get((family, edge), {})
    applicable = coverage.get("applicable_units")
    satisfied = coverage.get("satisfied_units")
    scope_confirmed = coverage.get("scope_confirmed")

    warnings = []
    if (
        metadata.get("business_semantics") == "eventually_follows"
        and metadata.get("graph_representation") == "directly_follows_proxy"
    ):
        warnings.append("directly-follows proxy cannot prove eventual non-fulfilment")

    if metadata.get("graph_representation") == "directly_follows_under-approximation":
        warnings.append("non-adjacent forbidden occurrences may be missed")

    if metadata.get("likely_data_quality"):
        warnings.append("investigate lineage before attributing business behavior")

    if status == "forbidden":
        classification = (
            "candidate_forbidden_behavior" if present
            else "forbidden_relation_absent"
        )

    elif status == "mandatory":
        if applicable is not None and satisfied is not None:
            if satisfied < applicable:
                classification = "candidate_missing_mandatory_behavior"
            else:
                classification = "mandatory_coverage_satisfied"
        elif present:
            classification = "mandatory_edge_present_aggregate_only"
            warnings.append("per-event or per-object coverage remains unproven")
        else:
            classification = "candidate_missing_mandatory_behavior"
            if warnings:
                classification = "missing_mandatory_or_abstraction_mismatch"

    elif status == "typical":
        classification = (
            "typical_pattern_observed" if present
            else "typical_pattern_absent_not_a_violation"
        )

    else:
        classification = (
            "observed_but_unregulated" if present
            else "unregulated_and_unobserved"
        )

    if scope_confirmed is False:
        classification = "out_of_scope"
    elif scope_confirmed is None and metadata.get("scope"):
        warnings.append("scope requires instance-level confirmation")

    return {
        "family": family,
        "edge": edge,
        "observed_weight": count,
        "norm_id": metadata.get("id"),
        "norm_status": status,
        "classification": classification,
        "scope": metadata.get("scope"),
        "coverage": coverage or None,
        "warnings": warnings,
    }


semantic_classification: Dict[str, list[Dict[str, Any]]] = {}

for family, graph in observed_models.items():
    counts = extract_edge_counts(family, graph)
    family_policy = POLICY[family]

    # Include both governed edges and observed open-world additions.
    all_edges = set(family_policy) | set(counts)
    findings = []

    for edge in sorted(all_edges, key=repr):
        metadata = family_policy.get(
            edge,
            {
                "id": None,
                "status": "unspecified",
                "scope": "not covered by this norm",
            },
        )
        findings.append(
            classify_edge(
                family=family,
                edge=edge,
                count=counts.get(edge, 0),
                metadata=metadata,
            )
        )

    semantic_classification[family] = findings


analysis_bundle = {
    "discovered_models": observed_models,
    "raw_pm4py_diagnostics": raw_pm4py_diagnostics,
    "semantic_classification": semantic_classification,
}

# raw_pm4py_diagnostics remains available exactly as returned by PM4Py.
# semantic_classification is a separate, auditable interpretation layer.
```

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** First, isolate the two N3 Orders, stop any remaining dispatch or delivery action, and reconcile whether cancellation was effective at dispatch time. Second, quarantine the E10 Sensor–Invoice relation and any reports, models, or exports produced by the reused-identifier join. Preserve the original rows and lineage before correction. Inspect the T6 cobirth occurrence next, but do not characterize it as employee misconduct without confirming lifecycle derivation.

**Data repair.** Correct the identifier namespace and join predicate responsible for E10, then re-run all derived OCEL relations. Backfill valid Delivery links for the two E6 events, Order links for confirmed E2 gaps, and the Invoice link or explicit unapplied-cash classification for the E8 event. Recompute OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT summaries after repair.

**Process investigation.** Retrieve complete object-level histories for the two N1 Orders and two N4 Packages. Test eventual validation and Pack-before-Dispatch rather than adjacency alone. For N3, search for every dispatch occurring after cancellation, including paths with intervening events; the two direct edges may not be the complete violation set.

**Norm revision.** Replace eventual obligations encoded as direct edges with explicit temporal rules. Define standard-order scope, cancellation reversal, external-shipment scope, per-event object cardinalities, unapplied-cash typing, Delivery-object requirements, and lifecycle-relation derivation.

**Monitoring.** Use zero-tolerance alerts for validated N3, T6, and E10 occurrences. Monitor mandatory rules as numerator/denominator coverage over mature applicable units. Track typical patterns by age and business segment without labeling rare or alternative behavior as noncompliant. Add cross-family reconciliation checks, particularly OTG-versus-ET–OT attachment discrepancies and identifier-domain collisions.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are that N3, T6, and E10 are explicitly forbidden graph relations; unspecified additions are not violations; and absence of N2, N5, or T3 patterns is not automatically noncompliant. The interpretation of N1 and N4 depends on eventual-follow semantics, scope, and lifecycle completeness. E6 and E8 depend partly on unresolved representation rules, while E10—and potentially T6 and several missing attachments—depends on suspected data-quality defects. Graph summaries alone cannot determine affected event identities, exact temporal order, per-event cardinality, eventual satisfaction, scope eligibility, or actual business harm.
