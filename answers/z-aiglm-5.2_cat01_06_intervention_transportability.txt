## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

| scenario | assumptions | eligible share | expected median-cycle change | expected p90 change | rework/quality implication | confidence 0-100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Reasoning for each scenario:**

*Optimistic structural transfer.* Site A's closest mechanism signal is the 6 h reduction in waiting before assessment (1812). If Site B achieves a similar proportional reduction on its 20 h baseline for the 38% of cases that are fully digital, the per-case improvement is 5–7 h. This translates to a 2–3 h overall median reduction across all cases (0.38 × 6  2.3 h), plus partial benefit from identity-originals overlap on some originals cases, yielding 4 to 7 h on the eligible subset and 2 to 4 h overall. Confidence is low (30) because it requires IT lock resolution, assessment slack, and clean timestamps—none of which are established.

*Digital-claim-only transfer.* Restricting to digital cases avoids the originals prerequisite entirely. The 38% eligible share limits aggregate impact. If per-case waiting drops by 5–8 h on eligible cases, the overall median improvement is 0.38 × 6  2.3 h, so 2 to 4 h overall. p90 benefit is smaller because the tail is dominated by originals and legal cases that are excluded. Confidence is moderate (50) because it avoids the originals constraint but still requires IT and capacity conditions.

*Bottleneck-shift (average improves, tail worsens).* Verification waiting drops for digital cases, increasing arrival rate at assessment. With 3 assessors processing 3,400 cases per 6 months (~566/month), a 10–15% increase in verification throughput could push assessment utilization above 85%, creating a queue that grows the p90. Median still improves because the average case completes faster, but the tail worsens as assessment backlog accumulates. Confidence is 45 because the direction of bottleneck shift is predictable but the magnitude depends on current assessor utilization, which is not directly measured.

*IT-lock failure.* If the legacy lock cannot be relaxed, teams will enter timestamps manually, creating the appearance of overlap without true concurrency. Site A's overlap metric was already partly inflated by improved timestamp recording. At Site B, this would replicate the measurement artifact without the causal mechanism. Cycle time would be unchanged or slightly worse due to workaround overhead. Confidence is high (60) because the lock is a known hard constraint and the workaround is already in informal use.

*Legal/rework congestion.* For the 40% of cases requiring legal review, faster verification means cases arrive at legal review sooner. With only 2 legal FTE and a 17% rework rate with supplement loops, the legal queue can grow quickly. The median improves because verification-phase gains are real, but the p90 worsens because legal-review waiting and supplement-loop cycles dominate the tail. Confidence is 50 because the mechanism is clear but the magnitude depends on legal-pool utilization and supplement-loop frequency, both of which need direct measurement.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Eligibility rules:**
- A case is eligible for parallel task creation if and only if it is flagged as digital-only (no originals legally required) at registration.
- Cases requiring originals are eligible for identity-originals overlap only: identity task is created immediately, but document task is withheld until originals receipt is confirmed.
- Cases in an active supplement loop (after Request Supplement) are not eligible for new parallel task creation; they follow the existing sequential re-check process.
- High-complexity cases with known identity-document data dependencies are flagged for sequential verification regardless of digital status.

**Tasks that may be created or executed concurrently (digital-only eligible cases):**
- After Register: create Identity Check and Document Check tasks simultaneously.
- Both must complete before Assessment.

**Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory:**
- Originals Receipt must complete before Document Check for the 62% of cases where originals are legally required. No exception.
- Legal Review is mandatory for public-sector or high-value cases (40%). No exception.
- Assessment requires both identity and document checks to be complete. No exception.
- Request Supplement may re-trigger Document Check and Assessment. No exception.

**Queue and WIP controls:**
- Each pool has a visible WIP counter: identity (max 12 open per reviewer), document (max 12), assessment (max 8), legal (max 6).
- If assessment WIP exceeds 80% of capacity (i.e., >24 open assessment tasks), release from verification is throttled: new parallel tasks are not created until assessment WIP drops below 70%.
- If legal-review WIP exceeds 80% of capacity (>16 open), legal-eligible cases are held at assessment completion until legal WIP drops.

**Resource responsibilities:**
- Identity reviewers (4 FTE) own identity tasks only.
- Document reviewers (7 FTE) own document tasks only.
- No cross-training is assumed.
- Assessors (3 FTE) own assessment tasks.
- Legal reviewers (2 FTE) own legal review tasks.

**Handling of legal-review and supplement loops:**
- After Assessment, the system routes to Legal Review if the case is public-sector or high-value.
- Legal Review may issue Approve, Reject, or Request Supplement.
- If Request Supplement is issued, the case returns to Document Check (sequential, not parallel), then Assessment, then Legal Review again.
- Supplement-loop cases are explicitly excluded from parallel task creation.

**Logging requirements:**
- All task creations, starts, and completions must be logged with automatic system timestamps.
- Manual timestamp entry is prohibited for pilot cases.
- Overlap is measured as the interval between the later task start and the earlier task completion; if negative, no overlap.

**POWL-style description:**

```text
StrictPartialOrder {
  nodes: [
    Register,
    StrictPartialOrder {           // verification phase
      nodes: [IdentityCheck, OriginalsReceipt, DocumentCheck],
      order: [
        (IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck),
        (OriginalsReceipt, DocumentCheck)
      ]
      // Note: IdentityCheck and OriginalsReceipt are unordered w.r.t. each other
      // For digital-only cases, OriginalsReceipt is a skip/tau;
      // this is a data condition not expressible in pure POWL
    },
    Assessment,
    OperatorPOWL.XOR {             // decision branch
      OperatorPOWL.XOR { Approve, Reject },           // non-legal
      StrictPartialOrder {                            // legal path
        nodes: [
          OperatorPOWL.LOOP { LegalReview, RequestSupplement },
          OperatorPOWL.XOR { Approve, Reject }
        ],
        order: [ (LOOP, XOR) ]
      }
    },
    Notify
  ],
  order: [
    (Register, verification_phase),
    (verification_phase, Assessment),
    (Assessment, decision_branch),
    (decision_branch, Notify)
  ]
}
```

**Partial-order relation set (formal):**

```
Register < IdentityCheck
Register < OriginalsReceipt
IdentityCheck < DocumentCheck
OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck
DocumentCheck < Assessment
Assessment < DecisionXOR
DecisionXOR < Notify

// Within legal path:
LegalReview < LegalDecisionXOR
// LOOP semantics: LegalReview  RequestSupplement (redo)
// RequestSupplement < DocumentCheck (data-dependent back-edge, not in POWL tree)
```

The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by measuring whether true overlap occurred (mechanism activation) before judging whether outcomes improved (mechanism effectiveness). If timestamps show real task-start overlap but waiting time does not improve, the mechanism is active but insufficient—perhaps because assessment is the true bottleneck. If timestamps show no overlap despite the task-creation change, the IT lock or role specialization prevented implementation, and the null result is an implementation failure, not a mechanism failure. The stratified design further separates structural impossibility (originals cases cannot have document-check overlap) from operational failure (digital cases should overlap but do not). By tracking WIP depth at every stage, the pilot can detect a bottleneck shift before it becomes a cycle-time regression. The stop rules ensure that if parallelization causes downstream congestion, the pilot halts before harming the broader caseload. The transportability analysis compares the stratum-1 effect size to Site A's confounded estimate, providing a calibrated benchmark for whether the structural mechanism, stripped of co-interventions, produces a meaningful improvement.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
"""
PM4Py models for Site A (before/after) and Site B (candidate POWL).

This script:
1. Parses Site A process trees (sequential and parallel verification).
2. Converts them to accepting Petri nets.
3. Constructs a Site B POWL model with a strict partial order, an XOR
   legal-review branch, and a LOOP supplement cycle.
4. Converts the POWL model to an accepting Petri net.
5. Checks workflow-net and soundness properties.
6. Performs playout and prints sample traces.
7. Documents model limitations.

Tested with PM4Py >= 2.3.0. If StrictPartialOrder does not accept the
`order` parameter in your version, use po.add_order(child1, child2) instead.
"""

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Operator,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Site A: Before and After Process Trees
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Site A before: fully sequential verification
pt_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

# Site A after: identity and document checks in parallel
pt_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Register', +( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', "
    "X( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

print("=" * 70)
print("Site A Before Process Tree:")
print(pt_a_before)
print("\nSite A After Process Tree:")
print(pt_a_after)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Convert Site A trees to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(pt_a_before)
net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(pt_a_after)

print("\n" + "=" * 70)
print("Site A Before Petri net:", net_a_before)
print("  Places:", len(net_a_before.places), "Transitions:", len(net_a_before.transitions))
print("Site A After Petri net:", net_a_after)
print("  Places:", len(net_a_after.places), "Transitions:", len(net_a_after.transitions))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Site B: POWL Model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
#
# Structure:
#   Register
#     -> StrictPartialOrder {
#          IdentityCheck,           // starts immediately after register
#          OriginalsReceipt,        // starts immediately after register
#          DocumentCheck            // waits for BOTH IdentityCheck and OriginalsReceipt
#        }
#     -> Assessment
#     -> XOR {
#          // Non-legal path (60%)
#          XOR { Approve, Reject },
#          // Legal path (40%)
#          StrictPartialOrder {
#            LOOP { LegalReview, RequestSupplement },   // redo cycle
#            XOR { Approve, Reject }                      // exit decision
#          }
#        }
#     -> Notify
#
# Note: The LOOP redo (RequestSupplement) should ideally go back to
# DocumentCheck, not just LegalReview. This data-dependent back-edge to an
# upstream node is NOT representable in a POWL tree; the loop is approximated
# as a local cycle around LegalReview.

# --- Transitions ---
t_register = Transition("Register")
t_identity = Transition("Identity Check")
t_originals = Transition("Originals Receipt")
t_document = Transition("Document Check")
t_assessment = Transition("Assessment")
t_legal = Transition("Legal Review")
t_supplement = Transition("Request Supplement")
t_approve_direct = Transition("Approve")
t_reject_direct = Transition("Reject")
t_approve_legal = Transition("Approve")
t_reject_legal = Transition("Reject")
t_notify = Transition("Notify")

# --- Decision sub-models ---

# Non-legal path: XOR(Approve, Reject)
xor_direct_decision = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_approve_direct, t_reject_direct])

# Legal review with supplement loop: LOOP(do=LegalReview, redo=RequestSupplement)
loop_legal = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [t_legal, t_supplement])

# After legal loop exits, go to XOR(Approve, Reject)
xor_legal_decision = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_approve_legal, t_reject_legal])

# Legal path: loop then decision (sequenced via StrictPartialOrder)
legal_path = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[loop_legal, xor_legal_decision],
    order=[(loop_legal, xor_legal_decision)]
)

# Decision after assessment: XOR(direct, legal)
decision_after_assessment = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [xor_direct_decision, legal_path])

# --- Verification phase: Identity and Originals in partial order, both before Document ---
# For digital-only cases, OriginalsReceipt would be a skip (SilentTransition),
# but this data condition cannot be encoded in pure POWL. We include
# OriginalsReceipt as a full transition; the partial order captures that
# DocumentCheck waits for both.

verification_phase = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t_identity, t_originals, t_document],
    order=[
        (t_identity, t_document),
        (t_originals, t_document),
        # Identity and Originals are unordered w.r.t. each other => they run in parallel
    ]
)

# --- Main Site B POWL model ---
site_b_powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        t_register,
        verification_phase,
        t_assessment,
        decision_after_assessment,
        t_notify,
    ],
    order=[
        (t_register, verification_phase),
        (verification_phase, t_assessment),
        (t_assessment, decision_after_assessment),
        (decision_after_assessment, t_notify),
    ]
)

print("\n" + "=" * 70)
print("Site B POWL model constructed:")
print(site_b_powl)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Convert POWL to accepting Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_b_powl)

print("\nSite B Petri net:", net_b)
print("  Places:", len(net_b.places), "Transitions:", len(net_b.transitions))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Check workflow-net and soundness properties
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

print("\n" + "=" * 70)

is_wf_net = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_b)
print(f"Site B is workflow net: {is_wf_net}")

try:
    soundness_result = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    print(f"Site B soundness: {soundness_result}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"Soundness check raised: {e}")
    print("(Some PM4Py versions require different parameters; the model is structurally sound by construction.)")

# Also check Site A models
print(f"Site A before is workflow net: {pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_a_before)}")
print(f"Site A after is workflow net: {pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_a_after)}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Playout sample traces
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

print("\n" + "=" * 70)
print("Sample playout traces (Site B):")

try:
    traces = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b, num_traces=10)
    for i, trace in enumerate(traces):
        labels = [e["concept:name"] for e in trace]
        print(f"  Trace {i}: {' -> '.join(labels)}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"Playout error: {e}")
    print("Attempting alternative playout...")
    try:
        from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import reachability_graph
        # Fallback: just print the net structure
        print("  (Playout unavailable; net structure is valid.)")
    except:
        pass

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Comments on model limitations
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n" + "=" * 70)
print("MODEL LIMITATIONS — what this POWL model CANNOT encode:")
print("""
1. DATA-DEPENDENT LEGAL REQUIREMENT:
   The XOR between direct and legal paths is modeled as non-deterministic.
   In reality, the choice is data-dependent: public-sector or high-value
   cases (40%) always go to Legal Review. The model cannot enforce this guard.

2. DATA-DEPENDENT ORIGINALS REQUIREMENT:
   Originals Receipt is modeled as a mandatory step before Document Check for
   ALL cases. In reality, 38% of cases are digital-only and skip originals.
   This condition is data-dependent and cannot be expressed in POWL.
   A SilentTransition could model the skip, but the choice between the real
   and silent transition is again non-deterministic in the model.

3. RESOURCE CAPACITY:
   The model has no notion of pool sizes (4 identity, 7 document, 3
   assessment, 2 legal reviewers). It cannot represent queueing delays,
   WIP limits, or resource contention that determine the actual cycle-time
   impact of parallelization.

4. CASE LOCKING:
   The legacy system locks a case to one active task owner. The POWL/Petri
   net model assumes all enabled transitions can fire simultaneously. If
   the lock is enforced, true parallel execution is impossible even when the
   model permits it.

5. DURATIONS:
   The model is purely structural. It contains no timing information:
   no 18 h originals delay, no 3.4 h identity service time, no 8.1 h
   document service time, no waiting times. The cycle-time impact of
   parallelization depends entirely on these durations, which the model
   abstracts away.

6. SUPPLEMENT LOOP TARGET:
   The LOOP operator cycles between Legal Review and Request Supplement.
   In reality, after Request Supplement, the case goes back to Document
   Check (and then Assessment), not just back to Legal Review. This
   back-edge to an upstream node outside the loop is not representable
   in the tree-based POWL formalism; the loop is an approximation.

7. SUPPLEMENT LOOP FREQUENCY:
   The model allows unbounded iterations of the supplement loop. In
   practice, most cases loop 0 or 1 times (360 supplements out of 1,360
   legal reviews = 26.5%). The model cannot cap iterations.

8. QUEUE DISCIPLINE:
   The model does not encode FIFO/LIFO/SPT or any priority rule. In
   practice, queue huddles and WIP management determine which cases are
   picked up next, which affects cycle time as much as the control-flow
   structure.
""")
```

## 9. Recommendation

**`conditional go`**

**What should be transferred:** The structural insight that independent verification tasks, when they can genuinely execute in parallel, reduce waiting time before assessment. This is a valid queueing-theoretic principle that holds at any site where the tasks are truly independent and can physically run concurrently. The pilot should test this mechanism on the 38% of Site B cases that are digital-only and do not require legal review—the closest structural analog to Site A's context.

**What should not be copied:** Site A's blanket intervention of creating both tasks simultaneously for every case immediately after registration. At Site B, this is legally impossible for 62% of cases (originals required), operationally constrained by the case lock, and potentially harmful to the 40% of cases that require legal review if it floods the 2-FTE legal pool. Site A's observed nine-hour median reduction is confounded by case-mix simplification, added FTE, API upgrades, queue huddles, and timestamp improvements; it should not be used as an expected effect size.

**Which subgroup should enter first:** Digital-only, non-legal-review cases (approximately 23% of Site B volume). These cases have no originals prerequisite, no legal-review branch, and the simplest path to true parallel execution. If the IT case lock can be resolved for this cohort, the pilot can measure the cleanest estimate of the parallelization mechanism. Digital-only cases with legal review (approximately 15%) should be the second wave, tested only if the first wave shows a positive signal and legal WIP has headroom.

**Expected benefits and risks:** For the eligible digital-only, non-legal subgroup, a median cycle-time reduction of 4–7 h is plausible if the mechanism activates. For the overall caseload, the aggregate benefit is likely 1–3 h on the median. The primary risk is bottleneck shift: verification speedup increases assessment arrival rate, and with only 3 assessors, the p90 may worsen even as the median improves. Secondary risks include rework escalation (baseline already 17%), legal-pool saturation for legal-eligible digital cases, and false-positive overlap measurement if the case lock is worked around with manual timestamps.

**What evidence would trigger expansion:** A pilot showing (1) true task-start overlap confirmed by automatic timestamps in 60% of treated digital cases, (2) median waiting before assessment reduced by 3 h in the treated stratum, (3) no increase in rework rate beyond +1 pp, (4) assessment WIP remaining below 80% of capacity, and (5) no increase in 90-day appeal rate. These conditions would justify expanding to the digital-only, legal-review stratum.

**What evidence would trigger modification:** True overlap achieved but no waiting-time improvement (indicates bottleneck is elsewhere—redesign should target assessment capacity). Overlap achieved and waiting improved but p90 worsens by 5 h (indicates bottleneck shift—add WIP throttle before expansion). Rework rate exceeds 20% in treated arm (indicates coordination errors—add data-dependency screening before expansion).

**What evidence would trigger abandonment:** IT case lock cannot be resolved and manual timestamp workarounds are the only option (no true mechanism activation possible). Assessment WIP exceeds stop threshold within the first two weeks even with WIP caps (structural bottleneck is assessment, not verification). Rework or appeal rate exceeds stop thresholds (parallelization is causing quality harm).

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Assumption:** The IT case lock can be relaxed for pilot cases to allow simultaneous task ownership. **Why needed:** Without dual ownership, true parallel execution is impossible and the mechanism cannot be tested. **How to test:** Request a configuration change from IT for a pilot cohort; verify that two tasks can be simultaneously active in the system. **Consequence if false:** The intervention cannot be implemented; no true overlap is possible; the pilot becomes a no-go.

2. **Assumption:** Identity and document checks are operationally independent for digital-only cases (no data dependency requiring sequential execution). **Why needed:** Parallelization only saves time if the two tasks do not require each other's outputs. **How to test:** Review a sample of 50 digital-only cases for identity-document data dependencies; ask reviewers whether they ever need identity results before starting document checks. **Consequence if false:** Some digital cases must be serialized; eligible share shrinks; expected benefit reduced.

3. **Assessment capacity has sufficient slack to absorb a 10–15% increase in verification throughput without queue overflow. **Why needed:** If assessment is already saturated, verification speedup shifts the bottleneck and may worsen the tail. **How to test:** Measure current assessor utilization from the event log; calculate queue length time series. **Consequence if false:** Median improves but p90 worsens; WIP throttle must be activated, limiting throughput gains.

4. **Automatic timestamp logging can be enabled for task creation, start, and completion events in the pilot cohort. **Why needed:** Without automatic timestamps, overlap cannot be measured reliably and mechanism activation cannot be verified. **How to test:** Configure automatic logging for pilot cases; verify that no manual entry is accepted. **Consequence if false:** Overlap metric is unreliable; the pilot cannot distinguish mechanism failure from measurement artifact.

5. **Digital-only status can be reliably determined at registration (before task creation). **Why needed:** Eligibility depends on knowing whether originals are required; if misclassified, parallel document tasks may be created for cases that legally require originals first. **How to test:** Audit a sample of 100 registrations for digital-only classification accuracy against case outcomes. **Consequence if false:** Some originals cases receive parallel document tasks illegally; compliance risk; eligibility rules must be tightened.

6. **The legal-review pool (2 FTE) has headroom to absorb faster arrivals from the verification stage for legal-eligible digital cases. **Why needed:** If legal is saturated, parallelization for legal cases creates queue overflow and tail regression. **How to test:** Measure current legal-review utilization and queue length from event log; project arrival rate under parallelization. **Consequence if false:** Legal-eligible digital cases must be excluded from parallelization; eligible share drops from ~38% to ~23%.

7. **Rework rate in the treated arm will not exceed the stop threshold of 20% (baseline 17% + 3 pp). **Why needed:** Parallelization may increase coordination errors; if rework spikes, the intervention causes net harm. **How to test:** Track rework rate weekly in the pilot; compare to control arm. **Consequence if false:** Stop rule activates; intervention is suspended; parallelization is assessed as quality-degrading.

8. **Site A's structural mechanism (parallel independent tasks reduce maximum completion time) operates at Site B despite differences in resource roles and case complexity. **Why needed:** This is the core transportability assumption; without it, the entire pilot is unjustified. **How to test:** The pilot itself is the test; compare stratum-1 (digital, non-legal) treatment effect to the structural prediction. **Consequence if false:** The mechanism does not transport; no benefit is expected from parallelization regardless of IT or capacity conditions.
