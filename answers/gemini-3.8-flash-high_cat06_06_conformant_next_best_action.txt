## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Operational decision support in compliance-critical environments must balance disparate, non-commensurate harm categories:

1. **Quantifiable Operational Deadline Loss (P-884):**
   - Meeting the 90-minute deadline saves €20,000–€35,000 in direct operational loss (penalties, liquidity/settlement default). While substantial, this loss is strictly bounded, symmetric, and standard commercial risk.

2. **Customer Harm from False Rejection or Prolonged Hold:**
   - Erroneously rejecting an established supplier payment of €480,000 causes estimated customer harm of €15,000–€80,000 (supply chain disruption, breach of commercial contract, litigation).
   - Because the predicted probability of ultimate approval upon conformant review is high (**0.55–0.75**), immediate summary rejection (Action F) incurs an expected customer harm of \(0.55 \times 15,000\) to \(0.75 \times 80,000\) (\(\approx €8,250–€60,000\)).
   - A prolonged hold (Action E) causes delay harm but avoids the catastrophic finality of a wrongful contract cancellation.

3. **Regulatory, Legal, and Compliance Harm (Unlawful Execution):**
   - Direct monetary penalties: Plausibly **€100,000–€500,000**.
   - Asymmetric non-monetary consequences: Potential revocation of banking/payment institution license, termination of correspondent banking relationships (an existential threat to cross-border payment operations), personal liability for compliance officers, and mandatory public enforcement actions.
   - **Dominance Argument:** The lower bound of monetary regulatory harm (€100,000) is almost **three times the maximum operational benefit** (€35,000) of meeting the deadline. When unquantifiable non-monetary harms (existential institutional ruin) are included, any action with a non-zero probability of an unlawful execution (Actions A and B) is strictly dominated by all conformant paths, regardless of operational speed.

4. **Resource Spillover Trade-off (P-884 vs. P-901):**
   - P-901 is also a high-value transaction with a tight 70-minute deadline.
   - Action C robs S2 from P-901 for 45 minutes, increasing P-901's breach probability by **0.12–0.28**. If P-901 carries a similar €20,000–€35,000 SLA penalty, Action C incurs an expected spillover loss of **€2,400–€9,800** on P-901.
   - Action D preserves P-901 completely while utilizing idle waiting time to execute parallel alternate screening, maximizing overall system utility across the entire portfolio.

## 4. Conformance constraints

### Process rule mapping
- **Rule 1 & Rule 2:** Satisfied (Payment Registered, Sanctions Screening Completed).
- **Rule 3 (Auto Clear):** Inapplicable. The risk score is 72 (must be <40) and there is an active fuzzy match (confidence 0.46).
- **Rule 4 & Rule 5 (Analyst Review & Evidence Cycle):** R17 completed the initial review, requested evidence, and evidence was validated. Rule 5 explicitly authorizes repeating this cycle (Action E).
- **Rule 6 (Mandatory Second Officer Review):** Triggered on two independent grounds: current risk score \(\ge 70\) (72) AND unresolved fuzzy sanctions match. Second Officer Review is strictly required prior to Approve or Payment Executed.
  - *Action A directly violates Rule 6.*
  - *Action B attempts an illegal shortcut.*
  - *Actions C and D satisfy Rule 6.*
- **Rule 7 (Four-Eyes Principle):** The analyst performing the first review (R17) and the second officer must be different individuals.
  - *Action B directly violates Rule 7.*
  - *Actions C and D satisfy Rule 7 (S2 and S4 are distinct from R17).*
- **Rule 8 (Outcome Permissibility):** Approval, Rejection, or Hold pending SAR are valid post-review outcomes. Filing a SAR does not authorize execution.
- **Rule 9 (Execution Precondition):** Payment Executed is conformant *only after a valid approval path*.
  - *Actions A and B violate Rule 9.*
- **Rule 10 (Emergency Manual Override):** Permitted *only* when a Compliance Director authorizes it *and* a valid incident identifier is recorded.
  - *Action G maps to Rule 10, but neither condition is currently satisfied.*
- **Rule 11 (Tipping-Off Prohibition):** Mandates that any SAR filed (Actions D, E, F) must not be disclosed to the customer.

### Why Actions A and B cannot be recommended by numerical forecasting
In predictive process monitoring, predictive models operate under **lexicographic constraints**: process conformance and legal mandates are hard feasibility constraints that precede operational objective optimization.
A naive expected-value model might observe that if the actual probability of a sanctions breach is low (e.g., 5%), the expected fine (\(0.05 \times €300,000 = €15,000\)) is lower than the certain SLA loss (€25,000), seemingly favoring Action A or B. This reasoning is invalid:
1. **Hard Control Gates:** Mandatory compliance controls are regulatory licensing conditions. Bypassing them converts a standard transaction into a willful operational failure.
2. **Breakdown of Observational Transportability:** Observational training data reflects historical cases where controls were obeyed. Under intervention (deliberately skipping a second review), the counterfactual risk distribution shifts: regulators treat bypassed controls as intentional circumvention, escalating penalties to statutory maximums.
3. **Auditability and Deception:** Action B (self-certification) falsifies internal compliance audit logs by spoofing independent four-eyes verification. This constitutes corporate governance fraud, far exceeding a civil SLA breach.

### Evidence required for Actions F or G to become valid
- **To validate Action F (Documented Summary Rejection):**
  1. *Substantive match proof:* Objective evidence linking the supplier to an SDN/sanctions list (e.g., matching tax identification number, verified SWIFT BIC, or confirmed sanctioned beneficial owner).
  2. *Policy authority:* Clear institutional compliance policy granting authority to reject immediately based on an unresolved match \(\ge 0.45\) in a border zone without exhausting customer inquiry or second-officer concurrence.
- **To validate Action G (Emergency Override):**
  1. *Incident Identifier:* A formal incident ticket from the IT/Operational Incident Management system documenting an extraordinary systemic malfunction (e.g., total screening database corruption or telecommunication outage), rather than a routine commercial queue delay.
  2. *Director Authorization:* Explicit, cryptographically signed or documented signoff from the Compliance Director acknowledging the sanctions hit and formally accepting institutional liability.

Alternate screening finishes by t=25m; S4 picks up the clarified file at t=35m, completes an expedited review by t=60m, and authorizes execution by t=65–70m. **Both P-884 and P-901 meet their SLAs**, and compliance is fully preserved.

If telemetry at t=35–40m confirms S4 is delayed toward 65m, check S2's progress: if S2 has completed P-901 (P-901 deadline is 70m), S2 can immediately take P-884 *without* interrupting P-901. If S2 is still busy and P-901 would fail, management must weigh P-901's breach cost against P-884's. In a multi-case portfolio view, accepting a minor single-case breach on P-884 while protecting P-901 and ensuring rigorous sanctions screening is superior to inducing a high double-breach probability.
