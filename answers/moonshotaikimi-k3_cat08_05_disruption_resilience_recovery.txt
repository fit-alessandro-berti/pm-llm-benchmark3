## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**The distinction.** Resilience is *graceful, visible, reversible degradation*: service is preserved by substituting weaker but legitimate controls, the deviation is logged, time-boxed, and approved, and the process can return to normal without lying about what happened. Concealed noncompliance is *invisible degradation*: service metrics are preserved by deleting controls, and the records either fail to show the deletion or actively disguise it (backfilled scans, spreadsheet closures outside the system of record).

Four tests separate them in this case:
1. **Control or appearance of control?** B substitutes a real check; C keeps the *appearance* of a validated order with no check.
2. **Is the deviation visible and attributable?** Manual-mode events with reason codes (good E) vs. reconstructed scans that look normal (bad E).
3. **Is the evidence contemporaneous?** A custody form signed at the dock (good G-replacement) vs. a phone call logged tomorrow (weak H).
4. **Does it reverse cleanly?** D with a 24-hour expiry reverses; D running into Day 4 silently corrupts T3.

**True resilience:** A (only when carrying the control floor), B, F (modified), I and J (once explicit), K, L (governed), D (time-boxed), E and H (with provenance and risk-tiered evidence rules). These degrade *form* while preserving *function*: someone still verified authorization, a pharmacist still released, custody still changed hands with a signature.

**Risky shortcuts (concealed noncompliance):** C, G as practiced, and the "reconstructed scans" element of E, plus the spreadsheet invoice hold flagged as a move-on-log deviation. Each preserved a throughput or closure metric by removing a legal control *and* the evidence that it was removed. The ~150 excess controlled-substance dual-control misses in T2 (162 observed vs. ~10 expected at baseline rates) are the sharpest example: this is plausibly a regulator-reportable exposure, not a KPI wobble.

**Ambiguous / risk-appetite dependent:** A without the floor (fast lane *through* substitute controls vs. fast lane *around* controls — same DFG shape, opposite meaning); H for non-controlled ambient with prompt signature cure; D in hours 24–72 with refresh; J if rules remain informal. These are acceptable only with explicit, documented risk acceptance by a named authority — which is precisely what improvisation lacked.

- *Traceability & control completeness:* traceability exception rate; missing temp-doc rate; missing dual-control rate; excess control-miss count vs. baseline expectation (T2: ~150 excess dual-control misses).
- *Backlog & rework:* backlog age p95 and % beyond SLA; rework rate (still 5.8% vs 3.5% at T4 — quality debt outlives the incident).
- *Hidden workload:* audit-remediation queue size, mean age, and burn-down; % of gaps cured with **contemporaneous** evidence vs. waived on attestation; conformance fitness and fitting-trace rate measured against the *degraded-mode* model as well as the normative one.
- *Fairness:* segment service-degradation gap (max minus min fill-rate drop). T2's gap was 30 points (91% vs 61%) — set an explicit bound (e.g., 20 points) justified by published tiering, plus 100% notification rate for deferred customers.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** bounded, visible service degradation for non-critical segments — Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk cycle times up to roughly 2–2.5× baseline for a defined window — plus the labor cost of dual control, paper custody, and same-day evidence capture, because that is the price of preserving both patients and compliance. It should **not accept** any controlled-substance shipment without dual control and documented custody, any authorization bypass dressed up as customer-history confidence, any backfilled scan presented as contemporaneous, or any declaration of "recovery" based on cycle-time medians while a remediation tail persists — those are not resilience, they are deferred liabilities accruing interest. The recommendation would soften if evidence showed the offline authorization lists were 24 h old with documented callbacks (then B scales and some C-class throughput is recoverable), or if remediation demonstrates contemporaneous evidence exists for most gaps; it would harden — into a formal compliance incident with self-report assessment — if reconstructed scans prove fabricated or any CS shipment lacked custody evidence, because at that point the organization no longer knows what it shipped, only what it hopes it shipped.
