## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Each case is interpreted as an ordered event stream, not as a single final-state record.

- **Event order:** Events are sorted by:
  1. `case:concept:name`
  2. `time:timestamp`
  3. `event_index`

  `event_index` is therefore decisive where timestamps are equal. For TX-311, `Enhanced_Review` at index 3 occurs before `Update_Jurisdiction` at index 4, despite sharing timestamp `16:20`.

- **Blank values:** Blank cells mean **no update at that event**. They are not nullification events. State is forward-filled only from earlier events in the same case.

- **State reconstruction:** At every event, the operative state is the latest known value for:
  - amount,
  - risk level,
  - jurisdiction,
  - customer type,
  - exemption reference.

  The state at `Release_Funds` is the state after applying all updates ordered before that release.

- **No backward filling:** Final attributes must not be copied backward. For example, TX-305’s settlement amount of EUR 150,000 on the following day does not establish the amount at release, although it is relevant later evidence.

- **Material changes:** A change is material if it affects an obligation or its satisfaction. This includes, for example:
  - amount increases greater than 20% after the relevant review;
  - a jurisdiction change affecting foreign/sanctions status;
  - a risk change to `HIGH`;
  - a customer-type change to `PEP`;
  - a sanctions-list addition for the current jurisdiction.

  R4 specifically defines an amount increase of more than 20% after the latest review as invalidating that review for amount-dependent obligations. Decreases do not invalidate a review by themselves.

- **Unknown amounts:** An increase from an unknown amount cannot safely be classified as greater than 20%. Likewise, an unknown release amount prevents a reliable R1 high-value determination unless other admissible evidence establishes the amount at release.

- **Review currency:** A review satisfies an obligation only if it is after the relevant triggering/material change and before release. For R2, `Compliance_Approve` must be after the relevant `Enhanced_Review`.

- **Exemption references:** A release-side exemption ID is merely a claimed reference. It is not by itself proof that an exemption existed, was approved, was effective, matched the scope, or was timely.

- **Time concepts:**
  - **Event time:** Timestamp assigned to the operational event, such as `Release_Funds`.
  - **Effective time:** When an attribute or exemption legally/operationally governs. Default: the event timestamp, unless reliable evidence establishes another effective time.
  - **Record time:** When a database entry was written, such as `Record_Exemption`.
  - **Evidence/signature time:** Time supported by reliable evidence that approval or authorization existed, such as a qualified signature timestamp.

  A later record can support an earlier effective approval only where the evidence is sufficiently reliable. A displayed backdated field without corroboration is not enough.

### Formal pseudo-logic

Let:

- `e < f` mean `e` occurs before `f` after sorting by `(timestamp, event_index)`.
- `t(e)` be the event timestamp.
- `State(c,e)` be the latest known attribute state for case `c` immediately after applying event `e`.
- `Release(c,r)` mean event `r` is `Release_Funds` for case `c`.
- `BeforeRelease(x,r)` mean `x < r`.
- `Between(x,y,r)` mean `x < y < r`.
- `Latest(E,c,r)` mean the latest event of type/set `E` for case `c` such that it occurs before `r`.
- `Current(a,c,e)` mean attribute `a` in `State(c,e)`.
- `Known(x)` means evidence supports a determinate value.
- `MaterialChange(m,c,r)` means a pre-release event that changes a rule-relevant attribute, including the R4-defined invalidating increase.
- `ValidExemption(ex,c,r,R1)` means exemption `ex` satisfies all R5 conditions for R1 at release `r`.

#### R1

```text
R1Trigger(c,r) :=
  Release(c,r)
  AND Known(Current(jurisdiction,c,r))
  AND Known(Current(amount,c,r))
  AND Current(jurisdiction,c,r) != "DE"
  AND Current(amount,c,r) >= 100000.

R1SatisfiedByReview(c,r) :=
  EXISTS er:
    Event(c,er,"Enhanced_Review")
    AND er < r
    AND NOT EXISTS m:
      MaterialChange(m,c,r)
      AND er < m < r.

R1Satisfied(c,r) :=
  R1SatisfiedByReview(c,r)
  OR EXISTS ex: ValidExemption(ex,c,r,R1).

R1Indeterminate(c,r) :=
  Release(c,r)
  AND (
    NOT Known(Current(amount,c,r))
    OR NOT Known(Current(jurisdiction,c,r))
  )
  AND R1Trigger cannot be established or excluded from the evidence.
```

#### R2

```text
R2Trigger(c,r) :=
  Release(c,r)
  AND (
    Current(risk_level,c,r) = "HIGH"
    OR Current(customer_type,c,r) = "PEP"
  ).

R2Satisfied(c,r) :=
  EXISTS er, ca:
    Event(c,er,"Enhanced_Review")
    AND Event(c,ca,"Compliance_Approve")
    AND er < ca < r
    AND er occurs after the relevant HIGH/PEP trigger or later relevant material change
    AND ca approves the enhanced-review basis, not merely a standard-review packet.

R2Indeterminate(c,r) :=
  Release(c,r)
  AND (
    risk_level or customer_type is unknown
  )
  AND R2Trigger cannot be established or excluded.
```

#### R3

```text
R3Trigger(c,r) :=
  Release(c,r)
  AND EXISTS lu, ss:
    Event(c,lu,"Sanctions_List_Update")
    AND Event(c,ss,"Sanctions_Screen")
    AND ss < lu < r
    AND Current(jurisdiction,c,lu) is added by lu.

R3Satisfied(c,r) :=
  EXISTS lu, ss2:
    R3Trigger(c,r)
    AND Event(c,ss2,"Sanctions_Screen")
    AND lu < ss2 < r.
```

#### R4

```text
AmountIncreaseOver20(c,u,rev) :=
  Event(c,u,"Update_Amount")
  AND rev < u
  AND Known(amount immediately before u)
  AND Known(amount at u)
  AND amount_at(u) > 1.20 * amount_before(u).

R4Invalidates(c,rev,r) :=
  EXISTS u:
    AmountIncreaseOver20(c,u,rev)
    AND rev < u < r.

ReviewCurrentForAmount(c,review,r) :=
  Event(c,review,"Standard_Review" or "Enhanced_Review")
  AND review < r
  AND NOT R4Invalidates(c,review,r).

R4Indeterminate(c,u) :=
  Event(c,u,"Update_Amount")
  AND amount immediately before u is unknown
  AND whether increase exceeds 20% is decisive.
```

#### R5

```text
ValidExemption(ex,c,r,rule) :=
  ClaimedFor(ex,c,r,rule)
  AND approved_by(ex) = "Head_Compliance"
  AND ReliableEvidence(approval_existed_at_or_before_trigger(ex,c,r,rule))
  AND effective_from(ex) <= TriggerTime(c,r,rule)
  AND release_time(r) < expires_at(ex)
  AND ScopeMatches(
        ex,
        Current(jurisdiction,c,r),
        Current(customer_type,c,r),
        Current(amount,c,r),
        rule
      ).
```

#### R6

```text
If a rule's trigger depends on amount, risk_level, jurisdiction, or customer_type,
and the required release-time value cannot be established or excluded from admissible evidence,
then outcome(rule,c,r) = INDETERMINATE,
unless independent evidence resolves the value at the relevant time.
```

#### R7

```text
EventOrder(e,f) :=
  t(e) < t(f)
  OR (t(e) = t(f) AND event_index(e) < event_index(f)).

State(c,e) is formed only from updates x where x <= e under EventOrder.
No value from x > e may be used in State(c,e).
```

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

### TX-302 — late record, but strong timely approval evidence

Current result: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**.

The `Record_Exemption` event occurs at 11:00, after the 09:40 release. That record time alone would not prove an exemption existed at release. However, EX-77 has stronger evidence:

- Head_Compliance approval;
- displayed effective time 09:00;
- qualified digital signature at 09:05;
- valid hash;
- scope matches GB, CORPORATE, and EUR 180,000;
- expiry is after release;
- documented outage explains delayed database insertion.

The signature timestamp is before release and supports the approval’s existence at the relevant time. Thus the exemption is not merely post-hoc.

The outcome would change to **NONCOMPLIANT** if the signature could not be tied to EX-77, if the outage evidence were unreliable, if the exemption scope did not cover EUR 180,000, or if “effective 09:00” were unsupported and the signature were after the R1 trigger under a stricter interpretation requiring approval before trigger rather than before release.

### TX-305 — unknown release amount

Current result: **INDETERMINATE**.

At release, the amount is absent. The next-day settlement amount of EUR 150,000 is reliable at settlement time, but the facts expressly say there is no immutable release payload. It may equal the release amount, but does not prove that it did.

Consequences:

- If the release amount was at least EUR 100,000, R1 would trigger.
- An `Enhanced_Review` exists at 11:15, so R1 may have been satisfied if it reviewed the actual release-time amount and no later material change occurred.
- If the release amount was below EUR 100,000, R1 would not trigger.
- The review document itself has no amount, which weakens proof that it covered an amount-dependent R1 obligation if the amount was high.

The result would become **COMPLIANT** if an immutable release message, ledger entry, signed review packet, or reliable contemporaneous amount feed established either:
1. release amount below EUR 100,000; or
2. release amount at least EUR 100,000 and the enhanced review covered that amount.

It would become **NONCOMPLIANT** if reliable release-time evidence established amount  EUR 100,000 and showed that the enhanced review lacked amount coverage or preceded a material amount increase.

### TX-310 — backdated exemption fields versus late signature evidence

Current result: **INDETERMINATE**.

R1 plainly triggers: AE is not DE and the release amount is EUR 200,000. A Standard_Review is insufficient because R1 requires `Enhanced_Review` unless a valid R1 exemption applies.

EX-88 has favorable displayed fields and matching scope, but the critical approval-existence evidence is weak and contradictory:

- displayed approval: 08:50;
- release: 10:00;
- signing certificate created: 10:30, after release;
- witness claims oral approval at 08:45;
- witness is the transaction requester, creating an evident interest concern.

Under R5, a backdated displayed field is not conclusive. The facts do not establish whether the oral approval was authorized, contemporaneously documented, sufficiently reliable, or legally capable of making the exemption effective before release. The case is therefore indeterminate rather than safely treated as exempt.

The result would become **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** if there were reliable contemporaneous evidence of Head_Compliance approval before release: authenticated call recording, signed approval email, workflow audit log, independently corroborated witness evidence, or proof that the 10:30 certificate issuance merely replaced an earlier valid authorization.

It would become **NONCOMPLIANT** if policy requires documented qualified approval before reliance, if oral approval is not permitted, or if investigation shows the displayed approval time was created after release.

### TX-311 — same timestamp and state sequencing

Current result: **COMPLIANT**.

At 16:20:

- index 3: `Enhanced_Review`;
- index 4: `Update_Jurisdiction` to DE.

Because index 3 precedes index 4, the review occurred while the jurisdiction was US. But the jurisdiction becomes DE before the 16:30 release. R1 is evaluated at release and therefore does not trigger.

The result would change under a different interpretation only if:

- the jurisdiction update was proven effective after release despite its event timestamp;
- the source-system `event_index` were unreliable or not an actual ordering field;
- R1 were interpreted as triggered based on jurisdiction at review initiation rather than release, contrary to its wording; or
- a separate rule required a new review after any jurisdiction change, which is not stated here.

### TX-301 — amount change invalidated the standard review

Current result: **NONCOMPLIANT**.

The amount rose from EUR 80,000 to EUR 125,000 after `Standard_Review`. The increase is 56.25%, exceeding the R4 threshold. At release, R1 triggers and requires an enhanced review after the latest material change.

A different result would require evidence that:
- the amount update had a later effective time than its event time;
- an omitted enhanced review occurred between 08:20 and 08:25;
- the release was cancelled/reversed before funds were actually released; or
- an otherwise valid R1 exemption existed and was omitted from the evidence.

### TX-306 — whether a sub-20% amount change should nevertheless require re-review

Current result: **COMPLIANT**.

The amount changed from EUR 220,000 to EUR 230,000, a 4.55% increase. R4 only invalidates a review after an increase of more than 20%. R2’s enhanced review and compliance approval remain ordered correctly.

A stricter internal-control interpretation could require re-review of any amount change for high-risk or PEP customers, but that would be an additional policy rule. Under the stated rules, the review remains valid.

### TX-308 — decrease to the threshold

Current result: **COMPLIANT**.

The amount fell from EUR 140,000 to exactly EUR 100,000. R1 still triggers because “at least EUR 100,000” includes EUR 100,000. The prior enhanced review remains valid because R4 says a decrease does not by itself invalidate a review.

The outcome would differ only if:
- EUR 100,000 were intended to be exclusive, contrary to “at least”; or
- the decrease reflected a new transaction requiring separate review rather than a changed amount on the same transaction.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

A control-flow-only model sees activity sequences such as:

```text
Enhanced_Review -> Release_Funds
```

but cannot faithfully determine compliance under these rules. At least the following failures arise.

1. **Final-state leakage**

   A process model that joins each case to final attributes may incorrectly use a final amount, jurisdiction, risk level, or customer type for an earlier review or release. TX-311 demonstrates why this is unsafe: jurisdiction was US at review time and DE at release time.

2. **Conditional activation is data-dependent**

   R1 applies only where the release-time jurisdiction is non-DE and amount is at least EUR 100,000. R2 applies only for HIGH risk or PEP. A trace with no enhanced review can be compliant for one release and noncompliant for another depending on the event-time state.

3. **Material amount changes invalidate earlier reviews**

   TX-301 and TX-309 can contain review-before-release patterns, but the relevant review is stale because a greater-than-20% amount increase occurs afterward. A standard precedence constraint cannot calculate the percentage change or determine review validity.

4. **Standard and enhanced reviews are semantically different**

   A generic “review before release” control-flow constraint would wrongly accept TX-301, TX-307, or TX-310. The policy explicitly says a Standard_Review is not an Enhanced_Review.

5. **Approval must be ordered after the relevant enhanced review**

   R2 is not satisfied merely because a compliance approval exists somewhere before release. It must be after the relevant enhanced review and must concern the enhanced-review basis. TX-307 has a standard-packet approval, which does not cure the missing enhanced review.

6. **Post-hoc exemptions cannot be accepted solely by trace order**

   A release may reference an exemption before the `Record_Exemption` event. The conformance question depends on approval evidence, effective date, scope, expiry, and reliability—not simply whether a record event appears before or after release. TX-302 and TX-310 have superficially similar late records but materially different evidence quality.

7. **Missing data cannot be converted to benign values**

   TX-305 cannot be treated as below threshold merely because release amount is blank. A control-flow model normally lacks a principled “unknown trigger” state and may either over-enforce or under-enforce.

8. **Same-timestamp ordering matters**

   Conventional event logs may treat equal timestamps as simultaneous or arbitrarily ordered. Here, `event_index` decides TX-311. If a conformance tool sorts only by timestamp, it can produce nondeterministic or incorrect results.

9. **External facts and effective times matter**

   R3 relies on sanctions-list updates, which may be external reference-data events. Exemption validity relies on effective, record, signature, and approval times. These are richer temporal concepts than ordinary activity ordering.

10. **Evidence credibility is not a trace property**

   EX-77 is supported by a qualified signature and documented outage; EX-88 depends on a late certificate and a self-interested witness. No ordinary process graph can assess evidential quality, authenticity, or authority.

Control-flow conformance is therefore useful as a screening layer—for example, detecting release before any enhanced review—but it must be combined with event-time state reconstruction, arithmetic, external-data synchronization, evidence evaluation, and explicit uncertainty handling.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

1. **Immutable release payloads**
   - Record the exact amount, currency, jurisdiction, risk level, customer type, sanctions-list version, rule version, and exemption ID used at release.
   - Hash or digitally sign the release payload.
   - Preserve release authorization and payment-submission timestamps separately.

2. **Explicit event, effective, record, and signature timestamps**
   - For each update, log:
     - source event time,
     - effective-from time,
     - record/ingestion time,
     - signature/approval time,
     - source-system sequence number.
   - Avoid relying on a displayed effective date without provenance.

3. **Stable global event ordering**
   - Retain `event_index` or a monotonic event sequence generated by the authoritative source.
   - Where events cross systems, include a globally comparable sequence or causal correlation identifier.
   - Preserve timezone and timestamp precision.

4. **Attribute change provenance**
   - Store old value, new value, actor, source system, reason code, and evidence reference for amount, jurisdiction, risk, and customer-type changes.
   - For amount updates, capture the prior amount explicitly so R4 arithmetic is auditable.
   - Record whether an update is correction, amendment, cancellation, or new transaction.

5. **Review scope and reviewed-state snapshots**
   - Each Standard_Review and Enhanced_Review should record:
     - reviewed amount,
     - reviewed jurisdiction,
     - reviewed risk level,
     - reviewed customer type,
     - review type,
     - rule/policy version,
     - reviewer identity and authority,
     - review completion time,
     - evidence/document identifiers.
   - This avoids inferring what the review covered from event ordering alone.

6. **Approval-to-review linkage**
   - `Compliance_Approve` should reference the specific enhanced-review ID/version it approves.
   - Include a field such as `approval_packet_type = ENHANCED`.
   - Prevent an approval of a standard packet from being reused as an approval of a later enhanced review.

7. **Exemption ledger with immutable evidence**
   - Log exemption ID, waiver rule(s), jurisdiction scope, customer-type scope, amount range/currency, effective-from, expiry, approver, approval authority, signature timestamp, and evidence hash.
   - Distinguish:
     - exemption approval time,
     - exemption effective time,
     - database insertion time,
     - later correction/amendment time.
   - Require an immutable evidence reference, not a free-text claim at release.

8. **Automated release-time policy snapshot**
   - At release, generate a deterministic policy-evaluation record:
     - applicable rules,
     - trigger values,
     - reviews/approvals found,
     - invalidating changes,
     - exemption decision,
     - sanctions-list version,
     - decision outcome,
     - reason codes.
   - This should be stored as an immutable audit artifact.

9. **Sanctions-list versioning and re-screen controls**
   - Record the list version and effective time used by every sanctions screen.
   - Emit a jurisdiction-specific list-change event with exact effective time.
   - Automatically block release or require re-screening when a relevant list version changes after screening.

10. **Unknown-data controls**
    - Represent missingness explicitly as `UNKNOWN`, `PENDING`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, or `UNAVAILABLE`, rather than leaving ambiguous blanks.
    - Configure release controls to block or escalate where a mandatory trigger attribute is unknown.
    - Ensure settlement data is linked as later evidence, not silently substituted for a missing release payload.

11. **Rule version and policy lineage**
    - Log the policy version, threshold, sanctions source/version, and exemption rules used for each review and release.
    - This permits accurate retrospective evaluation when policy thresholds or list sources change.

12. **Evidence-quality classification**
    - Associate each approval/exemption with evidence type and assurance level, for example:
      - qualified signature,
      - system workflow approval,
      - authenticated email,
      - contemporaneous call record,
      - witness statement.
    - Route weak or contradictory evidence to human review rather than automatically treating it as valid.

These changes would transform many currently inferential decisions into reproducible, event-time determinations while preserving a clear path for human judgment where evidence remains genuinely conflicting.
