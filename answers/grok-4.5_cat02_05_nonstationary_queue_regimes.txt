# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Opening-hours convention and units
- **Service window**: 07:00–19:00 daily (12 h = 720 min). No service while closed; cases may carry overnight.
- **arrivals / completed**: counts of cases arriving / finishing active handling during opening hours (not necessarily same-day matched).
- **median_service_min / p90_service_min**: active handling time only (excludes queue wait). These are order statistics of service, **not** sojourn/flow time.
- **mean_wip**: time-average number of cases in system from 15-minute snapshots (units: cases). Snapshot convention inside opening hours is not further specified.
- **staff_fte**: mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent servers during opening hours.
- **complex_pct**: share of arrivals on a multi-step complex variant (%).
- **outage_min**: minutes of capacity loss inside the opening window.
- **peak_08_10_pct**: share of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59.

## Queueing estimands (what we can and cannot target)
| Quantity | Definition used here | Observable from table? |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival intensity  | arrivals / 12 h (cases/h) | Yes (daily) |
| Completion intensity _out | completed / 12 h (cases/h) | Yes (daily); **not** the service rate of a single server |
| Service-time behavior | median and p90 of active handling | Partially (no means, no full distribution) |
| WIP L | mean_wip | Yes (snapshot mean) |
| Capacity pressure | rough L vs staff_fte, or completions per FTE-hour | Indirect only |
| Flow time W | sojourn time in system | **Not** supplied; median service  W |
| Backlog at open/close | inventory boundary conditions | **Not** supplied daily |

## At least five threats to naive inference
1. **Non-stationarity and regime mixing**: case-mix jump (~Apr 20), outage/recovery, staffing intervention (~Apr 29), weekday/weekend composition—if pooled, parameters are mixtures, not operating points.
2. **Carry-over backlog**: completed need not have arrived the same day; overnight inventory is invisible; daily  and throughput are not a closed system.
3. **Outage distortion**: 2026-04-15 has 180 min outage; 2026-04-16 shows completions  arrivals (recovery burn-down). Treating recovery days as “high productivity” confounds capacity with backlog release.
4. **Service statistics  flow time**: substituting median_service_min for W in Little’s Law is invalid; no mean service or mean wait is given.
5. **Endogeneity of staffing and demand**: staff_fte moves with distress and intervention; FTE is not an exogenous experimental factor.
6. **Complex_pct ambiguity**: may be true mix shift, coding change, or both—service-time inflation may be misattributed.
7. **Intraday burstiness hidden by daily totals**: peak_08_10_pct and hourly vectors show uneven load; daily M/M/c-style models understate peak pressure.
8. **Serial dependence**: consecutive high-WIP weekdays are not i.i.d.; variance estimates and significance tests that ignore dependence are overconfident.
9. **Snapshot WIP alignment**: 15-min means may under-sample peaks; relationship to arrival/completion timing is unknown.
10. **Simpson-type reversals**: weekend low  with moderate service times vs weekday high  can reverse apparent utilization or “efficiency” rankings when pooled.

**Assumption A1**: Daily arrivals and completions are measured only inside 07:00–19:00; rates below divide by 12 h.  
**Assumption A2**: mean_wip averages snapshots only while open (stated “from 15-minute snapshots” in scenario).  
**Assumption A3**: Staffing intervention is materially visible from 2026-04-29 onward (scenario: “began during 2026-04-29”).

# 3. Alternative segmentation

## Alternative A — Backlog-state / control-chart segmentation (inventory-first)

Define regimes by **net daily drift**  = completed  arrivals and **mean_wip** bands, ignoring calendar labels except outage:

| Label | Rule (illustrative) | Days (approx.) | Operational story |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-stable | \|\| small, WIP  [12, 25] | Early weekdays + early weekends | Matched flow, acceptable inventory |
| S-shock | outage_min > 0 | 04-15 | Exogenous capacity loss |
| S-drain |   0, WIP high then falling | 04-16–17, 04-29–05-01 | Recovery or intervention burn-down |
| S-build |  < 0 persistently, WIP  35 | 04-20–28 (and partial 04-27–28) | Unstable growth under insufficient effective capacity |
| S-weekend-low | weekend volume | all Sat–Sun | Different scale; optional sub-split early/late mix |

**Story**: Management should steer by **inventory and drift**, not by average arrivals alone. Staffing intervention and post-outage days look similar as “drain” states even though causes differ.

### Strengths vs primary
- **Strengths**: Directly tied to stability (queue growth); less dependent on interpreting complex_pct; natural for control policies (WIP caps, overtime triggers).  
- **Weaknesses**: Mixes mechanistically different causes (outage recovery vs planned FTE vs mix-driven overload); blurs weekday/weekend capacity design; harder to prescribe **preventive** staffing before WIP explodes; change-point on WIP is lagging.

### Strengths of primary vs alternative
- Primary separates **demand mix**, **outage**, and **intervention**—better for root-cause and for not calling R3 “good performance.”  
- Alternative is better for **real-time ops control** and for admitting that 04-29–05-01 and 04-16–17 share a drain signature.

**Assumption A4**: A material alternative is backlog-centric rather than mix-centric; both are defensible given aggregates only.

### Implications
- Daily totals hide **within-day peak load** (peak_08_10_pct rises from ~30% baseline to ~45–50% in R4).  
- A pooled Poisson hourly model calibrated to daily /12 **understates** peak congestion and staffing need for 08–11.  
- Four vectors are **illustrative strata**, not enough to fit a full nonhomogeneous Poisson or MAP model, estimate seasonal NHPP parameters stably, or validate dependence beyond lag 1–2 hours.

## Daily sequence: lag dependence and change points
- **WIP weekday path**: ~20 (stable)  47 (outage)  52/35 (recovery)  37…50 (climb/plateau in R4)  43…32 (R5 decline). Strong **positive persistence**.  
- **Net drift  = completed  arrivals**: clusters of negative  through R4 (build), positive cluster in R3 and R5 (drain).  
- **complex_pct**: step-like lift around 2026-04-20 (from ~25 to ~41+), coincident with service median/p90 lift and peak_08_10 lift—**joint change point**, not independent noise.  
- Simple serial indicator: lag-1 correlation of weekday mean_wip (excluding pure weekend breaks) is visibly high (order ~0.7–0.9 qualitatively from the monotone blocks); lag-1 of arrivals also positive within regimes.

**Stationarity**: Reject global stationarity. Piecewise quasi-stationarity is plausible inside R1 and (weakly) inside R4 levels, but R4 still shows rising stress (04-27–28 WIP 49–50). R5 is a short transient.

**Heuristic change-point evidence** (not ground truth):  
1. 04-15 outage (known).  
2. ~04-20 complex_pct / service / peak shift.  
3. ~04-29 staffing FTE step and sign change in .

**Why four hourly days  stochastic model**: no inter-arrival timestamps, no per-case service draws, no overnight backlog series, n=4 paths, and regimes differ—cannot identify arrival process class, abandonment, or routing.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law: \(L = \lambda W\) for a **stable** system over a window where time-average inventory L, arrival rate  (or throughput  in steady state), and mean sojourn W are consistently defined. Here L  mean_wip; true W unknown; median_service is **not** W.

| Single day inside R1 | Unknown open/close backlog | Daily arrivals or completions | One-day snapshot mean | High relative to flow time | Weak | Same formula numerically possible but biased | **Not defensible** as law claim |

**Units clarification for the one semi-defensible case (R1)**  
- Window: pooled opening hours across R1 weekdays.  
- L in cases;  in cases per opening-hour; W in opening-hours of system time (cases idle overnight still accrue calendar time not counted in this W).  
- **Do not** replace W with median_service_min (25 min); implied W ~146 min indicates queueing delay is the bulk of sojourn under R1—**hypothesis H1**, contingent on snapshot WIP  true time-average L and near-zero net drift.

# 7. Operational implications

## Staffing
- **Size to weekday R4/R5 peaks, not pooled mean**. Weekday  ~9–10/h with heavy tails (p90 service ~80 min) and Fano  1 needs either higher base FTE than R1’s 6 or flexible surge 08:00–11:00 (and mid-afternoon on bursty days).  
- R5 suggests **+1 FTE** (67) flipped net drift positive and reduced WIP ~10 cases over 3 days—continue measuring until WIP stabilizes near a target band (e.g. 20–25).  
- Weekends need separate rosters (2.5–3 FTE); do not “borrow” weekend spare capacity assumptions from weekday models.

## Backlog recovery
- After outages, expect **R3-like** days: plan explicit recovery FTE and stop declaring victory based on completionsarrivals.  
- R4-style multi-day build needs a **WIP cap policy**: when mean_wip > 35 or cumulative 3-day  < 20, trigger overtime or intake throttling.  
- Distinguish recovery throughput from structural capacity in KPIs.

## Peak smoothing
- peak_08_10_pct rising to ~45–50% with bursty hourly profile (04-21)  appointment windows, staggered intake, or triage diversion before 10:00.  
- Smoothing reduces effective burstiness (Fano) even if daily  unchanged—high leverage when service tails are long.

## Data collection
- Log **start-of-day and end-of-day backlog**, case-level arrival/finish timestamps, and **intervention effective time**.  
- Publish **mean** flow time and wait, not only median/p90 service.  
- Flag coding changes affecting complex_pct.  
- Keep 15-min WIP but add queue-wait vs service split.

## Policy that looks good pooled but risky segmented
**Policy P**: “Hold staff_fte at the historical pooled average (~5.3–6.0) because pooled completed/arrivals  0.96 and completed per FTE looks stable across the month.”  

- **Pooled view**: appears efficient and nearly balanced.  
- **Segmented view**: under R4 mix and burstiness, 6 FTE produces sustained queue growth (WIP 40–50, implied multi-hour delays); weekends and R1 mask the deficit; outage recovery days inflate perceived capacity. **Risk**: chronic delay, overtime spikes, and repeated emergency interventions.  
**Segmented alternative**: state-dependent staffing—baseline 6 for R1-like mix, pre-authorized 7 for high complex_pct or peak_08_10_pct > 40%, plus weekend-specific minimums.

def fano_cv(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    m = x.mean()
    v = x.var(ddof=1) if len(x) > 1 else np.nan
    fano = v / m if m > 0 else np.nan
    cv = np.sqrt(v) / m if m > 0 else np.nan
    return m, v, fano, cv

# 5) Little's Law implied flow times ONLY for explicitly chosen windows
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def little_window(mask, name, theta="balanced"):
    g = df.loc[mask]
    L = g["mean_wip"].mean()
    lam = g["lambda_per_h"].mean()
    comp = g["comp_per_h"].mean()
    if theta == "balanced":
        th = 0.5 * (lam + comp)
    elif theta == "arrivals":
        th = lam
    else:
        th = comp
    # net drift diagnostic
    drift = g["delta"].sum()
    W_h = L / th if th > 0 else np.nan
    print(f"{name}: n={len(g)} L={L:.2f} lambda={lam:.2f} comp={comp:.2f} "
          f"theta_{theta}={th:.2f} drift_sum={drift:.0f} => W{W_h:.2f} open-h "
          f"({W_h*60:.0f} min) | verdict deferred to narrative matrix")

---

**Bottom line**: A single pooled queueing model mixes weekend scale, an outage transient, recovery burn-down, a step change in complexity/peaks, and a staffing intervention. Segmented analysis shows stable baseline operation (R1), a non-equilibrium recovery (R3), a structurally stressed high-mix regime (R4), and a partial FTE-driven correction (R5). Little’s Law yields only an **approximate** opening-hours sojourn for quasi-stable R1 (~2.4 h); it is **not defensible** for outage, recovery, unstable backlog build, or the pooled month.
