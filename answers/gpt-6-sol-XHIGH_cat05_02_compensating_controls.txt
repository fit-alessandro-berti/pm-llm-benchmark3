## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is a separation rule for completed payments. Its underlying objective is an independent, effective decision before value becomes irrevocable. The controls address different threats:

| Threat or objective | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One operator creates and authorizes an improper transfer | Prevents only listed rule violations; cannot establish human independence | Can prevent settlement if an independent, qualified manager reviews the evidence and can veto in time | Detects later; may support recall | Allows single-operator processing only behind specified gates; does not itself review a payment |
| Duplicate or excessive payment, sanctions hit, or basic PO mismatch | Prevents when a full check blocks; a warning with an override is weaker | Can independently challenge the invoice, limit, and purpose | Detects missed defects; can seek recall | Requires an unoverridden full AV-2 pass and amount below EUR 100,000 |
| Changed or misdirected bank account | Checks ownership and recent changes, but cannot establish control of a compromised account | Can compare trustworthy bank evidence and challenge a change | May detect diversion; correction depends on bank timing and recipient | Excludes recent bank changes; requires AV-2 and manager review |
| No delivery, fabricated receipt, or unreasonable commercial purpose | Basic PO match only; does not prove receipt or commercial reasonableness | Can prevent if supporting receipt and transaction substance are genuinely reviewed | Can detect with independent source evidence; late detection often cannot recover funds | Requires MG-4, but its token and amount gates do not prove delivery |
| Stolen credentials, a shared account, or an unauthorized override | May flag a limit or change, but does not identify who controlled an account; override can defeat a warning | Independent authentication and a real veto help; a borrowed credential is no approval | Can detect through identity and access logs; correction is time limited | Cannot cure a compromised manager credential or an override |
| Collusion or a habitual approving pair | Does not test collusion | One independent manager adds a decision point, but may also collude or rubber-stamp | Pattern review can detect; it is generally too late to prevent settlement | Eligibility conditions reduce exposure but do not make actors independent by themselves |
| Delayed discovery and loss recovery | Blocks only before release | A pre-settlement veto can prevent loss | One-business-day 100% review detects; recall is only plausible while still available | Requires timely RA-7, without turning it into a preventive control |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Evaluate the whole causal chain, not the number of logged activities:

1. **Scope and timing:** Identify the last enforceable stop before irrevocable settlement. Verify that AV-2 completed before release, the manager could hold or cancel settlement, and audit occurred early enough for a realistic recall. A later signature cannot retroactively authorize a settled transfer.
2. **Independence and authority:** Confirm distinct humans, manager level L3 or higher, no unresolved conflict, individually controlled credentials, and evidence of substantive review. A system event, shared account, or supervisor's credential used by the operator does not supply a second human decision.
3. **Coverage and overrides:** Map the loss mechanism to the checks actually run. Confirm the AV-2 rule set was available and passed without override; independently verify receipt, bank details, and business purpose where AV-2 has gaps. Treat an override as an exception needing its own valid authority.
4. **Recoverability and evidence:** Establish settlement time, bank recall eligibility, audit scope and findings, source documents, and immutable actor/timestamp records. Missing records lower confidence; a legitimate outcome alone does not prove that the control worked.
5. **Collusion resistance and case risk:** Consider credible collusion or credential compromise, repeated pairings, shared workstations, changed bank accounts, novel vendors, and exposure size together. Size affects consequence, not the definition of independence.

**Fully equivalent:** The package gives an independent, informed, enforceable veto before settlement, covers the material fraud/error paths at least as effectively as an independent releaser, and has credible identity and review evidence. ED-1 is a sanctioned route to this result only if *every* condition is proven. **Partially equivalent:** A real independent check closes important paths but has a material timing, coverage, or evidence gap. **Merely mitigating:** Checks reduce likelihood or detect later while leaving the unilateral transfer path open; low realized loss does not upgrade it. **Ineffective:** Controls were absent, defeated, performed too late, or lacked credible independence. An overall classification can remain **INDETERMINATE** when evidence cannot distinguish two materially different states.

Equal *expected* loss is not a complete standard: different packages can have different catastrophic tails, ease of concealment, recovery chances, compliance with an explicit permission, and auditability even if their average modeled loss is similar. These estimates are also highly uncertain under collusion and credential compromise.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control/action | Preventive effect | Detective effect | Corrective effect and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 full, unoverridden pass | Blocks enumerated failures before release; a clean pass only clears those tests | Warning identifies a listed concern | None after funds move; override can nullify the block |
| MG-4 before release | Independent substantive veto can prevent release and challenge AV-2 gaps | Review may find a defect | Can request correction before release |
| MG-4 after release, before settlement | Preventive **only if** settlement is reliably held and manager can veto/cancel; otherwise no preventive effect | Detects errors in an already released instruction | Can cancel while hold is effective; otherwise depends on bank recall |
| MG-4 after settlement | None for that transfer | Detects or documents a past problem | Recovery is exceptional and uncertain; ratification does not cure timing |
| RA-7 timely 100% audit | No prevention of the initial release | Detects sampled or fully reviewed defects, depending on scope | Can initiate stop/recall while available; after the window, chiefly lessons and claims |
| ED-1 permission | Its gates constrain emergency use; the actual AV-2 and MG-4 steps supply prevention | RA-7 supplies detection | RA-7 may support recall; the permission itself recovers nothing |

Thus a post-release, pre-settlement manager action is a **hybrid**: detective with respect to the released instruction, and preventive with respect to irreversible loss *only* when an enforceable settlement hold and a substantive independent veto are shown. The label “ratify” does not establish either property.

| Case | Plausible loss chain and breaker | What remains exposed | Likely effect of a truly independent second releaser |
|---|---|---|---|

| AP-202 | Changed bank details plus an overridden warning could divert a large payment; no effective breaker operated | Funds already lost, possible credential compromise, unresolved relationship | A genuinely independent, authenticated releaser reviewing the change and receipt would likely have stopped or escalated it; collusion remains possible |

These are qualitative causal judgments, not numerical loss forecasts. A clean outcome and a passed automated check do not eliminate the exposed paths.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Retain case-linked, immutable preparation/release identities; individual authentication and credential-use logs; AV-2 rule versions, inputs, results, blocks, warnings and override authorization; token activation *and* event-recording times; source invoices, PO and independently sourced delivery evidence; manager level, conflict declarations, review checklist, decision and veto/hold capability; settlement and bank recall timestamps; and the complete RA-7 population, scope, findings, and recall attempts. For AP-204/209, obtain payment-cell design, workstation/session assignments, and independent evidence that both people actually reviewed transactions. For AP-202, investigate credential ownership without presuming that m09 shared it.

Monitor with denominators and case context:

| Rule | Suggested alert/measure |
|---|---|
| Same-user rate | Completed same-user releases ÷ all completed payments, split by emergency eligibility, team, vendor and time; investigate every non-ED-1 case |
| Override rate | Overrides ÷ AV-2 warnings/blocks and payments, by user, manager, rule and vendor; alert on operator use of another person's credential |
| Late manager approval | Flag approval after release, separately flag approval after settlement or without a proven settlement hold; record L3 and review depth |
| Audit before recall window | Track 100% of same-user emergency cases audited within one business day and before the bank's practical recall cutoff; use actual settlement time, not just release time |
| Repeated resource pairs | Alert on unusually frequent pairs, especially with disputed receipts or concentration at one vendor; compare with authorized payment-cell design |
| Shared workstations | Flag simultaneous/repeated prepare-release sessions on one device and shared credentials; correlate with user authentication and approved workspace arrangements |
| Bank-change proximity | Escalate changes within seven days, especially with new vendors, overrides, or absent independent ownership confirmation |
| Control outages | Stop or escalate releases during AV-2 outages; reconcile all attempted and completed payments during each outage, including unsigned manual checks |

Set thresholds from the actual process baseline and investigate clusters of signals. An alert selects a case for evidence-based review; pairing, a shared workstation, a late timestamp, or a changed bank account is not by itself proof of misconduct.

## 7. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

The script reconstructs the supplied log and facts in memory. Its output consists of **preliminary evidence flags, not a final compliance verdict**.

```python

AP-206,Manager_Approve,2026-05-04T13:04:00Z,m11
AP-206,Automated_Validate,2026-05-04T13:05:00Z,SYSTEM
AP-206,Release_Payment,2026-05-04T13:08:00Z,u52
AP-206,Bank_Settlement,2026-05-04T15:00:00Z,SYSTEM
AP-206,Audit_Review,2026-05-05T08:00:00Z,a05

AP-207,Automated_Validate,2026-05-04T14:02:00Z,SYSTEM
AP-207,Release_Payment,2026-05-04T14:05:00Z,u61

raw = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV))
raw["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
raw["org:resource"] = raw["org:resource"].fillna("SYSTEM")
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)
C, A, T, R = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
completed = set(log.loc[log[A].eq("Release_Payment"), C])
completed_log = log.loc[log[C].isin(completed)].copy()

# PM4Py is called on completed-payment cases only. Check its filter orientation.
filtered = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    completed_log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"
)
if isinstance(filtered, pd.DataFrame):
    returned_cases = set(filtered[C])
else:  # EventLog on some installations
    returned_cases = {trace.attributes["concept:name"] for trace in filtered}

actors = []
for case, g in completed_log.groupby(C):
    prep = g.loc[g[A].eq("Prepare_Payment"), R]
    rel = g.loc[g[A].eq("Release_Payment"), R]
    actors.append((case, prep.iloc[0], rel.iloc[0]))
actors = pd.DataFrame(actors, columns=[C, "preparer", "releaser"])
manual_violations = set(actors.loc[actors.preparer.eq(actors.releaser), C])
if returned_cases == manual_violations:
    nominal_violations = returned_cases
    filter_orientation = "PM4Py selected violations"
elif returned_cases == completed - manual_violations:
    nominal_violations = completed - returned_cases
    filter_orientation = "PM4Py selected conforming cases; complement taken"
else:
    nominal_violations = manual_violations
    filter_orientation = "Unexpected PM4Py selection; manual actor check used"

attempted_blocked = []
for case, g in log.groupby(C):
    events = set(g[A])
    if ("Attempt_Release" in events and "Automated_Validate_Block" in events
            and "Release_Payment" not in events):
        attempted_blocked.append(case)

# None means the question does not establish a fact; it is not a positive check.
facts = pd.DataFrame([
    ("AP-201", 1800, 2190, False, None, "pass", True, "2026-05-04T10:00:00Z"),
    ("AP-202", 480000, 0, True, None, "warning_override", False, None),
    ("AP-203", 75000, 1460, False, "2026-05-04T09:55:00Z", "pass", True, "2026-05-04T12:00:00Z"),
    ("AP-204", 220000, 730, False, None, "pass", None, None),
    ("AP-205", 12000, None, None, None, "block", None, None),
    ("AP-206", 95000, 1095, False, "2026-05-04T13:02:00Z", "pass", True, None),
    ("AP-207", 35000, 3285, False, "2026-05-04T13:50:00Z", "pass", True, None),
    ("AP-208", 900000, 240, True, None, "unavailable", None, None),
    ("AP-209", 42000, 1825, False, None, "pass", None, None),
], columns=[C, "amount_eur", "vendor_days_approx", "bank_change_within_7d",
            "token_started", "av_fact", "manager_independent_known", "settlement_fact"])
facts["token_started"] = pd.to_datetime(facts["token_started"], utc=True)
facts["token_active_reported"] = facts[C].isin(["AP-203", "AP-206", "AP-207", "AP-208"])
facts["settlement_fact"] = pd.to_datetime(facts["settlement_fact"], utc=True)

def first_time(g, activities):
    values = g.loc[g[A].isin(activities), T]
    return values.min() if not values.empty else pd.NaT

rows = []
for case, g in log.groupby(C):
    rows.append({
        C: case,
        "prepared_at": first_time(g, ["Prepare_Payment"]),
        "released_at": first_time(g, ["Release_Payment"]),
        "manager_at": first_time(g, ["Manager_Approve", "Manager_Ratify"]),
        "settled_at_log": first_time(g, ["Bank_Settlement"]),
        "audited_at": first_time(g, ["Audit_Review"]),
        "av_event_pass": "Automated_Validate" in set(g[A]),
        "override_seen": "Override_Warning" in set(g[A]),
        "av_outage_seen": "Validation_Service_Unavailable" in set(g[A]),
    })
flags = facts.merge(pd.DataFrame(rows), on=C).merge(actors, on=C, how="left")
flags["settled_at"] = flags["settled_at_log"].combine_first(flags["settlement_fact"])
flags["completed_payment"] = flags[C].isin(completed)
flags["nominal_violation"] = flags[C].isin(nominal_violations)
flags["attempt_blocked_no_release"] = flags[C].isin(attempted_blocked)
flags["av_full_pass"] = (flags.av_fact.eq("pass") & flags.av_event_pass
                         & ~flags.override_seen & ~flags.av_outage_seen)
flags["bank_change_alert"] = flags.bank_change_within_7d
flags["token_before_prepare"] = flags.apply(
    lambda x: (x.token_started <= x.prepared_at) if pd.notna(x.token_started)
              else (pd.NA if x.token_active_reported else False),
    axis=1
)
flags["manager_before_release"] = flags.apply(
    lambda x: pd.notna(x.manager_at) and pd.notna(x.released_at)
              and x.manager_at < x.released_at, axis=1
)
flags["manager_before_settlement"] = flags.apply(
    lambda x: (pd.notna(x.manager_at) and x.manager_at < x.settled_at)
              if pd.notna(x.settled_at) else pd.NA, axis=1
)
# Operational convention: through the end of the next business day, no holiday calendar.
flags["audit_by_next_business_day"] = flags.apply(
    lambda x: pd.notna(x.audited_at) and pd.notna(x.released_at)
              and x.audited_at < (x.released_at.normalize()
                                  + pd.offsets.BDay(1) + pd.Timedelta(days=1)),
    axis=1
)
# Approximation only: bank-specific recall cutoffs may differ.
flags["audit_before_24h_from_settlement"] = flags.apply(
    lambda x: (pd.notna(x.audited_at)
               and x.audited_at <= x.settled_at + pd.Timedelta(hours=24))
              if pd.notna(x.settled_at) else pd.NA, axis=1
)
pair_counts = (actors.groupby(["preparer", "releaser"]).size()
               .rename("pair_case_count").reset_index())
flags = flags.merge(pair_counts, on=["preparer", "releaser"], how="left")
flags["repeated_pair"] = flags.pair_case_count.fillna(0).ge(2)

def preliminary_ed1(x):
    if not x.completed_payment or not x.nominal_violation:
        return "NOT_APPLICABLE"
    checks = [
        bool(x.token_before_prepare) if pd.notna(x.token_before_prepare) else None,
        x.amount_eur < 100000,
        x.vendor_days_approx >= 180 if pd.notna(x.vendor_days_approx) else None,
        not bool(x.bank_change_within_7d)
            if pd.notna(x.bank_change_within_7d) else None,
        bool(x.av_full_pass),
        bool(x.manager_before_settlement)
            if pd.notna(x.manager_before_settlement) else None,
        bool(x.manager_independent_known)
            if pd.notna(x.manager_independent_known) else None,
        bool(x.audit_by_next_business_day),
    ]
    if any(v is False for v in checks):
        return "RECORDED_CONDITION_FAILED"
    if any(v is None for v in checks):
        return "CONDITIONS_UNVERIFIED"
    return "PRELIMINARY_PASS_L3_AUDIT_SCOPE_AND_VETO_UNVERIFIED"

flags["emergency_eligibility_flag"] = flags.apply(preliminary_ed1, axis=1)
print("PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE FLAGS — NOT A FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT")
print("PM4Py filter interpretation:", filter_orientation)
print("Nominal violating completed cases:", sorted(nominal_violations))
print("Attempted but blocked, without completed release:", sorted(attempted_blocked))
print(flags[[C, "nominal_violation", "attempt_blocked_no_release",
             "emergency_eligibility_flag", "token_before_prepare",
             "av_full_pass", "override_seen",
             "manager_before_release", "manager_before_settlement",
             "audit_by_next_business_day", "audit_before_24h_from_settlement",
             "bank_change_alert", "av_outage_seen", "repeated_pair"]]
      .to_string(index=False))
```

`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` may select conforming or violating cases, or return a DataFrame versus EventLog, depending on installed version; the script checks the returned case set against an explicit actor comparison before labeling violations. It does not infer manager authority, L3 level, audit completeness, a bank's actual recall deadline, or collusion from an event name. The next-business-day check uses a weekday calendar without holidays; replace it with the applicable business calendar in production.
