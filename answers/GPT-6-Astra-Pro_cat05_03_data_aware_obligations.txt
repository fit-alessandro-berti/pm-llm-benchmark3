## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

**The assessment must distinguish the state that governed release from evidence discovered later about that state.** A later record can establish that an approval already existed; it cannot automatically establish that a later transaction amount was also the earlier release amount.

**State reconstruction and ordering.** Within each case, order events lexicographically by `(time:timestamp, event_index)`. Apply each nonblank attribute update at its event, then carry that value forward. A blank means “no update”; an attribute remains unknown until an evidenced value becomes effective. Do not backfill or use a case’s final attributes to reconstruct earlier events. Updates carried by `Release_Funds` itself are included in its governing snapshot, but a required prior review must precede release. These choices implement the supplied sparse-update and event-ordering semantics.  

For TX-311, the review at `(16:20, 3)` precedes the jurisdiction change at `(16:20, 4)`. Consequently, the review concerns the US state, while release occurs under the DE state. Sorting only by timestamp could incorrectly reverse that relationship.  

**Materiality and review validity.** I use the following explicit interpretation:

* Amount increases are assessed against the amount covered by the relevant review, not merely against the immediately preceding update. More than 20% invalidates amount-dependent coverage; exactly 20% does not. Once invalidated, a review is not automatically restored by a later decrease. A later standard review cannot substitute for a required enhanced review.
* Actual jurisdiction, risk, or customer-type changes are treated conservatively as material for R1 freshness. For R2, changes to risk or customer type affect whether a review remains relevant. This extends the explicitly defined amount rule into a working interpretation of “material data change”; the source does not fully define categorical materiality.
* A decrease or sub-20% increase is not, by itself, an invalidating amount change. Otherwise, the general freshness wording in R1 would defeat R4’s specific qualifications.  

**Four distinct times matter.**

| Time dimension | Meaning in this assessment                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Event time     | When the business event occurred, as represented by the event timestamp and source ordering.                                                                                       |
| Effective time | When an attribute or exemption governs the transaction. Attribute updates default to their event time; an exemption has an explicit effective interval.                            |
| Record time    | When information entered a database. Both exemption insertions occur after their releases.                                                                                         |
| Evidence time  | A timestamp supporting a historical claim, such as a qualified signature timestamp. This is distinct from both a displayed approval time and when an auditor obtains the evidence. |

Thus EX-77’s 11:00 insertion can support an earlier approval because its valid qualified signature is timestamped 09:05. EX-88’s displayed 08:50 approval is weaker evidence because the certificate chronology conflicts with that apparent timing. TX-305’s settlement amount is reliable at settlement, but no supplied evidence establishes an earlier effective time for it. 

**Assessment assumptions.** I treat the listed review and approval activities as completed actions and assume activity history is complete except where the task identifies missing or contradictory evidence. Without that completeness assumption, an absent action is “not evidenced,” rather than necessarily absent in reality. Findings concern **R1–R7 only**: they do not establish compliance with an unstated general screening obligation, a prohibition on all payments to listed jurisdictions, or additional approval-authority requirements.

For TX-305, the primary assessment conservatively requires evidence that its enhanced review covered the amount-dependent obligation. This is an evidential interpretation, not a claim that R1 expressly mandates a particular review-document field. Section 4 explains the defensible alternative.

The substantive conditions above come from R1–R7; categorical materiality, sticky invalidation, and the half-open expiry interval are stated implementation interpretations. No supplied example falls exactly on an expiry boundary. 

**Formal pseudo-logic.**

Use three-valued predicates: **T** = established, **F** = disproved under the evidence-completeness assumption, and **U** = unresolved. Conjunction and disjunction preserve useful short-circuiting: `F ∧ U = F`, while `T ∨ U = T`.

Let \(S_c(e)[v]\) be the latest effective, nonblank update of attribute \(v\) at or before event \(e\) in case \(c\).

**R1.** Define

$$
T_1(r)=\bigl(S(r)[J]\neq DE\bigr)\land\bigl(S(r)[A]\ge100000\bigr).
$$

Let \(M(r)\) be the latest material change under the stated interpretation, using initial state establishment as the freshness baseline. Then:

$$
T_1(r)\Rightarrow
\left[
\exists q:\ ER(q)\land M(r)\prec q\prec r
\land AmountCoverageValid(q,r)
\right]
\lor ValidExemption(R1,r).
$$

A merely present enhanced-review label does not settle uncertain review coverage.

**R2.**

$$
T_2(r)=\bigl(S(r)[Risk]=HIGH\bigr)\lor\bigl(S(r)[Type]=PEP\bigr),
$$

$$
T_2(r)\Rightarrow
\exists q,a:\ ER(q)\land Relevant(q,r)\land
ComplianceApprove(a)\land q\prec a\prec r\land Covers(a,q).
$$

An approval expressly covering only a standard packet does not establish `Covers(a,q)`.

**R3.** For each relevant list addition \(u\) before release:

$$
Added(u,S(r)[J])\land u\prec r
\land\exists s_0:\ Screen(s_0)\land s_0\prec u
$$

requires

$$
\exists s_1:\ Screen(s_1)\land u\prec s_1\prec r.
$$

This formulation retains a historical rescreening obligation even after a later screen has discharged it. A missing prior-screen anchor does not justify inventing an unconditional baseline-screen requirement.

**R4.** For a review with known positive amount \(A(q)\):

$$
InvalidAmount(q,r)
\iff
\exists u:\ q\prec u\preceq r
\land AmountUpdate(u)
\land A(u)>1.2A(q).
$$

The monitor checks the latest review at an update, while retaining the validity history of any enhanced review later used for R1. An unknown baseline with a subsequent amount update yields **U**. An unknown baseline without such an update is **not itself evidence of R4 invalidation**.

**R5.**

$$
ValidExemption(R1,r)=
AuthorizedHeadApproval
\land ApprovalExistedBy(r)
\land EffectiveFrom\le t(r)<Expiry
\land ScopeMatches(S(r))
\land Waives(R1).
$$

`ApprovalExistedBy(r)` is an evidence-sensitive historical predicate, not a comparison against an unverified displayed date.

**R6.** An unresolved input produces an indeterminate finding only when it can change the result. For example, DE jurisdiction excludes R1 even if amount is unknown; HIGH risk activates R2 even if customer type is unknown.

**R7.**

$$
S_c(e)[v]=Value(u^*),\qquad
u^*=\max_{\preceq}\{u:\ Update(c,v,u)\land u\preceq e\}.
$$

An empty set yields **unknown**, never a future value.

**Verdict precedence:** a demonstrated unmet obligation produces **NONCOMPLIANT**, even if another issue remains uncertain. Otherwise, decisive uncertainty produces **INDETERMINATE**; a necessary and valid waiver produces **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**; ordinary satisfaction or established nonactivation produces **COMPLIANT**.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-302: late recording is not late approval.** EX-77 has strong evidence of approval at 09:05, matching scope, and an effective interval covering the 09:40 release. I interpret the obligation trigger as release because R1 expressly begins “At Release_Funds.” An alternative interpretation requiring approval before the transaction first became foreign and high-value would question the exemption’s timing at creation, but would **not change the overall compliant result**: enhanced review completed at 09:20. Labeling the case `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` because the release explicitly references an exemption is also defensible, provided it is not described as a necessary waiver of an otherwise unmet obligation.   

**TX-305: missing amount does not automatically imply missing review.** The conservative finding is indeterminate because neither the release amount nor the review’s amount coverage is established. However, a literal event-based reading can reach `COMPLIANT`: if the evidenced enhanced review satisfies R1 regardless of amount, and the complete history establishes no subsequent material change, then either R1 is inactive or it is already fulfilled. Under that reading, the unknown trigger is not decisive under R6.

The distinction should not be hidden. A contemporaneous immutable payload showing an amount below €100,000 would exclude R1. A payload and review attachment showing adequate coverage of a higher amount would establish satisfaction. Evidence of an invalidating post-review change without renewed review could establish noncompliance. The next-day settlement amount alone establishes none of those alternatives.   

**TX-310: conflicting evidence is not conclusive proof that no approval existed.** The certificate chronology undermines the claimed early digital approval, but does not logically disprove every possible earlier oral approval. The requester’s testimony needs corroboration; it should neither be accepted automatically nor discarded as impossible merely because the witness is interested.

Independent contemporaneous evidence of Head_Compliance approval, together with a defensible interpretation allowing that approval form, could produce `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. Reliable evidence that approval first occurred after release could produce `NONCOMPLIANT`. An audit standard requiring documented pre-release approval could also classify the supplied evidence as insufficient to demonstrate compliance—but that evidential standard should be stated rather than silently added to R5. **An operational release gate should withhold authorization while the exemption remains unresolved, even though the retrospective classification is indeterminate.**   

**TX-311: ordering changes the review snapshot, not necessarily the ultimate verdict.** The supplied order proves that review preceded the DE update. Nevertheless, R1 is evaluated at release, when jurisdiction is DE. No supplied rule says that foreign-high-value status permanently activates R1.

Evidence that the DE update was inaccurate would make R1 applicable, but would **not by itself prove noncompliance**: the 16:20 enhanced review covered the US/€115,000 state. A changed verdict would additionally require evidence that this review was invalid, materially superseded, or otherwise insufficient. There is no basis for alleging manipulative jurisdiction switching from the sequence alone.   

**TX-306 and TX-308: “any change invalidates review” would conflict with the specific amount rule.** Their compliant classifications depend on preserving review validity after, respectively, a 4.55% increase and a decrease. An additional policy binding approval to an exact immutable amount, or defining every monetary update as material, could change the result; neither requirement is supplied. Separately, evidence that TX-306’s approval concerned an unrelated packet would undermine R2 satisfaction even though the event order is correct.

def known(x):
    return not pd.isna(x)


def tri(x):
    return None if not known(x) else bool(x)


def ALL(values):
    values = [tri(x) for x in values]
    return False if False in values else (
        None if None in values else True
    )


def ANY(values):
    values = [tri(x) for x in values]
    return True if True in values else (
        None if None in values else False
    )


def compare(x, op, y):
    return None if not known(x) or not known(y) else bool(op(x, y))


def before(a, b):
    """Strict precedence; equal cross-case times lack a shared index."""
    if a[TS] != b[TS]:
        return bool(a[TS] < b[TS])
    if a[CASE] != b[CASE]:
        return None
    return bool(a[IDX] < b[IDX])


def amount_retained(q, history):
    """
    Review-relative cumulative threshold; invalidation is sticky.
    Returns (True/False/None, first invalidating or uncertain update).
    A later decrease does not automatically restore an invalidated review.
    """
    changes = [
        e for e in history
        if e["pos"] > q["pos"] and known(e["update:amount"])
    ]
    if not changes:
        # No observed increase, even if the baseline is unknown.
        return True, None
    if not known(q["amount"]):
        return None, changes[0]
    for e in changes:
        if e["amount"] * 5 > q["amount"] * 6:  # Strictly more than 20%.
            return False, e
    return True, None


def categories_retained(q, history, columns):
    checks = []
    for e in history:
        if e["pos"] > q["pos"]:
            for col in columns:
                if known(e["update:" + col]):
                    checks.append(
                        compare(e[col], lambda x, y: x == y, q[col])
                    )
    return ALL(checks)


def material_history(history):
    """
    Known/possible material change.
    Initial state is the freshness baseline.
    Categorical changes are material under the stated interpretation.
    Before any review, compare an increase with the previous known amount.
    """
    last, possible, last_review = history[0], None, None
    previous = None

    for e in history:
        certain, uncertain = False, False
        if previous is not None:
            for col in CATEGORIES:
                if known(e["update:" + col]):
                    if not known(previous[col]):
                        uncertain = True
                    elif e[col] != previous[col]:
                        certain = True

            if known(e["update:amount"]):
                old = previous["amount"]
                base = (
                    last_review["amount"]
                    if last_review is not None else old
                )
                # A decrease is not itself an invalidating change.
                if not known(old) or e["amount"] > old:
                    if not known(base):
                        uncertain = True
                    elif e["amount"] * 5 > base * 6:
                        certain = True

        if certain:
            last = e
        if uncertain:
            possible = e
        if e[ACT] in REVIEW:
            last_review = e
        previous = e

    return last, possible


list_updates = df.loc[
    df[ACT].eq("Sanctions_List_Update")
].to_dict("records")


def sanctions_check(release, prior):
    """
    Apply list additions globally, matching jurisdiction at release.
    No general baseline-screen requirement is invented from R3.
    """
    jurisdiction = release["jurisdiction"]
    candidates = [
        u for u in list_updates
        if not known(jurisdiction)
        or u["update:jurisdiction"] == jurisdiction
    ]
    if any(before(u, release) is None for u in candidates):
        return None, None, None

    updates = [
        u for u in candidates if before(u, release) is True
    ]
    latest = max(
        updates, key=lambda u: (u[TS], u[IDX]), default=None
    )

    if not updates:
        return False, True, latest
    if not known(jurisdiction):
        return None, None, latest

    screens = [e for e in prior if e[ACT] == "Sanctions_Screen"]
    if not screens:
        # Cannot establish the rule's previous-screen anchor.
        return None, None, latest

    pending = ANY([before(screens[-1], u) for u in updates])
    if pending is not False:
        return (
            pending,
            False if pending is True else None,
            latest
        )

    triggered_earlier = ANY([
        before(s, u) for s in screens for u in updates
    ])
    return triggered_earlier, True, latest

def exemption_valid(row):
    if not known(row["exemption_id"]):
        # No claimed/supported exemption in the supplied evidence.
        return False

    structural = ALL([
        compare(
            row["approved_by"],
            lambda x, y: x == y,
            "Head_Compliance"
        ),
        compare(row["waives"], lambda x, y: x == y, "R1"),
        compare(
            row["jurisdiction"],
            lambda x, y: x == y,
            row["scope_jurisdiction"]
        ),
        compare(
            row["customer_type"],
            lambda x, y: x == y,
            row["scope_customer_type"]
        ),
        compare(
            row["amount"],
            lambda x, y: x <= y,
            row["max_amount"]
        ),
        compare(
            row["effective_from"],
            lambda x, y: x <= y,
            row["release_at"]
        ),
        compare(
            row["release_at"],
            lambda x, y: x < y,
            row["expires_at"]
        )
    ])

    # Conflicted/late digital evidence does not disprove every
    # possible earlier oral approval.
    timely_proof = (
        True
        if tri(row["signature_verified"]) is True
        and known(row["trusted_signature_at"])
        and row["trusted_signature_at"] <= row["release_at"]
        else None
    )
    return ALL([structural, timely_proof])


def gate(trigger, satisfied):
    trigger, satisfied = tri(trigger), tri(satisfied)
    if trigger is False:
        return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    if satisfied is True:
        # Also resolves a trigger immaterial to the final outcome.
        return "SATISFIED"
    if trigger is None or satisfied is None:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    return (
        "FAILED" if COMPLETE_EVENT_AND_LIST_HISTORY else "UNKNOWN"
    )

The `R4` diagnostic is deliberately **not** a standalone release verdict: changing an amount is not itself prohibited, and an invalidated review matters only when an applicable obligation depends on it.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

| Failure mode                                      | What an activity-only check misses                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Final-state leakage**                           | Copying final attributes backward gives TX-305 an unsupported release amount and makes TX-311’s earlier review appear to have occurred under DE jurisdiction. Event-time snapshots are needed.                                                            |
| **Post-hoc exemption handling**                   | Requiring `Record_Exemption` before release rejects EX-77 despite strong timely-approval evidence. Accepting any later exemption record risks accepting EX-88 without adequate proof. Activity order alone cannot adjudicate these evidence differences.  |
| **Material changes after review**                 | TX-309 contains the expected enhanced-review-before-release sequence, yet its later 22.88% increase invalidates amount-dependent coverage. The review baseline and change magnitude matter.                                                               |
| **Conditional activation**                        | R1 depends on current jurisdiction and amount; R2 depends on HIGH risk or PEP status. Neither can be represented faithfully as an unconditional review-before-release requirement.                                                                        |
| **Missing data and three-valued outcomes**        | A binary trace result cannot distinguish an established nontrigger from an unknown trigger. TX-305 requires explicit treatment of whether its existing review resolves the uncertainty.                                                                   |
| **Same-timestamp ordering**                       | TX-311’s timestamp alone does not tell whether the review preceded the jurisdiction update. The supplied `event_index` resolves it and changes the review snapshot.                                                                                       |
| **External reference-data changes**               | TX-304’s earlier clear screening becomes insufficient because the relevant sanctions list changes. A case trace that excludes the jurisdiction-specific list history misses the rescreening obligation.                                                   |
| **Activity presence versus substantive coverage** | TX-307 contains Compliance_Approve, but it concerns a standard packet. Presence of an approval label is not equivalent to approval following the required enhanced review.                                                                                |

Control-flow conformance is therefore useful as a **structural check**, but the complete assessment must also evaluate historical state, quantitative change rules, review-to-packet relationships, exception scope, and evidence quality.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

**Capture an immutable release decision record.** At every release, persist the actual amount and currency, jurisdiction, risk level, customer type, applicable exemption IDs, evaluated obligations, rule version, referenced reviews and approvals, and the decision outcome. Include a state-version identifier and payload hash. Make execution conditional on that version still being current so that a change between evaluation and release cannot silently invalidate the authorization.

**Bind reviews and approvals to explicit evidence.** Record separate review IDs, review type, completion time, reviewed state version, reviewed amount, packet hash, and covered obligations. Every approval should identify its reviewed packet and enhanced-review ID. Record invalidation events with their triggering update, baseline amount, percentage change, affected obligations, and any replacement review. This would distinguish “an enhanced review happened” from “this enhanced review still covers this release.”

**Separate exemption approval from exemption recording.** Store actual approval time, claimed approval time, effective-from and expiry times, database insertion time, trusted signature timestamp, certificate chronology, verification result, approver identity and authority, scope, and waived rule IDs. Preserve the signed artifact and the evidence supporting any outage explanation or earlier oral approval. A displayed effective date should never serve as the sole evidence that approval already existed.

**Use append-only changes with stable ordering and provenance.** Each update should contain old and new values, event time, effective time, record time, actor, source system, reason, supporting evidence, and a stable source sequence. Represent “no update,” “value unknown,” and “explicitly cleared” as different operations. Corrections should append a new assertion with provenance rather than erase the history that governed an earlier decision.

**Version the policy and the sanctions reference data.** Clarify categorical materiality, cumulative amount comparisons, whether invalidation is permanent until renewed review, how long an approval remains relevant, whether any obligation persists after its trigger disappears, and admissible exemption evidence. For sanctions, log list version, jurisdiction added, effective/publication/ingestion times, and the exact version used by each screening. A shared reference-data identifier should connect one list update to all affected cases, rather than requiring it to appear independently in every case trace.

**Separate historical findings from operational authorization.** A release gate should distinguish “obligation not triggered,” “satisfied,” “validly waived,” “failed,” and “unknown.” Decisive unknowns should route to a hold or an explicitly governed exception process, not a low-risk default. An audit record should retain the original finding, evidence available at that assessment time, later evidence, the reviewer’s rationale, and any revised finding. This preserves the difference between **a control that failed**, **a valid control documented late**, and **a decision whose historical basis remains unresolved**.
