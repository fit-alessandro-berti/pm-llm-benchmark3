# 1. Executive memo

Approve **Alternative E**, a staged rollout of the simplified workflow for verified low-risk houses, with controlled regional tests before expansion to other segments. Authorize the next tranche of low-risk orders where teams can provide coordinator backup and enforce the billing and permit controls.

During the pilot, median cycle time fell from 14.2 to 10.8 days and the share of orders with three or more handovers fell from 34% to 12%. At the same time, p95 rose from 45 to 62 days, first-time-right installation fell from 82% to 76%, and 30-day complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%. Low-risk houses improved on both speed and quality, including an eight-day reduction at p90. Medium-risk apartments and older permit-sensitive buildings had p90 increases of 12 and 25 days, respectively, alongside more complaints. Pilot regions had a harder building mix, and early risk labels were incomplete. The results support testing the low-risk path at greater volume under safeguards. They leave the effect of each redesign component uncertain.

Keep coordinator ownership and combined validation and scheduling for verified low-risk houses. Restore the standard survey decision for medium-risk and unclassified orders, and require a documented permit check where applicable before dispatch. Require a documented successful activation test before billing in every segment, including cases handled by the manual activation team. Give each coordinator a named backup and a shared case record. Review open cases that are already billed without recorded activation success, verify their status with source systems, and prioritize unresolved service or billing issues.

Visible cost per order fell from €184 to €161. The incomplete estimate including complaint and revisit handling moved from €198 to €207. Work may have shifted from the front of the process to exception teams. Field-survey utilization fell from 78% to 52%, while second-visit utilization rose from 66% to 92%. Slower recovery after a failed visit in heavily loaded regions makes backup coverage and follow-up capacity part of the rollout decision.

Fund immediate event monitoring and downstream cost capture, validate the risk rule, and establish concurrent regional controls with complete risk and segment fields at intake. Keep the billing and permit safeguards in all arms. Test survey skipping only after rule validation identifies a stratum where either survey decision is defensible. Review control breaches and queues weekly, then make expansion decisions by segment once the service and cost cohorts mature. Expansion should require faster verified service, bounded tail and quality risk, acceptable fully loaded cost, and demonstrated compliance and backup coverage.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

- **High fitness and low precision.** Fitness measures how well the model can replay observed traces. Precision reflects how tightly it limits behavior to what the log supports. A simplified common path plus flexible combinations of survey skipping, revisits, manual activation, and billing can replay many pilot traces while allowing many sequences the log never exhibits. The lower generalization score adds caution about applying the model to new cases. Use the discovered model to investigate variants and explicit process rules to monitor billing and permit controls.
- **Faster median and slower tail.** Merged scheduling and fewer transfers can remove days from common cases. A failed first visit, a delayed return, or a stalled activation puts orders into much longer paths. The apartment installation-to-activation and failed-visit-to-second-visit intervals support that mechanism. An aggregate percentile also depends on the composition of the entire cohort. Compare intake cohorts with equal follow-up and separately time order receipt to verified working service, since moving billing earlier may alter a process-end measure.
- **Fewer handovers and lower resilience.** The handover network counts transfers of work. The working-together network shows which roles co-participate in cases. A single owner can streamline routine coordination while concentrating information and exception decisions. At high utilization, absences or surges can cause queues where colleagues lack the context or authority to take over.
- **Lower visible cost and higher rework or downstream cost.** Fewer surveys and administrative transfers can lower recorded costs while extra visits, manual activations, complaints, and billing corrections consume capacity elsewhere. The survey and revisit utilization shifts are consistent with this explanation. The incomplete complaint cost window makes the reported increase in total cost provisional.
- **Low-risk gains and complex-building deterioration.** Simple houses can benefit from merged tasks when little prerequisite work is needed. Apartments and older buildings have more opportunities for a missed survey, access constraint, or permit check to trigger a failed visit and a return queue. Business billing disputes and rural rescheduling add distinct exception paths. Risk-stratified comparisons with consistent labels are needed to distinguish changes within segments from changes in the pilot’s overall mix.

# 4. Decision alternatives

| Alternative | Advantage | Main risk | When reasonable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Scale R nationally** | Extends faster routine handling and simpler ownership quickly. | Exposes more orders to long tails, revisits, premature billing, and centralized queues. | Only after safeguards are built into the design and mature controlled cohorts meet segment-level service, cost, and capacity criteria across representative regions. |
| **B. Roll back to baseline** | Restores familiar exception routing and relieves exposure to risky pilot paths. | Gives up low-risk speed and first-time-right gains, and reintroduces work transfers. | If hard controls or safe routing cannot be enforced, or if safeguarded cohorts show broad harm after a fair comparison. Use a targeted rollback if the unsafe pathway can be isolated. |
| **C. Scale coordinator ownership only** | Retains single-case accountability and restores standard survey and activation sequencing. | Ownership alone may yield less of the observed speed gain and can create a coverage bottleneck. | If a guarded comparison isolates a benefit from coordinator ownership and the backup model maintains service through absences and peaks. |
| **D. Run a controlled regional experiment** | Separates component effects from case mix and period effects before wider commitments. | Delays potential low-risk gains if all deployment is held back. Short follow-up would miss costs and tail outcomes. | If reliable risk routing or operating controls are still being established, or if management needs evidence on a specific component before extending it to a complex segment. |
| **E. Stage the guarded low-risk rollout and test expansion** | Captures the clearest segment gains while limiting exposure and preserving a path to learn. | Classification errors can send complex orders down the simple path, and two workflows add operational complexity. | If risk eligibility is verified, missing or conflicting attributes route to standard checks, and supervisors can enforce controls and take over exceptions. |

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

The ranking reflects how likely each item is to change the next deployment decision and the cost of getting that decision wrong. Start long-lead collection immediately and sequence experiments around their prerequisites.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **M7. Monitor billing and permit conformance** | Match flagged traces to activation and permit source records to distinguish true bypasses from missing or delayed events. A verified bypass triggers a hold on the affected pathway and review of exposed orders. Reliable controls are a prerequisite for expansion. |
| 2 | **M4. Capture full downstream cost for 60 days after billing** | Determine whether lower process costs survive revisits, manual activation, complaint handling, and billing corrections. Results can tighten eligibility, retire the rule, or identify a group for a controlled test. |
| 4 | **M1. Randomize survey skipping within eligible risk strata** | After M6, estimate the incremental effect of skipping on speed, first-time-right, revisits, complaints, tail duration, and cost while holding the other workflow rules fixed. | 5 | **M3. # 6. Final recommendation

**Proceed with E and use controlled regional comparisons to decide each expansion.**

- **Act now.** Apply a documented successful activation gate before billing, verify applicable permits before dispatch, reconcile open exceptions, and assign coordinator backups with shared case records and clear ownership through verified activation. Hold the affected pathway if a control is bypassed and investigate any gaps in source records.
- **Pause and scale.** Pause medium-risk survey skipping and pilot deferrals of activation checks needed to establish successful service. Stage coordinator ownership and combined validation and scheduling for verified low-risk houses within staffed capacity. Use standard prerequisite and exception routing for other cases, including cases with missing or conflicting risk information.
- **Measure next.** Start M7, M4, and M6 now and add structured reason codes as cases encounter exceptions. Compare new intake cohorts with concurrent standard-workflow controls under the same hard safeguards. Randomize within strata where feasible, or by team where shared capacity would contaminate case assignment. Preserve the original assignments in analysis, include open orders in time-to-event analysis, and allow common follow-up for tail, complaints, and cost. Record building risk, region, and segment at intake and disclose missingness and uncertainty by cohort.
- **Set the expansion bar before enrollment.** As proposed decision margins, require a median reduction of at least two days from receipt to verified working service, with uncertainty bounds excluding no improvement. Rule out increases greater than two days at p90 or p95, and greater than one percentage point in SLA breaches, 30-day complaints, second visits, or first-time-right failures. Require fully loaded mean cost at or below the control estimate and rule out an increase above €5 per order. Require verified compliance with both hard gates and demonstrated backup coverage under planned load. Apply the criteria to each candidate segment, check performance across regions, and keep expansion on hold when follow-up or sample size leaves material harm unresolved.
