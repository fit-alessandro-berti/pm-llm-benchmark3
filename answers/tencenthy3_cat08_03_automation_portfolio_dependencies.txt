# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability:** A3, A9, and A12 must log model suggestion, human override, and final decision. *Control:* Immutable audit trail object linked to core banking case ID; compliance reviews overrides monthly.
- **False positives / negatives:** A3 KYC pre-classifier false negatives could let uncleared entities reach `Customer signing` (provisional risk 1.1% remediation). *Control:* Conservative threshold favoring escalation; specialist review sampling.
- **Model drift:** A2 classification and A6 extraction will drift as product mixes change. *Control:* Quarterly recalibration using loan-origination samples; fallback to manual classification if confidence < threshold.
- **Exception handling:** A5 rigid checklists may increase RM resistance and customer friction. *Control:* Adaptive guidance co-designed with RMs; monitor `Request missing documents  Financial document review` (2,800) loop rate.
- **Four-eyes compliance:** High-exposure SME month-end rush causes 5.8% same-person violations. *Control:* A12 workbench flags same-actor completion of credit analysis and committee packaging; system blocks committee submit if violation predicted.

# 7. Final recommendation

I recommend a **blended Compliance-First + Bottleneck portfolio** implemented via the phased plan above: **A1 + A3 + A9 in Phase 1, then A6 + A4 + A12 in Phase 2** (total six modules over year, but only four new in year-1 budget if we cap at four: prioritize A1, A3, A9, A12 in year 1, then A4/A6 next year—or trim to A1, A3, A6, A12 to respect the four-module cap while keeping control and bottleneck balance).

**Trade-off argument:** Senior management’s instinct to automate the highest-frequency activity (Register application, 11,880 cases) is partially correct but insufficient alone. A1 is indeed the right *foundational* automation because it is very high feasibility and politically visible, and it frees the 12 FTE back office. However, frequency ignores the fact that `Resolve entity mismatch` (only 1,750 cases) blocks 82% of critical paths with a 31h wait, and that KYC clearance failures enable illegal provisional signings. Automating only A1 or A9 downstream would “paint over” the real synchronization and control deficits. By pairing the high-frequency enabler (A1) with a control-strengthening module (A3 or A12) and a waiting-time dominator (A6 or A4), the bank achieves defensible process-level gains: it reduces end-to-end risk exposure, prevents four-eyes violations, and avoids shifting overloaded work onto the 3 FTE compliance specialists or 9 FTE credit analysts without prerequisite data quality. Thus, the highest-frequency task should be automated first *only as an enabler*, not as the sole objective.
