# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

1.  **Delayed arrival scans (up to 2 minutes):** Jobs may have actually arrived earlier than recorded. This could explain minor FIFO violations but is unlikely to change the major orderings (e.g., D08 arriving 2 mins earlier at 11:14 still doesn't beat D04 at 11:05).
2.  **Strategic/Noisy Estimates:** The `est_min` values may be inaccurate or manipulated by dispatchers. If estimates are purely advisory, SEPT-based logic would appear random.
3.  **Hidden Appointment Tolerances:** Appointments in QA and QD seem to have soft or hard windows. A03 arriving early and waiting until 08:20 implies a hard start window. D06 waiting until 11:30 implies D-X could not pull it forward despite being idle since 11:20.
4.  **Preemption Costs:** In QB, B05 failing to preempt B03 suggests preemption costs are high, and interruptions are only authorized for explicitly flagged "urgent" arrivals (like B02), rather than blanket P1 priority.

# 6. Additional event data needed

To resolve the ambiguities in these queues, the following data should be collected:
1.  **Queue-entry vs. Arrival Scan Time:** To accurately assess FIFO compliance and rule out 2-minute scan delays.
2.  **Dispatch-decision Timestamp:** To distinguish between when a job finished and when the next job was actually dispatched.
3.  **Appointment Tolerance Windows:** Hard start times vs. soft target times. This is critical for QA and QD.

**QA** is the most identifiable queue: it clearly operates under a FIFO discipline with a strict appointment override. **QD** is similarly identifiable as Skill-Based Routing with a strict FIFO queue for each resource, where appointments act as hard start times.
