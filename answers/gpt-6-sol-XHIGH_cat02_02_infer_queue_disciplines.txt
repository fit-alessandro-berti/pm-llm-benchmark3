# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

**Convention.** FIFO means effective queue-entry order. Priority means lowest number first, using entry order for ties. SPT uses `est_min` for an unstarted job and estimated remaining time for a suspended one; it is normally non-preemptive, so a preemptive *shortest-remaining-time* comparison is identified separately. An appointment prediction assumes the listed time is nominally due, since no admission or tolerance window is supplied. Skill routing determines who *can* serve a job, not which of several eligible jobs goes next. The displayed estimates equal recorded work durations here, but this does not establish what remaining-time estimates or revised estimates dispatch saw later.

The essential collection unit is a **decision event with its candidate set**, linked to job and resource histories. More completion-only records would repeat the present identification problem.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is most identifiable: FIFO repeatedly fits after one recorded appointment jump. QC is least identifiable: its choices need at least one unrecorded switch, commitment, or eligibility condition, while several rules sometimes predict the same job. QD clearly requires skill-aware eligibility, but this alone does not identify its ordering rule. The most dangerous claim is **strict preemptive priority in QB**: one urgent interruption demonstrates preemption, while several later decisions violate strict priority, so that claim would overpromise urgent-job treatment.
