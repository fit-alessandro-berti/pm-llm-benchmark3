## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** *partly causal through mechanisms* (confidence: 65/100)
The evidence suggests Orion’s longer cycle times are **not purely confounded by case mix**, but neither is the group label itself the sole driver. Orion’s higher cycle times are **mediated by operational mechanisms**—night shifts, compliance requirements, and rework—rather than direct inefficiency. The crude aggregate comparison (Orion: 21.96h vs. Nova: 15.24h) reverses within complexity strata (e.g., Orion’s high-complexity cases average 31.57h vs. Nova’s 35.00h), indicating that Orion handles harder cases *more efficiently* but is penalized by its case mix and structural conditions (e.g., 92.9% night shifts vs. Nova’s 25%).

**Strongest evidence for causality:**
- Orion’s **lower cycle times within complexity strata** (e.g., high-complexity: 31.57h vs. Nova’s 35.00h) suggest it may *mitigate* delays for difficult cases, possibly due to certified reviewers or night-shift focus.
- **Mediated effects:** Orion’s longer queue waits (e.g., high-complexity: 19.47h vs. Nova’s 22.75h) and higher compliance rates (64.3% vs. 25%) imply its delays stem from **process steps** (e.g., compliance checks) rather than the group itself.

**Strongest evidence against causality:**
- **Selection bias:** Only completed cases are observed; Orion’s night-shift cases may have higher abandonment rates, truncating its apparent cycle times.
- **Proxy role:** Orion may be a **bundle of mechanisms** (shift, compliance, reviewer skill) rather than a causal agent. Reassigning cases without addressing these (e.g., night-shift capacity) could backfire.

**Proposed 50% reassignment risks:**
- **Success:** If Orion’s delays are driven by **queueing** (e.g., night-shift bottlenecks), redistributing cases to Nova’s day shift could reduce waits.
- **Failure:** If Orion’s efficiency stems from **reviewer skill** (e.g., certified staff handling compliance/rework better), reassigning cases to Nova could increase rework or quality issues.
- **Backfire:** If Nova’s day-shift capacity is saturated, adding cases could worsen its queue waits, negating gains.

**Key uncertainty:** Whether Orion’s *apparent* efficiency within strata is real or an artifact of **unobserved case risk** (e.g., Orion’s "high-complexity" cases are less risky than Nova’s).

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention                                                                 | target mechanism                                                                 | assignment unit               | safeguards                                                                 | process-mining measurements                                                                 | causal learning value                                                                 | stop or rollback rule                                                                 |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | **Randomized routing pilot (10% of cases)**                                 | Isolate direct effect of `resource_group` on cycle time.                        | Individual cases              | - Stratify by complexity/shift.<br>- Cap at 10% of volume.<br>- Monitor quality. | - Cycle time, queue wait, review service time.<br>- Rework rates.<br>- Appeal rates.      | Tests whether group assignment **directly** affects performance (vs. mediation).      | Stop if appeal rates increase by >20% or cycle times worsen by >15% in either group.  |
| 2     | **Night-shift capacity expansion (add 2 reviewers to Orion)**               | Reduce queue waits by increasing night-shift capacity.                          | Shift-level                    | - Phase in gradually.<br>- Monitor day-shift spillover.                    | - Queue wait times.<br>- Cycle time.<br>- Compliance completion rates.                  | Tests whether Orion’s delays are **queue-driven** (vs. service-driven).               | Roll back if day-shift queue waits increase by >10%.                                 |
| 3     | **Compliance fast-track for low-risk cases (bypass check for 30% of cases)** | Reduce compliance-mediated delays.                                              | Case-level                     | - Risk-score cases.<br>- Audit compliance decisions.                       | - Cycle time for fast-tracked cases.<br>- Appeal rates for fast-tracked cases.          | Tests whether compliance is a **mediator** of Orion’s delays.                         | Stop if appeal rates for fast-tracked cases exceed 10%.                              |

**Final recommendation (120-200 words):**
The proposed 50% reassignment is **premature** and risks **disrupting Orion’s efficiency** without addressing root causes. Instead, adopt a **phased learning plan** to disentangle mechanisms:
1. **Start with a small randomized pilot** (Phase 1) to isolate the direct effect of `resource_group`. If Orion’s cycle times remain lower within strata, the group itself is not the problem—**mediated effects** (compliance, shift) are.
2. **Expand night-shift capacity** (Phase 2) to test whether Orion’s delays are queue-driven. If queue waits drop without increasing day-shift backlogs, the issue is **structural**, not group-specific.
3. **Fast-track compliance for low-risk cases** (Phase 3) to test compliance mediation. If cycle times improve without quality loss, compliance is a **key lever**.

**Key safeguards:**
- **Stratify by complexity/shift** to avoid confounding.
- **Monitor quality** (appeals) to detect unintended consequences.
- **Cap pilot size** (10%) to limit risk.

**Rollback triggers:**
- Appeal rates increase by >20%.
- Day-shift queue waits increase by >10%.
- Cycle times worsen by >15% in either group.

This approach **learns while limiting risk**, unlike blind reassignment.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved case risk:** *Add a risk score* (e.g., fraud flags, prior claims) to test whether Orion’s "high-complexity" cases are less risky than Nova’s.
2. **Abandoned cases:** *Track cases abandoned after classification* to assess selection bias in completed-case data.
3. **Reviewer-level data:** *Record individual reviewer IDs* to compare performance within and across groups.
4. **Queue-state dynamics:** *Log queue lengths at assignment time* to model how `arrival_load` affects `queue_wait`.
5. **Compliance decision timing:** *Record compliance check duration* to separate its effect from `queue_wait`.
