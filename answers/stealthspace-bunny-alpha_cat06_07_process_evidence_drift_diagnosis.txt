## 1. Drift diagnosis table

The prediction anchor must be the **correctly normalized diagnosis event**, using only information available at that event. The categories below are not mutually exclusive: routing changes can change the feature distribution; supplier delays can change both the process and the outcome relationship; and an immature outcome API can distort both the observed breach rate and predictive metrics.

“Concept drift” here specifically means deterioration in \(P(Y\mid X,t)\), not merely an increase in average breach prevalence. Evidence for a change conditional on route is stronger than evidence for a change conditional on every recorded predictor.

| Drift type | Months implicated | Supporting evidence | Contradicting or insufficient evidence | Confidence | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Covariate drift | April as **corrupted feature representation**; June and August–October as genuine case-mix changes | April’s unknown share rose to 17% and missing queue to 14%, coinciding with the activity rename. In June, V2 rose from 34% to 52%, with V1 falling from 43% to 30%. V3 rose from 22% in July to 35% in August and 42% in September. | April’s corrupted inputs should not be described as a real change in the world. Aggregate scores alone cannot identify which underlying features moved, and no prediction-coverage denominator is supplied. A route-mix shift can change aggregate metrics without changing any within-route relationship. | High for June and the V3 mix shift; high that April’s feature space was corrupted; lower for the exact contribution of individual features | Normalize and replay April; instrument feature distributions and score coverage; monitor V1/V2/V3 separately. Do not automatically retrain merely because route prevalence changes. |
| Concept drift | July warning; August–September strongest; October indeterminate | Matured V3 breach rose from 0.35 in June to 0.46 in July, 0.64 in August, and 0.61 in September, while V1 and V2 remained comparatively stable. A useful triangulation is to standardize the reported **matured variant rates** to each month’s variant mix:

- June: approximately \(0.30(0.16)+0.52(0.09)+0.18(0.35)=15.8\%\), close to the observed 15%.
- August: approximately \(0.25(0.18)+0.40(0.11)+0.35(0.64)=31.3\%\).
- September: approximately \(0.22(0.19)+0.36(0.12)+0.42(0.61)=34.1\%\).

These are illustrative calculations, **not mature reconstructions**: the variant tables may use different source populations and contain selected matured cases. Nevertheless, they show that August–September cannot be explained only by the growing V3 share. At August’s mix, even July’s higher V3 breach rate would produce roughly 25.5%; the subsequent increase within V3 adds substantial risk.

Also, the case-start denominator should not be assumed to match the scored or diagnosis cohort. Track:

\[
\text{started} \rightarrow \text{diagnosed} \rightarrow \text{validly scored}
\rightarrow \text{outcome label available}.
\]

A pipeline incident can affect both the quality of scores and which cases receive scores at all.

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

| Month | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **January** | A mature, comparatively stable baseline. AUROC is 0.81, mean predicted risk 0.19 versus 0.18 observed breach, and Brier/ECE are 0.132/0.025. V3’s part wait is 28 hours, with a matured V3 breach rate of 0.32. There is no strong evidence of a new failure mechanism. |
| **February** | Performance remains stable: AUROC 0.82, Brier 0.130, and ECE 0.022. The modest increase in part waiting to 31 hours is not accompanied by a clear V3 outcome jump. This helps distinguish ordinary process variation from the later supplier incident. |
| **March** | Another usable mature baseline. Part waiting reaches 35 hours and V3 breach is 0.30, but predictive and calibration metrics remain stable. These cases are outcome-mature and pipeline-plausible, although old data will not necessarily represent the later supplier and Partner-Z environments. |
| **April** | This is primarily a **schema and feature-pipeline incident**, not a maturity or concept-drift episode. Labels are 100% mature, and variant rates remain near their earlier range. Yet mean risk jumps to 0.28, AUROC falls to 0.67, and unknown/missing features spike. The renamed diagnosis event and incorrect prefixes provide a direct mechanism. The 18% unmapped share also means reported predictive metrics may omit difficult cases entirely. A fixed-model replay after normalization, deduplication, and full feature recomputation is the decisive test. |
| **May** | The event mapping largely recovers: unknown activity falls to 2% and missing queue to 3%, with AUROC 0.78 and Brier/ECE 0.146/0.050. The remaining concern is incomplete recomputation of April-derived features, especially cached event counts. It should not automatically be attributed to every May case. If affected historical records were not reused, May’s residual gap requires a separate lineage and performance investigation. |
| **June** | This is a **real routing/process-mix change**, not evidence by itself of concept drift. V2 jumps to 52%, V2 remains fast, and V2 breach remains 0.09. The lower aggregate breach rate is compatible with the new mix. AUROC remains 0.79, while ECE rises to 0.060 and mean risk is 0.20 versus 0.15 observed. Aggregate calibration or adaptation to the new mix is warranted; wholesale model replacement is not yet justified. Brier must also be interpreted relative to prevalence: a constant-risk benchmark improves from about 0.144 at 18% prevalence to 0.128 at 15%, so 0.130 to 0.139 is not a pure discrimination measure. |
| **July** | The first strong warning of a real V3 problem. The supplier issue begins on 10 July; part waiting rises to 65 hours and matured V3 breach to 0.46. Labels are 98% mature and data quality is nearly normal, so the 2% missingness cannot explain the V3 jump. Use diagnoses before and after 10 July to test exposure more precisely than a monthly average permits. |
| **August** | Performance/process, covariate, and target-context deterioration overlap. V3 reaches 35% of cases, waiting reaches 130 hours, and matured V3 breach reaches 0.64 while V1/V2 remain stable. Pipeline quality is normal, making the April-style schema explanation weak. However, 93% label maturity means the aggregate 28% and predictive metrics may still be selected downward toward faster-completing cases. The large V3 shift is real; its exact population-wide magnitude awaits mature outcomes. |
| **September** | This is the most heterogeneous month: ongoing supplier/process deterioration, greater V3 exposure, and the Partner-Z population shift. V3 breach remains high at 0.61, V3 share reaches 42%, and Partner Z is 24% of cases with a larger V3 concentration. Only 82% of labels are available, so the aggregate 31% and risk scores of 0.21 are not a mature population comparison. The observed 16% breach rate is not comparable to mature months. Likewise, AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205, and ECE 0.130 describe only the available-label subset. They cannot establish that discrimination or calibration has genuinely deteriorated to those values for the eventual cohort. Nevertheless, the continuing 155-hour V3 wait remains an operational risk and the 0.23 mean forecast remains provisional, not a validated mature calibration target. |

Thus April is a feature-construction incident; June is a genuine mix/routing change; August–September contain overlapping real target deterioration and population change; and October is heavily confounded by selective label availability. They should not be joined into one undifferentiated “model decline” time series.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

All comparative tests should use case-level bootstrap confidence intervals, adequate sample-size gates, and a fixed prediction cutoff. Small monthly/source cells should produce “insufficient evidence,” not unstable rankings.

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   - For each diagnosis cohort, calculate the matured-label fraction by elapsed time since diagnosis, overall and by variant, Partner Z, and source. Estimate time to label availability with Kaplan–Meier curves, treating unobserved label availability at the cutoff as right-censored. Report 30/45/60/90-day maturity and remaining pending fractions.
   - Reconstruct reporting snapshots for successive cutoffs using only labels available at each cutoff. Freeze original feature snapshots and original scores; do not silently recompute a historical “original prediction” with today’s pipeline.
   - Also reconstruct historical **training** snapshots, fit candidates using only labels available then, and evaluate on later, matured outcomes.
   - **If censoring explains much of the apparent decline:** October’s breach rate will rise and its metrics may change as the backlog clears; differences will be largest for V3 and Partner Z. A replay on the same final matured cohort and pipeline will perform substantially better than the original available-label evaluation.
   - **If deterioration is real:** differences will persist when the same fully matured cohorts and corrected features are evaluated. The final matured October snapshot should still show meaningful underprediction or weak conditional discrimination.
   - This falsifies a maturity-only explanation if clearing the backlog without changing features, cases, or model leaves the performance problem unchanged.
   - If \(M_i\) indicates label availability, a possible full-cohort validation estimator is
     \[
     \widehat r=\frac{\sum_i w_iM_i y_i}{\sum_i w_iM_i},
     \qquad w_i=\frac{1}{\widehat P(M_i=1\mid X_i,\text{month})}.
     \]
     Use this only if observation is ignorable given measured variables, estimate weights without the target, and show sensitivity to unobserved selection. Otherwise report bounds, not a falsely precise adjusted rate.

2. **Controlled activity normalization, duplicate cleaning, and feature recomputation**
   - Preserve immutable raw events. Create a replay pipeline with separately recorded stages: mapping normalization, duplicate removal, prefix reconstruction, feature extraction, and scoring.
   - Use the **same unchanged model** for the original-versus-replayed comparison. Otherwise a new model could hide an input repair.
   - Run a factorial comparison: original mapping versus normalized mapping, original duplicates versus cleaned duplicates, and original cached features versus fully recomputed features. Stratify April by source and before/after 20 April.
   - Verify that the replay prefix ends at the normalized diagnosis event and contains no later events.
   - **If pipeline failure is primary:** April’s affected cases will materially improve after replay, while matched clean cases will not systematically deteriorate. Unknown activity, missing queue, stale event counts, and prefix-anchor errors will decline.
   - **If pipeline repair is insufficient:** corrected April cases and already-clean cases will remain similarly poor, implicating genuine covariate, process, target, or selection changes.
   - A null replay effect with stable clean controls is the principal falsifier of “April was primarily a pipeline incident.”

3. **Stratified discrimination and calibration**
   - Report AUROC and PR-AUC, Brier, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves, and operational utility by:
     - month and month-within-route;
     - V1/V2/V3;
     - Partner Z versus other sources;
     - route × source, provided sample sizes support it.
   - Compare against prevalence-aware benchmarks and fixed route-mix reweighting. ECE alone is insufficient; assess binning sensitivity and uncertainty.
   - **If June is primarily mix/calibration shift:** within-route discrimination and conditional calibration will remain similar, while route reweighting or modest route-specific recalibration will improve aggregate performance.
   - **If genuine V3 concept deterioration exists:** errors, calibration intercepts, or within-V3 discrimination will worsen on comparable matured cases, even after variant-standardized evaluation.
   - **If selective labels explain the pattern:** stratified results will change materially across successive matured snapshots and differ by label-availability probability.
   - **If a pipeline issue remains:** poor performance will concentrate in invalid/missing/stale feature groups rather than uniformly across valid cases.
   - Recalibration cannot repair a genuine loss of within-stratum ranking; conversely, a worsening aggregate AUROC can disappear after controlling a changed case mix.

4. **Variant-specific DFG and performance-DFG comparison**
   - On corrected logs, compare trace frequencies and the full edge-duration distributions for each variant. Include median, p90, censoring status, and promised resolution duration.
   - Reproduce the `Order Part -> Part Received` result and verify that the remote-fix edge remains stable. Check whether an edge increase remains after deduplication and source normalization.
   - Standardize model predictions and matured outcomes to a common route mix. Separately standardize within V3 to common pre-diagnosis covariates.
   - **If process drift drives the aggregate change:** higher V3 prevalence and longer part waits explain much of the aggregate shift, while within-route performance remains stable until the target relationship changes.
   - **If the delay is a measurement artifact:** the apparent increase will shrink or disappear after lineage-aware normalization and deduplication.
   - **If the process bottleneck is real but not a new conditional predictive relationship:** waits can increase without a corresponding within-covariate shift in breach risk. That would require operational remediation, not necessarily model retraining.

5. **Has the outcome relationship changed on the same path and comparable covariates?**
   - On matured data, fit a hierarchical logistic model using only pre-diagnosis variables:
     \[
     \operatorname{logit}P(Y=1)=f(X)+\beta_1V3+\beta_2V3\times\text{post-supplier}
       +\gamma Z+\text{month effects}.
     \]
   - Evaluate on later-time cases, preferably splitting by case and time rather than random events. Use diagnoses before versus after 10 July and 1 September where sample sizes permit.
   - Examine calibration intercepts, residual risk, within-V3 ranking, and partial dependence at identical or reweighted covariate values.
   - **If concept drift is genuine:** V3 risk changes at comparable recorded covariates, or previously useful predictors have different effects. A V3 risk intercept shift with stable conditional ranking may require recalibration more than full retraining.
   - **If apparent drift is unobserved composition:** the change shrinks after controlling for queue, promised duration, device age, distance, or other pre-existing variables. In that case, \(P(Y\mid X_{\text{all}})\) may be stable while \(P(Y\mid X_{\text{old}})\) is not.
   - **If the change is label selection:** it attenuates after maturity/observation weighting.
   - **If corrected within-V3 residuals and ranking remain stable:** this falsifies a material V3 concept-drift claim.
   - Do not condition casually on future part-receipt time when estimating the diagnosis-time predictor. It is useful for root-cause and operational-nowcast analysis, but may be a mediator or unavailable future information.

6. **Partner Z population reweighting and transport checks**
   - Compare Partner Z with the historical population on route, queue, promised duration, rural distance, device age, and other variables available at diagnosis.
   - Estimate a diagnosis-time propensity score for Partner-Z status, reweight the overlapping historical cohort to Z’s distribution, and compare transport performance on mature data.
   - Report effective sample size, support overlap, truncation, and variance. If important strata do not overlap, explicitly conclude that transport is not identifiable and collect targeted observations instead.
   - **If population exposure shift is the main explanation:** reweighted historical performance will resemble performance in Z’s overlapping strata, and the remaining within-Z excess error will be small.
   - **If supplier-driven concept drift is also present:** old reweighted performance will still deteriorate within V3, particularly after maturity adjustment.
   - **If omitted rural/device-age context matters:** adding genuinely available context will reduce the conditional gap.
   - **If most Z cases lack adequate historical support:** no reweighting or fairness conclusion is justified from these data alone.

7. **Feature missingness, score coverage, and lineage audits**
   - Produce, by month/source/variant, counts and percentages for raw activity names, normalized diagnosis names, generic unknowns, duplicate event IDs, missing queue, anchor-to-diagnosis delays, unmapped traces, absent scores, stale cached features, and label-join coverage.
   - Trace every prediction to raw event IDs, mapping version, dedup version, prefix cutoff, feature version, model version, score timestamp, and outcome-definition version.
   - Compare training and serving feature distributions using standard methods such as PSI, KS, or Wasserstein distance. Examine whether missingness is associated with route, source, rurality, and outcome.
   - Test replay determinism: corrected features should reproduce from raw events with the same versioned transformations.
   - **If a pipeline failure is responsible:** errors, missingness, and stale features will align with the affected source/version, and replay will restore consistency.
   - **If missing queue is an informative covariate rather than a pipeline artifact:** missingness will differ systematically by route/source and predict breach even after other variables are controlled. The remedy is a defensible missingness strategy, not simply filling all values.
   - **If dropped cases drive the apparent improvement:** score coverage will recover but coverage-conditioned error will not. Silent exclusions must not be mistaken for a successful model.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Mature labels and valid features are separate gates.** A month can have 100% mature outcomes and still be unsuitable for training because its prediction features are wrong.

| Stage | Decision and conditions |
|---|---|
| **Immediate containment** | Freeze automatic retraining and recalibration on questionable cohorts. Stop trusting April-derived feature artifacts and any unvalidated replay. Preserve both original and corrected outputs. For currently unscorable cases, use a verified operational fallback such as manual review of V3/parts exposure; do not fabricate risk scores or silently drop cases. |
| **Short-term recalibration** | After the input pipeline is corrected, test global and route-specific recalibration on mature, trusted data, with later months held out. June is the appropriate first test of adaptation to the remote-route mix. V3 recalibration can be tested only on sufficiently mature and representative V3 outcomes. Prefer regularized intercept/slope or smoothed route-specific adjustments before flexible methods. |
| **Feature repair and enrichment** | Normalize both sources, remove duplicates by event identity, recompute all affected prefixes, and add only information genuinely available at diagnosis: route, promised duration, verified queue state, supplier context, rural travel burden, and device age. Preserve known-at-time lineage. This may fix the model without a wholesale algorithmic change if the original conditional ranking remains adequate. |
| **Full retraining** | Consider a new model when corrected, matured data show persistent conditional errors or ranking loss that calibration cannot address, or when validated new features add material predictive and operational value. Use an out-of-time champion/challenger test, route-standardized metrics, source-stratified calibration, and operational utility—not an aggregate AUROC alone. Do not retrain solely to explain October’s available-label metrics. |

### Which months are usable for training?

| Month | Training status until resolved |
|---|---|
| **January–March** | **Mature and generally pipeline-eligible historical training data**, subject to ordinary lineage verification. They are valid but may not represent current V3 or Partner-Z conditions. |
| **April** | **Unsafe as-is**, despite 100% label maturity. Recompute corrected features and replay the full month before admitting any case. |
| **May** | **Conditionally usable after lineage verification.** Correctly scored May cases may be valid; stale or reused April-derived cases are not. The whole month need not be discarded without evidence. |
| **June** | **Mature and pipeline-clean; eligible.** It is particularly useful for testing recalibration under the new route mix. |
| **July** | **Conditionally eligible.** The 98% matured subset can be used if scoring coverage is verified and the remaining 2% is explicitly excluded or addressed with justified observation weights. |
| **August** | **Unsafe for ordinary unweighted final training as-is.** The 93% matured subset is useful diagnostically, but delayed V3 outcomes can bias the training target. Reassess when the cohort matures. |
| **September** | **Unsafe as-is.** Only 82% are available, with strong selective delay in V3 and Partner Z. Do not turn unavailable labels into negatives. |
| **October** | **Excluded from training and recalibration at the stated 55% maturity.** Even positive-only use would not, by itself, provide a representative binary training target. |

A segment-specific model is not automatically justified for every variant:

- V1 and V2 have stable breach behavior and should initially share a backbone with route and context features.
- A V3-specific calibration or specialist model becomes plausible if the post-July change persists on mature, comparable cases.
- A uniform V3 risk increase with stable conditional ranking may justify **V3-specific recalibration**, not full retraining.
- A new V3 model is better justified if new supplier/queue features improve within-V3 ranking or materially improve operational decisions.
- Do not create a Partner-Z-only model merely because its current breach rate is higher. First add relevant pre-existing context and test whether the remaining gap is conditional, out of support, or associated with particular decision errors.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

### Architecture and auditability

Maintain an append-only, versioned evidence store connecting:

1. raw events and mapping lineage;
2. normalized, deduplicated events;
3. diagnosis-anchored prediction prefixes;
4. feature snapshots and model versions;
5. frozen scores and decision thresholds;
6. outcome records with availability timestamps and definition versions;
7. process variants, DFG versions, source/route context, and interventions.

Both prediction-time and as-of reporting snapshots should be reproducible. Historical evaluation must not silently replace an old score with a newly recomputed score. Include a monthly reconciliation of started, diagnosed, scored, matured, and pending cases.

| Monitor | Principal signals | Action and dependencies |
|---|---|---|
| **Predictive** | Risk distributions, AUROC/PR-AUC, lift at operational capacity, coverage, score drift, missing-score share | A score-distribution alert alone may reflect routing or population change. Performance evaluation requires trusted features, fixed predictions, and adequate mature outcomes. |
| **Calibration** | Reliability curves, calibration intercept/slope, Brier/log loss, observed-minus-expected risk, within-route calibration | Suppress or quarantine alerts when data-quality or maturity gates fail. Reweight to a common route mix before declaring deterioration. |
| **Label maturity** | Maturity curves, pending fraction, label-delay quantiles, API backlog age, time to label by variant/source | Alert to delayed evaluation and training eligibility. It must **block** unjustified retraining, not initiate it. Case closure in the event log is not equivalent to outcome-API availability. |
| **Data quality** | Rename counts, unknown activities, duplicate IDs, missing queue, stale cache, prefix correctness, schema version, feature lineage, timestamp errors | Trigger replay, lineage repair, and production scoring containment where necessary. A corrected pipeline must be reevaluated before recalibration. |
| **Process-variant** | V1/V2/V3 shares, unmapped paths, variant-specific score/label distributions, new trace templates | A route-share change opens a stratified investigation. It does not by itself establish concept drift or justify a new model. |
| **Performance-DFG** | Edge median/p90, queue length, promised-duration-normalized waits, open-path censoring, V3 supply indicators | Trigger operational bottleneck alerts and process remediation. Diagnose future wait, parts availability, and escalation actions. Do not use unobserved future waiting time in the original diagnosis-time score. |
| **Population** | Source share, route-by-source mix, rural distance, device age, volume, support overlap, transport performance | Trigger common-support and reweighting analyses, targeted data collection, and feature proposals. Do not infer source-specific unfairness from an immature raw breach-rate gap. |
| **Intervention/deployment** | Mapping patches, backfill status, feature/model versions, traffic allocation, shadow performance, rollback criteria, data gates | Maintain champion/challenger and an auditable rollback path. Separate “model released” from “data repaired” and “labels matured.” |

### Alert dependencies

A practical sequence is:

1. **Schema/data-quality alarm:** invalidate the affected evaluation and training gate; repair and replay.
2. **Label-maturity alarm:** mark the cohort “not yet evaluable”; do not calibrate or retrain to pending outcomes.
3. On a clean, sufficiently matured snapshot, run **within-route/source discrimination and calibration**.
4. Compare process-edge deterioration, population reweighting, and same-covariate target tests.
5. Open a retraining candidate only when genuine residual deterioration or validated new information remains and the process/data explanations do not already suffice.

Operational process alarms should remain independent of the model-retraining trigger. A label-delay alert may appropriately stop an update, but must not cause a new model to be fitted.

### Local PM4Py evidence extraction

The sketch below assumes `case_id`, `activity`, and `timestamp`; a unique `event_id` is preferred. It preserves the original activity for lineage, normalizes the renamed diagnosis, and treats exact repeated event identities as duplicates. Legitimate repeated activities with different event identities are not collapsed.

- **Case-start cohort:** cases whose first available event is in the month; useful for lead-time and process composition.
- **Event-calendar cohort:** cases with any event in the month; useful for monthly workload, but includes partial histories and right-censored paths.
- **Prediction features:** extracted only from the prefix ending at normalized diagnosis—not from the full case and not using future part-receipt times.

```python
import json
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TIME = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp"
DIAG, PART, RECEIVED = (
    "initial diagnosis completed", "order part", "part received"
)
REMOTE_FIX, CLOSED = "remote fix", "case closed"

def edge_dict(network):
    return network.edges if hasattr(network, "edges") else network

def path_mix(log):
    # One path per case preserves multiplicities of identical variants.
    paths = (
        log.groupby(CASE)["concept:name"]
           .agg(" -> ".join)
           .rename("variant")
           .reset_index()
    )
    return paths.groupby("variant").size().sort_values(ascending=False).to_dict()

def edge_median_hours(log):
    net = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    wanted = {(PART, RECEIVED), (DIAG, REMOTE_FIX)}
    return {
        f"{a} -> {b}": float(stats["median"]) / 3.6e12
        for (a, b), stats in edge_dict(net).items()
        if (a, b) in wanted
    }

def closed_share(log):
    if log.empty:
        return float("nan")
    closed = log.groupby(CASE)[ACT].apply(lambda s: s.eq(CLOSED).any())
    return float(closed.mean())

def unknown_share(log):
    if log.empty:
        return float("nan")
    return float(log[ACT].str.lower().eq("unknown_activity").mean())

def monthly_process_evidence(csv_path, as_of=None):
    raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    d = raw.copy()
    d["_activity_original"] = d[ACT].astype("string")
    d["_source_row"] = range(len(d))
    d[TIME] = pd.to_datetime(d[TIME], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    d[ACT] = d[ACT].astype("string").str.strip().str.lower()
    d[ACT] = d[ACT].fillna("MISSING_ACTIVITY")
    d[ACT] = d[ACT].replace({
        "diagnostic assessment completed": DIAG
    })  # Do not map UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY to diagnosis.

    d = d.dropna(subset=[CASE, TIME]).sort_values(
        [CASE, TIME, ACT, "_source_row"], kind="stable"
    )
    if "event_id" in d.columns and d["event_id"].notna().all():
        duplicate_key = ["event_id"]
    else:
        # Conservative exact-key strategy; audit near-duplicates separately.
        duplicate_key = [CASE, ACT, TIME]

    d["_duplicate_removed"] = d.duplicated(duplicate_key, keep="first")
    d = d.loc[~d["_duplicate_removed"]].copy()
    if d.empty:
        raise ValueError("No usable events after basic validation")

    if as_of is None:
        as_of = d[TIME].max().floor("D") + pd.Timedelta(days=1)
    else:
        as_of = pd.Timestamp(as_of, tz="UTC")

    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        d, case_id=CASE, activity=ACT, timestamp=TIME
    )
    # Trim future events but retain available earlier case history.
    history = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted, end=as_of, mode="trim"
    )
    first_event = history.groupby(CASE)[TIME].min()
    first_month = first_event.dt.strftime("%Y-%m")
    diagnosis = (
        history.loc[history[ACT].eq(DIAG)]
        .groupby(CASE, as_index=False)[TIME].min()
    )

    rows, rebuilt_features = [], []
    for month in sorted(first_month.unique()):
        month0 = pd.Period(month, freq="M").start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        month1 = month0 + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        month_end = min(month1, as_of)

        # Calendar-month workload: deliberately includes partial histories.
        event_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            formatted, start=month0, end=month_end, mode="trim"
        )
        # Case-start cohort: retain each selected case's history through as_of.
        start_ids = first_event.index[first_month.eq(month)]
        start_log = history.loc[history[CASE].isin(start_ids)]
        anchors = diagnosis.loc[
            diagnosis[TIME].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").eq(month)
        ]

        # Audit feature reconstruction at the actual diagnosis anchor.
        for case_id, anchor_time in anchors[[CASE, TIME]].itertuples(
            index=False, name=None
        ):
            prefix = history.loc[
                history[CASE].eq(case_id) & history[TIME].le(anchor_time)
            ]
            if prefix.empty:
                continue
            features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
                prefix, grouper=CASE, activity=None, mode="prefix"
            )
            features[CASE] = case_id
            features["anchor_ts"] = anchor_time
            features["case_start_ts"] = first_event.loc[case_id]
            features["diagnosis_month"] = month
            rebuilt_features.append(features)

        raw_month = d.loc[d[TIME].ge(month0) & d[TIME].lt(month_end)]
        clean_month = raw_month.loc[~raw_month["_duplicate_removed"]]
        rows.append({
            "month": month,
            "n_cases_started": int(start_log[CASE].nunique()),
            "n_cases_with_event": int(event_log[CASE].nunique()),
            "n_diagnosis_anchors": int(len(anchors)),
            "n_case_closed_start": round(closed_share(start_log), 4),
            "unknown_event_share_start": round(unknown_share(start_log), 4),
            "unknown_event_share_calendar": round(unknown_share(event_log), 4),
            "raw_events": int(len(raw_month)),
            "clean_events": int(len(clean_month)),
            "duplicate_event_share": round(
                1 - len(clean_month) / max(len(raw_month), 1), 4
            ),
            "renamed_diagnosis_rows": int(
                raw_month["_activity_original"].str.strip().str.lower()
                .eq("diagnostic assessment completed").sum()
            ),
            "n_case_start_dfg_edges": len(
                edge_dict(pm4py.discover_dfg(start_log))
            ),
            "n_calendar_dfg_edges": len(
                edge_dict(pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log))
            ),
            "n_discovered_variant_templates": len(
                pm4py.get_variants(start_log)
            ),
            "case_start_variant_mix": json.dumps(path_mix(start_log)),
            "part_wait_median_h_start": edge_median_hours(start_log).get(
                f"{PART} -> {RECEIVED}"
            ),
            "part_wait_median_h_calendar": edge_median_hours(event_log).get(
                f"{PART} -> {RECEIVED}"
            ),
            "remote_wait_median_h_start": edge_median_hours(start_log).get(
                f"{DIAG} -> {REMOTE_FIX}"
            ),
        })

    return {
        "monthly": pd.DataFrame(rows),
        "diagnosis_prefix_features": pd.concat(
            rebuilt_features, ignore_index=True
        ) if rebuilt_features else pd.DataFrame(),
        "raw_column_missingness": raw.isna().mean().to_dict(),
    }
```

This is a process-evidence extractor, not a model-fitting or PM4Py “drift detector.” It does not produce outcome maturity: a separate, as-of-dated outcome ledger is needed. The two cohorts should not be expected to have identical counts, and right-censored cases must not be treated as completed paths.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Why it is plausible here | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Retraining on immature labels** | October’s 16% observed breach rate and 55% maturity could encourage treating pending cases as negatives. Long V3 paths mature slowly and are disproportionately exposed to breach. | Block training on unavailable outcomes; create maturity strata and fixed-vintage snapshots; retrieve labels; use justified observation weights or bounds. Never equate “not yet labeled” with “did not breach.” |
| **Calibrating around a pipeline bug** | April’s mean-risk excess and high ECE would appear correctable with recalibration, but invalid prefixes and stale features are the immediate defect. This could preserve systematic errors or corrupt the calibrator. | Gate calibration on lineage and score coverage; replay the unchanged model after normalization/dedup; verify on clean controls; only then estimate a new calibration mapping. |
| **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven deterioration** | Waiting for perfect data could delay attention to a real V3 bottleneck. The large V3 breach increase is not plausibly explained by April’s mapping bug or a 2% maturity gap. | Escalate the process bottleneck immediately. In parallel, use matured V3 outcomes to test conditional drift and determine whether route-specific recalibration, new supplier/queue context, or a specialist model is justified. |
| **Attributing Partner Z’s gap to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence** | The raw 45% versus 27% breach comparison invites a source-level conclusion, but Z has more V3, rural travel, older devices, and slower labels. | Examine mature route/source calibration, coverage, and decision utility; measure common support and transport performance; add relevant pre-existing context. A source-level penalty or replacement model requires evidence of conditional error, not a raw prevalence gap. |
| **Treating June’s route mix as a global model collapse** | Brier and ECE worsened while AUROC remained approximately stable. Aggregate decline is vulnerable to composition changes. | Standardize to a fixed route mix; validate within V2 and V1; recalibrate to the new prevalence and route exposure. Reserve full retraining for demonstrated residual error or new predictive value. |
| **Using post-diagnosis process information as a diagnosis-time feature** | The growing part wait is striking, but it is generally observed after the prediction anchor. A retrospective model can look excellent through leakage. | Freeze prefix cutoffs at normalized diagnosis; timestamp every feature’s availability; allow post-diagnosis waits only in clearly labeled operational/nowcast models. |
| **Calling pipeline repair successful while difficult cases remain unscored** | April’s 18% unmapped share may disappear from the reported evaluation denominator. Better metrics among scored cases could coexist with poor population coverage. | Report end-to-end score coverage and case-flow attrition; audit excluded/unmapped cases separately; use an operational fallback until they can be scored reliably. |
