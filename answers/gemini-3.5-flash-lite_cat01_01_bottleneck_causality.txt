## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional Verdict:** Mainly confounded (with minor negative mediation through shift/compliance load).  
**Confidence Level:** 75%  

**Strongest Evidence For:** Stratified analysis completely reverses the crude aggregate picture. In the low and medium complexity strata, Nova is actually *slower* than Orion (`Low: Nova 9.64h vs Orion 7.50h`; `Medium: Nova 19.38h vs Orion 17.17h`). Even in the high complexity stratum, Nova is slightly slower (`Nova 35.00h vs Orion 31.57h`). The crude aggregate delay for Orion (mean 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h) is entirely a artifact of case-mix confounding: the routing engine disproportionately allocates complex cases, night shifts, and compliance-heavy workflows to Orion (`42.9% high complexity` vs `12.5%` for Nova).  
**Strongest Evidence Against:** In the high complexity stratum, Orion handles 6 cases to Nova's 2, leaving cells with sparse support. Furthermore, Orion's night-shift dominance and compliance exposure may introduce operational friction not fully captured by static complexity brackets.  
**Why the Proposed Reassignment Will Fail/Backfire:** Management's plan to blindly reassign 50% of Orion cases to Nova based on crude aggregates will fail because Nova is not inherently faster. Reassigning complex, compliance-heavy, night-shift cases to Nova—a group lacking Orion's specialized staffing and certified night-shift coverage—will inflate queue wait times (`wait_before_review_h`), strain Nova's capacity, and likely increase rework and quality appeal rates without yielding cycle-time gains.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Case-Mix Confounding** (Beneficial effect masked) | Routing engine assigns harder cases to Orion | Orion has 42.9% high-complexity vs Nova's 12.5%; stratified Orion is faster | Crude aggregate favors Nova | Complexity-standardized weighting (e.g., direct standardization using combined population weights) | Standardized Orion mean cycle time will be *lower* than Nova's. |
| 2 | **Night Shift Congestion** (Environmental) | Night shift has higher arrival load and slower queue clearance | Orion is 92.9% night shift; night cases have higher wait times | Low-complexity night cases (C01, C20) still finish fast | Compare Nova-Night vs Orion-Night within Low complexity | Nova-Night and Orion-Night will show negligible service-time differences. |
| 3 | **Compliance Bottleneck** (Mediated delay) | Compliance check requirement extends pre-review wait | 64.3% compliance in Orion vs 25% in Nova | Compliance is policy-mandated by complexity | Regress `wait_before_review_h` on group controlling for `compliance_required` | Group coefficient for Orion becomes zero or negative after controlling for compliance. |
| 4 | **True Orion Harm** (Process friction) | Orion reviewers over-scrutinize, causing extra delays | Orion overall mean is higher (21.96h vs 15.24h) | Stratified comparisons show Orion is faster in every stratum | Interaction model: `Cycle ~ Group * Complexity` | Interaction term for Orion * Complexity will be positive and significant. |

**Final Recommendation:**
Management must halt the uncalculated 50% reassignment proposal immediately. The apparent performance advantage of Nova is an illusion generated by case-mix confounding: Orion absorbs disproportionate volumes of high-complexity, compliance-heavy, night-shift work. When examined within complexity strata, Orion matches or outperforms Nova. Reassigning complex cases to Nova without expanding their specialized training or night shift capacity will degrade service levels, increase rework, and damage quality outcomes. Instead, management should execute Phase 2 (a controlled quasi-random routing pilot for Medium-complexity cases) and focus operational investments on queue management and compliance bottleneck reduction rather than shuffling resource group labels.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved Case Severity within Strata:** Cases classified as "High" complexity may still exhibit wide internal variance in legal or financial exposure. *Data/Design:* Introduce continuous monetary exposure and document-page-count covariates.
2. **Reviewer Skill & Experience Heterogeneity:** Orion has "more certified reviewers," but individual reviewer efficiency is unmeasured. *Data/Design:* Track reviewer IDs and tenure to model fixed effects.
3. **Truncation by Dropout/Abandonment:** The dataset contains 100% completed cases; withdrawn or abandoned claims are missing. *Data/Design:* Log initial inquiry timestamps and track dropout rates per resource group.
4. **Queue Priority Rules:** Exact queue dispatching logic (FIFO vs. priority-based) is unspecified. *Data/Design:* Reconstruct timestamped queue logs capturing arrival and service start times.
5. **Post-Process Quality Feedback Delay:** The 30-day appeal outcome is coarse and subject to external noise. *Data/Design:* Extend outcome tracking to 90 days and isolate rework root causes.
