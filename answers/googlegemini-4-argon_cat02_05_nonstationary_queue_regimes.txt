# 1. Data audit and estimands

### Definitions of Queueing Quantities and Conventions
* **Opening-Hours Convention ($T_{\text{open}}$):** The service center operates 12 active hours per day (07:00 to 19:00), or $720\text{ minutes/day}$. No arrivals or service completions occur during the 12 overnight closed hours, though unfinished cases remain in the system (overnight carry-over).
* **Arrival Intensity ($\lambda$):** The rate of incoming cases, measured either per operating day ($\text{cases/day}$) or per active operating hour ($\lambda_h = \text{arrivals} / 12\text{ cases/active hour}$).
* **Completion Intensity / Throughput ($\mu_{\text{eff}}$ or $X$):** The observed departure rate of completed cases during opening hours ($\text{completed/day}$ or $\text{completed}/12\text{ cases/active hour}$). This represents *effective* system output, which is bounded by both available capacity ($c \cdot \mu$) and available work (arrivals + initial backlog).
* **Service Time ($S$):** Active handling time per case (excluding waiting time), reported via daily order statistics: the 50th percentile ($S_{50} = \text{median\_service\_min}$) and 90th percentile ($S_{90} = \text{p90\_service\_min}$). **Crucial distinction:** The mean service time $E[S]$ is *not* provided, and because service times are right-skewed ($S_{90} \gg S_{50}$), $E[S] > S_{50}$.
* **Work-in-Process ($L$ or $\text{WIP}$):** Reported as `mean_wip`, the time-average number of cases in the system (waiting + in service) across 15-minute snapshots (48 snapshots per 12-hour day). 
* **Capacity ($c$):** Reported as `staff_fte`, the mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent agents during the 12-hour window. Total gross capacity per day is $\text{staff\_fte} \times 720\text{ staff-minutes}$. Net effective capacity on 2026-04-15 is reduced by `outage_min` ($180\text{ min}$, or $25\%$ of the 12-hour day).
* **Case Flow Time / Sojourn Time ($W$):** Total active time a case spends in the system ($W = W_q + S$, waiting time plus active service time), measured in active operating hours or minutes.

### Bounds and Estimators for Unobserved Mean Service Time ($E[S]$)
Because utilization $\rho = \frac{\lambda \cdot E[S]}{c}$ requires $E[S]$ rather than $S_{50}$, we must bound or estimate $E[S]$ under explicit distributional assumptions:
* **Assumption A1 (Lognormal Parametric Approximation):** If active handling times follow a lognormal distribution $\ln(S) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_L, \sigma_L^2)$, then $\mu_L = \ln(S_{50})$, $\sigma_L = \frac{\ln(S_{90} / S_{50})}{z_{0.90}}$ (where $z_{0.90} \approx 1.2816$), and $E[S]_{\text{LN}} = S_{50} \cdot \exp(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_L^2)$.
* **Assumption A2 (Two-Point Conservative Lower Bound):** Assuming service time is positive and at least $10\%$ of cases exceed $S_{90}$, a strict distribution-free lower bound is $E[S] > 0.50(0) + 0.40(S_{50}) + 0.10(S_{90})$, or more realistically under unimodal right-skew, $E[S] \in [S_{50}, \text{Complete-Work Capacity Bound}]$.

### Seven Threats to Naive Inference
1. **Unobserved Overnight Backlog ($B_0$) and Carry-Over Violation:** Daily rows are not independent renewal cycles. When `arrivals > completed` (e.g., 2026-04-15 with $+47$ net accumulation, or 2026-04-20 to 2026-04-28 with $+106$ cumulative net accumulation), the unrecorded 07:00 backlog grows sequentially. Completions on day $t$ include work that arrived on day $t-1$ or earlier.
2. **Median Service Time vs. Mean Service Time ($S_{50} \neq E[S]$) and Flow Time ($S \neq W$):** Substituting `median_service_min` for $E[S]$ understates true workload and utilization by $10\%\text{--}20\%$ due to heavy right tails ($S_{90}/S_{50}$ rises from $\sim 1.83$ in Week 1 to $\sim 2.40$ in Week 3). Worse, equating service time $S$ with total flow time $W$ ignores queueing delay $W_q$.
3. **Censored/Confounded Service Times by Completion Selection:** Assuming `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are measured on *completed* cases (or active segments), an outage day (Apr 15) or heavy congestion period may skew observed service times depending on whether interrupted cases accrue outage clock-time or whether simpler/harder cases finish first.
4. **Intra-Day Non-Stationarity and Snapshot Bias:** `peak_08_10_pct` (spanning 08:00–09:59) claims to represent a 2-hour window, yet in the hourly vector table (`2026-04-07`: bins 08–09 and 09–10 sum to $5+12=17$, whereas bins 09–10 and 10–11 sum to $12+20=32 \approx 31.7\%$). This indicates a potential 1-hour label shift or definitional discrepancy between the summary metric `peak_08_10_pct` and the hourly vector bins. Furthermore, massive intra-day bursts create transient queues that violate steady-state assumptions within a 12-hour shift.
5. **Confounding of Case-Mix Shift vs. Coding Artifact:** On 2026-04-20, `complex_pct` jumps abruptly from $23\%$ to $41\%$, accompanied by a jump in `peak_08_10_pct` ($18\% \to 42\%$) and `p90_service_min` ($56 \to 78\text{ min}$). Because a real arrival-process change (morning concentration) and service-time elongation occur simultaneously, the shift cannot be dismissed as a pure coding artifact, though coding rules may amplify the reported percentage.
6. **Outage Recovery vs. Normal High-Throughput Conflation:** On 2026-04-16 (`completed = 129`, `staff_fte = 7.0`), high throughput is driven by draining the 47-case outage backlog from Apr 15 with overtime/surge staffing. Treating Apr 16 as a sustainable baseline overestimates normal capacity.
7. **Aggregation over Heterogeneous Regimes (Simpson's Paradox):** Pooling weekdays (`staff_fte` $\approx 5.5\text{--}7.0$, `arrivals` $\approx 92\text{--}121$) with weekends (`staff_fte` $\approx 2.5\text{--}3.0$, `arrivals` $\approx 36\text{--}53$), or pooling pre- and post-Apr 20 regimes, creates artificial correlations and distorts average utilization and WIP metrics.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

### Alternative Taxonomy: 3-Epoch Macro-Structural + Backlog-State Segmentation
Instead of separating weekends and multi-stage recovery into 7 fine-grained regimes, a serious alternative segments the timeline into **Three Macro Chronological Epochs** defined by structural changepoints, or equivalently by **System Backlog State (Net Accumulation vs. Net Drain)**:

1. **Epoch A — Pre-Shift Low-Complexity Baseline (Apr 06 – Apr 14, $n=9\text{ days}$, pooling 7 weekdays + 2 weekend days):**
   * *Operational Story:* The service center operates in a stationary, stable equilibrium ($\sum A = 781, \sum C = 776$, net backlog change $+5$ over 9 days). Case complexity is low ($18\text{--}24\%$), and staffing scales proportionally to demand between weekdays ($5.5\text{--}6.0\text{ FTE}$) and weekends ($2.5\text{ FTE}$).
2. **Epoch B — Transitional Disruption & Stabilized Post-Outage Window (Apr 15 – Apr 19, $n=5\text{ days}$):**
   * *Operational Story:* Defined as a single 5-day disturbance cycle triggered by the Apr 15 outage ($180\text{ min}$), followed by ad-hoc management firefighting on Apr 16–17 ($7.0\text{ and }6.5\text{ FTE}$) and weekend settling on Apr 18–19. Over the full 5-day cycle, $\sum A = 403$ and $\sum C = 386$ (net residual backlog increase of $+17\text{ cases}$ entering Apr 20).
3. **Epoch C — High-Complexity / High-Burstiness Regime (Apr 20 – May 03, $n=14\text{ days}$), subdivided into:**
   * **Epoch C1 (Unmitigated Supercritical Accumulation, Apr 20 – Apr 28, $n=9\text{ days}$):** Every single day (including Saturday Apr 25 and Sunday Apr 26!) exhibits `arrivals > completed`. Cumulative arrivals $= 892$, cumulative completions $= 786$, creating a massive $+106\text{ case}$ backlog surge.
   * **Epoch C2 (Capacity-Mitigated Stabilization, Apr 29 – May 03, $n=5\text{ days}$):** Management permanently increases baseline staffing by $+1.0\text{ FTE}$ on weekdays ($7.0/6.5$) and $+0.5\text{ FTE}$ on weekends ($3.0$), reversing the backlog trajectory ($\sum A = 443, \sum C = 461$, net drain of $-18\text{ cases}$).

### Comparison of Strengths and Weaknesses
* **Strengths of the Primary (7-Regime) Segmentation:** Preserves queueing homogeneity within regimes. Because arrival rates ($\sim 105\text{ vs. }45\text{ /day}$) and server counts ($\sim 6.0\text{ vs. }2.5\text{ FTE}$) differ by a factor of $2.4\times$ between weekdays and weekends, separating R1/R2 and R4/R5/R6 prevents bimodal distributions of $\lambda$ and $L$ within regimes and enables valid weekday capacity sizing.
* **Strengths of the Alternative (Macro-Epoch / Backlog-State) Segmentation:** Captures **cross-day serial dependence and overnight backlog carry-over** seamlessly. In Epoch C1 (Apr 20–28), separating weekends (Apr 25–26) from weekdays (R4) obscures the fact that the weekend is suffering from the exact same structural under-capacity ($\rho > 1$, `arrivals > completed` on both Sat and Sun) and carries the weekday backlog forward from Friday Apr 24 (`mean_wip = 43`) into Monday Apr 27 (`mean_wip = 49`).
* **Weaknesses:** The Alternative Segmentation's Epoch A, B, and C mix weekdays and weekends, making intra-epoch mean arrival rates ($\lambda$) and `mean_wip` meaningless as direct inputs to standard $M/M/c$ or $G/G/c$ formulas without day-of-week conditioning. Conversely, the Primary Segmentation has very small sample sizes in R3a ($n=1$), R3b ($n=2$), and R5 ($n=3$), limiting statistical power.

### Four Ways Pooled Summaries Obscure Operational Reality
1. **Illusion of Subcritical Stability ($\rho_{\text{pooled}} \approx 91.4\%$ vs. Supercritical Overload $\rho_{\text{R4}} \approx 118.0\%$):**
   Pooled across all 28 days, total arrivals ($2,520$) and completions ($2,444$) differ by only $3.0\%$ ($+2.71\text{ cases/day}$), and estimated pooled utilization is $\sim 91.4\%$. A naive modeler would conclude the system is slightly heavily loaded but stable ($\rho < 1$). In reality, the system was comfortably under-utilized in R1 ($\rho \approx 66.2\%$, WIP $\approx 20.3$) and catastrophically overloaded in R4 ($\rho \approx 118.0\%$, where arrival workload required $\sim 7.0\text{ FTE}$ just to break even against $5.93\text{ available FTE}$, driving WIP to $50$).
2. **Completion Rate Censoring in R4 vs. R1 ($X_{\text{R1}} = 99.6\text{ /d}$ vs. $X_{\text{R4}} = 100.6\text{ /d}$):**
   Comparing R1 and R4 completions naively suggests throughput was unchanged ($\sim 100\text{ cases/day}$) despite arrivals jumping from $100.1$ to $114.0\text{ /day}$. Why didn't completions drop when service times increased by $+40\%$ ($S_{50}: 25 \to 35\text{ min}$)? Because in R1, servers had $\sim 34\%$ idle/slack capacity ($X$ was **demand-constrained** by $\lambda$). In R4, servers hit $100\%$ saturation ($X$ became **capacity-constrained** at $\sim 100.6\text{ cases/day}$, while excess arrivals piled into backlog). Pooling demand-constrained and capacity-constrained regimes invalidates throughput regression.
3. **Simpson's-Paradox-Like Reversal in Staffing vs. WIP and Service Time:**
   * *Pooled view:* Across all 20 weekdays, correlating `staff_fte` with `mean_wip` yields a **positive correlation** ($r = +0.28$), and `staff_fte` with `completed` yields $r = +0.74$. Even among high-staffed days (`staff_fte` $\ge 6.5$, i.e., Apr 16, 17, 29, 30, May 01), mean WIP is **39.6**, whereas on normal $6.0\text{ FTE}$ weekdays in R1, mean WIP is **20.3**. A naive observer looking at pooled cross-sectional averages might conclude that *adding staff increases WIP* or fails to lower congestion.
   * *Segmented reality:* Staffing was endogenously increased *in response to* severe backlog shocks (Apr 16–17 after the outage; Apr 29–May 01 after the R4 backlog buildup). Within the high-complexity regime (comparing R4 at $5.93\text{ FTE}$ vs. R5 at $6.83\text{ FTE}$), adding $\sim 0.9\text{ FTE}$ reversed net backlog growth from $+13.4\text{ /day}$ to $-6.0\text{ /day}$ and drove WIP *down* monotonically from $50$ (Apr 28) $\to 43 \to 36 \to 32$ (May 01).
4. **Weekday/Weekend Bimodality and Case-Mix Reversal:**
   In R1 vs. R2 (Weeks 1–2), weekends had **longer** median service times than weekdays ($29.0\text{ min}$ vs. $25.0\text{ min}$) despite similar `complex_pct` ($22.8\%$ vs. $20.9\%$). In R4 vs. R6 (Weeks 3–4), weekday and weekend median service times converged ($35.0\text{ min}$ vs. $35.0\text{ min}$). Pooling weekdays and weekends yields an artificial IQR of $62.2\text{ arrivals/day}$ that reflects deterministic calendar scheduling rather than stochastic arrival variance.

### Daily Serial Dependence, Autocorrelation, and Change-Point Evidence
* **Lag-1 and Lag-7 Autocorrelation:**
  * Across the 28-day sequence, `mean_wip` exhibits strong **lag-1 autocorrelation** ($r_1 = \mathbf{0.752}$), confirming that congestion is a sticky state variable governed by overnight backlog carry-over ($B_t = B_{t-1} + A_t - C_t$).
  * `arrivals` ($r_7 = \mathbf{0.938}$) and `staff_fte` ($r_7 = \mathbf{0.923}$) are dominated by **lag-7 weekly seasonality** (weekday vs. weekend cycles), whereas process complexity (`complex_pct`: $r_1 = \mathbf{0.916}$) and tail service time (`p90_service_min`: $r_1 = \mathbf{0.854}$) exhibit non-stationary **step-change behavior**.
* **Change-Point Evidence:**
  * **Change Point 1 (2026-04-15, Point Anomaly + Transient Decay):** `outage_min = 180` causes an immediate drop in completions ($105 \to 58$) and a spike in `mean_wip` ($23 \to 47$, $z\text{-score} > +10$ relative to R1 baseline).

### Why Four Representative Days Cannot Establish a Full Stochastic Model
1. **Unobserved Inter-Arrival and Service Distributions:** Hourly counts cannot distinguish between Poisson arrivals with a time-varying rate $\lambda(t)$ and batch arrivals (e.g., compound Poisson where batches of size $K$ arrive simultaneously).
2. **Missing Case-Level Linkage (FIFO vs. Priority / Overtaking):** We cannot observe whether complex cases ($44\%$) have mean service time $60\text{ min}$ while simple cases ($56\%$) take $20\text{ min}$, nor whether complex cases are routed to dedicated specialists or block general queues.
3. **Selection Bias of "Representative" Days:** Four cherry-picked days ($n=4/28$) provide zero estimate of day-to-day variance within a regime (e.g., whether the afternoon bursts on Apr 21 at 13:00 and 16:00 recur every post-mix-shift weekday or were unique to Apr 21).

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$, or $W = L / \lambda$) holds exactly over a finite observational window $[0, T]$ **only if** the system is either in stochastic steady state or satisfies **boundary stability** (the system starts and ends empty, or initial backlog equals final backlog $B(0) = B(T)$ with negligible boundary edge effects). 

When defensible, we express active flow time in **active operating hours**:
$$W_{\text{hours}} = \frac{\text{mean\_wip}}{\lambda_{\text{hourly}}} = \frac{\text{mean\_wip}}{\text{Throughput}_{\text{daily}} / 12\text{ hours}}$$
*(Note: Because no service occurs overnight, a case with $W_{\text{hours}} > 12\text{ active hours}$ spans multiple calendar days, incurring an additional $+12\text{ hours}$ of closed overnight clock time per night carried over.)*

# 7. Operational implications

### 1. Regime-Specific Staffing and Capacity Sizing
* **Maintain and Slightly Expand the R5 Staffing Intervention:** In the post-Apr 20 high-complexity regime (`complex_pct` $\approx 44\%$, $E[S]_{\text{LN}} \approx 40.3\text{--}44.2\text{ min}$), an arrival rate of $115\text{ cases/day}$ ($9.58\text{ /h}$) requires:
  $$\text{Minimum Break-Even FTE } (c_{\min}) = \frac{115 \times E[S]}{720\text{ min}} \approx \begin{cases} 7.06\text{ FTE} & \text{at R4 } E[S]_{\text{LN}} = 44.2\text{ min} \\ 6.44\text{ FTE} & \text{at R5 } E[S]_{\text{LN}} = 40.3\text{ min} \end{cases}$$
  This explains why $6.0\text{ FTE}$ in R4 resulted in runaway backlog growth ($+13.4\text{ cases/day}$), whereas increasing staffing to $7.0\text{ FTE}$ on Apr 29–30 and $6.5\text{ FTE}$ on May 01 allowed the center to complete $121\text{ cases/day}$ and drain $-6.0\text{ cases/day}$ of backlog. **Recommendation:** Lock in **$7.0\text{--}7.5\text{ FTE}$ on weekdays** and **$3.0\text{--}3.5\text{ FTE}$ on weekends** while `complex_pct` remains above $40\%$.

### 2. Intraday Peak Smoothing and Shift Staggering
* **Staggered Shifts for Morning and Afternoon Bursts:** In R4/R5, `peak_08_10_pct` reaches $40\text{--}50\%$ of daily volume, and representative day `2026-04-21` shows extreme bursts in bin 4 ($34\text{ arrivals}$), bin 7 ($24\text{ arrivals}$), and bin 10 ($14\text{ arrivals}$). Uniform staffing of $7.0\text{ FTE}$ across all 12 hours leaves the center under-staffed from 09:00 to 14:00 and over-staffed from 07:00 to 08:30. **Recommendation:** Shift from flat 12-hour coverage to overlapping core shifts (e.g., $5\text{ FTE}$ at 07:00, surging to $9\text{ FTE}$ between 09:00 and 15:00, tapering to $6\text{ FTE}$ from 15:00 to 19:00), and investigate whether the hourly bursts (`34`, `24`, `14`) are triggered by upstream batch-release IT jobs that can be throttled into continuous streams.

### 3. Outage Recovery Protocol vs. Structural Overload
* **Target Backlog Drain Explicitly:** After the 3-hour outage on Apr 15 ($+47\text{ net backlog}$), management added $+1.0\text{ FTE}$ on Apr 16 and $+0.5\text{ FTE}$ on Apr 17, draining $30\text{ cases}$ but leaving $+17\text{ cases}$ of residual backlog (WIP $= 35$ on Apr 17 vs. $20$ on Apr 10) right before the Apr 20 case-mix shock hit. **Recommendation:** Define a formal WIP recovery target ($\text{WIP} \le 22$) before stepping down surge FTEs, or use Saturday overtime to clear residual weekday backlog before Monday morning.

### 4. Policy Trap: Beneficial Under Pooled Averages, Risky Under Segmented View
* **The Trap ("Flat 6.0 FTE Weekday / 5.2 FTE Pooled Staffing Policy"):**
  Suppose management evaluates capacity using the **pooled 28-day average**: mean arrivals $= 90.0\text{ /day}$, pooled median service time $S_{50} = 32.5\text{ min}$ (or even pooled mean $E[S]_{\text{LN}} \approx 37.2\text{ min}$), and pooled `staff_fte` $= 5.12$. Under pooled averages:
  * Using $S_{50}$: $\text{Required FTE} = \frac{90 \times 32.5}{720} = \mathbf{4.06\text{ FTE}}$ (or on weekdays: $\frac{108 \times 33.0}{720} = \mathbf{4.95\text{ FTE}}$).
  * Even using $E[S]_{\text{LN}}$: Pooled weekday workload is $\frac{108 \times 37.7}{720} = \mathbf{5.66\text{ FTE}}$.
  A cost-cutting policy that sets weekday staffing to a flat **$6.0\text{ FTE}$** (and eliminates the Apr 29 $+1.0\text{ FTE}$ intervention as "unnecessary excess capacity") looks completely safe under pooled averages ($\rho_{\text{pooled\_wkdy}} = 5.66 / 6.0 = 94.3\%$, or $82.5\%$ using median service time).
  * **Why it fails catastrophically under the segmented view:** That $5.66\text{ FTE}$ average blends R1 (which only needed $4.0\text{ FTE}$) with R4/R5 (which requires $6.5\text{--}7.1\text{ FTE}$). Reverting from $7.0\text{ FTE}$ (R5) back to $6.0\text{ FTE}$ immediately plunges the service center back into the **R4 supercritical regime ($\rho \approx 118\%$)**, where backlog grows by $+13\text{ cases/day}$ indefinitely.

### 5. Data-Collection and Process-Mining Instrumentation Recommendations
1. **Log Case-Level Event Timestamps:** Capture exact `case_id`, `arrival_timestamp`, `service_start_timestamp`, `service_end_timestamp`, `suspend/resume_timestamps` (for overnight carry-over and outages), and `variant/complexity_flag` to compute true $E[S]$, $W_q$, and $W$ directly rather than relying on daily medians and p90s.
2. **Record Daily 07:00 Initial Backlog ($B_0$) and 19:00 Ending Backlog ($B_{12}$):** Allows exact conservation-of-flow verification ($B_{t,12} = B_{t,0} + A_t - C_t$) and detects abandoned/canceled cases.
3. **Audit the Apr 20 `complex_pct` Jump and `peak_08_10_pct` Clock Alignment:** Verify whether the Apr 20 jump from $23\%$ to $41\%$ reflects a genuine upstream product/intake change or a CRM/routing rule change, and reconcile the 1-hour offset between `peak_08_10_pct` and the hourly vector bins.

    summarize_group, include_groups=False
)

summary_table = pd.concat(
    [pooled_all, pooled_wkdy, pooled_wknd, primary_summary]
)

# ==============================================================================
# 3. BURSTINESS / DISPERSION INDICATORS & SERIAL DEPENDENCE
# ==============================================================================
hourly_metrics = []
for _, row in df_hourly.iterrows():
    a = np.array(row["arrivals_by_hour"], dtype=float)
    c = np.array(row["completions_by_hour"], dtype=float)
    cum_net = np.cumsum(a - c)
    hourly_metrics.append(
        {
            "day": row["representative_day"],
            "sum_A": int(a.sum()),
            "sum_C": int(c.sum()),
            "mean_A_hr": a.mean(),
            "fano_A": a.var(ddof=1)
            / a.mean(),  # Dispersion indicator 1: Fano factor (s^2 / mean)
            "cv_A": a.std(ddof=1)
            / a.mean(),  # Dispersion indicator 2: Coefficient of Variation
            "peak_to_mean_A": a.max()
            / a.mean(),  # Dispersion indicator 3: Peak-to-Mean ratio
            "fano_C": c.var(ddof=1) / c.mean(),
            "cv_C": c.std(ddof=1) / c.mean(),
            "max_intraday_queue_build": int(cum_net.max()),
            "end_of_day_net": int(cum_net[-1]),
        }
    )
df_hourly_metrics = pd.DataFrame(hourly_metrics)

# Serial dependence indicators on the daily time series (Lag-1 and Lag-7 autocorrelations)
serial_cols = [
    "arrivals",
    "completed",
    "mean_wip",
    "complex_pct",
    "p90_service_min",
    "net_flow",
]
serial_dep = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        "lag1_autocorr": [df[col].autocorr(lag=1) for col in serial_cols],
        "lag7_autocorr": [df[col].autocorr(lag=7) for col in serial_cols],
    },
    index=serial_cols,
)

# ==============================================================================
# 4. TRANSPARENT CHANGE-POINT / SEGMENTATION HEURISTIC
# ==============================================================================
# Heuristic:
# (a) Scan 1-D binary segmentation (maximizing between-segment variance reduction /
#     Welch t-stat) on `complex_pct` to detect the structural mix shift.
# (b) Flag operational point shocks where `outage_min > 0` or `net_flow` z-score > 2.5.
# (c) Detect capacity intervention on weekdays where `staff_fte` steps up >= 6.5.
best_split_date = None
best_sse_reduction = -1.0
total_sse = ((df["complex_pct"] - df["complex_pct"].mean()) ** 2).sum()

for idx in range(4, len(df) - 4):
    left = df["complex_pct"].iloc[:idx]
    right = df["complex_pct"].iloc[idx:]
    sse_split = ((left - left.mean()) ** 2).sum() + (
        (right - right.mean()) ** 2
    ).sum()
    reduction = total_sse - sse_split
    if reduction > best_sse_reduction:
        best_sse_reduction = reduction
        best_split_date = df["date"].iloc[idx]

# ==============================================================================
# 5. LITTLE'S LAW IMPLIED FLOW-TIME CALCULATIONS FOR EXPLICIT WINDOWS
# ==============================================================================
# Little's Law: W (in active operating hours) = L / lambda_hourly = 12 * mean_wip / Throughput_daily
littles_windows = [
    (
        "R1: Baseline Weekday (7d)",
        df[df["primary_regime"] == "R1: Baseline Wkdy"],
        "applicable",
    ),
    (
        "R2: Baseline Weekend (4d)",
        df[df["primary_regime"] == "R2: Baseline Wknd"],
        "approximate only",
    ),
    (
        "R3a: Outage Day (1d)",
        df[df["primary_regime"] == "R3a: Outage Shock"],
        "not defensible",
    ),
    (
        "R4: Overload Weekday (7d)",
        df[df["primary_regime"] == "R4: Overload Wkdy"],
        "not defensible",
    ),
    (
        "R5: Intervened Weekday (3d)",
        df[df["primary_regime"] == "R5: Intervened Wkdy"],
        "approximate only",
    ),
    ("Pooled: All 28 Days (28d)", df, "approximate only"),
]

ll_rows = []
for label, sub, verdict in littles_windows:
    L = sub["mean_wip"].mean()
    A_bar = sub["arrivals"].mean()
    C_bar = sub["completed"].mean()
    net_imbalance_pct = 100.0 * (A_bar - C_bar) / A_bar
    # Implied active flow time in hours (12 active hours per day)
    W_via_A_hr = 12.0 * L / A_bar
    W_via_C_hr = 12.0 * L / C_bar
    ll_rows.append(
        {
            "Window / Regime": label,
            "mean_WIP (L)": round(L, 2),
            "Arr/d (A)": round(A_bar, 1),
            "Comp/d (C)": round(C_bar, 1),
            "Imbalance (A-C)/A %": round(net_imbalance_pct, 1),
            "W_via_A (active hr)": (
                round(W_via_A_hr, 2) if verdict != "not defensible" else np.nan
            ),
            "W_via_C (active hr)": (
                round(W_via_C_hr, 2) if verdict != "not defensible" else np.nan
            ),
            "Verdict": verdict,
        }
    )
df_littles = pd.DataFrame(ll_rows)
