# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA (FIFO + appointments).** Supporting: A02 at 08:30, A04 at 08:58, A05 at 09:05, A07 at 09:32 — each is the oldest waiting job, and 09:05/09:32 specifically reject priority (A08) and SPT (A07 at 09:05). Contradicting: A03 at 08:20 violates FIFO but is fully explained by its appointment (note says "arrived early", and service starts exactly at the appointed minute — a hard start-at-time rule). A06 starting 5 minutes late is consistent with a soft tolerance: A05 (started 09:05, est 15) was allowed to run into the 09:15 slot rather than being held back. No undocumented exception is required for QA.

**QB (FIFO + P1 preemption).** Supporting: B02's preemption at 09:16 (P1, within scan delay of arrival), B05's jump at 10:00, and FIFO-consistent picks at 09:31, 10:14, 10:28, 10:36 — the last two passing over priority-2 B06 in favor of older priority-3 B04, which cleanly rejects general priority scheduling. Contradicting: (a) the 09:48 stop of B01 for B03 — a P2 job triggering preemption contradicts both the FIFO base and "P1-only" preemption; the explicit "dispatcher reassignment" label marks it as a manual, undocumented exception rather than policy (had it been policy, B06 should likewise have preempted or jumped later, and it did not). (b) B07's appointment is ignored twice; either QB has no appointment mechanism and the field is imported from another system, or the tolerance is so loose it is inoperative.

**QC (SPT + reactive SLA escalation).** Supporting: C02, C03, C06 selections; also the global shape (short jobs early, the 36-minute C01 pushed toward the end) is SPT's signature. Contradicting: (a) C04 over C06 at 10:32 — explicable if C06's estimate had not yet been entered at decision time (data-quality exception, plausible within minutes of arrival); (b) C05 at 10:54 — requires the escalation component, but it is internally coherent: the trigger is deadline breach, matching the prompt's hint that a queue may change policy when an SLA is endangered; (c) C01 over C07 at 11:40 — not explained by any candidate except FIFO/aging, and it caused an avoidable deadline miss for C07. This one genuinely requires an undocumented exception (starvation-prevention aging cap, a "finish the morning backlog" instruction, or an unlogged upward revision of C07's estimate).

**QD (skill routing + FIFO within skill + appointment reservation).** Supporting: all ten assignments respect skills; D04 before D08 (11:25) and D07 before D10 (11:48) reject priority and SPT while matching FIFO; the D-X hold for D06 supports hard appointment reservation; uniquely-qualified pairings (D05→D-F, D07→D-X) are honored. Contradicting: D-F idling 11:36–12:00 while D10 was feasible violates work conservation; explanations are an unlogged extension of the meeting, a reservation of D-F for D05, or D10 having already been committed to D-X's queue at scan time. The D10 note ("D-F still unavailable until 12:20") suggests the dispatcher treated D-F as blocked for the whole 11:36–12:20 span — an availability-data discrepancy rather than a discipline violation.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Test 1 — QC at 11:40 (C01 vs C07).** Competing explanations: (i) dynamic SPT with an aging/starvation override → picks C01 because its waiting time (100 min) crossed a threshold; (ii) SPT on stale or revised estimates → picks C01 if C07's dispatch-visible estimate had been revised above 36 minutes. Next-job predictions differ subtly: under (i), on any comparable day the oldest job should jump the queue at a similar wait threshold regardless of estimates; under (ii), jumps should track estimate revisions and never occur when estimates are stable. Discriminating observable: the estimate-revision history (est_min values as seen by dispatch at 11:40). A single field — "estimate at decision time" — separates the two: if C07 still showed 18, aging is proven; if it showed >36, plain SPT survives.

**Test 2 — QB at 09:31 (resume B01 vs start B03).** Competing explanations: (i) FIFO over all visible jobs (B01, arrival 09:00, is oldest, so it resumes); (ii) resume-first rule (interrupted work always resumes before new selections, regardless of age). Both predict B01 here and again at 10:14, so this log cannot separate them. Counterfactual: if a preempted job were *younger* than a waiting job (e.g., had B01 arrived at 09:25 and been preempted at 09:31 with B03 already waiting since 09:20), FIFO predicts B03 next while resume-first predicts B01. Discriminating observable: any preemption episode where the interrupted job is not the oldest eligible job; failing that, an explicit "resume vs fresh-dispatch" flag in the dispatch log.

**Test 3 — QD at 11:48 (D07 vs D10 on D-X).** Competing explanations: (i) FIFO within the skill-eligible pool (D07 arrived 11:14 < 11:25); (ii) least-flexible-job-first (D07 can only run on D-X, D10 could in principle run on D-F). Both predict D07 in every decision this log contains. Counterfactual: had D10 arrived at 11:10 (before D07), FIFO predicts D10 next at 11:48 while flexibility-based routing still predicts D07. Discriminating observable: an episode where the uniquely-qualified job is not the oldest eligible one; alternatively, the dispatcher's eligibility snapshot showing whether D-F was even considered an alternative server for D10 (if D-F was marked unavailable until 12:20, flexibility routing had nothing to act on and FIFO is the simpler surviving explanation).

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility (most plausible first), with impact on the diagnosis:

1. **Arrival-scan delay (up to 2 min) — high plausibility, documented.** Explains why B05 (arrived 09:46) was invisible at the 09:48 decision, which is what keeps QB's "FIFO + P1 preemption" model alive at that epoch. If scans were actually instantaneous, 09:48 becomes a double violation (wrong job and wrong trigger) and QB's confidence drops toward "undocumented hybrid with frequent manual dispatch."
2. **Estimate-entry lag or noisy/strategic estimates — high plausibility.** est_min is the dispatch-visible estimate; if C06's estimate was not yet recorded at 10:32, the C04 pick is pure SPT and QC's confidence rises materially. Conversely, if estimates are systematically gamed (inflated to push work back), all SPT conclusions in QC weaken, and the observed order might reflect estimate manipulation rather than a discipline.
3. **Unlogged resource reservations or calendar drift — medium-high plausibility.** D-F idling 11:36–12:00 is best explained by an unlogged hold (early meeting start, or D-F reserved for D05 as the only fully qualified resource). If reservations exist, QD becomes "skill routing + reservation scheduling," and D10's long wait is a capacity artifact, not a discipline signal. The same class of explanation covers C1's "prior job ends 10:10" (any pre-10:10 non-service is not evidence).
4. **Manual dispatcher overrides — medium plausibility, one explicitly logged.** B01's second stop ("dispatcher reassignment") shows overrides happen. If overrides are frequent but usually unlogged, every single-episode anomaly (QC's 11:40, QB's 09:48) may be an override, which caps achievable confidence for any queue at roughly the fraction of decisions that are policy-driven.
5. **Per-queue appointment tolerance differences — medium plausibility.** A03 starts exactly on time, A06 five minutes late, B07 twenty-six minutes late. A single system-wide appointment rule cannot produce all three; either QA enforces appointments and QB does not, or B07's appointment field is imported metadata with no operational meaning in QB. This changes whether QB is "FIFO ignoring appointments" (a policy) or "FIFO with a data-quality artifact."
6. **Reactive SLA escalation as designed behavior — medium plausibility.** C05's selection immediately after its deadline breach looks like an alarm-triggered switch. If confirmed, QC is a designed hybrid (SPT + SLA guardrail) rather than SPT with violations — a materially different managerial conclusion (the guardrail fires too late: C05 finished 44 minutes past deadline).
7. **One-minute timestamp granularity — lower plausibility as a driver.** Could reorder near-simultaneous events (e.g., B02's arrival vs the 09:16 preemption) but none of the diagnoses above hinge on sub-minute ordering.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized by which ambiguity in *these* data each field resolves:

1. **Dispatch-decision timestamp and the eligibility set snapshot at that instant** (which jobs the dispatcher saw, with their visible attributes). Resolves the largest ambiguity class: QB 09:48 (was B05 visible?), QC 10:32 (was C06's estimate visible?), QD 11:48 (was D-F considered for D10?).
2. **Queue-entry (scan) time separately from physical arrival time.** Directly bounds the scan-delay confound instead of assuming the 2-minute maximum.
3. **Estimate revision history: est_min value as of each decision, with revision timestamps and author.** Required before trusting any SPT conclusion in QC; discriminates Test 1.
4. **Resource state log: busy/idle/reserved/meeting with reasons, at minute granularity.** Explains D-F 11:36–12:00 and D-X 11:20–11:30; separates reservation scheduling from discipline choices.
5. **Appointment tolerance configuration per queue (earliest-start, latest-start, hold-capacity yes/no).** Distinguishes QA's hard start-at-time behavior from QB's apparent non-enforcement, and confirms whether D-X's idle hold is policy.
6. **Preemption and resumption records: trigger (automatic rule vs manual), reason code, remaining-work estimate at stop and at resume.** QB's model hinges on classifying the 09:48 stop; also enables the resume-first vs FIFO test (Test 2).
7. **Priority assignment time and any re-prioritization events.** Rules out that A04/A08/D10 were priority-1 only after their waits began (which would rescue a priority reading of QA/QD).
8. **Policy version / active-rule identifier per decision.** Directly tests the SLA-escalation hypothesis in QC (a rule switch at 10:50–10:54 should appear) and detects mid-day policy changes generally.
9. **Deadline/SLA alarm log (when an at-risk flag was raised and who saw it).** Explains why C05 was taken only after breach and whether C07's miss triggered anything.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue: eight starts, one clean appointment override, and repeated head-to-head episodes in which FIFO beats priority (A04, A08) and SPT (A07 at 09:05) — the discipline is FIFO with appointment insertion, and the priority field is operationally dead there. QD is nearly as identifiable at the routing layer (skill constraints are never violated, appointments reserve capacity), but its within-pool tie between FIFO and least-flexible-first is unresolved by this log. QB is moderately identifiable — FIFO plus priority-1 preemption fits all but one decision — but that fit leans on the scan-delay assumption and one manual override. QC is the least identifiable: no single mechanism covers more than about half of its informative decisions without auxiliary hypotheses, and its two deadline misses (C05, C07) occurred under *different* apparent rules, which is the signature of an unstable or manually driven process rather than a documented discipline.

The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone would be "priority scheduling." A dispatcher preemption for one priority-1 job (B02) pattern-matches to priority discipline, yet in three of four queues priority-1 work demonstrably starves: A04 waited 45 minutes and A08 61 minutes behind priority-3 jobs in QA, and D10 — a 12-minute priority-1 repair — waited 51 minutes in QD while a priority-3 job ran. Certifying the priority mechanism from this log would both misdescribe three queues and conceal the operational failure that the priority field is largely ignored. A close second is claiming SPT for QC: the same surface order is partly reproducible by estimate-entry timing and manual escalations, so an SPT certification there would rest on the least reliable field in the dataset — the dispatch-time estimate.
