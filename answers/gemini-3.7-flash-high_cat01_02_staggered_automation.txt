## 1. What is observed

The supplied dataset records 40 monthly observations across five branches from January through August 2026. A clear distinction exists across several operational dimensions:

1. Availability vs. Intensity: Binary availability (`automation=1`) was phased in for Alpha and Beta in April 2026 and Gamma in July 2026, while Delta and Epsilon remained unautomated. However, actual utilization (`automated_fast_path_pct`) ramped up gradually from a baseline of 4–6% to 48–50% in Alpha and Beta, and to 32% in Gamma, reflecting operational learning.
2. Endogenous Selection: Rollout was targeted specifically to Alpha and Beta due to high incoming volumes and historically poor cycle times, rather than through random assignment. Gamma adopted later following a readiness pilot.
3. Contemporaneous Confounders: Several major organizational shifts co-occurred with rollout: a centralized fraud desk opened in March 2026; routing campaigns shifted +10% volume to Alpha/Beta in April and +5% to Gamma in July; staffing fluctuated (Alpha lost 2 FTEs in May, Beta gained 1 in April, Gamma lost 1 in June, Delta gained 1 in June); a product update in May elevated high-complexity cases organization-wide; and branch managers possessed operational discretion.
4. Measurement Limitations: Metrics represent monthly branch-level aggregates. Median and 90th percentile cycle times cannot be linearly decomposed or averaged. CSAT survey response rates varied widely between 20% and 45% without recorded sample sizes, and fraud-desk queue durations were not logged.

### Interference and Treatment-Version Clarifications
- **Interference (SUTVA Violations):** Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA) is violated across branches via two operational mechanisms: (1) **Routing Campaigns:** The organization routed +10% volume to Alpha/Beta in April and +5% to Gamma in July, artificially altering queue loads in untreated branches. (2) **Centralized Fraud Desk:** All branches share the centralized fraud desk launched in March. The automated triage algorithm systematically sent more borderline cases to this desk, increasing queue congestion and turnaround times for unautomated branches sharing the same specialist pool.
- **Treatment-Version Variation:** The intervention is not a static binary switch. Treatment intensity evolved endogenously from 25% to 50% as staff learned the interface. Furthermore, local branch managers exercised autonomous discretion over staffing and workflows (e.g., Beta added 1 FTE while Alpha lost 2 FTEs), meaning the implemented treatment version varied substantially across branches and over time.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Clean-Cohort Staggered DiD / Event Study (Primary)** | Staggered binary adoption cohort indicators ($g \in \{\text{Apr}, \text{Jul}\}$) and relative event time $\tau$ | Clean never-treated branches (Delta, Epsilon) and not-yet-treated (Gamma pre-July) | `high_complexity_pct`, `workload_per_fte` (arrivals / FTE), month fixed effects | **E1**, **E2**, **E4**, **E5**, **E7** | Parallel counterfactual trends between adopters and clean controls; no unmodeled cross-branch queue spillovers | Pre-treatment trend equality ($\tau \in [-3, -1]$); cohort-specific placebo tests; leave-one-out control checks | Non-parallel macro trends; unmeasured cross-branch queue spillovers via shared fraud desk |
| **Instrumental Variables / 2SLS Panel (Alternative 1)** | Rollout schedule ($Z_{it}$) used as an exogenous instrument for continuous intensity $D_{it}$ | Unautomated branch-months ($D_{it} \le 6\%$) | `high_complexity_pct`, `workload_per_fte`, branch & month fixed effects | **E3** (Dose-Response) | Instrument relevance ($F > 10$); Exclusion restriction: rollout timing affects cycle time *only* through fast-path adoption | First-stage $F$-statistic; tests of overidentifying restrictions across staggered cohorts | Exclusion restriction violation: rollout directly altered manager effort or routing behavior |
| **Synthetic Control / Imputation Matrix (Alternative 2)** | Step intervention indicator at cohort adoption dates | Convex combination of untreated branches (Delta, Epsilon) | Pre-intervention values of cycle time, rework, escalation, and complexity | **E1**, **E2** | Linear factor model holds; convex hull of donor pool covers treated units | In-space placebos (assigning synthetic control to Delta/Epsilon); in-time placebos | Extreme extrapolation: donor pool ($N=2$) is too small to construct a valid convex hull for high-volume Alpha |

The staggered Difference-in-Differences design with clean comparison cohorts (never-treated and not-yet-treated branches) is the most credible identification strategy for this panel. Traditional two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) regressions with continuous or binary staggered adoption suffer from severe bias when treatment effects vary dynamically over time or across branches—specifically because already-treated units (Alpha and Beta in June/July) act as contaminated controls for newly treated units (Gamma in July). By decomposing the panel into clean cohort-specific 2x2 DiD contrasts (Alpha/Beta vs. Delta/Epsilon; Gamma vs. Delta/Epsilon), we eliminate negative weighting and forbidden comparisons.

Furthermore, this primary strategy directly accommodates the multi-month organizational shocks that occurred across the enterprise. The March rollout of the centralized fraud desk and the May product complexity surge affected both adopters and non-adopters simultaneously. Differencing against contemporaneous never-treated branches absorbs these enterprise-wide macro shocks. While Synthetic Control is appealing, constructing convex combinations from only two never-treated units (Delta and Epsilon) offers insufficient donor degrees of freedom and cannot span Alpha's high baseline cycle time. Instrumenting treatment intensity using the rollout schedule addresses gradual adoption, but relies heavily on linear exclusion restrictions. Clean cohort DiD balances transparent identification, robustness to dynamic learning curves, and interpretability given the small-cluster setting.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

Leadership should adopt: **expand with an embedded evaluation**.

A blanket rollout to Delta and Epsilon based on simple aggregate comparisons risks scaling unmeasured queue bottlenecks, whereas halting rollout foregoes demonstrated operational throughput gains. 

**Branch Sequencing & Assignment Mechanism:**
Rollout should next target **Epsilon**, followed by **Delta**, using a formal stepped-wedge randomized phase-in. Specifically, within Epsilon, we recommend an initial 8-week case-level micro-randomization (A/B evaluation where 50% of incoming eligible cases are routed to automated triage and 50% to manual triage based on odd/even case ID hash) before full branch enablement. This cleanly isolates the algorithmic effect from macro product shocks, volume surges, and staffing shifts.

**Measurement Windows & Data Requirements:**
The evaluation requires maintaining the current 8-month baseline panel (Jan–Aug 2026) as the historical pre-period, followed by a minimum 4-month prospective evaluation window (Sep–Dec 2026). Rather than relying solely on monthly summary quantiles, telemetry must record granular event logs: case-level registration, triage start/end timestamps, specialist queue wait times, fraud-desk wait times, rework loop count per case, and case-level CSAT linkage.

**Spillover Monitoring & Guardrails:**
To monitor interference across branches, the central fraud desk must log incoming referral sources, queue depths, and processing durations by originating branch. Automated guardrails will halt or throttle triage automation if:
1. Branch `rework_pct` exceeds a rolling 14-day threshold of 15.0% (indicating poor triage classification accuracy);
2. Branch `p90_cycle_h` worsens by >15% relative to pre-period controls;
3. Escalation backlog exceeds 48 hours in specialist review;
4. Survey-adjusted CSAT drops by >0.15 points on a 14-day rolling mean.

**Scale-Up Decision Rule:**
Full organization-wide rollout to Delta is triggered only if the embedded evaluation in Epsilon demonstrates at month 3:
1. A statistically significant reduction in case-level median cycle time (estimated $\Delta \ge 3.0$ hours);
2. A non-positive effect on rework rate ($\Delta \text{rework} \le 0.0$ percentage points);
3. No statistically significant degradation in customer satisfaction at the 95% confidence level.

def fit_cluster_ols(y_var, x_vars, cluster_var, data):
    """Fits OLS with cluster-robust sandwich covariance."""
    X = data[x_vars].copy()
    X.insert(0, 'Intercept', 1.0)
    y = data[y_var].values
    X_mat = X.values
    
    # Point estimates
    XtX_inv = np.linalg.pinv(X_mat.T @ X_mat)
    beta = XtX_inv @ X_mat.T @ y
    resid = y - X_mat @ beta
    
    # Cluster-robust sandwich estimator
    clusters = data[cluster_var].unique()
    G = len(clusters)
    N = len(y)
    K = X_mat.shape[1]
    
    meat = np.zeros((K, K))
    for c in clusters:
        idx = (data[cluster_var] == c).values
        X_c = X_mat[idx]
        e_c = resid[idx]
        score_c = X_c.T @ e_c
        meat += np.outer(score_c, score_c)
        
    df_adj = (G / (G - 1)) * ((N - 1) / (N - K)) if G > 1 else 1.0
    vcov_cluster = df_adj * (XtX_inv @ meat @ XtX_inv)
    se_cluster = np.sqrt(np.maximum(0, np.diagonal(vcov_cluster)))
    
    return pd.DataFrame({
        'Variable': X.columns,
        'Coef': beta,
        'Cluster_SE': se_cluster,
        't_stat': np.where(se_cluster > 0, beta / se_cluster, np.nan)
    })

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Rollout of the automated triage system was non-random and staggered (Alpha and Beta in April 2026, Gamma in July 2026), with actual fast-path case execution ramping up gradually from 4–6% to 48–50% in Alpha/Beta and 32% in Gamma, while contemporaneous operational changes—including a centralized fraud desk in March, an organizational product complexity surge in May, and routing adjustments—occurred across all branches.
- `Suggestive:` Adopting branches experienced a ~2 percentage point reduction in rework loops and successfully buffered against the severe queue blowouts observed in untreated branches during the May–June complexity surge, even though automated triage increased specialist escalations by approximately 1 percentage point.
- `Conditionally causal:` If we assume parallel counterfactual trends and negligible cross-branch queue interference from the centralized fraud desk, automation caused a net reduction in median cycle time of 2.0 to 5.0 hours (a 4% to 10% relative improvement) and mitigated 90th percentile tail delays by 3.0 to 8.0 hours relative to unautomated counterfactual congestion.
- `Not supported:` A blanket executive claim that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" or that it improves customer satisfaction across the board is wholly unsupported, because raw cycle times were confounded by volume and complexity surges, tail delays expanded relative to medians, and customer satisfaction survey nonresponse (20–45% response rate) precludes rigorous causal attribution.
