# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this case, **resilience** means a deviation is bounded, intentional, risk-aware, evidence-producing, and reversible. It preserves an essential service while maintaining the minimum controls needed for the affected product and customer. Examples include B, manual authorization, when done by trained compliance staff with offline authoritative lists and timestamps; F, temporary release control, when all controlled-substance orders are actually reviewed and cold-chain temperature checks are explicitly added; I, deferring noncritical bulk, when rules and time limits are explicit; J, backlog prioritization, when criteria are documented; and K, reservation reconciliation, when treated as a mandatory recovery workstream.

**Concealed noncompliance** occurs when throughput appears preserved but required controls or traceability disappear and become hidden in later rework, reconciliation, or audit queues. The clearest examples are C, skipping system authorization for known ambient customers; G, dispatch without scan; H, phone-only POD for controlled products; D, using stale inventory snapshots after they cease to be reliable; E, manual pick lists without lot/expiry/dual-control evidence for high-risk products; and spreadsheet invoice holds that defer evidence and revenue recognition outside normal controls. The DFG evidence supports this distinction: T2 fitness fell to 0.71 with only 44% trace fit, while traceability exceptions reached 9.5%, cold-chain documentation missing 4.8%, controlled dual-control missing 2.9%, and rework 12.5%. Hospital-critical fill remained 91%, but that did not mean the process was compliant for all products or segments.

Some adaptations are **ambiguous depending on risk appetite and execution**. The Hospital-critical fast lane is mission-valid but risky if it bypasses authorization for high-risk products. Manual inventory snapshots are reasonable for a few hours and risky once stale. Manual pick lists can be resilient if they carry lot/expiry and dual-control evidence, but noncompliant if they are only spreadsheets without traceability. Phone POD is acceptable for low-risk ambient products with callback verification and timestamps, but not as the primary evidence for controlled substances. Deferring noncritical orders is legitimate only with explicit rules, maximum deferral ages, fairness monitoring, and customer communication. Audit remediation is necessary to close gaps, but it does not convert retrospective fixes into real-time compliance.

# 4. Recovery process model

Use a tiered continuity process: **Detect -> Declare tier -> Triage by customer criticality and product risk -> Execute with minimum controls and offline evidence -> Recover systems -> Reconcile evidence and inventory -> Audit-remediate by risk -> Exit only when evidence debt is closed.**

**Explicit trigger conditions**

- **Level 1 degraded:** WMS/order intake unavailable for more than 15 minutes, scan failure affecting more than 5% of orders, or inventory snapshot age exceeds 2 hours. Activate manual fallback for critical orders; defer noncritical bulk; require full product-risk controls for high-risk products.
- **Level 2 extended:** outage exceeds 4 hours, backlog exceeds an agreed threshold such as 500 cases, or any controlled/cold/recall order has missing required evidence. Activate incident command, daily QA/compliance risk review, central triage queue, and customer communications.
- **Level 3 high-risk:** outage exceeds 24 hours, inventory synchronization remains unreliable, or high-risk evidence debt is accumulating. Escalate executive/QA ownership, stop nonessential bulk, consider alternate sites or approved sourcing, and require daily regulatory/compliance assessment where applicable.
- **Recovery trigger:** systems return but reconciliation, POD, temperature, scan, or authorization debt remains. Stay in incident/recovery mode until high-risk evidence debt is zero and overall evidence debt is declining below agreed thresholds.
- **Exit criteria:** systems stable, inventory reconciled, high-risk evidence debt closed, audit-remediation queue visible and below threshold, backlog age controlled, and QA/operations/compliance sign-off.

**Triage rules**

- Priority score combines **clinical/customer criticality**, **product risk**, and **age**.
- P0: Hospital-critical cold-chain, controlled-substance, or recall-sensitive orders, and urgent hospital-critical ambient orders.
- P1: Hospital-critical ambient, Pharmacy-standard cold/controlled/recall-sensitive orders.
- P2: Pharmacy-standard ambient orders.
- P3: Wholesale-bulk nonurgent ambient orders.
- Product risk overrides speed: a controlled/cold/recall order cannot be downgraded to “ship now” just because a customer is critical. If minimum controls cannot be met, hold/quarantine, seek approved alternate stock/source, and notify clinical stakeholders rather than dispatch noncompliantly.
- Defer P3 first, then P2 only within explicit time limits and with customer communication. Do not let informal call-in or legacy relationships replace the triage queue.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk**

| Risk segment | Minimum offline controls | Dispatch/release rule |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient low-risk, standard customer | Manual authorization against approved offline list or documented call-back; current inventory revalidation; pick record; POD with timestamp/callback | May proceed if evidence is captured and later reconciled |
| Ambient recall-sensitive | Above plus lot/batch/expiry capture, quantity verification, and manual scan or photo evidence | No dispatch without lot/expiry traceability |
| Cold-chain | Above plus temperature logger ID/reading at pack and dispatch, excursion SOP, packaging validation, QA release, courier temperature capability | No dispatch with missing temperature evidence; hold/quarantine if evidence cannot be captured |
| Controlled-substance | Authorized customer/license verification; secure inventory; dual sign-off at pick, pack, release, and dispatch; tamper evidence; courier signature; no phone-only POD | No skip authorization; no dispatch without dual-control and secure chain-of-custody evidence |
| Hospital-critical | Same product-specific controls, but fastest queue | Gated fast lane: priority allowed, control bypass not allowed |
| Pharmacy-standard | Product controls plus maximum deferral SLA and customer notification | Can be deferred within limits, but not indefinitely or informally |
| Wholesale-bulk | Product controls for high-risk products; otherwise batch/defer | Lowest priority; still traceable for controlled/cold/recall |

**Manual evidence capture**

- Use pre-numbered emergency order packets linked to the order ID.
- Capture timestamp, user ID, authorization source, customer, product, quantity, lot/batch/expiry, temperature device ID and reading, dual signatures, courier ID, and POD.
- Prefer offline mobile scanners with store-and-forward or paper forms photographed at each control point. Do not rely on free-text spreadsheets for high-risk products.
- Every deviation must have a reason code and approver. For high-risk products, no required field may be blank; if it cannot be captured, the order is held and escalated.
- Upload/scan evidence as soon as connectivity returns and link it to the order and inventory record.

**Backlog prioritization**

- Maintain one central recovery queue with published rules and daily review.
- Rank by product risk first, then customer criticality, then age/SLA.
- Apply fairness caps: do not let one customer group absorb all capacity; track fill and deferral rates by Hospital-critical, Pharmacy-standard, and Wholesale-bulk.
- Age escalations: high-risk orders older than 24 hours, pharmacy-standard beyond agreed deferral SLA, or controlled/cold/recall evidence gaps escalate to QA/compliance.
- Document exceptions and compare actual dispatch order against the prioritization rule.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow**

- On system restoration, automatically create an **evidence-debt case** for every manual order and every missing scan, POD, temperature, authorization, release, or dual-control event.
- Triage evidence debt by product risk: controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive first; then hospital-critical; then other segments.
- Set SLAs: high-risk evidence review within 24 hours, medium-risk within 3 days, low-risk within 10 days or by agreement.
- Reconcile physical stock, lot/expiry, reservations, and dispatch records before releasing affected inventory back to normal. Block high-risk dispatch/invoice release when required evidence remains unresolved.
- Do not close high-risk evidence debt by desk review alone. Require corroborated evidence, second review, courier/customer confirmation where appropriate, and QA sign-off.
- If required evidence cannot be reconstructed, record a deviation, perform risk assessment, quarantine or recall where necessary, notify QA/regulatory per policy, and initiate corrective action.
- Track the audit-remediation queue as a management metric until closed; do not declare recovery based only on cycle time or fill rate.
