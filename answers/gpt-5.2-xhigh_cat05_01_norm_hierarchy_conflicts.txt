## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### 1.1 Facts (from the record)
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved, globally scoped policy effective 2026-01-01 (Source A).
- DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev6 is a controlled local SOP for Aachen maintenance/production-support purchases (Source B).
- ECD-2 is a written incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander, time-limited to 2026-07-14 09:00 through 2026-07-18 18:00, scoped to “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event” (Source C).
- A CFO email provides finance/operational guidance during the ECD-2 window but is not a controlled policy artifact (Source D).
- ERP outage ON-44 affects evidential weight of ERP timestamps for 2026-07-14 08:30–13:00 (Evidence note).

### 1.2 Assumptions (explicit, defeasible)
- **A-primacy by issue**: GPIP-4 sets non-negotiable integrity controls unless a valid GPIP-4 A7 exception exists. Local SOP and incident directives can shape *how* controls are evidenced and can override *operational workflow timing* only where clearly stated.
- **What counts as the “supplier commitment” moment**: `Commit_to_Supplier` is treated as the commitment point for “before commitment” obligations (A2, A3, C3).
- **“Final business approver” under A1**: the last required *non-finance* approval gate prior to commitment. Under ECD-2, the C3 joint Incident Command authorization is treated as a business approval gate; if the requester or committer is one of those authorizers, A1 is presumptively violated.
- **ECD-2’s override scope**: C4 (“takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements”) is interpreted to override (i) PO timing constraints and (ii) local approved-supplier-register constraints, but **not** to silently waive GPIP-4’s segregation of duties (A1) or Compliance clearance trigger (A3). (This reflects A4/A5’s emphasis that emergencies do not remove integrity controls by default.)
- **Finance-control treatment of the CFO email**: because the CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls (D3), the CFO email is treated as a **standing Finance-Controller authorization mechanism** for the *finance-approval dimension only* during its stated window and thresholds, i.e., it can satisfy the “Finance Controller” element of A2 for purchases within the ECD-2 window **from 2026-07-15 07:20 onward**, up to EUR 150,000 (and, defeasibly, for >150,000 when D2’s additional legal safeguard is met). It does **not** waive Compliance clearance (A3) or segregation (A1).
- **Business/budget-owner approval under ECD-2**: where ECD-2 applies, C3 joint Incident Command authorization is treated as the emergency “independent business approval” mechanism, provided at least one authorizer is independent of the requester/committer (or, if independence is unknown, compliance is only as strong as evidence of role separation).
- **“New supplier” vs. “unapproved supplier register”**: “new supplier” in A3 is treated as “new to the purchasing entity’s approved usage context” unless evidence shows corporate pre-existence with Compliance clearance already in place. Thus, “new supplier” (P-502) clearly triggers A3; “unapproved supplier” (P-504/507/508) **likely** triggers A3 absent evidence it is already an approved corporate supplier.
- **ECD-2 scope (criticality)**: “critical sterilization-line lots” is applied as: items needed to maintain/restore sterilization-line capability needed to contain the contamination event within the directive window, including immediate replacements and scarce “only available” restoration parts; backup-line contingency parts with multi-day stock are presumptively out-of-scope unless documented as required to contain the incident (defeasible).
- **Burden of proof**: COMPLIANT/JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION require affirmative, contemporaneous evidence that the required pre-commitment acts occurred (or that a valid override/exception applied). Where a breach is clear (e.g., Compliance clearance timestamp after commitment), classification defaults to NONCOMPLIANT rather than INDETERMINATE.

### 1.3 Decision method (applied uniformly)
1. **Applicability**: determine whether ECD-2 applies by time window and scope; otherwise apply GPIP-4 + SOP baseline.
2. **Issue-by-issue conflict resolution**: for each control issue (timing, supplier onboarding, finance approval, compliance clearance, segregation of duties, logging), select the controlling rule set via the hierarchy in §2.
3. **Temporal evaluation at commitment**: evaluate whether required approvals/clearances existed **before** `Commit_to_Supplier`.
4. **Evidence weighting**: prefer contemporaneous external or signed artifacts over delayed ERP entries during outages; do not treat post-hoc approvals as curative (A6), but do credit them as mitigation.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

1. **P-502 (would shift NONCOMPLIANT → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**  
   - **Premise change**: Treat ECD-2 as a de facto GPIP-4 A7 exception (i.e., COO+Incident Commander directive is sufficient to waive GPIP-4 A3 timing for Compliance clearance when CRO unavailable).  
   - **Why credible**: Organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays; A7 is explicitly ambiguous on this point.  
   - **Why weak**: A7 reserves exceptions to CRO; CRO did not ratify later; ECD-2 never states “Compliance may be after commitment,” so this becomes a large implied waiver.

2. **P-501 (would shift JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION → NONCOMPLIANT)**  
   - **Premise change**: Interpret SOP B2 as strictly requiring Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve **before** any commitment, even in B4 emergencies (B4 only relaxes PO timing).  
   - **Why credible**: Control logic: approvals after commitment are not approvals but ratifications; B5 says emergency does not expressly waive finance approval.  
   - **Why weak**: SOP does not explicitly state the temporal relation for B2, and B4 implies a practical need to commit before administrative steps in true stoppage emergencies.

3. **P-505 (would shift COMPLIANT → NONCOMPLIANT)**  
   - **Premise change**: Treat ERP `Commit_to_Supplier` timestamp (18:06) as conclusive and reject clock-skew arguments; thus ECD-2 had expired at commitment and D1 cannot apply.  
   - **Why credible**: ON-44 outage note does not cover 07-18; absent clock-audit evidence, ERP time could be the system-of-record.  
   - **Why weak**: Supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is strong contemporaneous external evidence of a pre-18:00 commitment.

4. **P-507 (would shift COMPLIANT → NONCOMPLIANT)**  
   - **Premise change**: Require ECD-2 C3 recording to be in the ERP (or another specifically designated system) within 4 hours, rejecting the signed PDF as insufficient.  
   - **Why credible**: “Recorded” can be interpreted as “entered into the incident control system,” not merely created as a file.  
   - **Why weak**: The directive allows initial verbal authorization and is silent on the system; outage context supports using the earliest authenticated artifact (signed PDF) as the record.

5. **P-508 (would shift JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION → COMPLIANT)**  
   - **Premise change**: Interpret D2 (“For amounts above 150k, obtain Legal clearance as well”) as meaning D1’s “joint IC authorization is sufficient for all operational approvals” remains in force above 150k, provided Legal clearance is added.  
   - **Why credible**: That is a natural reading of “as well” and matches the observed control design (added legal safeguard).  
   - **Why weak**: The email explicitly draws a threshold boundary; absent explicit Finance Controller language, treating it as satisfying GPIP A2 is still interpretive.

6. **P-504 (would reduce severity, not necessarily change NONCOMPLIANT)**  
   - **Premise change**: Interpret “unapproved supplier” as “not in Aachen register” but **not** “new supplier” under GPIP A3 (supplier may be approved corporately).  
   - **Credible**: Corporate vs site registers can differ; GPIP triggers might intend “new to Northbridge globally.”  
   - **Weak**: No evidence is provided that the supplier is corporately approved; even under the narrow reading, SOP B3/B1 remain breached.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

### 6.1 Distinctions (what is wrong, exactly)
- **Clear policy breach (integrity gate missed)**:
  - P-502: Compliance clearance (A3) occurred after commitment; no plausible evidential re-interpretation without new artifacts.
  - P-503: Missing Compliance and approvals; plus segregation concern.
  - P-506: Post-expiry reliance on an undocumented “verbal extension” plus baseline workflow deviations.
  - P-504: Out-of-scope spend routed through emergency logic; unapproved supplier without directive applicability.
- **Logging defect / late system entry (timely act likely occurred)**:
  - P-507: Authorization “appears in ERP” late, but a signed PDF within 4 hours is strong substitute evidence under outage conditions.
- **Late evidence of a timely act (timing dispute, not necessarily breach)**:
  - P-505: ERP timestamp vs supplier acknowledgment near expiry; strongest evidence supports pre-expiry commitment.
- **Inability to prove compliance (governance/evidence gap)**:
  - P-508: Core clearances exist, but finance-controller approval above the CFO email threshold is not evidenced as a GPIP A2-equivalent control.

### 6.2 Immediate case-level remediation
- **P-502**:
  - Obtain an explicit post-incident Compliance memorandum documenting why pre-commit clearance was infeasible and what compensating checks were performed (sanctions screening, conflict-of-interest, supplier due diligence).
  - Record a formal exception rationale and submit to Internal Audit; treat as a control breach with corrective action, not as “fixed” by later clearance.
- **P-503**:
  - Conduct a segregation-of-duties review and determine whether the commitment can be renegotiated/ratified under proper approvals; document why a 3-day-stock item was deemed “critical.”
  - Require retrospective Compliance review (even if purchase completed) and enforce separation for future incidents.
- **P-504**:
  - Reclassify and isolate non-sterilization operational purchases from incident directives; require supplier onboarding/approval retroactively if continued use is needed; consider disciplinary coaching for scope misuse.
- **P-506**:
  - Treat the “verbal extension” claim as unsubstantiated; require written incident-extension protocol going forward; document approvals properly and assess whether any commitments should be re-authorized.
- **P-508**:
  - Obtain explicit Finance Controller (or CFO) ratification stating that D1/D2 was intended to cover >150k with legal safeguard; if not, record as a threshold-breach exception and require governance fix.

### 6.3 Monitoring actions (systemic)
- Create an **incident procurement audit pack** per purchase: commitment evidence, authorization evidence, compliance clearance, finance basis, scope justification, and timing proof.
- Implement **scope tagging** (“ECD-2 in-scope / out-of-scope”) as a required field with IC owner signoff to prevent scope creep (e.g., cafeteria repairs).
- Add automated checks for **A3 triggers** (new supplier / >100k) to prevent commitment without Compliance clearance, including an offline form path for ERP outages.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

Proposed replacement clause (for insertion into GPIP-4, replacing/expanding A7 and clarifying interaction with incident directives):

**Emergency Incident Procurement Overlay (EIPO) — Authority, Scope, and Non‑Waivable Controls**
1. **Authority & form**: Any temporary procurement overlay that modifies workflow steps must be issued as a controlled “EIPO” document, **co-signed by the Chief Risk Officer (or a pre-designated written delegate), the CFO, and the COO**, and must be stored in the controlled repository within 24 hours. Emails are advisory only.
2. **Scope & definition**: The EIPO must define “critical” explicitly (time-to-need, line impact, containment relevance), list covered lot types, state the exact **start/end timestamps**, and include an explicit statement that the overlay **expires automatically** and cannot be extended verbally.
3. **Non‑waivable integrity controls** (may only be waived if the EIPO explicitly states the waiver and its compensating controls):
   - **Segregation of duties**: the requester or committer cannot be a required business approver.
   - **New supplier / high-value clearance**: purchases from a new supplier or >EUR 100,000 require **pre‑commit Compliance clearance** (minimum: sanctions/COI screening and due diligence tiering).
4. **Emergency approval model (thresholded)**:
   - For **EUR 50,000–150,000**: pre‑commit approval by (i) an **Incident Budget Owner** (named role) and (ii) a **Finance Controller** (named role), both distinct from requester/committer.
   - For **>EUR 150,000**: add **Legal clearance** pre‑commitment.
5. **Operational workflow flexibility**: The EIPO may allow **commitment before PO** and may temporarily allow **non-registered suppliers**, but must require PO creation within a fixed time (e.g., 8 business hours) and specify supplier onboarding minimums.
6. **Retrospective recording & evidence**: Verbal approvals are permitted only if recorded within 4 hours in a designated system; during system outages, a signed PDF with authenticated timestamps is acceptable if later uploaded without alteration. Post-hoc approvals mitigate but do not cure missing pre-commit approvals unless the EIPO explicitly authorizes a narrow exception.
7. **Anti-normalization safeguard**: Any EIPO use triggers a mandatory after-action review within 10 business days with Internal Audit, including counts of waived steps, spend totals, and a recommendation on whether controls were over-used; repeated EIPOs beyond a defined limit (e.g., >5 days per quarter) require board audit-committee notification.
