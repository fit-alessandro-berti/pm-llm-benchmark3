| 7 | Data-integration artifacts | All | Lot events missing from Job flattening; external override; gaps inconsistent with idle windows | Not a physical delay | n/a | High (as a distortion) | Permanent until fixed |

**Alternative ranking by shipment critical path.** Dispatch times are set by the slowest job in each shipment.
- S1 is set by J4's pack, which follows B2's start at 14:50. That start is gated by RACK-7 and the L7 release.
- S2 is set by J3, through FIX-A and the 10-min batch wait.
- S3 is set by J6, through CAL-3.

Rack, lot and calibration coupling therefore rise, and B1 synchronization falls. Batch synchronization wastes job-time, but its effect on deliveries is masked by partner-hostage waits. This disagreement between the two rankings is itself evidence of coupling.

## 4. What flattening misses

| View | Makes visible | Hides or duplicates | Misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | StageProcessInspectPack sequence; rework loops (2 of 6) | Shared events such as the 12:05 cure (convergence); lot events; tool waits; partner waits | "No job is more than 1.8 SD above the mean, so there is no problem." The variance lives in coupling, not in any single job. |
| Machine | Utilization (54/44/58%), setup sequences | Why the machine was idle: the batch was incomplete, the rack was cleaning, the tool was held | "Machines are underloaded, so capacity is fine." Capacity is not the issue, but jobs still wait. |
| Batch | The barrier, its members, B1's 12:05 start | Upstream cause of the straggler (J3's FIX-A hold); RACK-7 sequencing between B1 and B2 | "Low Batch–Machine frequency (2) means low impact." |
| Tool | Holds and recalibration windows | Who was waiting (no Wait-for-Tool event); counts are partly inferred | "FIX-A was busy 160 min, so FIX-A is the bottleneck." C3 shows the delay was probably non-critical. |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine and release events; which components share a lot | Job delay unless joined; the L8 link between J1 and J3 | "Lot events do not affect jobs," which is what the Job view shows. |
| Shipment | CreateLoadDispatch (0.8 h + 0.3 h) | Packed-but-waiting time (J1 for 355 min) | "Shipping is fast." |

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting.** Utilization counts machine-busy time. Job waiting is time spent blocked by something other than the machine: batch partners, rack turnaround, fixture hand-offs, calibration, lot release or shipment partners.
- OVEN-1 idled 115 min while J4 was ready.
- CNC-2 idled 42 min during J4's wait.
- TEST-1 idled 50 min during J6's wait.

Idle capacity and waiting jobs are two sides of the same blocking. Machine-gap statistics also understate this, because non-processing events such as Calibrate may split idle windows.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**N1: J3  J4  J1 (Job, Tool, Machine, Component, Batch, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 and starts at 11:45. J4 is ready at 13:10, B2 starts at 14:50, J4 is packed at 19:10 and S1 dispatches at 19:25. J1 was packed at 13:30.
- *Resource feasibility:* one FIX-A, and only one job can hold it. CNC-2 was idle 10:05–10:47, so the machine was not the limit.
- *Policy constraints:* S1 consolidates J1 and J4. B2 needs RACK-7 and J5.
- *Speculation:* a second fixture might have let J4 run earlier, but FIX-A probably affects only local waiting (C3). J1's 330-min penalty is tied to J4's upstream delays, which is causally distant from J3.

**N2: L7  J2/J5  B1/B2  RACK-7  S2/S3 (ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* quarantine 10:20, B1 cure 12:05 (with J2/C2 inside), release 13:50, J5 ready 14:40, B2 start 14:50.
- *Resource feasibility:* RACK-7 was in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, so B2 could not start earlier even if J5 had been ready.
- *Policy constraints:* a quarantined lot should block C2 and C5. The J2 override from another system bypassed this for J2 only.
- *Speculation:* RACK-7's "quarantine" status may result from holding quarantined-lot C2 at 12:05, a contamination hypothesis. It might also be routine cleaning. If the first is true, the override created the cross-batch delay it was meant to avoid, and B2 was delayed indirectly.

**N3: J5  J6  S3 (Job, Machine, Tool, Shipment, Batch B2, Component).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J5 and J6 ready at 16:00 and 16:05. J5 tests 16:10–16:35. CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20. J6 tests 17:25–17:50. S3 then dispatches 35 min after J6's pack.
- *Resource feasibility:* TEST-1 could test any job, but every `Final Test` needs CAL-3, so the real capacity is the tool.
- *Policy constraints:* recalibration appears triggered by use. We do not know whether it is mandatory before J6.
- *Speculation:* recalibration after J5's test may be a usage threshold that would trigger on any job. The lateness is then a sequencing choice, not a physical necessity.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Bounded-wait batching (synchronization).**
- *Target:* the wait-for-all rule on B1.
- *Benefit:* J1 and J2 could save up to 220 min each (C1).
- *Helped:* J1, J2, plus a possibly earlier RACK-7 release for B2.
- *Harmed:* J3, which is pushed to a later mini-batch, and oven cycle efficiency.
- *Cost or risk:* more oven cycles and rack cleanings.
- *Perverse metric:* batch fill rate.
- *Evaluation:* compare per-object ready-to-cure waits before and after; check whether RACK-7 turnaround worsens.

**I2. Tool-aware sequencing (tools).**
- *Target:* CAL-3 and FIX-A.
- *Benefit:* schedule recalibration in idle windows or after the last queued test, and reserve the fixture for the critical job. This could save 45–50 min for J6 and about 100 job-minutes at S3.
- *Helped:* J6 and J5 (through S3).
- *Harmed:* J5 if recalibration shifts in front of it; J3's fixture slot.
- *Cost or risk:* calibration drift and quality risk.
- *Perverse metric:* tool utilization.
- *Evaluation:* measure tool-wait per job and S3 partner-wait, plus quality drift per recalibration interval.

**I3. Shipment max-hold policy (shipment).**
- *Target:* partner waiting.
- *Benefit:* split a shipment when a packed job has waited more than 60 min, which would cut J1's 355-min wait to about 25 min.
- *Helped:* J1, and J5 if S3 is similarly split.
- *Harmed:* J4, which ships alone with no consolidation benefit.
- *Cost or risk:* freight cost and more truck loads.
- *Perverse metric:* truck fill rate or cost per shipment.
- *Evaluation:* measure packed-to-dispatch wait per job, with shipment-level on-time rates as a control.

**I4. Logging improvement (logging).**
- *Target:* visibility.
- *Benefit:* add `Ready for batch`, `Request tool`, `Acquire tool`, `Wait for tool`, `Rack cleaning start/end (reason)` and `Override (with lot link)`, and relate lot events to jobs through `requires`.
- *Helped:* every analysis, and it enables the causal checks in section 8.
- *Harmed:* operator time.
- *Cost or risk:* timestamp gaming and inconsistent event definitions.
- *Perverse metric:* counting logged wait events, which rewards creating waits or hiding them.
- *Evaluation:* audit completeness by reconciling machine-gap statistics against job-centric idle windows.

**I5. Lot release SLA and logged overrides.**
- *Target:* L7-type coupling.
- *Benefit:* a disposition deadline, lot splitting and formal override logging. This could avoid the blocked-time risk seen with J5.
- *Helped:* J5, and J2's compliance position.
- *Harmed:* quality assurance, if overrides increase.
- *Cost or risk:* escape of nonconforming components.
- *Perverse metric:* quarantine duration, which rewards premature release.
- *Evaluation:* measure the job-minutes blocked per lot event, using the joined lot–job relation.

**I6. Second rack or decoupled cleaning (RACK-7).**
- *Target:* the B1B2 coupling.
- *Benefit:* up to 85 min for J4, but only if J5 is ready earlier.
- *Helped:* J4 and B2.
- *Harmed:* nobody directly; floor space.
- *Cost or risk:* cleaning compliance.
- *Perverse metric:* rack utilization.
- *Evaluation:* measure oven idle time while a job is ready.

**Spillovers.** Fixing single mechanisms can just move the wait.
- I2 on FIX-A alone gives J4 about 0 benefit (C3).
- I6 alone gives B2 very little, because J5's readiness (14:40) is only 5 min after the rack clears.
- I3 alone helps J1 by 330 min but leaves S1 unchanged for J4.
- I1 could improve B1 and also free RACK-7 earlier, a positive spillover.
- I5 could remove J2's override dependence but may delay B1 (up to 315 job-min) if lots are enforced strictly.
- Test combinations (I1+I6, I2+I3) rather than individual changes.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):**
1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was occupied or unavailable until about 12:00, so the wait was resource-driven.
2. A rule or chemistry requirement mandates full loads (J3 could never have been excluded).
3. J1 and J2 were not truly ready at 08:10 and 08:25 (for example, a provisional ready timestamp).
4. Cure start times are fixed by a schedule, so waiting for a slot is not waiting for partners.
5. Critical-path analysis shows B1 wait changed no dispatch time. S1 and S2 are set by J4 and J3, so this would demote B1 to a cycle-time issue.
6. Matched batches with similar ready-time spreads show no relation between spread and wait.

**Additional fields or event types that would discriminate:**
1. `Ready for Batch` and `Request/Acquire/Release Tool` events for each tool and job.
2. Oven and rack state timeline (loaded, cleaning, quarantined, with reason codes).
3. A `Supervisor Override` event linked to J2, L7 and C2.
4. Lot–job relations for L8 and for all lot events (so C1 and C3 are covered).
5. CAL-3 recalibration trigger (usage count, drift, time-based) and whether it is mandatory before J6.
6. Shipment policy parameters: cutoff times, carrier slots, and whether partial shipments are allowed.
7. Inspect and rework timestamps for J4, to explain the 190-min residual.
8. A reconciliation of machine-log gaps against idle windows (39 vs 50 min, 73 vs 115 min).
