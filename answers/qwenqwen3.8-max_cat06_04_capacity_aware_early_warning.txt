## 1. Decision rule

The policy is a **constrained, ambiguity-averse expected-utility rule**, not a single breach-probability threshold. It combines:

1. **Stage gate**
   - A Senior Diagnostic Swarm is only considered for cases in **Triage** or **Diagnosis Started**.
   - If `Diagnosis Complete` has occurred, the swarm is **not eligible**, regardless of how high `p_now` is.
   - There must be plausible runway for the swarm to matter: normally at least ~45–60 minutes before expected diagnosis completion or SLA breach. Very late cases may receive non-swarm escalation instead.

2. **Alert persistence and minimum evidence change**
   - A first alert can be actionable if it passes the stage, data-quality, and expected-net-benefit gates.
   - Repeated alerts caused only by the 30-minute model refresh are suppressed unless there is a **material evidence change**, such as:
     - new customer log, test artifact, or diagnostic evidence;
     - stage change or clear process blockage;
     - restoration of a previously missing live queue field;
     - calibrated risk increase of at least about **0.08** after segment adjustment;
     - sharply shrinking SLA runway where human review judges the alert credible.
   - If no material change occurs, the alert enters cool-down and is treated as alert fatigue, not as a new intervention opportunity.

3. **Calibration and probability robustification**
   - `p_now` is an **observational breach probability**, not the probability under intervention. It is used for alert credibility and urgency, not directly as the causal effect of the swarm.
   - Segment adjustments used for decision support:
     - **SME**: add approximately **+0.065** to `p_now` as a central adjustment, with an uncertainty range of +0.04 to +0.09.
     - **Strategic Enterprise**: subtract approximately **0.085**, with an uncertainty range of 0.05 to 0.12.
     - **Public Sector**: no central adjustment, but sample size is moderate, so use moderate confidence.
     - **Non-Native-Language Support**: treat probability as an interval, roughly `p_now ± 0.10`, and require human review plus language-capable support before intervention.
     - **Imputed critical features**: down-weight the prediction and require validation before spending a scarce slot.

4. **Expected direct benefit gate**
   For each case, compute approximate direct net benefit using the supplied effect ranges, but treat them as uncertain plausible intervals, not known causal effects:

   \[
   \text{Net direct benefit} \approx \text{ARR} \times \text{Value of avoiding breach} - €450 - \text{False-alarm burden}
   \]

   I use two versions:
   - **Lower-bound net benefit**: uses the lower end of the ARR range. This is the safety gate.
   - **Midpoint net benefit**: used for ranking, after confidence weighting.

   The listed false-alarm burden is deducted conservatively as an expected customer-disruption cost. If better data become available, this can later be replaced by probability-weighted false-alarm cost.

5. **Prioritization score**
   A practical score is:

   \[
   \text{Score}_i = w_i \times \text{Midpoint net benefit}_i
   \]

   where \(w_i\) is a confidence multiplier reflecting segment calibration, data quality, and alert evidence. Example weights:
   - complete data / acceptable calibration: 1.0;
   - Strategic Enterprise after workflow redesign: 0.85;
   - Non-Native-Language Support: 0.8;
   - imputed critical feature: 0.6;
   - repeated unchanged alert: 0.2 or suppression.

   The score is not the only rule. A case must also pass the lower-bound safety gate and be operationally eligible.

6. **Capacity, spillover, and reserve rule**
   - Four swarm slots remain, but one slot is reserved for a possible regulatory incident before 15:00 unless the expected cost of reserving becomes clearly excessive.
   - Each used slot creates queue externalities. The marginal spillover harm is approximated as:

     \[
     \text{Spillover per slot} \approx 6 \times \Delta p \times \bar{V}
     \]

     where \(\Delta p\) is the added breach probability for each untreated diagnosis-stage case and \(\bar{V}\) is the average exposure of those untreated cases.
   - For current planning, I assume six untreated diagnosis-stage cases and use a conservative average exposure proxy of about **€20,000**.
     - Low spillover: \(6 \times 0.01 \times €20,000 = €1,200\) per slot.
     - Base spillover: \(6 \times 0.02 \times €20,000 = €2,400\) per slot.
     - High spillover: \(6 \times 0.03 \times €20,000 = €3,600\) per slot.
   - The first three slots are allocated under a portfolio cap, but a fourth slot would need to clear a higher shadow-price bar because it would consume the reserve and likely add spillover harm exceeding the benefit of the remaining marginal cases.

7. **Tie-breakers and non-monetary obligations**
   If several cases have similar robust scores:
   - prefer the case with the nearest SLA deadline if the swarm can still act in time;
   - prefer the case with the stronger lower-bound net benefit;
   - avoid systematic exclusion of low-value, uncertain, or protected-segment cases;

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Repeated alerts are expected because the model refreshes every 30 minutes. The policy must distinguish **persistence due to unchanged model scoring** from **persistence due to genuinely new evidence**.

**Cool-down**
- After an alert is shown, suppress automatic swarm recommendations for the same case for **4 hours** unless a material new event occurs.
- If a swarm has already been performed, suppress repeat swarm recommendations for **8 hours** unless there is a material new event and the expected remaining benefit still exceeds cost and spillover.

**Suppression**
- Suppress duplicate alerts when all of the following are true:
  - no new customer artifact or log;
  - no stage change or process blockage;
  - no restored missing feature;
  - calibrated probability change is small, e.g. less than 0.05;
  - no major SLA deterioration.
- Suppressed alerts are still logged for monitoring, because repeated suppression may reveal model instability.

**Escalation**
- If an alert is suppressed but calibrated risk remains high and SLA time falls below about **2 hours**, route the case to a human dispatcher or queue master rather than automatically deploying a swarm.
- Human override is allowed but must be logged with a reason, because overrides affect future calibration and fairness monitoring.

**Re-entry**
- A case re-enters the eligible pool when a material new event occurs.
- On re-entry, recompute:
  - calibrated probability;
  - ARR interval;
  - lower-bound and midpoint net benefit;
  - data-quality weight;
  - language-support or other operational constraints.

**I-05 versus I-07**

- **I-05**:
  - Third alert.
  - No new event since previous alert.
  - Risk change is small.
  - SME underprediction raises concern, but the alert itself does not contain new evidence.
  - Policy action: **suppress / do not intervene now**, keep in cool-down, and monitor for a material event.

- **I-07**:
  - Third alert, but a **new customer diagnostic log** has been uploaded.
  - The risk estimate is slightly lower, but the evidence base has materially changed.
  - Policy action: **allow re-entry and re-score**.
  - It is not selected for a slot under the current portfolio because its direct net benefit is small relative to spillover and capacity scarcity, but it is treated as evidentially valid, unlike I-05.

This distinction prevents alert fatigue while still allowing genuinely new information to change the decision.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases**
- Risk:
  - A pure value-weighted rule would repeatedly favor large Strategic Enterprise cases and deprioritize SME or lower-value cases.
- Safeguards:
  - Use expected net benefit, not raw contract value alone.
  - Maintain a minimum evidence standard: low-value cases are not denied merely because value is low; they must fail the net-benefit, stage, or evidence gate.
  - Track intervention rates by value tier.
  - If eligible lower-value cases with positive lower-bound net benefit are repeatedly denied because of spillover, consider releasing one slot under a fairness exception or providing a lower-cost manual diagnostic action.
  - In the current portfolio, I-07 and I-10 are not funded because of scarcity and spillover, but they remain eligible for re-evaluation and should receive low-cost manual review where possible.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty**
- Risk:
  - I-09 could be under-served because validation data and intervention-effect estimates are sparse.
- Safeguards:
  - Do not exclude a case solely because of sparse segment data.
  - Assign a language-capable specialist or interpreter to the swarm.
  - Use wider uncertainty intervals and human review, but still allow intervention when lower-bound net benefit is positive.
  - Collect NLS-specific outcome and effect data to reduce future uncertainty.

**Differential calibration across segments**
- Risk:
  - SME cases may be under-triaged if raw probabilities are used.
  - Strategic Enterprise cases may be over-triaged if post-workflow-redesign overprediction is ignored.
  - Public Sector estimates may be acceptable but still based on moderate sample size.
- Safeguards:
  - Use segment-specific calibration adjustments.
  - Use interval-based probabilities for uncertain segments.
  - Pause automatic deployment for a segment if calibration error becomes large and persistent.
  - Recalibrate separately by segment rather than using one global model correction.

**Selective intervention changes future training labels**
- Risk:
  - Intervened cases may avoid breach, making the model think the original high risk was wrong.
  - Untreated cases may breach more often because of spillover, making them appear riskier.
  - Naive retraining can amplify policy feedback loops.
- Safeguards:
  - Log intervention status, timing, slot usage, specialist interruption, and queue delay.
  - Use causal estimators rather than simple supervised learning on post-intervention outcomes.
  - When capacity allows, randomize among marginal cases to obtain cleaner effect estimates.
  - Use inverse-probability weighting, target-trial emulation, or censoring methods when retraining.
  - Keep a distinction between observational breach risk and post-intervention outcomes.

**Spillover harms may fall systematically on untreated groups**
- Risk:
  - Swarms for high-value cases delay diagnosis for other cases.
  - If those harmed cases are lower-value, Public Sector, SME, or language-support cases, the policy can become regressive.
- Safeguards:
  - Monitor queue delay and breach rates among untreated diagnosis-stage cases by segment, value tier, and language-support status.
  - Include spillover harm in the allocation shadow price.
  - If spillover harm concentrates in a protected or historically underserved group, reduce slot usage, increase reserve, or temporarily rebalance allocation.
  - Consider load-leveling specialist capacity so that swarms do not always interrupt the same queue or shift.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
Track daily:
- swarm slots used;
- reserve slot usage and regulatory arrivals;
- specialist interruption minutes;
- aggregate delay added to non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases;
- time in Triage, Diagnosis Started, Diagnosis Complete, Resolution Work, and Customer Validation;
- SLA breaches by intervened and untreated cases;
- swarm timing relative to Diagnosis Complete.

Alert thresholds:
- average added delay per used slot exceeds 20 minutes;
- untreated diagnosis-stage breach rate rises sharply after swarm periods;
- reserve slot is unavailable when a regulatory incident arrives.

**Predictive monitoring**
Track weekly:
- observed versus predicted breach probability by segment;
- Brier score and calibration slope;
- calibration for SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and Non-Native-Language Support;
- alert precision: proportion of alerts that would have breached without intervention;
- false-alarm rate;
- imputation rate for live queue features;
- feature drift and data-quality degradation.

Recalibration triggers:
- SME underprediction persists beyond +0.04;
- Strategic Enterprise overprediction persists beyond 0.05;
- Public Sector calibration deteriorates materially;
- NLS interval coverage becomes unreliable.

**Causal-effect monitoring**
The supplied ARR ranges should not be treated as known causal effects. To estimate effects:
- Use marginal randomization when capacity is slack: randomly assign or withhold a swarm among near-threshold eligible cases, with governance approval.
- Use slot availability as an instrumental variable when randomization is not possible.
- Compare outcomes using difference-in-differences around swarm periods, controlling for queue congestion.
- Estimate ARR separately by:
  - stage;
  - segment;
  - language-support status;
  - time-to-SLA bucket;
  - data-quality status.
- Update ARR intervals using Bayesian updating or rolling confidence intervals.

Stop or shrink rule:
- If the lower credible interval of ARR for a segment or stage is near zero or negative, pause automatic swarm allocation for that group and require manual approval.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring**
Track:
- delay per used slot;
- number of untreated diagnosis-stage cases affected;
- breach probability change associated with concurrent swarms;
- congestion level at the time of swarm deployment.

Update the shadow price of a slot when:
- observed delay per slot changes;
- the number of untreated diagnosis-stage cases changes;
- average exposure of untreated cases changes;
- reserve usage patterns change.

Policy adjustment triggers:
- If reserve is unused for a sustained period and high-net-benefit cases are being denied, consider reducing reserve or adding capacity.
- If spillover harm repeatedly exceeds direct benefit, reduce the number of active slots or raise the allocation threshold.
- If adverse scenarios occur frequently, tighten the evidence gate and require stronger lower-bound net benefit.

**Fairness monitoring**
Monitor stratified metrics by:
- segment;
- language-support status;
- value tier;
- data-quality status;
- intervention status.

Key fairness metrics:
- intervention rate among eligible cases;
- breach rate after intervention;
- false-alarm burden by group;
- spillover delay experienced by untreated groups;
- net benefit distribution across segments.

Concrete safeguards:
- fairness dashboard reviewed weekly;
- disparity alerts if breach gaps widen by more than a pre-agreed margin;
- periodic audit of cases denied intervention;
- explicit review before any rule change that reduces intervention access for uncertain or low-value segments.

**Policy-induced drift and threshold changes**
Because intervention changes outcomes, the model and policy can create their own future training data. To detect policy-induced drift:
- maintain a log of intervention decisions and counterfactual reasoning;
- compare intervened cases with matched untreated cases;
- monitor whether high-risk alerts become systematically less predictive after intervention;
- avoid retraining directly on post-intervention breach labels without adjustment.

Change the allocation rule when:
- lower-bound net benefit of the marginal selected case is consistently below estimated spillover;
- calibration error is persistent and segment-specific;
- fairness metrics deteriorate;
- reserve adequacy changes;
- causal-effect estimates become materially narrower or wider.

The policy should be reviewed by an operational governance group, not changed automatically by a single metric. The decision rule, capacity cap, reserve preference, and fairness constraints should be changed together after reviewing operational, predictive, causal, and fairness evidence.
