## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

In March, fitness rises from 0.55 on raw data to 0.90 after the supplied corrections: a 0.35-point recovery. Against the January–February reference of 0.925, this restores about 93% of the apparent decline; against perfect fitness, it removes about 78% of the raw deficit. Neither number is the proportion of compliant cases, nor a causal attribution percentage. 

The large gap, simultaneous label expansion, and almost unchanged normalized March DFG strongly support semantic/provenance drift. However, mapping shows that two recorded labels can be treated as one activity under the chosen abstraction; it does not establish identical eligibility criteria, execution effort, routing decisions, or customer outcomes.  

Duplicated technical events can inflate event counts, create spurious transitions and variants, and penalize alignments. Remove only provenance-verified duplicates, retaining an immutable raw view. Missing resource identifiers chiefly compromise organizational networks, workload estimates, and resource-based segmentation; their non-random concentration makes complete-case resource analysis potentially biased. They do not automatically invalidate an activity-only DFG.  

After May, corrected information-request and document-return rates rise substantially, while cycle-time tails and reconstructed assessment waiting worsen. Those signals cannot reasonably be dismissed as the known eligibility rename alone. Nevertheless, the operational variant definitions and timestamp lineage require auditing because only the mapped-fitness series was explicitly normalized. More frequent use of an allowed rework loop is behavioral-frequency drift, not necessarily noncompliance; residual fitness loss may also reflect model incompleteness or case mix.

**Normalized DFG distance.** Let \(r_{t,e}\) be the supplied occurrences per 100 cases for relation category \(e\). For the same seven categories, define:

$$
L_1(t,s)=\sum_{e=1}^{7}|r_{t,e}-r_{s,e}|,\qquad
d(t,s)=\frac{L_1(t,s)}{7}.
$$

Here, \(d\) is the mean absolute relation-rate difference, measured in **occurrences per 100 cases per relation category**. It is not total variation between probability distributions: the rates need not sum to 100, and repeated occurrences are possible.

For a future distributional decomposition, let \(s\) denote intake strata and write:

$$
F_t(u)=\sum_s w_t(s)F_t(u\mid s).
$$

A descriptive composition counterfactual holds baseline conditional duration distributions fixed while replacing \(w_0(s)\) with \(w_t(s)\). Subsequent counterfactuals can replace routing, service, and waiting distributions. Recompute the cycle-time quantile after each replacement; compare alternative replacement orders to expose interactions. This is model-based standardization, not automatically causal identification.

Two sensitivity checks illustrate the ambiguity:

* **Supplier composition:** April-to-May rework can be reproduced mathematically with *unchanged* supplier-specific rates. Solving \(0.18a+0.82b=0.13\) and \(0.43a+0.57b=0.24\) gives \(a=49.08\%\) and \(b=5.08\%\). These are constructed counterexample values—not observed subgroup rates or a full-year explanation. They demonstrate that the aggregate rise alone cannot identify within-supplier behavioral change.
* **Senior assessment:** Assuming only high-complexity cases receive one senior assessment and their conditional escalation probability is unchanged, September’s rate predicts \(7\times41/26\approx11\) occurrences per 100 October cases, versus the observed 22. Under those assumptions, coarse complexity share alone is insufficient; finer mix, changed routing, or changed recording is needed.

## 6. Segmentation plan

Use a hierarchy rather than the full Cartesian product of supplier, complexity, priority, team, region, source, and month. **Proposed support floors:** approximately 100 unique cases per comparison arm for broad distribution/rate monitoring, 20 supporting cases for a motif-specific inference, and 200 completed cases for a standalone p90 estimate. These are practical starting points, not calibrated power guarantees.

Pool adjacent periods **within the same regime**; do not pool across the breakpoint under investigation. When support remains inadequate, use a parent-level or partially pooled estimate and explicitly mark the subgroup as uncertain.

**Primary monitoring population:** all eligible registrations in comparable follow-up cohorts, represented with canonical labels but retaining raw provenance, unknown-resource cases, and unresolved cases. Publish both the **actual portfolio experience** and an **intake-mix-standardized comparison**. Use supplier and complexity strata as the first diagnostic drill-down, not as exclusions from the primary population.

Live queue monitoring is a companion population: all currently ready or waiting episodes, including cases not yet closed. This prevents a completed-case dashboard from appearing to improve merely because the slowest cases remain unfinished.

**Seasonality.** Maintain a frozen early-year comparator so sustained deterioration is not absorbed into a rolling “normal.” Add same-season historical references once comparable prior-year logs are available. Until then, annotate renewal periods and standardize observable mix, but do not fit a confident annual seasonal pattern from one cycle. December should not automatically become the new baseline merely because it improved.

**Multiple testing.** Organize alerts into schema/provenance, behavior, time, population/organization, and outcome families. Gate exploratory subgroup testing behind a family-level signal where appropriate; control false discovery within diagnostic batches. Calibrate repeated sequential monitoring separately—batch false-discovery control alone does not address repeated looks. Resample cases and appropriate time/team blocks rather than treating repeated events as independent. Consolidate correlated alerts into one incident, while retaining immediate escalation for verified critical invariants.

**Outcomes are a monitoring requirement, not an observed drift finding:** the supplied panel does not provide a longitudinal outcome series sufficient to diagnose outcome deterioration.

The documented interfaces support the formatting, filtering, DFG, variant, temporal-profile, and optional skeleton operations used below. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

The event-level functions select **registration cohorts**, then retain their traces across month boundaries with a common follow-up horizon. They do not discover monthly process models from arbitrarily clipped event fragments. Cases without `Close` by that horizon are counted explicitly; completed-case artifacts remain conditional on closure and must be accompanied by unresolved-case analysis.

PM4Py temporal profiles describe time differences between eventually-following activity pairs, not pure queue waiting. Queue and service attribution requires separately validated readiness and lifecycle data. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

def require(condition: bool, message: str) -> None:
    if not condition:
        raise ValueError(message)


def aggregate_analysis() -> dict[str, Any]:
    m = pd.read_csv(StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV)).set_index("month")
    r = pd.read_csv(StringIO(DFG_CSV)).set_index("month")
    for name, frame in (("monthly", m), ("DFG", r)):
        require(frame.index.is_unique, f"Duplicate {name} months")
        require(frame.index.is_monotonic_increasing, f"Unsorted {name}")
        require(np.isfinite(frame.to_numpy(dtype=float)).all(),
                f"Missing/nonfinite {name} values")
    require(list(m.index) == list(pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-12", freq="M").astype(str)),
            "Expected all 12 supplied months")
    require(r.index.isin(m.index).all(), "Unmatched DFG month")
    for c in ("cases", "distinct_activity_labels"):
        require(((m[c] > 0) & (m[c] % 1 == 0)).all(), f"Invalid {c}")
    for c in m.filter(like="_pct").columns:
        require(m[c].between(0, 100).all(), f"Invalid percentage: {c}")
    for c in ("raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"):
        require(m[c].between(0, 1).all(), f"Invalid fitness: {c}")
    require((m.mapped_model_fitness >= m.raw_model_fitness).all(),
            "Unexpected recovery direction in the supplied panel")
    require(((m.median_cycle_h >= 0) & (m.p90_cycle_h >= m.median_cycle_h)).all(),
            "Invalid cycle-time quantiles")
    require((r >= 0).all().all(), "Negative DFG rate")
    # Occurrences/100 cases may exceed 100; they are NOT probabilities.
    # Do not require the selected variant percentages to sum to 100.

    baseline = m.loc[["2026-01", "2026-02"]].mean()
    delta = m.sub(baseline)  # percentage columns: percentage-point changes
    relative = 100 * delta.div(baseline.replace(0, np.nan))
    differences = r.sub(r.loc["2026-01"]).abs()
    distance = pd.DataFrame({
        "L1_from_January": differences.sum(axis=1),
        "MAE_from_January": differences.mean(axis=1),
        "L1_from_previous_observed": r.diff().abs().sum(axis=1, min_count=1),
        "MAE_from_previous_observed": r.diff().abs().mean(axis=1),
    })
    gap = m.mapped_model_fitness - m.raw_model_fitness
    recovery = pd.DataFrame({
        "fitness_gap": gap,
        "fraction_raw_deficit_removed": gap / (1 - m.raw_model_fitness),
        "fraction_baseline_loss_recovered": gap / (
            baseline.raw_model_fitness - m.raw_model_fitness
        ).where(m.raw_model_fitness < baseline.raw_model_fitness),
    })

    # Proposed screening thresholds, not fitted change-point tests.
    signals = m.assign(fitness_gap=gap)
    rules = {
        "logging": {"raw_model_fitness": 0.10, "fitness_gap": 0.04,
                    "missing_resource_pct": 0.5,
                    "duplicate_open_case_pct": 2.0,
                    "distinct_activity_labels": 1.0},
        "behavior_or_mix": {"rework_variant_pct": 5.0, "fast_variant_pct": 10.0},
        "performance": {"median_cycle_h": 3.0, "p90_cycle_h": 10.0},
        "population": {"high_complexity_pct": 10.0, "supplier_z_pct": 10.0},
    }
    changes = signals.diff()
    flags = []
    for i in range(1, len(signals)):
        for dimension, thresholds in rules.items():
            hit = [f"{c}: {changes.iloc[i][c]:+.3f}"
                   for c, threshold in thresholds.items()
                   if abs(changes.iloc[i][c]) + 1e-12 >= threshold]
            if hit:
                flags.append({"interval": f"{signals.index[i-1]} -> {signals.index[i]}",
                              "dimension": dimension, "trigger": "; ".join(hit)})
    for i in range(1, len(r)):
        value = distance.iloc[i]["MAE_from_previous_observed"]
        if value >= 4:
            flags.append({"interval": f"{r.index[i-1]} -> {r.index[i]}",
                          "dimension": "normalized_DFG_or_mix",
                          "trigger": f"fragment MAE={value:.3f}; sparse interval"})

    for title, table in (("Baseline: unweighted mean of Jan/Feb summaries", baseline),
                         ("Baseline-relative changes, original units", delta),
                         ("Relative changes (%)", relative),
                         ("DFG distances, occurrences/100 cases", distance),
                         ("Fitness recovery", recovery),
                         ("Candidate intervals, including improvements", pd.DataFrame(flags))):
        print("\n" + title + "\n" + table.round(4).to_string())
    print("\nCAVEATS: Omitted DFG edges are unknown, not zero. Rates count occurrences, "
          "not unique cases. DFG dates are sparse. Operational variant definitions "
          "and monthly cohort/denominator definitions need auditing. Monthly medians "
          "cannot be pooled or additively decomposed. Flags are descriptive screens, "
          "not significance tests or causal attribution; gradual drift can evade them.")
    return {"monthly": m, "rates": r, "baseline": baseline, "delta": delta,
            "distance": distance, "recovery": recovery, "flags": pd.DataFrame(flags)}


# ========== PART B: reusable functions; no event log is fabricated ==========
# PM4Py is imported lazily: Part A runs without it.
# Input: one row per activity occurrence (paired start/completion if available),
# not an unresolved mixture of start and complete lifecycle events.
CASE, ACT, TIME = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
KEYS = dict(case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TIME)


def pm_module():
    try:
        import pm4py
    except ImportError as exc:
        raise ImportError("Part B requires PM4Py; Part A does not.") from exc
    return pm4py


def as_utc(value, source_timezone: str | None = None) -> pd.Timestamp:
    t = pd.Timestamp(value)
    require(not pd.isna(t), "Missing timestamp")
    if t.tzinfo is None:
        require(source_timezone is not None, "Naive timestamp needs an explicit source timezone")
        t = t.tz_localize(source_timezone, ambiguous="raise", nonexistent="raise")
    return t.tz_convert("UTC")


def prepare_events(df: pd.DataFrame, *, source_timezone: str | None = None,
                   sequence_key: str | None = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    pm = pm_module()
    d = df.copy(deep=True)
    require({CASE, ACT, TIME}.issubset(d.columns), "Missing PM4Py columns")
    require(not d[[CASE, ACT, TIME]].isna().any().any(), "Missing case/activity/time")
    for c in (CASE, ACT):
        require(d[c].map(lambda x: isinstance(x, str) and bool(x.strip())).all(),
                f"{c} must contain nonempty strings")
    for c in (TIME, "start_timestamp"):
        if c in d:
            d[c] = pd.to_datetime([as_utc(x, source_timezone) for x in d[c]], utc=True)
    if "start_timestamp" in d:
        require((d["start_timestamp"] <= d[TIME]).all(), "Negative service interval")
    if "lifecycle:transition" in d:
        require(d["lifecycle:transition"].eq("complete").all(),
                "Pair lifecycle events before this analysis; do not count starts as rework")
    order = [CASE, TIME]
    if sequence_key is not None:
        require(sequence_key in d and not d[sequence_key].isna().any(), "Invalid source sequence")
        order.append(sequence_key)
    require(not d.duplicated(order).any(),
            "Unresolved timestamp ties: supply a trustworthy source sequence")
    d = d.sort_values(order, kind="mergesort").reset_index(drop=True)
    if "activity_raw" not in d:
        d["activity_raw"] = d[ACT]
    return pm.format_dataframe(d, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TIME)


def normalized_copy(raw: pd.DataFrame, *, duplicate_flag: str | None = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    d = raw.copy(deep=True)
    d[ACT] = d[ACT].replace({"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"})
    if duplicate_flag is None:
        warnings.warn("No verified duplicate flag: label-only normalization; Open Case retained.")
    else:
        require(duplicate_flag in d, "Duplicate flag column missing")
        require(pd.api.types.is_bool_dtype(d[duplicate_flag]), "Duplicate flag must be Boolean")
        drop = d[duplicate_flag].fillna(False)
        require(d.loc[drop, ACT].eq("Open Case").all(),
                "Removal is restricted to verified technical Open Case duplicates")
        d = d.loc[~drop].copy()
    d["normalization_version"] = "eligibility-v1/verified-technical-duplicates"
    return d


def arrival_window(log: pd.DataFrame, start, end, *, as_of,
                   horizon_days: float) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, dict[str, Any]]:
    """Register-anchored [start,end) cohort; equal follow-up; report unclosed cases."""
    pm = pm_module()
    start, end, as_of = map(as_utc, (start, end, as_of))
    require(start < end and horizon_days > 0, "Invalid window or follow-up horizon")
    horizon = pd.Timedelta(days=horizon_days)
    require(end + horizon <= as_of, "Entire arrival window must have mature follow-up")
    d = log.loc[log[TIME] <= as_of].copy()
    anchors = (d.loc[d[ACT].eq("Register")].sort_values(TIME, kind="mergesort")
               .drop_duplicates(CASE))
    if anchors.empty:
        return d.iloc[:0].copy(), {"registered": 0, "closed_by_horizon": 0,
                                   "unclosed_by_horizon": 0, "case_ids": []}
    selected = pm.filter_time_range(
        anchors, start.isoformat(), end.isoformat(), mode="events",
        timestamp_key=TIME, case_id_key=CASE)
    # Explicit half-open guard avoids double assignment at month boundaries.
    selected = selected.loc[(selected[TIME] >= start) & (selected[TIME] < end)]
    deadline = selected.set_index(CASE)[TIME] + horizon
    cohort = d.loc[d[CASE].isin(deadline.index)].copy()
    cohort = cohort.loc[cohort[TIME] <= cohort[CASE].map(deadline)].copy()
    closed = cohort.loc[cohort[ACT].eq("Close"), CASE].unique()
    complete = cohort.loc[cohort[CASE].isin(closed)].copy()
    metadata = {"registered": len(selected), "closed_by_horizon": len(closed),
                "unclosed_by_horizon": len(selected) - len(closed),
                "case_ids": sorted(selected[CASE]),
                "warning": "Artifacts condition on Close by horizon; analyze unclosed cases separately."}
    return complete, metadata


def discover_artifacts(log: pd.DataFrame, *, skeleton: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    if log.empty:
        return {"n_cases": 0}
    pm = pm_module()
    dfg, starts, ends = pm.discover_dfg(log, **KEYS)
    n = log[CASE].nunique()
    out = {"n_cases": n, "dfg": dfg, "starts": starts, "ends": ends,
           "dfg_per_100_cases": {edge: 100 * count / n for edge, count in dfg.items()},
           "variants": pm.get_variants(log, **KEYS),
           "temporal_profile": pm.discover_temporal_profile(log, **KEYS)}
    if skeleton:
        out["log_skeleton"] = pm.discover_log_skeleton(log, noise_threshold=0.05, **KEYS)
    return out


def compare_monthly_views(df: pd.DataFrame, months: list[str], *, as_of,
                          calendar_timezone: str, horizon_days: float,
                          source_timezone: str | None = None,
                          sequence_key: str | None = None,
                          duplicate_flag: str | None = None,
                          skeleton: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    raw = prepare_events(df, source_timezone=source_timezone, sequence_key=sequence_key)
    normalized = normalized_copy(raw, duplicate_flag=duplicate_flag)
    result = {"pm4py_version": pm_module().__version__, "months": {}}
    for month in months:
        start = pd.Timestamp(f"{month}-01").tz_localize(calendar_timezone)
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        pair = {}
        for name, log in (("raw", raw), ("normalized", normalized)):
            window, metadata = arrival_window(log, start, end, as_of=as_of,
                                              horizon_days=horizon_days)
            pair[name] = {"cohort": metadata,
                          "artifacts": discover_artifacts(window, skeleton=skeleton)}
        require(pair["raw"]["cohort"]["case_ids"] == pair["normalized"]["cohort"]["case_ids"],
                "Raw/normalized populations differ")
        result["months"][month] = pair
    # Input df is untouched. Resource missingness is retained, not listwise deleted.
    return result


if __name__ == "__main__":
    aggregate_analysis()

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is the March raw-fitness collapse to migration-related semantic and provenance changes: confidence is very high. Label replacement, technical duplication, increased missingness, substantial fitness recovery, and near-stable normalized adjacency agree. This does not establish that every business rule remained unchanged; it identifies the dominant explanation for this particular measurement discontinuity.  

Supplier disruption is a high-confidence contributor to the May–summer deterioration, but confidence is only moderate that it increased rework within otherwise comparable Supplier Z cases. The disruption is documented, yet supplier exposure and complexity also change. Increased document-return traffic plausibly adds both external waiting and assessment demand. The aggregate series cannot divide those channels or distinguish them cleanly from population replacement.  

Staffing loss is a moderately high-confidence amplifier of June–August waiting. Assessment service time changes modestly while reconstructed waiting increases much more, and deterioration persists while supplier share starts declining. Nevertheless, May worsening predates the departures, staffing capacity is not quantified, and inherited backlog can explain delayed responses. Priority-driven redistribution is plausible, not demonstrated: no class-specific waiting trajectories or dispatch decisions are supplied.  

October–November strongly support seasonal population/load drift, with moderate confidence that altered senior-routing or recording contributes beyond coarse complexity composition. Historical renewal seasonality is stated, but this panel alone cannot establish recurrence. December provides clear evidence of aggregate improvement, not an identified overtime or campaign effect: lower load and changing mix occur simultaneously.   

Persistent behavioral adaptation remains a lower-confidence explanation for elevated December rework and senior assessment. Backlog, incomplete supplier recovery, finer case mix, and recording changes remain viable alternatives. Supplier-generated extra visits, reduced staffing, and seasonal demand can all mediate their effects through the same queue; assigning each the full waiting increase would double-count. 

I would revise this account if normalized, intake-standardized mature cohorts showed stable within-stratum rework; corrected ready/start timestamps eliminated the queue increase; audited senior-assessment records showed unchanged execution but improved labeling; or season-matched historical cohorts reproduced the autumn pattern without excess residual delay.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** raw fitness falls while mapped fitness, canonical business relations, and validated timing remain stable, **THEN** investigate schema, label mapping, and technical-event provenance before redesigning the business process, **BECAUSE** the observed discontinuity is primarily consistent with measurement semantics rather than changed execution.

2. **IF** information-request incidence or loop intensity rises within comparable supplier and intake-complexity strata, **THEN** audit document defects and introduce targeted supplier-quality remediation while monitoring added assessment demand, **BECAUSE** within-stratum rework provides stronger evidence of changed document handling than an aggregate supplier-share shift alone.

3. **IF** validated ready-to-start waiting and backlog age rise while conditional service durations remain approximately stable, **THEN** assess staffed capacity, repeated-visit workload, and temporary capacity options, **BECAUSE** the dominant proximate problem is queue congestion rather than slower execution of each assessment.

4. **IF** standard-case waiting or maximum queue age deteriorates after the priority change while high-complexity waiting improves or deteriorates less, **THEN** audit eligible-case dispatch decisions and consider an approved aging or waiting-time safeguard, **BECAUSE** prioritization can redistribute delay and create starvation without materially changing within-case activity sequences.

5. **IF** aggregate DFGs and cycle times change but intake-standardized, season-matched patterns remain stable, **THEN** classify the signal primarily as population/load drift and adjust workload planning rather than infer a new routing rule, **BECAUSE** a different mixture of existing case types can change portfolio behavior and performance.

6. **IF** organizational conclusions vary materially between resource-complete records and analyses retaining missing-resource cases, **THEN** suspend individual productivity judgments and repair or reconcile resource lineage, **BECAUSE** non-random missingness can manufacture apparent staffing, handover, and workload differences.

7. **IF** canonical within-stratum rework, senior-routing, or excess waiting remains elevated after supplier quality, backlog, and seasonal load have demonstrably normalized—or a verified mandatory-control violation appears at any time—**THEN** investigate persistent operational change and review the process model and controls, **BECAUSE** persistence supports more than a temporary mixture effect, while a genuine mandatory violation warrants action regardless of aggregate recovery.
