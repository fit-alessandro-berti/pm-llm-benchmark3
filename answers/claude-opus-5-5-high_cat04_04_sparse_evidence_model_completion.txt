Abbreviations used throughout: **R** = Register Order, **CC** = Credit Check, **ER** = Engineering Review, **SC** = Supplier Confirmation, **EX** = Expedite Review, **AC** = Approve Configuration, **RP** = Release Production, **AR** = Archive Case. Traces are written ⟨R, CC, …⟩. All code uses the full activity labels.

# 1. Evidence Ledger

Derived facts used below (all recomputed from the seven variants; the given DFG is consistent with them):

- CC completes before ER in 19/30 cases (V1, V3, V5, V6); ER completes before CC in 11/30 (V2, V4, V7).
- SC is present in 23/30 cases and absent in 7/30 (V3: 4, V4: 3).
- SC completes after CC in **23/23** SC cases, after ER in 21/23, and between CC and ER in 2/23 (V5). SC never completes before CC.
- Among CC-first cases with SC (15), SC slips before ER in 2; among ER-first cases with SC (8), SC slips before CC in 0.
- EX occurs in 2/30 cases, always after both CC and ER, once after SC (V6) and once before SC (V7). EX never occurs without SC, but with only 2 EX cases that fact carries almost no information.
- ⟨AC, RP, AR⟩ is the closing sequence in 30/30 cases. No activity repeats in any trace.

**Why a two-way DFG edge does not prove concurrency.** CC→ER (17) and ER→CC (11) are both observed, but at least five processes produce exactly this pattern:

1. True concurrency with overlapping execution (S2).
2. Sequential execution in a varying order, for example chosen by whoever is free. This is interleaving without overlap, and its trace language is the same as concurrency.
3. A strict CC→ER sequence (S1) plus tie artifacts: if CC and ER often share a completion timestamp (batch closing), the ingestion index decides the order (S7).
4. A mixture of subpopulations with different fixed orders, for example by product line.
5. Clock skew between the finance and engineering systems.

For atomic activities, the trace languages of "A ∥ B" and "A;B or B;A" are identical. Only start/complete lifecycle data, resource data, or knowledge of the tie structure can tell these hypotheses apart. The low heuristic-miner dependency value, (17−11)/(17+11+1) ≈ 0.21, is a symptom of order variability, not a proof of parallelism. The same caution applies, even more strongly, to ER↔SC (13/2) and SC↔EX (1/1).

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. **Completion semantics.** Each event is the completion of an atomic activity instance. An edge A→B in any model means "B completes after A completes". It does not mean "B starts after A ends". The absence of an edge means the models make no claim about completion order; it does not assert simultaneous execution (D7).
2. **Tie handling.** Orders produced by ties (S7) cannot be identified in the data we have, so they are treated like real orders. This is the main threat to the CC/ER and SC-position conclusions.
3. **Case notion and repetition.** One order is one case. Every activity occurs at most once per case, because no repetition was observed. The possible reading of S3 as one SC per external component is not modeled.
4. **Fixed frame (all models).** R is the unique start and AR the unique end (D1). The case closes with AC→RP→AR (D2, 30/30). CC and ER are mandatory and precede AC (D3). SC, when present, precedes AC (D4). EX, when present, follows both checks and precedes AC (D6/S6).
5. **Missing SC events.** Events are not imputed. A case without SC is modeled by the τ-branch of XOR(SC, τ), which deliberately merges three causes: a legitimate stocked skip (S4), a lost event (S5), and a skip that violates S3. As a result, a fitting SC-free trace is not evidence of compliance with S3. This control-flow abstraction stands in for a data condition.
6. **Missing external/stocked attribute.** Without the attribute, the SC choice is an unguarded, non-deterministic XOR. No model contains data guards.
7. **How optionality is represented.** In the ProcessTree it is `X(a, tau)`. In POWL it is `OperatorPOWL(XOR, [Transition(a), SilentTransition()])`. In the conservative enumeration, optionality appears implicitly as branches that lack the activity.
8. **Noise.** All 30 traces are treated as legitimate behavior, so every model targets fitness 1.0.
9. **Control-flow only.** The models are not stochastic. Frequencies inform the design (Section 1) but are not encoded.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The conservative candidate assumes that the sample is the process. It admits exactly the seven observed variants and nothing else, so its precision is maximal and its generalization is zero. The high-confidence rules D1 and D2 hold automatically because every observed trace satisfies them. The model contains no abstraction of concurrency or optionality: every order and every omission it allows has been seen at least once. Its main value is as a novelty detector and as a precision baseline. It encodes the belief "anything unseen is suspicious until proven otherwise", which is appropriate when false acceptance is costlier than false alarms.

**Compact formal expression** (a prefix-factored XOR enumeration, language-equivalent to the flat 7-branch enumeration):

```text
->( R,
    X( ->( CC, X( ->( ER, X( τ, SC, ->(SC, EX) ) ),
                  ->( SC, ER ) ) ),
       ->( ER, CC, X( τ, SC, ->(EX, SC) ) ) ),
    AC, RP, AR )

L(model_conservative) = {V1, …, V7}   (7 traces, lengths 6–8, finite)
```

**Duplicate labels and scalability.** The flat form `->(R, X(σ1,…,σ7), AC, RP, AR)` has 25 visible leaves for 8 labels: CC and ER appear 7 times each, SC 5 times, EX twice. The prefix-factored form above cuts this to 16 visible leaves (CC×2, ER×3, SC×4, EX×2) without changing the language. The costs of this design are:

- Duplicate labels make alignments ambiguous, because several transitions can explain the same event.
- Token-based replay handles duplicate labels only heuristically.
- Generalization metrics drop, because each copy fires rarely.
- A BPMN rendering would be unreadable.

The model scales with the number of variants, and variants grow combinatorially: k unordered activities give k! interleavings, and adding optional ones multiplies that further. Every new legitimate variant also requires a manual model change. The Good–Turing estimate of unseen-variant mass is n₁/N = 2/30 ≈ 7%, where n₁ is the number of variants seen once. So roughly 5–10% of future legitimate cases can be expected to fall outside this model, before even counting cases with missing events.

**PM4Py code**

```python
import pm4py

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "X( ->( 'Credit Check', "
    "X( ->( 'Engineering Review', X( tau, 'Supplier Confirmation', "
    "->( 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', X( tau, 'Supplier Confirmation', "
    "->( 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ) ) ) ), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"

```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- Every unseen interleaving, including ⟨…ER, SC, CC…⟩, ⟨…CC, ER, EX, SC…⟩, ⟨…CC, SC, ER, EX…⟩ and ⟨…ER, CC, SC, EX…⟩.
- EX without SC, e.g. ⟨R, CC, ER, EX, AC, RP, AR⟩.
- SC completing before CC, and SC completing before both checks.
- Any repetition of an activity, and any violation of D1–D6.

**Known underfitting risks.** Strictly speaking, this model cannot underfit: it admits nothing beyond the data. Its risks are the overfitting dual, meaning it fails to cover legitimate behavior:

- It rejects legitimate but unseen interleavings (about 7% unseen-variant mass, estimated with high variance).
- It is not robust to missing SC. If SC is lost from a V6 or V7 case, the result ⟨R, CC, ER, EX, …⟩ or ⟨R, ER, CC, EX, …⟩ does not fit. Losing SC from V1, V2 or V5 happens to produce V3 or V4, which fit.
- It is structurally misleading. The early XOR suggests a decision made right after R, but no such decision exists in the business. It also expresses none of the dependencies in D3–D6, so it explains nothing.
- The two EX branches rest on singletons, so the model's EX behavior is anecdotal.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The balanced candidate generalizes only where the data and at least one medium-or-better rule agree, and keeps an order wherever the data show no counterexample and no rule explicitly permits one:

- CC and ER are unordered, because both completion orders are frequent and S2 supports this.
- SC is optional, because 7/30 absences are more than missingness alone would explain, and S4 supports this.
- EX is optional and placed after both checks (D6/S6), unordered with SC (S6, V6/V7).
- SC must complete after CC, because this held in 23/23 SC cases and D5 is only low–medium.

The model prefers under-claiming causality to over-claiming it. Where the rules are uncertain, it follows the data. Its extra behavior consists only of unseen combinations of independently observed features.

**Compact formal expression** (POWL; ≺ denotes the transitive closure of the listed edges):

```text
PO( V = { R, CC, ER, ×(SC, τ), ×(EX, τ), AC, RP, AR },
    ≺ = { R→CC, R→ER, CC→SC?, CC→EX?, ER→EX?,
          CC→AC, ER→AC, SC?→AC, EX?→AC, AC→RP, RP→AR } )

Declarative reading: init(R), end(AR), exactly1(CC, ER, AC, RP), atmost1(SC, EX),
precedence(CC, SC), precedence(CC, EX), precedence(ER, EX), {CC, ER, SC, EX} before AC.
|L(model_balanced)| = 12 (7 observed + 5 unobserved), finite.
```

**PM4Py code**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Transition(label=label), SilentTransition()])


def strict_partial_order(nodes, edges):
    """nodes: dict key -> POWL node; edges: (key, key) pairs. Adds the transitive closure."""
    keys = list(nodes)
    reach = set(edges)
    for k in keys:
        for i in keys:
            if (i, k) in reach:
                for j in keys:
                    if (k, j) in reach:
                        reach.add((i, j))
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[nodes[k] for k in keys])
    for a, b in reach:
        po.order.add_edge(nodes[a], nodes[b])
    return po


nodes = {
    "R": Transition(label="Register Order"),
    "CC": Transition(label="Credit Check"),
    "ER": Transition(label="Engineering Review"),
    "SC?": optional("Supplier Confirmation"),
    "EX?": optional("Expedite Review"),
    "AC": Transition(label="Approve Configuration"),
    "RP": Transition(label="Release Production"),
    "AR": Transition(label="Archive Case"),
}
edges = [
    ("R", "CC"), ("R", "ER"),
    ("CC", "SC?"),
    ("CC", "EX?"), ("ER", "EX?"),
    ("CC", "AC"), ("ER", "AC"), ("SC?", "AC"), ("EX?", "AC"),
    ("AC", "RP"), ("RP", "AR"),
]
model_balanced = strict_partial_order(nodes, edges)
```

**How sequence, concurrency, and partial order are decided.** For each pair of activities, the first rule below that applies decides:

1. A medium-or-better rule prescribes an order and the data contain no counterexample → **precedence edge**.
2. Both orders are observed, and the rarer order is supported by at least 2 cases or by an explicit rule → **no edge** (unordered). This means "no completion-order constraint", not "overlapping execution".
3. Only one order is observed and no rule explicitly permits the other → **keep the edge**, and state the power of the test.
4. A stakeholder claim contradicted by more than 10% of cases is treated as normative, not descriptive, and is not encoded.

| Pair | Data | Rules | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC – ER | 19 vs 11 | S1 (sequence) vs S2 (parallel), D7 | No edge | Rule 2; S1 is contradicted by 37% of cases (rule 4) |
| CC – SC | CC first 23/23 | D5 would allow SC before CC | CC→SC | Rule 3. The evidence is weak: under symmetric D5, P(0 of 8) ≈ 0.32. The permissive candidate carries the alternative |
| ER – SC | ER first 21, SC first 2 | D5 permits SC before ER | No edge | Rule 2 (2 cases plus an explicit rule) |
| CC, ER – EX | EX after both, 2/2 | D6, S6 | CC→EX, ER→EX | Rule 1 |
| SC – EX | 1 vs 1 | S6 explicitly permits both | No edge | Rule 2 |
| SC, EX – AC | 23/23, 2/2 | D4, D6 | Edges to AC | Rule 1 |
| SC existence | 7/30 absent | S3 vs S4, S5 | ×(SC, τ) | Absences exceed missingness; S4 |
| EX existence | 2/30 present | D6 | ×(EX, τ) | D6 |

**Why a partial order (POWL) and not a process tree.** The resulting order has CC→SC, CC→EX and ER→EX, with ER and SC unordered and SC and EX unordered. This is an "N" shape (ER→EX←CC→SC), which is not series-parallel. No duplicate-free process tree expresses it:

- ->(+(CC, ER), …) forces SC after ER, which loses V5.
- ->(CC, +(ER, SC), EX) forces EX after SC, which loses V7 and rejects ER-first cases.
- Anything looser, such as +(CC, ER, SC), over-generalizes.

POWL represents this generalization exactly, without duplicates.

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- SC completing before CC: ⟨…ER, SC, CC…⟩ and SC first.
- EX before either check completes.
- SC or EX after AC.
- Repetitions of any activity.
- Missing CC, ER, AC or RP.
- Any start other than R or end other than AR.

**Known underfitting risks**

- Five unobserved traces are admitted. Two of them have EX without SC. These are undesirable if EX is in fact triggered by supplier problems, in which case EX would require SC to exist.
- XOR(SC, τ) is unconditional, so S3 violations (external components without SC) are indistinguishable from stocked skips and data loss. The model is too permissive for S3 compliance.
- Leaving CC and ER unordered hides S1 violations if S1 is a binding norm.
- On the overfitting side, the CC→SC edge rests on 8 ER-first SC cases. It may reject legitimate ⟨…ER, SC, CC…⟩ traces (D5).

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The permissive candidate enforces only the broad, high-to-medium rules (D1–D4, D6/S6) and treats every order not forbidden by them as legitimate. It gives no weight to the low–medium D5 as a completion-order constraint, for two reasons. First, D5 speaks of SC starting once a check has "produced enough information", and that can happen before the check completes. Second, SC/CC ties ordered by ingestion index (S7) can put SC first in the log. So SC may complete anywhere between R and AC. The model maximizes robustness to unseen interleavings and to data defects, at the price of precision. Its underlying hypothesis is that the observed asymmetries come from the small sample, not from process logic.

**Compact formal expression** (series-parallel, so the POWL model below is language-equivalent to a duplicate-free process tree):

```text
->( R, +( ->( +(CC, ER), X(EX, τ) ), X(SC, τ) ), AC, RP, AR )

POWL: same nodes as the balanced candidate; edge CC→SC? replaced by R→SC?.
|L(model_permissive)| = 18 (7 observed + 11 unobserved), finite.
```

**Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behavior**

- *Reasonable, and therefore admitted:* any interleaving of the mandatory checks with the optional SC and the optional EX, provided that:
  - R is first;
  - CC and ER each occur exactly once before AC;
  - SC occurs at most once, before AC;
  - EX occurs at most once, after both checks and before AC;
  - AC→RP→AR closes the case.
- *Forbidden (hard):*
  - violations of D1 (start/end), D2 (RP without a preceding AC), D3 (AC before either check), D4 (SC after AC) and D6 (EX before a check, after AC, or twice);
  - repetition of any activity;
  - omission of CC, ER, AC or RP;
  - events after AR.

  These rules are high or medium confidence and are corroborated by 30/30 cases, so relaxing them would be speculation rather than generalization.
- *Deliberately not enforced:* S1 (contradicted by the data), S3 (the attribute is missing), and D5 as a completion-order rule.

**Intermediate sensitivity variant (optional).** A variant that enforces D5 literally, requiring SC to complete after at least one check, sits between the balanced and permissive candidates. It is not series-parallel as a single partial order, so it needs duplicated labels. Its language has 14 traces, and the two branches have disjoint languages. The XOR stands for "whichever check completes first", not for a business decision.

```text
->( R, X( ->( CC, +( ->(ER, X(EX, τ)), X(SC, τ) ) ),
          ->( ER, +( ->(CC, X(EX, τ)), X(SC, τ) ) ) ), AC, RP, AR )
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- EX before either check, EX twice, and EX after AC.
- SC after AC, and SC twice.
- AC before a check.
- RP without a preceding AC.
- Starts other than R and ends other than AR.

**Known underfitting risks**

- The model admits six traces in which SC completes before CC. Four of them have SC completing before both checks, which violates the literal reading of D5 and could hide premature supplier commitments, for example ordering from suppliers for customers who fail the credit check.
- It has the lowest precision of the three; this is quantified in Section 7.
- It cannot detect tie or ordering errors around SC.
- It inherits the balanced model's blind spots: SC optionality hides S3 violations, EX-without-SC is admitted, and S1 violations are invisible.

| Conservative | ⟨R, CC, ER, EX, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 4 (EX): after ⟨R, CC, ER⟩ only SC or AC is enabled. This trace is plausible if SC was lost, which shows the model's fragility |

| Conservative | ⟨R, CC, ER, EX, SC, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 4 (EX): the ordering EX-before-SC was only seen with ER first |
| Balanced | ⟨R, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 3 (SC): SC requires CC to have completed (CC→SC) |
| Balanced | ⟨R, CC, EX, ER, SC, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 3 (EX): EX requires ER (D6/S6) |
| Balanced | ⟨R, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 5 (SC): after AC fires, SC's τ-branch has been taken (D4) |
| Permissive | ⟨R, CC, EX, ER, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 3 (EX): D6 |
| Permissive | ⟨R, CC, ER, SC, RP, AC, AR⟩ | Position 5 (RP): D2 |
| Permissive | ⟨R, CC, SC, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 4 (AC): ER missing (D3) |
| Permissive | ⟨R, CC, ER, SC, SC, AC, RP, AR⟩ | Position 5 (second SC): at most one SC |

# 8. Validation Code

```python
"""
Validation harness for the three candidate models (pm4py >= 2.7, POWL support required).

Limitations:
- Extensive playout enumerates firing sequences up to a length bound. It is exhaustive here
  ONLY because every net is checked to be acyclic, which makes the language finite (max length 8).
  For models with loops, bounded or sampled playout cannot establish language inclusion or equality.
- Sampled playout is a sanity check, never a proof.
- Alignment precision (ETC) measures escaping behavior only along observed prefixes.
"""
from collections import Counter

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.petri_net import algorithm as pn_playout

R, CC, ER = "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review"
SC, EX = "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review"
AC, RP, AR = "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"

VARIANTS = [
    (12, [R, CC, ER, SC, AC, RP, AR]),
    (7, [R, ER, CC, SC, AC, RP, AR]),
    (4, [R, CC, ER, AC, RP, AR]),
    (3, [R, ER, CC, AC, RP, AR]),
    (2, [R, CC, SC, ER, AC, RP, AR]),
    (1, [R, CC, ER, SC, EX, AC, RP, AR]),
    (1, [R, ER, CC, EX, SC, AC, RP, AR]),
]
OBSERVED = {tuple(t) for _, t in VARIANTS}

EXPECTED_DFG = {
    (R, CC): 19, (R, ER): 11, (CC, ER): 17, (ER, CC): 11,
    (CC, SC): 9, (ER, SC): 13, (SC, ER): 2,
    (ER, AC): 6, (CC, AC): 3, (SC, AC): 20,
    (SC, EX): 1, (CC, EX): 1, (EX, SC): 1, (EX, AC): 1,
    (AC, RP): 30, (RP, AR): 30,
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. log
log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(t) for n, t in VARIANTS for _ in range(n)])
assert log["case:concept:name"].nunique() == 30
dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
assert dict(dfg) == EXPECTED_DFG, "reconstructed DFG differs from the one given"
assert dict(starts) == {R: 30} and dict(ends) == {AR: 30}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- models
def q(label):
    return "'" + label + "'"


def build_conservative():
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        f"->( {q(R)}, X( ->( {q(CC)}, X( ->( {q(ER)}, X( tau, {q(SC)}, ->( {q(SC)}, {q(EX)} ) ) ), "
        f"->( {q(SC)}, {q(ER)} ) ) ), ->( {q(ER)}, {q(CC)}, X( tau, {q(SC)}, ->( {q(EX)}, {q(SC)} ) ) ) ), "
        f"{q(AC)}, {q(RP)}, {q(AR)} )"
    )


def build_flat_enumeration():
    branches = ", ".join("->( " + ", ".join(q(a) for a in t[1:-3]) + " )" for _, t in VARIANTS)
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(f"->( {q(R)}, X( {branches} ), {q(AC)}, {q(RP)}, {q(AR)} )")


def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Transition(label=label), SilentTransition()])


def strict_partial_order(nodes, edges):
    keys = list(nodes)
    reach = set(edges)
    for k in keys:
        for i in keys:
            if (i, k) in reach:
                for j in keys:
                    if (k, j) in reach:
                        reach.add((i, j))
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[nodes[k] for k in keys])
    for a, b in reach:
        po.order.add_edge(nodes[a], nodes[b])
    return po


def build_powl_candidate(sc_predecessor):
    nodes = {
        "R": Transition(label=R), "CC": Transition(label=CC), "ER": Transition(label=ER),
        "SC?": optional(SC), "EX?": optional(EX),
        "AC": Transition(label=AC), "RP": Transition(label=RP), "AR": Transition(label=AR),
    }
    edges = [
        ("R", "CC"), ("R", "ER"), (sc_predecessor, "SC?"),
        ("CC", "EX?"), ("ER", "EX?"),
        ("CC", "AC"), ("ER", "AC"), ("SC?", "AC"), ("EX?", "AC"),
        ("AC", "RP"), ("RP", "AR"),
    ]
    return strict_partial_order(nodes, edges)


def build_permissive_tree():
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        f"->( {q(R)}, +( ->( +( {q(CC)}, {q(ER)} ), X( {q(EX)}, tau ) ), X( {q(SC)}, tau ) ), "
        f"{q(AC)}, {q(RP)}, {q(AR)} )"
    )


def build_d5_variant():
    def branch(first, second):
        return f"->( {q(first)}, +( ->( {q(second)}, X( {q(EX)}, tau ) ), X( {q(SC)}, tau ) ) )"
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        f"->( {q(R)}, X( {branch(CC, ER)}, {branch(ER, CC)} ), {q(AC)}, {q(RP)}, {q(AR)} )"
    )


model_conservative = build_conservative()
model_balanced = build_powl_candidate("CC")
model_permissive = build_powl_candidate("R")

CANDIDATES = {
    "conservative": model_conservative,
    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}
REFERENCES = {
    "conservative_flat": build_flat_enumeration(),
    "permissive_as_tree": build_permissive_tree(),
    "d5_variant": build_d5_variant(),
}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- helpers
def is_sound(net, im, fm):
    res = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    return bool(res[0]) if isinstance(res, tuple) else bool(res)


def net_is_acyclic(net):
    succ = {}
    for arc in net.arcs:
        succ.setdefault(arc.source, []).append(arc.target)
    color = {}

    def visit(node):
        color[node] = "grey"
        for nxt in succ.get(node, []):
            c = color.get(nxt)
            if c == "grey" or (c is None and not visit(nxt)):
                return False
        color[node] = "black"
        return True

    return all(visit(n) for n in list(net.places) + list(net.transitions) if n not in color)


def enumerate_language(net, im, fm, max_len=20):
    variant = pn_playout.Variants.EXTENSIVE
    params = {variant.value.Parameters.MAX_TRACE_LENGTH: max_len}
    sim = pn_playout.apply(net, im, final_marking=fm, variant=variant, parameters=params)
    return {tuple(ev["concept:name"] for ev in trace) for trace in sim}


def sampled_playout(net, im, fm, n=2000):
    variant = pn_playout.Variants.BASIC_PLAYOUT
    params = {variant.value.Parameters.NO_TRACES: n}
    sim = pn_playout.apply(net, im, final_marking=fm, variant=variant, parameters=params)
    return Counter(tuple(ev["concept:name"] for ev in trace) for trace in sim)


def trace_fitness(net, im, fm, trace):
    df = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(trace)])
    return pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(df, net, im, fm)[0]["fitness"]


def quality(net, im, fm):
    out = {}
    for name, fn in [
        ("fitness_alignments", lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("precision_alignments", lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("generalization_tbr", lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("simplicity_arc_degree", lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)),
    ]:
        try:
            out[name] = fn()
        except Exception as exc:
            out[name] = f"unavailable ({type(exc).__name__}: {exc})"
    return out


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2-5. nets, soundness, metrics, playout
nets, languages = {}, {}
for name, model in {**CANDIDATES, **REFERENCES}.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    nets[name] = (net, im, fm)
    acyclic = net_is_acyclic(net)
    lang = enumerate_language(net, im, fm)
    languages[name] = lang
    print(f"\n=== {name} ===")
    print("sound:", is_sound(net, im, fm), "| acyclic:", acyclic,
          "| |T| =", len(net.transitions), "| |P| =", len(net.places))
    print(("exact language size: " if acyclic else "BOUNDED (non-exhaustive) language size: ") + str(len(lang)))
    print("observed variants NOT reproduced:", OBSERVED - lang or "none")
    for t in sorted(lang - OBSERVED):
        print("  unobserved but admitted:", " > ".join(t))
    if name in CANDIDATES:
        for metric, value in quality(net, im, fm).items():
            print(f"  {metric}: {value}")
        sample = sampled_playout(net, im, fm)
        print("  sampled playout: distinct traces =", len(sample),
              "| all sampled traces inside enumerated language:", set(sample) <= lang)

# language relations (exact only because all nets are acyclic)
assert languages["conservative"] == languages["conservative_flat"] == OBSERVED
assert languages["permissive"] == languages["permissive_as_tree"]
assert languages["conservative"] <= languages["balanced"] <= languages["d5_variant"] <= languages["permissive"]
print("\nlanguage sizes:", {k: len(v) for k, v in languages.items()})  # expected 7, 12, 18, 7, 18, 14


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6. positive/negative suite
def T(*middle):
    return (R, *middle, AC, RP, AR)


ORDER = ["conservative", "balanced", "permissive", "d5_variant"]
SUITE = [
    (T(CC, ER, SC), (1, 1, 1, 1), "observed V1"),
    (T(CC, SC, ER), (1, 1, 1, 1), "observed V5"),
    (T(ER, CC, EX, SC), (1, 1, 1, 1), "observed V7"),
    (T(CC, ER, EX), (0, 1, 1, 1), "EX without SC (stocked or lost SC)"),
    (T(ER, CC, SC, EX), (0, 1, 1, 1), "mirror of V6"),
    (T(ER, SC, CC), (0, 0, 1, 1), "SC after ER only (D5)"),
    (T(SC, CC, ER), (0, 0, 1, 0), "SC before both checks"),
    (T(CC, EX, ER, SC), (0, 0, 0, 0), "EX before ER violates D6"),
    ((R, CC, ER, SC, RP, AC, AR), (0, 0, 0, 0), "RP before AC violates D2"),
    (T(CC, SC), (0, 0, 0, 0), "ER missing violates D3"),
    (T(CC, ER, SC, SC), (0, 0, 0, 0), "repeated SC"),
    ((R, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, AR), (0, 0, 0, 0), "SC after AC violates D4"),
    ((CC, R, ER, AC, RP, AR), (0, 0, 0, 0), "wrong start violates D1"),
    (T(CC, ER, EX, EX, SC), (0, 0, 0, 0), "EX twice violates D6"),
]

failures = 0
for trace, expected, why in SUITE:
    row = []
    for name, exp in zip(ORDER, expected):
        net, im, fm = nets[name]
        in_lang = trace in languages[name]
        fit_ok = trace_fitness(net, im, fm, trace) == 1.0
        ok = in_lang == bool(exp) == fit_ok
        failures += not ok
        row.append(f"{name}={'acc' if in_lang else 'rej'}{'' if ok else ' (UNEXPECTED)'}")
    print(f"{why:40s} | " + " | ".join(row))
print("suite failures:", failures)
```

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate use: `model_balanced`.** It fits all 30 cases, adds only 5 unseen traces, each a combination of independently observed features, and maps every constraint to a rule or to data. It is also the only candidate that encodes the N-shaped dependency structure without duplicates. Use it with two operating rules:

1. SC-absent cases are labeled "SC absent: cause unknown" and are never reported as compliant with S3.
2. Traces the model rejects because SC completed before CC, or admits only through the EX-without-SC paths, go to a review queue rather than being treated as violations.

**Sensitivity analysis: `model_permissive`,** together with the intermediate D5 variant. Rerun every conformance KPI under both. Any conclusion that changes between balanced and permissive depends on unresolved SC timing and must be reported as such. Keep `model_conservative` as a novelty detector: a sudden rise in cases it rejects signals process drift or new variants.

**Plan to resolve the ambiguities**

1. **Data archaeology (immediately, on existing data).**
   - Extract raw timestamps with their native precision and the ingestion index.
   - Count exact ties between CC/ER, CC/SC and SC/ER. If most of the 11 ER-first cases are ties, S1 becomes credible again.
   - Check whether the 7 SC-free cases cluster in time, which would indicate an integration outage (bursty missingness).
2. **Recover the missing attribute and events.**
   - Export `component_sourcing` (external / stocked / mixed) and `n_external_components` per order.
   - Reconcile SC against the source system (supplier portal or purchase-order confirmations) and record `sc_source_record_id`, so each absence can be classified as a legitimate skip, a lost event, or an S3 violation.
   - Once the attribute exists, replace XOR(SC, τ) with a data-guarded choice.
3. **Lifecycle and resource data.**
   - Collect start and complete timestamps (plus assign/schedule where available) and resource IDs for CC, ER, SC and EX.
   - Add information-readiness milestones for D5, for example "credit preliminary OK" and "engineering spec/BOM frozen".
   - Record `expedite_reason` and `expedite_requested_at`.
4. **Decision rules for the new data.**

   | Question | Measurement | Decision |
   |---|---|---|
   | CC/ER: sequence, interleaving, or concurrency? | Share of cases where the CC and ER execution intervals overlap; ER start relative to CC completion | Overlap in a substantial share (≥ 10–20%) → concurrency. Never overlapping, both orders → order-independent sequence (same trace language, but a resource constraint worth modeling). ER start ≥ CC completion in about all cases, with ER-first completions explained by ties → S1 holds; add CC→ER |
   | SC gating (D5) | SC start relative to CC and ER completion and to the readiness milestones | SC always starts after CC completes → keep the balanced model. SC starts after whichever check finishes first → D5 variant. SC starts before both complete but after a milestone → permissive, with the milestone as the real predecessor |
   | SC optionality vs missingness | SC absence rate by sourcing type | External and SC absent after reconciliation → violation. External and recovered from the source system → missing event (estimate the S5 rate). Stocked and SC absent → legitimate |
   | EX dependency | EX by sourcing type and reason | EX only when SC exists or for supplier reasons → add a co-existence constraint (EX requires SC), which removes 2 balanced traces |

5. **Maximally informative future traces**, verified with start times:
   - ⟨R, ER, SC, CC, …⟩ with SC starting before CC completes. Separates the balanced model from the D5 variant. Under a symmetric D5 its per-case rate is about 0.37 × 0.77 × 0.13 ≈ 3.7%, so about 80 new cases give a 95% chance of observing it at least once if the hypothesis is true. Its absence after about 80 cases would argue against symmetric D5.
   - ⟨R, SC, CC, ER, …⟩ that is not a tie. Separates the permissive candidate from the D5 variant.
   - ⟨R, CC, ER, EX, AC, …⟩ for a stocked order. Shows whether EX is independent of SC. At an expected rate of about 1.5% per case this needs roughly 200 cases; use stratified sampling of EX cases from a longer history.
   - External orders without SC after reconciliation. These separate true optionality and violations from missingness.
   - Overlapping CC/ER intervals, or ER starting before CC completes. These refute S1 as a description and confirm concurrency.
   - ⟨R, CC, EX, ER, …⟩ with verified times. This would refute D6/S6 as a description of practice and would be escalated as a compliance finding, not absorbed into the model.
   - Repeated SC for multi-component orders. This would require a loop or multi-instance construct in every model.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

The confidences below are subjective probabilities that each decision matches the real process. Estimates for n = 30 are coarse.

| Decision | Confidence | Main threat |
|---|---|---|
| R start, AR end, AC→RP→AR | ≈ 0.95 | Export may include only completed cases (cancellations unseen) |
| CC and ER mandatory before AC | ≈ 0.9 | Undocumented fast-track paths |
| CC/ER order not fixed in reality (rejecting S1 descriptively) | ≈ 0.7 | Tie artifacts (S7) could explain the 11 ER-first cases |
| CC and ER truly overlap in time (vs. order-independent sequence) | ≈ 0.5 | Unknowable without start times (D7); does not affect trace language |
| SC genuinely optional (some legitimate skips) | ≈ 0.8 | Bursty missingness could explain all 7 absences |
| SC before AC | ≈ 0.9 | – |
| SC must complete after CC (balanced edge) | ≈ 0.5 | Symmetric D5 is almost equally consistent with the data (P ≈ 0.32 of seeing no counterexample) |
| SC after at least one check, symmetric D5 | ≈ 0.35 (as the true rule) | – |
| SC may complete before both checks (permissive) | ≈ 0.15 | Could be tie artifacts only |
| SC unordered with ER | ≈ 0.75 | V5 may be 2 tie artifacts |
| EX after both checks, before AC | ≈ 0.8 | Rule-based; data n = 2 |
| EX unordered with SC | ≈ 0.7 | n = 1 per order |
| EX possible without SC | ≈ 0.55 | EX may be triggered by supplier issues |
| No repetitions / loops | ≈ 0.8 | S3 "every external component" may imply multiple SCs |

**Alternative interpretations that would change the recommendation**

- **S1 plus tie artifacts.** Suppose CC must finish before ER starts (S1) and the ER-first completions are ingestion-order artifacts of shared timestamps. Then the true process is sequential CC→ER, and the balanced model over-generalizes on the most frequent ambiguity. For compliance I would then recommend a normative sequence model, with CC before ER, SC after CC, and EX after ER, and treat the 11 ER-first cases as data-quality findings rather than behavior.
- **Bursty missingness.** Suppose the 7 SC absences come mainly from an integration outage in a period when nearly all orders had external components. Then SC should be mandatory. XOR(SC, τ) would hide both data loss and real S3 violations, and every candidate would under-report non-compliance. The recommendation would switch to a mandatory-SC model plus a data-quality flag for SC-free cases.
- **EX triggered by supplier problems.** If EX exists only in response to supplier problems, EX requires SC. The balanced model should then gain a co-existence construct, for example an XOR between "no SC, no EX" and "SC with optional EX", which removes its two EX-without-SC traces.
