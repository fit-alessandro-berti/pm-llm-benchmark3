## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Asymmetry dominates the comparison.** The operational benefit of fast execution is €20k–€35k. The customer-harm range for a false rejection or hold is €15k–€80k. The regulatory/compliance harm from an unlawful execution is €100k–€500k **plus** non-monetary consequences (supervisory action, reputational damage, potential license restrictions). This asymmetry means that no expected-value calculation over operational loss alone can justify Actions A or B: even if the probability of detection were modest, the expected regulatory harm dwarfs the maximum operational benefit by an order of magnitude.

**Dominance argument**: Action A is strictly dominated by the conformant alternatives because (i) it produces the same operational benefit as C or D-composite at best, (ii) it carries regulatory harm that the conformant paths do not, and (iii) the regulatory harm's lower bound (€100k) exceeds the operational benefit's upper bound (€35k) by a factor of ~3. Action B is similarly dominated and is worse than A on auditability because the four-eyes violation is directly visible in the event log (same resource ID in both review activities).

**Deadline loss vs. false rejection**: The SLA breach probability under normal processing (0.48–0.64) is significant but not catastrophic. The estimated value of executing within the deadline (€20k–€35k) is comparable to the lower bound of false-rejection harm (€15k). This means that the conformant strategies that preserve approval optionality (D-composite, C, E) are preferable to a premature rejection (F) unless the evidence documentation threshold is clearly met. The approval probability of 0.55–0.75 means that rejecting now has an expected false-rejection cost of roughly €8k–€60k (probability-weighted), which is non-trivial.

**Why D-composite ranks above C**: Both are conformant. D-composite uses waiting time productively (alternate screening may resolve the fuzzy match, which would reduce false-clear risk and could simplify the second officer's task) and defers the P-901 spillover decision, avoiding it entirely if S4 becomes available within ~35–50 minutes. C guarantees the spillover. The cost of D-composite is one API call (of three remaining) and slightly less timing certainty. Given that P-901 also has a tight deadline and the spillover is certain under C but conditional under D-composite, the composite is preferred.

**Why E ranks above F**: E is unambiguously conformant and preserves the option to approve correctly. F may be conformant but may also be a nonconformant or unsupported rejection. Even though E guarantees a deadline miss, the expected harm of a correct-but-late approval (€15k–€80k hold harm, but eventual correct outcome) is comparable to F's false-rejection harm, and E avoids the risk of an unsupported rejection finding.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 vs. S4 choice**: S2 is available immediately but only by interrupting P-901, which has a 70-minute deadline. The interruption cost is 45 minutes of S2's time, increasing P-901's breach probability by 0.12–0.28. S4 is expected in 35–65 minutes with no spillover. The trade-off is:

- If S4 arrives at 65 min: D-composite's S4 path gives ~80–105 min total, likely breaching the deadline. At this point, the fallback to S2 (interrupting P-901) should have been triggered at the ~50-minute decision point.

**Decision rule for the fallback**: Set a hard decision checkpoint at t = 50 minutes from now. If S4 is not available by then, and alternate screening is complete (or will complete within 5 minutes), trigger the S2 interruption of P-901. This leaves 40 minutes for S2's review (35–75 min estimate — at the fast end, fits; at the slow end, P-884 breaches but P-901's remaining window is also compressed). If S4 becomes available between 35 and 50 minutes, use S4 and avoid the spillover entirely.

**Effect on P-901**: P-901's 70-minute deadline means that if S2 is pulled away at t=50 (our fallback decision point), S2 returns at t=95, which is 25 minutes past P-901's deadline. This is severe. An earlier fallback (t=35) gives S2 more time but risks unnecessarily spilling over when S4 might have appeared at t=40. The 50-minute checkpoint balances these risks. If the organization has a policy that P-901's SLA is equally protected, the checkpoint should move earlier (t=40), accepting a higher P-884 breach risk to protect P-901.

**Alternate-screening API quota**: Three calls remain. Using one for P-884 leaves two for other cases today. This is a soft constraint — the screening provides genuine informational value (may resolve the fuzzy match, reducing false-clear risk and the second officer's burden) but is not a mandatory step. The API call is justified because (i) it may eliminate one of the two triggers for mandatory second review, (ii) it uses otherwise-idle waiting time, and (iii) the informational benefit to S2/S4's review quality is plausibly significant given the €480k amount.

**Time consumption risk for G**: Seeking an emergency override could consume 20–60+ minutes with uncertain payoff. If the Director is unreachable or declines, the remaining window for C or D is compressed. This is a time-sunk-cost risk: the time spent pursuing G cannot be recovered. For this reason, G should not be pursued in parallel with C/D unless the Director's response time is confirmed to be short (<20 min), because the operational paths (C, D) should proceed regardless.
