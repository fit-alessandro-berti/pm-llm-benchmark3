## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
Factual mechanism: Triage noticed sanctions ambiguity but did not escalate; D1 and A1 queues delayed the case, then C1 had to read complex evidence from scratch after A1.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, send a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot. Preliminary compliance work may occur while identity/document checks proceed, but final C1 must still follow final Assessment.

Temporal propagation: S1 can advise early while I1 and D1 are queued. C1 can pre-read packet and available documents before A1, reducing final C1 effort by roughly 1-3 h and potentially allowing final C1 immediately after A1 instead of waiting for the next C1 window. However, A1 still cannot start before Identity and Document complete, and final C1 cannot precede A1. The gain comes from earlier readiness and shorter final review, not from removing any prerequisite.

Process-feasibility check: Register and Triage first; Identity and Document before Assessment; final Compliance after latest Assessment; four-eyes holds because A1 and C1 are different resources. C1 is not double-booked if prelim and final intervals are separated.

Best/base/worst case: Best: final C1 Tue 19:30-22:30, Notify Tue 23:25, saving about 19 h. Base: final C1 Wed 08:00-11:00, saving 7-10 h. Worst: C1 reservation displaces urgent cases and prelim packet is incomplete; final C1 remains Wed 13:10 or later, with no net local gain and system harm.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 reservation is not honored; preliminary packet lacks key sanctions evidence; A1 remains delayed; or compliance policy treats early packet as unreliable, forcing full from-scratch final review.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but the case waited for D1 and started Document Check Tue 09:30.

Minimal intervention: Assign Document Check to D2 as soon as originals arrive instead of waiting for D1.

Temporal propagation: D2 can enter its queue earlier, potentially starting Mon 22:30 rather than Tue 09:30. D2 is slower, but the earlier start can still complete before D1’s factual completion. A2 may absorb some or all of that gain if the assessment queue is congested. If D2 flags handwritten ambiguity, Assessment may request more info, causing a new Document Check and Assessment after external response, which can erase or reverse the gain.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes Document Check; Identity precedes Assessment; no high-risk compliance step is required. Resource exclusion is respected if D2 is not already occupied.

Best/base/worst case: Best no-rework: Document D2 Mon 22:30-Tue 05:30, Assessment Tue 11:30-14:00, Decision Tue afternoon, saving about 18 h. Base: save 10-14 h. Worst: D2 clarification causes rework, adding 8-36 h external wait plus queues, making Notify later than factual.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 queue is longer than snapshot; D2’s clarification rate on handwritten documents is high; A2 backlog absorbs earlier document completion.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: After Assessment 2, the second Request More Info loop triggered a weekend external delay, then extra Document Check and Assessment, and only then Compliance Review.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop and require a decision using already available evidence. The process may approve with conditions or reject, but must document the unresolved discrepancy.

Temporal propagation: The external wait from Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00 is removed, as are Document Check 3 and Assessment 3. Compliance Review can start after Assessment 2 instead of after Assessment 3. Decision can occur Friday afternoon or Monday morning, depending on M1 availability, rather than Wednesday. This is not merely subtractive: the unresolved discrepancy moves into the decision rationale.

Process-feasibility check: High-risk/high-value case still requires final Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision. Since no new Request More Info occurs, Assessment 2 is the latest relevant Assessment. Four-eyes holds because A1 and C1 differ. The decision policy is explicitly changed.

Best/base/worst case: Best: C1 Fri 11:00-15:00, Decision Fri 15:30-16:00, saving about 110 h. Base: Decision Mon 08:30-09:00, saving 48-72 h. Worst: Compliance cannot defensibly approve or condition; rejection or appeal/loss exposure produces short cycle time but poor quality/compliance outcome.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The unresolved discrepancy is material to fraud/sanctions/value; policy requires external confirmation; conditional approval is unenforceable; appeal or loss rates rise.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: A legacy system lock created only the identity task, so Document Check was created after Identity Check and logged by batch entry.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can work concurrently. Require real-time timestamps rather than later batch entry.

Temporal propagation: Document Check can enter D2’s queue while Identity Check is still pending. If D2 availability is near midday, D2 can overlap with the latter part of I2. Both checks may finish Tue 16:30 instead of 19:30. But Assessment A2 is already congested; earlier verification readiness may only move A2 by 0-2 h, or not at all, because A2 remains the bottleneck.

Process-feasibility check: No originals are required, so Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently. Both must finish before Assessment. No compliance step or four-eyes issue applies. The workaround must be auditable; real-time timestamps distinguish true concurrency from false concurrency.

Best/base/worst case: Best: A2 Wed 08:00-10:30, Decision Wed 10:45-11:05, saving about 3.5 h. Base: A2 unchanged Wed 09:30-12:00, saving 0-1.5 h. Worst: workaround is poorly logged, creating audit exception, false concurrency finding, and rework.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 queue is not actually shorter; A2 backlog persists; logging cannot prove real-time task creation; controlled workaround is later deemed non-compliant.

For a 100-case weekly rollout, K-104 would reserve C1 capacity for many high-risk cases. With only C1 certified, this can starve other urgent compliance work and create idle reserved slots if assessments slip or packets are incomplete. K-219 would shift many handwritten standard cases to D2; D2 becomes the bottleneck while D1 is underused, and any clarification/rework creates extra Document Check and Assessment loops. K-337 avoids many D1/A1/C1 loops and weekend external waits, but it moves risk into conditional approvals, rejections, appeals, and monitoring, which can grow non-target queues downstream. K-411 parallelizes verification for low-risk cases, but if assessment is already congested, earlier verification readiness simply pushes more cases into the same A2 queue; final completion may not improve. Real-time timestamps are critical because a workaround can otherwise create false concurrency. Across all four, the system bottleneck migrates rather than disappears: C1 for K-104, D2 for K-219, decision quality/appeals for K-337, and A2 for K-411. Weekend and external-response effects matter most in K-337 and K-219; queue forecasts are not guarantees, so reservations and substitutions should be piloted with spillover metrics.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust because it is low-risk, no-originals, and does not change decision policy. Its main weakness is that earlier verification readiness may simply feed an already congested assessment queue. The value of the pilot is therefore not only speed; it is learning whether true concurrency can be logged reliably and whether A2 absorbs or amplifies the gain.

K-337 is most dangerous despite its large potential speed improvement. It suppresses a rework loop in a high-risk, high-value case and changes the decision policy into conditional approval or rejection. That can shorten the path by days, but it can also increase appeal risk, loss exposure, and compliance defensibility problems. Unlike a routing change, this decision-quality risk is hard to reverse once the decision is made.

K-104 is least transportable from a single-case counterfactual to a system-wide policy because C1 is a single certified resource. A local reservation looks beneficial, but 100 similar cases would create priority displacement, idle reserved capacity, and delays for other urgent compliance cases. The local counterfactual ignores the queue externality that dominates at scale.

The most valuable additional data would be real-time C1 queue and reservation data, D2 clarification/rework rates by document type, A2 assessment queue arrivals and service times, and event-level timestamps that distinguish task creation from batch entry. These would show whether apparent concurrency is real, whether compliance prework shortens final review, and whether local gains migrate congestion to other queues.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The normative prerequisites, four-eyes rule, and final compliance placement remain unchanged except where K-337 explicitly changes decision policy.
2. `temporal` — Queue snapshots are forecasts, not guarantees; service times are treated as uncertain ranges.
3. `resource` — Resource pools and capacities remain fixed, including C1 as the only fully certified compliance reviewer.
4. `behavioral` — Triage packet completeness affects whether preliminary compliance work saves later effort.
5. `quality` — Suppressing Request More Info changes decision risk by converting unresolved evidence into conditions, rejection, or appeal exposure.
6. `interference` — C1 reservations and D2 substitutions displace non-target cases and can shift bottlenecks.
7. `temporal` — External responses, especially weekends, may take 8-36 h or longer and dominate K-219/K-337.
8. `resource` — D2 is slower and more likely to request clarification on handwritten documents than D1 in K-219.
