## 1. Conformance semantics

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation explicitly marked `forbidden` is a presumptive violation, unless identity, temporal, or data-quality evidence shows the observation is spurious. Closed-world applies to forbidden relations: presence is meaningful.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: absence of a `mandatory` relation is a presumptive gap only when the stated scope applies and the model abstraction matches the rule. Closed-world applies to mandatory relations within scope, but scope flags, lifecycle abstraction, and missing data can negate a violation.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: a relation not present in the norm and not marked forbidden is open-world. It is not a violation merely because it is unmodeled; it may be legitimate, rare, or a norm gap.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: absence of a `typical` relation is not noncompliance. It may reflect legitimate exceptions such as open credit, write-offs, unapplied cash, or intermediate events.
- **Likely data-quality error**: an observation inconsistent with identifiers, event attributes, join logic, or object provenance should be quarantined before being treated as process noncompliance.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: a rule stated at lifecycle level, such as “eventually validate,” may be incorrectly tested as a directly-follows edge in an OC-DFG. The absence of a direct edge may be an artifact of the summary model.
- **Insufficient evidence**: aggregate counts may not identify whether the same object, event, or scope instance is involved. Where trace-level identity is unavailable, conclusions must remain provisional.

Open-world assumptions mean that unmodeled typical or unspecified behavior is not automatically bad. Closed-world assumptions apply narrowly: forbidden edges are presumptively bad when observed, and mandatory edges are presumptively required when absent within their stated scope.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG can establish direct successor frequencies between event types, such as `Create Order -> Validate Order` or `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`. It is useful for detecting forbidden direct sequences and for surfacing missing direct edges. It cannot establish eventual lifecycle completion, full trace ordering, object identity across events, scope conditions, or whether an intermediate event was omitted.

Counts and scopes matter. For N1, 98 of 100 Orders show the direct edge, but the norm says newly created standard Orders should **eventually** enter validation. Treating “eventually” as a directly-follows rule is dangerous: it converts legitimate intermediate review, unrecorded events, or summary abstraction into false violations. The two missing Orders are therefore not robust violations; they are abstraction mismatches or insufficient evidence until full traces show whether validation eventually occurred.

For N3, the two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` observations are strong because the relation is forbidden and directly observed. However, same-order identity and temporal order must still be verified. For N4, 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages satisfy `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`; the two gaps are meaningful only if the external-shipment scope is confirmed. For N5, 92 of 110 Invoices show `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`, but the relation is typical, so the 18 exceptions are not violations per se.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction and suspicious at another. For example, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` is not a violation under N2 because intermediate events are expected, but it may indicate that the norm should explicitly model invoicing as an intermediate state.

### OTG

The OTG establishes object-type relation weights, such as `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` or `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`. It can detect forbidden object relations and missing mandatory interaction types. It cannot establish whether a relation originated from the correct event, whether temporal order is valid, or whether aggregate weights hide individual missing links.

This is important for T1 and T2. The OTG shows 1450 `Order-Item` interactions, but ET-OT shows 1448 `Add Item -> Order` links against 1450 `Add Item -> Item` links. The object graph may look complete while two Add Item events lack order context. Similarly, the OTG shows 200 `Package-Delivery` interactions, but ET-OT shows only 198 `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links. The aggregate OTG edge can mask event-level missing mandatory relations.

T6 and E10 are forbidden object-level observations. The single `Order object_cobirth Payment` edge and the single `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` edge are presumptive violations unless data-quality evidence explains them. Unregulated edges such as `Order-Customer`, `Order-RiskCase`, and `Package-Delivery object_codeath` are not violations merely because they are absent from the normative edge set.

### ET–OT

ET-OT establishes event-object participation weights. It can verify that an event type references required object types, such as `Create Order -> Order` or `Dispatch Package -> Delivery`. It cannot establish object lifecycle order, whether an object is in scope, or whether a missing link is due to a missing event, missing object, or missing reference.

E1, E4, E5, E7, and E3 are largely satisfied: `Create Order -> Order` 100, `Pack Package -> Package` 200, `Dispatch Package -> Package` 200, `Record Payment -> Payment` 93, and `Add Item -> Item` 1450. The gaps are E2, E6, and E8. E2 has 1448 `Add Item -> Order` links against 1450 Item links; E6 has 198 `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links against 200 Package links; E8 has 92 `Record Payment -> Invoice` links against 93 Payment links.

These gaps are not automatically severe. E8 may be legitimate unapplied cash, but the event is not labeled as such. E6 may be a missing relation despite a carrier reference. E2 may be a missing order reference. Each requires scope and provenance checks before being classified as process noncompliance.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1: “eventually” is not operationalized.**  
   Risk: direct-follows diagnostics create false violations for Orders with intermediate review or unrecorded events.  
   Clarification: define “eventually validate” with a maximum time window, allowed intermediate events, and standard-Order scope.  
   Proposed status: **mandatory**, with lifecycle conformance rather than direct DFG conformance.

2. **N4: “externally shipped” scope is not observable in the graph.**  
   Risk: internal shipments or misclassified packages may be treated as missing mandatory packing.  
   Clarification: require a shipment-type attribute or package class flag.  
   Proposed status: **mandatory** when `shipment_type = external`; **unspecified** otherwise.

3. **N5/T3/E8: exceptions are not typed.**  
   Risk: write-offs, open credit, and unapplied cash may be misclassified as missing payments.  
   Clarification: require invoice state and payment application type, such as `paid`, `written_off`, `open_credit`, or `unapplied_cash`.  
   Proposed status: `Record Payment -> Invoice` remains **mandatory** unless the event is explicitly typed `unapplied_cash`; write-off and open credit remain **typical** exceptions.

4. **T1/T2: object-level mandatory edges can hide event-level gaps.**  
   Risk: aggregate OTG weights may appear complete while individual Add Item or Dispatch events lack required object references.  
   Clarification: state that every Add Item event must reference Order and Item, and every Dispatch Package event must reference Package and Delivery.  
   Proposed status: **mandatory** at the ET-OT level.

5. **Forbidden rules lack identifier-provenance safeguards.**  
   Risk: reused numeric identifiers can create false forbidden findings, as suspected for `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice`.  
   Clarification: define unique identifier namespaces and require event-object provenance for forbidden checks.  
   Proposed status: **forbidden** for the logical business relation, with separate data-quality rules for identifier collisions.

6. **Several observed relations are not explicitly classified.**  
   Risk: benign behavior may be escalated, or important behavior may remain ungoverned.  
   Clarification: explicitly classify `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` as an intermediate typical relation; classify `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `MCR-Customer`, and `Package-Delivery object_codeath` as unspecified.  
   Proposed status: **typical** for intermediate invoicing; **unspecified** for the others unless policy says otherwise.

7. **No statistical tolerance bands or aging thresholds are defined.**  
   Risk: rare events may be over-escalated, while persistent typical absences may go unnoticed.  
   Clarification: separate normative status from monitoring thresholds. Mandatory gaps should alert at the individual level; typical absences should alert based on aging or volume.  
   Proposed status: normative status remains **mandatory/typical/forbidden/unspecified**, while thresholds are operational monitoring policy.
