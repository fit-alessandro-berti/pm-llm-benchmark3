3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this incident, **resilience** is not the preservation of throughput alone; it is the preservation of mission-critical outcomes (hospital-critical supply, controlled-substance integrity, cold-chain quality) through transformed but **complete** controls, coupled with an explicit recovery plan that makes hidden work visible.

**True resilience (transformed controls, evidence preserved):**
- **B (Manual authorization check)** transforms electronic validation into an offline + phone process with staff sign-off; control intent survives.
- **A (Hospital-critical fast lane)** is resilient when paired with B: it protects the mission (91% fill) without necessarily abandoning authorization.
- **I (Defer noncritical)** is resilient when explicit: it allocates scarce capacity to critical customers according to declared rules.
- **J (Backlog prioritization)** is resilient when rules are documented and transparent.
- **K (Reconcile manual reservation)** is resilience as repair: it acknowledges that manual working creates debt and resources its resolution.

**Concealed noncompliance (operational flow preserved, control evidence erased):**
- **C (Skip system authorization check)** and **G (Dispatch without scan)** are the clearest cases. The DFG shows both as move-on-log deviations in T2; together they remove authorization and chain-of-custody evidence for a subset of cases. Throughput is preserved, but audit trails are not reconstructed until T4. The 44%76% trace-fitting trend shows that a quarter of cases still do not match the normative model even after stabilization.
- **E (Manual pick list)** is ambiguous: maintaining physical flow is resilient, but because scans were not reconstructed at pick (only later via audit), it contributes to the long audit-remediation tail (2,300 cases). Without front-loaded paper evidence, manual pick becomes concealment.
- **L (Audit remediation)** is not resilience; it is the symptom of concealed noncompliance. The temporal profile deviation (Invoice close  Audit remediation) proves that operational metrics (cycle time, fill rate) recovered while compliance work continued invisibly.

**Ambiguous / context-dependent:**
- **D (Manual inventory snapshot)** is resilient only if bounded by time (e.g., hourly refresh) and followed by reconciliation; here it persisted through T2 and caused the 1,400 reconciliation cases in T3–T4.
- **F (Temporary release control)** was resilient for controlled substances but concealed missing cold-chain temperature documentation because pharmacists focused sample checks on controlled drugs, missing temperature logs.
- **A (Fast lane)** is resilient if authorization is completed offline; if it merges with C, it becomes a hidden authorization gap.

Abductively, the co-occurrence of Skip auth, Dispatch without scan, and Phone POD in the T2 DFG suggests a **systematic corner-cutting pattern under time pressure**, not isolated errors. The fact that Pharmacy-standard fill collapsed while Hospital-critical held suggests **triage was effective but also created a two-tier system** that deferred lower-priority control rigor. The persistence of Audit remediation after operational recovery indicates that **manual work created compliance debt that outlived the downtime**, confirming that after-the-fact audit is not equivalent to real-time control.

4. Recovery process model

Notation: **IF** = trigger; **GATE** = minimum control check; **CAP** = manual evidence capture; **TRIAGE** = priority rule; **REC** = reconciliation loop; **AUD** = audit-remediation track (separate from operational close).

**Stage 0 — Trigger and Activation**
- IF WMS unavailable > 30 min OR inventory sync fails OR authorization system down.
- THEN activate **Disruption Response Protocol**; notify compliance, pharmacy, logistics.
- DO NOT allow “business as usual with manual shortcuts”; switch to structured manual mode.

**Stage 1 — Triage and Routing (explicit, documented)**
- **TRIAGE Level 1 (Critical / High Risk):** Hospital-critical + (Controlled-substance OR Cold-chain OR Recall-sensitive).
  - Mandatory manual authorization (B) with offline list + phone + staff sign-off.
  - Mandatory manual inventory snapshot (D) with time-stamped print; refresh every 4 h max.
- **TRIAGE Level 2 (Standard / Medium):** Pharmacy-standard + Ambient / Recall-sensitive.
  - Full manual authorization required; deferral permitted only if capacity < threshold.
- **TRIAGE Level 3 (Bulk / Low Risk):** Wholesale-bulk + Ambient.
  - Explicit deferral (I) allowed; prioritize by contract and backlog age.
- **TRIAGE Rule:** No “skip authorization” (C) permitted at any level; no dispatch without manifest (G).

**Stage 2 — Minimum Controls by Risk (GATE before next step)**
- **Authorization GATE:** Offline customer/product eligibility list; phone confirmation for exceptions; sign-off by compliance staff.
- **Inventory GATE:** Printed snapshot with timestamp; physical count for critical / controlled; record in paper log.
- **Pick GATE:** Paper pick list with lot/qty/product; picker and verifier signatures (E modified).
- **Pack / Release GATE:** 
  - Controlled-substance: 100% pharmacist dual-check (F modified).
  - Cold-chain: Mandatory manual temperature log at pack and at dispatch (not sample-only).
  - Quality/release: Check must occur; if skipped due to urgency, document as exception with supervisor sign-off.
- **Dispatch GATE:** Paper dispatch manifest with courier name, time, case list, signatures; scan at recovery (G retired).
- **POD GATE:** Phone POD (H) permitted only with structured script and confirmation code; for controlled / cold-chain, require photo/signature or courier digital proof.

**Stage 3 — Manual Evidence Capture (CAP)**
Every case processed under disruption must carry a **Recovery Evidence Packet**:
- Authorization record (offline check / phone note / sign-off)
- Inventory snapshot timestamp and staff ID
- Pick list (paper) with signatures
- Pack/release check record (pharmacist sign or exception note)
- Temperature/security log (cold-chain / controlled)
- Dispatch manifest with courier acknowledgment
- POD record (structured phone log or photo/signature)

CAP rules: Capture at point of action; do not rely solely on reconstruction.

**Stage 4 — Stabilization with Explicit Reconciliation**
- When Order intake / Inventory sync / Scanning partially return:
  - **REC Stream (K):** All T2–T3 cases enter Reconcile manual reservation before invoice close. Resolve reservation mismatches; correct inventory; cancel unrecoverable orders explicitly.
  - **REC Target:** Complete reconciliation within 48 h of system return for critical orders; 5 days for bulk.
- **Backlog Prioritization (J):** Use matrix (Criticality × Product Risk) documented in protocol; avoid informal favoritism.

**Stage 5 — Separate Compliance Recovery (AUD)**
- **Operational close** (Invoice close) may occur only after REC is complete for the case.
- **Audit-remediation (L)** is a separate track: Cases still missing any event (auth, scan, temp, POD) after REC enter AUD.
- **Track separately:** Report “Operational cycle time” and “Compliance remediation time” independently. Do not allow audit queue to disappear behind operational metrics.
- **AUD target:** <0.5% of cases remaining in audit after 7 days of stabilization; baseline is ~0.8% exceptions total.

**Stage 6 — Model Validation and Exit**
- Monitor DFG conformance (fitness + % fitting traces). Exit manual mode when fitness > 0.95 and fitting traces > 85% for 3 consecutive days.
- Confirm audit-remediation queue declining; confirm reconciliation completed.

5. Resilience indicators

**Leading indicators (during disruption — signal whether adaptation is resilient or drifting into concealment):**
- **Critical fill rate by segment:** Hospital-critical % (protect if >85%); Pharmacy-standard % (watch if <75%).
- **Manual authorization completeness:** % of cases with completed manual check vs % skipped (C). Target: <1% skip.
- **Evidence capture rate:** % of cases with complete Recovery Evidence Packet (CAP) at time of dispatch. Target: >95% in manual mode.
- **Inventory snapshot age / refresh rate:** Time since last print; target refresh 4 h.
- **Capacity allocation by triage:** % deferred (I) vs % processed; target explicit, not hidden.
- **Control completeness:** Real-time % with dual-control (controlled) and temperature log (cold-chain) completed at pack, not reconstructed later.

**Lagging indicators (after / stabilization — expose hidden workload):**
- **Traceability exception rate:** Should return to <1.0% by end of T4; current 2.2% shows lingering debt.
- **Cold-chain / controlled completeness:** Temperature docs <1.0%; dual-control <0.5%.
- **Rework / correction rate:** Should trend to <4.5% by Day 21; 5.8% indicates residual errors.
- **Audit-remediation queue:** Count and age; average days from invoice close to audit close. Target: <5% of cases; <3 days.
- **Reconciliation rate (K):** Cases resolved / total manual cases; should reach 100% before audit close.
- **Backlog age distribution:** p90 backlog age by customer segment; detect unfair prioritization.
- **Model conformance:** Fitness and % fitting traces; target >0.95 / >85%.
- **Cancellation rate by segment:** Monitor for equity; wholesale-bulk should not exceed 3.5% disproportionately versus hospital-critical.

7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** temporary manual control with complete evidence capture (B, E, F, H modified), explicit triage that protects hospitals (A, I, J with rules), and a dedicated audit-remediation workforce after operational restart (L tracked separately); it should **not accept** authorization skips (C), dispatch without manifest or scan (G), hidden backlog prioritization, or the conflation of operational cycle-time recovery with real-time compliance. The critical evidence that would change this recommendation is whether, in a future disruption, manual authorization completeness exceeds 99%, evidence-capture rate exceeds 95%, and audit-remediation queue falls below 0.5% of cases within 7 days of stabilization—if those conditions hold, structured manual fallback is an acceptable resilience strategy; if not, the organization must invest in redundant electronic controls (P4) and stricter prohibition of shortcuts (P1) rather than relying on after-the-fact audit. Until that evidence is demonstrated, preserving throughput without control evidence is not resilience—it is concealed risk that accumulates as hidden workload long after the incident appears over.
