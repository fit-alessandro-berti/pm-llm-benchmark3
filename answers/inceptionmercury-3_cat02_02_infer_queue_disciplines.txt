# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Assumptions used throughout:

- A job becomes eligible when its arrival scan is recorded; because arrival scans can be delayed by up to 2 minutes, the true arrival may be earlier, but this does not change any main conclusion below.
- For a preempted job, estimated remaining time is approximated as `est_min - cumulative service minutes already worked`. This is necessary for SPT comparisons.
- Resource exceptions are hard blocks: A1 is unavailable 08:52–08:58, C1 is unavailable until 10:10, D-F is unavailable 12:00–12:20, and D-X is unavailable before 11:20.
- Priority 1 is highest, priority 3 is lowest.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

- Supporting:
  - A02 at 08:32 is the earliest waiting arrival; A04 priority 1 waits behind it. This rejects a priority rule.
  - A05 at 09:05 is the earliest waiting arrival; A08 priority 1 waits behind it.
  - A07 at 09:32 is earlier than A08, not A07 runs first.
  - A04 starts at 08:58 because A1 is calibrating 08:52–08:58; A04 is simply the FIFO-next job after A02.
- Contradicting:
  - A03 at 08:20 violates FIFO. This is explained by the 08:20 appointment.
  - A06 starts at 09:20, not at its 09:15 appointment. If appointments are exact, this is a violation; if appointments are tolerant windows or targets, it is acceptable.
  - No episode supports priority, so a priority-only claim would be very weak.

## QB

- Supporting:
  - B04 is served before B06 at 10:28 despite B06 having priority 2 and B04 priority 3.
  - B07 is served after B06 even though B07 had a 10:30 appointment.
  - B01 resumes at 09:31 before B03 priority 2, consistent with FIFO once the priority-1 interruption is over.
  - B05, priority 1, jumps ahead at 10:00, supporting a limited urgent-priority override.
- Contradicting:
  - B02 preempts B01 at 09:16. That contradicts FIFO as a pure rule.
  - At 09:48, B01 is stopped and B03 priority 2 runs even though B05 priority 1 arrived at 09:46. A coherent priority rule would run B05 first.
  - B07’s appointment is not honoured on time, so appointment scheduling is not a reliable explanation for QB.

## QC

- Supporting:
  - Initial backlog order C02 (8), C03 (14), C04 (22), C01 (36) is exactly increasing expected processing time. The note says C01 is oldest but executed last among initial backlog.
  - C03 runs before C05 at 10:18 even though C05 is priority 1 and has a tight deadline.
  - C06 (6) runs before C01 and C07 at 11:34.
- Contradicting:
  - C04 runs at 10:32 before C06 (6). If C06 is already eligible, this is an SPT violation; it can be explained only by a queue-entry/snapshot delay or an unrecorded exception.
  - C01 runs at 11:40 before C07 (18, priority 2, deadline 12:00). This contradicts SPT, priority, and deadline-driven scheduling; it looks like aging/fairness.
  - C05 runs at 10:54 before C06, which is the opposite of SPT, but is explainable by an SLA/priority emergency.

## QD

- Supporting:
  - D02 can only go to D-F; D05 can only go to D-F because D-F is the only fully qualified resource.
  - D-M serves D01D04D08D09 in arrival order.
  - D-X serves D06D07D10 in arrival order.
  - D06 starts exactly at its 11:30 appointment; D-X remains idle from 11:20 until then.
- Contradicting:
  - D10 priority 1 waits behind D07 priority 3 on D-X, so a global priority rule is not active.
  - D08 priority 1 waits behind D04 priority 3 on D-M, again rejecting global priority.
  - D-F is idle 11:36–12:00 even though D05 and D10 are waiting. A meeting-avoidance rule explains D05, but it does not easily explain why D10 was not started on D-F.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Test 1: QA appointment as hard slot versus permissive appointment

- Episode: QA, 09:05 decision before A06’s 09:15 appointment.
- Competing explanations:
  1. FIFO with a non-binding appointment target: start A05, then A06 at 09:20.
  2. Hard appointment slot scheduling: do not start a 15-minute job immediately before 09:15; instead run A07 or idle until 09:15, then start A06.
- Observed: A05 runs 09:05–09:20, then A06 starts at 09:20. This favours explanation 1.
- Discriminating observable: Record the estimate used at decision time and the planned next job. If the dispatcher planned A06 at 09:20, appointments are permissive; if it planned A06 at 09:15 but A05 overran, that would indicate an estimate error or dispatch execution failure.

## Test 2: QC SPT with immediate eligibility versus batching/snapshot

- Episode: QC, 10:32 decision; C06 arrived at 10:25 with e6.
- Competing explanations:
  1. Dynamic SPT with immediate eligibility: C06 should be selected.
  2. SPT applied to a snapshot of jobs already visible at the previous dispatch point: C06 is not in the snapshot, so C04 is selected.
- Observed: C04 is selected. This favours the snapshot/batching explanation, or an unrecorded eligibility delay.
- Discriminating observable: Add a queue-entry timestamp separate from the arrival scan. If C06’s queue-entry time is after 10:32, then the snapshot explanation is real; if it is before 10:32, then the SPT rule itself is not being followed at that moment.

## Test 3: QB preemptive priority versus dispatcher reassignment

- Episode: QB, 09:48 preemption of B01; B05 priority 1 arrived at 09:46.
- Competing explanations:
  1. Preemptive priority: B05 should be the next job.
  2. FIFO plus a manual dispatcher reassignment: B03 was already selected and started at 09:48.
- Observed: B03 starts at 09:48, not B05. This favours explanation 2, or a policy that only allows priority-1 preemption under specific “urgent” conditions.
- Discriminating observable: Record the dispatch-decision timestamp and the preemption reason code. If the decision to reassign B1 was made before B05’s scan, the B03 start is explainable; if it was made after B05 was visible, then the priority rule is violated.

## Test 4: QD D-F idle and D10 assignment

- Episode: QD, 11:36–12:00 D-F idle while D05 and D10 are waiting.
- Competing explanations:
  1. D10 was not eligible because it was already assigned to D-X, not D-F.
  2. D-F avoids starting any job that cannot finish before the 12:00 meeting.
  3. D-F is implicitly unavailable before the meeting, even though only 12:00–12:20 is recorded.
- Observed: D-F starts nothing until D05 at 12:20; D10 starts on D-X at 12:16.
- Discriminating observable: Record the “ready-for-assignment” time and the list of eligible resources considered for each job. If D10 was in an assigned-to-D-X state at 11:36, then the D-F idle is not a contradiction. If D10 was ready and eligible for D-F, then an undocumented meeting-buffer rule or resource assignment policy exists.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility:

1. **SLA/priority override for urgent deadlines.**  
   C05 is selected at 10:54 even though SPT would select C06. B02 and B05 also produce priority-like preemptions. This is the most plausible exception because it is directly supported by the C05 note and the B02 “urgent arrival” reason. It would change QC from pure SPT to “SPT with emergency override,” and QB from pure FIFO to “FIFO with urgent-priority preemption.”

2. **Manual dispatcher reassignment.**  
   B01’s second interruption has an explicit “dispatcher reassignment” reason. This is a documented-looking operational override rather than a clean scheduling rule. It explains the B03-before-B05 anomaly and makes any simple QB rule unreliable.

3. **Resource-absence avoidance before meetings/calibration.**  
   D-F is idle 11:36–12:00 before its 12:00 meeting. A rule that prevents starting a job that would straddle an absence explains D05’s delay. It would alter QD by adding a calendar constraint to the routing rule. It does not explain D10 unless D10 was already assigned elsewhere.

4. **Dispatch batching/snapshot.**  
   QC’s C04-before-C06 decision suggests that the dispatcher may only consider jobs that were already in the queue at the previous decision point, not all physically arrived jobs. This is plausible in manual or periodically polled dispatch systems. It would rescue SPT for QC but only over batch snapshots.

5. **Appointment tolerance/windows.**  
   A06 starts five minutes after its appointment; B07 starts well after its appointment. If appointments are windows rather than exact times, these are not violations. This would reduce the strength of the appointment component in QA/QB and make A03/D06 the only exact-appointment evidence.

6. **Arrival scan delays.**  
   Scans can be delayed by up to two minutes. This affects exact FIFO tie-breaking but does not change the main Q because all jobs used in decisions were already recorded by the decision time. It is still a data-quality limitation for minute-level conclusions.

7. **Estimate noise or strategic misreporting.**  
   All observed service segment sums equal `est_min`, so there is no visible estimate error. However, if estimates are noisy or deliberately lowered to get priority under SPT, several choices could be misread. The data do not support this strongly, but it remains a theoretical identifiability threat.

# 7. Final synthesis

QD is the most identifiable queue: skill constraints create strong routing evidence, and the FIFO order within each skill-defined subqueue is consistent. QA is also relatively identifiable, but its appointment component has only one clean supporting episode.

QB is the least identifiable queue. The data show preemptive-looking events, FIFO-looking resumptions, and at least one manual dispatcher reassignment that contradicts both. No single listed discipline fits all QB episodes.

The most dangerous inference would be to declare QB a preemptive-priority queue based on B02 and B05. Those episodes are real, but they are surrounded by FIFO-consistent decisions and a manual exception, so inferring global preemptive priority from them would overstate the evidence. A close second would be declaring QC a pure SPT queue, because the initial backlog order is compelling but later decisions require an SLA emergency and an unexplained C06 skip.
