## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1's stated purpose ("prevent one person from both initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer") decomposes into at least seven distinct objectives. Each is protected differently, and each exposes a different failure mode of the compensating-control family.

| # | Objective / threat scenario | Prevention | Detection | Correction | Where the control family fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | Single-actor fraud or error (one person initiates and authorizes) | FEP-1 structurally; MG-4 pre-release substitutes; ED-1 governs exceptions | RA-7 retrospective audit | Recall only within ~24h window | Fails entirely under credential sharing or collusion; AV-2 does not test independence |
| O2 | Transaction accuracy (duplicate invoice, wrong amount/vendor, PO mismatch) | AV-2 (duplicates, limits, basic PO match); MG-4 if review is genuine | AV-2 warnings; RA-7 | Recall 24h; block pre-release | AV-2 blind to goods receipt and commercial reasonableness; rubber-stamped MG-4 adds nothing |
| O3 | Beneficiary integrity (bank-detail tampering, account takeover, sanctions) | AV-2 (ownership, 7-day change window, sanctions); ED-1 condition 4 freezes changes | AV-2 warnings; RA-7 | Recall 24h only | Cannot establish *who controls* a changed account; fails against insiders and collusion; post-settlement detection is usually unrecoverable |
| O4 | Independence quality / collusion resistance (formal four-eyes satisfied by dependent actors) | MG-4 independence rules (level, conflict) partially | RA-7 + pattern analytics (repeated pairs, shared devices) | None intrinsic | **No control in the family prevents collusion.** AV-2, MG-4, RA-7 all assume at least one honest, independent actor |
| O5 | Authorization completes before irrevocability (preserves the corrective option) | MG-4 timing; ED-1 condition 6 | RA-7 measures the gap | Recall window is the correction itself | Post-settlement ratification is detective-only; settlement destroys the corrective lever |
| O6 | Governed emergency continuity (deviation without control collapse) | ED-1's seven conditions collectively | RA-7 100% same-user audit (condition 7) | Recall if audit is fast enough | Fails when tokens are minted late (retroactive rationalization), when conditions are waived under pressure, or when the emergency channel becomes the default path |
| O7 | Attribution and evidence integrity (who *really* acted; credential provenance; signed checklists) | Weak — none of the four prevents credential sharing | Log/device forensics; RA-7 | Limited | Unsigned manual fallbacks (AP-208) and borrowed credentials (AP-202) defeat attribution; requires telemetry the control family does not mandate |

Key structural observation: the control family is strong against **random error and opportunistic single-actor fraud** (O1–O3, O5), weak against **coordination** (O4, O7), and its corrective capacity is entirely time-boxed (O5).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

### Tier definitions

A compensating package is judged against the *nominal dual-human path*, not against "nothing":

| Tier | Definition | Typical signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent** | Every ED-1 condition verifiably holds, the reviewer demonstrably examined invoice + bank details + receipt evidence, and audit closed inside the recall window. Residual risk profile  nominal path. | AP-203, AP-207 |
| **Partially equivalent** | Core protections present (independent review before irrevocability, AV coverage, timely audit) but at least one condition is unevidenced, procedurally irregular, or unauthorized — even if the outcome was clean. | AP-201, AP-206 |
| **Merely mitigating** | Controls reduce probability or severity but with material gaps in timing, independence, or coverage; expected loss sits between "protected" and "unprotected." | AP-204 (audit as late backstop), AP-209 (pending independence resolution) |
| **Ineffective** | Controls absent, captured by conflicted actors, overridden, or executed after irrecoverability. Occurrence of the control activity is not sufficiency. | AP-202, AP-208 |

### Sufficiency dimensions (all seven must be checked, not counted)

1. **Timing** — before release? before settlement? within the 24h recall window? A control after irrevocability cannot correct, whatever its label.
2. **Independence** — organizational level (L3+), conflict of interest, credential provenance (did the actor use someone else's identity?), physical/logical separation (shared workstation degrades attestation value). Shared service accounts are non-persons for this purpose.
3. **Coverage** — which threats does the control actually test? A full AV-2 pass says nothing about goods receipt; a manager approval says nothing if bank details weren't examined.
4. **Overrideability** — hard block vs. warning-plus-override; and critically, *who* holds the override right and whether the override path is itself independent.
5. **Recoverability** — pre-settlement (fully correctable), post-settlement within recall (usually correctable), post-recall (forensic only).
6. **Evidence quality** — signed checklists, artifact-level review traces, immutable token issuance logs vs. assertions after the fact.
7. **Collusion resistance** — would the package survive two coordinated actors? Only pattern analytics and structural rotation address this; no single control does.

### Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard

Expected loss (probability × severity) is one input, not the criterion:

- **Tail shape differs.** Two packages with equal expected loss can differ radically in worst case: a recoverable EUR 12k duplicate versus an irrecoverable EUR 900k misdirection. Risk-bearing capacity is asymmetric.
- **Correlated failure modes.** An AV-2 outage simultaneously disables prevention *and* the evidence base; packages whose controls fail together are worse than their arithmetic suggests.
- **Deterrence and moral hazard.** Visible pre-commitment independent review deters; a purely retrospective audit invites rationalization ("we'll fix it if caught"). Equal expected loss today diverges over time as behavior adapts.
- **Normalization of deviance.** Frequent unpunished exceptions raise the underlying probability *p* itself, so static expected-loss comparisons embed a decaying assumption.
- **Defensibility.** Regulators and auditors typically require equivalence in *design* (structural safeguards), not actuarial equivalence of outcomes.

So the operative standard is: **equivalence in kind plus a comparable loss distribution**, with expected loss as one supporting metric.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control (mode) | Class | Decisive timing | Threat coverage | Overrideable? | Collusion-resistant? | Corrective reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FEP-1 dual-human (nominal) | Preventive (structural) | Before release | Broad — any threat needing a second judgment | n/a | **No** — defeated by collusion, credential sharing, dependency | Prevents; does not correct |
| AV-2 (blocking) | Preventive (automated) | Pre-release | Duplicates, sanctions, ownership, 7-day change, limits, basic PO match | Hard block (limited override) | No (rule-based) | Stops completion entirely |
| AV-2 (warning) | Detective + conditional preventive | Pre-release | Same subset | Yes — manager override | No | Enables a stop before settlement *if* the override is honest |
| MG-4 pre-release | Preventive | Before Release_Payment | Reasonableness, receipt, bank details — *only if actually reviewed* | Rubber-stamp risk | Only if genuinely independent | Blocks before execution |
| MG-4 post-release, pre-settlement | **Hybrid** | Before settlement | Same as above | Rubber-stamp risk | Only if genuinely independent | Can halt/cancel before irrevocability |
| MG-4 post-settlement | Detective only | After irrevocability | Same | n/a | No | Forensic only |
| RA-7 within ~24h | Detective + corrective | Recall window | Broad retrospective | n/a | Pattern detection (partial) | Recall usually possible |
| RA-7 after window | Detective / forensic | Post-recall | Broad retrospective | n/a | Pattern detection (partial) | Recovery uncertain to nil |
| ED-1 | Meta-control (governed exception) | Spans lifecycle | Only as strong as conditions 5–7 | Conditions *are* the override | No | Inherits MG-4/RA-7 properties |

**On the hybrid case — manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement.** I adopt **loss-horizon semantics**: a control is "preventive" if it can still alter the outcome at the moment the loss becomes irreversible, and "detective" if it can only establish facts afterward. The alternative, activity-horizon semantics (classify by whether the controlled *activity* has already occurred), would label AP-201's and AP-207's ratifications "detective" — technically true about the release act, but decision-useless, because the entire corrective value of those ratifications lies in the settlement that had *not yet happened*. Under loss-horizon semantics, ratification in the release-to-settlement gap is **hybrid**: detective toward the release decision already taken, preventive toward the loss event still avoidable. Two consequences follow. First, class  sufficiency: a ratification one minute before settlement is preventive in class yet practically fragile. Second, the same activity migrates across classes purely with the clock — which is exactly why settlement timestamps belong in the compliance record alongside approval timestamps.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

**AP-202.**
- *Most plausible loss mechanism:* insider-directed misdirection — bank details changed 2 hours before payment to a relative-linked account; the only preventive tripwire (AV-2 warning) was silenced with a supervisor's credential; the conflicted supervisor then ratified, laundering the authorization.
- *Chain-breaking control:* none fired. The controls that *should* break it: hard block on (recent bank change × new vendor) combinations; cryptographically personal credentials (making borrowing technically impossible); prohibition on overrides by anyone in the payer's reporting line.
- *Still exposed:* cross-unit collusion, social engineering of legitimate vendors, and any scheme where the override-holder and preparer coordinate.
- *Truly independent second releaser:* **insufficient by itself** — if the pair colludes or the second person rubber-stamps, four-eyes adds theater. It helps only when paired with substantive verification duties and rotation.

**AP-204.**
- *Mechanism:* payment against a fabricated service receipt — the four-eyes structure was formally satisfied by two actors whose independence is doubtful (37 joint payments, one workstation).
- *Chain breaker:* RA-7 audit, on day 4 — detective, late, with recovery uncertain.
- *Still exposed:* the entire pre-audit window; any period where audit coverage is sampled rather than universal; mid-value payments below audit-priority thresholds.
- *Independent releaser:* a second actor with an explicit receipt-inspection duty and a separate workstation likely breaks the chain pre-release. The current pair structure breaks nothing.

**AP-208.**
- *Mechanism:* classic bank-change fraud window — AV outage + 3-day-old bank change + unsigned manual fallback + post-settlement ratification. Nothing in the executed package could distinguish this case from the fraud scenario; legitimacy was luck.
- *Chain breaker (should exist):* an automatic hold rule — outage OR recent bank change  no release above a threshold without out-of-band callback verification of account ownership.
- *Still exposed:* the full EUR 900k as an irrecoverable tail; additionally, every future outage becomes an advertised fraud window (moral hazard).
- *Independent releaser:* helps only if empowered and *required* to perform out-of-band verification — a co-signer without verification duty reproduces this exact failure.

**AP-201.**
- *Mechanism:* ordinary human error by a solo operator (typo'd amount or beneficiary) — the mundane threat FEP-1 primarily targets at low values.
- *Chain breaker:* m04's pre-settlement ratification plus the AV-2 pass; recall remained possible until 10:00.
- *Still exposed:* the 08:00–08:30 sole-control window, and counterfactually, any repetition where settlement precedes ratification. As an illustrative (not measured) figure: if convenience-driven same-user handling occurs even 1–2% of the time and settlement timing slips in a fraction of those, the aggregate small-loss stream becomes material.
- *Independent releaser:* eliminates the window entirely and — more importantly — removes the normalization signal that unauthorized same-user handling is cost-free.

**AP-206.**
- *Mechanism:* retroactive emergency rationalization — a token minted *after* the commitment decision suggests either logging latency or a culture where condition 1 is backfilled. If backfilling is possible, ED-1's first condition is decorative.
- *Chain breaker:* m11's pre-release, pre-settlement approval — the strongest link in this case's chain.
- *Still exposed:* token governance itself; a mint-on-demand token makes condition 1 unfalsifiable.
- *Independent releaser:* marginal preventive gain (the manager already front-ran the release); the real gain is removing the same-user default so emergencies are exceptional by friction, not by paperwork.

**AP-209.**
- *Mechanism:* slow-burn collusion — each payment individually clean, with exploitation living in aggregate choices (vendor steering, kickbacks) rather than per-case fabrication. This is the pattern AP-204's fabrication made plausible.
- *Chain breaker:* currently none; candidates are pair-rotation policy, workstation binding, and periodic independent re-review of the pair's portfolio.
- *Still exposed:* the pair's full payment history, which should be treated as a review population, not a clean record.
- *Independent releaser/rotation:* breaks the stable-reciprocity structure that makes long-run collusion sustainable. Note the competing benign hypothesis (a deliberately designed two-person cell) — resolving it requires organizational records, not more transaction data.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

For each metric: what triggers an **alert** (warrants review) versus what would upgrade it to a **finding** (corroborated conclusion). An alert is a statistical anomaly; proof requires convergent evidence — interviews, document forensics, device logs, HR records.

1. **Same-user rate.** Monthly share of completed payments with identical human preparer and releaser, split into ED-1-documented vs. undocumented. *Alert:* any undocumented occurrence (as in AP-201) or upward trend. *Upgrade to finding:* pattern persistence after coaching, or coincidence with settlements preceding ratification.
2. **Override rate and override provenance.** Overrides per 1,000 validations; separately flag overrides following bank-change warnings, and any session/device evidence that the overriding actor  credential owner (AP-202's u23/m09 pattern). *Alert:* credential-actor mismatch or rising override density. *Finding:* corroborating device/IP logs plus witness or documentary evidence of deliberate sharing.
3. **Late manager approval.** Distribution of (ratify  release) and (ratify  settlement); percentage post-settlement; per-manager aging league table; dwell-time analysis (seconds-long approvals suggest rubber-stamping). *Alert:* post-settlement ratifications or implausibly short dwell times. *Finding:* manager-specific pattern plus evidence the supporting documents were never opened.
4. **Audit-before-recall-window.** Percentage of emergency/same-user payments audited 1 business day; percentage audited within 24h; maintain a standing KPI of "audited too late to recall" — this is quantified unrecoverable exposure (AP-202, AP-208).
5. **Repeated resource pairs.** Pairwise prepare/release co-occurrence against a random-assignment baseline (illustrative threshold: flag pairs exceeding ~3 above expected frequency, or >k joint payments per 30 days). *Alert:* the u12/u13 pattern. *Finding:* requires org-design records, workload models, or admissions — a dedicated cell is a benign explanation that must be confirmed, not assumed away.
6. **Shared workstations/devices.** Terminal or device-fingerprint equality across the two roles within a case. Treat as *independence-degrading evidence*: it lowers the evidentiary weight of four-eyes conformance (AP-204, AP-209) without proving misconduct.
7. **Bank-change proximity.** Any payment within 7 days of a bank-detail change, sub-flagged for new vendor (<180 days), first-time payment, or amount spike versus the vendor's median. Recommended hard rule: cooling-off period plus callback verification. *Alert:* the combination itself. *Finding:* out-of-band verification failure or beneficiary-link discoveries (as in AP-202).
8. **Control outages.** Track AV-2 downtime minutes × exposed payment volume; auto-hold above a value threshold during outages; treat unsigned manual checklists as *control-not-performed*, triggering an alert automatically (AP-208 should have generated one in real time).

Cross-cutting discipline: publish exception statistics to prevent normalization of deviance; require written, pre-approved justification for every same-user completion; and re-baseline thresholds quarterly so controls do not silently decay.

**PM4Py version caveats I cannot guarantee:**

- **Retention direction of `filter_four_eyes_principle`.** Across pm4py 2.x builds, the four-eyes filter has been documented/implemented both as "keep cases where the two activities were done by *different* resources" and, in effect, its complement depending on version and parameter handling. The code therefore computes the violation set manually and *empirically determines* which interpretation your installed version implements, rather than trusting either.
- **`format_dataframe` signatures.** Keyword names (`resource_key`, `timest_format`) have been added, renamed, or deprecated between versions. The code pre-parses timestamps with pandas (removing format-string dependence) and falls back to a default-argument call on `TypeError`.
- **Timezone handling.** Some pm4py internals reject tz-aware timestamps; the code coerces to naive UTC defensively. If your version expects aware timestamps, the coercion is harmless for ordering computations used here.
- **Business-day proxy.** The 33-hour audit proxy assumes Monday releases and no holidays; production code should use a real business-calendar (`numpy.busday_count` or equivalent).
- **Settlement coverage.** `Bank_Settlement` events exist only for some cases; manager-before-settlement booleans are therefore transcribed from the case-facts table, not derived solely from the log. Treating missing settlement events as "no settlement" would silently corrupt the timing analysis — a general lesson for log-only conformance checking.
