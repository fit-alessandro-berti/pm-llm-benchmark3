## 3. Robust redesign package

These interventions remain effective across multiple scenarios without blindly adding permanent capacity everywhere:

* **R1: Metadata-Gated Registration Automation + 9h Clerk Shift Cap (Modified I8)**
  * *Design:* Automate registration exclusively for orders arriving with complete, validated digital metadata, and enforce a strict 9h shift cap on `Registration clerks` (redeploying saved registration hours to handle exception queues and physician callbacks).
  * *Why robust:* Cuts `Registration` utilization (85% peak) and directly eliminates fatigue-induced transcription errors that feed the 2-person `Manual accession correction` bottleneck (14h queue) and pre-analytical failures across all scenarios.
  * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes 60% of incoming orders carry clean digital metadata and upstream LIS checks reject malformed fields at source. Fails to relieve total cycle time if downstream `Pre-analytical check` (96%) and `Assay` (98%) are not simultaneously protected (R2, R3).
* **R2: Early Pre-Analytical Defect Triage (<2h) + Automated Physician Recollection Alerts (Modified I6)**
  * *Design:* Screen sample integrity, tube count, and label mismatches within 2 hours of `Receive sample`, paired with automated/template physician recollection notices (replacing manual clerk phone calls for routine rejections).
  * *Why robust:* Stops defective samples before they consume `Preparation benches`, `Assay` (98%), and `Technical validation` (101%), while breaking the vicious cycle where recollection callbacks shrink intake capacity. Crucial in S1, S2, and S4.
  * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes staff can reliably separate fixable metadata issues from true physical rejects within 2h; overly aggressive rejection would worsen the 41.6% peak cancellation rate after recollection requests.
* **R3: Courier Pickup Slot Smoothing & Wave-Aligned Pre-Analytical Staffing (I7)**
  * *Design:* Stagger routine clinic courier pickups away from the 10:00 and 17:00 cliffs (keeping Urgent pickups unbatched/express) and align `Pre-analytical check` shift starts to smoothed arrival windows.
  * *Why robust:* Cuts queue spikes at `Courier intake` (7.5h) and `Pre-analytical check` (9.5h) without adding headcount, stabilizing downstream flow into `Preparation benches` across normal and peak periods.
  * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes referring clinics accept staggered pickup windows; breaks down during severe weather/traffic disruption (S4), where arrivals re-compress regardless of schedule.
* **R4: Two-Lane Preparation & Dynamic Batch-Cap with Aging Escalation (Modified I1 + Modified I2)**
  * *Design:* Replace blanket Urgent-first (I1) and fixed 24-sample Routine batching (I2) with two preparation/dispatch lanes:
    1. *Fast-Track Lane:* Urgent cases and repeat-urgent runs, dispatched with a hard max-wait cap of 1.5h (avoiding unbounded schedule fragmentation while protecting the 18h SLA).
    2. *Batched Lane (Routine & Complex-panel):* Dynamic batch size of 12–16 OR a 4h max-wait timeout (whichever comes first), plus automatic priority escalation when Routine age reaches 48h (protecting the 72h target) or Complex-panel reaches 60h.
  * *Why robust:* Protects Urgent p90 (<18h) without starving Routine/Complex-panel or letting `Batch wait -> Assay run` explode from 9h to 22h. Works across S1, S3, and S4.
  * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Assumes prep benches can operate two parallel lane setups without major reagent changeover penalties; fails if Urgent share exceeds ~45% (overflowing Fast-Track) or if Assay goes offline (S2).
* **R5: Off-Peak Cross-Training Buffer for Technical Validation & Accession (Modified I4)**
  * *Design:* Execute the 4-week cross-training during **normal periods** (when `Technical validation` is at 76% and `Accession correction` is at 62%) in *both* directions: cross-train senior pre-analytical/prep technicians to cover `Manual accession correction`, and cross-train float/backup lab staff into `Technical validation` for Routine assays.
  * *Why robust:* Avoids I4's fatal flaw (pulling from 101%-utilized `Technical validation` during peaks) while eliminating the 2-specialist single-point-of-failure at Accession and creating surge headroom at `Technical validation`.
  * *Assumptions & failure modes:* Requires 4 weeks of lead time before winter waves and regulatory sign-off for Routine validation scope; vulnerable if multiple validators fall ill simultaneously before cross-trained backups maintain proficiency.

  * *Deactivate OFF:* `Specialist review` queue < 8h AND `Medical validation` queue < 6h for 72h.
  * *Target scenarios:* S1, S3 (and S2 during prolonged M1 outage). Accepts the 12h transport delay only when internal specialist/medical validation queues exceed 18–48h.

  * *Activate ON:* Assay utilization > 85% OR rolling 24h `Assay -> Repeat assay` rate > 10% OR M1/M2 hardware fault.

  * *Target scenarios:* S2, S1.

Because Routine p90 already reaches 120h (vs. 72h target) in peaks and `Medical validation -> Release result` is only 2h normal / 5h peak, holding Routine releases adds waiting without relieving `Assay` or `Technical validation`. ## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with the robust package and conditional policies in place, four fragilities remain—including vulnerabilities introduced by the redesign itself:

1. **Redesign-Induced Surge Amplification (Upstream Speed-Up Eliminates Natural Buffering):**
   * R1 (automated registration), R2 (2h triage), and R3 (smoothed intake) intentionally remove upstream queues. While this reduces cycle time and rework, it removes the "shock absorber" that previously slowed arrivals into the lab floor. If an unforecasted infection spike (S1) coincides with an M1 outage (S2), clean samples hit `Preparation benches` and `Assay` at full velocity, creating rapid floor WIP saturation before P2 (outsourcing) can ramp up.
2. **Redesign-Induced Traceability & Split-Order Friction (Partner Outsourcing, P2):**
   * Routing overflow Complex-panel orders to a partner lab (12h transport delay) relieves `Specialist review` (36h peak) and `Assay`, but creates chain-of-custody vulnerabilities for multi-tube orders (e.g., if one tube runs internally and another ships externally), risking asynchronous `Medical validation` or transit-induced sample degradation that triggers late recollections.
3. **Redesign-Induced Lane Oscillation & Aging Waves (R4):**
   * If the 48h aging escalation threshold in the Batched Lane triggers for a large cohort of Routine samples simultaneously (e.g., right after recovering from a temporary Assay slowdown), those escalated Routine orders flood the Fast-Track Lane at the exact moment new Urgent orders arrive—briefly recreating the Urgent-starvation or fragmentation dynamics R4 was designed to prevent.
4. **External Recollection Latency & Compound Disruption Ceiling:**
   * Even with <2h pre-analytical rejection (R2), the `Request recollection -> Receive sample` loop (42h normal / 60h peak) depends on external physicians and patients. Poor field sampling cannot be fixed inside the lab and will continue producing ~40% cancellations after recollection requests. Likewise, a simultaneous prolonged M1+M2 failure during a peak infection wave exceeds any internal scheduling fix and requires pre-contracted Assay overflow capacity.

3. **Pre-Analytical Defect & Accession-Correction Rate (% of intake) + Clerk Overtime Hours**
   * *Bands:* **Green** <7% defects and 0 shifts >9h | **Amber** 7–10% or any shift >9h | **Red** >10%
   * *Why:* Early indicator of S4 quality shocks and incoming 60h recollection loops before `Manual accession correction` (2 specialists) backs up.
4. **WIP Aging & Bottleneck Headroom (% WIP >6h at Prep/Assay; >8h at Tech Val / Med Val / Specialist Hold)**
   * *Bands:* **Green** <15% aged (Tech Val util <85%) | **Amber** 15–25% (Tech Val util 85–95%) | **Red** >25% (Tech Val util >95% or Med Val queue >10h)
   * *Why:* Directly triggers P1 (evening Med Val shift) and P2 (Complex-panel outsourcing) with enough lead time to prevent Urgent p90 >18h breaches.
5. **Courier Arrival Concentration (% of daily samples arriving within peak 2-hour window)**
   * *Bands:* **Green** <35% | **Amber** 35–50% | **Red** >50%
   * *Why:* Spots S4 courier compression at the dock immediately so pre-analytical triage (R2) can rebalance staffing that same day.
